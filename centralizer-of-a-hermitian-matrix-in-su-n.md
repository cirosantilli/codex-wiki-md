<h1 id="centralizer-of-a-hermitian-matrix-in-su-n">Centralizer of a Hermitian matrix in SU(n)</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Centralizer of a subset](centralizer-of-a-subset.md)

Let a [Hermitian matrix](hermitian-operator.md) have distinct real [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) with multiplicities $n_1,\ldots,n_k$. A [unitary matrix](unitary-matrix.md) commuting with it must preserve each [eigenspace](eigenspace.md): if $Av=\lambda v$, then $A(Uv)=UAv=\lambda Uv$. Conversely, any unitary transformation acting independently on these mutually orthogonal eigenspaces commutes with $A$. The [centralizer](centralizer.md) in $U(n)$ is therefore $\prod_jU(n_j)$. Imposing total determinant one gives the displayed subgroup inside the [special unitary group](special-unitary-group.md) $SU(n)$.

For multiplicities $(2,1)$ in [SU(3)](su-3-group.md), it is $S(U(2)\times U(1))\cong U(2)$, embedded by $U\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(U,(\det U)^{-1})$. Three distinct eigenvalues leave the diagonal [maximal torus](maximal-torus.md) $U(1)^2$. This determines the unbroken group for a diagonal [adjoint scalar field](adjoint-scalar-field.md) vacuum, including its global determinant constraint.
