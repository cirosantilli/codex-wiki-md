# Spectral projection of a diagonalizable matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Spectral decomposition](spectral-decomposition.md)

For a [diagonalizable matrix](diagonalizable-matrix.md) with distinct [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda,\mu,\ldots$, this polynomial is the [projection operator](projection-linear-algebra.md) onto the [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) for $\lambda$ along the direct sum of the other [eigenspaces](eigenspace.md). It is one on its own [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) and zero on the others. Consequently $P_\lambda P_\mu=0$ for different [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md), $\sum P_\lambda=I$ and $A=\sum\lambda P_\lambda$. These projections need not be [orthogonal projections](orthogonal-projection.md); that stronger property holds for a [normal matrix](normal-matrix.md). This is the [polynomial interpolation](polynomial-interpolation.md) form of the [spectral decomposition](spectral-decomposition.md).
