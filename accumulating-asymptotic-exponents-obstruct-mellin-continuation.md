# Accumulating asymptotic exponents obstruct Mellin continuation

↑ **Parent:** [Meromorphic continuation of a Mellin transform from an asymptotic expansion](meromorphic-continuation-of-a-mellin-transform-from-an-asymptotic-expansion.md)

Strict increase alone does not suffice in the preceding theorem. Let $\sigma_j=1-1/j$, $c_j=2^{-j}$ and $f(y)=\eta(y)\sum_{j\geq1}2^{-j}y^{1-1/j}$, where $\eta$ is continuous, equals one on $(0,1]$ and vanishes on $[2,\infty)$. After division by the next power, every finite remainder extends continuously to zero. Nevertheless the [Mellin transform](mellin-transform.md) has genuine poles at $-\sigma_j$ accumulating at $-1$, so it cannot be a [meromorphic function](meromorphic-function.md) on the whole plane.
