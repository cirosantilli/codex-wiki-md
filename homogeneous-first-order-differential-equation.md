# Homogeneous first-order differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md)

A [homogeneous first-order differential equation](homogeneous-first-order-differential-equation.md) has the form $y'=F(y/x)$ on a domain where $x\ne0$. The substitution $v=y/x$, so $y'=v+xv'$, reduces it to

$$
xv'=F(v)-v.
$$

Where $F(v)-v\ne0$, this is a [separable differential equation](separable-differential-equation.md), with $\int dv/[F(v)-v]=\ln|x|+C$. Values $v_0$ satisfying $F(v_0)=v_0$ give the additional straight-line solutions $y=v_0x$, which must be checked before division. This scaling-based use of “homogeneous” is different from a [homogeneous linear differential equation](homogeneous-linear-differential-equation.md).

For example, $y'=y/x+x/y$ gives $F(v)=v+1/v$, so $vv'=1/x$ and $y^2=x^2(2\ln|x|+C)$. Real explicit branches are $y=\pm x\sqrt{2\ln|x|+C}$ on [intervals](interval-mathematics.md) where the expression under the square root is positive. Zeros of a denominator and endpoints of a branch must still be checked in the original [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md).
