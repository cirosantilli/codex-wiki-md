# Exoplanet

↑ **Parent:** [Astrophysics](astrophysics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanet)

An [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) is a [planet](planet.md) outside the [Solar system](solar-system.md); here the focus is on [planets](planet.md) orbiting [stars](star.md) other than the [Sun](sun.md), including [pulsars](pulsar.md). Most [exoplanets](exoplanet-split.md) are detected indirectly: a [star](star.md)'s changing [Doppler shift](doppler-effect.md) reveals its motion, or a small dip in its [light curve](light-curve.md) reveals an [exoplanet transit](exoplanet-transit.md). The striking idea is that an unresolved point of light can tell us about an otherwise unseen world's [mass](mass.md), [radius](radius.md), and [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md).

The measurements answer different questions. [Exoplanet transit photometry](exoplanet-transit-photometry.md) primarily gives a [radius](radius.md) ratio, while [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md) constrains [mass](mass.md) together with [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md). Combining them with the host [star](star.md)'s properties gives a mean [mass density](density.md), $\bar\rho_p=3M_p/(4\pi R_p^3)$, useful for [exoplanet interior](exoplanet-interior.md) models. Even this combination does not uniquely determine composition: see [exoplanet interior-composition degeneracy](exoplanet-interior-composition-degeneracy.md). [Transit spectroscopy](transit-spectroscopy.md) and an [exoplanet secondary eclipse](exoplanet-secondary-eclipse.md) then probe an [exoplanet atmosphere](exoplanet-atmosphere.md).

The discovery history has two distinct milestones. In 1992 Aleksander Wolszczan and Dale Frail reported two [planet](planet.md)-mass companions to the [pulsar](pulsar.md) PSR B1257+12 from periodic changes in pulse arrival times; their paper also suggested an additional companion. These were the first confirmed [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) discoveries, around a [neutron star](neutron-star.md). [Wolszczan and Frail (1992)](https://www.nature.com/articles/355145a0). Wolszczan's contemporary conference account, archived by NASA, likewise distinguishes the two reported companions from a possible third. [Wolszczan (1992), NASA archive](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19920021993).

In 1995 Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz discovered 51 Pegasi b through [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md), the landmark first confirmed [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) around a [Sun](sun.md)-like [star](star.md). This was a [hot Jupiter](hot-jupiter.md), not the first [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md) of every kind: the earlier [pulsar](pulsar.md) discoveries belong to a different host category. [Mayor and Queloz (1995)](a-jupiter-mass-companion-to-a-solar-type-star-by-michel-mayor-and-didier-queloz.md). NASA's contemporary account describes the confirmation and the approximately four-day [orbital period](orbital-period.md). [NASA account (December 1995)](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-1995-december-01-51-pegasi-a-new-planet-discovered/).
