# Hausdorff factorization through a cofinite product factor

↑ **Parent:** [Continuous maps from an infinite cofinite space to a Hausdorff space are constant](continuous-maps-from-an-infinite-cofinite-space-to-a-hausdorff-space-are-constant.md)

Let $Z$ be a nonempty infinite [set](set-split.md) with the [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md), let $T$ be any [topological space](topological-space.md), and let $Y$ be [Hausdorff](hausdorff-space.md). For a [continuous map](continuous-map.md) $F:Z\times T\to Y$, every slice $z\mapsto F(z,t)$ is constant. Thus $F(z,t)=g(t)$, where $g(t)=F(z_0,t)$ for any fixed $z_0$. The map $g$ is [continuous](continuous-function.md), since $t\mapsto(z_0,t)$ is [continuous](continuous-function.md) into the [product topology](product-topology.md). It is unique because the [coordinate projection](coordinate-projection.md) $\pi_T$ is a [surjection](surjective-function.md). If $F$ is a [surjection](surjective-function.md), so is $g$.

For $T=[0,1]$ and $Y$ [homeomorphic](homeomorphism.md) to the [circle](circle.md), a surjective $g$ cannot be injective: otherwise the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md) would contradict the fact that the [circle and interval are not homeomorphic](circle-and-interval-are-not-homeomorphic.md). Hence distinct $a,b\in[0,1]$ satisfy $F(z,a)=F(z,b)$ simultaneously for all $z\in Z$.
