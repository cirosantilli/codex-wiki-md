# Characteristic function

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function_(probability_theory))

The characteristic function of a real [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$ is

$$
\varphi_X(t)=\mathbb E[e^{itX}].
$$

It determines the [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) of $X$.

Here $t\in\mathbb R$ and $i^2=-1$. By [Euler's formula](euler-s-formula.md), its [real part](real-part.md) and [imaginary part](imaginary-part.md) are $\mathbb E[\cos(tX)]$ and $\mathbb E[\sin(tX)]$. The [complex exponential](complex-exponential-function.md) has absolute value one, so this [expected value](expected-value-split.md) exists for every real [random variable](random-variable-split.md), even without finite [absolute moments](absolute-moment.md). Moreover,

$$
\varphi_X(0)=1,\qquad |\varphi_X(t)|\leq\mathbb E[|e^{itX}|]=1,\qquad
\varphi_X(-t)=\overline{\varphi_X(t)}.
$$

The bound uses the [triangle inequality for a vector-valued integral](triangle-inequality-for-a-vector-valued-integral.md); the last identity is [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) symmetry.[§3.3.1 definition, printed p. 125; Theorem 3.3.1(a)–(c), printed p. 126](probability-theory-and-examples-fifth-edition-by-rick-durrett.md) Consequently the [real part](real-part.md) is an [even function](even-function.md) and the [imaginary part](imaginary-part.md) is an [odd function](odd-function.md).

For a [discrete probability distribution](discrete-probability-distribution-split.md) with values $x_j$ and [probabilities](probability.md) $p_j$, the [characteristic function](characteristic-function-split.md) is $\sum_jp_je^{itx_j}$: a weighted average of points on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) in the [complex plane](complex-plane.md). For example, if $B$ has the [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md) with $\mathbb P(B=1)=1/3$ and $\mathbb P(B=0)=2/3$, then

$$
\varphi_B(t)=\frac23+\frac13e^{it},\qquad
\varphi_B(2\pi/3)=\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{6}i,\qquad
|\varphi_B(2\pi/3)|=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
$$

Each point being averaged has absolute value one, but their directions partially cancel. At $t=\pi$, the same average is $1/3$; at $t=2\pi$, it returns to one. The [characteristic function](characteristic-function-split.md) is thus neither a [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) nor a [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md): its argument is a frequency parameter, and its values can be [complex numbers](complex-number.md). This [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md) has no [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) with respect to [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md), yet its [characteristic function](characteristic-function-split.md) is defined at every frequency.

<a id="image-a-bernoulli-characteristic-function-as-a-weighted-average-on-the-unit-circle"></a>
![](characteristic-function-weighted-phasors.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-a-bernoulli-characteristic-function-as-a-weighted-average-on-the-unit-circle). A Bernoulli characteristic function as a weighted average on the unit circle**. At $t=2\pi/3$, the two [complex exponentials](complex-exponential-function.md) are $1$ and $e^{it}$. Left: their weighted average lies one third of the way along their connecting segment. Right: adding the weighted arrows $2/3$ and $e^{it}/3$ gives $\varphi_B(t)=1/2+i\sqrt3/6$. The dashed [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) makes the absolute bound one visible.

For a [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) $f$, the same definition becomes $\varphi_X(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}f(x)\,dx$, the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) with the displayed sign convention. The [Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable](laplace-transform-of-a-nonnegative-random-variable.md) instead uses $e^{-sX}$ for $s\geq0$, a real decaying weight. For the [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md) above, it is $2/3+e^{-s}/3$; neither transform is the [probability density function](probability-density-function.md). The [uniqueness theorem for characteristic functions](uniqueness-theorem-for-characteristic-functions.md) explains why the oscillating averages still retain the entire [probability law](probability-distribution-split.md), while the [characteristic function of a sum of independent variables](characteristic-function-of-a-sum-of-independent-variables.md) turns combining laws into multiplication. These two properties make the representation useful even when directly computing a sum's [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) is cumbersome.
