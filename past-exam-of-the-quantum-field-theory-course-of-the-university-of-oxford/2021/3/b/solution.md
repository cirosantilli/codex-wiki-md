<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $L$ be the number of independent [loop momenta](../../../../loop-momentum.md) in a connected [Feynman diagram](../../../../feynman-diagram.md). Every [scalar propagator](../../../../scalar-propagator.md) falls as $k^{-2}$, and every [loop momentum](../../../../loop-momentum.md) integration contributes three powers, so $\omega=3L-2e$. Counting line ends and using connectedness gives

$$
4n_4+6n_6=2e+n,\qquad L=e-n_4-n_6+1.
$$

Eliminating $L$ and $e$ yields

$$
\boxed{\omega=3(e-n_4-n_6+1)-2e=3-\frac n2-n_4.}
$$

Thus increasing the sextic [interaction vertex](../../../../interaction-vertex.md) count does not worsen the [superficial degree of divergence](../../../../superficial-degree-of-divergence.md); adding a quartic [interaction vertex](../../../../interaction-vertex.md) improves it. Only finitely many external-line structures can have an overall [ultraviolet divergence](../../../../ultraviolet-divergence.md), even though arbitrarily high [loop order](../../../../loop-order.md) is possible.

After subtracting [ultraviolet subdivergences](../../../../ultraviolet-subdivergence.md), locality and [Lorentz invariance](../../../../lorentz-invariance.md) restrict the divergent [counterterms](../../../../counterterm.md) to a constant [vacuum energy density](../../../../vacuum-energy-density.md), a [mass counterterm](../../../../mass-counterterm.md), a [wave-function renormalization](../../../../wave-function-renormalization.md), and quartic and sextic [field interaction terms](../../../../field-interaction-term.md). Odd powers are forbidden by the $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ [Z2 symmetry](../../../../z2-symmetry.md). In particular, $n=2$ permits a constant and $p^2$, $n=4$ permits only a constant, and $n=6$ permits only a constant. This is perturbative [renormalizability](../../../../renormalizable-quantum-field-theory.md), not a guarantee that every superficially allowed divergence actually occurs or that every unrenormalized graph with $\omega<0$ lacks divergent subgraphs.
