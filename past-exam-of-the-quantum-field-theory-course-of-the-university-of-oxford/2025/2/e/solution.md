<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For $g=0$, the [invariant scattering amplitude](../../../../invariant-scattering-amplitude.md) from the [tree-level Feynman diagram](../../../../tree-level-feynman-diagram.md) in [i](../c/i.md) has a [pole](../../../../pole.md) at $s=M^2$. The intermediate heavy [antiparticle](../../../../antiparticle.md) can go on shell and live for a time of order its [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md). Near this [scattering resonance](../../../../scattering-resonance.md), inserting a single [self-energy](../../../../self-energy.md) is no longer a uniformly small correction. [Dyson resummation](../../../../dyson-resummation.md) gives, to leading accuracy in a narrow [decay width](../../../../decay-width.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathcal M(s)\simeq-\frac{h^2}{s-M^2+iM\Gamma_{\rm phys}}.}
$$

Here $M$ is understood as the [renormalized](../../../../renormalization.md) resonance mass; the real [self-energy](../../../../self-energy.md) shifts the original pole position. The imaginary term removes the unphysical divergence of the fixed-order [invariant scattering amplitude](../../../../invariant-scattering-amplitude.md).

For equal incoming and outgoing light masses, the two-body [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) ratio cancels in the [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md). The angle-independent [invariant scattering amplitude](../../../../invariant-scattering-amplitude.md), integrated over the full solid angle with the [identical final-state symmetry factor](../../../../identical-particle-factor-in-a-final-state-phase-space-integral.md) $1/2!$, gives $\sigma=|\mathcal M|^2/(32\pi s)$. Thus the [Breit-Wigner resonance cross section](../../../../breit-wigner-resonance-cross-section.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(s)\simeq\frac{h^4}{32\pi s\left[(s-M^2)^2+M^2\Gamma_{\rm phys}^2\right]},\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm phys}=\frac{h^2\beta}{32\pi M}.}
$$

At $s\gg M^2$ it reproduces $h^4/(32\pi s^3)$. At the resonance peak,

$$
\boxed{\sigma(M^2)\simeq\frac{32\pi}{M^2\beta^2}\simeq\frac{32\pi}{M^2}\quad(M\gg m).}
$$

This is also $8\pi/k_*^2$ for $k_*=M\beta/2$, the elastic identical-boson [S wave](../../../../s-wave.md) [partial-wave unitarity](../../../../partial-wave-unitarity.md) limit when this is the only open [decay channel](../../../../decay-channel.md). The approximation assumes $\Gamma_{\rm phys}\ll M$ and a resonance sufficiently far from threshold for the width to vary slowly across it.

If one mechanically uses the unsymmetrized $\Gamma_{\rm printed}=2\Gamma_{\rm phys}$ from [d](../d.md), the same displayed [Breit-Wigner resonance cross section](../../../../breit-wigner-resonance-cross-section.md) would have that doubled width and predict a peak $8\pi/(M^2\beta^2)$. This is the literal-width substitution, rather than the consistent physical result: the given high-energy [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) already includes the identical-final-particle factor that the printed [decay width](../../../../decay-width.md) integral omits.
