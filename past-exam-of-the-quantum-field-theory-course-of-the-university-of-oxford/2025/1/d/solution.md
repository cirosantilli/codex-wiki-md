<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Writing $x_P=(t,-\mathbf x)$, the standard [parity transformation of a Dirac field](../../../../parity-transformation-of-a-dirac-field.md) is

$$
\boxed{\psi'(x)=\eta_P\gamma^0\psi(x_P),\qquad \bar\psi'(x)=\eta_P^*\bar\psi(x_P)\gamma^0,\qquad |\eta_P|=1.}
$$

Since $\gamma^0\gamma^5\gamma^0=-\gamma^5$, the [fermion bilinear](../../../../fermion-bilinear.md) $\bar\psi\gamma^5\psi$ changes sign. Invariance of the [pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction](../../../../pseudoscalar-yukawa-interaction.md) therefore requires

$$
\boxed{\phi'(x)=-\phi(x_P).}
$$

Thus $\phi$ is a [pseudoscalar](../../../../pseudoscalar.md), despite having the usual scalar-form [kinetic term](../../../../kinetic-term.md) and [mass term](../../../../mass-term.md). In this convention $(\gamma^5)^\dagger=-\gamma^5$, but $\bar\psi\gamma^5\psi$ is Hermitian because of the additional $\gamma^0$ in the [Dirac adjoint](../../../../dirac-adjoint.md). A real $g$ is consequently consistent with a Hermitian [Lagrangian density](../../../../lagrangian-density.md).

In [perturbation theory](../../../../perturbation-theory.md) about the parity-invariant [vacuum state](../../../../vacuum-state.md), [renormalization](../../../../renormalization.md) can preserve this non-anomalous [parity symmetry in quantum field theory](../../../../parity-symmetry-in-quantum-field-theory.md). Every local [counterterm](../../../../counterterm.md) must then be parity even, at every [loop order](../../../../loop-order.md). The allowed terms are precisely $\mathcal L_{\rm ct}^{(+)}$ in [b](../b.md); all coefficients in $\mathcal L_{\rm ct}^{(-)}$ are zero. In particular, **no $\phi$ or $\phi^3$ counterterm is generated**, whereas $\phi^4$ is allowed and required. The one-point and three-point pure-boson vertices also vanish to all orders: a parity-odd [Lorentz scalar](../../../../lorentz-scalar.md) cannot be formed from their at most two independent external [four-momenta](../../../../four-momentum.md). This last reasoning is specific to these low-point vertices; it does not assert that [parity symmetry in quantum field theory](../../../../parity-symmetry-in-quantum-field-theory.md) is an independent $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ internal symmetry with the fermions held fixed.
