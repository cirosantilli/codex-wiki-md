<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Treat $\phi$ and $\phi^*$ as independent variables in the [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md). Write $s=\phi^*\phi$, and assume the [scalar potential](../../../../scalar-potential.md) $f(s)$ is real and differentiable. Variation with respect to $\phi^*$ gives

$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^*}=-f'(s)\phi,\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}=\partial^\mu\phi.
$$

Consequently the equations for the [complex scalar field](../../../../complex-scalar-field.md) and its [complex conjugate](../../../../complex-conjugate.md) are

$$
\boxed{\Box\phi+f'(\phi^*\phi)\phi=0,\qquad
\Box\phi^*+f'(\phi^*\phi)\phi^*=0.}
$$

No polynomial assumption is needed: only the first derivative of the [scalar potential](../../../../scalar-potential.md) enters. For $f(s)=m^2s$, both reduce to the free [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../../klein-gordon-equation.md).
