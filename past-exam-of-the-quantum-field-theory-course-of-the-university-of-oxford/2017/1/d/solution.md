<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The two oscillator families commute with each other. Their [canonical commutation relations](../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) give

$$
[Q,b_{\mathbf p}]=+b_{\mathbf p},\qquad
[Q,c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger]=+c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,
\qquad
[Q,b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger]=-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,\qquad
[Q,c_{\mathbf p}]=-c_{\mathbf p}.
$$

Both terms in the [mode expansion of a free field](../../../../mode-expansion-of-a-free-field.md) therefore acquire the same factor, giving

$$
\boxed{[Q,\phi(x)]=\phi(x),\qquad [Q,\phi^\dagger(x)]=-\phi^\dagger(x).}
$$

The [Noether charge](../../../../noether-charge.md) is the generator of the internal [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md). In the active-operator convention $U(\alpha)=e^{i\alpha Q}$, repeated [commutators](../../../../commutator.md) give

$$
\boxed{U(\alpha)\phi(x)U(\alpha)^{-1}=e^{i\alpha}\phi(x).}
$$

The sign agrees with part b. A negative sign for $[Q,\phi]$ would correspond to the opposite current convention, not to the current used here. Subtracting a multiple of the identity from $Q$ to fix the [vacuum](../../../../vacuum.md) [Noether charge](../../../../noether-charge.md) has no effect on this generator relation.
