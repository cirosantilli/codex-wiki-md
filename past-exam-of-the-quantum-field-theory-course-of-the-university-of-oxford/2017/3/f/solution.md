<h1 id="3/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

To first order, invert $m_0^2=m^2[1+g/(8\pi^2\epsilon)]$ and use $g=\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}+O(\lambda_0^2)$:

$$
\boxed{m^2=m_0^2\left[1-\frac{\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}}{8\pi^2\epsilon}\right]+O(\lambda_0^2).}
$$

The remainder is a perturbative statement at fixed regulator. When differentiating, hold the [bare mass](../../../../bare-mass.md) $m_0^2$ fixed as well as $\lambda_0$; otherwise the [mass](../../../../mass.md) running would not be defined by a fixed bare theory. Since $\mu\,\partial_\mu\mu^{-\epsilon}=-\epsilon\mu^{-\epsilon}$,

$$
\mu\frac{d\log m^2}{d\mu}\bigg|_{\mathrm{bare}}
=\frac{\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}}{8\pi^2}+O(g^2)
=\frac{g}{8\pi^2}+O(g^2).
$$

Thus, with the source's extra factor of one half,

$$
\boxed{\gamma_m(g)=\frac{g}{16\pi^2}+O(g^2).}
$$

The factor $-\epsilon$ from the running dimensionless [bare coupling](../../../../bare-coupling.md) cancels the [counterterm](../../../../counterterm.md) pole. Differentiating as though $g$ were independent of $\mu$ would miss this finite result. Some conventions call $\mu\,d\log m^2/d\mu$ itself the [mass](../../../../mass.md) [anomalous dimension](../../../../anomalous-dimension.md); that convention is twice the one defined here.
