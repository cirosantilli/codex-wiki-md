<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a [fixed point](../../../../fixed-point.md) $g^*$ with $\beta(g^*)=0$, put $g=g^*+h$. To first order in the displacement,

$$
\frac{dh}{d\log\mu}=\beta'(g^*)h,\qquad
h(\mu)=h(\mu_s)\left(\frac\mu{\mu_s}\right)^{\beta'(g^*)}.
$$

Hence $\beta'(g^*)<0$ means ultraviolet attraction, since $h\to0$ as $\mu\to\infty$; $\beta'(g^*)>0$ means infrared attraction, since $h\to0$ as $\mu\to0$. If the derivative vanishes, the linear test is inconclusive and higher terms determine the direction of flow.

For $\delta=4-D>0$ and $b=3/(16\pi^2)$, the retained [beta function](../../../../beta-function.md) is $-\delta g+bg^2$. Its two zeros and slopes are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
 g&\beta'(g)\\\hline
 0&-\delta\\
 \delta/b&+\delta
\end{array}.
$$

Thus the positive interacting [Wilson-Fisher fixed point](../../../../wilson-fisher-fixed-point.md) is

$$
\boxed{g^*=(4-D)\frac{16\pi^2}{3},\qquad \beta'(g^*)=4-D>0,}
$$

and is infrared attractive in the coupling direction. Couplings between zero and $g^*$ increase towards $g^*$ when the scale decreases; those just above $g^*$ decrease towards it. The Gaussian [fixed point](../../../../fixed-point.md) $g=0$ is ultraviolet attractive for $D<4$.

The conclusion is local and perturbative. For $4-D\ll1$, $bg^*=4-D$ is small, making the expansion controlled; extrapolation far below four dimensions requires more information. It is also a statement about the displayed coupling flow, not stability against every possible deformation of the full theory. In particular, the [mass term](../../../../mass-term.md) is a relevant perturbation of the critical theory and must be tuned to reach the massless critical surface. Calling this fixed point infrared stable must not erase that additional relevant direction.
