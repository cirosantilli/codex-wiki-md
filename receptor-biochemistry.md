# Receptor (biochemistry)

↑ **Parent:** [Protein](protein.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_(biochemistry))

A biochemical [receptor](receptor-biochemistry.md) is a [protein](protein.md) that recognizes a signal and couples its detection to a biological effect. Its binding partner is a [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md); recognition depends on compatible shape and chemical interactions at a binding site within the [protein structure](protein-structure.md). Specificity is selective recognition, not a guarantee that only one [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md) can bind. Binding also need not activate the [receptor](receptor-biochemistry.md): a competing [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md) may occupy it while preventing activation by another [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md). See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition: protein binding and specificity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26911/).

Compare a [cell-surface receptor](cell-surface-receptor.md), accessible to an extracellular [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md), with an [intracellular receptor](intracellular-receptor.md), whose [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md) must reach the interior. A [cell-surface receptor](cell-surface-receptor.md) can detect a [hydrophilic](hydrophilicity.md) [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md) without transporting it through the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). In contrast, some membrane-permeable signals reach [intracellular receptors](intracellular-receptor.md) that regulate [gene expression](gene-expression.md). Location and downstream coupling are separate distinctions: an [ionotropic receptor](ionotropic-receptor.md) directly gates an [ion channel](ion-channel.md), whereas a [metabotropic receptor](metabotropic-receptor.md) works through intracellular [signal transduction](signal-transduction.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 9.1: receptor locations and mechanisms](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/9-1-signaling-molecules-and-cellular-receptors).

[Receptor binding occupancy](receptor-binding-occupancy.md) quantifies recognition separately from response. A [dissociation constant](dissociation-constant.md) measures binding in a specified equilibrium model; it does not by itself predict the cellular effect. This separation is useful when comparing signals that bind equally well but produce different responses, or when interpreting a binding experiment alongside a functional measurement.
