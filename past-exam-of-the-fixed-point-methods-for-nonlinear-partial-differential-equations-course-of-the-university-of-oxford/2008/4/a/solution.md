<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

One-dimensional [Sobolev embedding](../../../../failure-of-first-order-sobolev-embedding-into-linfinity-in-two-dimensions.md) gives a continuous representative for each $v\in H_0^1(0,l)$, with zero endpoint values, so the pointwise obstacle condition is meaningful. The [positive part](../../../../positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md) $g_+=\max\{g,0\}$ is [Lipschitz](../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) and vanishes near both endpoints because $g(0),g(l)<0$. It therefore belongs to $H_0^1$ and satisfies $g_+\geq g$, proving that $M$ is nonempty.

If $v,w\in M$ and $t\in[0,1]$, then $(1-t)v+tw\geq g$, so $M$ is a [convex set](../../../../convex-set-split.md). If $v_n\to v$ in $H_0^1$, the estimate $\|v_n-v\|_\infty\leq\sqrt l\,\|v_n'-v'\|_2$ gives [uniform convergence](../../../../uniform-convergence.md). Passing to the limit in $v_n(x)\geq g(x)$ shows $v\in M$. Thus **the admissible set is nonempty, closed and convex**.
