↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Here “contractive” means [strictly distance-decreasing](../../../../strictly-distance-decreasing-map.md): $\boxed{d(Tx,Ty)<d(x,y)\text{ whenever }x\ne y}$. “Strongly contractive” means a [contraction mapping](../../../../contraction-mapping.md) with one uniform constant $q\in[0,1)$ satisfying $\boxed{d(Tx,Ty)\leq qd(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in M}$.

If distinct $p,q$ were both [fixed points](../../../../fixed-point.md), strict decrease would give $d(p,q)=d(Tp,Tq)<d(p,q)$, a contradiction. Thus there is **at most one fixed point**. This proves uniqueness if a fixed point exists; the pointwise strict inequality alone does not assert existence.
