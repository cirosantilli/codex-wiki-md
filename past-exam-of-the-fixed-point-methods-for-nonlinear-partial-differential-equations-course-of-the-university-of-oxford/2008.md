## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Here “contractive” means [strictly distance-decreasing](../convex-optimization.md#strictly-distance-decreasing-map): $\boxed{d(Tx,Ty)<d(x,y)\text{ whenever }x\ne y}$. “Strongly contractive” means a [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping) with one uniform constant $q\in[0,1)$ satisfying $\boxed{d(Tx,Ty)\leq qd(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in M}$.

If distinct $p,q$ were both [fixed points](../function.md#fixed-point), strict decrease would give $d(p,q)=d(Tp,Tq)<d(p,q)$, a contradiction. Thus there is **at most one fixed point**. This proves uniqueness if a fixed point exists; the pointwise strict inequality alone does not assert existence.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The [contraction mapping theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) states that a self-map $T$ of a nonempty [complete metric space](../topological-analysis.md#complete-metric-space) with a uniform contraction constant $q<1$ has a unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) $p$. For every starting point, the iteration $x_{n+1}=Tx_n$ converges to $p$, with $d(x_n,p)\leq q^nd(x_1,x_0)/(1-q)$.

For the [cosine fixed-point iteration](../analysis.md#cosine-fixed-point-iteration), any real $x_0$ gives $x_1\in[-1,1]$. This [closed interval](../real-analysis.md#closed-real-interval) is [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness), and cosine maps it into $[\cos1,1]\subseteq[-1,1]$. The [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) gives

$$
|\cos x-\cos y|\leq\sin(1)|x-y|\qquad(x,y\in[-1,1]),
$$

with $\sin(1)<1$. Apply the [contraction mapping theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) to the tail starting at $x_1$. Therefore $\boxed{x_n\to p,\quad p=\cos p}$ for every real initial value. Numerically $p\approx0.739085$. The global real-line [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) bound is only one; entering the [invariant interval](../dynamical-systems.md#invariant-interval) supplies the strict uniform bound needed by the theorem.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) says that every [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $T:\overline B_R(0)\to\overline B_R(0)$ has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point). To use the supplied [outward vector field zero theorem](../topological-analysis.md#outward-vector-field-zero-theorem), first extend the construction to all of $\mathbb R^n$. Let

$$
P_R(x)=\begin{cases}x,&|x|\leq R,\\Rx/|x|,&|x|>R,\end{cases}\qquad g(x)=x-T(P_R(x)).
$$

The [radial retraction onto a closed ball](../topological-analysis.md#radial-retraction-onto-a-closed-ball) $P_R$ is continuous, so $g$ is a continuous vector field on the required whole space. On $|x|=R$, the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives

$$
g(x)\cdot x=R^2-T(x)\cdot x\geq R^2-|T(x)|R\geq0.
$$

The supplied theorem gives $g(x_0)=0$ with $|x_0|\leq R$. There $P_R(x_0)=x_0$, hence $\boxed{T(x_0)=x_0}$. This explicit extension avoids presuming that the original map $T$ was defined outside the ball.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

A [nonlinear compact operator](../compact-operator.md#nonlinear-compact-operator) is a continuous map that sends [bounded sets](../topological-analysis.md#bounded-set) of its domain to [relatively compact subsets](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset) of the target space; linearity is not part of this general definition.

For the specified [diagonal operator on sequence space](../functional-analysis.md#diagonal-operator-on-sequence-space) on [l2 sequence space](../banach-space.md#l2-sequence-space),

$$
\|Tx\|_2^2=\sum_{i\geq1}\frac{|x_i|^2}{i^2}\leq\sum_{i\geq1}|x_i|^2,
$$

so it is well-defined, [linear](../vector-space.md#linearity) and [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness). Let $T_N$ retain only its first $N$ output coordinates. This is a [finite-rank operator](../compact-operator.md#finite-rank-operator), and

$$
\boxed{\|T-T_N\|=\frac1{N+1}\longrightarrow0.}
$$

The upper bound follows by estimating each tail multiplier by $1/(N+1)$; equality follows on the unit coordinate vector $e_{N+1}$. Finite-rank operators between [Banach spaces](../banach-space.md) are compact, and a limit of compact linear operators in [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) is compact, the [norm-closedness of compact operators](../compact-operator.md#norm-closedness-of-compact-operators). Therefore $\boxed{T\text{ is compact}}$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) states that a continuous compact self-map of a nonempty closed bounded [convex set](../convex-set.md) in a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point). Equivalently in this bounded-domain version, it suffices for the image of the map to be relatively compact. The theorem guarantees existence, without requiring a contraction estimate or promising uniqueness.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Work in the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $C([0,1])$ with its [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm), and take the nonempty closed bounded convex set $M=\{u:100\leq u(t)\leq121\text{ for all }t\}$. Define

$$
(Tu)(t)=101+\int_0^t\bigl(\sqrt{|u(s)|}+1\bigr)\,ds.
$$

For $u\in M$ the absolute value is unnecessary, and $\boxed{101\leq Tu(t)\leq101+12=113\leq121}$. Thus $T:M\to M$. Also, for $a,b\geq100$,

$$
|\sqrt a-\sqrt b|=\frac{|a-b|}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}\leq\frac1{20}|a-b|,
$$

so $\|Tu-Tv\|_\infty\leq\tfrac1{20}\|u-v\|_\infty$ and in particular $T$ is continuous.

The image is uniformly bounded, and $|Tu(t)-Tu(s)|\leq12|t-s|$, so it is [equicontinuous](../topological-analysis.md#equicontinuity). The [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](../topological-analysis.md#arzela-ascoli-theorem) says that a uniformly bounded equicontinuous family in $C([0,1])$ has [relatively compact](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset) [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) in the uniform norm. Hence $T$ is compact, and the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) supplies $\boxed{u=Tu}$, the required solution. In fact the displayed contraction estimate also lets the [contraction mapping theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) give uniqueness within $M$ and convergence of the corresponding [Picard iteration](../differential-equation.md#picard-iteration). The initial constant here is the printed integer $101$.

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Using the [dual pairing](../dual-space.md#dual-pairing), a [monotone operator](../convex-optimization.md#monotone-operator) satisfies $\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle\geq0$ for all $u,v\in M$. It is a [strongly monotone operator](../convex-optimization.md#strongly-monotone-operator) if some $m>0$ gives

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle\geq m\|u-v\|^2\quad(u,v\in M).}
$$

A [nonlinear coercive operator](../functional-analysis.md#nonlinear-coercive-operator) with respect to $u_0\in M$ satisfies $\langle A(u),u-u_0\rangle/\|u-u_0\|\to+\infty$ as $\|u-u_0\|\to\infty$ in $M$.

Strong monotonicity, with $v=u_0$, gives

$$
\langle A(u),u-u_0\rangle\geq m\|u-u_0\|^2+\langle A(u_0),u-u_0\rangle
\geq m\|u-u_0\|^2-\|A(u_0)\|_{X^*}\|u-u_0\|.
$$

After division, the lower bound is $m\|u-u_0\|-\|A(u_0)\|_{X^*}\to+\infty$, proving coercivity. Reflexivity and separability are not needed for this particular estimate.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Take the [zero-boundary Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-boundary-sobolev-space) $X=H_0^1(\Omega)$ with norm $\|u\|_X=\|\nabla u\|_2$. The [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) makes this equivalent to the usual [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm). Define

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u),v\rangle=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_\Omega u^3v,\qquad\langle f,v\rangle=\int_\Omega v.}
$$

The [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) is $u\in X$ and $\langle A(u),v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle$ for every $v\in X$.

In dimension three the [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem) gives $\|u\|_6\leq C_6\|u\|_X$, so $u^3\in L^2$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) and [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) yield

$$
|\langle A(u),v\rangle|\leq\bigl(\|u\|_X+C_PC_6^3\|u\|_X^3\bigr)\|v\|_X,
\qquad|\langle f,v\rangle|\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}C_P\|v\|_X.
$$

Thus $A(u)\in X^*$ for every $u$, and $f\in X^*$. Finally

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle A(u)-A(w),u-w\rangle
&=\|u-w\|_X^2+\int_\Omega(u-w)^2(u^2+uw+w^2)\\
&\geq\boxed{\|u-w\|_X^2},
\end{aligned}
$$

since $u^2+uw+w^2\geq0$. Hence $A$ is strongly monotone with constant one in the chosen norm.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

One-dimensional [Sobolev embedding](../sobolev-space.md#failure-of-first-order-sobolev-embedding-into-linfinity-in-two-dimensions) gives a continuous representative for each $v\in H_0^1(0,l)$, with zero endpoint values, so the pointwise obstacle condition is meaningful. The [positive part](../function.md#positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function) $g_+=\max\{g,0\}$ is [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) and vanishes near both endpoints because $g(0),g(l)<0$. It therefore belongs to $H_0^1$ and satisfies $g_+\geq g$, proving that $M$ is nonempty.

If $v,w\in M$ and $t\in[0,1]$, then $(1-t)v+tw\geq g$, so $M$ is a [convex set](../convex-set.md). If $v_n\to v$ in $H_0^1$, the estimate $\|v_n-v\|_\infty\leq\sqrt l\,\|v_n'-v'\|_2$ gives [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence). Passing to the limit in $v_n(x)\geq g(x)$ shows $v\in M$. Thus **the admissible set is nonempty, closed and convex**.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

For any $v\in M$, the segment $u+t(v-u)$ stays in $M$ for $0\leq t\leq1$. Since $u$ is a minimizer, the right derivative of its energy at $t=0$ is nonnegative. The [first variation](../calculus-of-variations.md#first-variation) therefore gives the [variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#variational-inequality)

$$
\boxed{\int_0^l\bigl(2a(x)u'(x)(v'(x)-u'(x))+\gamma\cos(u(x))(v(x)-u(x))\bigr)\,dx\geq0
\quad(v\in M).}
$$

The factor two comes from the quadratic gradient term. Only a one-sided inequality follows because a variation pointing below the obstacle need not be admissible.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

The [noncontact set](../partial-differential-equation.md#noncontact-set) $D=\{x\in(0,l):u(x)>g(x)\}$ is open. For $\varphi\in C_0^\infty(D)$, the positive gap above the obstacle has a positive minimum on the support of $\varphi$. Thus both $u+t\varphi$ and $u-t\varphi$ are admissible for sufficiently small $t>0$. Applying the [variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#variational-inequality) to both signs yields

$$
\int_D\bigl(2au'\varphi'+\gamma\cos(u)\varphi\bigr)\,dx=0.
$$

Hence the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) on the noncontact set is

$$
\boxed{-2(au')'+\gamma\cos u=0\quad\text{in }D.}
$$

It holds classically under the assumed smoothness. On the [contact set](../partial-differential-equation.md#contact-set-of-an-obstacle-problem) the obstacle prevents the negative variations used to obtain this equality, so a variational inequality remains the appropriate global description.

<h3 id="4/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

Let $u,w\in M$ both solve the [variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#variational-inequality). Test the inequality for $u$ with $w$ and the one for $w$ with $u$, then add. Writing $d=u-w$ gives

$$
2\int_0^la|d'|^2\,dx\leq-\gamma\int_0^l(\cos u-\cos w)d\,dx\leq\gamma\|d\|_2^2,
$$

since [cosine](../geometry-and-topology.md#cosine) is [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) with constant one. Since $d(0)=0$, the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus) and [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) give $|d(x)|\leq\sqrt l\,\|d'\|_2$; integrating gives $\|d\|_2^2\leq l^2\|d'\|_2^2$. Therefore

$$
(2c_0-\gamma l^2)\|d'\|_2^2\leq0.
$$

For $\boxed{0<\gamma<2c_0/l^2}$, the coefficient is positive, so $d'=0$ and the zero boundary value gives $d=0$. This proves uniqueness with an explicit sufficient smallness condition. More generally any [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) constant $C_P$ gives the condition $\gamma C_P^2<2c_0$; optimizing that constant is unnecessary for the requested conclusion. The underlying principle is [uniqueness for a strongly monotone variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#uniqueness-for-a-strongly-monotone-variational-inequality).
