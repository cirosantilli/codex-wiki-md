<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

The [Banach fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) states that a [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping) $T:X\to X$ of a nonempty [complete metric space](../topological-analysis.md#complete-metric-space) $(X,d)$ has a unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), and [iterations of a map](../discrete-dynamical-system.md#iterated-function) $x_{n+1}=T(x_n)$ converge to it from every $x_0\in X$. Precisely, assume a single constant $q\in[0,1)$ satisfies $d(Tx,Ty)\leq qd(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in X$.

Induction gives $d(x_{n+1},x_n)\leq q^n d(x_1,x_0)$. The [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) and the [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series) therefore give, for $m>n$,

$$
d(x_m,x_n)\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}q^j d(x_1,x_0)
\leq\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0).
$$

Thus $(x_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence). By [completeness](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) it converges to some $x_*\in X$. The [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping) is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map), so $Tx_*=\lim_n Tx_n=\lim_n x_{n+1}=x_*$. If $y_*$ is another [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), then $d(x_*,y_*)\leq qd(x_*,y_*)$, forcing $x_*=y_*$. Letting $m\to\infty$ in the estimate also gives the [contraction iteration error bound](../analysis.md#contraction-iteration-error-bound):

$$
\boxed{Tx_*=x_*,\qquad d(x_n,x_*)\leq\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0).}
$$

If $q=0$, $T$ is constant and the [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) reaches its unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) after the first step; this also removes any ambiguity about $0^0$ in the displayed estimate at $n=0$.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

The native paper prints $n\geq1$ while specifying $x_0$ alone. This does not determine $x_1$. We use the intended convention $x_{n+1}=\sin x_n$ for $n\geq0$; even with an independently chosen real $x_1$, the same proof gives the requested convergence.

For the [sine fixed-point iteration](../discrete-dynamical-system.md#sine-fixed-point-iteration), after one step $x_1\in[-1,1]$. On this [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics), the [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) has the same sign as its argument, and

$$
0<\sin t<t\qquad(0<t\leq1).
$$

For example, $t-\sin t=\int_0^t(1-\cos s)\,ds>0$, since the [integrand](../integral.md#integrand) is positive for $0<s\leq1$. By oddness of the [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine), $a_n=|x_n|$ consequently satisfies $a_{n+1}=\sin a_n\leq a_n$ for $n\geq1$. This [monotone sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#monotone-sequence) is bounded below by zero, so it has a limit $a\in[0,1]$. The [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of the [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) gives $a=\sin a$, and the strict inequality above forces $a=0$.

Hence $x_n\to0$, and $0$ is a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) of the [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine). It is the only real [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point): every such point has absolute value at most one, where the preceding strict inequality applies. Thus

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n=0\quad\text{for every real initial value}.}
$$

The [Banach fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) does not directly apply to the [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) on $[-1,1]$: the ratios $|\sin t-\sin0|/|t-0|$ tend to one as $t\to0$, so no uniform [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping) constant smaller than one exists there.

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

A [retraction](../topology.md#retraction) from a [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) $X$ onto a [subset](../set.md#subset) $Y$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $r:X\to Y$ satisfying $r(y)=y$ for every $y\in Y$. Equivalently, $r\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_Y$, where $\iota:Y\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion.

The [no-retraction theorem](../topological-analysis.md#no-retraction-theorem) for the finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) says

$$
\boxed{\text{For }n\geq1,\text{ no retraction }\mathbb B^n\longrightarrow\partial\mathbb B^n\text{ exists}.}
$$

Indeed, if $r$ were such a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction), $F(x)=-r(x)$ would be a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) from $\mathbb B^n$ into itself. The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) would give $x=F(x)=-r(x)$. Since $|r(x)|=1$, $x$ lies on the [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), where $r(x)=x$. Thus $x=-x$, contradicting $|x|=1$. Here the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) asserts that every [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) of a finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) into itself has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

The [radial retraction of a punctured ball](../topology.md#radial-retraction-of-a-punctured-ball) gives a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction): project away from the removed interior point until reaching the [unit sphere](../topology.md#unit-sphere). Write $a=x_0$, $v=x-a\ne0$, and set

$$
\tau(x)=\frac{-a\cdot v+\sqrt{(a\cdot v)^2+(1-|a|^2)|v|^2}}{|v|^2},
\qquad
\boxed{r(x)=a+\tau(x)(x-a).}
$$

The equation $|a+tv|^2=1$ is the [quadratic equation](../polynomial.md#quadratic-equation)

$$
|v|^2t^2+2(a\cdot v)t+|a|^2-1=0.
$$

Its constant term is negative, so it has exactly one positive root, namely $\tau(x)$. Since $|a+v|=|x|\leq1$, that positive root satisfies $\tau(x)\geq1$. Thus $r(x)\in\partial\mathbb B^n$ for every $x\ne a$ in the [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball).

The displayed expression is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) on the punctured [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball): its denominator is nonzero there, and its [square root](../algebra.md#square-root) has strictly positive argument. If $|x|=1$, $t=1$ is the unique positive root, so $r(x)=x$. These are exactly the requirements for a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction). The missing interior point is essential: the formula need not extend continuously to $a$, in agreement with the [no-retraction theorem](../topological-analysis.md#no-retraction-theorem).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The [retraction of a Hilbert ball onto its sphere](../topology.md#retraction-of-a-hilbert-ball-onto-its-sphere) exists. In the real [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) $\ell^2$, let $S(x_0,x_1,\ldots)=(0,x_0,x_1,\ldots)$ be the [unilateral shift operator](../linear-operator-theory.md#unilateral-shift-operator), and let $e_0=(1,0,\ldots)$. For $x$ in the [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) $\mathbb B$, define

$$
F(x)=\sqrt{1-\|x\|^2}\,e_0+Sx.
$$

The [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) of $e_0$ and $Sx$ gives $\|F(x)\|^2=1$. Thus $F$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) into the [unit sphere](../topology.md#unit-sphere). It has no [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point): $F(x)=x$ would imply $x_{j+1}=x_j$ for every $j\geq0$. A constant [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) in $\ell^2$ must be zero, whereas $F(0)=e_0\ne0$.

Put $d=x-F(x)$, which is nonzero everywhere, and define

$$
\alpha(x)=\frac{2(1-\langle F(x),x\rangle)}{\|x-F(x)\|^2},
\qquad
\boxed{r(x)=F(x)+\alpha(x)(x-F(x)).}
$$

This is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) on the whole [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball), including zero. To check its range, observe that $\langle F(x),d\rangle=\langle F(x),x\rangle-1$, so

$$
\|F(x)+\alpha d\|^2
=1+2\alpha(\langle F(x),x\rangle-1)+\alpha^2\|d\|^2=1.
$$

If $\|x\|=1$, then $\|x-F(x)\|^2=2(1-\langle F(x),x\rangle)$, hence $\alpha(x)=1$ and $r(x)=x$. Therefore $r:\mathbb B\to\partial\mathbb B$ is a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction).

Geometrically this is the second intersection of the line from $F(x)$ through $x$ with the [unit sphere](../topology.md#unit-sphere). In fact

$$
\alpha(x)-1=\frac{1-\|x\|^2}{\|x-F(x)\|^2}\geq0.
$$

The construction illustrates why the finite-dimensional hypothesis in the [no-retraction theorem](../topological-analysis.md#no-retraction-theorem) matters. The [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) of $\ell^2$ is not a [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space) in the [norm topology](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-topology).

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Let $S_+^{n-1}$ be the nonnegative portion of the [unit sphere](../topology.md#unit-sphere) specified in the paper. If $Ax=0$ for some $x\in S_+^{n-1}$, then this nonzero [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) is an [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) with [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) zero, which already proves the assertion.

Otherwise $Ax\ne0$ for every $x\in S_+^{n-1}$, and

$$
T(x)=\frac{Ax}{|Ax|}
$$

is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) from $S_+^{n-1}$ to itself. Indeed, every entry of $Ax$ is nonnegative because $A$ is a [nonnegative matrix](../vector-space.md#nonnegative-matrix) and every entry of $x$ is nonnegative. By the supplied [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) $h:\mathbb B^{n-1}\to S_+^{n-1}$, the [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $h^{-1}Th$ maps the finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) into itself. The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) supplies a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), hence $T(x_*)=x_*$ for some $x_*\in S_+^{n-1}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{Ax_*=\lambda x_*,\qquad x_*\ne0,\qquad\lambda=|Ax_*|>0.}
$$

Together with the zero case this proves that $A$ has a nonnegative [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue), with a nonnegative [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector). For $n=1$, the auxiliary [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) is the one-point space $\mathbb B^0$ and the argument remains valid. No claim of strict positivity of every entry of the [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) is needed.

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

For [Banach spaces](../banach-space.md) $X,Y$, a [nonlinear compact operator](../compact-operator.md#nonlinear-compact-operator) $T:X\to Y$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) that sends every [bounded set](../topological-analysis.md#bounded-set) to a [relatively compact subset](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset) of $Y$: the closure of its image is a [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space). For a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map), the image condition is equivalent to requiring that every bounded [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) $(x_j)$ has a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) along which $T(x_j)$ converges in the [norm topology](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-topology) of $Y$. For a [linear operator](../vector-space.md#linear-operator), this is the usual [compact operator](../compact-operator.md) definition; the continuity requirement is included explicitly here because the subsequent applications are nonlinear.

The compact-domain version of the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) says

$$
\boxed{K\ne\varnothing\text{ compact and convex in a Banach space},\quad
T:K\to K\text{ continuous}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Tu=u\text{ for some }u\in K.}
$$

A useful equivalent version replaces $K$ by a nonempty closed bounded [convex set](../convex-set.md) $C$ and assumes $T:C\to C$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) with [relatively compact](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset) image. This latter form will be used for the [partial differential equation](../partial-differential-equation.md). Both forms assert existence of a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), without asserting uniqueness.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Let $E=\operatorname{aff}K$ be the [affine hull](../vector-space.md#affine-hull) and put $k=\dim E\leq n$. If $k=0$, $K$ is a singleton and is [homeomorphic](../topology.md#homeomorphism) to $\mathbb B^0$. Suppose $k>0$. Choose $k+1$ [affinely independent](../geometry-and-topology.md#affine-independence) points of $K$ spanning $E$. Their [simplex](../algebraic-topology.md#simplex) is contained in $K$ because $K$ is a [convex set](../convex-set.md), and its [barycentre](../topological-vector-space.md#barycenter) $a$ has a neighbourhood in $E$ contained in that [simplex](../algebraic-topology.md#simplex). Identifying $E-a$ with $\mathbb R^k$, put $D=K-a$. For some $0<r\leq R<\infty$,

$$
r\mathbb B^k\subset D\subset R\mathbb B^k.
$$

Here the inner inclusion follows from that neighbourhood, and the outer one follows from the [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) of $K$.

Use the [Minkowski functional](../topological-vector-space.md#minkowski-functional)

$$
p(z)=\inf\{t>0:z\in tD\}.
$$

The inclusions imply $|z|/R\leq p(z)\leq|z|/r$. The fact that $D$ is a [convex set](../convex-set.md) implies $p(z+w)\leq p(z)+p(w)$: if $z\in sD$ and $w\in tD$, then $(z+w)/(s+t)$ is a [convex combination](../convex-set.md#convex-combination) of points of $D$. Also $p(tz)=tp(z)$ for $t\geq0$. Consequently

$$
p(z)-p(w)\leq p(z-w)\leq|z-w|/r,
\qquad |p(z)-p(w)|\leq|z-w|/r.
$$

Thus $p$ is a [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function). Since $D$ is closed and contains zero, $D=\{p\leq1\}$: the forward inclusion is immediate, and for the reverse inclusion one takes a limit of $z/t_j\in D$ with $t_j\to p(z)\leq1$, using [positive homogeneity](../real-analysis.md#positively-homogeneous-function-degree-one) and the fact that $D$ is a [convex set](../convex-set.md). For $z\ne0$, $p(z)>0$, so this limiting argument has no division by zero.

Define a radial [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) by

$$
h(0)=a,\qquad h(t\theta)=a+\frac{t}{p(\theta)}\theta
\quad(0<t\leq1,\ |\theta|=1).
$$

Each ray meets $D$ in exactly $\{s\theta:0\leq s\leq1/p(\theta)\}$. Hence $h$ is a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) from $\mathbb B^k$ to $K$. Away from zero its [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) follows from that of $p$, and at zero $|h(t\theta)-a|\leq Rt$. A [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) that is a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) from a [compact space](../topology.md#compact-space) to a [Hausdorff space](../topology.md#hausdorff-space) is a [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism). Therefore

$$
\boxed{K\cong\mathbb B^k,\qquad k=\dim(\operatorname{aff}K).}
$$

Using the [affine hull](../vector-space.md#affine-hull) is essential: $K$ can have empty interior in the ambient $\mathbb R^n$.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

We first prove the compact-domain [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem). Let $K$ be a nonempty compact [convex set](../convex-set.md) in a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $X$, and let $T:K\to K$ be a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map). The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) states that every [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) of a finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#closed-unit-ball) into itself has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point). By the [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism) proved in part (a)(ii), it applies equally to any nonempty finite-dimensional compact [convex set](../convex-set.md), including a singleton.

For $\varepsilon>0$, use the supplied [Schauder approximation lemma](../analysis.md#schauder-approximation-lemma) to obtain a finite-dimensional [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) $L_\varepsilon$ and a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $T_\varepsilon:K\to K\cap L_\varepsilon$ with $\|Tx-T_\varepsilon x\|\leq\varepsilon$. The set $K_\varepsilon=K\cap L_\varepsilon$ is nonempty because it contains $T_\varepsilon(K)$. It is a compact [convex set](../convex-set.md), since a finite-dimensional [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) is closed. Restricting $T_\varepsilon$ to $K_\varepsilon$ and applying the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) gives $x_\varepsilon=T_\varepsilon x_\varepsilon$, and thus

$$
\|Tx_\varepsilon-x_\varepsilon\|\leq\varepsilon.
$$

Take $\varepsilon_j=1/j$. The [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) of $K$ yields a [convergent subsequence](../real-analysis.md#convergent-subsequence) $x_{\varepsilon_{j_m}}\to x_*\in K$. The [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $T$ and the preceding estimate give

$$
\boxed{Tx_*=x_*.}
$$

For completeness, this also proves the version on a nonempty closed bounded [convex set](../convex-set.md) $C$ when $T(C)$ is [relatively compact](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset). Let $K=\overline{\operatorname{conv}}T(C)\subset C$. The [closed convex hull of a compact set](../convex-set.md#closed-convex-hull-of-a-compact-set) in a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) is compact. To verify the fact needed here, cover $T(C)$ by finitely many [balls](../topological-analysis.md#ball-mathematics) of radius $\delta$. Replacing every term of a [convex combination](../convex-set.md#convex-combination) by a centre changes it by at most $\delta$ in [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). The [convex hull](../convex-set.md#convex-hull) of those finitely many centres is compact in a finite-dimensional [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace), so it has a [finite net](../topological-analysis.md#finite-net) of radius $\delta$. Thus $\operatorname{conv}T(C)$ is [totally bounded](../topological-analysis.md#totally-bounded-space). Its closure is [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) and [totally bounded](../topological-analysis.md#totally-bounded-space), hence compact. Now $T(K)\subset T(C)\subset K$, so the compact-domain result supplies a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) in $C$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Use the real [zero-trace Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-trace-sobolev-space) $H_0^1(\Omega)$ with [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) $\|\nabla u\|_2$. The [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) on the bounded smooth [domain](../topology.md#domain-mathematical-analysis) gives $\|u\|_2\leq C_P\|\nabla u\|_2$, making this an equivalent [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm). The [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) is to find $u\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ with $u\geq0$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) and

$$
\boxed{\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx
=\int_\Omega(1+\sqrt u)v\,dx\qquad\text{for every }v\in H_0^1(\Omega).}
$$

The zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) is encoded in the [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator). Formally, the identity follows by multiplying the [partial differential equation](../partial-differential-equation.md) by a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) and using [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts); it then extends to all $v\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ by density.

To check all terms, boundedness of $\Omega$ gives finite [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) $m=|\Omega|$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives $u\in L^1(\Omega)$ and

$$
\|\sqrt u\|_2^2=\int_\Omega u\,dx\leq m^{1/2}\|u\|_2,
\qquad
\|1+\sqrt u\|_2\leq m^{1/2}+m^{1/4}\|u\|_2^{1/2}.
$$

Thus the right side is a bounded [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) of $v$, since its absolute value is at most $\|1+\sqrt u\|_2 C_P\|\nabla v\|_2$. The left side is finite by the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality). These estimates work for every stated $n\geq2$.

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Let $P=\{w\in L^2(\Omega):w\geq0\text{ almost everywhere}\}$. For $g\in L^2(\Omega)$, define $Gg=z\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ by

$$
\int_\Omega\nabla z\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_\Omega gv\,dx
\qquad(v\in H_0^1(\Omega)).
$$

The [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) guarantees a unique $z$: the [bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md) on the left is bounded and [coercive](../bilinear-form.md#coercive-bilinear-form) in the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) $\|\nabla\cdot\|_2$, and the right side is a bounded [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) by the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality). Testing with $z$ gives

$$
\|\nabla Gg\|_2\leq C_P\|g\|_2,
\qquad\|Gg\|_2\leq C_P^2\|g\|_2.
$$

Define $T:P\to L^2(\Omega)$ by $Tw=G(1+\sqrt w)$. This actually maps into $P$. Indeed the [weak maximum principle](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators) says that a [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) $z\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ of $-\Delta z=g\geq0$ is nonnegative [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). Here $1+\sqrt w\geq0$ and belongs to $L^2(\Omega)$. Directly, taking the [negative part](../function.md#negative-part-of-a-real-valued-function) $z^-=\max\{-z,0\}$ as a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) gives $-\|\nabla z^-\|_2^2=\int_\Omega gz^-\,dx\geq0$, so $z^-=0$ by the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality).

The required [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) formulation is

$$
\boxed{u\in P,\qquad u=Tu=G(1+\sqrt u).}
$$

Every such [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) belongs automatically to $H_0^1(\Omega)$ and satisfies part (b)(i). Conversely, uniqueness in the [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) makes every nonnegative [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) of $T$.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

We apply the closed-bounded-domain version of the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) in the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $L^2(\Omega)$. Write $m=|\Omega|$ and use $G,T,C_P$ from part (b)(ii). Choose

$$
a=C_P^2m^{1/2},\qquad b=C_P^2m^{1/4},\qquad
R=\max\{1,2a,4b^2\},
\qquad C=\{w\in L^2(\Omega):w\geq0\text{ a.e.},\ \|w\|_2\leq R\}.
$$

The set $C$ is nonempty, bounded, closed and a [convex set](../convex-set.md). For closedness of its positivity condition, if $w_j\geq0$ and $w_j\to w$ in $L^2$, then pointwise $w^-\leq|w-w_j|$, whence $\|w^-\|_2\to0$.

The [weak maximum principle](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators) already gives $Tw\geq0$. Our estimates give, for $w\in C$,

$$
\|Tw\|_2\leq C_P^2\bigl(m^{1/2}+m^{1/4}\|w\|_2^{1/2}\bigr)
\leq a+b\sqrt R\leq R/2+R/2=R.
$$

Thus $T(C)\subset C$.

To prove [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function), use the pointwise [square root](../algebra.md#square-root) estimate $|\sqrt s-\sqrt t|^2\leq|s-t|$ for $s,t\geq0$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) then gives

$$
\|\sqrt w-\sqrt z\|_2^2\leq\|w-z\|_1\leq m^{1/2}\|w-z\|_2,
\qquad
\|Tw-Tz\|_2\leq C_P^2m^{1/4}\|w-z\|_2^{1/2}.
$$

Hence $T$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) on $C$, even though the [square root](../algebra.md#square-root) need not have a bounded [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) near zero.

Finally,

$$
\|\nabla Tw\|_2\leq C_P\bigl(m^{1/2}+m^{1/4}\sqrt R\bigr)
\qquad(w\in C).
$$

The [Rellich-Kondrachov theorem](../sobolev-space.md#rellich-kondrachov-theorem) says that $H_0^1(\Omega)$ embeds compactly into $L^2(\Omega)$ on a bounded smooth [domain](../topology.md#domain-mathematical-analysis). For $n>2$, $2<2n/(n-2)$, and for $n=2$ every finite target exponent is available, so the required [compact embedding](../functional-analysis.md#compact-embedding) holds in every dimension in the question. Thus $T(C)$ is [relatively compact](../topological-analysis.md#relatively-compact-subset) in $L^2(\Omega)$.

All hypotheses of the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) are met. It gives $u=Tu\in C$, and part (b)(ii) converts this [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) into the required [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution):

$$
\boxed{\text{There exists }u\in H_0^1(\Omega),\ u\geq0\text{ a.e., with }
\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_\Omega(1+\sqrt u)v\,dx
\quad\forall v\in H_0^1(\Omega).}
$$

Only existence is needed. The construction does not assume that $\Omega$ is small or that $T$ is a [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping).

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A useful form of [Minty's lemma](../convex-optimization.md#minty-s-lemma) is the following. Let $X$ be a real [Banach space](../banach-space.md), $K\subset X$ a nonempty [convex set](../convex-set.md), and $A:K\to X^*$ a [monotone operator](../convex-optimization.md#monotone-operator) that is [hemicontinuous](../nonlinear-analysis.md#hemicontinuity). Here [operator monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#operator-monotonicity) means $\langle Ax-Ay,x-y\rangle\geq0$ for $x,y\in K$, and [hemicontinuity](../nonlinear-analysis.md#hemicontinuity) means that $t\mapsto\langle A(x+t(y-x)),z\rangle$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ for $x,y\in K$ and $z\in X$. For $u\in K$,

$$
\boxed{\langle Au,v-u\rangle\geq0\ \forall v\in K
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\langle Av,v-u\rangle\geq0\ \forall v\in K.}
$$

The forward implication follows from [operator monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#operator-monotonicity):

$$
\langle Av,v-u\rangle
=\langle Au,v-u\rangle+\langle Av-Au,v-u\rangle\geq0.
$$

For the reverse implication, fix $w\in K$ and take $v_t=u+t(w-u)\in K$ for $0<t\leq1$. The assumed inequality gives

$$
0\leq\langle Av_t,v_t-u\rangle=t\langle A(u+t(w-u)),w-u\rangle.
$$

Divide by $t$ and send $t\downarrow0$. [Hemicontinuity](../nonlinear-analysis.md#hemicontinuity) gives $\langle Au,w-u\rangle\geq0$, as required.

In the whole-space version, for a [monotone operator](../convex-optimization.md#monotone-operator) $A:X\to X^*$ that is [hemicontinuous](../nonlinear-analysis.md#hemicontinuity),

$$
\boxed{Au=f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\langle Av-f,v-u\rangle\geq0\quad\forall v\in X.}
$$

Apply the just-proved result to $A-f$ and $K=X$; the resulting inequality $\langle Au-f,v-u\rangle\geq0$ for all $v$ forces $Au=f$ by choosing $v=u\pm z$. This is the [variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#variational-inequality) form of [Minty's lemma](../convex-optimization.md#minty-s-lemma).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Choose the real [zero-trace Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-trace-sobolev-space)

$$
X=W_0^{1,4}(\Omega),\qquad\|u\|_X=\|\nabla u\|_4,
\qquad Y=L^{8/7}(\Omega).
$$

The [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) makes this an equivalent [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm). Since the dimension is five, the [Sobolev conjugate exponent](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-conjugate-exponent) is $4^*=5\cdot4/(5-4)=20$. The [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem) gives $X\hookrightarrow L^{20}(\Omega)$ continuously. Boundedness of $\Omega$ and [Lp inclusion on a finite measure space](../measure-theory.md#lp-inclusion-on-a-finite-measure-space) then give $X\hookrightarrow L^8(\Omega)$ continuously, say $\|u\|_8\leq C_8\|u\|_X$.

For $f\in Y$, the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) is

$$
\boxed{u\in X,\qquad
\int_\Omega |\nabla u|^2\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx
+\int_\Omega u^7v\,dx=\int_\Omega fv\,dx
\quad\forall v\in X.}
$$

The zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) is imposed through the [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator). [Hölder's inequality](../real-analysis.md#holder-s-inequality) verifies every term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_\Omega|\nabla u|^2\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx\right|
&\leq\|\nabla u\|_4^3\|\nabla v\|_4,\\
\left|\int_\Omega u^7v\,dx\right|
&\leq\|u\|_8^7\|v\|_8,\\
\left|\int_\Omega fv\,dx\right|
&\leq\|f\|_{8/7}\|v\|_8.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $|\nabla u|^2\nabla u\in L^{4/3}(\Omega;\mathbb R^5)$, while $u^7\in L^{8/7}(\Omega)$, and all three pairings are finite bounded [linear functionals](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) in $v$. The choice $Y=L^{8/7}(\Omega)$ is sufficient; it is not intended to be the largest possible space of data.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

For the spaces in part (b)(i) and fixed $f\in Y$, define $A:X\to X^*$ by

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u),v\rangle
=\int_\Omega|\nabla u|^2\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx
+\int_\Omega u^7v\,dx-\int_\Omega fv\,dx.}
$$

For each $u$, this is a [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) in $v$, and the estimates in part (b)(i) show

$$
\|A(u)\|_{X^*}\leq\|u\|_X^3+C_8^8\|u\|_X^7+C_8\|f\|_{8/7}.
$$

Hence $A(u)$ belongs to the [continuous dual space](../continuous-dual-space.md) $X^*$, and $A$ sends bounded [sets](../set.md) to bounded [sets](../set.md). The [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) is exactly

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u),v\rangle=0\quad\forall v\in X,\quad\text{equivalently }A(u)=0\text{ in }X^*.}
$$

The forcing term is included in $A$ so that its right side is zero, as requested.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

For $u,w\in X$, the forcing terms cancel, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle A(u)-A(w),u-w\rangle
={}&\int_\Omega\bigl(|\nabla u|^2\nabla u-|\nabla w|^2\nabla w\bigr)\cdot(\nabla u-\nabla w)\,dx\\
&+\int_\Omega(u^7-w^7)(u-w)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$

The scalar [function](../function.md) $s\mapsto s^7$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb R$, so the second [integrand](../integral.md#integrand) is nonnegative. For the first one, use the [quartic gradient monotonicity inequality](../convex-optimization.md#quartic-gradient-monotonicity-inequality). Put $d=\xi-\eta$ and $m=(\xi+\eta)/2$ for real [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) $\xi,\eta$. Expanding gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
(|\xi|^2\xi-|\eta|^2\eta)\cdot(\xi-\eta)
&=|m|^2|d|^2+2(m\cdot d)^2+\frac14|d|^4\\
&\geq\frac14|\xi-\eta|^4.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u)-A(w),u-w\rangle
\geq\frac14\|\nabla(u-w)\|_4^4
=\frac14\|u-w\|_X^4>0\quad(u\ne w).}
$$

The last strict inequality uses the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality): if the [weak gradient](../distribution-theory.md#weak-gradient) of $u-w\in W_0^{1,4}(\Omega)$ vanishes, then $u=w$. This proves that $A$ is a [strictly monotone operator](../convex-optimization.md#strictly-monotone-operator).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

We prove existence by the [direct method in the calculus of variations](../calculus-of-variations.md#direct-method-in-the-calculus-of-variations), making the step with [weakly compact sets](../weak-topology.md#weakly-compact-set) explicit. For $X=W_0^{1,4}(\Omega)$ and $f\in L^{8/7}(\Omega)$, introduce the [functional](../calculus-of-variations.md#functional)

$$
E(u)=\frac14\|\nabla u\|_4^4+\frac18\|u\|_8^8-\int_\Omega fu\,dx.
$$

It is finite by part (b)(i). [Hölder's inequality](../real-analysis.md#holder-s-inequality) and the [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem) give

$$
E(u)\geq\frac14\|u\|_X^4-C_8\|f\|_{8/7}\|u\|_X\longrightarrow+\infty
\quad\text{as }\|u\|_X\to\infty.
$$

Thus $E$ is a [coercive function](../real-analysis.md#coercive-function), bounded below, and every [minimizing sequence](../calculus-of-variations.md#minimizing-sequence) is bounded in $X$.

The space $X$ is a [reflexive Banach space](../functional-analysis.md#reflexive-banach-space): it is isomorphic to a closed [linear subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) of the finite product of the [reflexive spaces](../functional-analysis.md#reflexive-space) $L^4(\Omega)$ recording the [function](../function.md) and its [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative). A bounded [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) in a [reflexive Banach space](../functional-analysis.md#reflexive-banach-space) has a [weakly convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence). Hence, after passing to a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence), a [minimizing sequence](../calculus-of-variations.md#minimizing-sequence) satisfies $u_j\rightharpoonup u_*$ in $X$. The continuous [linear maps](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $u\mapsto\nabla u$ into $L^4$ and $u\mapsto u$ into $L^8$ preserve [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence), so

$$
\nabla u_j\rightharpoonup\nabla u_*\text{ in }L^4,
\qquad u_j\rightharpoonup u_*\text{ in }L^8.
$$

The [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) in any [Banach space](../banach-space.md) has [weak lower semicontinuity](../functional-analysis.md#weak-lower-semicontinuity), since it is the supremum of absolute values of bounded [linear functionals](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) of [dual norm](../normed-vector-space.md#dual-norm) at most one, by the [Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem). Therefore the two nonnegative [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) powers in $E$ have [weak lower semicontinuity](../functional-analysis.md#weak-lower-semicontinuity). Also $u\mapsto\int_\Omega fu\,dx$ belongs to $X^*$ and is continuous for [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence). It follows that

$$
E(u_*)\leq\liminf_j E(u_j)=\inf_{u\in X}E(u).
$$

For any $v\in X$, the real [function](../function.md) $t\mapsto E(u_*+tv)$ has a minimum at $t=0$. Its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) there is

$$
0=\left.\frac{d}{dt}E(u_*+tv)\right|_{t=0}
=\int_\Omega|\nabla u_*|^2\nabla u_*\cdot\nabla v\,dx
+\int_\Omega u_*^7v\,dx-\int_\Omega fv\,dx.
$$

This differentiation is justified directly by finite polynomial expansions: the fourth power of the [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm) of $\nabla u_*+t\nabla v$ and the eighth power of $u_*+tv$ have integrable coefficients by [Hölder's inequality](../real-analysis.md#holder-s-inequality). Thus $A(u_*)=0$, giving existence of a [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution).

If $u,w$ are two [weak solutions](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution), testing the difference by $u-w$ and using part (b)(iii) gives

$$
0=\langle A(u)-A(w),u-w\rangle\geq\tfrac14\|u-w\|_X^4.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{For every }f\in L^{8/7}(\Omega),\text{ there exists exactly one weak solution }u_*\in W_0^{1,4}(\Omega).}
$$

No sign assumption on $f$ or on the [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) is required.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Take the [functional](../calculus-of-variations.md#functional)

$$
\boxed{J(u)=\int_\Omega\left(\frac14|\nabla u|^4+\frac18|u|^8-fu\right)dx,
\qquad u\in W_0^{1,4}(\Omega).}
$$

The factor $1/8$ is essential: the [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) of $|s|^8/8=s^8/8$ is $s^7$, while the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) with respect to $\xi$ of $|\xi|^4/4$ is $|\xi|^2\xi$. As in part (c), finite polynomial expansions and [Hölder's inequality](../real-analysis.md#holder-s-inequality) justify differentiation along every direction $v\in X$, giving $DJ(u)[v]=\langle A(u),v\rangle$.

We prove the global and strict minimum directly. The [strictly convex functions](../real-analysis.md#strictly-convex-function) $\Phi(\xi)=|\xi|^4/4$ and $\Psi(s)=|s|^8/8$ satisfy their tangent inequalities

$$
\Phi(\eta)\geq\Phi(\xi)+|\xi|^2\xi\cdot(\eta-\xi),
\qquad
\Psi(t)\geq\Psi(s)+s^7(t-s),
$$

with strict inequality when the respective arguments differ. For example, strict [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of $\Phi$ follows from strict [operator monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#operator-monotonicity) of its [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient), proved by the midpoint identity in part (b)(iii); strict [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of $\Psi$ follows because its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) $s^7$ is strictly increasing.

Apply these inequalities pointwise to $\nabla u_*,\nabla v$ and $u_*,v$, then integrate. The [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) at $u_*$ with [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $v-u_*$ gives

$$
J(v)-J(u_*)\geq\langle A(u_*),v-u_*\rangle=0.
$$

If $v\ne u_*$ in $X$, the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) implies $\nabla v\ne\nabla u_*$ on a set of positive [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure). The first tangent inequality is strict on that set and the second is nonnegative everywhere. Their integrable remainders therefore have strictly positive total [integral](../integral.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{J(v)>J(u_*)\quad\text{for every }v\in X\setminus\{u_*\}.}
$$

Thus $u_*$ is the unique global [minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) of $J$. Conversely, every [minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) has zero directional [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) in every direction and so satisfies $A(u)=0$, which also identifies it with the unique [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) from part (c).
