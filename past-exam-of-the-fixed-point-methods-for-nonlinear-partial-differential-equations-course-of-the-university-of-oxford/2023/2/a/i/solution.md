<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For [Banach spaces](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) $X,Y$, a [nonlinear compact operator](../../../../../nonlinear-compact-operator.md) $T:X\to Y$ is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) that sends every [bounded set](../../../../../bounded-set.md) to a [relatively compact subset](../../../../../relatively-compact-subset.md) of $Y$: the closure of its image is a [compact set](../../../../../compact-space.md). For a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md), the image condition is equivalent to requiring that every bounded [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $(x_j)$ has a [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) along which $T(x_j)$ converges in the [norm topology](../../../../../norm-topology.md) of $Y$. For a [linear operator](../../../../../linear-operator.md), this is the usual [compact operator](../../../../../compact-operator-split.md) definition; the continuity requirement is included explicitly here because the subsequent applications are nonlinear.

The compact-domain version of the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](../../../../../schauder-fixed-point-theorem.md) says

$$
\boxed{K\ne\varnothing\text{ compact and convex in a Banach space},\quad
T:K\to K\text{ continuous}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Tu=u\text{ for some }u\in K.}
$$

A useful equivalent version replaces $K$ by a nonempty closed bounded [convex set](../../../../../convex-set-split.md) $C$ and assumes $T:C\to C$ is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) with [relatively compact](../../../../../relatively-compact-subset.md) image. This latter form will be used for the [partial differential equation](../../../../../partial-differential-equation-split.md). Both forms assert existence of a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md), without asserting uniqueness.
