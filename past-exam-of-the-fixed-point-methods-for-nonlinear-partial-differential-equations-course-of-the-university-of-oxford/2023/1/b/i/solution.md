<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [retraction](../../../../../retraction.md) from a [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md) $X$ onto a [subset](../../../../../subset.md) $Y$ is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) $r:X\to Y$ satisfying $r(y)=y$ for every $y\in Y$. Equivalently, $r\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_Y$, where $\iota:Y\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion.

The [no-retraction theorem](../../../../../no-retraction-theorem.md) for the finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../../../../../closed-unit-ball.md) says

$$
\boxed{\text{For }n\geq1,\text{ no retraction }\mathbb B^n\longrightarrow\partial\mathbb B^n\text{ exists}.}
$$

Indeed, if $r$ were such a [retraction](../../../../../retraction.md), $F(x)=-r(x)$ would be a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) from $\mathbb B^n$ into itself. The [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../../../../../brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md) would give $x=F(x)=-r(x)$. Since $|r(x)|=1$, $x$ lies on the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md), where $r(x)=x$. Thus $x=-x$, contradicting $|x|=1$. Here the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../../../../../brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md) asserts that every [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) of a finite-dimensional [closed unit ball](../../../../../closed-unit-ball.md) into itself has a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md).
