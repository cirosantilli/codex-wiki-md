# Monolithic integrated circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Integrated circuit](integrated-circuit.md)

A [monolithic integrated circuit](monolithic-integrated-circuit.md) has its circuit elements fabricated in or on one [semiconductor die](semiconductor-die.md). Their connections are essential: an unconnected collection of [transistors](transistor.md) does not implement the intended [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md).

An original ideal example separates function from fabrication. Connect two [resistors](resistor.md), $R_1=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ above $R_2=20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, in a [series circuit](series-circuit.md) across an ideal $3\,\mathrm V$ [voltage source](voltage-source.md). With no load [electric current](electric-current.md) drawn from their meeting point, [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) and the [Kirchhoff cycle law](kirchhoff-cycle-law.md) give

$$
I=\frac{3\,\mathrm V}{R_1+R_2}=0.1\,\mathrm{mA},\qquad
V_{\rm out}=IR_2=2\,\mathrm V.
$$

These equations apply to either a [discrete circuit](discrete-circuit.md) or an idealized [monolithic integrated circuit](monolithic-integrated-circuit.md) realizing those same [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md). Integration changes construction; the [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md) equations still determine the output. A real load, unwanted [capacitance](capacitance.md) or finite [resistor tolerance](resistor-tolerance.md) changes the ideal calculation. Manufacturing many functions together also couples their fate: a fatal defect can discard an entire [semiconductor die](semiconductor-die.md), motivating the [Poisson defect yield](poisson-defect-yield.md) model.
