# Zero second fundamental form implies planar image

↑ **Parent:** [Second fundamental form](second-fundamental-form-split.md)

Differentiate the orthogonality of the unit normal to the two independent coordinate tangent vectors. If the [second fundamental form](second-fundamental-form-split.md) vanishes, each derivative of the normal is orthogonal to both tangent vectors; differentiating its unit length also makes it orthogonal to the normal. Thus both normal derivatives vanish. On a connected parameter domain the normal is constant, and differentiating the position's scalar product with that constant normal proves the surface lies in a plane.
