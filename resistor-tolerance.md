# Resistor tolerance

↑ **Parent:** [Resistor](resistor.md)

[Resistor tolerance](resistor-tolerance.md) specifies the permissible manufacturing deviation of a [resistor](resistor.md)'s [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) from its nominal value under stated conditions. For nominal [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) $R_{\mathrm{nom}}>0$ and fractional [resistor tolerance](resistor-tolerance.md) $0\le\tau<1$, the specified range is $R_{\mathrm{nom}}(1-\tau)\le R\le R_{\mathrm{nom}}(1+\tau)$. This differs from [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md): [resistor tolerance](resistor-tolerance.md) describes a component specification, while [measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md) describes knowledge of a measured value; the tolerance alone is not a [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) for that value.

Two nominally equal [resistors](resistor.md) can have different actual [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md), changing their [voltage divider](voltage-divider.md) ratio even without an external [electrical load](electrical-load.md). In a [loaded voltage divider](loaded-voltage-divider.md), use the actual [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md) to calculate both the unloaded output and the [Thévenin resistance](thevenin-resistance.md) governing [voltage divider loading error](voltage-divider-loading-error.md). A mismatch from [resistor tolerance](resistor-tolerance.md) and a change from [voltmeter loading](voltmeter-loading.md) are distinct effects that can occur together.
