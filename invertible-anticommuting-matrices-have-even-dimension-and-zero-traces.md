# Invertible anticommuting matrices have even dimension and zero traces

↑ **Parent:** [Anticommutator](anticommutator.md)

For complex [invertible matrices](invertible-matrix.md) $A,B$, $AB=-BA$ implies $B^{-1}AB=-A$ and $A^{-1}BA=-B$. Invariance of [matrix trace](matrix-trace.md) under [matrix similarity](matrix-similarity.md) gives both zero traces. Invariance of [determinant](determinant.md) gives $\det A=(-1)^n\det A$, and invertibility forces $n$ even. Equivalently, [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) occur in pairs $\lambda,-\lambda$ with equal [algebraic multiplicity](algebraic-multiplicity.md), and zero is excluded. Both invertibility assumptions matter: $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)$ and $B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0)$ anticommute in dimension three but are singular and have nonzero traces.
