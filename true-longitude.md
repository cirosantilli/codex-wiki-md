# True longitude

↑ **Parent:** [Orbital element](orbital-element.md)

True longitude is the conventional orbital angle formed from the [longitude of ascending node](longitude-of-ascending-node.md), argument of [periapsis](periapsis.md) and [true anomaly](true-anomaly.md). For a coplanar [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) it is the actual polar longitude of the position vector. For an inclined [orbital plane](orbital-plane.md) the sum of angles is generally not the projected azimuth. The [mean longitude](mean-longitude.md) uses [mean anomaly](mean-anomaly.md) $M$ in place of $f$, so $\ell_{\rm true}-\lambda=f-M$. For small [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md), [Kepler's equation](kepler-s-equation.md) gives $E=M+e\sin M+O(e^2)$, while the eccentric-to-true anomaly relation gives $f=E+e\sin E+O(e^2)$; hence $f-M=2e\sin M+O(e^2)$. This periodic difference matters when separating a slow [resonant argument](resonant-argument.md) from a rapidly varying geometric angle.
