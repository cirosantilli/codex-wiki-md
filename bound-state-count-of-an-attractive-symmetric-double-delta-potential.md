# Bound-state count of an attractive symmetric double-delta potential

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric double-delta potential](symmetric-double-delta-potential.md)

For $V(x)=g[\delta(x-a)+\delta(x+a)]$ with $a>0$ and $\nu=-mg/\hbar^2>0$, an even [bound state](bound-state.md) obeys $\kappa=\nu(1+e^{-2a\kappa})$, and an odd [bound state](bound-state.md) obeys $\kappa=\nu(1-e^{-2a\kappa})$, with [energy](energy-split.md) $E=-\hbar^2\kappa^2/(2m)$ and $\kappa>0$. The even right-hand side is strictly decreasing from $2\nu$, so it meets $\kappa$ once. For the odd equation, $h(\kappa)=\nu(1-e^{-2a\kappa})-\kappa$ is strictly [concave](concave-function.md), starts at zero with slope $2a\nu-1$, and tends to negative infinity. It has one positive [equation root](root-of-an-equation.md) precisely when $2a\nu>1$. Thus there is one [bound state](bound-state.md) for $-\hbar^2/(2ma)\leq g<0$, and two for $g<-\hbar^2/(2ma)$. For $g\geq0$ positivity of the [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) excludes negative [energy](energy-split.md), and square integrability excludes nonnegative-[energy](energy-split.md) exterior solutions. At $g=g_c$ the odd zero-[energy](energy-split.md) solution is bounded but not square integrable: it is a [zero-energy scattering resonance](zero-energy-scattering-resonance.md), not a second [bound state](bound-state.md).
