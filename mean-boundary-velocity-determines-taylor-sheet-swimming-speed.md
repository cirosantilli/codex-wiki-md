# Mean boundary velocity determines Taylor-sheet swimming speed

↑ **Parent:** [Taylor swimming sheet](taylor-swimming-sheet.md)

A [Taylor expansion](taylor-expansion.md) of the [no-slip boundary condition](no-slip-boundary-condition.md) about the flat sheet makes the second-order mean tangential velocity $\langle u_2(0)\rangle=-\langle y_1\partial_yu_1(0)\rangle$. The mean mode of [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md) is linear in height when no pressure gradient is imposed. In an unbounded fluid, bounded velocity excludes mean shear; in a confined fluid, the [force-free](force-free.md) condition excludes it. The remaining mean velocity is uniform and equals the swimming speed in the sheet frame. Thus the leading speed can be found from the first-order field without solving the oscillatory second-order field.
