<h1 id="e4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For each $b\in B$, use the supplied [Hausdorff condition](../../../../hausdorff-space.md) fact to choose disjoint [open sets](../../../../open-set.md) $U_b,V_b$ with $A\subseteq U_b$ and $b\in V_b$. [Compactness](../../../../compact-space.md) of $B$ gives finitely many $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ whose $V_{b_j}$ cover $B$. Set

$$
\boxed{U=\bigcap_{j=1}^rU_{b_j},\qquad V=\bigcup_{j=1}^rV_{b_j}.}
$$

Both are open; $U$ contains $A$ and $V$ contains $B$. They are disjoint because membership in $U\cap V$ would put a point in some $U_{b_j}\cap V_{b_j}$. If one [compact](../../../../compact-space.md) set is empty, use the empty set and $X$ directly. Thus **disjoint [compact](../../../../compact-space.md) subspaces of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../hausdorff-space.md) admit disjoint open neighborhoods**.
