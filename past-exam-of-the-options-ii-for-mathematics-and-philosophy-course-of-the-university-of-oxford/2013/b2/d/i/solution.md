<h1 id="b2/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order six has exactly one [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of each order $1,2,3,6$, and no others. Since it is abelian, these are also its four conjugacy classes of [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md). Thus there is one transitive [group action](../../../../../group-action-split.md) of each size $1,2,3,6$.

An action on six points is a disjoint union of its [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). Its equivalence type is therefore determined by nonnegative integers satisfying

$$
n_1+2n_2+3n_3+6n_6=6.
$$

If $n_6=1$ there is one possibility. If $n_6=0$, the possibilities $n_3=0,1,2$ allow respectively $4,2,1$ choices of $n_2$, with $n_1$ then fixed. Hence

$$
\boxed{1+4+2+1=8\text{ inequivalent actions}.}
$$

This is [counting finite group actions by transitive orbit types](../../../../../counting-finite-group-actions-by-transitive-orbit-types.md). Equivalently, a generator acts as a [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) whose cycles have lengths dividing six, and these are its eight possible cycle types on six points.
