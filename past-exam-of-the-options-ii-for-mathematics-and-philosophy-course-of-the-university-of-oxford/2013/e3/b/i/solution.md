↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $a^{(m)}=(1,1/2,\ldots,1/m,0,0,\ldots)$. Each has finite support, and in the [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) metric it converges to $a=(1,1/2,1/3,\ldots)$, because $d(a^{(m)},a)=1/(m+1)$.

The limit is bounded but does not have finite support. Thus the finite-support subspace is not closed in $X$, and by part (a)(iii) **it is not complete**. Equivalently, the displayed [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is Cauchy in that subspace but has no limit there.
