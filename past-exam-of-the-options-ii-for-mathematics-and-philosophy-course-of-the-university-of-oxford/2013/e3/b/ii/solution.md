<h1 id="e3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Show that the subspace $C$ of convergent real [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) is closed in $X$. Suppose $a^{(m)}\in C$ converges uniformly in the coordinate index to $a\in X$. Given $\epsilon>0$, choose $m$ so $\sup_j|a_j-a_j^{(m)}|<\epsilon/3$. Since the fixed [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $a^{(m)}$ converges, it is Cauchy, so for sufficiently large $j,k$ its coordinate difference is below $\epsilon/3$. Then

$$
|a_j-a_k|\leq|a_j-a_j^{(m)}|+|a_j^{(m)}-a_k^{(m)}|+|a_k^{(m)}-a_k|<\epsilon.
$$

Thus $a$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) of real numbers and converges by completeness of $\mathbb R$. Hence $a\in C$, so $C$ is closed. Part (a)(iii) proves **the convergent-sequence subspace is complete**.
