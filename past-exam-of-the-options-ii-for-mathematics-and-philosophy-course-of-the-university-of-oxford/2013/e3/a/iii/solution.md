<h1 id="e3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If $A$ is closed in the complete ambient [metric space](../../../../../metric-space.md), any [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in $A$ converges in $X$ by completeness, and its limit belongs to $A$ by part (i). Thus $A$ is complete with its restricted metric.

Conversely, suppose $A$ is complete and a [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $a_n\in A$ converges in $X$ to $x$. A convergent [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is Cauchy, also in the restricted metric, so completeness of $A$ gives $a_n\to a$ for some $a\in A$. Uniqueness of a metric limit gives $x=a\in A$. Part (i) now makes $A$ closed. Hence

$$
\boxed{A\text{ complete}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\text{ closed in }X.}
$$
