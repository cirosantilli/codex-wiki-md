↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [metric space](../../../../../metric-space.md) is **[complete](../../../../../completeness.md)** when every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in it converges to a point of that space. A [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is Cauchy when, for each $\epsilon>0$, there is $N$ such that $d(x_m,x_n)<\epsilon$ whenever $m,n\geq N$. The requirement that the limit lie in the space distinguishes completeness from having limits only in a larger ambient space.
