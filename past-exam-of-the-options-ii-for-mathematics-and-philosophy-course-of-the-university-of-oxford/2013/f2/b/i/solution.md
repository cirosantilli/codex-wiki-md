↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Lagrange multiplier theorem](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) for one regular constraint says: if $f,k$ are $C^1$ near $x$, $f(x)=0$, $\nabla f(x)\ne0$, and $x$ is a local extremum of $k$ restricted to $f^{-1}(0)$, then

$$
\boxed{\nabla k(x)=\lambda\nabla f(x)\quad\text{for some }\lambda\in\mathbb R.}
$$

For several constraints with linearly independent [gradients](../../../../../gradient.md), the objective [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) lies in their linear span. The regularity assumption rules out singular points where the usual multiplier conclusion can fail.
