# Radius of a Maclaurin series from successive derivatives

↑ **Parent:** [Radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md)

Write the [Maclaurin series](maclaurin-series.md) coefficients as $a_n=f^{(n)}(0)/n!$. When the successive ratios exist eventually and have a limit, the [ratio test](ratio-test.md) gives the displayed [radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md), including zero and infinity. The unconditional formula is $R^{-1}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}$, from the [Cauchy-Hadamard theorem](cauchy-hadamard-theorem.md). Endpoints must be checked separately.

The existence and convergence of this [power series](power-series.md) do not by themselves imply that it equals $f$: equality requires [analyticity](analyticity.md) or control of the [Taylor remainder](taylor-remainder.md). The smooth [function](function-split.md) $f(x)=e^{-1/x^2}$ for $x\ne0$, $f(0)=0$, has all derivatives zero at zero, so its Maclaurin series is identically zero despite the nonzero values away from zero.
