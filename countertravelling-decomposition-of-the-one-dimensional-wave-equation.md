# Countertravelling decomposition of the one-dimensional wave equation

↑ **Parent:** [One-dimensional wave equation](one-dimensional-wave-equation.md)

Let $H$ be any [antiderivative](antiderivative.md) of the initial [velocity](velocity.md), $H'=g$. One choice of profiles is

$$
F(s)=\frac{f(s)}2-\frac{H(s)}{2c},\qquad G(s)=\frac{f(s)}2+\frac{H(s)}{2c}.
$$

Opposite additive constants in $F$ and $G$ cancel, so the [displacement](displacement.md) is independent of the chosen [antiderivative](antiderivative.md). Even for [compact support](compact-support.md) of $g$, these separate profiles can approach nonzero constants; cancellation in their sum is essential to [support widening for the wave equation](support-widening-for-the-wave-equation.md).

For a short derivation, the [characteristic coordinates](characteristic-coordinate.md) $\xi=x-ct$, $\eta=x+ct$ give $u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx}=-4c^2u_{\xi\eta}$ by the [chain rule](chain-rule.md). Thus $u=F(\xi)+G(\eta)$, and the [Cauchy data](cauchy-data.md) imply $F+G=f$ and $-cF'+cG'=g$, giving the profiles above.
