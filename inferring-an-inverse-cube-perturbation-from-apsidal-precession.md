# Inferring an inverse-cube perturbation from apsidal precession

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse-cube perturbation of Kepler apsidal motion](inverse-cube-perturbation-of-kepler-apsidal-motion.md)

For radial acceleration $-GM(1+\gamma/r)/r^2$, conserved [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) $h$ gives an angular radial frequency $\alpha=\sqrt{1-GM\gamma/h^2}$ through the [Binet equation](binet-equation.md). Successive pericentres differ by $2\pi/\alpha$, so a small perturbation produces [apsidal precession](apsidal-precession.md) $\Delta\varpi\simeq\pi GM\gamma/h^2$. Writing the unperturbed semilatus rectum as $p=h^2/(GM)$ gives the displayed inverse relation. For a nearly circular orbit of period $P$, use $p\simeq[GM(P/(2\pi))^2]^{1/3}$. Positive $\gamma$ means prograde precession; $\gamma$ is a length. A measured rate per century must first be converted to an angle per orbit.
