# Matter wave

↑ **Parent:** [Wave function](wave-function-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_wave)

A matter wave is the quantum wave associated with a material particle. Its phase can interfere after coherent paths recombine.

In the position representation, a [matter wave](matter-wave.md) is described by a [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md): a [complex number](complex-number.md) [probability amplitude](probability-amplitude.md), with detection probabilities given by the [Born rule](born-rule.md). A localized [wave packet](wave-packet.md) combines different [momenta](momentum-split.md); its envelope can move while its individual [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) crests move at another speed. Neither curve is a literal mechanical oscillation of material along a particle's path. The [de Broglie phase harmony](de-broglie-phase-harmony.md) explains the historical route to $\lambda=h/p$; the [relativistic matter-wave velocities](relativistic-matter-wave-velocities.md) distinguish the two speeds. The [Davisson–Germer experiment](davisson-germer-experiment.md) and the [Thomson electron transmission diffraction experiment](thomson-electron-transmission-diffraction-experiment.md) connect that spatial [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) to measured [electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md).
