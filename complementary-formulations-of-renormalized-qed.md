# Complementary formulations of renormalized QED

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum electrodynamics](quantum-electrodynamics.md)

The achievement depended on complementary ways to organize the same physics. In his Nobel lecture, delivered in 1966 for the 1965 prize, [Sin-Itiro Tomonaga](sin-itiro-tomonaga.md) described covariant contact transformations and replacing the unobservable bare [mass](mass.md) and [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) by experimental values. He also credited [Julian Seymour Schwinger](julian-seymour-schwinger.md)'s magnetic-moment work and [Richard Feynman](richard-feynman.md)'s methods for extending the approximation.[Development of Quantum Electrodynamics, May 6, 1966; concluding discussion of renormalization and complementary calculations](development-of-quantum-electrodynamics-personal-recollections-by-sin-itiro-tomonaga.md)

[Richard Feynman](richard-feynman.md)'s 1965 lecture traced his spacetime and [path integral](path-integral.md) viewpoint and the [Feynman diagrams](feynman-diagram.md) that made [perturbation theory](perturbation-theory.md) easier to organize. He emphasized that the [path integral](path-integral.md) viewpoint helped him find expressions without being essential to every calculation. These accounts support shared credit for workable [renormalized](renormalization.md) [QED](quantum-electrodynamics.md), not attribution of the whole theory to a single person.[The Development of the Space-Time View of Quantum Electrodynamics, December 11, 1965; paragraphs on manifest covariance, diagrams and the final assessment of path integrals](the-development-of-the-space-time-view-of-quantum-electrodynamics-by-richard-feynman.md)
