# Field of algebraic numbers

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic number](algebraic-number.md)

The complex [algebraic numbers](algebraic-number.md) form a [field](field.md), the [algebraic closure](algebraic-closure.md) of $\mathbb Q$ inside $\mathbb C$. To see closure under the field operations, if $\alpha,\beta$ are [algebraic numbers](algebraic-number.md), the [tower law](tower-law.md) makes $\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta)$ a finite-dimensional [field extension](field-extension.md) of $\mathbb Q$. Every element of this extension is an [algebraic element](algebraic-element.md), because its successive powers are [linearly dependent](linear-dependence.md). In particular it contains $\alpha\pm\beta$, $\alpha\beta$ and $\alpha/\beta$ when $\beta\ne0$.

It is an infinite-degree [algebraic extension](algebraic-extension.md). A primitive $p$th [root of unity](root-of-unity.md) has [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) $1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}$ of degree $p-1$ for every prime $p$, so the degrees of its finite subextensions are unbounded. Finally a complex root of a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) whose coefficients are [algebraic numbers](algebraic-number.md) is itself algebraic over their finite-degree coefficient field and hence over $\mathbb Q$, again by the [tower law](tower-law.md). Thus every such [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) splits in this [field](field.md), establishing the [algebraic closure](algebraic-closure.md) assertion.
