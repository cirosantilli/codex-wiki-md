↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The integral and differential forms of [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) in [electrostatics](../../../../electrostatics-split.md) are

$$
\boxed{\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf A
=\frac{Q_{\rm enc}}{\epsilon_0},\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}.}
$$

Here $\partial\mathcal V$ is a closed surface bounding a volume $\mathcal V$, $d\mathbf A=\hat{\mathbf n}\,dA$ is its outward area [vector](../../../../vector.md), $\mathbf E$ is the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md), $\rho$ is total volume [charge density](../../../../charge-density.md), and $Q_{\rm enc}=\int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,d^3r$ is enclosed [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md). The [divergence theorem](../../../../divergence-theorem.md) converts one form to the other. For ideal sheets or [point charges](../../../../point-charge.md), interpret the differential equation with their distributional [charge densities](../../../../charge-density.md).
