<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Fermat's principle](../../../../fermat-principle.md) states that the travel time, or equivalently the [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md) $\int n\,ds$ in a stationary medium, is stationary under small variations of the path with endpoints held fixed. It need not be a global minimum.

Let a [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) join a point at height $h_1$ above a plane interface to a point at depth $h_2$ below it, with horizontal separation $D$. If the crossing point is horizontally $\xi$ from the first endpoint, its [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md) is

$$
\mathcal L(\xi)=n_1\sqrt{h_1^2+\xi^2}
+n_2\sqrt{h_2^2+(D-\xi)^2}.
$$

Setting its derivative to zero gives

$$
\frac{n_1\xi}{\sqrt{h_1^2+\xi^2}}
=\frac{n_2(D-\xi)}{\sqrt{h_2^2+(D-\xi)^2}}.
$$

The two geometric ratios are the sines of the incident and refracted [angles](../../../../angle.md), measured from the interface [normal vector](../../../../normal-vector.md). Hence [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md) is

$$
\boxed{n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2.}
$$
