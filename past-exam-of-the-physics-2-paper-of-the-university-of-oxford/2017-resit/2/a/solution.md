<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The central [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) and spherical boundaries give a [spherically symmetric charge distribution](../../../../spherically-symmetric-charge-distribution.md). A [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](../../../../conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md) has zero [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) within its material. Consequently a spherical [Gaussian surface](../../../../gaussian-surface.md) inside the metal encloses zero net [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md), so the inner surface carries $-q$. The shell's prescribed total [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) then leaves $-2q$ on its outer surface. [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(r)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\,\hat{\mathbf r},&0<r<r_1,\\[4pt]
\mathbf0,&r_1<r<r_2,\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{2q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\,\hat{\mathbf r},&r>r_2.
\end{cases}}
$$

The [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is singular at the ideal central [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md). At each charged surface the normal component has distinct one-sided limits, as required by the [electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor](../../../../electrostatic-boundary-conditions-at-a-conductor.md). Spherical symmetry makes the outer [surface charge density](../../../../surface-charge-density.md) uniform:

$$
\boxed{Q_{\rm inner}=-q,\qquad \sigma_{\rm outer}=-\frac{2q}{4\pi r_2^2}.}
$$
