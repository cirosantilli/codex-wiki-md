<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Use the [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md) approximation, neglecting the [fringing field of a capacitor](../../../fringing-field-of-a-capacitor.md). With plate area $A=\pi(0.10\,\mathrm m)^2$, the initial [capacitance](../../../capacitance.md) and opposite plate [electric charges](../../../electric-charge-split.md) are

$$
C_i=\frac{\epsilon_0A}{0.010\,\mathrm m}=2.78163\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm F,
\qquad
\boxed{Q_\pm=\pm C_i(100\,\mathrm V)=\pm2.78163\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C.}
$$

After the [electric battery](../../../electric-battery.md) is disconnected, the isolated plate [electric charges](../../../electric-charge-split.md) stay fixed. Doubling the separation halves the [capacitance](../../../capacitance.md). The quasistatic pulling work is the increase of [electrostatic energy](../../../electrostatic-energy.md):

$$
W=\frac{Q^2}{2C_f}-\frac{Q^2}{2C_i}
=\frac12C_i(100\,\mathrm V)^2,
\qquad
\boxed{W=1.39081\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J.}
$$

The final [electric potential difference](../../../electric-potential-difference.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta V_f=\frac Q{C_f}=200\,\mathrm V.}
$$

An [electron](../../../electron.md) released at rest gains [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) $e\Delta V_f$, where $e$ is the positive [elementary charge](../../../elementary-charge.md). Neglecting radiation and the negligible perturbation caused by this one [electron](../../../electron.md), [conservation of energy](../../../conservation-of-energy.md) gives

$$
\frac12m_ev^2=e\Delta V_f,
\qquad
\boxed{v=\sqrt{\frac{2e(200\,\mathrm V)}{m_e}}
=8.39\times10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This is $0.0280c$, so the nonrelativistic [kinetic energy](../../../kinetic-energy.md) is adequate. The circular plates have finite radii; the numerical results are leading [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md) values rather than exact finite-plate [capacitances](../../../capacitance.md).
