<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [method of images](../../../../method-of-images.md) replaces a conductor's induced [surface charge](../../../../surface-charge.md) by suitable fictitious [image charges](../../../../image-charge.md) located outside the physical region in which the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is sought. Choose them so that the combined [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) satisfies the specified conductor boundary values, has the required behaviour at infinity, and has the correct physical sources. The [uniqueness theorem for the electrostatic potential](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-the-electrostatic-potential.md) then identifies this constructed [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) with the physical solution in that region.

For an infinite grounded [conducting plane](../../../../conducting-plane.md) at $z=0$, a [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) $q$ at $(x,y,z)$ with $z>0$ has an [image charge](../../../../image-charge.md) $-q$ at $(x,y,-z)$. Distances to the pair are equal on the plane, so their [electric potentials](../../../../electric-potential.md) cancel there. This construction determines the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) above the plane; below it the actual conductor has constant [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) and zero [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md), rather than the fictitious image's continuation.
