<h1 id="2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For a [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) $q_j$, [Coulomb's law](../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) gives $\mathbf E_j=q_j\hat{\mathbf r}/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$. The flux through an oriented surface element is

$$
\mathbf E_j\cdot d\mathbf S=\frac{q_j}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot d\mathbf S}{r^2}=\frac{q_j}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\,d\Omega.
$$

Here $d\Omega$ is its signed [solid angle](../../../../solid-angle.md) seen from the charge. A closed surface subtends total oriented solid angle $4\pi$ for a point inside and zero for a point outside. Thus the flux of each inside charge is $q_j/\epsilon_0$ and each outside charge has zero net flux. By [superposition](../../../../superposition-principle.md),

$$
\boxed{\oint_S\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf S=\frac1{\epsilon_0}\sum_{j\text{ inside}}q_j=\frac{q_{\rm enclosed}}{\epsilon_0}.}
$$

This is [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md); a surface passing through a point-charge singularity is excluded.
