<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define the requested [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) as $\Delta V=V(b)-V(-a)$. Since $E_x=-dV/dx$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=-\int_{-a}^{b}E_x(x)\,dx\\
&=\frac{C}{\epsilon_0a}\int_{-a}^{0}(x+a)\,dx+\frac{C}{\epsilon_0b}\int_0^b(b-x)\,dx,
\end{aligned}
$$

so

$$
\boxed{V(b)-V(-a)=\frac{C(a+b)}{2\epsilon_0}}.
$$

Geometrically, the integral is the area of two triangles under the signed [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) profile. For $C>0$, the right-hand plane is at higher [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md). Choosing $V(-a)=0$, a continuous interior [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) is $V=C(x+a)^2/(2\epsilon_0a)$ in the first slab and $V=Ca/(2\epsilon_0)+C(x-x^2/(2b))/\epsilon_0$ in the second. These expressions also verify the boundary values directly.
