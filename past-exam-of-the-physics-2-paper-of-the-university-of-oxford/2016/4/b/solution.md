<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) is $\boldsymbol\mu=IA\hat{\mathbf n}$ and its magnetic [torque](../../../../torque.md) is $\boldsymbol\tau_{\rm mag}=\boldsymbol\mu\times\mathbf B$. Its component along the rotation axis opposes the imposed rotation. For positive $\omega$, the external driving [torque](../../../../torque.md) needed to keep the [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) constant is

$$
\tau_{\rm drive}(t)=IAB\sin\omega t=\frac{B^2A^2\omega}{R}\sin^2\omega t.
$$

Averaging over a complete rotation, with $\langle\sin^2\omega t\rangle=1/2$, gives

$$
\boxed{\langle\tau_{\rm drive}\rangle=\frac{B^2\pi^2r^4\omega}{2R}}.
$$

As an independent sign and dimension check, $\omega\langle\tau_{\rm drive}\rangle=R\langle I^2\rangle$. Thus the mechanical input [power](../../../../power.md) equals the mean [Joule heating](../../../../joule-heating.md); a passive loop cannot supply a sustained driving [torque](../../../../torque.md). This average assumes the same negligible-[self-inductance](../../../../self-inductance.md) approximation as the current expression.
