<h1 id="11/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A series connection has the same [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) through each element, so [complex impedances](../../../../../electrical-impedance.md) add:

$$
\boxed{Z_{\rm s}=R+i\left(\omega L-\frac1{\omega C}\right)}.
$$

For $R>0$, define $\phi=\arg\widetilde V-\arg\widetilde I=\arg Z_{\rm s}$. Then

$$
\phi=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega L-1/(\omega C)}R\right).
$$

Above resonance, the circuit is inductive and [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) leads current; below resonance it is capacitive and [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) lags current. At

$$
\boxed{\omega_0=\frac1{\sqrt{LC}},\qquad f_0=\frac1{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}},
$$

the inductive and capacitive [reactances](../../../../../electrical-reactance.md) cancel and $Z_{\rm s}=R$ is purely resistive. The same resonance condition holds for an ideal series circuit independent of the drive amplitude.
