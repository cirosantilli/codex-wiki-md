<h1 id="11/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A parallel connection has the same [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) across each element, so the [electrical admittances](../../../../../electrical-admittance.md) add. With $G=1/R$ and $B=\omega C-1/(\omega L)$,

$$
Y_{\rm p}=G+iB,\qquad\boxed{Z_{\rm p}=\left[\frac1R+i\left(\omega C-\frac1{\omega L}\right)\right]^{-1}=\frac{G-iB}{G^2+B^2}}.
$$

For a prescribed sinusoidal current, $\widetilde V=Z_{\rm p}\widetilde I$. Thus the voltage-minus-current [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is

$$
\phi=-\tan^{-1}\left[R\left(\omega C-\frac1{\omega L}\right)\right].
$$

Below $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ the circuit is inductive, so [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) leads current; above it the circuit is capacitive, so [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) lags current. At $\omega_0$, the reactive branch currents cancel, $Y_{\rm p}=1/R$ and $\boxed{Z_{\rm p}=R}$. This is the impedance maximum for positive finite $R$, whereas the series circuit has its impedance minimum at the same [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md). It does not mean that either individual reactive branch current vanishes.
