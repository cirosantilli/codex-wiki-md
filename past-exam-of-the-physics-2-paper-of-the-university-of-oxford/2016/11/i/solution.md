<h1 id="11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose the [phasor](../../../../phasor.md) convention $V(t)=\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde V e^{i\omega t})$ and similarly for the [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md). In a linear sinusoidal steady state, differentiation becomes multiplication by $i\omega$, and [complex impedance](../../../../electrical-impedance.md) is $Z=\widetilde V/\widetilde I$. [Kirchhoff's current law](../../../../kirchhoff-node-law.md) and [Kirchhoff's voltage law](../../../../kirchhoff-cycle-law.md) then become algebraic equations that retain both amplitude and [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) information.

For a [resistor](../../../../resistor.md), $V=RI$, so $Z_R=R$. For a [capacitor](../../../../capacitor.md), $I=C\dot V$, while an [inductor](../../../../inductor.md) obeys $V=L\dot I$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{Z_R=R,\qquad Z_C=\frac1{i\omega C}=-\frac{i}{\omega C},\qquad Z_L=i\omega L}.
$$

The resistor's [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) and current are in [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md). The inductor's [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) leads its current by $\pi/2$; the capacitor's current leads its [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) by $\pi/2$. Replacing $e^{i\omega t}$ by $e^{-i\omega t}$ conjugates every impedance but leaves these physical lead-and-lag statements unchanged. The [phasor](../../../../phasor.md) method describes the steady state; switch-on transients must be added separately when initial conditions are relevant.
