<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) magnitude falls as $1/r^2$ throughout the vacuum gap, so its maximum occurs just outside the inner sphere:

$$
E_{\max}=\frac{|V_1|b}{a(b-a)}=\frac{10^3\times0.4}{0.1(0.4-0.1)}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{E_{\max}=1.333\times10^4\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}},\qquad r=a^+}.
$$

Inside the metal the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is zero. The one-sided gap value is the relevant value for dielectric breakdown; the ideal zero-thickness conducting boundary itself should not be assigned a single field value across its surface-charge discontinuity.
