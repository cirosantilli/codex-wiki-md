<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Inserting an independently biased conducting sphere makes two vacuum gaps, each with its own constant radial [electric flux](../../../../electric-flux.md). Let $A_1$ and $A_2$ be the coefficients of $1/r^2$. The two potential drops are

$$
V_1-V_2=A_1\left(\frac1a-\frac1c\right),\qquad V_2=A_2\left(\frac1c-\frac1b\right).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(r)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(V_1-V_2)ac}{(c-a)r^2}\hat{\mathbf r},&a<r<c,\\
\dfrac{V_2bc}{(b-c)r^2}\hat{\mathbf r},&c<r<b.
\end{cases}}
$$

The middle conductor can carry net [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md), so $A_1$ and $A_2$ need not be equal. Its charge is $4\pi\epsilon_0(A_2-A_1)$, consistent with the jump in the normal [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md). For $0<V_2<V_1$ both fields point outwards; outside that interval one of the gap fields reverses direction.
