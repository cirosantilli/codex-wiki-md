<h1 id="10/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a lens between an object and a screen, $u+v=D$ and the [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) relation requires $uv=fD$. Eliminating $v$ gives the quadratic equation

$$
u^2-Du+fD=0,\qquad u_\pm=\frac{D\pm\sqrt{D^2-4fD}}2.
$$

For $D>4f$, both roots lie between $0$ and $D$, and they exchange the object and image distances. Both therefore focus the image on the same screen, with reciprocal absolute [magnifications](../../../../magnification.md). The distance between lens positions is

$$
\boxed{l=|u_+-u_-|=\sqrt{D^2-4fD}=\sqrt{D(D-4f)}}.
$$

The existence qualification is essential: for $D=4f$ the two roots coincide and there is only one distinct position; for $D<4f$ there is none. For example, $D=3f$ makes the discriminant negative, providing a concrete counterexample to an unconditional assertion of two positions. This is the basis of the [Bessel lens displacement method](../../../../bessel-lens-displacement-method.md), with $f=(D^2-l^2)/(4D)$ when two distinct positions can be measured.
