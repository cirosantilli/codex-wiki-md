<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Place eight identical cubes around the charged corner to make one larger cube, with the [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) at its centre. Each large face has [electric flux](../../../../electric-flux.md) $q/(6\epsilon_0)$ and consists of four equivalent small faces. Each of the original cube's three faces opposite the charged corner therefore has

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{\rm opposite}=\frac{q}{24\epsilon_0}.}
$$

On each of its three faces containing the corner, the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) of the [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) obeying [Coulomb's law](../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) is tangent to the face at every regular point; its outward-normal component is zero. Defining the face integral by omitting the singular corner gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{\rm incident}=0,\qquad\Phi_{\rm original\ cube}=\frac{q}{8\epsilon_0}.}
$$

A boundary [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md) does not satisfy the ordinary nonsingular hypotheses for simply counting it as an interior charge in [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md). The $1/8$ result expresses the octant [solid angle](../../../../solid-angle.md) and the specified improper surface integrals. Moving a charge towards the corner from inside a cube before integrating is a different limiting operation; its total closed-surface [electric flux](../../../../electric-flux.md) stays $q/\epsilon_0$ until it reaches the boundary.
