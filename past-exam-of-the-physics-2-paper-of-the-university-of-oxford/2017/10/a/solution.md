<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md), the source and observation geometry make the incident and outgoing [wavefronts](../../../../wavefront.md) effectively planar across the [aperture](../../../../aperture.md). For direct propagation over distance $z$, the quadratic variation in propagation [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) across a slit of width $w$ must be negligible, approximately $w^2/(\lambda z)\ll1$. The far-field angular [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is then the [Fourier transform](../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) of the [aperture](../../../../aperture.md)'s complex illumination. A converging [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) also produces this angular distribution in its rear focal plane for collimated illumination, without requiring a physically enormous observation distance. The approximation concerns the aperture-dependent [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md), not the absence of [diffraction](../../../../diffraction.md).
