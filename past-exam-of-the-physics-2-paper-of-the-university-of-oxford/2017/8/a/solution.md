<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define $V=\phi(r_2)-\phi(r_1)$ and $\eta=\ln(r_2/r_1)>0$. Cylindrical [symmetry](../../../../symmetry-physics.md) and [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) imply a radial [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) proportional to $1/r$. Integrating $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$ fixes the sign:

$$
\phi(r)-\phi(r_1)=\frac{V\ln(r/r_1)}\eta,\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf E(r)=-\frac{V}{\eta r}\hat{\mathbf r}.}
$$

If the inner cylinder has [linear charge density](../../../../linear-charge-density.md) $\lambda_q$, a coaxial [Gaussian surface](../../../../gaussian-surface.md) gives $2\pi r E_r=\lambda_q/\epsilon_0$, so $\lambda_q=-2\pi\epsilon_0V/\eta$. [Capacitance](../../../../capacitance.md) is positive regardless of the chosen terminal polarity:

$$
\boxed{C'=\frac{|\lambda_q|}{|V|}=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0}{\ln(r_2/r_1)}.}
$$

End effects are neglected for the long cylinders, as required by the cylindrical approximation.
