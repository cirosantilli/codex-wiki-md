<h1 id="10/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take the positive horizontal direction along B. The initial direction is $(\alpha-90^\circ)-\theta$. Reflection at the horizontal face B reverses the vertical direction. At C, symmetry of the equilateral geometry gives internal incidence $r$, so [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md) makes the emerging angle to C's normal equal to $\theta$. Its direction is therefore $(90^\circ-\alpha)+\theta$. Subtracting the incoming direction gives

$$
\boxed{\delta=180^\circ-2\alpha+2\theta=60^\circ+2\theta.}
$$

This is the smaller angle between the incident and emergent propagation directions in the requested reflection interval. The cancellation of $n$ occurs because the entry and exit [refraction angles](../../../../angle-of-refraction.md) have equal magnitudes; [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) still determines whether the reflected route exists and where it exits.
