<h1 id="10/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Assume [normal dispersion](../../../../normal-dispersion.md), so shorter visible wavelengths have larger [refractive indices](../../../../refractive-index.md). At fixed external angle $\theta$, the relevant criticality parameter is

$$
n\sin i_B=\sin\alpha\sqrt{n^2-\sin^2\theta}-\cos\alpha\sin\theta,
$$

which increases with $n$ on this route. Thus **shorter-wavelength blue light is totally reflected at B and reaches C, while longer-wavelength red light can transmit through B**; green lies at the critical threshold. The prism separates the routes by wavelength near the green cutoff.

The totally reflected rays emerging from C have the same external deviation $180^\circ-2\alpha+2\theta$ despite their different [refractive indices](../../../../refractive-index.md); their exit positions can differ. Red light also has a partially reflected component by the [Fresnel equations](../../../../fresnel-equations.md), so C is not a mathematically perfect colour cutoff. The observation concerns total versus partial reflection, with the transmitted B rays spread by wavelength. Anomalous dispersion, if present, would require the actual $n(\lambda)$ instead of the normal ordering assumed here.
