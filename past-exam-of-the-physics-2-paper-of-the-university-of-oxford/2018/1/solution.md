<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md) of a signed point [electric charge](../../../electric-charge-split.md) in vacuum follows [Coulomb's law](../../../coulomb-s-law.md):

$$
E(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{r}{|r|^3}.
$$

For the [conducting plane](../../../conducting-plane.md), the [method of images](../../../method-of-images.md) replaces each charge $Q_j$ at height $h_j$ by an [image charge](../../../image-charge.md) $-Q_j$ at height $-h_j$. The real and image [electric fields](../../../electric-field-split.md) have cancelling tangential components and equal normal components at the surface. Thus the [electric field at a conducting plane from axial point charges](../../../electric-field-at-a-conducting-plane-from-axial-point-charges.md), just above the point directly below them, is

$$
E_z(0^+)=-\frac{2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{Q_j}{h_j^2}
=\frac{60}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac1{5000^2}-\frac1{8000^2}\right).
$$

Numerically,

$$
\boxed{E(0^+)\simeq1.31\times10^4\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\,\hat z,\quad\text{upwards}.}
$$

The surface value means the air-side limit. Inside the ideal [conductor](../../../electrical-conductor.md) the electrostatic [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md) is zero; its discontinuity across the surface is supported by induced [surface charge](../../../surface-charge.md). Omitting the [image charges](../../../image-charge.md) would give half the external answer.
