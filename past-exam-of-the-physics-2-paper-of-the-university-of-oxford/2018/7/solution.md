<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

The integral and differential forms of [Gauss's law](../../../gauss-s-law.md) in vacuum are

$$
\oint_{\partial V}E\cdot n\,dA=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{enc}}}{\epsilon_0},\qquad
\nabla\cdot E=\frac\rho{\epsilon_0}.
$$

For an isolated spherical [conductor](../../../electrical-conductor.md), symmetry gives $E(r)=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$ outside and zero inside. Taking [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) zero at infinity, $V(R)=\int_R^\infty E(r)\,dr=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0R)$. Thus the [capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere](../../../capacitance-of-an-isolated-conducting-sphere.md) is

$$
\boxed{C=4\pi\epsilon_0R.}
$$

Charging from zero gives the [electrostatic potential energy](../../../electrostatic-energy.md)

$$
\boxed{W=\int_0^Q\frac qC\,dq=\frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0R}=\frac12CV^2.}
$$

This is also the integral of [electric field energy density](../../../electric-field-energy-density.md) $\epsilon_0E^2/2$ outside the sphere. The later insertion into a [coaxial cylindrical capacitor](../../../coaxial-cylindrical-capacitor.md) conserves its [electric charge](../../../electric-charge-split.md), not its original isolated [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md).
