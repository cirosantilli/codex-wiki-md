<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The fixed [point charge](../../../../../point-charge.md) produces [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md) $\phi(r)=k_eq/r$, where $k_e=1/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$. The external quasistatic [work](../../../../../work.md) is the increase in [Coulomb potential energy](../../../../../coulomb-potential-energy.md):

$$
\boxed{W_{+}=q\,[\phi(d)-\phi(2d)]=\frac{k_eq^2}{2d}=\frac{q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0d}.}
$$

The endpoints have radii $2d$ and $d$, irrespective of their opposite coordinate signs. One must use a path avoiding the singular [point charge](../../../../../point-charge.md) at the origin. The [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) is conservative on such paths, so the endpoint calculation suffices; a literal straight path through the origin is inadmissible. The [work](../../../../../work.md) done by the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) has the opposite sign.
