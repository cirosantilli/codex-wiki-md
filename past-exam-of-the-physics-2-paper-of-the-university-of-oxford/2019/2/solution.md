<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [method of images](../../../method-of-images.md) replaces [electrical conductors](../../../electrical-conductor.md) by fictitious [image charges](../../../image-charge.md) outside the physical region. Choose the [image charges](../../../image-charge.md) so that the physical [electric charges](../../../electric-charge-split.md) and their images produce the prescribed constant [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) on every conducting boundary. The [uniqueness theorem for the electrostatic potential](../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-the-electrostatic-potential.md) then identifies this constructed [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) with the physical solution in the charge-containing region. Several conducting planes can require several, or infinitely many, [image charges](../../../image-charge.md); a single reflected image is sufficient for one infinite plane.

Put the plane at $z=0$ and the [point charge](../../../point-charge.md) at $(0,0,d)$. For $z>0$, use an [image charge](../../../image-charge.md) $-q$ at $(0,0,-d)$:

$$
\phi(x,y,z)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-d)^2}}-\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z+d)^2}}\right].
$$

This vanishes on the plane. An arbitrary constant can be added if a different reference [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) is chosen. Excluding the [point charge](../../../point-charge.md)'s own [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md), [Coulomb's law](../../../coulomb-s-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0d^2}\,\hat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

The separation from the [image charge](../../../image-charge.md) is $2d$, and the [force](../../../force-split.md) is attractive for either sign of $q$. No imposed external [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md) is included.
