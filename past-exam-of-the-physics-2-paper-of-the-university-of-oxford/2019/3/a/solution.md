<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At [electrostatic equilibrium](../../../../electrostatic-equilibrium.md) the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) in the metal is zero. The empty cavity also has zero [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md), by the [uniqueness theorem for the electrostatic potential](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-the-electrostatic-potential.md). The [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) lies on the outer surface. For the isolated [conducting sphere](../../../../conducting-sphere.md), [spherical symmetry](../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) and [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(r)=\begin{cases}0,&r<R,\\ \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\hat{\mathbf r},&r>R.\end{cases}}
$$

Taking the [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) to vanish at infinity and using $E_r=-d\phi/dr$ gives

$$
\boxed{\phi(r)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0R},&r\leq R,\\ \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r},&r\geq R.\end{cases}}
$$

The [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) is continuous at the surface, while the normal [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) jumps by $\sigma/\epsilon_0$, with [surface charge density](../../../../surface-charge-density.md) $\sigma=Q/(4\pi R^2)$.
