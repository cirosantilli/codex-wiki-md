<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [paraxial approximation](../../../../paraxial-approximation.md), apply [refraction](../../../../refraction.md) at each surface of the [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) in air. With signed radii $R_1$ and $R_2$, the first surface obeys $n/v_1-1/u=(n-1)/R_1$, and the second obeys $1/v-n/v_1=(1-n)/R_2$. The separation of the surfaces is neglected, so the intermediate image coordinate $v_1$ cancels:

$$
\frac1v-\frac1u=(n-1)\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right)=\frac1f.
$$

This is [Lensmaker's equation](../../../../lensmaker-s-equation.md), with [Cartesian coordinate system](../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md) object coordinate $u<0$ for a real object. For an equiconvex [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) and light travelling left to right, $R_1=R$ and $R_2=-R$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac1f=\frac{2(n-1)}R,\qquad f=\frac{R}{2(n-1)}.}
$$
