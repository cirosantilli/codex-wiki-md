<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set the red image at coordinate zero, the blue image at $\delta=0.010\,\mathrm m$, and the screen at $D=1.000\,\mathrm m$. Let the correcting [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) be at $x$, with $\delta<x<D$. Both original images are then real objects for the correcting [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md), with positive object distances $u_r=x$, $u_b=x-\delta$, and common positive image distance $v=D-x$. The [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) equation gives

$$
\boxed{f_r=\frac{x(D-x)}D,\qquad f_b=\frac{(x-\delta)(D-x)}{D-\delta}.}
$$

The [refractive indices](../../../../refractive-index.md) and [Lensmaker's equation](../../../../lensmaker-s-equation.md) require

$$
\frac{f_b}{f_r}=\frac{n_r-1}{n_b-1}=\frac{10}{11}=\rho.
$$

Substitute the two [focal lengths](../../../../focal-length.md) and cancel $D-x$:

$$
D(x-\delta)=\rho x(D-\delta),\qquad
x=\frac{D\delta}{D-\rho(D-\delta)}=0.1000\,\mathrm m.
$$

Thus the correcting [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) is $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ beyond the red image and $900\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the screen. The required [focal lengths](../../../../focal-length.md) and surface radius are

$$
\boxed{f_r=90.0\,\mathrm{mm},\qquad f_b=81.818\ldots\,\mathrm{mm},\qquad R=2(n_r-1)f_r=72.0\,\mathrm{mm}.}
$$

The blue [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) is shorter because the blue [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) is larger; these two differing object distances are mapped to the same screen, removing the specified longitudinal [chromatic aberration](../../../../chromatic-aberration.md).
