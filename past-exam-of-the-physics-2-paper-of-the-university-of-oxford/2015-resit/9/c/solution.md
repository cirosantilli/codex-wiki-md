<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At equality, $I_1=V/(3R)-I_2/3=I_2$, so the common [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) is $V/(4R)$. Substituting the [capacitor](../../../../capacitor.md) [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) gives

$$
\frac{2V}{5R}e^{-t/\tau}=\frac{V}{4R},\qquad e^{-t/\tau}=\frac58.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{t=\tau\log\frac85=\frac{5RC}{3}\log\frac85}.
$$

For $R=50\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=0.2\ \mathrm{microfarads}$, $RC=0.0100\ \mathrm s$ and $\tau=0.01667\ \mathrm s$, giving $\boxed{t\simeq7.83\ \mathrm{ms}}$. The battery [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) cancels, as expected because every [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) scales linearly with it in this ideal linear circuit. The result assumes a nonzero source [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md); with $V=0$ both [electric currents](../../../../electric-current.md) vanish at all times.
