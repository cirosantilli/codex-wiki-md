<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take the [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) to vanish at infinity and work at $r>0$. For a positive [point charge](../../../point-charge.md) $q$ in vacuum,

$$
\boxed{V(r)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r},\qquad\mathbf E(r)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}}.
$$

The field is radial and outward. Since a radial scalar function has [gradient](../../../gradient.md) $\nabla V=(dV/dr)\hat{\mathbf r}$,

$$
-\nabla V=-\left(-\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\right)\hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf E.
$$

Thus the two expressions have the required vector relationship. Adding an arbitrary constant to $V$ would leave the [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md) unchanged; the chosen reference at infinity fixes that constant. The point-charge singularity itself is excluded from the ordinary [derivative](../../../derivative.md) calculation.
