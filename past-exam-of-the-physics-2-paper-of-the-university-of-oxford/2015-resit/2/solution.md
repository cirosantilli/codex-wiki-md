<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Let $\psi=kx-\omega t$ and use bold $\mathbf B$ for the [magnetic flux density](../../../magnetic-field-split.md), to distinguish it from the scalar amplitude $B$ in $E_z$. For a [plane electromagnetic wave](../../../plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) travelling along $+x$, [Faraday's law](../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives the wave-associated field

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{k}{\omega}\hat{\mathbf x}\times\mathbf E.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{B_x=0,\qquad B_y=-\frac{kB}{\omega}\cos\psi,\qquad B_z=\frac{kA}{\omega}\sin\psi}.
$$

For an explicit component check, $\nabla\times\mathbf E=(0,kB\sin\psi,kA\cos\psi)$, while differentiating the displayed field gives $-\partial\mathbf B/\partial t$ equal to that vector. The field is transverse and also satisfies $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$.

In vacuum, [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) compatibility requires $\omega/k=c$, so each factor $k/\omega$ is $1/c$. More generally it is the inverse [phase velocity](../../../phase-velocity.md) on a suitable homogeneous-medium plane-wave branch. A time-independent background field could be added without changing the electric wave's Faraday equation; the requested propagating-wave field takes that independent background to be zero. Finally, $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B=(k/\omega)(E_y^2+E_z^2)\hat{\mathbf x}$ confirms the propagation direction and the relative signs.
