<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Define $\mathbf d$ to point from the negative charge to the positive charge. The [electric dipole moment](../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is $\mathbf p=q\mathbf d$. In a uniform [electric field](../../../electric-field-split.md), the two [forces](../../../force-split.md) cancel, so **the net force is zero**. They nevertheless form a [couple](../../../couple-mechanics.md) with [torque](../../../torque.md)

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf p\times\mathbf E,\qquad |\boldsymbol\tau|=pE|\sin\theta|.}
$$

For a positive angular displacement increasing $\theta$, the restoring torque component is $-pE\sin\theta$. Thus $dU/d\theta=pE\sin\theta$ and

$$
\boxed{U=-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E=-pE\cos\theta,}
$$

with the additive constant chosen to make $U=0$ at $\theta=\pi/2$. Alignment is a stable minimum and antialignment an unstable maximum. The zero-force result uses a uniform field; a field gradient can exert a net [force](../../../force-split.md) on a dipole.
