<h1 id="11/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Initially the open switch leaves a series capacitor-resistor-inductor circuit driven by the battery. After a long time the [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) is zero and the [capacitor voltage](../../../../capacitor-voltage.md) is $V_0$. Closing the switch bypasses and, under the stated idealization, instantaneously discharges the capacitor. The inductor current remains continuous at zero.

The remaining [RL circuit](../../../../rl-circuit.md) obeys $L\dot I+RI=V_0$, so

$$
I(t)=\frac{V_0}{R}\left(1-e^{-Rt/L}\right),\qquad\boxed{I(0.005\,\mathrm s)=2(1-e^{-1.875})\simeq1.69\,\mathrm A.}
$$

The capacitor's impulsive discharge through the ideal short is distinct from the finite [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) through the coil. A finite switch resistance would resolve that separate fast transient.
