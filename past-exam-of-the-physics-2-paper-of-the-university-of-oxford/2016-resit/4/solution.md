<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

[Faraday's law of induction](../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives the [electromotive force](../../../electromotive-force.md) $\mathcal E=-d\Phi_B/dt$. For a fixed circuit, $\mathcal E=\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l$; for moving material wire, include the motional term $\mathbf u\times\mathbf B$ in that line integral. [Lenz's law](../../../lenz-s-law.md) fixes the sign: the induced response opposes the change of linked flux, rather than necessarily opposing the field itself.

For the long solenoid, [Ampère's law](../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) gives $B\simeq\mu_0nI$ inside and negligible exterior field away from the ends. With $N=nD$ turns and area $\pi a^2$, the linked flux is $N\Phi_B=\mu_0n^2D\pi a^2I$. Hence the [self-inductance](../../../self-inductance.md) is

$$
\boxed{L=\frac{N\Phi_B}{I}=\mu_0n^2\pi a^2D.}
$$

This assumes $D\gg a$, closely spaced turns, negligible end fringing and an air core with relative [magnetic permeability](../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) close to one. A finite core permeability or significant fringing changes the answer.
