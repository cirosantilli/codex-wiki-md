<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

[Huygens' principle](../../../huygens-principle.md) treats every point of an advancing [wavefront](../../../wavefront.md) as a source of secondary wavelets; their common envelope forms the next wavefront. Consider two points of an incident plane wave reaching the interface at different times. During the same interval $\Delta t$, the remaining incident portion travels $v_1\Delta t$ while the first transmitted wavelet expands a distance $v_2\Delta t$. The two wavefront right triangles share their interface segment, so

$$
\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}=\frac{n_2}{n_1},\qquad\boxed{n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2.}
$$

This is [Snell's law](../../../snell-s-law.md), with both angles measured from the surface normal. The speeds are phase velocities at the common frequency in stationary isotropic media. If the equation requires $|\sin\theta_2|>1$, there is no propagating refracted ray and [total internal reflection](../../../total-internal-reflection.md) applies instead.
