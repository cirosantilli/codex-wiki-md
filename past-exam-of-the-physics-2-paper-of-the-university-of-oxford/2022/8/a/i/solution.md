<h1 id="8/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let the unknown [series circuit](../../../../../series-circuit.md) contain [electrical resistance](../../../../../electrical-resistance.md) $R_s>0$ and [inductance](../../../../../inductance.md) $L>0$. Its [electrical impedance](../../../../../electrical-impedance.md) is $Z=R_s+i\omega L$. Thus $A=R_s$, $B=L$, and conjugate [impedance matching](../../../../../impedance-matching.md) is possible with

$$
\boxed{R=R_s,\qquad \omega=\frac1{\sqrt{LC}}.}
$$

At this [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md), the [inductor](../../../../../inductor.md) and load [capacitor](../../../../../capacitor.md) have opposite [electrical reactances](../../../../../electrical-reactance.md) of equal magnitude. Literal equality of their separate [electrical impedances](../../../../../electrical-impedance.md) is impossible; for example, $R_s=L=C=1$ gives $1+i\omega$ versus $R-i/\omega$ for every $\omega>0$.
