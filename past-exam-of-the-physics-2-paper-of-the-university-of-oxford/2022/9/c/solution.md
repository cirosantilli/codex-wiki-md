<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) within a [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](../../../../conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md) vanishes. The normal [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) boundary condition, with outward [normal vector](../../../../normal-vector.md) $+\hat{\mathbf z}$, therefore gives $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_z(0^+)$. Directly below the monopole, $a=0$:

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm mono}(0)=-\frac{q}{2\pi h^2}.}
$$

For the [electric dipole](../../../../electric-dipole.md),

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm dip}(0)=-\frac{qd}{\pi h^3}+O(qd^2/h^4).}
$$

The exact finite-separation answer is

$$
\sigma(0)=\frac{q}{2\pi}\left[\frac1{(h+d)^2}-\frac1{h^2}\right],
$$

whose [Taylor series](../../../../taylor-series.md) agrees with the boxed leading term. Although the real [point charges](../../../../point-charge.md) have zero total [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md), their unequal distances leave negative induced [surface charge density](../../../../surface-charge-density.md) immediately beneath them.
