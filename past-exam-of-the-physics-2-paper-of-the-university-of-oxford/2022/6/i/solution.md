<h1 id="6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The distance from the origin to the incident straight [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) is $d_a$ (taking $d_a\geq0$). At its intersection with the sphere, let $i$ and $t$ be the [angle of incidence](../../../../angle-of-incidence.md) and [angle of refraction](../../../../angle-of-refraction.md), measured from the radial [normal vector](../../../../normal-vector.md). Elementary [right triangle](../../../../right-triangle.md) geometry gives $d_a=r_b\sin i$. By [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md),

$$
n_a\sin i=n_b\sin t.
$$

The refracted straight [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) inside the sphere has distance $d_b=r_b\sin t$ from the origin. Therefore

$$
\boxed{d_b=\frac{n_a}{n_b}d_a,\qquad r\cos(\theta-\theta_b)=\frac{n_a}{n_b}d_a.}
$$

The angle $\theta_b$ describes the [normal vector](../../../../normal-vector.md) of the new line; it need not equal the propagation angle. The existence of a crossing requires $|d_a|<r_b$. A signed convention for $d_a$ gives the same line equation after choosing the orientation of its [normal vector](../../../../normal-vector.md) consistently.
