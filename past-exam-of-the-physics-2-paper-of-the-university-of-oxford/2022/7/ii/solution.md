<h1 id="7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For viewing near the nadir, the required [angular resolution](../../../../angular-resolution.md) is

$$
\delta\theta\simeq\frac{s}{H}=\frac{100}{840000}=1.19048\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$

Using the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) with the first minimum of the [single-slit diffraction](../../../../single-slit-diffraction.md) pattern gives $\delta\theta\simeq\lambda/w$. Thus the minimum collecting width in that model is

$$
\boxed{w_{\min}=\frac{\lambda H}{s}=\frac{12\times10^{-6}\times840000}{100}\,\mathrm m=0.1008\,\mathrm m\simeq10.1\,\mathrm{cm}.}
$$

The numerical coefficient depends on the aperture shape and the chosen resolution criterion. If the mirror has an unobstructed circular aperture, the [Airy disk](../../../../airy-disk.md) version of the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) is $\delta\theta=1.22\lambda/D$, giving $D_{\min}=0.12298\,\mathrm m\simeq12.3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The question does not specify mirror shape; $10.1\,\mathrm{cm}$ follows the slit-width model of part (i).
