<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Fermat's principle](../../../../fermat-principle.md) makes the [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md), or transit time in a stationary medium, stationary under small variations of the [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) with fixed endpoints. It need not be a global minimum. In the result of part (a), the coefficient of $d^2$ is

$$
\frac1{2a}+\frac1{2b}-\alpha(n-1).
$$

For a family of paraxial [light rays](../../../../light-ray.md) from the object to its image, stationarity with respect to $d$ requires this coefficient to vanish. Therefore the [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) equation is

$$
\frac1a+\frac1b=2\alpha(n-1)=\frac1f.
$$

For a distant object, $a\to\infty$, and the image position $b=f$ defines the [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{f=\frac1{2\alpha(n-1)}.}
$$

The axial member $d=0$ is stationary by symmetry for arbitrary $b$; requiring the neighbouring non-axial [light rays](../../../../light-ray.md) to meet the same image is what determines $b$.
