<h1 id="11/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the convex vacuum void, use internal [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) $1$, external [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) $n$, and the original $\alpha>0$ in the immersed [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) formula:

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm void}=\frac{n}{2\alpha(1-n)}=-\frac{n}{2\alpha(n-1)}.}
$$

A concave glass [thin lens](../../../../thin-lens.md) of the same curvature magnitude in vacuum has $\alpha_{\rm concave}=-\alpha$ and therefore

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm concave}=-\frac1{2\alpha(n-1)},\qquad f_{\rm void}=n f_{\rm concave}.}
$$

Both elements diverge, but their physical [focal lengths](../../../../focal-length.md) differ by a factor $n$ for $n>1$. For example, $n=1.5$ makes the void's [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) magnitude $1.5$ times that of the concave glass element. Their reduced [optical powers](../../../../optical-power.md) $n_{\rm outside}/f$ are equal; physical [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) also depends on the surrounding [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md).
