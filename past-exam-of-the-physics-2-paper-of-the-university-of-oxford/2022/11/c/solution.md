<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In surrounding [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) $n'$, the geometrical segments contribute $n'[\sqrt{a^2+d^2}+\sqrt{b^2+d^2}]$ to the [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md), while replacement by the lens adds $(n-n')\ell(d)$. [Fermat's principle](../../../../fermat-principle.md) therefore gives

$$
n'\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right)=2\alpha(n-n').
$$

The physical [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) measured in the surrounding medium is

$$
\boxed{f(n')=\frac{n'}{2\alpha(n-n')}=\frac1{2\alpha(n/n'-1)}.}
$$

For $\alpha>0$ and $n>1$, increasing $n'$ from $1$ towards $n$ makes the positive [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) grow monotonically from its vacuum value to infinity: $df/dn'=n/[2\alpha(n-n')^2]>0$. At $n'=n$, there is no [refraction](../../../../refraction.md) at the boundary, so the element has zero [optical power](../../../../optical-power.md) and no finite [optical focus](../../../../focus-optics.md). If $n'>n$, its [optical power](../../../../optical-power.md) changes sign and the same shape becomes diverging.
