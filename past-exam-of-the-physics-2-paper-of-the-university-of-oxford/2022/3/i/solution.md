<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume the [electrical conductor](../../../../electrical-conductor.md) is nonmagnetic, with [magnetic permeability](../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md) $\mu_0$, and neglect end effects. [Rotational symmetry](../../../../rotational-symmetry.md) and [translational symmetry](../../../../translational-symmetry.md) make the [magnetic flux density](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) azimuthal and constant along a coaxial circle. The [magnetostatic Ampère law](../../../../magnetostatic-ampere-law.md) therefore gives

$$
2\pi rB(r)=\mu_0 I_{\rm enc}(r).
$$

Uniform [current density](../../../../current-density.md) is $j=I/(\pi a^2)$, so $I_{\rm enc}(r)=j\pi r^2=Ir^2/a^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{B(r)=\frac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi a^2}\quad(0\leq r\leq a).}
$$

The direction follows the [right-hand rule](../../../../right-hand-rule.md) around the [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md). For a uniform linear magnetic material, replace $\mu_0$ by its [magnetic permeability](../../../../permeability-electromagnetism.md).
