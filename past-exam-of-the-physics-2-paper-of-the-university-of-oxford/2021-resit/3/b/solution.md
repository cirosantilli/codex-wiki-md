<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Add a uniform [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) satisfying $\mathbf E=-\mathbf v_0\times\mathbf B$. Then the [Lorentz force](../../../../lorentz-force.md) $q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v_0\times\mathbf B)$ vanishes for the desired [velocity](../../../../velocity.md). With the axes used above, $\mathbf E=E\widehat{\mathbf y}$, perpendicular to both $\mathbf v_0$ and $\mathbf B$, with

$$
\boxed{E=Bv_0=B\sqrt{2U(q/m)_0},\qquad (q/m)_0=\frac{E^2}{2UB^2}.}
$$

The crossed fields form a [velocity selector](../../../../velocity-selector.md). All particles must first have traversed the same [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) from rest: that is what converts selection of [speed](../../../../speed.md) into selection of [charge-to-mass ratio](../../../../charge-to-mass-ratio.md). Equal [charge-to-mass ratios](../../../../charge-to-mass-ratio.md) yield equal [speeds](../../../../speed.md) even if the separate masses and charges differ. For other ratios, the initial transverse [force](../../../../force-split.md) is $q(E-vB)$ and is nonzero.
