<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The [real image](../../../../real-image.md) is upside down.** A converging [optical lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) produces negative [transverse magnification](../../../../transverse-magnification.md) on the screen: a point above the [optical axis](../../../../optical-axis.md) sends a central [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) through the [optical lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) centre to a point below the [optical axis](../../../../optical-axis.md). The same happens in the horizontal direction. Viewed directly from behind, the screen therefore shows a picture rotated through $180^\circ$ relative to the scene.
