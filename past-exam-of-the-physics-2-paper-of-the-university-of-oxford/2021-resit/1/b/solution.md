<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) $+Q$ on the inner [electrical conductor](../../../../electrical-conductor.md) and $-Q$ on the outer one. In the gap, [spherical symmetry](../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) makes the [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) radial and constant on a spherical [Gaussian surface](../../../../gaussian-surface.md). [Gauss's law](../../../../gauss-s-law.md) gives

$$
4\pi r^2E(r)=\frac Q{\epsilon_0},\qquad a<r<b.
$$

The inner-to-outer [electric potential difference](../../../../electric-potential-difference.md) is therefore

$$
V=\int_a^b\frac Q{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2}\,dr
=\frac Q{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac1a-\frac1b\right).
$$

Thus the [capacitance](../../../../capacitance.md) of the [spherical capacitor](../../../../spherical-capacitor.md) is

$$
\boxed{C=4\pi\epsilon_0\frac{ab}{b-a}.}
$$

As $b\to\infty$ this gives the [capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere](../../../../capacitance-of-an-isolated-conducting-sphere.md), $4\pi\epsilon_0a$; for $b-a\ll a$ it approaches the [parallel-plate capacitor](../../../../parallel-plate-capacitor.md) result with [area](../../../../surface-area.md) $4\pi a^2$.
