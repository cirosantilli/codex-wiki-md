<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $r_1,r_2$ be the internal [angle of refraction](../../../../angle-of-refraction.md) values measured from the two face normals, and let $i,e$ be the external incidence and emergence [angles](../../../../angle.md). Geometry of an [optical prism](../../../../prism-optics.md) gives $r_1+r_2=A$ and total deviation $\delta=(i-r_1)+(e-r_2)=i+e-A$.

For an isosceles [optical prism](../../../../prism-optics.md) whose internal beam is parallel to its base, reflection symmetry gives $r_1=r_2=A/2$. Applying [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md) at both faces, with air [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) approximated by one, gives $\sin i=n\sin(A/2)=\sin e$. For transmitted forward rays, $i=e$, so $i=(\delta+A)/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{n=\frac{\sin[(\delta+A)/2]}{\sin(A/2)}.}
$$

This is the symmetric [minimum deviation](../../../../minimum-deviation.md) geometry at a single [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md). Transmission requires $n\sin(A/2)\leq1$; otherwise the proposed symmetric path cannot enter and leave through the two faces.
