<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A distant satellite supplies an approximately collimated incident beam. A [concave spherical mirror](../../../../concave-spherical-mirror.md) of [radius of curvature](../../../../radius-of-curvature.md) $R_c=1.5\,\mathrm m$ has paraxial [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) $f_m=R_c/2$. Put the receiving feed at the principal focus, on the [optical axis](../../../../optical-axis.md) in front of the dish:

$$
\boxed{f_m=0.750\,\mathrm m\text{ from the dish vertex}.}
$$

The [radius of curvature](../../../../radius-of-curvature.md) relation is a [paraxial approximation](../../../../paraxial-approximation.md). A practical dish is usually a [parabolic reflector](../../../../parabolic-reflector.md), which focuses an on-axis collimated beam without [spherical aberration](../../../../spherical-aberration.md); a literal spherical dish has [spherical aberration](../../../../spherical-aberration.md), so an exact optimum over its full [aperture](../../../../aperture.md) would need a specified focusing criterion.
