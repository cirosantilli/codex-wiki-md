<h1 id="7/a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $I_s$ be the intensity that either narrow slit would produce alone, and let $y$ measure screen displacement from the perpendicular bisector. The [optical path difference](../../../../../optical-path-difference.md) is approximately $\delta=b\sin\theta\simeq by/D$. Equal coherent field amplitudes then give

$$
I=I_s+I_s+2I_s\cos\left(\frac{2\pi\delta}{\lambda}\right)
=\boxed{4I_s\cos^2\left(\frac{\pi by}{\lambda D}\right)}.
$$

The maxima are at $y_m=m\lambda D/b$; the minima are halfway between them. Here

$$
\boxed{\Delta y=\frac{\lambda D}{b}=0.532\,\mathrm{mm}.}
$$

The expression uses the paraxial approximation and neglects the slowly varying single-slit [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) envelope because the slit widths are unspecified.
