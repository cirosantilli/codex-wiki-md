<h1 id="2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The loop area is $A=\pi(5\times10^{-3})^2\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Integrating [Ohm's law](../../../../ohm-s-law.md) and [Faraday's law](../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) through the whole decay gives

$$
RQ_{\rm passed}=\int\mathcal E\,dt=NA B_0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{B_0=\frac{RQ_{\rm passed}}{NA}=\frac{30(5\times10^{-6})}{50\pi(5\times10^{-3})^2}\simeq3.82\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm T.}
$$

The switching time cancels: a faster linear flux decay produces a larger [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) for a proportionally shorter interval. If the coil's self-[inductance](../../../../inductance.md) is retained, integrating $L\,dI/dt$ gives zero provided the initial and final currents vanish and the stated total charge includes the decay tail.
