<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

[Huygens' principle](../../../huygens-principle.md) treats each point of a [wavefront](../../../wavefront.md) as the source of a secondary wavelet. The later [wavefront](../../../wavefront.md) is the common forward envelope of those wavelets. Consider a plane front reaching a flat interface between media with speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$. In the same interval $\Delta t$, the incident front advances $v_1\Delta t$ while the first transmitted wavelet grows to radius $v_2\Delta t$.

The two right triangles sharing the interface segment of length $d$ give $d\sin i=v_1\Delta t$ and $d\sin r=v_2\Delta t$, where $i$ and $r$ are ray angles to the normal. Hence

$$
\frac{\sin i}{\sin r}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}=\frac{n_2}{n_1},\qquad\boxed{n_1\sin i=n_2\sin r.}
$$

This is [Snell's law](../../../snell-s-law.md). The frequency is unchanged at a stationary interface, while wavelength changes with the propagation speed.
