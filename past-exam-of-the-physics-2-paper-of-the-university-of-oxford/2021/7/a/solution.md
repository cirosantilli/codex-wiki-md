<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a uniformly illuminated single slit of width $b$, summing the [wave amplitudes](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) across the slit gives the [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md) profile

$$
I(\theta)=I(0)\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2,\qquad \beta=\frac{\pi b\sin\theta}{\lambda}.
$$

The first minimum occurs at $\sin\theta=\lambda/b$. The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) places one source's central maximum at the other source's first minimum; hence the slit gives $\Delta\theta\simeq\lambda/b$. A circular [aperture](../../../../aperture.md) instead produces an [Airy disk](../../../../airy-disk.md), whose first minimum is at $1.22\lambda/D$. For the camera's circular [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md),

$$
\boxed{\Delta\theta=\frac{1.22\lambda}{D}=4.88\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}=0.0280^\circ\simeq101\text{ arcseconds}.}
$$

The factor $1.22$ belongs to circular-[aperture](../../../../aperture.md) [diffraction](../../../../diffraction.md), rather than the single-slit profile.
