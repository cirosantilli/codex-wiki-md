<h1 id="9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The screen now lies in the back [focal plane](../../../../focal-plane.md) of the [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md). In paraxial [geometrical optics](../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md), a [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) arriving at the [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) at height $y$ and angle $\theta$ leaves at angle $\theta-y/f$, and reaches the screen at

$$
x=y+f(\theta-y/f)=f\theta.
$$

Thus all [light rays](../../../../light-ray.md) with the same incident direction reach the same screen coordinate, independently of their height at the [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md). The [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md) pattern is an angular pattern mapped to $x=f\theta$:

$$
\boxed{I(x)=4I_{s,f}\cos^2\left(\frac{\pi sx}{\lambda f}\right),\qquad \Delta x=\frac{\lambda f}{s}.}
$$

Here $I_{s,f}$ is the one-slit [light intensity](../../../../light-intensity.md) after focusing. Its absolute value depends on collected light and the [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) [aperture](../../../../aperture.md), which are unspecified; it need not equal the no-[lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) value $I_s$. Propagation from the slits to the [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) introduces a common angular [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md), so it does not alter the relative [wave phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) responsible for these fringes, provided the [aperture](../../../../aperture.md) passes the required light.
