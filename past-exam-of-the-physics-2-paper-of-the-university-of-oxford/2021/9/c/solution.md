<h1 id="9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The slide adds [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md) $\Delta=(n-1)t=0.5(0.1\,\mathrm{mm})=50\,\mu\mathrm m$ to the covered beam. Put the covered slit at transverse coordinate $+s/2$. For positive screen coordinate $x$ its geometrical path is shorter by approximately $sx/d$, so the total covered-minus-uncovered [optical path difference](../../../../optical-path-difference.md) is

$$
\Delta_{\rm tot}=(n-1)t-\frac{sx}{d}.
$$

The zero-order fringe follows equal total [optical path lengths](../../../../optical-path-length.md), $\Delta_{\rm tot}=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{x_0=\frac{d(n-1)t}{s}=0.050\,\mathrm m=5.0\,\mathrm{cm},\quad\text{towards the covered slit}.}
$$

The covered beam needs a shorter geometric path to compensate its extra [optical path length](../../../../optical-path-length.md). This is the paraxial estimate; exact point-to-point distances give $x_0\simeq5.006\,\mathrm{cm}$. “Central” here follows the zero-order fringe continuously as the slide is introduced. At one fixed [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md), the final [monochromatic wave](../../../../monochromatic-wave.md) [interference](../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) pattern is periodic, so intensity alone identifies a fringe position only modulo the fringe spacing. Slide reflections and absorption have been neglected.
