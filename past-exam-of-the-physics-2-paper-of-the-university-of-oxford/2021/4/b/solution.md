<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [condenser lens](../../../../condenser-lens.md) should image the point source onto the small projection [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md). Then every illuminated LCD point directs its central [light ray](../../../../light-ray.md) into the projection [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md), rather than wasting light elsewhere. In the paraxial idealization this gives approximately uniform useful illumination.

Put the LCD at $z=0$, the source at $z=-100\,\mathrm{mm}$, the projection [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) at $z=u=29.5567\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the [condenser lens](../../../../condenser-lens.md) at $z=-h$. The cone from the [condenser lens](../../../../condenser-lens.md)'s rim, of radius $15\,\mathrm{mm}$, to the projection [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) has radius

$$
r_{\rm LCD}=15\frac{u}{u+h}\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

at the LCD. Maximum approximately uniform illumination without unnecessary overfilling makes this equal the half-diagonal $7.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. Thus $h=u$: place the [condenser lens](../../../../condenser-lens.md) $29.56\,\mathrm{mm}$ before the LCD, or $70.44\,\mathrm{mm}$ after the source. Its object and image distances are $100-h$ and $u+h$, so

$$
\boxed{f_c=\frac{(100-h)(u+h)}{100+u}=32.14\,\mathrm{mm}.}
$$

The illuminated disk just contains the rectangular LCD. Exact spatially uniform [irradiance](../../../../irradiance.md) is not guaranteed by a single spherical [lens](../../../../lens-optics.md) and a point source; the result uses paraxial [geometrical optics](../../../../geometrical-optics-split.md), negligible [optical aberration](../../../../optical-aberration.md), and an approximately uniform incident cone.
