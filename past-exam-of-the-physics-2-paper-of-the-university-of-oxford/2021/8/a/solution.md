<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take the grounded plane as $z=0$. The [method of images](../../../../method-of-images.md) replaces it, in $z>0$, by an [image charge](../../../../image-charge.md) $-q$ at $z=-d$ and an [image charge](../../../../image-charge.md) $+q$ at $z=-2d$. These make the [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) zero on the plane; the [uniqueness theorem for the electrostatic potential](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-the-electrostatic-potential.md) establishes the solution in the physical half-space.

At $z=3d/2$, both real [point charges](../../../../point-charge.md) contribute upward [electric fields](../../../../electric-field-split.md), each of magnitude $kq/(d/2)^2$. The nearer [image charge](../../../../image-charge.md) contributes downward and the farther one upward. Hence

$$
\mathbf E=\frac{kq}{d^2}\left(4+4-\frac4{25}+\frac4{49}\right)\widehat{\mathbf z}
=\boxed{\frac{9704}{1225}\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0d^2}\widehat{\mathbf z}.}
$$

This is upward for $q>0$; retaining signed $q$ automatically handles the opposite sign. No self-[electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md) is involved because the evaluation point is distinct from every [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md).
