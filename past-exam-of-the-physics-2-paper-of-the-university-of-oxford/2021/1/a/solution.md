<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $k=1/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$. The separation of the two [point charges](../../../../point-charge.md) is $2l\sin\phi$, so [Coulomb's law](../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) gives the horizontal repulsive [force](../../../../force-split.md) $F=kQ^2/(4l^2\sin^2\phi)$. Resolving the rod's [tension](../../../../tension.md) vertically and horizontally gives $T\cos\phi=mg$ and $T\sin\phi=F$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\sin^2\phi\tan\phi=\frac{Q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0mgl^2}.}
$$

The left side increases from zero to infinity for $0<\phi<\pi/2$, so this determines a unique outward [equilibrium](../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). For a small angle, $\phi^3\simeq Q^2/(16\pi\epsilon_0mgl^2)$. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, direct numerical solution gives

$$
\boxed{\phi=7.71\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}=0.442^\circ.}
$$
