<h1 id="8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The component of [magnetic force](../../../../magnetic-force.md) along the slope has magnitude $IBs\cos\phi$. The rate at which the sliding bar does work against this magnetic braking is

$$
P_{\mathrm{brake}}=IBs v\cos\phi=I\mathcal E.
$$

By [Ohm's law](../../../../ohm-s-law.md), $\mathcal E=IR$, so

$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{brake}}=I^2R=\frac{B^2s^2v^2\cos^2\phi}{R}.}
$$

This exactly equals the [Joule heating](../../../../joule-heating.md) in the [resistor](../../../../resistor.md) in the negligible-[self-inductance](../../../../self-inductance.md) approximation. The [normal force](../../../../normal-force.md) does no work because it is perpendicular to the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md).

The gravitational [power](../../../../power.md) is instead $mgv\sin\phi$. By the [work-energy theorem](../../../../work-energy-theorem.md),

$$
mgv\sin\phi=I^2R+\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac12mv^2\right).
$$

It equals the [Joule heating](../../../../joule-heating.md) only at constant speed. For a concrete counterexample, during the initial acceleration the [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) increases, so some gravitational [power](../../../../power.md) remains mechanical.
