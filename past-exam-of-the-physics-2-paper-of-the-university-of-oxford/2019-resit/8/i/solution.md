<h1 id="8/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the coordinates in [8](../../8.md). At downhill speed $v$, the moving bar has $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B=-Bv\cos\phi\,\hat{\mathbf b}$. The [electromotive force](../../../../electromotive-force.md) and induced [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E=Bs v\cos\phi,\qquad I=\frac{Bs v\cos\phi}{R}.}
$$

Here $I$ is positive along $-\hat{\mathbf b}$ across the sliding bar, then returns through the rails and [resistor](../../../../resistor.md). This direction makes the [magnetic force](../../../../magnetic-force.md) oppose downhill motion, as required by [Lenz's law](../../../../lenz-s-law.md). The factor $\cos\phi$ is essential: only the component of [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) normal to the rail plane threads the circuit. Rail and bar [electrical resistances](../../../../electrical-resistance.md) and circuit [self-inductance](../../../../self-inductance.md) are neglected relative to the stated large $R$ model.
