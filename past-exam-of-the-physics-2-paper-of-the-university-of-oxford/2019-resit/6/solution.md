<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Treat the given orbital separation as the projected separation on the sky. The small [angular separation](../../../angular-separation.md) is

$$
\theta\simeq\frac{1.5\times10^{11}}{2\times10^{17}}=7.5\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$

For an ideal circular [telescope](../../../telescope.md) aperture of diameter $D$, the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) requires $1.22\lambda/D\leq\theta$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{D\geq\frac{1.22(500\times10^{-9})}{7.5\times10^{-7}}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.81\,\mathrm m.}
$$

This is the [diffraction](../../../diffraction.md) limit, assuming comparable image brightness and adequate sampling. Atmospheric image blurring can require correction or space-based observing. A smaller actual projected separation of the [binary star](../../../binary-star.md) would require a larger aperture.
