The following solves the surviving request for a single convex entrance surface; it is conditional on that interpretation and does not reconstruct the missing diagram or supply a rod length.

For [refraction at a spherical surface](../../../refraction-at-a-spherical-surface.md), take incident [light](../../../light.md) travelling rightwards from [refractive index](../../../refractive-index.md) $n_0$ to $n_g$, object distance $u>0$ to the left of the vertex, image distance $v$ positive to the right, and signed curvature $R_s$ positive when the centre is inside the rod. In the [paraxial approximation](../../../paraxial-approximation.md), applying [Snell's law](../../../snell-s-law.md) to a [light ray](../../../light-ray.md) at height $y$ gives

$$
n_0\left(\frac yu+\frac y{R_s}\right)
=n_g\left(\frac y{R_s}-\frac yv\right).
$$

After cancelling $y$, the image position and transverse [magnification](../../../magnification.md) are

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_0}{u}+\frac{n_g}{v}=\frac{n_g-n_0}{R_s},\qquad
v=\frac{n_guR_s}{(n_g-n_0)u-n_0R_s},\qquad
M=-\frac{n_0v}{n_gu}.}
$$

The [magnification](../../../magnification.md) follows by considering a paraxial [light ray](../../../light-ray.md) through the vertex: [Snell's law](../../../snell-s-law.md) there gives $n_0(-y_o/u)=n_g(y_i/v)$.

For a convex entrance, $R_s=+20\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $u=60\,\mathrm{mm}$. In air, the denominator $(n_g-n_0)/R_s-n_0/u$ is $1/75\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}$, giving

$$
\boxed{v_{\mathrm{air}}=120\,\mathrm{mm},\qquad M_{\mathrm{air}}=-1.25.}
$$

This is a real, inverted image within a sufficiently long glass rod, before any subsequent surface.

For water with [refractive index](../../../refractive-index.md) $1.33$, the denominator is $-13/1500\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}$, and

$$
\boxed{v_{\mathrm{water}}=-\frac{2400}{13}\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq-184.6\,\mathrm{mm},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{water}}=\frac{133}{52}\simeq+2.558.}
$$

The emerging [light rays](../../../light-ray.md) diverge as though from an upright virtual image on the object side. The change of sign is consistent with $v$ diverging when $n_0=n_gu/(u+R_s)=1.20$. A reversed curvature or a second exit surface changes these answers; the unavailable diagram prevents a definitive answer for the full illustrated arrangement.
