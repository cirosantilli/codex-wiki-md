<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Place charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ on the inner and outer conductors. In the vacuum gap, [Gauss's law](../../../gauss-s-law.md) gives $E(r)=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$. Their [electric potential difference](../../../electric-potential-difference.md) is

$$
V_a-V_b=\int_a^bE(r)\,dr=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac1a-\frac1b\right).
$$

Consequently the [spherical capacitor](../../../spherical-capacitor.md) has

$$
\boxed{C=\frac{Q}{V_a-V_b}=4\pi\epsilon_0\frac{ab}{b-a}.}
$$

For a narrow gap $b-a\ll a$, this approaches $\epsilon_0(4\pi a^2)/(b-a)$, the local parallel-plate approximation. For $b\to\infty$, it becomes the capacitance $4\pi\epsilon_0a$ of an isolated sphere.
