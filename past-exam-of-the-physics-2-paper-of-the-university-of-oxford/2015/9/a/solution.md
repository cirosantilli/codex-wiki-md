<h1 id="9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [electric dipole moment](../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) is $\mathbf p=2aq\hat{\mathbf z}$. By the [superposition principle](../../../../superposition-principle.md), the exact [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) is

$$
V(\mathbf r)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac1{\sqrt{r^2+a^2-2ar\cos\theta}}-\frac1{\sqrt{r^2+a^2+2ar\cos\theta}}\right].
$$

For $a/r\ll1$, the [binomial expansion](../../../../binomial-expansion.md) gives each reciprocal [distance](../../../../distance.md) as $r^{-1}(1\pm(a/r)\cos\theta+O(a^2/r^2))$. The leading $1/r$ terms cancel because the total [electric charge](../../../../electric-charge-split.md) is zero. The next even terms also cancel between the two signs. Consequently

$$
V(\mathbf r)=\frac{2aq}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\cos\theta}{r^2}+O\left(\frac{qa^3}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^4}\right),\qquad \boxed{\alpha=\frac{2aq}{4\pi\epsilon_0}=\frac{p}{4\pi\epsilon_0}.}
$$

This is the leading [electric dipole](../../../../electric-dipole.md) term of the [multipole expansion](../../../../electric-multipole-expansion.md); it describes the far field, not the [electric potential](../../../../electric-potential.md) arbitrarily close to either [point charge](../../../../point-charge.md).
