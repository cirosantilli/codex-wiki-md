<h1 id="10/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Faraday's law of induction](../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) states that the induced [electromotive force](../../../../electromotive-force.md) around a material [electrical circuit](../../../../electrical-circuit-split.md) equals the negative rate of change of its linked [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E=\oint_{C(t)}(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S.}
$$

The [surface](../../../../topological-surface-split.md) orientation and direction around its boundary are related by the [right-hand rule](../../../../right-hand-rule.md). [Lenz's law](../../../../lenz-s-law.md) supplies the physical meaning of the minus sign: the induced [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) produces a [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) opposing the change in linked [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md). For a stationary contour the local form is $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B$; for this moving wire, the magnetic [Lorentz force](../../../../lorentz-force.md) term is also needed in the [electromotive force](../../../../electromotive-force.md).
