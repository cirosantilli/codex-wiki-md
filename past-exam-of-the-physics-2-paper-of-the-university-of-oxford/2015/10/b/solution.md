<h1 id="10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write the wire diameter as $d_w$ to distinguish it from other geometric labels. Its [cross-sectional area](../../../../cross-sectional-area.md) is $A_w=\pi d_w^2/4$, and the total wire length is $4a$. From the [electrical resistivity](../../../../electrical-resistivity.md), its [electrical resistance](../../../../electrical-resistance.md) is

$$
R=\frac{4a\rho_c}{A_w}=\frac{16\rho_ca}{\pi d_w^2}.
$$

While the loop straddles the boundary, let $h$ be the height of each vertical side remaining in the [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). The linked [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md) has magnitude $Bah$, with $\dot h=-v$. [Faraday's law](../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) therefore gives $|\mathcal E|=Bav$. Neglecting the loop's [self-inductance](../../../../self-inductance.md), [Ohm's law](../../../../ohm-s-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{I=\frac{Bav}{R}=\frac{\pi d_w^2Bv}{16\rho_c}.}
$$

The into-page [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md) is decreasing, so [Lenz's law](../../../../lenz-s-law.md) requires a **clockwise** [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) as viewed in the diagram. If the loop is wholly inside a uniform [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md), or wholly outside it, its [magnetic flux](../../../../magnetic-flux.md) is constant and this induced [electromotive force](../../../../electromotive-force.md) is zero. During a rapid transient one should instead solve $L\dot I+RI=Bav$; at the assumed steady speed the same boxed relation holds.
