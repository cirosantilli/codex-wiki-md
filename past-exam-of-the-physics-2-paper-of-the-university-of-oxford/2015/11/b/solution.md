<h1 id="11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Regard the [refraction angle](../../../../angle-of-refraction.md) as a function of the [angle of incidence](../../../../angle-of-incidence.md), $\beta=\arcsin(\sin\alpha/n)$. [Implicit differentiation](../../../../implicit-differentiation.md) of [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md) gives $\beta'=\cos\alpha/(n\cos\beta)$. Differentiating the [deviation angle](../../../../deviation-angle.md) gives $\delta'=2-4\beta'$. An interior [stationary point](../../../../stationary-point.md) therefore satisfies

$$
\boxed{\cos\alpha=\frac n2\cos\beta,\qquad A=2.}
$$

The [stationary deviation of a primary rainbow ray](../../../../stationary-deviation-of-a-primary-rainbow-ray.md) is a minimum: differentiating $\cos\alpha=n\cos\beta\,\beta'$ again and using [Snell's law](../../../../snell-s-law.md) gives $\beta''=\tan\beta((\beta')^2-1)$. At the [stationary point](../../../../stationary-point.md), $\beta'=1/2$, and hence $\delta''=-4\beta''=3\tan\beta>0$ for a positive interior [refraction angle](../../../../angle-of-refraction.md). This minimum is responsible for the concentration of emerging [light rays](../../../../light-ray.md) near the primary [rainbow](../../../../rainbow.md) direction.
