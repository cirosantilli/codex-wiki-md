<h1 id="11/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

With the Sun behind the observer and raindrops ahead, the one-reflection branch sends many [light rays](../../../../light-ray.md) toward directions close to the minimum [deviation angle](../../../../deviation-angle.md). Nearby values of the [angle of incidence](../../../../angle-of-incidence.md) produce nearly the same outgoing direction there, forming a [wave caustic](../../../../wave-caustic.md). For water the resulting cone is approximately $42^\circ$ from the antisolar direction. The observer sees the intersecting drops as a circular [rainbow](../../../../rainbow.md), or the part of that circle visible above the ground.

[Normal dispersion](../../../../normal-dispersion.md) makes the [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) larger for violet than for red [light](../../../../light.md). At the minimum, increasing the [refractive index](../../../../refractive-index.md) increases the [deviation angle](../../../../deviation-angle.md), and therefore decreases the viewing angle $\pi-\delta_{\min}$. Red is on the outer edge of the primary [rainbow](../../../../rainbow.md), violet on the inner edge. The internal [specular reflection](../../../../specular-reflection.md) is partial: with $n=4/3$, the relevant $\beta\simeq40.2^\circ$ is below the [critical angle](../../../../critical-angle-for-total-internal-reflection.md) $\arcsin(1/n)\simeq48.6^\circ$. [Diffraction](../../../../diffraction.md) and the finite angular size of the Sun smooth the geometrical concentration; the physical [intensity](../../../../intensity-physics.md) does not actually become infinite.
