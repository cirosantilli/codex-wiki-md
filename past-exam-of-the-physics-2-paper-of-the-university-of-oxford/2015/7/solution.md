<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Use [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md), appropriate to observation in the far field or in the focal plane of a [lens](../../../lens-optics.md), and uniform illumination across the slit. At observation angle $\theta$, points separated by transverse coordinate $u$ acquire a [phase difference](../../../phase-difference.md) $ku\sin\theta$, where $k=2\pi/\lambda$ is the [wavenumber](../../../wavenumber.md). The [superposition principle](../../../superposition-principle.md) for the coherent [wave amplitudes](../../../wave-amplitude.md) gives

$$
A(\theta)\propto\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}e^{iku\sin\theta}\,du=w\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta},\qquad \beta=\frac{\pi w\sin\theta}{\lambda}.
$$

The [intensity](../../../intensity-physics.md) is proportional to the squared modulus of the [wave amplitude](../../../wave-amplitude.md), so

$$
\boxed{I(\theta)=I(0)\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2.}
$$

The limit at $\beta=0$ is one. The noncentral zeros satisfy $w\sin\theta=m\lambda$ for nonzero integers $m$ for which the angle is physically allowed. In the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../rayleigh-criterion.md), two mutually incoherent point images are just resolved when the central maximum of one [diffraction pattern](../../../diffraction-pattern.md) lies at the first minimum of the other. For [single-slit diffraction](../../../single-slit-diffraction.md) this gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta\theta_R=\arcsin\left(\frac\lambda w\right)\simeq\frac\lambda w\quad(w\gg\lambda).}
$$

The exact statement here takes one image on the slit normal; for nearby paraxial images the [angular resolution](../../../angular-resolution.md) is $\lambda/w$. The first propagating minimum exists only for $w\geq\lambda$. The numerical factor $1.22$ belongs to a circular aperture and does not apply to this slit.
