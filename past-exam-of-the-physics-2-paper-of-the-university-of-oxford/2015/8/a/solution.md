<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a linear element in sinusoidal steady state, the [complex impedance](../../../../electrical-impedance.md) is the ratio of the [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) [phasor](../../../../phasor.md) to the [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) [phasor](../../../../phasor.md), $Z=\widetilde V/\widetilde I$. Use the convention that the physical signal is the real part of its [phasor](../../../../phasor.md) times $e^{i\omega t}$. Then time [differentiation](../../../../differentiation.md) becomes multiplication by $i\omega$.

For a [resistor](../../../../resistor.md), [Ohm's law](../../../../ohm-s-law.md) gives $\widetilde V=R\widetilde I$. For a [capacitor](../../../../capacitor.md), $I=C\dot V$ gives $\widetilde I=i\omega C\widetilde V$. For an [inductor](../../../../inductor.md), $V=L\dot I$ gives $\widetilde V=i\omega L\widetilde I$. Hence

$$
\boxed{Z_R=R,\qquad Z_C=\frac1{i\omega C}=-\frac{i}{\omega C},\qquad Z_L=i\omega L.}
$$

The imaginary part encodes the relative [phase](../../../../phase-waves.md) of [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) and [voltage](../../../../voltage.md): [electric current](../../../../electric-current.md) leads [voltage](../../../../voltage.md) in an ideal [capacitor](../../../../capacitor.md) and lags it in an ideal [inductor](../../../../inductor.md).
