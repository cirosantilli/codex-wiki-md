<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Take the [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) to vanish at infinity. The [distance](../../../distance.md) from the centre to each [point charge](../../../point-charge.md) is half the [diagonal of a polygon](../../../diagonal-of-a-polygon.md) of the [square](../../../square.md), namely $a/\sqrt2$. The [superposition principle](../../../superposition-principle.md) for the [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) therefore gives, for four charges $+q$,

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{4q}{a/\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2 q}{\pi\epsilon_0a}.}
$$

Strictly, specifying only the _magnitude_ of each [electric charge](../../../electric-charge-split.md) leaves its sign undetermined. For signed charges $q_j$, the general answer is $V=\sqrt2\sum_{j=1}^4q_j/(4\pi\epsilon_0a)$. For example, two positive and two negative charges of equal magnitude give zero [electric potential](../../../electric-potential.md) at the centre. The boxed result uses the usual interpretation that all four charges have the same positive sign.
