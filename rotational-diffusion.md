# Rotational diffusion

↑ **Parent:** [Diffusion](diffusion.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_diffusion)

Rotational diffusion is the random change of a particle's [rigid body orientation](rigid-body-orientation.md) through [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md). A suspended [ellipsoid](ellipsoid.md) can turn while its centre hardly moves; [translational diffusion](translational-diffusion.md) instead spreads its position. Random molecular collisions supply fluctuating [torques](torque.md) as well as [forces](force-split.md). The rotational [diffusion coefficient](diffusion-coefficient.md) $D_r$ measures how quickly angular uncertainty grows, rather than a steady spin speed. Its units are $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, often written $\mathrm{rad^2\,s^{-1}}$ to distinguish angular spreading from translational spreading, whose [diffusion coefficient](diffusion-coefficient.md) $D_t$ has units $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. The [radian](radian.md) is [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md), but writing angular [variance](variance-split.md) in $\mathrm{rad^2}$ remains useful.

First identify what the image can reveal. A marked end of an elongated particle defines an arrow: turning it through $\pi$ reverses that arrow. An unmarked [ellipsoid](ellipsoid.md) with identical ends reveals only its long axis, a [director](director-liquid-crystals.md): angles $\phi$ and $\phi+\pi$ describe the same visible state. An unmarked perfect [sphere](sphere.md) has no visible [rigid body orientation](rigid-body-orientation.md) at all. Tracking its centre cannot measure its [rotational diffusion](rotational-diffusion.md); a surface mark or another identifiable internal direction is needed.

This distinction makes [rotational diffusion](rotational-diffusion.md) useful: it measures how long a particle remembers its pointing direction. For an elongated particle, turning also changes which laboratory direction is easiest for [translational diffusion](translational-diffusion.md). Randomization of [rigid body orientation](rigid-body-orientation.md) can therefore erase directional preferences in the observed displacement, even though the particle's shape has not changed.
