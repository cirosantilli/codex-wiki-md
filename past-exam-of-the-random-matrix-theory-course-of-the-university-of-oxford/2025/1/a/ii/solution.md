<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [joint probability distribution](../../../../../joint-probability-distribution.md) of all entries of $X$ is unchanged by $X\mapsto-X$, so $M$ and $-M$ have the same [probability distribution](../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md). For the specified cyclic product $P=M_{i_1i_2}\cdots M_{i_ki_1}$, this transformation multiplies $P$ by $(-1)^k$. Every such product is an [integrable random variable](../../../../../integrable-random-variable.md), since [normal random variables](../../../../../gaussian-random-variable.md) have finite [moments](../../../../../moment.md) of all orders. Thus, for odd $k$,

$$
\mathbb EP=\mathbb E[(-1)^kP]=-\mathbb EP,
\qquad\boxed{\mathbb E[M_{i_1i_2}\cdots M_{i_ki_1}]=0.}
$$

Repeated indices cause no difficulty: all factors change sign simultaneously. It would be incorrect to assume that these factors are [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).
