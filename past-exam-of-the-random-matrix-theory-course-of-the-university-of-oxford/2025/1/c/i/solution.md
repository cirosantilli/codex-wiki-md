<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

First $W^T=W$, so the [spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices](../../../../../spectral-theorem-for-real-symmetric-matrices.md) gives real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md). Moreover, for every real [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $v$,

$$
v^TWv=\frac1n v^TXX^Tv=\frac1n\lVert X^Tv\rVert^2\geq0.
$$

Thus $W$ is a [positive semidefinite matrix](../../../../../positive-semidefinite-matrix.md). For any nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$,

$$
\lambda\lVert v\rVert^2=v^TWv\geq0,
\qquad\boxed{\lambda\in[0,\infty).}
$$

This [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is a scaled example of the [Wishart distribution](../../../../../wishart-distribution.md).
