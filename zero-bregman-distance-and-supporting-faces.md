# Zero Bregman distance and supporting faces

↑ **Parent:** [Bregman divergence](bregman-divergence.md)

For $p\in\partial J(v)$, zero [Bregman distance](bregman-divergence.md) means that $J(u)$ equals the supporting affine function $J(v)+\langle p,u-v\rangle$. If $J$ is [strictly convex](strictly-convex-function.md), two distinct contact points are impossible: convexity along their segment would be strict, while the [subgradient inequality](subgradient-inequality.md) supplies the opposite bound. Thus zero distance then implies $u=v$. Without strict convexity, $J(s)=|s|$, $v=1$, $p=1$ gives zero distance for every $u\geq0$. These are all on the same supporting face.
