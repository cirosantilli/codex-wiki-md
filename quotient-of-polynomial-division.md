# Quotient of polynomial division

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial division](polynomial-division.md)

In [polynomial division](polynomial-division.md) $q=Qs+r$ over a [field](field.md), the polynomial quotient is the [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $s$. The [polynomial remainder](remainder-of-polynomial-division.md) $r$ has smaller [polynomial degree](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) than the nonzero divisor $Q$ unless it is zero.

In the notation $f=gq+r$, the [polynomial quotient](quotient-of-polynomial-division.md) is instead named $q$. It is a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) determined by the divisor and the [polynomial degree](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) bound. The pointwise ratio of the associated [polynomial functions](polynomial-function.md) satisfies

$$
\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=q(x)+\frac{r(x)}{g(x)}\qquad\text{when }g(x)\ne0.
$$

For the worked example, $f(0)/g(0)=6/(-2)=-3$, while $q(0)=1$; the correction is $r(0)/g(0)=8/(-2)=-4$. Thus a [polynomial quotient](quotient-of-polynomial-division.md) need not equal the [rational function](rational-function.md) $f/g$. At $x=1$ and $x=-2$, the pointwise ratio is undefined, but the [polynomial quotient](quotient-of-polynomial-division.md) still has perfectly well-defined values. When $r=0$, the divisor is a [polynomial factor](polynomial-factor.md) of $f$ and the ratio agrees with $q$ wherever $g(x)\ne0$.
