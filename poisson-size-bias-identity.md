# Poisson size-bias identity

↑ **Parent:** [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md)

For $N$ with [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md) of mean $\lambda$, the equality $n\mathbb P(N=n)=\lambda\mathbb P(N=n-1)$ proves the identity by summation. For an independent [random sum of independent claims](random-sum-of-independent-claims.md) $S$ with this count, exchangeability of the summands gives $\mathbb E[S h(S)]=\lambda\mathbb E[X h(S+X)]$, where $X$ on the right is independent of $S$ and has the severity law. These identities hold for nonnegative measurable functions, and for integrable signed functions. They obtain [moments](moment.md) without assuming that a positive [exponential moment](exponential-moment.md) exists.
