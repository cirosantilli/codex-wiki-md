# Integer

↑ **Parent:** [Number theory](number-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer)

An integer is a whole number, positive, negative, or zero. The [set](set-split.md) of all integers is

$$
\mathbb Z=\{\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots\}.
$$

The [positive integers](positive-integer.md) are $1,2,3,\ldots$; the negative [integers](integer-split.md) are their opposites $-1,-2,-3,\ldots$. Zero is neither positive nor negative. The [nonnegative integers](natural-number.md) include zero and all the [positive integers](positive-integer.md). On a [number line](number-line.md), consecutive [integers](integer-split.md) are one unit apart, negative [integers](integer-split.md) lie to the left of zero, and [positive integers](positive-integer.md) lie to its right. [Prealgebra 2e, §3.1: Locate Positive and Negative Numbers on the Number Line; Opposite Notation; Integers](prealgebra-2e-by-lynn-marecek-and-others/3-1-introduction-to-integers.md)

The [integers](integer-split.md) let counting describe changes in both directions. If a counter starts at $-3$ and increases by five, it reaches $2$: $-3+5=2$. Decreasing that result by four gives $2+(-4)=-2$. In [addition](addition.md) on the [number line](number-line.md), the sign of the added [integer](integer-split.md) chooses the direction and its [absolute value](absolute-value.md) gives the distance. The [additive inverse](additive-inverse.md) $-a$ reverses the change made by $a$.

<a id="image-signed-integers-and-addition-on-a-number-line"></a>
![](integer-signed-number-line-addition.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-signed-integers-and-addition-on-a-number-line). Signed integers and addition on a number line**. Starting at $-3$, a change of $+5$ ends at $2$. A subsequent change of $-4$ ends at $-2$. The arrows indicate changes; the labelled points indicate [integers](integer-split.md). Zero separates the negative and positive sides.

The [integers](integer-split.md) have [closure under a binary operation](closure-under-a-binary-operation.md) for [addition](addition.md), [subtraction](subtraction.md) and [multiplication](multiplication.md): whenever $a,b\in\mathbb Z$, the results $a+b$, $a-b$ and $ab$ are still [integers](integer-split.md). Together these operations give the [integers](integer-split.md) their [ring](ring.md) structure.[§7.1, p. 166, Definition 7.1; p. 167, Example 7.1: the integers with their usual addition and multiplication](a-computational-introduction-to-number-theory-and-algebra-by-victor-shoup.md) For instance, $-3+5=2$, $-3-5=-8$, and $(-3)\cdot5=-15$. General [division](division-mathematics.md) has no such closure: $1/2$ is a [rational number](rational-number.md) but not an [integer](integer-split.md). Some divisions are exact, such as $(-6)/3=-2$; [division](division-mathematics.md) by zero is undefined. [Euclidean division](euclidean-division.md) instead produces an integer [quotient](quotient.md) and a [remainder](remainder.md), so $7=3\cdot2+1$ retains the information that ordinary [division](division-mathematics.md) puts into the [fraction](fraction.md) $7/3$.

An [integer](integer-split.md) is the value, whereas its written [numeral](numeral.md) is an encoding. The [decimal representation](decimal-representation.md) $205_{10}$ and the [binary representation](binary-number.md) $11001101_2$ name the same [integer](integer-split.md). Changing [positional notation](positional-notation.md) changes how that value is displayed and how calculations are organized; it does not change the value itself.
