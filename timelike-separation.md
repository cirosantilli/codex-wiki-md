# Timelike separation

↑ **Parent:** [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md)

Two distinct [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) have [timelike separation](timelike-separation.md) when their displacement has negative [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md) in [metric signature](metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$, equivalently $c|\Delta t|>|\Delta\mathbf x|$. A massive observer can connect them along a straight [timelike curve](timelike-curve.md). If the later [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) has $\Delta t>0$, every [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) in the [orthochronous Lorentz group](orthochronous-lorentz-group.md) keeps it later. The elapsed [proper time](proper-time.md) on the straight inertial segment is $\sqrt{-s^2}/c$.

In the [light-second](light-second.md) units of the [Minkowski spacetime](minkowski-spacetime.md) example, $O$ to $A$ has $s^2=(-4^2+3^2)\,\mathrm{ls}^2=-7\,\mathrm{ls}^2$. Its straight moving clock therefore records $\sqrt7\,\mathrm s$, approximately $2.646\,\mathrm s$, while the coordinate time difference is $4\,\mathrm s$. Coordinate time and [proper time](proper-time.md) answer different questions.
