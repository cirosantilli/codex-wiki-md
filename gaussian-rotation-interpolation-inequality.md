# Gaussian rotation interpolation inequality

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian concentration inequality](gaussian-concentration-inequality.md)

For [independent and identically distributed random variables](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) $X,Y$ with a centered [multivariate normal distribution](multivariate-normal-distribution.md), a [continuously differentiable function](continuously-differentiable-function.md) $f$ with bounded [gradient](gradient.md), and a [convex function](convex-function.md) $\Psi$ for which the expressions are integrable, the displayed inequality holds. First apply [Jensen inequality](jensen-s-inequality.md) conditionally to $f(X)-f(Y)$. For the [Gaussian rotation of independent copies](gaussian-rotation-of-independent-copies.md), the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) gives $f(X)-f(Y)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\langle\nabla f(U_\theta),V_\theta\rangle\,d\theta$. A second application of [Jensen inequality](jensen-s-inequality.md) to the uniform measure on $[0,\pi/2]$, followed by the [Gaussian rotation of independent copies](gaussian-rotation-of-independent-copies.md), proves the inequality. The useful choice $\Psi(v)=e^{\lambda|v|}$ yields a [Gaussian concentration inequality](gaussian-concentration-inequality.md) through an [exponential moment of an absolute standard normal variable](exponential-moment-of-an-absolute-standard-normal-variable.md).
