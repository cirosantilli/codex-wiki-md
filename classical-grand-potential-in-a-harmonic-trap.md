# Classical grand potential in a harmonic trap

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md)

For noninteracting particles in a three-dimensional isotropic [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md), measure the one-particle ground energy as zero. The single-particle [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md) is $Z_1=(1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega})^{-3}$, from the product of three [geometric series](geometric-series.md). In the dilute [Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics](maxwell-boltzmann-statistics.md) regime, $\ln\Xi=e^{\beta\mu}Z_1$, so the [grand potential](grand-potential.md) is the displayed expression and $N=e^{\beta\mu}Z_1$. For $k_BT\gg\hbar\omega$,

$$
\Phi\simeq-\frac{(k_BT)^4}{(\hbar\omega)^3}e^{\beta\mu},\qquad\mu\simeq k_BT\ln\left[N\left(\frac{\hbar\omega}{k_BT}\right)^3\right].
$$

The same approximation follows from integrating the [density of states](density-of-states.md) $g(E)\simeq E^2/[2(\hbar\omega)^3]$ against $e^{-\beta E}$. Extrapolating the classical formula until the fugacity is one gives the scale of [Bose condensation in a three-dimensional harmonic trap](bose-condensation-in-a-three-dimensional-harmonic-trap.md), but the quantitative transition temperature requires the Bose occupation law rather than Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics.
