# Markov process

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process)

A Markov process is a [stochastic process](stochastic-process-split.md) whose present state contains all the information in its observed past needed to predict its future. Precisely, if $\mathcal F_s$ is the [natural filtration](natural-filtration.md) generated by observations up to time $s$, then, for $t>s$ and any [measurable set](measurable-set.md) $A$ of states,

$$
\mathbb P(X_t\in A\mid\mathcal F_s)=\mathbb P(X_t\in A\mid X_s).
$$

The equality is between [conditional probabilities](conditional-probability.md) and holds [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md). It expresses the [Markov property](markov-property.md): the past and future have [conditional independence](conditional-independence.md) given the present. It does not assert [independence](independent-random-variables.md) of consecutive states. In the sunny/rainy example below, tomorrow's sunny [probability](probability.md) is $0.8$ after sunshine but $0.4$ after rain, so knowing today's state matters.

The memory assumption concerns the chosen [state space](state-space.md). If an equipment model's failure [probability](probability.md) depends on its age, recording only “working” or “failed” may lose relevant information; recording age as part of the state can retain it. Similarly, a dependence on two previous observations can sometimes be represented by using their ordered pair as the current state. Whether such a description fits observations is a modeling question.

Transition [Markov kernels](markov-kernel.md) describe the [conditional distribution](conditional-distribution.md) of a state at a later time. Successive kernels and the initial [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) determine every [finite-dimensional distribution](finite-dimensional-distribution.md), through repeated [conditional probability](conditional-probability.md). The [time-homogeneous Markov property](time-homogeneous-markov-property.md) is an additional assumption: the kernel depends on elapsed time rather than calendar time. The attraction is compression: prediction can proceed from a current state instead of an entire recorded history. [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) and [continuous-time Markov chains](continuous-time-markov-chain-split.md) illustrate that a [Markov process](markov-process-split.md) need not evolve in discrete time.
