# Threefold phase-locked equilibria

↑ **Parent:** [Three-to-one spatially forced amplitude equation](three-to-one-spatially-forced-amplitude-equation.md)

The canonical [three-to-one spatially forced amplitude equation](three-to-one-spatially-forced-amplitude-equation.md) has nonzero [equilibrium points](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) $C=Re^{i\theta}$ satisfying $\cos3\theta=(R^2-\mu)/R$ and $\sin3\theta=-\omega/R$. Thus $(R^2-\mu)^2+\omega^2=R^2$. Each positive amplitude has three phases separated by $2\pi/3$: two amplitude branches normally mean six complex [equilibrium points](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). For $\mu>\omega^2-1/4$ the larger branch is asymptotically stable and the smaller consists of [saddle equilibria](saddle-equilibrium.md), except that its zero-amplitude root at $(\mu,\omega)=(0,0)$ is not a nonzero [equilibrium point](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md). Equality gives a [saddle-node bifurcation](saddle-node-bifurcation.md). This [phase locking](phase-locking.md) breaks continuous translation symmetry down to threefold symmetry.
