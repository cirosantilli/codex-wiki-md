# Log-log plot

↑ **Parent:** [Logarithmic scale](logarithmic-scale.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log–log_plot)

A [log-log plot](log-log-plot.md) uses [logarithmic scales](logarithmic-scale.md) on both axes. For a positive [power law](power-law.md) $y=Cx^p$ with $x,C>0$, positive references $x_0,y_0$ give

$$
\log_b(y/y_0)=\log_b(Cx_0^p/y_0)+p\log_b(x/x_0).
$$

Its transformed [slope](slope.md) is the exponent $p$ when both axes use the same [logarithm](logarithm.md) base and the [slope](slope.md) is measured in logarithmic coordinate units. For example, $y=2x^2$ takes the values $2,8,32$ at $x=1,2,4$. Every doubling of $x$ quadruples $y$, giving [slope](slope.md) two on a [log-log plot](log-log-plot.md).

This differs from a [semi-log plot](semi-log-plot.md): a [power law](power-law.md) has constant proportional response to a proportional change of input, whereas [exponential growth](exponential-growth.md) has constant proportional response to an additive change of input. Both can appear straight with appropriate axes, so one must read the scale labels before interpreting a [slope](slope.md).
