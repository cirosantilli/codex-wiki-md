# Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear force](nuclear-force-split.md)

In 1935, [Hideki Yukawa](hideki-yukawa.md) proposed a new massive exchange quantum to explain [nucleon](nucleon.md) binding. His paper began with the inadequacy of an [electron](electron.md)–[neutrino](neutrino.md) exchange description of the binding strength. It then required a [potential energy](potential-energy.md) falling faster with distance than a [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md), introduced an exponentially decaying field, and related its inverse range to the exchange particle's [rest mass](invariant-mass.md). This was a theoretical proposal, not a detection with an apparatus of his own.[On the Interaction of Elementary Particles. I, opening motivation, equations (1)–(3) and (12); text inspected in a retypeset mirror](on-the-interaction-of-elementary-particles-i-by-hideki-yukawa.md)

The crucial idea is that a finite [rest mass](invariant-mass.md) supplies a length scale even in empty space. In a scalar model, the static limit of a [Klein-Gordon equation](klein-gordon-equation.md) produces the [modified Helmholtz equation](modified-helmholtz-equation.md). Its decaying [Green's function](green-s-function-split.md) gives the [Yukawa potential](yukawa-potential.md). The derivation below isolates this range mechanism; [one-pion exchange](one-pion-exchange.md) also carries [spin](spin.md) and [isospin](isospin.md) structure.

The [nuclear emulsion track experiment](nuclear-emulsion-track-experiment.md) used developed [photographic emulsions](photographic-emulsion.md) exposed to [cosmic rays](cosmic-ray.md) and inspected microscopically. Stopping [pions](pion.md) gave daughter [muon](muon.md) tracks with closely grouped ranges. Comparing penetrating [muons](muon.md) with [pion](pion.md)-induced nuclear breakup distinguished their interactions; this was not a direct static [force](force-split.md) measurement. The [muon](muon.md) is a [lepton](lepton.md), not Yukawa's strongly interacting [meson](meson.md).[The Cosmic Radiation, printed pp. 144–145 and 151–152, especially Fig. 5; inspected in the Columbia-hosted Nobel volume](the-cosmic-radiation-by-cecil-frank-powell.md)
