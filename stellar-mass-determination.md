# Stellar mass determination

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar astrophysics](stellar-astrophysics-split.md)

Orbital dynamics gives the most direct stellar masses: a resolved binary orbit and distance determine the relative semimajor axis, and [Kepler's third law](kepler-s-third-law.md) gives the total mass. The barycentric size or radial-velocity amplitude ratio gives individual masses. An [eclipsing binary](eclipsing-binary.md) inclination removes the sine-cubed projection factor from spectroscopic masses; a single-lined [binary mass function](binary-mass-function.md) alone does not uniquely determine both masses. Surface-gravity/radius and stellar-track estimates are model-dependent and are calibrated against dynamical binary measurements.
