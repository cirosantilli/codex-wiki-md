# Gel band

↑ **Parent:** [Gel electrophoresis](gel-electrophoresis.md)

A [gel band](gel-band.md) is a localized population of stained or otherwise detected [molecules](molecule-split.md) with sufficiently similar migration in one sample lane. One visible [gel band](gel-band.md) need not contain only one [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md). Two different linear [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) fragments of 375 [base pairs](base-pair.md) can occupy the same unresolved [gel band](gel-band.md); equal migration supplies size evidence under suitable conditions, not a sequence determination.

[Fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) from a bound stain can support an estimate of [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) quantity, but suitable staining and detector response matter.[Step 4, Sample visualization, Fluorescent dyes: relation between fluorescence and bound nucleic acid](five-main-nucleic-acid-gel-electrophoresis-steps-by-thermo-fisher-scientific.md)

For a quantitative model with equal staining response per unit [mass](mass.md), background-subtracted, integrated [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) $I$ satisfies $I=km$. This requires an unsaturated detector and a common proportionality constant $k$; visual peak brightness alone does not supply $I$.

The [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) is $n=m/M$, where $M$ is [molar mass](molar-mass.md). For double-stranded [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) with approximately equal average [mass](mass.md) per [base pair](base-pair.md), $M$ is proportional to length $L$, so $n$ is proportional to $I/L$. In a constructed example, equal integrated signals from 250-[base pair](base-pair.md) and 1000-[base pair](base-pair.md) [gel bands](gel-band.md) imply approximately equal [masses](mass.md) but four times as many short [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md). Conversely, equal numbers of those [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md) would give about four times the signal for the longer fragments in this model. Neither conclusion follows from darkness in a schematic drawing.
