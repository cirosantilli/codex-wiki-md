# Inositol trisphosphate

↑ **Parent:** [Second messenger](second-messenger.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inositol_trisphosphate)

In [signal transduction](signal-transduction-split.md), [inositol trisphosphate](inositol-trisphosphate.md) usually means inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate: a soluble intracellular [second messenger](second-messenger.md). An extracellular [ligand](ligand-biochemistry.md), its [cell-surface receptor](cell-surface-receptor.md), [IP3](inositol-trisphosphate.md) and the released [calcium](calcium.md) [ions](ion.md) are distinct components. [IP3](inositol-trisphosphate.md) binds an [IP3 receptor](inositol-trisphosphate-receptor.md) on the [endoplasmic reticulum](endoplasmic-reticulum.md), promoting stored $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ release into the [cytosol](cytosol.md).[Phospholipids and Ca2+, Figure 13.27: soluble IP3 and intracellular calcium release](the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/pathways-of-intracellular-signal-transduction.md)

The useful separation is spatial: an extracellular message can control an internal store without entering it. The existing intracellular-injection experiment under [metabotropic receptor](metabotropic-receptor.md) tests bypassing reception; the [Pancreatic IP3 store fractionation experiment](pancreatic-ip3-store-fractionation-experiment.md) below tests which internal compartment supplies the released [calcium](calcium.md). Neither question is answered merely by observing a downstream membrane [electric current](electric-current.md).
