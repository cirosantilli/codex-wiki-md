# Induced current in a tilted resistive loop

↑ **Parent:** [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md)

Choose the positive loop normal and its positive circulation by the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md). A spatially uniform external [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) of fixed direction and signed magnitude $B_0\cos\omega t$, at angle $\theta$ to that normal, gives [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) $\Phi=\pi r^2B_0\cos\theta\cos\omega t$. Neglecting the loop's [self-inductance](self-inductance.md), [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) and [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) give the displayed [electric current](electric-current.md). At a quarter-cycle the external field is zero but its normal component is decreasing most rapidly, so the current produces a field in the direction of the initial normal flux, as required by [Lenz's law](lenz-s-law.md). With appreciable self-inductance the circuit instead obeys $L\dot I+RI=-\dot\Phi_{\rm ext}$.
