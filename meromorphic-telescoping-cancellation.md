# Meromorphic telescoping cancellation

↑ **Parent:** [Telescoping sum](telescoping-sum.md)

For a [meromorphic function](meromorphic-function.md) $f$, the displayed [telescoping sum](telescoping-sum.md) identity holds wherever every summand is defined. If the two endpoint terms are finite at $w=w_0$, their difference gives a [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) continuation of the entire sum to $w_0$, even when individual summands have [poles](pole.md) there. This continued value is a common-shift [limit](limit-of-a-function.md); it does not make an originally undefined sum of [complex numbers](complex-number.md) well defined without an additional convention.

The proof is finite cancellation on the common domain of definition, followed by [analytic continuation](analytic-continuation.md). For $f(w)=\tanh w$, a common shift away from the imaginary axis avoids its [poles](pole.md). Different independently chosen shifts of individual summands need not give the same [limit](limit-of-a-function.md), so specifying the common shift matters.
