# Common-pressure melting-point slope

↑ **Parent:** [Clausius-Clapeyron relation](clausius-clapeyron-relation.md)

When the solid and liquid have the same [pressure](pressure.md), equality of [chemical potentials](chemical-potential.md) implies $-(s_l-s_s)dT+(1/\rho_l-1/\rho_s)dp=0$. With [latent heat of fusion](latent-heat-of-fusion.md) $L=T_m(s_l-s_s)$, this gives the displayed slope. If solid is less dense than liquid, raising the common pressure depresses the [melting](melting.md) temperature. $T_m$ is absolute [temperature](temperature.md). This coexistence-curve derivative differs from a thermomolecular pressure difference between equal-density phases.
