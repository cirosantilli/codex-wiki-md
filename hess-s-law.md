<h1 id="hess-s-law">Hess's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Thermochemistry](thermochemistry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hess's_law)

[Hess's law](hess-s-law.md) states that compatible [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) [enthalpies](enthalpy-split.md) add when their equations add. Reversing a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) reverses its [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) change; multiplying the entire [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) equation by a factor multiplies that change by the same factor. The reason is that [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) is a [state function](state-function.md): intermediate states cancel from the differences. A cycle supplies one independent balance relation; it cannot determine two unknown inputs without further information.

The law is named after [Germain Henri Hess](germain-henri-hess.md).

For specified initial and final [thermodynamic states](thermodynamic-state.md), the change is $\Delta H=H_{\rm final}-H_{\rm initial}$. Because [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) is an [extensive variable](extensive-quantity.md), a quoted reaction $\Delta H$ belongs to the [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) specified for each species in its [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md). Reversing the equation changes the sign; scaling every coefficient scales $\Delta H$; adding compatible equations adds their $\Delta H$ values. A per-[mole](mole-unit.md) value must say which [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) and normalization it uses. Match [temperature](temperature-split.md), [pressure](pressure-split.md), [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md), [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) compositions and reference states before cancellation; the same formula in a [gas](gas.md) and a [liquid](liquid.md) represents different states.[Chemistry 2e, §5.3: enthalpy conventions before Example 5.8; Hess’s Law, equation scaling and reversal](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/5-3-enthalpy.md)

The value of [Hess's law](hess-s-law.md) is that an inaccessible reaction [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) can be obtained from measurable transformations. The algebra does not require those transformations to be steps of the actual [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). Neither an [activation energy](activation-energy.md) nor a [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) follows from the endpoint difference: a [catalyst](catalyst.md) may change the [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) while preserving the compatible initial and final [enthalpies](enthalpy-split.md).
