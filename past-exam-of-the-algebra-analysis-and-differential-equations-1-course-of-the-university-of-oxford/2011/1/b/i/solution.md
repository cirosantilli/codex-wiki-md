<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\varepsilon_i$ denote the coordinate [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) dual to the $i$th vector of the [standard basis](../../../../../standard-basis.md). Writing a vector in the supplied [basis](../../../../../basis.md) gives

$$
x=c_1v_1+c_2v_2+c_3v_3+c_4v_4
=(c_1+c_2+c_3+c_4,c_2+c_3+c_4,c_3+c_4,c_4)^T.
$$

Successive differences recover the coefficients. Hence the [dual basis](../../../../../dual-basis.md) is

$$
\boxed{v_1'=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2,\quad
v_2'=\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3,\quad
v_3'=\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_4,\quad v_4'=\varepsilon_4.}
$$

These formulas directly give $v_i'(v_j)=\delta_{ij}$ and exemplify the [dual basis of cumulative coordinate sums](../../../../../dual-basis-of-cumulative-coordinate-sums.md).
