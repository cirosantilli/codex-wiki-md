<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Away from zero, singularities occur exactly when $e^{1/z}=-1$, namely

$$
\boxed{z_k=\frac1{(2k+1)\pi i},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

For $D(z)=z(e^{1/z}+1)$, differentiation at $z_k$ gives $D'(z_k)=1/z_k\ne0$. Thus every $z_k$ is a simple [pole](../../../../pole.md), with [residue](../../../../residue.md) $z_k$.

As $|k|\to\infty$, these [poles](../../../../pole.md) accumulate at zero. Therefore **zero is a [non-isolated singularity](../../../../non-isolated-singularity.md)**. Every punctured disc around it contains poles, so the function is not holomorphic on any punctured disc there. In particular zero cannot be classified as an isolated [essential singularity](../../../../essential-singularity.md); the isolated-singularity classification does not apply. The displayed poles and their [accumulation point](../../../../limit-point.md) zero exhaust the finite singularities.
