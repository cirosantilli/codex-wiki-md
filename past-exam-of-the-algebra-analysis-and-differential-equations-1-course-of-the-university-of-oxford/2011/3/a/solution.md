<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) for two comaximal two-sided [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) $I,J$ of a ring states that the natural [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) induces

$$
\boxed{R/(I\cap J)\cong R/I\times R/J\qquad(I+J=R).}
$$

Indeed, the map $r\mapsto(r+I,r+J)$ has [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) $I\cap J$. Choose $i\in I$, $j\in J$ with $i+j=1$. Given target representatives $a,b\in R$, the element $aj+bi$ is congruent to $a$ modulo $I$ and to $b$ modulo $J$, proving [surjectivity](../../../../surjective-function.md). The [first isomorphism theorem for rings](../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem-for-rings.md) gives the result. For a commutative ring, $I\cap J=IJ$: if $x\in I\cap J$, then $x=xi+xj\in IJ$, while the reverse inclusion follows from the ideal properties.
