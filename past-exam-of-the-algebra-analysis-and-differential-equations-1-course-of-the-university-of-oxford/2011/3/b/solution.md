<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $R=\mathbb Z$, $I=a\mathbb Z$ and $J=b\mathbb Z$. Since $a,b$ are [coprime integers](../../../../coprime-integers.md), [Bézout's identity](../../../../bezout-s-identity.md) gives $ra+sb=1$, so $I+J=\mathbb Z$. Their intersection is $ab\mathbb Z$: it consists of integers divisible by both $a$ and $b$, and coprimality makes this equivalent to divisibility by $ab$. Thus the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z_{ab}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_a\times\mathbb Z_b,\qquad
[n]_{ab}\longmapsto([n]_a,[n]_b)}
$$

as a [ring isomorphism](../../../../ring-isomorphism.md). This includes $a=1$ or $b=1$, where the corresponding factor is the zero ring.
