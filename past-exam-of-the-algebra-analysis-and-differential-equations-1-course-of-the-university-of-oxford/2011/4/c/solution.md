<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The union is bounded but is not a [closed set](../../../../closed-set.md): the points $-1+1/n$, $n\geq2$, belong to the open disc and tend to $-1$, which lies in neither disc. Thus it is **not compact** by the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md).

Nevertheless it is **connected**, indeed [path-connected](../../../../path-connected-space.md). Every point of the open disc joins $0$ by a straight segment. The segment from $0$ to $1$ stays in the union, since its points before the endpoint belong to the open disc and the endpoint $1$ belongs to the closed disc. Every point of that closed disc joins $1$ inside it by [convexity](../../../../convex-function.md). Concatenating these paths joins any two points of the union. The fact that the two discs are disjoint does not imply disconnection: their contact point belongs to one of them. This illustrates [connectedness at a one-sided contact point](../../../../connectedness-at-a-one-sided-contact-point.md).
