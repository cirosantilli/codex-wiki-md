↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Yes.** The matrix is a [cyclic shift operator](../../../../cyclic-shift-operator.md) on three coordinates. Since $e_1,Ae_1,A^2e_1$ are the three standard [basis vectors](../../../../basis-vector.md), no [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree below three annihilates $A$. Also $A^3=I$, so its [minimal polynomial](../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is $X^3-1$. Over the [complex numbers](../../../../complex-number.md) this has the distinct roots $1,\omega,\omega^2$, where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. The [minimal polynomial criterion for diagonalizability](../../../../diagonalizability-criterion-using-the-minimal-polynomial.md) therefore proves [diagonalizability](../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md).

Explicitly, $v_\lambda=(1,\lambda^2,\lambda)^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies $Av_\lambda=\lambda v_\lambda$ when $\lambda^3=1$. These three [eigenvectors](../../../../eigenvector.md) form an [eigenbasis](../../../../eigenbasis.md). This is a special case of [diagonalization of a cyclic shift over a field](../../../../diagonalization-of-a-cyclic-shift-over-a-field.md).
