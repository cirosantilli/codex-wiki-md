<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

A [holomorphic function](../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) with $f'(z)\ne0$ at every point is locally a [conformal map](../../../conformal-map.md). Write $a=z_0$. Since $|a|<1$ and $|z|<1$, $1-\overline az\ne0$, and

$$
\boxed{f'(z)=\frac{|a|^2-1}{(1-\overline az)^2}\ne0.}
$$

Thus the specified [Möbius transformation](../../../mobius-transformation.md) is conformal throughout the disk. Expanding the two squared absolute values gives

$$
\boxed{1-|f(z)|^2=\frac{(1-|a|^2)(1-|z|^2)}{|1-\overline az|^2}>0,}
$$

so $f$ maps the [open unit disk](../../../unit-disc.md) into itself. Solving $w=(a-z)/(1-\overline az)$ gives $z=(a-w)/(1-\overline aw)=f(w)$. Equivalently $f(f(z))=z$. Hence $f$ is its own inverse, proving both [injectivity](../../../injective-function.md) and [surjectivity](../../../surjective-function.md) on the disk. Nonvanishing derivative establishes the local angle-preserving property; the explicit inverse supplies the separate global bijection claim.
