<h1 id="algebra/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) $T^*$ is characterized by

$$
\boxed{\langle Tv,w\rangle=\langle v,T^*w\rangle\quad\text{for all }v,w\in V.}
$$

This identity works with either convention for which argument of the complex [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) is linear, provided the convention is used consistently.

For uniqueness, subtract the two given identities to get $\langle v,(S-T^*)w\rangle=0$ for every $v,w$. Fix $w$ and choose $v=(S-T^*)w$. Then [positive definiteness](../../../../../positive-definiteness.md) gives

$$
\|(S-T^*)w\|^2=0,
$$

so $(S-T^*)w=0$. As $w$ was arbitrary, $\boxed{S=T^*}$. The decisive fact is that a vector orthogonal to every vector, including itself, must be zero.
