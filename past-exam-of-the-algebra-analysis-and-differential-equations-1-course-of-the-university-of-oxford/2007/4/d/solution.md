<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Since $A$ is nonempty, choose $a_0\in A$ and put $n_0=f(a_0)$. The [fiber](../../../../fiber-of-a-function.md) $U=f^{-1}(\{n_0\})$ is nonempty and open by part (c). Its complement is the union of the other [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) [fibers](../../../../fiber-of-a-function.md) and is therefore also open. If the image contained a second value, that complement would be nonempty, and these two disjoint [open subsets](../../../../open-set.md) would separate $A$. [connectedness](../../../../connected-space.md) excludes this. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(A)=\{n_0\}.}
$$

Equivalently a [continuous function](../../../../continuous-function.md) from a [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) space to the discrete space of [integers](../../../../integer-split.md) is constant. The proof uses relative openness and does not require the original subset $A$ to be open in the plane.
