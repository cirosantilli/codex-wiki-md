The set $A$ is [disconnected space](../../../../disconnected-space.md) if it has a [separation of a topological space](../../../../separation-of-a-topological-space.md): two nonempty disjoint subsets $U,V$ open in $A$ with $A=U\cup V$. Each is then also closed in $A$, being the other's complement.

The set $A$ is [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) if no such [separation of a topological space](../../../../separation-of-a-topological-space.md) exists. Equivalently its only subsets that are both open and closed are $\varnothing$ and $A$. A [separation of a topological space](../../../../separation-of-a-topological-space.md) uses openness in $A$ rather than necessarily in the entire [complex plane](../../../../complex-plane.md).
