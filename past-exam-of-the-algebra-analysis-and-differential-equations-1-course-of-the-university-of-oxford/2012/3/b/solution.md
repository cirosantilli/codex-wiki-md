<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If the [quotient ring](../../../../quotient-ring.md) $R/I$ is an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md), then $I\ne R$, because an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) has $1\ne0$. For $ab\in I$,

$$
(a+I)(b+I)=0+I.
$$

There are no nonzero [zero divisors](../../../../zero-divisor.md) in an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md), so $a+I=0+I$ or $b+I=0+I$. Thus $a\in I$ or $b\in I$, proving **$I$ is a prime ideal**.

If $R/I$ is a [field](../../../../field.md), again $I$ is proper. Let $J$ be an [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) properly containing $I$, and choose $a\in J\setminus I$. The nonzero element $a+I$ has a [multiplicative inverse](../../../../multiplicative-inverse.md) $b+I$, so $ab-1\in I\subset J$. Since $ab\in J$, subtraction gives $1\in J$, hence $J=R$. Therefore **$I$ is a maximal ideal**. These arguments establish the relevant [prime ideal quotient criterion](../../../../prime-ideal-quotient-criterion.md) and [maximal ideal quotient criterion](../../../../maximal-ideal-quotient-criterion.md).
