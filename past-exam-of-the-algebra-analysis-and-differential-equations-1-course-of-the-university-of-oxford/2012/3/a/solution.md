<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) is a proper [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I\ne R$ such that $ab\in I$ implies $a\in I$ or $b\in I$. A [maximal ideal](../../../../maximal-ideal.md) is a proper [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I\ne R$ such that there is no [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) strictly between $I$ and $R$.

Every [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) of the [integers](../../../../integer-split.md) has the form $(n)=n\mathbb Z$ for a unique nonnegative integer $n$. Its [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md) and [maximal ideals](../../../../maximal-ideal.md) are respectively

$$
\boxed{\{(0)\}\cup\{(p):p\text{ prime}\},\qquad
\{(p):p\text{ prime}\}.}
$$

The zero [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) is a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) because the [integers](../../../../integer-split.md) form an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md). For a [prime number](../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$, divisibility of $ab$ by $p$ forces divisibility of one factor, so $(p)$ is a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md). Also any [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $(d)$ containing $(p)$ has $d\mid p$, so it equals $(p)$ or $(1)$; thus $(p)$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../../maximal-ideal.md).

If $n>1$ is composite, write $n=ab$ with $1<a,b<n$. Then $ab\in(n)$ while neither factor belongs to $(n)$, so $(n)$ is not a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md). A [prime number](../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p\mid n$ gives $(n)\subsetneq(p)\subsetneq\mathbb Z$, so $(n)$ is not a [maximal ideal](../../../../maximal-ideal.md) either. The zero [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) is not maximal since $(0)\subsetneq(2)\subsetneq\mathbb Z$, and $(1)=\mathbb Z$ is not proper.
