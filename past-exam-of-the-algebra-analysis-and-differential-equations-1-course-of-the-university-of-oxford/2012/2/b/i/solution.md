<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Expansion down the last column gives the [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md)

$$
\chi_A(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2-\lambda-1).
$$

The quadratic [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has [roots](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) $(1\pm\sqrt5)/2$, which are not [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md). For example, the [rational root theorem](../../../../../rational-root-theorem.md) restricts any rational root of this monic integer [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) to $1$ or $-1$, and neither works. If $A$ had a [diagonalization](../../../../../diagonalization-of-a-matrix.md) over $\mathbb Q$, its [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) would be a product of linear factors with [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md) as their [roots](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md). It is not, so **$A$ is not diagonalizable over $\mathbb Q$**.
