<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Over the [complex numbers](../../../../../complex-number.md), the [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md)

$$
1,\qquad \lambda_+=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
\lambda_-=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}
$$

are distinct. Thus [distinct eigenvalues imply diagonalizability](../../../../../distinct-eigenvalues-imply-diagonalizability.md), and **$A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$**, in fact already over $\mathbb R$.

An explicit [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md) is

$$
e_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v_+=\begin{pmatrix}1\\\lambda_+\\\lambda_++1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v_-=\begin{pmatrix}1\\\lambda_-\\\lambda_-+1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For either quadratic [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) $\lambda$, the identity $\lambda^2=\lambda+1$ verifies $Av_\lambda=\lambda v_\lambda$. Taking these three columns as $P$ gives $P^{-1}AP=\operatorname{diag}(1,\lambda_+,\lambda_-)$. Their distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) ensure [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md).
