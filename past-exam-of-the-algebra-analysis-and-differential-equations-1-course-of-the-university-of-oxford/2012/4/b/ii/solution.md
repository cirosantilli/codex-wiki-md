<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $x\in F$, using $y=x$ in the [infimum](../../../../../infimum.md) gives $0\leq f(x)\leq0$, so $f(x)=0$.

Conversely, if $f(x)=0$, the definition of the [infimum](../../../../../infimum.md) allows a choice $y_j\in F$ with $|x-y_j|<1/j$. The [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $y_j$ is therefore a [convergent sequence](../../../../../convergent-sequence.md) with [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) $x$. Since $F$ is a [closed set](../../../../../closed-set.md), its [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) $x$ belongs to $F$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0\iff x\in F.}
$$

Closedness matters: without it, zero [distance to a set](../../../../../distance-to-a-set.md) characterizes its [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md), which can contain additional points.
