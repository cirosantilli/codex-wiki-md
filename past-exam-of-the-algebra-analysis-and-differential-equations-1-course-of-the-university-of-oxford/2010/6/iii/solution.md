<h1 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $f$ has no zero in the open disk. The strict boundary inequality implies no boundary zero either. Thus $1/f$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk: compactness and continuity keep $f$ nonzero in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Apply the $k=0$ [Cauchy estimate](../../../../cauchy-estimate.md) to $1/f$:

$$
\frac1{|f(z_0)|}\leq\max_{|z-z_0|=r}\frac1{|f(z)|}
=\frac1{\min_{|z-z_0|=r}|f(z)|}.
$$

This contradicts the assumed strict inequality. If $f(z_0)=0$, the conclusion is immediate without taking a reciprocal. Therefore **$f$ has a zero in the open disk**. This is a local form of the [minimum modulus principle](../../../../minimum-modulus-principle.md).
