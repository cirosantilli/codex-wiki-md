<h1 id="analysis/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Writing $z=x+iy$ and $f=u+iv$, the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) are

$$
\boxed{u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x.}
$$

With continuous second derivatives, mixed derivatives commute. Thus

$$
u_{xx}+u_{yy}=v_{yx}-v_{xy}=0,\qquad v_{xx}+v_{yy}=-u_{yx}+u_{xy}=0.
$$

Both real and imaginary parts are [harmonic](../../../../harmonic-function.md).

For $g(z)=\operatorname{Re}z$, the real and imaginary parts are $x$ and zero, so $u_x=1\ne0=v_y$. For $h(z)=\bar z$, they are $x$ and $-y$, so $u_x=1\ne-1=v_y$. The necessary [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) fail at every point. Hence **both functions are nowhere [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md)**, indeed nowhere [complex differentiable](../../../../complex-differentiability.md).
