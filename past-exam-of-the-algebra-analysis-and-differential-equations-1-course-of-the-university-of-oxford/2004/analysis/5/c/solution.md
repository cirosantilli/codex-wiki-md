<h1 id="analysis/5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md) is an [isolated singularity](../../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) which is neither removable nor a [pole](../../../../../pole.md); equivalently its [Laurent expansion](../../../../../laurent-series.md) has infinitely many nonzero negative-power terms.

For the final unnumbered classification, the only possible finite singularities are the zeros of the denominator, namely $z=\pm i$ and $z=k\pi$ for integers $k$. At zero,

$$
f(z)=\frac1{(1+z^2)(\sin z/z)^2}\longrightarrow1,
$$

so **zero is removable, with extension value one**. At each nonzero $k\pi$, $\sin z$ has a simple zero, the other denominator factor is nonzero and the numerator is nonzero. These points are **[poles](../../../../../pole.md) of order two**, with leading coefficient $(k\pi)^2/((k\pi)^2+1)$.

At $z=\pm i$, the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) denominator has a simple zero. Since $\sin(\pm i)=\pm i\sinh1\ne0$ and the numerator is $-1$, these are **simple [poles](../../../../../pole.md)**. There are no essential singularities on $\mathbb C$ and no other finite singularities.
