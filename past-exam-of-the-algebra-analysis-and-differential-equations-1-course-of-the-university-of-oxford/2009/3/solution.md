<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A cubic over a [field](../../../field.md) is reducible exactly when it has a [root](../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) in that [field](../../../field.md). For $p(X)=X^3+2X^2+1$ modulo five, direct evaluation gives

$$
\bigl(p(0),p(1),p(2),p(3),p(4)\bigr)=(1,4,2,1,2).
$$

No value is zero, so **$p$ is irreducible in $\mathbb Z_5[X]$**.

The integer [polynomial](../../../polynomial-split.md) in the second part reduces to precisely $p$, because $-2008\equiv2$, $2010\equiv0$ and $-2009\equiv1\pmod5$. It is monic, hence primitive, and its [degree](../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is preserved by reduction. The [irreducibility by degree-preserving reduction modulo a prime](../../../irreducibility-by-degree-preserving-reduction-modulo-a-prime.md) criterion states that a primitive integer [polynomial](../../../polynomial-split.md) with irreducible reduction of the same [degree](../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is irreducible over the rationals. This follows from [Gauss's lemma](../../../gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry.md): a rational factorization would give integer factors whose leading coefficients remain nonzero modulo five, contradicting the irreducible reduction. Thus

$$
\boxed{X^3-2008X^2+2010X-2009\text{ is irreducible in }\mathbb Q[X].}
$$
