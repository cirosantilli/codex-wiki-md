<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [modulus](../../../../modulus.md) map is continuous, the denominator $1+|z|$ is positive everywhere, and sums, products and quotients with nonzero denominator preserve [continuity](../../../../continuous-function.md). Hence **$f$ is continuous throughout $\mathbb C$**.

For $g$, $|g(z)|=|z|$, so $g(z)\to0=g(0)$ as $z\to0$. The chosen argument is continuous locally away from the negative real axis, making $g$ continuous there as well. At a point $-r$, $r>0$, approach along $z=r e^{i\theta}$. From $\theta\uparrow\pi$, $g(z)\to ir$; from $\theta\downarrow-\pi$, $g(z)\to-ir$. The value assigned on the cut is $ir$. Thus **$g$ is discontinuous at every strictly negative real point**, and therefore is not a continuous map on the entire complex plane. Its radial factor is $r$, as printed, rather than the $\sqrt r$ of a square [root](../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md).
