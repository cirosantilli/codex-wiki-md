# Countability of point atoms of a finite measure

↑ **Parent:** [Point atom of a measure](point-atom-of-a-measure.md)

For a finite [measure](measure-split.md) on a [measurable space](measurable-space.md) whose singletons are [measurable sets](measurable-set.md), the [set](set-split.md) of [point atoms of a measure](point-atom-of-a-measure.md) is at most [countable](countable-set.md). For each positive integer $m$, let $D_m$ consist of the points with singleton [measure](measure-split.md) at least $1/m$. If $D_m$ contained more than $m\mu(\Omega)$ distinct points, [finite additivity](finite-additivity-of-a-set-function.md) on those disjoint singletons would exceed the total [measure](measure-split.md). Thus each $D_m$ is finite.

Every strictly positive real number exceeds or equals $1/m$ for some $m$, so the [point atoms of a measure](point-atom-of-a-measure.md) form the [countable union](countable-union.md) $\bigcup_{m\geq1}D_m$. This proves that the [set](set-split.md) is [countable](countable-set.md). It also shows that a [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) has only countably many jumps. The result does not say the [measure](measure-split.md) is concentrated on those points: a [non-atomic measure](non-atomic-measure.md) may assign them total [measure](measure-split.md) zero.
