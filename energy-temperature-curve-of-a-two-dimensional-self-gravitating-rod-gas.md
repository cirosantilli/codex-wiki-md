# Energy-temperature curve of a two-dimensional self-gravitating rod gas

↑ **Parent:** [Two-dimensional self-gravitating rod equilibrium](two-dimensional-self-gravitating-rod-equilibrium.md)

With $\Theta=T/T_{\min}>1$, the [two-dimensional self-gravitating rod equilibrium](two-dimensional-self-gravitating-rod-equilibrium.md) has the displayed energy per unit length for the potential reference $\psi(R_e)=-GM\log(V/V_0)$. Integration of $-\rho\psi/2$ gives its gravitational energy; its two-dimensional [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution.md) has kinetic energy $NkT$. At fixed area, differentiation gives $C=Nk[2+2\Theta\log(1-\Theta^{-1})+\Theta/(\Theta-1)]$. Expanding the logarithm shows $C/(Nk)=1+\sum_{j\geq3}(j-2)/(j\Theta^{j-1})>1$. The energy increases continuously from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, so there is no finite-energy endpoint or turning point of the regular sequence. This excludes the classical [gravothermal catastrophe](gravothermal-catastrophe.md) in this model, while leaving a distinct canonical collapse at or below its minimum temperature.
