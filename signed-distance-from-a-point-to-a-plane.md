# Signed distance from a point to a plane

↑ **Parent:** [Distance from a point to a plane](distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md)

Orient the [plane](plane.md) $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=d$ with a [unit normal](unit-normal.md) $\mathbf n$. A [point](point-geometry.md) $\mathbf p$ then has [signed distance from a point to a plane](signed-distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md)

$$
s=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p-d.
$$

Its [absolute value](absolute-value.md) is the ordinary [distance from a point to a plane](distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md). The [closest point on a plane](closest-point-on-a-plane.md) is $\mathbf p-s\mathbf n$, so the [displacement](displacement.md) from the [point](point-geometry.md) to its [orthogonal projection onto a plane](closest-point-on-a-plane.md) is $-s\mathbf n$. Reversing the [unit normal](unit-normal.md) and $d$ reverses $s$ without changing the [plane](plane.md) or the [distance from a point to a plane](distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md). A [sphere](sphere.md) of [radius](radius.md) $q$ centred at $\mathbf p$ has [sphere-plane intersection](sphere-plane-intersection.md) radius $\sqrt{q^2-s^2}$ when $|s|<q$.

For a general, not necessarily unit, [normal vector](normal-vector.md) $\mathbf n_0$ in $\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf x=d_0$, the oriented distance is $(\mathbf n_0\cdot\mathbf p-d_0)/\|\mathbf n_0\|$. In the worked example of [distance from a point to a plane](distance-from-a-point-to-a-plane.md), orientation toward $(2,-1,2)$ assigns $+8/3$ to $(4,1,2)$; reversing that [unit normal](unit-normal.md) assigns $-8/3$. The [closest point on a plane](closest-point-on-a-plane.md) and the unsigned distance are unaffected.
