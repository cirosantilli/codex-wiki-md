# Lenard cathode-ray window experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md)

The [Lenard cathode-ray window experiment](lenard-cathode-ray-window-experiment.md) lets [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md) leave a [gas-discharge tube](gas-discharge-tube.md) through a thin [aluminium](aluminium.md) foil while preserving the tube's [vacuum](vacuum.md). This separates production from propagation.

[Philipp Lenard](philipp-lenard.md)'s lecture, delivered on 28 May 1906, describes sealing a small hole in a metal plate with [aluminium](aluminium.md) foil. Luminous phosphor grains first rested on the window, then glowed above it in air. His improved tube separated production and observation chambers; coated paper served as a [fluorescent screen](fluorescent-screen.md). An aperture revealed spreading beyond a narrow straight beam. Evacuating the observation chamber reduced spreading and attenuation without interrupting production. Interposed layers and stepped foil stacks gave shadow comparisons; approximate scaling with mass had exceptions.[On cathode rays, title page p. 105; pp. 108–114, Figs. 3–8: foil window, phosphor readout, aperture, layer comparisons and evacuated observation chamber](on-cathode-rays-by-philipp-lenard.md)

**[Figure 6](#image-lenard-window-tube-and-observation-chamber). The Lenard window separates a cathode ray source from an observation chamber**. Original functional schematic, with component sizes and paths chosen for explanation. The seal sustains a pressure difference; transmission does not mean that air can leak through. The lower panels contrast ideal straight propagation with angular redistribution by [gas](gas.md); these paths are illustrative, not reconstructed measurements. A [fluorescent screen](fluorescent-screen.md) converts incident [electron](electron.md) [energy](energy-split.md) into visible [light](light.md).

The experimental advantage is unusually clean: reducing the [gas](gas.md) content in the observation chamber tests whether [gas](gas.md) is needed to carry the [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md), while retaining the conditions needed to generate them. The window is a boundary that transmits some [electrons](electron.md) while confining the source environment. A spot outside establishes a transmitted effect; the aperture and changed observation environment provide additional ways to distinguish propagation from the source's behavior. This logic is useful whenever a complicated source would otherwise be changed by the very intervention intended to test the emitted beam.

Several inferences require care. A darker [fluorescent screen](fluorescent-screen.md) can mean fewer arriving [electrons](electron.md), less deposited [energy](energy-split.md) per [electron](electron.md), or redistribution by [electron scattering](electron-scattering.md) beyond the observed area. A visible endpoint depends on the screen's sensitivity and background. It is not an intrinsic [electron](electron.md) range independent of initial [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), [gas](gas.md) [density](density.md), and observation geometry. For a modern comparison, measure a reference at fixed source conditions, use [background subtraction](background-subtraction.md), and compare the whole luminous patch as well as a small central region. A wider collection area tests redistribution; changing source-to-screen separation also changes collection geometry. These are proposed controls for interpreting a readout, not additional historical observations attributed to Lenard.

Nor is [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) a unique signature of [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md): [X-rays](x-ray.md) can also excite it. Lenard notes that a later tube could transmit both, and that slow [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md) failed to pass its window.[On cathode rays, pp. 114–115 and 120–121: X-rays in the later tube; limited transmitted velocity range](on-cathode-rays-by-philipp-lenard.md) In modern terms, [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md) are [electrons](electron.md) carrying negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), whereas [X-rays](x-ray.md) are [photons](photon.md) with zero [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). Magnetic bending of the incident [electrons](electron.md), interpreted through the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md), supplies a discrimination that brightness alone cannot. Passage through foil alone determines neither the sign of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) nor its magnitude, and it does not measure [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md). The separate [Thomson charge-to-mass experiment](thomson-charge-to-mass-experiment.md) explains that inference. Even a measured [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) needs an independent [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) measurement to determine [mass](mass.md).
