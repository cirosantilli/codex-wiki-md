# Inverse-square potential

↑ **Parent:** [Potential energy](potential-energy.md)

Here [inverse-square potential](inverse-square-potential.md) means the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) associated with an attractive [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) for a [central force](central-force.md): the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) itself varies as inverse distance. For $k>0$ and [position](position.md) $\mathbf r$ with $r=|\mathbf r|>0$,

$$
V(r)=-\frac{k}{r},\qquad V(\infty)=0,\qquad \mathbf F=-\nabla V=-\frac{k}{r^2}\widehat{\mathbf r}=-\frac{k\mathbf r}{r^3}.
$$

The [gradient](gradient.md) makes the distinction explicit: doubling $r$ halves the magnitude of [potential energy](potential-energy.md) but quarters the magnitude of [force](force.md). The neighboring [repulsive inverse-square potential](repulsive-inverse-square-potential.md) instead has [potential energy](potential-energy.md) proportional to $r^{-2}$ and an inverse-cube [force](force.md); the two naming conventions must be distinguished.

For [Newton's law of universal gravitation](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md), $k=GMm$, where $G$ is the [gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md). Treat the attracting [mass](mass.md) $M$ as an isolated fixed [point mass](point-mass.md), or as a body with [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md) and exterior [test particle](test-particle.md) of constant [mass](mass.md) $m\ll M$. The [spherical shell theorem](spherical-shell-theorem.md) gives the same exterior [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md) $U=-GMm/r$ without requiring uniform [mass density](density.md). Here $r$ is measured from the centre, and the formula applies outside the body; it does not describe its interior. The [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) per unit [mass](mass.md) is $\Phi=U/m=-GM/r$.[§13–2, equations 13.11–13.12: radial work and conserved energy; §13–4: gravitational field outside a sphere](the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-i-by-richard-feynman-and-others/chapter-13-work-and-potential-energy-a.md)

The negative sign records binding relative to infinite separation, rather than a negative [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). Moving from $r_1$ to $r_2>r_1$ increases [potential energy](potential-energy.md) by

$$
\Delta V=k\left(\frac1{r_1}-\frac1{r_2}\right)>0,\qquad W_{\mathrm{gravity}}=-\Delta V.
$$

Because the [force](force.md) is a [conservative force](conservative-force.md), this [work](work.md) depends on the endpoints, including for a curved path. Integrating $dV/dr=k/r^2$ with zero at infinity gives $V=-k/r$; adding a constant leaves the [force](force.md) unchanged but shifts the numerical escape [energy](energy.md) threshold by that same constant. This is why the reference at infinity must accompany an [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md) calculation.

For an exact synthetic comparison, stipulate the [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md) $\mu=GM=8\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ [test particle](test-particle.md). At $r=2\,\mathrm m$, $U=-4\,\mathrm J$ and the inward [force](force.md) has magnitude $2\,\mathrm N$; at $r=4\,\mathrm m$, $U=-2\,\mathrm J$ and the inward [force](force.md) has magnitude $0.5\,\mathrm N$. The increase is $2\,\mathrm J$ of [potential energy](potential-energy.md). These stipulated parameters are an arithmetic example, not observations of a particular body. The [escape-energy balance diagram](escape-energy-balance-diagram.md) uses the same parameters.
