<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For every $i\geq0$, the inclusion $P^{i+1}\subseteq P^i$ is strict: equality, followed by multiplication by the inverse of $P^i$, would give $P=R$. Hence one can choose

$$
\pi\in P^i\setminus P^{i+1}.
$$

Put $J=(\pi)+P^{i+1}$. Then

$$
P^{i+1}\subsetneq J\subseteq P^i.
$$

The classification just proved, applied modulo $P^{i+1}$, leaves no [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) strictly between consecutive powers. It follows that

$$
\boxed{P^i=(\pi)+P^{i+1}}.
$$

For $i=0$, the same argument says $(\pi)+P=R$, using $\pi\notin P$ and maximality.

The extension to every $r\geq1$ follows by [mathematical induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md). Suppose $P^i=(\pi)+P^{i+r}$. Multiplication of the initial equality by $P^r$ gives

$$
P^{i+r}=\pi P^r+P^{i+r+1}.
$$

Substitution yields

$$
P^i=(\pi)+\pi P^r+P^{i+r+1}=(\pi)+P^{i+r+1}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{P^i=(\pi)+P^{i+r}\quad\text{for every }r\geq1}.
$$

Taking $r=n-i$ for $0\leq i<n$ gives

$$
\boxed{P^i/P^n=(\pi+P^n)}.
$$

This is a [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) of the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) $R/P^n$. Its zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is generated by zero, so every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of this [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) is a [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md).
