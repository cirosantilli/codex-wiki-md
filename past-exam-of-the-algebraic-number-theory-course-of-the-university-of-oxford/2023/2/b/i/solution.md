<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

By the [correspondence theorem for ideals](../../../../../correspondence-theorem-for-ideals.md), an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $R/P^n$ has the form $J/P^n$ with $P^n\subseteq J\subseteq R$. If $J\ne R$, apply [unique factorization of ideals in a number field](../../../../../unique-factorization-of-ideals-in-a-number-field.md) to $J$. Every [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $Q$ dividing $J$ contains $J$ and hence $P^n$. Primality gives $P\subseteq Q$, and maximality gives $Q=P$. Thus $J=P^i$ for some $i\geq1$.

The inclusion $P^n\subseteq P^i$ forces $i\leq n$: if $i>n$, cancellation using the [invertible fractional ideal](../../../../../invertible-fractional-ideal.md) $P^n$ would give $R\subseteq P^{i-n}$, which is impossible. The zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) corresponds to $i=n$, while the unit [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) corresponds to $i=0$. Consequently the nonzero proper [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) are exactly

$$
\boxed{P^i/P^n,\qquad 1\leq i<n}.
$$

When $n=1$, there are none, in agreement with $R/P$ being a [field](../../../../../field.md).
