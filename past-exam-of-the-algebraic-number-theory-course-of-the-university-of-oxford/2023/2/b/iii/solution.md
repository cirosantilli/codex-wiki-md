↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**The printed domain assertion is false.** For example, take $K=\mathbb Q$ and $I=(4)$. Then $\mathcal O_K/I=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ has the nonzero [zero divisor](../../../../../zero-divisor.md) $2+(4)$, so it is not an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) and cannot be a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md). The valid conclusion is that $\mathcal O_K/I$ is a [principal ideal ring](../../../../../principal-ideal-ring.md). This error is present in the original PDF, not just its transcription.

To prove the corrected conclusion, factor

$$
I=\prod_{j=1}^sP_j^{n_j}
$$

into powers of distinct nonzero [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md). These powers are pairwise [comaximal ideals](../../../../../comaximal-ideals.md). Indeed, a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) containing two such powers would contain both underlying [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md), forcing them to be equal. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) gives

$$
R/I\simeq\prod_{j=1}^s R/P_j^{n_j}.
$$

Every factor is a [principal ideal ring](../../../../../principal-ideal-ring.md) by the preceding part. Every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $H$ in a finite [direct product of rings](../../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md) is a product $H_1\times\cdots\times H_s$ of [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) in the factors: multiplication by the coordinate [idempotent elements](../../../../../idempotent.md) separates the coordinates of every element of $H$. Write $H_j=(h_j)$. Then

$$
H_1\times\cdots\times H_s=((h_1,\ldots,h_s)),
$$

so $R/I$ is a [principal ideal ring](../../../../../principal-ideal-ring.md). This proves the [principal ideal property of quotients of a Dedekind domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-property-of-quotients-of-a-dedekind-domain.md) in the present setting. In fact, this finite [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) precisely when $I$ is a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md), in which case it is a [field](../../../../../field.md).

For the final deduction, choose any $0\ne a\in I$ and apply the result to the nonzero proper [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $(a)$. The [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $I/(a)$ of $R/(a)$ is generated by one residue class $b+(a)$, which can be represented by $b\in I$. For each $x\in I$, some $r\in R$ satisfies $x-rb\in(a)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{I=(a,b)}.
$$

This proves [two generators for an ideal in a Dedekind domain](../../../../../two-generators-for-an-ideal-in-a-dedekind-domain.md); moreover, its first generator can be any prescribed nonzero element of $I$.
