<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $\alpha=\sqrt{m_1}$, $\beta=\sqrt{m_2}$ and $F=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. The hypotheses make $L/\mathbb Q$ a [biquadratic extension](../../../../../biquadratic-extension.md). To check the [degree of a field extension](../../../../../degree-of-a-field-extension.md) is four, if $\beta=x+y\alpha$ with rational $x,y$, squaring gives $xy=0$. The case $y=0$ would make $m_2$ a rational square; the case $x=0$ would make $m_2/m_1$ a rational square. Neither can occur for the specified distinct coprime [square-free integers](../../../../../square-free-integer.md). Hence $[L:F]=2$.

The nonidentity [field automorphism](../../../../../field-automorphism.md) of $L/F$ fixes $\alpha$ and sends $\beta$ to $-\beta$. Thus, for $\gamma=(r+s\alpha)+(t+u\alpha)\beta$, the [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) and [field trace](../../../../../field-trace.md) are

$$
\boxed{N_{L/F}(\gamma)=(r+s\alpha)^2-m_2(t+u\alpha)^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(\gamma)=2(r+s\alpha)}.
$$

If $\gamma$ is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../algebraic-integer.md), so is its conjugate, and their sum and product are [algebraic integers](../../../../../algebraic-integer.md). Conversely, put $T=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(\gamma)$ and $N=N_{L/F}(\gamma)$. The identity

$$
\gamma^2-T\gamma+N=0
$$

shows that $\gamma$ is integral over $\mathbb Z[T,N]$. If $T,N$ are [algebraic integers](../../../../../algebraic-integer.md), the [ring generated by algebraic integers](../../../../../ring-generated-by-algebraic-integers.md) $\mathbb Z[T,N]$ is integral over $\mathbb Z$. Since [integral dependence is transitive](../../../../../integral-dependence-is-transitive.md), $\gamma$ is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../algebraic-integer.md). This proves the [trace and norm integrality criterion for a quadratic extension](../../../../../trace-and-norm-integrality-criterion-for-a-quadratic-extension.md), including elements already in $F$.
