<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

To compute the [Ideal class group of Q of square root thirty-five](../../../../ideal-class-group-of-q-of-square-root-thirty-five.md), put $\theta=\sqrt{35}$. The [ring of integers of a quadratic field](../../../../ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field.md) formula gives $R=\mathbb Z[\theta]$, and the [field discriminant](../../../../field-discriminant.md) is $4\cdot35=140$. Both [field embeddings](../../../../field-embedding.md) are real, so the [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is

$$
\frac{2!}{2^2}\sqrt{140}=\sqrt{35}<6.
$$

Every [ideal class](../../../../ideal-class.md) therefore has an [integral ideal](../../../../integral-ideal.md) representative of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most five. By [prime ideal factorization](../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) and multiplicativity of the [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md), the [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is generated by [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most five.

Apply the [Dedekind factorization theorem](../../../../dedekind-factorization-theorem.md) to $X^2-35$. At two and five it has a repeated linear factor, giving

$$
(2)=P_2^2,\quad P_2=(2,\theta-1),\quad N(P_2)=2;
\qquad
(5)=P_5^2,\quad P_5=(5,\theta),\quad N(P_5)=5.
$$

At three, $X^2-35\equiv X^2+1$ has no root, so $(3)$ itself is a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) nine, outside the bound. These are all the relevant [prime ideals](../../../../prime-ideal.md): a [prime ideal](../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) four would have to lie above two with [residue degree](../../../../residue-degree.md) two, but the factorization above two has only the single factor of degree one. Consequently $[P_2]$ and $[P_5]$ generate, and each has [order of a group element](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) dividing two.

The [algebraic integer](../../../../algebraic-integer.md) $5+\theta$ has [field norm](../../../../field-norm.md) $25-35=-10$. It belongs to both $P_2$ and $P_5$. Hence its [principal ideal](../../../../principal-ideal.md) is contained in $P_2\cap P_5=P_2P_5$. Both have [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) ten, so

$$
(5+\theta)=P_2P_5.
$$

It follows that $[P_5]=[P_2]^{-1}=[P_2]$, and the [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is generated by one element of [order of a group element](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) at most two.

Finally, $P_2$ is not a [principal ideal](../../../../principal-ideal.md). If $P_2=(a+b\theta)$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$, the equality of [ideal norm](../../../../ideal-norm.md) and absolute [field norm](../../../../field-norm.md) would imply

$$
a^2-35b^2=\pm2.
$$

Modulo five this requires $a^2\equiv2$ or $3$, whereas squares modulo five are $0,1,4$. Thus $[P_2]\ne1$, proving

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{35}))\simeq\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z}.
$$

This computes the ordinary [ideal class group](../../../../ideal-class-group.md), with no positivity restriction on generators.
