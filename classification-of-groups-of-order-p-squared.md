# Classification of groups of order p squared

↑ **Parent:** [Finite p-group](finite-p-group.md)

The [nontrivial center of a finite p-group](nontrivial-center-of-a-finite-p-group.md) theorem implies that the [center of a group](center-of-a-group.md) of order $p^2$ has order $p$ or $p^2$. If it has order $p$, the quotient by the center has prime order and is cyclic; a [cyclic quotient by the center](cyclic-quotient-by-the-center.md) forces the whole [group](group-split.md) to be abelian, contradicting the assumed size of its center. Thus the [group](group-split.md) is abelian. An element of order $p^2$ makes it cyclic. Otherwise choose $a\ne1$ and $b\notin\langle a\rangle$; their order-$p$ [cyclic subgroups](cyclic-subgroup.md) intersect trivially and generate the [direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) $C_p\times C_p$. The two possibilities are nonisomorphic because only the cyclic one has an element of order $p^2$.
