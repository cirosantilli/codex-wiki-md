<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose first that $E+F$ is a [linear projection](../../../../../projection-linear-algebra.md). Part (i) gives $EF=-FE$. Multiplying on the left by $E$ gives $EF=-EFE$, and multiplying on the right by $E$ gives $EFE=-FE=EF$. Combining them yields

$$
EF=-EF,\qquad 2EF=0.
$$

In a [field](../../../../../field.md) of [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) different from two, the nonzero [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) $2$ is invertible. Hence $EF=0$, and $FE=-EF=0$ too. Conversely these two zero products make the expansion in part (i) equal zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E+F\text{ is a linear projection}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad EF=FE=0.}
$$

The argument cancels a nonzero [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md), never a possibly singular [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md).
