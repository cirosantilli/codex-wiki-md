<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The same [invariant subspaces](../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) work for $S=\alpha I+\beta T$: it acts by $\alpha$ on $\ker T$ and by $\alpha+\beta$ on $\operatorname{im}T$. Its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is therefore the product of the distinct [linear polynomials](../../../../../linear-polynomial.md) belonging to the nonzero summands. Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{m_S(x)=\begin{cases}
x-\alpha,&\beta=0\text{ or }T=0,\\
x-(\alpha+\beta),&\beta\ne0,\ T=I,\\
(x-\alpha)(x-\alpha-\beta),&\beta\ne0,\ T\ne0,I.
\end{cases}}
$$

When $\beta\ne0$, the two candidate [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are distinct in any [field](../../../../../field.md). This is the [minimal polynomial of an affine function of a projection](../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-affine-function-of-a-projection.md).
