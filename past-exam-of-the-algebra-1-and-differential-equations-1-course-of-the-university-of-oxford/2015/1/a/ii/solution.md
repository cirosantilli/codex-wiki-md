<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $n=\dim V>0$ and $r=\operatorname{rank}T$. The [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) in part (i) gives

$$
\boxed{\chi_T(x)=x^{n-r}(x-1)^r.}
$$

The [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are the members of $\{0,1\}$ actually present: zero occurs exactly when $r<n$, and one exactly when $r>0$. Since $T$ is [diagonalizable](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md), its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) contains each present [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) once:

$$
\boxed{m_T(x)=\begin{cases}
x,&T=0,\\
x-1,&T=I,\\
x(x-1),&T\ne0,I.
\end{cases}}
$$

In particular, a nonzero [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) does not force both [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) to occur. The zero and [identity maps](../../../../../identity-function.md) are important special cases.
