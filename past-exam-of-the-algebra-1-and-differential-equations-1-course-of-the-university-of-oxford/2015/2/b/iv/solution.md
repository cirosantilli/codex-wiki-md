<h1 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

On each nonzero [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) $V_\lambda$, the restricted [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) $S_\lambda=S|_{V_\lambda}$ is [diagonalizable](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md). Indeed $m_S(S_\lambda)=0$, so the [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) of $S_\lambda$ divides $m_S$. A divisor of a product of distinct [linear polynomials](../../../../../linear-polynomial.md) has the same property, proving the [diagonalizable restriction lemma](../../../../../diagonalizable-restriction-lemma.md).

Choose a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $S_\lambda$-[eigenvectors](../../../../../eigenvector.md) in each $V_\lambda$. Every chosen vector is simultaneously a $T$-[eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$. The [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) in part (ii) shows that joining these bases gives a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $V$. Therefore **$S$ and $T$ have a common [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md)**, which is exactly [simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../simultaneous-diagonalization.md).
