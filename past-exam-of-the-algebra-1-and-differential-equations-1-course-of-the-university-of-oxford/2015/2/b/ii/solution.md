<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $T$ is [diagonalizable](../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md), its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is $\prod_{j=1}^r(x-\lambda_j)$ with distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda_j$ in the [field](../../../../../field.md). Apply the [primary decomposition theorem for linear maps](../../../../../primary-decomposition-theorem-for-linear-maps.md):

$$
\boxed{V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^r\ker(T-\lambda_jI)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb F}V_\lambda.}
$$

For a [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) that is not an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), the associated [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) is zero, so the sum indexed by the whole [field](../../../../../field.md) has only finitely many nonzero summands. The [eigenspaces](../../../../../eigenspace.md) need not be one-dimensional.
