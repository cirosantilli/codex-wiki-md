<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $U=\ker p(T)$, $W=\ker q(T)$. The [linear operator](../../../../../linear-operator.md) $E=a(T)p(T)$ vanishes on $U$. On $W$, the identity $E=I-b(T)q(T)$ gives $Ew=w$.

By the [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) in part (i), every vector has a unique expression $u+w$, and

$$
E(u+w)=w.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{E^2=E,\qquad\operatorname{im}E=\ker q(T),\qquad\ker E=\ker p(T).}
$$

The complementary [projection operator](../../../../../projection-linear-algebra.md) is $I-E=b(T)q(T)$, onto $U$ along $W$. These are [polynomial projections for a coprime decomposition](../../../../../polynomial-projections-for-a-coprime-decomposition.md). In particular the projection does not depend on which Bézout coefficients are chosen: its action on the two summands uniquely determines it.
