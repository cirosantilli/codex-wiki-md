<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The **[minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md)** $m_T$ is the monic [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of least [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) such that $m_T(T)=0$. Here a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $p(x)=\sum_jc_jx^j$ is evaluated at the [linear operator](../../../../../linear-operator.md) by $p(T)=\sum_jc_jT^j$, with the constant term acting as $c_0I$.

An annihilating [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) exists because $I,T,T^2,\ldots$ lie in the finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $\operatorname{End}_F(V)$ and are therefore eventually [linearly dependent](../../../../../linear-dependence.md). Uniqueness follows by subtracting two monic annihilators of the same least degree: their difference has smaller degree and still annihilates $T$.

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{m_T\mid p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p(T)=0.}
$$

For the forward implication evaluate the factorization at $T$. For the reverse implication divide $p$ by $m_T$ using [polynomial division](../../../../../polynomial-division.md); the remainder annihilates $T$, so minimality makes it zero. This also identifies the annihilating polynomials as the [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) generated by $m_T$.
