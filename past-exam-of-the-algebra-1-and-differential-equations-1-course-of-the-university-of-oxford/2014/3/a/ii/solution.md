<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [annihilator of a vector subspace](../../../../../annihilator-of-a-vector-subspace.md) $W\leq V^*$ is

$$
\boxed{W^\circ=\{L\in V^{**}:L(\phi)=0\text{ for every }\phi\in W\}.}
$$

If $\dim W=k$, extend a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $W$ to one of $V^*$. In the resulting [dual basis](../../../../../dual-basis.md) of $V^{**}$, the last $n-k$ coordinate functionals span $W^\circ$. Hence $\boxed{\dim W+\dim W^\circ=n}$.

The [bidual evaluation map](../../../../../bidual-evaluation-map.md) $j:V\to V^{**}$ is $j(x)(\phi)=\phi(x)$. It is a [linear isomorphism](../../../../../linear-isomorphism.md) in finite dimension. Its inverse image of the annihilator is

$$
\boxed{j^{-1}(W^\circ)=\{x\in V:\phi(x)=0\text{ for every }\phi\in W\}.}
$$

This identification uses evaluation only; no choice of [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) is involved.
