<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the $m$-dimensional space $V_m$ for this [change of basis](../../../../../change-of-basis.md). With the columns of $P$ recording the new basis in the old one, bilinearity gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varphi(f_i,f_j)
&=\varphi\left(\sum_{k=1}^mP_{ki}e_k,\sum_{\ell=1}^mP_{\ell j}e_\ell\right)\\
&=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^mP_{ki}(A_m)_{k\ell}P_{\ell j}\\
&=(P^TA_mP)_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the matrices of the [bilinear form](../../../../../bilinear-form-split.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{B_m=P^TA_mP.}
$$

This is [matrix congruence](../../../../../matrix-congruence.md), rather than the similarity rule for an endomorphism: both arguments of the bilinear form change basis.
