# Resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md)

The motivating observation is an abrupt disappearance of detectable [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) on cooling, rather than a smooth improvement in conduction. The diagram pairs the measuring arrangement with an idealized transition. Its curves are deliberately synthetic; neither their scales nor their abrupt step are a reconstruction of measured mercury data.

**[Figure 4](#image-separate-current-and-voltage-leads-reveal-a-synthetic-superconducting-resistance-transition). Separate current and voltage leads reveal a synthetic superconducting resistance transition**. The left panel shows [four-terminal sensing](four-terminal-sensing.md); the right compares an idealized zero-[electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) branch with a stipulated normal-state continuation. All curves and the resolution band are illustrative, not measured data. The dashed vertical segment marks an idealized jump, not a fitted transition width.

In a report communicated in November 1911, Heike Kamerlingh Onnes described a mercury thread in seven connected glass U-tubes immersed in a stirred liquid-helium bath. Expansion spaces kept freezing mercury from breaking the glass or interrupting the thread. Separate leads supplied [electric current](electric-current.md) and sensed [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). He compensated a troublesome [thermoelectric effect](thermoelectric-effect-split.md) and reversed the main [electric current](electric-current.md) to extract the resistive signal. He reported a rapid fall between $4.21$ and $4.19\,\mathrm K$ on his stated historical [temperature](temperature-split.md) scale, with [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) disappearing at the lower value. The apparatus and control of spurious [electric potential differences](electric-potential-difference.md) are part of the discovery, not incidental details. [Kamerlingh Onnes, Communication 124c (1911), §§2–3, pp. 819–821](https://dwc.knaw.nl/DL/publications/PU00013242.pdf)

A modern version places a contacted sample and a calibrated [thermometer](thermometer.md) close together in a cooling environment. Use [four-terminal sensing](four-terminal-sensing.md), record the small applied [electric current](electric-current.md) $I$, the sensed [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V$, and the sample [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T$, then plot $R(T)=V/I$. Repeat on warming as well as cooling, and allow readings to stabilize near [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md). Disagreement between warming and cooling is a reason to check sample–[thermometer](thermometer.md) lag and reproducibility before quoting a [superconducting transition temperature](superconducting-transition-temperature.md). Mikhailov and Kordosky's undergraduate experiment uses this strategy with liquid-nitrogen cooling. [Mikhailov and Kordosky, Physics 251 superconductivity lab, measurement procedure and discussion](https://physics.wm.edu/~evmik/classes/2021_fall_Experimental_Atomic_Physics_251/lab_descriptions/superconductivity/)

Choose the measuring [electric current](electric-current.md) small enough that [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) does not appreciably change the sample [temperature](temperature-split.md), and repeat with a smaller [electric current](electric-current.md) to check that the transition persists. Cancel stable offsets using the reversal described in [four-terminal sensing](four-terminal-sensing.md). A reading indistinguishable from zero supplies an experimental upper bound, not a proof of exact zero [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md). If $\delta V$ bounds the unresolved resistive [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), then

$$
|R|\lesssim\frac{\delta V}{|I|}.
$$

For a stipulated sensitivity $\delta V=20\,\mathrm{nV}$ and $I=1\,\mathrm{mA}$, this is $20\,\mathrm{\mu\Omega}$. The bound applies between the sensing contacts; it is not the [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) of the complete wiring. A conducting path through part of an inhomogeneous sample can also make $V$ negligible, so the [Field-cooled Meissner experiment](field-cooled-meissner-experiment.md) adds independent evidence about the material's magnetic response.
