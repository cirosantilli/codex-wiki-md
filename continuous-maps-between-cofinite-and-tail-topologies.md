# Continuous maps between cofinite and tail topologies

↑ **Parent:** [Tail topology on the natural numbers](tail-topology-on-the-natural-numbers.md)

For a map $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ from the [cofinite topology](cofinite-topology.md) to the [tail topology on the natural numbers](tail-topology-on-the-natural-numbers.md), [continuity](continuous-function.md) is equivalent to every set $\{n:f(n)\ge k\}$ being empty or cofinite. If the image is unbounded, every such set is nonempty and cofinite, so $f(n)\to\infty$. If it is bounded, its maximum $M$ exists, and the inverse image of the tail at $M$ forces $f(n)=M$ eventually, with $f(n)\le M$ always. Conversely either condition makes every tail preimage empty or cofinite.
