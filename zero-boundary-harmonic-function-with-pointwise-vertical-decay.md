# Zero-boundary harmonic function with pointwise vertical decay

↑ **Parent:** [Laplace equation](laplace-equation.md)

The imaginary part of the entire function $e^{z^2}$ is a [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md). On the upper half-plane it vanishes at $y=0$ and tends to zero as $y\to\infty$ for every fixed $x$, but is nonzero and grows rapidly in the horizontal direction. Adding it to a [Poisson integral](poisson-integral.md) preserves those boundary and vertical-decay conditions. A boundedness or suitable transform-growth condition is therefore needed to make the half-plane [Dirichlet problem](dirichlet-problem.md) unique.
