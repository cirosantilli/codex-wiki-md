# Homomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic structure](algebraic-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homomorphism)

A homomorphism is a map between [algebraic structures](algebraic-structure.md) preserving their specified operations. For a single [binary operation](binary-operation.md), this means $f(a\ast b)=f(a)\star f(b)$, where the source and target may use different operation symbols. A [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) preserves the group operation; a [ring homomorphism](ring-homomorphism.md) preserves addition and multiplication, with the identity convention specified; a [module homomorphism](module-homomorphism.md) preserves addition and scalar multiplication.

Reduction modulo $5$ is a [ring homomorphism](ring-homomorphism.md) $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$: adding or multiplying first and then reducing gives the same residue as reducing first and then operating. For example, $12\cdot14=168\equiv3\pmod5$, while the reduced operands give $2\cdot4=8\equiv3\pmod5$. This lets a complicated integer calculation be projected into simpler finite arithmetic. It also explains why a [polynomial equation](polynomial-equation.md) over the [integers](integer-split.md) imposes necessary [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic-split.md) conditions on any integer solution.

A homomorphism can lose information: $0$ and $5$ have the same image under that reduction. An [isomorphism](isomorphism.md) loses none of the structure because it has an operation-preserving inverse. Preservation of the operation, rather than preservation of arbitrary labels or sizes, is the essential condition.
