# Maximal cylinder and box in an elliptic paraboloid

↑ **Parent:** [Elliptic paraboloid](elliptic-paraboloid.md)

For the rotational [elliptic paraboloid](elliptic-paraboloid.md) $z=H(1-(x^2+y^2)/R^2)$ above $z=0$, allow inscribed shapes to touch the boundary. A coaxial [cylinder](cylinder-geometry.md) of radius $r$ and height $h$ obeys $h+Hr^2/R^2=H$. Applying a [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) to $\pi r^2h$ gives $h=Hr^2/R^2$, hence $h=H/2$ and $r=R/\sqrt2$.

A centred [rectangular parallelepiped](rectangular-parallelepiped.md) with horizontal side lengths $a,b$ and height $c$ obeys $c+H(a^2+b^2)/(4R^2)=H$. The [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) equations for $abc$ give $a=b$ and $c=Ha^2/(2R^2)$, hence $a=b=R,c=H/2$. These yield the displayed maximal volumes; both positive stationary shapes are interior optima among the dimension variables, while their degenerate boundary shapes have zero volume. The cylinder's volume is $\pi/2$ times the box's. If strict containment in the open region is required, these are suprema approached by slightly smaller shapes rather than attained maxima.
