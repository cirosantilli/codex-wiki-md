# Schur complement determinant identity

↑ **Parent:** [Schur complement](schur-complement.md)

When $A$ is an [invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md), the factorization in [block elimination](block-elimination.md) has outer triangular factors with [determinant](determinant.md) one. Hence $\det M=\det A\det S$, without a symmetry assumption. For the original example under [block matrix inverse](block-matrix-inverse.md), this gives $\det M=(2\cdot3)(19/6)=19$. An invertible block $A$ therefore reduces the question of whether the full [matrix](matrix.md) is an [invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md) to the smaller [Schur complement](schur-complement.md); a singular $S$ makes $M$ a [singular matrix](singular-matrix.md).
