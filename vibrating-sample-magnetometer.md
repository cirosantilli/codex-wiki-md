# Vibrating-sample magnetometer

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](magnetization.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrating-sample_magnetometer)

A [vibrating-sample magnetometer](vibrating-sample-magnetometer.md) measures a specimen's [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) through the induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) as it moves relative to stationary pickup coils. Dividing that [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) by the specimen's independently determined [volume](volume.md) gives its average [magnetization](magnetization.md).

In [Simon Foner](simon-foner.md)'s 1959 apparatus, the specimen was weighed and glued to a mount. A loudspeaker drive moved it perpendicular to an applied uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) between magnet poles; stationary coils detected the induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md). A reference magnet on the same vibrating assembly generated a second coil [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md). Adjusting the reference [voltage divider](voltage-divider.md) to an electrical null compared the specimen signal with this reference. The [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md) substituted a magnetically saturated pure [nickel](nickel.md) sphere of known [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md); a specimen-removed reading corrected the mount's contribution. The calibrated signal inferred the specimen's [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md), not its local [magnetization](magnetization.md) map. [Measurement uncertainty](measurement-uncertainty.md) in the standard, positioning, finite specimen size, coil noise, and mechanical coupling limited the inference.[Versatile and Sensitive Vibrating-Sample Magnetometer, pp. 549–550, §I and Figs. 1–2; p. 553, §IV and Fig. 9; p. 554, §§V.A–B; p. 556, §§V.G and VI.A.1](versatile-and-sensitive-vibrating-sample-magnetometer-by-simon-foner.md)

The readout follows [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md). In a small-displacement model with constant projected [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) $m$, let the pickup-coil [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) linkage be $\Phi(z)=g(z)m$ and the specimen motion be $z(t)=z_0+a\cos\omega t$. Here $g$ incorporates coil geometry and turn count, $a$ is the displacement [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), and $\omega$ is [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md). To first order in displacement, with $g'(z_0)$ the [derivative](derivative.md) at the mean position,

$$
V(t)=-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\simeq m\,g'(z_0)a\omega\sin\omega t.
$$

This is an original idealized readout relation: a larger induced [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) can mean larger [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md), greater motion, or a changed coil position. A synchronously moving reference allows common motion factors to cancel in the ratio of induced [electromotive forces](electromotive-force.md). The [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md) and specimen-removed control are therefore integral to the measurement. A single [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) reading cannot by itself distinguish microscopic alignment from a change in individual [magnetic dipole moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md).
