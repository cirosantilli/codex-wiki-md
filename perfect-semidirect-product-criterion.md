# Perfect semidirect product criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Semidirect product of a Lie algebra and a module](semidirect-product-of-a-lie-algebra-and-a-module.md)

For a [Lie algebra representation](lie-algebra-representation.md) of $\mathfrak g$ on $V$, let $\mathfrak gV$ denote the [linear span](linear-span.md) of $xv$ for $x\in\mathfrak g$ and $v\in V$. The [derived algebra](derived-algebra.md) of the [semidirect product of a Lie algebra and a module](semidirect-product-of-a-lie-algebra-and-a-module.md) is the displayed [direct sum of vector spaces](direct-sum.md). Every [Lie bracket](lie-bracket.md) has first component in $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$ and second component in $\mathfrak gV$. Conversely, brackets of $(x,0)$ with $(y,0)$ span the former component, and brackets of $(x,0)$ with $(0,v)$ span the latter. Therefore this [semidirect product of a Lie algebra and a module](semidirect-product-of-a-lie-algebra-and-a-module.md) is a [perfect Lie algebra](perfect-lie-algebra.md) exactly when $\mathfrak g$ is a [perfect Lie algebra](perfect-lie-algebra.md) and $\mathfrak gV=V$. When $V\ne0$, its nonzero [abelian Lie algebra](abelian-lie-algebra.md) ideal prevents it from being a [semisimple Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra-split.md). The [sl2 Lie algebra](sl2-lie-algebra.md) acting on its two-dimensional [defining representation](defining-representation-of-a-matrix-lie-algebra.md) supplies a five-dimensional example.
