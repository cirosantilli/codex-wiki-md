# Sigma-algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Measure theory](measure-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra)

A sigma-algebra on a [set](set-split.md) $\Omega$ is a collection $\mathcal F$ of [subsets](subset.md) such that $\Omega\in\mathcal F$, $A\in\mathcal F$ implies $\Omega\setminus A\in\mathcal F$, and $A_n\in\mathcal F$ for all $n$ implies $\bigcup_nA_n\in\mathcal F$. Thus it contains the [empty set](empty-set.md) and is closed under countable [intersections](set-intersection.md) as well. This is precisely the closure needed to apply [countable additivity](countable-additivity.md) without leaving the domain of a [measure](measure-split.md).

For $\Omega=\{a,b,c\}$,

$$
\mathcal F=\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{b,c\},\Omega\}
$$

is a [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md). It describes observations that distinguish $a$ from the pair $\{b,c\}$, but cannot distinguish $b$ from $c$. The [power set](power-set.md) provides a finer [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) with all eight [subsets](subset.md). Both are valid; choosing a [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) records which questions the model can answer.

On the [real numbers](real-number.md), the [Borel sigma-algebra](borel-sigma-algebra.md) contains all [open sets](open-set.md) and everything generated from them by these operations. The [Lebesgue measurable sets](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) form a larger [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md), including all [subsets](subset.md) of [Borel null sets](borel-null-set.md). Closure under [countable unions](countable-union.md) accommodates constructions involving an entire [sequence](sequence.md) of observations or approximations.
