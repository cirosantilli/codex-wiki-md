# Uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire

↑ **Parent:** [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md)

In a homogeneous [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md), a steady [electric current density](current-density.md) obeys $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf j=0$. Uniform axial current in a straight cylinder follows if its side is insulated and its flat end faces are equipotentials. The [electric potential](electric-potential.md) then solves [Laplace's equation](laplace-equation.md) with those boundary conditions, and uniqueness makes it linear in the axial coordinate. Steadiness alone does not impose these contact conditions: nonuniform end potentials can produce nonuniform current even with an insulating side. Bending also generally redistributes current; in an annular conductor the azimuthal current density is proportional to the inverse radius.
