# Finite zero-energy vacuum for a single exponential superpotential

↑ **Parent:** [No-scale supergravity](no-scale-supergravity.md)

For $K=-\log(S+\bar S)-3\log(T+\bar T)$ and $W=ae^{-\alpha S}+b$ with $\alpha>0$, the potential is $|b+[1+\alpha(S+\bar S)]ae^{-\alpha S}|^2/[(S+\bar S)(T+\bar T)^3]$. With nonzero $a,b$, a finite zero-energy vacuum exists exactly when $0<|b/a|\le2e^{-1/2}$. To prove this, set $x=\alpha\operatorname{Re}S>0$; the magnitude equation is $|b/a|=(1+2x)e^{-x}$, whose derivative changes sign at $x=1/2$ and whose maximum is $2e^{-1/2}$. The phase fixes the imaginary part of $S$. At a zero-energy solution the $T$ [auxiliary field](auxiliary-field.md) remains nonzero, so [supersymmetry](supersymmetry-split.md) is broken and both real components of $T$ are flat. If $a=b=0$, the family is instead supersymmetric.
