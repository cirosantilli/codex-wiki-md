# Small archimedean value of a large unit

↑ **Parent:** [Dirichlet's unit theorem](dirichlet-s-unit-theorem.md)

Write $u=\zeta\prod\varepsilon_i^{b_i}$ in a fixed fundamental-unit basis. The logarithmic embedding is injective on the exponent lattice and lands in the weighted sum-zero hyperplane. Norm equivalence gives a logarithmic coordinate of size at least a constant times $\|b\|_\infty$; the weighted sum-zero relation then forces a negative coordinate of comparable size. There are only finitely many torsion choices. This exponential smallness, together with $\alpha u+\beta v=1$ and a [linear form in logarithms of algebraic numbers](linear-form-in-logarithms-of-algebraic-numbers.md) for the nearby value $\beta v$, bounds the [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md) exponents effectively.
