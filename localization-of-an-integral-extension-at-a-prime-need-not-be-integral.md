# Localization of an integral extension at a prime need not be integral

↑ **Parent:** [Integral extension](integral-extension.md)

An [integral extension](integral-extension.md) $A\subset B$ need not give an [integral extension](integral-extension.md) $A_{P\cap A}\subset B_P$. For example, take $\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z[i]$ and $P=(2+i)$, whose contraction is $(5)$. The element $1/(2-i)$ belongs to $\mathbb Z[i]_P$ but is not integral over $\mathbb Z_{(5)}$: multiplying a hypothetical monic equation by a power of $2-i$ and reducing modulo the other Gaussian [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) $(2-i)$ gives $1=0$ in $\mathbb F_5$. Localizing both rings by the same [multiplicative subset](multiplicatively-closed-set.md) does preserve integrality.
