# Phase velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Phase velocity and group velocity](phase-velocity-and-group-velocity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_velocity)

The [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) is the motion of a constant-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) surface. For a one-dimensional [plane wave](plane-wave.md) $u=a\cos(kx-\omega t+\phi_0)$ with real $k\ne0$ and real $\omega$, fixing $kx-\omega t+\phi_0$ gives

$$
k\frac{dx}{dt}-\omega=0,\qquad c_p=\frac{\omega}{k}.
$$

Each [wave crest](wave-crest.md) of this monochromatic [travelling wave](travelling-wave.md) advances at that signed [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md). Its magnitude is the [phase speed](phase-speed.md) $|c_p|=\lambda/T$, where $\lambda=2\pi/|k|$ is the [wavelength](wavelength.md) and $T=2\pi/|\omega|$ is the temporal [period](period-of-a-function.md) when $\omega\ne0$. Choosing $\omega>0$ makes positive $k$ mean rightward [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) and negative $k$ mean leftward [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md). Reversing both signs leaves the real [plane wave](plane-wave.md) and its [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) unchanged. At $k=0$ there are no spatial crests to track, so $\omega/k$ is undefined; a finite limit as $k\to0$ is a limiting [phase speed](phase-speed.md), not the speed of a spatially uniform oscillation.

In several dimensions, a [plane wave](plane-wave.md) has [wave phase](phase-waves.md) $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x-\omega t+\phi_0$. Constant-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) planes obey $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v=\omega$. Their normal [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) is

$$
\mathbf c_p=\frac{\omega\mathbf k}{|\mathbf k|^2}\qquad(\mathbf k\ne0).
$$

Tangential motion within a plane does not move that [wavefront](wavefront.md), which is why the normal direction fixes the vector convention. In an [anisotropic](anisotropy.md) medium the [group velocity](group-velocity.md) need not point along this [wavevector](wavevector.md). Likewise the [horizontal phase velocity](horizontal-phase-velocity.md) of a tilted [wavefront](wavefront.md) is its crossing speed along a horizontal line, rather than a component of the normal [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md).

For the [nondispersive wave](nondispersive-wave.md) branch $\omega=ck$, a [wave crest](wave-crest.md) and a [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) both move at $c$. For deep-water [surface gravity waves](surface-gravity-wave.md), $\omega=\sqrt{gk}$ gives $c_p=\sqrt{g/k}$ and [group velocity](group-velocity.md) $c_g=c_p/2$. With the illustrative choices $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $\lambda=1\,\mathrm m$, the model predicts $c_p\simeq1.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $c_g\simeq0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. These are calculated values for negligible [surface tension](surface-tension.md) and sufficiently large $kh$, not measurements. A [wave crest](wave-crest.md) moves forward through the [wave packet](wave-packet.md), so timing a crest does not time the packet's journey.

There is a direct laboratory meaning to this distinction. MIT's 2005 surface-wave lab uses a long water tank, a controllable hydraulic paddle, and an absorbing beach. Resistance probes convert submerged length into voltage; calibration converts their readouts into [free surface](free-surface.md) elevation versus time. Two probes a known distance apart, backed by a ruler and stopwatch, let students time a [wave crest](wave-crest.md) and separately time a feature of the [wave packet](wave-packet.md). The lab records depth and varies [frequency](frequency.md) and [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md).[Surface Waves Kinematics (MIT 2.016 Hydrodynamics) by Massachusetts Institute of Technology, 2.016 course staff](surface-waves-kinematics-mit-2-016-hydrodynamics-by-massachusetts-institute-of-technology-2-016-course-staff.md)

For a clean measurement of [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md), fit the periodic portion of both records or follow one identifiable [wave crest](wave-crest.md). A phase difference is only known modulo $2\pi$: probe spacing below half the shortest relevant [wavelength](wavelength.md), or continuity while sweeping [frequency](frequency.md), avoids assigning the wrong whole-cycle delay. Then compare the timed [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) with $\lambda/T$. Tracking pepper grains instead measures fluid-particle motion, which has a different meaning from either [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) or [group velocity](group-velocity.md).

The [Klein-Gordon packet velocities](klein-gordon-packet-velocities.md) example makes the distinction especially sharp: its [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) exceeds $c$, yet its narrow [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) moves below $c$. A [carrier wave](carrier-wave.md) labels successive oscillations; its speed alone does not establish [information velocity](information-velocity.md). The [front velocity](front-velocity.md) depends on the full causal model, including [angular frequencies](angular-frequency.md) far outside the carrier's narrow range.
