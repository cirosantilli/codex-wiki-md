<h1 id="jordan-chevalley-decomposition">Jordan–Chevalley decomposition</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Jordan normal form](jordan-normal-form.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Chevalley_decomposition)

Over an [algebraically closed field](algebraically-closed-field.md), a [linear operator](linear-operator.md) $X$ has a unique decomposition into a commuting [diagonalisable endomorphism](diagonalizable-matrix.md) $X_s$ and [nilpotent endomorphism](nilpotent-linear-map.md) $X_n$. On its [generalized eigenspace](generalized-eigenspace.md) for $\lambda$, set $X_s=\lambda I$ and $X_n=X-\lambda I$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](chinese-remainder-theorem.md) makes both parts polynomials in $X$, so they preserve every $X$-invariant subspace. Uniqueness follows by restricting any other commuting decomposition to those generalized eigenspaces. Over a perfect field the semisimple part need only become diagonalizable after scalar extension.
