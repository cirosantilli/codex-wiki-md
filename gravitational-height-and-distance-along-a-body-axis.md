# Gravitational height and distance along a body axis

↑ **Parent:** [Potential energy](potential-energy.md)

In uniform [gravity](gravity.md) a body's [gravitational potential energy](gravitational-energy.md) is its mass times [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) times the Cartesian height of its [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md). If that height is represented by an axis distance $h$ and tilt angle $\theta$, it is $h\cos\theta$ and the energy becomes $Mgh\cos\theta$. Treating $h$ simultaneously as a Cartesian height would introduce an extra, unjustified cosine.
