# Ferromagnetism

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](magnetization.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism)

[Ferromagnetism](ferromagnetism.md) is collective magnetic order with [spontaneous magnetization](spontaneous-magnetization.md) below a [Curie temperature](curie-temperature.md). Interactions favor aligned [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md); the [Ising model](ising-model.md) represents a two-orientation version with positive coupling.

A [magnetic domain](magnetic-domain.md) can possess [spontaneous magnetization](spontaneous-magnetization.md) even when other domains cancel its contribution to the whole specimen. Compare this spatial cancellation between domains with [antiferromagnetism](antiferromagnetism.md), where opposing [magnetic sublattices](magnetic-sublattice.md) compensate within the ordered structure, and [ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md), where their contributions are generally unequal. The [magnetic sublattice](magnetic-sublattice.md) diagram makes the three local patterns explicit.

Smoothly sweeping an applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) can reveal abrupt [magnetization](magnetization.md) changes through the [Barkhausen effect](barkhausen-effect.md). A [pickup coil](pickup-coil.md) detects the resulting changes of [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md), making collective [magnetic domain wall](magnetic-domain-wall.md) motion accessible as an electrical [signal](signal.md).
