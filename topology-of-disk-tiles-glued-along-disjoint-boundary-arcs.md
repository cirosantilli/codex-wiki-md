# Topology of disk tiles glued along disjoint boundary arcs

↑ **Parent:** [Surface with boundary](surface-with-boundary.md)

Consider a connected assembly of $n$ [closed discs](closed-disc.md) formed by identifying $e$ pairs of boundary arcs, each arc used once and all arcs in a given tile having disjoint endpoints. Its gluing [graph](graph-split.md) has one vertex per tile and one edge per paired arc. A [spanning tree](spanning-tree.md) joins the initial discs into a single [closed disc](closed-disc.md); each of the remaining $e-n+1$ pairings attaches a band. This is a thickening of the gluing [graph](graph-split.md), so it is [homotopy equivalent](homotopy-inverse.md) to that [graph](graph-split.md). Thus its [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md) is $n-e$ and its [fundamental group](fundamental-group.md) is the [free group](free-group.md) $F_{e-n+1}$. If the gluing [graph](graph-split.md) is a tree, the assembled surface is itself a [closed disc](closed-disc.md).

Suppose each pairing preserves the positive boundary parameter of the reference discs. A global [orientation](orientation-of-a-simplex.md) must then assign opposite orientation signs to adjacent tiles, because their induced [boundary orientations](boundary-orientation.md) on an interior seam must oppose one another. Such signs exist exactly when the gluing [graph](graph-split.md) is [bipartite](bipartite-graph.md). An odd gluing cycle therefore proves that the assembly is a [nonorientable surface](non-orientable-surface.md). A [Dirichlet boundary condition](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) or a [Neumann boundary condition](neumann-boundary-condition.md) on an unpaired arc has no effect on this topological calculation.
