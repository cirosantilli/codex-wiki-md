<h1 id="2/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In a [local inertial frame](../../../../../local-inertial-frame.md) in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md), [gravitational waves](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) travel at $c$ in [general relativity](../../../../../general-relativity-split.md); there is no ordinary material [index of refraction](../../../../../refractive-index.md) analogous to a transparent dielectric. Ordinary matter couples so weakly that laboratory prisms for [gravitational waves](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) are implausible. Propagation through a varying background [gravitational field](../../../../../gravitational-field.md) nevertheless changes ray directions and travel times, giving [gravitational lensing](../../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md) and [Shapiro time delay](../../../../../shapiro-time-delay.md). In weak static coordinates $ds^2\simeq-(1+2\Phi_N/c^2)c^2dt^2+(1-2\Phi_N/c^2)d\mathbf x^2$, the null condition gives an effective coordinate [index of refraction](../../../../../refractive-index.md) $n_{\rm eff}\simeq1-2\Phi_N/c^2$. This useful description depends on coordinates and does not change the local propagation [speed](../../../../../speed.md). Thus astronomical gravitational refraction is plausible, while a substantial everyday material effect is not.
