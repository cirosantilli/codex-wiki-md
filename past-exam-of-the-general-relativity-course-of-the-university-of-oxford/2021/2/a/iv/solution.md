↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Yes, for genuine precession of a nonspherical disc.** Let its unit normal be $\mathbf n=(\sin\alpha\cos\omega t,\sin\alpha\sin\omega t,\cos\alpha)$. Its trace-free [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) has the axisymmetric form

$$
Q_{ij}=Q_0\left(n_i n_j-\frac13\delta_{ij}\right),\qquad Q_0\ne0.
$$

The $xz,yz$ entries vary at $\omega$, and $xx-yy,xy$ at $2\omega$. Their nonzero second and third [time derivatives](../../../../../time-derivative.md) give an oscillating far-field [metric perturbation](../../../../../linearized-gravity-split.md) and positive average radiated [power](../../../../../power.md). For $\alpha=\pi/2$ the $\omega$ terms vanish but the $2\omega$ terms remain. The precise condition is $\omega\ne0$ and $\sin\alpha\ne0$: the literal inequality $\alpha\ne0$ also allows $\alpha=\pi$, where the normal is fixed and no such [gravitational radiation](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) occurs. For a uniform thin disc of [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $M_d$ and radius $b$, $Q_0=-M_db^2/4$, confirming that it is not zero.
