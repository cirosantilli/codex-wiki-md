<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**No, for steady rotation of the uniform sphere.** Its [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) is zero by [spherical symmetry](../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md); its rotating [mass current](../../../../../mass-current.md) is stationary when the rotation axis and rate are fixed. A stationary source does not emit [gravitational waves](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md). Rotation by itself is insufficient: a time-dependent nonaxisymmetric distribution, or another changing radiative [multipole moment](../../../../../multipole-moment.md), is required. The conclusion concerns a sphere with the stated constant density and steady motion, rather than a deformed or time-varying rotator.
