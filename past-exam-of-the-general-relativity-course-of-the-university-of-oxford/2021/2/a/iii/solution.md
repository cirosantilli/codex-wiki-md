<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**No, for a uniform or otherwise axisymmetric disc in steady rotation.** Although individual material elements accelerate, the [mass density](../../../../../density.md) and [mass current](../../../../../mass-current.md) at every spatial point remain constant, and the [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) is time independent. There is no [gravitational radiation](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md). Axisymmetry of the material distribution is necessary for this conclusion: the source does not explicitly specify the disc's density. A counterexample to an unconditional answer is a disc with two equal dense spots at opposite radii; their rotating [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) produces [gravitational waves](../../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) at twice the rotation [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md).
