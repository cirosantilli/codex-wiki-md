<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On large scales the appropriate background is the spatially flat [FLRW metric](../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md),

$$
\boxed{ds^2=-c^2dt^2+R^2(t)\left[dr^2+r^2d\theta^2+r^2\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right].}
$$

Here $t$ is [cosmic time](../../../../cosmic-time.md), the [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) of a [comoving observer](../../../../comoving-observer.md); $R(t)$ is the [scale factor](../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md); and $(r,\theta,\phi)$ are dimensionless [comoving coordinates](../../../../comoving-coordinate.md). With this convention $R$ has units of [length](../../../../length.md). A fixed comoving radial interval has instantaneous [proper length](../../../../proper-length.md) $R(t)\,dr$. The [cosmological principle](../../../../cosmological-principle.md) assumes [spatial homogeneity](../../../../spatial-homogeneity.md) and [cosmological isotropy](../../../../cosmological-isotropy.md) on the scales where this background description applies.

The general [FLRW metric](../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) instead has $dr^2/(1-kr^2)$ in its radial part, with normalized $k=+1,0,-1$ describing positive, zero or negative [spatial curvature of an FLRW universe](../../../../spatial-curvature-of-an-flrw-universe.md). The best simple description takes $k=0$; observations constrain spatial [curvature](../../../../curvature.md) to be small rather than proving it identically zero. The late accelerated expansion is encoded by the time dependence of $R$, determined by the [stress-energy tensor](../../../../stress-energy-tensor-split.md) through the [Friedmann equations](../../../../friedmann-equations.md), rather than by an additional term in the [FLRW metric](../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md). Small-scale galaxies and other inhomogeneities require perturbations of this background.
