<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Approximate Earth by an isolated, nonrotating, spherically symmetric body. By [Birkhoff's theorem](../../../../birkhoff-s-theorem.md), the exterior [vacuum](../../../../vacuum.md) [metric tensor](../../../../metric-tensor.md) is the [Schwarzschild metric](../../../../schwarzschild-spacetime-split.md). With $x^0=ct$ it is

$$
\boxed{ds^2=-f(r)c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2),\qquad
f(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}.}
$$

Here $r$ is the [areal radius](../../../../areal-radius.md), $\theta$ and $\phi$ are spherical angles, $t$ is [Schwarzschild time](../../../../schwarzschild-time.md) normalized to the [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) of a static observer at infinity, $M$ is Earth's [mass](../../../../mass.md), $G$ the [gravitational constant](../../../../gravitational-constant.md), and $c$ the [speed of light](../../../../speed-of-light.md). The nonzero diagonal [metric tensor](../../../../metric-tensor.md) components are $g_{00}=-f$, $g_{rr}=f^{-1}$, $g_{\theta\theta}=r^2$, $g_{\phi\phi}=r^2\sin^2\theta$.

The apple is a [test particle](../../../../test-particle.md); neglect its effect on the [metric tensor](../../../../metric-tensor.md), air resistance, Earth's rotation and nonspherical structure, and external [tidal forces](../../../../tidal-force.md). Near Earth's surface, $GM/(R_Ec^2)\simeq7\times10^{-10}$, $h/R_E\ll1$, and the falling [speed](../../../../speed.md) remains much smaller than $c$. Thus the [Newtonian limit](../../../../newtonian-limit.md) of this [metric tensor](../../../../metric-tensor.md) is appropriate, while the exact radial [geodesic](../../../../geodesic.md) is available as a check. A static observer under the tree has [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) $T=\sqrt{f(R_E)}\,t$, up to an additive constant. Such an observer is supported by the ground and has nonzero [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md); the released apple instead has zero [proper acceleration](../../../../proper-acceleration.md).
