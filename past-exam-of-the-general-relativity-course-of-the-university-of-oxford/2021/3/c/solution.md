<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a [timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic](../../../../timelike-circular-schwarzschild-geodesic.md), the coordinate [orbital period](../../../../orbital-period.md) and onboard [proper time](../../../../proper-time.md) per orbit are

$$
T_\infty=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}},\qquad
T_{\rm crew}=\int_0^{T_\infty}\frac{dt}{u^0}
=T_\infty\sqrt{1-\frac{3GM}{Rc^2}}.
$$

Using $G=6.67430\times10^{-11}\,{\rm m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ and $c=299792458\,{\rm m\,s^{-1}}$, the [Schwarzschild radius](../../../../schwarzschild-radius.md) and orbital [areal radius](../../../../areal-radius.md) are $R_s=2GM/c^2\simeq1.21789\times10^{10}\,\mathrm m$ and $R=200R_s\simeq2.43578\times10^{12}\,\mathrm m$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
T_\infty&\simeq1.02100\times10^6\,\mathrm s=11.8172\,\mathrm{days},\\
T_{\rm crew}&=T_\infty\sqrt{\frac{397}{400}}
\simeq1.01717\times10^6\,\mathrm s=11.7728\,\mathrm{days}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The crew accumulates about $3836$ fewer seconds, or $63.9$ fewer minutes, per orbit. Both [gravitational time dilation](../../../../gravitational-time-dilation.md) and kinematic [time dilation](../../../../time-dilation.md) contribute: a static clock at $R$ has $dT_{\rm static}=\sqrt{1-R_s/R}\,dt$, while the orbiting clock additionally has $d\tau=dT_{\rm static}/\gamma$, where $\gamma=\sqrt{398/397}$. A distant observer measures the coordinate period; comparing arrivals emitted at the same orbital phase also removes the repeated light propagation delay.
