<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the [thin-lens approximation for gravitational lensing](../../../../thin-lens-approximation-for-gravitational-lensing.md), consider a ray crossing the lens plane at transverse distance $b$ above the aligned source-observer axis. With the observer at axial position $0$, the [black hole](../../../../black-hole-split.md) at $L$ and the source at $D$, the segment joining the lens plane to the source has slope $-b/(D-L)$, while the segment joining the observer to the lens plane has slope $b/L$. The observer sees the ray at apparent angle $\theta\simeq b/L$ to the axis. When following the [photon](../../../../photon.md) toward the observer, the two small angles to the axis have magnitudes $b/(D-L)$ and $b/L$. The physical bend is their sum:

$$
\delta\simeq\frac bL+\frac b{D-L}
=\frac{bD}{L(D-L)}.
$$

Substitute the [Schwarzschild light deflection](../../../../schwarzschild-light-deflection.md), $\delta\simeq4GM/(bc^2)$, and use $b\simeq L\theta$:

$$
b^2\simeq\frac{4GM}{c^2}\frac{L(D-L)}D,
\qquad
\boxed{2\theta\simeq2\sqrt{\frac{4GM}{c^2}\frac{D-L}{LD}}.}
$$

Every azimuth around the axis is equivalent by rotational symmetry. Isotropic emission therefore supplies rays at all these azimuths, and the ideal image is an [Einstein ring](../../../../einstein-ring.md) centered on the [black hole](../../../../black-hole-split.md), with [Einstein angular radius](../../../../einstein-angular-radius.md) $\theta$ and angular diameter $2\theta$. A finite source size or imperfect alignment broadens or breaks the ideal ring.

The stated expression uses small angles, distant endpoints and [weak-field approximation](../../../../weak-field-approximation.md) $b\gg2GM/c^2$, with ordinary Euclidean distances. For cosmological [gravitational lensing](../../../../gravitational-lensing-split.md), use [angular diameter distances](../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) $D_l,D_s,D_{ls}$ in $\theta^2=4GM D_{ls}/(c^2D_lD_s)$; generally $D_{ls}\ne D_s-D_l$.
