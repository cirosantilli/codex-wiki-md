<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

There are two first-order effects: an intrinsic [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) change from [adiabatic compression](../../../../adiabatic-compression.md), and the [gravitational redshift](../../../../gravitational-redshift.md) of the emerging [photons](../../../../photon.md). Evaluate the excess [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) at the boundary of the spherical fluctuation, with the exterior reference potential subtracted:

$$
\delta\Phi=-\frac{G\delta M}{r_0}.
$$

In the [weak-field approximation](../../../../weak-field-approximation.md), [gravitational time dilation](../../../../gravitational-time-dilation.md) gives $d\tau_{\rm local}/dt\simeq1+\delta\Phi/c^2$. A local emitter's successive wave crests therefore arrive farther apart at a reference observer outside the potential well. Their [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) changes by $\delta\nu/\nu=\delta\Phi/c^2<0$. Since a redshifted [black-body radiation](../../../../black-body-radiation.md) spectrum has its [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) scaled by the same factor as its [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), the gravitational contribution is

$$
\left(\frac{\Delta T}{T}\right)_{\rm grav}
=\frac{\delta\Phi}{c^2}=-\frac{G\delta M}{r_0c^2}.
$$

For the intrinsic effect, compare the local [scale factor](../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) $R+\Delta R$ with the unperturbed value $R$ at the reference epoch. An equilibrium [photon gas](../../../../photon-gas.md) has [energy density](../../../../energy-density.md) $u=a_{\rm rad}T^4$ and [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) $p=u/3$. For [adiabatic compression](../../../../adiabatic-compression.md), the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) applied to a comoving volume $V\propto R^3$ gives

$$
d(uV)=-p\,dV
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4\frac{dT}{T}=-\frac43\frac{dV}{V}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dT}{T}=-\frac{dR}{R}.
$$

Thus $TR$ is constant along this [adiabatic process](../../../../adiabatic-process.md), and the initial intrinsic contribution is $\Delta T/T=-\Delta R/R$. The smaller local expansion associated with the fluctuation leaves its radiation hotter; this is the compression or local expansion-history contribution, distinct from the clock-rate effect on the escaping radiation.

Multiplying the two first-order factors, and cancelling the common subsequent background redshift, gives

$$
\frac{T_{\rm obs}}{\bar T_{\rm obs}}
\simeq\left(1-\frac{\Delta R}{R}\right)
\left(1+\frac{\delta\Phi}{c^2}\right),
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\Delta T}{T}=-\frac{G\delta M}{r_0c^2}-\frac{\Delta R}{R}.}
$$

For the overdense region produced by [adiabatic compression](../../../../adiabatic-compression.md), $\Delta R<0$, so **the final term is positive**. It competes with the negative [gravitational redshift](../../../../gravitational-redshift.md) term. Their sum is the intrinsic-plus-endpoint part of the [Sachs-Wolfe combination](../../../../sachs-wolfe-combination.md); computing a full observed [Cosmic microwave background anisotropy](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy-split.md) can also require [Doppler CMB anisotropy](../../../../doppler-cmb-anisotropy.md) and the [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../../../../integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect.md).[Sections 3.4 and 3.4.1, equations (3.210)–(3.212): adiabatic temperature perturbations and gravitational redshift contributions](../../../../cosmology-by-david-tong.md)
