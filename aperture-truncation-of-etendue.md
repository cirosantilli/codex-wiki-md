# Aperture truncation of etendue

↑ **Parent:** [Etendue](etendue.md)

Insert a centred opaque circular stop of [diameter](diameter.md) $d=6\,\mathrm{mm}$ directly in the plane of the $D=10\,\mathrm{mm}$ bundle. It transmits the same $30^\circ$ directions at every surviving position, but discards the surrounding annulus. Therefore

$$
\frac{G_{\rm transmitted}}{G_{\rm incident}}=\frac{d^2}{D^2}=0.36,
\qquad
G_{\rm transmitted}=\frac{9\pi^2}{4}\,\mathrm{mm^2\,sr}
\approx22.207\,\mathrm{mm^2\,sr}.
$$

With the uniform [radiance](radiance.md) of [etendue of a circular aperture and cone](etendue-of-a-circular-aperture-and-cone.md), the transmitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) is $2.2207\,\mathrm{mW}$ and the blocked fraction is $64\%$. Surviving [light rays](light-ray.md) retain their [radiance](radiance.md); the stop has not concentrated them. Lossless propagation after the stop preserves the [etendue](etendue.md) of this surviving subset, not that of the original incident bundle. A stop placed elsewhere can select positions and directions together, so its physical [area](surface-area.md) ratio alone need not give the transmitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) fraction.
