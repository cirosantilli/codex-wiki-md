# Geometric progression

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence](sequence.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_progression)

A geometric progression is a [sequence](sequence.md) obtained by repeatedly multiplying by one fixed [common ratio](common-ratio.md) $r$:

$$
a_0=a,\qquad a_{n+1}=ra_n,\qquad a_n=ar^n\quad(n\ge0).
$$

Here $a$ and $r$ may be [real numbers](real-number.md) or [complex numbers](complex-number.md). The recurrence is meaningful even when $a=0$ or $r=0$; expressing $r$ as a [ratio](ratio.md) of successive terms requires a nonzero denominator. As a polynomial convention, $r^0=1$, including at $r=0$.

For example, quantities $100,80,64,51.2,\ldots$ retain four fifths of their preceding value at every step. The decrement changes, but the retained fraction does not. With the same initial value and [common ratio](common-ratio.md) $2$, the terms are $100,200,400,800,\ldots$. This multiplicative structure is useful whenever the same proportional rule repeats: it turns many updates into the single [exponentiation](exponentiation.md) $r^n$. An [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md) instead repeats an additive increment.

For nonzero $a$, the [modulus](modulus.md) of $a_n$ tends to zero when $|r|<1$, stays fixed when $|r|=1$, and grows without bound when $|r|>1$. For a negative real [common ratio](common-ratio.md), successive nonzero terms also alternate in sign. Thus the terms $1,-1/2,1/4,-1/8,\ldots$ decay in size while crossing zero at every step. Adding the terms gives a [geometric series](geometric-series.md); the individual terms and their accumulated [partial sums](partial-sum.md) are different [sequences](sequence.md).
