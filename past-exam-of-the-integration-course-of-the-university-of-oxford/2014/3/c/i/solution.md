<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Fix $0<x<1$. On the open [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $(0,x)$, the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $y\mapsto(x-y)^{\beta-1}$ is finite, positive and continuous, and $f$ is nonnegative and [Lebesgue measurable](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md). Their product is therefore a nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md). Its [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md) is always defined as an element of $[0,\infty]$, with no possibility of cancellation of two infinities. Thus **$g(x)$ is well defined as a nonnegative extended integral**.

The endpoint $y=x$ need not receive its formal power value: for $\beta<1$ that value would be singular. The integral is over $(0,x)$, and any chosen value at the endpoints has no effect because endpoints form a [null set](../../../../../null-set.md).

Finiteness is not implied by the present assumptions. For example, $f(y)=1/y$ is an admissible nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md). For any fixed $x$, the factor $(x-y)^{\beta-1}$ is bounded below by a positive constant on $0<y<x/2$, so $g(x)\geq C_x\int_0^{x/2}dy/y=\infty$. The later [L1 norm](../../../../../l1-norm.md) bound will establish finiteness [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) when $f$ is integrable. The construction is a [Volterra power-kernel integral](../../../../../volterra-power-kernel-integral.md).
