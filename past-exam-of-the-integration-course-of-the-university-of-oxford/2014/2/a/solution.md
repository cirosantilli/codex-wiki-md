<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) states: on a [measure space](../../../../measure-space.md) $(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$, let $u_n$ be [measurable functions](../../../../measurable-function.md) converging [almost everywhere](../../../../almost-everywhere.md) to a measurable $u$. Suppose there is a nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../measurable-function.md) $G$ with $\int_XG\,d\mu<\infty$ and $|u_n|\leq G$ [almost everywhere](../../../../almost-everywhere.md) for every $n$. Then $u$ is integrable and

$$
\boxed{\int_X|u_n-u|\,d\mu\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\int_Xu_n\,d\mu\longrightarrow\int_Xu\,d\mu.}
$$

Here integrability means integrability with respect to the given [measure](../../../../measure-split.md); for [Lebesgue measure](../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) it is [Lebesgue integrability](../../../../integrability.md). Neither a [finite measure](../../../../finite-measure.md) space nor monotonicity of the sequence is required. The same single integrable dominator must control the whole sequence.
