The [monotone convergence theorem](../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) states: on a [measure space](../../../../measure-space.md) $(X,\mathcal F,\mu)$, let $u_n:X\to[0,\infty]$ be [measurable functions](../../../../measurable-function.md) with

$$
0\leq u_1(x)\leq u_2(x)\leq\cdots,\qquad u(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_n(x).
$$

Then $u$ is measurable and

$$
\boxed{\int_Xu\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_Xu_n\,d\mu.}
$$

The [integrals](../../../../integral-split.md) and their [limit](../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) may be infinite. No integrable dominator, [finite measure](../../../../finite-measure.md) hypothesis, or [sigma-finiteness](../../../../sigma-finite-measure.md) is required. The nonnegativity and increasing order are the essential hypotheses in this form of the theorem.
