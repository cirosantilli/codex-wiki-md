<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

By part [i](../i.md), $\chi_{E_n}|g|\to0$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), while $0\leq\chi_{E_n}|g|\leq|g|$ and $g$ is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). The [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\int_{E_n}|g(x)|\,dx\longrightarrow0.}
$$

To deduce [absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral](../../../../../absolute-continuity-of-the-lebesgue-integral.md), suppose the stated uniform conclusion failed for some $\varepsilon_0>0$. For each $n$ one could choose a [measurable set](../../../../../measurable-set.md) $A_n$ with $m(A_n)<2^{-n}$ but $\int_{A_n}|g|\geq\varepsilon_0$. Applying the result just proved to this [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) contradicts the lower bound. Hence

$$
\boxed{\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0:\quad m(A)<\delta\Longrightarrow\int_A|g|<\varepsilon}
$$

for every [measurable set](../../../../../measurable-set.md) $A$.
