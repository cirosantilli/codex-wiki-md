<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The absolute value is bounded by $e^{-ax}$, whose integral is $1/a$. Thus the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). The complex integral of an [exponential function](../../../../../exponential-function.md) is

$$
\int_0^\infty e^{(-a+ib)x}\,dx=\left[\frac{e^{(-a+ib)x}}{-a+ib}\right]_0^\infty=\frac1{a-ib}=\frac{a+ib}{a^2+b^2}.
$$

Taking the [real part](../../../../../real-part.md), justified by [absolute integrability](../../../../../integrability.md), gives

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty e^{-ax}\cos(bx)\,dx=\frac a{a^2+b^2}.}
$$
