<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

On [sigma-finite measure](../../../../../sigma-finite-measure.md) spaces, including the real line with [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md), [Tonelli's theorem](../../../../../tonelli-theorem.md) says that a nonnegative measurable function $H$ has equal product and iterated integrals:

$$
\int_{X\times Y}H\,d(\mu\times\nu)
=\int_X\left(\int_YH(x,y)\,d\nu(y)\right)d\mu(x)
=\int_Y\left(\int_XH(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\right)d\nu(y),
$$

with infinity allowed. Finiteness is not a prerequisite.

[Fubini's theorem](../../../../../fubini-s-theorem.md) says that if a measurable signed or complex $H$ is absolutely integrable on the product, then its sections are integrable for [almost every](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) value of the other variable, the iterated integral functions are integrable, and the same equalities hold with finite values. For completed Lebesgue measure, exceptional sections need not themselves be measurable; the integrals use the almost-everywhere defined sections. Thus Tonelli can first establish absolute integrability using $|H|$, after which Fubini permits changing the order for $H$.
