<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\phi:I\to J$ be a $C^1$ [diffeomorphism](../../../../../diffeomorphism.md) between [intervals](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md), which may be unbounded. For a nonnegative [Lebesgue measurable function](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-function.md) $f$ on $J$, [Lebesgue substitution on an interval](../../../../../lebesgue-substitution-on-an-interval.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\int_J f(y)\,dy=\int_I f(\phi(x))|\phi'(x)|\,dx,}
$$

allowing the value infinity. The same identity applied to $|f|$ shows that $f$ is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md) exactly when the transformed integrand is integrable; in that case the displayed formula holds for signed or complex $f$ as well. The absolute value on the [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) makes the formula valid for both increasing and decreasing substitutions. Finite endpoints can be omitted without changing a [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md), because individual points are [null sets](../../../../../null-set.md).
