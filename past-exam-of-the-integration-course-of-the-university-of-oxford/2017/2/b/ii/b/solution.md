<h1 id="2/b/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $A(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n(x)$ on the full-measure set where part (A) gives absolute convergence, and set $A=0$ on its null complement. After similarly replacing the individual terms by zero on that common [null set](../../../../../../null-set.md), the partial sums are finite measurable functions and converge to $A$. Hence $A$ is a [Lebesgue measurable function](../../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-function.md). The [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
|A(x)|\leq\sum_{n\geq1}|a_n(x)|=S(x)
$$

almost everywhere. Part (A) proved $S$ integrable, so

$$
\boxed{\int_E|A|\leq\sum_{n\geq1}\int_E|a_n|<\infty.}
$$

Therefore the summed function is [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). Its values on the exceptional null set do not affect the integral. This is the integrability step in [integration of a series with summable L1 norms](../../../../../../integration-of-a-series-with-summable-l1-norms.md).
