<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If the [intervals](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $J_k$ cover $A$, their translates $J_k+a$ cover $A+a$ and have the same lengths. Every candidate total length in the definition of $m^*(A)$ is therefore also a candidate for $m^*(A+a)$. Taking [infima](../../../../../infimum.md) gives $m^*(A+a)\leq m^*(A)$. Apply the same argument to $A+a$ and the translation $-a$ to obtain the reverse inequality. Hence

$$
\boxed{m^*(A+a)=m^*(A).}
$$

This [translation invariance of Lebesgue outer measure](../../../../../translation-invariance-of-lebesgue-outer-measure.md) holds for every [subset](../../../../../subset.md) $A$, including non-measurable sets and sets of infinite outer measure.
