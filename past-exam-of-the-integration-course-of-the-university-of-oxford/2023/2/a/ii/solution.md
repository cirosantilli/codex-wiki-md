<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Again the [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is continuous on its domain and therefore a [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md). For $0<x<1$, $|(\log x)e^{-x}|\leq-\log x$, and

$$
\int_0^1-\log x\,dx=1.
$$

This standard [improper integral](../../../../../improper-integral.md) follows from the [antiderivative](../../../../../antiderivative.md) $x-x\log x$ and $x\log x\to0$ at zero. For $x\geq1$, $0\leq\log x\leq x$, and [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives $\int_1^\infty xe^{-x}\,dx=2/e$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty|(\log x)e^{-x}|\,dx\leq1+\frac2e<\infty.}
$$

The logarithmic singularity is integrable and the exponential factor controls the tail, so this too is a [Lebesgue integrable function](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md).
