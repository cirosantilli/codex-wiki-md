<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [function](../../../../../function-split.md) is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, hence a [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md). On $(0,1)$ its absolute value is at most one, while on $[1,\infty)$ the elementary estimate $|\sin u|\leq|u|$ gives $|\sin(1/x^3)|\leq x^{-3}$. By comparison for the [Lebesgue integral](../../../../../lebesgue-integral.md),

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty|\sin(1/x^3)|\,dx\leq1+\int_1^\infty x^{-3}\,dx=\frac32<\infty.}
$$

It is therefore a [Lebesgue integrable function](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). The oscillations near zero do not obstruct absolute integrability because their amplitude stays bounded on an interval of finite measure.
