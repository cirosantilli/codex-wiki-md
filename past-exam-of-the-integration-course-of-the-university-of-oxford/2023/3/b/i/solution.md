<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**False.** A [typewriter sequence](../../../../../typewriter-sequence.md) sweeps successively through finer dyadic partitions. For $k\geq0$ and $j=0,\ldots,2^k-1$, define the [indicator function](../../../../../indicator-function.md)

$$
f_{2^k+j}(x)=\mathbf1_{[j2^{-k},(j+1)2^{-k})}(x),\qquad x\in(0,1).
$$

Its [L2 norm](../../../../../l2-norm.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\|f_{2^k+j}\|_2^2=2^{-k}\longrightarrow0.}
$$

As $n$ tends to infinity so does its block index $k$, proving [norm convergence](../../../../../norm-convergence.md) to zero. However, every $x\in(0,1)$ belongs to exactly one of the half-open intervals in each block, so $f_n(x)=1$ infinitely often. For $k\geq1$ there is also an interval in each block that does not contain $x$, so $f_n(x)=0$ infinitely often. Thus there is no [pointwise convergence](../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md) at any $x\in(0,1)$, let alone convergence [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md).
