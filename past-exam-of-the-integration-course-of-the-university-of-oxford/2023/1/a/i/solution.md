↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Writing $\mathcal L$ for the collection of [Lebesgue measurable sets](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md), saying that $\mathcal L$ is a [sigma-algebra](../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) means that $\mathbb R\in\mathcal L$, that $\mathbb R\setminus A\in\mathcal L$ whenever $A\in\mathcal L$, and that $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\in\mathcal L$ whenever every $A_n\in\mathcal L$. These properties also imply closure under countable intersections and differences.

A real-valued [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $f$ is a Lebesgue [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md) precisely when the [inverse image](../../../../../preimage.md) of every [Borel set](../../../../../borel-set.md) is in $\mathcal L$. Equivalently, it suffices to test the half-lines:

$$
\boxed{\{x:f(x)>a\}\in\mathcal L\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The equivalence follows because these half-lines generate the [Borel sigma-algebra](../../../../../borel-sigma-algebra.md), and [inverse images](../../../../../preimage.md) preserve complements and countable unions.
