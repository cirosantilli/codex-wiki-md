<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** Suppose instead that $B=A_1\times A_2$ were a [Lebesgue measurable set](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md) in the plane. The section consequence of [Fubini's theorem](../../../../../fubini-s-theorem.md) says that there is a [Lebesgue null set](../../../../../lebesgue-null-set.md) $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ such that

$$
B_x=\{y:(x,y)\in B\}
$$

is a [Lebesgue measurable set](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md) for every $x\notin N$. This version applies to completed [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md); it can be obtained on bounded rectangles and then extended by a countable exhaustion, so no finite-measure assumption on $B$ is needed.

For every $x\in A_1$ the section is exactly $A_2$, which is not measurable. Thus $A_1\subseteq N$. By [completeness of Lebesgue measure](../../../../../completeness-of-lebesgue-measure.md), every subset of $N$ is measurable, contradicting the hypothesis on $A_1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{A_1\times A_2\text{ is not Lebesgue measurable}.}
$$
