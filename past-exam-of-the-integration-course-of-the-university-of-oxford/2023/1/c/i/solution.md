↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**False.** Take $E=\{0\}$ and any [non-measurable set](../../../../../non-measurable-set.md) $A\subseteq\mathbb R$. The vertical line $\{0\}\times\mathbb R$ is a planar [Lebesgue null set](../../../../../lebesgue-null-set.md): each segment $\{0\}\times[-N,N]$ can be enclosed in rectangles of arbitrarily small area, and the line is a countable union of these segments. Consequently its [subset](../../../../../subset.md) $E\times A$ is a [Lebesgue measurable set](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-set.md) of planar measure zero, by [completeness of Lebesgue measure](../../../../../completeness-of-lebesgue-measure.md).

A nonempty factor can therefore have too little [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) to transmit the other factor's failure of measurability. Its exceptional vertical section at $x=0$ is consistent with [Lebesgue measurable sections](../../../../../lebesgue-measurable-sections.md) being guaranteed only [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md).
