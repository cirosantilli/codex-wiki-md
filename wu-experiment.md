# Wu experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Parity violation](parity-violation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_experiment)

The [Wu experiment](wu-experiment.md) was performed by [Chien-Shiung Wu](chien-shiung-wu.md), [Ernest Ambler](ernest-ambler.md), [Raymond Webster Hayward](raymond-webster-hayward.md), [Dale D. Hoppes](dale-d-hoppes.md), and [Ralph Percy Hudson](ralph-percy-hudson.md); its 1957 report was Experimental Test of Parity Conservation in Beta Decay. Their experiment supplied the evidence that the [Lee-Yang proposal for a parity test](lee-yang-proposal-for-a-parity-test.md) had called for.[Experimentalists: the five named biographies; theorists: Lee and Yang](the-fall-of-parity-photo-gallery-with-short-biographies-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)

A [cobalt-60](cobalt-60.md) surface layer on a [cerium magnesium nitrate](cerium-magnesium-nitrate.md) [crystal](crystal.md) was cooled by [adiabatic demagnetization](adiabatic-demagnetization.md); a solenoid's [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) polarized its [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md). An internal [anthracene](anthracene.md) counter detected [electrons](electron.md) by [particle scintillation](scintillation-physics.md); a light pipe carried flashes to a [photomultiplier](photomultiplier.md). Polar and equatorial [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) counters monitored [nuclear polarization](nuclear-polarization.md) and effective [temperature](temperature-split.md); bulk thermometry could miss surface heating.[p. 1413, Fig. 1 and apparatus paragraphs; p. 1414, demagnetization followed by vertical solenoid](experimental-test-of-parity-conservation-in-beta-decay-by-chien-shiung-wu-and-others.md)

Reversing [nuclear polarization](nuclear-polarization.md) reversed the [electron](electron.md) excess; warming erased it with [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) anisotropy. Warm rates were field-independent. Cooling-field reversal checked remanence; poorly polarized sources checked magnetic deflection. [Electrons](electron.md) favored the direction opposite [spin](spin.md).[p. 1414, Fig. 2; warm rates, cooling-field reversal, sign, and two control-source preparations](experimental-test-of-parity-conservation-in-beta-decay-by-chien-shiung-wu-and-others.md)

**[Figure 1](#image-what-the-apparatus-measures-and-what-spatial-inversion-changes). What the apparatus measures, and what spatial inversion changes**. Functional layout, with paths separated for readability. The right-hand construction inverts every spatial coordinate: a polar momentum reverses while an axial spin remains fixed. It is a geometric argument, not a second historical apparatus.

The [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) anisotropy diagnoses the preparation; the [electron](electron.md) asymmetry diagnoses [parity violation](parity-violation.md). These are different jobs. A directionally uneven [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) signal alone need not violate [parity conservation](parity-conservation.md): a distribution even in the cosine of the angle can distinguish an axis from its perpendicular without distinguishing its two ends. The [spin](spin.md)-[momentum](momentum-split.md) correlation instead distinguishes those ends.

The inference requires control of changes in [detection efficiency](detection-efficiency.md), [electron](electron.md) transport and [nuclear polarization](nuclear-polarization.md). A fixed detector under [spin](spin.md) reversal avoids physically exchanging detector positions, but still requires checks for [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) effects. The result establishes [parity violation](parity-violation.md) in this [weak interaction](weak-interaction.md) process. A precise microscopic coupling requires more information than the existence or sign of a counting asymmetry; the following model makes that distinction explicit.
