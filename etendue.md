# Etendue

↑ **Parent:** [Geometrical optics](geometrical-optics.md)

[Etendue](etendue.md) measures how much position-and-direction space a bundle of [light rays](light-ray.md) occupies. A small spot accepting a wide range of directions can have the same [etendue](etendue.md) as a large spot accepting a narrow range. This makes [etendue](etendue.md) useful for deciding whether an [optical instrument](optical-instrument.md) can deliver an extended source's entire admitted [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) to a small receiver. For forward crossings of a surface, the [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics.md) definition is

$$
G=\int_{\mathcal B}n^2\cos\theta\,dA\,d\Omega,
\qquad 0\leq\theta<\frac{\pi}{2},
$$

where $\mathcal B$ is the occupied set of surface positions and directions, $n$ is the local [refractive index](refractive-index.md), $dA$ is actual surface [area](surface-area.md), $d\Omega$ is [solid angle](solid-angle.md), and $\theta$ is the [angle](angle.md) to the forward [unit normal](unit-normal.md). The [cosine](cosine.md) supplies the [projected area](projected-area.md). Opposite crossing directions must be counted separately with positive [projected area](projected-area.md), rather than cancelling them in a signed [integral](integral.md). [Étendue, p. 1, equation (1.1): refractive-index-weighted projected-area integral](etendue-photon-snacks-by-r-john-koshel.md)

The shorthand $G\simeq n^2A\Omega$ assumes constant [refractive index](refractive-index.md), a narrow nearly normal bundle, and the same admitted directions across the whole [area](surface-area.md) $A$. At larger [angles](angle.md), use the [cosine](cosine.md)-weighted [integral](integral.md); a position-dependent angular acceptance cannot generally be replaced by the product of two independent extents.
