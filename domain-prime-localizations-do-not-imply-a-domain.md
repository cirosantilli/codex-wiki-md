# Domain prime localizations do not imply a domain

↑ **Parent:** [Localization at a prime ideal](localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md)

The [ring](ring.md) $k\times k$, for a [field](field.md) $k$, has two [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md), $0\times k$ and $k\times0$. Their [localizations at a prime ideal](localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) are both isomorphic to $k$. Nevertheless $(1,0)(0,1)=0$ is a product of nonzero elements, so $A$ is not an [integral domain](integral-domain.md). Nonzero orthogonal [idempotents](idempotent.md) separate the two components of its [spectrum of a commutative ring](spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md).
