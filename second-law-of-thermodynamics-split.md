# Second law of thermodynamics

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_law_of_thermodynamics)

For an [isolated system](isolated-system.md), the second law requires $\Delta S\ge0$. More generally, include all affected surroundings to obtain

$$
\Delta S_{\rm tot}=\Delta S_{\rm system}+\Delta S_{\rm surroundings}\ge0.
$$

Equality describes the reversible limit; irreversible macroscopic processes produce [entropy](entropy-split.md). A [closed system](closed-system.md) can lose [entropy](entropy-split.md) by exporting [heat](heat-split.md), so nondecrease does not apply to that system alone. The [Clausius statement of the second law](clausius-statement-of-the-second-law.md) forbids a cyclic device whose sole effect is transferring [heat](heat-split.md) from cold to hot. The [Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law](kelvin-planck-statement-of-the-second-law.md) forbids a cyclic device whose sole effect is converting [heat](heat-split.md) from one [thermal reservoir](heat-bath.md) completely into [work](work.md). "Sole effect" matters: a [refrigerator](refrigerator.md) moves [heat](heat-split.md) from cold to hot by consuming [work](work.md). [5.1 Concept and Statements of the Second Law (Why do we need a second law?)](thermodynamics-and-propulsion/5-1-concept-and-statements-of-the-second-law-why-do-we-need-a-second-law.md)

For direct transfer of $Q=1000\,\mathrm J$ from a [thermal reservoir](heat-bath.md) at $600\,\mathrm K$ to one at $300\,\mathrm K$, with no [work](work.md) extracted,

$$
\Delta S_{\rm tot}=-\frac{1000}{600}+\frac{1000}{300}
=\frac53\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}>0.
$$

The reverse transfer alone would have negative total [entropy](entropy-split.md) change. Both directions conserve [energy](energy-split.md), but only the hot-to-cold direction is spontaneous. The finite-body example under [entropy](entropy-split.md) replaces these constant [temperatures](temperature-split.md) by changing ones.

[Friction](friction.md), [free expansion](free-expansion.md) and [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) across a finite [temperature](temperature-split.md) difference all introduce irreversibility. Moving arbitrarily slowly is insufficient if [friction](friction.md) remains. A [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md) must permit restoration of both the system and surroundings. This gives engineers a useful ideal benchmark: irreversibility spends an opportunity to obtain [work](work.md), even though [energy](energy-split.md) itself is conserved. [MIT, Irreversibility and lost opportunities for work](https://www.ocw.mit.edu/ans7870/16/16.unified/thermoF03/chapter_7.htm)
