<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The incident [X-ray](../../../../x-ray.md) drives the [electrons](../../../../electron.md) of an atom. Away from an [absorption edge](../../../../absorption-edge.md), and when the photon [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) greatly exceeds the relevant electronic binding energies but is small compared with $m_ec^2$, each electron has approximately the same [Thomson scattering](../../../../thomson-scattering.md) amplitude. The much heavier nucleus makes a negligible contribution because its acceleration is much smaller.

For [elastic scattering](../../../../elastic-scattering.md), the amplitudes from different bound [electrons](../../../../electron.md) add coherently. In units of the single-electron amplitude, the [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) is the [Fourier transform](../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) of the bound-electron [number density](../../../../number-density.md):

$$
f(\mathbf q)=\int n_e(\mathbf r)e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\,d^3r,
\qquad f(\mathbf0)=N_e.
$$

Consequently, when the [electron](../../../../electron.md) cloud is not well resolved by the [scattering vector](../../../../scattering-vector.md), its internal phase differences are small and

$$
\boxed{f\simeq N_e,\qquad I\propto N_e^2.}
$$

This is an approximation to the coherent [scattering amplitude](../../../../scattering-amplitude.md), not an assertion that independently scattered intensities add as $N_e$. At larger momentum transfer, destructive interference within the cloud reduces $f$; near [absorption edges](../../../../absorption-edge.md), the [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) acquires dispersive corrections. [Inelastic scattering](../../../../inelastic-scattering.md) is a separate contribution. In an [ionic crystal](../../../../ionic-crystal.md), use the ion's bound-electron count rather than automatically using the neutral atom's atomic number.
