<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A substitutional group-V atom such as [phosphorus](../../../../phosphorus.md) has one more [valence electron](../../../../valence-electron.md) than [germanium](../../../../germanium.md) and acts as a [donor impurity](../../../../donor-impurity.md), giving an [n-type semiconductor](../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md). Approximate its outer [electron](../../../../electron.md) by a [hydrogenic donor state](../../../../hydrogenic-donor-state.md) with screened Coulomb attraction and [effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) $m_e^*$. The [ionization energy](../../../../ionization-energy.md) is the positive binding-energy magnitude

$$
E_D=13.606\,\mathrm{eV}\,\frac{m_e^*/m_e}{\epsilon_r^2}
=13.606\frac{0.22}{15.8^2}\,\mathrm{eV},
\qquad\boxed{E_D\simeq0.0120\,\mathrm{eV}=12.0\,\mathrm{meV}.}
$$

Its [effective Bohr radius of a hydrogenic donor](../../../../effective-bohr-radius-of-a-hydrogenic-donor.md) is about $3.80\,\mathrm{nm}$, much larger than an atomic core, consistent with a shallow extended bound state.

At $300\,\mathrm K$, $k_BT=25.9\,\mathrm{meV}$, while the mean three-dimensional classical translational [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $3k_BT/2=38.8\,\mathrm{meV}$. Both exceed the donor binding [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), so substantial thermal ionization is expected, unlike excitation across the much larger $0.661\,\mathrm{eV}$ host [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md). A precise ionized fraction also depends on the [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), [dopant density](../../../../dopant-density.md) and [donor charge-state degeneracy](../../../../donor-charge-state-degeneracy.md); comparing two energy scales alone does not prove complete ionization at arbitrary concentration.
