<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

With [spin degeneracy](../../../../spin-degeneracy.md), each [energy band](../../../../energy-band.md) contains two [electrons](../../../../electron.md) per [primitive unit cell](../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md). Divalency therefore permits a filled first band, but only if its maximum lies below the second band's minimum. With negligible ionic [periodic potential](../../../../periodic-potential.md), the circular [Fermi surface](../../../../fermi-surface.md) and the rectangular zone have equal enclosed area, not equal shape. Electrons occupy some states outside the first zone while some higher-[energy](../../../../energy-split.md) first-zone states remain empty. Folded bands overlap, and the material is a [metal](../../../../metal.md).

A [periodic potential](../../../../periodic-potential.md) mixes [plane waves](../../../../plane-wave.md) differing by a [reciprocal lattice vector](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md). At a two-wave [Bragg point](../../../../bragg-point.md), the [nearly-free electron model](../../../../nearly-free-electron-model.md) gives a direct gap $2|V_{\mathbf G}|$. Increasing appropriate [Fourier coefficients](../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) can remove the band overlap. With the first band filled, the system is then a [band insulator](../../../../band-insulator.md), provided the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) is small compared with the insulating [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md) in energy units.

The [rectangular free-electron band-overlap scale](../../../../rectangular-free-electron-band-overlap-scale.md) follows from two different points in reciprocal space. Put $C=\hbar^2/(2m_e)$. Before gap opening, the highest first-zone energy is at the corner, while the lowest second-zone energy is at the midpoint of the nearer face:

$$
E_{1,\max}^{(0)}=C(K^2+L^2),\qquad E_{2,\min}^{(0)}=CK^2.
$$

Thus the downward shift $\delta_v$ of the first-band maximum plus the upward shift $\delta_c$ of the second-band minimum must overcome

$$
\boxed{\Delta_{\rm overlap}=CL^2=\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2}{2m_eb^2},\qquad
E_g^{\rm indirect}=\delta_v+\delta_c-\Delta_{\rm overlap}>0.}
$$

In a rigid-splitting estimate, an opening $\Delta$ lowers the first band by $\Delta/2$ and raises the second by $\Delta/2$, giving the familiar threshold $\Delta>\hbar^2\pi^2/(2m_eb^2)$. Equality is only band touching, not a positive insulating gap. This expression is the **overlap that must be removed**, under that explicit band-shift approximation.

[Positive direct gaps can coexist with band overlap](../../../../positive-direct-gaps-can-coexist-with-band-overlap.md): a local direct splitting and the global [indirect band gap](../../../../indirect-band-gap.md) are not interchangeable. The shape and [Fourier coefficients](../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) of the ionic potential are unspecified, so there is no universal exact critical direct-gap width determined by $a,b$ alone. For a concrete demonstration, a separable potential $2V_x\cos(2Kx)+2V_y\cos(2Ly)$ has axial direct gaps $\Delta_x=2|V_x|$ and $\Delta_y=2|V_y|$. To first order, the corner's lowest level is $C(K^2+L^2)-|V_x|-|V_y|$, and the two candidate second-band minima are $CK^2+|V_x|$ and $CL^2+|V_y|$. Therefore, within this model and neglecting higher-order band shifts, nonoverlap requires

$$
\Delta_x+\frac{\Delta_y}{2}>CL^2,
\qquad \frac{\Delta_x}{2}+\Delta_y>CK^2.
$$

Equal axial gaps would give the first-order estimate $\Delta>2CL^2/3$, rather than the rigid-splitting estimate. Such a large opening can exceed the quantitative range of weak-potential perturbation theory, but the example identifies the missing model assumption: corner levels feel both couplings. The exact general answer is the positive indirect-gap criterion above; a geometry-only direct-gap threshold would claim more than the supplied potential information supports.
