<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Wigner-Seitz cell](../../../../wigner-seitz-cell.md) is the region of real space at least as close to a chosen [Bravais lattice](../../../../bravais-lattice.md) point as to any other. It is bounded by perpendicular bisectors of vectors to neighboring lattice points and is a [primitive unit cell](../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md).

A [reciprocal lattice vector](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) $\mathbf G$ satisfies $e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf R}=1$ for every [lattice translation](../../../../lattice-translation.md) $\mathbf R$. With primitive vectors $\mathbf a_i$, reciprocal primitive vectors satisfy $\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}$, and $\mathbf G=\sum_jn_j\mathbf b_j$ for integers $n_j$.

The [first Brillouin zone](../../../../first-brillouin-zone.md) is the [Wigner-Seitz cell](../../../../wigner-seitz-cell.md) of that [reciprocal lattice](../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md), centered at $\mathbf k=0$. Its boundaries are [Bragg planes](../../../../bragg-plane.md), $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf G=G^2/2$. Higher [Brillouin zones](../../../../brillouin-zone.md) are the regions separated from the origin by successively more such planes: an interior point of zone $j$ has crossed exactly $j-1$ planes. A [second Brillouin zone](../../../../second-brillouin-zone.md) is generally disconnected in this extended-zone representation; it is not just a translated first-zone rectangle.
