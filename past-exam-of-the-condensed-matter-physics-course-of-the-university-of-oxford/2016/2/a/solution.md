↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [free electron model](../../../../free-electron-model.md), [conduction electrons](../../../../conduction-electron.md) occupy independent [plane-wave](../../../../plane-wave.md) states with [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $\varepsilon_{\mathbf k}=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$ in a uniform mean background. [Crystal-periodic](../../../../periodic-potential.md) band structure and explicit [electron](../../../../electron.md)–[electron](../../../../electron.md) correlations are omitted. [Electrons](../../../../electron.md) still obey the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) and [Fermi–Dirac statistics](../../../../fermi-dirac-statistics.md); free does not mean distinguishable classical particles. A scattering time can be added phenomenologically to describe transport.

The [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md) is the highest occupied single-particle [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) at zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), measured here from the bottom of the [parabolic band](../../../../parabolic-band-dispersion.md). The [Fermi temperature](../../../../fermi-temperature.md) is the corresponding scale $T_F=E_F/k_B$, not the actual [thermal temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) of the sample. Metallic [electron](../../../../electron.md) densities are large, so filling all distinct states up to the required [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) gives [Fermi energies](../../../../fermi-energy.md) of several electronvolts, equivalent to tens of thousands of kelvin or more. At ordinary [temperatures](../../../../temperature-split.md), only a narrow region around the [Fermi surface](../../../../fermi-surface.md) has appreciably changing occupations.

A room-temperature metal and its lattice are nevertheless at room [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), not at $T_F$. High occupied [kinetic energies](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) are present even in the zero-temperature ground state and cannot all be liberated as [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) without violating the allowed occupancy structure. A metal below skin [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) often feels cold because it transfers [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) rapidly out of the skin; its [thermal conductivity](../../../../thermal-conductivity.md) and [thermal effusivity](../../../../thermal-effusivity.md) matter, not the value of its [Fermi temperature](../../../../fermi-temperature.md). At the same actual [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), a poorly conducting material can therefore feel warmer while neither material is thermodynamically hotter.
