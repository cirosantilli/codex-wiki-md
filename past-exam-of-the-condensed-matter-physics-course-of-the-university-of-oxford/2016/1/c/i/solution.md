<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The 110 plane spacing of the conventional cubic cell is

$$
d_{110}(0)=\frac{0.287}{\sqrt2}\ \mathrm{nm}=0.202940\ \mathrm{nm}.
$$

For the first-order Bragg condition,

$$
\sin\theta_0=\frac{\lambda}{2d_{110}}
=\frac{0.154\sqrt2}{2(0.287)}=0.37942\ldots,
\qquad\boxed{2\theta_0=44.596^\circ.}
$$

The quoted experimental deflection is twice the angle between the beam and the reflecting planes; reporting only $22.298^\circ$ would report the [Bragg angle](../../../../../bragg-angle.md) instead of the requested [scattering angle](../../../../../bragg-angle.md). The 110 reflection is allowed for the identical-Fe body-centered lattice because $1+1+0$ is even. [Wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) and spacing have been expressed in the same units, so their ratio is dimensionless. Higher Bragg orders are conventionally represented by the corresponding higher-index reflection, rather than inserted as an unannounced extra order here.
