<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the integrated constant-compressibility model from [c](../../c.md), with $\kappa p=0.0059\times100=0.59$:

$$
a(100)=0.287e^{-0.59/3}\ \mathrm{nm}=0.235760\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
\sin\theta(100)=0.37942\ldots e^{0.59/3}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{2\theta(100\ \mathrm{GPa})=55.018^\circ.}
$$

Compression reduces the spacing and moves the same allowed reflection to larger deflection, as the positive [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) predicts. The input [compressibility](../../../../../isothermal-compressibility.md) corresponds to a constant [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md) $1/\kappa\simeq169.5$ GPa in this ideal model.

This is the stipulated **unchanged cubic-phase, constant-compressibility extrapolation**, rather than a reliable prediction for real Fe at 100 GPa. Real Fe exhibits a pressure-induced [body-centered-cubic](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) to [hexagonal-close-packed](../../../../../hexagonal-close-packed-structure.md) transformation, and [compressibility](../../../../../isothermal-compressibility.md) can change with [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md).[2008 primary diffraction study, abstract: pressure-driven structural transformation in Fe](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.78.220101) A real high-pressure [diffraction](../../../../../x-ray-diffraction.md) interpretation would require its actual phase and [equation of state](../../../../../equation-of-state.md). The source gives neither, so its numerical exercise is answered within its explicitly stated idealization.
