<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $B=0.05$ T, use the two [spin-only](../../../../../spin-only-magnetic-moment.md) moment states. Their probabilities and normalized [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) are

$$
p_+=\frac{e^x}{2\cosh x}=\frac{1+\tanh x}{2},\quad
p_-=1-p_+,\quad \frac{M}{M_s}=p_+-p_-=\tanh x,
\quad x=\frac{\mu_B B}{k_BT}.
$$

At 300 K, $x=1.11952\times10^{-4}$; at 2 K, $x=0.0167928$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cc}
T&\text{aligned population }p_+&\text{net polarization }M/M_s\\\hline
300\ \mathrm K&0.50005598&1.11952\times10^{-4}\\
2\ \mathrm K&0.50839563&0.0167913
\end{array}}
$$

The phrase “fraction aligned” is ambiguous if it is used to mean net alignment. Half the moments are already in the parallel state at zero field, but the net [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) is then zero because the antiparallel half cancels them. The table reports both the literal parallel-state population and the imbalance that produces [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md). At this weak field even the 2 K sample is far from saturation, so the [Curie approximation](../../../../../curie-s-law.md) is accurate within this independent-spin model.
