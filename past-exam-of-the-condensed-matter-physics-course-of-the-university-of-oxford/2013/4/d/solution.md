<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Linear Curie behavior fails gradually when the Zeeman [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) of a substantial moment is comparable to thermal [energy](../../../../energy-split.md). Its appropriate scale is

$$
\boxed{g\mu_BJ B\sim k_BT,\qquad
B_{\rm nl}\sim\frac{k_BT}{g\mu_BJ}.}
$$

At $T=300\,\mathrm K$, $k_BT/\mu_B=446.6\,\mathrm T$. Thus $B_{\rm nl}\sim446.6/(gJ)\,\mathrm T$: about $223\,\mathrm T$ for $g=2,J=1$, or $447\,\mathrm T$ for a spin-one-half moment with $g=2$. Larger moments saturate at smaller fields. The source does not specify numerical $g,J$, so it determines this parametric scale rather than one universal room-temperature threshold. At sufficiently large field the moment tends toward $g\mu_BJ$ and cannot keep increasing linearly with $B$.
