<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the SI linear-response convention $\mathbf M=\chi_H\mathbf H$, a diamagnetic contribution has negative susceptibility: the induced moment opposes the applied field. Orbital motion supplies a familiar microscopic mechanism, and closed-shell atoms can be diamagnetic even without permanent moments. [Paramagnetism](../../../../paramagnetism.md) has positive susceptibility, with induced [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) along the field; thermal alignment of permanent localized moments is one mechanism, while conduction-electron [spin](../../../../spin.md) response is another.

Neither sign alone is a definition of [ferromagnetic ordering](../../../../ferromagnetic-ordering.md). An ordinary paramagnetic phase has no spontaneous zero-field [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md). A diamagnetic background can coexist with a larger paramagnetic contribution in the same specimen. Curie's inverse-temperature law describes an appropriate localized-moment model, not every possible paramagnet.
