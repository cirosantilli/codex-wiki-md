<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Room-temperature iron is ferromagnetic: interactions among its electronic moments produce spontaneous local order. The independent-ion [Curie law](../../../../curie-s-law.md) therefore needs an interaction and an ordered-state description. In a Weiss mean-field approximation, replace the applied field by $H_{\rm eff}=H+\Lambda M$. Above the ordering [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), linearizing the single-ion response yields

$$
M=\frac{C_\chi}{T}(H+\Lambda M),\qquad
\boxed{\chi_H=\frac{C_\chi}{T-T_C},\qquad T_C=\Lambda C_\chi.}
$$

This is the Curie–Weiss approximation on the paramagnetic side. Merely substituting room [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) below $T_C$ into that high-temperature formula would produce a negative response and is not a valid model of stable iron.

Below $T_C$, use the full nonlinear moment response to the effective field; it has nonzero zero-field solutions. On a chosen ordered branch the local small-field response has the form $M\simeq M_s(T)+\chi_{\rm diff}(T)H$, rather than $M\propto H/T$ through the origin. Macroscopic [magnetic domains](../../../../magnetic-domain.md) and [hysteresis](../../../../hysteresis.md) further determine the measured net moment. Exchange interactions, arising from electronic quantum statistics and Coulomb correlations, supply the alignment [energy](../../../../energy-split.md); bare dipolar fields alone do not explain ordinary iron's strong [ferromagnetism](../../../../ferromagnetism.md). A localized-ion Weiss model describes the qualitative ordering, while iron's itinerant electronic structure limits a literal independent-fixed-ion interpretation.
