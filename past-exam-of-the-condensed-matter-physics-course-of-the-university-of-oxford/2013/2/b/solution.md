<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a repulsive short-range potential and a stable finite minimum, assume $A>0$ and integer $n>1$. Setting $u'(R)=C/R^2-2nA/R^{n+1}=0$ gives

$$
\boxed{R_0=\left(\frac{2nA}{C}\right)^{1/(n-1)}
=\left(\frac{8\pi\epsilon_0nA}{\alpha q^2}\right)^{1/(n-1)}.}
$$

At this point $2A/R_0^n=C/(nR_0)$, so

$$
\boxed{u_0=-\frac{C}{R_0}\left(1-\frac1n\right),\qquad
u''(R_0)=\frac{(n-1)C}{R_0^3}>0.}
$$

Here $u''$ denotes the second derivative of the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $u$, not a displacement variable. The negative equilibrium [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is the cohesive [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) per pair relative to infinite separation.

The qualifiers on $A,n$ matter: an arbitrary real coefficient and integer exponent do not automatically describe stabilizing repulsion. For instance $A<0,n>1$ gives collapse as $R\to0$, whereas $n=1$ gives $u=(2A-C)/R$ and has no isolated finite equilibrium. The displayed minimum therefore applies to the physically repulsive, faster-than-Coulomb branch of the stated model.
