<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Diffraction thermal-expansion thermometry](../../../../diffraction-thermal-expansion-thermometry.md) needs a stable monochromatic [X-ray](../../../../x-ray.md) beam, controlled collimation, an accurately calibrated angular scale, and an X-ray-sensitive detector with enough counts to fit reproducible line centroids. Crystal monochromators or appropriate [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) selection are relevant here; an apparatus for visible-light interference does not directly measure these [X-ray](../../../../x-ray.md) peaks. Sample displacement and angular-zero drift should be checked with a suitable reference standard.

The first-order angular sensitivities are about $6.93\times10^{-5}$ and $1.17\times10^{-4}$ degrees per kelvin, respectively. A one-kelvin inference therefore requires comparable centroid stability, with high count statistics and control of systematic errors; a centroid can be fitted more precisely than the line width. Fractional [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) drift also mimics lattice expansion, so the desired [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) precision constrains $\Delta\lambda/\lambda$ relative to $\alpha_L\Delta T$.

The powder must be at a known uniform [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and allowed to equilibrate. Mechanical stress, clamping, composition changes, oxidation and phase changes can also shift or broaden peaks. Accurate work requires a calibrated $a(T)$, including the actual [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence of the [thermal expansion coefficient](../../../../coefficient-of-thermal-expansion.md), rather than assuming the supplied constant describes every [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) exactly. Using both lines provides a consistency check on a common cubic expansion.
