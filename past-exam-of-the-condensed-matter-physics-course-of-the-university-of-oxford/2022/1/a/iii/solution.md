<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In [elastic scattering](../../../../../elastic-scattering.md), the incoming and outgoing [wave vectors](../../../../../wavevector.md) have equal magnitude $k_0=2\pi/\lambda$. Their mutual angle is $2\theta_{hkl}$. The [scattering vector](../../../../../scattering-vector.md) $\mathbf q=\mathbf k_f-\mathbf k_i$ therefore has magnitude

$$
q^2=2k_0^2\left[1-\cos(2\theta_{hkl})\right]
=4k_0^2\sin^2\theta_{hkl}.
$$

The [elastic Bragg scattering condition](../../../../../elastic-bragg-scattering-condition.md) is $\mathbf q=\mathbf G_{hkl}$, giving

$$
\boxed{G_{hkl}=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\sin\theta_{hkl}.}
$$

Equivalently, $G_{hkl}=2\pi/d_{hkl}$ turns this relation into [Bragg's law](../../../../../bragg-s-law.md), $2d_{hkl}\sin\theta_{hkl}=\lambda$, with the reflection order incorporated into the [Miller indices](../../../../../miller-index.md).
