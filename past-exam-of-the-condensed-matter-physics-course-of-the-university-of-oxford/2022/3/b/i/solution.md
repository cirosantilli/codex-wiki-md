<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a single [impurity level in a crystal](../../../../../impurity-level-in-a-crystal.md) with allowed occupations zero and one and equal occupied/empty statistical weights, the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) gives

$$
f_D=\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon_D-\mu)}+1}.
$$

Since $n_{\rm full}=N_D f_D$ and $n_{\rm empty}=N_D(1-f_D)$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_{\rm full}}{n_{\rm empty}}=\frac{f_D}{1-f_D}
=e^{\beta(\mu-\epsilon_D)}.}
$$

The same result follows from the two [grand canonical ensemble](../../../../../grand-canonical-ensemble.md) weights $1$ and $e^{-\beta(\epsilon_D-\mu)}$.

The equal-statistical-weight convention is necessary for the printed formula when $n_{\rm full}$ and $n_{\rm empty}$ count whole [donor impurities](../../../../../donor-impurity.md). More generally, [donor charge-state degeneracy](../../../../../donor-charge-state-degeneracy.md) gives $n_{\rm full}/n_{\rm empty}=(g_{\rm full}/g_{\rm empty})e^{\beta(\mu-\epsilon_D)}$. An ordinary singly occupied donor with two [spin](../../../../../spin.md) orientations and a nondegenerate empty state has ratio $2e^{\beta(\mu-\epsilon_D)}$; at $\mu=\epsilon_D$ its occupied-to-empty ratio is two, a concrete counterexample to treating the printed expression as universal. The following part uses the paper's ratio-one convention.
