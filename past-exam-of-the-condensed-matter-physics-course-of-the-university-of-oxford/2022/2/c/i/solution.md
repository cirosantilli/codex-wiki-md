<h1 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Regard $V(x)$ as an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md), and take $G=2\pi/a$. Each cosine supplies half its coefficient to each of its two [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md). The nonzero coefficients are

$$
V_{\pm G}=-\pi V_0\delta,\qquad
V_{\pm2G}=\frac{V_0}{2},\qquad
V_{\pm3G}=+\pi V_0\delta.
$$

The [Fourier coefficient](../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) connecting the two [plane waves](../../../../../plane-wave.md) at $k=\pm\pi/a$ is $V_G$, so the two-wave [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Delta=2\pi|V_0|\,|\delta|,\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{\pi|V_0|}{E_{\rm ZB}}|\delta|.}
$$

At $\delta=0$ the actual [periodic potential](../../../../../periodic-potential.md) has period $a/2$ and no Fourier harmonic at $G$. The crossing at the artificially halved [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) boundary then has zero [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md). Higher-order weak-potential corrections can change the proportionality coefficient at nonzero $\delta$; the displayed coefficient belongs to the given two-wave approximation.

There is also a small-displacement qualification in the source: the claimed positions of the [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) minima are correct to first order in $\delta$, not exactly. Put $t=2\pi x/a$. Then

$$
\frac1{V_0}\frac{dV}{dt}=-2\sin2t+2\pi\delta(\sin t-3\sin3t).
$$

At $\delta=0.01$ and $x/a=1/4-\delta=0.24$, this equals approximately $-2.802\times10^{-3}$, rather than zero. For $V_0>0$, expanding about $x/a=1/4$ and $3/4$ gives minima at $1/4-\delta+O(\delta^3)$ and $3/4+\delta+O(\delta^3)$. This does not alter the leading [Fourier coefficient](../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) or the linear opening of the [band gap](../../../../../band-gap.md).
