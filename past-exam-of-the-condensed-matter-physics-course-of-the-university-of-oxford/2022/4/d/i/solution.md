<h1 id="4/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The given [electron configuration](../../../../../electron-configuration.md) has an inert closed $[\mathrm{Xe}]$ core and two $4f$ [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). The [Aufbau principle](../../../../../aufbau-principle.md) identifies the occupied subshells; the free-[ion](../../../../../ion.md) term within the open $4f^2$ configuration is then selected by [Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md). A closed core contributes $L=S=0$.

Each $f$ [electron](../../../../../electron.md) has orbital [angular momentum quantum number](../../../../../angular-momentum-quantum-number.md) $l=3$. Place the two [spins](../../../../../spin.md) parallel, so $S=1$. The [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) then requires distinct orbital projections. The largest allowed total projection is $M_L=3+2=5$, obtained from $m_l=3$ and $m_l=2$, giving the maximum allowed $L=5$. A larger $M_L=6$ would place two parallel-[spin](../../../../../spin.md) [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) in the same [atomic orbital](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) and is forbidden.

The $f$ subshell holds fourteen [electrons](../../../../../electron.md); $f^2$ is less than half filled. The third of [Hund's rules](../../../../../hund-s-rules.md) therefore selects $J=L-S=4$. Hence

$$
\boxed{L=5,\qquad S=1,\qquad J=4,\qquad \text{ground term }{}^3H_4.}
$$

The [term symbol](../../../../../term-symbol.md) uses $2S+1=3$ and the spectroscopic letter $H$ for $L=5$. The [praseodymium](../../../../../praseodymium.md) [ion](../../../../../ion.md) retains an orbital contribution, so a spin-only estimate would be inappropriate.
