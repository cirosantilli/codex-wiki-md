<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A consistent mechanism is a transition to G-type [antiferromagnetism](../../../../antiferromagnetism.md) at the [Néel temperature](../../../../neel-temperature.md). On the manganese [primitive cubic lattice](../../../../primitive-cubic-lattice.md), take the [magnetic moments](../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) to be

$$
\mathbf m_{j_xj_yj_z}=\mathbf m_0(-1)^{j_x+j_y+j_z}.
$$

Every nearest neighbour has the opposite [magnetic moment](../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md). The nuclear [crystal structure](../../../../crystal-structure.md) can retain its original [lattice constant](../../../../lattice-constant.md), while the [magnetic order](../../../../magnetic-order.md) introduces the propagation [wavevector](../../../../wavevector.md)

$$
\mathbf Q=\frac{2\pi}{a}\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac12\right).
$$

Summing the [scattering amplitude](../../../../scattering-amplitude.md) from the [magnetic moments](../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) over sites gives peaks at $\mathbf q=\mathbf G+\mathbf Q$, where $\mathbf G$ is a nuclear [reciprocal lattice vector](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md). Thus all three components of the new [Miller indices](../../../../miller-index.md), expressed relative to the original [unit cell](../../../../unit-cell.md), are half-integers. The two smallest squared magnitudes in units of $(2\pi/a)^2$ are

$$
\frac34\quad\text{for }\left\{\frac12,\frac12,\frac12\right\},\qquad
\frac{11}{4}\quad\text{for }\left\{\frac32,\frac12,\frac12\right\}.
$$

The first predicts $2\theta\simeq21.29^\circ$, agreeing with the new ring. The next squared magnitude is $19/4$, outside the measured angular range. Taking the observed first magnetic ring as the calibration, [Bragg's law](../../../../bragg-s-law.md) predicts

$$
\sin\theta_2=\sqrt{\frac{11}{3}}\sin(10.65^\circ),\qquad
\boxed{2\theta_2\simeq41.45^\circ\simeq41.4^\circ.}
$$

Using the independently fitted [lattice constant](../../../../lattice-constant.md) gives $41.42^\circ$, within the input rounding. One ring alone does not uniquely determine a [magnetic order](../../../../magnetic-order.md); this proposed nearest-neighbour [antiferromagnetic](../../../../antiferromagnetism.md) pattern explains both the observed ring and the count of two new rings in the specified interval.
