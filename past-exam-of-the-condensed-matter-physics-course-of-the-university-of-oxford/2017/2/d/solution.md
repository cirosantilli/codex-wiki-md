<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the one-band electron [Hall effect](../../../../hall-effect.md) model, $R_H=-1/(ne)$, with $e>0$. The measured [Hall coefficient](../../../../hall-coefficient.md) therefore gives

$$
n=\frac1{e|R_H|}=2.4966\times10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.
$$

A monovalent [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) has two conduction electrons per conventional [unit cell](../../../../unit-cell.md). Thus $na^3=2$, and

$$
\boxed{a=\left(\frac2n\right)^{1/3}=4.3108\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

Counting two spin states within the [Fermi sphere](../../../../fermi-sphere.md) gives $k_F=(3\pi^2n)^{1/3}=9.0418\times10^9\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Using the specified [effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) in the dispersion,

$$
\boxed{E_F=\frac{\hbar^2k_F^2}{2m^*}=2.3960\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad
T_F=E_F/k_B=2.7805\times10^4\,\mathrm K.}
$$

There is one mole of conduction electrons per mole of sodium. Its molar [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) at $E_F$ is $3N_A/(2E_F)$, giving

$$
\boxed{\gamma_{\mathrm{mol}}=\frac{\pi^2N_Ak_B^2}{2E_F}
=1.4757\,\mathrm{mJ\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-2}}.}
$$

Using the volume [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) in [c](../c.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\chi_p=1.0543\times10^{-5}\quad\text{(dimensionless SI volume susceptibility)}.}
$$

The [effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) changes the dispersion and [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md); the electron [Bohr magneton](../../../../bohr-magneton.md) used for the spin coupling retains the bare electron mass. Replacing it by $e\hbar/(2m^*)$ would be an additional, unjustified magnetic-moment assumption. A Hall factor or a multiband metal would also invalidate the simple density inference; the stated calculation uses the requested one-band approximation.
