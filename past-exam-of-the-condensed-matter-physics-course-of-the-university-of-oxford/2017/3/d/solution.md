<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A macroscopic nickel specimen can split into [magnetic domains](../../../../magnetic-domain.md), each with nonzero local [spontaneous magnetization](../../../../spontaneous-magnetization.md) but with different orientations. Their vector contributions can cancel, giving an almost zero measured net [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) below the [Curie temperature](../../../../curie-temperature.md). This reduces the external stray-field energy, at the expense of [magnetic domain wall](../../../../magnetic-domain-wall.md) energy; the balance favours a multidomain state in many ordinary specimens.

An applied [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) can move [magnetic domain walls](../../../../magnetic-domain-wall.md) and rotate the domain moments, revealing a large response and eventually [saturation magnetization](../../../../saturation-magnetization.md). Thus a zero average does not mean that each local moment has become thermally disordered or that the material is above its [Curie temperature](../../../../curie-temperature.md). Preparation, shape and domain-wall pinning influence remanence.
