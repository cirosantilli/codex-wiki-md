<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take two localized [spin](../../../../spin.md) orientations with moments $\pm\mu_B$ along the applied [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). This assumes an electron-like spin $g$ factor of two. Their [energies](../../../../energy-split.md) are $\mp\mu_BB$, so the one-moment [partition function](../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) is $Z_1=2\cosh x$, with $x=\mu_BB/(k_BT)$. The thermal mean moment is $k_BT\,\partial_B\ln Z_1=\mu_B\tanh x$, and the [spin-one-half paramagnetic magnetization](../../../../spin-one-half-paramagnetic-magnetization.md) is

$$
\boxed{M=n\mu_B\tanh\!\left(\frac{\mu_BB}{k_BT}\right).}
$$

For $|x|\ll1$, $M\simeq n\mu_B^2B/(k_BT)$. With the same SI convention $\chi=\partial M/\partial H$ and $B\simeq\mu_0H$,

$$
\boxed{\chi=\frac CT,\qquad C=\frac{n\mu_0\mu_B^2}{k_B}.}
$$

This [Curie law](../../../../curie-s-law.md) is a zero-field or weak-field susceptibility. At arbitrary field the differential response is $(C/T)\operatorname{sech}^2x$, and $M$ saturates at $n\mu_B$; extending the linear law to unlimited field would contradict that finite [saturation magnetization](../../../../saturation-magnetization.md).
