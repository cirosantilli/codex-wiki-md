<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The cubic [Miller indices](../../../../miller-index.md) give an [interplanar spacing](../../../../interplanar-spacing.md) $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$. Equivalently, the [reciprocal lattice vector](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) normal to these [lattice planes](../../../../lattice-plane.md) has length $2\pi/d_{hkl}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{d_{111}=\frac{a}{\sqrt3},\qquad
\mathbf G_{111}=\frac{2\pi}{a}(1,1,1).}
$$

The eight $\{111\}$ [reciprocal lattice vectors](../../../../reciprocal-lattice-vector.md) are the nearest nonzero reciprocal points. Their perpendicular bisectors form the eight hexagonal faces of the first [Brillouin zone](../../../../brillouin-zone.md), a truncated octahedron. For the positive $(111)$ direction, that [Bragg plane](../../../../bragg-plane.md) is $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf G_{111}=|\mathbf G_{111}|^2/2$, or $k_x+k_y+k_z=3\pi/a$. It is parallel to the direct $(111)$ [lattice planes](../../../../lattice-plane.md). The six remaining square zone faces correspond to the $\{200\}$ reciprocal points. The zone boundary is halfway to a reciprocal point, not the point itself.
