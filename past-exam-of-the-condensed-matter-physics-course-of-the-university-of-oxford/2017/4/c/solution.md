<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A substitutional phosphorus atom supplies one more valence electron than the silicon atom it replaces. In the shallow [hydrogenic donor state](../../../../hydrogenic-donor-state.md) model, the remaining positive impurity attracts that extra electron through the screened [Coulomb potential](../../../../coulomb-potential-energy.md)

$$
V(r)=-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r r}.
$$

Rescaling the [hydrogen atom](../../../../hydrogen-atom.md) binding energy by the [effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) and dielectric screening gives the ionization energy

$$
\boxed{E_D^{\mathrm{bind}}=13.6057\,\mathrm{eV}\,
\frac{m_e^*/m_e}{\epsilon_r^2}
=13.6057\frac{0.26}{11.7^2}\,\mathrm{eV}
=0.02584\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$

The donor level lies at $E_c-E_D^{\mathrm{bind}}$; its binding magnitude is positive. The associated [effective Bohr radius of a hydrogenic donor](../../../../effective-bohr-radius-of-a-hydrogenic-donor.md) is $a_B^*=\epsilon_r(m_e/m_e^*)a_0\simeq2.38\,\mathrm{nm}$, consistent with a shallow state extended over several lattice spacings.

At $1000\,\mathrm K$, $k_BT=0.08617\,\mathrm{eV}$, approximately $3.3$ times this binding energy. Dilute donors are consequently expected to be largely ionized, rather than frozen out. The actual ionized fraction also depends on the [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), available conduction states and [donor charge-state degeneracy](../../../../donor-charge-state-degeneracy.md); comparing $k_BT$ to the binding energy alone is not a normalized ionization probability. At sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), intrinsic electron-hole production can dominate the total carriers even though donors are ionized. Band anisotropy and short-range impurity corrections limit the numerical hydrogenic estimate.
