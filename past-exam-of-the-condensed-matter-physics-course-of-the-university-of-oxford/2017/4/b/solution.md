<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For an undoped neutral [intrinsic semiconductor](../../../../intrinsic-semiconductor.md), $n=p$. Equating the two expressions from [a](../a.md) and taking logarithms gives

$$
\ln N_c-\frac{E_c-\mu}{k_BT}
=\ln N_v-\frac{\mu-E_v}{k_BT},
$$

and hence the [intrinsic-semiconductor chemical potential](../../../../intrinsic-semiconductor-chemical-potential.md)

$$
\boxed{\mu_i(T)=\frac{E_c+E_v}{2}
+\frac{k_BT}{2}\ln\frac{N_v}{N_c}
=\frac{E_c+E_v}{2}+\frac34k_BT\ln\frac{m_h^*}{m_e^*}.}
$$

Therefore $\boxed{\alpha=\tfrac34\ln(m_h^*/m_e^*)}$. The logarithm is [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md)-independent for fixed parabolic [effective masses](../../../../band-effective-mass.md). The [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) is exactly at midgap only if the two [effective density of states](../../../../effective-density-of-states.md) values coincide; for $m_h^*>m_e^*$ it moves above midgap. Additional valley multiplicities would add their logarithmic contribution.
