<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A bulk [ferromagnet](../../../../ferromagnet.md) generally separates into [magnetic domains](../../../../magnetic-domain.md). Within each domain many moments align and the local [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) can be large, but different domain orientations partially or almost completely cancel the vector sum measured for the entire specimen. This reduces its external stray field and magnetostatic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md). Formation of [magnetic domain walls](../../../../magnetic-domain-wall.md) costs exchange and anisotropy [energy](../../../../energy-split.md), so the actual pattern balances these costs with the reduction in stray-field [energy](../../../../energy-split.md).

A strong applied field can move domain walls and rotate moments toward a nearly [saturation magnetization](../../../../saturation-magnetization.md). When the field is removed, pinning and the sample's magnetic history determine the remanent moment. Thus a small net moment does not show that local ferromagnetic order has disappeared, or that moments within each domain are all randomly oriented. At finite [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), thermal [spin](../../../../spin.md) fluctuations also reduce the local ordered moment below its zero-temperature maximum even in a single-domain specimen. These two reductions—between-domain cancellation and within-domain thermal reduction—should be distinguished.
