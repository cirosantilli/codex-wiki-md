<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A point [magnetic dipole](../../../../magnetic-dipole.md) $\boldsymbol\mu$ produces

$$
\mathbf B(\mathbf r)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}
\left[3(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\hat r)\hat r-\boldsymbol\mu\right].
$$

For $|\mu|\sim\mu_B$ and neighbour separation $r\sim0.2\,\mathrm{nm}$, its characteristic magnitude is

$$
\boxed{B_{\rm dip}\sim\frac{\mu_0\mu_B}{4\pi r^3}\simeq0.116\,\mathrm T.}
$$

The axial field is twice this scale; the exact orientation and crystal geometry are unspecified. The corresponding pair [magnetic dipole interaction](../../../../magnetic-dipole-interaction.md) has [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) scale $U_{\rm dip}\sim\mu_BB_{\rm dip}\simeq1.08\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm J$, giving

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm dip}\sim\frac{U_{\rm dip}}{k_B}\simeq0.078\,\mathrm K\sim0.1\,\mathrm K.}
$$

An axial orientation changes this to about $0.16\,\mathrm K$. A real ordering [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) depends on a lattice sum, symmetry and fluctuations, so this is a nearest-neighbour [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) estimate, not a complete ordering calculation. It establishes that ordinary dipolar coupling is far too weak to explain a thousand-kelvin ferromagnetic transition.
