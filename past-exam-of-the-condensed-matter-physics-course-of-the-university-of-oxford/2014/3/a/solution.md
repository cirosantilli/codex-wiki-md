An [intrinsic semiconductor](../../../../intrinsic-semiconductor.md) has its carrier populations set primarily by thermal excitation across the [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md); in the ideal pure, neutral case its conduction-electron and hole densities are equal, $n=p=n_i$. An [extrinsic semiconductor](../../../../extrinsic-semiconductor.md) has carrier populations significantly controlled by donor or acceptor impurities, producing predominantly [n-type semiconductor](../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md) or [p-type semiconductor](../../../../p-type-semiconductor.md) behavior. Chemical purity alone does not fix conductivity independently of [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and gap size.

The positive [charge carrier mobility](../../../../charge-carrier-mobility.md) is the magnitude of mean drift speed per unit weak [electric field](../../../../electric-field-split.md). For [electrons](../../../../electron.md) $\mathbf v_e=-\mu_e\mathbf E$, for holes $\mathbf v_h=\mu_h\mathbf E$, and its SI unit is $\mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$. In an isotropic relaxation-time model, $\mu=|q|\tau/m^*$.

A [band effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md) characterizes how an [electron](../../../../electron.md) wave packet accelerates in an [energy band](../../../../energy-band.md), rather than replacing its vacuum rest [mass](../../../../mass.md). In one dimension and its tensor generalization,

$$
\boxed{\frac1{m^*}=\frac1{\hbar^2}\frac{d^2E}{dk^2},\qquad
(M^{*-1})_{ij}=\frac1{\hbar^2}\frac{\partial^2E}{\partial k_i\partial k_j}.}
$$

These follow by differentiating $v_i=\hbar^{-1}\partial E/\partial k_i$ and using $\hbar\dot k_j=F_j$. Effective masses can be anisotropic and [electron](../../../../electron.md) band curvature can be negative.
