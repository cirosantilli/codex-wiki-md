<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A monovalent FCC metal supplies one conduction [electron](../../../../electron.md) per atom and four atoms per conventional cell. Hence

$$
n_m=\frac4{(0.36\times10^{-9})^3}=8.57\times10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.
$$

With a one-carrier conductivity $\sigma=n_me\mu_m$,

$$
\boxed{\mu_m=\frac1{\rho_mn_me}=\frac1{(1.7\times10^{-8})(8.57\times10^{28})e}
=4.28\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

This is about $84$ times smaller than the stated germanium [electron mobility](../../../../electron-mobility.md). Nevertheless the metal is vastly more conductive because its carrier density is about $3.7\times10^9$ times larger. Resistivity depends on both density and mobility; low resistivity alone does not establish high mobility or long scattering time.
