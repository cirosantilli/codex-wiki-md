<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Removing an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) of charge $-e$ leaves charge $+e$ relative to the filled-band reference. This also reproduces the current. If the missing [electron](../../../../../electron.md) state had [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v_e(\mathbf k)$, subtracting its contribution from the zero current of a filled band gives

$$
\Delta\mathbf j=-(-e)\mathbf v_e(\mathbf k)=+e\mathbf v_e(\mathbf k).
$$

The hole dispersion with $\mathbf k_h=-\mathbf k$ gives $\mathbf v_h(\mathbf k_h)=\mathbf v_e(\mathbf k)$, so

$$
\boxed{q_h=+e,\qquad \Delta\mathbf j=q_h\mathbf v_h.}
$$

Likewise $\hbar\dot{\mathbf k}=-e\mathbf E$ becomes $\hbar\dot{\mathbf k}_h=+e\mathbf E$. Positive hole charge and positive hole [effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) consistently describe drift along the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), while the valence [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) themselves drift through an almost full band. A hole is a quasiparticle, not a proton moving through the crystal.
