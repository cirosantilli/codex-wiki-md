A [Bravais lattice](../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is an infinite periodic set of equivalent points $\mathbf R=n_1\mathbf a_1+n_2\mathbf a_2+n_3\mathbf a_3$, with integer $n_i$ and independent primitive translations $\mathbf a_i$. It describes translation symmetry, without assigning a chemical species to a point. An [atomic basis of a crystal](../../../../atomic-basis-of-a-crystal.md) is the motif of atoms, with their species and relative positions, repeated identically at every [lattice point](../../../../lattice-point.md). The resulting [crystal structure](../../../../crystal-structure.md) consists of the lattice decorated by that basis; the basis can contain several atoms even when the lattice is primitive.

A [primitive unit cell](../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) tiles space under lattice translations and contains exactly one [lattice point](../../../../lattice-point.md), counting shared boundary points fractionally. Its [volume](../../../../volume.md) is $|\mathbf a_1\cdot(\mathbf a_2\times\mathbf a_3)|$. A [conventional unit cell](../../../../conventional-unit-cell.md) is chosen to display useful crystal symmetry and may contain several [lattice points](../../../../lattice-point.md). Thus a centered conventional cell is not primitive, but the crystal still has a primitive cell. Neither cell size nor the number of dots drawn on its boundary should be confused with the number of atoms in the crystal basis.
