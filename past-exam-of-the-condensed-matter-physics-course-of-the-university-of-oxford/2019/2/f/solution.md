<h1 id="2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Square the stated [Lindemann melting criterion](../../../../lindemann-melting-criterion.md) and insert the high-temperature displacement:

$$
\frac{\hbar^2T_m}{m_{\mathrm{Ar}}k_BT_E^2}=(0.05d)^2.
$$

Thus, with $m_{\mathrm{Ar}}=(0.040\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}})/N_A$,

$$
\boxed{T_m=0.05^2d^2\frac{m_{\mathrm{Ar}}k_BT_E^2}{\hbar^2}\simeq1.00\times10^2\,\mathrm K.}
$$

Here $T_m/T_E\simeq1.66$, so the use of the high-temperature approximation is only moderately justified. It is an estimate, not a controlled $T/T_E\to\infty$ limit. Retaining the exact [Einstein mean-square displacement](../../../../einstein-mean-square-displacement.md) in the same heuristic criterion gives about $97\,\mathrm K$, indicating the size of that particular correction. The empirical threshold $0.05d$ and the independent-oscillator model introduce additional uncertainty.
