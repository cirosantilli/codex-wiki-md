<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A material showing [paramagnetism](../../../../paramagnetism.md) has an induced [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) in the direction of a weak applied field, giving positive [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md), and no spontaneous magnetization once the field is removed in the paramagnetic phase. Alignment of permanent atomic or nuclear moments is one mechanism.

A material showing [diamagnetism](../../../../diamagnetism.md) develops an induced moment opposing the applied field, giving negative [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md). Closed electronic shells supply a familiar orbital contribution; the response need not require permanent moments.

A [ferromagnet](../../../../ferromagnet.md) has a phase with a spontaneous ordered [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) below its [Curie temperature](../../../../curie-temperature.md), produced by cooperative alignment of moments. Its [magnetic domains](../../../../magnetic-domain.md) can cancel in a macroscopic zero-field sample, so a measured zero net moment alone does not show that the material lacks [ferromagnetism](../../../../ferromagnetism.md).
