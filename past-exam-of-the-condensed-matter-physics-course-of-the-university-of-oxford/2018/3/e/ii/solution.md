<h1 id="3/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Quantum statistics matter when thermal [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is comparable with a characteristic vibrational level spacing. The chain's natural [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md) is $\omega_0=\sqrt{\kappa/m}=5.7471\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, giving

$$
\boxed{T_Q\sim\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{k_B}=43.9\,\mathrm K.}
$$

Factors of order unity depend on which vibrational scale is used. The largest chain [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md) is $2\omega_0$, corresponding to $87.8\,\mathrm K$. An isolated two-atom bond has reduced [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m/2$ and [angular frequency](../../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\sqrt2\omega_0$, corresponding to $62.1\,\mathrm K$. Thus quantum corrections become appreciable on the scale of several tens of kelvin, and all modes are classical only when $T$ is comfortably above the largest relevant vibrational scale. Below it, high-[frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) [phonons](../../../../../phonon.md) are progressively frozen out and [equipartition](../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md) fails.

For the isolated bond, the zero-temperature root-mean-square relative displacement is

$$
x_{\mathrm{zp}}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(m/2)(\sqrt2\omega_0)}}\simeq0.0061\,\mathrm{nm}\simeq0.073\xi.
$$

It is small enough for a local bond expansion. The classical weakly anharmonic estimate in part d therefore has a useful parametric window

$$
\boxed{10^2\,\mathrm K\ll T\ll\text{a few }10^3\,\mathrm K,}
$$

where the lower condition means comfortably above the vibrational scale, rather than a sharp threshold. The model's upper bound concerns local anharmonicity and binding, not a prediction of the melting temperature.
