<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The two body-centre/origin sites and the other six sites have different local environments. Their effective [atomic scattering factors](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) therefore need not be identical even in an elemental, chemically pure [crystal](../../../../crystal.md). Write them as $f_A$ and $f_B$, respectively. The [crystal basis structure factor](../../../../crystal-basis-structure-factor.md) becomes

$$
F_{hkl}=f_A[1+(-1)^{h+k+l}]+2f_B\left[(-1)^k\cos\frac{\pi h}{2}+(-1)^h\cos\frac{\pi l}{2}+(-1)^l\cos\frac{\pi k}{2}\right].
$$

For the low-angle families this gives

$$
F_{100}=0,\qquad \boxed{F_{110}=2(f_A-f_B),}\qquad F_{111}=0.
$$

Thus $\{110\}$ can produce a weak extra [powder diffraction](../../../../powder-diffraction.md) ring below $\{200\}$. This is an [accidental diffraction extinction from equal atomic scattering factors](../../../../accidental-diffraction-extinction-from-equal-atomic-scattering-factors.md): its cancellation in part d is exact only in the identical-atom-scattering approximation. Small differences in the bonding [electron density](../../../../electron-density.md), or site-dependent [Debye–Waller factors](../../../../debye-waller-factor.md), can leave a small nonzero amplitude. For instance, taking $f_A=f+\delta f$ and $f_B=f$ gives $F_{110}=2\delta f$ while $F_{200}=4f+2\delta f$; the corresponding intensity suppression is quadratic in $|\delta f/f|$. No impurity is required. The $\{100\}$ and $\{111\}$ cancellations remain for arbitrary scalar $f_A,f_B$ in this two-site-class model.
