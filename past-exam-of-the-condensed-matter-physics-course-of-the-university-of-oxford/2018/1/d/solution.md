<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Initially approximate every [tungsten](../../../../tungsten.md) atom by the same [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) $f$. Pairing the quarter-coordinate sites in the [crystal basis structure factor](../../../../crystal-basis-structure-factor.md) gives

$$
\frac{F_{hkl}}f=1+(-1)^{h+k+l}+2\left[(-1)^k\cos\frac{\pi h}{2}+(-1)^h\cos\frac{\pi l}{2}+(-1)^l\cos\frac{\pi k}{2}\right].
$$

For cubic [powder diffraction](../../../../powder-diffraction.md), increasing deflection corresponds to increasing $h^2+k^2+l^2$, since $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$. The first possibilities are

| Family | $h^2+k^2+l^2$ | $F/f$ \\ | $\{100\}$ | $1$ | $0$ \\ | $\{110\}$ | $2$ | $0$ \\ | $\{111\}$ | $3$ | $0$ \\ | $\{200\}$ | $4$ | $4$ \\ | $\{210\}$ | $5$ | $4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Hence the first two strong rings have [Miller indices](../../../../miller-index.md)

$$
\boxed{\{200\},\qquad\{210\}.}
$$

The [powder diffraction multiplicity](../../../../powder-diffraction-multiplicity.md) is six for $\{200\}$, from three choices of nonzero axis and two signs. For $\{210\}$ it is $3!\,2^2=24$, from six permutations and four sign choices. If the [atomic scattering factors](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md) and angular corrections are taken as approximately equal for these nearby rings, their integrated [diffraction intensity](../../../../diffraction-intensity.md) obey

$$
\boxed{I_{200}:I_{210}\simeq6|4f|^2:24|4f|^2=1:4.}
$$

This is a multiplicity-based estimate. An exact measured ratio also depends on the momentum-transfer dependence of the [atomic scattering factor](../../../../atomic-scattering-factor.md), the [Lorentz-polarization factor](../../../../lorentz-polarization-factor.md), the [Debye–Waller factor](../../../../debye-waller-factor.md), absorption and instrument geometry. In particular, the source does not provide the beta-phase [lattice constant](../../../../lattice-constant.md) or instrument corrections needed to calculate those angular factors. Peak heights additionally depend on peak widths; the estimate refers to integrated ring [intensities](../../../../intensity-physics.md).
