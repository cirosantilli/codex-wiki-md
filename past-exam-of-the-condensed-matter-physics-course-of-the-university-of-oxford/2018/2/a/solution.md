↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) is the change in [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md) when particles are added at fixed [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and [volume](../../../../volume.md):

$$
\boxed{\mu=\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial N}\right)_{T,V}.}
$$

Equivalently, $dU=T\,dS-P\,dV+\mu\,dN$ identifies it as the derivative of [internal energy](../../../../internal-energy-split.md) at fixed [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) and [volume](../../../../volume.md). In equilibrium, systems that exchange particles have equal [chemical potentials](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md).

The [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md) is the zero-temperature [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md), or, for independent [electrons](../../../../electron.md), the highest occupied single-particle [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in the zero-temperature limit. Its numerical value depends on the chosen [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) reference; the [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md) need not be measured from the bottom of a band unless that convention is specified.
