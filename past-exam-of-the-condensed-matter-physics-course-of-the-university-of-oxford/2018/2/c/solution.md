<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Monovalency gives one [electron](../../../../electron.md) per atom, so the two-spin-state band is half filled. At zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), occupying $-k_F<k<k_F$ gives $2(Na/2\pi)(2k_F)=N$. Hence

$$
\boxed{k_F=\frac{\pi}{2a},\qquad E_F=E(k_F)=\epsilon_0.}
$$

Relative to the band minimum, the [Fermi energy](../../../../fermi-energy.md) is $2t$.

The [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) obeys $g(\epsilon_0+x)=g(\epsilon_0-x)$. At $\mu=\epsilon_0$, the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) satisfies

$$
f(\epsilon_0+x)+f(\epsilon_0-x)=1.
$$

Pairing the energies above and below the band centre therefore leaves exactly half of the two available states per atom occupied, at every positive [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). Increasing $\mu$ strictly increases the mean particle number, so this solution at fixed half filling is unique. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mu(T)=\epsilon_0\quad\text{for every }T\text{ in this isolated symmetric-band model}.}
$$

This [particle-hole symmetry of a half-filled bipartite band](../../../../particle-hole-symmetry-of-a-half-filled-bipartite-band.md) argument uses the symmetric [tight binding](../../../../tight-binding.md) band and fixed half filling; it is not a general temperature-independence property of [chemical potential](../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) in metals with additional bands or an asymmetric [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md).
