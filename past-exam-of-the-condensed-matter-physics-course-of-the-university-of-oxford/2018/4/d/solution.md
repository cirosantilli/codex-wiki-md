<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Ferromagnetic interactions enhance the response above the ordering transition. The [Curie–Weiss law](../../../../curie-weiss-law.md) improves the independent-spin approximation:

$$
\boxed{\chi(T)\simeq\frac{C}{T-\Theta},\qquad T>T_c,}
$$

with positive [Weiss temperature](../../../../weiss-temperature.md) $\Theta$ and the same high-[temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) coefficient $C$. In a simple [mean-field theory](../../../../mean-field-theory.md), take $\Theta=T_c=70\,\mathrm K$. More generally $\Theta$ is fitted and need not equal $T_c$, and the simple pole does not give exact critical behaviour arbitrarily close to the transition.

At zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), a selected ferromagnetic orientation has all magnetic moments aligned. Its [saturation magnetization](../../../../saturation-magnetization.md) is

$$
\boxed{M_s(0)=n\mu=\frac4{a^3}(7\mu_B)=2.077\times10^6\,\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}.}
$$

Equivalently, there are $28\mu_B$ per conventional cell. The moment points along either allowed easy-axis direction at zero [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md). This is the local or single-domain spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md); a macroscopic multidomain sample can have smaller, even zero, net [magnetization](../../../../magnetization.md) in the absence of a selecting field.
