An [intrinsic semiconductor](../../../../intrinsic-semiconductor.md) has thermally generated conduction [electrons](../../../../electron.md) and valence holes, with $n=p=n_i$ at equilibrium in the ideal undoped neutral material. Their abundance is controlled by its [band gap](../../../../band-gap.md), [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and band [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md).

An [extrinsic semiconductor](../../../../extrinsic-semiconductor.md) has its mobile carrier populations substantially modified by electrically active impurities. A [donor impurity](../../../../donor-impurity.md) can supply [electrons](../../../../electron.md), producing n-type material; an [acceptor impurity](../../../../acceptor-impurity.md) can capture a valence [electron](../../../../electron.md) and leave a mobile [electron hole](../../../../electron-hole.md), producing p-type material. Compensation and incomplete ionization can modify these simple counts. At low [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) [dopant freeze-out](../../../../dopant-freeze-out.md) can occur; at sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) intrinsic pair production can overwhelm a fixed dopant density. Doping and the dominant transport regime are related but are not interchangeable descriptions at every [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md).
