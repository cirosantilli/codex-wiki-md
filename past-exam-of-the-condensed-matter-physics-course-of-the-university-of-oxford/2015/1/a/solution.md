<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take one conduction [electron](../../../../electron.md) per atom and a three-dimensional [free electron gas](../../../../free-electron-model.md), with [spin degeneracy](../../../../spin-degeneracy.md) two and [electron](../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../mass.md) $m_e$. State counting inside the occupied [Fermi sphere](../../../../fermi-sphere.md) gives

$$
n=\frac2{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi k_F^3}{3}=\frac{k_F^3}{3\pi^2}.
$$

The highest occupied [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) at zero [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) is $E_F=\hbar^2k_F^2/(2m_e)$, so

$$
\boxed{T_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_ek_B}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}}.
$$

This relates the atomic [number density](../../../../number-density.md) to the [Fermi temperature](../../../../fermi-temperature.md) only because monovalency and the free-electron dispersion have been assumed. A real [energy band](../../../../energy-band.md) can require a different [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) and [band effective mass](../../../../band-effective-mass.md).

For the isotropic [Debye model](../../../../debye-model.md), count three acoustic [polarizations](../../../../polarization-waves.md) up to one spherical [wavevector](../../../../wavevector.md) cutoff $q_D$. The number of vibrational modes per [volume](../../../../volume.md) is

$$
3\frac1{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi q_D^3}{3}=3n,
\qquad q_D=(6\pi^2n)^{1/3}.
$$

With $\omega=cq$ and $\hbar\omega_D=k_B\theta_D$,

$$
\boxed{\theta_D=\frac{\hbar c}{k_B}(6\pi^2n)^{1/3}}.
$$

The effective $c$ is the [Debye sound speed](../../../../debye-sound-speed.md): matching the low-frequency mode density of one longitudinal and two transverse branches requires $3/c^3=1/c_L^3+2/c_T^3$. It is not generally the arithmetic average of their speeds. The cutoff preserves the mode count; it need not coincide with the actual nonspherical [Brillouin zone](../../../../brillouin-zone.md) boundary.
