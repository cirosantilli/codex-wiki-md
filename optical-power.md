# Optical power

↑ **Parent:** [Focal length](focal-length.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_power)

The [optical power](optical-power.md) is reciprocal signed [focal length](focal-length.md), $\Phi=1/f$. With $f$ expressed in metres, [optical power](optical-power.md) is expressed in [dioptres](dioptre.md): $f=1\,\mathrm m$ gives $\Phi=+1\,\mathrm D$, while $f=-0.5\,\mathrm m$ gives $\Phi=-2\,\mathrm D$. The sign distinguishes converging and diverging [optical lens](lens-optics.md) elements; the magnitude measures how strongly they change the direction of a [light ray](light-ray.md) at a given height. [Urone and Hinrichs, OpenStax College Physics 2e, §25.6: Optical power and diopters](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/25-6-image-formation-by-lenses).

For two ideal [thin lens](thin-lens.md) elements in contact in air, [optical powers](optical-power.md) add: $\Phi_{\rm total}=\Phi_1+\Phi_2$. Thus $+2\,\mathrm D$ and $-1\,\mathrm D$ combine to give $+1\,\mathrm D$, or $f_{\rm total}=1\,\mathrm m$. Separation changes the rule: for two ideal [thin lens](thin-lens.md) elements separated by $d$ in air, their equivalent [optical power](optical-power.md) is $\Phi_1+\Phi_2-d\Phi_1\Phi_2$. The contact rule is the $d=0$ case.[§J: Composite lenses](mit-2-71-and-2-710-optics-spring-2014-by-nicholas-x-fang/lecture-notes-on-geometrical-optics.md)
