The [moment-generating function](../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) of a real [random variable](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) $X$ is

$$
\boxed{M_X(t)=\mathbb E[e^{tX}]}
$$

for real $t$ for which this [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) is finite. Because the integrand is nonnegative, the extended [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) is always defined, but it may equal $+\infty$. We always have $M_X(0)=1$; the usual uniqueness and convergence theorems require finiteness on an open [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing zero. When this condition holds, derivatives at zero recover the [moments](../../../../../moment.md), $M_X^{(r)}(0)=\mathbb E[X^r]$.
