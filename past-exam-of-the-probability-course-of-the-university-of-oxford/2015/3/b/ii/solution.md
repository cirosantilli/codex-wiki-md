<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $k=0$, the target is reached at time zero, so $h_0=1$. For $k<0$, the preceding [almost sure convergence](../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) gives $Y_n\to+\infty$ with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) one. A [sample path](../../../../../sample-path.md) with unit upward and downward steps must visit every intermediate [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) when it crosses from a negative value to a positive value. It must therefore visit zero at a finite time. Thus

$$
\boxed{h_k=1\quad\text{for every }k\leq0.}
$$

The unit-step property matters: positive long-run displacement alone would not force a walk with larger upward jumps to land exactly on zero.
