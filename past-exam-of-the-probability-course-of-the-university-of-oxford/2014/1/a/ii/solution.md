<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $\mu=\mathbb E[X_1]$ and $\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(X_1)\in(0,\infty)$. For [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md), the [central limit theorem](../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_n-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).}
$$

The limit is the [standard normal distribution](../../../../../standard-normal-distribution.md). If the [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) is zero, then $X_i=\mu$ [almost surely](../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md); the centered sums are zero, and division by $\sigma$ is not defined.
