<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Independence](../../../../independent-random-variables.md) makes the [moment-generating function](../../../../moment-generating-function.md) of the sum the product of the two [moment-generating functions](../../../../moment-generating-function.md). For $t<\lambda$,

$$
M_{X_1+X_2}(t)=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_2}
=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_1+r_2}.
$$

The [expectations](../../../../expected-value-split.md) are finite on, for example, $(-\lambda/2,\lambda/2)$, as is the [moment-generating function](../../../../moment-generating-function.md) of the candidate [Gamma distribution](../../../../gamma-distribution.md). The [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md) therefore applies, proving

$$
\boxed{X_1+X_2\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r_1+r_2,\lambda).}
$$

This is [additivity of independent gamma distributions with a common rate](../../../../additivity-of-independent-gamma-distributions-with-a-common-rate.md); a common rate is essential to this specific formula.
