<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write the [stationary distribution](../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) as a row [vector](../../../../../vector.md) $\pi=(a,b,c,d)$. For the first [transition matrix](../../../../../stochastic-matrix.md), $\pi P=\pi$ gives $a=a/2+d$, $b=a/2+b/2$, $c=b/2$ and $d=c$. Hence $b=a$ and $c=d=a/2$. Normalization yields

$$
\boxed{\pi=\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac16,\frac16\right).}
$$

By the stated common-[stationary distribution](../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) property, this is also the answer for the second [transition matrix](../../../../../stochastic-matrix.md).
