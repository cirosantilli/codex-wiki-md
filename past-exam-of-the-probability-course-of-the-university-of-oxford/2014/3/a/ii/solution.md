<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The first [Markov chain](../../../../../markov-chain-split.md) is a finite [irreducible Markov chain](../../../../../irreducible-markov-chain.md): its positive-probability directed cycle reaches all four states. The self-loop at state 1 makes it an [aperiodic Markov chain](../../../../../aperiodic-markov-chain.md). [Convergence to an invariant distribution](../../../../../convergence-to-an-invariant-distribution.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{p_{11}^{(n)}\longrightarrow\pi_1=\frac13\quad\text{for the first matrix}.}
$$

The second chain alternates between the [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,4\}$. It is a [periodic Markov chain](../../../../../periodic-markov-chain.md) with period two, so $p_{11}^{(2m+1)}=0$. From either state 2 or state 4 the next-step [probability](../../../../../probability.md) of entering state 1 is $2/3$. Therefore $p_{11}^{(2m)}=2/3$ for every $m\geq1$. The subsequences have different [limits](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md), so **the second return-probability sequence does not converge**.
