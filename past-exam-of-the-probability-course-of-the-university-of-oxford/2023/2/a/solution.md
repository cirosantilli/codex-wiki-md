The [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md) states that if $M_X$ and $M_Y$ are finite and equal on an open [real interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing zero, then

$$
\boxed{X\overset d=Y.}
$$

For the [convergence theorem for moment-generating functions](../../../../convergence-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md), suppose that the [moment-generating functions](../../../../moment-generating-function.md) $M_{X_n}$ are finite on a common open [real interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $(-a,a)$, $a>0$, and $M_{X_n}(t)\to M_X(t)$ for every $|t|<a$, where $M_X$ is finite there. Then

$$
\boxed{X_n\overset d\longrightarrow X.}
$$

One may equivalently assume convergence to a finite function $M$ on that interval: the theorem then asserts existence of a unique limiting [probability distribution](../../../../probability-distribution-split.md) with [moment-generating function](../../../../moment-generating-function.md) $M$ as well as [convergence in distribution](../../../../convergence-in-distribution.md).
