↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The inverse [transformation of a random variable](../../../../../transformation-of-a-random-variable.md) is $u=e^{-v}$, with absolute [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) $e^{-v}$. The condition $0<u<b$ becomes $v>-\log b$. The [change of variables for probability densities](../../../../../change-of-variables-for-probability-densities.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f_{V_n}(v)=\begin{cases}b^{-1}e^{-v},&v>-\log b,\\0,&v\leq-\log b.\end{cases}}
$$

Equivalently, $V_n+\log b$ has the [exponential distribution](../../../../../exponential-distribution.md) of rate one. Applying a separate measurable [transformation of a random variable](../../../../../transformation-of-a-random-variable.md) to each independent $U_n$ preserves [independence of random variables](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), so the $V_n$ are [independent and identically distributed](../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md).
