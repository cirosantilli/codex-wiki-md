## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2014](2014.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

[Convergence in distribution](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-distribution) means that the [cumulative distribution functions](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) satisfy

$$
\mathbb P(X_n\leq x)\longrightarrow\mathbb P(X\leq x)
$$

at every $x$ where the [cumulative distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) of $X$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). Values at its discontinuities need not converge. This definition uses only the [probability distributions](../probability-distribution.md), so the [random variables](../random-variable.md) need not be defined on the same [probability space](../probability-space.md).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Let $\mu=\mathbb E[X_1]$ and $\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(X_1)\in(0,\infty)$. For [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables), the [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) states

$$
\boxed{\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_n-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).}
$$

The limit is the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution). If the [variance](../variance.md) is zero, then $X_i=\mu$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence); the centered sums are zero, and division by $\sigma$ is not defined.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

The implication [convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-probability-implies-convergence-in-distribution) follows by comparing nearby [events](../probability-space.md#event). Fix a [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) point $x$ of the [cumulative distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) $F_X$ and let $\varepsilon>0$. If $X\leq x-\varepsilon$ and $|X_n-X|\leq\varepsilon$, then $X_n\leq x$. Conversely, $X_n\leq x$ and $|X_n-X|\leq\varepsilon$ imply $X\leq x+\varepsilon$. Thus

$$
F_X(x-\varepsilon)-\mathbb P(|X_n-X|>\varepsilon)
\leq F_{X_n}(x)
\leq F_X(x+\varepsilon)+\mathbb P(|X_n-X|>\varepsilon).
$$

The error [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) tends to zero by [convergence in probability](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-probability). Taking lower and upper [limits](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) and then letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) at $x$ squeezes both bounds to $F_X(x)$. This is exactly [convergence in distribution](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-distribution).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Subtract adjacent values of the [cumulative distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function). Its value at zero is zero, so the same calculation applies also to $k=1$:

$$
\boxed{p_k=\left(1-\frac1{k+1}\right)-\left(1-\frac1k\right)=\frac1{k(k+1)},\qquad k\geq1.}
$$

The [probability mass function](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass-function) sums to one by a [telescoping series](../sequence-and-series.md#telescoping-series).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

For $x>0$, the event $M_n/n\leq x$ says that each of the [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) is at most $\lfloor nx\rfloor$. Hence, for all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
\mathbb P(M_n/n\leq x)=\left(1-\frac1{\lfloor nx\rfloor+1}\right)^n.
$$

Since $n/(\lfloor nx\rfloor+1)\to1/x$ and $\log(1-u)=-u+o(u)$ as $u\to0$, taking [logarithms](../calculus.md#logarithm) gives the [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) $e^{-1/x}$. For $x\leq0$ the [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{M_n}{n}\xrightarrow{d}W,\qquad F_W(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\leq0,\\ e^{-1/x},&x>0.\end{cases}}
$$

This is the [Fréchet limit for a reciprocal integer tail](../extreme-value-theory.md#frechet-limit-for-a-reciprocal-integer-tail), with a [Fréchet distribution](../extreme-value-theory.md#frechet-distribution) of shape one. Its [cumulative distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) is continuous even at zero, so the pointwise calculation establishes [convergence in distribution](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-distribution).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Write $S_{n+1}=S_n+X_{n+1}$. The new [random variable](../random-variable.md) $X_{n+1}$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the entire history generated by $X_1,\ldots,X_n$, hence of $(S_1,\ldots,S_n)$. This is a [Markov chain obtained from independent innovations](../markov-chain.md#markov-chain-obtained-from-independent-innovations): conditional on the past, only the current sum enters the next state. Its time-homogeneous [transition probabilities](../markov-chain.md#transition-probability) are

$$
\boxed{P_{st}=\begin{cases}\dfrac1{(t-s)(t-s+1)},&t>s,\\0,&t\leq s,\end{cases}\qquad s,t\in\{1,2,\ldots\}.}
$$

Every jump is a positive integer, and each row sums to one by the [probability mass function](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass-function) from part (b).

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Now $M_{n+1}=\max(M_n,X_{n+1})$. Again $X_{n+1}$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the whole previous history, so the recursion is a [Markov chain obtained from independent innovations](../markov-chain.md#markov-chain-obtained-from-independent-innovations). To remain at $s$, the new observation must be at most $s$; to jump to $t>s$, it must equal $t$. Thus the [transition probabilities](../markov-chain.md#transition-probability) are

$$
\boxed{P_{st}=\begin{cases}0,&t<s,\\\dfrac{s}{s+1},&t=s,\\\dfrac1{t(t+1)},&t>s.\end{cases}}
$$

The upper tail sums to $1/(s+1)$, complementing the holding [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $s/(s+1)$.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2014](2014.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Integrating $e^{tx}$ against the [Gamma density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#gamma-density) gives the [moment-generating function of a gamma distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function-of-a-gamma-distribution):

$$
M_X(t)=\frac{\lambda^r}{(r-1)!}\int_0^\infty x^{r-1}e^{-(\lambda-t)x}\,dx
=\boxed{\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^r},\qquad t<\lambda.
$$

The [change of variables](../multivariable-calculus.md#change-of-variables-formula) $y=(\lambda-t)x$ reduces the integral to $\Gamma(r)=(r-1)!$ using the [gamma function](../complex-analysis.md#gamma-function). For $t\geq\lambda$ the defining integral is infinite. In particular the [moment-generating function](../probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function) is finite on a [neighbourhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of zero.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

[Independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) makes the [moment-generating function](../probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function) of the sum the product of the two [moment-generating functions](../probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function). For $t<\lambda$,

$$
M_{X_1+X_2}(t)=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_2}
=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}\right)^{r_1+r_2}.
$$

The [expectations](../expected-value.md) are finite on, for example, $(-\lambda/2,\lambda/2)$, as is the [moment-generating function](../probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function) of the candidate [Gamma distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#gamma-distribution). The [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](../probability-distribution.md#uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions) therefore applies, proving

$$
\boxed{X_1+X_2\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r_1+r_2,\lambda).}
$$

This is [additivity of independent gamma distributions with a common rate](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#additivity-of-independent-gamma-distributions-with-a-common-rate); a common rate is essential to this specific formula.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

An [exponential distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution) of rate $\lambda$ is a [Gamma distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#gamma-distribution) with shape one. Repeated [additivity of independent gamma distributions with a common rate](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#additivity-of-independent-gamma-distributions-with-a-common-rate) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{E_1+\cdots+E_n\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(n,\lambda).}
$$

With integer shape this is also called an [Erlang distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#erlang-distribution).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/d/i)

The [Poisson interarrival times](../poisson-process.md#poisson-interarrival-times) of a homogeneous [Poisson process](../poisson-process.md) are [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) with the [exponential distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution) of rate $\lambda$. Consequently $Y_1,Y_2$ are [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) and their [joint probability density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#joint-probability-density) is

$$
\boxed{f_{Y_1,Y_2}(y_1,y_2)=\lambda^2e^{-\lambda(y_1+y_2)}\mathbf1_{\{y_1>0,\ y_2>0\}}.}
$$

Here $Y_2$ is the second interarrival duration, rather than the time of the second arrival.

<h4 id="2/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/d/ii)

The inverse [change of variables](../multivariable-calculus.md#change-of-variables-formula) is $y_1=uv$, $y_2=(1-u)v$, on $0<u<1$, $v>0$. Its absolute [Jacobian determinant](../multivariable-calculus.md#jacobian-determinant) is

$$
\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}v&u\\-v&1-u\end{pmatrix}\right|=v.
$$

The [change of variables for probability densities](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#change-of-variables-for-probability-densities) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{f_{U,V}(u,v)=\lambda^2v e^{-\lambda v}\mathbf1_{\{0<u<1,\ v>0\}}.}
$$

This factorizes on a product domain into a unit [uniform density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) in $u$ and a shape-two [Gamma density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#gamma-density) in $v$. Thus **$U$ and $V$ are independent**, with $U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$ and $V\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,\lambda)$. This is the shape-$(1,1)$ case of [beta-gamma independence](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#beta-gamma-independence).

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

By part (c) with $\lambda=1$, let $T_n=E_1+\cdots+E_n\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(n,1)$ for [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) [exponential random variables](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution). The requested integral is $\mathbb P(T_n\leq n)$. Each summand has [expectation](../expected-value.md) one and [variance](../variance.md) one, so the [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) gives

$$
\frac{T_n-n}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).
$$

Zero is a [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) point of the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution), and consequently

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_0^n x^{n-1}e^{-x}\,dx=\Phi(0)=\frac12.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2014](2014.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Write the [stationary distribution](../markov-chain.md#stationary-distribution) as a row [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) $\pi=(a,b,c,d)$. For the first [transition matrix](../markov-chain.md#stochastic-matrix), $\pi P=\pi$ gives $a=a/2+d$, $b=a/2+b/2$, $c=b/2$ and $d=c$. Hence $b=a$ and $c=d=a/2$. Normalization yields

$$
\boxed{\pi=\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac16,\frac16\right).}
$$

By the stated common-[stationary distribution](../markov-chain.md#stationary-distribution) property, this is also the answer for the second [transition matrix](../markov-chain.md#stochastic-matrix).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

The first [Markov chain](../markov-chain.md) is a finite [irreducible Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#irreducible-markov-chain): its positive-probability directed cycle reaches all four states. The self-loop at state 1 makes it an [aperiodic Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#aperiodic-markov-chain). [Convergence to an invariant distribution](../markov-chain.md#convergence-to-an-invariant-distribution) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{p_{11}^{(n)}\longrightarrow\pi_1=\frac13\quad\text{for the first matrix}.}
$$

The second chain alternates between the [sets](../set.md) $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,4\}$. It is a [periodic Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#periodic-markov-chain) with period two, so $p_{11}^{(2m+1)}=0$. From either state 2 or state 4 the next-step [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) of entering state 1 is $2/3$. Therefore $p_{11}^{(2m)}=2/3$ for every $m\geq1$. The subsequences have different [limits](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function), so **the second return-probability sequence does not converge**.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Start the [Markov chain](../markov-chain.md) at a state $i$ and define the strictly positive [first return time](../markov-chain.md#first-return-time) $T_i^+=\inf\{n\geq1:X_n=i\}$. A [recurrent state](../markov-chain.md#recurrent-state) satisfies $\mathbb P_i(T_i^+<\infty)=1$; a [transient state](../markov-chain.md#transient-state) has this [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) strictly below one. Equivalently a [recurrent state](../markov-chain.md#recurrent-state) is visited infinitely often [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) when started there, whereas a [transient state](../markov-chain.md#transient-state) receives finitely many visits [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The [recurrence criterion by return probabilities](../markov-chain.md#recurrence-criterion-by-return-probabilities) gives the equivalent test

$$
i\text{ recurrent}\iff\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{ii}^{(n)}=\infty.
$$

States in a [communicating class](../markov-chain.md#communicating-class) share this classification. In an [irreducible Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#irreducible-markov-chain) all states communicate, so the chain is called a [recurrent Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#recurrent-markov-chain) if all its states are recurrent and a [transient Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#transient-markov-chain) if all are transient.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Represent the [simple random walk](../random-walk.md#simple-random-walk) by $X_n=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_n$, where the increments are [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) and satisfy $\mathbb P(\xi_k=1)=p$, $\mathbb P(\xi_k=-1)=1-p$. They are integrable and have [expectation](../expected-value.md) $2p-1$, so the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{X_n}{n}\longrightarrow\mu=2p-1\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

If $p\ne1/2$, this nonzero drift implies that $X_n$ eventually leaves every fixed finite interval and tends to the appropriate infinity. Hence it visits zero only finitely many times [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), so zero is a [transient state](../markov-chain.md#transient-state). By translation of the starting state, every state is transient: **the chain is transient for $p\ne1/2$**. This also covers the deterministic endpoint cases $p=0,1$, without assuming [Markov chain irreducibility](../markov-chain.md#irreducible-markov-chain) at those endpoints.

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

For the symmetric [simple random walk on the integer line](../random-walk.md#simple-random-walk-on-the-integer-line), odd return times are impossible. A return after $2m$ steps requires exactly $m$ positive increments, so

$$
p_{00}^{(2m)}=\binom{2m}{m}2^{-2m}\sim\frac1{\sqrt{\pi m}}
$$

by [Stirling's approximation](../real-analysis.md#stirling-formula). The resulting sum diverges by the [p-series](../sequence-and-series.md#p-series) criterion. The [recurrence criterion by return probabilities](../markov-chain.md#recurrence-criterion-by-return-probabilities) therefore makes zero a [recurrent state](../markov-chain.md#recurrent-state). The [simple random walk](../random-walk.md#simple-random-walk) is an [irreducible Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#irreducible-markov-chain) on $\mathbb Z$, so **the symmetric chain is recurrent**.

<h4 id="3/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/c/iii)

This is the [synchronous product of simple symmetric random walks](../markov-chain.md#synchronous-product-of-simple-symmetric-random-walks), rather than a walk that changes only one coordinate at a time. Independence of the three coordinate processes gives

$$
\mathbb P\bigl((X_{2m},Y_{2m},Z_{2m})=(0,0,0)\bigr)
=\left[\binom{2m}{m}2^{-2m}\right]^3
\sim\frac1{(\pi m)^{3/2}}.
$$

Odd-time return probabilities vanish. The [p-series](../sequence-and-series.md#p-series) with exponent $3/2$ converges, so the sum of all return probabilities is finite. By the [recurrence criterion by return probabilities](../markov-chain.md#recurrence-criterion-by-return-probabilities), the origin is a [transient state](../markov-chain.md#transient-state): **the three-dimensional process is transient**. From the origin its [communicating class](../markov-chain.md#communicating-class) consists of integer triples whose coordinates have the same parity; translation gives the same classification for every other class.
