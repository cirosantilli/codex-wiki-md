<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The update defines a time-homogeneous [Markov process](../../../../markov-process-split.md): given its present value, the next value depends only on independent fresh input. Part (b)(i) identifies an invariant law: initializing with the rate-one [exponential distribution](../../../../exponential-distribution.md) preserves that distribution at every time. Part (b)(iii) shows convergence of the one-time laws to this invariant law from every deterministic initial value.

For a countable [state space](../../../../state-space.md), the analogous [countable-state Markov chain convergence theorem](../../../../countable-state-markov-chain-convergence-theorem.md) says that an [irreducible Markov chain](../../../../irreducible-markov-chain.md) that is [aperiodic](../../../../aperiodic-markov-chain.md) and [positive recurrent](../../../../positive-recurrent-markov-chain.md) converges to its unique [stationary distribution](../../../../stationary-distribution.md) from every initial state. Here the state space is continuous, so that countable-state theorem does not directly supply the proof; the explicit [coupling](../../../../coupling.md) does. The two conclusions have the same equilibrium interpretation.
