<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $Y_1,Y_2,\ldots$ be real [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with finite [expected value](../../../../../expected-value-split.md) $\mu$ and [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) $0<\sigma^2<\infty$. The [central limit theorem](../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^nY_k-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\xrightarrow dN(0,1).}
$$

Equivalently, the [cumulative distribution functions](../../../../../cumulative-distribution-function.md) of these normalized sums converge at every real point to the [standard normal distribution function](../../../../../standard-normal-distribution-function.md). The theorem itself does not require a finite moment-generating function.
