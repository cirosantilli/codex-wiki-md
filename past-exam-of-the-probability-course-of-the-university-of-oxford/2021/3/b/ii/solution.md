<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A return path with $u$ upward steps and $v$ downward steps satisfies $au=bv$. Since $a,b$ are [coprime](../../../../../coprime-integers.md), this is equivalent to $u=bk$ and $v=ak$ for an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $k\geq1$. Its length is therefore $k(a+b)$.

Conversely, for each such $k$, any ordering of $bk$ upward and $ak$ downward steps is an allowed positive-[probability](../../../../../probability.md) return path. The possible strictly positive return times are exactly the positive multiples of $a+b$. By the definition of the [period of a state in a Markov chain](../../../../../period-of-a-state-in-a-markov-chain.md) as their [greatest common divisor](../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md),

$$
\boxed{\text{Every state has period }a+b\geq2.}
$$

In particular, none of the states is [aperiodic](../../../../../aperiodic-markov-chain.md).
