The [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md) states: if two real [random variables](../../../../random-variable-split.md) $V,W$ have finite [moment-generating functions](../../../../moment-generating-function.md) on a common open [interval](../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $(-\delta,\delta)$ with $\delta>0$, and

$$
M_V(t)=\mathbb E e^{tV}=\mathbb E e^{tW}=M_W(t)\qquad(|t|<\delta),
$$

then $\boxed{V\text{ and }W\text{ have the same probability distribution}.}$ Finiteness throughout a neighborhood of zero is a hypothesis; the identity $M_V(0)=M_W(0)=1$ alone carries no information.
