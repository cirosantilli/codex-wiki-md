# Moment of inertia of a polytropic star

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar polytrope](stellar-polytrope.md)

For a spherical [stellar polytrope](stellar-polytrope.md), the axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) is $I=(8\pi/3)\int_0^R\rho(r)r^4\,dr$. With [Lane-Emden variables for a stellar polytrope](lane-emden-variables-for-a-stellar-polytrope.md), first zero $\xi_1$ and [Lane-Emden surface mass constant](lane-emden-surface-mass-constant.md) $\omega_n$, its dimensionless form is

$$
\frac{I}{MR^2}=\frac{2}{3\omega_n\xi_1^2}\int_0^{\xi_1}\xi^4\theta(\xi)^n\,d\xi.
$$

A [polytrope of index zero](polytrope-of-index-zero.md) gives $2/5$, while a [polytrope of index one](polytrope-of-index-one.md) gives $(2/3)(1-6/\pi^2)$. The latter is smaller because more of the mass lies near the center. The scalar second mass moment $\int r^2dm$ is $3I/2$, not the axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md).
