# Vibrational overtone

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular vibration](molecular-vibration.md)

A [vibrational overtone](vibrational-overtone.md) excites more than one vibrational quantum: $v=0\to2$ is the first overtone, and $0\to3$ the second. Such transitions vanish in the combined harmonic-potential and linear-dipole approximation. [Anharmonicity](anharmonicity.md) changes [wavefunctions](wave-function-split.md) and level spacing; nonlinear terms in the [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) can also supply otherwise missing transition [matrix elements](matrix-element.md).[Lecture 14, overtone section, Table 1 and following potential-expansion discussion](14-harmonic-oscillators-and-ir-spectroscopy-ucd-chem-110a-by-delmar-s-larsen.md)

For an original illustrative level model, set

$$
E_v-V_0=\hbar\omega\left[(v+1/2)-\epsilon(v+1/2)^2\right],\qquad 0<\epsilon\ll1.
$$

Then $E_1-E_0=\hbar\omega(1-2\epsilon)$ and $E_2-E_0=2\hbar\omega(1-3\epsilon)$. With chosen $\epsilon=0.02$, the fundamental gap is $0.96\hbar\omega$ and the first-overtone gap $1.88\hbar\omega$, below twice the fundamental, $1.92\hbar\omega$. This low-level illustration does not determine transition intensity or justify extrapolation to arbitrarily large $v$. It shows why even a well-resolved fundamental band origin need not equal the harmonic curvature scale $\widetilde\nu_{\rm harm}$.
