# Momentum relaxation time

↑ **Parent:** [Drude model](drude-model.md)

The [momentum relaxation time](momentum-relaxation-time.md) is the [exponential relaxation time](exponential-relaxation-time.md) of mean directed [momentum](momentum-split.md) after the driving [electric field](electric-field-split.md) is removed. In the simplest [Drude model](drude-model.md), independent [collisions](collision.md) occur with probability $dt/\tau$ during a short [time](time-in-physics.md) interval $dt$ and reset the mean [velocity](velocity.md) of the collided population to zero. The remaining fraction preserves its previous mean [momentum](momentum-split.md). To first order in $dt$, averaging gives $d\langle\mathbf p\rangle/dt=q\mathbf E-\langle\mathbf p\rangle/\tau$. This is a population equation: it does not assert that every particle stops at equally spaced instants.[pp. 2–3, Eqs. (1.2)–(1.4): constant collision probability and vanishing post-collision mean momentum](lecture-notes-in-solid-state-3-classical-free-electron-model-for-metals-the-drude-model-by-eytan-grosfeld.md)

For real materials, a [collision](collision.md) that changes a particle's direction only slightly need not erase its directed [momentum](momentum-split.md), so the [momentum relaxation time](momentum-relaxation-time.md) need not equal the time between all scattering events. This transport time is also distinct from the bulk [charge relaxation](charge-relaxation.md) time $\epsilon/\sigma$: one describes loss of directed motion, the other redistribution of [charge density](charge-density.md).
