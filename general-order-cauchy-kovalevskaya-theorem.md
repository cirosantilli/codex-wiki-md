# General-order Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md)

A [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) system solved for the highest [normal derivatives](normal-derivative.md), with [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) [Cauchy data](cauchy-data.md), has a unique local [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) solution. For common order $m$, its normal form is $\partial_t^mU=F(t,x,\{\partial_t^j\partial_x^\alpha U:j<m,\ j+|\alpha|\le m\})$. An invertible coefficient/Jacobian matrix for the highest [normal derivatives](normal-derivative.md) is the system's [non-characteristic hypersurface](non-characteristic-hypersurface.md) condition. A [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) coordinate change flattens the initial [hypersurface](hypersurface.md), and adjoining [derivatives](derivative.md) through order $m-1$ reduces the problem to a first-order [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) system. Uniqueness here is in the [real analytic](real-analytic-function.md) class; it does not assert [Hadamard well-posedness](well-posed-problem.md) in smooth or Sobolev [norms](norm.md).
