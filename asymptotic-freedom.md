# Asymptotic freedom

↑ **Parent:** [Beta function (physics)](beta-function-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_freedom)

An asymptotically free theory has a [running coupling](running-coupling.md) that tends to zero at arbitrarily high energy. Its negative weak-coupling beta function makes short-distance perturbation theory increasingly accurate.

In [Quantum chromodynamics](quantum-chromodynamics.md), this means that a short-distance probe can resolve [quarks](quark.md) with a small [QCD coupling](strong-coupling-constant.md), even though isolated [quarks](quark.md) are absent from the observed [hadron](hadron.md) spectrum. This made [asymptotic freedom](asymptotic-freedom.md) a bridge between the approximately free constituents of the [parton model](parton-model.md) and an interacting [quantum field theory](quantum-field-theory-split.md). [Asymptotic freedom](asymptotic-freedom.md) supplies control at large [momentum transfer](momentum-transfer.md); it does not itself prove [confinement](confinement.md).[Sections 1.1 and 2, pp. 5–6; 2005 version of the 2004 lecture](asymptotic-freedom-from-paradox-to-paradigm-by-frank-wilczek.md)

Use the four-dimensional [QCD coupling](strong-coupling-constant.md) convention $\alpha_s=g_s^2/(4\pi)$. For the [SU(3)](su-3-group.md) [gauge group](gauge-group.md) and $n_f$ active [quark flavors](quark-flavor.md) in the fundamental [representation](group-representation.md), the one-[loop order](loop-order.md) [beta function](beta-function-physics.md) is

$$
\mu\frac{dg_s}{d\mu}=-\frac{\beta_0g_s^3}{16\pi^2}+O(g_s^5),\qquad
\beta_0=11-\frac{2n_f}{3},\qquad
\frac{d\alpha_s}{d\ln\mu^2}=-\frac{\beta_0}{4\pi}\alpha_s^2+O(\alpha_s^3).
$$

Here $n_f$ counts [quark flavors](quark-flavor.md), not the three values of [color charge](color-charge.md). The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) converts the first equation to the last; changing from $\ln\mu$ to $\ln\mu^2$ supplies a factor of two. For $\beta_0>0$, the negative sign makes $g_s=0$ an attractive high-[energy](energy-split.md) [renormalization-group fixed point](renormalization-group-fixed-point.md).[1973, p. 1344, Eqs. 8–9; SU(3) fermion example](ultraviolet-behavior-of-non-abelian-gauge-theories-by-david-j-gross-and-frank-wilczek.md)

For $\beta_0>0$, set the [renormalization scale](renormalization-scale.md) $\mu=Q$, where $Q$ is a positive [energy](energy-split.md) scale. The [one-loop running of the strong coupling](one-loop-running-of-the-strong-coupling.md) then gives

$$
\alpha_s(Q)=\frac{4\pi}{\beta_0\ln(Q^2/\Lambda^2)}.
$$

The [strong-coupling scale](strong-coupling-scale.md) $\Lambda$ has the same [units of measurement](unit-of-measurement.md) as $Q$, so the [logarithm](logarithm.md) has a [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md) argument. This is a fixed-$n_f$ approximation on the positive branch $Q>\Lambda$; controlled [perturbative quantum field theory](perturbative-quantum-field-theory-split.md) requires a small [QCD coupling](strong-coupling-constant.md) and suitably small omitted corrections, not merely a positive denominator. Heavy [quark masses](quark-mass.md) require [matching the QCD scale across a quark threshold](matching-the-qcd-scale-across-a-quark-threshold.md), changing $n_f$ and the associated $\Lambda$. Both the [renormalization scheme](renormalization-scheme.md) and the [loop order](loop-order.md) matter when quoting a numerical $\Lambda$.
