# Euclidean geometry

↑ **Parent:** [Geometry and topology](geometry-and-topology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_geometry)

[Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry.md) describes flat [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) using [lengths](length.md), [angles](angle.md), and [rigid motions](rigid-transformation.md). A coordinate realization uses points $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$, [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md)

$$
d(x,y)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-y_i)^2},
$$

and [angles](angle.md) determined by the [dot product](dot-product.md). The coordinate axes are a convenience: [translations](translation-geometry.md) and [rotations](rotation-mathematics.md) change coordinates while preserving these measurements. This makes [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry.md) useful for comparing figures and converting measurements along different directions into a single distance.

In the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md), through a point outside a line there is exactly one parallel line. This is a standard equivalent formulation of the [parallel postulate](parallel-postulate.md). Euclid's fifth postulate in the [Elements](euclid-s-elements.md) instead describes when two lines meet using the interior [angles](angle.md) made by a crossing line.[Euclid, Elements I, Postulate 5](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/post5.html) The assumption has consequences we often take for granted: a [triangle](triangle.md)'s interior [angles](angle.md) sum to $\pi$, and [similar triangles](similar-triangle.md) can differ in size while keeping their [angles](angle.md). Comparing with [spherical geometry](spherical-geometry.md) reveals that these are properties of a particular geometry.

As a computed example, the diagonal from $(0,0)$ to $(4,3)$ in a [rectangle](rectangle.md) has [length](length.md) $\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$. The [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) explains why perpendicular contributions add as squares. If the directions cease to meet at a [right angle](right-angle.md), the [law of cosines](law-of-cosines.md) supplies the missing [angle](angle.md)-dependent term. The beauty is that a single distance formula unites geometric constructions, coordinate calculations, and [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md) in [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md).

Make the assumptions visible: in a [Cartesian coordinate system](cartesian-coordinate-system.md) with [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) axes and the same [length](length.md) unit on each axis, take $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,0)$ and $C=(0,3)$. The [vectors](vector.md) $B-A=(4,0)$ and $C-A=(0,3)$ have [dot product](dot-product.md) zero, establishing a [right angle](right-angle.md) at $A$ before using the [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md). A sketch that merely looks like a [right triangle](right-triangle.md) supplies no such guarantee. Likewise, an image stretched horizontally and vertically by different amounts can distort both [lengths](length.md) and [angles](angle.md); a displayed shape and the assumptions defining it must be kept separate.

For a less origin-dependent measurement, take $P=(1,2)$ and $Q=(5,5)$. Their displacement [vector](vector.md) is $Q-P=(4,3)$, so their [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md) is again $5$. Its [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) records the direct separation, whereas travelling first horizontally and then vertically covers [length](length.md) $4+3=7$. This connects measuring a diagonal to locating two positions from a [Cartesian coordinate system](cartesian-coordinate-system.md). A [translation](translation-geometry.md) changes where the positions are recorded without changing their separation; a [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) changes the component values without changing the [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md).
