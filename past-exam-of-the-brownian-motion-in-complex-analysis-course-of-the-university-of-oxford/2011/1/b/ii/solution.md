<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Start a [planar Brownian motion](../../../../../planar-brownian-motion.md) $B$ at zero and apply part (a) to the entire function $f(z)=e^z$. Then

$$
e^{B_t}=1+\widetilde Z_{A_t},\qquad
A_t=\int_0^t e^{2\operatorname{Re}B_s}\,ds.
$$

The left side never vanishes. The clock is continuous, starts at zero, and tends to infinity almost surely, so its range is all nonnegative times. Consequently the complex Brownian motion $1+\widetilde Z_s$ never reaches zero at any time $s$. Every [planar Brownian motion](../../../../../planar-brownian-motion.md) started at one has that same law, hence

$$
\boxed{P(T<\infty)=0.}
$$

This is the [complex exponential construction of planar Brownian motion](../../../../../complex-exponential-construction-of-planar-brownian-motion.md) and proves that a fixed point is a [polar point for planar Brownian motion](../../../../../polar-point-for-planar-brownian-motion.md). The divergence of the clock is essential: it makes this a conclusion about all Brownian times.
