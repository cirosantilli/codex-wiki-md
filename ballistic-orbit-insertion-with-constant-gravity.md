# Ballistic orbit insertion with constant gravity

↑ **Parent:** [Projectile motion](projectile-motion.md)

In the flat-surface, constant-[gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) model, a coasting projectile conserves horizontal [velocity](velocity.md). If it arrives at the top of its trajectory with horizontal [speed](speed.md) $v_o$, its earlier [velocity](velocity.md) at [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ above the horizontal has vertical component $v_o\tan\theta$. Its remaining height gain is therefore $v_o^2\tan^2\theta/(2g)$. The displayed burnout height follows. This is a local [projectile motion](projectile-motion.md) approximation, not an exact [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md). Computing $v_o=\sqrt{g(R_E+h_o)}$ assumes constant gravitational magnitude also at the orbit; computing $v_o=\sqrt{g_0R_E^2/(R_E+h_o)}$ instead assumes an [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) with surface value $g_0$. Those assumptions must be distinguished.
