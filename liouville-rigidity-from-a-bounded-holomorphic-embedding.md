# Liouville rigidity from a bounded holomorphic embedding

↑ **Parent:** [Liouville theorem](liouville-theorem.md)

Suppose $D\subseteq\mathbb C$ is [open](open-set.md) and admits an [injective](injective-function.md) [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) map $\Phi:D\to\mathbb C$ whose [image](image-of-a-function.md) is [bounded](boundedness.md). Every [entire function](entire-function.md) $f:\mathbb C\to D$ is constant: the [function composition](function-composition.md) $\Phi\circ f$ is a [bounded](boundedness.md) [entire function](entire-function.md), hence constant by [Liouville theorem](liouville-theorem.md), and [injectivity](injective-function.md) of $\Phi$ then forces $f$ to be constant. Surjectivity of $\Phi$ onto a disk is unnecessary. For example $z\mapsto z/(1-z)$ is a [conformal map](conformal-map.md) from $\mathbb C\setminus[0,1]$ onto $\bigl(\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)\bigr)\setminus\{-1\}$. A square-root branch with argument in $(0,2\pi)$ followed by the [Cayley transform between the half-plane and disk](cayley-transform-between-the-half-plane-and-disk.md) maps this to the punctured unit disk. It therefore supplies a [bounded](boundedness.md) [injective](injective-function.md) [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) coordinate on the interval complement, giving the same rigidity even though that domain is not simply connected.
