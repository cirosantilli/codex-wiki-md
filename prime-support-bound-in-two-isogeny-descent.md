# Prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent

↑ **Parent:** [Two-isogeny descent](two-isogeny-descent.md)

For integral $a,b$ with $b(a^2-4b)\ne0$, every square class in the image of the [two-isogeny descent](two-isogeny-descent.md) map on $y^2=x(x^2+ax+b)$ has a signed square-free representative supported on [primes](prime-number.md) dividing $b$. At a [prime](prime-number.md) not dividing $b$, a positive [valuation](valuation.md) of $x$ must be even from the curve equation; a negative [valuation](valuation.md) is even because the cubic leading term determines $2v(y)=3v(x)$. Thus its image has at most $2^{\nu(b)+1}$ elements. Apply this also to the two-isogenous curve and use the [square-class index formula for two-isogeny descent](square-class-index-formula-for-two-isogeny-descent.md) to obtain $r\leq\nu(b)+\nu(a^2-4b)$, where $\nu$ counts distinct [prime](prime-number.md) divisors of the nonzero absolute value.
