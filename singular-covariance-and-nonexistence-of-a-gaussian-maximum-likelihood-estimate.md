# Singular covariance and nonexistence of a Gaussian maximum likelihood estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Maximum likelihood estimation](maximum-likelihood-estimation.md)

For independent $p$-variate [multivariate normal](multivariate-normal-distribution.md) observations with unknown mean and unrestricted [positive-definite](positive-definite-bilinear-form.md) [covariance matrix](covariance-matrix.md), the centered scatter has [rank](rank-one-quadratic-form.md) at most $n-1$. If it is singular, choose the mean to be the [sample mean](sample-mean.md) and shrink the [covariance](covariance.md) on a null direction of the scatter. The quadratic [likelihood](likelihood-function.md) term stays fixed while the [determinant](determinant.md) tends to zero, so the density [likelihood](likelihood-function.md) tends to infinity. Thus a nonsingular [covariance](covariance.md) maximum [likelihood](likelihood-function.md) estimate exists only when the centered scatter is positive definite; for a nonsingular [Gaussian](normal-distribution.md) population this occurs almost surely exactly when $n>p$.
