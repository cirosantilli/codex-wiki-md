# Absolutely summable sequence space

↑ **Parent:** [L-p sequence space](l-p-sequence-space.md)

The [Banach space](banach-space-split.md) $\ell^1$ consists of absolutely summable scalar sequences, with [norm](norm.md) $\|x\|_1=\sum_n|x_n|$. Its continuous dual is $\ell^\infty$, under the pairing $\sum_nx_ny_n$. Finitely supported sequences are [norm](norm.md) dense, which identifies a [linear functional](linear-functional.md) from its values on coordinate vectors. The [space of sequences converging to zero](space-of-sequences-converging-to-zero.md) inside this dual is a [norming subspace](norming-subspace-of-a-dual-space.md) of infinite codimension.

For example, $x=(2^{-1},2^{-2},\ldots)$ belongs to the [absolutely summable sequence space](absolutely-summable-sequence-space.md) and has [norm](norm.md) one, by the [geometric series](geometric-series.md) formula. Merely tending to zero is insufficient: $(1/n)$ tends to zero but its [series](series-mathematics.md) of absolute values diverges. Thus this [sequence space](sequence-space.md) records total absolute size, rather than just the eventual size of individual coordinates. Its [completeness](completeness.md) ensures that a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) in this total-error [norm](norm.md) has an absolutely summable limit.[Michael Jury, MAA6617 course notes, section 20.1(b): Banach sequence spaces](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/mjury/files/6617notes30nov2015.pdf)
