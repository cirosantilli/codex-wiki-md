# Glycine titration

↑ **Parent:** [Glycine protonation](glycine-protonation.md)

Adding [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) to acidified [glycine](glycine.md) successively removes its two titratable [protons](proton.md). Two broad [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md) regions appear around the two [pKa](pka.md) values, separated by a steep [pH](ph.md) rise near the [isoelectric point](isoelectric-point.md). This is useful evidence for two separated [acid-base chemistry](acid-base-chemistry.md) steps even though the changing [chemical species](chemical-species.md) are invisible. In the approximation that added [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) only changes [glycine protonation](glycine-protonation.md), $b=1-\bar z$ is the added base [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) per initial [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of [glycine](glycine.md). Then $b\approx0.5$, $1$ and $1.5$ correspond to $\mathrm pK_{a1}$, $\mathrm{pI}$ and $\mathrm pK_{a2}$; endpoints are not exactly pure [chemical species](chemical-species.md). [§26.2, Figure 26.2: calculated two-leg titration and half-equivalent interpretation](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry.md)

For the solid-curve model, start with $n_G=0.00200\,\mathrm{mol}$ of [glycine](glycine.md) in $V_0=0.0200\,\mathrm L$ and exactly $n_G$ of fully dissociated [hydrochloric acid](hydrochloric-acid.md); this fixes the [chloride](chloride.md) inventory, but does not force every [glycine](glycine.md) [molecule](molecule.md) to remain $G^+$. Let the [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) [concentration](concentration.md) be $C_B=0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $b=n_{\mathrm{NaOH}}/n_G$. Assume [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) is additive and no other [ions](ion.md) are present; stipulate $K_w=10^{-14}$ for [self-ionization of water](self-ionization-of-water.md) at a [temperature](temperature.md) of $25\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}$. Then

$$
V=V_0+\frac{bn_G}{C_B},\qquad C_T=\frac{n_G}{V},\qquad
[\mathrm{Na^+}]=bC_T,\qquad[\mathrm{Cl^-}]=C_T.
$$

Balancing all positive and negative [electric charges](electric-charge.md) requires

$$
c^\circ\left(h-\frac{K_w}{h}\right)+C_T\left(b+\bar z-1\right)=0.
$$

Solving this equation gives the solid curve. It remains finite at $b=0$ and beyond $b=2$, where ignoring free [hydronium](hydronium.md) and [hydroxide](hydroxide.md) would fail. At the first [pKa](pka.md), the full model gives $b\approx0.436$, rather than $0.5$: appreciable free [hydronium](hydronium.md) makes that half-equivalent shortcut inaccurate. At the second [pKa](pka.md), $b\approx1.502$. Known acid inventory, [dilution](dilution.md), and [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md) assumptions are therefore part of any quantitative interpretation, not optional corrections inferred from the curve's shape.
