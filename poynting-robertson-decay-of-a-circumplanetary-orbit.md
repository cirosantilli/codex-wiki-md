<h1 id="poynting-robertson-decay-of-a-circumplanetary-orbit">Poynting–Robertson decay of a circumplanetary orbit</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting–Robertson drag](poynting-robertson-drag.md)

Consider a circular [circumplanetary orbit](circumplanetary-orbit.md) well inside the [Hill sphere](hill-sphere.md), with dust speed $u^2=GM_p/a$ and unit plane normal $\widehat{\boldsymbol k}$. During one dust [orbital period](orbital-period.md), take the stellar direction $\widehat{\boldsymbol R}$ and flux at the [planet](planet.md)'s stellar radius $a_p$ as constant. The velocity-dependent [Poynting–Robertson drag](poynting-robertson-drag.md) has relative [work](work.md)

$$
\langle\dot\varepsilon_p\rangle=-\frac A{a_p^2}
\langle u^2+(\boldsymbol u\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol R})^2\rangle,\qquad
A=\frac{\beta GM_\star}c.
$$

The constant terms have zero [work](work.md) over the closed circular [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md). Since $\langle u_i u_j\rangle=(u^2/2)(\delta_{ij}-k_i k_j)$,

$$
\langle\dot a\rangle=-\frac A{a_p^2}a
[3-(\widehat{\boldsymbol k}\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol R})^2].
$$

The coefficient is three for coplanar stellar and dust [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md); it is two for a dust plane normal to the instantaneous stellar direction. Averaging over a circular planetary year at fixed dust [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md) $I$ gives $3-\sin^2I/2$. Conservative perturbations must remain small enough for this circular-orbit average.
