# Hyperbolic Pythagorean theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Hyperbolic geometry](hyperbolic-geometry.md)

For a right-angled geodesic triangle in curvature minus one, with legs $a,b$ and hypotenuse $c$, the displayed identity replaces the Euclidean theorem. Normalize the right vertex to $i$ in the upper half-plane, one endpoint to $iy$ with $y>1$, and the other to $s+it$ with $s^2+t^2=1$ and $t>0$. The [Hyperbolic distance in the Poincare half-plane](hyperbolic-distance-in-the-poincare-half-plane.md) gives $\cosh a=(y+y^{-1})/2$, $\cosh b=1/t$ and $\cosh c=(1+y^2)/(2yt)$, proving the identity. For small lengths its expansion gives $c^2=a^2+b^2$ to leading order.
