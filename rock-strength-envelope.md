# Rock strength envelope

↑ **Parent:** [Rheology](rheology-split.md)

A depth-dependent estimate of the [differential stress](differential-stress.md) a [rock](rock-geology.md) column can sustain at a specified [strain rate](strain-rate.md), composition, thermal structure and fluid state. In a simple competing-mechanism model, failure occurs by whichever of [frictional sliding](frictional-sliding.md) and ductile [material creep](creep-deformation.md) requires the lower [stress](stress.md), giving the displayed lower envelope.

Effective confining [pressure](pressure-split.md) generally increases [frictional strength](frictional-strength.md) with depth. In contrast, a [power-law creep](power-law-creep.md) model $\dot\epsilon=A\sigma^n\exp[-(Q+PV^*)/(RT)]$ gives $\sigma_{\rm creep}=(\dot\epsilon/A)^{1/n}\exp[(Q+PV^*)/(nRT)]$. At fixed [strain rate](strain-rate.md), the rise of [temperature](temperature-split.md) along a [geothermal gradient](geothermal-gradient.md) generally lowers the required creep [stress](stress.md). Their crossing is a [brittle-ductile transition](brittle-ductile-transition.md), while composition changes at the [Moho](mohorovicic-discontinuity.md) can produce additional peaks. A [rock strength envelope](rock-strength-envelope.md) is not uniquely determined by depth or by the labels “continental” and “oceanic”.
