# Invariant form on an irreducible sl2 module

↑ **Parent:** [Classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations](classification-of-finite-dimensional-sl2-representations.md)

In the irreducible [sl2 Lie algebra](sl2-lie-algebra.md) module $V(m)$, use $v_j=f^jv_0$, with $0\le j\le m$. The [Lie-invariant bilinear form](lie-invariant-bilinear-form.md) $B(v_j,v_k)=(-1)^j\mathbf1_{\{j+k=m\}}$ is [nondegenerate](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md): its anti-diagonal is nonzero. Invariance follows from $hv_j=(m-2j)v_j$, $fv_j=v_{j+1}$ and $ev_j=j(m-j+1)v_{j-1}$. Its transpose equals $(-1)^mB$, so it is a [symmetric bilinear form](symmetric-bilinear-form.md) when $m$ is an [even number](even-number.md), and an [alternating bilinear form](alternating-bilinear-form.md) when $m$ is an [odd integer](odd-integer.md). The [Weyl complete reducibility theorem](weyl-complete-reducibility-theorem.md) gives a nondegenerate invariant form on any finite-dimensional module by taking the orthogonal [direct sum](direct-sum.md) of these forms.
