## Thermoelectric effect

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoelectric_effect)

A [thermoelectric effect](#thermoelectric-effect) couples heat flow and electrical transport. Temperature differences can generate an electromotive force, while [electric currents](#electric-current) can transport heat. These effects concern nonequilibrium transport and must be distinguished from equilibrium contact potentials.

### Seebeck effect

↑ **Parent:** [Thermoelectric effect](#thermoelectric-effect)

The [Seebeck effect](#seebeck-effect) is the generation of a thermoelectric voltage by a temperature difference. Two materials with different thermopowers form a circuit whose electromotive force is the temperature integral of their Seebeck-coefficient difference, with sign set by circuit orientation. In a nondegenerate [semiconductor](physics.md#semiconductor), [Electron](physics.md#electron) and hole transport usually give opposite thermopower signs.

#### Seebeck coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Seebeck effect](#seebeck-effect)

The [Seebeck coefficient](#seebeck-coefficient) is thermoelectric voltage per temperature difference in the small-gradient limit, with its sign depending on the stated voltage and orientation convention. In a two-material loop the net electromotive force is an oriented integral $\int_{T_1}^{T_2}(S_1-S_2)\,dT$. It is a transport coefficient, so equilibrium density-of-states data alone do not determine it.

## Lorenz-gauge reduction of the electromagnetic action

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

The [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor) action differs from the component-gradient potential action by $(2\mu_0)^{-1}\int \partial^bA^a\partial_aA_b\,d^4x$. Integration by parts expresses this term as a boundary integral plus $(2\mu_0)^{-1}\int(\partial_aA^a)^2\,d^4x$. With the [Lorenz gauge](#lorenz-gauge-condition) and fixed or vanishing boundary data, the two actions therefore differ only by a boundary term. Varying the component-gradient action without restriction gives the potential wave equations; the Lorenz condition must be supplied to recover all [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations).

### Volume conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Bioelectromagnetism](#bioelectromagnetism)

A [volume conductor](#volume-conductor) is a conducting three-dimensional medium through which [electric currents](#electric-current) flow. In the quasistatic bioelectric model, the total [current density](#current-density) is $\mathbf J=\mathbf J^p-\sigma\nabla u$, comprising an impressed source current and its Ohmic return current. [Electric charge conservation](#charge-conservation) gives $\nabla\cdot(\sigma\nabla u)=\nabla\cdot\mathbf J^p$. An insulating boundary has zero normal total current.

#### Geselowitz formula

↑ **Parent:** [Volume conductor](#volume-conductor)

For a homogeneous [volume conductor](#volume-conductor) with an insulating boundary $S$, the [Geselowitz formula](#geselowitz-formula) expresses the exterior [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) as the primary [Biot-Savart law](#biot-savart-law) field minus a surface contribution from the induced [electric potential](#electric-potential). For a [current dipole](#current-dipole), $\mathbf B^p=\mu_0\mathbf Q\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0)/(4\pi|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|^3)$. On a spherical boundary, $\mathbf n'$ is parallel to $\mathbf r'$, so the surface term has zero radial component at the observation point.

#### Spherical volume conductor model

↑ **Parent:** [Volume conductor](#volume-conductor)

A [spherical volume conductor model](#spherical-volume-conductor-model) approximates a conducting body by a sphere, usually with uniform or radially stratified conductivity. It provides explicit forward models for [electroencephalography](biology.md#electroencephalography) and [magnetoencephalography](biology.md#magnetoencephalography). Its symmetry makes a [radial current dipole](#radial-current-dipole) magnetically silent outside the sphere; departures from spherical symmetry need not retain that property.

##### Sarvas formula

↑ **Parent:** [Spherical volume conductor model](#spherical-volume-conductor-model)

For the exterior of a [spherical volume conductor model](#spherical-volume-conductor-model), put $P=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|$, $r=|\mathbf r|$ and $F=rP^2+P\mathbf r\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0)$. The displayed [Sarvas formula](#sarvas-formula) follows by taking the radial component of the [Geselowitz formula](#geselowitz-formula) and integrating the [magnetic scalar potential](#magnetic-scalar-potential) from infinity. It is independent of a spherically symmetric conductivity profile. The sign shown uses $\mathbf B=+\mu_0\nabla U/(4\pi)$; conventions defining a magnetic potential with a minus sign reverse $U$. [Sarvas's original paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3823129/) derives the spherical-conductor field and its consequences for the inverse problem.

###### Spherical current-dipole logarithmic kernel

↑ **Parent:** [Sarvas formula](#sarvas-formula)

For $r>b$ and $P=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|$, the displayed kernel satisfies $\phi=\int_r^\infty ds/[s|s\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf r_0|]$. The limit at $b=0$ is $1/r$. Differentiating along $\mathbf Q\times\mathbf r_0$, which preserves $b$, gives $(\mathbf Q\times\mathbf r_0)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf r_0}\phi=(\mathbf Q\times\mathbf r_0)\cdot\mathbf r/F$. Thus it generates the normalized [magnetic scalar potential](#magnetic-scalar-potential) in the [Sarvas formula](#sarvas-formula).

<h2 id="maxwell-chern-simons-one-form-equation">Maxwell–Chern–Simons one-form equation</h2>

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

For a [differential one-form](differential-form.md#one-form) $A$ on an oriented three-dimensional spacetime, varying $\frac12\int(F\wedge*F+kA\wedge F)$, with $F=dA$, gives $d*F+kF=0$. With signature $(-,+,+)$, ${*}^2=-1$ on one- and two-forms; writing $B=*F$ gives $dB-k*B=0$ and the identity $d*B=0$. An Abelian [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation) changes the action by $\frac k2\int_{\partial M}\Lambda F$. Thus the bulk field equation is invariant, while invariance of the integrated action depends on boundary conditions.

## Electrical circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

An electrical circuit connects components that carry [electric current](#electric-current) and sustain [electric potential difference](#electric-potential-difference) differences. Its dynamics may couple [electrical resistance](#electrical-resistance), [inductance](#inductance) and [capacitance](#capacitance).

### RLC circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical circuit](#electrical-circuit)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RLC_circuit)

An RLC circuit combines [electrical resistance](#electrical-resistance), [inductance](#inductance) and [capacitance](#capacitance). Stored [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) can oscillate between magnetic and electric forms, while [Joule heating](#joule-heating) dissipates it.

#### Series RLC circuit

↑ **Parent:** [RLC circuit](#rlc-circuit)

In a series RLC circuit, the same [electric current](#electric-current) $I=q'$ flows through a resistor, inductor and [capacitor](#capacitor). Summing their [electric potential difference](#electric-potential-difference) drops gives $Lq''+Rq'+q/C=V$, and differentiating gives

$$
LI''+RI'+I/C=V'.
$$

The homogeneous [damped harmonic oscillator](analysis.md#damped-harmonic-oscillator) has angular frequency $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ and decay rate $\gamma=R/(2L)$.

##### Resonant series RLC response

↑ **Parent:** [Series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit)

For a [series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit) driven by $V(t)=V_M\sin(\omega_0t)$ with $R>0$ and $\omega_0^2=1/(LC)$, substitution shows that $I_0=V_M\sin(\omega_0t)/R$ solves the current equation. The inductive and capacitive terms cancel at this [resonance](dynamical-systems.md#resonance). Every homogeneous contribution decays, so every solution approaches this periodic response. Its [Q factor](dynamical-systems.md#q-factor) is $L\omega_0/R$.

##### Overdamped RLC response

↑ **Parent:** [Series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit)

When $R^2>4L/C$, the homogeneous [series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit) has two distinct negative [characteristic roots](differential-equation.md#characteristic-root-of-a-constant-coefficient-differential-equation), $[-R\pm\sqrt{R^2-4L/C}]/(2L)$. Its current is a [linear combination](vector-space.md#linear-combination) of the corresponding decaying [exponential functions](calculus.md#exponential-function).

##### Critically damped RLC response

↑ **Parent:** [Series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit)

When $R^2=4L/C$, the homogeneous [series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit) has one repeated [characteristic root](differential-equation.md#characteristic-root-of-a-constant-coefficient-differential-equation), $-R/(2L)$. Its current is $e^{-Rt/(2L)}(A+Bt)$, with constants determined by the initial data.

##### Underdamped RLC response

↑ **Parent:** [Series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit)

When $R^2<4L/C$, the homogeneous [series RLC circuit](#series-rlc-circuit) response oscillates with angular frequency $\sqrt{1/(LC)-R^2/(4L^2)}$ and envelope $e^{-Rt/(2L)}$. Its [characteristic roots](differential-equation.md#characteristic-root-of-a-constant-coefficient-differential-equation) form a complex-conjugate pair with negative [real part](complex-analysis.md#real-part) when $R>0$.

## Capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor)

A capacitor stores opposite electric charges on two conductors separated by an insulator. Its capacitance is $C=Q/V$.

### Parallel-plate capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitor](#capacitor)

Two parallel conducting plates of area $A$ separated by a small gap $d$, filled by material of [permittivity](#permittivity) $\epsilon$, have [capacitance](#capacitance) $C\simeq\epsilon A/d$ when fringing fields are negligible. Indeed, the interior [electric field](#electric-field) is approximately $Q/(\epsilon A)$, and the [electric potential difference](#electric-potential-difference) is approximately $Qd/(\epsilon A)$. A narrow curved capacitor gap has the same leading local approximation when its width is much less than the curvature radius.

### Spherical capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitor](#capacitor)

Two concentric conducting spherical shells of radii $a<b$ with charges $Q,-Q$ have an [electric field](#electric-field) only in the intervening vacuum: $E_r=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$ by [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law). Integrating gives a potential difference $Q(1/a-1/b)/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$, and hence the displayed [capacitance](#capacitance). The [electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy) is $Q^2/(2C)$, either by charging work or by integrating $\epsilon_0|E|^2/2$ over the gap.

### Capacitor energy

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitor](#capacitor)

A capacitor of capacitance $C$ carrying charge magnitude $Q$ stores

$$
U=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac12CV^2=\frac12QV.
$$

#### Fixed-voltage capacitor energy balance

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitor energy](#capacitor-energy)

For a quasistatic change of [capacitance](#capacitance) at fixed voltage, the [capacitor energy](#capacitor-energy) is $U=CV^2/2$ and charge is $Q=CV$. Electrical work supplied to the field is $V\,dQ$, so mechanical work is $dU-V\,dQ=-V^2dC/2$. Increasing the separation of parallel plates makes $dC<0$: mechanical work is positive even though field energy decreases, because electrical energy returns to the source.

## Electromagnetic interaction

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

The electromagnetic interaction is the long-range interaction of electrically charged matter with the electromagnetic field. Its massless gauge boson is the [photon](quantum-mechanics.md#photon).

## Electromagnetic radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation)

[Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation) consists of propagating oscillations of the [electric field](#electric-field) and [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) that carry [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) and [momentum](classical-mechanics.md#momentum).

### X-ray

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)

X-rays are high-frequency [electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation), conventionally with wavelengths of roughly $0.01$ to $10\,\mathrm{nm}$. The precise wavelength convention varies with context. Hot accreting gas and energetic electronic processes can emit X-rays, whose short wavelengths make them useful probes of compact objects and atomic structure.

### Larmor formula

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larmor_formula)

The nonrelativistic radiated power of an accelerated point charge is $P=q^2\mu_0a^2/(6\pi c)$ in SI units. Integrating its dipole radiation pattern over solid angle gives this result; relativistic motion requires the Lienard generalization.

<h4 id="lienard-radiated-power-formula">Liénard radiated-power formula</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Larmor formula](#larmor-formula)

The relativistic generalization of the [Larmor formula](#larmor-formula) uses the invariant proper-acceleration magnitude: $P=-\mu_0q^2 A^aA_a/(6\pi)$ in signature $(+---)$. In coordinate time this is the displayed expression. For perpendicular magnetic circular motion, $\gamma m|a|=|q|vB$, giving $P=\mu_0q^4(\gamma^2-1)B^2/(6\pi m^2)$. The statement concerns total instantaneous emitted power; directional radiation flux is not itself invariant.

### Mie scattering

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_scattering)

The electromagnetic scattering solution for a sphere describes the transition between [Rayleigh scattering](#rayleigh-scattering) and [geometrical optics](optics.md#geometrical-optics). Grain-size-dependent efficiencies can produce structure in the [radiation-pressure coefficient](planetary-science.md#radiation-pressure-coefficient).

### Bremsstrahlung

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremsstrahlung)

[Bremsstrahlung](#bremsstrahlung) is [electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation) emitted when a charged particle accelerates during interaction with another charged particle or field. The microscopic process $e^-+Z\to e^-+Z+\gamma$ creates a [photon](quantum-mechanics.md#photon), while its inverse absorbs one. Thus it contributes to a photon-number collision source in the [photon number current](statistical-physics.md#photon-number-current), unlike ordinary [Compton scattering](physics.md#compton-scattering). [Thermal bremsstrahlung](astrophysics.md#thermal-bremsstrahlung) averages this emission over a thermal distribution of charged particles.

### Thermal radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_radiation)

Thermal radiation is [electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation) emitted by matter because of its temperature. Matter in [thermal equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermal-equilibrium) emits a spectrum governed by its absorptivity, approaching the [Planck law](statistical-physics.md#planck-s-law) for a [blackbody](astrophysics.md#blackbody).

### Rayleigh scattering

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](#electromagnetic-radiation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_scattering)

Rayleigh scattering is elastic scattering by particles much smaller than the wavelength. For a fixed dielectric sphere, its scattering efficiency scales as the fourth power of the size-to-wavelength ratio.

## Right-hand rule

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-hand_rule)

The right-hand rule fixes the orientation of a [cross product](vector-space.md#cross-product) and relates the circulation direction of an [electric current](#electric-current) to the direction of its [magnetic field](#magnetic-field).

## Permittivity

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permittivity)

Permittivity describes a medium's response to an [electric field](#electric-field). In a linear isotropic medium, the electric displacement is $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$; [vacuum permittivity](#vacuum-permittivity) is its vacuum value $\epsilon_0$.

### Vacuum permittivity

↑ **Parent:** [Permittivity](#permittivity)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_permittivity)

Vacuum permittivity is the electromagnetic constant $\epsilon_0$ appearing in [Coulomb's law](#coulomb-s-law) and the SI vacuum [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations).

## Electric charge

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_charge)

Electric charge is the conserved source of the electric field and the coupling carried by charged matter.

### Charge neutrality

↑ **Parent:** [Electric charge](#electric-charge)

A system is charge neutral when its total positive and negative [electric charge](#electric-charge) cancel.

### Charge conservation

↑ **Parent:** [Electric charge](#electric-charge)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_conservation)

Electric charge obeys the [continuity equation](physics.md#continuity-equation)

$$
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot J=0.
$$

For a steady current, this reduces to $\nabla\cdot J=0$.

### Charge density

↑ **Parent:** [Electric charge](#electric-charge)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_density)

Charge density is electric charge per unit volume, area, or length according to the dimension of its support.

#### Linear charge density

↑ **Parent:** [Charge density](#charge-density)

Linear charge density is [electric charge](#electric-charge) divided by length along a charged line, or locally $dQ/d\ell$. Its SI unit is coulombs per metre. For a uniformly charged long cylindrical conductor, it equals the total [surface charge density](#surface-charge-density) integrated around the cylinder's circumference; it need not represent a charge literally supported on a one-dimensional line.

#### Line charge

↑ **Parent:** [Charge density](#charge-density)

A line charge is a charge distribution idealized as lying on a curve, described by charge per unit length. An infinite straight line with constant density $\mu$ produces the radial [electric field](#electric-field) $E_r=\mu/(2\pi\varepsilon_0r)$ by [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law), and a logarithmic [electrostatic potential](#electric-potential).

### Electric current

↑ **Parent:** [Electric charge](#electric-charge)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current)

Electric current is the rate at which charge crosses a surface.

#### Current density

↑ **Parent:** [Electric current](#electric-current)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_density)

The current density $J$ is the local charge flux, and its surface integral gives electric current.

##### Current dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Current density](#current-density)

A [current dipole](#current-dipole) idealizes a localized impressed [electric current density](#current-density) by the displayed [Dirac delta function](distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) distribution. Its moment $\mathbf Q$ has units of current times length. In a homogeneous [volume conductor](#volume-conductor) of conductivity $\sigma$, its primary [electric potential](#electric-potential) is $\Psi(\mathbf r;\mathbf r_0)=\mathbf Q\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0)/(4\pi\sigma|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|^3)$.

###### Radial current dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Current dipole](#current-dipole)

A [radial current dipole](#radial-current-dipole) has moment parallel to its displacement from the sphere centre. The [Sarvas formula](#sarvas-formula) gives identically zero exterior [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) for such a source in a [spherical volume conductor model](#spherical-volume-conductor-model), although its [electric potential](#electric-potential) need not vanish. This is a nonuniqueness mechanism for the inverse problem in [magnetoencephalography](biology.md#magnetoencephalography).

##### Four-current

↑ **Parent:** [Current density](#current-density)

The four-current combines charge density and [current density](#current-density) into the [four-vector](special-relativity.md#four-vector) $J^\mu=(c\rho,\boldsymbol J)$. With $x^\mu=(ct,\boldsymbol x)$, its [four-divergence](special-relativity.md#four-divergence) vanishes by the charge [continuity equation](physics.md#continuity-equation): $\partial_\mu J^\mu=\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol J=0$. It is the source in the covariant [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations).

##### Surface current density

↑ **Parent:** [Current density](#current-density)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_current_density)

A surface current density is electric current per unit transverse length confined to a surface. Integrating a thin layer's [current density](#current-density) through its thickness gives $\mathbf K$.

###### Current sheet

↑ **Parent:** [Surface current density](#surface-current-density)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_sheet)

A current sheet idealizes electric current as supported on a surface. It produces a jump in the tangential [magnetic field](#magnetic-field).

###### Force on a current sheet from the mean magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Current sheet](#current-sheet)

The [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) per unit area on an ideal [current sheet](#current-sheet) is computed using the mean of its limiting [magnetic fields](#magnetic-field). The sheet's own local [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) is equal and opposite on its two sides and cancels in this mean. Equivalently, integrate the [Lorentz force density](#lorentz-force-density) through a thin regularized layer: its self-field contribution averages to zero, leaving the external field acting on the [surface current density](#surface-current-density).

###### Magnetic-field jump across a surface current

↑ **Parent:** [Current sheet](#current-sheet)

For a surface current density $\mathbf K$ on an interface with unit normal $\mathbf n$, [Ampère's law](#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives

$$
\mathbf n\times(\mathbf B_+-\mathbf B_-)=\frac{4\pi}{c}\mathbf K
$$

in Gaussian units, or $\mu_0\mathbf K$ in SI units.

###### Surface conductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Surface current density](#surface-current-density)

Surface conductivity is the conductivity of a thin layer integrated through its thickness. The sheet form of [Ohm's law](#ohm-s-law) is $\mathbf K=\Sigma\mathbf E_\parallel$.

#### Line current

↑ **Parent:** [Electric current](#electric-current)

A line current idealizes an [electric current](#electric-current) as flowing along a curve; its current-density volume integral reduces to $I\,d\ell$ along that curve.

#### Electromotive force

↑ **Parent:** [Electric current](#electric-current)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromotive_force)

Electromotive force is work supplied per unit charge around a circuit.

##### Motional electromotive force

↑ **Parent:** [Electromotive force](#electromotive-force)

Motional electromotive force is the contribution caused by a conductor moving through a [magnetic field](#magnetic-field). The magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) separates charges within the conductor. For a bar of length $L$ moving at velocity $v$ perpendicular to a uniform field, its magnitude is $BL|v|$; the sign follows the chosen circuit orientation. Together with the electric-field contribution it enters [Faraday's law](#faraday-s-law-of-induction) for moving circuits.

###### Electromagnetic braking of a sliding rod

↑ **Parent:** [Motional electromotive force](#motional-electromotive-force)

A conducting rod sliding on rails separated by $\ell$ in a transverse magnetic field has induced current magnitude $B\ell v/R$ when self-inductance is neglected and resistance is $R$. The [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) opposes motion with magnitude $B^2\ell^2v/R$, so free motion decays exponentially on the displayed time constant. [Joule heating](#joule-heating) integrates to the rod's lost kinetic energy. Current direction follows the [Lenz's law](#lenz-s-law).

###### Magnetic flux transport for a moving circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Motional electromotive force](#motional-electromotive-force)

For a smoothly moving circuit and a divergence-free [magnetic field](#magnetic-field), the [magnetic flux](#magnetic-flux) derivative is $d\Phi/dt=\int_S\partial_t\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S-\oint_C(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\mathbf r$. The minus sign follows by orienting the swept side surface compatibly with the two spanning surfaces. Combining this transport identity with [Faraday's law](#faraday-s-law-of-induction) yields the moving-loop electromotive-force law.

#### Electrical resistance and conductance

↑ **Parent:** [Electric current](#electric-current)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance_and_conductance)

For an ohmic element, electrical resistance is $R=V/I$ and conductance is its reciprocal $G=I/V$. They describe the same voltage-current response using reciprocal units. The relation $V=IR$ is [Ohm's law](#ohm-s-law).

##### Electrical resistance

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical resistance and conductance](#electrical-resistance-and-conductance)

Electrical resistance relates voltage and current by Ohm's law $V=IR$.

<h6 id="ohm-s-law">Ohm's law</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical resistance](#electrical-resistance)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm's_law)

Ohm's law states that the potential difference across an ohmic component equals its current times its resistance: $V=IR$.

###### Electrical conductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Ohm's law](#ohm-s-law)

Electrical [electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity) relates [current density](#current-density) to the local [electric field](#electric-field) in the rest frame of an isotropic ohmic medium: $\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E$. It is a bulk material coefficient, distinct from a sheet surface [electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity). A moving conductor instead uses the [moving-conductor Ohm law](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#moving-conductor-ohm-law).

###### Electrical resistivity

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity)

Electrical resistivity is the inverse of scalar [electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity) in an isotropic Ohmic medium. In its rest frame, $\mathbf E=\rho_{\rm el}\mathbf j$, so the [electric field](#electric-field) does work per unit volume $\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf E=\rho_{\rm el}|\mathbf j|^2$. In [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units), finite conductivity gives [magnetic diffusivity](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-diffusivity) $\eta=c^2/(4\pi\sigma)$; neglecting this diffusion is a distinct approximation from neglecting electron inertia in [Hall magnetohydrodynamics](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#hall-magnetohydrodynamics).

###### Uniaxial conductivity tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity)

A uniaxial [electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity) has one distinguished direction $n$ and equal response in its orthogonal plane. Its eigenvalues are $\alpha$ in $n^\perp$ and $\alpha+\gamma$ along $n$, so it is invertible precisely when $\alpha(\alpha+\gamma)\ne0$. For an [electric field](#electric-field) perpendicular to $n$, the [electric current density](#current-density) is $J=\alpha E$. Positive dissipation requires both eigenvalues to be nonnegative, a physical constraint separate from algebraic invertibility. For $D_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}n_k$, one has $D^2=nn^{\mathsf T}-I$, and $\sigma D^2=-\sigma$ is equivalent to $\gamma=-\alpha$; the conductivity then vanishes along $n$.

###### Electrical conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor)

An electrical conductor supports mobile charge carriers and hence an electric current under a field. In a band description, a partially filled band or available overlapping bands supply nearby accessible states. The contrast with a [band insulator](quantum-theory.md#band-insulator) depends on band filling as well as spectral gaps; [semiconductors](physics.md#semiconductor) provide carriers through thermal excitation or doping.

###### Ohmic conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity)

A material whose [current density](#current-density) is proportional to its [electric field](#electric-field), with proportionality given by its [electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity).

###### Uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire

↑ **Parent:** [Ohmic conductor](#ohmic-conductor)

In a homogeneous [Ohmic conductor](#ohmic-conductor), a steady [electric current density](#current-density) obeys $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf j=0$. Uniform axial current in a straight cylinder follows if its side is insulated and its flat end faces are equipotentials. The [electric potential](#electric-potential) then solves [Laplace's equation](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation) with those boundary conditions, and uniqueness makes it linear in the axial coordinate. Steadiness alone does not impose these contact conditions: nonuniform end potentials can produce nonuniform current even with an insulating side. Bending also generally redistributes current; in an annular conductor the azimuthal current density is proportional to the inverse radius.

###### Charge relaxation

↑ **Parent:** [Ohmic conductor](#ohmic-conductor)

In a homogeneous [Ohmic conductor](#ohmic-conductor), the [charge continuity equation](#charge-continuity-equation) and [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) imply exponential decay of the bulk [charge density](#charge-density), $\rho(t)=\rho(0)e^{-\sigma t/\epsilon}$. For an isolated finite body, bulk charge moves to its boundary; the accumulating [surface charge density](#surface-charge-density) preserves total [electric charge](#electric-charge). The formula uses a constant permittivity $\epsilon$ and the corresponding free-charge convention, or $\epsilon_0$ for total charge in the vacuum form of [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law).

###### Charge relaxation in an isolated conducting cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Charge relaxation](#charge-relaxation)

A uniform cylindrical volume charge relaxes exponentially under [Ohm's law](#ohm-s-law) and the [continuity equation for electric charge](#charge-continuity-equation). In a cylinder of radius $R$ initially carrying no surface charge, the radial conduction current deposits $\rho_s(t)=R\rho_0(1-e^{-\sigma t/\epsilon_0})/2$ on its surface. Total charge per unit length remains $\pi R^2\rho_0$, so the exterior radial [electric field](#electric-field) is time independent even though the interior field decays. Neglecting the deposited surface charge violates charge conservation.

###### Drude model

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](#electrical-conductivity)

The [Drude model](#drude-model) describes classical charge carriers accelerated by an applied electric field and randomized by collisions of constant mean waiting time $\tau$. Its mean drift obeys $\dot v+v/\tau=eE/m$, and harmonic conductivity is $ne^2\tau/[m(1-i\omega\tau)]$ in the negative-frequency exponential convention.

###### High-frequency Drude plasma cutoff

↑ **Parent:** [Drude model](#drude-model)

The [Drude model](#drude-model) relative permittivity is $1-\omega_p^2/[\omega(\omega+i/\tau)]$. At frequencies large compared with the collision rate but below $\omega_p$, its leading real part is negative and the physical spatial wave-number branch has positive imaginary part. The field therefore decays into the conductor; close to the threshold the full complex response must be retained.

###### Joule heating

↑ **Parent:** [Ohm's law](#ohm-s-law)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule_heating)

Joule heating converts electrical work into internal [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy). A resistor dissipates $P=IV=I^2R$; in an ohmic continuum the local rate per volume is $\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf E=\eta j^2$, with resistivity $\eta$. Wind-driven currents are a proposed source of [heating of an inflated giant planet](exoplanet.md#heating-of-an-inflated-giant-planet).

## Electric field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field)

The electric field is the [force](classical-mechanics.md#force) per unit charge on a stationary test particle.

### Electric flux

↑ **Parent:** [Electric field](#electric-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_flux)

The electric flux through an oriented surface is the surface integral of the normal component of its [electric field](#electric-field). For a closed surface with outward normal, [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) makes the [electric flux](#electric-flux) equal to enclosed charge divided by $\varepsilon_0$ in SI units. It depends on surface orientation and may be negative.

### Electric potential

↑ **Parent:** [Electric field](#electric-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential)

In electrostatics the electric field is $E=-\nabla\Phi$, where $\Phi$ is the electric potential.

#### Constrained electrostatic equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)

A particle constrained to a surface can have a stable minimum of [electrostatic potential](#electric-potential) restricted to that surface even when the unrestricted equilibrium is unstable. Away from source charges, [Laplace's equation](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation) makes the full potential [Hessian matrix](calculus.md#hessian-matrix) trace zero, which rules out a positive-definite three-dimensional Hessian but does not rule out a positive-definite restriction to two dimensions. Equal positive charges at the corners of a square give a planar minimum at its centre and an unstable perpendicular direction.

#### Electrostatic reciprocity identity

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)

For two [electrostatic potentials](#electric-potential) satisfying the [Poisson equation](partial-differential-equation.md#poisson-equation) $\Delta\phi_i=-\rho_i/\varepsilon_0$, [Green's second identity](partial-differential-equation.md#green-second-identity) yields the displayed reciprocity formula. The [product rule for divergence](calculus.md#product-rule-for-divergence) applied to $\phi_1\nabla\phi_2-\phi_2\nabla\phi_1$ cancels the cross-gradient terms. The normal points outward from the integration region; for an exterior region this means inward toward the excluded object on its inner boundary. Boundary terms may be removed only under suitable decay or boundary conditions.

#### Image charge

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)

An image charge is a fictitious point charge placed outside the physical solution region so that its potential, combined with the real sources, enforces the required conductor boundary values. The [Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem](analysis.md#uniqueness-of-the-dirichlet-problem) then identifies the constructed potential with the physical one. The image need not describe the actual induced surface-charge distribution.

##### Fall time of a charge toward a grounded plane

↑ **Parent:** [Image charge](#image-charge)

The image of a charge $q$ at height $z$ is $-q$ at height $-z$. Their separation is $2z$, giving force $-q^2/(16\pi\varepsilon_0z^2)$. Starting at rest at height $d$, energy conservation gives $\dot z^2=q^2(1/z-1/d)/(8\pi\varepsilon_0m)$. Integrating the reciprocal speed to the plane yields the stated finite time in the electrostatic Newtonian model. The induced-conductor potential energy is $-q^2/(16\pi\varepsilon_0z)$; using the full image-pair energy would double the force incorrectly.

##### Falling charge above a grounded conducting plane

↑ **Parent:** [Image charge](#image-charge)

A real charge of height $z$ has a reflected [image charge](#image-charge) at $-z$. Its acceleration is $\ddot z=-\kappa/z^2$, with $\kappa=q^2/(16\pi\varepsilon_0m)$. Released from rest at height $d$, its ideal-model arrival time is

$$
T=\frac{\pi d^{3/2}}{2\sqrt{2\kappa}}.
$$

The image follows the instantaneous reflected position. The induced-charge energy carries one-half the full real-image pair interaction factor, consistent with this force.

##### Image charge for a grounded conducting sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Image charge](#image-charge)

For a charge $q$ at $\mathbf p$, $R=|\mathbf p|>a$, outside a grounded sphere of radius $a$, the displayed image gives zero potential on the sphere and decay at infinity. On $|\mathbf x|=a$, one has $|\mathbf x-\mathbf p'|=(a/R)|\mathbf x-\mathbf p|$, proving boundary cancellation. The induced force is inward, of magnitude $q^2aR/[4\pi\varepsilon_0(R^2-a^2)^2]$, from [Coulomb's law](#coulomb-s-law) applied to the image field. The self-field is excluded.

#### Electric potential difference

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_potential_difference)

An electric potential difference compares the [electric potentials](#electric-potential) at two points. In an [electrical circuit](#electrical-circuit) it is the voltage across the terminals of a component. A vertex potential in an [electrical network](markov-process.md#electrical-network) is specified relative to a reference vertex; terminal differences are independent of an additive change of reference.

#### Point charge

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_charge)

A point charge idealizes electric charge concentrated at one point. In vacuum, a charge $Q$ at the origin has potential $Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$.

#### Equipotential

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](#electric-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equipotential)

An equipotential is a level set of the electric potential. The electric field is normal to every smooth equipotential.

## Magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_field)

The magnetic field contributes the velocity-dependent term $qv\times B$ to the [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force).

### Solenoid

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)

A solenoid is a helical winding of conducting wire. For an ideal very long winding with $N$ turns per unit length and negligible pitch, [Ampère's law](#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives a uniform axial [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) $B=\mu_0NI$ inside and zero field outside. Finite-length end fields and the pitch correction are excluded by this approximation. The [self-inductance](#self-inductance) of a length $l$ and cross-sectional area $A$ is $L=\mu_0N^2Al$.

### Solenoidal magnetic-field constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)

The [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) has zero divergence. The [ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#ideal-magnetohydrodynamic-induction-equation) preserves this constraint from initial data, since the divergence of its curl is zero.

### Magnetic field line

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)

A magnetic field line is an integral curve tangent to the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field). In coordinates $x^i$, it obeys $dx^i/B^i=dx^j/B^j$ wherever the relevant components are nonzero.

#### Flux tube

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field line](#magnetic-field-line)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_tube)

A flux tube is a bundle of [magnetic field lines](#magnetic-field-line) bounded by a surface everywhere tangent to the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field). [Gauss's law for magnetism](#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism) makes the [magnetic flux](#magnetic-flux) through any transverse cross-section constant along the tube. For a narrow tube with nearly uniform field magnitude $B$ and transverse area $A$, this gives $BA=\mathrm{constant}$.

##### Flux-tube area

↑ **Parent:** [Flux tube](#flux-tube)

The cross-sectional area of a narrow [flux tube](#flux-tube), measured normal to its [magnetic field](#magnetic-field). Constancy of the [magnetic flux](#magnetic-flux) gives $A|\mathbf B|=\mathrm{constant}$ along the tube. This area controls the mass transport when flow follows the field.

#### Magnetic-field-line equation

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field line](#magnetic-field-line)

The magnetic-field-line equation states that the tangent to a field line is parallel to $\mathbf B$. In spherical coordinates for an axisymmetric poloidal field, it can be written $dr/(r\,d\theta)=B_r/B_\theta$.

#### Magnetic dipole field

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field line](#magnetic-field-line)

Outside a localized magnetic dipole $\boldsymbol\mu$, the vacuum field in Gaussian conventions is

$$
\mathbf B(\mathbf r)=\frac{3(\boldsymbol\mu\mathbin\cdot\mathbf r)\mathbf r}{r^5}-\frac{\boldsymbol\mu}{r^3}.
$$

For a dipole along the polar axis, $B_r=2\mu\cos\theta/r^3$ and $B_\theta=\mu\sin\theta/r^3$.

##### Dipole magnetic-field line

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole field](#magnetic-dipole-field)

An axisymmetric dipole field line satisfies $r=C\sin^2\theta$, where $C$ is its equatorial crossing radius. This follows by integrating $dr/(r\,d\theta)=B_r/B_\theta=2\cot\theta$.

###### Dipolar flux-tube area

↑ **Parent:** [Dipole magnetic-field line](#dipole-magnetic-field-line)

Near the polar axis of a [magnetic dipole field](#magnetic-dipole-field), $B\propto r^{-3}$. Conservation of [magnetic flux](#magnetic-flux) in a [flux tube](#flux-tube) consequently gives $A(r)\propto r^3$. A [dipole magnetic-field line](#dipole-magnetic-field-line) intersecting a sphere of radius $R_\star$ at small polar angle $\theta_\star$ obeys

$$
\theta(r)^2\simeq\theta_\star^2\frac r{R_\star},
\qquad
A(r)\simeq\pi r^2\theta(r)^2
=\pi\theta_\star^2\frac{r^3}{R_\star}.
$$

This area is for one polar tube. The approximation requires $\theta_\star^2r/R_\star\ll1$; the full [magnetic dipole field](#magnetic-dipole-field) does not remain a narrow polar tube at arbitrarily large radius.

### Magnetostatic equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetostatic_equilibrium)

A magnetostatic equilibrium is a time-independent configuration in which magnetic, pressure, gravitational, and any other body-force densities balance.

#### Spherical magnetostatic pressure balance

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium)

For an axisymmetric [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) $\mathbf B=\psi\mathbf e_\phi/(r\sin\theta)+\nabla\times[\chi\mathbf e_\phi/(r\sin\theta)]$, the [poloidal magnetic flux function](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#poloidal-magnetic-flux-function) gives $B_r=\chi_\theta/(r^2\sin\theta)$ and $B_\theta=-\chi_r/(r\sin\theta)$. Force balance implies locally $p=p(\chi)$ and $\psi=\psi(\chi)$ on regular connected flux surfaces. Direct calculation gives $(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\times\mathbf B=-(\Delta_*\chi+\psi\psi')\nabla\chi/(r^2\sin^2\theta)$, with $\Delta_*\chi=\chi_{rr}+\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta(\chi_\theta/\sin\theta)/r^2$. Equating this to $\mu_0\nabla p$ proves the displayed [magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium) equation, including its sign and dimensions.

##### Confined linear-pressure spherical magnetic equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Spherical magnetostatic pressure balance](#spherical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance)

Set $\psi=\alpha\chi$, $p=p_0+P\chi$ and $\chi=F(r)\sin^2\theta$. A regular family is $F=A[\cos(\alpha r)-\sin(\alpha r)/(\alpha r)]+cr^2$, where $P=-\alpha^2c/\mu_0$. A zero [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) at radius a requires $F(a)=F'(a)=0$. For nonzero A this yields the displayed condition with $x=\alpha a$ and $c=-A[\cos x-\sin x/x]/a^2$. The boundary pressure is $p_0$, independent of angle. The trigonometric pair is tied by regularity and the radial differential equation; its coefficients are not independent.

#### Magnetized isothermal atmosphere

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium)

A plane-parallel [magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium) with constant downward [gravitational acceleration](classical-mechanics.md#gravitational-acceleration) $g$, horizontal [magnetic field](#magnetic-field), constant [isothermal sound speed](compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed) $c_s$, and constant [Alfvén speed](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#alfven-speed) $v_a$ has

$$
H=\frac{c_s^2+v_a^2/2}{g},\qquad \rho=\rho_0e^{-z/H},\qquad p=c_s^2\rho,\qquad B=B_0e^{-z/(2H)}.
$$

Indeed the [magnetohydrodynamic total pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetohydrodynamic-total-pressure) is $(c_s^2+v_a^2/2)\rho$, and its vertical derivative is $-g\rho$. The [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure) therefore increases the [scale height](statistical-physics.md#scale-height) above that of an unmagnetized atmosphere.

#### Cylindrical magnetostatic equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium)

A cylindrical magnetostatic equilibrium is an [axisymmetric vector field](calculus.md#axisymmetric-vector-field) and [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) configuration independent of the axial coordinate. Its radial [Lorentz force density](#lorentz-force-density) balances the [pressure gradient](fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient), as expressed by [cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance).

##### Z-pinch

↑ **Parent:** [Cylindrical magnetostatic equilibrium](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-equilibrium)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-pinch)

In a Z-pinch, an axial [electric current](#electric-current) produces a [toroidal magnetic field](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#toroidal-magnetic-field) whose inward [magnetic tension](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-tension) confines a plasma. A cylindrical [magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium) balances the resulting [Lorentz force density](#lorentz-force-density) against a [pressure gradient](fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient).

##### Cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance

↑ **Parent:** [Cylindrical magnetostatic equilibrium](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-equilibrium)

For an [axisymmetric vector field](calculus.md#axisymmetric-vector-field) independent of the axial coordinate and regular on the axis, [Gauss's law for magnetism](#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism) gives $B_R=0$. The magnetostatic [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law) then gives $j_R=0$, $j_\phi=-cB_z'/(4\pi)$, and $j_z=c(RB_\phi)'/(4\pi R)$ in Gaussian units. Radial [magnetostatic equilibrium](#magnetostatic-equilibrium) is

$$
\frac d{dR}\left(p+\frac{B_z^2+B_\phi^2}{8\pi}\right)+\frac{B_\phi^2}{4\pi R}=0.
$$

For enclosed axial [electric current](#electric-current) $I(R)$, the magnetostatic [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives $B_\phi=2I/(cR)$, hence

$$
\frac d{dR}\left(p+\frac{B_z^2}{8\pi}\right)
+\frac1{2\pi c^2R^2}\frac{dI^2}{dR}=0.
$$

The first term contains [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) and axial [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure); the second combines toroidal [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure) with the hoop force from [magnetic tension](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-tension).

###### Flux-tube axial field virial identity

↑ **Parent:** [Cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance)

For a regular cylindrical [flux tube](#flux-tube) with $\mathbf B=(0,B_\phi(s),B_z(s))$, vanishing field beyond radius $s_0$, and exterior [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) $p_e$, define $\langle h\rangle=2s_0^{-2}\int_0^{s_0}sh(s)\,ds$ and $B_p^2=2\mu_0p_e$. Integrating [cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance) against $s^2$ gives the displayed identity. The toroidal [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure) and hoop [magnetic tension](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-tension) cancel in this cross-sectional axial-field average. Nonnegative gas [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) gives $\langle B_z^2\rangle\leq B_p^2$, with strict inequality precisely when $\langle p\rangle>0$.

###### Uniform-twist central axial field bound

↑ **Parent:** [Flux-tube axial field virial identity](#flux-tube-axial-field-virial-identity)

For a [flux tube](#flux-tube) with $B_\phi=\gamma(s/s_0)B_z$, regularity at the axis and [cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance) give $B_z(0)^2=B_p^2-2\mu_0p(0)+\gamma^2\langle B_z^2\rangle$. The [flux-tube axial field virial identity](#flux-tube-axial-field-virial-identity) and $p\geq0$ give the bound. A twisted [toroidal magnetic field](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#toroidal-magnetic-field) can support a central axial field above the untwisted [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure) limit even though its cross-sectional mean obeys that limit.

###### Power-law pressure-supported axial current

↑ **Parent:** [Cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance)

For constant axial [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) and $p=p_0(1+R^2/a^2)^{-k}$ with $p_0>0$ and $k>0$, [cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance) gives, with $x=R^2/a^2$,

$$
I^2(R)=2\pi c^2p_0a^2
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{1-(1+kx)(1+x)^{-k}}{k-1},&k\ne1,\\
\log(1+x)+(1+x)^{-1}-1,&k=1.
\end{cases}
$$

The nontrivial [electric current](#electric-current) is finite at infinity exactly when $k>1$, in which case $I^2(\infty)=2\pi c^2p_0a^2/(k-1)$. Either sign of $I$ is possible because the [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) balance fixes its square.

###### Magnetic pressure support with vanishing axial field

↑ **Parent:** [Cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance)

With no axial [electric current](#electric-current), [cylindrical magnetostatic pressure balance](#cylindrical-magnetostatic-pressure-balance) reduces to constant $p+B_z^2/(8\pi)$. For $p=p_0e^{-R^2/a^2}$ and $B_z(0)=0$, its radial solutions are

$$
B_z=\sigma\sqrt{8\pi p_0}\sqrt{1-e^{-R^2/a^2}},
\qquad
j_\phi=-\sigma\frac{c\sqrt{8\pi p_0}}{4\pi a^2}
\frac{R e^{-R^2/a^2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-R^2/a^2}}},
\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}.
$$

As $R\downarrow0$, $B_z\sim\sigma\sqrt{8\pi p_0}R/a$ and $j_\phi\to-\sigma c\sqrt{8\pi p_0}/(4\pi a)$. These finite scalar limits do not give a [smooth function](analysis.md#smooth-function) of Cartesian position: $R=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ has a cusp at the axis, and the azimuthal unit vector has no unique limit there. Thus these data describe radial equilibrium away from the axis, but cannot satisfy smooth vector-field regularity on the axis without modifying the data.

### Motional electric field

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)

A conductor or plasma moving with nonrelativistic velocity $\mathbf u$ through a magnetic field experiences the motional electric field $\mathbf E=-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B/c$ in Gaussian units when the electric field vanishes in its rest frame.

### London equations

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_equations)

The London equations relate the superconducting [electric current](#electric-current) to the [electromagnetic field](#electromagnetic-field): $\partial_t\mathbf J=\mathbf E/(\mu_0\lambda_L^2)$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf J=-\mathbf B/(\mu_0\lambda_L^2)$. In a gauge with constant condensate phase the second follows from $\mathbf J=-\mathbf A/(\mu_0\lambda_L^2)$; it implies exponential [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) screening.

#### London penetration depth

↑ **Parent:** [London equations](#london-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_penetration_depth)

For uniform vortex-free [superconductivity](statistical-physics.md#superconductivity), the [London equation](#london-equations) and stationary Maxwell equations give $\nabla^2\mathbf B=\mathbf B/\lambda_L^2$. A field tangential to a planar superconducting half-space decays as $\mathbf B(z)=\mathbf B(0)e^{-z/\lambda_L}$. A nonzero normal boundary field is incompatible with this translation-invariant decaying Meissner solution, because divergence-free magnetic flux would require that component to remain constant.

#### Meissner effect

↑ **Parent:** [London equations](#london-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissner_effect)

The Meissner effect is the expulsion of magnetic field from the bulk of a superconductor. A static field decays from its surface over the London penetration depth.

### Magnetic flux

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](#magnetic-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_flux)

The magnetic flux through an oriented surface $S$ is $\Phi_B=\int_S B\mathbin{\cdot}dS$.

## Capacitance

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitance)

Capacitance is charge stored per potential difference, $C=Q/V$.

### Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitance](#capacitance)

An isolated conducting sphere of radius $R$ in a medium of [permittivity](#permittivity) $\epsilon$ has surface [electrostatic potential](#electric-potential) $V=Q/(4\pi\epsilon R)$ relative to infinity. Thus its [capacitance](#capacitance) is $C=Q/V=4\pi\epsilon R$.

### Sphere-plane capacitance

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitance](#capacitance)

For a conducting sphere of radius $R$ at center height $h>R$ above a grounded infinite plane,

$$
C(R,h)=4\pi\epsilon R\sinh\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{\sinh(n\alpha)},
\qquad\cosh\alpha=\frac hR.
$$

The distance $h$ is measured to the center, so the surface gap is $h-R$. Relative to the [capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere](#capacitance-of-an-isolated-conducting-sphere), the enhancement is $c(R/h)=C(R,h)/(4\pi\epsilon R)$.

The [method of images](mathematics.md#method-of-images) gives a constructive proof. At fixed sphere potential $V_s$, start with $q_0=4\pi\epsilon RV_s$ at height $z_0=h$ and place its opposite image below the plane. Cancel that image's nonconstant sphere potential by the successive charges

$$
q_{n+1}=\frac R{h+z_n}q_n,
\qquad z_{n+1}=h-\frac{R^2}{h+z_n},
$$

with opposite mirror charges below the plane at every step. The resulting sphere potential is $V_s$ and the plane potential is zero. Since $q_n=4\pi\epsilon RV_s\sinh\alpha/\sinh((n+1)\alpha)$, summing the sphere charges gives the displayed [capacitance](#capacitance). In particular $c(y)=1+y/2+O(y^2)$ for small $y$.

The exact sphere-plane boundary-value problem is also treated in [Behunin and colleagues' analysis of sphere-plane electrostatics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6034).

### Capacitance per unit length

↑ **Parent:** [Capacitance](#capacitance)

Capacitance per unit length is the charge per unit length divided by the potential difference. A vacuum coaxial cable with radii $a<b$ has

$$
C'=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon_0}{\log(b/a)}.
$$

## Inductance

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductance)

Inductance measures magnetic flux linkage per current and gives the self-induced electromotive force $-L\,dI/dt$.

### Self-inductance

↑ **Parent:** [Inductance](#inductance)

For a circuit whose flux linkage $\Lambda$ is proportional to its current, the self-inductance is the coefficient $L$ in $\Lambda=LI$. [Faraday's law](#faraday-s-law-of-induction) gives the induced [electromotive force](#electromotive-force) $-L\dot I$ when $L$ is constant. In series with a resistance $R$ and a constant applied electromotive force, the current obeys $L\dot I+RI=\mathcal E_0$, with [time constant](analysis.md#time-constant) $L/R$.

## Bound charge and bound current

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

Polarization and magnetization produce the effective sources

$$
\rho_{\rm b}=-\nabla\cdot P,
\qquad
J_{\rm b}=\partial_tP+\nabla\times M.
$$

Defining $D=\epsilon_0E+P$ and $H=B/\mu_0-M$ moves these sources into the macroscopic fields.

### Bound current

↑ **Parent:** [Bound charge and bound current](#bound-charge-and-bound-current)

The current associated with [electric polarization](#polarization-density) and [magnetization](#magnetization) in matter. If the magnetic response is neglected, it is $\mathbf J_b=\partial_t\mathbf P$. It is distinguished from a free conduction current in the [Maxwell equations in matter](#maxwell-equations-in-matter).

### Polarization density

↑ **Parent:** [Bound charge and bound current](#bound-charge-and-bound-current)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarization_density)

Electric polarization is electric dipole moment per unit volume. It produces bound volume and surface charge

$$
\rho_{\rm b}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P,
\qquad
\sigma_{\rm b}=\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n.
$$

Across an interface, the net bound sheet charge is $(\mathbf P_1-\mathbf P_2)\cdot\mathbf n$, where $\mathbf n$ points from medium one to medium two.

#### Electric displacement field

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization density](#polarization-density)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_displacement_field)

The electric displacement is $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P$ and obeys $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_{\rm free}$. In a linear isotropic dielectric, $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$.

### Magnetization

↑ **Parent:** [Bound charge and bound current](#bound-charge-and-bound-current)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetization)

Magnetization is magnetic dipole moment per unit volume. It produces bound volume and surface currents

$$
\mathbf J_{\rm b}=\nabla\times\mathbf M,
\qquad
\mathbf K_{\rm b}=\mathbf M\times\mathbf n.
$$

Across an interface, the net bound sheet current is $(\mathbf M_1-\mathbf M_2)\times\mathbf n$.

#### Curie temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](#magnetization)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_temperature)

The Curie temperature is the transition temperature above which a ferromagnetic material loses spontaneous [magnetization](#magnetization). A hot conducting planetary core can generate a [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) through [dynamo action](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#dynamo-action) without behaving as a permanent magnet. The blocking temperature at which a mineral records crustal remanence also depends on its relaxation timescale and need not equal its Curie temperature.

#### Remanent magnetization

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](#magnetization)

Remanent magnetization is [magnetization](#magnetization) retained after the applied [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) has been removed. Magnetic minerals cooling in an ambient field can preserve information about that field in a planetary crust. Such a record may outlive the [planetary dynamo](planetary-science.md#planetary-dynamo) which originally produced it. Crustal remanence therefore differs from a present field maintained by conducting-fluid motion.

#### Magnetization current

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](#magnetization)

A relativistic magnetization current has the form $J_{\mathrm{mag}}^\mu=\partial_\nu M^{\nu\mu}$ for an antisymmetric polarization-magnetization tensor. It is identically conserved because commuting derivatives contract an antisymmetric tensor.

#### Magnetic field intensity

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetization](#magnetization)

The magnetic field intensity is $\mathbf H=\mathbf B/\mu_0-\mathbf M$ and obeys $\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_{\rm free}$ in magnetostatics. In a linear isotropic medium, $\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H$.

## Magnetic spherical-shell matching

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

Across an interface with no free surface current, the normal component of $B$ and tangential component of $H$ are continuous. For an axisymmetric spherical shell, harmonic scalar potentials have dipole form $(ar+b/r^2)\cos\theta$ in each radial region.

## Maxwell stress tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_stress_tensor)

With electromagnetic momentum density $\mathbf g=\epsilon_0\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$, the momentum-flux convention

$$
\sigma_{ij}=-\epsilon_0E_iE_j+\frac{\epsilon_0}{2}E^2\delta_{ij}
-\frac1{\mu_0}B_iB_j+\frac1{2\mu_0}B^2\delta_{ij}
$$

satisfies

$$
\partial_tg_j+\partial_i\sigma_{ij}=-(\rho\mathbf E+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B)_j.
$$

### Electromagnetic momentum density

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell stress tensor](#maxwell-stress-tensor)

The electromagnetic momentum density in vacuum is $\mathbf g=\epsilon_0\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$. It equals the [Poynting vector](#poynting-vector) divided by $c^2$, expressing the relativistic relation between energy flux and momentum density.

### Local conservation of electromagnetic momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell stress tensor](#maxwell-stress-tensor)

With the momentum-flux sign convention used for the [Maxwell stress tensor](#maxwell-stress-tensor), Maxwell's equations imply

$$
\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol\sigma
+\partial_t\mathbf g=-\mathbf f_{\rm L},
$$

where $\mathbf g$ is the [electromagnetic momentum density](#electromagnetic-momentum-density) and $\mathbf f_{\rm L}$ is the [Lorentz force density](#lorentz-force-density) exerted by the field on matter. Thus total field-plus-matter momentum is locally conserved.

### Radiation pressure on a perfectly absorbing surface

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell stress tensor](#maxwell-stress-tensor)

A normally incident vacuum wave of time-averaged intensity $I$ transfers momentum $I/c$ per unit area and time to a perfectly absorbing surface, so its radiation pressure is

$$
p=\frac Ic.
$$

A perfect reflector reverses the normal momentum and gives $2I/c$.

## Proca action

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proca_action)

The Proca action adds $m^2A_\mu A^\mu/2$ to the Maxwell Lagrangian. It describes a massive spin-one field and explicitly breaks the Abelian gauge symmetry of the massless theory.

## Proca equation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

A massive vector field coupled to a conserved current obeys

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}-m^2A^\nu=-\mu_0J^\nu.
$$

The mass term breaks electromagnetic gauge invariance.

### Proca field

↑ **Parent:** [Proca equation](#proca-equation)

A Proca field is a massive spin-one vector field. Its field equation enforces transversality and leaves three physical polarizations.

#### Proca propagator

↑ **Parent:** [Proca field](#proca-field)

For metric signature $(+---)$, the momentum-space Feynman propagator of a Proca field is

$$
\widetilde\Delta_{\mu\nu}(p)=\frac{-i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{m^2}\right).
$$

The longitudinal numerator term inverts the massive vector kinetic operator without gauge fixing.

##### High-energy obstruction for a hard vector mass

↑ **Parent:** [Proca propagator](#proca-propagator)

A free [Proca field](#proca-field) is consistent and has three physical [particle polarizations](special-relativity.md#particle-polarization). The difficulty for generic interactions is the ultraviolet behavior of its [Proca propagator](#proca-propagator): the longitudinal numerator makes part of it approach order $1/m^2$, rather than decay as $1/k^2$. Longitudinal external [polarization vectors](relativistic-quantum-field.md#polarization-vector) also behave as $\varepsilon_L^\mu=k^\mu/m+O(m/E)$. Without suitable identities and cancellations, amplitudes grow with energy and perturbative counterterms of increasing dimension are needed. Conserved-current Abelian models and the [Stueckelberg mechanism](relativistic-quantum-field.md#stueckelberg-mechanism) are important exceptions; a massive interacting vector is not intrinsically inconsistent as an [effective field theory](quantum-field-theory.md#effective-field-theory).

##### Proca time-ordering contact term

↑ **Parent:** [Proca propagator](#proca-propagator)

For a free massive [Proca field](#proca-field) in the mostly-plus [Minkowski metric](special-relativity.md#minkowski-metric), the covariant inverse of the quadratic [action](classical-mechanics.md#action) and the canonical [time-ordered product](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#time-ordered-product) of vector potentials differ locally. With [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) $e^{iq\cdot(x-y)}$, the covariant [Proca propagator](#proca-propagator) is $-i(\eta_{ab}+q_aq_b/m^2)/(q^2+m^2-i0)$, while the literal canonical [two-point correlation function](critical-phenomenon.md#two-point-correlation-function) subtracts $i\delta_a^0\delta_b^0/m^2$ in momentum space. The time component is nondynamical; its physical-mode numerator is $|\boldsymbol q|^2/m^2$, rather than $-1+(q^0)^2/m^2$. The difference is an instantaneous [Dirac delta function](distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function), not another propagating state. Both expressions agree away from coincidence. This distinction matters when identifying a covariant [Green function](analysis.md#green-s-function) with a canonical correlator in a constrained field theory.

#### Transverse vector field

↑ **Parent:** [Proca field](#proca-field)

A vector field is transverse when $\partial_\mu V^\mu=0$ in spacetime, or $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf V=0$ for a spatial Fourier mode.

### Lorenz constraint in Proca theory

↑ **Parent:** [Proca equation](#proca-equation)

Taking a divergence and using antisymmetry of $F^{\mu\nu}$ and current conservation gives $m^2\partial_\mu A^\mu=0$. For nonzero mass, the Lorenz condition follows from the field equation rather than from a gauge choice.

### Yukawa potential

↑ **Parent:** [Proca equation](#proca-equation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukawa_potential)

The static Green function of $\nabla^2-m^2$ in three dimensions is proportional to $e^{-mr}/r$. It describes an interaction screened beyond range $m^{-1}$ and tends to the Coulomb potential as $m\to0$.

## Relativistic Lorentz force

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)

$m\,du^\mu/d\tau=qF^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$ gives $d\mathbf p/dt=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ and $d(\gamma mc^2)/dt=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v$.

### Constant-field relativistic Lorentz-force solution

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force)

For a constant [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor), the [relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force) is a constant-coefficient linear system on [four-velocity](special-relativity.md#four-velocity), with solution

$$
u(\tau)=\exp\!\left(\frac qmF\tau\right)u(0).
$$

The electric-type blocks of $F$ generate hyperbolic rotations, while magnetic-type blocks generate ordinary rotations.

#### Relativistic motion in parallel electric and magnetic fields

↑ **Parent:** [Constant-field relativistic Lorentz-force solution](#constant-field-relativistic-lorentz-force-solution)

When uniform $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ are parallel, the [relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force) splits into independent hyperbolic motion along the fields and circular motion in the perpendicular plane. The circle is periodic in [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time), while electric acceleration makes the helix's longitudinal pitch grow exponentially from one proper-time cyclotron period to the next.

### Relativistic hyperbolic motion in a uniform electric field

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force)

A particle released from rest in a constant electric field parallel to its motion follows a spacetime hyperbola and has rapidity proportional to proper time.

This force law realizes [hyperbolic motion](special-relativity.md#hyperbolic-motion) with constant [proper acceleration](special-relativity.md#proper-acceleration).

### Null crossed electromagnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force)

Perpendicular fields with $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B=0$ and $E^2-c^2B^2=0$ form a null electromagnetic field. The constant-field Lorentz-force generator is nilpotent, so its exponential terminates as a polynomial.

Both invariants of the [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor) vanish for this field class.

#### Relativistic trajectory in a constant null crossed field

↑ **Parent:** [Null crossed electromagnetic field](#null-crossed-electromagnetic-field)

Choose

$$
\mathbf B=B\,\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf E=cB\,\mathbf e_x,
\qquad
\Omega=\frac{qB}{m}.
$$

A particle released from rest at the origin has the proper-time trajectory

$$
t(\tau)=\tau+\frac{\Omega^2\tau^3}{6},
\qquad
x(\tau)=\frac{c\Omega\tau^2}{2},
\qquad
y(\tau)=-\frac{c\Omega^2\tau^3}{6},
\qquad z(\tau)=0.
$$

Eliminating [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time) gives the semicubical parabola

$$
\boxed{y^2=\frac{2qB}{9mc}x^3.}
$$

This signed coefficient assumes that the positive $x$ direction is chosen along $\mathbf E$; reversing that axis changes the signs of both $x^3$ and its coefficient.

## Charged particle in a uniform magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charged_particle_in_a_uniform_magnetic_field)

A velocity cross magnetic-field force bends perpendicular motion into a circle while doing no work.

### Magnetic angular-momentum invariant

↑ **Parent:** [Charged particle in a uniform magnetic field](#charged-particle-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field)

For a particle obeying $m\dot{\mathbf v}=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ in a constant uniform [magnetic field](#magnetic-field), the displayed combination is conserved. The derivative of mechanical [angular momentum](classical-mechanics.md#angular-momentum) projected onto the field is canceled by the quadratic position term. This is the rotational analogue of the distinction between mechanical and [canonical momentum](classical-mechanics.md#canonical-momentum) in an electromagnetic field.

### Helical motion in a uniform magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Charged particle in a uniform magnetic field](#charged-particle-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field)

In a uniform [magnetic field](#magnetic-field), the [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) leaves the component of [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) parallel to the field constant and rotates the perpendicular component at the nonrelativistic [cyclotron frequency](quantum-theory.md#cyclotron-frequency) $|q|B/m$. The resulting path is a [helix](topology.md#helix) around a field line.

### Guiding center

↑ **Parent:** [Charged particle in a uniform magnetic field](#charged-particle-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiding_center)

The guiding center is the center of a charged particle's circular cyclotron orbit. In a nonuniform field it drifts slowly compared with the rapid [cyclotron frequency](quantum-theory.md#cyclotron-frequency).

### Damped cyclotron motion

↑ **Parent:** [Charged particle in a uniform magnetic field](#charged-particle-in-a-uniform-magnetic-field)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damped_cyclotron_motion)

Adding linear drag to cyclotron motion produces an exponentially contracting spiral.

## Electrostatics

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatics)

Electrostatics studies time-independent electric fields and charge distributions.

### Electrostatic interface boundary conditions

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)

Across a charged dielectric interface the tangential [electric field](#electric-field) is continuous and the normal displacement jump is the fixed [surface charge density](#surface-charge-density). Without a dipole sheet the [electrostatic potential](#electric-potential) is continuous. For a charge sheet with equal [permittivity](#permittivity) on both sides, its normal potential derivative has jump $-\sigma_{\rm fixed}/\varepsilon$. Setting the field to zero on one side is an additional physical boundary assumption, as in a conductor or a negligible-exterior-admittance idealization.

<h3 id="debye-huckel-approximation">Debye–Hückel approximation</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)

The Debye–Hückel approximation linearizes mobile-ion Boltzmann densities about a neutral bulk electrolyte. It is valid for ionic potential energies small compared with $k_BT$. The inverse [Debye–Hückel screening length](mathematical-biology.md#debye-huckel-screening-length) satisfies $\kappa_D^2=\sum_i n_i z_i^2e^2/(\varepsilon k_BT)$. The quadratic screened charging free energy includes both [electric field](#electric-field) energy and the linearized ionic-response term $\varepsilon\kappa_D^2\phi^2/2$.

#### Screened sinusoidal surface-charge mode

↑ **Parent:** [Debye–Hückel approximation](#debye-huckel-approximation)

A lateral sinusoidal [surface charge density](#surface-charge-density) with wave number $k$ in the [Debye–Hückel approximation](#debye-huckel-approximation) generates vertical exponential or hyperbolic functions with inverse length $p=\sqrt{k^2+\kappa_D^2}$. In a homogeneous electrolyte filling all space, an isolated sheet at $z_s$ produces $\phi=\sigma_k(x)e^{-p|z-z_s|}/(2\varepsilon p)$. Boundary conditions determine the normalization when the electrolyte occupies only a gap.

##### Phase registration of screened charged sheets

↑ **Parent:** [Screened sinusoidal surface-charge mode](#screened-sinusoidal-surface-charge-mode)

Two equally modulated charge sheets in identical full-space electrolyte have charging energy per area $\alpha^2[1+e^{-pd}\cos\theta]/(4\varepsilon p)$, where $p=\sqrt{k^2+\kappa_D^2}$. Its phase-dependent interaction favors a half-wavelength offset, with opposite charge patches facing each other. For an electrolyte gap with negligible exterior displacement response, the energy is instead $\alpha^2[\coth(pd)+\cos\theta/\sinh(pd)]/(2\varepsilon p)$, with the same minimizing phase. Absolute energies require specified exterior [electrostatic interface boundary conditions](#electrostatic-interface-boundary-conditions).

### Bjerrum length

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjerrum_length)

For a chosen reference charge $q$, the separation at which its Coulomb interaction energy equals $k_BT$. The charge-$q$ convention is $\ell_B=q^2/(4\pi\epsilon_{\rm abs}k_BT)$ in SI, or $\ell_B=\beta q^2/\epsilon$ in [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units). The commonly tabulated value uses the elementary charge; changing [counterion](chemistry.md#counterion) valence requires tracking that reference convention. It sets the strength of [counterion condensation](#counterion-condensation).

### Poisson-Boltzmann equation

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson–Boltzmann_equation)

A mean-field coupling of the [Poisson equation](partial-differential-equation.md#poisson-equation) to an ideal [Boltzmann distribution](thermodynamics.md#boltzmann-distribution) of mobile [ions](chemistry.md#ion). In [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units) with uniform dielectric constant $\epsilon$, $\nabla^2\phi=-(4\pi/\epsilon)[\rho_f+\sum_i q_i n_{i,0}e^{-\beta q_i\phi}]$. The potential reference and chemical potentials determine the normalization factors. It neglects discrete-ion correlations and assumes local [thermal equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermal-equilibrium). A counterion-only model is not the same equation as a symmetric salt reservoir.

#### Neutral cylindrical cell model

↑ **Parent:** [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](#poisson-boltzmann-equation)

Confine the [counterions](chemistry.md#counterion) of a uniformly charged infinite cylinder within a coaxial cylinder of radius $R$, with total mobile line charge $\lambda$. The [electric field](#electric-field) vanishes at $R$, while its surface value at $a$ is fixed by the bare line charge. This gives $a\phi'(a)=2\lambda/\epsilon$ and $R\phi'(R)=0$ in [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units). Sending $R/a\to\infty$ defines the [infinite-dilution limit of cylindrical counterions](#infinite-dilution-limit-of-cylindrical-counterions).

##### Infinite-dilution limit of cylindrical counterions

↑ **Parent:** [Neutral cylindrical cell model](#neutral-cylindrical-cell-model)

In a neutral counterion-only cylindrical cell, send the outer radius to infinity at fixed cylinder charge. Below the [Manning parameter](#manning-parameter) threshold, number density tends to zero at each fixed radius. Above threshold, the [cylindrical counterion-only Poisson-Boltzmann profile](#cylindrical-counterion-only-poisson-boltzmann-profile) retains line charge $\lambda-q/\ell_B$ locally, while the rest recedes to infinity. Therefore the limit of the full finite-cell charge integral is not the charge integral of the pointwise limiting density.

#### Cylindrical counterion-only Poisson-Boltzmann profile

↑ **Parent:** [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](#poisson-boltzmann-equation)

For $u=\log(r/a)$ and $\Phi=\beta q\phi-2u$, a counterion-only [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](#poisson-boltzmann-equation) reduces to $\Phi''=-Ce^{-\Phi}$. Conditions $\Phi(0)=0$, $\Phi\to\infty$ and $\Phi'\to0$ give $\Phi=2\log(1+bu)$, $b=\sqrt{C/2}>0$. Matching surface [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) gives $b=\eta-1$, so this branch requires the [Manning parameter](#manning-parameter) $\eta>1$. Its number density is $n(r)=(\eta-1)^2(a/r)^2/[2\pi\ell_Ba^2(1+(\eta-1)\log(r/a))^2]$. It leaves residual line-charge magnitude $q/\ell_B$.

#### Counterion condensation

↑ **Parent:** [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](#poisson-boltzmann-equation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterion_condensation)

Localization of a finite fraction of oppositely charged [ions](chemistry.md#ion) near a charged object even as the confining volume grows without bound. In the salt-free infinite-cylinder mean-field model, the [Manning parameter](#manning-parameter) must exceed one for a nonzero limiting local density. The condensed charge does not include [ions](chemistry.md#ion) that escape to infinity; finite-cell charge neutrality and the local infinite-volume limit must not be interchanged.

##### Manning condensed fraction

↑ **Parent:** [Counterion condensation](#counterion-condensation)

In the mean-field infinite-dilution cylinder model, the fraction of neutralizing charge that remains localized is $f_c=\max(1-1/\eta,0)$, where $\eta$ is the [Manning parameter](#manning-parameter). For $\eta>1$, integrating the [cylindrical counterion-only Poisson-Boltzmann profile](#cylindrical-counterion-only-poisson-boltzmann-profile) gives $Q_c=\lambda-q/\ell_B$. Below threshold, the local limiting density vanishes even though every finite neutral cell contains the full [counterion](chemistry.md#counterion) charge.

##### Cylindrical screening charge

↑ **Parent:** [Counterion condensation](#counterion-condensation)

The positive mobile charge per unit axial length accumulated from cylinder radius $a$ to $r$ is $Q(r)=2\pi\int_a^r s\rho(s)\,ds$. For fixed negative line charge $-\lambda$, [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) gives signed radial [electric field](#electric-field) $E_r=2[-\lambda+Q(r)]/(\epsilon r)$ in [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units). Its absolute value is the field magnitude. An integral over a local infinite-dilution profile measures condensed charge, rather than every [ion](chemistry.md#ion) in a finite neutral cell.

##### Manning parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Counterion condensation](#counterion-condensation)

The dimensionless line-charge coupling $\eta=\lambda\ell_B/q$, with negative cylinder line charge $-\lambda$ and [counterions](chemistry.md#counterion) of charge $q>0$. Here the [Bjerrum length](#bjerrum-length) uses the same reference charge $q$. The mean-field infinite-dilution threshold is $\eta=1$. If $\ell_B$ is instead tabulated for elementary charge $e$ and [counterions](chemistry.md#counterion) have charge $ze$, the corresponding coupling is $z\lambda\ell_B/e$.

<h3 id="coulomb-s-law">Coulomb's law</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb's_law)

Point charges $q_1,q_2$ separated by a displacement $r$ exert the force

$$
F=\frac{q_1q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{r}{|r|^3}.
$$

#### Coulomb potential energy

↑ **Parent:** [Coulomb's law](#coulomb-s-law)

Two point charges have the displayed [potential energy](classical-mechanics.md#potential-energy) when it is set to zero at infinite separation. Integrating the radial force in [Coulomb's law](#coulomb-s-law), or differentiating $V$ via $F_r=-dV/dr$, gives this expression. Opposite charges give an attractive negative potential, as in the [hydrogen atom](physics.md#hydrogen-atom). Dividing by the test charge instead gives the source charge's scalar [electric potential](#electric-potential).

### Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)

The electric field vanishes inside a conductor at electrostatic equilibrium, so each connected conductor is equipotential and the exterior field is normal to its surface.

#### Electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](#conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium)

A Gaussian pillbox gives

$$
(E_{\rm out}-E_{\rm in})\cdot n=\frac\sigma{\epsilon_0}.
$$

Since $E_{\rm in}=0$, the surface charge density is $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_{\rm out}\cdot n$.

##### Surface charge density

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor](#electrostatic-boundary-conditions-at-a-conductor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_charge_density)

Surface charge density is electric charge per unit area. At a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium it equals $\epsilon_0\mathbf E_{\rm out}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf n$ for the normal pointing out of the conductor.

#### Charge sharing between distant connected spheres

↑ **Parent:** [Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](#conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium)

Two widely separated conducting spheres of radii $R_1,R_2$ connected by a thin wire have equal potentials, so a total charge $Q$ divides as

$$
Q_i=\frac{R_i}{R_1+R_2}Q.
$$

#### Charge on connected concentric spherical shells

↑ **Parent:** [Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](#conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium)

Two connected concentric spherical shells form one equipotential conductor. In the absence of charge in the inner cavity, all net charge lies on the exterior surface of the outer shell.

#### Induced charge on a conducting sphere in a uniform electric field

↑ **Parent:** [Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](#conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium)

A neutral conducting sphere of radius $R$ in the field $E\widehat z$ has exterior potential and induced surface charge

$$
\Phi=-E\left(r-\frac{R^3}{r^2}\right)\cos\theta,
\qquad
\sigma=3\epsilon_0E\cos\theta.
$$

The surface integral of $\sigma$ is zero.

### Electrostatic energy

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic_energy)

Electrostatic energy can be written as one half the charge-potential integral or epsilon-zero over two times the electric-field energy integral.

#### Electrostatic energy of a three-plate capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

In the parallel-plate approximation, a central plate at potential $V$ between grounded plates at $z=\pm H$ forms two parallel capacitors of gaps $H+a$ and $H-a$. Summing their field energies gives the displayed formula. At fixed voltage it is minimized at $a=0$. Finite circular plates have fringe fields; the uniform-gap expression assumes those edge corrections are neglected.

#### Electrostatic energy of a uniformly charged solid sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

A solid sphere of radius $R$ and uniform [charge density](#charge-density) has total [electric charge](#electric-charge) $Q$. [Gauss law](#gauss-s-law) gives the radial [electric field](#electric-field) $E_r=Qr/(4\pi\epsilon_0R^3)$ inside and $E_r=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$ outside. The [electric potential](#electric-potential) vanishing at infinity is $Q(3-r^2/R^2)/(8\pi\epsilon_0R)$ inside and $Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$ outside. Integrating the [electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy) density $\epsilon_0E^2/2$ over both regions gives $U=3Q^2/(20\pi\epsilon_0R)$. In a fixed-density family with $Q\propto Z$ and $R^3\propto Z$, the [electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy) scales as $Z^{5/3}$.

#### Electrostatic energy of alternating-charge concentric shells

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

A first spherical shell with charge $q$ followed by shells of charges $2(-1)^{n+1}q$ leaves enclosed charge $(-1)^{n+1}q$ after each shell. [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) therefore gives the same squared [electric field](#electric-field) $q^2/(16\pi^2\epsilon_0^2r^4)$ in every gap outside the first radius. Integrating the [electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy) density over all space gives $W=q^2/(8\pi\epsilon_0r_1)$, independent of the other shell radii.

#### Electrostatic energy of a uniformly charged spherical shell

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

A thin spherical shell of radius $a$ carrying total charge $Q$ has surface potential

$$
\Phi(a)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0a}
$$

and electrostatic self-energy

$$
U=\frac12Q\Phi(a)=\frac{Q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0a}.
$$

#### Electrostatic potential energy of point charges

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

For distinct [point charges](#point-charge) $q_i$ at positions $r_i$, the electrostatic [potential energy](classical-mechanics.md#potential-energy) is

$$
U=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{i<j}\frac{q_iq_j}{|r_i-r_j|}.
$$

When some charges are fixed, the terms depending on a movable charge form its potential energy in their [electric potential](#electric-potential).

#### Coaxial cylindrical capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic energy](#electrostatic-energy)

For coaxial cylinders of radii $a<b$ and length $L\gg b$, carrying charges per unit length $\pm\lambda$,

$$
E(r)=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0r}\widehat r,
\qquad
V=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\log\frac ba,
\qquad
C=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0L}{\log(b/a)}.
$$

The field energy is $U=\lambda^2L\log(b/a)/(4\pi\epsilon_0)=QV/2$.

##### Coaxial capacitor with grounded inner and outer cylinders

↑ **Parent:** [Coaxial cylindrical capacitor](#coaxial-cylindrical-capacitor)

A driven cylindrical conductor between two grounded coaxial conductors forms two [coaxial cylindrical capacitors](#coaxial-cylindrical-capacitor) in parallel. For radii $a,\lambda a,2a$, the [capacitance per unit length](#capacitance-per-unit-length) is the sum $C'=2\pi\epsilon_0[1/\log\lambda+1/\log(2/\lambda)]$. Writing $u=\log\lambda$ and $L=\log2$, the function $1/u+1/(L-u)$ is strictly [convex](real-analysis.md#convex-function) on $(0,L)$ and symmetric about $L/2$. Hence $C'$ has its unique minimum at $\lambda=\sqrt2$, with value $8\pi\epsilon_0/\log2$. As $\lambda=1+\delta$ approaches the inner conductor, $C'\sim2\pi\epsilon_0/\delta$, agreeing with the local [parallel-plate capacitor](#parallel-plate-capacitor) approximation.

### Electric dipole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_dipole_moment)

The electric dipole moment is the integral of position weighted by charge density.

#### Time derivative of the electric dipole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole moment](#electric-dipole-moment)

For a localized charge and current distribution satisfying [charge conservation from Maxwell equations](#charge-conservation-from-maxwell-equations),

$$
\dot{\mathbf p}=\int\mathbf J(x,t)\,d^3x.
$$

#### Electric dipole

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole moment](#electric-dipole-moment)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_dipole)

An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges in a small separation limit with their charge-times-separation vector held fixed.

##### Electric dipole-dipole interaction

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole](#electric-dipole)

Two point dipoles separated by $\mathbf r$ have interaction proportional to

$$
\frac{\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf p_2}{r^3}
-\frac{3(\mathbf p_1\cdot\mathbf r)(\mathbf p_2\cdot\mathbf r)}{r^5}.
$$

The formula combines the source [electric dipole](#electric-dipole) field with the second dipole's coupling to that field.

### Electric multipole expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](#electrostatics)

For a localized charge distribution, the far [electric potential](#electric-potential) is an inverse-radius expansion whose first terms are the total charge monopole, the electric dipole moment, and the electric quadrupole moment.

#### Electric quadrupole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Electric multipole expansion](#electric-multipole-expansion)

The trace-free [electric quadrupole moment](#electric-quadrupole-moment) is a symmetric tensor measuring the second-order term in an [electric multipole expansion](#electric-multipole-expansion). For a localized charge distribution its contribution to the far-field [electrostatic potential](#electric-potential) is $Q_{ij}\widehat r_i\widehat r_j/(8\pi\epsilon_0r^3)$, with summation over indices. The moment may be nonzero even when the total [electric charge](#electric-charge) or [electric dipole moment](#electric-dipole-moment) is nonzero; it should not be confused with an [electric quadrupole](#electric-quadrupole) whose lower moments vanish. Changing the origin generally changes the tensor when lower moments are nonzero.

#### Electric quadrupole

↑ **Parent:** [Electric multipole expansion](#electric-multipole-expansion)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_quadrupole)

An electric quadrupole is a charge distribution whose total charge and [electric dipole moment](#electric-dipole-moment) vanish while its second charge moment does not. Its potential decays as $r^{-3}$.

#### Multipole expansion of three collinear charges

↑ **Parent:** [Electric multipole expansion](#electric-multipole-expansion)

For charges $-Q,NQ,-MQ$ at $z=d,0,-d$, respectively,

$$
\Phi(r,\theta)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[
\frac{N-M-1}{r}+\frac{(M-1)d\cos\theta}{r^2}
-\frac{(M+1)d^2P_2(\cos\theta)}{r^3}+O(r^{-4})\right].
$$

The monopole vanishes when $N=M+1$, the dipole vanishes when $M=1$, and both vanish when $(N,M)=(2,1)$.

##### Dipole and quadrupole scaling limits of three collinear charges

↑ **Parent:** [Multipole expansion of three collinear charges](#multipole-expansion-of-three-collinear-charges)

Subject to $N=M+1$, keeping $Qd$ fixed as $d\to0$ gives a finite point-dipole limit when $M\ne1$. Keeping $Qd^2$ fixed gives a finite pure-quadrupole limit only when $M=1$; otherwise the dipole diverges.

##### Transverse force from a collinear electric quadrupole

↑ **Parent:** [Multipole expansion of three collinear charges](#multipole-expansion-of-three-collinear-charges)

For charges $-Q,2Q,-Q$ at $z=d,0,-d$, the exact force on a charge $-Q$ at $(x,0,0)$ is

$$
F=-\frac{Q^2x}{2\pi\epsilon_0}
\left(\frac1{|x|^3}-\frac1{(x^2+d^2)^{3/2}}\right)\widehat x.
$$

It points toward the origin for every nonzero $x$.

## Magnetostatics

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetostatics)

Magnetostatics studies time-independent magnetic fields and steady currents. In vacuum its field equations are $\nabla\mathbin\cdot\mathbf B=0$ and $\nabla\mathbin\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J$.

### Magnetic scalar potential

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetostatics](#magnetostatics)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_scalar_potential)

In a simply connected current-free region of constant [magnetic permeability](#permeability-electromagnetism), [Ampère's circuital law](#ampere-s-circuital-law) makes the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) curl-free, so it admits a scalar potential. The absence of magnetic monopoles then gives [Laplace's equation](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation) for that potential. Some conventions define the potential for $\mathbf H$ instead of $\mathbf B$, differing by the constant permeability. Boundary conditions, including imposed fields at infinity, determine the potential up to an additive constant.

#### Gravity-equivalent magnetic surface density

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic scalar potential](#magnetic-scalar-potential)

For an isolated razor-thin disk, rescale its exterior [magnetic scalar potential](#magnetic-scalar-potential) to satisfy $\sqrt{4\pi G/\mu_0}\mathbf B=-\nabla\Phi_M$. Comparing the normal magnetic boundary derivative with $\partial_z\Phi=2\pi G\Sigma$ identifies the displayed equivalent signed [surface density](astrophysics.md#surface-density-of-a-disk). Both exterior potentials satisfy [Laplace's equation](partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-equation). The equivalent [surface density](astrophysics.md#surface-density-of-a-disk) is a representation of the magnetic boundary data, rather than physical negative mass.

## Maxwell equations

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equations)

Maxwell’s equations relate electric and magnetic fields to charge and current:

$$
\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}E=\rho/\epsilon_0,
\qquad
\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}B=0,
\qquad
\nabla\times E=-\partial_tB,
\qquad
\nabla\times B=\mu_0J+\mu_0\epsilon_0\partial_tE.
$$

### Electromagnetic duality

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_duality)

Vacuum [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) without sources are invariant under $E'=E\cos\alpha+cB\sin\alpha$, $cB'=cB\cos\alpha-E\sin\alpha$. At $\alpha=\pi/2$ this is $E'=cB$, $B'=-E/c$. Electric-conductor boundary conditions are not invariant under arbitrary duality rotations; the transformed fields must be tested separately against the wall conditions.

### Derivative-order ambiguity in covariant Maxwell potential equations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

Use the negative curvature convention defined in [curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities](general-relativity.md#curvature-sign-convention-in-killing-derivative-identities), and $C_{ab}=C^c{}_{acb}$. Covariant derivatives of a vector obey $[\nabla_b,\nabla^a]A^b=-C^a{}_bA^b$. Hence the flat-space equivalent expressions $\Box A^a-\partial_b\partial^aA^b$ and $\Box A^a-\partial^a\partial_bA^b$ acquire a difference $+C^a{}_bA^b$ under the [comma-to-semicolon prescription](general-relativity.md#comma-to-semicolon-prescription). Covariantizing $F_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A_{b]}$ and its [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) gives $\Box A^a-\nabla^a(\nabla_bA^b)+C^a{}_bA^b=\mu_0J^a$. This form respects [gauge invariance](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-invariance). Dropping the Ricci term is generally not gauge invariant. The two expressions coincide in a Ricci-flat [vacuum spacetime](general-relativity.md#vacuum-spacetime); vanishing matter alone with nonzero [cosmological constant](cosmology.md#cosmological-constant) does not imply Ricci flatness.

### Magnetic Galilean limit of the pre-Maxwell equations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

The magnetic nonrelativistic limit neglects displacement current and uses $\partial_t\mathbf B=-\nabla\times\mathbf E$, $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$. Under a [Galilean transformation](special-relativity.md#galilean-transformation) $\mathbf x'=\mathbf x-\mathbf vt$, $t'=t$, its consistent field transformations are $\mathbf B'=\mathbf B$, $\mathbf J'=\mathbf J$ and $\mathbf E'=\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$. Indeed $\partial_{t'}=\partial_t+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla$ and $\nabla\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)=-(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B$, giving covariance of the induction equation. The full charge-current transformation has $\mathbf J'=\mathbf J-\rho_e\mathbf v$; neglecting its convective correction belongs to the magnetic quasineutral approximation, not exact relativistic electrodynamics. With $\mathbf u'=\mathbf u-\mathbf v$, the combination $\mathbf E+\mathbf u\times\mathbf B$ is invariant. Vanishing electric field in the fluid rest frame therefore gives the [ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#ideal-magnetohydrodynamic-induction-equation).

<h3 id="gauss-s-law-for-magnetism">Gauss's law for magnetism</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss's_law_for_magnetism)

Gauss's law for magnetism is $\nabla\mathbin\cdot\mathbf B=0$. Its integral form says that the net magnetic flux through every closed surface vanishes, so the normal magnetic field is continuous across an ordinary interface.

### Vacuum Maxwell equations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

For the electromagnetic two-form $F$, the source-free Maxwell equations are $dF=0$ and $d(*F)=0$. The first is the Bianchi identity and the second is the dynamical equation.

#### Anti-self-dual Maxwell equations

↑ **Parent:** [Vacuum Maxwell equations](#vacuum-maxwell-equations)

In the [two-component spinor](connection-1-form.md#two-component-spinor) convention with primed symmetric [gauge curvature](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field-strength) self-dual, an [Abelian](group.md#abelian-group) [Maxwell field](#electromagnetic-field) is anti-self-dual when its primed symmetric [gauge curvature](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-field-strength) [two-component spinor](connection-1-form.md#two-component-spinor) vanishes. For a locally exact field $F=dA$, the [Bianchi identity](fiber-bundle.md#bianchi-identity) gives $dF=0$; the duality relation then also gives $d{*}F=0$. Thus it obeys the source-free [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations). In complexified Lorentzian signature the [Hodge star](differential-form.md#hodge-star-operator) [eigenvalues](linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $\pm i$; the [two-component spinor](connection-1-form.md#two-component-spinor) condition specifies which component is meant without imposing a Euclidean eigenvalue on that signature.

### Maxwell equations in matter

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

In macroscopic matter with free charge density $\rho$ and free current density $J$,

$$
\nabla\cdot D=\rho,
\quad
\nabla\cdot B=0,
\quad
\nabla\times E=-\partial_tB,
\quad
\nabla\times H=J+\partial_tD.
$$

The electric displacement $D$ and magnetic field strength $H$ encode the material response.

These are the [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) after separating free and bound material sources.

#### Constitutive relations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations in matter](#maxwell-equations-in-matter)

Constitutive relations specify how matter responds to fields. A homogeneous isotropic linear electromagnetic medium obeys $\mathbf D=\varepsilon\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H$.

##### Permeability (electromagnetism)

↑ **Parent:** [Constitutive relations](#constitutive-relations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeability_(electromagnetism))

Electromagnetic permeability relates magnetic flux density to magnetic field strength through $\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H$ in an isotropic linear medium.

#### Linear anisotropic dielectric

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations in matter](#maxwell-equations-in-matter)

A linear anisotropic medium has constitutive relations $D_i=\varepsilon_{ij}E_j$ and $B_i=\mu_{ij}H_j$. Symmetric, time-independent tensors make the field-energy density a quadratic form.

Here [permittivity](#permittivity) is a tensor, so the electric displacement need not be parallel to the electric field.

##### Birefringence

↑ **Parent:** [Linear anisotropic dielectric](#linear-anisotropic-dielectric)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birefringence)

[Birefringence](#birefringence) is the existence of different optical propagation constants for different eigenpolarizations in an anisotropic material. It provides the propagation-mode separation needed for [adiabatic optical following in a twisted nematic](critical-phenomenon.md#adiabatic-optical-following-in-a-twisted-nematic).

<h3 id="ampere-s-circuital-law">Ampère's circuital law</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampère's_circuital_law)

The Ampère-Maxwell equation

$$
\nabla\times B=\mu_0J+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial E}{\partial t}
$$

relates magnetic circulation to conduction and displacement current.

#### Amperian loop

↑ **Parent:** [Ampère's circuital law](#ampere-s-circuital-law)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amperian_loop)

An Amperian loop is a closed curve used in the integral form of Ampère's circuital law or the Ampère-Maxwell equation.

<h4 id="magnetostatic-ampere-law">Magnetostatic Ampère law</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ampère's circuital law](#ampere-s-circuital-law)

In vacuum [magnetostatics](#magnetostatics), the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law) reduces to $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J$. Applying [Stokes theorem](calculus.md#stokes-theorem) gives $\oint_C\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu_0I_{\mathrm{enc}}$, where $I_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is the [electric current](#electric-current) crossing an oriented spanning surface. Time-dependent [electric fields](#electric-field) require the additional [displacement current](#displacement-current) term in the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law).

#### Displacement current

↑ **Parent:** [Ampère's circuital law](#ampere-s-circuital-law)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_current)

In vacuum, the displacement-current density is $\epsilon_0\partial E/\partial t$. Its contribution to magnetic circulation makes the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law) compatible with changing electric charge.

<h3 id="faraday-s-law-of-induction">Faraday's law of induction</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday's_law_of_induction)

Faraday's law is

$$
\nabla\times E=-\frac{\partial B}{\partial t},
\qquad
\mathcal E=-\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}.
$$

<h4 id="lenz-s-law">Lenz's law</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Faraday's law of induction](#faraday-s-law-of-induction)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenz's_law)

The induced current flows so that its magnetic effect opposes the change of flux that produced it.

##### Terminal speed of a falling conducting loop

↑ **Parent:** [Lenz's law](#lenz-s-law)

If a loop of resistance $R$ has magnetic flux $\Phi_B=-kz$, then its induced current is $I=k\dot z/R$. When its magnetic force is $-kI$ in the vertical direction, its equation under gravity is

$$
m\ddot z=-mg-\frac{k^2}{R}\dot z,
$$

so its downward terminal speed is $Rmg/k^2$.

###### Lossless limit of magnetic loop braking

↑ **Parent:** [Terminal speed of a falling conducting loop](#terminal-speed-of-a-falling-conducting-loop)

For an external loop flux $Kz$, positive current orientation and upward [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) $KI$, including [self-inductance](#self-inductance) gives $\mathcal L\dot I+RI=-K\dot z$ and $m\ddot z=-mg+KI$. At zero resistance total linked flux is conserved. If initially $I=0$ at $z=z_0$, then $I=-K(z-z_0)/\mathcal L$ and the mechanical motion is an undamped oscillator about $z_0-mg\mathcal L/K^2$. The finite-resistance terminal speed tends to zero as $R\to0$, but neglecting [self-inductance](#self-inductance) is a singular approximation in that limit. A lossless loop does not dispose of [energy](classical-mechanics.md#energy) through [Joule heating](#joule-heating).

<h3 id="gauss-s-law">Gauss's law</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss's_law)

Gauss's law states

$$
\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}E=\rho/\epsilon_0,
\qquad
\int_{\partial V}E\mathbin{\cdot}dS=Q_{\rm enclosed}/\epsilon_0.
$$

#### Hyperbolic-space Coulomb field

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law)

In static time times round three-dimensional [hyperbolic space](geometry-and-topology.md#hyperbolic-space) of curvature radius $R$, a geodesic sphere has area $4\pi R^2\sinh^2(\xi/R)$. The [Gauss law](#gauss-s-law) therefore gives the displayed radial field. It approaches the flat-space inverse-square field near the charge and falls exponentially at large geodesic distance. The curvature changes the area of a sphere, not the conserved flux.

#### Force between hemispheres of a uniformly charged ball

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law)

For uniform [charge density](#charge-density) $\rho_e=3Q/(4\pi R^3)$, [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) gives the interior [electric field](#electric-field) $E=\rho_e r/(3\varepsilon_0)$. Integrate its vertical force density $\rho_e^2z/(3\varepsilon_0)$ over the upper hemisphere: $\int z\,dV=\pi R^4/4$. Internal pair forces in that hemisphere cancel, so its net force is exactly the force from the lower hemisphere. The result is upward and has magnitude $3Q^2/(64\pi\varepsilon_0R^2)$.

#### Gaussian surface

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_surface)

A Gaussian surface is a closed surface used with the integral form of [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) to relate electric flux to enclosed charge.

#### Gaussian pillbox

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law)

A Gaussian pillbox is a thin cylindrical [Gaussian surface](#gaussian-surface) crossing an interface. Shrinking its height converts a divergence law into a jump condition for the field's normal component.

### Charge conservation from Maxwell equations

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

Taking the divergence of the Ampère-Maxwell equation and using Gauss's law gives

$$
\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot J=0.
$$

Hence the charge in a fixed volume changes only through current crossing its boundary.

#### Charge continuity equation

↑ **Parent:** [Charge conservation from Maxwell equations](#charge-conservation-from-maxwell-equations)

The local form of [conservation of electric charge](#charge-conservation) relates [charge density](#charge-density) to [current density](#current-density). Taking the [divergence](calculus.md#divergence) of the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](#ampere-s-circuital-law) and substituting [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) proves the equation, using that the [divergence of a curl is zero](calculus.md#divergence-of-a-curl-is-zero).

### Covariant Maxwell equation with the minus-plus-plus-plus metric

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

With metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $A_0=-\Phi/c$, and $j^\mu=(c\rho,J)$, the field tensor satisfies

$$
\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0j^\mu,
\qquad
\partial_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu]}=0.
$$

Its components are $F_{0i}=-E_i/c$ and $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, so these tensor equations reproduce all four Maxwell equations.

### Source-free Maxwell equations in a linear medium

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations)

For $D=\epsilon E$ and $B=\mu H$ with no free charge or current,

$$
\nabla\cdot D=0,\qquad
\nabla\cdot B=0,\qquad
\nabla\times E=-\partial_tB,\qquad
\nabla\times H=\partial_tD.
$$

#### Plane electromagnetic wave in a linear medium

↑ **Parent:** [Source-free Maxwell equations in a linear medium](#source-free-maxwell-equations-in-a-linear-medium)

A plane wave in a homogeneous linear medium satisfies

$$
\omega^2=\frac{|k|^2}{\epsilon\mu},
\qquad
v=\frac1{\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}},
\qquad
B_0=\frac1\omega k\times E_0.
$$

##### Transversality of an electromagnetic plane wave

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave in a linear medium](#plane-electromagnetic-wave-in-a-linear-medium)

The source-free divergence equations imply $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf E_0=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B_0=0$, so both fields are transverse to the wavevector.

## Magnetic vector potential

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_vector_potential)

A magnetic vector potential satisfies B equal to its curl and is defined up to a gradient.

### Magnetic helicity

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_helicity)

The [magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity) integrates the [dot product](linear-algebra.md#dot-product) of a [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential) and its [magnetic field](#magnetic-field). It measures linkage and twisting of magnetic flux. For a [solenoidal vector field](calculus.md#solenoidal-vector-field) $\mathbf B$ tangent to the boundary, it is unchanged by the gauge transformation $\mathbf A\mapsto\mathbf A+\nabla\chi$, since the change is $\int_V\nabla\cdot(\chi\mathbf B)\,dV=\int_{\partial V}\chi\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S=0$. If its density satisfies $\partial_t h=\nabla\cdot(f\mathbf B-h\mathbf u)$ with both vector fields tangent to the boundary of a fixed volume, the [divergence theorem](calculus.md#divergence-theorem) gives conservation of its integral. This is an instance of [helicity conservation by tangent boundary conditions](calculus.md#helicity-conservation-by-tangent-boundary-conditions).

#### Magnetic-energy lower bound from helicity

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity)

In a bounded regular domain, fix the global extension or circulation convention for the [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential). A bounded inverse of [curl](calculus.md#curl) on tangent [solenoidal vector fields](calculus.md#solenoidal-vector-field) gives $\|\mathbf A\|_2\leq C_D\|\mathbf B\|_2$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) then gives $|H_M|\leq C_D\|\mathbf B\|_2^2=2\mu_0 C_DE_B$. One construction extends the tangent field by zero outside the domain and uses the whole-space [Biot-Savart law](#biot-savart-law); its kernel of size $|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^{-2}$ is integrable over a bounded region, giving the required $L^2$ bound. Nonzero conserved [magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity) therefore prevents complete decay of [magnetic energy](#magnetic-energy).

#### Current helicity

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity)

Current helicity correlates a [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) with its [curl](calculus.md#curl), which is proportional to [electric current density](#current-density). It differs from [magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity), which uses a [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential). For a solenoidal monochromatic field of wavenumber $k$ in a periodic domain, choose $\mathbf a_b=\nabla\times\mathbf b/k^2$. Then $\nabla\times\mathbf a_b=\mathbf b$ and $H_c=k^2\langle\mathbf a_b\cdot\mathbf b\rangle$.

#### Gauge dependence of helicity in an open magnetic flux tube

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity)

An open [flux tube](#flux-tube) carries [magnetic flux](#magnetic-flux) through its end caps. Its ordinary [magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity) can therefore change under a single-valued [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation). For $\mathbf B=aR\mathbf e_\phi+b\mathbf e_z$ inside $R<R_0$ and zero outside, a regular family of [magnetic vector potentials](#magnetic-vector-potential) is $A_\phi=bR/2$, $A_z=C-aR^2/2$ inside and $A_\phi=bR_0^2/(2R)$, $A_z=C-aR_0^2/2$ outside. Their tangential components agree at $R_0$. Inside, $\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=bC$, so the helicity per unit length is $\pi bCR_0^2$. Changing $C$ is the gauge transformation $\Lambda=\Delta C\,z$, and changes this result whenever $b\ne0$. The convention $A_z=0$ outside gives $C=aR_0^2/2$ and helicity per unit length $\pi abR_0^4/2$, while $C=0$ gives zero. Without an end-cap or gauge convention the ordinary helicity is not unique.

#### Material transport of magnetic helicity density

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity)

For smooth [ideal magnetohydrodynamics](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#ideal-magnetohydrodynamics), write $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ and $\partial_t\mathbf A=\mathbf u\times\mathbf B-\nabla\varphi$. The [continuity equation](physics.md#continuity-equation) and induction equation give $D_t(\mathbf B/\rho)=(\mathbf B/\rho)\cdot\nabla\mathbf u$. The vector-potential equation becomes $D_t\mathbf A=\nabla(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf A-\varphi)-(\nabla\mathbf u)^T\mathbf A$. Contracting these two equations cancels their velocity-gradient terms and proves

$$
D_t\left(\frac{\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B}{\rho}\right)=\frac{\mathbf B}{\rho}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf A-\varphi).
$$

Consequently the [magnetic helicity](#magnetic-helicity) in a material volume satisfies $dH_m/dt=\int_{\partial V}(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf A-\varphi)\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf n\,dS$. It is conserved when the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) is tangent to the boundary. A single-valued [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation) changes $H_m$ by $\int_{\partial V}\Lambda\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf n\,dS$, which also vanishes under that condition.

### Radial-gauge potential for a divergence-free magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)

For a smooth [divergence](calculus.md#divergence)-free field on $\mathbb R^3$, the [differential form](differential-form.md) $F=\iota_{\mathbf B}(dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3)$ is closed. The radial homotopy formula gives a global [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential) $\mathbf A(\mathbf x)=\int_0^1s\,\mathbf B(s\mathbf x)\times\mathbf x\,ds$ with [curl](calculus.md#curl) $\mathbf B$. For constant $\mathbf B$, this reduces to $\mathbf A=\tfrac12\mathbf B\times\mathbf x$.

<h3 id="neumann-s-mutual-inductance-formula">Neumann's mutual-inductance formula</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)

For thin closed wire loops $C_1,C_2$, the mutual inductance is

$$
L_{12}=L_{21}
=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\oint_{C_1}\oint_{C_2}
\frac{dx_1\cdot dx_2}{|x_1-x_2|}.
$$

### Coulomb gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_gauge)

The Coulomb gauge imposes

$$
\nabla\cdot A=0
$$

on the [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential). A [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation) $A\mapsto A+\nabla\chi$ reaches this gauge when $\chi$ solves

$$
\nabla^2\chi=-\nabla\cdot A.
$$

#### Radiation gauge

↑ **Parent:** [Coulomb gauge](#coulomb-gauge)

For a free [electromagnetic field](#electromagnetic-field) with suitable boundary conditions, combine [Coulomb gauge](#coulomb-gauge) $\partial_iA_i=0$ with $A_0=0$. [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law) makes the second condition consistent in the source-free problem. The remaining field operator is spatially transverse and describes the two radiative [photon](quantum-mechanics.md#photon) polarizations. A constrained scalar potential must be restored when charges are present.

### Gauge transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation)

The transformation $A\mapsto A+\nabla\chi$ leaves $B=\nabla\times A$ unchanged because the [curl](calculus.md#curl) of a [gradient](calculus.md#gradient) vanishes.

#### Gauge-covariant time translation

↑ **Parent:** [Gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation)

For the [Maxwell field](#electromagnetic-field), the variation $\delta A_\nu=\alpha F_{0\nu}=\alpha(\partial_0A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_0)$ is time translation combined with a [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation). With constant $\alpha$, $\delta F_{\mu\nu}=\alpha\partial_0F_{\mu\nu}$, and the Lagrangian changes by $\alpha\partial_0\mathcal L$. Its [Noether current](quantum-field-theory.md#noether-current) is directly the time component of the [gauge-invariant Maxwell stress-energy tensor](quantum-field-theory.md#gauge-invariant-maxwell-stress-energy-tensor), providing the physical energy density without a potential-dependent improvement step.

### Magnetic dipole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_dipole_moment)

For a localized steady current density,

$$
m=\frac12\int x\times J(x)\,d^3x.
$$

It has dimensions $\mathrm{A\,m^2}$.

#### Localized steady-current moment identities

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment)

A localized steady [electric current density](#current-density) satisfies $\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$. Integrate the divergences of $x_i\mathbf j$ and $x_ix_k\mathbf j$ over a region enclosing its support; all boundary fluxes vanish. The first identity removes the monopole term from the magnetic vector potential expansion. The antisymmetric first moment is equivalent to the [magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment) $\frac12\int\mathbf x\times\mathbf j\,dV$.

#### Magnetic dipole potential energy

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment)

In an external field that varies slowly across a localized magnetic dipole,

$$
U=-\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B,
\qquad
\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B).
$$

The [magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment) determines the coupling to the external magnetic field.

#### Bohr magneton

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_magneton)

The Bohr magneton,

$$
\mu_B=\frac{e\hbar}{2m_e},
$$

is the natural unit of an electron's orbital and spin magnetic moment.

#### Coulomb-gauge vector potential of a localized steady current

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment)

If $\nabla\cdot J=0$ and the current decays enough for boundary terms to vanish, then

$$
A(x)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int\frac{J(x')}{|x-x'|}\,d^3x'
$$

satisfies $\nabla\cdot A=0$. Its far field is

$$
A(x)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{m\times x}{|x|^3}+\cdots.
$$

#### Magnetic dipole moment of a rigidly rotating charge distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](#magnetic-dipole-moment)

A circular charge distribution rotating with angular speed $\omega$ can be decomposed into current loops. A hoop of line density $\eta$ and radius $R$ has $m=\pi\eta\omega R^3\hat n$; a disc of surface density $\sigma$ has $m=\pi\sigma\omega R^4\hat n/4$.

## Biot-Savart law

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biot-Savart_law)

For a steady [current density](#current-density) $J$, the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) is

$$
B(r)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int
\frac{J(r')\times(r-r')}{|r-r'|^3}\,d^3r'.
$$

### On-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop

↑ **Parent:** [Biot-Savart law](#biot-savart-law)

A circular loop of radius $R$ carrying [electric current](#electric-current) $I$ has, on its axis,

$$
B(z)=\frac{\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}\,\hat z,
$$

where the sign of $\hat z$ is fixed by the [right-hand rule](#right-hand-rule). At its centre, $B(0)=\mu_0I/(2R)$.

### Magnetic-field cancellation between oppositely driven coaxial loops

↑ **Parent:** [Biot-Savart law](#biot-savart-law)

If coaxial loops of radii $R$ and $2R$, separated by $D$, carry currents $I$ and $2I$ in opposite senses, their on-axis fields cancel between the loops at distance $D/3$ from the smaller loop.

## Beltrami field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltrami_field)

A Beltrami field is a vector field parallel to its curl:

$$
\nabla\times J=\lambda J.
$$

If it is divergence-free and used as a magnetostatic current with constant nonzero $\lambda$, then $B=\mu_0J/\lambda$ solves $\nabla\times B=\mu_0J$ and $\nabla\cdot B=0$.

## Relativistic electromagnetism

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_electromagnetism)

Relativistic electromagnetism combines electric and magnetic fields into Lorentz tensors.

### Relativistic charged-particle action

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic electromagnetism](#relativistic-electromagnetism)

For metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$, a particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ coupled to a four-potential has the reparametrization-invariant action

$$
S=-mc\int\sqrt{-\dot x^2}\,d\lambda
+q\int A_\mu(x)\dot x^\mu\,d\lambda.
$$

#### Electromagnetic minimal coupling

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic charged-particle action](#relativistic-charged-particle-action)

Electromagnetic minimal coupling replaces the free kinetic momentum in an action or Hamiltonian by a combination involving the electromagnetic potential. For $L=-m\sqrt{\dot x^a\dot x_a}-qA_a\dot x^a$, the canonical covector is $P_a=-m\dot x_a/\sqrt{\dot x^2}-qA_a$, so $P_a+qA_a$ recovers the potential-independent covector. Under $A_a\mapsto A_a+\partial_a\chi$, the action changes by an endpoint term. The fixed-endpoint equations are the [relativistic Lorentz force](#relativistic-lorentz-force) equation and are gauge invariant.

#### Gauge invariance of the charged-particle worldline action

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic charged-particle action](#relativistic-charged-particle-action)

Under $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi$, the interaction changes by

$$
q\int\frac{d\chi(x(\lambda))}{d\lambda}\,d\lambda
=q\,[\chi]_{\rm endpoints}.
$$

Fixed-endpoint equations of motion are therefore gauge invariant.

#### Lorentz-force equation from the worldline action

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic charged-particle action](#relativistic-charged-particle-action)

Varying the [relativistic charged-particle action](#relativistic-charged-particle-action) with fixed endpoints gives

$$
m\frac{du_\mu}{d\tau}=qF_{\mu\nu}u^\nu,
$$

or the equivalent raised-index equation.

### Electromagnetic four-potential

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic electromagnetism](#relativistic-electromagnetism)

The four-potential combines the [electric potential](#electric-potential) and [magnetic vector potential](#magnetic-vector-potential) as $A^\mu=(\phi/c,\mathbf A)$. The electromagnetic four-potential $A_\mu$ transforms under

$$
A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi,
$$

while the antisymmetric field tensor $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$ is unchanged.

#### Electromagnetic multipole expansion

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic four-potential](#electromagnetic-four-potential)

An electromagnetic multipole expansion expresses the potentials or fields of a localized source as successively smaller monopole, dipole, quadrupole, and higher moments in the ratio of source size to observation distance.

#### Retarded potential

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic four-potential](#electromagnetic-four-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retarded_potential)

A retarded potential evaluates each source at the event on the source's past light cone. This accounts for propagation at the speed of light and enforces [causality](special-relativity.md#causality).

##### Retarded time

↑ **Parent:** [Retarded potential](#retarded-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retarded_time)

For a source point $y(s)$ and field event $(x,t)$, the retarded time solves

$$
t_{\rm ret}+\frac{|x-y(t_{\rm ret})|}{c}=t.
$$

###### Uniqueness of retarded time for a subluminal source

↑ **Parent:** [Retarded time](#retarded-time)

For $R(s)=|x-y(s)|$ and $|v|<c$, the function $F(s)=s+R(s)/c$ has

$$
F'(s)=1-\widehat R\cdot v/c>0.
$$

It is strictly increasing, so the retarded-time equation has at most one solution.

<h5 id="lienard-wiechert-potential">Liénard–Wiechert potential</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Retarded potential](#retarded-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liénard–Wiechert_potential)

The potentials of a point charge $q$ are

$$
\phi=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac1{R-\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf R/c},
\qquad
\mathbf A=\frac{\mathbf v}{c^2}\phi,
$$

with every source quantity evaluated at the [retarded time](#retarded-time).

#### Lorenz gauge condition

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic four-potential](#electromagnetic-four-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_gauge_condition)

The Lorenz gauge condition is

$$
\frac1{c^2}\partial_t\phi+\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0,
$$

or $\partial_\mu A^\mu=0$ in covariant notation.

##### Residual Lorenz gauge symmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Lorenz gauge condition](#lorenz-gauge-condition)

An Abelian [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation) $A_a\mapsto A_a+\partial_a\lambda$ changes $\partial\cdot A$ by $\Box\lambda$. Consequently [gauge fixing](relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-fixing) by a quadratic function of $\partial\cdot A$ retains transformations satisfying $\Box\lambda=0$, subject to the same [boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#boundary-condition). Eliminating those residual transformations is part of specifying an inverse with a particular [Green function](analysis.md#green-s-function) prescription.

#### Electromagnetic field tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic four-potential](#electromagnetic-four-potential)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field_tensor)

The electromagnetic field tensor is

$$
F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu A^\nu-\partial^\nu A^\mu.
$$

It packages the electric and magnetic fields into an antisymmetric rank-two Lorentz tensor and transforms as

$$
F'^{\mu\nu}(x')
=\Lambda^\mu{}_\rho\Lambda^\nu{}_\sigma F^{\rho\sigma}(x).
$$

##### Dual electromagnetic field tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)

The dual electromagnetic field tensor is obtained by applying the [Hodge star operator](differential-form.md#hodge-star-operator) to the [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor). For metric signature $(+---)$ and $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$, define $\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$. With $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, it has components $\widetilde F^{0i}=-B_i$ and $\widetilde F^{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}E_k/c$. The homogeneous [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) become $\partial_\mu\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=0$.

##### Maxwell Lagrangian

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)

The source-free electromagnetic [Lagrangian density](quantum-field-theory.md#lagrangian-density) is quadratic in the [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor). Its [Euler-Lagrange field equation](quantum-field-theory.md#euler-lagrange-field-equation) is $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=0$. Antisymmetry of $F$ makes the density invariant under [gauge transformations](#gauge-transformation), and contraction of its indices makes it a [Lorentz scalar](special-relativity.md#lorentz-scalar).

###### Maxwell action

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell Lagrangian](#maxwell-lagrangian)

The source-free [Maxwell Lagrangian](#maxwell-lagrangian) gives $S=-\tfrac14\int F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,d^4x$ in mostly-minus Lorentzian signature. Its [Euclidean action](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#euclidean-action) is the displayed positive quadratic functional of the [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor). Variation, with boundary terms vanishing, yields $\partial_\mu F_{\mu\nu}=0$.

###### First-order Maxwell action

↑ **Parent:** [Maxwell action](#maxwell-action)

Vary the antisymmetric auxiliary [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor) and the [electromagnetic four-potential](#electromagnetic-four-potential) independently. The first variation sets $F_{ab}=\nabla_aA_b-\nabla_bA_a$ algebraically, and variation of the potential gives $\nabla_bF^{ab}=0$. Substitution of the algebraic equation reduces the first-order action to the usual negative quadratic [Maxwell action](#maxwell-action). Metric variation gives the same stress tensor after eliminating the auxiliary field, because the chain-rule term multiplying its variational equation then vanishes.

##### Electromagnetic Bianchi identity

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)

Because the [electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor) is the [exterior derivative](differential-form.md#exterior-derivative) $F=dA$ of a potential, $dF=d^2A=0$. For a torsion-free connection this is

$$
\nabla_aF_{bc}+\nabla_bF_{ca}+\nabla_cF_{ab}=0,
$$

which is the covariant form of the two homogeneous [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations).

##### Lorentz force

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_force)

For [four-momentum](special-relativity.md#four-momentum) $p^\mu$ and [four-velocity](special-relativity.md#four-velocity) $u^\mu$, the covariant Lorentz-force law is

$$
\frac{dp^\mu}{d\tau}=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu.
$$

Its spatial and temporal components are

$$
\frac{d\mathbf p}{dt}=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B),
\qquad
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

###### Larmor theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

For particles with the same charge-to-mass ratio, rotationally invariant mutual [central forces](physics.md#central-force), and a weak uniform [magnetic field](#magnetic-field), a frame with the displayed [angular velocity](classical-mechanics.md#angular-velocity) cancels the velocity-dependent magnetic [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) against the [Coriolis force](physics.md#coriolis-force). The remaining rotational and magnetic terms are quadratic in $\mathbf B$. Thus, to first order in the field, the rotating-frame equations coincide with the zero-field equations. This is the classical dynamical basis of [Larmor precession](quantum-mechanics.md#larmor-precession); equality is an approximation rather than an exact removal of the magnetic field.

###### Radial escape in an inverse-radius axial magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

For an axial [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) $\mathbf B=(B_0/r)\mathbf e_z$, let $L=mr^2\dot\theta$. The [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) gives $\dot L=-qB_0\dot r$, so $\ell=(L+qB_0r)/m$ is constant. Since [magnetic forces do no work](#magnetic-forces-do-no-work) and $\dot z$ is constant, the transverse speed $v_\perp$ is constant and $\dot r^2=v_\perp^2-(\ell/r-c)^2$. If $c\ell>0$ and $0<v_\perp<|c|$, two finite radial [turning points](classical-mechanics.md#turning-point) confine the motion. If $v_\perp>|c|$, an outward branch escapes to infinite radius. At equality a marginal escaping branch exists when $c\ell>0$, but $\ell=0$ gives a circular orbit instead. The transverse [escape velocity](classical-mechanics.md#escape-velocity) threshold is $|qB_0|/m$; motion parallel to the [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) cannot provide transverse escape energy.

###### Velocity rotation in a time-dependent axial magnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

Under the magnetic-only [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force), planar velocity has constant magnitude and an accumulated phase determined by the time integral of the axial field. For $B_z(t)=\beta t$, the planar position involves [Fresnel integrals](analysis.md#fresnel-integral). With initial planar velocity $(1,0)$ and zero initial position, its limit is $(\sqrt{\pi m/(4q\beta)},-\sqrt{\pi m/(4q\beta)})$. The model prescribes a magnetic-only force; a physical time-dependent field may also produce an induced electric field.

###### Magnetic forces do no work

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

The magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) is perpendicular to the particle [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity). Its power is therefore zero, so with no other forces the [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) remains constant. This remains true relativistically because $dE/dt=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v$; a magnetic force can change momentum direction without changing total energy.

###### Geometrization of the Lorentz force by an affine connection

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

For smooth stationary fields on $\mathbb R^3$, set $\Gamma^i{}_{tt}=-E^i$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{tj}=\Gamma^i{}_{jt}=\epsilon^i{}_{jk}B^k$, with all other [Christoffel symbols](riemannian-geometry.md#christoffel-symbol) zero. This [torsion-free connection](fiber-bundle.md#torsion-free-connection) makes the equation $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf E+2\mathbf B\times\dot{\mathbf x}$ a [geodesic equation](riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation) with time as [affine parameter](riemannian-geometry.md#affine-parameter). Arbitrary smooth fields suffice; a metric realization imposes additional potential conditions.

###### Electric drift in crossed uniform fields

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

In uniform perpendicular electric and [magnetic fields](#magnetic-field), the guiding centre of a charged particle drifts with $\mathbf v_D=\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/|\mathbf B|^2$. This [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity) is independent of the charge and mass; cyclotron motion is superposed on it.

###### Lorentz four-force

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

The covariant force on a charged particle is its charge times the electromagnetic field-strength tensor contracted with its [four-velocity](special-relativity.md#four-velocity). Antisymmetry keeps the four-force orthogonal to that velocity and preserves the rest mass.

###### Magnetic force on a current-carrying wire

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

A wire carrying current $I$ in a magnetic field experiences

$$
d\mathbf F=I\,d\boldsymbol\ell\times\mathbf B.
$$

This follows by summing the magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force) over the moving charge carriers.

###### Relativistic cyclotron orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

In a constant [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) $B\widehat z$ with no [electric field](#electric-field), a charged relativistic particle moves in a circle in the perpendicular plane. Its angular frequency with respect to [proper time](special-relativity.md#proper-time) is

$$
\omega_\tau=\frac{qB}{m},
$$

whereas its angular frequency in an inertial coordinate time is $\omega_t=qB/(\gamma m)$. The orbit radius at speed $v$ is

$$
R=\frac{\gamma mv}{|q|B}.
$$

###### Charged-particle action over one relativistic cyclotron orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic cyclotron orbit](#relativistic-cyclotron-orbit)

For $qB>0$, choose $A_y=Bx$ and parametrize one clockwise orbit by $x=R\cos(\omega_\tau\tau)$ and $y=-R\sin(\omega_\tau\tau)$. Over $\Delta\tau=2\pi/\omega_\tau$, the [relativistic charged-particle action](#relativistic-charged-particle-action) is

$$
S=-mc^2\Delta\tau+q\oint A\mathbin\cdot dx
=-\frac{\pi mc^2}{\omega_\tau}(1+\gamma^2).
$$

The same formula with $|\omega_\tau|$ applies for either orbit orientation.

###### Lorentz force density

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

The Lorentz force per unit volume is $\mathbf f_{\rm L}=\rho_e\mathbf E+\mathbf j\times\mathbf B/c$ in Gaussian units.

###### E-cross-B drift

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-cross-B_drift)

In uniform perpendicular electric and magnetic fields, a charged particle's cyclotron orbit has guiding-centre velocity

$$
\mathbf v_D=\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2},
$$

independent of its charge and mass.

###### Relativistic electromagnetic work-energy theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

For $\mathcal E=\gamma mc^2$, the temporal component of the covariant Lorentz-force equation gives

$$
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

The magnetic field does no work because $\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)=0$.

###### Coordinate acceleration of a relativistic charged particle

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](#lorentz-force)

Combining $d(\gamma m\mathbf v)/dt=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ with $mc^2\,d\gamma/dt=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v$ gives

$$
\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}
=\frac q{m\gamma}
\left[
\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B
-\frac{\mathbf v(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf E)}{c^2}
\right].
$$

##### Electromagnetic field invariants

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field_invariants)

For signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and a consistent orientation,

$$
F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
=2\left(B^2-\frac{E^2}{c^2}\right),
\qquad
F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}
=-\frac4c\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B.
$$

Overall signs depend on tensor and orientation conventions, while their vanishing does not.

###### Null electromagnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field invariants](#electromagnetic-field-invariants)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_electromagnetic_field)

A null field has both electromagnetic invariants zero:

$$
\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B=0,
\qquad
E^2=c^2B^2.
$$

For a nonzero constant null field, the mixed tensor is nilpotent of index three,

$$
F^\mu{}_\rho F^\rho{}_\sigma F^\sigma{}_\nu=0.
$$

##### Electromagnetic stress-energy tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic field tensor](#electromagnetic-field-tensor)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_stress–energy_tensor)

For metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$,

$$
T^{\mu\nu}=\frac1{\mu_0}
\left(F^{\mu\rho}F^\nu{}_{\rho}
-\frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\right).
$$

In particular, $T^{00}=\tfrac12(\epsilon_0E^2+B^2/\mu_0)$.

###### Electromagnetic stress-energy divergence identity

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic stress-energy tensor](#electromagnetic-stress-energy-tensor)

For signature $(+---)$ in Gaussian electromagnetic units, $T^{ab}=(4\pi)^{-1}[-F^a{}_cF^{bc}+g^{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd}/4]$. The [Electromagnetic Bianchi identity](#electromagnetic-bianchi-identity) gives $F^{bc}\nabla_bF^a{}_c=\nabla^a(F_{cd}F^{cd})/4$, so differentiating the stress tensor proves the displayed identity. Stress conservation implies the source-free [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) if the matrix $F^a{}_c$ is invertible; otherwise it only forces the field divergence into that matrix's kernel. Conversely source-free Maxwell equations always imply stress conservation.

###### Gauge-compensated time translation in electromagnetism

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic stress-energy tensor](#electromagnetic-stress-energy-tensor)

With signature $(+,-,-,-)$ and $\mathcal L=-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4$, combine a time translation $\alpha\partial_0A_\nu$ with the [gauge transformation](#gauge-transformation) of parameter $-\alpha A_0$. Its [Noether current](quantum-field-theory.md#noether-current) is $J^\mu=-F^{\mu\nu}F_{0\nu}-\delta^\mu_0\mathcal L$, the gauge-invariant energy current. The density is $(|\mathbf E|^2+|\mathbf B|^2)/2$. On the source-free field equations it differs from the canonical translation current by $\partial_\nu(A_0F^{\mu\nu})$, an antisymmetric superpotential divergence, so total energies agree when the boundary term vanishes.

###### Null energy condition for the electromagnetic field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic stress-energy tensor](#electromagnetic-stress-energy-tensor)

For every null vector $k$, put $q^\rho=k_\mu F^{\mu\rho}$. Antisymmetry gives $q\cdot k=0$, so $q$ has nonnegative norm and

$$
T^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu=\frac1{\mu_0}q^\rho q_\rho\geq0.
$$

### Lorentz transformation of electromagnetic fields

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic electromagnetism](#relativistic-electromagnetism)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_transformation_of_electromagnetic_fields)

Lorentz boosts mix transverse electric and magnetic field components.

#### Relativistic charge density of counterstreaming beams

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz transformation of electromagnetic fields](#lorentz-transformation-of-electromagnetic-fields)

Two equal opposite line charge densities moving in opposite directions have zero net line charge but a nonzero total current $I$. Under a longitudinal [Lorentz boost](special-relativity.md#lorentz-boost), the charge-current four-vector gives $\lambda'=\gamma(\lambda-vI/c^2)=-\gamma vI/c^2$. Equivalently, for lab densities $n$ and speeds $\pm u$, their separate boosted number densities are $\gamma n(1\mp vu/c^2)$. Their imbalance creates the boosted radial [electric field](#electric-field), consistently with [Lorentz transformation of electromagnetic fields](#lorentz-transformation-of-electromagnetic-fields) and [Gauss's law](#gauss-s-law).

#### Electromagnetic jump conditions across a normally moving plane

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz transformation of electromagnetic fields](#lorentz-transformation-of-electromagnetic-fields)

If a plane is stationary in one frame and an observer moves normally to it with velocity $\mathbf v$, then

$$
[B'_n]=0,
\qquad
[\mathbf E'_t]=\mathbf v\mathbin\times[\mathbf B'_t].
$$

The second relation accounts for the magnetic-field discontinuity moving through the observer's frame.

## Electromagnetic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_wave)

An electromagnetic wave is a propagating coupled oscillation of electric and magnetic fields governed by Maxwell's equations.

### Electromagnetic waveguide

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)

An electromagnetic waveguide confines an [electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave) transversely while allowing propagation along its axis. Conducting or dielectric boundaries select transverse modes. In a uniform guide a mode with transverse eigenvalue $k_\perp^2$ has longitudinal wavenumber $k_x^2=k^2-k_\perp^2$; below cutoff it is evanescent. At an open [aperture](optics.md#aperture), the confined field radiates into an angular spectrum rather than retaining a single guided mode.

#### Scalar-potential conducting waveguide modes

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic waveguide](#electromagnetic-waveguide)

For dependence $e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$, a [Helmholtz equation](partial-differential-equation.md#helmholtz-equation) $(\Delta_\perp+\gamma^2)\psi=0$ generates transverse-magnetic fields $e_\perp=\nabla_\perp\psi$, $e_z=-i\gamma^2\psi/k$, $b_\perp=\omega\,\widehat z\times\nabla_\perp\psi/(kc^2)$, $b_z=0$, for $k\ne0$. The conducting wall requires a [Dirichlet boundary condition](differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) on $\psi$. A transverse-electric representation is $e_\perp=\widehat z\times\nabla_\perp\psi$, $e_z=0$, $b_\perp=-k\nabla_\perp\psi/\omega$, $b_z=i\gamma^2\psi/\omega$, with a [Neumann boundary condition](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition). These are families of modes, not a completeness assertion for multiply connected guides and their possible TEM fields.

#### Electromagnetic aperture angular spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic waveguide](#electromagnetic-waveguide)

For an aperture normal to $\mathbf e_x$, let $F_y,F_z$ be its tangential [electric field](#electric-field) traces extended by zero outside the aperture. With the outgoing branch of $\kappa$, the [Weyl plane-wave representation](partial-differential-equation.md#weyl-plane-wave-representation) and the curl aperture integral give a spectrum proportional to $(-(\nu\widehat F_y+\eta\widehat F_z)/\kappa,\widehat F_y,\widehat F_z)$. Each wave is transverse to $\mathbf q$. The longitudinal electric component is regenerated by oblique diffraction even when it vanishes at the input. For $G=e^{ikr}/(4\pi r)$ and a unit prefactor on the stated curl integral, the spectrum has an overall factor $1/2$; a doubled half-space Green kernel gives factor one. The normalization must follow the chosen Green kernel and equivalent-current convention.

### Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)

A rectangular electromagnetic waveguide has a vacuum interior bounded by four [perfect conductor](#perfect-conductor) walls parallel to its axis. The harmonic fields obey zero tangential [electric field](#electric-field) and zero normal [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) at the walls. [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) determine transverse fields from longitudinal components; a purely longitudinal oscillating [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) by itself cannot propagate, since its divergence would be nonzero.

#### Waveguide cutoff frequency

↑ **Parent:** [Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide](#rectangular-electromagnetic-waveguide)

For a [transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide](#transverse-electric-mode-of-a-rectangular-waveguide), the dispersion relation is $\omega^2=c^2(k^2+\kappa_{mn}^2)$, with $\kappa_{mn}^2=(m\pi/a)^2+(n\pi/b)^2$. Below $\omega_{mn}=c\kappa_{mn}$, the longitudinal wave number is imaginary and the mode is evanescent. If $a\ge b$, the lowest propagating cutoff is $\omega_{10}=\pi c/a$, or ordinary frequency $c/(2a)$.

##### Waveguide phase and group velocities

↑ **Parent:** [Waveguide cutoff frequency](#waveguide-cutoff-frequency)

Above a mode's [waveguide cutoff frequency](#waveguide-cutoff-frequency) $\omega_c$, its [phase velocity](wave-equation.md#phase-velocity) is $c/[1-(\omega_c/\omega)^2]^{1/2}$ and its [group velocity](wave-equation.md#group-velocity) is $c[1-(\omega_c/\omega)^2]^{1/2}$. Differentiate $\omega^2=c^2k^2+\omega_c^2$ to obtain the latter. Phase travels faster than $c$ while the wave packet travels slower, so the phase speed does not imply superluminal information transport.

#### Transverse electric mode of a rectangular waveguide

↑ **Parent:** [Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide](#rectangular-electromagnetic-waveguide)

For a transverse electric mode, $E_z=0$ and $B_z=B_0(x,y)e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$. Put $\Delta=(\omega/c)^2-k^2$. [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) give $B_x=ik\partial_xB_z/\Delta$, $B_y=ik\partial_yB_z/\Delta$, $E_x=i\omega\partial_yB_z/\Delta$, and $E_y=-i\omega\partial_xB_z/\Delta$. The wall conditions become [Neumann boundary conditions](differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) for $B_0$, whose separated solutions are $\cos(m\pi x/a)\cos(n\pi y/b)$. The constant $(0,0)$ candidate is excluded by Faraday's equation because its corresponding electric field vanishes.

### Transverse magnetic polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)

Relative to a propagation plane, transverse magnetic polarization has its magnetic field perpendicular to that plane. At a perfectly conducting corrugation its scalar magnetic component obeys a Neumann condition. It reduces to a scalar two-dimensional problem, but not to the Dirichlet problem for the transverse electric component.

### Transverse electric polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)

Relative to a propagation plane, transverse electric polarization has its electric field perpendicular to that plane. For a conducting corrugation independent of the perpendicular coordinate, the scalar electric component obeys the Helmholtz equation with Dirichlet data at the metal surface.

#### TE wave equation in a stratified magnetodielectric

↑ **Parent:** [Transverse electric polarization](#transverse-electric-polarization)

With permittivity and permeability depending only on $z$ and time dependence $e^{i\omega t}$, a [transverse electric polarization](#transverse-electric-polarization) $\mathbf E=\widehat{\mathbf y}E_y(x,z)$ obeys the displayed equation. [Maxwell's equations](#maxwell-equations) give $H_x=E_{y,z}/(i\omega\mu)$ and $H_z=-E_{y,x}/(i\omega\mu)$. The latter satisfies $H_{z,xx}+H_{z,zz}+(\mu'/\mu)H_{z,z}+[\omega^2\mu\varepsilon+(\log\mu)'']H_z=0$. At normal incidence $H_z=0$; the nonzero magnetic component is $H_x$.

### Refractive index

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refractive_index)

The refractive index is the ratio of the vacuum speed of light to a medium's phase speed. In a homogeneous isotropic medium, $n=c\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}$ in compatible units.

#### Inverse-square refractive-index profile

↑ **Parent:** [Refractive index](#refractive-index)

A [transverse electric polarization](#transverse-electric-polarization) with constant permeability and conserved transverse wavenumber $k_0\sin\theta_0$ satisfies $e''+[\beta^2-(\nu^2-1/4)/(z-z_0)^2]e=0$, with $\beta^2=k_0^2(a^2-\sin^2\theta_0)$ and $\nu^2=1/4+k_0^2b^2$. Substitution $e=\sqrt{|z-z_0|}Z_\nu(\beta|z-z_0|)$ reduces it to the [Bessel equation](analysis.md#bessel-differential-equation). The profile is singular at $z_0$, so each side requires its own boundary conditions.

### Electromagnetic wave equation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_wave_equation)

In vacuum, each field obeys $\nabla^2E-c^{-2}E_{tt}=0$ and $\nabla^2B-c^{-2}B_{tt}=0$.

### Plane electromagnetic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_electromagnetic_wave)

For a vacuum plane wave, $k\cdot E_0=0$, $\omega=c|k|$, and $B_0=k\times E_0/\omega$.

#### Polarization of an electromagnetic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave](#plane-electromagnetic-wave)

The polarization of an electromagnetic wave describes the path traced by its transverse electric-field vector at a fixed point. A monochromatic wave may be linearly, circularly, or elliptically polarized; in vacuum it has two independent transverse polarization states.

This specializes [polarization of waves](physics.md#polarization-waves) to electric-field oscillations.

##### Faraday effect

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraday_effect)

The Faraday effect rotates the orientation of linear [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave) during propagation through a magnetized medium. The two opposite [circular polarizations](#circular-polarization) accumulate different phases; their recombination rotates the linear polarization. In a sufficiently high-frequency cold [plasma](physics.md#plasma-physics), this angle varies as the squared [wavelength](wave-equation.md#wavelength) times a [rotation measure](#rotation-measure).

###### Rotation measure

↑ **Parent:** [Faraday effect](#faraday-effect)

For the high-frequency [Faraday effect](#faraday-effect) in a cold [plasma](physics.md#plasma-physics), the rotation measure is the coefficient in $\Delta\phi=\lambda^2\mathrm{RM}$. It is a density-weighted line integral of the line-of-sight [magnetic field](#magnetic-field); the coefficient $a_0$ depends on the units. A sign convention for the line of sight fixes its sign. Correlations across an extended source can constrain a foreground [magnetic energy spectrum](#magnetic-energy-spectrum), subject to statistical assumptions.

###### Long-path projection of a magnetic correlation

↑ **Parent:** [Rotation measure](#rotation-measure)

For constant [Electron](physics.md#electron) density, common path length $L$, and a [solenoidal isotropic spectral tensor](probability-and-statistics.md#solenoidal-isotropic-spectral-tensor), the rotation-measure [correlation](variance.md#pearson-correlation-coefficient) contains the window $|\int_0^Le^{ik_zz}dz|^2$. As $L$ becomes large compared with the [correlation length](critical-phenomenon.md#correlation-length), this window acts as $2\pi L\delta(k_z)$ within the [integral](calculus.md#integral). The transverse projector then has $zz$ component one, yielding the displayed two-dimensional [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform). For finite $L$, the windowed integral retains longitudinal modes and endpoint corrections. The method of using such [rotation measure](#rotation-measure) correlations to infer magnetic spectra is developed by [Enßlin and Vogt](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302426).

###### Hankel inversion of a rotation-measure correlation

↑ **Parent:** [Long-path projection of a magnetic correlation](#long-path-projection-of-a-magnetic-correlation)

Invert the two-dimensional [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) in the [long-path projection of a magnetic correlation](#long-path-projection-of-a-magnetic-correlation), then perform the angular integration in [polar coordinates](calculus.md#polar-coordinates). The angular integral is $2\pi J_0(kr)$, a [Bessel function of the first kind](analysis.md#bessel-function-of-the-first-kind), giving the displayed [Hankel transform](analysis.md#hankel-transform) with the stated [Fourier-Hankel normalization for an isotropic spectrum](analysis.md#fourier-hankel-normalization-for-an-isotropic-spectrum). The result recovers the scalar tensor coefficient $H$, whose shell-integrated [magnetic energy spectrum](#magnetic-energy-spectrum) has an additional factor $k^2$.

##### Polarizer

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarizer)

A linear [polarizer](#polarizer) transmits one [linear polarization](#linear-polarization) and suppresses the orthogonal one. Two ideal crossed [polarizers](#polarizer) block light unless an intervening medium changes its [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave). A [twisted nematic field effect](critical-phenomenon.md#twisted-nematic-field-effect) uses this selection to convert field-driven reorientation into brightness modulation.

##### Stokes parameters

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave)

The Stokes parameters $I,Q,U,V$ describe intensity, two linear-polarization components and circular polarization. $Q$ and $U$ depend on the chosen transverse basis. On the celestial sphere, $Q\pm iU$ transform as spin-two fields rather than as ordinary scalars.

##### Linear polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave)

A wave is linearly polarized when its oscillating [electric field](#electric-field) always lies along one fixed line. For a transverse [plane wave](quantum-mechanics.md#plane-wave), that line is perpendicular to the [wavevector](continuum-mechanics.md#wavevector). Unlike [circular polarization](#circular-polarization), the field oscillates along the line rather than rotating through directions.

##### Circular polarization

↑ **Parent:** [Polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_polarization)

An electromagnetic wave has circular polarization when its transverse [electric field](#electric-field) has constant magnitude and rotates once during each period. In complex-amplitude notation, two orthogonal components have equal magnitude and a relative phase of one quarter-cycle.

#### Perfect conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave](#plane-electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_conductor)

At the surface of a perfect conductor, the tangential [electric field](#electric-field) and normal [magnetic field](#magnetic-field) vanish. An incident [plane electromagnetic wave](#plane-electromagnetic-wave) is therefore accompanied by a reflected wave whose tangential electric field cancels the incident field at the surface.

##### Scalar polarization reduction at a conducting corrugation

↑ **Parent:** [Perfect conductor](#perfect-conductor)

If a metal surface is $z=h(x)$ and the incident field is independent of $y$, Maxwell's equations separate into [TE polarization](#transverse-electric-polarization) and [TM polarization](#transverse-magnetic-polarization). The metal condition that the tangential electric field vanish sets $E_y=0$ in the TE sector. In the TM sector $\mathbf E=i\nabla\times(H_y\widehat{\mathbf y})/(\omega\epsilon)$, so the same condition gives $(\partial_z-h'\partial_x)H_y=0$. This explains why the TE problem agrees with scalar Dirichlet acoustics while the TM problem has different rough-surface boundary forcing.

##### Normal reflection of an electromagnetic wave from a perfect conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Perfect conductor](#perfect-conductor)

At normal incidence, the reflected electric amplitude is the negative of the incident amplitude at the surface, while the reflected magnetic amplitude has the same sign. Their superposition forms a [standing wave](physics.md#standing-wave) with an electric node and a magnetic antinode at the conductor.

#### Interface conditions for electromagnetic fields

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave](#plane-electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interface_conditions_for_electromagnetic_fields)

Across an interface with unit normal $\mathbf n$ from medium one to medium two, [Maxwell equations](#maxwell-equations) imply $\mathbf n\times(\mathbf E_2-\mathbf E_1)=0$, $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf B_2-\mathbf B_1)=0$, $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf D_2-\mathbf D_1)=\sigma_f$, and $\mathbf n\times(\mathbf H_2-\mathbf H_1)=\mathbf K_f$. Here $\sigma_f$ and $\mathbf K_f$ are the free surface [electric charge](#electric-charge) and [electric current](#electric-current) densities.

##### Dielectric-interface boundary conditions

↑ **Parent:** [Interface conditions for electromagnetic fields](#interface-conditions-for-electromagnetic-fields)

Without free surface charge or current, the tangential components of $E$ and $H$ and the normal components of $D$ and $B$ are continuous across an interface.

###### Phase matching at a planar wave interface

↑ **Parent:** [Dielectric-interface boundary conditions](#dielectric-interface-boundary-conditions)

Equality of the boundary fields for every tangential position and time forces equal frequency and equal tangential wavevector for incident, reflected, and transmitted plane waves.

###### Snell law for electromagnetic waves

↑ **Parent:** [Phase matching at a planar wave interface](#phase-matching-at-a-planar-wave-interface)

For refractive indices $n_-$ and $n_+$,

$$
\theta_R=\theta_I,
\qquad
n_-\sin\theta_I=n_+\sin\theta_T.
$$

###### Total internal reflection

↑ **Parent:** [Snell law for electromagnetic waves](#snell-law-for-electromagnetic-waves)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)

Total internal reflection occurs when a wave travels from higher to lower [refractive index](#refractive-index) above the critical incidence angle, leaving no propagating transmitted plane wave normal to the interface.

###### Critical angle for total internal reflection

↑ **Parent:** [Total internal reflection](#total-internal-reflection)

For refractive indices $n_->n_+$, the critical angle satisfies $\sin\theta_c=n_+/n_-$. Above it, the transmitted normal wavenumber is imaginary and the transmitted field is an [evanescent wave](continuum-mechanics.md#evanescent-wave).

###### Fresnel equations

↑ **Parent:** [Dielectric-interface boundary conditions](#dielectric-interface-boundary-conditions)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_equations)

The [Fresnel equations](#fresnel-equations) give reflection and transmission amplitudes for both transverse-electric and transverse-magnetic [polarization of an electromagnetic wave](#polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave) at a planar interface. They follow by applying [interface conditions for electromagnetic fields](#interface-conditions-for-electromagnetic-fields) to incident, reflected and refracted [plane electromagnetic waves](#plane-electromagnetic-wave).

###### Transverse-electric Fresnel reflection coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Fresnel equations](#fresnel-equations)

When the permeabilities agree, the signed reflected-to-incident electric-field ratio for polarization normal to the plane of incidence is

$$
r_{\rm TE}
=\frac{n_-\cos\theta_I-n_+\cos\theta_T}
{n_-\cos\theta_I+n_+\cos\theta_T}.
$$

For incidence from lower to higher refractive index it never vanishes.

###### Reflected-to-transmitted transverse-electric amplitude ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Transverse-electric Fresnel reflection coefficient](#transverse-electric-fresnel-reflection-coefficient)

For equal permeabilities on the two sides of an interface, tangential-field continuity and [Snell law for electromagnetic waves](#snell-law-for-electromagnetic-waves) give

$$
\frac{E_R}{E_T}
=\frac12\left(1-\frac{\tan\theta_R}{\tan\theta_T}\right)
$$

for consistently signed electric amplitudes normal to the plane of incidence.

#### Plane wave in an anisotropic dielectric

↑ **Parent:** [Plane electromagnetic wave](#plane-electromagnetic-wave)

For $D_i=\varepsilon_{ij}E_j$, $B=\mu H$, and a plane wave with electric polarization $e$, the source-free Maxwell equations imply

$$
k\times(k\times e)+\omega^2\mu\varepsilon e=0.
$$

If $e$ is an eigenvector of $\varepsilon$, then $e\perp k$, the dispersion relation is $|k|^2=\omega^2\mu\varepsilon_e$, and the [Poynting vector](#poynting-vector) is parallel to $k$.

### Poynting vector

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poynting_vector)

The Poynting vector $S=E\times B/\mu_0$ is electromagnetic energy flux.

#### Poynting theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting vector](#poynting-vector)

For electromagnetic energy density

$$
u=\frac12\left(\epsilon_0E^2+\frac{B^2}{\mu_0}\right),
$$

Maxwell's equations imply

$$
\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot S=-J\cdot E.
$$

The field energy lost from a volume becomes outward electromagnetic flux or work on charges.

##### Closed-surface Poynting flux of a plane wave

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting theorem](#poynting-theorem)

For a real linearly polarized monochromatic vacuum [plane wave](quantum-mechanics.md#plane-wave), the local [Poynting vector](#poynting-vector) is proportional to $\cos^2(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)\widehat{\mathbf k}$. Its flux through a closed surface generally oscillates at $2\omega$, with zero cycle average, although particular volume geometries can cancel it identically. For a circularly polarized plane wave of constant amplitude the [Poynting vector](#poynting-vector) is spatially and temporally constant, so the net flux through every closed surface is exactly zero. A nonzero constant local energy flux must not be confused with the net closed-surface flux.

##### Electromagnetic energy

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting theorem](#poynting-theorem)

In vacuum the electromagnetic energy density is

$$
u=\frac{\varepsilon_0E^2}{2}+\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0},
$$

and the field energy in a region is $U_{\rm EM}=\int u\,dV$.

###### Magnetic energy

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic energy](#electromagnetic-energy)

In vacuum-permeability [magnetohydrodynamics](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetohydrodynamics), the [magnetic energy](#magnetic-energy) in a volume is $E_B=\int B^2/(2\mu_0)\,dV$. Its volume density equals the [magnetic pressure](astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#magnetic-pressure).

###### Magnetic energy spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic energy](#magnetic-energy)

The shell-integrated magnetic energy spectrum distributes [magnetic energy](#magnetic-energy) over [wavenumbers](wave-equation.md#wavenumber). With a [solenoidal isotropic spectral tensor](probability-and-statistics.md#solenoidal-isotropic-spectral-tensor) normalized to $\widehat C_{ii}=2H(k)$ and the inverse [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) measure $d^3k/(2\pi)^3$, one has $\mathbb E|\mathbf B|^2=\pi^{-2}\int k^2H(k)dk$. In [Gaussian units](#gaussian-units), $E_B(k)=k^2H(k)/(8\pi^3)$ integrates to the energy density $\mathbb E|\mathbf B|^2/(8\pi)$. A spectrum per unit mass or normalized to $B^2/2$ has different constant factors. Scale-local fluctuation estimates give $\delta B_l^2$ proportional to $kE_B(k)$ at $k\sim l^{-1}$ when the spectrum is normalized consistently.

##### Energy density and flux of a plane electromagnetic wave

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting theorem](#poynting-theorem)

For a vacuum plane wave, $B=\widehat k\times E/c$, so

$$
S=\epsilon_0cE^2\widehat k.
$$

The electric and magnetic energy densities are equal, and the time averages satisfy $\langle S\rangle=c\langle u\rangle\widehat k$.

##### Poynting theorem in a linear anisotropic medium

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting theorem](#poynting-theorem)

For symmetric time-independent constitutive tensors,

$$
u=\frac12(\varepsilon_{ij}E_iE_j+\mu_{ij}H_iH_j),
\qquad
S=E\times H,
$$

and the macroscopic Maxwell equations give $\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot S=-E\cdot J$.

### Near and far field

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](#electromagnetic-wave)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_and_far_field)

The [near and far field](#near-and-far-field) distinguish source-dependent electromagnetic field regions. Close to a localized source, reactive fields and induction terms contribute; sufficiently far away, the leading transverse fields decrease as the inverse distance and carry outgoing power. The [radiation zone](#radiation-zone) describes this far-field limit.

#### Radiation zone

↑ **Parent:** [Near and far field](#near-and-far-field)

In the radiation zone of a localized source, the leading electromagnetic fields decay as $1/R$, are transverse to the observation direction, and satisfy $B=\widehat R\times E/c$. Terms decaying faster than $1/R$ do not contribute to the limiting radiated power.

##### Intensity of an isotropic radiator

↑ **Parent:** [Radiation zone](#radiation-zone)

An isotropic source of average power $P$ has intensity

$$
\langle S\rangle=\frac{P}{4\pi r^2}
$$

at distance $r$. For monochromatic vacuum radiation, its electric-field amplitude is $E_0=\sqrt{2\langle S\rangle/(\epsilon_0c)}$.

##### Multipole radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Radiation zone](#radiation-zone)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipole_radiation)

[Multipole radiation](#multipole-radiation) expands radiation from localized time-dependent sources into electric and magnetic multipoles. [Electric dipole radiation](#electric-dipole-radiation) is the leading electric term, while [magnetic dipole radiation](#magnetic-dipole-radiation) and higher electric multipoles contribute when the leading term vanishes or higher precision is needed.

###### Electric dipole radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Multipole radiation](#multipole-radiation)

For a localized source whose size is small compared with its radiation wavelength, the radiation fields of its electric dipole moment $p$ are

$$
E_{\rm rad}=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi R}\widehat R\times(\widehat R\times\ddot p(t-R/c)),
\qquad
B_{\rm rad}=\frac1c\widehat R\times E_{\rm rad}.
$$

Their total instantaneous power is

$$
\mathcal P=\frac{\mu_0}{6\pi c}|\ddot p(t-R/c)|^2.
$$

###### Electric-dipole approximation for radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole radiation](#electric-dipole-radiation)

For a source of characteristic size $a$ and [angular frequency](classical-mechanics.md#angular-frequency) $\omega$, the electric-dipole approximation requires

$$
\frac{\omega a}{c}\ll1,
$$

so the source is much smaller than its radiation wavelength. Observing the pure $1/R$ radiation field additionally requires the [radiation zone](#radiation-zone), $R\gg c/\omega$ and $R\gg a$.

###### Angular distribution of electric dipole radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole radiation](#electric-dipole-radiation)

If $\theta$ is the angle between the observation direction and $\ddot p$, then

$$
\frac{dP}{d\Omega}
=\frac{\mu_0}{16\pi^2c}|\ddot p|^2\sin^2\theta.
$$

Integrating over [solid angle](geometry-and-topology.md#solid-angle) uses $\int\sin^2\theta\,d\Omega=8\pi/3$ and gives the total [electric dipole radiation](#electric-dipole-radiation) power.

###### Radiation damping of a harmonically oscillating charge

↑ **Parent:** [Electric dipole radiation](#electric-dipole-radiation)

A charge $q$ undergoing [simple harmonic motion](classical-mechanics.md#simple-harmonic-motion) of [angular frequency](classical-mechanics.md#angular-frequency) $\omega$ and slowly varying amplitude $A$ radiates the averaged power

$$
\langle P\rangle=\frac{\mu_0q^2A^2\omega^4}{12\pi c}.
$$

Its mechanical energy $E=mA^2\omega^2/2$ therefore has [exponential decay](analysis.md#exponential-decay) rate $\gamma=\mu_0q^2\omega^2/(6\pi mc)$ and [half-life](analysis.md#half-life)

$$
t_{1/2}=\frac{6\pi mc\log2}{\mu_0q^2\omega^2}.
$$

###### Magnetic dipole radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Multipole radiation](#multipole-radiation)

Electromagnetic duality replaces the electric dipole $p$ by $m/c$ for a magnetic dipole moment $m$. Therefore

$$
\mathcal P=\frac{\mu_0}{6\pi c^3}|\ddot m|^2.
$$

This is the magnetic-dipole term of the [multipole radiation](#multipole-radiation) expansion.

###### Magnetic-dipole spin-down

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole radiation](#magnetic-dipole-radiation)

If a rigid body's inclined magnetic dipole rotates at angular speed $\Omega$, its radiated power is $C\Omega^4$. Conservation of rotational energy $I\Omega^2/2$ then gives

$$
\dot\Omega=-\frac CI\Omega^3,
\qquad
\Omega(t)^{-2}=\Omega_0^{-2}+\frac{2C}{I}t.
$$
