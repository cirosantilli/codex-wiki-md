# Probability space

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_space)

A probability space is a [measure space](measure-space.md) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ whose total [measure](measure-split.md) is one. The [sample space](sample-space.md) $\Omega$ contains the elementary outcomes; the [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) $\mathcal F$ contains the [events](event.md) to which the model assigns [probabilities](probability.md); the [probability measure](probability-measure.md) $\mathbb P$ supplies those values. The [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) lets us combine observable questions using [complements](complement-of-a-set.md) and countable [unions](set-union.md) while remaining within the model.

For two independent uniform dice, take $\Omega=\{1,\ldots,6\}^2$, $\mathcal F=\mathcal P(\Omega)$, and give each ordered pair mass $1/36$. A [random variable](random-variable-split.md) might record the sum, the larger result, or whether both results are [even integers](even-number.md). These are different [functions](function-split.md) on the same [sample space](sample-space.md), rather than different elementary experiments. A [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) records the mass of the values of one such [random variable](random-variable-split.md) and can forget information retained by the full [probability space](probability-space-split.md).
