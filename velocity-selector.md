# Velocity selector

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md)

A [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md) uses perpendicular [electric field](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) to transmit a narrow range of [speeds](speed.md) along a chosen beam direction. Assume uniform static [electric field](electric-field-split.md) $\mathbf E=E\widehat{\mathbf y}$ and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B_s=B_s\widehat{\mathbf z}$, with $E,B_s>0$, and an incident [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v=v\widehat{\mathbf x}$. The [cross product](cross-product.md) gives $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B_s=-vB_s\widehat{\mathbf y}$, so the transverse [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) is

$$
F_y=q(E-vB_s),\qquad F_y=0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_0=\frac{E}{B_s}.
$$

For an [electron](electron.md), the electric [force](force-split.md) points downward and the magnetic [force](force-split.md) upward. A faster [electron](electron.md) initially bends upward; a slower one initially bends downward. At the selected [speed](speed.md), both [forces](force-split.md) cancel along the whole straight trajectory. The cancellation is independent of [mass](mass.md) and nonzero [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md); it selects [velocity](velocity.md), not a particular [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md). A component of [velocity](velocity.md) parallel to the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is not constrained by the cancellation, so the incident beam must also have its direction restricted.

**[Figure 4](#image-an-ideal-velocity-selector-passes-electrons-with-speed-e-b-s). An ideal velocity selector passes electrons with speed $E/B_s$**. Original teaching geometry with upper negative and lower positive plates, upward [electric field](electric-field-split.md), and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) out of the page. The three trajectories solve the ideal nonrelativistic [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) equations in rescaled units. The right panel shows the opposite [forces](force-split.md) on a selected [electron](electron.md). This is not a drawing of the 1897 apparatus.

A real exit slit accepts a finite range of [speeds](speed.md) and incident directions. A zero displacement at one screen alone does not establish zero [force](force-split.md) throughout the path: a curved trajectory could return to that screen position. The ideal [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md) interpretation requires a straight aligned beam, known overlapping [electric field](electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), and sufficiently narrow entrance and exit slits. Its useful role in the [Thomson charge-to-mass experiment](thomson-charge-to-mass-experiment.md) teaching model is to supply [speed](speed.md) independently of the subsequent magnetic bending.
