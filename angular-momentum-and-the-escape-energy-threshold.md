# Angular momentum and the escape energy threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse-square potential](inverse-square-potential.md)

In the isolated model, [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) and [angular momentum conservation](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) reduce the exterior [test particle](test-particle.md)'s motion to

$$
\varepsilon=\frac{E}{m}=\frac{\dot r^2}{2}+\frac{h^2}{2r^2}-\frac{\mu}{r},\qquad h=|\mathbf r\times\mathbf v|,\qquad \mu=GM.
$$

Here $\varepsilon$ is [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) and $h$ is [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md). The [effective potential](effective-potential.md) is $\Phi_{\mathrm{eff}}=h^2/(2r^2)-\mu/r$. A zero radial [velocity](velocity.md) is a radial [turning point](turning-point.md), and need not mean zero total [speed](speed.md): at the [periapsis](periapsis.md) or [apoapsis](apoapsis.md) of a nonradial [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md), tangential [velocity](velocity.md) remains. Thus the vertical gap between $\varepsilon$ and $-\mu/r$ represents total [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) per unit [mass](mass.md), whereas the gap above $\Phi_{\mathrm{eff}}$ represents its radial part.

For $h>0$, the [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md) obeys $e^2=1+2\varepsilon h^2/\mu^2$. Negative $\varepsilon$ gives an [elliptic orbit](elliptic-orbit.md), including a [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) at its minimum allowed [energy](energy-split.md); zero $\varepsilon$ gives a [parabolic trajectory](parabolic-trajectory.md); positive $\varepsilon$ gives a [hyperbolic Kepler orbit](hyperbolic-kepler-orbit.md). The [effective potential](effective-potential.md) tends to zero from below at infinity, so its outer region creates no extra positive escape barrier. This explains why [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md) depends on [energy](energy-split.md) even though [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) changes the path. The $h=0$ case is radial motion, rather than a nondegenerate [parabola](parabola.md) or [hyperbola](hyperbola.md); inward radial motion can end in collision.
