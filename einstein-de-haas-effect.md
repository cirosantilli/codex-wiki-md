# Einstein-de Haas effect

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein–de_Haas_effect)

The [Einstein-de Haas effect](einstein-de-haas-effect.md) is mechanical rotation or twisting caused by a change in a body's [magnetization](magnetization.md). The microscopic carriers of [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) also carry [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md): changing their net orientation can transfer [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) to the body. A freely suspended [ferromagnet](ferromagnet.md) can therefore turn when its [magnetization](magnetization.md) reverses. A suspension instead stores part of the response as an elastic twist. This makes a microscopic property accessible through a macroscopic [angle](angle.md).

The central measurement compares a change of [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) with transferred [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md), giving a [gyromagnetic ratio](gyromagnetic-ratio.md). It complements an ordinary [magnetic dipole torque](magnetic-dipole-torque.md) measurement: even with the applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) parallel to the suspension axis, so that an ideal axial [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) has $\mathbf m\times\mathbf B=\mathbf0$, changing [magnetization](magnetization.md) can drive an axial twist. The external drive supplies [energy](energy-split.md); [conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) still requires accounting for the suspension and any external axial [torque](torque.md).
