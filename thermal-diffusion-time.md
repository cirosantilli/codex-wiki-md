# Thermal diffusion time

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md)

The [thermal diffusion time](thermal-diffusion-time.md) $t_D=L^2/\alpha$, with $\alpha=\kappa$ the [thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md), is a characteristic scale for smoothing a [temperature](temperature-split.md) variation over [length](length.md) $L$. It is the thermal instance of the [diffusive time scale](diffusive-time-scale.md). Rescaling $x=L\widehat x$ and $t=(L^2/\alpha)\widehat t$ removes $L$ and $\alpha$ from the constant-coefficient [heat equation](heat-equation.md). Doubling [length](length.md) therefore multiplies comparable internal equilibration times by four; increasing [thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md) by four divides them by four.

This scale is not a universal time of complete [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md). A nonzero [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) decays exponentially and never becomes exactly zero at finite time. The geometry, [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md), initial pattern and accepted residual determine the numerical factor. For example, an insulated bar's slowest nonuniform [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) has decay time $L^2/(\pi^2\alpha)$, and reducing its amplitude to a fraction $\varepsilon$ requires

$$
t_\varepsilon=\frac{L^2}{\pi^2\alpha}\log\frac1\varepsilon,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.
$$

External [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) can impose a longer cooling time even when internal [temperature](temperature-split.md) differences smooth quickly. [Thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md) alone does not determine cooling to the room's [temperature](temperature-split.md).
