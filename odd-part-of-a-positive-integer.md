# Odd part of a positive integer

↑ **Parent:** [Integer factorization](integer-factorization.md)

The odd part of a positive [integer](integer.md) $n$ is the unique odd positive [integer](integer.md) $d$ obtained by repeatedly dividing $n$ by two. The process terminates because the positive [integers](integer.md) decrease. If $2^r d=2^s e$ with $d,e$ odd, then $r>s$ would make $e$ even and $s>r$ would make $d$ even; hence $r=s$ and $d=e$. Two positive [integers](integer.md) have the same odd part exactly when their ratio is an integral power of two. The odd positive [integers](integer.md) therefore give representatives for the resulting [equivalence classes](equivalence-class.md).
