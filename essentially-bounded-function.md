# Essentially bounded function

↑ **Parent:** [L-infinity norm](l-infinity-norm.md)

A [measurable function](measurable-function.md) is [essentially bounded](essentially-bounded-function.md) if some finite nonnegative [real number](real-number.md) $M$ bounds its [absolute value](absolute-value.md) [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). The exceptional [null set](null-set.md) may contain arbitrarily large values. With [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) on $[0,1]$, a [function](function-split.md) equal to zero outside $\{1/n:n\in\mathbb N_{>0}\}$ and equal to $n$ at $1/n$ is [essentially bounded](essentially-bounded-function.md) despite being unbounded pointwise: its exceptional [countable set](countable-set.md) is a [null set](null-set.md), so $M=0$ works. Its [L-infinity norm](l-infinity-norm.md) is zero because it represents the zero [equivalence class](equivalence-class.md) in [Lp space](lp-space.md).

Contrast this with $f(x)=1/x$ on $(0,1]$. For every $M\geq1$, the values exceeding $M$ occupy $(0,1/M)$, a [set](set-split.md) of positive [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md). Hence no finite [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) bound works, and this [function](function-split.md) is not [essentially bounded](essentially-bounded-function.md). Being finite at every input does not imply essential boundedness.
