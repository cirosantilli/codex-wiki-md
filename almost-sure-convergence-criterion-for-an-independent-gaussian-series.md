# Almost sure convergence criterion for an independent Gaussian series

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian series with square-summable coefficients](gaussian-series-with-square-summable-coefficients.md)

For [independent](independent-random-variables.md) standard [Gaussian random variables](gaussian-random-variable.md), the partial sums have [characteristic functions](characteristic-function.md) $\exp(-u^2v_n/2)$, where $v_n=\sum_{k\leq n}a_k^2$. A finite almost-sure limit implies convergence of these [characteristic functions](characteristic-function.md) to one continuous at zero; $v_n\to\infty$ would instead give zero at every nonzero $u$. Conversely, bounded $v_n$ makes the partial sums an [martingale](martingale-split.md) bounded in [L2 space](l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md), which converges [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) and in [L2 space](l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md).
