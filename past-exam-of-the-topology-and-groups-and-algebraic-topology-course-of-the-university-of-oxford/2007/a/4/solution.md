<h1 id="a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

The based lifting criterion is

$$
\boxed{f_*\pi_1(Y,y_0)\subseteq p_*\pi_1(\widetilde X,\widetilde b)}.
$$

Under the stated [path-connected](../../../../path-connected-space.md) and [locally path-connected](../../../../locally-path-connected-space.md) domain hypotheses, it is necessary and sufficient for a continuous based lift. Two based coverings are equivalent if a homeomorphism between their total spaces takes one chosen basepoint to the other and commutes with their projections.

For the usual classification by subgroups, total spaces are taken to be [connected](../../../../connected-space.md). They are then [path-connected](../../../../path-connected-space.md) and [locally path-connected](../../../../locally-path-connected-space.md) because they cover a [locally path-connected](../../../../locally-path-connected-space.md) base. If two such coverings have the same subgroup $H$, apply the lifting criterion to each projection to obtain based maps $F:\widetilde X\to\widetilde X'$ and $G:\widetilde X'\to\widetilde X$ over $X$. Their composites and the identity maps are based lifts of the same projections. Uniqueness of lifts on a [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) domain gives $GF=\operatorname{id}$ and $FG=\operatorname{id}$. Thus $F$ is an equivalence, proving **at most one [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) based covering realizes a given subgroup**.

The [connected](../../../../connected-space.md)-total-space qualification is necessary. If arbitrary disconnected coverings are allowed, the identity cover of a circle and the disjoint union of two identity covers have the same subgroup at a basepoint in the first component, yet are inequivalent. The subgroup only records the chosen component in that setting.

The degree is the cardinality of a fibre. Over a [path-connected](../../../../path-connected-space.md) base this is independent of the basepoint, by path lifting. For a [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) total space it equals the [subgroup index](../../../../index-of-a-subgroup.md) $[\pi_1(X,b):H]$, and it may be infinite.
