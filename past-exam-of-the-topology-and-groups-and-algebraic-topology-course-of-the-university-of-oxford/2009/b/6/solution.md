<h1 id="b/6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

A [closed manifold](../../../../closed-manifold.md) is a compact [manifold](../../../../topological-manifold-split.md) without boundary. Here $M$ is Hausdorff and every point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$; the usual countability condition is included in the [manifold](../../../../topological-manifold-split.md) convention. Compactness also gives a finite atlas and hence second countability.

A [local orientation](../../../../local-orientation-of-a-manifold.md) at $x$ is a choice of generator of $H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. An [orientation of a manifold](../../../../orientation-of-a-topological-manifold.md) is a compatible choice of these generators as $x$ varies: locally they are induced by the same coordinate-ball [orientation](../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md), or equivalently by a local [relative homology](../../../../relative-homology.md) class. For an oriented [closed manifold](../../../../closed-manifold.md), the [fundamental class](../../../../fundamental-class.md) $[M]\in H_n(M;\mathbb Z)$ is the unique class mapping to the chosen generator at every point. On disconnected closed manifolds this is the sum of the fundamental classes of their finitely many components.

For a triangulated or [Delta complex](../../../../delta-complex.md) description, orient the top-dimensional simplices compatibly with the local [orientation](../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) and sum them with their corresponding signs. Each codimension-one face appears twice with opposite induced orientations, so the sum is a cycle. At a point inside any top [simplex](../../../../simplex.md) its image is the prescribed local generator, so this cycle represents $[M]$. In the [connected](../../../../connected-space.md) case $H_n(M)=\mathbb Z$ generated by this class.

The homology conclusions below use the opening closed-[manifold](../../../../topological-manifold-split.md) hypothesis for $M_1,M_2$. That hypothesis is necessary if their later description is read independently: removing a ball from $\mathbb R^n$ for $n\geq2$ creates a nonzero $(n-1)$st homology group, so the claimed [isomorphism](../../../../isomorphism.md) would fail without closedness.
