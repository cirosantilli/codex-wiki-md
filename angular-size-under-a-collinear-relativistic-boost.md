# Angular size under a collinear relativistic boost

↑ **Parent:** [Relativistic aberration](relativistic-aberration.md)

Let an observer move towards the centre of a small distant source with [velocity](velocity.md) $\beta c$, where $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). For source-direction [angle](angle.md) $\theta$, the photon arrives with longitudinal [momentum](momentum-split.md) $-E\cos\theta/c$. A [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) gives $E'=\gamma E(1+\beta\cos\theta)$ and therefore the [relativistic aberration](relativistic-aberration.md) law

$$
\cos\theta'=\frac{\cos\theta+\beta}{1+\beta\cos\theta},\qquad
\tan\frac{\theta'}2=e^{-\eta}\tan\frac\theta2,
$$

where $\eta=\operatorname{artanh}\beta$ is the [rapidity](rapidity.md). For a small [angular diameter](angular-diameter.md) $\Theta$, the displayed angular-size ratio follows by linearizing at $\theta=0$. Two momentarily coincident observers can consequently measure different angular sizes without any change in their observation event. If their diameter ratio is $q\ge1$, their relative speed is $c(q^2-1)/(q^2+1)$.
