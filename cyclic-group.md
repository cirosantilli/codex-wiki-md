# Cyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Group](group-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_group)

A [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) is a [group](group-split.md) that can be generated by repeating a single [group element](group-element.md) $g$ and its [inverse element](inverse-element.md):

$$
G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}.
$$

For an additive [group](group-split.md), the same expression is written $\{kg:k\in\mathbb Z\}$: $kg$ means repeated [addition](addition.md), including subtraction when $k$ is negative. A [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) has a repeating cycle, while an [infinite cyclic group](infinite-cyclic-group.md) is isomorphic to the additive [integers](integer-split.md). The notation $C_n$ refers to a [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) of order $n$.

This definition compresses a whole composition rule into one repeatable operation. Once $g$ is chosen, $g^ag^b=g^{a+b}$, so working with [group elements](group-element.md) becomes working with [integer](integer-split.md) exponents. In the finite case those exponents are reduced using [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic-split.md). It also proves that every [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) is an [abelian group](abelian-group.md), because $a+b=b+a$. A [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) of order $n$ has exactly $d$ solutions of $x^d=e$ for every positive [integer divisor](divisor.md) $d$ of $n$: in exponents, $n\mid dk$ has exactly $d$ solutions modulo $n$. The standard finite and infinite models are described in [Group Theory (v4.01) by James S. Milne](group-theory-v4-01-by-james-s-milne.md) (section 1.16).
