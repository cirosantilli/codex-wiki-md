# Glycine titration experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Glycine titration](glycine-titration.md)

The Amrita Virtual Lab procedure uses $0.1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ [glycine](glycine.md), [hydrochloric acid](hydrochloric-acid.md) and [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md), two [burettes](burette.md), a [pH](ph.md) meter, beaker and stirrer. A [pipette](pipette.md) transfers $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ of [glycine](glycine.md) into a $100\,\mathrm{mL}$ beaker. Calibrate the meter with standard [buffer solutions](buffer-solution.md) at [pH](ph.md) $4$, $7$ and $10$, then record the starting [pH](ph.md). Add [hydrochloric acid](hydrochloric-acid.md) in $0.3\,\mathrm{mL}$ increments, recording [pH](ph.md) after each addition until $1.6$. Rinse the [electrode](electrode.md) in distilled [water](water.md), take a separate fresh $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ portion, and add [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) in $0.3\,\mathrm{mL}$ increments until [pH](ph.md) $12.5$. Its observed readout is paired added [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) and [pH](ph.md), plotted as an [acid-base titration](acid-base-titration.md) curve; it does not measure each [glycine](glycine.md) form directly. [Materials Required, items 1–8; Working steps, items 1–9: separate acid and base titrations](titration-curves-of-aminoacids-amrita-virtual-lab/procedure.md)

These are two titrations from the starting [glycine](glycine.md) [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md), rather than the single acidified-sample protocol assumed by the solid curve in [glycine titration](glycine-titration.md). Retain each portion's initial [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) and the signed added acid/base [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md); do not concatenate raw [burette](burette.md) readings and assume the result begins at $b=0$. For this procedure's $0.00200\,\mathrm{mol}$ of [glycine](glycine.md), each $0.3\,\mathrm{mL}$ addition supplies $0.000030\,\mathrm{mol}$ of acid or base, a $0.015$ increment relative to the [glycine](glycine.md) inventory. This calculated resolution is not a reported measurement.

Useful additional controls are a fresh repeat portion, independently checked acid/base [concentrations](concentration.md), a [water](water.md) blank, and a calibration-check [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md) after the run. Stir without striking the [glass electrode](glass-electrode.md), keep its sensing region immersed and wait for stable readings; record [temperature](temperature.md) and background salts. Reduce addition size near a steep [pH](ph.md) change. These are suggested controls for testing the model, beyond the published steps. A [water](water.md) blank can reveal contamination but must not be subtracted directly from [pH](ph.md), which is a [logarithm](logarithm.md).

The standards at [pH](ph.md) $4$, $7$ and $10$ do not bracket either endpoint of this protocol. Extreme readings therefore require independently checked instrument performance, and alkaline samples are sensitive to atmospheric [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) uptake. [Electrode](electrode.md) drift, incomplete mixing, [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) error, [dilution](dilution.md) and changing [ionic strength](ionic-strength.md) can shift apparent [pKa](pka.md) values. A titration constrains the acid/base model; it cannot establish [chirality](chirality-chemistry.md) or separate every microscopic [protonation](protonation.md) form. The two original diagrams show calculated predictions to compare with measured points, not reconstructed experimental data.
