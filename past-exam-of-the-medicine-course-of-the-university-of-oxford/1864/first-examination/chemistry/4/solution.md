<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**In modern notation the formulas are HCN, HCOOH, $\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4}$, $\mathrm{Hg_2Cl_2}$, $\mathrm{HgCl_2}$, $\mathrm{As_2O_3}$, a hydrated potassium antimony tartrate, and $\mathrm{KClO_3}$, respectively.** Several historical names need qualification.

- [Hydrocyanic acid](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) is [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md), $\boxed{\mathrm{HCN}}$. The pharmaceutical name normally concerns an aqueous [chemical solution](../../../../../solution-chemistry.md); its [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) is not encoded by the molecular [chemical formula](../../../../../chemical-formula.md).
- [Formic acid](../../../../../formic-acid.md) is $\boxed{\mathrm{HCOOH}}$, equivalently $\mathrm{CH_2O_2}$.
- Anhydrous [oxalic acid](../../../../../oxalic-acid.md) is $\boxed{\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4}}$, or $\mathrm{(COOH)_2}$. Its common crystalline dihydrate is $\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4\cdot2H_2O}$; the [water of crystallization](../../../../../water-of-crystallization.md) must be included when calculating a weighed quantity.
- [Calomel](../../../../../mercury-i-chloride.md) is [mercury(I) chloride](../../../../../mercury-i-chloride.md), $\boxed{\mathrm{Hg_2Cl_2}}$. The formula $\mathrm{HgCl}$ gives only the simplest composition ratio and conceals the paired [mercury(I) ion](../../../../../mercury-i-ion.md).
- [Corrosive sublimate](../../../../../mercury-ii-chloride.md) is [mercury(II) chloride](../../../../../mercury-ii-chloride.md), $\boxed{\mathrm{HgCl_2}}$. It is chemically distinct from [calomel](../../../../../mercury-i-chloride.md).
- The solid historically sold as [arsenious acid](../../../../../arsenic-trioxide.md) is normally [arsenic trioxide](../../../../../arsenic-trioxide.md), $\boxed{\mathrm{As_2O_3}}$, often written $\mathrm{AsO_3}$ in older equivalent notation. The genuinely hydrated molecular [arsenous acid](../../../../../arsenous-acid.md) is $\mathrm{H_3AsO_3}$, also written $\mathrm{As(OH)_3}$. One should not quietly assign the hydrated formula to the dry commercial [oxide](../../../../../oxide.md).
- [Tartar emetic](../../../../../potassium-antimony-tartrate.md), or [potassium antimony tartrate](../../../../../potassium-antimony-tartrate.md), is conventionally represented as $\boxed{\mathrm{K(SbO)C_4H_4O_6\cdot\tfrac12H_2O}}$. A structurally more informative doubled formula is $\mathrm{K_2Sb_2(C_4H_2O_6)_2\cdot3H_2O}$: the complete elemental totals agree. The older “antimonyl tartrate” formula is a compositional convention and does not imply isolated $\mathrm{SbO^+}$ units in the crystal.[compound 73415808, elemental formula C8H10K2O15Sb2](../../../../../antimony-potassium-tartrate-in-pubchem.md)
- [Chlorate of potash](../../../../../potassium-chlorate.md) is [potassium chlorate](../../../../../potassium-chlorate.md), $\boxed{\mathrm{KClO_3}}$.

The [chemical formulas](../../../../../chemical-formula.md) above use modern [atomic weights](../../../../../relative-atomic-mass.md) and distinguish [molecular formulas](../../../../../molecular-formula.md), [formula units](../../../../../formula-unit.md) and [hydrates](../../../../../hydrate.md). Historical equivalent notation sometimes halves the subscripts attached to divalent [chemical elements](../../../../../chemical-element.md) or writes [water](../../../../../water.md) as $\mathrm{HO}$. Such formulas must be read together with their contemporary [atomic weights](../../../../../relative-atomic-mass.md), rather than treated as inconsistent modern [molecular formulas](../../../../../molecular-formula.md).
