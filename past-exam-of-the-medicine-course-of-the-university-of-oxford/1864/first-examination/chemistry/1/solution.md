<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

**For complete neutralization, the required masses of anhydrous potassium carbonate, anhydrous sodium carbonate and magnesium oxide are in the ratio $138:106:40=69:53:20$.** Here “carbonate of potash” means [potassium carbonate](../../../../../potassium-carbonate.md), “carbonate of soda” means [sodium carbonate](../../../../../sodium-carbonate.md), and “caustic magnesia” means [magnesium oxide](../../../../../magnesium-oxide.md), rather than [magnesium hydroxide](../../../../../magnesium-hydroxide.md) or a [magnesium carbonate](../../../../../magnesium-carbonate.md) preparation. The [hydration state](../../../../../hydration-state.md) of the [sodium carbonate](../../../../../sodium-carbonate.md) matters considerably.

Each [formula unit](../../../../../formula-unit.md) of either [carbonate](../../../../../carbonate.md) consumes two [protons](../../../../../proton.md); each [formula unit](../../../../../formula-unit.md) of [magnesium oxide](../../../../../magnesium-oxide.md) also consumes two [protons](../../../../../proton.md). Thus one [mole](../../../../../mole-unit.md) of each [base](../../../../../base-chemistry.md) neutralizes one [mole](../../../../../mole-unit.md) of [sulfuric acid](../../../../../sulfuric-acid.md) when the final [salts](../../../../../salt-chemistry.md) are the normal [sulfates](../../../../../sulfate.md):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{K_2CO_3+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{K_2SO_4+CO_2+H_2O},\\
\mathrm{Na_2CO_3+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Na_2SO_4+CO_2+H_2O},\\
\mathrm{MgO+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{MgSO_4+H_2O}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) evolved in the first two [chemical reactions](../../../../../chemical-reaction-split.md) escapes, whereas [magnesium oxide](../../../../../magnesium-oxide.md) produces no [gas](../../../../../gas.md). The [stoichiometric coefficients](../../../../../stoichiometric-coefficient.md), rather than the visibly different behaviour of the [bases](../../../../../basis.md), determine the required amounts.

Let $m$ be the [mass](../../../../../mass.md) of pure $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ in the given [chemical solution](../../../../../solution-chemistry.md). Using the rounded [relative atomic masses](../../../../../relative-atomic-mass.md) $\mathrm{H}=1$, $\mathrm{C}=12$, $\mathrm{O}=16$, $\mathrm{Na}=23$, $\mathrm{Mg}=24$ and $\mathrm{K}=39$ gives

$$
\boxed{m_{\mathrm{K_2CO_3}}=\frac{138}{98}m,\qquad m_{\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}}=\frac{106}{98}m,\qquad m_{\mathrm{MgO}}=\frac{40}{98}m.}
$$

Thus 98 mass units of pure [sulfuric acid](../../../../../sulfuric-acid.md) require respectively 138, 106 and 40 mass units. Present [atomic weights](../../../../../relative-atomic-mass.md) slightly change these rounded values, without changing the one-to-one [mole](../../../../../mole-unit.md) ratios. If $m_s$ denotes the [mass](../../../../../mass.md) of the supplied [acid](../../../../../acid-chemistry.md) and $w$ its [mass fraction](../../../../../mass-fraction.md) of $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, substitute $m=wm_s$. A [volume](../../../../../volume.md) alone is insufficient without its [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) or an equivalent [density](../../../../../density.md) and composition.

If “carbonate of soda” is instead the crystalline decahydrate, [sodium carbonate decahydrate](../../../../../sodium-carbonate-decahydrate.md), its rounded [molar mass](../../../../../molar-mass.md) is $286\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, so the corresponding factor is $286/98$, rather than $106/98$. Likewise an impure [base](../../../../../base-chemistry.md) requires correction for its assayed content. The unspecified given quantity can therefore be handled exactly by proportional reasoning; no missing numerical measurement is needed.
