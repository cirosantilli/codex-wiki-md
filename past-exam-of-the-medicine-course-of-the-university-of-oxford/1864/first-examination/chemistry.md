## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What are the proportions respectively required of Carbonate of Potash, Carbonate of Soda, and Caustic Magnesia to neutralize a given quantity of Sulphuric Acid ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**For complete neutralization, the required masses of anhydrous potassium carbonate, anhydrous sodium carbonate and magnesium oxide are in the ratio $138:106:40=69:53:20$.** Here “carbonate of potash” means [potassium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-carbonate), “carbonate of soda” means [sodium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#sodium-carbonate), and “caustic magnesia” means [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide), rather than [magnesium hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-hydroxide) or a [magnesium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#magnesium-carbonate) preparation. The [hydration state](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydration-state) of the [sodium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#sodium-carbonate) matters considerably.

Each [formula unit](../../../chemical-substance.md#formula-unit) of either [carbonate](../../../chemistry.md#carbonate) consumes two [protons](../../../physics.md#proton); each [formula unit](../../../chemical-substance.md#formula-unit) of [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide) also consumes two [protons](../../../physics.md#proton). Thus one [mole](../../../physics.md#mole-unit) of each [base](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#base-chemistry) neutralizes one [mole](../../../physics.md#mole-unit) of [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) when the final [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) are the normal [sulfates](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{K_2CO_3+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{K_2SO_4+CO_2+H_2O},\\
\mathrm{Na_2CO_3+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Na_2SO_4+CO_2+H_2O},\\
\mathrm{MgO+H_2SO_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{MgSO_4+H_2O}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) evolved in the first two [chemical reactions](../../../chemical-reaction.md) escapes, whereas [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide) produces no [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas). The [stoichiometric coefficients](../../../chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometric-coefficient), rather than the visibly different behaviour of the [bases](../../../vector-space.md#basis), determine the required amounts.

Let $m$ be the [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) of pure $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$ in the given [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry). Using the rounded [relative atomic masses](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass) $\mathrm{H}=1$, $\mathrm{C}=12$, $\mathrm{O}=16$, $\mathrm{Na}=23$, $\mathrm{Mg}=24$ and $\mathrm{K}=39$ gives

$$
\boxed{m_{\mathrm{K_2CO_3}}=\frac{138}{98}m,\qquad m_{\mathrm{Na_2CO_3}}=\frac{106}{98}m,\qquad m_{\mathrm{MgO}}=\frac{40}{98}m.}
$$

Thus 98 mass units of pure [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) require respectively 138, 106 and 40 mass units. Present [atomic weights](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass) slightly change these rounded values, without changing the one-to-one [mole](../../../physics.md#mole-unit) ratios. If $m_s$ denotes the [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) of the supplied [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) and $w$ its [mass fraction](../../../mixture.md#mass-fraction) of $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$, substitute $m=wm_s$. A [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) alone is insufficient without its [concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) or an equivalent [density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) and composition.

If “carbonate of soda” is instead the crystalline decahydrate, [sodium carbonate decahydrate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#sodium-carbonate-decahydrate), its rounded [molar mass](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass) is $286\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, so the corresponding factor is $286/98$, rather than $106/98$. Likewise an impure [base](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#base-chemistry) requires correction for its assayed content. The unspecified given quantity can therefore be handled exactly by proportional reasoning; no missing numerical measurement is needed.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What formula would you assign to a substance which gave the following results to analysis :—

| Constituent | Analysis |
| --- | --- |
| Carbon | 54.35 |
| Hydrogen | 9.11 |
| Oxygen | 36.54 |
| Total | 100.00 |

and of which the vapour density was 3.05.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The molecular formula is $\boxed{\mathrm{C_4H_8O_2}}$, interpreting the vapour density as relative to air.** The [organic elemental analysis](../../../chemistry.md#organic-elemental-analysis) first determines a simpler [empirical formula](../../../chemical-substance.md#empirical-formula); the [vapor-density molar mass measurement](../../../chemistry.md#vapor-density-molar-mass-measurement) then fixes its multiple.

Take 100 mass units of the [chemical substance](../../../chemical-substance.md) and divide each elemental contribution by its rounded [relative atomic mass](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass):

$$
 n_{\mathrm C}:n_{\mathrm H}:n_{\mathrm O}
 =\frac{54.35}{12}:\frac{9.11}{1}:\frac{36.54}{16}
 =4.5292:9.1100:2.2838
 \simeq 2:4:1.
$$

The [empirical formula](../../../chemical-substance.md#empirical-formula) is therefore $\mathrm{C_2H_4O}$, with [empirical formula mass](../../../chemical-substance.md#empirical-formula-mass) approximately 44. Its calculated [mass percentages](../../../mixture.md#mass-percentage) are 54.55% [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon), 9.09% [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) and 36.36% [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). The small discrepancies from the given [organic elemental analysis](../../../chemistry.md#organic-elemental-analysis) are consistent with rounded experimental data; they should not be converted into large, meaningless integer subscripts.

For equal [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of [ideal gases](../../../ideal-gas.md) at equal [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and [pressure](../../../pressure.md), the [density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) ratio equals the [molar mass](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass) ratio. On the [air](../../../mixture.md#air) convention,

$$
M\simeq 3.05\times28.97\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=88.36\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}},\qquad
\frac{M}{44\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}\simeq2.
$$

Doubling $\mathrm{C_2H_4O}$ gives $\mathrm{C_4H_8O_2}$; its rounded [relative molecular mass](../../../chemistry.md#relative-molecular-mass) of 88 predicts an [air](../../../mixture.md#air)-relative [vapor density](../../../chemistry.md#vapor-density) of approximately $88/28.97=3.04$, close to 3.05. If the stated [vapor density](../../../chemistry.md#vapor-density) were instead relative to [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen), it would imply a [relative molecular mass](../../../chemistry.md#relative-molecular-mass) near 6.1, smaller than the [empirical formula mass](../../../chemical-substance.md#empirical-formula-mass) and incompatible with the analysis. That interpretation cannot describe a pure, undecomposed molecular [vapor](../../../thermodynamics.md#vapor) with the stated composition.

Under the older [equivalent weight](../../../chemical-reaction.md#equivalent-weight) convention $\mathrm C=6$, $\mathrm O=8$, $\mathrm H=1$, the same composition and formula weight could be written $\mathrm{C_8H_8O_4}$. This is a change of notation, not eight modern [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) [chemical atoms](../../../physics.md#atom-physics) in the [molecule](../../../molecule.md). The result specifies composition and [molar mass](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass); it does not distinguish among [structural isomers](../../../chemistry.md#structural-isomer), such as [ethyl acetate](../../../chemical-substance.md#ethyl-acetate) and [butyric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#butyric-acid).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What observations are required for the accurate comparison of the volumes of two gases, and for what reasons ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Observe each gas volume, temperature, pressure and moisture condition, and reduce both volumes to the same temperature, pressure and dry-gas basis.** Equal scale readings alone do not establish equal amounts of [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas).

Read the calibrated [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of each [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) in a graduated tube or [eudiometer](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#eudiometer), with the eye at the [meniscus](../../../surface-tension.md#meniscus-liquid) to avoid [parallax](../../../astronomy.md#parallax). Allow the [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) and the surrounding bath to reach [thermal equilibrium](../../../thermal-equilibrium.md), then read a [thermometer](../../../temperature.md#thermometer) close to the [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas). Heating expands a fixed amount of [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas), so a larger reading could reflect a higher [temperature](../../../temperature.md) rather than more [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas). The [temperature](../../../temperature.md) used in a [gas law](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas-law) must be an [thermodynamic temperature](../../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-temperature).

Read the atmospheric [pressure](../../../pressure.md) with a [barometer](../../../pressure.md#barometer). If a [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) is confined over a [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid), also observe the difference between the inside and outside [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) levels. With $h$ the internal surface height above the external surface, the total internal [pressure](../../../pressure.md) is

$$
p_g=p_{\mathrm{atm}}-\rho_\ell gh.
$$

Here $\rho_\ell$ is the confining [density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) of the [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) and $g$ the [gravitational acceleration](../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-acceleration); the sign reverses automatically when $h$ is negative. Equalizing the levels before taking the [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) reading removes this [hydrostatic pressure](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) correction. A [mercury barometer](../../../pressure.md#mercury-barometer) can itself require [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and calibration corrections for accurate work.

If the [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) is saturated with [water vapor](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor), subtract the [vapor pressure](../../../thermodynamic-potential.md#vapor-pressure) of [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) at its measured [temperature](../../../temperature.md):

$$
p_{\mathrm{dry}}=p_g-p_{\mathrm{H_2O}}(T).
$$

The observed [pressure](../../../pressure.md) includes both the test [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) and [water vapor](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor); counting it all as test [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) overestimates the amount. The saturation assumption needs time for equilibration. For a dry [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas), no [water vapor](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor) subtraction is made; another collecting [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) may require its own [vapor pressure](../../../thermodynamic-potential.md#vapor-pressure) correction. A collecting [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) must also be chosen so that it neither appreciably dissolves nor reacts with the [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas): [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), for instance, is unsuitable for preserving the whole amount of freely soluble [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia).

For dilute [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) obeying the [ideal gas law](../../../ideal-gas.md#ideal-gas-law), the [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) $V_0$ at a common chosen $T_0,p_0$ is

$$
\boxed{V_0=V\frac{p_{\mathrm{dry}}}{p_0}\frac{T_0}{T}.}
$$

Consequently the corrected comparison is

$$
\boxed{\frac{V_{1,0}}{V_{2,0}}=\frac{V_1}{V_2}\frac{p_{1,\mathrm{dry}}}{p_{2,\mathrm{dry}}}\frac{T_2}{T_1}.}
$$

This is also the ratio of [amounts of substance](../../../physics.md#amount-of-substance) in the [ideal gas](../../../ideal-gas.md) approximation. Check for leaks, trapped [air](../../../mixture.md#air), changing composition and incomplete transfer. At high [pressure](../../../pressure.md) or near [condensation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#condensation), [real gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#real-gas) behaviour may require [compressibility factors](../../../pressure.md#compressibility-factor); with $pV=ZnRT$, the inferred amount ratio contains the further factor $Z_2/Z_1$.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Give the Chemical Formulæ of the following substances :—Hydrocyanic Acid, Formic Acid, Oxalic Acid, Calomel, Corrosive Sublimate, Arsenious Acid, Tartar-Emetic, Chlorate of Potash.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**In modern notation the formulas are HCN, HCOOH, $\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4}$, $\mathrm{Hg_2Cl_2}$, $\mathrm{HgCl_2}$, $\mathrm{As_2O_3}$, a hydrated potassium antimony tartrate, and $\mathrm{KClO_3}$, respectively.** Several historical names need qualification.

- [Hydrocyanic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) is [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide), $\boxed{\mathrm{HCN}}$. The pharmaceutical name normally concerns an aqueous [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry); its [concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) is not encoded by the molecular [chemical formula](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-formula).
- [Formic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#formic-acid) is $\boxed{\mathrm{HCOOH}}$, equivalently $\mathrm{CH_2O_2}$.
- Anhydrous [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) is $\boxed{\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4}}$, or $\mathrm{(COOH)_2}$. Its common crystalline dihydrate is $\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4\cdot2H_2O}$; the [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization) must be included when calculating a weighed quantity.
- [Calomel](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride) is [mercury(I) chloride](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride), $\boxed{\mathrm{Hg_2Cl_2}}$. The formula $\mathrm{HgCl}$ gives only the simplest composition ratio and conceals the paired [mercury(I) ion](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-ion).
- [Corrosive sublimate](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride) is [mercury(II) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride), $\boxed{\mathrm{HgCl_2}}$. It is chemically distinct from [calomel](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride).
- The solid historically sold as [arsenious acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide) is normally [arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide), $\boxed{\mathrm{As_2O_3}}$, often written $\mathrm{AsO_3}$ in older equivalent notation. The genuinely hydrated molecular [arsenous acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenous-acid) is $\mathrm{H_3AsO_3}$, also written $\mathrm{As(OH)_3}$. One should not quietly assign the hydrated formula to the dry commercial [oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxide).
- [Tartar emetic](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate), or [potassium antimony tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate), is conventionally represented as $\boxed{\mathrm{K(SbO)C_4H_4O_6\cdot\tfrac12H_2O}}$. A structurally more informative doubled formula is $\mathrm{K_2Sb_2(C_4H_2O_6)_2\cdot3H_2O}$: the complete elemental totals agree. The older “antimonyl tartrate” formula is a compositional convention and does not imply isolated $\mathrm{SbO^+}$ units in the crystal.[compound 73415808, elemental formula C8H10K2O15Sb2](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#antimony-potassium-tartrate-in-pubchem)
- [Chlorate of potash](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate) is [potassium chlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate), $\boxed{\mathrm{KClO_3}}$.

The [chemical formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-formula) above use modern [atomic weights](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass) and distinguish [molecular formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-formula), [formula units](../../../chemical-substance.md#formula-unit) and [hydrates](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrate). Historical equivalent notation sometimes halves the subscripts attached to divalent [chemical elements](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-element) or writes [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) as $\mathrm{HO}$. Such formulas must be read together with their contemporary [atomic weights](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass), rather than treated as inconsistent modern [molecular formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-formula).

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Explain briefly how each of the substances named in the preceding question may be best prepared.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**A suitable historical answer gives both the chemical transformation and the means of isolating a reasonably pure product.** The following are preparative principles, with modern [chemical formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-formula); they are not instructions for self-preparation of medicines. In particular, [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide), [mercury compounds](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-compound) and [arsenic compounds](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-compound) present serious [toxicity](../../../biology.md#toxicity) hazards. [Hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) is extremely volatile, can be absorbed by several routes, and its odour is an unreliable warning.[Agent Characteristics; Routes of Exposure; warning that smell does not provide adequate protection](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#hydrogen-cyanide-emergency-response-information-by-niosh)

For [hydrocyanic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide), a customary early nineteenth-century route is the controlled [distillation](../../../chemistry.md#distillation) of [potassium ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-ferrocyanide) with dilute [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). [Hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) passes into the distillate; nonvolatile [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) remain behind. A generating flask, efficient [laboratory condenser](../../../chemistry.md#laboratory-condenser) and chilled receiver are needed because [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) is readily lost. The contemporary account of [hydrocyanic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) preparation in [Pharmacopoeia Castrensis Ruthenica in The British and Foreign Medical Review (1842)](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#pharmacopoeia-castrensis-ruthenica-in-the-british-and-foreign-medical-review-1842) specifically describes the [ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferrocyanide)–[sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) method and its losses on imperfect condensation. The [concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) must be determined analytically before any defined dilution: a nominal starting mixture does not establish the final strength.[Processes for obtaining Hydrocyanic Acid](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#pharmacopoeia-castrensis-ruthenica-in-the-british-and-foreign-medical-review-1842) At the level of the released [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide), the essential [acid-base reaction](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-base-reaction) is $\mathrm{CN^-+H^+\rightarrow HCN}$. This equation alone is not a complete equation for decomposing the stable [ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferrocyanide) [coordination complex](../../../chemistry.md#coordination-complex). Concentrated [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) can lead to different decomposition chemistry, so the specified dilution is meaningful.

For [formic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#formic-acid), gently heat [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) with [glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol) and some [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), then separate the aqueous [formic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#formic-acid) by [distillation](../../../chemistry.md#distillation). The useful net transformation is

$$
\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4\longrightarrow HCOOH+CO_2}.
$$

The [glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol) participates through intermediate [esters](../../../chemical-substance.md#ester) and is regenerated rather than consumed in this net equation. [Marcellin Berthelot](../../../chemistry.md#marcellin-berthelot)'s 1856 account describes the [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) evolution, subsequent [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) addition and [distillation](../../../chemistry.md#distillation), and reuse of the residual [glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol). Overheating is disadvantageous because the [formic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#formic-acid) can decompose further to [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). The method therefore separates a volatile [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) from a much less volatile carrier; it is not merely the dry destructive heating of [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid).[Polytechnisches Journal 140 (1856), p.78, report of the March 1856 communication](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#verfahren-zur-darstellung-der-ameisensaure-by-marcellin-berthelot)

For [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid), oxidize a [carbohydrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbohydrate), such as [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose) or [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch), with [nitric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitric-acid); concentrate the resulting [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry) appropriately and cool it to obtain crystals, followed by [recrystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#recrystallization). [Nitrogen oxides](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-oxides) evolve, and excessive [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) can carry [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) onward to [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide), so an idealized single-product equation would conceal losses. The use of [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch) and [nitric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitric-acid) for [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) production is explicitly documented in an 1850 account of [nitric oxide](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-monoxide) recovery.[p.466, “Verfahren das Salpetergas in Salpetersäure zu verwandeln”, opening example](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-117-1850) An alternative already established before 1864 is alkaline treatment of [sawdust](../../../organism.md#sawdust), producing an [oxalate](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalate) which can be purified through sparingly soluble [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate).[report on chemical manufacture in South Lancashire, Oxalsäure section: alkaline sawdust route](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-162-1861) Decomposing the isolated [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate) with [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) separates much of the [calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium) as [calcium sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-sulfate):

$$
\mathrm{CaC_2O_4+H_2SO_4\longrightarrow H_2C_2O_4+CaSO_4}.
$$

Filter off the [calcium sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-sulfate) and crystallize the [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) from the filtrate. The usual crystals contain two [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization); a calculation for the anhydrous [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) must not treat their entire [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) as $\mathrm{H_2C_2O_4}$.

For [calomel](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride), intimately combine [mercury(II) sulfate](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-ii-sulfate), metallic [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) and dry [sodium chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium-chloride), then collect the volatile [mercury(I) chloride](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride) in a cooler receiving chamber. The net change is

$$
\mathrm{HgSO_4+Hg+2NaCl\longrightarrow Hg_2Cl_2+Na_2SO_4}.
$$

The extra metallic [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) lowers the average [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) from +2 to +1. The product is purified by appropriate [sublimation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) and washing to remove soluble contamination, particularly [mercury(II) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride). Both the [chemical composition](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-composition) and the fine subdivision of the product matter: condensation through a chamber can give a powder rather than an adherent crystalline crust. This physical part of [calomel](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride) preparation is described in the 1843 primary account in [Polytechnisches Journal volume 87 (1843)](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-87-1843); the account's historical therapeutic claims are not adopted here.[pp.209 onward, “Ueber Darstellung des Calomels als höchst zartes Pulver”, condensation and collecting chamber](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-87-1843)

For [corrosive sublimate](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride), heat dry [mercury(II) sulfate](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-ii-sulfate) with [sodium chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium-chloride) and collect the [mercury(II) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride) in the cooler part of a suitable [sublimation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) apparatus:

$$
\mathrm{HgSO_4+2NaCl\longrightarrow HgCl_2+Na_2SO_4}.
$$

Here no metallic [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) is added. Nonvolatile [sodium sulfate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#sodium-sulfate) stays behind, while [mercury(II) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride) is transferred and condensed. Further [sublimation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) or [recrystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#recrystallization) separates impurities. [Mercury, p.141, bichloride preparation and the following calomel account](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#dictionary-of-arts-manufactures-and-mines-by-andrew-ure) The crucial distinction from [calomel](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride) preparation is whether [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) remains in the +2 [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) or is converted to +1.

For the solid called [arsenious acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide), roast an [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic)-bearing [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore) in [air](../../../mixture.md#air) and collect the volatile [arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide) in cooler chambers; purify by resublimation. The essential [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) is represented by

$$
\mathrm{4As+3O_2\longrightarrow2As_2O_3}.
$$

Actual [ores](../../../engineering.md#ore) also contain other [chemical elements](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-element), so their roasting equations and gaseous by-products depend on composition. A contemporary 1860 report describes roasting arseniferous [ores](../../../engineering.md#ore), collecting the volatile [arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide) in chambers and refining it by another [sublimation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation). It also demonstrates that apparently good material could contain [antimony(III) oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-iii-oxide): physical appearance alone does not establish purity.[p.77, report on the antimony content of glassy arsenious acid from Andreasberg](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-156-1860) The product intended here is dry $\mathrm{As_2O_3}$; dissolving it does not make the bottled solid a pure crystalline hydrate $\mathrm{H_3AsO_3}$.

For [tartar emetic](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate), react [antimony(III) oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-iii-oxide) with [potassium hydrogen tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-hydrogen-tartrate) in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). A small amount of [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) first gives intimate contact in a paste; subsequent heating with more [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) completes formation of the soluble [potassium antimony tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate). Filter away unreacted insoluble matter and cool the clear filtrate to crystallize the product. This sequence is directly described in a January 1864 discussion of the newly issued [British Pharmacopoeia](../../../pharmacology.md#british-pharmacopoeia), rather than inferred from its later editions.[30 January 1864, p.53, Antimonium Tartaratum: paste, standing, boiling and the oxide–bitartrate route](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#chemical-news-volume-9-1864) In the traditional antimonyl notation the underlying reaction, before assigning crystal [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization), is

$$
\mathrm{Sb_2O_3+2KHC_4H_4O_6\longrightarrow2K(SbO)C_4H_4O_6+H_2O}.
$$

This notation expresses the overall composition; [tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#tartrate) [coordination complexes](../../../chemistry.md#coordination-complex) and [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization) need the qualification given in [4](#4). The historical pharmaceutical use of [tartar emetic](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate) does not make it a safe contemporary emetic.[Toxicity; gastrointestinal effects and systemic poisoning](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#antimony-potassium-tartrate-by-the-national-poisons-information-service)

For [potassium chlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate), pass [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine) into hot concentrated [potassium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-hydroxide). The [disproportionation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#disproportionation) gives [potassium chloride](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-chloride) together with [potassium chlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate):

$$
\mathrm{3Cl_2+6KOH\longrightarrow KClO_3+5KCl+3H_2O}.
$$

Concentration and cooling permit [fractional crystallization](../../../planetary-science.md#fractional-crystallization) of the much less soluble cold-water [potassium chlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate), leaving much of the [potassium chloride](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-chloride) in the mother liquor; [recrystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#recrystallization) improves purity. Cold dilute [alkali](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#alkali) instead favours [hypochlorite](../../../chemistry.md#hypochlorite), so the heating condition is essential. An economical contemporary manufacturing alternative uses hot [calcium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-hydroxide) and [potassium chloride](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-chloride); the overall accounting is

$$
\mathrm{6Cl_2+6Ca(OH)_2+2KCl\longrightarrow2KClO_3+6CaCl_2+6H_2O}.
$$

The 1850 account in [Polytechnisches Journal volume 118 (1850)](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-118-1850) describes a steam-heated stirred vessel and recovery by cooling, rather than an electrical manufacturing process introduced retrospectively.[pp.424 onward, “Die neue Bereitungsart des chlorsauren Kalis”](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#polytechnisches-journal-volume-118-1850)

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What are the constituents of cows' milk, and how may they be separated ?

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Cow's milk](../../../organism.md#cow-s-milk) contains [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), dispersed [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat), [casein](../../../protein.md#casein), soluble [milk proteins](../../../organism.md#milk-protein), [lactose](../../../chemical-substance.md#lactose) and [mineral](../../../planetary-science.md#mineral) constituents, with smaller amounts of other substances.** These constituents occupy different physical states, which makes their separation possible: the [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) is present as an [emulsion](../../../mixture.md#emulsion), much of the [casein](../../../protein.md#casein) as [colloidal](../../../mixture.md#colloid) [casein micelles](../../../protein.md#casein-micelle), and the [lactose](../../../chemical-substance.md#lactose) and many [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) in [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry). [Vitamins](../../../biology.md#vitamin), [enzymes](../../../enzyme.md) and small nitrogenous constituents accompany these main fractions. The proportions vary between samples and are not fixed by the name [cow's milk](../../../organism.md#cow-s-milk).[chapter 2, principal constituents and Tables 2.1–2.2](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#the-chemistry-of-milk-in-the-dairy-processing-handbook)

First let fresh unhomogenized [milk](../../../organism.md#milk) stand so that the less dense [fat globules](../../../organism.md#fat-globule) rise as [cream](../../../organism.md#cream); skimming gives a [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat)-enriched layer and [skimmed milk](../../../organism.md#skimmed-milk) beneath. This separates fractions rather than pure [chemical substances](../../../chemical-substance.md): [cream](../../../organism.md#cream) still contains [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) and other constituents. Churning [cream](../../../organism.md#cream) coalesces the [fat globules](../../../organism.md#fat-globule) into butter; extraction of a separately prepared dried portion with a suitable organic [solvent](../../../mixture.md#solvent) provides another way to isolate [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat). A modern [centrifugal separator](../../../centrifugation.md#centrifugal-separator) accelerates the same density-based separation, but it is not needed to explain an 1864 bench separation.

Next add dilute [acetic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#acetic-acid) carefully to [skimmed milk](../../../organism.md#skimmed-milk). Lowering its [pH](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) toward the [isoelectric point](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#isoelectric-point) of [casein](../../../protein.md#casein), about 4.6, produces a curd that can be collected by [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) and washed. Excess [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry), retained [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat), and shifts of [calcium phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-phosphate) between curd and liquid limit purity. [Rennet](../../../enzyme.md#rennet) offers another route to a curd, but its [enzyme](../../../enzyme.md)-catalysed action and the [mineral](../../../planetary-science.md#mineral) content of the resulting curd differ from simple [acid precipitation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#acid-precipitation); the two should not be treated as identical mechanisms.[chapter 2, Casein, precipitation and acidification](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#the-chemistry-of-milk-in-the-dairy-processing-handbook)

The liquid after removal of the curd is [whey](../../../organism.md#whey). Heat this fraction under suitable acidity to induce [protein denaturation](../../../protein.md#denaturation-biochemistry) and aggregate much of its residual [whey protein](../../../organism.md#whey-protein); collect the coagulum by [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory). Older descriptions commonly grouped this fraction under “albumin”, but modern [milk chemistry](../../../organism.md#milk-chemistry) distinguishes several [proteins](../../../protein.md). Heating alone does not make every minor constituent fall into a single pure fraction.[chapter 18, Recovery of denatured whey proteins](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#milk-and-whey-fractionation-in-the-dairy-processing-handbook)

Concentrate the deproteinized [whey](../../../organism.md#whey) and cool it to crystallize [lactose](../../../chemical-substance.md#lactose). Filter and wash the crystals, then purify them by [recrystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#recrystallization); dissolved [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) and residual [organic matter](../../../chemistry.md#organic-matter) remain substantially in the mother liquor. This provides a separation from most [protein](../../../protein.md), not an automatic complete separation from all [minerals](../../../planetary-science.md#mineral).[chapter 18, Lactose recovery; Crystallisation; Lactose separation](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#milk-and-whey-fractionation-in-the-dairy-processing-handbook)

Finally use separate measured portions for [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) and [mineral](../../../planetary-science.md#mineral) determinations. Controlled drying gives an operational [total solids](../../../chemistry.md#total-solids) measurement; subtraction from the initial [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) estimates the [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) lost. Ignition of a dried portion destroys most [organic matter](../../../chemistry.md#organic-matter) and leaves [ash](../../../chemistry.md#ash) for [mineral analysis](../../../chemistry.md#mineral-analysis). [Ash](../../../chemistry.md#ash) is a transformed residue: it does not preserve all original [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) unchanged, and some constituents can volatilize. [Calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium), [phosphorus](../../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus), [sodium](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium), [potassium](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium) and [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride) are among the important [mineral](../../../planetary-science.md#mineral) constituents. **Skimming, curd formation, heat coagulation, lactose crystallization, drying and ashing separate or determine the principal fractions; none alone constitutes a complete pure-component analysis.**

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How may Glucose be prepared and detected ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**[Glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) can be obtained by [hydrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) of [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch) and detected as a [reducing sugar](../../../chemical-substance.md#reducing-sugar) by a heated alkaline [copper(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii) test. A positive copper test alone does not uniquely identify [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose).**

Suspend [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch) in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), warm it to make a paste, and heat with a dilute [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry), such as dilute [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid), to cause [hydrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) of the [glycosidic bonds](../../../covalent-bond.md#glycosidic-bond). The [chemical reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md) passes through shorter [carbohydrates](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbohydrate); sufficient [hydrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) ultimately gives [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose). The usual repeat-unit accounting is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{(C_6H_{10}O_5)_n+nH_2O\longrightarrow nC_6H_{12}O_6}.}
$$

This idealized [polymer](../../../polymer.md) formula omits end-group details. The [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) catalyses cleavage and is not consumed in the displayed equation. Merely using [amylase](../../../enzyme.md#amylase) from malt should not be described as necessarily producing pure [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose); it can leave [maltose](../../../chemical-substance.md#maltose) and shorter [polysaccharides](../../../chemical-substance.md#polysaccharide).

When [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) is used, neutralization with [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) removes the acidity and much of the [sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate) as [calcium sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-sulfate). Filter out the insoluble residue, then concentrate the [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry) gently and crystallize the [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose); further [recrystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#recrystallization) is required to distinguish a reasonably pure [chemical substance](../../../chemical-substance.md) from a crude syrup. Overheating and unnecessarily prolonged strong [acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) treatment degrade the [sugar](../../../chemical-substance.md#sugar). [Hydrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis) of [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose) is a less selective preparative answer because it gives [fructose](../../../chemical-substance.md#fructose) alongside [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose).

For the historical copper test, use freshly mixed [Fehling's reagent](../../../chemical-substance.md#fehling-s-reagent): an aqueous [copper(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii-sulfate) portion and an alkaline [tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#tartrate) portion. The [tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#tartrate) keeps [copper(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii) in [coordination complexes](../../../chemistry.md#coordination-complex) instead of allowing immediate bulk precipitation of [copper(II) hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#copper-ii-hydroxide). Add a suitably prepared sample to a small test portion and warm in a hot [water bath](../../../chemistry.md#water-bath). A yellow-orange to brick-red [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate) of [copper(I) oxide](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#copper-i-oxide) indicates [reduction](../../../chemical-reaction.md#reduction-chemistry) of the [copper(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii) reagent. Run a known [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) sample and a [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) blank alongside it. In a simple sample, a visible difference between these controls helps distinguish a true positive from a deteriorated reagent or contamination.[Theory, uses of Fehling's solution and formation of red copper(I) oxide](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#estimation-of-glucose-by-amrita-vishwa-vidyapeetham)

The key [redox](../../../chemical-reaction.md#redox) accounting on the [copper](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper) side is

$$
\mathrm{2Cu^{2+}+2OH^-+2e^-\longrightarrow Cu_2O+H_2O}.
$$

The required [electrons](../../../physics.md#electron) come from [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) of the [sugar](../../../chemical-substance.md#sugar). This half-equation deliberately omits the changing [tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#tartrate) [ligands](../../../protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) and does not assign a unique organic product. The often printed claim that hot alkaline [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) simply becomes [gluconate](../../../chemical-substance.md#gluconate) in this test is too simple: competing [isomerization](../../../chemical-reaction.md#isomerization), [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) and fragmentation make the organic chemistry more complicated.[§2.3, interpretation of copper tests and product complexity](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#the-iodine-test-for-reducing-sugars-by-holger-fleischer)

[Fructose](../../../chemical-substance.md#fructose), [lactose](../../../chemical-substance.md#lactose), [maltose](../../../chemical-substance.md#maltose) and various other [reducing substances](../../../chemistry.md#reducing-substance) can also give a positive [Fehling's test](../../../chemical-substance.md#fehling-s-test). In particular, [fructose](../../../chemical-substance.md#fructose) can form [aldoses](../../../chemical-substance.md#aldose) by [tautomerization](../../../chemical-reaction.md#tautomerization) in [alkaline solution](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#alkaline-solution); an initial [ketone](../../../chemical-substance.md#ketone) functional group does not make it reliably negative.[Oxidation of Monosaccharides; fructose rearrangement](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/25-6-reactions-of-monosaccharides) Fermentation by suitable [yeast](../../../organism.md#yeast), with [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) formation, and [optical rotation](../../../stereochemistry.md#optical-rotation) can supply further historical evidence, but neither is individually specific to [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose). These are anticipated diagnostic observations, not measurements supplied by the paper. In present laboratory practice an appropriately validated [enzymatic glucose assay](../../../chemical-substance.md#enzymatic-glucose-assay) can establish [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) more selectively; the old reducing-sugar test is not by itself a diagnosis of [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes).[Monitoring: Diagnosis, blood tests and glucose-oxidase assay explanation](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#diagnosis-of-diabetes-in-pdb-101)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Give the ratio which equal volumes of the following gases bear to the volumes of the elements of which they consist :—Hydrochloric Acid, Water, Protoxide of Nitrogen, Binoxide of Nitrogen, Ammonia.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**At the same temperature and pressure, one volume of HCl corresponds to $\tfrac12$ volume of hydrogen and $\tfrac12$ volume of chlorine; one of steam to one of hydrogen and $\tfrac12$ of oxygen; one of $\mathrm{N_2O}$ to one of nitrogen and $\tfrac12$ of oxygen; one of NO to $\tfrac12$ of each element; and one of ammonia to $\tfrac12$ of nitrogen and $\tfrac32$ of hydrogen.** All elemental [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) here refer to their ordinary diatomic [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas).

The simplest way to avoid an erroneous factor of two is to balance each [chemical reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md) using $\mathrm{H_2}$, $\mathrm{Cl_2}$, $\mathrm{N_2}$ and $\mathrm{O_2}$. Under [Avogadro's law](../../../ideal-gas.md#avogadro-s-law), their [stoichiometric coefficients](../../../chemical-reaction.md#stoichiometric-coefficient) are also [gas volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#gas-volume) ratios, provided all [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) are compared at the same [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and [pressure](../../../pressure.md) and [ideal gas](../../../ideal-gas.md) behaviour is a good approximation.

- For gaseous [hydrogen chloride](../../../electronegativity.md#hydrogen-chloride), $\mathrm{H_2+Cl_2\rightarrow2HCl}$. Thus $\boxed{V_{\mathrm{HCl}}:V_{\mathrm{H_2}}:V_{\mathrm{Cl_2}}=2:1:1}$. There is no change in total [gas volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#gas-volume) in this idealized combination.
- For [water vapor](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor), $\mathrm{2H_2+O_2\rightarrow2H_2O(g)}$. Thus $\boxed{V_{\mathrm{H_2O(g)}}:V_{\mathrm{H_2}}:V_{\mathrm{O_2}}=2:2:1}$: three reactant [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) give two product [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume).
- “Protoxide of nitrogen” is [nitrous oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrous-oxide), $\mathrm{N_2O}$. The composition equation $\mathrm{2N_2+O_2\rightarrow2N_2O}$ gives $\boxed{V_{\mathrm{N_2O}}:V_{\mathrm{N_2}}:V_{\mathrm{O_2}}=2:2:1}$. This equation expresses composition; it does not assert that direct mixing gives an easy preparative reaction.
- “Binoxide of nitrogen” in the historical equivalent nomenclature is [nitric oxide](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-monoxide), modern $\mathrm{NO}$, rather than modern [nitrogen dioxide](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-dioxide). The equation $\mathrm{N_2+O_2\rightarrow2NO}$ gives $\boxed{V_{\mathrm{NO}}:V_{\mathrm{N_2}}:V_{\mathrm{O_2}}=2:1:1}$. Older $\mathrm{NO_2}$ notation with [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) equivalent 8 describes the same oxygen-to-nitrogen mass ratio as modern $\mathrm{NO}$ with [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) atomic weight 16; reading its subscript by modern rules changes the substance.[Nitrogen, p.115, “Binoxide of Nitrogen; Nitric Oxide”, older formula and equal elemental volumes](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#review-of-chemistry-for-students-by-john-g-murphy)
- For [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia), $\mathrm{N_2+3H_2\rightarrow2NH_3}$ gives $\boxed{V_{\mathrm{NH_3}}:V_{\mathrm{N_2}}:V_{\mathrm{H_2}}=2:1:3}$: four reactant [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) correspond to two product [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume).

If the common chosen [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of each compound is $V$, the corresponding ordered elemental [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) are therefore $(V/2,V/2)$, $(V,V/2)$, $(V,V/2)$, $(V/2,V/2)$ and $(V/2,3V/2)$. The ratios concern molecular counts and hypothetical free elemental [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas), not separate compartments of elemental [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) still present inside a compound.

“Hydrochloric acid” must here mean gaseous [hydrogen chloride](../../../electronegativity.md#hydrogen-chloride), rather than its aqueous [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry); [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) must remain as [steam](../../../chemical-substance.md#steam). If [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) condenses or [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) dissolves in the collecting [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid), measured [volumes](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) no longer show these simple ratios. For an experimental comparison, dry-gas and [hydrostatic pressure](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) corrections are those discussed in [3](#3).
