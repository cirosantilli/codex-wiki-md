**These names refer to several unrelated chemical classes: [blood](../../../../../blood.md) and bile pigments, a photosynthetic pigment family, an amino sulfonic acid, a disputed historical faecal extract, and storage [polysaccharides](../../../../../polysaccharide.md) or [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md).** Similar colour or a historical name does not establish chemical identity.

_[Haematocrystallin](../../../../../hemoglobin.md)_ denotes the crystallizable colouring substance of red [blood](../../../../../blood.md), now identified with [hemoglobin](../../../../../hemoglobin.md). It combines a globin [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) with iron-containing [haem](../../../../../haem.md) groups. Within intact red [cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) it reversibly binds [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md); oxygenated and deoxygenated forms have different absorption spectra. Crystals obtained from [blood](../../../../../blood.md) differ with species and preparation. This is not the same thing as a crystal formed from an old tissue haemorrhage. The word does not mean that all circulating [blood](../../../../../blood.md) normally consists of free pigment crystals.[Hemoglobin: globin, haem and oxygen carriage](../../../../../examples-of-hemoproteins-by-the-university-of-utah.md)

_[Haematoidin](../../../../../haematoidin.md)_ is the yellow-orange to reddish pigment characteristically encountered in old extravasated [blood](../../../../../blood.md), sometimes as rhomboidal crystals or radiating clusters. It is associated with [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md) and lacks the iron retained in [haemosiderin](../../../../../haemosiderin.md). The historical inference that any reddish [blood](../../../../../blood.md)-derived crystal must be the same substance as [haematocrystallin](../../../../../hemoglobin.md) is therefore wrong. In a tissue preparation the local matrix and other constituents can complicate an exact chemical identification from appearance alone.[Results; Discussion: crystal morphology, bilirubin association and absence of iron](../../../../../hematoidin-crystals-in-sputum-smears-cytopathology-and-clinical-associations.md)

_[Haematin](../../../../../hematin.md)_ is a dark iron-containing derivative of [haem](../../../../../haem.md), obtained when the globin association and the iron's chemical environment are altered. In modern chemical usage, [hematin](../../../../../hematin.md) is the ferric hydroxide form of the [haem](../../../../../haem.md) pigment; [hemin](../../../../../hemin.md) is the corresponding chloride form. Historical preparations and terminology were less consistently separated. Neither should be equated with iron-free [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md), and a [haem](../../../../../haem.md)-derived crystal test is not the crystallization of intact [hemoglobin](../../../../../hemoglobin.md).[Identity and chemical description: hydroxyl ferric protoporphyrin](../../../../../hematin-in-pubchem.md)[CHEBI:50385, formula and chlorido(protoporphyrinato)iron(III) identity](../../../../../hemin-in-chebi.md)

_[Chlorophyll](../../../../../chlorophyll.md)_ is the green pigment system of photosynthetic organisms, present in organized pigment-[protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) assemblies in [chloroplasts](../../../../../chloroplast.md) in green plants. Modern chemistry distinguishes several [chlorophylls](../../../../../chlorophyll.md): [chlorophyll a](../../../../../chlorophyll-a.md), for example, has a magnesium-containing tetrapyrrolic ring system and a hydrophobic side chain. It absorbs light used in photosynthetic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) conversion. Extraction into alcohol can reveal the green pigment, but a leaf extract is a mixture rather than a chemically pure universal [chlorophyll](../../../../../chlorophyll.md). Its green colour must not be attributed to iron in the same way as [haem](../../../../../haem.md).[Molecular formula; description: magnesium-containing photosynthetic pigment](../../../../../chlorophyll-a-in-pubchem.md)

_[Biliverdin](../../../../../biliverdin.md)_ is a green, open-chain tetrapyrrolic pigment. In modern [haem catabolism](../../../../../haem-catabolism.md), ring cleavage by [haem](../../../../../haem.md) oxygenase releases iron and forms [biliverdin](../../../../../biliverdin.md); reduction then produces [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md). The pigments contribute to changing colours of some resolving haemorrhages and to bile-pigment chemistry. This sequence concerns degradation of the [haem](../../../../../haem.md) group, not conversion of the entire globin [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) directly into one green molecule.[Cellular Level: haem oxygenase and biliverdin reductase](../../../../../biochemistry-biliverdin-statpearls.md)

_[Taurin](../../../../../taurine.md)_, now [taurine](../../../../../taurine.md), is a colourless crystalline amino sulfonic acid with the structure $\mathrm{H_2NCH_2CH_2SO_3H}$. It contains sulfur and [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md) and can form conjugates with bile acids, including [taurocholic acid](../../../../../taurocholic-acid.md). It is not itself a green bile pigment, an ordinary carboxylic [amino acid](../../../../../amino-acid.md), or a standard [amino acid](../../../../../amino-acid.md) directly inserted into [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md) by translation. The chemical classification and formula distinguish it from neighbouring names in the list.[CHEBI:15891: identity as 2-aminoethanesulfonic acid and formula](../../../../../taurine-in-chebi.md)

_[Excretin](../../../../../excretin.md)_ was the name [William Marcet](../../../../../william-marcet.md) gave to a crystalline material extracted from human [faeces](../../../../../faeces.md). His contemporary account describes treatment with hot alcohol, removal of material with lime, extraction with ether, and formation of silky needle-like crystals; the material was insoluble in [water](../../../../../water.md), soluble in ether and hot alcohol, and melted in the low-to-mid 90s Celsius. His 1859 account reports [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md), hydrogen, [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and sulfur and gives an equivalent-weight formula based on an assumed sulfur content. These are historical analytical claims, not a modern authenticated molecular specification. Later work questioned the purity and chemical individuality of [excretin](../../../../../excretin.md), so that old name alone does not establish a definite modern structure.[p. 523: later doubts about identity and possible impure sterol material](../../../../../excretin-in-a-text-book-of-physiological-chemistry-1914.md)[Archives of Medicine, volume 1, 1859, pp. 100–102: preparation, melting behaviour and composition](../../../../../on-excretine-by-william-marcet.md)

_[Glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md)_ is an animal [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md)-storage [polysaccharide](../../../../../polysaccharide.md), particularly important in the [liver](../../../../../liver.md) and [skeletal muscles](../../../../../skeletal-muscle.md). Its highly branched chains contain alpha-(1→4) links with alpha-(1→6) branch points. Preparations form opalescent dispersions and commonly show a reddish-brown [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) reaction rather than the intense blue associated with long [amylose](../../../../../amylose.md) chains. [Hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) yields [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md). [Liver](../../../../../liver.md) [glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md) can support the regulation of circulating [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md), whereas [muscle](../../../../../muscle.md) [glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md) mainly supports local [muscle](../../../../../muscle.md) [metabolism](../../../../../metabolism-split.md); a modern account must distinguish those physiological destinations.[Introduction; Cellular Level: glucose storage, branching and distinct liver/muscle roles](../../../../../biochemistry-glycogen-statpearls.md)

_[Gluten](../../../../../gluten.md)_ is the cohesive [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md)-rich material left when wheat dough is washed to remove much of its [starch](../../../../../starch.md). It is not a single small molecule: modern analysis distinguishes gliadin and glutenin families whose associations contribute extensibility and elasticity. It is poorly soluble in [water](../../../../../water.md) as a dough network, contains [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md), and on full [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) supplies [amino acids](../../../../../amino-acid.md). Its nutritive role is that of a plant [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) mixture, not a [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md)-free [carbohydrate](../../../../../carbohydrate.md).[Introduction; experimental comparison of the rheology of the two protein fractions](../../../../../changes-in-the-rheology-and-aggregation-behavior-of-wheat-glutenin-and-gliadin-mediated-by-low-sodium-salt.md)

_[Legumin](../../../../../legumin.md)_ is a seed storage globulin associated particularly with peas and related legumes. A classical preparation extracts [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) into suitable dilute salt solution and precipitates it under changed conditions. Modern [legumins](../../../../../legumin.md) are 11S globulin assemblies, often hexamers formed from processed subunits. They are [nitrogen](../../../../../nitrogen.md)-containing [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md), chemically distinct from [starch](../../../../../starch.md) despite occurring beside it in a seed. The older name vegetable casein indicates an analogy of precipitation or nutritive role; it does not establish identity with milk casein.[Abstract; results on precursor processing and 11S hexamer assembly](../../../../../pea-legumin-overexpressed-in-wheat-endosperm-assembles-into-an-ordered-paracrystalline-matrix.md)
