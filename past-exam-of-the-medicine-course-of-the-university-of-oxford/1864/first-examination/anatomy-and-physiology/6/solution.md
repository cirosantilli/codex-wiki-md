↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**A seven-month [fetus](../../../../../fetus.md) is recognizably human in its formed organs and external parts but remains immature in [fat](../../../../../fat.md) stores, respiratory function, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) control and feeding coordination.** The month count must be stated: calendar months, four-week obstetric months and time since conception are not interchangeable.

A useful modern benchmark is around 28 completed gestational weeks, counted from the last menstrual period, about two weeks more than the usual time since fertilization. At that stage a typical crown-to-heel length is about 38 cm and mass about 1 kg, with substantial normal variation. Those are illustrative modern averages, not measurements of a particular [fetus](../../../../../fetus.md) or exact historical thresholds for a seventh month. A historical count from conception would place the same numerical week count later in modern gestational dating.[What does my baby look like? Crown-to-heel length at 28 gestational weeks](../../../../../week-28-of-pregnancy-by-the-nhs.md)[How your baby is changing: approximate mass, eyelid opening and developing fat](../../../../../week-28-of-pregnancy-by-healthlink-bc.md)

The body is slender compared with a term newborn because subcutaneous [fat](../../../../../fat.md) is still limited. The [skin](../../../../../skin.md) is relatively thin and reddish, with wrinkles, [lanugo](../../../../../lanugo.md) and a variable covering of [vernix caseosa](../../../../../vernix-caseosa.md). Hair and nails are present but their growth and the firmness of the ear cartilage remain immature. The eyelids have separated and can open around this period; this should not be confused with adult visual acuity or fully mature pupillary responses. Limb movements occur, but sustained flexor posture and coordinated behaviour are less developed than at term.[Developmental characteristics at 23–27, 28–31 and 32–35 weeks: skin, posture, movement and eye responses](../../../../../developmental-care-guideline-by-nhs-greater-glasgow-and-clyde.md)

The major organs are present, yet their organization and functional reserve continue to mature. The [brain](../../../../../brain.md) is growing rapidly and its surface folding increases. The [lungs](../../../../../lung.md) are developing gas-exchanging regions and [pulmonary surfactant](../../../../../pulmonary-surfactant.md) production, but an early birth can still entail serious respiratory difficulty. Fetal respiratory movements do not demonstrate that the [lungs](../../../../../lung.md) are already performing ordinary air breathing: before birth the [placenta](../../../../../placenta.md) supplies gas exchange. [Fetal circulation](../../../../../fetal-circulation.md) still includes its characteristic shunts. Genital appearance and testicular descent, when relevant, can contribute to maturity assessment but do not provide a precise independent calendar.

Survival after birth is possible, especially with current neonatal support, but viability is a probability conditioned by maturity, circumstances and available care, not a switch thrown on one date. Modern intensive-care results must not be represented as outcomes achievable in 1864. Nor is there a physiological rule that a healthy eight-month [fetus](../../../../../fetus.md) is intrinsically less viable than a seven-month [fetus](../../../../../fetus.md). A description of several concordant maturity features is more defensible than assigning an exact age or prognosis from size alone.
