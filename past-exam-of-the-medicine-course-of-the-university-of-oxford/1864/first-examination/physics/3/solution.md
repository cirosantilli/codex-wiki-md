<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In a uniform [gravitational field](../../../../../gravitational-field.md), the [centre of gravity](../../../../../centre-of-gravity.md) is the point $G$ through which the resultant [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md) acts. It coincides with the [center of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r_G=\frac{1}{M}\int\mathbf r\,dm,\qquad
\mathbf W=M\mathbf g.}
$$

Indeed the sum of the elemental gravitational [torques](../../../../../torque.md) about any origin is

$$
\int\mathbf r\times\mathbf g\,dm
=M\mathbf r_G\times\mathbf g.
$$

Replacing the distributed gravitational forces by the resultant at $G$ therefore preserves both total force and torque. A strongly nonuniform field need not permit the same identification.

For equilibrium, all external forces and torques must balance. A freely suspended rigid body supported at a pivot requires $G$ on the vertical through that pivot. With $G$ below the pivot, a small angular displacement raises its gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) and gives a restoring torque; with $G$ above it, the equilibrium is unstable. A body supported on a horizontal base can remain at rest under its weight only if the vertical projection of $G$ lies within the region that the support forces can sustain. At an edge the overturning [torque](../../../../../torque.md) becomes decisive.

For motion, the [center-of-mass equation](../../../../../center-of-mass-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{M\ddot{\mathbf r}_G=\sum\mathbf F_{\rm external}.}
$$

The centre moves as a particle carrying the total mass under the resultant external force, while rotation about it is governed by the external torque and [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md). This separates translation from rotation; it does not determine a rigid body's full orientation from its centre's trajectory alone. In uniform gravity and negligible air resistance, a thrown rotating body has a parabolic centre-of-mass path even while its parts follow more complicated paths.
