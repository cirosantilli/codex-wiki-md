A [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md) is an electrically neutral bound assembly of more than one [atom](../../../../../atom-physics.md). It can contain identical atoms, as in $\mathrm O_2$, or different atoms, as in $\mathrm H_2O$. Discrete molecules are not a universal description of matter: an extended ionic crystal does not consist of separate neutral molecules corresponding to each empirical formula unit. The IUPAC definition also specifies binding sufficient to support a vibrational state.[Definition M04002](../../../../../iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/molecule-in-the-iupac-gold-book.md)

[Elasticity](../../../../../elasticity-physics-split.md) is a body's tendency to recover its original configuration when the deforming [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is removed. Within a linear regime, [Hooke's law](../../../../../hooke-s-law.md) makes [stress](../../../../../stress.md) proportional to [strain](../../../../../strain.md), but elasticity does not require exact linearity. The [elastic limit](../../../../../elastic-limit.md) matters: permanent [plastic deformation](../../../../../plastic-deformation.md) is a different response.

[Inertia](../../../../../inertia.md) is the property expressed by [Newton's first law](../../../../../newton-s-first-law.md): without a resultant external [force](../../../../../force-split.md), a body's [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) remains constant in an [inertial frame of reference](../../../../../inertial-frame.md). [Mass](../../../../../mass.md) measures translational inertia. It is not a force acting against motion, and a moving body does not require a continuing forward force merely to keep moving.

A [force](../../../../../force-split.md) describes an interaction that changes [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md). In [Newtonian mechanics](../../../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F_{\rm resultant}=\frac{d\mathbf p}{dt};\qquad
\mathbf F_{\rm resultant}=m\mathbf a\quad\text{for constant }m.}
$$

A force has magnitude and direction; its point of application also matters for the [torque](../../../../../torque.md) and hence the body's rotational response.
