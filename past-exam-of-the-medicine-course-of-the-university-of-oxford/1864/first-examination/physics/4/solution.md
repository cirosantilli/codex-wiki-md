↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Here an [elastic fluid](../../../../../elastic-fluid.md) means a [gas](../../../../../gas.md) or vapour. Such a fluid is mobile, occupies the accessible space of its container, and is appreciably [compressible](../../../../../compressibility.md). Its [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) acts normally on a confining surface. In static equilibrium the stress at a point is isotropic: a fluid has no equilibrium resistance to a sustained shear deformation. Pressure can still vary with height through [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md); isotropy does not mean the same pressure at every position.

Reducing a confined gas's [volume](../../../../../volume.md) at fixed [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) normally raises its pressure. Heating at fixed volume normally raises the pressure, and gases exchange material through [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md). Their particles are in continual thermal motion, whose momentum transfer gives a microscopic account of gas pressure in the [kinetic theory of gases](../../../../../kinetic-theory-of-gases.md).

For a dilute gas away from condensation, the approximate [ideal gas law](../../../../../ideal-gas-law.md) is

$$
\boxed{pV=nRT.}
$$

It contains [Boyle's law](../../../../../boyle-s-law.md) at fixed temperature and the corresponding temperature-expansion laws. These simple proportionalities are approximations, not exact properties of every elastic fluid at every density. Intermolecular interactions matter in a dense gas, and a vapour can [condense](../../../../../condensation.md); the classification does not promise that every sample remains gaseous under arbitrary compression or cooling.
