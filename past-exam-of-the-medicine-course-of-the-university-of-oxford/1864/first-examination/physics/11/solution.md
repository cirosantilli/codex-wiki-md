<h1 id="11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

A closed conducting coil linked by a changing [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) develops an [electromotive force](../../../../../electromotive-force.md). For $N$ turns, [Faraday's law of induction](../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E=-N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt},\qquad
\Phi_B=\int_S\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S.}
$$

With a permanent steel magnet, connect the coil to a sensitive [galvanometer](../../../../../galvanometer.md) and move a pole into or out of it. Entry and withdrawal give opposite deflections; exchanging the pole reverses the signs. Holding both magnet and coil stationary gives no sustained induction merely because a magnetic field is present. Moving the coil instead, or changing its orientation, can produce the same necessary flux change.

Earth's [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) can serve as the field source. Turn a many-turn coil so that its area normal changes direction relative to the terrestrial field. In an approximately uniform field, a steadily rotating coil has

$$
\Phi_B=BA\cos\omega t,\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal E=NBA\omega\sin\omega t.}
$$

Reversing a coil from one normal orientation to the opposite changes its flux linkage by $2NBA$. The Earth's weak field calls for an appropriate coil area, number of turns and detector sensitivity; it does not require a chemically supplied current in the detecting circuit. Faraday described terrestrial magneto-electric induction in his Second Series.[Second Series, §5: terrestrial magneto-electric induction](../../../../../experimental-researches-in-electricity-volume-1-by-michael-faraday.md)

The coil must provide a conducting return path for a circulating current. Its [electrical resistance](../../../../../electrical-resistance.md) and [self-inductance](../../../../../self-inductance.md) affect the response. [Lenz's law](../../../../../lenz-s-law.md) fixes the sign: the induced effect opposes the change responsible for it. The moving system supplies mechanical work when current delivers energy or dissipates heat; a permanent magnet is not thereby an inexhaustible fuel.
