↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

[Frictional electricity](../../../../../frictional-electricity.md) and [voltaic electricity](../../../../../voltaic-electricity.md) name different methods of producing electrical effects. Their identity is supported by showing common kinds of response and quantitative continuity between them, rather than merely observing that both can give a sensation.

Charges from either source show the same attraction and repulsion, can charge a conductor or capacitor, and can discharge through a circuit. A current from either source can generate a [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md), produce resistive heating, and cause appropriate [electrolysis](../../../../../electrolysis.md). Reversing the sign or current direction reverses the corresponding directional effects. Different observed strengths need not imply different electrical substances.

[Michael Faraday](../../../../../michael-faraday.md) carried out a [comparison of common and voltaic electricity](../../../../../faraday-s-comparison-of-common-and-voltaic-electricity.md). In a specific magnetic test, he discharged a machine-charged jar battery through a galvanometer, using a wet thread in the discharge path; the needle's deflection connected ordinary static charge with a current's magnetic action. He also compared common and voltaic electricity quantitatively.[Third Series, §§7–8; paragraph 296 for the galvanometer arrangement and paragraphs 361–378 for quantitative comparison](../../../../../experimental-researches-in-electricity-volume-1-by-michael-faraday.md)

The modern distinction is mainly in the source characteristics. A friction machine commonly supplies a small charge at high [electric potential difference](../../../../../electric-potential-difference.md), while a chemical cell can sustain a much larger continuing [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) at a lower voltage. An insulating gap or a poorly conducting path can therefore affect the demonstrations differently. Once charge passes through a specified circuit, its electric and magnetic action is not determined by whether mechanical rubbing or a chemical reaction originally supplied the energy.

For a specified electrode reaction with controlled current efficiency, [Faraday's laws of electrolysis](../../../../../faraday-s-laws-of-electrolysis.md) express the quantitative relation

$$
\boxed{m=\frac{M}{zF}\,Q,\qquad Q=\int I\,dt.}
$$

Here $M$ is the product's molar mass, $z$ the electrons per reaction unit and $F$ the Faraday constant. Equal transferred charges give equal reaction equivalents under those conditions. Charge, voltage and current must be distinguished: equal voltages alone do not imply equal delivered quantities of electricity.
