<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The relevant claim concerns a machine that repeats its complete operating cycle while delivering a continuing net output of [work](../../../../../work.md) without a corresponding supply of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). **A machine cannot supply net work in a cycle without taking in energy or depleting an energy store.** This follows from [conservation of energy](../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md), an experimentally supported physical law rather than a theorem of pure logic.

Let $E$ include the machine's [internal energy](../../../../../internal-energy-split.md), [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md). With $Q$ the net [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) received and $W$ the net work delivered, the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) gives

$$
\Delta E=Q-W.
$$

For a complete cycle all stores return to their initial values, so $\Delta E=0$ and $W=Q$. If there is neither heat input nor another energy input, $W=0$. A descending [mass](../../../../../mass.md), unwinding [spring](../../../../../spring.md), consumed fuel or discharged [battery](../../../../../electric-battery.md) supplies energy, but it cannot be omitted from the accounting while calling the result [perpetual motion](../../../../../perpetual-motion.md).

Changing an overbalanced wheel's geometry does not evade the argument. A [conservative force](../../../../../conservative-force.md) gives zero net work around a closed configuration cycle, while [friction](../../../../../friction.md) dissipates usable mechanical energy. Resetting a mechanism or returning a raised weight costs work that must be included.

Indefinitely continuing motion without useful output is a different possibility: an ideal frictionless oscillator or freely moving body need not stop. Connecting a load removes energy and changes that situation. A cyclic machine supplied with heat from only one reservoir and converting all that heat into work is excluded instead by the [Kelvin-Planck statement](../../../../../kelvin-planck-statement.md) of the [Second law of thermodynamics](../../../../../second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md). The first-law and second-law objections address different proposed energy balances.
