## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

By what symptoms would you seek to form a diagnosis between intoxication from alcoholic drinks, and concussion of the brain ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**Neither the smell of [alcohol](../../../chemical-substance.md#alcohol) nor a drunken appearance excludes [concussion](../../../neurology.md#concussion) or a more serious [head injury](../../../neurology.md#head-injury).** The useful distinction comes from the history, a complete examination and repeated assessment; [alcohol intoxication](../../../biology.md#alcohol-intoxication) and [head injury](../../../neurology.md#head-injury) may occur together.

First establish the order of events. Witnesses may describe drinking followed by progressive slurring, unsteadiness and drowsiness, or a blow or fall followed immediately by altered [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness), confusion or a gap in [human memory](../../../neuroscience.md#human-memory). Ask about the amount and timing of the drink, the mechanism of injury and any interval of apparent recovery. A person may have fallen because of [alcohol intoxication](../../../biology.md#alcohol-intoxication), and subsequently have suffered a [head injury](../../../neurology.md#head-injury). Conversely, an injured person may be mistakenly described as drunk because of slurred speech and an unsteady gait.

Examine the level of [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness), orientation, speech, [human memory](../../../neuroscience.md#human-memory) and ability to follow commands. In [alcohol intoxication](../../../biology.md#alcohol-intoxication), disinhibition, slurred speech, incoordination, [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting) and increasing drowsiness can occur; severe poisoning can progress to [coma](../../../geometrical-optics.md#coma-optics) and impaired [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). After [concussion](../../../neurology.md#concussion), confusion, [amnesia](../../../psychology.md#amnesia), [headache](../../../neurology.md#headache), dizziness, [nausea](../../../biology.md#nausea) and [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting) may occur, with or without an observed loss of [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness). Thus [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting) or drowsiness alone does not decide the diagnosis.[Symptoms of alcohol poisoning](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#alcohol-poisoning-by-the-national-health-service)[Symptoms and warning signs after a head injury](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#head-injury-and-concussion-by-the-national-health-service)

Look for a scalp wound, swelling or other evidence of impact, and examine the [pupils](../../../anatomy.md#pupil), their reaction to light, limb movements and any focal [neurological deficit](../../../neurology.md#neurological-deficit). Unequal [pupils](../../../anatomy.md#pupil), one-sided weakness, a worsening level of [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness) or a [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure) should prompt concern for significant [traumatic brain injury](../../../neurology.md#traumatic-brain-injury), rather than be dismissed as ordinary drunkenness or uncomplicated [concussion](../../../neurology.md#concussion). The absence of an external wound does not exclude an internal injury. Nor are a flushed face, a particular [pulse](../../../wave.md#pulse) or the smell of drink sufficiently specific to settle the question.

The defensible diagnostic conclusion may initially be uncertainty requiring observation and investigation. Current guidance specifically requires important [traumatic brain injury](../../../neurology.md#traumatic-brain-injury) to be excluded before depressed [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness) is attributed to intoxication. Modern [computed tomography](../../../inverse-problem.md#computed-tomography) and measurement of [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar) can resolve problems that an 1864 bedside examination could not; these are later diagnostic aids, not equipment to attribute to the examination's period.[Recommendations 1.4.1–1.4.2: initial assessment and intoxication](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#head-injury-assessment-and-early-management-by-nice)

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

How would you proceed for the purpose of testing the contents of the stomach of a person suspected of having been poisoned by Arsenic ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**Keep an untouched portion, use clean apparatus and reagent blanks, separate the suspected [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic) from the organic material, and confirm it by independent chemical reactions.** A coloured deposit alone would be inadequate evidence.

Record the container, seal and condition of the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach) contents before opening. Examine and describe the material, including any visible particles, without assuming that a white powder is [arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide). Retain an unaltered portion for a repeat examination. Because a solid poison may settle, examine representative liquid and solid portions separately rather than testing only the clear upper liquid. Label each portion and each subsequent extract so that a positive reaction can be traced back to its source.

A practicable historical preliminary separation is the [Reinsch test](../../../biology.md#reinsch-test). Warm a portion with [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid) and a clean strip of [copper](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper); a dark coating suggests deposition of a substance requiring further identification. Carry out a parallel blank with the same [copper](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper), [chemical acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry), water and apparatus. A coating may be caused by substances other than [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic), and contaminated [copper](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper) or reagents can create a false accusation. Wash and dry a coated strip, then heat it in a small tube: an arsenical deposit can give a sublimate of [arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide), whose crystals can be examined with a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope). The deposit and sublimate, rather than their mere colour, are the material for confirmation.[1844, pp.3–5: copper separation, sublimation, reagent purity and corroborating tests](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#on-arsenic-as-a-poison-its-tests-and-antidote-by-e-j-shearman)

On a separate suitably prepared portion, the [Marsh test](../../../biology.md#marsh-test) provides another mode of separation. The original [James Marsh](../../../chemistry.md#james-marsh) apparatus collected a hydrogen mixture for deposition tests; a heated-tube implementation separates collection from the subsequent heating zone. [Zinc](../../../chemical-substance.md#zinc) and dilute [chemical acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-chemistry) generate [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen); [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic) in a suitable chemical form is converted to volatile [arsine](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsine), and heating the gas in a narrow tube can produce a metallic mirror. First run the generator with the actual reagents alone, until their freedom from [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic) has been demonstrated. Keep the sample out of the gas outlet, and distinguish chemical deposition from mechanical carry-over. The apparatus separates a volatile derivative from much of the organic matrix, which explains its analytical value. [Arsine](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsine) is extremely toxic: this is a description of historical analytical principles, not a procedure for an uncontained demonstration.[Original gas-generating and deposition apparatus](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#separation-of-arsenic-by-james-marsh)

A mirror is not uniquely arsenical: [antimony](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony) can form [stibine](../../../chemical-substance.md#stibine) and give a misleading deposit. Recover or oxidize the material and use additional reactions, such as formation of yellow [arsenic trisulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trisulfide) under appropriate acidic conditions, together with the characteristic oxide sublimate. Separate aliquots, suitable controls and agreement between chemically distinct tests are more persuasive than repeatedly observing the same ambiguous stain. Organic material must be removed or treated by a controlled preparation that neither introduces nor loses [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic); indiscriminate charring or heating can invalidate the examination.

Finally distinguish detection from interpretation. A demonstrated arsenical compound supports exposure, but the amount, distribution and circumstances determine its significance. A negative test on the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach) contents alone does not exclude poisoning after [absorption](../../../radiometry.md#absorption-electromagnetic-radiation) or expulsion of the poison. Further organs or excretions could be examined if available, but no positive finding in such material is assumed here.

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

What are the symptoms of a poisonous dose of Belladonna and of Hydrocyanic Acid ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Poisoning by [belladonna](../../../pharmacology.md#belladonna) characteristically combines delirium with dry secretions and dilated [pupils](../../../anatomy.md#pupil); poisoning by hydrocyanic acid can cause very rapid collapse with failure of [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation).** The two patterns are distinctive descriptions, not infallible diagnoses from one symptom.

With [belladonna](../../../pharmacology.md#belladonna), expect dryness of the mouth and throat, difficulty swallowing, blurred vision and [mydriasis](../../../anatomy.md#mydriasis). The skin may become hot, dry and flushed; the [pulse](../../../wave.md#pulse) is often rapid. Restlessness, confusion, visual [hallucinations](../../../psychology.md#hallucination) and agitated [delirium](../../../neurology.md#delirium) may be prominent: the person may talk incoherently or pick at imaginary objects. Reduced intestinal activity and [urinary retention](../../../biology.md#urinary-retention) can accompany the dryness. Severe poisoning can proceed to [seizures](../../../neurology.md#seizure), depressed [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness), [coma](../../../geometrical-optics.md#coma-optics) and circulatory failure. This is now understood chiefly as an [antimuscarinic poisoning](../../../biology.md#antimuscarinic-poisoning) syndrome produced by alkaloids including [atropine](../../../pharmacology.md#atropine), which oppose the action of [acetylcholine](../../../neuron.md#acetylcholine) at [muscarinic receptors](../../../protein.md#muscarinic-acetylcholine-receptor).[Background; characteristic central and peripheral signs; severity table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#anticholinergic-syndrome-by-the-royal-children’s-hospital-melbourne)

Hydrocyanic acid is [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide), historically also called prussic acid. Depending on the route and amount, early complaints may include dizziness, [headache](../../../neurology.md#headache), anxiety, [nausea](../../../biology.md#nausea), weakness and a sense of suffocation. Rapid [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) may give way to slow or failing [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation), followed by [seizures](../../../neurology.md#seizure), [coma](../../../geometrical-optics.md#coma-optics), circulatory collapse and death. A sufficiently large exposure may be so rapid that an elaborate sequence of preliminary symptoms is not observed. There is no single obligatory interval from exposure to collapse.[How cyanide works; signs and symptoms; severity and route of exposure](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#cyanide-chemical-fact-sheet-by-cdc)

In modern terms, [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) impairs cellular use of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), including inhibition of [cytochrome c oxidase](../../../biochemistry.md#cytochrome-c-oxidase) in [cellular respiration](../../../metabolism.md#cellular-respiration). An apparently oxygenated colour therefore cannot exclude serious poisoning. A bitter-almond odour is an unreliable clue: it may be absent or not perceived, and no examiner should intentionally smell a suspect sample. The historical term “narcotic poison” does not mean that either [belladonna](../../../pharmacology.md#belladonna) or [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) acts like an [opioid](../../../pharmacology.md#opioid). The receptor and cellular explanations above are present scientific interpretations, rather than molecular explanations available in 1864.[Signs/symptoms and chemical identification: odour is an inadequate warning](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#hydrogen-cyanide-emergency-response-information-by-niosh)

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

What are the symptoms of poisoning by Oxalic Acid, and what the post mortem appearances ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**[Oxalic acid poisoning](../../../biology.md#oxalic-acid-poisoning) may produce corrosive gastrointestinal injury, early circulatory collapse and later [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney) injury; no single post-mortem appearance proves the diagnosis.** The severity of local injury and systemic poisoning need not be identical in every case.

After ingestion, burning pain in the mouth, throat or upper abdomen may be followed by [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting), sometimes containing [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), abdominal tenderness and [diarrhoea](../../../biology.md#diarrhoea). Weakness, a small rapid [pulse](../../../wave.md#pulse), cold clammy skin and collapse may develop. A dilute preparation may produce less obvious surface injury than concentrated [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid), while still giving systemic poisoning. Current chemical safety information recognizes both corrosive effects and disturbance of [calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium) balance.[Ingestion hazards and effects of short-term exposure](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#oxalic-acid-international-chemical-safety-card-0529)

The absorbed [oxalate](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalate) can bind [calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium) and form [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate). A fall in ionized [calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium) can contribute to neuromuscular disturbance and cardiac instability, but [hypocalcaemia](../../../biology.md#hypocalcaemia) must not be asserted to occur in every case or inferred from a symptom alone. If the person survives the early illness, [acute kidney injury](../../../biology.md#acute-kidney-injury) can follow. In modern pathological investigations, [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate) crystals in renal tubules and associated tubular damage provide a mechanism for this later complication.[1967, five patients: plasma oxalate, calcium and tissue deposits](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#plasma-oxalic-acid-and-calcium-levels-in-oxalate-poisoning)[Clinical course and renal biopsy: tubular injury with oxalate crystals](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#acute-renal-failure-following-oxalic-acid-poisoning-a-case-report)

At [autopsy](../../../biology.md#autopsy), examine the mouth, [oesophagus](../../../anatomy.md#oesophagus) and [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach) for corrosive changes. Depending on concentration and survival, the [mucous membrane](../../../anatomy.md#mucous-membrane) may be pale or discoloured, inflamed, eroded, haemorrhagic or softened; blood-stained contents and severe gastric damage may be present. Perforation is possible, but is not a necessary finding. The pre-examination report [Case of Criminal Poisoning with Oxalic Acid, in Which Perforation of the Stomach Took Place by Henry Duncan Littlejohn](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#case-of-criminal-poisoning-with-oxalic-acid-in-which-perforation-of-the-stomach-took-place-by-henry-duncan-littlejohn) documents a particular severe example with marked softening and perforation. It is evidence that these changes can occur, rather than a description that every fatal case must reproduce.[1861, pp.13–20: examination of the damaged stomach](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#case-of-criminal-poisoning-with-oxalic-acid-in-which-perforation-of-the-stomach-took-place-by-henry-duncan-littlejohn)

Examine the [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney) as well as the gastrointestinal tract; histological tubular changes and crystals may matter especially after delayed death. Nevertheless, [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate) deposition is not unique to direct ingestion of [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid), and gross corrosive damage is not unique to this poison. Preserve material for controlled [toxicological analysis](../../../biology.md#toxicological-analysis), and interpret the chemical findings alongside the history and pathological distribution. The older classification of [oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) as a “narcotic” does not adequately explain the corrosive, electrolyte and renal mechanisms now recognized.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

What evidence would satisfy you that an infant found dead had not breathed ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**An undecomposed infant with uniformly unaerated [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) provides evidence supporting absence of breathing, but a negative [lung flotation test](../../../biology.md#lung-flotation-test) cannot establish with certainty that no breath was ever taken.** The strength of the conclusion depends on the whole investigation.

First establish the circumstances of discovery, apparent maturity, preservation and any manipulation of the body. Reliable observations during and immediately after delivery would be particularly valuable, but none are supplied here and none should be invented. Record whether [artificial respiration](../../../physiology.md#artificial-respiration) was attempted and whether [putrefaction](../../../biology.md#putrefaction) has begun. Both can affect the interpretation of gas in the [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung).

At examination, lungs that remain small, dense and unexpanded, rather than aerated and expanded, support the hypothesis of no established [pulmonary ventilation](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). In the historical hydrostatic procedure, test the [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) in water, followed by individual lobes and representative pieces; record differences between regions. Uniform sinking in a fresh, unmanipulated body is more consistent with lack of aeration than a mixed result. Inspection and section of the tissue help distinguish an apparently unexpanded lung from one containing localized gas. This is a method of assembling evidence, not a claim that any particular infant in the question has those findings.

The logical limitation is essential. Breathing can have been brief or ineffective, and diseased or incompletely expanded [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) may sink despite prior life and breathing. A modern prospective [autopsy](../../../biology.md#autopsy) study recorded four false-negative flotation results among 208 examined newborns: the [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) sank despite evidence of prior life. That observation disproves an absolute rule equating sinking with never having breathed; it is not a universal accuracy estimate for all forensic populations.[Results: four false-negative cases among 208 tests](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#is-the-lung-floating-test-a-valuable-tool-or-obsolete-a-prospective-autopsy-study)

Conversely, floating tissue need not prove spontaneous breathing: [putrefaction](../../../biology.md#putrefaction) or introduced air during [artificial respiration](../../../physiology.md#artificial-respiration) may produce gas. Historical case-record research also documents why investigators combined the flotation result with the other findings and circumstances.[Discussion of the hydrostatic test, decomposition and artificial respiration](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#forensic-examinations-of-newborns-1910-1912)

Thus several concordant observations can justify the cautious opinion that no effective breathing has been demonstrated and that absence of breathing is the best-supported explanation. They do not justify certainty from a single negative test. Finally, the absence of breathing and the absence of every sign of [life](../../../biology.md#life) are different propositions: an assessment of [live birth](../../../reproduction.md#live-birth) cannot simply be replaced by a test of lung buoyancy.

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

What are the several ways by which a room, or a ward, may be ventilated ?

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**The several methods are natural ventilation by wind or buoyancy, mechanical supply, mechanical extraction, and combinations of these.** Each must provide both a route for clean outdoor [air](../../../mixture.md#air) to enter and a route for used [air](../../../mixture.md#air) to leave.

For [natural ventilation](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#natural-ventilation), opening windows on different sides permits [cross ventilation](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#cross-ventilation) when the [wind](../../../geophysics.md#wind) creates a suitable [pressure difference](../../../pressure.md#pressure-difference). Even one window can exchange [air](../../../mixture.md#air), but the amount and distribution are more dependent on conditions. Openings at different heights use the [stack effect](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#stack-effect): warmer, less dense indoor [air](../../../mixture.md#air) tends to rise and leave through an upper opening, drawing replacement [air](../../../mixture.md#air) through a lower one. A flue or ventilating shaft can extend this route. A fireplace may assist extraction through its chimney, provided replacement [air](../../../mixture.md#air) enters and combustion products are carried safely away.

Permanent inlets, adjustable window openings and outlet shafts can make these principles more controllable. Their positions should distribute fresh [air](../../../mixture.md#air) through the occupied part of the ward instead of allowing it to pass directly from inlet to outlet. An open door into an adjoining foul space is not necessarily a source of clean [air](../../../mixture.md#air). [Florence Nightingale](../../../biology.md#florence-nightingale)'s pre-1864 discussions emphasize outside [air](../../../mixture.md#air), openings and the relation between ventilation and warmth; admitting fresh [air](../../../mixture.md#air) need not mean exposing a patient to an avoidable cold draught.[Ventilation and warming](../../../biology-bibliography.md#notes-on-nursing-by-florence-nightingale)[Third edition, 1863, discussion of ward ventilation and window arrangements](../../../biology-bibliography.md#notes-on-hospitals-by-florence-nightingale)

With mechanical extraction, a fan or other air-moving apparatus removes indoor [air](../../../mixture.md#air), while planned inlets admit replacement [air](../../../mixture.md#air). With mechanical supply, the apparatus introduces outdoor [air](../../../mixture.md#air), and outlets allow displacement of the used [air](../../../mixture.md#air). A balanced installation provides both mechanically. Natural openings can also be combined with mechanical assistance. Heating the incoming [air](../../../mixture.md#air) can reduce chilling without making it necessary to recirculate all the used [air](../../../mixture.md#air).

What matters is the actual [airflow](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#airflow) through the room, including its direction. Outlet flow can be investigated with an [anemometer](../../../geophysics.md#anemometer); short-circuiting between nearby openings and stagnant regions must be considered. Moving the same indoor [air](../../../mixture.md#air) around does not by itself supply fresh [air](../../../mixture.md#air). Nor does a perfume remove a source of contamination. Source removal, sufficient exchange and reasonable temperature control belong together; windows or fans are means to those ends, not proof that they have been achieved.

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

What impurities have been detected in the air of sick rooms ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**Sick-room [air](../../../mixture.md#air) may contain excess [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor), volatile products from excreta and soiled materials, suspended dust, and infectious [aerosols](../../../chemistry.md#aerosol); defective drains or combustion can add further contaminants.** Their sources and evidential significance differ.

[Breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) adds [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor); washing, damp bedding and evaporation add further moisture. [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) and other volatile products may arise from excreta, soiled linen or decomposing material. Suspended dust can include textile fibres, skin particles and other organic matter. These are plausible objects of chemical or microscopic examination, rather than a single substance called “hospital poison”.

The sources need to be stated conditionally. Defective drainage can introduce sewer gases, including [hydrogen sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-sulfide); fires, smoky stoves or gas lighting can introduce combustion products such as [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide), smoke and, with appropriate fuel, [sulfur dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur-dioxide). Such gases are not inevitable constituents of every sick-room atmosphere. [Carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) is especially important because smell is not a reliable warning of its presence.[Sources and the odourless character of carbon monoxide](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#carbon-monoxide-poisoning-basics-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)[Sources and exposure effects](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#hydrogen-sulfide-by-the-national-institute-for-occupational-safety-and-health)

There were already quantitative methods for some constituents in 1864. A measured volume of [air](../../../mixture.md#air) could be brought into contact with a known alkaline absorbent and the absorbed [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) determined; [Chemical News volume 9 (1864)](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#chemical-news-volume-9-1864) describes baryta-based air analysis. Humidity can be investigated with a [psychrometer](../../../physiology.md#psychrometer), and collected dust examined microscopically. Detection of [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) requires suitable chemical controls, rather than identification by a disagreeable smell alone.[p.80: measured air volumes and baryta in carbon-dioxide determination](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#chemical-news-volume-9-1864)

Present science further distinguishes infectious particles from nonspecific dust and gases. Some patients release pathogen-bearing [aerosols](../../../chemistry.md#aerosol), but their presence and infectivity cannot be established merely from a high [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) measurement, an odour or an unidentified microscopic particle. Modern environmental microbiology also requires sampling methods appropriate to the organism and a clear purpose; routine indiscriminate sampling is not a universal test of ward safety. This later understanding should be kept separate from the varied nineteenth-century theories of miasma.[Air sampling: indications, methods and interpretation limitations](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#environmental-sampling-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

How is Typhoid Fever said to be propagated ?

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**The important transmission chain is from an infected person's intestinal discharges to another person's mouth, especially through contaminated [drinking water](../../../biology.md#drinking-water), food or hands.** In the terminology of 1864, the specific infectious material was associated with the bowel discharges of [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever).

[William Budd](../../../physician.md#william-budd)'s 1861 argument makes this a disease propagated from preceding cases. Excreta enter a privy, drain or sewer; contamination can then reach a water supply, food or domestic surroundings and expose another person. Soiled linen and attendants' hands can also carry faecal material into the ingestion pathway. A shared polluted supply can therefore distribute infection to people who have never met the original patient. Removing and safely disposing of the discharges, protecting the supply and preventing transfer to food interrupt successive parts of this chain.[Part III, 30 November 1861, p.575: intestinal discharges, sewers and specific propagation](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#observations-on-typhoid-or-intestinal-fever-the-pythogenic-theory-by-william-budd)

This contemporary explanation should be distinguished from the competing suggestion that decomposing organic matter spontaneously generates [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever). A sewer can transmit infection because it receives infected excreta; its smell is not itself proof of the causal material or of a primary inhalation route. The question's historical wording reflects a period of dispute, rather than an obligation to present all competing beliefs as equally correct.

The present explanation identifies [Salmonella Typhi](../../../organism.md#salmonella-typhi) and [faecal–oral transmission](../../../biology.md#faecal-oral-transmission). People who are ill, convalescent or asymptomatic carriers can shed the organism; contaminated food and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) convey it to another person. This specific bacteriological account is later knowledge, corroborating the route without attributing modern identification methods to 1864. [Typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) must also be distinguished from [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus), which has a different transmission history and biology.[Causes and spread: human sources, carriers, food and water](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#about-typhoid-fever-and-paratyphoid-fever-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

Under what conditions is drinking water usually contaminated by Lead?

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**Contamination requires a lead-bearing source and conditions that dissolve it or detach lead-containing particles. Soft, poorly buffered [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) standing in contact with [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead) is a classical risk, but neither hardness nor clarity guarantees safety.**

The source may be a pipe, cistern, service connection or fitting. [Robert Christison](../../../physician.md#robert-christison)'s pre-examination experiments addressed the action of different waters on metallic [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead): very pure waters, including [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water) or [rainwater](../../../chemical-substance.md#rainwater), could attack it, whereas salts in many natural waters modified the process. This provides a contemporary basis for treating the character of the supply, as well as the material of the cistern or pipe, as important.[1844, pp.265–276: pure waters, natural waters and the effects of dissolved salts](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#on-the-action-of-water-upon-lead-by-robert-christison)

The dissolving tendency is called [plumbosolvency](../../../biology.md#plumbosolvency). In modern chemical terms, low [pH](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph) and low [alkalinity](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#alkalinity) can favour the release of [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead). [Dissolved oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#dissolved-oxygen) supports corrosion of the metal; dissolved [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) affects both acidity and carbonate chemistry. [Carbonate](../../../chemistry.md#carbonate) deposits can provide a partly protective surface layer, while conditions favouring their dissolution can increase release. It is therefore misleading to say simply that all [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) prevents contamination or that all waters containing it dissolve [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead) to the same degree. [Water hardness](../../../biology.md#water-hardness) and [alkalinity](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#alkalinity) are related features of many waters but are not interchangeable quantities.[Lead solubility, pH, alkalinity and carbonate deposits](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#pipe-and-fittings-corrosion-by-the-drinking-water-inspectorate)

Long contact, especially stagnation overnight or through disuse, can increase contamination. Warmer conditions, changes in the supply chemistry, disturbance of an old pipe and the condition of accumulated deposits can also matter. Water can carry dissolved [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead) or particles containing [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead); an old apparently coated pipe is not an assurance that release has ceased. Modern supplies may additionally encounter lead-bearing solder or brass, which should be recognized as a present comparison rather than assumed to be the construction of a particular 1864 installation.[Factors affecting lead concentration; lead pipes and fittings; standing water and supply condition](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#lead-in-drinking-water-by-the-drinking-water-inspectorate)

A clear appearance and acceptable taste cannot exclude [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead). Investigate the actual plumbing materials and analyse appropriately collected samples, recording the period of standing and other conditions instead of inventing a concentration. The most direct way to remove the source is replacement of lead-bearing components; treatment that controls [corrosion](../../../engineering.md#corrosion) is chemistry-dependent and is not equivalent to a universal guarantee from a hard-water scale. These considerations answer when contamination is likely without imposing an unsupported period numerical threshold.

## 10

↑ **Parent:** [Forensic medicine and hygiene](forensic-medicine-and-hygiene.md)

**Original question.**

Can you state any diseases which make butchers' meat wholly unfit for human food ?

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**[Anthrax](../../../biology.md#anthrax), generalized suppurative or septic disease, and generalized [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis) are clear examples of conditions requiring rejection of the whole carcass; extensive parasitic disease supplies further examples.** The reason for [whole-carcass condemnation](../../../biology.md#whole-carcass-condemnation) is systemic disease or distribution too extensive to be dealt with as an isolated local lesion.

With [anthrax](../../../biology.md#anthrax), infection is not a harmless change confined to one removable piece of meat. Infected animal products also pose risks during handling, and eating infected raw or undercooked meat can cause gastrointestinal [anthrax](../../../biology.md#anthrax). The appropriate conclusion is exclusion from the food supply, not a promise that ordinary preparation will make a suspect carcass acceptable.[Exposure through infected animal products and contaminated meat](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#about-anthrax-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)

Generalized [pyemia](../../../biology.md#pyemia) or [septicaemia](../../../biology.md#septicaemia), with disseminated infection, multiple suppurative foci or systemic deterioration, likewise makes trimming one visible lesion an inadequate response. Generalized [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis), particularly with widespread lesions or associated wasting, differs from an isolated local abnormality. For a clearly identified modern comparison, current United States meat-inspection rules require condemnation for generalized [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis) and for disease presenting a food-poisoning risk, expressly including [septicaemia](../../../biology.md#septicaemia) and [pyemia](../../../biology.md#pyemia). Those are current comparative criteria, not rules to attribute to English law in 1864.[§311.2(a), generalized tuberculosis; §311.16, systemic disease and food-poisoning risk](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#current-united-states-meat-inspection-regulations-9-cfr-part-311)

Parasitic meat was already a concrete contemporary concern. [On Poisoning by Diseased Pork by Julius Althaus](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#on-poisoning-by-diseased-pork-by-julius-althaus), published in 1864, discusses [trichinellosis](../../../biology.md#trichinellosis) from infected pork. [Trichinella](../../../ecology.md#trichinella) larvae in muscle can be missed by casual naked-eye inspection; the historical importance of the [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope) is that apparently ordinary flesh can contain parasites. Widespread infestation cannot be assumed to be confined to an easily discarded patch. Cysticerci in meat are another foodborne parasitic hazard, but exact inspection and disposition depend on the organism and distribution, and should not be invented as a universal 1864 statutory rule.[1864, opening discussion of trichinae in pork and microscopic investigation](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#on-poisoning-by-diseased-pork-by-julius-althaus)[Life cycle: ingestion of larvae in infected meat](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-trichinellosis)

A useful biological distinction is that eating viable cysticerci in beef or pork can cause intestinal [taeniasis](../../../biology.md#taeniasis); human [cysticercosis](../../../biology.md#cysticercosis) follows ingestion of [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium) eggs. These are different routes and disease stages, so one should not describe cysticercosis as simply the direct result of eating pork containing cysticerci.[Life cycle: cysticerci in meat produce adult intestinal tapeworms](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-taeniasis)[Life cycle: ingestion of eggs and tissue infection](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-cysticercosis)

The contemporary English legal framework is independently verifiable. Section 2 of the [Nuisances Removal Act for England (Amendment) Act 1863](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#nuisances-removal-act-for-england-amendment-act-1863) empowered inspection and seizure of food appearing diseased, unsound, unwholesome or unfit, followed by a justice's disposal order where unfitness was established. It did not supply a disease-by-disease table equating every local lesion with whole-carcass rejection. Thus the answer gives diseases and the medical reason for excluding the whole animal, while keeping localized lesions, generalized disease and legal procedure distinct.[26 & 27 Vict. c.117, §2; enacted 28 July 1863](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#nuisances-removal-act-for-england-amendment-act-1863)
