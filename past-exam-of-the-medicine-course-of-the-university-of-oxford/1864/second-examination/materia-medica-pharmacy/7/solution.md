↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

**[Decoction](../../../../../decoction.md) suits material whose useful constituents are water-soluble and withstand boiling; [infusion](../../../../../infusion.md) avoids sustained boiling and can better preserve delicate or volatile constituents.** The choice depends on both the plant's structure and the chemistry of the constituent being extracted.

Hard [bark](../../../../../bark.md), [wood](../../../../../wood.md) and many kinds of [root](../../../../../root-botany.md) need comminution and more prolonged contact with hot [water](../../../../../water.md) to penetrate their tissues; a [decoction](../../../../../decoction.md) can assist extraction when the desired material is stable. Boiling is unsuitable when it drives off a needed [volatile oil](../../../../../volatile-oil.md), decomposes the active constituent, or extracts an unwanted excess of [starch](../../../../../starch.md) or other material. A hard [root](../../../../../root-botany.md) is therefore not automatically an instruction to boil. Period examples suitable for [decoction](../../../../../decoction.md) include [oak bark](../../../../../oak-bark.md), [logwood](../../../../../logwood.md) and [pareira root](../../../../../pareira-root.md); appropriate [infusions](../../../../../infusion.md) include aromatic [cloves](../../../../../clove.md) and [chamomile](../../../../../chamomile.md). Cold [infusion](../../../../../infusion.md) of [calumba](../../../../../calumba.md) avoids dissolving its [starch](../../../../../starch.md), illustrating that suitability depends on the desired selectivity of [solvent extraction](../../../../../solvent-extraction.md), not merely on whether a drug is a leaf or a root. [Relation of measures to weights, part 2, Infusa and cold calumba; corresponding British plant monographs](../../../../../the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865.md)

For an [infusion](../../../../../infusion.md), the appropriately divided drug is left in a measured solvent for a prescribed time, commonly in a covered vessel. Hot [water](../../../../../water.md) assists extraction, while a cover reduces loss of volatile matter. Cold [infusion](../../../../../infusion.md) can be chosen when heat is undesirable. The result must be strained or filtered; time, temperature, particle size and solvent quantity affect its strength. Neither method guarantees that every constituent is extracted in the same proportion.

**A [liquid extract](../../../../../liquid-extract.md) is made by extracting the specified drug with a suitable solvent, separating the liquid from the exhausted material, and bringing the extract to its prescribed concentration and volume.** [Maceration](../../../../../maceration.md) can precede [percolation](../../../../../percolation-theory-split.md); expression or washing recovers retained liquid. A percolator passes fresh solvent through a packed bed, rather than indefinitely soaking the drug in an increasingly saturated liquid. Where concentration is necessary, controlled evaporation limits damage; an alcoholic fraction can be reserved or recombined as the monograph directs. Different [liquid extracts](../../../../../liquid-extract.md) can use aqueous, alcoholic or ethereal processes. For example, the [liquid extract](../../../../../liquid-extract.md) of [male fern](../../../../../male-fern.md) is an ethereal extraction of a resinous and oily fraction, whereas the [liquid extract](../../../../../liquid-extract.md) of [opium](../../../../../opium.md) is compounded from a previously made aqueous extract. These are chemically different operations. There is no warrant for assigning all 1864 extracts one modern universal drug-to-extract ratio. [pp. 20–21 and 42–43, differing liquid extracts and strengths; p. 15, percolation](../../../../../the-prescriber-s-analysis-of-the-british-pharmacopoeia-by-john-birkbeck-nevins-1864.md)
