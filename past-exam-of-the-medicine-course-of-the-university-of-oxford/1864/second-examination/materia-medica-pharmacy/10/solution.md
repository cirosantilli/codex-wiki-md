<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

**The 1864 solid units are the [avoirdupois pound](../../../../../avoirdupois-pound.md), [avoirdupois ounce](../../../../../avoirdupois-ounce.md) and [grain](../../../../../grain-unit.md); the liquid units are the Imperial measures.** The [grain](../../../../../grain-unit.md) is unchanged, while the old apothecaries' pound and ounce are not the units of the new formulary. [pp. 16–17, change of weights and unchanged liquid measures](../../../../../the-prescriber-s-analysis-of-the-british-pharmacopoeia-by-john-birkbeck-nevins-1864.md)

The relations for solids are

$$
1\,\mathrm{lb}=16\,\mathrm{oz}=7000\,\mathrm{gr},\qquad
1\,\mathrm{oz}=437.5\,\mathrm{gr}.
$$

Their approximate French metric equivalents are:

- one [grain](../../../../../grain-unit.md): $\boxed{0.0648\,\mathrm{g}}$, about $64.8\,\mathrm{mg}$;
- one [avoirdupois ounce](../../../../../avoirdupois-ounce.md): $\boxed{28.35\,\mathrm{g}}$;
- one [avoirdupois pound](../../../../../avoirdupois-pound.md): $\boxed{453.59\,\mathrm{g}}$.

For liquids,

$$
1\,\mathrm{gal}=8\,\mathrm{pt},\qquad
1\,\mathrm{pt}=20\,\mathrm{fl\,oz},\qquad
1\,\mathrm{fl\,oz}=8\,\mathrm{fl\,dr},\qquad
1\,\mathrm{fl\,dr}=60\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

The approximate metric volumes are:

- one [minim](../../../../../minim-unit.md): $\boxed{0.0592\,\mathrm{mL}}$;
- one [fluid drachm](../../../../../fluid-drachm.md): $\boxed{3.552\,\mathrm{mL}}$;
- one [fluid ounce](../../../../../fluid-ounce.md): $\boxed{28.41\,\mathrm{mL}}$;
- one [Imperial pint](../../../../../imperial-pint.md): $\boxed{568.26\,\mathrm{mL}}$;
- one [Imperial gallon](../../../../../imperial-gallon.md): $\boxed{4.5461\,\mathrm{L}}$.

A [millilitre](../../../../../millilitre.md) is a cubic centimetre, and a [litre](../../../../../litre.md) is a cubic decimetre. These displayed equivalents are rounded; small differences from a period table's last digits reflect rounding and historical calibration conventions. A [fluid ounce](../../../../../fluid-ounce.md) is a [volume](../../../../../volume.md) and an [avoirdupois ounce](../../../../../avoirdupois-ounce.md) is a [mass](../../../../../mass.md). They cannot be interchanged for an arbitrary liquid without its [density](../../../../../density.md). Likewise, a drop is not a defined [minim](../../../../../minim-unit.md): its volume depends on the liquid and the dropper.
