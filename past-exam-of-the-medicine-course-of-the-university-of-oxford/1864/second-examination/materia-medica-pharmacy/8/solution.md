<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

**For the contemporary formulary, use boiling water for the [linseed](../../../../../linseed.md), [charcoal](../../../../../charcoal.md) and [mustard](../../../../../mustard.md) [poultices](../../../../../poultice.md), and water at 100°F for the [yeast](../../../../../yeast.md) [poultice](../../../../../poultice.md).** These are preparation temperatures, not temperatures at which boiling material is placed on [skin](../../../../../skin.md).

For [Cataplasma Lini](../../../../../cataplasma-lini.md), hot [water](../../../../../water.md) hydrates the ground [linseed](../../../../../linseed.md) into a cohesive moist mass; the contemporary account also notes the addition of [olive oil](../../../../../olive-oil.md) when oil has been removed from the meal. For [Cataplasma Carbonis](../../../../../cataplasma-carbonis.md), the hot bread-and-[linseed](../../../../../linseed.md) basis is made with boiling [water](../../../../../water.md), then the [charcoal](../../../../../charcoal.md) is incorporated and part used at the surface. At ordinary atmospheric pressure the boiling-water preparation temperature is approximately $\boxed{212\,{}^\circ\mathrm{F}=100\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}}$. [Relation of measures to weights of the British Pharmacopoeia, Cataplasmata; Carbo Ligni, charcoal cataplasm](../../../../../the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865.md)

For [Cataplasma Fermenti](../../../../../cataplasma-fermenti.md), the stated $\boxed{100\,{}^\circ\mathrm{F}\approx37.8\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}}$ permits the [yeast](../../../../../yeast.md) to ferment the flour mixture. Boiling would injure its fermenting activity. The flour, [yeast](../../../../../yeast.md) and warm [water](../../../../../water.md) are mixed and kept warm while the mass rises. This is a historical compounding process, not evidence that a fermented dressing treats every [wound](../../../../../wound.md). [Cataplasmata, exception for Cataplasma Fermenti](../../../../../the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865.md)

The [mustard](../../../../../mustard.md) entry requires a historical qualification. The contemporary British recipe reproduced by the text directs powdered [mustard](../../../../../mustard.md) and [linseed](../../../../../linseed.md) to be mixed, then stirred into boiling [water](../../../../../water.md); the same text observes that excessive heat impairs development of the pungent [volatile oil](../../../../../volatile-oil.md). Thus **“boiling” is the contemporary formula's instruction, while lukewarm preparation better preserves development of mustard's pungent [volatile oil](../../../../../volatile-oil.md)**. These are different claims. The later practical preference for lukewarm [water](../../../../../water.md) should not silently replace the 1864 recipe, nor should that recipe be mistaken for optimal modern biochemical practice. [Cruciferae or Brassicaceae, Cataplasma Sinapis and effect of excessive heat](../../../../../the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865.md)
