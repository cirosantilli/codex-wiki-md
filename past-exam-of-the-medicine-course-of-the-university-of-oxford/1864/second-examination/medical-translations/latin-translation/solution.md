<h1 id="latin-translation/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Latin translation](../latin-translation.md)

There was a very distinguished knight, sixty-five years old, but with sturdy limbs and a sound balance of the [humoral fluids](../../../../../humoral-fluid.md), except that some years earlier he had suffered from long-lasting, obstinate [ulcers](../../../../../ulcer.md) of the legs. Weary of these, he had at last completely healed them by remedies both internal and external. More recently he had occasionally been subject to rheumatic [pains](../../../../../pain.md), but [pains](../../../../../pain.md) so slight that they did not prevent him from leaving home, as he had indeed done on the day before his death. On 1 June these seized him more severely, at the [sternum](../../../../../sternum.md) and in the arms, accompanied by disturbance of the head. After they had been treated with the measures judged most suitable, he was already largely relieved by about midday and behaved cheerfully, although his [pulse](../../../../../pulse.md) was weak.

After resting following dinner, he awoke and felt the same [pains](../../../../../pain.md) as in the morning. He therefore began walking about his room, for in this way he bore them more easily. But after doing this sufficiently, he returned to bed and complained of vapours rising to his head and of tightness in his chest; in his restlessness he turned this way and that. After doing so for a considerable time, he suddenly felt that he was dying and, at the same moment, grew pale, struggled, and died immediately.

When the abdomen was opened, everything was found in its natural condition. But when the chest was opened and the [lungs](../../../../../lung.md), which had been pushed forwards, were moved aside, the [pericardium](../../../../../pericardium.md) was found distended with dark, clotted [blood](../../../../../blood.md). This had escaped from the left [cardiac ventricle](../../../../../cardiac-ventricle.md) through a tear half an inch long, running in the direction of its length. Around it the fibres of the [heart](../../../../../heart.md) were visibly eaten away by an erosion that was not recent.

**The anatomical finding is rupture of the left ventricular wall into the [pericardium](../../../../../pericardium.md).** This is [Morgagni](../../../../../giovanni-battista-morgagni.md)'s account in [The Seats and Causes of Diseases Investigated by Anatomy by Giovanni Battista Morgagni](../../../../../the-seats-and-causes-of-diseases-investigated-by-anatomy-by-giovanni-battista-morgagni.md), Book II, Letter XXVII, article 8. The language of balanced [humoral fluids](../../../../../humoral-fluid.md), rheumatic [pain](../../../../../pain.md), rising vapours and erosion belongs to the account's historical descriptive framework. It does not establish a modern rheumatological diagnosis or a microscopic cause of rupture. The Roman date is the fifth day before the Nones of June, counted inclusively: 1 June. The translation retains the historical half-inch measure without assigning an unjustified modern measurement to the specimen.
