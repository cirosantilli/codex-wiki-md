<h1 id="greek-aphorism-translation/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Greek aphorism translation](../greek-aphorism-translation.md)

**“For the remedies will be found in [food](../../../../../food.md).”** More literally, the sentence says that the remedies “will lie in nourishment”. The connective “for” is retained, and the future tense expresses where the therapeutic resource is to be sought. The statement allows [diet](../../../../../diet.md) to act as treatment; it does not say that every illness can be cured by ordinary eating.

The attribution is to [Aretaeus of Cappadocia](../../../../../aretaeus-of-cappadocia.md). The sentence also occurs in the dietary discussion of [pleurisy](../../../../../pleurisy.md) in [On the Treatment of Acute Diseases by Aretaeus of Cappadocia](../../../../../on-the-treatment-of-acute-diseases-by-aretaeus-of-cappadocia.md), I.10. Thus the printed “Ib.” need not place this separate aphorism inside the preceding chapter on [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md). The wider dietary recommendations there belong to ancient medicine; the short maxim can be illustrated without endorsing all of them.
