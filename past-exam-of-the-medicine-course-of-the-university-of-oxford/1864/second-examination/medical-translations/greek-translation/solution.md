The forms of [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md) are innumerable in their varieties, but there is only one general kind: taken as a whole, it is a lasting derangement of the mind without [fever](../../../../../fever.md). For if [fever](../../../../../fever.md) should at some time also occur, it would not arise as a feature peculiar to the [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md), but from some other coinciding cause. Thus [wine](../../../../../wine.md) also inflames the mind into delirious behaviour in [drunkenness](../../../../../alcohol-intoxication.md), and some things that are eaten, such as [mandrake](../../../../../mandrake.md) and [henbane](../../../../../henbane.md), drive people out of their minds. Yet these conditions are not called [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md): arising from a temporary cause, they settle more quickly, whereas [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md) has persistence.

The [senile dotage](../../../../../senile-dotage.md) that is a misfortune of old age is quite unlike this [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md). It is a torpor of sensation and a numbing of judgement and intellect through coldness; [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md), by contrast, has something hot and dry in its cause and is turbulent in its actions. For [senile dotage](../../../../../senile-dotage.md) beginning in old age neither remits nor leaves the sufferer before death, whereas [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md) does remit and, with care, can come to a complete end.

But a remission is incomplete when it occurs in keeping with the course of the [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md), without the illness having been properly cured by treatment or by the mildness of the season. For in some people who seemed to be unharmed, the spring season, an error in their way of life, or anger arising from some incident has brought the condition back.

**[Aretaeus of Cappadocia](../../../../../aretaeus-of-cappadocia.md) distinguishes persistent mental illness, transient intoxication and the cognitive decline attributed to old age.** The opening of [On the Causes and Signs of Chronic Diseases by Aretaeus of Cappadocia](../../../../../on-the-causes-and-signs-of-chronic-diseases-by-aretaeus-of-cappadocia.md), I.6, organizes these distinctions by persistence, attributed cause and course, ending with examples of relapse. [I.6, opening paragraph, from the classification of mania through relapse in spring](../../../../../on-the-causes-and-signs-of-chronic-diseases-by-aretaeus-of-cappadocia.md)

Several terms need historical interpretation. “Derangement” renders a displacement from one's ordinary mental state, not a diagnosis based on a modern symptom checklist. “Way of life” includes [diet](../../../../../diet.md) and regimen; it is wider than a single meal. The sentence about incomplete remission concerns an apparent improvement within the illness's course, distinguished from a thorough cure through treatment or favourable seasonal conditions. A quiet interval can therefore be followed by recurrence. The hot, dry and cold explanations are historical physiology, not established modern causes.

The scope of [ancient mania](../../../../../mania-in-ancient-greek-medicine.md) is broader than present-day [mania](../../../../../manic-episode.md). Current assessment examines the course of mood and activity changes, symptoms between episodes, medication and substance exposure, physical illness and alternative diagnoses; it does not define the disorder simply as every persistent mental disturbance without [fever](../../../../../fever.md). Nor should historical [senile dotage](../../../../../senile-dotage.md) be equated with a precise diagnosis of one modern [dementia](../../../../../dementia.md), or the passage's assertions about old age be accepted as a modern prognosis.
