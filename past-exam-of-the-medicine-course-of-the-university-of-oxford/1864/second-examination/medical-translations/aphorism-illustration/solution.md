<h1 id="aphorism-illustration/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Aphorism illustration](../aphorism-illustration.md)

**[Scurvy](../../../../../scurvy.md) supplies a clear illustration available to an 1864 candidate: an appropriate change in [diet](../../../../../diet.md) can act as a remedy.** In [A Treatise of the Scurvy by James Lind](../../../../../a-treatise-of-the-scurvy-by-james-lind.md), [James Lind](../../../../../james-lind.md) described a shipboard comparison begun on 20 May 1747. Twelve affected sailors lived in the same sick quarters and shared a common basic [diet](../../../../../diet.md), while pairs received different additions. The additions included cider, acidic preparations, sea water, a compound preparation, and fresh oranges and lemons. Thus the comparison asked whether a particular addition helped, rather than merely observing that patients ate while recovering.

The pair given fresh citrus each received two oranges and one lemon daily. Supplies lasted six days. [James Lind](../../../../../james-lind.md) reported that one was then fit for duty, while the other had recovered sufficiently to help nurse the remaining sick. The exact contrast matters: two recoveries are not proof that every sour substance is equivalent to citrus, and [James Lind](../../../../../james-lind.md)'s physiological theories should not be confused with the observed result. The record is also a small comparison with incompletely specified allocation, rather than evidence for a modern randomized design. It nevertheless supplies a concrete, pre-1864 illustration of a therapeutic resource carried in [food](../../../../../food.md). [1753, pp. 191–196, common diet, six paired treatments and the course of the citrus recipients; original pages reproduced by the James Lind Library](../../../../../a-treatise-of-the-scurvy-by-james-lind.md)

**Present explanation.** [Scurvy](../../../../../scurvy.md) is caused by deficient [vitamin C](../../../../../vitamin-c.md) intake. Humans require a dietary source of [vitamin C](../../../../../vitamin-c.md); it is needed for normal [collagen](../../../../../collagen.md) formation. Deficiency weakens [connective tissue](../../../../../connective-tissue.md) and can produce fragile small [blood vessels](../../../../../blood-vessel.md), bleeding gums and poor [wound healing](../../../../../wound-healing.md). Suitable [food](../../../../../food.md) can therefore correct the deficiency, not merely disguise its symptoms. This explanation is later knowledge: an 1864 candidate could cite the effect of fresh antiscorbutic [food](../../../../../food.md) without knowing the chemical identity of [vitamin C](../../../../../vitamin-c.md). The effective factor is not simply acidity, nor is the claim that more [vitamin C](../../../../../vitamin-c.md) cures every unrelated disease. [Introduction, Vitamin C Deficiency and Food Sources: dietary requirement, collagen, scurvy and citrus sources](../../../../../vitamin-c-fact-sheet-for-health-professionals-by-nih.md)

There is also a broader nutritional sense. In [malnutrition](../../../../../malnutrition.md), adequate [food](../../../../../food.md) supplies energy and [protein](../../../../../protein-split.md) needed for recovery, and the composition and route of nutritional support depend on the person's capacity to eat and absorb it. Current guidance distinguishes ordinary enriched [food](../../../../../food.md) and oral supplements from enteral or parenteral support when required. This extends the principle of using nourishment therapeutically, while distinguishing it from a specific cure such as correcting [vitamin C](../../../../../vitamin-c.md) deficiency. These present-day care pathways are not attributed to ancient practice or to the 1864 examiner. [CG32 §§1.3–1.4 and 1.6–1.8: indications, prescription and routes of nutrition support](../../../../../nutrition-support-for-adults-by-nice.md)

The illustration therefore supports **a specific relation between nutritional need, a suitable [food](../../../../../food.md), and a measurable clinical effect**. It gives no warrant for replacing every other form of treatment with a general dietary slogan.
