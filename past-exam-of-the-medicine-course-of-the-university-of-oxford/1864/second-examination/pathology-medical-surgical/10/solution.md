**The principal modes are growth within the uterine wall, towards the cavity, and towards the outer surface: [intramural uterine fibroids](../../../../../intramural-uterine-fibroid.md), [submucosal uterine fibroids](../../../../../submucosal-uterine-fibroid.md) and [subserosal uterine fibroids](../../../../../subserosal-uterine-fibroid.md).** “Fibrous tumour” is the historical label; the usual modern lesion is a [leiomyoma](../../../../../leiomyoma.md), a benign growth of [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md) with a variable connective-tissue component, rather than a mass of collagen alone.[Overview: muscle origin and variation in size, shape and location](../../../../../uterine-fibroids-by-the-american-college-of-obstetricians-and-gynecologists.md)

An [intramural uterine fibroid](../../../../../intramural-uterine-fibroid.md) enlarges within the [myometrium](../../../../../myometrium.md). It can remain enclosed by uterine muscle, push the cavity aside, or expand both inner and outer contours. Several nodules can give an irregularly enlarged [uterus](../../../../../uterus.md). Expansion compresses surrounding tissue into a cleavage plane or pseudocapsule; this differs from the destructive infiltrative growth typical of a malignant lesion.

A [submucosal uterine fibroid](../../../../../submucosal-uterine-fibroid.md) grows towards the [endometrium](../../../../../endometrium.md), projecting into and distorting the uterine cavity. It may remain broad-based or acquire a stalk as the surrounding wall is displaced. A [pedunculated uterine fibroid](../../../../../pedunculated-uterine-fibroid.md) can be pushed through the cervical canal and appear in the [vagina](../../../../../vagina.md) as a [uterine polyp](../../../../../uterine-polyp.md). A polyp formed in this way is a mode of presentation of the same smooth-muscle tumour, not necessarily a different histological species. Its relation to the cavity helps explain why a comparatively small lesion may be associated with substantial bleeding.

A [subserosal uterine fibroid](../../../../../subserosal-uterine-fibroid.md) projects outwards beneath the serosal covering. It may have a broad attachment or a long pedicle and become a large extrauterine-looking mass. Lateral outward growth can extend between the layers of the broad ligament; cervical growth can displace neighbouring pelvic structures. Rarely, a detached pedunculated lesion gains a blood supply from adjacent tissues and persists as a parasitic growth; this is not ordinary distant [metastasis](../../../../../metastasis.md).[Anatomical locations: intramural, submucosal, subserosal, cervical, intraligamentous and parasitic forms](../../../../../uterine-fibroids-by-merck-manual-professional-edition.md)

The three directions are not mutually exclusive: a large nodule may affect both the uterine cavity and its external surface. Modern location classifications describe its relation to [endometrium](../../../../../endometrium.md), [myometrium](../../../../../myometrium.md) and serosa more precisely, but those later type numbers are unnecessary to explain the anatomical modes requested here.

Growth is also distinct from secondary change. A poorly supplied area can undergo hyaline or cystic degeneration, haemorrhagic change or calcification; these changes alter consistency without establishing a new malignant mode of growth. [Uterine fibroids](../../../../../uterine-fibroid.md) often respond to the reproductive hormonal environment and commonly shrink after menopause, but neither every lesion's course nor its rate of growth is identical.[Etiology, histopathology and secondary changes; hormone-related growth and postmenopausal involution](../../../../../uterine-leiomyomata-in-statpearls.md) **Location and attachment describe the mode of expansion; tissue examination establishes the nature of the tumour.**
