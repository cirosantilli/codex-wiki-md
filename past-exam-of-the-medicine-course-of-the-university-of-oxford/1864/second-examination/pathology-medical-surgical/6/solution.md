<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**[Jaundice](../../../../../jaundice.md) results from accumulation of [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md); its reversibility depends on the cause, not on the yellow colour alone.** In 1864, obstruction of bile flow and disease of the [liver](../../../../../liver.md) were important practical distinctions. Modern physiology also separates excess production from impaired uptake, conjugation and excretion.

Excess breakdown of [red blood cells](../../../../../red-blood-cell.md) can overwhelm the usual handling of [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md), producing predominantly unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia. Impaired hepatic uptake or conjugation supplies another non-obstructive mechanism. Hepatocellular injury, as in [hepatitis](../../../../../hepatitis.md) or advanced [cirrhosis](../../../../../cirrhosis.md), impairs processing and secretion. Intrahepatic [cholestasis](../../../../../cholestasis.md) and extrahepatic obstruction prevent normal disposal through [bile](../../../../../bile.md). Obstruction can result from a [gallstone](../../../../../gallstone.md), a duct stricture, inflammation or compression/invasion by a [tumour](../../../../../tumour.md).[Pathophysiology and mechanisms: increased production, impaired handling and intrahepatic or extrahepatic cholestasis](../../../../../jaundice-by-merck-manual-professional-edition.md)

Conjugated [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md) can enter the [urine](../../../../../urine.md), making it dark; predominantly unconjugated [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md), which is strongly protein-bound, does not ordinarily produce bilirubinuria. Marked interruption of bile delivery can make stools pale. These findings help locate the problem but do not by themselves distinguish a stone from a stricture or [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md). Modern fractionated [bilirubin](../../../../../bilirubin.md) tests, liver enzymes and [ultrasonography](../../../../../ultrasonography.md) supplement the clinical pattern.[Urine bilirubin, liver disease and bile duct obstruction; relation to dark urine and pale stool](../../../../../bilirubin-in-urine-by-medlineplus.md)

Recovery “by nature” is possible when an obstruction is temporary, a small [gallstone](../../../../../gallstone.md) passes, a reversible inflammatory process settles, or the cause of increased pigment production ceases. An episode of jaundice can clear as [liver](../../../../../liver.md) function recovers. It is nevertheless unsafe to infer a harmless or self-limiting cause merely because the jaundice fluctuates; partial malignant obstruction can also fluctuate.

Recovery “by art,” in modern terms, includes treating a reversible underlying illness and relieving a remediable obstruction. A persistent common-duct stone can be removed endoscopically or surgically; an obstructed duct may require drainage. Selected strictures and tumours can be treated, but established extensive hepatic destruction or disseminated malignancy may prevent full recovery even if bile drainage is improved.[Recommendations on common bile duct stones and bile duct clearance](../../../../../gallstone-disease-diagnosis-and-management-by-nice.md) These are present capabilities, not a claim that modern endoscopy was available to the 1864 examiner.

The period question therefore calls for a conditional prognosis: a movable obstruction or reversible disturbance offers a better prospect than irreversible tissue destruction or an untreatable growing obstruction. **A disappearing colour is not, by itself, proof that the underlying disease has been cured.**
