**Original question.**

Describe the microscopical appearances of typical specimens of encephaloid Carcinoma, and Fibro-plastic growth.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The typical contrast is between a highly cellular, relatively scantily supported [encephaloid cancer](../../../biology.md#encephaloid-cancer) and a [fibroplastic growth](../../../biology.md#fibroplastic-growth) composed predominantly of elongated cells and developing fibrous material.** Neither historical name is a complete modern tumour classification.

In [encephaloid cancer](../../../biology.md#encephaloid-cancer), a thin preparation from a viable region shows crowded, variably sized cells, often rounded, oval, polygonal or irregular, with conspicuous [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) and a prominent [cell nucleolus](../../../biological-cell.md#nucleolus-biology). Some cells have more than one [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus); the [cytoplasm](../../../biological-cell.md#cytoplasm) and nuclear proportions vary. In sections, the cells form masses or groups separated by a comparatively delicate vascular [stroma](../../../anatomy.md#stroma). Where the lesion is a [carcinoma](../../../biology.md#carcinoma), cohesive epithelial arrangements and invasion of surrounding tissue help establish its nature. The scant support helps explain the soft, brain-like gross consistency denoted by “encephaloid”; it does not mean that the growth is made of [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) tissue.

A rapidly growing soft [tumour](../../../biology.md#tumour) may also show evidence of [mitosis](../../../cell-cycle.md#mitosis), [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), [necrosis](../../../biology.md#necrosis) and granular or fatty cellular debris. These features vary with sampling and are not the definition of every historical encephaloid lesion. The viable advancing edge is more informative about cell type and invasion than a completely broken-down centre. A modern interpretation combines architecture, cell morphology and, where needed, [immunohistochemistry](../../../anatomy.md#immunohistochemistry); one large nucleolated cell is not a universal “cancer corpuscle.”[Malignant neoplasms: cytological atypia, nuclear features, epithelial and mesenchymal classification](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#neoplasia-by-the-university-of-utah-webpath)[How cancer differs from normal cells; invasion and tumour classification](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#what-is-cancer-by-the-national-cancer-institute)

In a typical [fibroplastic growth](../../../biology.md#fibroplastic-growth), the conspicuous elements are oval or spindle-shaped cells, with elongated [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus), tapering processes and intercellular material taking on a fibrillar character. The cells may run in parallel bundles or interlacing groups. Some areas contain many apparently free oval [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus); other areas show more mature fibres and fewer obvious cell bodies. A teased preparation helps display individual elongated cells and fibres, whereas a section shows how the bundles relate to vessels and surrounding tissue.

The historical interpretation was of a growth resembling developing [connective tissue](../../../anatomy.md#connective-tissue). It should not be translated automatically into either a harmless [fibroma](../../../biology.md#fibroma) or a specific modern [fibrosarcoma](../../../biology.md#fibrosarcoma). Contemporary descriptions recorded recurrence and malignant behaviour in some growths put in this broad category.[pp. 35–37: fibres, elongated nuclei, fibroplastic cells and variable clinical behaviour](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#illustrations-of-the-pathology-of-cancer-by-john-zachariah-laurence) **Cell shape must be read with tissue architecture and behaviour.** Apparent resemblance to [fibroblasts](../../../biological-cell.md#fibroblast) does not establish benignity, and a scant-fibred soft growth is not by itself proof of epithelial origin.

**Original question.**

Describe the condition called Leucocythemia.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**[Leucocythaemia](../../../biology.md#leucocythaemia) was the historical description of a persistent excess of white corpuscles in the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), commonly accompanied by enlargement of blood-forming or lymphoid organs.** The corresponding modern disease group is [leukaemia](../../../biology.md#leukaemia), but a raised [leukocyte](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) count alone is not a sufficient modern diagnosis.

A period investigation would compare the proportions and appearances of the white and red corpuscles in a fresh blood preparation and examine the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) and [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver). Splenic enlargement could be striking, and enlarged lymphatic glands supplied another important pattern. The white material was not simply [pus](../../../biology.md#pus) circulating from an unnoticed [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess); microscopic resemblance between a [leukocyte](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) and a pus corpuscle does not establish suppuration. A persistent excess must also be distinguished from a temporary reactive rise during [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) or [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation).

In present terms, [leukaemia](../../../biology.md#leukaemia) comprises malignant disorders of blood-forming cells. Acute and chronic forms, and myeloid and lymphoid lineages, differ in the maturity and identity of their abnormal cells and in their course. [Bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) disease can interfere with normal production of [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell), [platelets](../../../physiology.md#platelet) and functioning [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte). Thus pallor, fatigue and breathlessness may reflect [anaemia](../../../biology.md#anaemia); bruising or bleeding may reflect inadequate [platelets](../../../physiology.md#platelet); recurrent [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) can occur despite a large total white count. Infiltration or enlargement of the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) and [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) can produce swelling, early satiety or abdominal discomfort.[Definition, major classifications, symptoms and organ enlargement](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#leukaemia-by-cancer-council-australia)

The useful modern examination includes a complete [blood count](../../../psychology.md#blood-count) and differential, a stained [blood film](../../../anatomy.md#blood-film), and investigation of [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) and cellular markers when indicated. The morphology and proportions of mature and immature cells matter. A count can be normal or low in some [leukaemias](../../../biology.md#leukaemia), while a high count can have a non-malignant cause.[Interpretation: a count alone cannot confirm a diagnosis; differential, film and marrow investigations](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#white-blood-count-by-medlineplus) **The historical appearance suggests a disorder of blood formation, but classification requires more than counting white cells.**

**Original question.**

Distinguish between what are termed Typhus and Typhoid Fever.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**[Typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus) and [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) are distinct infections, despite their overlapping fever, prostration and delirium.** In the British historical comparison, typhus chiefly means epidemic louse-borne [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus), not every disease now called a typhus fever.

Classical [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus) often begins relatively abruptly with severe headache, fever, prostration and a conspicuously ill mental state. Its eruption is commonly a more extensive dusky maculopapular rash on the [anatomical trunk](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-trunk) and limbs and may become petechial. Crowded conditions and infestation with body lice support the epidemiological distinction. Modern knowledge identifies [Rickettsia prowazekii](../../../organism.md#rickettsia-prowazekii) and transmission involving infected louse material. Injury to small-vessel [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) helps explain the widespread illness; it is not chiefly a local ulcerating disease of the ileal lymphoid tissue.[Transmission; clinical characteristics; rash and small-vessel injury](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-overview-of-epidemic-typhus-by-cdc)

[Typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) more often develops insidiously, with persistent fever, headache and progressive weakness, together with abdominal discomfort, distension and either constipation or [diarrhoea](../../../biology.md#diarrhoea). Sparse, transient rose spots may occur on the [anatomical trunk](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-trunk). Splenic enlargement and intestinal complications are important. The characteristic pathological process involves intestinal lymphoid tissue, especially [Peyer's patches](../../../physiology.md#peyer-s-patch) in the terminal [ileum](../../../physiology.md#ileum), with enlargement, [necrosis](../../../biology.md#necrosis) and ulceration. Severe ulcers can bleed or perforate.[Pathophysiology and histopathology: intestinal lymphoid lesions and complications](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#typhoid-fever-in-statpearls)

Modern causation separates [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever), due to [Salmonella Typhi](../../../organism.md#salmonella-typhi), from louse-borne [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus). [Typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) spreads through food or water contaminated by human excreta, including material from carriers; it is not acquired merely because an air space is crowded. A prolonged course, abdominal findings and intestinal haemorrhage favour it, whereas abrupt severe illness with a widespread dusky rash and louse exposure favour [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus).[Infectious agent; transmission; clinical presentation and complications](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#typhoid-and-paratyphoid-fever-in-the-cdc-yellow-book)

Neither the rash nor the bowel habit is invariable, and the old contrast between an approximately two-week typhus and a longer enteric illness is a tendency, not a diagnostic clock. An absent rose spot does not exclude [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever), and an absent rash does not exclude [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus). Modern confirmation uses organism-appropriate investigations, including [blood culture](../../../biology.md#blood-culture) for enteric fever and appropriate serological or molecular testing for [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus). The important historical achievement is the separation of the syndromes; the later microbiological explanation should not be projected into every 1864 diagnostic account.

**Original question.**

What causes may produce general Anasarca ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**General [anasarca](../../../biology.md#anasarca) is extensive interstitial fluid accumulation, produced when vascular filtration exceeds the capacity of [lymphatic drainage](../../../physiology.md#lymphatic-drainage) and the body's regulation of salt and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water).** Several mechanisms commonly act together.

Raised venous and capillary [hydrostatic pressure](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) promotes filtration. [Heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure), especially when systemic venous pressure is elevated, is a major example; renal retention of [sodium](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium) and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) reinforces the swelling. Severe [kidney failure](../../../biology.md#kidney-failure) can itself cause general fluid overload when salt and water cannot be excreted adequately. Acute [glomerulonephritis](../../../biology.md#glomerulonephritis) may therefore cause widespread [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema) without requiring the extremely low plasma protein concentration of every nephrotic case.[Etiology and Some Causes of Edema: cardiac, renal and hydrostatic mechanisms](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#edema-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Reduced plasma [oncotic pressure](../../../cell-membrane.md#oncotic-pressure) contributes when circulating [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) is markedly depleted. Causes include heavy urinary protein loss in [nephrotic syndrome](../../../biology.md#nephrotic-syndrome), reduced synthesis in advanced [liver disease](../../../biology.md#liver-disease), protein loss through the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine), and severe [malnutrition](../../../physiology.md#malnutrition). In [cirrhosis](../../../biology.md#cirrhosis), altered circulation and renal salt retention add to reduced protein synthesis; the mechanism cannot be reduced to a single blocked vein. Likewise, [nephrotic syndrome](../../../biology.md#nephrotic-syndrome) can include primary renal salt retention as well as a fall in [oncotic pressure](../../../cell-membrane.md#oncotic-pressure).[Etiology: cardiac, renal, hepatic and protein-losing conditions](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#anasarca-in-statpearls)

Increased vascular permeability during severe systemic [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation), [sepsis](../../../biology.md#sepsis) or extensive tissue injury can allow fluid and protein into the interstitium. Impaired [lymphatic drainage](../../../physiology.md#lymphatic-drainage) can amplify systemic [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema), although a single obstructed lymphatic or vein ordinarily causes regional rather than general swelling. Excess administered fluid and some drugs can contribute by salt retention or altered vascular tone. Multiple mechanisms are particularly likely in a severely ill patient.

A nineteenth-century description would group much of this as cardiac, renal or hepatic “dropsy,” and might invoke an impoverished state of the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). Modern physiology specifies the abnormalities that those broad descriptions leave unresolved. A general examination should distinguish pitting fluid accumulation from a predominantly non-pitting disorder such as [myxoedema](../../../physiology.md#myxoedema), and from isolated [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites). The distribution, jugular venous filling, heart and lung findings, urinary [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin), renal function and plasma [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) help distinguish causes. **Anasarca is a sign with several possible causes, not a diagnosis of renal disease by itself.**

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

Under what several conditions is the Urine found to contain (1) Albumen, (2) Sugar, and (3) Pus globules ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**Urinary [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin), [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) and [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) represent different abnormalities, and none alone identifies one disease.** A fresh, appropriately collected specimen and confirmation of the constituent are essential before interpreting it.

Persistent [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) commonly reflects increased passage of [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) across an injured [glomerular filtration barrier](../../../anatomy.md#glomerular-filtration-barrier). Examples include [glomerulonephritis](../../../biology.md#glomerulonephritis), [diabetic kidney disease](../../../biology.md#diabetic-kidney-disease) and other protein-losing glomerular disorders. Renal vascular congestion and severe systemic illness can also increase excretion. Transient [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) can accompany fever, strenuous exercise or a [urinary tract infection](../../../biology.md#urinary-tract-infection); an upright-posture-related pattern may disappear in an early-morning specimen. Blood or inflammatory material added downstream can contribute protein, so a protein-positive specimen need not mean that every protein molecule crossed a diseased [glomerulus](../../../anatomy.md#glomerulus).[Causes: temporary and persistent albuminuria; importance of repeat testing](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#albuminuria-by-the-national-kidney-foundation)

[Albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) should be distinguished from the broader term [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria). Reduced tubular recovery and overproduction of other small proteins can produce different urinary protein patterns. A modern urine [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin)-to-[creatinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#creatinine) measurement helps quantify albumin loss and reduce the effect of variable dilution.[Detection: urine albumin measurement and albumin-to-creatinine ratio](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#albuminuria-albumin-in-the-urine-by-niddk) The historical “albumen” category does not imply that every old heat or precipitation test was chemically specific for [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin).

[Glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) results either from an excessive filtered [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) load or reduced renal recovery. [Diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) with [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia) is the major systemic example. Transient hyperglycaemic states may also cause it; [renal glycosuria](../../../biology.md#renal-glycosuria), pregnancy-related changes and proximal tubular dysfunction can produce [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) without sustained diabetic [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia). Modern drugs that deliberately reduce renal [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) recovery provide another mechanism, but should not be attributed to 1864 practice.[Definition and etiology: glucose without hyperglycaemia; isolated or associated tubular defects](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#renal-glucosuria-by-merck-manual-professional-edition) [Chapter 139, Basic Science: excessive filtered load, diminished tubular recovery and pregnancy](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#glucosuria-in-clinical-methods) Urinary sugar is therefore a reason to examine [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar), not proof of one diabetes subtype. Historical reducing-sugar reactions also require care because chemical reduction is not uniquely specific to [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose).

“Pus globules” correspond chiefly to [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte), especially [neutrophils](../../../biological-cell.md#neutrophil). [Pyuria](../../../biology.md#pyuria) occurs with [cystitis](../../../anatomy.md#cystitis), [pyelonephritis](../../../anatomy.md#pyelonephritis), urethral or prostatic infection and suppuration draining into the urinary tract. It can also occur with stones, [urinary tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#urinary-tuberculosis), [interstitial nephritis](../../../biology.md#interstitial-nephritis) and other inflammatory disorders, or through contamination by genital secretions. Previous antimicrobial treatment or organisms not recovered by a routine culture can leave apparently sterile [pyuria](../../../biology.md#pyuria).[Urine testing: leukocytes, casts, noninfective causes and contamination](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bacterial-urinary-tract-infections-by-merck-manual)[§5.13, Pyuria: sterile pyuria and alternative causes](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#living-donor-kidney-transplantation-guidelines-by-the-british-transplantation-society-and-renal-association-2011)

A wet sediment examined with a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope) can show [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte), [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) and [urinary casts](../../../biology.md#urinary-cast); [urine culture](../../../biology.md#urine-culture) addresses a different question about detectable organisms. White-cell casts suggest inflammation within the kidney rather than simply pus added in the bladder, but are not unique to bacterial [pyelonephritis](../../../anatomy.md#pyelonephritis). **“Pus in urine” neither localises every lesion nor proves an ordinary bacterial infection.**

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

By what conditions is Jaundice produced; and in which may it be considered remediable either by nature or by art ?

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Jaundice](../../../biology.md#jaundice) results from accumulation of [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin); its reversibility depends on the cause, not on the yellow colour alone.** In 1864, obstruction of bile flow and disease of the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) were important practical distinctions. Modern physiology also separates excess production from impaired uptake, conjugation and excretion.

Excess breakdown of [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) can overwhelm the usual handling of [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin), producing predominantly unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia. Impaired hepatic uptake or conjugation supplies another non-obstructive mechanism. Hepatocellular injury, as in [hepatitis](../../../biology.md#hepatitis) or advanced [cirrhosis](../../../biology.md#cirrhosis), impairs processing and secretion. Intrahepatic [cholestasis](../../../biology.md#cholestasis) and extrahepatic obstruction prevent normal disposal through [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile). Obstruction can result from a [gallstone](../../../biology.md#gallstone), a duct stricture, inflammation or compression/invasion by a [tumour](../../../biology.md#tumour).[Pathophysiology and mechanisms: increased production, impaired handling and intrahepatic or extrahepatic cholestasis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#jaundice-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Conjugated [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin) can enter the [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine), making it dark; predominantly unconjugated [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin), which is strongly protein-bound, does not ordinarily produce bilirubinuria. Marked interruption of bile delivery can make stools pale. These findings help locate the problem but do not by themselves distinguish a stone from a stricture or [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer). Modern fractionated [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin) tests, liver enzymes and [ultrasonography](../../../physics.md#ultrasonography) supplement the clinical pattern.[Urine bilirubin, liver disease and bile duct obstruction; relation to dark urine and pale stool](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bilirubin-in-urine-by-medlineplus)

Recovery “by nature” is possible when an obstruction is temporary, a small [gallstone](../../../biology.md#gallstone) passes, a reversible inflammatory process settles, or the cause of increased pigment production ceases. An episode of jaundice can clear as [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) function recovers. It is nevertheless unsafe to infer a harmless or self-limiting cause merely because the jaundice fluctuates; partial malignant obstruction can also fluctuate.

Recovery “by art,” in modern terms, includes treating a reversible underlying illness and relieving a remediable obstruction. A persistent common-duct stone can be removed endoscopically or surgically; an obstructed duct may require drainage. Selected strictures and tumours can be treated, but established extensive hepatic destruction or disseminated malignancy may prevent full recovery even if bile drainage is improved.[Recommendations on common bile duct stones and bile duct clearance](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gallstone-disease-diagnosis-and-management-by-nice) These are present capabilities, not a claim that modern endoscopy was available to the 1864 examiner.

The period question therefore calls for a conditional prognosis: a movable obstruction or reversible disturbance offers a better prospect than irreversible tissue destruction or an untreatable growing obstruction. **A disappearing colour is not, by itself, proof that the underlying disease has been cured.**

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the characteristics of what is termed ‘general Paralysis of the Insane?’ and state the diseases for which it may be mistaken.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**[General paresis](../../../biology.md#general-paresis) combines progressive mental deterioration with a characteristic disorder of speech, fine movement and motor control.** The historical phrase “general paralysis of the insane” does not mean complete, simultaneous immobility of every muscle.

Early changes may include impaired judgment, forgetfulness, altered personality, irritability, inappropriate confidence and loss of ordinary social restraint. Expansive or grandiose delusions are characteristic in some cases, but depression, suspiciousness or less spectacular cognitive failure can occur. Increasing [dementia](../../../neurology.md#dementia) and reduced insight are more fundamental than any one extravagant belief.

The physical examination may disclose hesitating or slurred articulation, tremulous lips and [tongue](../../../anatomy.md#tongue), facial tremor, deteriorating handwriting and impaired coordination of the hands. Gait becomes unsteady and weakness advances. Unequal or abnormally reacting [pupils](../../../anatomy.md#pupil), altered reflexes and seizures may occur. Later disease can bring severe cognitive incapacity, swallowing difficulty, incontinence and dependence. Mental and motor manifestations need not develop in a fixed order or with equal severity.[Late neurosyphilis: general paresis, psychiatric change, dysarthria, tremor, pupils and reflexes](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neurosyphilis-guide-by-the-california-department-of-public-health)

The name [general paresis](../../../biology.md#general-paresis) was already advocated in a January 1864 pathological study to avoid the ambiguity of “general paralysis.”[Opening, Journal of Mental Science 9, pp. 467–493, January 1864](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#the-pathology-of-general-paresis-by-william-henry-octavius-sankey) Modern medicine recognises the classical disease as a form of [neurosyphilis](../../../biology.md#neurosyphilis), involving chronic injury to the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain). This causal diagnosis should be separated from the historical clinical label; it was not securely established by the wording of an 1864 examination. [Syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis) serology, relevant [cerebrospinal fluid](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebrospinal-fluid) findings and exclusion of alternative causes now help establish the diagnosis.[Neurological involvement: cognitive or motor signs and cerebrospinal-fluid examination](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neurosyphilis-ocular-syphilis-and-otosyphilis-treatment-guidance-by-cdc)

Several mimics deserve explicit comparison. [Mania](../../../psychiatry.md#manic-episode) can resemble the expansive mental phase, and depression or psychotic illness can resemble other presentations, but a sustained progressive combination of cognitive decline, characteristic speech disturbance and neurological signs argues for organic disease. [Alcohol intoxication](../../../biology.md#alcohol-intoxication) can produce tremor, slurred speech and an unsteady gait; an acute time course or improvement with clearance differs from progressive [general paresis](../../../biology.md#general-paresis), although chronic alcohol-related disease may complicate the distinction. Other dementias, chronic cerebral vascular disease, tumours and some toxic or metabolic disorders can likewise combine cognitive and motor abnormalities.

A focal [stroke](../../../neurology.md#stroke) generally gives a more abrupt and lateralised deficit; a series of strokes can produce a stepwise course. [Parkinsonism](../../../neurology.md#parkinsonism) can impair speech and gait but has a different predominant motor pattern. [Tabes dorsalis](../../../biology.md#tabes-dorsalis) can produce marked sensory [ataxia](../../../neurology.md#ataxia), absent tendon reflexes and bladder disturbance with relatively preserved intellect; it can also coexist with paretic disease. **The diagnosis rests on the pattern, course and corroborating investigation, not on grandiosity or a tremulous tongue alone.**[General paresis and tabes dorsalis: contrasting clinical manifestations and diagnostic framework](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#german-guidelines-on-the-diagnosis-and-treatment-of-neurosyphilis-2020)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

By what symptoms would you form a diagnosis of Hæmorrhage into the spinal cord ?

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**A sudden spinal syndrome—often pain followed rapidly by weakness, a [sensory level](../../../neurology.md#sensory-level) and sphincter disturbance—would raise suspicion of [haematomyelia](../../../neurology.md#haematomyelia).** Symptoms can localise injury to the [spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-cord), but cannot prove that the material within it is [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood).

The classical presentation is abrupt severe spinal or radicular pain followed by rapid loss of motor power below the affected level. A thoracic lesion can produce [paraplegia](../../../neurology.md#paraplegia); a cervical lesion can affect all four limbs, and a high cervical lesion can impair [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). Bladder retention or loss of control and bowel disturbance support interruption of descending spinal pathways. Sensory examination may show a [sensory level](../../../neurology.md#sensory-level) below which sensation is altered; the distribution need not be perfectly symmetrical.

A centrally placed haemorrhage can especially injure crossing [spinothalamic pathways](../../../neuroscience.md#spinothalamic-pathway) and the [anterior horns](../../../neuroscience.md#anterior-horn). There may therefore be disproportionate loss of pain and temperature with relative preservation of some other modalities, together with segmental weakness, reduced reflexes or later wasting at the level of the lesion. Damage to [descending motor pathways](../../../neuroscience.md#descending-motor-pathway) produces weakness below it. Acute [spinal shock](../../../neurology.md#spinal-shock) can initially suppress reflexes below the lesion; later reflex behaviour depends on the pathways destroyed and should not be predicted from the first examination alone.[Introduction and case discussion: spinal/radicular pain, myotomal signs, acute and other presentations](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#non-traumatic-hematomyelia-a-rare-finding-in-clinical-practice)

The history should look for trauma, a bleeding disorder or anticoagulant exposure, a vascular malformation or an underlying spinal lesion. In a modern assessment these are possible causes, not prerequisites. A haemorrhage can also develop subacutely or recur, so the apoplectic onset is suggestive rather than universal.[Etiologies and alternative acute, subacute and chronic presentations](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#non-traumatic-hematomyelia-a-rare-finding-in-clinical-practice)

Important alternatives are [spinal cord infarction](../../../neurology.md#spinal-cord-infarction), acute inflammatory [myelitis](../../../biology.md#myelitis), traumatic injury without intramedullary bleeding, and an epidural or other extramedullary collection compressing the [spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-cord). A spinal meningeal bleed is anatomically different from [haematomyelia](../../../neurology.md#haematomyelia), even if pain and weakness overlap. Preserved consciousness and absence of cranial signs may support spinal localisation, but neither is an absolute rule.

Modern [magnetic resonance imaging](../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#magnetic-resonance-imaging) is required to demonstrate the location and nature of the lesion; it can distinguish intramedullary blood from external compression and guide investigation of an underlying vascular or neoplastic cause.[Symptoms, signs and diagnosis: competing acute spinal syndrome and MRI evaluation](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#spinal-cord-infarction-by-merck-manual-professional-edition) **In an 1864 answer, sudden onset and anatomical localisation justify a suspected diagnosis; they do not justify claiming certainty from symptoms alone.**

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the varieties of Hernia that occur in the abdomen; and enumerate the swellings which may occur in the inguinal and scrotal regions, besides inguinoscrotal Hernia.

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**Abdominal [hernias](../../../biology.md#hernia) are classified primarily by the opening through which tissue protrudes; groin and scrotal swellings have a substantially wider differential diagnosis.** Reducibility, obstruction and strangulation describe the state of a [hernia](../../../biology.md#hernia), rather than different anatomical sites.

The principal external varieties are [indirect inguinal hernia](../../../biology.md#indirect-inguinal-hernia) and [direct inguinal hernia](../../../biology.md#direct-inguinal-hernia), [femoral hernia](../../../biology.md#femoral-hernia), [umbilical hernia](../../../biology.md#umbilical-hernia) and other anterior abdominal-wall hernias, including [epigastric hernia](../../../biology.md#epigastric-hernia) and [incisional hernia](../../../biology.md#incisional-hernia). An [indirect inguinal hernia](../../../biology.md#indirect-inguinal-hernia) enters through the deep inguinal ring lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels and can follow the canal into the [scrotum](../../../anatomy.md#scrotum); a [direct inguinal hernia](../../../biology.md#direct-inguinal-hernia) passes through the posterior wall medial to those vessels. A [femoral hernia](../../../biology.md#femoral-hernia) passes through the femoral canal below the [inguinal ligament](../../../anatomy.md#inguinal-ligament), rather than along the inguinal canal.[Types of hernia: inguinal, femoral, incisional, ventral, umbilical and hiatal](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#surgical-mesh-used-for-hernia-repair-by-the-fda)

Less common wall or pelvic varieties include [lateral ventral hernia](../../../biology.md#lateral-ventral-hernia), [lumbar hernia](../../../biology.md#lumbar-hernia), [obturator hernia](../../../biology.md#obturator-hernia), [sciatic hernia](../../../biology.md#sciatic-hernia) and [perineal hernia](../../../biology.md#perineal-hernia). [Diaphragmatic hernia](../../../biology.md#diaphragmatic-hernia) defects allow abdominal contents into the thorax. [Internal hernias](../../../biology.md#internal-hernia) can pass through a mesenteric or peritoneal aperture while remaining within the abdominal cavity, including paraduodenal recesses or the epiploic foramen. Contents may be [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine), [omentum](../../../anatomy.md#omentum) or other viscera; a developmental route and an acquired wall weakness can yield different forms at the same broad site.[Anatomical varieties and distinction of inguinal and femoral defects](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#hernias-of-the-abdominal-wall-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)[Classification by aperture and internal location](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#internal-hernias-a-difficult-diagnostic-challenge-review-of-ct-signs-and-clinical-findings)

In the groin, alternatives to an inguinoscrotal [hernia](../../../biology.md#hernia) include enlarged or inflamed [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node), an [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess), [lipoma](../../../biology.md#lipoma) of the [spermatic cord](../../../anatomy.md#spermatic-cord) or other soft tissue, a retained or undescended [testis](../../../anatomy.md#testis), a [hydrocele](../../../biology.md#hydrocele) of the cord, a [saphena varix](../../../biology.md#saphena-varix), and a [femoral aneurysm](../../../physiology.md#femoral-aneurysm) or pseudoaneurysm. A [femoral hernia](../../../biology.md#femoral-hernia) is also an important separate diagnosis. A fluid collection along a persistent female inguinal peritoneal process can produce a corresponding inguinal swelling. These lesions are not interchangeable merely because they lie near the inguinal opening.[Differential diagnosis of groin swelling](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#inguinal-hernias-diagnosis-and-management-by-the-american-academy-of-family-physicians)

In the [scrotum](../../../anatomy.md#scrotum), consider [hydrocele](../../../biology.md#hydrocele), [haematocele](../../../anatomy.md#haematocele), [spermatocele](../../../anatomy.md#spermatocele) or an epididymal cyst, [varicocele](../../../anatomy.md#varicocele), [epididymitis](../../../anatomy.md#epididymitis), [orchitis](../../../anatomy.md#orchitis), [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess), traumatic swelling and a [testicular tumour](../../../biology.md#testicular-tumour). Torsion of the [testis](../../../anatomy.md#testis) or its appendages can cause acute painful swelling. General [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema) or [lymphoedema](../../../biology.md#lymphoedema) can enlarge the scrotal wall diffusely rather than create one discrete lump.[Etiology: hydrocele, varicocele, cystic masses, haematocele, oedema and tumour](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#painless-scrotal-mass-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)[Differential diagnosis: torsion, trauma, infection and other acute swelling](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#the-acute-scrotum-by-the-american-urological-association)

The discriminating examination locates the swelling relative to the [inguinal ligament](../../../anatomy.md#inguinal-ligament) and to the [testis](../../../anatomy.md#testis), asks whether it is cystic or solid, and notes posture-related change, reducibility, cough impulse and transillumination when relevant. A cough impulse is not exclusive to a [hernia](../../../biology.md#hernia); a communicating fluid collection or a venous swelling may vary with pressure. Modern [ultrasonography](../../../physics.md#ultrasonography), including assessment of [blood flow](../../../physiology.md#blood-flow) when indicated, can clarify uncertain anatomy. **The site and character of a swelling must be established before it is called an inguinoscrotal hernia.**

## 10

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology. medical, surgical](pathology-medical-surgical.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the various modes of growth in Fibrous Tumours of the Uterus.

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**The principal modes are growth within the uterine wall, towards the cavity, and towards the outer surface: [intramural uterine fibroids](../../../biology.md#intramural-uterine-fibroid), [submucosal uterine fibroids](../../../biology.md#submucosal-uterine-fibroid) and [subserosal uterine fibroids](../../../biology.md#subserosal-uterine-fibroid).** “Fibrous tumour” is the historical label; the usual modern lesion is a [leiomyoma](../../../biology.md#leiomyoma), a benign growth of [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) with a variable connective-tissue component, rather than a mass of collagen alone.[Overview: muscle origin and variation in size, shape and location](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#uterine-fibroids-by-the-american-college-of-obstetricians-and-gynecologists)

An [intramural uterine fibroid](../../../biology.md#intramural-uterine-fibroid) enlarges within the [myometrium](../../../anatomy.md#myometrium). It can remain enclosed by uterine muscle, push the cavity aside, or expand both inner and outer contours. Several nodules can give an irregularly enlarged [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus). Expansion compresses surrounding tissue into a cleavage plane or pseudocapsule; this differs from the destructive infiltrative growth typical of a malignant lesion.

A [submucosal uterine fibroid](../../../biology.md#submucosal-uterine-fibroid) grows towards the [endometrium](../../../anatomy.md#endometrium), projecting into and distorting the uterine cavity. It may remain broad-based or acquire a stalk as the surrounding wall is displaced. A [pedunculated uterine fibroid](../../../biology.md#pedunculated-uterine-fibroid) can be pushed through the cervical canal and appear in the [vagina](../../../anatomy.md#vagina) as a [uterine polyp](../../../anatomy.md#uterine-polyp). A polyp formed in this way is a mode of presentation of the same smooth-muscle tumour, not necessarily a different histological species. Its relation to the cavity helps explain why a comparatively small lesion may be associated with substantial bleeding.

A [subserosal uterine fibroid](../../../biology.md#subserosal-uterine-fibroid) projects outwards beneath the serosal covering. It may have a broad attachment or a long pedicle and become a large extrauterine-looking mass. Lateral outward growth can extend between the layers of the broad ligament; cervical growth can displace neighbouring pelvic structures. Rarely, a detached pedunculated lesion gains a blood supply from adjacent tissues and persists as a parasitic growth; this is not ordinary distant [metastasis](../../../biology.md#metastasis).[Anatomical locations: intramural, submucosal, subserosal, cervical, intraligamentous and parasitic forms](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#uterine-fibroids-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The three directions are not mutually exclusive: a large nodule may affect both the uterine cavity and its external surface. Modern location classifications describe its relation to [endometrium](../../../anatomy.md#endometrium), [myometrium](../../../anatomy.md#myometrium) and serosa more precisely, but those later type numbers are unnecessary to explain the anatomical modes requested here.

Growth is also distinct from secondary change. A poorly supplied area can undergo hyaline or cystic degeneration, haemorrhagic change or calcification; these changes alter consistency without establishing a new malignant mode of growth. [Uterine fibroids](../../../biology.md#uterine-fibroid) often respond to the reproductive hormonal environment and commonly shrink after menopause, but neither every lesion's course nor its rate of growth is identical.[Etiology, histopathology and secondary changes; hormone-related growth and postmenopausal involution](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#uterine-leiomyomata-in-statpearls) **Location and attachment describe the mode of expansion; tissue examination establishes the nature of the tumour.**
