## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the different ways in which the Nervous system may influence the functions of organic or vegetative life.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The [nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#nervous-system) regulates visceral movement, [glandular secretion](../../../physiology.md#glandular-secretion), [blood flow](../../../physiology.md#blood-flow) and coordinated bodily responses, while also influencing [nutrition](../../../physiology.md#nutrition) indirectly through those functions.** Organic or vegetative life here means activities maintaining the body, such as [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation), [digestion](../../../physiology.md#digestion), [pulmonary ventilation](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation), secretion and excretion. It does not designate an independent form of living substance.

One influence is directly motor. [Autonomic nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#autonomic-nerve) alter the rate and force of the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), contract or relax [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) in the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine), [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder), airways and [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel), and coordinate movement with the opening and closure of sphincters. Their effect need not be excitatory: an active [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) can slow the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), while other pathways accelerate it. In the modern account, [sympathetic](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic) and [parasympathetic](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic) effects depend on the target and its [receptors](../../../protein.md#receptor-biochemistry); they are not respectively universal accelerator and brake systems. Some organs receive both divisions, while others do not have a simple balanced double supply.[Opening comparison; Sympathetic Division; Parasympathetic Division; target-specific actions](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/15-1-divisions-of-the-autonomic-nervous-system)

A second influence is secretomotor. Activity in appropriate [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) can increase [salivary secretion](../../../anatomy.md#salivary-secretion), alter [gastric acid secretion](../../../physiology.md#gastric-acid-secretion) and promote [sweating](../../../physiology.md#sweating). This is distinguishable from moving a duct or changing its calibre. A convincing experiment collects a secretion from a cannulated duct while a [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) is stimulated, rather than inferring secretion from a change of colour or organ size. For example, stimulation of the [chorda tympani](../../../neuroscience.md#chorda-tympani) can evoke fluid from the [submandibular gland](../../../anatomy.md#submandibular-gland). The response depends on functioning secretory [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md); the [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) communicates a command rather than supplying the material constituents of saliva.

A third influence is vasomotor. Changing the calibre of [arterioles](../../../physiology.md#arteriole) changes local [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance) and [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion). The classic comparison of an ear before and after interruption of its cervical [sympathetic](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic) supply illustrates loss of vasoconstrictor tone: the vessels dilate and the ear becomes warmer because more warm [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) reaches it. Stimulation of the peripheral cut pathway can produce the converse vascular effect. Warmth therefore need not mean that the local [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) themselves generate more [heat](../../../heat.md). Vascular control also changes the delivery of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and [nutrients](../../../biology.md#nutrient) to a [gland](../../../anatomy.md#gland) or [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology), so it can modify function indirectly.

These outputs are organized by sensory feedback and [reflexes](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex). Distension of the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine) can evoke coordinated movement; changes in [blood pressure](../../../physiology.md#blood-pressure) or blood-gas composition influence [heart rate](../../../physiology.md#heart-rate) and [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). Local circuits in the [enteric nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#enteric-nervous-system) permit some digestive activity without an intact connection to the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain), while longer [reflexes](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex) involve the [spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-cord) or [brainstem](../../../neuroscience.md#brainstem). Central states also matter: the sight or expectation of food can initiate preparatory digestive responses, and fear can alter [heart rate](../../../physiology.md#heart-rate), [sweating](../../../physiology.md#sweating) and visceral movement.[Long and Short Reflexes; control of cardiovascular and digestive functions](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/15-2-autonomic-reflexes-and-homeostasis)

Somatic motor activity also supports vegetative functions. [Breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) uses the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) and other [skeletal muscles](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle); swallowing and continence use coordinated [skeletal muscle](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle) as well as [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle). Thus organic function cannot be identified solely with the anatomical distribution of the [sympathetic nervous system](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic-nervous-system).

The historical idea of a direct trophic influence requires discrimination. A [denervation](../../../neuroscience.md#denervation) region may change through loss of movement, altered [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion), loss of protective sensation or changed secretion. Those observations alone do not establish a special nutritive fluid flowing down the [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve). Modern biology recognizes molecular signalling between [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) and their targets, as well as [neuroendocrine signalling](../../../physiology.md#neuroendocrine-signalling) pathways: the [hypothalamus](../../../physiology.md#hypothalamus) can influence [hormone](../../../signal-transduction.md#hormone) release, and [sympathetic](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic) input can evoke [adrenal medulla](../../../physiology.md#adrenal-medulla) secretion. These are mechanisms added to, rather than evidence for an undefined historical nutritive force. A useful experimental design measures the motor, vascular and secretory consequences separately before assigning a residual effect to direct trophic action.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What do you know of the development, relations, and functions of the Eustachian Tube ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The [Eustachian tube](../../../neuroscience.md#eustachian-tube), or [auditory tube](../../../neuroscience.md#eustachian-tube), connects the [middle ear](../../../neuroscience.md#middle-ear) with the [nasopharynx](../../../anatomy.md#nasopharynx).** Its principal functions are ventilation and [pressure](../../../pressure.md) equalization, clearance of middle-ear secretions, and protection against inappropriate entry of nasopharyngeal material.

Developmentally, its lumen and epithelial lining arise chiefly from the proximal part of the [tubotympanic recess](../../../neuroscience.md#tubotympanic-recess), an extension of the first [pharyngeal pouch](../../../developmental-biology.md#pharyngeal-pouch). The expanded distal part contributes to the [tympanic cavity](../../../neuroscience.md#tympanic-cavity). The neighbouring first pharyngeal cleft is associated with the external ear passage and must not be substituted for the pouch as the origin of the [auditory tube](../../../neuroscience.md#eustachian-tube). Supporting [cartilage](../../../anatomy.md#cartilage) and [connective tissue](../../../anatomy.md#connective-tissue) develop from surrounding [mesenchyme](../../../developmental-biology.md#mesenchyme). At birth the tube is shorter and more nearly horizontal; craniofacial growth lengthens it and changes its orientation. The developmental account distinguishes formation of the channel from later growth of its surrounding structures.[Middle Ear: tubotympanic recess, proximal auditory tube, distal tympanic cavity and postnatal orientation](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#embryology-ear-statpearls)

In an adult the [auditory tube](../../../neuroscience.md#eustachian-tube) is roughly 3.5–4 cm long. From its tympanic opening in the anterior wall of the [middle ear](../../../neuroscience.md#middle-ear), it runs forwards, downwards and medially towards its pharyngeal opening in the lateral wall of the [nasopharynx](../../../anatomy.md#nasopharynx), behind the nasal cavity. Its lateral third is bony, lying in the temporal bone; the medial two thirds are chiefly cartilaginous with a membranous part. The narrow junction is the tubal isthmus. The cartilaginous portion is normally collapsed, so an open passage for sound or reflux does not continuously connect [pharynx](../../../anatomy.md#pharynx) and [middle ear](../../../neuroscience.md#middle-ear).

The bony part is closely related medially to the canal of the [internal carotid artery](../../../physiology.md#internal-carotid-artery); the canal for the [tensor tympani muscle](../../../neuroscience.md#tensor-tympani-muscle) lies above it. The medial cartilaginous part rests towards the skull base. Its pharyngeal end makes the tubal prominence, or [torus tubarius](../../../anatomy.md#torus-tubarius), near the [soft palate](../../../anatomy.md#soft-palate). The [tensor veli palatini muscle](../../../anatomy.md#tensor-veli-palatini-muscle) acts on the cartilaginous and membranous tube and is the main active dilator. The [levator veli palatini muscle](../../../anatomy.md#levator-veli-palatini-muscle) passes beneath it and participates in the palatal and tubal functional arrangement. Swallowing and yawning intermittently open the tube.[Eustachian Tube; Muscles: bony/cartilaginous portions, opening muscles and three principal functions](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#middle-ear-anatomy-and-physiology-statpearls)

Opening permits gas transfer to compensate for a difference between middle-ear and ambient [pressure](../../../pressure.md). If the tube remains obstructed, absorption of gas from the cavity can make middle-ear [pressure](../../../pressure.md) fall; movement of the [tympanic membrane](../../../anatomy.md#tympanic-membrane) and sound transmission then change. Clearance is helped by the mucociliary lining, which carries material towards the [nasopharynx](../../../anatomy.md#nasopharynx). Closure between openings limits reflux and transmission of nasopharyngeal pressure disturbances. A tube that remains persistently open therefore also functions abnormally; more openness is not invariably better.

The physiological point can be demonstrated by observing the [tympanic membrane](../../../anatomy.md#tympanic-membrane) while swallowing during a change of external [pressure](../../../pressure.md), or by recording the membrane's mechanical response over a controlled range of ear-canal [pressures](../../../pressure.md) using [tympanometry](../../../neuroscience.md#tympanometry). These observations examine pressure communication and membrane mobility, not merely whether air can be forcibly driven through a passage. The relatively immature geometry and opening mechanism in young children helps explain their vulnerability to middle-ear fluid retention, but geometry alone is not an exhaustive account of infection risk. The anatomical structures were recognizable in 1866; the modern measurement method should not be mistaken for apparatus supplied by the examination.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Write an account of the origin, course, and functions of the chorda tympani, the buccal, the musculospiral, and the obturator nerves.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**The four names identify distinct pathways: [chorda tympani](../../../neuroscience.md#chorda-tympani), the sensory [buccal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#buccal-nerve), the [radial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#radial-nerve) formerly called musculospiral, and the [obturator nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#obturator-nerve).** Origin must include the parent [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) or [nerve plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve-plexus), while the function must follow the actual fibre supply rather than the name of a structure crossed.

**[Chorda tympani](../../../neuroscience.md#chorda-tympani).** This branch leaves the [facial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#facial-nerve) in its descending facial canal before the [stylomastoid foramen](../../../anatomy.md#stylomastoid-foramen). It enters the [tympanic cavity](../../../neuroscience.md#tympanic-cavity) from behind, passes forwards between the [malleus](../../../anatomy.md#malleus) and [incus](../../../anatomy.md#incus) close to the inner surface of the [tympanic membrane](../../../anatomy.md#tympanic-membrane), and leaves through the [petrotympanic fissure](../../../anatomy.md#petrotympanic-fissure). In the [infratemporal fossa](../../../anatomy.md#infratemporal-fossa) it joins the [lingual nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#lingual-nerve), a branch of the mandibular division of the [trigeminal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#trigeminal-nerve). It then travels with that carrier towards the [tongue](../../../anatomy.md#tongue) and [submandibular ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#submandibular-ganglion).

The [chorda tympani](../../../neuroscience.md#chorda-tympani) carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the [tongue](../../../anatomy.md#tongue), with sensory cell bodies in the [geniculate ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#geniculate-ganglion) and central connections to the [nucleus of the solitary tract](../../../neuroscience.md#nucleus-of-the-solitary-tract). It also carries preganglionic [parasympathetic](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic) fibres from the [superior salivatory nucleus](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-salivatory-nucleus) towards the [submandibular ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#submandibular-ganglion); postganglionic fibres supply the [submandibular gland](../../../anatomy.md#submandibular-gland) and [sublingual gland](../../../anatomy.md#sublingual-gland). It does not provide the general touch supply of that part of the [tongue](../../../anatomy.md#tongue); that travels through the [lingual nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#lingual-nerve)'s [trigeminal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#trigeminal-nerve) pathway. Taste and tactile testing can consequently distinguish two functions that briefly share one peripheral trunk.[Introduction; Structure and Function: facial origin, middle-ear passage, lingual connection and gustatory/secretomotor fibres](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#anatomy-head-and-neck-chorda-tympani-statpearls)

**[Buccal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#buccal-nerve).** In the usual anatomical meaning intended by this list, this is the long [buccal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#buccal-nerve) from the anterior division of the mandibular [trigeminal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#trigeminal-nerve). It commonly emerges between the heads of the [lateral pterygoid muscle](../../../anatomy.md#lateral-pterygoid-muscle), descends near the anterior border of the mandibular ramus and the [temporalis muscle](../../../anatomy.md#temporalis-muscle) insertion, and passes towards the cheek. Its branches reach cheek [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin), the [buccal mucosa](../../../anatomy.md#buccal-mucosa), and the buccal [gingiva](../../../anatomy.md#gingiva) beside the lower molars. It is principally sensory. The motor supply of the [buccinator muscle](../../../anatomy.md#buccinator-muscle) comes from the [facial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#facial-nerve), whose buccal branches are a different set of [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve). Thus the ability to hold the cheek against the teeth and sensation in the cheek are not tests of the same efferent pathway.[Introduction; Structure and Function: mandibular origin, relations to lateral pterygoid and sensory distribution](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#anatomy-head-and-neck-buccal-nerve-statpearls)

**[Radial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#radial-nerve), historically musculospiral.** This is the major continuation of the posterior cord of the [brachial plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#brachial-plexus), usually containing fibres from C5–T1. It passes from the [axilla](../../../anatomy.md#axilla) into the posterior arm, accompanying the [profunda brachii artery](../../../physiology.md#profunda-brachii-artery) in the [radial groove](../../../anatomy.md#radial-groove) of the [humerus](../../../anatomy.md#humerus). It pierces the lateral intermuscular septum and reaches the anterior compartment between [brachialis](../../../physiology.md#brachialis) and [brachioradialis](../../../anatomy.md#brachioradialis). Near the lateral elbow it divides into a superficial sensory branch and a deep motor branch. The latter passes through the [supinator muscle](../../../anatomy.md#supinator-muscle) and continues as the [posterior interosseous nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#posterior-interosseous-nerve).

The [radial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#radial-nerve) supplies [triceps brachii](../../../physiology.md#triceps-brachii) and [anconeus](../../../anatomy.md#anconeus), then [brachioradialis](../../../anatomy.md#brachioradialis), the radial wrist extensors and, through its deep continuation, the principal finger and thumb extensors and associated posterior forearm [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle). Its sensory branches supply posterior arm and forearm regions and part of the dorsolateral hand. A lesion's level matters: an arm lesion can impair wrist and finger extension, while an isolated superficial branch lesion is sensory and does not itself cause a wrist drop. Conversely, a [posterior interosseous nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#posterior-interosseous-nerve) lesion chiefly affects motor function. The fingertip pads are not the characteristic autonomous sensory territory of the superficial [radial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#radial-nerve).[Structure and Function; Sensory; Motor; Muscles; Physiologic Variants](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#anatomy-shoulder-and-upper-limb-radial-nerve-statpearls)

**[Obturator nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#obturator-nerve).** It arises from the anterior divisions of the anterior rami of L2–L4 in the [lumbar plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#lumbar-plexus). It emerges from the medial border of [psoas major](../../../anatomy.md#psoas-major), crosses the pelvic side wall and leaves through the [obturator canal](../../../anatomy.md#obturator-canal) with the [obturator vessels](../../../physiology.md#obturator-vessels). Its anterior and posterior divisions characteristically run on opposite sides of [adductor brevis](../../../anatomy.md#adductor-brevis), although the level of division varies. The anterior division supplies [adductor longus](../../../anatomy.md#adductor-longus), [gracilis](../../../anatomy.md#gracilis) and commonly [adductor brevis](../../../anatomy.md#adductor-brevis); the posterior division supplies [obturator externus](../../../anatomy.md#obturator-externus) and the adductor portion of [adductor magnus](../../../anatomy.md#adductor-magnus), with variable additional contributions. The hamstring portion of [adductor magnus](../../../anatomy.md#adductor-magnus) has a different supply. Cutaneous supply to a variable medial-thigh patch and articular branches to the [hip](../../../anatomy.md#hip) and [knee](../../../anatomy.md#knee) supplement its principal motor role in thigh adduction. [Obturator internus](../../../anatomy.md#obturator-internus) is supplied by its own sacral-plexus [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve), not by the [obturator nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#obturator-nerve) merely because the names resemble one another.[Nerves: L2–L4 supply of obturator externus versus separate supply of obturator internus](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-abdomen-and-pelvis-obturator-muscles-statpearls)[MeSH definition: lumbar origin, thigh adductors and medial-thigh sensation](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#obturator-nerve-by-the-national-library-of-medicine)

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the physiological limits within which the size of the liver may vary, and what are the conditions which produce such variations ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) has a substantial normal range of size, determined by age, body dimensions, nutritional state and its variable contents; there is no universal absolute minimum and maximum valid for every healthy person.** One must also distinguish true organ volume from the position of its lower border, which moves with the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) during [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation).

An ordinary adult [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) is on the order of 1.5 kg and roughly a few percent of [body mass](../../../biology.md#body-mass), but neither figure is a diagnostic boundary. As an explicitly modern example, an autopsy study of adult men reported a mean hepatic mass of 1561 g and observed values from 838 to 2584 g in its selected population. Those are sample observations, not a declaration that every [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) inside those limits is healthy or that every one outside is diseased. A newer large-sample study similarly found an association of liver mass with [body mass](../../../biology.md#body-mass); much of an apparent sex difference disappears when body size is taken into account.[Abstract and reported organ-mass data; selected male autopsy population, 2012](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#normal-organ-weights-in-men-part-ii-the-brain-lungs-liver-spleen-and-kidneys)[Results and Conclusions: liver mass, body size and sex after adjustment](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#updating-normal-organ-weights-using-a-large-current-sample-database)

Age changes the proportion as well as the absolute size. The fetal and infant [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) is relatively large; the adult organ is smaller in proportion to the whole body. Its fetal role in [haematopoiesis](../../../physiology.md#haematopoiesis) and subsequent changes of growth and function make an adult mass standard inappropriate for an infant. Constitutional body size and normal anatomical shape also vary: a long lower projection is not necessarily the same thing as an enlarged total organ.

Reversible differences within one person arise because the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) is a store and a vascular organ:

- [Glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen) is accumulated after suitable feeding and mobilized during fasting and exercise. Its storage is associated with [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), so changing [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen) stores changes more than the dry mass of the stored [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) units.
- Hepatic [lipids](../../../chemical-substance.md#lipid) and their turnover also contribute to variation. This does not make substantial [hepatic steatosis](../../../biology.md#hepatic-steatosis) a normal consequence of every meal or justify treating pathological fatty enlargement as physiological.
- The amount of [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) contained in the [hepatic sinusoids](../../../physiology.md#hepatic-sinusoid) and larger vessels can change with circulation and posture. Physiological vascular filling must be distinguished from persistent congestion caused by [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure).
- Hydration and longer-term nutritional state influence the organ and its stores. Severe wasting or a persistent progressive enlargement requires a different explanation from a reversible difference between standardized fed and fasted observations.

A meal therefore changes hepatic activity and [splanchnic blood flow](../../../physiology.md#splanchnic-blood-flow) without requiring a large immediate increase in total liver volume. In a modern paired study of 20 healthy volunteers, [single-photon emission computed tomography](../../../inverse-problem.md#single-photon-emission-computed-tomography) before and after a standardized meal showed a nonsignificant 1.5% decrease in estimated liver volume. The authors judged the change to be within methodological error. This is a useful check against assuming that greater digestive activity proves gross swelling.[Abstract: standardized meal, paired measurements and liver-volume result, 1997](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#effect-of-food-intake-on-liver-and-spleen-volume-assessment-with-single-photon-emission-computed-tomography)

The experimental approach is to compare like with like: specify age and [body mass](../../../biology.md#body-mass), use a fixed interval after food, standardize hydration, posture and respiratory phase, and distinguish a volume measurement from a palpable edge or a single linear dimension. [Magnetic resonance imaging](../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#magnetic-resonance-imaging) can measure volume while [magnetic resonance spectroscopy](../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md#magnetic-resonance-spectroscopy) measures [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen) or [lipid](../../../chemical-substance.md#lipid) signals; the two observations answer different questions. The size of an excised organ additionally depends on how much [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) remains in it. The physiological point appropriate to the 1866 question is reversible variation with growth, alimentation and vascular filling. Modern imaging results refine that point; they are not numerical standards that the historical exam could have expected.

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Trace the course taken by the chyle from its formation up to the spot where it is poured into the blood-vascular system.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**[Chyle](../../../physiology.md#chyle) is intestinal [lymph](../../../physiology.md#lymph) enriched with absorbed dietary [lipids](../../../chemical-substance.md#lipid); its usual route ends in the venous circulation at the left jugulosubclavian junction.** The milky appearance after a fat-containing meal is chiefly due to suspended lipid-rich particles, now called [chylomicrons](../../../metabolism.md#chylomicron).

Formation begins with [lipid digestion](../../../physiology.md#lipid-digestion) in the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine). [Pancreatic lipase](../../../enzyme.md#pancreatic-lipase) helps hydrolyse dietary [triglycerides](../../../chemical-substance.md#triglyceride); [bile salts](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt) help the digestion products reach the intestinal absorptive surface in [mixed micelles](../../../physiology.md#mixed-micelle). [Enterocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#enterocyte) take up the products, reconstruct much of the long-chain [triglyceride](../../../chemical-substance.md#triglyceride), and package it with other [lipids](../../../chemical-substance.md#lipid) and [apolipoproteins](../../../protein.md#apolipoprotein) into [chylomicrons](../../../metabolism.md#chylomicron). These leave the basolateral side of the [enterocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#enterocyte) and enter the central [lacteals](../../../physiology.md#lacteal) of the [intestinal villi](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-villus). Thus the particles carried in [chyle](../../../physiology.md#chyle) are not simply intact globules of a meal passing through an open hole from the intestinal lumen.[Lipid Absorption: micelles, enterocytes, chylomicrons and lacteals](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/23-7-chemical-digestion-and-absorption-a-closer-look)

From the [lacteals](../../../physiology.md#lacteal), [chyle](../../../physiology.md#chyle) enters lymphatic channels in the intestinal wall and then collecting vessels in the [mesentery](../../../anatomy.md#mesentery). These vessels pass through [mesenteric lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#mesenteric-lymph-node), with afferent vessels entering and efferent vessels leaving the nodes. Successive collectors join the intestinal lymphatic trunk or trunks. They usually communicate with the [cisterna chyli](../../../physiology.md#cisterna-chyli), an expanded origin of the [thoracic duct](../../../physiology.md#thoracic-duct) in the upper lumbar region, near the bodies of L1–L2 and to the right of the [aorta](../../../physiology.md#aorta). The [cisterna chyli](../../../physiology.md#cisterna-chyli) also receives lymph from lumbar trunks; the stream beyond it therefore contains material from more than the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine). A conspicuous sac is not present in every individual.

The [thoracic duct](../../../physiology.md#thoracic-duct) passes through the [aortic hiatus](../../../anatomy.md#aortic-hiatus) of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) and ascends in the posterior [mediastinum](../../../anatomy.md#mediastinum), typically between the [aorta](../../../physiology.md#aorta) and [azygos vein](../../../physiology.md#azygos-vein), behind the [oesophagus](../../../anatomy.md#oesophagus). It crosses towards the left in the middle or upper [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax), continues to the root of the neck and arches to its terminal venous opening. The usual endpoint is the left venous angle, where the [left internal jugular vein](../../../anatomy.md#left-internal-jugular-vein) joins the [left subclavian vein](../../../physiology.md#left-subclavian-vein); terminal branches and the precise opening show anatomical variation. It is not normally the right venous angle or a direct entrance into the [portal vein](../../../physiology.md#portal-vein).[Structure and Course: cisterna chyli, thoracic course, left cervical arch and variable venous termination](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-thorax-thoracic-duct-statpearls)

The flow is promoted by intrinsic contraction of collecting lymphatic vessels, movements of surrounding [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) and respiratory changes of [pressure](../../../pressure.md). [Lymphatic valves](../../../physiology.md#lymphatic-valve) limit backward movement. [Lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) are interposed biological structures, not pumps that propel every particle independently.[Lymphatic Capillaries; Larger Lymphatic Vessels, Trunks, and Ducts; lymph transport](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/21-1-anatomy-of-the-lymphatic-and-immune-systems)

This route applies particularly to the long-chain dietary [lipids](../../../chemical-substance.md#lipid) carried by [chylomicrons](../../../metabolism.md#chylomicron). Most absorbed [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) and [amino acids](../../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid), and much shorter-chain [fatty acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#fatty-acid) material, follow portal vascular routes instead. Confusing these pathways would obscure the reason for asking separately about [chyle](../../../physiology.md#chyle). In a classical anatomical demonstration, the mesenteric channels of an appropriately fed animal appear white, and an injected or dissected preparation can trace their continuity towards the [thoracic duct](../../../physiology.md#thoracic-duct). The timing after a fatty meal matters: an empty-looking channel in a fasted preparation does not prove absence of a [lymphatic vessel](../../../physiology.md#lymphatic-vessel). The molecular account of [chylomicrons](../../../metabolism.md#chylomicron) is a modern explanation of the older gross observation.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the different muscles, membranes, and vessels which you would meet with in dissecting from the external integument down to the level of Cowper's glands.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Cowper's glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland) are the paired [bulbourethral glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland), deep to the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) on either side of the membranous [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra).** The relevant dissection is of the male anterior [perineum](../../../anatomy.md#perineum), or [urogenital triangle](../../../anatomy.md#urogenital-triangle). A broad exposure reveals structures at different lateral positions; they are not all piled one above another along a single central line.

From superficial to deep, the principal layers and contents are:

- **[Skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) and superficial [fascia](../../../anatomy.md#fascia).** The superficial fascia includes a fatty layer and a deeper membranous layer, [Colles' fascia](../../../anatomy.md#colles-fascia). The latter attaches laterally to the ischiopubic rami and posteriorly towards the posterior border of the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) and [perineal body](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-body); anteriorly it is continuous with fascia of the scrotum and lower abdominal wall. Superficial vessels and [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) accompany these layers. Cutaneous arterial supply includes branches of the [perineal artery](../../../physiology.md#perineal-artery) and, more anteriorly, contributions associated with the external pudendal circulation.
- **The superficial perineal compartment.** Removing the membranous fascia exposes the superficial pouch and the investing fascia over its [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle). Centrally, [bulbospongiosus](../../../anatomy.md#bulbospongiosus) covers the bulb of the penis and proximal [corpus spongiosum](../../../anatomy.md#corpus-spongiosum). On either side, [ischiocavernosus](../../../anatomy.md#ischiocavernosus) covers a crus of the penis. Posteriorly, the [superficial transverse perineal muscle](../../../anatomy.md#superficial-transverse-perineal-muscle) runs towards the [perineal body](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-body). Older descriptions call these the accelerator urinae, erector penis and transversus perinei respectively. The names should be matched to the structures rather than counted as additional [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle).
- **Superficial perineal vessels.** The [perineal artery](../../../physiology.md#perineal-artery), a branch of the [internal pudendal artery](../../../physiology.md#internal-pudendal-artery), supplies this region and gives posterior scrotal and muscular branches; accompanying [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) drain it. These vessels lie in relation to the pouch and its [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle), rather than constituting a complete vascular sheet. The bulb and crura are the associated [erectile tissues](../../../anatomy.md#erectile-tissue), not additional membranes.
- **The [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane).** This dense fibrous sheet spans the anterior pelvic outlet between the ischiopubic rami. It is the structure traditionally described as the triangular ligament's inferior layer. It is pierced by the [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra) and by the ducts of the [bulbourethral glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland), as well as related vascular passages. The [artery of the bulb of the penis](../../../physiology.md#artery-of-the-bulb-of-the-penis) passes to the bulb in close relation to the membrane. The deep and dorsal penile arterial routes lie more anteriorly; they should be identified where the exposure reaches them rather than imagined to cross every central dissection plane.
- **The deep perineal structures.** Beyond the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) are the [external urethral sphincter](../../../anatomy.md#external-urethral-sphincter) complex, traditionally described deep transverse perineal fibres, the membranous [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra) and the [bulbourethral glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland). The glands lie in close relation to sphincteric [skeletal muscle](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle). Their ducts run forwards through the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) and open into the bulbar [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra), so the position of a duct's opening is not the position of its gland.

The superficial [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle), erectile bodies and pudendal branches identify the shallow compartment; the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) separates it from the gland-bearing deeper region.[Structure and Function; Blood Supply and Lymphatics; Muscles](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-abdomen-and-pelvis-superficial-perineal-space-statpearls) The deep space contains the sphincter complex and male [bulbourethral glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland), with their ducts traversing the membrane.[Introduction; Structure and Function; Blood Supply and Lymphatics](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-abdomen-and-pelvis-deep-perineal-space-statpearls)

The main [internal pudendal vessels](../../../physiology.md#internal-pudendal-vessels) themselves run laterally in the [pudendal canal](../../../anatomy.md#pudendal-canal), in relation to the fascia over [obturator internus](../../../anatomy.md#obturator-internus), before their terminal distribution. A median approach to a gland does not necessarily expose their whole trunks. The [deep dorsal vein of the penis](../../../physiology.md#deep-dorsal-vein-of-the-penis) has a distinct anterior route beneath the pubic arch and should not be confused with the small accompanying perineal [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein). These positional qualifications are necessary for an accurate enumeration of the vessels one may meet.

Nineteenth-century descriptions often arranged the deep structures as a [urogenital diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#urogenital-diaphragm) enclosed between two neat fascial layers, the superior and inferior layers of the triangular ligament. Modern dissection finds a more complex sphincteric and connective-tissue arrangement. The historically named layers explain the older vocabulary, but the [perineal membrane](../../../anatomy.md#perineal-membrane) and surrounding [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) should not be made into a fictitious uniform sandwich. Nor does reaching the [bulbourethral glands](../../../anatomy.md#bulbourethral-gland) require dissecting through the whole [pelvic diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#pelvic-diaphragm) above them.

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give some account of the organisms known as “Infusoria,” “Vibriones,” and “Bacteria.” What relations have they been supposed by different authorities to hold to the production and to the products of Fermentation and Putrefaction ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**The names are historical microscopic groupings, and the central dispute is whether the organisms cause the chemical changes, arise as their products, or merely grow in the altered liquid.** Those are different causal propositions. Seeing small moving bodies in a fermenting infusion does not by itself choose among them.

[Infusoria](../../../organism.md#infusoria) were organisms encountered in infusions of organic material. Many recognizable examples are now classified as [ciliates](../../../organism.md#ciliate) or other unicellular [eukaryotes](../../../organism.md#eukaryote), with a [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus), organized feeding structures and locomotory [cilia](../../../biological-cell.md#cilium) or [flagella](../../../biological-cell.md#flagellum). Historical usage could extend much more widely than a modern taxonomic group. Some organisms found in an infusion feed on the [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria) already growing there; their later appearance can thus reflect ecological succession rather than production of the first chemical change.

[Vibriones](../../../organism.md#vibriones) commonly denoted minute actively moving, often elongated, curved or sinuous forms. It does not follow that every historical vibrion belongs to the modern genus [Vibrio](../../../organism.md#vibrio). [Bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria), in some nineteenth-century classifications, meant a narrower set of minute rod-like bodies than the present bacterial domain. In modern terms [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria) are [prokaryotes](../../../organism.md#prokaryote), lacking a membrane-bound [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus); they generally multiply by [binary fission](../../../biological-cell.md#binary-fission-biology). Shape and apparent movement alone do not establish their species. [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) of nonliving particles also must not be mistaken for independent locomotion.

Three positions illustrate the debate available in 1866:

- **Chemical decomposition transmitted by contact.** [Justus von Liebig](../../../chemistry.md#justus-von-liebig) treated a ferment as unstable nitrogenous material whose own alteration communicates decomposition to fermentable substances. On such an account, multiplication of a definite living organism is not the necessary primary explanation; organisms found in the altered material can be secondary accompaniments or consequences. This is a claim about the initiating cause, rather than merely the true observation that fermentation has chemical reactants and products.[Part II, chemical processes of fermentation, decay and putrefaction; contact with decomposing nitrogenous matter, 1840](../../../plant-biology-bibliography.md#organic-chemistry-in-its-applications-to-agriculture-and-physiology-by-justus-von-liebig)
- **A specific living ferment.** [Louis Pasteur](../../../biologist.md#louis-pasteur)'s work connected particular transformations with the growth of particular microscopic agents. His lactic-fermentation account describes inoculating a suitable medium with a small amount of ferment, observing its multiplication and the associated chemical change. For alcoholic fermentation the familiar agent is [yeast](../../../organism.md#yeast), now classified among [fungi](../../../organism.md#fungus), not an infusorian animal or a bacterium. Different products can indicate different microbial processes rather than interchangeable effects of any decomposing material.[§II, Fermentation lactique; inoculation, growth and the concluding contrast with the chemical-contact interpretation, communicated 1857](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-la-fermentation-appelee-lactique-by-louis-pasteur)
- **Spontaneous generation or heterogenesis.** [Félix Archimède Pouchet](../../../biology.md#felix-archimede-pouchet) defended the production of microscopic life from suitable organic materials without the introduction of pre-existing organisms of the same kind, a position developed in his 1859 [Hétérogénie by Félix Archimède Pouchet](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#heterogenie-by-felix-archimede-pouchet). That interpretation makes some apparent organisms products of the putrefying preparation. It must be separated from the question whether, once present, they can accelerate further decomposition. [Louis Pasteur](../../../biologist.md#louis-pasteur) opposed this explanation of ordinary laboratory infusions through controlled exclusion of contaminating particles.[Historical discussion of heterogenesis and experiments excluding atmospheric dust, 1861](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#sur-les-corpuscules-organises-qui-existent-dans-l-atmosphere-by-louis-pasteur)

The decisive experimental question is not whether fresh air is present, but what accompanies it. In a curved-neck flask, a boiled nutrient liquid remains in communication with air while much airborne dust settles in the neck. An otherwise comparable preparation can be exposed by breaking the neck or bringing trapped material into contact with the liquid. The comparison tests whether access of contaminating material, rather than air as such, permits growth and putrefaction. Heat treatment, the preparation's composition, possible surviving resistant forms and accidental contamination all affect interpretation. A negative culture under one condition is not a universal proof about the origin of life.[Curved-neck experiments: unaltered liquid with air access; alteration following removal of the neck or disturbance admitting particles](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#sur-les-corpuscules-organises-qui-existent-dans-l-atmosphere-by-louis-pasteur)

Modern [fermentation](../../../metabolism.md#fermentation) is an enzyme-mediated metabolic process; [putrefaction](../../../biology.md#putrefaction) commonly involves microbial degradation of nitrogenous organic matter, with mixed and sometimes foul-smelling products. Organisms can be causal agents, and their products can subsequently feed other organisms. Thus the two directions of influence need not be mutually exclusive in a mixed infusion. The mere coexistence of [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria), [ciliates](../../../organism.md#ciliate), gas and odour supplies much weaker evidence than controlled inoculation, time-resolved [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) and chemical measurement.

A later qualification is essential. [Eduard Buchner](../../../biochemistry.md#eduard-buchner)'s cell-free fermentation experiments, first reported in 1897 and described in his 1907 lecture, showed that an extract of [yeast](../../../organism.md#yeast) could carry out alcoholic fermentation without intact living yeast cells. This supports chemical machinery made by organisms; it does not restore the earlier indiscriminate decomposition-by-contact hypothesis. These later findings must not be put into an 1866 candidate's mouth.[1907 lecture, preparation of pressed yeast juice and demonstration of fermentation; historical account of the 1897 discovery](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#cell-free-fermentation-by-eduard-buchner)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Explain what is meant by the Botanical Terms “Stomata,” “Primordial Utricle,” “Cambium Layer;” and give a short history of the physiology of the sap.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**[Stomata](../../../organism.md#stoma) are regulated pores of the plant epidermis; the [primordial utricle](../../../biological-cell.md#primordial-utricle) is an older name for living peripheral cell contents; the [vascular cambium](../../../organism.md#vascular-cambium) is a tissue that produces secondary conducting tissues.** These are respectively an opening apparatus, part of a cell's living contents, and a region of dividing cells.

A [stoma](../../../organism.md#stoma) is an aperture bounded by two [guard cells](../../../botany.md#guard-cell), commonly with associated subsidiary cells. Changes of [guard cell](../../../botany.md#guard-cell) [turgor pressure](../../../biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure) alter the aperture. [Stomata](../../../organism.md#stoma) permit entry of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) for [photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#photosynthesis) and loss of [water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor) through [transpiration](../../../botany.md#transpiration), while also allowing other gas exchange. Most leaves have many on their lower surface, but distribution varies, so absence from the upper surface is not a definition. A pore is not itself a minute leaf [blood vessel](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel).

The [primordial utricle](../../../biological-cell.md#primordial-utricle) was distinguished in nineteenth-century microscopy as the protoplasmic layer inside the [cell wall](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-wall), especially conspicuous around the large central [vacuole](../../../biological-cell.md#vacuole) of a mature [plant cell](../../../biological-cell.md#plant-cell). In modern vocabulary this includes peripheral [cytoplasm](../../../biological-cell.md#cytoplasm) bounded externally by the [cell membrane](../../../cell-membrane.md); the vacuolar boundary is the [tonoplast](../../../biological-cell.md#tonoplast). The old term should not be reduced to a synonym of the rigid [cell wall](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-wall) or assumed to designate an independently living bag. In [plasmolysis](../../../biological-cell.md#plasmolysis), an appropriate external [hypertonic solution](../../../cell-membrane.md#hypertonic-solution) draws [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) from the cell and the living contents withdraw from the wall, making the distinction visible.[Tonicity in Living Systems; plasmolysis and Figure 5.13](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/5-2-passive-transport)

The [vascular cambium](../../../organism.md#vascular-cambium) is a lateral [meristem](../../../organism.md#meristem), characteristically situated between the [xylem](../../../organism.md#xylem) and [phloem](../../../organism.md#phloem) of a woody stem. Its divisions produce secondary [xylem](../../../organism.md#xylem) towards the inside and secondary [phloem](../../../organism.md#phloem) towards the outside; radial derivatives also contribute to vascular rays. Activity connects the growth of the conducting system with thickening of the axis. It is not simply a film of nutritious liquid that spontaneously turns into wood. The [cork cambium](../../../organism.md#cork-cambium) is a different lateral [meristem](../../../organism.md#meristem), concerned with the protective outer covering.[Secondary Growth in Stems: vascular cambium, secondary xylem/phloem and cork cambium](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-2-stems)

The course of sap can now be followed from soil to growing tissue. Root cells acquire mineral ions, and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) enters along a [water potential gradient](../../../cell-membrane.md#water-potential-gradient). Water and dissolved minerals move into the [xylem](../../../organism.md#xylem) and upwards through the root and stem to the leaves. In a transpiring plant, evaporation from moist internal leaf surfaces and diffusion out through [stomata](../../../organism.md#stoma) maintain a pull on the continuous water columns. [Cohesion](../../../continuum-mechanics.md#cohesion) between water molecules and the mechanical support of xylem walls allow tension to be transmitted. [Root pressure](../../../botany.md#root-pressure) can drive exudation or guttation under some conditions, but it is not a sufficient universal explanation for supply to the tops of tall trees.

In leaves, [photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#photosynthesis) incorporates [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) into organic compounds. Soluble products, especially [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose), enter the [phloem](../../../organism.md#phloem) and move from sources to sinks, such as growing roots, buds, fruits or storage organs. Thus elaborated sap is not invariably a single downward stream: the direction depends on where a source and a sink lie. The modern [pressure-flow hypothesis](../../../organism.md#pressure-flow-hypothesis) explains bulk movement through differences of [hydrostatic pressure](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) associated with loading and unloading solutes. Neither the ascending nor the assimilate stream forms an exact analogue of an animal's closed [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation).[Water Potential; Transpiration; Movement of Photosynthates: the cohesion-tension and pressure-flow accounts](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants)

A short experimental history makes these distinctions concrete. In [Vegetable Staticks by Stephen Hales](../../../plant-biology-bibliography.md#vegetable-staticks-by-stephen-hales) (1727), [Stephen Hales](../../../botany.md#stephen-hales) measured water use and perspiration by plants and attached tubes and pressure gauges to cut vines. Exudation into a sealed vertical tube demonstrates a positive pushing pressure generated below the cut. The corresponding observation after the bleeding season differed, showing that root pushing and the supply to a leafy, transpiring shoot need not have the same explanation. Weighing a potted plant while limiting evaporation from the soil provides another balance: water lost from the system must be compared with its supply and changes of storage.[Experiments on perspiration; Chapter III, Experiments XXXIV–XXXV, pp. 100–103, vine-stump tubes and seasonal contrast](../../../plant-biology-bibliography.md#vegetable-staticks-by-stephen-hales) A cut shoot taking up water is informative, but its uptake alone does not establish all the forces acting in an intact rooted plant.

For the [phloem](../../../organism.md#phloem), removal of a ring of bark can interrupt assimilate transport while leaving a substantial wood pathway intact; accumulation above the interruption distinguishes the two transport systems, although injury complicates any inference about a natural circulation. In an 1866 account, terms such as crude and elaborated sap could summarize these routes. The modern quantitative [water potential](../../../cell-membrane.md#water-potential) and [pressure-flow hypothesis](../../../organism.md#pressure-flow-hypothesis) accounts extend beyond that historical vocabulary.

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the distinctive characters of the classes Vermes and Arachnida ? Specify parasitic members from each of these classes.

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**The historical [Vermes](../../../organism.md#vermes) is a broad assemblage of worm-like animals; [Arachnida](../../../organism.md#arachnida) is an arthropod group with a fundamentally different appendage-bearing plan.** [Vermes](../../../organism.md#vermes) has no single set of characters defining an equivalent modern natural [clade](../../../evolution.md#clade), so it is necessary to state the scope of the comparison.

Animals called [Vermes](../../../organism.md#vermes) typically have elongated or flattened bodies and lack paired jointed walking legs of the arthropod type. Many show [bilateral symmetry](../../../organism.md#bilateral-symmetry). Their internal organization is highly variable: some possess a complete alimentary canal, some a blind-ending one, and [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda) lack an alimentary canal entirely. Body cavities, body coverings and segmentation also vary. Modern [Annelida](../../../organism.md#annelida), [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda) and [flatworms](../../../organism.md#flatworm) are consequently distinct groups; the segmented body of an annelid and the chain of reproductive [proglottids](../../../organism.md#proglottid) in a [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda) should not be identified as the same organization. It would be inaccurate to characterize all [Vermes](../../../organism.md#vermes) as either segmented or unsegmented, or all as having no digestive tract.

Parasitic examples within that historical assemblage include [Ascaris lumbricoides](../../../organism.md#ascaris-lumbricoides), [Enterobius vermicularis](../../../organism.md#enterobius-vermicularis), [Trichuris trichiura](../../../organism.md#trichuris-trichiura) and [Trichinella spiralis](../../../organism.md#trichinella-spiralis) among [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda); [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium) and [Taenia saginata](../../../organism.md#taenia-saginata) among [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda); and [Fasciola hepatica](../../../organism.md#fasciola-hepatica) or [Schistosoma haematobium](../../../organism.md#schistosoma-haematobium) among [Trematoda](../../../organism.md#trematoda). These illustrate intestinal adults, tissue larvae and blood- or bile-duct inhabitants rather than one obligatory habitat. [Leeches](../../../organism.md#leech) provide annelid examples, including blood-feeding temporary external parasites. A parasitic mode of life alone cannot unite these forms into a natural zoological class.[1864 classification and separate accounts of Trematoda, Nematoda and Cestoda](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#entozoa-an-introduction-to-the-study-of-helminthology-by-thomas-spencer-cobbold)[Causal Agents; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-ascariasis)

An adult typical [arachnid](../../../organism.md#arachnida) has a [chitinous exoskeleton](../../../anatomy.md#chitinous-exoskeleton), jointed appendages, and a body organized into a [prosoma](../../../organism.md#prosoma) and [opisthosoma](../../../organism.md#opisthosoma), though their boundaries may be inconspicuous or fused in some groups. The prosoma bears [chelicerae](../../../organism.md#chelicera), [pedipalps](../../../organism.md#pedipalp) and four pairs of walking legs. There are no antennae and no wings. These features distinguish the usual arachnid plan from the three pairs of adult insect walking legs and the antenna-bearing insect head. Respiration may use [book lungs](../../../organism.md#book-lung), [arthropod tracheae](../../../organism.md#arthropod-trachea), body-surface exchange or combinations according to the group; [book lungs](../../../organism.md#book-lung) are not a universal requirement.[Chelicerata: Arachnida, body regions, chelicerae, pedipalps and four walking-leg pairs](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/28-6-superphylum-ecdysozoa-arthropods)

The strongest parasitic examples are [mites](../../../organism.md#mite) and [ticks](../../../organism.md#tick). [Sarcoptes scabiei](../../../organism.md#sarcoptes-scabiei), the itch mite, burrows in the superficial [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) and has eight legs in its nymphal and adult stages; its larva has six. The [Demodex](../../../organism.md#demodex) mites occupy hair follicles and associated glandular sites, with elongated bodies that should not make them nematode worms. [Ixodes](../../../organism.md#ixodes) and [Argas](../../../organism.md#argas) include blood-feeding ticks. Their compact or fused body regions do not abolish their arachnid appendage plan. The six-legged larva of a tick or mite is a developmental exception to the adult eight-leg character, not evidence that it becomes an insect.[Causal Agents; Life Cycle: superficial burrows, three larval leg pairs and four nymphal/adult pairs](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-scabies)[Causal Agents; Life Cycle: hard and soft ticks and developmental stages](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-ticks)

A final useful negative example is the [louse](../../../organism.md#louse): although a familiar ectoparasite, it is an [insect](../../../organism.md#insect), not an [arachnid](../../../organism.md#arachnida). Conversely, a free-living spider is an [arachnid](../../../organism.md#arachnida) without being a parasite. The comparison therefore requires structural characters as well as a separate specification of which members are parasitic.

## 10

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the main points of difference and what the main points of interdependence existing between the Animal and Vegetable Kingdoms ?

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**The main differences concern cellular construction and the usual modes of obtaining food, movement and development; the main interdependence concerns transfers of matter and [energy](../../../energy.md) through [ecosystems](../../../ecology.md#ecosystem).** The nineteenth-century animal–vegetable division is useful as a comparison of familiar forms, but it is not a complete modern classification of life.

A typical [plant cell](../../../biological-cell.md#plant-cell) has a [cell wall](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-wall), a large central [vacuole](../../../biological-cell.md#vacuole) and, in photosynthetic tissue, [chloroplasts](../../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast). An [animal cell](../../../biological-cell.md#animal-cell) lacks a cellulose wall and chloroplasts and has a different extracellular and tissue organization. Both possess a [cell membrane](../../../cell-membrane.md), [cytoplasm](../../../biological-cell.md#cytoplasm), a [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) and, in their usual aerobic cells, [mitochondria](../../../biological-cell.md#mitochondria). Roots and other non-green parts of a plant need not contain functional [chloroplasts](../../../biological-cell.md#chloroplast); an erythrocyte's loss of its [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) is likewise not a definition of all animal cells.[Animal Cells versus Plant Cells; plant cell walls, vacuoles and chloroplasts](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/4-3-eukaryotic-cells)

Most green [plants](../../../organism.md#plant) acquire carbon through [photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#photosynthesis), taking [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) from their surroundings and mineral nutrients and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) chiefly through roots. Familiar [animals](../../../organism.md#animal) obtain preformed organic material by feeding and digesting it or absorbing material derived from a host. Animals generally have conspicuous coordinated movement, and many have [nervous systems](../../../neuroscience.md#nervous-system) and [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle); higher plants regulate growth and movement without such organs. These are useful generalizations with exceptions. Some plants depend on other organisms for carbon; some animals are permanently attached; and neither lack of locomotion nor a green appearance alone settles every microscopic classification. [Fungi](../../../organism.md#fungus) and [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria) should not be forced into one of these two kingdoms in a modern account.

One major interdependence is nutritional. [Photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#photosynthesis) converts light input into chemical [energy](../../../energy.md) stored in organic material. Plant material feeds herbivores and, through them, carnivores; it also enters detrital food webs. An animal that eats an animal can therefore remain indirectly dependent on primary production by [plants](../../../organism.md#plant), algae or other photosynthetic organisms. Chemical elements pass between organisms, but [energy](../../../energy.md) is progressively dissipated as [heat](../../../heat.md); it is not recycled in the same closed manner as an element.[Overview and food-chain examples: light capture, organic products and heterotrophic dependence](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/8-1-overview-of-photosynthesis)

A second relation is gas exchange. In its simplest overall form, oxygenic photosynthesis may be represented by

$$
6\mathrm{CO_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}+\text{light energy}
\longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\mathrm{O_2}.
$$

Aerobic utilization of the resulting [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) has the complementary overall balance

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\mathrm{O_2}
\longrightarrow6\mathrm{CO_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}+\text{released energy}.
$$

These balanced equations show complementary material transfers without making the underlying pathways literal reversals of one another. **Plants also perform [cellular respiration](../../../metabolism.md#cellular-respiration), by day as well as by night.** They can therefore release [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) while consuming [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen); a sufficiently illuminated leaf may simultaneously show net uptake of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) because [photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#photosynthesis) exceeds respiration. Stating that plants only purify air and animals only spoil it would erase this essential distinction between gross processes and net exchange.

Other relations close material cycles. Animals return organic matter through excretion and death; microbial decomposition and mineral transformations return nutrients in forms available for plant uptake. Most atmospheric [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is not directly available to ordinary plants merely because animals have breathed or excreted near them: [nitrogen fixation](../../../ecology.md#nitrogen-fixation), [ammonification](../../../ecology.md#ammonification) and often [nitrification](../../../biology.md#nitrification) involve particular microorganisms. Plants and animals also interact through [pollination](../../../reproduction.md#pollination), [seed dispersal](../../../reproduction.md#seed-dispersal), grazing, parasitism and shelter. Such relations may benefit both partners, one partner at the other's expense, or different partners under different conditions; interdependence is not universal mutual benevolence.

A closed illuminated aquarium containing plants and animals gives an instructive experiment, provided gas exchange with air, illumination, nutrient supply and organismal amounts are specified. Dissolved [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) and [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) should be followed over light and dark periods. Survival alone is an ambiguous readout: microorganisms, stored food and air exchange may support it temporarily. A more controlled comparison includes illuminated and dark plant vessels and animal-only vessels. The expected alternation of net gas exchange makes the physiological distinction visible while exposing why no simple rule guarantees indefinite balance in any arbitrarily stocked closed jar.
