↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

**[Stomata](../../../../../stoma.md) are regulated pores of the plant epidermis; the [primordial utricle](../../../../../primordial-utricle.md) is an older name for living peripheral cell contents; the [vascular cambium](../../../../../vascular-cambium.md) is a tissue that produces secondary conducting tissues.** These are respectively an opening apparatus, part of a cell's living contents, and a region of dividing cells.

A [stoma](../../../../../stoma.md) is an aperture bounded by two [guard cells](../../../../../guard-cell.md), commonly with associated subsidiary cells. Changes of [guard cell](../../../../../guard-cell.md) [turgor pressure](../../../../../turgor-pressure.md) alter the aperture. [Stomata](../../../../../stoma.md) permit entry of [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) for [photosynthesis](../../../../../photosynthesis.md) and loss of [water vapour](../../../../../water-vapor.md) through [transpiration](../../../../../transpiration.md), while also allowing other gas exchange. Most leaves have many on their lower surface, but distribution varies, so absence from the upper surface is not a definition. A pore is not itself a minute leaf [blood vessel](../../../../../blood-vessel.md).

The [primordial utricle](../../../../../primordial-utricle.md) was distinguished in nineteenth-century microscopy as the protoplasmic layer inside the [cell wall](../../../../../cell-wall.md), especially conspicuous around the large central [vacuole](../../../../../vacuole.md) of a mature [plant cell](../../../../../plant-cell.md). In modern vocabulary this includes peripheral [cytoplasm](../../../../../cytoplasm.md) bounded externally by the [cell membrane](../../../../../cell-membrane-split.md); the vacuolar boundary is the [tonoplast](../../../../../tonoplast.md). The old term should not be reduced to a synonym of the rigid [cell wall](../../../../../cell-wall.md) or assumed to designate an independently living bag. In [plasmolysis](../../../../../plasmolysis.md), an appropriate external [hypertonic solution](../../../../../hypertonic-solution.md) draws [water](../../../../../water.md) from the cell and the living contents withdraw from the wall, making the distinction visible.[Tonicity in Living Systems; plasmolysis and Figure 5.13](../../../../../biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/5-2-passive-transport.md)

The [vascular cambium](../../../../../vascular-cambium.md) is a lateral [meristem](../../../../../meristem.md), characteristically situated between the [xylem](../../../../../xylem.md) and [phloem](../../../../../phloem.md) of a woody stem. Its divisions produce secondary [xylem](../../../../../xylem.md) towards the inside and secondary [phloem](../../../../../phloem.md) towards the outside; radial derivatives also contribute to vascular rays. Activity connects the growth of the conducting system with thickening of the axis. It is not simply a film of nutritious liquid that spontaneously turns into wood. The [cork cambium](../../../../../cork-cambium.md) is a different lateral [meristem](../../../../../meristem.md), concerned with the protective outer covering.[Secondary Growth in Stems: vascular cambium, secondary xylem/phloem and cork cambium](../../../../../biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-2-stems.md)

The course of sap can now be followed from soil to growing tissue. Root cells acquire mineral ions, and [water](../../../../../water.md) enters along a [water potential gradient](../../../../../water-potential-gradient.md). Water and dissolved minerals move into the [xylem](../../../../../xylem.md) and upwards through the root and stem to the leaves. In a transpiring plant, evaporation from moist internal leaf surfaces and diffusion out through [stomata](../../../../../stoma.md) maintain a pull on the continuous water columns. [Cohesion](../../../../../cohesion.md) between water molecules and the mechanical support of xylem walls allow tension to be transmitted. [Root pressure](../../../../../root-pressure.md) can drive exudation or guttation under some conditions, but it is not a sufficient universal explanation for supply to the tops of tall trees.

In leaves, [photosynthesis](../../../../../photosynthesis.md) incorporates [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) into organic compounds. Soluble products, especially [sucrose](../../../../../sucrose.md), enter the [phloem](../../../../../phloem.md) and move from sources to sinks, such as growing roots, buds, fruits or storage organs. Thus elaborated sap is not invariably a single downward stream: the direction depends on where a source and a sink lie. The modern [pressure-flow hypothesis](../../../../../pressure-flow-hypothesis.md) explains bulk movement through differences of [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) associated with loading and unloading solutes. Neither the ascending nor the assimilate stream forms an exact analogue of an animal's closed [blood circulation](../../../../../blood-circulation.md).[Water Potential; Transpiration; Movement of Photosynthates: the cohesion-tension and pressure-flow accounts](../../../../../biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants.md)

A short experimental history makes these distinctions concrete. In [Vegetable Staticks by Stephen Hales](../../../../../vegetable-staticks-by-stephen-hales.md) (1727), [Stephen Hales](../../../../../stephen-hales.md) measured water use and perspiration by plants and attached tubes and pressure gauges to cut vines. Exudation into a sealed vertical tube demonstrates a positive pushing pressure generated below the cut. The corresponding observation after the bleeding season differed, showing that root pushing and the supply to a leafy, transpiring shoot need not have the same explanation. Weighing a potted plant while limiting evaporation from the soil provides another balance: water lost from the system must be compared with its supply and changes of storage.[Experiments on perspiration; Chapter III, Experiments XXXIV–XXXV, pp. 100–103, vine-stump tubes and seasonal contrast](../../../../../vegetable-staticks-by-stephen-hales.md) A cut shoot taking up water is informative, but its uptake alone does not establish all the forces acting in an intact rooted plant.

For the [phloem](../../../../../phloem.md), removal of a ring of bark can interrupt assimilate transport while leaving a substantial wood pathway intact; accumulation above the interruption distinguishes the two transport systems, although injury complicates any inference about a natural circulation. In an 1866 account, terms such as crude and elaborated sap could summarize these routes. The modern quantitative [water potential](../../../../../water-potential.md) and [pressure-flow hypothesis](../../../../../pressure-flow-hypothesis.md) accounts extend beyond that historical vocabulary.
