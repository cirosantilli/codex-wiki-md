**The [Eustachian tube](../../../../../eustachian-tube.md), or [auditory tube](../../../../../eustachian-tube.md), connects the [middle ear](../../../../../middle-ear.md) with the [nasopharynx](../../../../../nasopharynx.md).** Its principal functions are ventilation and [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) equalization, clearance of middle-ear secretions, and protection against inappropriate entry of nasopharyngeal material.

Developmentally, its lumen and epithelial lining arise chiefly from the proximal part of the [tubotympanic recess](../../../../../tubotympanic-recess.md), an extension of the first [pharyngeal pouch](../../../../../pharyngeal-pouch.md). The expanded distal part contributes to the [tympanic cavity](../../../../../tympanic-cavity.md). The neighbouring first pharyngeal cleft is associated with the external ear passage and must not be substituted for the pouch as the origin of the [auditory tube](../../../../../eustachian-tube.md). Supporting [cartilage](../../../../../cartilage.md) and [connective tissue](../../../../../connective-tissue.md) develop from surrounding [mesenchyme](../../../../../mesenchyme.md). At birth the tube is shorter and more nearly horizontal; craniofacial growth lengthens it and changes its orientation. The developmental account distinguishes formation of the channel from later growth of its surrounding structures.[Middle Ear: tubotympanic recess, proximal auditory tube, distal tympanic cavity and postnatal orientation](../../../../../embryology-ear-statpearls.md)

In an adult the [auditory tube](../../../../../eustachian-tube.md) is roughly 3.5–4 cm long. From its tympanic opening in the anterior wall of the [middle ear](../../../../../middle-ear.md), it runs forwards, downwards and medially towards its pharyngeal opening in the lateral wall of the [nasopharynx](../../../../../nasopharynx.md), behind the nasal cavity. Its lateral third is bony, lying in the temporal bone; the medial two thirds are chiefly cartilaginous with a membranous part. The narrow junction is the tubal isthmus. The cartilaginous portion is normally collapsed, so an open passage for sound or reflux does not continuously connect [pharynx](../../../../../pharynx.md) and [middle ear](../../../../../middle-ear.md).

The bony part is closely related medially to the canal of the [internal carotid artery](../../../../../internal-carotid-artery.md); the canal for the [tensor tympani muscle](../../../../../tensor-tympani-muscle.md) lies above it. The medial cartilaginous part rests towards the skull base. Its pharyngeal end makes the tubal prominence, or [torus tubarius](../../../../../torus-tubarius.md), near the [soft palate](../../../../../soft-palate.md). The [tensor veli palatini muscle](../../../../../tensor-veli-palatini-muscle.md) acts on the cartilaginous and membranous tube and is the main active dilator. The [levator veli palatini muscle](../../../../../levator-veli-palatini-muscle.md) passes beneath it and participates in the palatal and tubal functional arrangement. Swallowing and yawning intermittently open the tube.[Eustachian Tube; Muscles: bony/cartilaginous portions, opening muscles and three principal functions](../../../../../middle-ear-anatomy-and-physiology-statpearls.md)

Opening permits gas transfer to compensate for a difference between middle-ear and ambient [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). If the tube remains obstructed, absorption of gas from the cavity can make middle-ear [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) fall; movement of the [tympanic membrane](../../../../../tympanic-membrane.md) and sound transmission then change. Clearance is helped by the mucociliary lining, which carries material towards the [nasopharynx](../../../../../nasopharynx.md). Closure between openings limits reflux and transmission of nasopharyngeal pressure disturbances. A tube that remains persistently open therefore also functions abnormally; more openness is not invariably better.

The physiological point can be demonstrated by observing the [tympanic membrane](../../../../../tympanic-membrane.md) while swallowing during a change of external [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), or by recording the membrane's mechanical response over a controlled range of ear-canal [pressures](../../../../../pressure-split.md) using [tympanometry](../../../../../tympanometry.md). These observations examine pressure communication and membrane mobility, not merely whether air can be forcibly driven through a passage. The relatively immature geometry and opening mechanism in young children helps explain their vulnerability to middle-ear fluid retention, but geometry alone is not an exhaustive account of infection risk. The anatomical structures were recognizable in 1866; the modern measurement method should not be mistaken for apparatus supplied by the examination.
