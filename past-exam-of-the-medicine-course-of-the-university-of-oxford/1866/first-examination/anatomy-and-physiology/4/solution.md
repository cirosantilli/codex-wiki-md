<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**The [liver](../../../../../liver.md) has a substantial normal range of size, determined by age, body dimensions, nutritional state and its variable contents; there is no universal absolute minimum and maximum valid for every healthy person.** One must also distinguish true organ volume from the position of its lower border, which moves with the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md) during [breathing](../../../../../pulmonary-ventilation.md).

An ordinary adult [liver](../../../../../liver.md) is on the order of 1.5 kg and roughly a few percent of [body mass](../../../../../body-mass.md), but neither figure is a diagnostic boundary. As an explicitly modern example, an autopsy study of adult men reported a mean hepatic mass of 1561 g and observed values from 838 to 2584 g in its selected population. Those are sample observations, not a declaration that every [liver](../../../../../liver.md) inside those limits is healthy or that every one outside is diseased. A newer large-sample study similarly found an association of liver mass with [body mass](../../../../../body-mass.md); much of an apparent sex difference disappears when body size is taken into account.[Abstract and reported organ-mass data; selected male autopsy population, 2012](../../../../../normal-organ-weights-in-men-part-ii-the-brain-lungs-liver-spleen-and-kidneys.md)[Results and Conclusions: liver mass, body size and sex after adjustment](../../../../../updating-normal-organ-weights-using-a-large-current-sample-database.md)

Age changes the proportion as well as the absolute size. The fetal and infant [liver](../../../../../liver.md) is relatively large; the adult organ is smaller in proportion to the whole body. Its fetal role in [haematopoiesis](../../../../../haematopoiesis.md) and subsequent changes of growth and function make an adult mass standard inappropriate for an infant. Constitutional body size and normal anatomical shape also vary: a long lower projection is not necessarily the same thing as an enlarged total organ.

Reversible differences within one person arise because the [liver](../../../../../liver.md) is a store and a vascular organ:

- [Glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md) is accumulated after suitable feeding and mobilized during fasting and exercise. Its storage is associated with [water](../../../../../water.md), so changing [glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md) stores changes more than the dry mass of the stored [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) units.
- Hepatic [lipids](../../../../../lipid.md) and their turnover also contribute to variation. This does not make substantial [hepatic steatosis](../../../../../hepatic-steatosis.md) a normal consequence of every meal or justify treating pathological fatty enlargement as physiological.
- The amount of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) contained in the [hepatic sinusoids](../../../../../hepatic-sinusoid.md) and larger vessels can change with circulation and posture. Physiological vascular filling must be distinguished from persistent congestion caused by [heart failure](../../../../../heart-failure.md).
- Hydration and longer-term nutritional state influence the organ and its stores. Severe wasting or a persistent progressive enlargement requires a different explanation from a reversible difference between standardized fed and fasted observations.

A meal therefore changes hepatic activity and [splanchnic blood flow](../../../../../splanchnic-blood-flow.md) without requiring a large immediate increase in total liver volume. In a modern paired study of 20 healthy volunteers, [single-photon emission computed tomography](../../../../../single-photon-emission-computed-tomography.md) before and after a standardized meal showed a nonsignificant 1.5% decrease in estimated liver volume. The authors judged the change to be within methodological error. This is a useful check against assuming that greater digestive activity proves gross swelling.[Abstract: standardized meal, paired measurements and liver-volume result, 1997](../../../../../effect-of-food-intake-on-liver-and-spleen-volume-assessment-with-single-photon-emission-computed-tomography.md)

The experimental approach is to compare like with like: specify age and [body mass](../../../../../body-mass.md), use a fixed interval after food, standardize hydration, posture and respiratory phase, and distinguish a volume measurement from a palpable edge or a single linear dimension. [Magnetic resonance imaging](../../../../../magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) can measure volume while [magnetic resonance spectroscopy](../../../../../magnetic-resonance-spectroscopy.md) measures [glycogen](../../../../../glycogen.md) or [lipid](../../../../../lipid.md) signals; the two observations answer different questions. The size of an excised organ additionally depends on how much [blood](../../../../../blood.md) remains in it. The physiological point appropriate to the 1866 question is reversible variation with growth, alimentation and vascular filling. Modern imaging results refine that point; they are not numerical standards that the historical exam could have expected.
