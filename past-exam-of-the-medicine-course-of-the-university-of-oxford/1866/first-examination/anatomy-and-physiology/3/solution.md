**The four names identify distinct pathways: [chorda tympani](../../../../../chorda-tympani.md), the sensory [buccal nerve](../../../../../buccal-nerve.md), the [radial nerve](../../../../../radial-nerve.md) formerly called musculospiral, and the [obturator nerve](../../../../../obturator-nerve.md).** Origin must include the parent [nerve](../../../../../nerve.md) or [nerve plexus](../../../../../nerve-plexus.md), while the function must follow the actual fibre supply rather than the name of a structure crossed.

**[Chorda tympani](../../../../../chorda-tympani.md).** This branch leaves the [facial nerve](../../../../../facial-nerve.md) in its descending facial canal before the [stylomastoid foramen](../../../../../stylomastoid-foramen.md). It enters the [tympanic cavity](../../../../../tympanic-cavity.md) from behind, passes forwards between the [malleus](../../../../../malleus.md) and [incus](../../../../../incus.md) close to the inner surface of the [tympanic membrane](../../../../../tympanic-membrane.md), and leaves through the [petrotympanic fissure](../../../../../petrotympanic-fissure.md). In the [infratemporal fossa](../../../../../infratemporal-fossa.md) it joins the [lingual nerve](../../../../../lingual-nerve.md), a branch of the mandibular division of the [trigeminal nerve](../../../../../trigeminal-nerve.md). It then travels with that carrier towards the [tongue](../../../../../tongue.md) and [submandibular ganglion](../../../../../submandibular-ganglion.md).

The [chorda tympani](../../../../../chorda-tympani.md) carries taste from the anterior two thirds of the [tongue](../../../../../tongue.md), with sensory cell bodies in the [geniculate ganglion](../../../../../geniculate-ganglion.md) and central connections to the [nucleus of the solitary tract](../../../../../nucleus-of-the-solitary-tract.md). It also carries preganglionic [parasympathetic](../../../../../parasympathetic.md) fibres from the [superior salivatory nucleus](../../../../../superior-salivatory-nucleus.md) towards the [submandibular ganglion](../../../../../submandibular-ganglion.md); postganglionic fibres supply the [submandibular gland](../../../../../submandibular-gland.md) and [sublingual gland](../../../../../sublingual-gland.md). It does not provide the general touch supply of that part of the [tongue](../../../../../tongue.md); that travels through the [lingual nerve](../../../../../lingual-nerve.md)'s [trigeminal nerve](../../../../../trigeminal-nerve.md) pathway. Taste and tactile testing can consequently distinguish two functions that briefly share one peripheral trunk.[Introduction; Structure and Function: facial origin, middle-ear passage, lingual connection and gustatory/secretomotor fibres](../../../../../anatomy-head-and-neck-chorda-tympani-statpearls.md)

**[Buccal nerve](../../../../../buccal-nerve.md).** In the usual anatomical meaning intended by this list, this is the long [buccal nerve](../../../../../buccal-nerve.md) from the anterior division of the mandibular [trigeminal nerve](../../../../../trigeminal-nerve.md). It commonly emerges between the heads of the [lateral pterygoid muscle](../../../../../lateral-pterygoid-muscle.md), descends near the anterior border of the mandibular ramus and the [temporalis muscle](../../../../../temporalis-muscle.md) insertion, and passes towards the cheek. Its branches reach cheek [skin](../../../../../skin.md), the [buccal mucosa](../../../../../buccal-mucosa.md), and the buccal [gingiva](../../../../../gingiva.md) beside the lower molars. It is principally sensory. The motor supply of the [buccinator muscle](../../../../../buccinator-muscle.md) comes from the [facial nerve](../../../../../facial-nerve.md), whose buccal branches are a different set of [nerves](../../../../../nerve.md). Thus the ability to hold the cheek against the teeth and sensation in the cheek are not tests of the same efferent pathway.[Introduction; Structure and Function: mandibular origin, relations to lateral pterygoid and sensory distribution](../../../../../anatomy-head-and-neck-buccal-nerve-statpearls.md)

**[Radial nerve](../../../../../radial-nerve.md), historically musculospiral.** This is the major continuation of the posterior cord of the [brachial plexus](../../../../../brachial-plexus.md), usually containing fibres from C5–T1. It passes from the [axilla](../../../../../axilla.md) into the posterior arm, accompanying the [profunda brachii artery](../../../../../profunda-brachii-artery.md) in the [radial groove](../../../../../radial-groove.md) of the [humerus](../../../../../humerus.md). It pierces the lateral intermuscular septum and reaches the anterior compartment between [brachialis](../../../../../brachialis.md) and [brachioradialis](../../../../../brachioradialis.md). Near the lateral elbow it divides into a superficial sensory branch and a deep motor branch. The latter passes through the [supinator muscle](../../../../../supinator-muscle.md) and continues as the [posterior interosseous nerve](../../../../../posterior-interosseous-nerve.md).

The [radial nerve](../../../../../radial-nerve.md) supplies [triceps brachii](../../../../../triceps-brachii.md) and [anconeus](../../../../../anconeus.md), then [brachioradialis](../../../../../brachioradialis.md), the radial wrist extensors and, through its deep continuation, the principal finger and thumb extensors and associated posterior forearm [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md). Its sensory branches supply posterior arm and forearm regions and part of the dorsolateral hand. A lesion's level matters: an arm lesion can impair wrist and finger extension, while an isolated superficial branch lesion is sensory and does not itself cause a wrist drop. Conversely, a [posterior interosseous nerve](../../../../../posterior-interosseous-nerve.md) lesion chiefly affects motor function. The fingertip pads are not the characteristic autonomous sensory territory of the superficial [radial nerve](../../../../../radial-nerve.md).[Structure and Function; Sensory; Motor; Muscles; Physiologic Variants](../../../../../anatomy-shoulder-and-upper-limb-radial-nerve-statpearls.md)

**[Obturator nerve](../../../../../obturator-nerve.md).** It arises from the anterior divisions of the anterior rami of L2–L4 in the [lumbar plexus](../../../../../lumbar-plexus.md). It emerges from the medial border of [psoas major](../../../../../psoas-major.md), crosses the pelvic side wall and leaves through the [obturator canal](../../../../../obturator-canal.md) with the [obturator vessels](../../../../../obturator-vessels.md). Its anterior and posterior divisions characteristically run on opposite sides of [adductor brevis](../../../../../adductor-brevis.md), although the level of division varies. The anterior division supplies [adductor longus](../../../../../adductor-longus.md), [gracilis](../../../../../gracilis.md) and commonly [adductor brevis](../../../../../adductor-brevis.md); the posterior division supplies [obturator externus](../../../../../obturator-externus.md) and the adductor portion of [adductor magnus](../../../../../adductor-magnus.md), with variable additional contributions. The hamstring portion of [adductor magnus](../../../../../adductor-magnus.md) has a different supply. Cutaneous supply to a variable medial-thigh patch and articular branches to the [hip](../../../../../hip.md) and [knee](../../../../../knee.md) supplement its principal motor role in thigh adduction. [Obturator internus](../../../../../obturator-internus.md) is supplied by its own sacral-plexus [nerve](../../../../../nerve.md), not by the [obturator nerve](../../../../../obturator-nerve.md) merely because the names resemble one another.[Nerves: L2–L4 supply of obturator externus versus separate supply of obturator internus](../../../../../anatomy-abdomen-and-pelvis-obturator-muscles-statpearls.md)[MeSH definition: lumbar origin, thigh adductors and medial-thigh sensation](../../../../../obturator-nerve-by-the-national-library-of-medicine.md)
