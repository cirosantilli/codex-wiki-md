<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**In a suitably dilute, nonoxidizing [chemical acid](../../../../../acid-chemistry.md), [hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md) precipitates the sufficiently insoluble sulfides, including those of [copper](../../../../../copper.md), [lead](../../../../../lead.md), [Mercury](../../../../../mercury-planet.md), [bismuth](../../../../../bismuth.md), [cadmium](../../../../../cadmium.md), [arsenic](../../../../../arsenic.md), [antimony](../../../../../antimony.md) and [tin](../../../../../tin.md).** “Sulphuretted hydrogen” is the historical name for [hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md), $\mathrm H_2\mathrm S$. Oxidation state and acidity must be specified: the statement is not independent of every possible [chemical solution](../../../../../solution-chemistry.md) condition.

| Representative dissolved species | Principal precipitate | Usual appearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Copper(II)](../../../../../copper-ii.md) | $\mathrm{CuS}$ | Black |
| [Lead(II)](../../../../../lead-ii.md) | $\mathrm{PbS}$ | Black |
| [Mercury(II)](../../../../../mercury-ii.md) | $\mathrm{HgS}$ | Black when freshly precipitated |
| [Bismuth(III)](../../../../../bismuth-iii.md) | $\mathrm{Bi}_2\mathrm S_3$ | Brown-black |
| [Cadmium(II)](../../../../../cadmium-ii.md) | $\mathrm{CdS}$ | Yellow |
| [Arsenic(III)](../../../../../arsenic-iii.md) | $\mathrm{As}_2\mathrm S_3$ | Yellow |
| [Antimony(III)](../../../../../antimony-iii.md) | $\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3$ | Orange |
| [Tin(II)](../../../../../tin-ii.md) | $\mathrm{SnS}$ | Brown or brown-black |
| [Tin(IV)](../../../../../tin-iv.md) | $\mathrm{SnS}_2$ | Yellow |

[Arsenic](../../../../../arsenic.md) is now classed as a [metalloid](../../../../../metalloid.md); the historical analytical grouping was not a rigorous modern division into metals and nonmetals. Dissolved [silver](../../../../../silver.md) also gives black $\mathrm{Ag}_2\mathrm S$ in acidic conditions. It is often absent from the conventional second analytical group because a preceding [hydrochloric acid](../../../../../hydrochloric-acid.md) treatment has already removed it as [silver chloride](../../../../../silver-chloride.md). Suitable [gold](../../../../../gold.md) and [platinum](../../../../../platinum.md) solutions can likewise yield dark sulfide precipitates, although their speciation and reduction chemistry make a simple universal colour rule inappropriate. Thus an analytical group is a separation convention, not a list of every ion that can possibly react.

For a divalent [metal ion](../../../../../metal-ion.md), a useful net balance is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm M^{2+}+\mathrm H_2\mathrm S\longrightarrow\mathrm{MS}\downarrow+2\mathrm H^+.}
$$

The selectivity follows from two coupled [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md) conditions. [Hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md) is a [weak acid](../../../../../weak-acid.md); increasing $[\mathrm H^+]$ suppresses its ionization. Approximately,

$$
[\mathrm S^{2-}]=\frac{K_{a1}K_{a2}[\mathrm H_2\mathrm S]}{[\mathrm H^+]^2}.
$$

Even this small sulfide activity can exceed the [solubility product](../../../../../solubility-product.md) of very insoluble sulfides. More soluble sulfides, such as those of [zinc](../../../../../zinc.md) and [manganese](../../../../../manganese.md), generally require less acidic or alkaline conditions. Consequently an excessively strong [chemical acid](../../../../../acid-chemistry.md), complex formation or an oxidizing medium can change the result. Colours alone are preliminary indications, not final identification.[Experiment 2–3, Introduction and Group II discussion, printed II & III-1–3 and II & III-9–10](../../../../../qualitative-analysis-of-metal-ions-in-solution-by-williams-college.md)

The traditional preparation treats [iron(II) sulfide](../../../../../iron-ii-sulfide.md) with a dilute nonoxidizing [chemical acid](../../../../../acid-chemistry.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{FeS}+2\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow\mathrm{FeCl}_2+\mathrm H_2\mathrm S.}
$$

A gas-generating flask with controlled acid entry and a delivery outlet conducts the product to the test solution. The apparatus must permit gas flow and contain the effluent rather than accumulating [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) in a sealed vessel. [Sulfuric acid](../../../../../sulfuric-acid.md) in dilute form can replace [hydrochloric acid](../../../../../hydrochloric-acid.md); concentrated oxidizing acids are unsuitable for this simple preparation. [Hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md) is acutely toxic, and loss of odour perception makes smell an unreliable warning: the historical apparatus describes a laboratory principle, not a household test.[Physical Description, odour fatigue, Symptoms and Target Organs](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide-by-the-national-institute-for-occupational-safety-and-health.md)
