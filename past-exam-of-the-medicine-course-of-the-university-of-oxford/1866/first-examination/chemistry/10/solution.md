**The historical name [fermentation](../../../../../fermentation.md) covered several transformations of organic matter: alcoholic, lactic, butyric and acetous changes, as well as some processes now classified separately.** In modern biochemistry, [fermentation](../../../../../fermentation.md) more narrowly means energy-yielding metabolism that regenerates oxidized cofactors without requiring an external respiratory electron acceptor. The older use of the word should not be forced into that modern definition.

In [alcoholic fermentation](../../../../../ethanol-fermentation.md), suitable [yeast](../../../../../yeast.md) converts a fermentable [sugar](../../../../../sugar.md) into [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) and [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md). For [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md), the principal net balance is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm C_6\mathrm H_{12}\mathrm O_6\longrightarrow2\mathrm C_2\mathrm H_5\mathrm{OH}+2\mathrm{CO}_2.}
$$

A vessel with an outlet for the evolved [gas](../../../../../gas.md) and a separate receiver illustrates the change: test the [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) with [limewater](../../../../../limewater.md) and identify the volatile alcohol in a separated distillate. Gas evolution alone does not prove that the liquid contains [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md). Actual yeast cultures also make biomass and minor products; the [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md) describes the principal conversion. In modern terms, [glycolysis](../../../../../glycolysis.md) supplies [ATP](../../../../../adenosine-triphosphate.md), and the reduction leading to [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) regenerates [NAD+](../../../../../nad-plus.md) from [NADH](../../../../../nadh.md).

In [lactic acid fermentation](../../../../../lactic-acid-fermentation.md), an ideal homolactic balance is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm C_6\mathrm H_{12}\mathrm O_6\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH(OH)COOH}.}
$$

The souring of suitable carbohydrate-containing materials, including milk, supplied a familiar historical example. Hydrolysis of [lactose](../../../../../lactose.md) supplies monosaccharides, while acid production lowers the [pH](../../../../../ph.md) and can precipitate [casein](../../../../../casein.md). [Casein](../../../../../casein.md) coagulation is a consequence of the changed conditions, not evidence that the protein has itself been converted into [lactic acid](../../../../../lactic-acid.md). Some organisms instead make additional products, so not every lactic fermentation obeys only this homolactic balance.

In [butyric acid fermentation](../../../../../butyric-acid-fermentation.md), carbohydrates or [lactate](../../../../../lactate.md) can give [butyric acid](../../../../../butyric-acid.md), [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) and [molecular hydrogen](../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md). An idealized balance from [lactic acid](../../../../../lactic-acid.md) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm C_3\mathrm H_6\mathrm O_3\longrightarrow\mathrm C_4\mathrm H_8\mathrm O_2+2\mathrm{CO}_2+2\mathrm H_2.}
$$

It contrasts with the simple homolactic balance because both gaseous products and a different organic acid appear. Mixed cultures and alternative pathways can alter the product distribution; odour is neither a sufficiently specific nor a necessary laboratory test.

[Acetous fermentation](../../../../../acetic-acid-fermentation.md) denotes the conversion of [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) into [acetic acid](../../../../../acetic-acid.md), as in vinegar production:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm C_2\mathrm H_5\mathrm{OH}+\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

It depends on [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and is now understood as an aerobic microbial oxidation, rather than a fermentation in the narrow biochemical sense. This explains why access to [air](../../../../../air.md) matters differently here than in the main alcohol-producing stage.

Other older extensions of the word include **[saccharification](../../../../../saccharification.md) or diastatic changes**, such as the [hydrolysis](../../../../../hydrolysis.md) of [starch](../../../../../starch.md) into smaller sugars by malt enzymes, and **[sugar inversion](../../../../../sugar-inversion.md)**, in which [sucrose](../../../../../sucrose.md) takes up [water](../../../../../water.md) to form [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) and [fructose](../../../../../fructose.md):

$$
\mathrm C_{12}\mathrm H_{22}\mathrm O_{11}+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O\longrightarrow2\mathrm C_6\mathrm H_{12}\mathrm O_6.
$$

The two hexose products have the same elemental formula but different structures. Such hydrolyses prepare substrates for later metabolism; they do not by themselves constitute the alcohol-producing conversion. **[Viscous fermentation](../../../../../viscous-fermentation.md) or [mucilaginous fermentation](../../../../../viscous-fermentation.md)** described transformations yielding gum-like material and sometimes [mannitol](../../../../../mannitol.md); it is an operational historical category without one universal balanced [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md). **Putrefactive fermentation** referred to the decomposition of nitrogen-containing organic matter, including [proteolysis](../../../../../proteolysis.md) and subsequent reactions producing [Ammonia](../../../../../ammonia.md), amines and sometimes [hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md). It likewise has no single formula covering every mixed substrate and product.

The unifying historical interest is that small amounts of a “ferment” bring about major changes in a large amount of organic substrate. Modern explanation distinguishes organisms, enzymes and metabolic pathways. The later demonstration of [fermentation](../../../../../fermentation.md) in cell-free yeast juice showed that alcoholic conversion need not require intact living yeast cells in the reacting liquid; that result belongs to 1897, not to knowledge to demand of an 1866 candidate.[1897 primary report, preparation of yeast juice and fermentation without intact yeast cells](../../../../../cell-free-fermentation-by-eduard-buchner.md)
