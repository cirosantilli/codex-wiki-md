<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**[Oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) can be obtained by decomposing an oxygen-rich [chemical compound](../../../../../chemical-compound.md), or by separating it from [air](../../../../../air.md).** For the historical laboratory, heating [potassium chlorate](../../../../../potassium-chlorate.md), heating certain [metal oxides](../../../../../metal-oxide.md), and [electrolysis](../../../../../electrolysis.md) of [water](../../../../../water.md) are useful examples. The substance collected is [molecular oxygen](../../../../../molecular-oxygen.md), $\mathrm O_2$.

A common preparation heats [potassium chlorate](../../../../../potassium-chlorate.md), often with [manganese dioxide](../../../../../manganese-dioxide.md) as a [catalyst](../../../../../catalyst.md):

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{KClO}_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}2\mathrm{KCl}+3\mathrm O_2.}
$$

A heat-resistant vessel and delivery tube conduct the evolved [gas](../../../../../gas.md) to an inverted collecting vessel over [water](../../../../../water.md), after the initial displaced [air](../../../../../air.md) has been excluded. [Oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) is only slightly soluble in [water](../../../../../water.md), making this collection principle useful. The [manganese dioxide](../../../../../manganese-dioxide.md) changes the reaction rate and required heating conditions; it is not the net source of the [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) in this catalysed balance. Without the [catalyst](../../../../../catalyst.md), [potassium perchlorate](../../../../../potassium-perchlorate.md) can occur as an intermediate.

Two older preparations use heated [metal oxides](../../../../../metal-oxide.md):

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{HgO}\xrightarrow{\Delta}2\mathrm{Hg}+\mathrm O_2,}
$$



$$
\boxed{4\mathrm{MnO}_2\xrightarrow{\text{strong heating}}2\mathrm{Mn}_2\mathrm O_3+\mathrm O_2.}
$$

The first explains the historical decomposition of [mercury(II) oxide](../../../../../mercury-ii-oxide.md), with metallic [Mercury](../../../../../mercury-planet.md) deposited on cooler parts of the apparatus. It is historical chemistry, not a preferred present-day teaching preparation. The second is one stage in the reduction of [manganese dioxide](../../../../../manganese-dioxide.md); at still higher [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) further oxygen loss can give $\mathrm{Mn}_3\mathrm O_4$, so the oxide residue depends on the conditions. Strong heating of [potassium nitrate](../../../../../potassium-nitrate.md) provides another net balance:

$$
2\mathrm{KNO}_3\longrightarrow2\mathrm{KNO}_2+\mathrm O_2.
$$

In [electrolysis](../../../../../electrolysis.md), suitable separated [electrodes](../../../../../electrode.md) collect [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) at the [anode](../../../../../anode.md) and [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) at the [cathode](../../../../../cathode.md). With a suitable electrolyte and electrodes, the net [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O\longrightarrow2\mathrm H_2+\mathrm O_2.}
$$

The gases must be kept in separate collecting spaces if the experiment is to demonstrate their separate identities and the approximately $2:1$ [volume](../../../../../volume.md) ratio. Choice of electrolyte matters: a chloride-containing electrolyte can give [chlorine](../../../../../chlorine.md) instead of clean [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) evolution.

For a modern small-scale comparison, catalytic decomposition of [hydrogen peroxide](../../../../../hydrogen-peroxide.md) gives

$$
2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O+\mathrm O_2.
$$

This is a different feedstock from [potassium chlorate](../../../../../potassium-chlorate.md). Large-scale separation of [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) from [air](../../../../../air.md) by cryogenic fractionation belongs to later industrial practice and should not be substituted for an 1866 laboratory answer. A collected sample can be tested by its vigorous support of [combustion](../../../../../combustion.md), conventionally the relighting of a glowing splint; this observation identifies a property, while the preparation and exclusion of interfering gases establish the sample's provenance.
