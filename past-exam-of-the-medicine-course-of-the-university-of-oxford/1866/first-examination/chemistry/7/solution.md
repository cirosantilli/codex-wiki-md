<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

**The relevant historical route starts with phosphate-rich bone ash, converts it into a reducible phosphate preparation, and heats that preparation with [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) to obtain elemental [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md).** Bone contains [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) in [phosphate](../../../../../phosphate.md), not as free pieces of [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) awaiting extraction.

Burning cleaned bones removes most organic matter and leaves [bone ash](../../../../../bone-ash.md), rich in calcium phosphates. Although natural bone mineral is not pure $\mathrm{Ca}_3(\mathrm{PO}_4)_2$, that formula is a useful historical reaction model. Treatment with [sulfuric acid](../../../../../sulfuric-acid.md) forms sparingly soluble [calcium sulfate](../../../../../calcium-sulfate.md) and a soluble acid phosphate fraction. With partial removal of [calcium](../../../../../calcium.md), an idealized balance is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Ca}_3(\mathrm{PO}_4)_2+2\mathrm H_2\mathrm{SO}_4\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ca(H}_2\mathrm{PO}_4)_2+2\mathrm{CaSO}_4.}
$$

Separate the sulfate-rich residue, concentrate the phosphate-bearing liquor, and heat the resulting material with [charcoal](../../../../../charcoal.md) in a retort. Dehydration and high-temperature [reduction](../../../../../reduction-chemistry.md) produce phosphorus vapour together with carbon oxides, while a calcium phosphate residue remains. One overall balance for the acid-phosphate route is

$$
\boxed{3\mathrm{Ca(H}_2\mathrm{PO}_4)_2+10\mathrm C\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ca}_3(\mathrm{PO}_4)_2+\mathrm P_4+10\mathrm{CO}+6\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

This is a summed process balance; it does not imply that the hydrated phosphate survives unchanged until one elementary reaction suddenly occurs. It also makes clear that the partial-acid route leaves some of the original [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) in the calcium phosphate residue. Contemporary descriptions distinguish this route from treating the ash with enough [sulfuric acid](../../../../../sulfuric-acid.md) to give a more fully acidified phosphorus-bearing liquid.[1868 printing, Lesson XV, printed pp. 131–133: bone ash, acid phosphate and charcoal reduction; later printing used as historical description, not a pharmacopoeia](../../../../../lessons-in-elementary-chemistry-by-henry-enfield-roscoe.md)

The retort outlet leads to a water-covered receiver: phosphorus vapour condenses and is protected from [air](../../../../../air.md). The collected product is principally [white phosphorus](../../../../../white-phosphorus.md), with molecular form $\mathrm P_4$. Its readiness to oxidize and ignite is why collection under [water](../../../../../water.md) belongs to the explanation of the apparatus. This historical product must be distinguished from [red phosphorus](../../../../../red-phosphorus.md), obtained by a subsequent controlled transformation rather than directly identified with the freshly condensed product.

For comparison, the well-known silica-assisted phosphate-reduction balance is

$$
2\mathrm{Ca}_3(\mathrm{PO}_4)_2+6\mathrm{SiO}_2+10\mathrm C\longrightarrow\mathrm P_4+6\mathrm{CaSiO}_3+10\mathrm{CO}.
$$

Here [silica](../../../../../silica.md) binds [calcium](../../../../../calcium.md) into a silicate-rich residue. Modern electric-furnace manufacture uses this principle; describing an electric furnace as the ordinary 1866 apparatus would be anachronistic. The historical answer is the sequence **[bone ash](../../../../../bone-ash.md) → acid phosphate preparation → [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) reduction in a retort → condensation of [phosphorus](../../../../../phosphorus.md) under [water](../../../../../water.md)**.
