<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**[Potassium iodide](../../../../../potassium-iodide.md) is prepared by converting [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) into [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md), then obtaining a pure crystalline [potassium](../../../../../potassium.md) salt.** One chemical route uses [potassium hydroxide](../../../../../potassium-hydroxide.md); an alternative first forms [iron(II) iodide](../../../../../iron-ii-iodide.md). The account here describes the chemical operations and their balances, without assigning a later pharmacopoeial formula to 1866.

In the alkaline route, [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) reacts with hot [potassium hydroxide](../../../../../potassium-hydroxide.md) to give a mixture of [potassium iodide](../../../../../potassium-iodide.md) and [potassium iodate](../../../../../potassium-iodate.md):

$$
\boxed{3\mathrm I_2+6\mathrm{KOH}\longrightarrow5\mathrm{KI}+\mathrm{KIO}_3+3\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

This is [disproportionation](../../../../../disproportionation.md): [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) goes from [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md) $0$ to both $-1$ and $+5$. Merely evaporating the solution would leave the unwanted [iodate](../../../../../iodate.md) with the [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md). Heating the dried salts with [charcoal](../../../../../charcoal.md) reduces the [iodate](../../../../../iodate.md) to [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md). A limiting balance with [carbon monoxide](../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) as the carbon oxide product is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{KIO}_3+3\mathrm C\longrightarrow\mathrm{KI}+3\mathrm{CO}.}
$$

The carbon oxides can depend on the reaction conditions; the indispensable chemical point is removal of the iodate's [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) without losing its [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md). Extraction with [water](../../../../../water.md), filtration of insoluble residue, concentration and crystallization then recover [potassium iodide](../../../../../potassium-iodide.md). A modern USDA technical evaluation explicitly describes this same reaction sequence; it is corroboration of the chemistry, not evidence that its modern document was the 1866 official recipe.[2011 report, printed pp. 5–6, Evaluation Question 1, method (a)](../../../../../potassium-iodide-technical-evaluation-by-united-states-department-of-agriculture.md)

In the alternative iron route,

$$
\mathrm{Fe}+\mathrm I_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{FeI}_2,
$$

followed by

$$
\mathrm{FeI}_2+\mathrm K_2\mathrm{CO}_3\longrightarrow2\mathrm{KI}+\mathrm{FeCO}_3\downarrow.
$$

The insoluble iron-containing material is separated and the [potassium iodide](../../../../../potassium-iodide.md) recovered from the liquid. Oxidation of the ferrous precipitate during working can alter its final form, so the displayed [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md) is the ideal metathesis balance rather than a guarantee that every collected particle remains pure [iron(II) carbonate](../../../../../iron-ii-carbonate.md).

Pure [potassium iodide](../../../../../potassium-iodide.md) is a colourless or white crystalline ionic [solid](../../../../../solid.md), commonly in cubic crystals, readily soluble in [water](../../../../../water.md) and less soluble in [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md). It has no intrinsic brown iodine colour. Its aqueous solution contains [potassium](../../../../../potassium.md) [ions](../../../../../ion.md) and [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) [ions](../../../../../ion.md). Free [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) dissolves in the presence of [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) through [triiodide](../../../../../triiodide.md) formation,

$$
\mathrm I_2+\mathrm I^-\rightleftharpoons\mathrm I_3^-.
$$

With [silver nitrate](../../../../../silver-nitrate.md), [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) gives yellow [silver iodide](../../../../../silver-iodide.md), sparingly soluble in [water](../../../../../water.md) and resistant to ordinary aqueous [Ammonia](../../../../../ammonia.md):

$$
\mathrm{Ag}^++\mathrm I^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{AgI}\downarrow.
$$

A little [chlorine](../../../../../chlorine.md) liberates [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md), which gives the characteristic colour with [starch](../../../../../starch.md):

$$
\mathrm{Cl}_2+2\mathrm I^-\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Cl}^-+\mathrm I_2.
$$

By contrast, [iodate](../../../../../iodate.md) contamination can liberate [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) simply on acidification in the presence of [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md):

$$
\mathrm{IO}_3^-+5\mathrm I^-+6\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow3\mathrm I_2+3\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.
$$

That distinction explains why removal of the [iodate](../../../../../iodate.md) is part of preparing a well-defined product, rather than an optional cosmetic step. These are chemical properties and recognition principles, not advice to administer the salt.
