↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

A [free reed](../../../../../free-reed.md) is a thin elastic tongue fixed at one end over a closely fitting opening in a rigid frame. A pressure difference drives an airflow; displacement of the tongue alters the available opening, and the changing aerodynamic force can sustain its vibration against elastic restoring force and damping. Unlike a beating reed, the tongue passes through the opening without regularly striking its rim. The air supply provides the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md); the reed's dimensions and stiffness largely set the pitch, while its enclosure and any attached pipe modify the response and radiated [sound waves](../../../../../acoustic-wave.md).

The useful analogy with [phonation](../../../../../phonation.md) is an airflow-driven valve coupled to an elastic oscillator. Air from the [lungs](../../../../../lung.md) passes between the [vocal folds](../../../../../vocal-fold.md) in the [larynx](../../../../../larynx.md). Their motion periodically modulates the flow, generating a rich [sound spectrum](../../../../../sound-spectrum.md); the [pharynx](../../../../../pharynx.md), mouth and nasal passages filter it, while the [tongue](../../../../../tongue.md) and lips help form speech sounds. The [vocal folds](../../../../../vocal-fold.md) are paired living tissues with adjustable tension and geometry, rather than a single metal tongue. Their oscillation should not be explained as a passive string struck repeatedly by air, nor does the free-reed analogy establish every detail of tissue motion. [“What is voice?” and the labelled structures of voice production](../../../../../taking-care-of-your-voice-by-the-national-institute-on-deafness-and-other-communication-disorders.md)

Changing vocal-fold tension and effective vibrating mass changes the source pitch. Changing the resonating passages changes its spectral envelope and the vowel quality. Those are different controls: a singer can change pitch while retaining a recognisable vowel, or change vowel while sustaining approximately the same pitch.
