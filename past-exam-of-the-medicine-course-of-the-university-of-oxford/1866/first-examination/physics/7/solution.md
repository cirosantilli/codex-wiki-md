<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

An optically active [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) solution rotates the direction of [linear polarization](../../../../../linear-polarization.md). With monochromatic illumination, rotating the [polarimeter analyzer](../../../../../polarimeter-analyzer.md) again produces a maximum and an extinction, but at angles shifted from the water-filled reference tube. In an ideal transparent sample, [Malus's law](../../../../../malus-s-law.md) gives

$$
I(\phi)=I_0\cos^2(\phi-\alpha),\qquad \boxed{\alpha=[\alpha]^T_\lambda\ell c.}
$$

The [specific rotation](../../../../../specific-rotation.md) $[\alpha]^T_\lambda$ is defined for a stated [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md), solvent and concentration convention. With $\ell$ in decimetres and $c$ in grams per millilitre, the conventional numerical value of $[\alpha]$ must use the matching units. A broadband source generally gives coloured appearances because the rotation varies with [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md); one analyser angle need not extinguish every colour.

[Biot's saccharimeter](../../../../../biot-s-saccharimeter.md) is an angular [polarimeter](../../../../../polarimeter.md): a polarizing element, a tube of accurately known optical path length holding the sample between plane end windows, and a rotatable analysing element with an angular scale. The original arrangement used a black-glass polarizing mirror and a calcite analyser; later two-prism designs implement the same angular measurement. It should be distinguished from a later quartz-wedge compensating saccharimeter, which cancels the sample's rotation instead of directly reading the analyser rotation.[pp.84–85, original Biot polariscope and simple angular polariscope; chapter VI, quartz-wedge compensation](../../../../../a-handbook-of-sugar-analysis-by-charles-albert-browne-jr.md)

For a historical [urine](../../../../../urine.md) estimate, clarify a sample without changing its sugar inventory, fill the tube without bubbles, make a solvent blank at the same settings, and measure the signed rotation relative to that blank. With a known dilution factor $D$ and an appropriate equilibrium [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) calibration,

$$
\boxed{c_{\rm original}=D\frac{\alpha_{\rm sample}-\alpha_{\rm blank}}{[\alpha]^T_\lambda\ell}.}
$$

Repeat the reading and check a known [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) solution. [Mutarotation](../../../../../mutarotation.md) makes the time since preparation relevant. The inference is selective only to the extent that other optically active solutes have been excluded or accounted for; a net rotation is not proof that every contribution is [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md). This is a historical analytical application, not a replacement for present clinical assessment of [diabetes](../../../../../diabetes.md).
