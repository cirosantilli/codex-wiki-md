<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

For direct conversion, descending masses can drive paddles in an insulated vessel of [water](../../../../../water.md). Measure the lost gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) and the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) rise, including the vessel's [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) and corrections for unwanted [heat transfer](../../../../../heat-transfer.md). Repeated reversal of the paddles and fixed baffles inhibit simple bulk co-rotation, directing more of the input towards dissipative fluid motion. [Joule's paddle-wheel experiment](../../../../../joule-s-paddle-wheel-experiment.md) tests a quantitative mechanical-to-thermal balance, rather than merely observing that stirring can warm water.[1850, friction experiments and calorimetric corrections](../../../../../on-the-mechanical-equivalent-of-heat-by-james-prescott-joule.md)

For conversion through [electricity](../../../../../electricity.md), mechanical [work](../../../../../work.md) drives a generator, whose [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) passes through a resistor in a [calorimeter](../../../../../calorimeter.md). The measured electrical input is $\int VI\,dt$, or $I^2Rt$ for constant [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) and [electrical resistance](../../../../../electrical-resistance.md). [Joule heating](../../../../../joule-heating.md) increases the [internal energy](../../../../../internal-energy-split.md) of the resistor and surrounding [water](../../../../../water.md). Compare the generator's mechanical input with the total thermal gains, including generator and circuit losses if the system boundary contains them. A battery alone demonstrates electrical-to-thermal conversion; without accounting for a mechanically driven source it does not establish the requested mechanical intermediate chain.

Using the given value relative to [water](../../../../../water.md), the copper requires

$$
Q=(500\,\mathrm g)(0.095\,\mathrm{cal\,g^{-1}K^{-1}})(20-10)\,\mathrm K
=475\,\mathrm{cal}.
$$

With the modern thermochemical [calorie](../../../../../calorie.md), $1\,\mathrm{cal}=4.184\,\mathrm J$, so the ideal required [work](../../../../../work.md) is

$$
\boxed{W=1987.4\,\mathrm J\simeq1.99\,\mathrm{kJ}.}
$$

Equivalently it is about $203\,\mathrm{kgf\,m}$ using standard gravity. A nineteenth-century numerical equivalent of heat could give a slightly different rounded result. The stipulated constant [specific heat capacity](../../../../../specific-heat-capacity.md) is used here; an unprovided vessel [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) and environmental losses would add to the required input.
