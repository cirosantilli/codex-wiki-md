<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [hydrometer](../../../../../hydrometer.md) is a sealed floating instrument with a weighted bulb and a narrow graduated stem. The ballast keeps the stem upright. By [Archimedes' principle](../../../../../archimedes-principle.md), its [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md) equals the [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md):

$$
mg=\rho gV_{\rm displaced},\qquad \boxed{\rho=\frac{m}{V_{\rm displaced}}.}
$$

It therefore sinks farther into a less dense [liquid](../../../../../liquid.md), displacing more volume, and floats higher in a denser one. The stem makes a small volume change readily visible as a change in immersion. If its cross-sectional area is $A$, then $V_{\rm displaced}=V_0+A z$ over a uniform part of the stem. A [density](../../../../../density.md) scale is consequently not generally uniform in $z$.

Float the clean instrument freely in a sufficiently deep sample, without its touching the wall or bottom. Remove adhering bubbles, allow the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and motion to settle, and read the liquid surface at eye level using the meniscus convention specified for that instrument. A calibration for a particular [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) or reference [density](../../../../../density.md) cannot be transferred without correction: both the sample and the instrument expand. An ordinary [hydrometer](../../../../../hydrometer.md) measures [density](../../../../../density.md) or [specific gravity](../../../../../relative-density.md); a special scale for a particular [chemical solution](../../../../../solution-chemistry.md) does not make it chemically selective. In particular, dissolved substances other than [glucose](../../../../../glucose.md) can affect the [density](../../../../../density.md) of [urine](../../../../../urine.md).
