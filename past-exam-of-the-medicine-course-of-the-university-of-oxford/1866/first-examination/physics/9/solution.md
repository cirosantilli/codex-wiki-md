<h1 id="9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9](../9.md)

Evaporation cools the remaining material when the escaping vapour takes [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) from it faster than the surroundings supply [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md). For an observable demonstration, compare two similar thermometer bulbs at the same initial [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), one dry and the other wetted with a volatile [liquid](../../../../../liquid.md). Expose both to the same controlled airflow. The wetted bulb falls in [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); the effect depends on vapour removal and environmental conditions. Another demonstration is continued heating during boiling at constant [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md): vapour is produced while the boiling [liquid](../../../../../liquid.md)'s [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) remains nearly constant. The supplied [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) has not vanished; it contributes to the [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md) and expansion associated with the [phase transition](../../../../../phase-transition.md).

One [calorimetry](../../../../../calorimetry-split.md) method estimates the specific [latent heat of vaporization](../../../../../specific-latent-heat-of-vaporization.md) by condensing a known mass $m_s$ of dry saturated steam at $T_s$ into a known mass $m_w$ of cooler [water](../../../../../water.md). Let $C_{\rm cal}$ be the vessel's [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md), and $T_i,T_f$ the initial and final water temperatures. Assuming complete condensation without escaped steam or thermal losses,

$$
m_s\{L_v+c_w(T_s-T_f)\}=(m_wc_w+C_{\rm cal})(T_f-T_i),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{L_v=\frac{(m_wc_w+C_{\rm cal})(T_f-T_i)}{m_s}-c_w(T_s-T_f).}
$$

The last term removes the sensible cooling of the condensed steam. Weighing the receiving vessel before and after gives $m_s$ only if splashing, evaporation and liquid carried in with the steam are controlled. A steam trap and an insulated transfer path address two distinct errors: droplets falsely counted as condensing steam, and cooling before the calorimeter.

The historical local application was [Richardson's ether spray](../../../../../richardson-s-ether-spray.md). A bellows atomizer dispersed [diethyl ether](../../../../../diethyl-ether.md) into fine droplets, promoting rapid [vaporization](../../../../../vaporization.md) and withdrawal of [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) from the surface; the cooling could produce local loss of sensation. [Benjamin Ward Richardson](../../../../../benjamin-ward-richardson.md)'s 1866 account describes this evaporative method and its apparatus. Its historical physiological language is not adopted as a modern description of [nerve](../../../../../nerve.md) signalling, and the account does not establish that the method is appropriate for every operation.[1866, description of the spray apparatus and cooling mechanism](../../../../../on-a-new-and-ready-mode-of-producing-local-anaesthesia-by-benjamin-ward-richardson.md)
