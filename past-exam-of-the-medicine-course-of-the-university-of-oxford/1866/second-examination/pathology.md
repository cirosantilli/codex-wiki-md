## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What influences are supposed to favour the deposition of scrofulous material in the lung? What tissues of the organ appear to be primarily concerned therein, and what are the microscopical characteristics of this material ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The historical material was a tuberculous or caseous product, whose formation was associated with constitutional susceptibility, poor nourishment and unhealthy living conditions; its anatomical origin was disputed.** The word [scrofulous material](../../../biology.md#scrofulous-material) must not be taken to identify a chemically uniform substance deposited from the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood).

In the period's constitutional account, an inherited [scrofulous diathesis](../../../biology.md#scrofulous-diathesis), defective nourishment, overcrowding, ill-ventilated accommodation and prolonged debilitating illness were thought to favour its formation. Damp or cold surroundings were also blamed, but are not in themselves a sufficient cause of [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis). A contemporary clinical discussion explicitly linked inadequate diet and crowded, badly ventilated dormitories with scrofulous illness.[p. 913, discussion preceding the Margate infirmary diet lists](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#scrofulous-diet-and-ventilation-in-the-british-medical-journal-24-november-1860) Familial clustering could reflect shared exposure and living conditions as well as host susceptibility; it did not establish inheritance of a ready-made morbid deposit.

The modern explanation separates acquisition of [Mycobacterium tuberculosis](../../../organism.md#mycobacterium-tuberculosis) from progression after infection. Infectious airborne particles can reach the [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus); close exposure and poor indoor air circulation increase transmission. Impaired [immunity](../../../biology.md#immunity-medical), [malnutrition](../../../physiology.md#malnutrition) and some chronic illnesses increase the risk of progression. Thus some old observations about housing and nutrition concerned real risks, while the old constitutional explanation did not identify the infectious cause.[Risk factors; How it spreads](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-overview-of-tuberculosis-by-cdc)[Airborne transmission and progression from inactive infection](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#tuberculosis-causes-and-how-it-spreads-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)

The primary sites considered were the [pulmonary interstitium](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-interstitium), including supporting [connective tissue](../../../anatomy.md#connective-tissue) around small [airways](../../../physiology.md#airway) and [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel), and the lining or contents of the [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus). A cellular nodule in the supporting [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) is structurally different from degenerated inflammatory material filling air spaces. Contemporary authors disputed formation in exuded fluid versus origin from local tissue elements.[1863 lecture, discussion of tubercle-corpuscles, pp. 895–897](../../../biology-bibliography.md#the-present-aspect-of-the-doctrine-of-cellular-pathology-by-william-turner)

Under the [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope), a cellular nodule and its degenerated centre look different. In present terminology a [tuberculous granuloma](../../../biology.md#tuberculous-granuloma) commonly contains [epithelioid macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#epithelioid-macrophage), [lymphocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#lymphocyte) and sometimes multinucleated [giant cells](../../../biological-cell.md#giant-cell), with central [caseous necrosis](../../../biology.md#caseous-necrosis). The caseous portion is granular, largely structureless debris with loss of recognizable viable [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md); lipid droplets and remnants may be seen. Old descriptions of small granular “tubercle corpuscles” did not establish a uniquely diagnostic cell. Caseation can occur in other processes, and a severely degraded specimen may conceal its origin.[Histopathological diagnosis, pulmonary granulomas and caseous necrosis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#diagnosis-of-tuberculosis-a-consensus-statement-from-the-brazilian-thoracic-association)

A discriminating examination therefore samples both the centre and the advancing margin, preserves the relation to [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus) and interstitium in sections, and examines adjoining [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung). Modern [acid-fast staining](../../../biology.md#acid-fast-staining), [microbiological culture](../../../biology.md#microbiological-culture) and molecular tests address the infectious cause; they are later methods, not observations supplied by this 1866 paper. **Architecture and causal evidence are more informative than the appearance of the debris alone.**

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What is meant by Leukhæmia, and what are the symptoms which are apt to arise in those affected by it ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**“Leukhæmia” denotes [leukaemia](../../../biology.md#leukaemia): historically a persistent excess of colourless corpuscles in the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), now understood as a group of malignant disorders of blood-forming [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md).** It is distinct from a temporary rise of ordinary [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) during [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) or [infection](../../../biology.md#infection).

The historical laboratory finding was an unusually large proportion of colourless [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) corpuscles on [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy), often accompanied by enlargement of the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen) or [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node). Such a blood appearance did not mean that ordinary [pus](../../../biology.md#pus) had simply entered the circulation. The present classification separates acute and chronic disorders and myeloid and lymphoid lineages. The abnormal population can interfere with production of normal [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell), [platelets](../../../physiology.md#platelet) and effective [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) in the [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow). A high total white-cell count is common, but some acute [leukaemias](../../../biology.md#leukaemia) present with a normal or low count; the count alone is neither a complete definition nor a modern subtype diagnosis.[Introduction; Classification; Findings at Diagnosis table; Leukemoid reaction](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#overview-of-leukemia-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The symptoms follow several mechanisms:

- Reduced normal [red blood cell](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) production causes [anaemia](../../../biology.md#anaemia), with pallor, fatigue, weakness, exertional [dyspnoea](../../../biology.md#dyspnoea) and sometimes palpitations.
- Reduced [platelet](../../../physiology.md#platelet) production can cause easy bruising, pinpoint [petechiae](../../../biology.md#petechiae), nosebleeds, bleeding gums or other [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage). Bleeding is a tendency rather than an inevitable finding in every subtype.
- Defective production or function of effective [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) predisposes to [infection](../../../biology.md#infection), [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever), mouth lesions and slow recovery from infections. A large number of abnormal [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) does not guarantee normal defence.
- Expansion or infiltration of the [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) can produce bone pain. Enlargement of [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node), the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen) and sometimes the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) may produce visible swellings, abdominal discomfort or early fullness after eating.
- Systemic illness can cause diminished appetite, loss of body mass and night sweats. Conversely, some chronic [leukaemias](../../../biology.md#leukaemia) are initially detected in apparently well people.

These are possible manifestations, not a required syndrome in every affected person.[What is leukemia?; Symptoms; Diagnosis](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#leukemia-by-medlineplus)[How leukemia starts; Features and symptoms of acute and chronic leukemia](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#leukemia-by-the-american-cancer-society)

For an 1866 explanation, the important connection is between the persistent abnormal [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) picture, enlargement of blood-forming or lymphatic organs, progressive debility and bleeding. Modern diagnosis goes further: a [complete blood count](../../../biology.md#complete-blood-count) with differential and a stained [blood film](../../../anatomy.md#blood-film) establish which cellular populations are abnormal; selected [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) studies, [immunophenotyping](../../../biology.md#immunophenotyping) and genetic tests identify the disease. A microscope image of numerous white corpuscles is the beginning of that distinction, not proof of every later molecular category. **The dominant clinical problem can be failure of normal blood formation despite an apparent abundance of white cells.**

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the conditions in which Sugar and Albumen may be found in the urine. What value is to be attached to their presence under various circumstances ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**The significance of [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) and [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) depends on persistence, amount, mechanism and accompanying findings.** The older words “sugar” and “albumen” do not guarantee that a nineteenth-century test identified one particular molecule.

For urinary sugar, distinguish the following situations:

- In [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes), an excessive filtered [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) load can exceed recovery by the [proximal tubule](../../../anatomy.md#proximal-tubule). Persistent [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) with measured [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia), thirst, excessive urination and loss of body mass has much greater diagnostic significance than one weak urinary reaction.
- Transient [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia) during acute stress or illness can produce transient [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria). Changes after a large carbohydrate load depend on the individual's renal handling; a positive sample does not by itself establish chronic [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes).
- In [renal glycosuria](../../../biology.md#renal-glycosuria), impaired tubular recovery permits loss despite an ordinary [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar) level. This may be an isolated inherited trait or accompany wider [proximal tubule](../../../anatomy.md#proximal-tubule) dysfunction, as in [Fanconi syndrome](../../../biology.md#fanconi-syndrome) or tubular injury. Pregnancy can also lower the effective threshold for urinary loss.
- A specimen can contain other [reducing substances](../../../chemistry.md#reducing-substance), including sugars other than [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose). A general reducing test therefore needs chemical confirmation before its result is labelled [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria).

Modern glucose-specific assays and concurrent [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar) measurements distinguish excess filtered load from reduced recovery.[Technique, nonspecific copper reduction; Basic Science, glucose load, tubular defects and pregnancy](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#glucosuria-in-clinical-methods)[Definition and causes, isolated transport defect versus generalized tubular dysfunction](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#renal-glucosuria-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

For “albumen”, the principal pathological groups are injury to the [glomerular filtration barrier](../../../anatomy.md#glomerular-filtration-barrier) in [glomerulonephritis](../../../biology.md#glomerulonephritis), [renal amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#renal-amyloidosis), [diabetic kidney disease](../../../biology.md#diabetic-kidney-disease) and other [glomerular diseases](../../../biology.md#glomerular-disease); changes associated with [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure) and renal venous [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion); and pregnancy-associated injury such as [pre-eclampsia](../../../biology.md#pre-eclampsia). [Fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) and severe exertion may produce transient [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria), while [orthostatic proteinuria](../../../biology.md#orthostatic-proteinuria) can disappear during recumbency. Impaired tubular recovery chiefly increases smaller urinary [proteins](../../../protein.md); it should not automatically be called a selective loss of [serum albumin](../../../protein.md#serum-albumin).[Pathophysiology; Etiology; functional and orthostatic proteinuria](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#proteinuria-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

A collected specimen can also receive [blood plasma](../../../physiology.md#blood-plasma) [proteins](../../../protein.md) from [haematuria](../../../biology.md#haematuria) or inflammatory material below the filtering apparatus. Moreover, [immunoglobulin light chains](../../../protein.md#immunoglobulin-light-chain) in [multiple myeloma](../../../biology.md#multiple-myeloma) can give a positive general protein test without being [serum albumin](../../../protein.md#serum-albumin). Historical heat and acid precipitation tested a physical property of material in a tube, not a modern molecular diagnosis.[Technique and Basic Science, precipitation versus protein identification](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#proteinuria-in-clinical-methods)[Clinical Significance, serum proteins accompanying haematuria](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#hematuria-in-clinical-methods)

The diagnostic value consequently rises with reproducibility and coherent accompanying evidence. Persistent heavy [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria), especially with [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema) and abnormal sediment, strongly suggests significant renal disease; [red blood cell casts](../../../biology.md#red-blood-cell-cast) support a renal rather than merely lower-tract source of bleeding. Repeated [urinalysis](../../../biology.md#urinalysis), sediment [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) and a [urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio](../../../biology.md#urine-albumin-to-creatinine-ratio) help quantify and localize the problem.[Detection by dipstick and urine albumin and creatinine measurement](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#albuminuria-albumin-in-the-urine-by-niddk) Coexisting [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) and [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) may reflect [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) with renal injury, or another combination of mechanisms. **Neither their coexistence nor either isolated result is sufficient to establish the whole diagnosis.**

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the structure of the lesions of the bowels in continued fever and phthisis.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The classical contrast is between ulceration centred on intestinal lymphoid tissue in enteric fever and granulomatous, often circumferential ulceration in intestinal [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis).** The historical expression [continued fever](../../../physiology.md#continued-fever) covered more than one illness. The characteristic bowel lesions meant here point especially to [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever); they should not be attributed indiscriminately to [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus) or every prolonged [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever).

In [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever), [Peyer's patches](../../../physiology.md#peyer-s-patch) and solitary lymphoid follicles, particularly in the terminal [ileum](../../../physiology.md#ileum), enlarge and become infiltrated. Their breakdown and [necrosis](../../../biology.md#necrosis) lead to sloughing and ulceration. The larger ulcers characteristically follow the long axis of the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine), reflecting the shape of the affected aggregated lymphoid patch on the side opposite the [mesentery](../../../anatomy.md#mesentery). Depending on their stage, one may see swollen patches, necrotic sloughs, open ulcers or healing surfaces. Depth varies; erosion into a [blood vessel](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) can cause [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), while full-thickness destruction can cause [intestinal perforation](../../../biology.md#intestinal-perforation) and [peritonitis](../../../biology.md#peritonitis).

Microscopically, the important cellular component includes swollen [macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#macrophage) with ingested organisms and cellular debris; this is not simply a dense collection of [neutrophils](../../../biological-cell.md#neutrophil) forming an ordinary [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess). The surrounding mucosal architecture may become severely disturbed. These current cellular descriptions refine the gross lesion recognized before the causative organism was identified.[Histopathology, mononuclear phagocytes, typhoid cells and lymphoid ulceration](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#typhoid-fever-in-statpearls)[Salmonella, macrophage-rich lymphoid infiltration and deep ulcers](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#infective-disorders-of-the-gastrointestinal-tract)

In historical [phthisis](../../../biology.md#phthisis), bowel disease frequently meant [intestinal tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#intestinal-tuberculosis) associated with [pulmonary tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-tuberculosis). Lesions commonly involve the terminal [ileum](../../../physiology.md#ileum) and ileocaecal region, although other sites occur. Nodules in mucosal or submucosal lymphoid [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) undergo [caseous necrosis](../../../biology.md#caseous-necrosis), coalesce and break through the mucosa. The ulcerated surface tends to spread transversely and may encircle the bowel. The edges can be irregular and undermined, with additional [tuberculous granulomas](../../../biology.md#tuberculous-granuloma) in the wall, serosal surface or draining [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node). Progressive [fibrosis](../../../anatomy.md#fibrosis) may narrow the lumen and produce a [stricture](../../../biology.md#stricture); thickened masses as well as ulcers can occur.[Intestinal tuberculosis, morphology, ileocaecal involvement, caseating granulomas and obstructive complications](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#abdominal-tuberculosis-of-the-gastrointestinal-tract-revisited)

A section from a tuberculous ulcer may show central [caseous necrosis](../../../biology.md#caseous-necrosis) bordered by [epithelioid macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#epithelioid-macrophage), [giant cells](../../../biological-cell.md#giant-cell) and [lymphocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#lymphocyte). A section from an enteric-fever ulcer instead emphasizes destruction of lymphoid patches with macrophage-rich infiltration. A practical anatomical examination records site, orientation, depth, edges, intervening mucosa, serosal nodules and draining [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node), rather than judging one detached fragment in isolation.

**Longitudinal lymphoid-patch ulcers favour the classical enteric-fever pattern; transverse, caseating and often stenosing lesions favour intestinal [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis).** These are useful patterns rather than absolute rules. Modern [microbiological culture](../../../biology.md#microbiological-culture), organism-specific molecular tests and appropriately sampled [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology) can establish the cause when gross appearances overlap.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Distinguish histologically the several skin diseases vulgarly confounded under the title “Ring-worm.”

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**A ring-shaped patch is a clinical shape, not one histological disease.** The historical category included several named scalp and skin affections whose relationship was disputed. In particular, old claims that [alopecia areata](../../../biology.md#alopecia-areata) was a fungal disease cannot be carried into modern [pathology](pathology.md). A contemporary review actually records competing classifications of favus, scalp ringworm, circinate eruptions, beard disease and patchy baldness, and disagreement about whether their fungi were identical.[Discussion of proposed parasites of favus, herpes tonsurans, herpes circinatus, sycosis, tinea decalvans and pityriasis versicolor](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#on-the-parasitic-affections-of-the-skin-review-1864)

The microscopic distinctions should be organized by the structure infected or injured:

- **Ordinary scalp [ringworm](../../../biology.md#ringworm), or [tinea capitis](../../../biology.md#tinea-capitis).** The [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus) invades keratinized [hair](../../../anatomy.md#hair) and adjacent surface material. [Hyphae](../../../organism.md#hypha) and [arthroconidia](../../../organism.md#arthroconidium) can occur within the shaft, the [endothrix](../../../biology.md#endothrix) pattern, or around its exterior, the [ectothrix](../../../biology.md#ectothrix) pattern. The weakened shaft breaks, producing short stumps or dark dots. Inflammation varies from limited scale to a severe follicular reaction. Modern species cannot reliably be assigned from the shape of the bald patch alone.[Classification by invasion of the hair shaft; clinical variants and microscopy](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#tinea-capitis-by-dermnet)
- **[Favus](../../../biology.md#favus).** The characteristic yellow cups, or [scutula](../../../biology.md#scutulum), consist of a mass of fungal filaments and keratinous debris around a [hair follicle](../../../physiology.md#hair-follicle). Hyphae may also involve the hair. Longstanding destructive disease can scar the follicle and cause permanent [hair loss](../../../biology.md#hair-loss). The historical name of its organism was Achorion schoenleinii; the usual modern designation is [Trichophyton schoenleinii](../../../organism.md#trichophyton-schoenleinii). Those names describe the history of classification, not two different appearances inferred from an unseen specimen.[Introduction and Etiology, scutula and fungal cause](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#favus-in-statpearls)
- **[Tinea corporis](../../../biology.md#tinea-corporis) and fungal beard disease, [tinea barbae](../../../biology.md#tinea-barbae).** On smooth [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin), superficial hyphae occupy the [stratum corneum](../../../physiology.md#stratum-corneum), with variable epidermal reaction; the advancing margin can be the most productive site for scrapings. In beard infection the hair-bearing [hair follicles](../../../physiology.md#hair-follicle) are involved, and the reaction may be follicular, pustular or granulomatous. The historical word [sycosis](../../../biology.md#sycosis) also covered nonfungal follicular inflammation, so its name alone does not establish a dermatophyte.[Histology and special stains](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#tinea-corporis-pathology-by-dermnet)[Microscopy of scrapings and hairs; inflammatory follicular disease](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#tinea-barbae-by-dermnet)
- **[Pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor).** This is associated with [Malassezia](../../../organism.md#malassezia), not the usual dermatophytes of true [ringworm](../../../biology.md#ringworm). Numerous rounded yeast forms and short fungal filaments occur predominantly in the [stratum corneum](../../../physiology.md#stratum-corneum); a hair shaft filled by arthroconidia is not the defining lesion. This explains why the older name “tinea versicolor” should not imply identity with scalp [ringworm](../../../biology.md#ringworm).[Histology, yeast and short hyphae in the stratum corneum](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pityriasis-versicolor-pathology-by-dermnet)
- **[Alopecia areata](../../../biology.md#alopecia-areata), historically entangled with “tinea decalvans”.** The modern lesion is immune-mediated disturbance of hair follicles, with characteristic lymphocytic inflammation around and sometimes within the bulb in active disease and changes in the hair-growth cycle. The inflammatory infiltrate may be inconspicuous in chronic disease. Finding short or altered hairs is not proof of fungi.[Histopathological diagnostics, active peribulbar inflammation and chronic-stage variation](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#s3-guideline-diagnostics-and-therapy-of-alopecia-areata-part-1)

Annular [eczema](../../../biology.md#eczema) or [psoriasis](../../../biology.md#psoriasis) supplies a further clinical mimic: an inflammatory epidermal pattern alone is not evidence of an invading [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus). For an actual microscopic investigation, collect scale from an active margin and affected hairs when hair is involved, mount the material on a [microscope slide](../../../physics.md#microscope-slide) with [potassium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-hydroxide) to clear keratin, and examine under a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope). A modern [biopsy](../../../biology.md#biopsy) can be examined with [Periodic acid-Schiff staining](../../../biology.md#periodic-acid-schiff-staining) to highlight fungal structures. Distribution within the surface layer, hair shaft, follicle or bulb matters as much as the presence of particles. [Fungal culture](../../../biology.md#fungal-culture) or molecular identification can resolve species when morphology cannot.[B 39, issue 3.2, Microscopy and specimen examination](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#investigation-of-dermatological-specimens-for-superficial-mycoses-by-the-uk-standards-for-microbiology-investigations) **Distinguish the organism, its anatomical location and the host reaction; do not diagnose all circular lesions as one infection.**

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Distinguish between Gout and Rheumatism, acute and chronic.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Gout](../../../biology.md#gout) is a crystal-deposition disorder; “[rheumatism](../../../biology.md#rheumatism)” was a broader historical label that must be separated into acute rheumatic fever and several chronic joint disorders.** In particular, chronic rheumatism is not a precise synonym for modern [rheumatoid arthritis](../../../biology.md#rheumatoid-arthritis).

In **acute [gout](../../../biology.md#gout)**, a sudden, intensely painful, red, hot and swollen [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) is characteristic. The first metatarsophalangeal [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) is a classic site, and early attacks often affect one [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) with intervals of recovery. The material responsible is [monosodium urate](../../../chemical-substance.md#monosodium-urate) deposited in or around the [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint), with an acute inflammatory reaction. High [serum urate](../../../physiology.md#serum-urate) supports the diagnosis but neither guarantees clinical [gout](../../../biology.md#gout) nor excludes it when a sample during a flare is ordinary.[Introduction; crystal deposition; Diagnosis, serum urate](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gout-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

In **chronic [gout](../../../biology.md#gout)**, persistent deposits can form [tophi](../../../biology.md#tophus), including periarticular nodules and deposits in the ear or other cooler sites. Surrounding inflammatory [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) and [fibrosis](../../../anatomy.md#fibrosis) accompany the deposits; recurrent damage can erode articular structures and cause deformity and persistent symptoms. More than one [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) may then be affected. The chalk-like material is a deposit of urate, not new [bone](../../../anatomy.md#bone), and not merely an accumulation of ordinary [pus](../../../biology.md#pus).[Pathophysiology; chronic tophaceous gout](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gout-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The classical **acute rheumatism** of this comparison is usually [acute rheumatic fever](../../../biology.md#acute-rheumatic-fever). In its typical articular presentation, [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) accompanies painful inflammation that migrates between large [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint), rather than repeatedly selecting one small distal site. In modern terms it is a delayed immune-mediated sequel of [Group A streptococcal infection](../../../biology.md#group-a-streptococcal-infection). [Carditis](../../../biology.md#carditis), especially involvement of cardiac [cardiac valves](../../../physiology.md#heart-valve), is a major distinguishing concern; chorea and characteristic skin manifestations may also occur. The articular illness usually subsides without the progressive urate deposits of chronic [gout](../../../biology.md#gout). The later understanding of the streptococcal cause must be distinguished from nineteenth-century constitutional theories.[Cause; Clinical features, migratory arthritis and carditis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-acute-rheumatic-fever-by-cdc)

For **chronic rheumatism**, distinguish at least two important modern patterns. [Rheumatoid arthritis](../../../biology.md#rheumatoid-arthritis) causes persistent inflammatory [synovitis](../../../biology.md#synovitis), commonly affecting corresponding small [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) on both sides. Thickened inflamed [synovium](../../../anatomy.md#synovium) and invasive [pannus](../../../biology.md#pannus) can damage [cartilage](../../../anatomy.md#cartilage) and [bone](../../../anatomy.md#bone), producing lasting deformity. [Osteoarthritis](../../../biology.md#osteoarthritis), also historically included among chronic or deforming rheumatic complaints, instead emphasizes loss of articular [cartilage](../../../anatomy.md#cartilage), altered underlying [bone](../../../anatomy.md#bone) and [osteophytes](../../../anatomy.md#osteophyte). These conditions cannot be identified merely by saying that the [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) have been painful for a long time.[Pathophysiology; Symptoms and Signs; Differential diagnosis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#rheumatoid-arthritis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)[Pathophysiology and structural changes](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#osteoarthritis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

A modern discriminating examination for suspected [gout](../../../biology.md#gout) examines aspirated [synovial fluid](../../../anatomy.md#synovial-fluid) with a [polarizing microscope](../../../optical-instrument.md#polarizing-microscope). Needle-like, strongly negatively birefringent [monosodium urate](../../../chemical-substance.md#monosodium-urate) crystals support the diagnosis; a suitable compensator helps assess their optical behaviour.[Crystal morphology, polarized examination and preservation in alcohol](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gouty-tophus-by-weill-cornell-medicine) This is later diagnostic technique, not a test claimed to have been supplied in 1866. [Microbiological culture](../../../biology.md#microbiological-culture) addresses a different alternative, [septic arthritis](../../../biology.md#septic-arthritis); crystals and infection can coexist. Clinical distribution and duration remain useful, but neither appearance nor a single [serum urate](../../../physiology.md#serum-urate) value settles every case.[NG219, recommendations 1.1.3–1.1.9, differential diagnosis, serum urate and aspiration](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gout-diagnosis-and-management-by-nice)

**Acute versus chronic describes the course; gout versus a particular rheumatic disease identifies a mechanism.** The two distinctions must be made separately.

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What arguments have been advanced to shew a connection between Tubercle, Cancer, and Inflammation ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**The historical arguments rested on similarities of cellular origin, degeneration and tissue reaction; they did not establish that [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis), [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer) and [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) were one disease.** The question asks for the arguments advanced, so their evidential force must be separated from their historical importance.

First, [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) revealed [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) in all three lesions. An older theory made morbid cells arise in an exuded formative fluid; [cellular pathology](../../../biology.md#cellular-pathology) sought their origins in pre-existing [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology). [William Turner](../../../anatomy.md#william-turner)'s 1863 lecture explicitly compared the origins of corpuscles in [pus](../../../biology.md#pus), [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer) and tubercle, but acknowledged their later divergence and unexplained specific characters. A common cellular origin could therefore suggest a relationship without establishing identity.[Opening statement of purpose; pp. 896–897, origin and subsequent divergence](../../../biology-bibliography.md#the-present-aspect-of-the-doctrine-of-cellular-pathology-by-william-turner)

Second, microscopic particles thought specific to tubercle could resemble shrunken [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md), free [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) and degenerated products in malignant or inflammatory lesions. Yellow caseous material was also not uniquely tuberculous. The 1860 year-book records the argument that so-called yellow tubercle could be the outcome of degeneration rather than a specific material already present in [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). This weakened the claim that the appearance of the end product alone established a separate deposited substance.[General Pathology and Symptomatology, p. 135, lack of a diagnostic tubercle corpuscle and tuberculoid metamorphosis](../../../biology-bibliography.md#a-year-book-of-medicine-surgery-and-their-allied-sciences-1860)

Third, inflamed, injured or ulcerated regions can show exuberant growth, increased vascularity and infiltrating [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte). A [tumour](../../../biology.md#tumour) can itself ulcerate and become inflamed; a tuberculous focus can provoke surrounding [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) and scarring. Such anatomical continuity offered an argument for a connection, but several explanations fit it: the inflammatory reaction might cause some changes, respond to the primary lesion, or merely coexist with it. A section containing all these features does not by itself identify their temporal order.

The correct assessment distinguishes **shared cellular responses from shared cause**. Modern [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis) involves an infectious organism and a host response that can include [granulomas](../../../biology.md#granuloma) and [caseous necrosis](../../../biology.md#caseous-necrosis). Modern [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer) involves malignant cellular growth with disease-specific molecular and tissue features. [Inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) is a response to diverse injuries and infections, not automatically an incipient [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer) or a tubercle. Modern [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) also commonly recruits [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) from circulating [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), so the historical proposal of universal local connective-tissue origin was itself incomplete. [Histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology) must retain viable margins and tissue architecture rather than comparing only amorphous debris.

There is nevertheless a real modern connection between persistent [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) and some [cancers](../../../biology.md#cancer): chronic inflammatory processes can damage [DNA](../../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) and alter the environment in which [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) proliferate. That supports particular causal pathways, not universal identity, and it does not mean that an ordinary inflammatory lesion inevitably becomes malignant.[Inflammation, chronic persistence and DNA damage](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#chronic-inflammation-by-the-national-cancer-institute) **The strongest historical insight was a common cellular basis of pathology; the overreach was to infer a common disease from a common reaction or a similar degenerated product.**

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What is meant by “Symmetrical Diseases”? Cite some instances.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**A [symmetrical disease](../../../biology.md#symmetrical-disease) produces corresponding lesions in matching parts on the two sides of the body.** The correspondence may involve site, form, extent and timing; it need not be mathematically exact in every patient or at every stage.

The idea concerned the distribution of pathological change. For example, similar lesions at both elbows have a stronger anatomical correspondence than two unrelated lesions anywhere on the right and left. Bilateral involvement is therefore not always the same as close mirror symmetry. Conversely, a disorder may characteristically show a symmetrical pattern while an individual case is uneven because lesions began at different times or received different local irritation.

The concept was already a subject of organized study before 1866. [William Budd](../../../physician.md#william-budd)'s paper on diseases of corresponding parts described paired articular and periarticular lesions and developed the argument that a systemic influence acts selectively on tissues with corresponding susceptibilities. The inference was that neither the circulating influence alone nor the local tissue alone explains the complete pattern; their interaction determines where disease appears. The historical account used the language of disordered nutrition and morbid material in the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), which should not be mistaken for identification of a modern molecular cause.[Opening clinical observations and discussion of constitutional disease and tissue susceptibility](../../../biology-bibliography.md#on-diseases-which-affect-corresponding-parts-of-the-body-in-a-symmetrical-manner-by-william-budd)

Useful examples include:

- [Psoriasis](../../../biology.md#psoriasis), whose plaques often occupy matching extensor sites such as the elbows and knees. This is a distributional tendency, not a requirement that every plaque have an identical partner.[Definition and typical symmetrical distribution](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#chronic-plaque-psoriasis-by-dermnet)
- Chronic inflammatory polyarthritis, exemplified today by [rheumatoid arthritis](../../../biology.md#rheumatoid-arthritis), with corresponding small [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) of the hands or feet affected. The modern diagnosis is more specific than the historical phrase chronic rheumatism.[Symptoms and Signs, characteristic symmetric peripheral arthritis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#rheumatoid-arthritis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)
- A [distal symmetric polyneuropathy](../../../neurology.md#distal-symmetric-polyneuropathy), for example in [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes), with similar sensory deficits beginning in both feet and later involving the hands. The matching distribution reflects a systemic process acting on similarly vulnerable long [nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve), rather than one local injury at each spot.[Clinical features, distal symmetric patterns and length-dependent axonal neuropathies](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#polyneuropathy-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The first two also illustrate a distinction between surface lesions and deeper structural disease; the third illustrates functional deficits following anatomical correspondence of a nerve population. The symmetry is the shared descriptive property, not evidence that these disorders share one agent.

To document it, compare matching sites, the same tissue compartments and the stage of each lesion; record exceptions rather than filling in an absent partner. **Symmetry is a clue to systemic influence and selective tissue susceptibility, not a diagnosis or proof of a single mechanism.**
