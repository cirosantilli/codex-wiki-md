**The classical contrast is between ulceration centred on intestinal lymphoid tissue in enteric fever and granulomatous, often circumferential ulceration in intestinal [tuberculosis](../../../../../tuberculosis.md).** The historical expression [continued fever](../../../../../continued-fever.md) covered more than one illness. The characteristic bowel lesions meant here point especially to [typhoid fever](../../../../../typhoid-fever.md); they should not be attributed indiscriminately to [typhus](../../../../../typhus.md) or every prolonged [fever](../../../../../fever.md).

In [typhoid fever](../../../../../typhoid-fever.md), [Peyer's patches](../../../../../peyer-s-patch.md) and solitary lymphoid follicles, particularly in the terminal [ileum](../../../../../ileum.md), enlarge and become infiltrated. Their breakdown and [necrosis](../../../../../necrosis.md) lead to sloughing and ulceration. The larger ulcers characteristically follow the long axis of the [intestine](../../../../../intestine.md), reflecting the shape of the affected aggregated lymphoid patch on the side opposite the [mesentery](../../../../../mesentery.md). Depending on their stage, one may see swollen patches, necrotic sloughs, open ulcers or healing surfaces. Depth varies; erosion into a [blood vessel](../../../../../blood-vessel.md) can cause [haemorrhage](../../../../../haemorrhage.md), while full-thickness destruction can cause [intestinal perforation](../../../../../intestinal-perforation.md) and [peritonitis](../../../../../peritonitis.md).

Microscopically, the important cellular component includes swollen [macrophages](../../../../../macrophage.md) with ingested organisms and cellular debris; this is not simply a dense collection of [neutrophils](../../../../../neutrophil.md) forming an ordinary [abscess](../../../../../abscess.md). The surrounding mucosal architecture may become severely disturbed. These current cellular descriptions refine the gross lesion recognized before the causative organism was identified.[Histopathology, mononuclear phagocytes, typhoid cells and lymphoid ulceration](../../../../../typhoid-fever-in-statpearls.md)[Salmonella, macrophage-rich lymphoid infiltration and deep ulcers](../../../../../infective-disorders-of-the-gastrointestinal-tract.md)

In historical [phthisis](../../../../../phthisis.md), bowel disease frequently meant [intestinal tuberculosis](../../../../../intestinal-tuberculosis.md) associated with [pulmonary tuberculosis](../../../../../pulmonary-tuberculosis.md). Lesions commonly involve the terminal [ileum](../../../../../ileum.md) and ileocaecal region, although other sites occur. Nodules in mucosal or submucosal lymphoid [tissue](../../../../../tissue-biology.md) undergo [caseous necrosis](../../../../../caseous-necrosis.md), coalesce and break through the mucosa. The ulcerated surface tends to spread transversely and may encircle the bowel. The edges can be irregular and undermined, with additional [tuberculous granulomas](../../../../../tuberculous-granuloma.md) in the wall, serosal surface or draining [lymph nodes](../../../../../lymph-node.md). Progressive [fibrosis](../../../../../fibrosis.md) may narrow the lumen and produce a [stricture](../../../../../stricture.md); thickened masses as well as ulcers can occur.[Intestinal tuberculosis, morphology, ileocaecal involvement, caseating granulomas and obstructive complications](../../../../../abdominal-tuberculosis-of-the-gastrointestinal-tract-revisited.md)

A section from a tuberculous ulcer may show central [caseous necrosis](../../../../../caseous-necrosis.md) bordered by [epithelioid macrophages](../../../../../epithelioid-macrophage.md), [giant cells](../../../../../giant-cell.md) and [lymphocytes](../../../../../lymphocyte.md). A section from an enteric-fever ulcer instead emphasizes destruction of lymphoid patches with macrophage-rich infiltration. A practical anatomical examination records site, orientation, depth, edges, intervening mucosa, serosal nodules and draining [lymph nodes](../../../../../lymph-node.md), rather than judging one detached fragment in isolation.

**Longitudinal lymphoid-patch ulcers favour the classical enteric-fever pattern; transverse, caseating and often stenosing lesions favour intestinal [tuberculosis](../../../../../tuberculosis.md).** These are useful patterns rather than absolute rules. Modern [microbiological culture](../../../../../microbiological-culture.md), organism-specific molecular tests and appropriately sampled [histology](../../../../../histology.md) can establish the cause when gross appearances overlap.
