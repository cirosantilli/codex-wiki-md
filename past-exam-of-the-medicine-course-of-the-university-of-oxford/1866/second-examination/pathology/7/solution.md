<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

**The historical arguments rested on similarities of cellular origin, degeneration and tissue reaction; they did not establish that [tuberculosis](../../../../../tuberculosis.md), [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md) and [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) were one disease.** The question asks for the arguments advanced, so their evidential force must be separated from their historical importance.

First, [microscopy](../../../../../microscopy.md) revealed [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) and [cell nuclei](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md) in all three lesions. An older theory made morbid cells arise in an exuded formative fluid; [cellular pathology](../../../../../cellular-pathology.md) sought their origins in pre-existing [tissue](../../../../../tissue-biology.md). [William Turner](../../../../../william-turner.md)'s 1863 lecture explicitly compared the origins of corpuscles in [pus](../../../../../pus.md), [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md) and tubercle, but acknowledged their later divergence and unexplained specific characters. A common cellular origin could therefore suggest a relationship without establishing identity.[Opening statement of purpose; pp. 896–897, origin and subsequent divergence](../../../../../the-present-aspect-of-the-doctrine-of-cellular-pathology-by-william-turner.md)

Second, microscopic particles thought specific to tubercle could resemble shrunken [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md), free [cell nuclei](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md) and degenerated products in malignant or inflammatory lesions. Yellow caseous material was also not uniquely tuberculous. The 1860 year-book records the argument that so-called yellow tubercle could be the outcome of degeneration rather than a specific material already present in [blood](../../../../../blood.md). This weakened the claim that the appearance of the end product alone established a separate deposited substance.[General Pathology and Symptomatology, p. 135, lack of a diagnostic tubercle corpuscle and tuberculoid metamorphosis](../../../../../a-year-book-of-medicine-surgery-and-their-allied-sciences-1860.md)

Third, inflamed, injured or ulcerated regions can show exuberant growth, increased vascularity and infiltrating [leukocytes](../../../../../leukocyte.md). A [tumour](../../../../../tumour.md) can itself ulcerate and become inflamed; a tuberculous focus can provoke surrounding [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) and scarring. Such anatomical continuity offered an argument for a connection, but several explanations fit it: the inflammatory reaction might cause some changes, respond to the primary lesion, or merely coexist with it. A section containing all these features does not by itself identify their temporal order.

The correct assessment distinguishes **shared cellular responses from shared cause**. Modern [tuberculosis](../../../../../tuberculosis.md) involves an infectious organism and a host response that can include [granulomas](../../../../../granuloma.md) and [caseous necrosis](../../../../../caseous-necrosis.md). Modern [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md) involves malignant cellular growth with disease-specific molecular and tissue features. [Inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) is a response to diverse injuries and infections, not automatically an incipient [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md) or a tubercle. Modern [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) also commonly recruits [leukocytes](../../../../../leukocyte.md) from circulating [blood](../../../../../blood.md), so the historical proposal of universal local connective-tissue origin was itself incomplete. [Histology](../../../../../histology.md) must retain viable margins and tissue architecture rather than comparing only amorphous debris.

There is nevertheless a real modern connection between persistent [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) and some [cancers](../../../../../cancer.md): chronic inflammatory processes can damage [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) and alter the environment in which [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) proliferate. That supports particular causal pathways, not universal identity, and it does not mean that an ordinary inflammatory lesion inevitably becomes malignant.[Inflammation, chronic persistence and DNA damage](../../../../../chronic-inflammation-by-the-national-cancer-institute.md) **The strongest historical insight was a common cellular basis of pathology; the overreach was to infer a common disease from a common reaction or a similar degenerated product.**
