**[Antimony](../../../../../antimony.md) is the brittle, silvery crystalline element $\mathrm{Sb}$, encountered especially in sulfide ores and in compounds of oxidation states $+3$ and $+5$.** Its historical treatment as a metal is understandable from appearance and alloying behaviour; modern classifications commonly call it a [metalloid](../../../../../metalloid.md). It is too brittle to behave like a readily drawn, ductile metal and is used in suitable alloys to alter properties such as hardness.

The important ore is [stibnite](../../../../../stibnite.md), $\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3$. A historical reduction can use [iron](../../../../../iron.md) to bind the [sulfur](../../../../../sulfur.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3+3\mathrm{Fe}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}+3\mathrm{FeS}.}
$$

Another route roasts suitable sulfide material to an oxide and then reduces that oxide with [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md). For an ideal trioxide product,

$$
2\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3+9\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm O_3+6\mathrm{SO}_2,
$$



$$
\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm O_3+3\mathrm C\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}+3\mathrm{CO}.
$$

Real ore processing requires control of impurities and volatile losses, so these balances do not alone constitute a complete extraction flowsheet.[Appearance, Uses and Natural abundance](../../../../../periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/antimony-element-information-properties-and-uses.md)

Among its principal combinations, [antimony(III) oxide](../../../../../antimony-iii-oxide.md) is an amphoteric oxide. It dissolves under suitable acidic or alkaline conditions rather than fitting a universal rule that a metal oxide must be solely basic. [Antimony(III) chloride](../../../../../antimony-iii-chloride.md), historically “butter of antimony”, hydrolyses on dilution and can deposit a white oxychloride-containing material. A useful simplified balance is

$$
\mathrm{SbCl}_3+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{SbOCl}\downarrow+2\mathrm{HCl}.
$$

Added acid favours dissolution; a more extensively hydrolysed precipitate need not be pure $\mathrm{SbOCl}$. Pentavalent compounds include [antimony(V) chloride](../../../../../antimony-v-chloride.md) and antimonate species; chloride-rich solutions and their hydrolysis require attention to the medium.

[Antimony(III) sulfide](../../../../../antimony-iii-sulfide.md) can occur as the dark crystalline ore and as an orange precipitate obtained from a suitable solution with [hydrogen sulfide](../../../../../hydrogen-sulfide.md):

$$
2\mathrm{Sb}^{3+}+3\mathrm H_2\mathrm S\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3\downarrow+6\mathrm H^+.
$$

Its dissolution in suitable alkaline sulfide solutions forms thioantimony species, providing an analytical separation from some other sulfides. Acidification can reverse that operational separation. A volatile hydride, [stibine](../../../../../stibine.md), $\mathrm{SbH}_3$, can decompose to a dark antimony deposit and thus interfere with the [Marsh test](../../../../../marsh-test.md) for [arsenic](../../../../../arsenic.md).

[Antimony potassium tartrate](../../../../../potassium-antimony-tartrate.md), “tartar emetic”, is a historically important complex salt, not merely an arbitrary mixture of an antimony salt and a potassium salt. It was used medicinally, but historical use is not proof of safety or a present indication; antimony-containing substances can cause serious poisoning. No dosage or treatment recommendation follows from describing them.[Identity and toxicity](../../../../../antimony-potassium-tartrate-by-the-national-poisons-information-service.md)
