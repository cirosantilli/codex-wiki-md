## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the various agencies which aid the propelling power of the heart in carrying on the circulation and elucidate their mode of action.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**Arterial recoil, the [muscle pump](../../../physiology.md#muscle-pump), respiratory movements, venous tone and the mechanics of cardiac filling assist [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation).** The [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) remains its principal continuing source of mechanical [energy](../../../energy.md). These agencies differ in whether they store cardiac [work](../../../energy.md#work), contribute new muscular [work](../../../energy.md#work), or direct flow without supplying power.

During ventricular [systole](../../../physiology.md#systole), the [aorta](../../../physiology.md#aorta) and other large elastic [arteries](../../../physiology.md#artery) expand and store [elastic potential energy](../../../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy). During [diastole](../../../physiology.md#diastole), their recoil continues to drive [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) towards the peripheral vessels, while closure of the [aortic valve](../../../physiology.md#aortic-valve) limits return into the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart). This elastic reservoir smooths an intermittent cardiac input into a more continuous downstream flow. It does not manufacture extra [energy](../../../energy.md): it releases part of the [work](../../../energy.md#work) performed during the preceding ejection.

Contraction of surrounding [skeletal muscles](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle) compresses [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein). [Venous valves](../../../physiology.md#venous-valve) favour displacement towards the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) and limit backward flow when the compression is released. Alternating contraction and relaxation therefore produces a [muscle pump](../../../physiology.md#muscle-pump), especially useful for return from dependent limbs. The added [work](../../../energy.md#work) comes from the contracting [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle); valves guide the displacement but supply no power themselves. Persistent compression without refilling is not equivalent to a series of pumping strokes.

Spontaneous inspiration supplies another assistance. Descent of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) raises abdominal [pressure](../../../pressure.md) while expansion of the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax) lowers intrathoracic [pressure](../../../pressure.md). The resulting changes favour a [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient) towards the thoracic [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) and right side of the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart). The effect depends on posture, the respiratory phase and whether vessels remain open. Positive-pressure inflation does not reproduce the same pressure changes as spontaneous inspiration.[Venous System; Skeletal Muscle Pump; Respiratory Pump](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-2-blood-flow-blood-pressure-and-resistance)

Venous [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) can reduce the storage capacity of peripheral [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein), transferring [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) towards the central circulation. This changes the volume available for cardiac filling; it does not require a travelling wave of venous peristalsis. Relaxation and recoil of the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), together with movement of the atrioventricular plane, also influence filling pressures. The older expression cardiac suction is therefore best interpreted through specific changes of chamber volume and [pressure](../../../pressure.md), rather than a separate force pulling uniformly through every vessel.

[Gravity](../../../physics.md#gravity) assists a downward column and opposes an upward one. For a complete circuit returning to its initial height it supplies no net gravitational [work](../../../energy.md#work). Its important effects include hydrostatic pressure differences and dependent pooling. Likewise, the residual [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient) across the [capillaries](../../../physiology.md#capillary) is chiefly the downstream consequence of the cardiac pump. Ordinary [capillary action](../../../surface-tension.md#capillary-action) at a liquid meniscus is not a universal extra pump inside a closed, fluid-filled vascular network. Local [vasodilation](../../../physiology.md#vasodilation) can improve [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion) by reducing [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance), but it does not create the necessary pressure source.

With $Q$ the mean [volumetric flow rate](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#volumetric-flow-rate), $R$ an effective [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance), and $\Delta P$ the driving pressure difference, the useful bookkeeping relation is

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{\Delta P}{R}}.
$$

It separates an increase of driving [pressure](../../../pressure.md) from a reduction of resistance. An instructive apparatus compares the outflow of a pulsatile pump through rigid tubing with its outflow through an elastic reservoir feeding the same resistance. Continued outflow between strokes demonstrates stored [elastic potential energy](../../../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy). A separately compressed valved flexible tube demonstrates the additional [muscle pump](../../../physiology.md#muscle-pump) principle. Neither observation requires a mysterious attraction of [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) by the [tissues](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the various arrangements by which the special requirements of the intracranial circulation are provided for.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) needs continuous, locally matched [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion) within a nearly rigid [skull](../../../anatomy.md#skull).** Its arterial connections, venous drainage, fluid compartments and vascular regulation meet different parts of that requirement. No single arrangement guarantees an unchanging intracranial [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) volume or an uninterrupted supply under every condition.

There are two principal arterial approaches: the paired [internal carotid arteries](../../../physiology.md#internal-carotid-artery) and the [vertebral arteries](../../../physiology.md#vertebral-artery), the latter joining as the [basilar artery](../../../physiology.md#basilar-artery). At the base of the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain), the [cerebral arterial circle](../../../physiology.md#cerebral-arterial-circle) connects the anterior and posterior supplies through communicating [arteries](../../../physiology.md#artery). Connections between superficial cerebral arterial territories provide additional collateral routes. This permits redistribution if one route becomes inadequate, but individual anatomy varies, and some deep penetrating [arteries](../../../physiology.md#artery) have limited effective collateral supply. The existence of an anatomical circle therefore does not establish that any one [artery](../../../physiology.md#artery) can be obstructed harmlessly.[Arteries; Collaterals: cerebral arterial circle, pial anastomoses and variation in collateral capacity](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#the-cerebral-circulation-by-marilyn-j-cipolla/anatomy-and-ultrastructure)

The bends of the entering [arteries](../../../physiology.md#artery) and their branching course accompany a distributed [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance) and compliance. They should not be described as mechanically abolishing every pulse: cerebral [blood flow](../../../physiology.md#blood-flow) remains pulsatile. Numerous small branches spread the supply over the surface before penetrating the tissue, while dense [capillary](../../../physiology.md#capillary) networks bring exchange close to [neurons](../../../neuron.md) and supporting [cells](../../../biological-cell.md).

Superficial and deep cerebral [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) drain into [dural venous sinuses](../../../physiology.md#dural-venous-sinus). These are channels supported by folds and attachments of [dura mater](../../../anatomy.md#dura-mater), rather than freely collapsible thin-walled [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein). Their connections lead chiefly towards the [internal jugular veins](../../../anatomy.md#internal-jugular-vein), with communicating vertebral, diploic and emissary routes providing additional possibilities. The valveless nature of many of these communications allows pressure-dependent changes of direction; it is not an assurance of one-way flow. Head position, neck pressure and the available alternative routes consequently matter to drainage.

The [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain), [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) and [cerebrospinal fluid](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebrospinal-fluid) share the cranial enclosure. A small increase in one component can initially be accommodated by displacement of venous [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) and [cerebrospinal fluid](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebrospinal-fluid), including fluid movement towards the spinal compartment. This reserve is finite. When compensation is exhausted, further volume increase can produce a large rise of [intracranial pressure](../../../neuroscience.md#intracranial-pressure) and reduce [cerebral perfusion pressure](../../../physiology.md#cerebral-perfusion-pressure). The skull does not make cerebral anaemia physically impossible, as an overly literal historical constant-volume argument might suggest.

A simplified pressure relation, useful in the modern account, is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CPP}\simeq\mathrm{MAP}-\mathrm{ICP}},
$$

where CPP is [cerebral perfusion pressure](../../../physiology.md#cerebral-perfusion-pressure), MAP is [mean arterial pressure](../../../physiology.md#mean-arterial-pressure) and ICP is [intracranial pressure](../../../neuroscience.md#intracranial-pressure). The approximation assumes that the effective downstream pressure is represented adequately by ICP; high venous pressure can complicate it. [Cerebral autoregulation](../../../physiology.md#cerebral-autoregulation) adjusts arteriolar tone and hence resistance when [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion) pressure changes. It has limited capacity and depends on physiological state, so a fixed universal pressure interval should not be inferred.[Mechanism; Cerebral Blood Flow Regulation and Physiology: perfusion pressure, resistance and regulatory responses](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#physiology-cerebral-autoregulation-statpearls)

Local metabolic regulation and [neurovascular coupling](../../../neurology.md#neurovascular-coupling) also match supply to activity. A rise of arterial [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) tends to dilate cerebral vessels; vigorous overbreathing can lower arterial [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and reduce cerebral [blood flow](../../../physiology.md#blood-flow). This is distinct from pressure-driven autoregulation. The [blood-brain barrier](../../../physiology.md#blood-brain-barrier), based principally on specialized [capillary](../../../physiology.md#capillary) [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) with tight junctions, helps stabilize the chemical environment while permitting regulated exchanges. It is a selective exchange arrangement, not the structure that supports the dural sinuses or generates the arterial pressure. These modern mechanisms refine the anatomical account without attributing all their molecular details to 1865.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the various physiological conditions which are known to increase or diminish the amount of Carbonic Acid exhaled from the lungs ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Exhaled carbonic acid here means [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide); its amount per unit time must be distinguished from its percentage in expired air.** A person can breathe a larger volume of more dilute expired gas and still exhale more total [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide).

At metabolic steady state, pulmonary [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) output approximately reflects its production by [cellular respiration](../../../metabolism.md#cellular-respiration). Increased muscular activity is therefore a major physiological cause of increased output: walking, climbing and strenuous exercise increase substrate oxidation and usually increase both ventilation and total [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) elimination. Rest lowers the requirement; during ordinary sleep, reduced activity and metabolic demand generally lower output relative to the waking active state. A comparison needs the same time interval and should not confuse a short transient with a sustained resting measurement.

Food affects output in two ways. Digestion, absorption and processing increase metabolic expenditure after a meal, with a time course depending on the meal. The material oxidized also matters. The [respiratory quotient](../../../metabolism.md#respiratory-quotient) is the ratio of metabolic [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) production to [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) use. For complete oxidation of [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose),

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow6\mathrm{CO_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O},
\qquad \boxed{\mathrm{RQ}=1}.
$$

A typical [fatty acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#fatty-acid) gives less [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) per unit [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) consumed; for [palmitic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#palmitic-acid),

$$
\mathrm{C_{16}H_{32}O_2}+23\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow16\mathrm{CO_2}+16\mathrm{H_2O},
\qquad \boxed{\mathrm{RQ}=\frac{16}{23}\simeq0.70}.
$$

These are substrate comparisons, not a rule that any high-fat meal immediately reduces a person's total daily output. Fasting changes fuel selection as well as metabolic rate, so both processes must be considered.[Absorptive and Postabsorptive States: storage, mobilization and changing fuel use](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/24-5-metabolic-states-of-the-body)

Cold exposure increases output when maintaining body [temperature](../../../temperature.md) requires extra [thermogenesis](../../../physiology.md#thermogenesis), especially [shivering](../../../physiology.md#shivering). Moving from cold to a thermally comfortable environment can reduce that extra production. Heating does not simply increase or decrease output in proportion to air [temperature](../../../temperature.md): passive warming, heat stress, activity and the thermoregulatory response differ. Growth, body size, age, pregnancy and lactation can change whole-body metabolic requirements. Comparisons between people therefore need to specify whether output is absolute or normalized to [body mass](../../../biology.md#body-mass) or another appropriate size measure. Biological sex alone is not an explanation independent of these factors. Daily variation also mixes circadian physiology with sleep, meals and activity.

Ventilatory changes require a separate account. Voluntary [hyperventilation](../../../physiology.md#hyperventilation) can initially wash [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) out of body stores faster than it is being produced; breath holding temporarily prevents its pulmonary escape while production continues. Subsequent recovery changes the output again. The ratio measured at the mouth, the [respiratory exchange ratio](../../../metabolism.md#respiratory-exchange-ratio), need not equal the tissue [respiratory quotient](../../../metabolism.md#respiratory-quotient) during those transitions or during intense exercise with acid buffering. Reduced expired output for a few seconds is therefore not evidence that tissue [metabolism](../../../metabolism.md) has stopped.[Carbon-dioxide production, transport in dissolved and bicarbonate forms, and pulmonary elimination](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#physiology-carbon-dioxide-transport-statpearls)

A measurement should collect total expired gas over a known time and determine its dry [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) fraction, allowing for inspired [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide). With inspired and expired dry gas volumes expressed under the same reference conditions,

$$
\boxed{\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=\dot V_EF_{E,\mathrm{CO_2}}-\dot V_IF_{I,\mathrm{CO_2}}}.
$$

Here $\dot V_E$ and $\dot V_I$ are expired and inspired volume rates and the $F$ terms their respective fractions. Recording food intake, physical activity, ambient [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and time since the preceding intervention makes the comparison interpretable. [Carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) output is a measured flux, not merely an observation that an animal makes a gas collection vessel cloudy.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Write a description of the Anatomy of the knee joint.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The [knee](../../../anatomy.md#knee) is a compound [synovial joint](../../../anatomy.md#synovial-joint) comprising tibiofemoral and patellofemoral articulations.** It permits chiefly flexion and extension, with controlled rotation and translation. The head of the [fibula](../../../anatomy.md#fibula) provides important ligamentous attachments nearby but does not form a principal articular surface of the [knee](../../../anatomy.md#knee) cavity.

The medial and lateral femoral condyles articulate with the corresponding superior surfaces of the [tibia](../../../anatomy.md#tibia). Anteriorly, the [patella](../../../anatomy.md#patella) articulates with the femoral patellar surface. These load-bearing surfaces are covered by [hyaline cartilage](../../../anatomy.md#hyaline-cartilage). The relatively incongruent tibiofemoral surfaces are supplemented by the medial and lateral [knee menisci](../../../anatomy.md#meniscus-of-the-knee), wedges of [fibrocartilage](../../../anatomy.md#fibrocartilage) whose thicker outer borders attach towards the capsule and tibial margins. Their anterior and posterior roots attach in the intercondylar tibial region. The medial [meniscus of the knee](../../../anatomy.md#meniscus-of-the-knee) is more C-shaped and relatively less mobile; the lateral is more nearly circular and more mobile. An anterior transverse connection may join them.

The fibrous capsule encloses the articulations but is strengthened and partly replaced by neighbouring tendons and ligaments. Anteriorly, the extensor apparatus includes the [quadriceps tendon](../../../anatomy.md#quadriceps-tendon), [patella](../../../anatomy.md#patella), [patellar ligament](../../../anatomy.md#patellar-ligament) to the tibial tuberosity, and medial and lateral patellar retinacula. Posteriorly, the capsule is reinforced by the [oblique popliteal ligament](../../../anatomy.md#oblique-popliteal-ligament), an expansion related to [semimembranosus](../../../anatomy.md#semimembranosus), and the arcuate posterolateral complex. The [popliteus tendon](../../../anatomy.md#popliteus-tendon) passes in relation to the posterolateral capsule and lateral meniscus.

The [tibial collateral ligament](../../../anatomy.md#tibial-collateral-ligament) extends from the medial femoral side to the medial [tibia](../../../anatomy.md#tibia) and is closely related to the capsule and medial [meniscus of the knee](../../../anatomy.md#meniscus-of-the-knee). The cord-like [fibular collateral ligament](../../../anatomy.md#fibular-collateral-ligament) runs from the lateral femoral epicondyle to the fibular head. It is separated from the lateral meniscus by the [popliteus tendon](../../../anatomy.md#popliteus-tendon). Thus the two collateral ligaments do not have identical meniscal attachments.

The [anterior cruciate ligament](../../../anatomy.md#anterior-cruciate-ligament) runs from the anterior intercondylar tibial area towards the inner surface of the lateral femoral condyle. The [posterior cruciate ligament](../../../anatomy.md#posterior-cruciate-ligament) runs from the posterior intercondylar tibial area towards the inner surface of the medial femoral condyle. They cross within the fibrous capsule but lie outside the synovial cavity proper. The [anterior cruciate ligament](../../../anatomy.md#anterior-cruciate-ligament) checks excessive anterior displacement of the [tibia](../../../anatomy.md#tibia) relative to the [femur](../../../anatomy.md#femur) and contributes to rotational control; the [posterior cruciate ligament](../../../anatomy.md#posterior-cruciate-ligament) checks excessive posterior displacement. Their effects depend on joint position and interact with the collateral ligaments and muscular support.[Structure and Function: articulations, menisci, capsule and cruciate/collateral attachments](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-bony-pelvis-and-lower-limb-knee-statpearls)

The [synovial membrane](../../../anatomy.md#synovial-membrane) lines the inner non-articular capsule, reflects around the cruciate region and the [infrapatellar fat pad](../../../anatomy.md#infrapatellar-fat-pad), and forms recesses. The fat pad is intracapsular but excluded from the synovial cavity by the membrane covering it. The [suprapatellar recess](../../../anatomy.md#suprapatellar-recess) extends above the [patella](../../../anatomy.md#patella) beneath the quadriceps. The [prepatellar bursa](../../../anatomy.md#prepatellar-bursa) and the superficial and deep [infrapatellar bursae](../../../anatomy.md#infrapatellar-bursa) reduce friction outside the principal cavity and should not all be described as compulsory communications with it. Posterior bursal communications are variable.

The [quadriceps femoris](../../../anatomy.md#quadriceps-femoris) extends the [knee](../../../anatomy.md#knee); the [hamstrings](../../../anatomy.md#hamstring) and other crossing [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) contribute to flexion and dynamic stability. Rotation is freer in flexion than in full extension. Terminal extension includes a coupled locking movement, commonly described as external rotation of the unloaded [tibia](../../../anatomy.md#tibia) or the corresponding internal rotation of the loaded [femur](../../../anatomy.md#femur). [Popliteus](../../../anatomy.md#popliteus) helps initiate unlocking. This is a constrained three-dimensional articulation rather than a simple door hinge.[Knee Joint: ligaments, menisci and movements](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/9-6-anatomy-of-selected-synovial-joints)

The genicular arterial anastomoses, chiefly associated with [popliteal artery](../../../physiology.md#popliteal-artery) branches and contributions from neighbouring arterial systems, supply the region; accompanying [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) drain it. Articular sensory supply comes from branches of the [femoral nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#femoral-nerve), [obturator nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#obturator-nerve), [tibial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#tibial-nerve) and [common fibular nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#common-fibular-nerve). Their relation to the [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) crossing the articulation illustrates the connection between movement, stabilization and sensation. Dissection with the joint flexed and extended, and inspection of a section through the cruciate and synovial reflections, reveal relations that a single external view cannot show.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the different muscles employed and the different types of movement observable under varying Physiological conditions in the act of Respiration ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**Quiet [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) chiefly uses active inspiration and passive expiration; deeper or more forceful [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) recruits additional [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) and changes their coordination.** The vertical, anteroposterior and transverse dimensions of the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax) can all change, and different patterns need not represent different mechanisms of gas exchange.

The [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) is the principal inspiratory [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle). Its contraction lowers and flattens its domes, increases the thoracic vertical dimension and displaces abdominal contents so the relaxed abdominal wall moves outwards. Its lower-[rib](../../../anatomy.md#rib) attachments also contribute to expansion of the costal margin. The [phrenic nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#phrenic-nerve), chiefly C3–C5, provide its motor supply. The [external intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#external-intercostal-muscle) elevate and stabilize the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib), while the interchondral or parasternal portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#internal-intercostal-muscle) also assist inspiration. It would be wrong to give every internal intercostal fibre an exclusively expiratory function.

Elevation of upper [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) and the [sternum](../../../anatomy.md#sternum) increases the anteroposterior dimension, the pump-handle component. Outward movement of the lateral portions of lower [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) increases transverse diameter, the bucket-handle component. These are components of oblique [rib](../../../anatomy.md#rib) rotation rather than two mechanically isolated systems. Abdominal or diaphragmatic [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) makes descent of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) particularly conspicuous; costal [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) makes chest-wall expansion more conspicuous. Both contribute in ordinary respiration, with their proportions changing with posture, mechanical loading and individual anatomy.

As the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax) expands, [pleural pressure](../../../physiology.md#pleural-pressure) becomes more negative and [transpulmonary pressure](../../../physiology.md#transpulmonary-pressure) increases. The [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) expand and [alveolar pressure](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-pressure) briefly falls below [atmospheric pressure](../../../pressure.md#atmospheric-pressure), admitting air. Relaxation of inspiratory [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) lets elastic recoil reverse the flow in quiet expiration. The abdominal wall and [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) return towards their resting arrangement without requiring active abdominal compression for each quiet breath.[Mechanisms of Breathing; Pulmonary Ventilation: pressure changes and inspiratory versus expiratory activity](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/22-3-the-process-of-breathing)

With deeper inspiration, the [scalene muscles](../../../anatomy.md#scalene-muscle) help stabilize or elevate the upper [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib), and the [sternocleidomastoid muscle](../../../anatomy.md#sternocleidomastoid-muscle) can elevate the [sternum](../../../anatomy.md#sternum) when the head is fixed. With the upper limbs or shoulder girdle fixed, [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) such as [pectoralis minor](../../../anatomy.md#pectoralis-minor) and [serratus anterior](../../../anatomy.md#serratus-anterior) can assist expansion in greater effort. Recruitment is graded; an anatomical list of possible accessory [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) does not mean all are active in every ordinary breath.

Active expiration uses the [abdominal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#abdominal-muscle), including rectus, oblique and transversus components, to compress the abdominal contents and drive the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) upwards. The interosseous portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#internal-intercostal-muscle) help depress the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib). This is important in vigorous exercise and expulsive actions. At rest, elasticity supplies much of the expiratory work; during high demand, active [muscle contraction](../../../anatomy.md#muscle-contraction) adds to it.

The observable pattern also changes with the physiological task. Exercise increases breath depth and usually frequency. A sigh is an enlarged inspiration followed by expiration; yawning includes a prolonged deep inspiratory movement. Coughing coordinates an inspiration, closure of the [glottis](../../../anatomy.md#glottis), pressure-generating expiratory effort and sudden glottic opening. Speech imposes a controlled prolonged expiration with laryngeal regulation. Swallowing briefly interrupts ordinary airflow. Thus respiratory observation should include phase, depth, frequency and coordination, not merely count which [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) move. Fixed claims that men must breathe abdominally and women must breathe costally overstate a variable tendency. Pregnancy or a change of posture can alter mechanical contributions without replacing the basic pressure mechanism.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give the minute Anatomy of a Lymphatic, a Lieberkahnian, and a Brunner's Gland.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**A lymphatic gland is a [lymph node](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node); a Lieberkühnian gland is an [intestinal crypt](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-crypt); a [Brunner gland](../../../physiology.md#brunner-gland) is a predominantly mucous duodenal gland.** The first is a lymphoid organ with traversing lymphatic channels; the other two are epithelial glands opening towards the intestinal lumen. Similar historical terminology must not erase that difference.

A [lymph node](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) has a fibrous [connective tissue](../../../anatomy.md#connective-tissue) capsule sending trabeculae into the organ and an internal reticular framework supporting mobile [cells](../../../biological-cell.md). Its outer cortex contains lymphoid follicles, whose appearance changes with immune activity; activated secondary follicles have paler [germinal centres](../../../physiology.md#germinal-centre) surrounded by more densely packed [lymphocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#lymphocyte). The deeper cortex or [paracortex](../../../physiology.md#paracortex) lies between follicular cortex and medulla. The medulla contains branching cellular cords separated by medullary sinuses. A [hilum](../../../anatomy.md#hilum) provides the entry and exit of the main [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) and the exit of efferent lymphatics.

Afferent [lymphatic vessels](../../../physiology.md#lymphatic-vessel) enter at the convex surface and deliver [lymph](../../../physiology.md#lymph) to the subcapsular sinus. Flow then passes through cortical or trabecular channels into medullary sinuses and out through efferent vessels near the [hilum](../../../anatomy.md#hilum). [Macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#macrophage) and the reticular arrangement bring carried material into contact with the cellular apparatus. The node's [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation) is distinct from that lymphatic route. In modern terms, follicles are predominantly [B cell](../../../biological-cell.md#b-cell) territories, the [paracortex](../../../physiology.md#paracortex) is rich in [T cells](../../../biological-cell.md#t-cell), and medullary cords contain many antibody-producing [plasma cells](../../../biological-cell.md#plasma-cell). Those cellular classifications go beyond an ordinary 1865 microscopic description.[Structure; Function; Histochemistry and Cytochemistry: cortex, paracortex and medulla](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#histology-lymph-nodes-statpearls)

Thin stained sections show the overall architecture, while a silver stain highlights the supporting reticular fibres rather than every [lymphocyte](../../../biological-cell.md#lymphocyte). The fibres include [type III collagen](../../../protein.md#type-iii-collagen); the thicker capsule has a different connective-tissue organization. One must not interpret the meshwork itself as an epithelial secretory tube, or assume a section missing the [hilum](../../../anatomy.md#hilum) proves that the node has no efferent vessel.[Slides 22 and 31: silver-stained reticulum, sinuses, capsule, hilum and postcapillary venules](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#lymphatic-tissues-lymph-nodes-by-columbia-university)

The [intestinal crypts](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-crypt) are simple tubular epithelial invaginations opening between the [intestinal villi](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-villus) in the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine). Their bases approach the [muscularis mucosae](../../../physiology.md#muscularis-mucosae), remaining within the mucosa and surrounded by [lamina propria](../../../physiology.md#lamina-propria). Each gland has a lumen continuous with the intestinal surface and a lining supported by a [basement membrane](../../../anatomy.md#basement-membrane). It contains columnar epithelial [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) at different stages of differentiation, [goblet cells](../../../biological-cell.md#goblet-cell) producing mucus, endocrine [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and, in small-[intestinal crypt](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-crypt) bases, conspicuous granular [Paneth cells](../../../biological-cell.md#paneth-cell). A proliferative compartment supplies replacement [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) that move towards the surface; this modern [cell](../../../biological-cell.md)-renewal account explains why dividing [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) are common in the crypts. Crypts also occur in the [colon](../../../physiology.md#colon), whose mucosa lacks ordinary small-intestinal villi. One should therefore specify the region rather than make villi part of every crypt's definition.

A [Brunner gland](../../../physiology.md#brunner-gland) lies chiefly in the submucosa of the proximal [duodenum](../../../physiology.md#duodenum), below the [muscularis mucosae](../../../physiology.md#muscularis-mucosae). It is a branched, compound tubular gland with pale mucus-rich secretory [cells](../../../biological-cell.md), basal nuclei and small lumina. Its ducts pass through the [muscularis mucosae](../../../physiology.md#muscularis-mucosae) and commonly join overlying [intestinal crypts](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-crypt) before opening into the lumen. Its alkaline, mucus-rich secretion helps protect the mucosa from acidic gastric contents. The position below the [muscularis mucosae](../../../physiology.md#muscularis-mucosae) distinguishes it particularly well from a [Lieberkühn gland](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-crypt) in the mucosa.[Small Intestine; regional differences: crypts, cell types and submucosal Brunner glands with ducts joining crypts](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#gi-tract-notes-by-duke-university)

The most useful comparison is consequently one of compartments: a reticular lymphoid organ containing sinuses, a mucosal epithelial tube, and a branched submucosal secretory system. Recognition of the capsule, epithelial lining, [muscularis mucosae](../../../physiology.md#muscularis-mucosae) and duct connections establishes those distinctions more securely than a name alone.

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What has Experimentation shewn as to the functions of the Cervical Sympathetic ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**Section and stimulation show that the cervical [sympathetic](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic) pathway carries influences controlling head-and-neck vascular tone and several ocular functions.** The effects of cutting the pathway must be compared with those of stimulating its peripheral continuation, and neither should be confused with direct heating of the [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve).

In the classical unilateral experiment, dividing the cervical [sympathetic trunk](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic-trunk) produces dilation of vessels on the same side of the head, particularly visible in the rabbit's ear, with redness and increased [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) [temperature](../../../temperature.md). The opposite side provides a useful simultaneous comparison, though it is not immune to systemic changes. The observation establishes that an intact pathway normally contributes a constricting influence. More warm [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) reaching a relatively cool ear explains the warming without requiring a special heat-producing material conducted by the [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve).

Electrical stimulation of the cranial or peripheral cut end produces the complementary effect: vessels narrow, the ear becomes paler and its [temperature](../../../temperature.md) tends to fall. When stimulation stops, dilation returns. A stimulating circuit and appropriately placed electrodes supply a controlled perturbation, while direct observation of vessel calibre and a [thermometer](../../../temperature.md#thermometer) provide separate readouts. Stimulation of the central end instead can engage pathways through the [central nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#central-nervous-system) and is not the same local test. These comparisons distinguish an efferent vasomotor mechanism from a mere correlation between an operation and warmth.

[Claude Bernard](../../../physiology.md#claude-bernard)'s section experiments in 1851 and the subsequent work of [Charles-Édouard Brown-Séquard](../../../physiology.md#charles-edouard-brown-sequard) helped establish this interpretation. A contemporary report published before the examination records the reversal of ocular and vascular signs on galvanizing the upper end of the divided cervical sympathetic. The report also describes recovery differences after section and removal of a segment, warning against treating an early and a late observation as the same experiment.[Account of section, removal of a segment and repeated galvanization; ocular and vascular effects](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#observations-on-the-experiments-of-professor-budge-upon-the-influence-of-the-great-sympathetic-1853)

The ocular effects include a smaller [pupil](../../../anatomy.md#pupil) after interruption and dilation with stimulation of the surviving peripheral pathway. Modern anatomy explains this through the sympathetic supply to the radial [iris dilator muscle](../../../anatomy.md#iris-dilator-muscle). Interruption can also narrow the palpebral opening by reducing activity in sympathetically supplied eyelid [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle). In species with a conspicuous [nictitating membrane](../../../anatomy.md#nictitating-membrane), its position changes; apparent recession or protrusion of the eye is species-dependent and should not be transferred uncritically to humans. The [pupil](../../../anatomy.md#pupil)'s constrictor supply through the [parasympathetic nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic-nervous-system) is a different pathway, so sympathetic section does not amount to paralysis of every ocular [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle).

The cervical pathway includes ascending preganglionic fibres reaching cervical ganglia, notably the [superior cervical ganglion](../../../physiology.md#superior-cervical-ganglion), and postganglionic routes towards cranial targets, including periarterial plexuses. The source of this sympathetic output is thoracic spinal circuitry; it is not generated merely because the chain lies in the neck. Modern extension of the account includes sympathetic control of [sweat glands](../../../physiology.md#sweat-gland) in relevant territories. The distribution of lost sweating depends on the lesion and the species; a rabbit-ear [temperature](../../../temperature.md) experiment is not itself a demonstration of the entire human sweating pathway.[Structure and Function: ascending pathway, cranial distribution and ocular/vasomotor functions](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neuroanatomy-superior-cervical-ganglion-statpearls)

The experimental lesson is therefore specific. The same intervention can alter calibre, [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and [pupil](../../../anatomy.md#pupil) size through different effector structures. Measuring those outcomes separately, documenting stimulus timing, and distinguishing section from stimulation establish more than naming a general nutritive or calorific function of the sympathetic. Modern [neurotransmitter](../../../neuron.md#neurotransmitter) and [receptor](../../../protein.md#receptor-biochemistry) descriptions can explain these observations further, but they were not available in their present form in 1865.

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the relations and connexions subsisting between the Amnios, Allantois, and Umbilical Vesicle in the human Fœtal envelopes.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**The [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion), [allantois](../../../developmental-biology.md#allantois) and [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) are distinct extraembryonic structures whose relations change as the embryo folds and the [umbilical cord](../../../biology.md#umbilical-cord) forms.** The historical umbilical vesicle is the [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac); its duct must not be identified with the allantoic channel.

Early in development the embryonic disc lies between the amniotic cavity dorsally and the [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) ventrally. The [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion) has an epithelial lining facing its fluid and an outer supporting mesodermal component. The [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) has an endodermal lining and an external splanchnic mesodermal covering. Outside them is the [chorionic cavity](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorionic-cavity), interrupted by the [connecting stalk](../../../developmental-biology.md#connecting-stalk) linking the embryonic region to the [chorion](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorion). The [chorion](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorion) consists of trophoblastic tissues and extraembryonic somatic [mesoderm](../../../developmental-biology.md#mesoderm); it is not simply another name for the [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion) or an outgrowth of yolk-sac [endoderm](../../../developmental-biology.md#endoderm).

The human [allantois](../../../developmental-biology.md#allantois) appears as a small endodermal diverticulum from the caudal yolk-sac region and extends into the [connecting stalk](../../../developmental-biology.md#connecting-stalk). With incorporation of the caudal region into the embryo it becomes related to the [cloaca](../../../developmental-biology.md#cloaca) and developing [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder). Its intraembryonic remnant is associated with the [urachus](../../../anatomy.md#urachus), later represented by the [median umbilical ligament](../../../anatomy.md#median-umbilical-ligament). Unlike the large allantoic sac of a bird, the human [allantois](../../../developmental-biology.md#allantois) is not an expanding external urine reservoir or an independent aerial respiratory organ.[Extra-embryonic Coelom; Allantois: cloacal diverticulum, connecting stalk and subsequent bladder relation](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#coelomic-cavity-development-by-the-university-of-new-south-wales)

Embryonic folding converts part of the original endodermal roof of the [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) into the gut tube. The remaining sac lies outside the embryo and communicates with the midgut through the narrowing [vitelline duct](../../../developmental-biology.md#vitelline-duct). Vitelline vessels accompany its early vascular apparatus. The sac supports early nutritional and [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood)-forming functions even though it contains no large yolk store in humans. Its connection normally regresses; persistence of a [vitelline duct](../../../developmental-biology.md#vitelline-duct) is a different anomaly from persistence of the [urachus](../../../anatomy.md#urachus). The presence of a duct alone does not make the two structures interchangeable.[Introduction; Development; Mechanism: umbilical vesicle, midgut connection, vitelline circulation and regression](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#embryology-yolk-sac-statpearls)

As the [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion) expands, it surrounds the embryo more completely and encloses the roots of the [connecting stalk](../../../developmental-biology.md#connecting-stalk) and yolk-sac connection at the umbilical region. Those structures are brought together within an amniotic covering to form the early [umbilical cord](../../../biology.md#umbilical-cord). The cord initially includes the stalk with allantoic remnants and developing umbilical vessels, together with the vitelline connection and associated structures. The mature cord chiefly contains two [umbilical arteries](../../../biology.md#umbilical-artery) and one [umbilical vein](../../../biology.md#umbilical-vein) embedded in [Wharton's jelly](../../../biology.md#wharton-s-jelly), beneath an amniotic surface. The vessels arise within vascular mesodermal tissues; it would be inaccurate to say that the endodermal allantoic tube simply turns into all three [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel).

The [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion) subsequently comes into apposition and fuses with the [chorion](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorion), greatly reducing the intervening [chorionic cavity](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorionic-cavity). Its surface continues along the cord towards the fetal placental surface. The [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) remains outside the amniotic cavity rather than becoming a normal free object floating in amniotic fluid. The mature [amnion](../../../developmental-biology.md#amnion) is avascular, whereas the placental [chorion](../../../developmental-biology.md#chorion) and the cord have crucial fetal vascular connections.[Introduction and Development: connecting stalk, vitelline connection, amniotic enclosure and mature vessels](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#embryology-umbilical-cord-statpearls)

These relations can be understood by comparing developmental stages in section: first a disc between two cavities, then a folded embryo with narrowed ventral connections, and finally an expanded amniotic covering around the cord. A section through a later cord cannot by itself reveal every early channel that has regressed. The same membrane names are used across vertebrates, but their relative sizes and functions are not identical in a human fetus and a yolk-rich bird egg.

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What is the Natural History of the different organisms known by the name of “Hydatids ?”

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**Hydatids, in the broader nineteenth-century zoological usage, include several bladder-like larval forms of [tapeworms](../../../organism.md#cestoda); their adult and larval appearances belong to connected life cycles.** The main forms to distinguish are the [cysticercus](../../../organism.md#cysticercus), [coenurus](../../../organism.md#coenurus) and echinococcal [hydatid cyst](../../../organism.md#hydatid-cyst). A fluid-filled cyst is not by itself proof of a living parasite.

A [cysticercus](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) is a fluid bladder with a single invaginated [protoscolex](../../../organism.md#protoscolex), the future attachment region of a [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda). The historical [Cysticercus cellulosae](../../../organism.md#cysticercus-cellulosae) is the larval stage of [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium), not a separate adult species. Eggs from a human intestinal [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda) carrier can be ingested by pigs, and the released embryos penetrate the gut and reach tissues where [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) develop. A human eating infected pork containing [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) can acquire an intestinal adult. Humans can also acquire tissue [cysticercosis](../../../biology.md#cysticercosis) by ingesting eggs. **Egg ingestion causing tissue larvae and larval ingestion causing an intestinal adult are different routes.** The same distinction prevents the false conclusion that a bladder worm inside a person's [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) came there as an adult from the intestine.[Causal Agent; Life Cycle: eggs versus pork-borne cysticerci and tissue development](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-cysticercosis)

A [coenurus](../../../organism.md#coenurus) bears numerous [protoscolices](../../../organism.md#protoscolex) on the wall of a common bladder. The familiar cerebral form in sheep, historically [Coenurus cerebralis](../../../organism.md#coenurus-cerebralis), belongs chiefly to the life cycle of [Taenia multiceps](../../../organism.md#taenia-multiceps). Canids carry the adult and shed eggs; sheep and other suitable herbivores develop the tissue larva. A canid eating affected tissue can then acquire adults. [Taenia serialis](../../../organism.md#taenia-serialis) produces another [coenurus](../../../organism.md#coenurus) form, commonly associated with rabbits and with a different distribution of tissue lesions. Humans are accidental intermediate hosts rather than normal hosts of intestinal adults of these [coenurus](../../../organism.md#coenurus)-producing species. The multiple heads on one larval bladder distinguish a [coenurus](../../../organism.md#coenurus) from the ordinary one-headed [cysticercus](../../../organism.md#cysticercus).[Causal Agents; Life Cycle; Hosts; Gross coenurus specimens: common bladder with numerous protoscolices](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-coenurosis)

In cystic [echinococcosis](../../../biology.md#echinococcosis), the larva of the [Echinococcus granulosus](../../../organism.md#echinococcus-granulosus) complex forms a cyst with an outer laminated parasite layer and an inner living [germinal layer of a hydatid cyst](../../../organism.md#germinal-layer-of-a-hydatid-cyst). The host adds its own surrounding tissue reaction, which is not the same structure as the parasite wall. The [germinal layer of a hydatid cyst](../../../organism.md#germinal-layer-of-a-hydatid-cyst) can produce [brood capsules](../../../organism.md#brood-capsule), [protoscolices](../../../organism.md#protoscolex) and daughter cysts; free brood material may form hydatid sand. A cyst without visible heads, historically called an acephalocyst, need not represent a fundamentally unrelated animal. It can be an immature or sterile form, so absence of heads in one preparation is not evidence for spontaneous generation.

The mature echinococcal [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda) is small and inhabits the intestine of a suitable definitive host, classically a dog or another canid. Eggs shed in faeces reach herbivorous intermediate hosts; the released six-hooked embryos cross the intestinal wall and develop in organs, especially [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung). The definitive host completes the cycle by eating infected tissue. Humans acquire the larval infection accidentally by ingesting eggs and are not the usual carriers of the adult. The route is therefore not ordinary consumption of hydatid-bearing meat followed by a human adult echinococcus. Modern classification also distinguishes organisms such as [Echinococcus multilocularis](../../../organism.md#echinococcus-multilocularis), whose branching alveolar larval growth differs from a single typical cyst. Those finer distinctions should not be projected without qualification onto every historical hydatid name.[Causal Agents; Life Cycle: canid adults, intermediate-host cysts, daughter cysts and modern species distinctions](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-echinococcosis)

The natural-history discovery was that dramatic differences of size, habitat and body form can be stages of one organism. Feeding and transmission experiments, together with the continuity of hooks and attachment structures during development, supplied stronger evidence than resemblance between two isolated cysts. [Thomas Spencer Cobbold](../../../physician.md#thomas-spencer-cobbold)'s 1864 treatment already organizes larval forms alongside their cestode relationships.[Cestoda; particular larvae, including coenuri; separate treatment of the echinococcus and hydatids](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#entozoa-an-introduction-to-the-study-of-helminthology-by-thomas-spencer-cobbold) The broader word hydatid thus needs a stage and a host attached to it. Its descriptive use for some non-parasitic cysts is not a licence to assign every cyst to one of these life cycles.

## 10

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the vegetable growths known as the “Yeast Plant” and the “Vinegar Plant.”

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**The yeast plant is a [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus), whereas the characteristic vinegar-forming organisms are [acetic acid bacteria](../../../organism.md#acetic-acid-bacteria).** Calling both vegetable growths reflects an older classification of microscopic organisms; it does not make them green plants or two stages of one species.

The familiar brewing and baking yeast is [Saccharomyces cerevisiae](../../../organism.md#saccharomyces-cerevisiae). Its oval or rounded [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) possess a [cell wall](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-wall), [cell membrane](../../../cell-membrane.md), [cytoplasm](../../../biological-cell.md#cytoplasm), [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) and other eukaryotic structures. Ordinary multiplication is by [budding](../../../reproduction.md#budding): a small outgrowth enlarges, nuclear division supplies it, and separation leaves a scar or a temporary attached cluster. Under suitable conditions, the life cycle also includes mating between compatible haploid [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and formation of spores by diploid [cells](../../../biological-cell.md). Those processes are distinct from simply breaking a chain of accidentally adherent [cells](../../../biological-cell.md). Neither roots nor a leaf-like apparatus is required for its growth.[The Yeast Life Cycle; budding, haploid/diploid phases, mating and sporulation](../../../molecular-biology-and-genetics-bibliography.md#budding-yeast-for-budding-geneticists-a-primer-on-the-saccharomyces-cerevisiae-model-system-by-andrea-a-duina-and-others)

Yeast obtains organic nutrients from its surroundings. In alcoholic [fermentation](../../../metabolism.md#fermentation) it converts fermentable sugar into [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) and [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide); the idealized [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) balance is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}+2\mathrm{CO_2}}.
$$

A real culture also uses material to build [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and produces additional compounds. The process does not require [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) as a reactant in that net balance, although yeast can respire and its growth and product yields depend on conditions. In bread the gas expands the dough; in a fermenting liquid both gas evolution and chemical change can be followed. Gas bubbles alone do not identify the organism responsible.

The historical vinegar plant or mother of vinegar appears as a surface film or gelatinous mass in a suitable alcoholic liquid. [Louis Pasteur](../../../biologist.md#louis-pasteur) used the name [Mycoderma aceti](../../../organism.md#mycoderma-aceti) for the film-forming agent in his investigations of acetification. In modern terms, members of several acetic-acid-bacterial groups can occur in vinegar cultures. Rod-shaped bacterial [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) reproduce by [binary fission](../../../biological-cell.md#binary-fission-biology); some form an extracellular, often [cellulose](../../../chemical-substance.md#cellulose)-rich, matrix that helps produce a visible pellicle. The gross mother is therefore a growth structure or microbial community, not a single large [cell](../../../biological-cell.md) and not necessarily a pure culture of one modern species.[Abstract; microscopy and isolation of pellicle-forming acetic acid bacteria from rice vinegar](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#surface-film-formation-in-static-fermented-rice-vinegar-a-case-study)

Its essential chemical role is oxidation of [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) to [acetic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#acetic-acid) using atmospheric [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}+\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3COOH}+\mathrm{H_2O}}.
$$

Thus the historical term acetic [fermentation](../../../metabolism.md#fermentation) denotes an oxidative microbial process, unlike the [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen)-independent net alcoholic-[fermentation](../../../metabolism.md#fermentation) reaction above. Surface access to air favours the process. With suitable organisms and conditions, further oxidation of the acid can occur, so indefinitely leaving an exhausted culture exposed to air does not guarantee indefinite accumulation of vinegar.

[Louis Pasteur](../../../biologist.md#louis-pasteur)'s [Mémoire sur la fermentation acétique by Louis Pasteur](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-la-fermentation-acetique-by-louis-pasteur), published in 1864, supplies contemporary experimental evidence appropriate to this paper. Inoculating an appropriate alcoholic medium with material from an active film, observing film development and measuring the rise of acidity connects the growth with the chemical process. Comparisons with uninoculated or differently aerated preparations distinguish mere atmospheric exposure from the action of the growth. His experiments also examined the effects of submergence and the conditions under which the film performed acetification.[§III, Manière d'agir du mycoderma aceti: inoculation and experimental vessels; later comparisons of submerged film and substrate conditions](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#memoire-sur-la-fermentation-acetique-by-louis-pasteur)

Wine turning to vinegar therefore illustrates succession: yeast can first supply an alcoholic substrate from sugar, and [acetic acid bacteria](../../../organism.md#acetic-acid-bacteria) can then oxidize that substrate when [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) is available. A white surface growth is not automatically the active vinegar agent; other yeasts and moulds can form films or spoil the liquid. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy), controlled transfer and chemical measurement together provide a natural-history account stronger than identifying every surface skin by its household name.
