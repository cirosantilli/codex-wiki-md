**Original question.**

What other substances besides Oxygen and Nitrogen may be found in Atmospheric Air ? By what tests is their presence or absence indicated ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**Ordinary [air](../../../mixture.md#air) contains [water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor), [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide), variable trace gases and suspended particles in addition to [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen).** Which traces are detected depends on location, sampling volume and test sensitivity. A negative small-sample test is not proof of complete absence.

[Water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor) is shown by condensation on a sufficiently cold surface or by the colour change of a suitable dry indicator: white anhydrous [copper(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii-sulfate) becomes blue on hydration. Passing a known air stream through a drying tube and observing a controlled mass increase gives a more quantitative principle, provided other absorbable vapours are excluded.

[Carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) gives a [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) turbidity when a suitable air sample is passed into clear [limewater](../../../chemical-substance.md#limewater):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm{Ca(OH)}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaCO}_3\downarrow+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

Large excess of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) can redissolve the deposit as dissolved bicarbonate species, so excessive exposure can obscure the initial result. Absorption into an alkaline solution, followed by an appropriate carbonate test, concentrates the trace component.

[Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) and ammonium-bearing particles may be collected by an acid wash and examined after suitable neutralization or liberation of the [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia). [Nessler's reagent](../../../chemistry.md#nessler-s-reagent) supplies a sensitive colour test, with a blank to exclude contaminated reagents. Simple moist [litmus](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#litmus) is much less sensitive. Acidic gases and nitrogen oxides may instead be collected in appropriate wash solutions and examined with tests for the resulting anions; their identity cannot be inferred from acidity alone.

Near relevant sources, [hydrogen sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-sulfide) can blacken moist [lead acetate](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead-acetate) paper by forming [lead sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead-sulfide). Ozone-sensitive starch–iodide paper can indicate an oxidizing trace constituent through liberation of [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine), but other oxidants can give the same response: the historical observation is not a specific proof of [ozone](../../../chemical-substance.md#ozone). Dust and droplets can be collected on a filter or surface and examined by [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy), with chemical tests applied to separated material as needed.

Modern analysis also recognizes [argon](../../../chemical-substance.md#argon) and other noble gases, as well as many low-concentration pollutants, using instrumental separation and [spectroscopy](../../../physics.md#spectroscopy). Their modern recognition should not be silently included among discoveries available to an 1865 candidate. **The useful answer pairs each proposed constituent with a collection method, a specific reaction or observation, and the limits of that test.**

**Original question.**

Why is rain water always ‘soft’ ? Describe and explain the method of estimating quantitatively the ‘hardness’ of water.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**Fresh [rainwater](../../../chemical-substance.md#rainwater) is ordinarily soft because [evaporation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#evaporation) leaves most nonvolatile dissolved salts behind; “always” is an idealization, not an exceptionless law.** [Water hardness](../../../biology.md#water-hardness) is principally caused by dissolved [calcium](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium) and [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) [ions](../../../chemistry.md#ion). Water vapour rising from a hard-water source does not carry these ions with it as vapour. Condensation consequently supplies low-mineral water before contact with dust, surfaces or soil. Mineral particles, sea spray and a collecting surface can add salts, so actual collected [rainwater](../../../chemical-substance.md#rainwater) need not have zero [water hardness](../../../biology.md#water-hardness). Nor does softness establish purity or freedom from microorganisms.[Chemical fact sheet, natural waters and the low mineral content of rainwater](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#hardness-in-drinking-water-by-world-health-organization)

The historically appropriate quantitative principle is [soap titration of water hardness](../../../biology.md#soap-titration-of-water-hardness). Soluble [soap](../../../surface-tension.md#soap) reacts with hardness-producing [ions](../../../chemistry.md#ion) to form poorly soluble salts, using up soap before a persistent lather can form. For a representative carboxylate soap,

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}+2\mathrm{RCOO}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ca(RCOO)}_2\downarrow.}
$$

[Magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) behaves similarly. Once the dissolved hardness-producing ions have substantially reacted, additional soap can sustain the specified lather. The endpoint is therefore an operational observation under standardized shaking, standing time, temperature and sample volume; it is not the first transient bubble.

Use a measured [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of sample in a stoppered vessel with enough headspace for shaking. Add a soap solution of known strength incrementally from a [burette](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#burette), shaking in a reproducible way, until the chosen persistent-lather endpoint appears. Determine a blank with a corresponding amount of soft or distilled [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), because some soap is needed for the lather even when no hardness salts are present. Standardize the soap against a reference solution containing a known calcium amount, expressed as an equivalent [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) of [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate). This standardization is essential: the apparent strength cannot safely be inferred from the mass of an incompletely characterized commercial soap.

Let $V_s$ be the sample [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) in millilitres, $V$ the soap titre in millilitres and $B$ the corresponding blank. If $f$ is the standardized capacity in milligrams of [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) equivalent per millilitre of soap solution, then

$$
\boxed{H=\frac{1000f(V-B)}{V_s}\quad\mathrm{mg\,L^{-1}}\text{ as }\mathrm{CaCO}_3.}
$$

For a standard containing $m_0$ milligrams of [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) equivalent and requiring blank-corrected titre $V_0-B_0$, $f=m_0/(V_0-B_0)$. This makes the measurement quantitative without assuming an unreported soap concentration or inventing a sample titre. Historical results could instead be stated as grains of [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) equivalent per gallon, provided the gallon convention was specified.

Repeating the examination after controlled boiling, cooling, removal of the precipitate and restoration of the original volume estimates the residual or permanent hardness. The reduction estimates the boiling-removable fraction, often called temporary hardness. A representative reaction is

$$
\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}+2\mathrm{HCO}_3^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaCO}_3\downarrow+\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.
$$

Carbonate equilibria and magnesium chemistry make this more subtle than assuming every dissolved salt is destroyed by boiling. Very hard samples may need measured dilution; magnesium, other metal ions and contamination can disturb the lather endpoint. Thus the soap result is an experimentally calibrated hardness equivalent, not an exact identification of every original salt.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How would you examine a sample of water for (1) Lead, (2) Nitrates, (3) Organic matter ? Under what circumstances is water liable to contain each of these ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Examine separate, properly collected aliquots for [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead), [nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#nitrate) and [organic matter](../../../chemistry.md#organic-matter), and interpret the results with the source and sampling conditions.** These are three different analytical questions. No sample observations are supplied, so no actual contaminant concentration or positive finding can be assigned.

For [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead), preserve a representative portion in clean apparatus, avoiding lead-containing fittings or contaminated reagents. Distinguish dissolved material from suspended or particulate material if that distinction is wanted; filtering at the outset otherwise changes what is being measured. Historically, a suitable concentration step followed by [hydrogen sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-sulfide) gives dark [lead sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead-sulfide):

$$
\mathrm{Pb}^{2+}+\mathrm H_2\mathrm S\longrightarrow\mathrm{PbS}\downarrow+2\mathrm H^+.
$$

A separate confirmation uses [potassium iodide](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-iodide) to give yellow [lead(II) iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead-ii-iodide). Its characteristic recrystallization behaviour on heating and cooling a suitable aqueous preparation is useful supporting evidence. Dark sulfide alone is insufficient, because other metals give dark precipitates. Establish a reagent blank, compare with known material, and record the sensitivity; a negative visual test cannot exclude all low-level [lead](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead).

The principal circumstance is contact with lead-bearing pipes, cisterns, solder or other plumbing materials. Stagnation, acidity and water chemistry influence release; newly exposed metal and disturbed corrosion deposits can also matter. Low hardness does not by itself predict a universally safe or unsafe supply: [pH](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph), alkalinity, dissolved gases, protective films and the actual materials must be considered together. Current CDC guidance confirms the importance of corrosion of plumbing and of contact time and water chemistry.[Overview and Exposure: plumbing sources, corrosion, acidity, mineral content and contact conditions](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#about-lead-in-drinking-water-by-cdc)

For [nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#nitrate), the historical [brown-ring test](../../../chemistry.md#brown-ring-test) uses fresh [iron(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-sulfate) and a carefully formed strongly acidic interface. Reduction of nitrate produces [nitrogen monoxide](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-monoxide), which forms the brown nitrosyl complex with excess iron reagent:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{NO}_3^-+3\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}+4\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow\mathrm{NO}+3\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}+2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

The observable ring is associated with the resulting iron–nitrosyl species, not with free nitrate having a brown colour. Use nitrate-free reagents and, if necessary, a suitably concentrated sample. [Nitrite](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) can interfere and must be distinguished independently; adding [nitric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitric-acid) during an earlier preparation would invalidate this test for nitrate originally in the water. Strong acid and heating can disturb the ring, so the observation depends on the procedure as well as the constituents.

[Nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#nitrate) may occur after oxidation of nitrogenous wastes in soil, from manure, sewage, cesspit leakage, fertilizer leaching or natural nitrogen cycling. It can persist after much readily oxidizable organic material has disappeared. Its presence alone therefore neither proves recent sewage entry nor identifies a particular source. Current EPA source-water guidance includes agricultural runoff, septic leakage, sewage and atmospheric inputs among nitrate sources.[Contaminants That Can Affect Drinking Water, Nutrients](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#common-considerations-for-source-water-protection-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)

For [organic matter](../../../chemistry.md#organic-matter), inspect colour, turbidity and sediment, and examine a suitable concentrate by [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy). Evaporating a measured portion and examining the residue for charring or change on ignition supplies further evidence, but loss on heating is not specific: salts and retained water can also change. A controlled [potassium permanganate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-permanganate) consumption experiment compares a sample with a reagent blank under stated acidity, reaction time and temperature. It detects oxidizable material, which can include inorganic reducing substances, and may incompletely oxidize organic material. The amount consumed is consequently an operational observation, not a universal conversion into a mass of “sewage”.

Sources include decaying vegetation, peat and soil-derived material as well as animal wastes, sewage and some industrial discharges. Natural organic colour is not synonymous with sewage, and clear water may contain dissolved organic contaminants or microorganisms. A modern safety assessment therefore adds validated microbiological and chemical measurements appropriate to the source; these historical qualitative tests alone cannot establish potability.[Groundwater and surface-water contamination; nutrients and pathogens](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#septic-system-impacts-on-water-sources-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)

**Original question.**

Give an account of some method by which Arsenic may be detected in the presence of organic substances.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The historical [Marsh test](../../../biology.md#marsh-test) separates arsenic into a volatile hydride and then into a visible deposit, allowing it to be examined apart from much of the original organic matrix.** It requires controls and confirmation because a dark deposit is not unique to [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic). The test is associated with [James Marsh](../../../chemistry.md#james-marsh); its primary account explicitly addresses mixtures containing animal and vegetable matter.[Opening pages, analytical purpose and hydrogen-generating apparatus](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#separation-of-arsenic-by-james-marsh)

Retain representative material and a control portion before destructive preparation. A complex organic sample can be clarified and, where needed, subjected to a validated wet oxidation that destroys interfering organic matter while retaining the arsenic in the analytical preparation. Avoid uncontrolled heating to dryness or conditions that volatilize an arsenic compound; successful mineralization cannot simply be assumed. The reagent sequence must leave the arsenic in a reducible form and must not leave enough oxidant to defeat the subsequent reduction. A recovery experiment with a known addition is a check on the entire preparation, not merely on the last colour reaction.

In the hydrogen-generating stage, arsenic-free [zinc](../../../chemical-substance.md#zinc) reacts with a suitable dilute acid. The sample's reducible arsenic is converted to [arsine](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsine), which passes with the carrier gas out of the generating vessel. A representative net reduction balance for [arsenic(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-iii) is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm H_3\mathrm{AsO}_3+3\mathrm{Zn}+6\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow\mathrm{AsH}_3+3\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}+3\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

This is a reaction balance, not a claim that the old phrase “nascent hydrogen” identifies one uniquely established microscopic mechanism.

The apparatus needs controlled reagent entry, an unobstructed gas path and a means of excluding droplets carried from the sample. In the later heated-tube form, the hydride decomposes at a hot section:

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{AsH}_3\longrightarrow2\mathrm{As}+3\mathrm H_2.}
$$

Arsenic collects as a mirror on the cooler glass beyond it. Marsh's original flame method deposited material on a cold surface held in relation to the burning jet. The original arrangement and the heated-tube improvement should not be treated as identical apparatus. In either case the useful analytical separation is transfer of the arsenic away from the nonvolatile matrix, not just darkening of that matrix.

Run the complete reagent blank first: contaminated zinc or acid can otherwise make an arsenic-free sample appear positive. A positive known control checks that hydride production and collection work. [Antimony](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony) can form [stibine](../../../chemical-substance.md#stibine) and a similar dark deposit, so mirror formation alone is not conclusive. Confirm with independent chemistry, for example controlled oxidation of the collected deposit followed by appropriate arsenic tests; the greater susceptibility of an arsenic mirror to suitable hypochlorite treatment supplies an additional historical distinction from an antimony deposit. No single visual resemblance should replace these discriminations.

A negative test is meaningful only relative to the validated preparation, detection limit and controls. Modern arsenic analysis uses instrumental methods with defined recovery and sensitivity; the historical demonstration is not itself a contemporary forensic certification. [Arsine](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsine) is acutely toxic, and the hydrogen-bearing gas stream introduces further apparatus hazards: this account explains the analytical chemistry, not a household procedure.[Physical Description, Symptoms and Target Organs](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#arsine-in-the-niosh-pocket-guide)

**Original question.**

What reactions do the solutions exhibit which are obtained by dissolving (1) Iron and (2) Tin in Hydrochloric and Nitrohydrochloric acids respectively ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**[Iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) in nonoxidizing [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid) principally gives an [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) solution; [tin](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin) in oxidizing [aqua regia](../../../chemistry.md#aqua-regia) principally gives a [tin(IV)](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-iv) solution.** These are the two preparations paired most directly by “respectively”. Their contrast is clearer when the other acid treatment of each metal is considered as well. “Nitrohydrochloric acid” is the historical name for [aqua regia](../../../chemistry.md#aqua-regia), containing nitric and hydrochloric acids.

For [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) in dilute [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid),

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fe}+2\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow\mathrm{FeCl}_2+\mathrm H_2.}
$$

The fresh solution is typically pale green when sufficiently concentrated. [Hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) gives a pale green precipitate of [iron(II) hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-hydroxide), which darkens and turns brown on exposure to air as oxidation proceeds. [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) can similarly precipitate a ferrous hydroxide rather than forming a stable soluble excess-ammonia complex under the usual test conditions. [Ferricyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferricyanide) gives a deep blue product; the modern structural account of this blue material should not be replaced by an overly literal formula inferred solely from the reagent names. Ferrous solution also reduces a suitable oxidant such as acidic [permanganate](../../../chemistry.md#permanganate):

$$
\mathrm{MnO}_4^-+5\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}+8\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}+5\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}+4\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.
$$

If instead oxidizing acid treatment produces [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii), the solution exhibits ferric reactions: [hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) or [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) gives red-brown [iron(III) hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii-hydroxide); [thiocyanate](../../../chemical-substance.md#thiocyanate) gives a strong red colour; and [ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferrocyanide) gives [Prussian blue](../../../chemistry.md#prussian-blue). Chloride complex formation affects colour and some equilibria, so “yellow” is not a universal iron(III) identity test. Oxidizing [aqua regia](../../../chemistry.md#aqua-regia) supplies this ferric comparison rather than merely generating more of the same ferrous salt.

[Tin](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin) in ordinary [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid) gives a [tin(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-ii) solution with hydrogen evolution:

$$
\mathrm{Sn}+2\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow\mathrm{SnCl}_2+\mathrm H_2.
$$

Its characteristic property is reducing power. With [mercury(II) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#mercury-ii-chloride), controlled addition can first form white [mercury(I) chloride](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-i-chloride) and then, with further reductant, grey metallic [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet):

$$
2\mathrm{HgCl}_2+\mathrm{SnCl}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hg}_2\mathrm{Cl}_2\downarrow+\mathrm{SnCl}_4,
$$



$$
\mathrm{Hg}_2\mathrm{Cl}_2+\mathrm{SnCl}_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Hg}\downarrow+\mathrm{SnCl}_4.
$$

The chloride formulae are useful net bookkeeping, not a complete statement of all aqueous chloro-complexes.

In [aqua regia](../../../chemistry.md#aqua-regia), [tin](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin) is oxidized predominantly to [tin(IV)](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-iv). A representative balance, choosing [nitrogen monoxide](../../../chemistry.md#nitrogen-monoxide) as the nitrate-reduction product, is

$$
\boxed{3\mathrm{Sn}+4\mathrm{HNO}_3+12\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow3\mathrm{SnCl}_4+4\mathrm{NO}+8\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

Actual gas products depend on the acid conditions. Sufficient [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride) and acidity retain tin in solution; dilution can cause hydrolysis and precipitation of hydrated [tin(IV) oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-iv-oxide). This differs from the simple expectation that every dilution leaves unchanged molecular $\mathrm{SnCl}_4$ in water. [Hydrogen sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-sulfide) in suitable acidic conditions gives yellow [tin(IV) sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-iv-sulfide), compared with brown [tin(II) sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-ii-sulfide) from a stannous solution. [Tin(IV)](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-iv) does not reproduce the characteristic reducing reaction of fresh [tin(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#tin-ii) with mercuric chloride; reduction to tin(II) is needed first. Oxidation during storage can therefore change the reactions of a nominally stannous preparation.

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Give some account of the metal Antimony and its combinations.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Antimony](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony) is the brittle, silvery crystalline element $\mathrm{Sb}$, encountered especially in sulfide ores and in compounds of oxidation states $+3$ and $+5$.** Its historical treatment as a metal is understandable from appearance and alloying behaviour; modern classifications commonly call it a [metalloid](../../../chemical-substance.md#metalloid). It is too brittle to behave like a readily drawn, ductile metal and is used in suitable alloys to alter properties such as hardness.

The important ore is [stibnite](../../../chemical-substance.md#stibnite), $\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3$. A historical reduction can use [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) to bind the [sulfur](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3+3\mathrm{Fe}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}+3\mathrm{FeS}.}
$$

Another route roasts suitable sulfide material to an oxide and then reduces that oxide with [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon). For an ideal trioxide product,

$$
2\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3+9\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm O_3+6\mathrm{SO}_2,
$$



$$
\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm O_3+3\mathrm C\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Sb}+3\mathrm{CO}.
$$

Real ore processing requires control of impurities and volatile losses, so these balances do not alone constitute a complete extraction flowsheet.[Appearance, Uses and Natural abundance](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/antimony-element-information-properties-and-uses)

Among its principal combinations, [antimony(III) oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-iii-oxide) is an amphoteric oxide. It dissolves under suitable acidic or alkaline conditions rather than fitting a universal rule that a metal oxide must be solely basic. [Antimony(III) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-iii-chloride), historically “butter of antimony”, hydrolyses on dilution and can deposit a white oxychloride-containing material. A useful simplified balance is

$$
\mathrm{SbCl}_3+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{SbOCl}\downarrow+2\mathrm{HCl}.
$$

Added acid favours dissolution; a more extensively hydrolysed precipitate need not be pure $\mathrm{SbOCl}$. Pentavalent compounds include [antimony(V) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-v-chloride) and antimonate species; chloride-rich solutions and their hydrolysis require attention to the medium.

[Antimony(III) sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#antimony-iii-sulfide) can occur as the dark crystalline ore and as an orange precipitate obtained from a suitable solution with [hydrogen sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-sulfide):

$$
2\mathrm{Sb}^{3+}+3\mathrm H_2\mathrm S\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sb}_2\mathrm S_3\downarrow+6\mathrm H^+.
$$

Its dissolution in suitable alkaline sulfide solutions forms thioantimony species, providing an analytical separation from some other sulfides. Acidification can reverse that operational separation. A volatile hydride, [stibine](../../../chemical-substance.md#stibine), $\mathrm{SbH}_3$, can decompose to a dark antimony deposit and thus interfere with the [Marsh test](../../../biology.md#marsh-test) for [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic).

[Antimony potassium tartrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate), “tartar emetic”, is a historically important complex salt, not merely an arbitrary mixture of an antimony salt and a potassium salt. It was used medicinally, but historical use is not proof of safety or a present indication; antimony-containing substances can cause serious poisoning. No dosage or treatment recommendation follows from describing them.[Identity and toxicity](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#antimony-potassium-tartrate-by-the-national-poisons-information-service)

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the modes of preparation and the properties of Carbonic Oxide.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**“Carbonic oxide” means [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide), $\mathrm{CO}$: a combustible, reducing, colourless gas distinct from [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide).** Several preparations remove part of the oxygen from a carbon–oxygen compound or prevent complete oxidation of carbon.

Passing [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) over sufficiently hot [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm C\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm{CO}.}
$$

High temperature favours substantial conversion. The reaction is important in furnaces, but gas emerging from such a process need not be pure [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide); residual [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and other gases must be considered. Incomplete [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) of carbonaceous fuel is another source, with the ideal net balance $2\mathrm C+\mathrm O_2\to2\mathrm{CO}$. It is a source principle rather than a selective method guaranteeing a pure sample.

For a historical laboratory preparation, [formic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#formic-acid) can be dehydrated by a suitable strongly dehydrating medium:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{HCOOH}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CO}+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

[Oxalic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxalic-acid) under an appropriate dehydrating treatment yields a mixture:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm H_2\mathrm C_2\mathrm O_4\longrightarrow\mathrm{CO}+\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

An alkaline absorption stage removes much of the [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide), while [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) passes on. A generator, droplet trap, purifier and collecting vessel illustrate why preparation and purification are separate operations. The gas is only slightly soluble in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), so collection over water is possible in principle. Concentrated [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) acting on carbonaceous material can also give mixed carbon oxides and [sulfur dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur-dioxide); that route requires additional purification and should not be presented as clean CO evolution without by-products.

The [relative density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#relative-density) of [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) against dry [air](../../../mixture.md#air) is approximately $28.01/28.97\simeq0.967$. It has no reliable warning odour. It burns with a blue flame when supplied with suitable [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen):

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{CO}+\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CO}_2.}
$$

A CO sample itself does not ordinarily support combustion of a taper. Nor does it immediately give the usual limewater test of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide); oxidation of the CO first gives a gas that does. This distinguishes fuel behaviour from the property of supporting combustion of another material.

Its reducing power is illustrated by

$$
\mathrm{CuO}+\mathrm{CO}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}+\mathrm{CO}_2
$$

and by reduction of suitable iron oxides in metallurgy. The products reflect transfer of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) from the oxide to [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide). Its ability to bind metal centres is also chemically important. In biological exposure, binding to [haemoglobin](../../../protein.md#hemoglobin) and other effects disrupt oxygen delivery and use; it is a serious poison, not merely an innocuous replacement of atmospheric oxygen. This modern explanation must not turn an historical gas-preparation answer into a proposed exposure experiment.[Sources and the colourless, odourless character of carbon monoxide](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#carbon-monoxide-poisoning-basics-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)[How carbon monoxide affects the body](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-carbon-monoxide-poisoning-by-cdc)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How are Ether, Bisulphide of Carbon, and Chloroform prepared ?

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**The three preparations use different chemistry: [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) from [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) by acid-catalysed dehydration, [carbon disulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-disulfide) by combination of [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and [sulfur](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur), and [chloroform](../../../chemical-substance.md#chloroform) by chlorination and cleavage of a suitable organic starting material.** “Ether” here means [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether), not every member of the modern ether class; “bisulphide of carbon” means [carbon disulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-disulfide).

For [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether), heating [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) with a suitable proportion of [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) favours intermolecular dehydration:

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm C_2\mathrm H_5\mathrm{OH}\longrightarrow\mathrm C_2\mathrm H_5\mathrm O\mathrm C_2\mathrm H_5+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

The historical explanation invokes formation of [ethyl hydrogen sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#ethyl-hydrogen-sulfate) and its reaction with a further ethanol molecule, regenerating sulfuric acid. The acid therefore need not be consumed stoichiometrically in the overall balance. The modern mechanism treats a protonated alcohol or related activated species as the substrate for attack by another ethanol molecule.[Opening paragraph and reaction mechanism: sulfuric-acid-catalysed formation of symmetrical ethers from primary alcohols](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/18-2-preparing-ethers) Conditions matter: excessive heating favours formation of [Ethylene](../../../polymer.md#ethylene) rather than ether, and strongly oxidizing conditions can give additional products. Condensation of the volatile product, removal of acidic and water-soluble impurities, drying and redistillation separate production from purification. A cold receiver is appropriate to the volatile ether; an open flame near the escaping vapour would be incompatible with its ready flammability.

For [carbon disulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-disulfide), sulfur vapour is brought into contact with sufficiently hot [charcoal](../../../chemical-substance.md#charcoal) in a retort:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm C+2\mathrm S\longrightarrow\mathrm{CS}_2.}
$$

Here $\mathrm S$ records sulfur atoms in the balance, not a claim that the sulfur vapour has only monatomic molecules. The outgoing vapours are cooled and the liquid separated from excess sulfur and other impurities, then purified by distillation. The high-temperature reaction zone and the cooled collecting region have different functions. [Carbon disulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-disulfide) is a volatile, highly flammable solvent; its historical use does not establish harmlessness.[Physical Description, flammability and Symptoms](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#carbon-disulfide-in-the-niosh-pocket-guide)

For [chloroform](../../../chemical-substance.md#chloroform), a historically important route uses [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) with a slurry containing [bleaching powder](../../../chemistry.md#bleaching-powder). Oxidation first supplies [acetaldehyde](../../../chemical-substance.md#acetaldehyde); further chlorination gives [chloral](../../../chemical-substance.md#chloral), which alkali cleaves to [chloroform](../../../chemical-substance.md#chloroform) and [formate](../../../chemical-substance.md#formate). An illustrative final step with [calcium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-hydroxide) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{CCl}_3\mathrm{CHO}+\mathrm{Ca(OH)}_2\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CHCl}_3+\mathrm{Ca(HCOO)}_2.}
$$

The bleaching material supplies oxidizing and chlorinating capacity, while the alkaline conditions permit cleavage. It is misleading to describe the entire operation as one simple substitution directly replacing three hydrogens of intact ethanol. It is also unnecessary to pretend that commercial [bleaching powder](../../../chemistry.md#bleaching-powder) has a single invariant molecular composition.

[Acetone](../../../chemical-substance.md#acetone) supplies an alternative methyl-carbonyl starting material for the same broad haloform chemistry. In ionic notation a clean idealized balance is

$$
\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COCH}_3+3\mathrm{OCl}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{CHCl}_3+\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COO}^-+2\mathrm{OH}^-.
$$

The volatile product is condensed with water-containing material; a chloroform-rich phase is separated, washed appropriately, dried and redistilled. Identity and purity require more than observing a dense lower layer. These operations describe preparation chemistry rather than a specified pharmacopoeial recipe or a claim of suitability for anaesthesia. The historical anaesthetic importance of [chloroform](../../../chemical-substance.md#chloroform) does not remove its toxic effects.[Acute and delayed toxicity](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#chloroform-toxicological-overview-by-ukhsa)

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the process by which the percentage composition of a substance consisting of Carbon, Hydrogen, and Oxygen is determined.

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**Burn a known [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) of pure, suitably dry material completely, collect the resulting [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) separately, calculate the carbon and hydrogen masses, and obtain oxygen by difference under the stated three-element assumption.** This is [organic elemental analysis](../../../chemistry.md#organic-elemental-analysis), not an identification of molecular structure.

Weigh a representative portion of mass $m$ in a clean combustion boat or suitable carrier. In the classical apparatus, a heated combustion tube contains an oxidizing material such as [copper(II) oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper-ii-oxide), with a controlled stream of clean, dry oxygen-bearing gas where the arrangement requires it. The purpose is complete conversion of [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) into [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) into [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), without unburnt material escaping. An equation for a model substrate is

$$
\mathrm C_x\mathrm H_y\mathrm O_z+\left(x+\frac y4-\frac z2\right)\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow x\mathrm{CO}_2+\frac y2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O,
$$

with the oxygen amount understood as the net external requirement when positive.

Downstream, a preweighed water-absorption tube collects the [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). A following preweighed alkaline absorber collects the [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide); the order prevents water from being incorrectly attributed to the carbon dioxide absorber. Guards exclude atmospheric moisture and carbon dioxide during handling, and a final clean-gas sweep transfers products still held in the apparatus. Let the blank-corrected mass increases be $a$ for the water collector and $b$ for the carbon dioxide collector.

Using the simple historical atomic-mass ratios $\mathrm C=12$, $\mathrm H=1$, $\mathrm O=16$,

$$
\boxed{m_{\rm C}=\frac{12}{44}b=\frac3{11}b,\qquad m_{\rm H}=\frac2{18}a=\frac19a.}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\%\mathrm C=100\frac{3b}{11m},\qquad\%\mathrm H=100\frac{a}{9m},}
$$



$$
\boxed{\%\mathrm O=100-\%\mathrm C-\%\mathrm H.}
$$

Modern work uses the appropriate modern molar masses in the same ratios. No sample mass or absorption results are supplied, so no numerical percentages are justified.

The oxygen already present in the sample cannot be obtained merely by measuring the oxygen in the combustion products: the oxidant supplies additional oxygen. Difference works here only because the sample is assumed to contain exclusively carbon, hydrogen and oxygen and to be free of unaccounted water, solvent, ash and other elements. If the combustion leaves a mineral residue, that mass cannot silently be reported as oxygen. A separate check of purity and residue therefore tests an assumption of the calculation.

Incomplete combustion, escaped vapour, exhausted absorbers, moisture entering from the carrier gas, and contamination of the carbon dioxide absorber all bias the result. A blank and an independent repeat with a suitable reference material check recovery. Oxygen by difference accumulates the errors in both other determinations; forcing the percentages to sum to 100 is not independent proof that the analysis is correct.

## 10

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What other substances besides those named in the preceding question may an organic substance contain, and how is their presence discovered ?

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**An organic substance may also contain [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen), [sulfur](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur), [phosphorus](../../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus), [halogens](../../../chemical-substance.md#halogen) and, in some cases, [metals](../../../chemical-substance.md#metal) or other elements.** “Organic” does not mean “made of only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen”. Detection generally requires converting a covalently bound element into a species that an appropriate inorganic test can recognize.

A historically available general method is the [sodium fusion test](../../../chemistry.md#sodium-fusion-test). Fusion converts suitable nitrogen-containing material into [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide), sulfur into [sulfide](../../../chemistry.md#sulfide), and chlorine-, bromine- or iodine-containing material into the corresponding soluble halides. The cooled reaction material is worked up to an aqueous extract, with separate portions reserved for separate tests. The transformations are analytical conversion principles, not claims that every nitrogen or sulfur compound must give quantitative recovery under any fusion conditions. Blanks and a known positive material are essential when a negative result is to be interpreted.[Organic qualitative analysis, sodium-fusion screening for nitrogen, sulfur and halogens, printed p. 203 and associated tables](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#basic-tables-for-chemical-analysis-by-national-bureau-of-standards)

For [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen), a suitable cyanide-containing extract is treated with a fresh [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) reagent and then brought through the appropriate oxidation and acidification sequence to obtain [Prussian blue](../../../chemistry.md#prussian-blue). Formation of [ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferrocyanide) is represented by

$$
\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}+6\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}.
$$

The eventual ferric ferrocyanide material provides the blue observation. It is not sufficient simply to add any iron salt and assume that a positive blue must appear. Another classical principle converts nitrogen in suitable organic materials into [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) by heating with alkaline reagents, then recognizes the ammonia in a receiver. That test has substrate-dependent limitations and is not a universal replacement for complete nitrogen analysis. A later specialized nitrogen-digestion protocol must not be silently attributed to 1865.

For [sulfur](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur), a sulfide-containing alkaline extract can give black [lead sulfide](../../../chemical-substance.md#lead-sulfide) with a suitably introduced lead reagent:

$$
\mathrm{Pb}^{2+}+\mathrm S^{2-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{PbS}\downarrow.
$$

The strongly acidic form of the reagent conditions changes the available sulfide, so conditions must be controlled. Oxidizing the material to [sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate) and identifying a persistent [barium sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#barium-sulfate) precipitate supplies another principle, using sulfur-free reagents and excluding pre-existing sulfate contamination. If nitrogen and sulfur occur together, thiocyanate formation can alter a fusion test; failure of the expected cyanide reaction is not conclusive evidence that nitrogen is absent.

For [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine), [bromine](../../../chemical-substance.md#bromine) and [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine), remove interfering cyanide and sulfide by an appropriate controlled treatment of a separate extract portion, then use [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate) under suitable nitric-acid conditions. The precipitates are ordinarily white [silver chloride](../../../optics.md#silver-chloride), cream [silver bromide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-bromide) and yellow [silver iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-iodide). Their differing behaviour with aqueous [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) provides corroboration: chloride dissolves relatively readily, bromide requires stronger conditions, and iodide is resistant. Do not use [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid) to prepare the portion in which original chloride is being sought. [Fluorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#fluorine) requires a different recognition method: soluble [silver fluoride](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-fluoride) means this precipitation scheme does not provide an equivalent fluoride test.

For [phosphorus](../../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus), oxidize a suitable portion to [phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#phosphate), then apply a phosphate test such as yellow [ammonium phosphomolybdate](../../../chemistry.md#ammonium-phosphomolybdate) formation under the proper acidic conditions. [Arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic) species and other interferences must be excluded when relevant. Mineral ash can reveal [metal](../../../chemical-substance.md#metal) constituents through their specific precipitation or flame reactions, but oxidation during ashing can change their original chemical form. Absence of a visible ash does not prove absence of every metallic or otherwise volatile element.

A positive result identifies an element or an operationally generated ion; it does not establish which [functional group](../../../chemistry.md#functional-group) contained it in the original molecule. Conversely, an incomplete conversion can give a false negative. This is why the three-element premise used in the preceding combustion calculation should be checked rather than accepted merely because a substance is described as organic.
