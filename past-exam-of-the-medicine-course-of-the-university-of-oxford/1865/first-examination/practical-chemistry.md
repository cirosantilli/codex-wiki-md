## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Practical chemistry](practical-chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Each of the tubes, $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, contains a single substance.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

TODO: The single substances in tubes a, b, c and d are absent and no identifying test observations are given.

**The identities of a, b, c and d remain undetermined.** The assertion that each tube holds a single [chemical substance](../../../chemical-substance.md) narrows the type of problem but gives no [chemical test](../../../chemistry.md#chemical-test) result from which to choose a name.

A useful examination would give each specimen its own labelled record and preserve an untouched portion. Record physical appearance and behaviour in suitable [solvents](../../../mixture.md#solvent) before choosing tests. A soluble inorganic [salt](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) calls for separate investigation of its [cation](../../../chemistry.md#cation) and [anion](../../../chemistry.md#anion); an insoluble material requires a justified preparation step before solution tests can be interpreted. A colour change, [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate) or evolved [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) must be recorded as an observation before it is used as evidence for a composition. Fresh portions prevent one [reagent](../../../chemical-reaction.md#reagent) from introducing the ion sought by the next test.

In [qualitative chemical analysis](../../../chemistry.md#qualitative-chemical-analysis), a discriminating result is followed by an independent confirmation and comparison with a [reagent blank](../../../chemistry.md#reagent-blank) and a known reference. For example, the behaviour of a metal [hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) depends on both the [cation](../../../chemistry.md#cation) and the chemical conditions; “a precipitate formed” is not a complete identification.[Metal hydroxides and negative-ion tests: colour, behaviour in excess reagent and separate anion tests](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#testing-salts-for-anions-and-cations-by-the-royal-society-of-chemistry) The record supplied here contains none of those observations, so no particular reaction or identity can be assigned to any of the four letters.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Practical chemistry](practical-chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

The solution $e$ is to be examined for hydrocyanic acid, and the solution $f$ for iron and magnesia.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

TODO: Solutions e and f and their test observations are absent. Hydrocyanic acid, iron and magnesia are testing targets, not supplied positive findings; general test methods can still be described.

**The presence or absence of [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) in e, and of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) or [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) in f, remains unestablished.** The named substances are targets of the examination. They are not positive findings.

The period name “hydrocyanic acid” refers to aqueous [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide). “Magnesia” is the historical name for [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide); examination of a [chemical solution](../../../mixture.md#solution-chemistry) for magnesia normally concerns dissolved [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) compounds, rather than a claim that intact [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide) particles are dissolved unchanged. The modern [ionic equations](../../../chemical-reaction.md#ionic-equation) below express test principles and are not observations made on f.

For e, the analytical task is to obtain a response attributable to [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide), distinguish it from responses of other constituents, and check the entire procedure against a [reagent blank](../../../chemistry.md#reagent-blank) and a known reference. The distinction between free [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) and [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) held in a [coordination complex](../../../chemistry.md#coordination-complex) matters: a test for one fraction need not measure the other. As a modern comparison, the [Cyanide in Waters and Extracts, Method 9014 by United States Environmental Protection Agency](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency) provides [titrimetric analysis](../../../chemistry.md#titration) and [spectrophotometric analysis](../../../chemistry.md#spectrophotometric-analysis), with specified sample preparation, interferences and quality controls. This 2014 procedure illustrates how a result is validated; it is not being attributed to the 1865 candidate.[§§1–2: analytical scope and measurement principles; §§4 and 9: interferences and controls](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency) No numerical [concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration), positive colour or negative result can be extracted from the label e.

An odour is neither an acceptable identifying experiment nor a reliable negative test. [Hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) is volatile and highly toxic, and many people cannot detect its supposed characteristic smell. Any actual analysis belongs in a suitably contained laboratory method; uncontrolled acidification of a possible [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) solution can release [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide).[Agent Characteristics: volatility, toxicity and unreliable odour warning](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#hydrogen-cyanide-emergency-response-information-by-niosh)[§5.2: hydrogen cyanide release on acidification](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)

For f, distinguish [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) from [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) when describing a test. On suitable separate portions, [hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#hydroxide) can produce a green [iron(II) hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-hydroxide) [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate) or a brown hydrated [iron(III) hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii-hydroxide) [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate). [Iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) products can oxidise during handling, so the time at which a colour is observed matters. The simplified precipitation balances are:

$$
\mathrm{Fe^{2+}+2OH^-\longrightarrow Fe(OH)_2(s)},\qquad
\mathrm{Fe^{3+}+3OH^-\longrightarrow Fe(OH)_3(s)}.
$$

These equations describe possible chemical responses, not a choice between them for the missing f. Tests intended for a particular [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) cannot be treated as universal tests of every form of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron).

Dissolved [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) can give a white [magnesium hydroxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-hydroxide) [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate):

$$
\mathrm{Mg^{2+}+2OH^-\longrightarrow Mg(OH)_2(s)}.
$$

The white solid does not characteristically dissolve in excess [sodium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium-hydroxide) under the usual qualitative-test conditions. This differentiates it from some amphoteric [hydroxides](../../../chemistry.md#hydroxide), but is not uniquely specific for [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium).[Metal hydroxides: iron(II), iron(III), magnesium and the distinction from aluminium in excess sodium hydroxide](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#testing-salts-for-anions-and-cations-by-the-royal-society-of-chemistry) If [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) and [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium) were together, a single mixed [precipitate](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitate) could obscure their separate behaviour. An analytical separation with controlled [pH](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph), followed by confirmation on the separated fractions, would be needed; indiscriminately adding excess alkali is not proof of separation.[printed II & III-15–17: hydroxide concentration, magnesium interference and iron confirmation](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#qualitative-analysis-of-metal-ions-in-solution-by-williams-college) The actual [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) of any [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron), the presence of [magnesium](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnesium), and the outcome of such a separation all remain unknown.

**Original question.**

Under what circumstances can you obtain a fixed gas from the solid substance $g$, and what is the nature of the gas ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

TODO: Solid substance g is an unidentified supplied specimen. Without its identity or relevant observations, the particular gas and conditions for obtaining it cannot be determined.

**Neither the gas nor the conditions required to obtain it from g can be identified.** The word “fixed” does not supply a chemical formula, and the existence of a labelled solid does not establish that it is a [carbonate](../../../chemistry.md#carbonate) or any other particular [chemical compound](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-compound).

A defensible investigation would first characterise g and distinguish physical release of an absorbed [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) from a [chemical reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md) that produces a new [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas). Whether [heating](../../../heat.md#heat-transfer), contact with a [solvent](../../../mixture.md#solvent), or a selected [reagent](../../../chemical-reaction.md#reagent) is relevant depends on that characterisation. An arbitrary [thermal decomposition](../../../chemical-reaction.md#thermal-decomposition) equation would therefore assume the very composition that is missing.

If an identified reaction produced a [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas), the apparatus would be chosen for its actual properties: a reaction vessel and delivery system, a means of distinguishing the product from initially enclosed [air](../../../mixture.md#air), and a compatible collection or detection method. Collection over [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) would be unsuitable when dissolution or reaction with [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) materially changes the product; a [gas syringe](../../../second-law-of-thermodynamics.md#gas-syringe) is an alternative only when compatible with the substances and conditions. The gas would then require discriminating [chemical tests](../../../chemistry.md#chemical-test), and the residue could help check the proposed reaction. No evolution, odour, combustion behaviour, [precipitation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry) result or residue is reported for g, so none of those observations can be supplied as its answer.
