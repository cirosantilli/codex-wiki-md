↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $V$ is the three-dimensional space occupied by the body. Its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $m$ measures its inertia: in [Newtonian mechanics](../../../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) the resultant [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is $m\mathbf a$. Its [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md) is the [force](../../../../../force-split.md) exerted on it by [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf W=m\mathbf g.}
$$

For a uniform body, $m=\rho V$, where $\rho$ is its [mass density](../../../../../density.md); more generally $m=\int\rho\,dV$. [Volume](../../../../../volume.md) can change with [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) or [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) while the body's [mass](../../../../../mass.md) remains fixed in the classical closed-system model. Equal volumes of different materials need not have equal [masses](../../../../../mass.md).

**The same [mass](../../../../../mass.md) need not have the same [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md).** The local [gravitational acceleration](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) changes between planets and with position on Earth. Outside a spherical Earth, $g(r)=GM_\oplus/r^2$, so increasing altitude reduces it. Earth's rotation and shape also affect terrestrial weighing: a scale at rest on the rotating Earth responds to the effective acceleration including the centrifugal contribution, rather than simply to a spherical gravitational field.

Distinguish [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md) from [apparent weight](../../../../../apparent-weight.md). A supporting scale measures a contact [force](../../../../../force-split.md). In a vertically accelerating cabin, upward [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) $b$ gives $N=m(g+b)$ while contact is maintained. During common [free fall](../../../../../free-fall.md), $N=0$ although $mg$ remains nonzero. Immersion in a [fluid](../../../../../fluid.md) can similarly reduce the support needed through [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md), without removing the gravitational attraction. These distinctions explain why a change in a balance reading is not automatically a change of [mass](../../../../../mass.md).
