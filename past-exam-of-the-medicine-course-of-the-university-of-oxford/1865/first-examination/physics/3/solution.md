<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Take a body floating at rest in a homogeneous [fluid](../../../../../fluid.md), with uniform [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md), no tether or other support, and negligible air [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md). Imagine replacing the submerged part by the same volume of the surrounding [fluid](../../../../../fluid.md). That imaginary fluid parcel would be at rest: the surrounding [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) would balance its [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md). The actual body experiences the same pressure traction over the submerged boundary, so the resulting [buoyant force](../../../../../buoyancy.md) is upward with magnitude

$$
B=\rho_f gV_{\rm displaced}.
$$

The body's vertical [force](../../../../../force-split.md) balance therefore gives [Archimedes' principle](../../../../../archimedes-principle.md) in the requested floating case:

$$
\boxed{mg=\rho_f gV_{\rm displaced}.}
$$

The argument determines displaced submerged volume, not necessarily the total [volume](../../../../../volume.md) of a partly emerged body.

The pressure resultant acts through the [centre of buoyancy](../../../../../centre-of-buoyancy.md), the [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) of the imaginary displaced fluid. Let $G$ be the body's [centre of gravity](../../../../../centre-of-gravity.md) and $B_c$ the [centre of buoyancy](../../../../../centre-of-buoyancy.md). The two remaining external [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) are equal and opposite vertical forces. If their lines of action were distinct, they would form a nonzero [couple](../../../../../couple-mechanics.md) and the body would turn. Rotational equilibrium consequently requires

$$
\boxed{G\text{ and }B_c\text{ lie on the same vertical line}.}
$$

This is a condition for equilibrium, not sufficient evidence of its stability. Tilting changes the displaced shape and hence the position of the [centre of buoyancy](../../../../../centre-of-buoyancy.md); the resulting [torque](../../../../../torque.md) determines whether the body returns towards its original orientation. A [centre of gravity](../../../../../centre-of-gravity.md) above the [centre of buoyancy](../../../../../centre-of-buoyancy.md) does not by itself settle that floating-body stability question.
