For a primary [rainbow](../../../../../rainbow.md), sunlight is refracted into a water drop, internally reflected once, and refracted out. The [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) varies with [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md), producing [optical dispersion](../../../../../dispersion-optics.md). If $i$ is the incidence angle and $r$ the refracted angle, a spherical-drop ray has deviation

$$
D=\pi+2i-4r,\qquad \sin i=n\sin r.
$$

A stationary deviation concentrates neighbouring rays into a bright angular region. For visible light in water, the primary bow lies around $42^\circ$ from the antisolar direction, with red outside violet. Each observer receives the suitable colour from drops on the corresponding cone; the bow is not a fixed coloured object attached to one particular group of drops. Further internal reflections produce secondary bows with different angles and colour order.

A [soap film](../../../../../soap-film.md) uses [thin-film interference](../../../../../thin-film-interference.md). Rays reflected from its two surfaces acquire an optical path difference $2nt\cos r$. With air on both sides of a nonabsorbing film, one of the surface reflections has a phase reversal. A constructive reflected condition is consequently

$$
\boxed{2nt\cos r=\left(j+\frac12\right)\lambda.}
$$

Different [thicknesses](../../../../../thickness.md), angles and wavelengths therefore give different reflected colours. A sufficiently thin symmetric film becomes dark because its leading reflected contributions cancel. The colour is chiefly an interference effect, rather than a pigment carried by the soap.

[Mother-of-pearl](../../../../../nacre.md), or [nacre](../../../../../nacre.md), has a structured arrangement of mineral tablets and organic layers. Repeated refractive-index contrasts produce wavelength-selective reflection; surface grooves can also act as [diffraction gratings](../../../../../diffraction-grating.md). Thus both multilayer interference and surface diffraction can contribute. A representative repeating-layer condition is $2(n_1d_1\cos r_1+n_2d_2\cos r_2)=j\lambda$. Modern structural-colour measurements and modelling distinguish these two contributions instead of treating every nacre appearance as a single grating effect.[2021, Introduction and equation 1: aragonite–conchiolin layers versus surface grooves](../../../../../investigation-of-nacre-nanostructure-by-analyzing-its-structural-color-pattern-by-nicole-fan-and-others.md) That microscopic account uses later structural evidence, not techniques available to an 1865 candidate.
