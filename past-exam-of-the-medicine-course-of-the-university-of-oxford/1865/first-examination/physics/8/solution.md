<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

A [plane mirror](../../../../../plane-mirror.md) sends each incident ray into its specularly reflected direction, with equal incidence and reflection [angles](../../../../../angle.md) measured from the surface normal. For a point object in front of the mirror, the backward extensions of the reflected rays meet at the reflected point behind the plane. The image is therefore **virtual, upright, equal in size and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front**. The actual rays do not travel behind the mirror. In coordinates with mirror plane $x=0$, the object point $(x,y,z)$ is mapped to $(-x,y,z)$.

A converging [convex lens](../../../../../convex-lens.md) refracts a paraxial pencil according to the [thin-lens equation](../../../../../thin-lens-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac1u+\frac1v=\frac1f,\qquad m=-\frac vu,}
$$

where $u>0$ is the distance to a real object, $f>0$ is the [focal length](../../../../../focal-length.md), and $v>0$ denotes an image on the opposite side. Two constructed rays locate the image: a ray parallel to the [optical axis](../../../../../optical-axis.md) emerges towards the far [optical focus](../../../../../focus-optics.md), and a ray through the ideal thin lens's optical centre proceeds undeviated. Their intersection is a real image; if they diverge, their backward extensions locate a virtual one.

For $u>2f$ the real inverted image is reduced; at $u=2f$ it is equal in size; for $f<u<2f$ it is enlarged. At $u=f$ the emergent pencil is parallel and the image is at infinity in the ideal model. For $0<u<f$, the image is virtual, upright and enlarged on the object's side. A distant object gives an image near the far focal plane. The [lensmaker's equation](../../../../../lensmaker-s-equation.md) explains why shape alone does not guarantee convergence: the surrounding medium and [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) also matter. [Spherical aberration](../../../../../spherical-aberration.md), [chromatic aberration](../../../../../chromatic-aberration.md) and [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) limit the ideal point-image construction. The statement that the centre ray is undeviated is the thin-lens approximation, rather than a general rule for a thick compound optical instrument.
