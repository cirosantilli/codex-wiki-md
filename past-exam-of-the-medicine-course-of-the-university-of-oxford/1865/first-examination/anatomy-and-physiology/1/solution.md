<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

**Arterial recoil, the [muscle pump](../../../../../muscle-pump.md), respiratory movements, venous tone and the mechanics of cardiac filling assist [blood circulation](../../../../../blood-circulation.md).** The [heart](../../../../../heart.md) remains its principal continuing source of mechanical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). These agencies differ in whether they store cardiac [work](../../../../../work.md), contribute new muscular [work](../../../../../work.md), or direct flow without supplying power.

During ventricular [systole](../../../../../systole.md), the [aorta](../../../../../aorta.md) and other large elastic [arteries](../../../../../artery.md) expand and store [elastic potential energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md). During [diastole](../../../../../diastole.md), their recoil continues to drive [blood](../../../../../blood.md) towards the peripheral vessels, while closure of the [aortic valve](../../../../../aortic-valve.md) limits return into the [heart](../../../../../heart.md). This elastic reservoir smooths an intermittent cardiac input into a more continuous downstream flow. It does not manufacture extra [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md): it releases part of the [work](../../../../../work.md) performed during the preceding ejection.

Contraction of surrounding [skeletal muscles](../../../../../skeletal-muscle.md) compresses [veins](../../../../../vein.md). [Venous valves](../../../../../venous-valve.md) favour displacement towards the [heart](../../../../../heart.md) and limit backward flow when the compression is released. Alternating contraction and relaxation therefore produces a [muscle pump](../../../../../muscle-pump.md), especially useful for return from dependent limbs. The added [work](../../../../../work.md) comes from the contracting [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md); valves guide the displacement but supply no power themselves. Persistent compression without refilling is not equivalent to a series of pumping strokes.

Spontaneous inspiration supplies another assistance. Descent of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md) raises abdominal [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) while expansion of the [thorax](../../../../../thorax.md) lowers intrathoracic [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). The resulting changes favour a [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) towards the thoracic [veins](../../../../../vein.md) and right side of the [heart](../../../../../heart.md). The effect depends on posture, the respiratory phase and whether vessels remain open. Positive-pressure inflation does not reproduce the same pressure changes as spontaneous inspiration.[Venous System; Skeletal Muscle Pump; Respiratory Pump](../../../../../anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-2-blood-flow-blood-pressure-and-resistance.md)

Venous [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md) can reduce the storage capacity of peripheral [veins](../../../../../vein.md), transferring [blood](../../../../../blood.md) towards the central circulation. This changes the volume available for cardiac filling; it does not require a travelling wave of venous peristalsis. Relaxation and recoil of the [heart](../../../../../heart.md), together with movement of the atrioventricular plane, also influence filling pressures. The older expression cardiac suction is therefore best interpreted through specific changes of chamber volume and [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), rather than a separate force pulling uniformly through every vessel.

[Gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) assists a downward column and opposes an upward one. For a complete circuit returning to its initial height it supplies no net gravitational [work](../../../../../work.md). Its important effects include hydrostatic pressure differences and dependent pooling. Likewise, the residual [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) across the [capillaries](../../../../../capillary.md) is chiefly the downstream consequence of the cardiac pump. Ordinary [capillary action](../../../../../capillary-action.md) at a liquid meniscus is not a universal extra pump inside a closed, fluid-filled vascular network. Local [vasodilation](../../../../../vasodilation.md) can improve [perfusion](../../../../../perfusion.md) by reducing [vascular resistance](../../../../../vascular-resistance.md), but it does not create the necessary pressure source.

With $Q$ the mean [volumetric flow rate](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md), $R$ an effective [vascular resistance](../../../../../vascular-resistance.md), and $\Delta P$ the driving pressure difference, the useful bookkeeping relation is

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{\Delta P}{R}}.
$$

It separates an increase of driving [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) from a reduction of resistance. An instructive apparatus compares the outflow of a pulsatile pump through rigid tubing with its outflow through an elastic reservoir feeding the same resistance. Continued outflow between strokes demonstrates stored [elastic potential energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md). A separately compressed valved flexible tube demonstrates the additional [muscle pump](../../../../../muscle-pump.md) principle. Neither observation requires a mysterious attraction of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) by the [tissues](../../../../../tissue-biology.md).
