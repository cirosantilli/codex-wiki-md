## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the general appearance and the microscopical structure of a lung affected by pneumonia, discriminating between the several stages of the disease.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The classical sequence in [lobar pneumonia](../../../biology.md#lobar-pneumonia) is [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion), red [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), grey [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), and resolution.** These are overlapping morphological phases, not a clock that every [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia) follows. An affected [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) may contain adjacent areas in different phases; [bronchopneumonia](../../../biology.md#bronchopneumonia) has scattered airway-centred foci rather than uniform involvement of a whole lobe. Modern [antibiotic](../../../biology.md#antibiotic) treatment often interrupts the textbook sequence.[Histopathology: lobar pneumonia and its four stages](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pneumonia-pathology-in-statpearls)

During [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion), the [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) is heavy, dark red and boggy but still contains some [air](../../../mixture.md#air). Its cut surface gives a blood-stained watery fluid. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) shows distended [alveolar capillaries](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-capillary), [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema) within [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus), and an early cellular [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate), before dense [pulmonary consolidation](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-consolidation) has developed. Reduced [alveolar ventilation](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-ventilation) begins as fluid replaces the normal air space.

In red [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), the affected lobe is enlarged, firm, red and substantially airless. Its granular cut surface resembles [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver), which explains the name; it is not a transformation into [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) tissue. The [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus) are occupied by a mesh of [fibrin](../../../protein.md#fibrin) containing abundant [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) and [neutrophils](../../../biological-cell.md#neutrophil), while the [alveolar capillaries](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-capillary) remain congested. Filling of neighbouring air spaces produces continuous [pulmonary consolidation](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-consolidation). Associated [pleurisy](../../../biology.md#pleurisy) may put a [fibrinous exudate](../../../biology.md#fibrinous-exudate) on the overlying [pleura](../../../anatomy.md#pleura).

In grey [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), [pulmonary consolidation](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-consolidation) remains, but the colour becomes grey, grey-brown or yellowish as intact [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) disappear and the [fibrin](../../../protein.md#fibrin) and inflammatory [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) dominate. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) shows degenerating [neutrophils](../../../biological-cell.md#neutrophil), debris and persistent intra-alveolar [fibrin](../../../protein.md#fibrin); the conspicuous vascular engorgement of the preceding phase diminishes. Grey colour by itself does not establish a separate pathogen or mean that an [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess) has formed.[Histopathology: red and grey hepatization](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#typical-bacterial-pneumonia-in-statpearls)

In resolution, the intra-alveolar [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate) is broken down, ingested by [macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#macrophage), removed through airways and cleared by absorption. The lobe softens and becomes aerated again. Where the [alveolar septa](../../../anatomy.md#alveolar-septum) have survived, the architecture can return nearly to normal; an air-space [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate) need not become a permanent scar. Failure of clearance may instead lead to organisation and [fibrosis](../../../anatomy.md#fibrosis); destructive infection can produce [abscess](../../../biology.md#abscess) or [empyema](../../../biology.md#empyema). These are possible outcomes, not obligatory further stages.

A useful examination compares a cut section and a thin [histological section](../../../anatomy.md#histological-section) from each region, following the [alveolar septa](../../../anatomy.md#alveolar-septum) to distinguish filling of existing air spaces from destruction of the framework. A dense airless piece may sink in water, but this physical test only shows reduced [air](../../../mixture.md#air) content: [atelectasis](../../../biology.md#atelectasis) can also sink. The distribution and the [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) establish why the tissue is airless.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

State the pathology of Diabetes mellitus, and mention other forms of disease with which it is apt to be associated.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**[Diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) is a disorder of [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) regulation with persistent [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia); the [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) is a consequence, not a sufficient explanation of its cause.** In 1865 the relation between hepatic [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen), circulating [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) and urinary sugar was actively disputed. A contemporary account could reasonably discuss failure to assimilate food and excessive delivery of sugar from the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver); it could not possess the later demonstration of [insulin](../../../protein.md#insulin) deficiency. An 1863 discussion of [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) documents competing interpretations of the hepatic experiments, rather than an agreed single lesion.[pp. 644–648, review of the 1862 Researches on the Nature and Treatment of Diabetes: competing explanations of hepatic sugar formation](../../../biology-bibliography.md#book-reviews-in-the-edinburgh-medical-journal-1863)

In the modern account, [type 1 diabetes](../../../biology.md#type-1-diabetes) results from destruction of pancreatic [beta cells](../../../physiology.md#beta-cell) and severe deficiency of [insulin](../../../protein.md#insulin). [Type 2 diabetes](../../../biology.md#type-2-diabetes) combines [insulin resistance](../../../biology.md#insulin-resistance) with inadequate compensatory [insulin secretion](../../../protein.md#insulin-secretion). Disease of the [pancreas](../../../physiology.md#pancreas) can also cause [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes). Reduced restraint of hepatic [glucose production](../../../metabolism.md#glucose-production) and impaired peripheral disposal of [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) contribute to [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia). These mechanisms distinguish types that were not separated in the question's terminology.[Type 1, type 2 and other types of diabetes](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#what-is-diabetes-by-niddk)

When the filtered [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) load exceeds renal reabsorptive capacity, [glycosuria](../../../biology.md#glycosuria) causes an [osmotic diuresis](../../../anatomy.md#osmotic-diuresis): loss of water and [electrolytes](../../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte) produces [polyuria](../../../physiology.md#polyuria), [dehydration](../../../chemical-reaction.md#dehydration) and [polydipsia](../../../physiology.md#polydipsia). Inadequate use of fuels and increased breakdown of stored [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) and [protein](../../../protein.md) explain wasting despite appetite. Severe [insulin](../../../protein.md#insulin) deficiency promotes [ketogenesis](../../../metabolism.md#ketogenesis) and can cause [diabetic ketoacidosis](../../../biology.md#diabetic-ketoacidosis); it is not correct to make this the inevitable state of everyone with [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes). Conversely, [renal glycosuria](../../../biology.md#renal-glycosuria) can occur without the systemic metabolic disorder, so a urinary sugar test alone does not settle the diagnosis.[Symptoms and signs; complications: glycosuria and osmotic diuresis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#type-1-diabetes-mellitus-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)[Diabetic ketoacidosis: insulin deficiency and ketogenesis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#acute-complications-of-diabetes-mellitus-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The associated diseases should be separated into predispositions, complications and coincident illnesses. Pulmonary wasting called “phthisis” was prominent in period accounts, but the historical label does not establish that every reported lung cavity was tuberculous.[pp. 648–649: discussion of pulmonary disease in diabetes](../../../biology-bibliography.md#book-reviews-in-the-edinburgh-medical-journal-1863) Modern [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) increases susceptibility to active [tuberculosis](../../../biology.md#tuberculosis); it does not convert sugar into tubercle. [Diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) also increases susceptibility to some other infections and can accompany recurrent skin infection, poor wound healing and [gangrene](../../../biology.md#gangrene).[Overview: progression from tuberculosis infection to active disease](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#tb-risk-and-people-with-diabetes-by-cdc) Long-standing [hyperglycaemia](../../../physiology.md#hyperglycaemia) is associated with [retinopathy](../../../neuroscience.md#retinopathy), [nephropathy](../../../anatomy.md#nephropathy) and [peripheral neuropathy](../../../neuroscience.md#peripheral-neuropathy); accelerated [atherosclerosis](../../../physiology.md#atherosclerosis) contributes to [coronary artery disease](../../../biology.md#coronary-artery-disease), [stroke](../../../neurology.md#stroke) and limb [ischemia](../../../biology.md#ischemia). [Obesity](../../../biology.md#obesity) is commonly associated with [type 2 diabetes](../../../biology.md#type-2-diabetes), although severe untreated disease may cause loss of body mass. None of these associations licenses a claim that every diabetic patient has the same anatomical lesions.[Health problems associated with diabetes: eye, kidney, nerve and cardiovascular disease](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#what-is-diabetes-by-niddk)

For an 1865 investigation, repeated urine testing could demonstrate reducing sugar and its variation with diet; simultaneous study of the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) and [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) was needed to investigate the source. The distinction between [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) already present during life and [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) formed in an unfixed [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) after death is crucial experimental design: post-mortem chemical change must not be mistaken for the living organ's output. Modern diagnosis measures [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar) and longer-term glycaemic exposure rather than inferring the entire disorder from the taste or appearance of [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine).[Diagnosis: plasma glucose, glycated haemoglobin and oral glucose tolerance testing](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#type-1-diabetes-mellitus-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the varieties of softening which are found in the cerebro-spinal axis, and what are the most usual causes of the disease in the several parts of that structure ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**“Softening” describes disintegration of nervous tissue; it does not identify one cause.** The 1865 colour categories were chiefly red, yellow and white softening. They must be distinguished from the causal classification into vascular injury, [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation), pressure-related injury, trauma and post-mortem change. Historical observers sometimes interpreted pale ventricular softening as a consequence of [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema); modern [pathology](pathology.md) requires evidence of tissue death before calling such a region [encephalomalacia](../../../biology.md#encephalomalacia).[Volume 22, p. 177: white, yellow and red brain softenings](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#pathological-anatomy-of-insanity-in-the-american-journal-of-insanity-1865)

Red softening contains conspicuous extravasated [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), sometimes with vascular engorgement. It may occur in a haemorrhagic [cerebral infarction](../../../neurology.md#cerebral-infarction), a contusion or destructive [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation). Yellow softening is a later discoloured, friable focus containing products of tissue and blood breakdown, often with lipid-laden [macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#macrophage). White or pale softening has little visible [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage); an ischaemic focus in the [white matter](../../../neuroscience.md#white-matter) can be pale, as can waterlogged tissue around a [cerebral ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#ventricular-system). These appearances overlap through time. Neither red colour proves primary [encephalitis](../../../biology.md#encephalitis) nor white colour proves absence of vascular disease.

In the [cerebral hemispheres](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-hemisphere), a major cause of genuine destructive softening is interruption of arterial [blood flow](../../../physiology.md#blood-flow): [thrombosis](../../../biology.md#thrombosis) on diseased arteries, an [embolus](../../../biology.md#embolus) from the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) or another artery, or inadequate perfusion during severe systemic circulatory failure. The territory supplied by the obstructed vessel helps explain a focal lesion; prolonged global failure particularly injures vulnerable neurons and boundary zones between arterial territories. Cortical lesions and deep lesions involving the [basal ganglia](../../../neuroscience.md#basal-ganglia) or internal pathways can therefore have different distributions. [Cerebral infarction](../../../neurology.md#cerebral-infarction) evolves through neuronal injury, removal of dead tissue by [microglia](../../../neuroscience.md#microglia) and [macrophages](../../../biological-cell.md#macrophage), and [liquefactive necrosis](../../../biology.md#liquefactive-necrosis); a chronic lesion may leave a fluid-filled cavity with surrounding [gliosis](../../../biology.md#gliosis).[Cerebral infarction: gross and microscopic progression from acute injury to liquefaction](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#central-nervous-system-pathology-by-the-national-university-of-singapore)

In the [cerebellum](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebellum) and [brainstem](../../../neuroscience.md#brainstem), interruption of the vertebrobasilar circulation is likewise an important vascular cause; local [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), trauma and infection are alternatives. Suppuration can cause destructive softening in any region, and a nearby [tumour](../../../biology.md#tumour) can injure tissue by compression, interference with its blood supply or associated [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema). These mechanisms must be investigated rather than inferred from consistency alone.

In the [spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-cord), destructive [myelomalacia](../../../biology.md#myelomalacia) can follow vascular interruption, severe compression, direct trauma or inflammatory [myelitis](../../../biology.md#myelitis). Vertebral disease or an epidural mass may compress both the [spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-cord) and its vessels. Infarction often involves the territory of the [anterior spinal artery](../../../physiology.md#anterior-spinal-artery); reduced perfusion can preferentially damage the vulnerable central [gray matter](../../../neuroscience.md#gray-matter). [Myelitis](../../../biology.md#myelitis) can also disrupt tissue without a primary arterial obstruction. It would be misleading to transfer the relative frequency of cerebral embolism unchanged to every spinal lesion.[Causes and anterior spinal artery territory](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#spinal-cord-infarction-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

At examination, record the location, vascular territory, firmness, colour and adjacent membranes; inspect the arteries and search for an embolic source or compression. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) asks whether there is [necrosis](../../../biology.md#necrosis), [inflammatory exudation](../../../biology.md#inflammatory-exudate), [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), myelin debris, cellular clearance or repair. Finally exclude simple post-mortem [autolysis](../../../paleontology.md#autolysis).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Mention the best known varieties of Pityriasis, and describe their microscopical characters.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The historical name [pityriasis](../../../biology.md#pityriasis) grouped finely scaling eruptions; its varieties were not all one [fungal infection](../../../biology.md#fungal-infection).** A useful period list is simple or scalp [pityriasis](../../../biology.md#pityriasis), [pityriasis rubra](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-rubra), [pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor) and [pityriasis nigra](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-nigra). The names describe distribution or colour, and classifications differed. In particular, “nigra” was sometimes a very dark form of “versicolor,” sometimes a pigmentation disorder with little true scaling. It cannot safely be translated into one present species of [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus).[Pityriasis: capitis, rubra, versicolor and nigra; limits of the nigra category](../../../biology-bibliography.md#the-practice-of-medicine-by-robley-dunglison-1844)

Simple [pityriasis](../../../biology.md#pityriasis), often called [pityriasis capitis](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-capitis) on the scalp, gives loose, whitish, bran-like scales with little or variable redness. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) of a scale principally shows flattened keratinised [epithelial cells](../../../biological-cell.md#epithelial-cell) and debris. An oily or inflamed scalp can also contain organisms normally living there; this does not make every case identical to [pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor). Present descriptions of [dandruff](../../../biology.md#dandruff) and [seborrhoeic dermatitis](../../../biology.md#seborrhoeic-dermatitis) explain many such appearances, but the historical label cannot retrospectively specify the cause of each case.[Clinical features and relationship to seborrhoeic dermatitis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#seborrhoeic-dermatitis-by-dermnet)

[Pityriasis rubra](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-rubra) denoted a conspicuously red, scaling eruption, sometimes extensive. Detached scales are again principally epidermal material. A modern [skin biopsy](../../../biology.md#skin-biopsy) may show an inflammatory pattern, but there is no single microscopical organism that defines the historical category. It must not automatically be renamed [pityriasis rubra pilaris](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-rubra-pilaris), a distinct follicular disorder. An extensive red and peeling [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) is a pattern requiring causal diagnosis, rather than proof of one infection.[Definition, causes and nonspecific biopsy findings](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#erythroderma-by-dermnet)

[Pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor) produces irregular pale, yellowish-brown or brown patches, commonly on the upper [anatomical trunk](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-trunk), with a fine surface scale. Here the [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) is especially discriminating: a superficial scraping, cleared with [potassium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-hydroxide), shows clusters of round yeast forms and short curved [hyphae](../../../organism.md#hypha) within the [stratum corneum](../../../physiology.md#stratum-corneum). Modern classification places the organisms in [Malassezia](../../../organism.md#malassezia). The older “Microsporon furfur” terminology should not be read as identification of a modern [Microsporum](../../../organism.md#microsporum) dermatophyte. On a [histological section](../../../anatomy.md#histological-section) the organisms occupy the horny surface layer and are made conspicuous by suitable [fungal stains](../../../anatomy.md#fungal-stain).[Histology and special stains: yeasts and short hyphae in the stratum corneum](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pityriasis-versicolor-pathology-by-dermnet)

[Pityriasis nigra](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-nigra) calls first for verification of what is actually being described. If it is dark [pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor), the same superficial yeast and [hyphae](../../../organism.md#hypha) may be found; if the lesion is pigmentation without a fungal eruption, this finding cannot be assumed. A contemporary discussion explicitly recorded the overlap of nigra and versicolor and disputes over the significance of the supposed parasite.[pp. 208–209 and 218–219: versicolor, nigra and conflicting fungal interpretations](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#on-the-phytopathology-of-the-skin-review-1864) Thus **epidermal scales are common to the description, but demonstrable fungal structures particularly distinguish [pityriasis versicolor](../../../biology.md#pityriasis-versicolor)**. Examine actual scrapings with a [optical microscope](../../../optical-instrument.md#optical-microscope), compare several fields and use appropriate controls before attributing a different scaling disorder to a [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus).

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Give a sketch of the natural history, and shortly describe the external appearance, of the entozoa found in the human abdominal viscera in this country.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**The principal intestinal [Entozoa](../../../ecology.md#entozoa) to describe are roundworms, threadworms, whipworms and tapeworms; the chief visceral larval form is the hydatid.** “In this country” refers to the British setting of the paper. It is not a claim that all human [helminths](../../../ecology.md#helminth) were common or acquired locally. A contemporary account already distinguished [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda), [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda) and [Trematoda](../../../organism.md#trematoda), and recognised that an adult worm and a larval cyst could be different phases of one life cycle.[1864; systematic treatments of human nematodes, cestodes and trematodes](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#entozoa-an-introduction-to-the-study-of-helminthology-by-thomas-spencer-cobbold) The life cycles below use present knowledge to explain transmission; their inclusion does not imply that every migratory stage or intermediate host was established in 1865.

[Ascaris lumbricoides](../../../organism.md#ascaris-lumbricoides) lives as a large, cylindrical, unsegmented, tapering roundworm in the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine); adults are cream-coloured or pinkish and roughly 15–35 cm long, with the male generally smaller and its tail curved. Eggs leave in [faeces](../../../physiology.md#faeces) and become infective after development in the environment. After ingestion, larvae cross the intestinal wall, pass through the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung), ascend the airway, are swallowed and mature in the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine). Adults can wander into the [bile ducts](../../../physiology.md#bile-duct). The pulmonary migration belongs to the larval history, not to the usual location of the adult.[Life cycle; adult morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-ascariasis)

[Enterobius vermicularis](../../../organism.md#enterobius-vermicularis), the threadworm, is a small white nematode of the caecal and adjacent colonic region. The female is about 8–13 mm long and has a conspicuous pointed tail; the male is shorter. The gravid female migrates to the perianal skin and deposits eggs, often at night, so itching and transfer by contaminated fingers favour renewed infection. Eggs become infective rapidly; a pulmonary migration is not part of its usual life cycle. This explains why a perianal adhesive sample can be more useful than an ordinary stool examination.[Life cycle; adult worms; diagnostic findings](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-enterobiasis)

[Trichuris trichiura](../../../organism.md#trichuris-trichiura), historically [Trichocephalus dispar](../../../organism.md#trichuris-trichiura), inhabits the [caecum](../../../physiology.md#caecum) and [colon](../../../physiology.md#colon). The adult is about 3–5 cm long, with a very slender anterior portion embedded in the [mucous membrane](../../../anatomy.md#mucous-membrane) and a thicker posterior portion projecting into the lumen: the outline resembles a whip. Eggs pass in [faeces](../../../physiology.md#faeces), mature in soil and infect after ingestion. The adult shape and the characteristic eggs with polar plugs distinguish it from the preceding worms.[Life cycle and morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-trichuriasis)

Adult [Taenia](../../../organism.md#taenia) are flat, ribbon-like [tapeworms](../../../organism.md#cestoda), several metres long, with a small attachment head or [scolex](../../../organism.md#scolex), a narrow neck and a chain of [proglottids](../../../organism.md#proglottid). [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium) has four suckers and a hooked rostellum; [Taenia saginata](../../../organism.md#taenia-saginata), also called [Taenia mediocanellata](../../../organism.md#taenia-saginata) in period literature, has four suckers but lacks that crown of hooks. Their larval [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) occur in pig and cattle tissues respectively; ingestion of inadequately cooked infected meat permits an adult to develop in the human [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine). Eggs and segments pass in [faeces](../../../physiology.md#faeces). Egg appearance alone does not reliably distinguish these two species.[Life cycles; scolices, proglottids and eggs](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-taeniasis)

For [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium), swallowing eggs is a different event from swallowing pork [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus): eggs can establish tissue [cysticercosis](../../../biology.md#cysticercosis) in humans, including abdominal tissues, whereas pork larvae cause intestinal [taeniasis](../../../biology.md#taeniasis). A [cysticercus](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) is a small fluid-filled bladder with an invaginated [scolex](../../../organism.md#scolex), not a miniature chain of segments.[Life cycle and tissue cyst morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-cysticercosis)

The hydatid of [Echinococcus granulosus](../../../organism.md#echinococcus-granulosus) is particularly important in the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver). Humans acquire eggs from contamination associated with infected dogs and act as intermediate hosts. Larvae reach viscera and form a fluid-filled cyst with a laminated wall and an inner germinal layer; brood capsules, protoscolices and sometimes daughter cysts develop inside. The tiny adult tapeworm belongs in the dog's [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine), not as the normal human liver worm. Dogs complete the cycle by eating infected viscera from an intermediate host.[Cystic echinococcosis life cycle and hydatid morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-echinococcosis)

Less usual examples deserve proportionate mention. [Diphyllobothrium latum](../../../organism.md#dibothriocephalus-latus) is a broad, segmented fish tapeworm with two longitudinal attachment grooves rather than four suckers; aquatic intermediate hosts and infected fish link it to humans. It should not be presented as a uniformly common British infection.[Life cycle; adult morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-diphyllobothriasis) [Fasciola hepatica](../../../organism.md#fasciola-hepatica), a leaf-shaped fluke roughly 2–3 cm long with oral and ventral suckers, can inhabit the [bile ducts](../../../physiology.md#bile-duct); its cycle requires a freshwater snail and infective stages on aquatic vegetation. Human disease was an unusual counterpart of an important infection of grazing animals.[Life cycle and adult morphology](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-fascioliasis) [Trichinella spiralis](../../../organism.md#trichinella-spiralis) has minute intestinal adults after ingestion of infected meat, but its enduring encysted larvae occupy skeletal [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle); those muscle cysts should not be misdescribed as the typical abdominal hydatid.[Life cycle: intestinal adults and muscle larvae](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-trichinellosis)

Gross form and location guide identification, but [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) of eggs, larvae or segments and knowledge of the life cycle supply the confirmation.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

State the chemical composition of biliary calculi.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**[Gallstones](../../../biology.md#gallstone) contain chiefly [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) and/or [bile pigment](../../../physiology.md#bile-pigment), with variable [calcium salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-salt) and an organic matrix.** They are mixtures with different predominant components, not one compound with a universal formula. The period word [cholesterin](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) denotes [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol).

Cholesterol-rich stones contain crystalline [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol), often with a smaller pigmented nucleus and layers of other material. Mixed stones combine [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol), pigment, [calcium salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-salt) and an organic matrix including [mucin](../../../biochemistry.md#mucin). Pigment stones contain much less [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol); a major constituent is [calcium bilirubinate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-bilirubinate), related to the [bile pigment](../../../physiology.md#bile-pigment) [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin). Minor inorganic constituents can include [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) and [calcium phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-phosphate). Not every visibly dark stone has the same composition, and no fixed percentage follows from the question.[Cholelithiasis: cholesterol, black pigment and brown pigment stone composition](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gallstone-composition-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The useful modern subdivision of pigment stones is into black and brown types. Black stones commonly contain polymerised pigment with [calcium salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-salt); brown stones include [calcium bilirubinate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-bilirubinate) and calcium salts of fatty acids, particularly in infected stagnant duct bile. These refinements explain why neither “all stones are [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol)” nor “all stones are lime salts” is adequate.[Definition and facts; symptoms and causes: cholesterol, bilirubin and bile composition](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#gallstones-by-niddk)

Chemical examination should distinguish the fractions rather than infer them merely from the external colour. [Cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) can be extracted with a suitable organic solvent and recognised in recrystallised material; microscopy can show characteristic plate-like crystals. A mineral residue after removal or destruction of organic matter indicates an inorganic fraction, but residue alone cannot identify a particular salt. Layering, facets and colour describe growth and packing and are not chemical analyses. In particular, [calcium bilirubinate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-bilirubinate) is not interchangeable with [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate), and [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) is not a [bile salt](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt).

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the causes of the condition described as Angina Pectoris ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**[Angina pectoris](../../../biology.md#angina-pectoris) is most usefully explained by transient inadequacy of the myocardial [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) supply relative to demand.** It is a clinical syndrome, rather than the name of one visible anatomical lesion. The characteristic constricting chest discomfort may radiate to the arm, neck or jaw and be provoked by effort; the question concerns its causes, not a claim that every chest pain is [angina pectoris](../../../biology.md#angina-pectoris).[What causes angina; coronary disease, microvascular disease and spasm](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#angina-causes-and-risk-factors-by-nhlbi)

The common structural basis is [coronary atherosclerosis](../../../physiology.md#coronary-atherosclerosis). Narrowing limits the increase of [coronary blood flow](../../../physiology.md#coronary-blood-flow) that should accompany exercise. An affected [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) may receive sufficient [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) at rest and insufficient [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) when its rate, force of contraction and wall stress rise. Plaque disruption with [thrombosis](../../../biology.md#thrombosis) can abruptly worsen supply; persistent severe [ischemia](../../../biology.md#ischemia) can then cause [myocardial infarction](../../../biology.md#myocardial-infarction). **Pain from transient [ischemia](../../../biology.md#ischemia) does not by itself prove myocardial [necrosis](../../../biology.md#necrosis).**

Other mechanisms include [coronary artery spasm](../../../biology.md#coronary-artery-spasm), impaired regulation of small coronary vessels, or increased demand in [left ventricular hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#left-ventricular-hypertrophy), severe [aortic stenosis](../../../biology.md#aortic-stenosis), or [tachycardia](../../../biology.md#tachycardia). Reduced delivery through severe [anaemia](../../../biology.md#anaemia), low arterial [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) or low perfusion pressure may provoke symptoms even without a new obstructing plaque. Structural narrowing, vasomotor change, blood oxygen-carrying capacity and cardiac work therefore have to be considered together.[Recommendations 1.3.7 and factors intensifying ischaemia: anaemia, spasm, tachycardia and ventricular hypertrophy](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#recent-onset-chest-pain-of-suspected-cardiac-origin-by-nice)

For an 1865 interpretation, coronary “ossification” or degeneration, diseased cardiac or aortic structures and neural or spasmodic explanations belonged to the historical discussion. These did not establish a single universal cause. A report published in January 1865 described a pathological case associated [angina pectoris](../../../biology.md#angina-pectoris) with diseased coronary arteries, but isolated cases cannot demonstrate that a particular valve or vessel lesion is necessary in every patient.[pp. 255–256, section XII: coronary openings narrowed and disease around the cardiac plexus](../../../biology-bibliography.md#half-yearly-report-on-pathology-and-principles-and-practice-of-medicine-1865) The modern supply-and-demand account explains why severe symptoms can coexist with relatively inconspicuous large coronary arteries, and why substantial coronary disease need not cause pain at every moment.

A pathological investigation inspects the coronary origins and course, their lumen and wall, the [myocardium](../../../developmental-biology.md#myocardium), the [aortic valve](../../../physiology.md#aortic-valve) and ventricular thickness. Clinical investigation adds evidence of reversible [myocardial ischaemia](../../../biology.md#myocardial-ischaemia) and distinguishes it from infarction or non-cardiac pain. Modern [electrocardiography](../../../biology.md#electrocardiography) and appropriate imaging address functions that a nineteenth-century post-mortem inspection alone could not measure. A normal-looking vessel on gross inspection cannot exclude [coronary microvascular disease](../../../biology.md#coronary-microvascular-disease) or a transient [coronary artery spasm](../../../biology.md#coronary-artery-spasm).

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Give the differential diagnosis between Syphilis in the infant, Varicella, Varioloid, and Variola, as usually described.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**Infantile [syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis), [varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox), modified [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox) and ordinary [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox) are distinguished by the whole illness: age and history, prodrome, distribution, lesion depth, successive crops and the speed of evolution.** No isolated “spot” supplies an infallible diagnosis. [Varioloid](../../../biology.md#varioloid) is the historical name for modified [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox), particularly after [vaccination](../../../biology.md#vaccination); it is not a synonym for [varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox).

In an infant, the relevant [syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis) is chiefly [congenital syphilis](../../../biology.md#congenital-syphilis), transmitted during pregnancy. It can be unapparent at birth and declare itself later with persistent nasal discharge or “snuffles,” poor growth, fissuring near the mouth, a copper-coloured maculopapular or peeling eruption, and lesions on palms and soles. Bullae or pustular appearances can occur. Associated [hepatosplenomegaly](../../../biology.md#hepatosplenomegaly), [jaundice](../../../biology.md#jaundice) and painful bone disease support a systemic process rather than a simple crop of transient vesicles. The eruption is not required to pass synchronously through the stereotyped smallpox sequence. Maternal infection and appropriate [serology](../../../biology.md#serology) matter; absence of a rash at birth does not exclude infection.[Clinical description in children younger than two years: rhinitis, rash, bone and visceral findings](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#congenital-syphilis-2026-case-definition-by-cdc)[Signs and symptoms; infants without symptoms at birth](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#about-congenital-syphilis-by-cdc)

[Varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox), or [chickenpox](../../../biology.md#chickenpox), typically produces delicate superficial itchy vesicles on a red base, especially on the [anatomical trunk](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-trunk), with successive crops. Consequently fresh papules, vesicles and crusts coexist in one area. In children the preceding illness is often mild or absent. Individual lesions evolve quickly, and ordinary superficial lesions usually heal without the deep pitting associated with destructive smallpox lesions, although secondary infection can scar. The distribution is generally centripetal rather than most dense on the distal limbs; palms and soles are much less commonly affected.[Differential diagnosis and diagnostic criteria](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#diagnosis-and-evaluation-of-smallpox-by-cdc)

Ordinary [variola](../../../biology.md#smallpox), or [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox), classically begins with a marked febrile prodrome, often including headache, backache and prostration, before the rash. Lesions favour the face and extremities, can affect palms and soles, and proceed through firm papules to deep-seated vesicles and pustules, often with central depression. Within a given region they are relatively alike in developmental stage. The lesions evolve over days rather than the rapid successive crops of [varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox); crusting and separation may leave depressed scars. Severe confluent, flat or haemorrhagic forms depart from the uncomplicated textbook pattern, so the absence of perfect pustules is not a universal exclusion.[Major and minor criteria; ordinary smallpox rash and comparison with chickenpox](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#evaluating-patients-for-smallpox-by-cdc)[Flat-type and haemorrhagic smallpox](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-signs-and-symptoms-of-smallpox-by-cdc)

[Varioloid](../../../biology.md#varioloid) retains the cause of [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox) but has a modified course. Prior [vaccination](../../../biology.md#vaccination) can yield fewer and more superficial lesions, faster evolution and less secondary fever. The prodrome can still be substantial. Sparse lesions and shortened evolution can resemble [chickenpox](../../../biology.md#chickenpox), so vaccination history and the entire time course help, but neither a vaccination scar nor mild disease proves the diagnosis. [Varioloid](../../../biology.md#varioloid) and [variola](../../../biology.md#smallpox) belong on a spectrum of the same infection, whereas [varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox) is caused by a different [virus](../../../biology.md#virus).[Modified-type smallpox: prodrome, fewer superficial lesions and more rapid crusting](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-signs-and-symptoms-of-smallpox-by-cdc)

Thus the characteristic contrast is persistent multisystem infantile infection for [congenital syphilis](../../../biology.md#congenital-syphilis), superficial lesions in mixed-stage crops for [varicella](../../../biology.md#chickenpox), deeper regionally synchronous lesions with a severe preceding fever for ordinary [smallpox](../../../biology.md#smallpox), and attenuation of the latter pattern for [varioloid](../../../biology.md#varioloid). Current confirmation uses tests appropriate to the suspected organism; the historical clinical distinctions remain valuable descriptions, but they do not replace modern laboratory confirmation.
