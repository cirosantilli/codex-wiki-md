↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

**External [water](../../../../../water.md) treatment can clean, transfer [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md), provide moisture and apply mechanical pressure; the useful method depends on the object, not on a general claim that water expels disease.** Historical classifications distinguished general and local bathing, cold and warm applications, affusion, showers, compresses, packing and vapour baths.

Cold applications included sponging, pouring water over the body, immersion, a local wet compress and a cold shower or douche. A renewed wet cloth removes [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) by contact and [evaporation](../../../../../evaporation.md); a wrapping allowed to warm and then covered is a different intervention. In 1865 these methods were used for febrile heat, local pain or swelling, and sometimes to obtain a supposed stimulating reaction in chronic debility. A cold douche also adds mechanical impact and an abrupt circulatory stimulus. Historical wet-sheet packing enclosed the body in a wet sheet with outer coverings and was either renewed to sustain cooling or left to warm. The contemporary description distinguishes these uses; the claims about stimulation and cure are historical claims.[wet-sheet packing, renewed cooling compresses and warming compresses](../../../../../hydrotherapy-in-the-edinburgh-medical-journal-1865-review.md)

Tepid and warm immersion, a warm local bath or sitz bath, and warm moist fomentations were used to soothe painful or spasmodic conditions, support cleansing and soften crusts or secretions. Local applications avoid heating the whole body. The apparatus might be a bath, basin, irrigating vessel or repeatedly renewed cloth; a warm moist dressing must not become a source of sustained scalding. Historically, hot immersion and vapour baths were also used to induce sweating in rheumatic complaints and supposed retention of harmful material. Heat-induced sweating is real, but removal of a disease-specific poison by that route is a separate claim requiring evidence. Showering and douching similarly cannot be treated as dose-equivalent to quiet immersion.[water and mineral waters, external thermal-bath uses](../../../../../the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865.md)

Current examples show why the distinction matters. [Heatstroke](../../../../../heatstroke.md) requires rapid cooling and emergency care: cold-water immersion or other active cooling is used to reduce dangerous body temperature, not to provoke a later “reaction”. In contrast, routine tepid sponging is not recommended for [fever](../../../../../fever.md) in children under five. Febrile [thermoregulation](../../../../../thermoregulation.md) can provoke shivering and distress with external cooling, so the treatment of ordinary [fever](../../../../../fever.md) should not be inferred from the management of [hyperthermia](../../../../../hyperthermia.md).[heatstroke, emergency response and rapid cooling](../../../../../heat-related-illnesses-by-cdc-and-niosh.md)[NG143, recommendations 1.6.2–1.6.6](../../../../../fever-in-under-5s-by-nice.md)

For a thermal [burn](../../../../../burn.md), current first aid uses **cool running [water](../../../../../water.md) for 20 minutes**, with the rest of the person kept warm; ice or an ice-water application is not the appropriate substitute. Cleansing or irrigation has a further purpose independent of temperature: physically removing contamination, with the fluid and technique chosen for the tissue and injury. A steam application to the face is not a harmless version of warm bathing: spilled hot water and steam cause [scalds](../../../../../scald.md). The historical use of inhalation therefore does not validate leaning over boiling water as a general respiratory treatment. These examples specify an actual therapeutic objective without endorsing the broad claims of nineteenth-century hydropathy.[first aid and avoidance of inappropriate applications](../../../../../burns-and-scalds-by-nhs.md)[lack of strong evidence for treating colds and risk of scald injury](../../../../../steam-inhalation-warning-by-nhs-burns-specialists.md)
