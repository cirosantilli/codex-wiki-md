**[Muguet](../../../../../oral-candidiasis.md) denotes the adherent white fungal coating of oral thrush, now [oral candidiasis](../../../../../oral-candidiasis.md); it is not simply any white tongue or any mouth ulcer.** Patches occur on the tongue and oral mucosa and may merge into broader plaques. The underlying surface can be red, sore and liable to bleed when a plaque is disturbed. An infant may feed poorly because the mouth hurts. Milk residue lacks the same persistent inflamed adherent lesion, while an [aphthous ulcer](../../../../../aphthous-ulcer.md) is a focal ulcer rather than a confluent fungal plaque. [Symptoms in adults and babies, white patches and feeding difficulty](../../../../../oral-thrush-by-the-nhs.md)

**Cause and nature.** The modern explanation is opportunistic overgrowth of [Candida](../../../../../candida.md), commonly [Candida albicans](../../../../../candida-albicans.md), on a susceptible mucosal surface. An immature or impaired [immune system](../../../../../immune-system.md), illness and altered local conditions favour it. [Antibiotics](../../../../../antibiotic.md), [corticosteroids](../../../../../corticosteroid.md), [diabetes](../../../../../diabetes.md) and other causes of reduced host defence are relevant modern risk factors. These were not all understood in 1865, and exposure to the organism alone does not explain every case. Local [oral candidiasis](../../../../../oral-candidiasis.md) also does not, by itself, establish invasive fungal disease. [Mouth and throat candidiasis: infancy, host conditions and medication-related risks](../../../../../risk-factors-for-candidiasis-by-cdc.md)

**Evidence available in the nineteenth century.** Fungal forms could already be observed with a [microscope](../../../../../microscope.md). A contemporary 1849 review reports [Charles West](../../../../../charles-west.md)'s suggestion that the organisms could be transferred by shared cups and spoons, while the reviewer preferred an explanation involving decomposition and acidity. The review is evidence of the disagreement, not authority for spontaneous generation of a [fungus](../../../../../fungus.md). It also illustrates why historical “aphthae”, ulceration and [Muguet](../../../../../oral-candidiasis.md) should not all be treated as identical modern diagnoses. [pp. 996–997, discussion of muguet, microscopic growths and competing interpretations](../../../../../review-of-west-s-lectures-on-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-1849.md)

**Diagnosis and treatment.** Clinical inspection is often sufficient; when uncertain or atypical, material from the lesion can be examined microscopically for fungal forms, with further laboratory testing when needed. Repeated or unusually severe disease calls for attention to underlying illness and host defence, rather than simply repeating local cleansing. [Candidiasis of the mouth or throat: examination and microscopy of a lesion sample](../../../../../testing-and-diagnosis-for-candidiasis-by-cdc.md)

The historically defensible measures include maintaining adequate feeding, gentle mouth care and clean feeding utensils. Harsh scraping and treating every white patch as the same disease are inappropriate. Present-day treatment adds an age-appropriate [antifungal drug](../../../../../antifungal-drug.md), such as a suitable topical [nystatin](../../../../../nystatin.md) preparation; more extensive or resistant disease may require systemic treatment such as [fluconazole](../../../../../fluconazole.md). Drug choice and formulation depend on the patient's age and clinical state. Those agents are later additions and must not be placed in an 1865 prescription. Hygiene and correction of predisposing factors accompany, rather than replace, effective antifungal treatment. [Mouth and throat disease: topical agents and systemic treatment for severe infection](../../../../../treatment-of-candidiasis-by-cdc.md)
