## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the sources of haemorrhage from the organs of generation in the female, and state the mode of treatment in each case.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**Classify the bleeding by anatomical source and by its relation to [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy), childbirth and [menstruation](../../../reproduction.md#menstruation); treatment must address that source.** The amount of visible genital [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage) does not measure all the loss: a ruptured [ectopic pregnancy](../../../biology.md#ectopic-pregnancy) or concealed [placental abruption](../../../biology.md#placental-abruption) can cause major internal [blood loss](../../../biology.md#blood-loss). Initial assessment of severe bleeding concerns the circulation, [anaemia](../../../biology.md#anaemia) and the need for resuscitation while the cause is established. Bleeding from the [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra) or [rectum](../../../physiology.md#rectum) must first be distinguished from genital bleeding.

**Local sources outside the uterine cavity.** The [vulva](../../../anatomy.md#vulva) and [vagina](../../../anatomy.md#vagina) may bleed from lacerations after childbirth or other trauma, an inflamed lesion, a foreign body, a vascular lesion or [cancer](../../../biology.md#cancer). Examination localizes the loss; treatment is local compression and repair of a significant laceration, removal of the foreign body, cause-directed treatment of [infection](../../../biology.md#infection), or specialist management of the lesion. A [cervical polyp](../../../anatomy.md#cervical-polyp), [cervical ectropion](../../../anatomy.md#cervical-ectropion), [cervicitis](../../../biology.md#cervicitis) or [cervical cancer](../../../biology.md#cervical-cancer) can cause bleeding from the [cervix](../../../anatomy.md#cervix), often noticed between periods or after intercourse. A suitable [polyp](../../../biology.md#polyp) can be removed and examined; inflammation requires its cause to be established, while a suspicious lesion requires diagnostic assessment rather than indiscriminate cauterization. After menopause, thin, fragile vulvovaginal tissues can also bleed; this does not remove the need to investigate a suspicious lesion. These are different conditions, not one disease called “uterine [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage)”. [Etiology, examination and cause-directed treatment of vulvar, vaginal and cervical bleeding](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#vaginal-bleeding-by-merck-manual)

**Bleeding from the [endometrium](../../../anatomy.md#endometrium).** Heavy or irregular [menstruation](../../../reproduction.md#menstruation) may accompany [ovulatory dysfunction](../../../biology.md#ovulatory-dysfunction), [uterine fibroids](../../../biology.md#uterine-fibroid), [endometrial polyps](../../../biology.md#endometrial-polyp), [adenomyosis](../../../biology.md#adenomyosis), a [coagulopathy](../../../biology.md#coagulopathy), medication effects or [endometrial cancer](../../../biology.md#endometrial-cancer). Modern assessment includes the menstrual and drug history, a full [blood count](../../../psychology.md#blood-count), and selected [ultrasound](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#ultrasound), [hysteroscopy](../../../biology.md#hysteroscopy) or tissue sampling according to the suspected cause. Where appropriate, menstrual bleeding can be reduced with [tranexamic acid](../../../pharmacology.md#tranexamic-acid) or hormonal treatment; an intracavitary [polyp](../../../biology.md#polyp) or [uterine fibroid](../../../biology.md#uterine-fibroid) may instead need targeted removal. Persistent postmenopausal bleeding must not simply be treated as an ordinary period. [NG88 §§1.2–1.5: assessment, blood count, selected investigation and medical or surgical treatment](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#heavy-menstrual-bleeding-by-nice)

**Early [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy).** A [miscarriage](../../../biology.md#miscarriage) may bleed while [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy) tissue separates or remains in the [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus). In a stable, confirmed case, expectant, medical or surgical management may be appropriate; heavy continuing loss or [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) changes the urgency. [Ectopic pregnancy](../../../biology.md#ectopic-pregnancy) requires a separate plan: selected stable cases may be monitored or treated medically, but rupture with haemorrhagic collapse requires urgent operative control, not treatment of the [vagina](../../../anatomy.md#vagina) as though it were the principal source. A [hydatidiform mole](../../../biology.md#hydatidiform-mole) is another [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy)-related source of abnormal bleeding; current management includes removal by suction evacuation and subsequent [human chorionic gonadotropin](../../../signal-transduction.md#human-chorionic-gonadotropin) surveillance for persistent disease. [Hydatidiform moles: bleeding, evacuation and biochemical follow-up](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#gestational-trophoblastic-disease-treatment-by-nci) [Early pregnancy loss, ectopic pregnancy and the distinction between vaginal and internal bleeding](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bleeding-during-pregnancy-by-acog) [NG126, management of tubal ectopic pregnancy and diagnostic precautions](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#ectopic-pregnancy-and-miscarriage-by-nice)

**Later [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy) and labour.** [Placenta praevia](../../../biology.md#placenta-praevia) commonly produces relatively painless bleeding, whereas [placental abruption](../../../biology.md#placental-abruption) more often causes pain and uterine tenderness; these are useful patterns, not infallible rules. Management depends on the mother's circulation, the [fetus](../../../biology.md#fetus), the placental lesion and the stage of [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy), with urgent delivery when required. A blind digital cervical examination is inappropriate before a possible [placenta praevia](../../../biology.md#placenta-praevia) has been excluded. Ruptured [vasa praevia](../../../biology.md#vasa-praevia) causes fetal rather than maternal bleeding and demands a different interpretation. A small [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood)-stained mucous “show” during cervical change is not equivalent to these pathological losses. [Green-top 63, history, examination and management; exclusion of placenta praevia before digital examination](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#antepartum-haemorrhage-by-the-royal-college-of-obstetricians-and-gynaecologists) [Vasa praevia and fetal bleeding](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#placenta-praevia-placenta-accreta-and-vasa-praevia-by-the-royal-college-of-obstetricians-and-gynaecologists)

**After delivery.** The principal categories are failure of [uterine contraction](../../../anatomy.md#uterine-contraction), retained [placental tissue](../../../biology.md#placental-tissue), trauma and disordered [coagulation](../../../biology.md#coagulation). For [uterine atony](../../../anatomy.md#uterine-atony), the aim is to restore contraction with uterine massage and an appropriate [uterotonic](../../../pharmacology.md#uterotonic). Retained tissue, a genital laceration or [uterine inversion](../../../anatomy.md#uterine-inversion) requires correction of that particular cause; a [coagulopathy](../../../biology.md#coagulopathy) requires haemostatic support. With [placenta accreta spectrum](../../../biology.md#placenta-accreta-spectrum), attempted forcible separation can provoke severe [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage). Current care combines resuscitation with cause-directed measures, and can require [tranexamic acid](../../../pharmacology.md#tranexamic-acid), balloon tamponade, embolisation or [surgery](../../../biology.md#surgery) when initial measures fail. Later postpartum bleeding also raises retained tissue and [endometritis](../../../anatomy.md#endometritis). [Causes and treatment, including retained placenta, uterine contraction, trauma and escalation](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#heavy-bleeding-after-birth-by-rcog)

**The 1865 distinction.** An answer of the period could distinguish menstrual loss, local lesions, [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy)-related [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage) and post-delivery failure of contraction through the history and physical examination. Compression, local plugging, removal of an accessible lesion and obstetric intervention were among the available approaches; their use was debated. The 1864 discussion of [placenta praevia](../../../biology.md#placenta-praevia) documents such disagreement. Modern imaging, [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) banking, the present drug regimens and current operative pathways must not be attributed to that practice. Nor should historical astringents or depletion be substituted for identifying the source. [pp. 262–268, discussion of placenta praevia and competing methods of management](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#midwifery-in-the-edinburgh-medical-journal-september-1864)

**Original question.**

State the signs of Pregnancy.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**Separate evidence produced by the mother from evidence identifying the [fetus](../../../biology.md#fetus).** The classical presumptive, probable and positive grouping expresses increasing diagnostic confidence; it is not a numerical probability scale.

**Presumptive signs** include missed [menstruation](../../../reproduction.md#menstruation), nausea or [pregnancy-associated vomiting](../../../physiology.md#pregnancy-associated-vomiting), [breast](../../../anatomy.md#breast) enlargement and tenderness, changes in the [areola](../../../anatomy.md#areola), urinary frequency and the mother's sensation of [fetal movement](../../../biology.md#fetal-movement), traditionally called [quickening](../../../biology.md#quickening). Each can have another explanation. [Amenorrhoea](../../../biology.md#amenorrhoea) may result from illness or endocrine disturbance; abdominal enlargement can arise from a pelvic mass, [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites) or other causes. A report of movement is not equivalent to movement detected independently by the examiner.

**Probable physical signs** include a progressively enlarged [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus) with the appropriate change in contour, softening of the [cervix](../../../anatomy.md#cervix) and uterine tissues, increased vascularity and colour change of the genital mucosa, and intermittent [uterine contractions](../../../anatomy.md#uterine-contraction). [Ballottement](../../../biology.md#ballottement) is the rebound of a movable object within fluid when it is displaced: in the right clinical setting it supports a gravid [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus), but the physical phenomenon alone does not identify a living [fetus](../../../biology.md#fetus). A vascular sound heard over the [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus) follows the maternal circulation and is not the fetal heartbeat.

**Positive evidence available to the 1865 examiner** was a fetal heartbeat heard with a [stethoscope](../../../psychology.md#stethoscope), unmistakable fetal movements appreciated during examination, and identification of fetal parts. Repeated examination as [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy) advances strengthens their interpretation. The examiner should compare the perceived heartbeat with the maternal pulse rather than accept any abdominal sound as fetal. These findings generally become useful later than the earliest symptoms, so their absence early on does not exclude [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy). The mother’s [quickening](../../../biology.md#quickening) remains a symptom; independently established [fetal movement](../../../biology.md#fetal-movement) is a different kind of evidence.

**Modern additions.** [Human chorionic gonadotropin](../../../signal-transduction.md#human-chorionic-gonadotropin) testing can detect [pregnancy](../../../biology.md#pregnancy)-related tissue before the classical physical signs become conclusive. A positive test does not establish location or continuing viability: it can occur in [ectopic pregnancy](../../../biology.md#ectopic-pregnancy) and must be interpreted in context. [Ultrasound](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#ultrasound) can establish location and show an embryo or [fetus](../../../biology.md#fetus) with cardiac activity. Very early uncertain findings may require repeat assessment rather than a premature conclusion. These technologies are later developments, not signs that an 1865 candidate could have obtained. [Diagnosis and confirmation of pregnancy: hormonal testing, ultrasound, fetal heart activity and examiner-detected movement](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#evaluation-of-the-obstetric-patient-by-merck-manual) [NG126 §§1.5–1.8: pregnancy location, repeat scans and the limits of serum hCG](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#ectopic-pregnancy-and-miscarriage-by-nice)

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

With what diseases may Scarlet Fever and Croup be confounded, and how would you distinguish them from those diseases ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Compare the pattern and sequence of findings; neither a red [rash](../../../biology.md#rash) nor [stridor](../../../biology.md#stridor) is a diagnosis.** Historical disease names also need care: nineteenth-century “true” or membranous [croup](../../../biology.md#croup) can overlap with laryngeal [diphtheria](../../../biology.md#diphtheria), whereas common modern [croup](../../../biology.md#croup) usually means viral [laryngotracheitis](../../../biology.md#laryngotracheitis).

**[Scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever).** An acute throat illness with [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever), a fine rough or sandpaper-like [rash](../../../biology.md#rash), a characteristic red tongue and subsequent peeling suggests [scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever). Modern [scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever) is associated with toxin-producing group A [Streptococcus](../../../organism.md#streptococcus); symptoms alone do not uniquely establish the organism. The main comparisons are:

- **[Measles](../../../biology.md#measles):** prominent [cough](../../../biology.md#cough), [coryza](../../../biology.md#coryza) and [conjunctivitis](../../../biology.md#conjunctivitis), followed by a more blotchy eruption spreading from the head downwards, favour [measles](../../../biology.md#measles). Small characteristic lesions inside the mouth can help. Their absence does not, by itself, rule it out. [Clinical features: respiratory prodrome, oral lesions and spreading maculopapular rash](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-overview-of-measles-by-cdc)
- **[Rubella](../../../biology.md#rubella) and other viral exanthems:** an acute [rash](../../../biology.md#rash) may resemble the early eruption. Exposure history, associated findings and the course matter; [rash](../../../biology.md#rash) colour alone cannot distinguish them from [scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever).
- **A drug eruption or [urticaria](../../../biology.md#urticaria):** a relevant exposure and a different evolution or fleeting raised lesions favour these explanations. Coexisting throat symptoms still require assessment.
- **Ordinary [pharyngitis](../../../biology.md#pharyngitis) or [tonsillitis](../../../biology.md#tonsillitis):** throat redness, exudate and [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) without the characteristic general eruption are insufficient for [scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever). Conversely, [pharyngitis](../../../biology.md#pharyngitis) with a [rash](../../../biology.md#rash) has several possible causes. [Differential diagnosis and confirmation by rapid antigen test, nucleic acid amplification or throat culture](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-scarlet-fever-by-cdc)
- **[Diphtheria](../../../biology.md#diphtheria):** a firmly adherent membrane on the pharyngeal or laryngeal mucosa, sometimes with neck swelling and systemic illness, favours [diphtheria](../../../biology.md#diphtheria). The membrane can bleed when forcibly disturbed; removal is not an appropriate diagnostic experiment. It differs from the usual scarlet-fever exanthem and tongue changes. [Clinical features: adherent pseudomembrane, bleeding on attempted removal and airway obstruction](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#diphtheria-in-the-cdc-pink-book)

A modern throat test can confirm group A streptococcal [infection](../../../biology.md#infection); it was unavailable in 1865. The useful historical answer therefore rests on careful examination and the clinical sequence, while admitting uncertain or overlapping cases.

**[Croup](../../../biology.md#croup) and its mimics.** A harsh barking [cough](../../../biology.md#cough), hoarseness and [stridor](../../../biology.md#stridor) suggest narrowing of the upper [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway). In common viral [croup](../../../biology.md#croup), the child may have an antecedent respiratory illness without the marked toxicity of a severe bacterial process. In the historical question, however, distinguishing “[croup](../../../biology.md#croup)” from [diphtheria](../../../biology.md#diphtheria) cannot mean assuming they were mutually exclusive labels. A membrane extending from the throat into the [larynx](../../../anatomy.md#larynx) could form part of what was called membranous [croup](../../../biology.md#croup).

- **A laryngeal spasm:** brief episodic obstruction with intervals of recovery and without a sustained inflammatory illness suggests the historical category [laryngismus stridulus](../../../biology.md#laryngismus-stridulus), rather than progressive membranous disease.
- **An inhaled foreign body:** abrupt choking and [cough](../../../biology.md#cough) during eating or play, a very sudden onset, or asymmetric chest findings favour an obstruction by material rather than ordinary viral [croup](../../../biology.md#croup).
- **[Epiglottitis](../../../biology.md#epiglottitis):** a very unwell child with drooling, difficulty swallowing, a muffled voice and little barking [cough](../../../biology.md#cough) needs a different interpretation from ordinary [croup](../../../biology.md#croup).
- **[Bacterial tracheitis](../../../biology.md#bacterial-tracheitis):** marked systemic illness, progressive obstruction and thick secretions suggest a bacterial tracheal process.
- **A [retropharyngeal abscess](../../../biology.md#retropharyngeal-abscess):** neck pain or stiffness, swallowing difficulty, drooling and a different pharyngeal picture point towards a deep collection rather than uncomplicated [croup](../../../biology.md#croup). [Differential diagnoses and distinguishing clinical features](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#acute-upper-airway-obstruction-by-the-royal-children-s-hospital-melbourne)

The practical priority is the severity of obstruction, not winning a verbal distinction at the bedside. Current guidance minimizes distress and unnecessary throat examination, obtains skilled airway support for deterioration, and does not require routine radiography or swabs in typical [croup](../../../biology.md#croup). These modern precautions also explain why a child should not be forcibly examined merely to demonstrate a membrane. [Assessment, investigations and management](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#croup-by-the-royal-children-s-hospital-melbourne)

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

Describe Muguet—its causes, nature, and treatment.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**[Muguet](../../../biology.md#oral-candidiasis) denotes the adherent white fungal coating of oral thrush, now [oral candidiasis](../../../biology.md#oral-candidiasis); it is not simply any white tongue or any mouth ulcer.** Patches occur on the tongue and oral mucosa and may merge into broader plaques. The underlying surface can be red, sore and liable to bleed when a plaque is disturbed. An infant may feed poorly because the mouth hurts. Milk residue lacks the same persistent inflamed adherent lesion, while an [aphthous ulcer](../../../biology.md#aphthous-ulcer) is a focal ulcer rather than a confluent fungal plaque. [Symptoms in adults and babies, white patches and feeding difficulty](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#oral-thrush-by-the-nhs)

**Cause and nature.** The modern explanation is opportunistic overgrowth of [Candida](../../../organism.md#candida), commonly [Candida albicans](../../../organism.md#candida-albicans), on a susceptible mucosal surface. An immature or impaired [immune system](../../../biology.md#immune-system), illness and altered local conditions favour it. [Antibiotics](../../../biology.md#antibiotic), [corticosteroids](../../../pharmacology.md#corticosteroid), [diabetes](../../../biology.md#diabetes) and other causes of reduced host defence are relevant modern risk factors. These were not all understood in 1865, and exposure to the organism alone does not explain every case. Local [oral candidiasis](../../../biology.md#oral-candidiasis) also does not, by itself, establish invasive fungal disease. [Mouth and throat candidiasis: infancy, host conditions and medication-related risks](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#risk-factors-for-candidiasis-by-cdc)

**Evidence available in the nineteenth century.** Fungal forms could already be observed with a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope). A contemporary 1849 review reports [Charles West](../../../physician.md#charles-west)'s suggestion that the organisms could be transferred by shared cups and spoons, while the reviewer preferred an explanation involving decomposition and acidity. The review is evidence of the disagreement, not authority for spontaneous generation of a [fungus](../../../organism.md#fungus). It also illustrates why historical “aphthae”, ulceration and [Muguet](../../../biology.md#oral-candidiasis) should not all be treated as identical modern diagnoses. [pp. 996–997, discussion of muguet, microscopic growths and competing interpretations](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#review-of-west-s-lectures-on-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-1849)

**Diagnosis and treatment.** Clinical inspection is often sufficient; when uncertain or atypical, material from the lesion can be examined microscopically for fungal forms, with further laboratory testing when needed. Repeated or unusually severe disease calls for attention to underlying illness and host defence, rather than simply repeating local cleansing. [Candidiasis of the mouth or throat: examination and microscopy of a lesion sample](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#testing-and-diagnosis-for-candidiasis-by-cdc)

The historically defensible measures include maintaining adequate feeding, gentle mouth care and clean feeding utensils. Harsh scraping and treating every white patch as the same disease are inappropriate. Present-day treatment adds an age-appropriate [antifungal drug](../../../pharmacology.md#antifungal-drug), such as a suitable topical [nystatin](../../../pharmacology.md#nystatin) preparation; more extensive or resistant disease may require systemic treatment such as [fluconazole](../../../pharmacology.md#fluconazole). Drug choice and formulation depend on the patient's age and clinical state. Those agents are later additions and must not be placed in an 1865 prescription. Hygiene and correction of predisposing factors accompany, rather than replace, effective antifungal treatment. [Mouth and throat disease: topical agents and systemic treatment for severe infection](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#treatment-of-candidiasis-by-cdc)

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

How would you perform the operation of tapping the abdomen and the thorax, and in what pathological states may it be expedient to resort to it?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**Tapping must follow identification of the fluid-containing cavity: [abdominal paracentesis](../../../biology.md#abdominal-paracentesis) for [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites), [thoracentesis](../../../biology.md#thoracentesis) for a [pleural effusion](../../../biology.md#pleural-effusion).** An enlarged abdomen or a dull area of chest is not, by itself, a sufficient indication for blind puncture. The object may be relief of pressure or obtaining fluid for diagnosis; neither necessarily removes the underlying cause.

**Abdomen: states in which tapping is useful.** Tense or troublesome [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites) can interfere with breathing, eating and comfort, particularly when it persists despite treatment of its cause. Causes include [cirrhosis](../../../biology.md#cirrhosis) with [portal hypertension](../../../biology.md#portal-hypertension), [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure), malignant peritoneal disease and some inflammatory or infective conditions. New or unexplained [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites) can also require diagnostic sampling. General swelling without a drainable peritoneal collection is a different problem. An ovarian cyst, a gravid [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus), a distended [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) and gaseous distension must not be mistaken for free [ascites](../../../biology.md#ascites). [Diagnostic paracentesis and large-volume paracentesis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#guidelines-on-the-management-of-ascites-by-the-bsg-and-basl) [Initial diagnosis: cirrhosis and alternative causes, imaging and diagnostic paracentesis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#ascites-practice-guidance-by-aasld-2021)

**Abdomen: operative account.** The characteristic historical apparatus is a sharp [trocar](../../../biology.md#trocar) within a hollow [cannula](../../../biology.md#cannula). The patient is supported, the presence and distribution of fluid established, and the [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) emptied to keep it away from the lower abdominal puncture route. Traditional descriptions use an appropriate abdominal site, often in the midline below the umbilicus; a landmark is not sufficient if an enlarged organ, a vessel or adherent [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine) is in the way. Once the instrument has entered fluid, withdrawal of the [trocar](../../../biology.md#trocar) leaves the [cannula](../../../biology.md#cannula) as the drainage channel. The patient and the flow are observed, and the opening is dressed after removal. This describes the operation's principle, not a universally safe blind puncture point.

Modern performance uses sterile preparation, appropriate local [anaesthesia](../../../biology.md#anaesthesia), a selected fluid pocket and a [catheter](../../../biology.md#catheter) system rather than assuming that every distended abdomen can be tapped in the same place. [Ultrasound](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#ultrasound) helps identify a safe pocket and avoid organs and vessels. Samples may be sent for cell counts, [protein](../../../protein.md) and [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) measurements, culture or [cytology](../../../biology.md#cytology) according to the clinical question. Therapeutic drainage requires reassessment because [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), intestinal injury, leakage, [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) and circulatory or renal disturbance can occur. In cirrhotic large-volume drainage, [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) replacement has a specific modern role; it is not an 1865 routine to be projected backwards. [Recommendations 1–2 and 5–6: fluid assessment, ultrasound guidance, drainage and albumin](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#guidelines-on-the-management-of-ascites-by-the-bsg-and-basl)

**Thorax: states in which tapping is useful.** A large [pleural effusion](../../../biology.md#pleural-effusion) producing respiratory difficulty can be relieved, and an unexplained effusion can be sampled to identify its cause. Serous fluid, [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) and [pus](../../../biology.md#pus) have different implications: [pleural effusion](../../../biology.md#pleural-effusion) associated with [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure), malignant disease or inflammation is not identical to [empyema](../../../biology.md#empyema). A purulent collection may require continuing drainage and treatment of the [infection](../../../biology.md#infection), not merely one palliative aspiration. Contemporary experience already included tapping in hydrothorax; an 1864 report with subsequent anatomical examination illustrates how removal of fluid could coexist with serious underlying disease. [Opening report: hydrothorax, thoracic paracentesis, subsequent death and anatomical examination](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#paracentesis-report-in-the-medical-society-of-the-county-of-kings-1864)

**Thorax: operative account.** The supported position exposes a suitable part of the chest; the operator first distinguishes fluid from consolidated [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) and identifies its upper extent and the [diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#diaphragm). An appropriate intercostal route enters the fluid above a [rib](../../../anatomy.md#rib), avoiding the principal vessels and [nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#nerve) running along its lower border. The drainage apparatus should permit controlled outflow without freely introducing air. The position of the [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung), [diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#diaphragm), [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen) makes an arbitrarily low puncture unsafe. Historic [trocar](../../../biology.md#trocar)-and-[cannula](../../../biology.md#cannula) entry and modern small [catheter](../../../biology.md#catheter) aspiration share the need to enter the collection without injuring these structures.

Current guidance requires thoracic [ultrasound](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#ultrasound) for aspiration of a [pleural effusion](../../../biology.md#pleural-effusion), a suitable needle or [catheter](../../../biology.md#catheter), and slow controlled drainage. Chest tightness, [pain](../../../neuroscience.md#pain), persistent [cough](../../../biology.md#cough) or increasing breathlessness are reasons to stop and reassess, not simply to continue until no more fluid runs. Complications include [pneumothorax](../../../biology.md#pneumothorax), [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), injury to an abdominal organ, [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) and [re-expansion pulmonary oedema](../../../biology.md#re-expansion-pulmonary-oedema). These precautions belong to current practice; they must not be represented as technology available in 1865. [Pleural aspiration: ultrasound, entry above a rib, controlled drainage and stopping symptoms](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#pleural-procedures-by-the-british-thoracic-society-2023)

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

What are the circumstances which determine the selection of Lithotomy or Lithotrity in the treatment of Stone.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**Choose the method that can remove the whole [bladder stone](../../../biology.md#bladder-stone) with the least overall danger in that patient.** Here [lithotomy](../../../biology.md#lithotomy) means cutting into the [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) to extract the stone; [lithotrity](../../../biology.md#lithotrity) means instrumental crushing through the [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra). The latter is not the later technique of external shock-wave treatment, and “no incision” does not mean “no tissue injury”.

**The stone.** A small, accessible, freely movable stone that can be grasped and broken without excessive force favours [lithotrity](../../../biology.md#lithotrity). Great bulk, great hardness, an awkward shape, several substantial stones or a stone fixed in a recess increases the difficulty of fragmentation and complete clearance. The amount of debris matters: pieces left in the [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) can irritate it, obstruct the passage or provide material for further growth. A historical comparison must count the burden of manipulation and clearance, not imagine that pulverization makes the stone disappear. No single size threshold can be specified independently of the instrument, the stone and the patient.

**The access passage and the [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder).** [Urethral stricture](../../../biology.md#urethral-stricture), inadequate urethral calibre, an existing false passage or difficult prostatic obstruction can make introduction of a useful crushing instrument unsafe. A [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) that tolerates exploration, holds a reasonable volume and empties effectively is more favourable than one with marked [cystitis](../../../anatomy.md#cystitis), bleeding, extreme irritability or retention of [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine). The operator must also consider [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney) function, active [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) and general reserve. A severely ill patient should not be exposed to a prolonged elective sequence of instrumentation merely because the stone itself seems crushable.

**Age and anatomy.** In a small child, the [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra) may not admit an adequately strong instrument, which helps explain the historical preference for [lithotomy](../../../biology.md#lithotomy) in many children. Age is a proxy for anatomy and tolerance, not an absolute law: small instruments and different modern access routes change the comparison. In an older man, the [prostate](../../../anatomy.md#prostate), [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder) emptying and general illness can be more important than age alone. In a woman, easier access through a shorter [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra) may favour an endoscopic route, but forceful dilation or extraction can still damage the continence mechanism.

**The operation and its operator.** Cutting risks [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), urinary leakage, injury and postoperative [infection](../../../biology.md#infection); crushing risks injury from the instrument and retained or impacted fragments. Each method requires skill, appropriate apparatus and aftercare. A contemporary 1859 lecture explicitly contrasts older complicated drilling apparatus with the curved crushing instrument then in use. That is evidence that the available instrument was part of the decision, rather than a timeless property of “[lithotrity](../../../biology.md#lithotrity)”. [8 January 1859, p. 23, contrast of earlier drilling and later curved crushing apparatus](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#lithotrity-instrument-lecture-in-the-british-medical-journal-1859)

**Practical selection.** With a modest stone, passable [urethra](../../../physiology.md#urethra), tolerably healthy [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder), effective emptying and suitable expertise, [lithotrity](../../../biology.md#lithotrity) has a strong rationale. When the stone is too large or resistant for safe fragmentation, access is unsafe or fragments cannot reasonably be cleared, extraction through another route becomes preferable. At the same time, poor general health may make either operation hazardous, so the preliminary task can be stabilization and management of associated disease rather than immediate choice of an instrument.

Modern guidance usually favours transurethral endoscopic fragmentation where feasible, offers a percutaneous route when transurethral treatment is unsuitable, and retains open [surgery](../../../biology.md#surgery) as an option for very large stones. In children, urethral injury risk can determine the route. These current options illustrate the same anatomical trade-offs but must not be imported wholesale into an 1865 recommendation. Nor should a historical preference for cutting in children be treated as a modern rule. [Interventions and recommendations: transurethral, percutaneous and open approaches; patient and access factors](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bladder-stones-in-the-eau-guidelines-on-urolithiasis)

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Diseases of women and children, and surgery](diseases-of-women-and-children-and-surgery.md)

**Original question.**

What are the chances of the return of Cancer after its removal from the Breast.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**The historical prognosis was guarded: malignant disease could return after apparently complete removal, and operative recovery was not equivalent to cure.** There is no defensible single percentage for the unspecified case in this question. An answer should explain the seriousness of recurrence, its forms and the evidence needed to estimate it, rather than invent an exact probability.

**What counts as return?** A new nodule in the scar or nearby [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) is a local recurrence; involvement of regional [lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) is regional recurrence; disease in distant organs is [metastasis](../../../biology.md#metastasis). These may represent residual local disease, disease already present but undetected, or later growth of disseminated cells. A grossly clear wound therefore does not prove absence of malignant cells elsewhere. A benign mammary tumour erroneously included with cancers would distort any estimate in the opposite direction.

**What an 1865 observer could reasonably say.** Visible removal could relieve an ulcerating, painful or bleeding [breast](../../../anatomy.md#breast) lesion, but the subsequent course still had to be observed. The extent of the original growth, fixation to surrounding parts, involvement of axillary nodes, the ability to remove it completely and the interval free from disease all mattered to prognosis. Neither early apparent healing nor a short symptom-free interval established that return could no longer occur.

A contemporary 1863 review makes an especially relevant methodological point: recurrence after operation could not be accurately determined among hospital patients who disappeared from observation unless a rapid return brought them back. Thus a statement about the proportion who returned to the surgeon is not the same as a complete recurrence risk among everyone operated on. This contemporary evidence supports a cautious answer without borrowing a later operation or a modern survival statistic. [p. 78, entry XXI, discussion of cancer statistics and loss of postoperative follow-up](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#medico-chirurgical-transactions-reviewed-in-1863)

**Present-day explanation.** [Breast cancer recurrence](../../../biology.md#breast-cancer-recurrence) risk depends on stage, tumour size and grade, [lymph node](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node) involvement, completeness of excision and tumour biology. Modern assessment also distinguishes hormone-receptor and other molecular subtypes and takes account of additional treatment. Positive margins, more advanced disease and certain aggressive subtypes increase risk. Consequently a modern estimate requires the actual tumour and treatment information; it cannot be inferred from the bare fact that a [breast cancer](../../../biology.md#breast-cancer) was removed. [Risk of recurrence: stage, nodes, grade, tumour type and margins](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#recurrent-breast-cancer-by-nci)

Any numerical claim must specify the population, what event is counted, the treatment, the follow-up period and how patients lost to observation are handled. Death before observed recurrence and the difference between local recurrence and distant disease also affect interpretation. A recurrence percentage is not automatically an operative death rate or a survival rate. The appropriate concise conclusion for this historical question is therefore **a substantial concern for return, with prognosis conditioned by extent and completeness of removal, and no guaranteed disease-free interval or universal percentage**.
