**[Angina pectoris](../../../../../angina-pectoris.md) is most usefully explained by transient inadequacy of the myocardial [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) supply relative to demand.** It is a clinical syndrome, rather than the name of one visible anatomical lesion. The characteristic constricting chest discomfort may radiate to the arm, neck or jaw and be provoked by effort; the question concerns its causes, not a claim that every chest pain is [angina pectoris](../../../../../angina-pectoris.md).[What causes angina; coronary disease, microvascular disease and spasm](../../../../../angina-causes-and-risk-factors-by-nhlbi.md)

The common structural basis is [coronary atherosclerosis](../../../../../coronary-atherosclerosis.md). Narrowing limits the increase of [coronary blood flow](../../../../../coronary-blood-flow.md) that should accompany exercise. An affected [heart](../../../../../heart.md) may receive sufficient [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) at rest and insufficient [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) when its rate, force of contraction and wall stress rise. Plaque disruption with [thrombosis](../../../../../thrombosis.md) can abruptly worsen supply; persistent severe [ischemia](../../../../../ischemia.md) can then cause [myocardial infarction](../../../../../myocardial-infarction.md). **Pain from transient [ischemia](../../../../../ischemia.md) does not by itself prove myocardial [necrosis](../../../../../necrosis.md).**

Other mechanisms include [coronary artery spasm](../../../../../coronary-artery-spasm.md), impaired regulation of small coronary vessels, or increased demand in [left ventricular hypertrophy](../../../../../left-ventricular-hypertrophy.md), severe [aortic stenosis](../../../../../aortic-stenosis.md), or [tachycardia](../../../../../tachycardia.md). Reduced delivery through severe [anaemia](../../../../../anaemia.md), low arterial [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) or low perfusion pressure may provoke symptoms even without a new obstructing plaque. Structural narrowing, vasomotor change, blood oxygen-carrying capacity and cardiac work therefore have to be considered together.[Recommendations 1.3.7 and factors intensifying ischaemia: anaemia, spasm, tachycardia and ventricular hypertrophy](../../../../../recent-onset-chest-pain-of-suspected-cardiac-origin-by-nice.md)

For an 1865 interpretation, coronary “ossification” or degeneration, diseased cardiac or aortic structures and neural or spasmodic explanations belonged to the historical discussion. These did not establish a single universal cause. A report published in January 1865 described a pathological case associated [angina pectoris](../../../../../angina-pectoris.md) with diseased coronary arteries, but isolated cases cannot demonstrate that a particular valve or vessel lesion is necessary in every patient.[pp. 255–256, section XII: coronary openings narrowed and disease around the cardiac plexus](../../../../../half-yearly-report-on-pathology-and-principles-and-practice-of-medicine-1865.md) The modern supply-and-demand account explains why severe symptoms can coexist with relatively inconspicuous large coronary arteries, and why substantial coronary disease need not cause pain at every moment.

A pathological investigation inspects the coronary origins and course, their lumen and wall, the [myocardium](../../../../../myocardium.md), the [aortic valve](../../../../../aortic-valve.md) and ventricular thickness. Clinical investigation adds evidence of reversible [myocardial ischaemia](../../../../../myocardial-ischaemia.md) and distinguishes it from infarction or non-cardiac pain. Modern [electrocardiography](../../../../../electrocardiography.md) and appropriate imaging address functions that a nineteenth-century post-mortem inspection alone could not measure. A normal-looking vessel on gross inspection cannot exclude [coronary microvascular disease](../../../../../coronary-microvascular-disease.md) or a transient [coronary artery spasm](../../../../../coronary-artery-spasm.md).
