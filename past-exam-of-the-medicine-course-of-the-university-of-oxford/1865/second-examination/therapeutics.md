## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

How would you treat a case of Rheumatic Fever and its most common complications ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The historical priorities were rest, relief of painful [arthritis](../../../biology.md#arthritis), and repeated examination for [carditis](../../../biology.md#carditis); modern treatment also eradicates preceding [streptococcal infection](../../../biology.md#streptococcal-infection) and prevents recurrences.** In an 1865 account, the patient would be nursed in bed, the painful [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) supported, drinks and tolerable nourishment supplied, and temperature, pulse, breathing and [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine) observed. Moist flannel applications and [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) preparations were used for severe pain and sleeplessness. Alkaline salts were commonly prescribed under a theory that the disease involved an abnormal acid material; attempts to make the [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine) alkaline were a therapeutic practice, not proof that an acid caused the disease. There was already disagreement about the value of specific drugs and [blistering agents](../../../biology.md#blistering-agent). A contemporary critique describes these competing treatments and stresses the inadequacy of small uncontrolled case series.[14 January, p. 31, ordinary management and criticism of blistering](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#remarks-on-the-treatment-of-acute-rheumatism-in-the-british-medical-journal-1865)

The important complications are [rheumatic carditis](../../../biology.md#rheumatic-carditis), including [valvulitis](../../../biology.md#valvulitis) and sometimes [pericarditis](../../../biology.md#pericarditis), with possible [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure); [Sydenham's chorea](../../../neurology.md#sydenham-s-chorea) is another manifestation. New [dyspnoea](../../../biology.md#dyspnoea), a changed [heart murmur](../../../psychology.md#heart-murmur), chest pain, a [pericardial friction rub](../../../biology.md#pericardial-friction-rub), or signs of circulatory failure require reassessment rather than stronger treatment of the painful [anatomical joint](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) alone. Historically, local bleeding, [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) and [counterirritation](../../../biology.md#counterirritation) were employed against supposed cardiac inflammation. Their historical use does not justify them now. A heart examination also matters when [arthritis](../../../biology.md#arthritis) improves, because improvement of pain does not establish freedom from cardiac injury.[clinical manifestations: carditis, arthritis and chorea](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-acute-rheumatic-fever-by-cdc)

Present management confirms the diagnosis and cardiac involvement with appropriate clinical criteria, [electrocardiography](../../../biology.md#electrocardiography) and [echocardiography](../../../psychology.md#echocardiography). [Penicillin](../../../biology.md#penicillin) or an appropriate alternative eradicates residual group A [streptococci](../../../organism.md#streptococcus), even if the throat symptoms have resolved. An [anti-inflammatory drug](../../../pharmacology.md#anti-inflammatory-drug) relieves [arthritis](../../../biology.md#arthritis); severe [carditis](../../../biology.md#carditis), [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure) or disabling [Sydenham's chorea](../../../neurology.md#sydenham-s-chorea) needs specialist management. Fluid-retaining [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure) can require a [diuretic](../../../pharmacology.md#diuretic) with monitoring of renal function and [electrolytes](../../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte); treatment is selected according to haemodynamic findings. The 2025 guideline allows [corticosteroids](../../../pharmacology.md#corticosteroid) for selected severe [carditis](../../../biology.md#carditis) or [pericarditis](../../../biology.md#pericarditis) with effusion, under specialist care. Mild [Sydenham's chorea](../../../neurology.md#sydenham-s-chorea) may need only rest and a calm environment; greater functional impairment can justify [carbamazepine](../../../pharmacology.md#carbamazepine) or another carefully selected symptomatic medicine. These measures accompany, rather than replace, [antibiotic prophylaxis](../../../pharmacology.md#antibiotic-prophylaxis). [Secondary antibiotic prophylaxis](../../../pharmacology.md#secondary-antibiotic-prophylaxis) reduces future attacks and further valve injury. Neither [aspirin](../../../pharmacology.md#aspirin) nor a later [salicylate](../../../pharmacology.md#salicylate) regimen should be presented as ordinary Oxford practice in 1865. These are historical explanations and general medical principles, not a treatment plan for an individual.[acute management, cardiac assessment, management of chorea and secondary prophylaxis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#australian-guideline-for-acute-rheumatic-fever-and-rheumatic-heart-disease-2025)

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

What treatment would you ordinarily pursue in the Delirium of Typhus, and in the severe Diarrhœa of Typhoid Fever ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The [delirium](../../../neurology.md#delirium) of [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus) calls for treatment of systemic illness and careful nursing; severe [diarrhoea](../../../biology.md#diarrhoea) in [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) additionally demands replacement of losses and vigilance for intestinal injury.** These are different [infections](../../../biology.md#infection), despite the similar names.

For an 1865 description of [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus), provide a quiet, ventilated room, continuous attentive nursing, fluid and nourishment, mouth care, and protection from falls or wandering. Examine for retention of [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine), constipation, chest complications and exhaustion. A restless sleepless patient was sometimes given a cautiously selected [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) preparation; wine or other alcoholic stimulants were also employed in supposed exhaustion. Such practice was contested: contemporary observations warned that recovery after a drug did not establish that the drug had caused recovery. Sedation was especially questionable with deepening stupor or respiratory impairment. Routine depletion of an already prostrate patient was not a rational substitute for support.[1865, discussion of stimulants, nursing and inference from recovery](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#typhus-statistics-of-the-dundee-royal-infirmary-with-remarks-by-thomas-john-maclagan)

Modern epidemic [typhus](../../../biology.md#typhus) is caused by [Rickettsia prowazekii](../../../organism.md#rickettsia-prowazekii) and requires prompt [doxycycline](../../../biology.md#doxycycline), alongside management of [hypoxia](../../../biology.md#hypoxia), [dehydration](../../../chemical-reaction.md#dehydration), organ dysfunction and the precipitating [infection](../../../biology.md#infection). Testing should not postpone treatment when the clinical suspicion is strong. A low-stimulation environment and observation remain useful, while historical alcohol stimulation and indiscriminate [opioid](../../../pharmacology.md#opioid) sedation are not disease-specific therapy. Control of the transmitting body lice is part of preventing further [infection](../../../biology.md#infection).[treatment, severe manifestations and transmission](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-overview-of-epidemic-typhus-by-cdc)

In the historical treatment of severe [typhoid diarrhoea](../../../biology.md#typhoid-diarrhoea), rest, readily tolerated nourishment and measured replacement of drinks were accompanied by attempts to quiet the bowel with [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium), sometimes with an [astringent](../../../pharmacology.md#astringent) such as chalk or a lead-containing preparation. These aimed at reducing stool frequency; they did not eliminate the cause. An apparent reduction of stools could conceal deterioration, and intestinal pain, distension, bleeding or collapse required renewed examination. Contemporary criticism of routine [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) use in febrile bowel disease shows that this was never a uniformly established cure.[lecture read 1 December 1865, published February 1866, pp. 76–81; adverse effects and disputed indications](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#opium-in-certain-forms-of-fever-and-in-diarrhoea-and-dysentery-1865-lecture)

Today [typhoid fever](../../../biology.md#typhoid-fever) requires an effective [antibiotic](../../../biology.md#antibiotic) selected with travel history and local [antimicrobial resistance](../../../pharmacology.md#antimicrobial-resistance) in mind, then refined by [bacterial culture](../../../biology.md#bacterial-culture) and susceptibility results. Assess fluid balance, circulation, [electrolytes](../../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte) and kidney function, using oral or intravenous replacement as severity requires. [Gastrointestinal bleeding](../../../biology.md#gastrointestinal-bleeding), increasing abdominal tenderness, guarding, distension, [shock](../../../transport-equation.md#shock-wave) or a sudden change in condition can indicate [intestinal perforation](../../../biology.md#intestinal-perforation) or other severe complications and need urgent hospital assessment. Historical lead preparations are toxic; suppressing [diarrhoea](../../../biology.md#diarrhoea) is not a substitute for treating the [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) and its complications.[diagnosis, antibiotic use and complications](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-typhoid-fever-and-paratyphoid-fever-by-cdc)

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

When are topical applications to the Pharynx, or Larynx, desirable? How do you apply them, and with what object? What drugs are used for the purpose, and in what doses ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Local treatment is useful when there is an accessible lesion or a defined symptom to relieve; its concentration, delivered quantity and route must be specified separately.** Historical indications included chronic [pharyngitis](../../../biology.md#pharyngitis) with excessive secretion, ulceration, enlarged or unhealthy tonsillar surfaces, and some forms of chronic [laryngitis](../../../biology.md#laryngitis). The intended effects were cleansing, soothing, reducing secretion, or cauterising a circumscribed unhealthy surface. Merely having a sore throat was not sufficient reason for a caustic application.

The accessible [pharynx](../../../anatomy.md#pharynx) could be treated by a gargle, a wash, a lozenge, or a carefully directed brush or sponge. A gargle does not reliably medicate the [larynx](../../../anatomy.md#larynx): during normal swallowing the entrance is protected, and aspiration is an unwanted event. For laryngeal painting, the period apparatus included a light source, reflector, small warmed laryngeal mirror and a suitably bent brush. Illumination and the mirror image allowed inspection of the lesion and guidance of the instrument, instead of blind probing. A small measured loading reduced uncontrolled flow into the [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway). An [atomiser](../../../biology.md#atomiser) instead broke liquid into droplets with a jet of air or steam; the concentration in its reservoir was not the amount retained by the patient. A contemporary review discusses brushes, mirrors, caustic holders and the limitations of spraying caustic solutions.[pp. 310–319, local treatment and equipment](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#recent-works-on-the-laryngoscope-1865-review)

For a numerical period example, the 1865 account of medicated inhalation gives **[silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate), 3–5 grains, sometimes 10 grains, per Imperial fluid ounce of distilled [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water)**. These are historical reservoir concentrations, approximately 0.7–2.3 percent mass per volume, not oral doses and not modern recommendations for inhaling a corrosive. Other concentrations in the same 1865 account, each per fluid ounce of [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), are **[tannic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#tannic-acid), 3–10 grains; [alum](../../../chemical-substance.md#alum), 4–15 grains; [zinc sulfate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#zinc-sulfate), 1/2–5 grains; and [opium tincture](../../../pharmacology.md#opium-tincture), 1–10 minims**. The first three were intended chiefly as local astringent applications, and the last as a soothing or narcotic application. These are reports of historical practice, including hazardous applications, rather than evidence that atomising these drugs is beneficial or safe today.[vol. 52, pp. 102–106, especially p. 105, numbered list of drugs and silver-nitrate strengths](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#medicated-inhalation-in-the-retrospect-of-medicine-1865)

Direct historical cauterisation used still stronger [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate) solutions or the solid salt for selected ulcers and growth bases. A contemporary review records disagreement over aggressive treatment and reports a recommendation of 40 grains per fluid ounce in one of the works reviewed. That disagreement is part of the answer: **40 grains per ounce for local painting must not be confused with a dilute gargle, an inhalation concentration or a swallowed dose**. For a less destructive mode of local medication, the period dosage table lists **one to four or more [tannic acid lozenges](../../../pharmacology.md#tannic-acid-lozenge)**, illustrating again the distinction between strength per item and the number administered.[discussion of strong silver solutions and missing dose information](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#recent-works-on-the-laryngoscope-1865-review)[tannic acid lozenges and posological table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

Modern uncomplicated acute sore throat usually receives symptom relief, adequate drinking, and selective [antibiotic](../../../biology.md#antibiotic) treatment when indicated by the assessed cause and severity. Medicated lozenges can give a small reduction in pain in adults; that modest benefit does not validate the historical corrosive sprays. Persistent hoarseness, a focal ulcer or a suspected growth requires diagnosis rather than repeated empirical painting. [Stridor](../../../biology.md#stridor), difficulty managing secretions or respiratory distress raises an [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway) problem, for which irritating instrumentation can be hazardous. Local treatment of a syphilitic or other specific lesion cannot replace systemic treatment of its cause.[NG84, self-care, antibiotics and referral recommendations](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#sore-throat-acute-antimicrobial-prescribing-by-nice)[persistent voice symptoms, assessment and emergency breathing symptoms](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#laryngitis-by-nhs)

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

State the most usual modes of treating Syphilis in its several stages or varieties.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**In 1865 [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) was the principal systemic remedy for early constitutional [syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis), while [potassium iodide](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-iodide) was especially associated with later destructive lesions; neither is an acceptable substitute for modern [penicillin](../../../biology.md#penicillin) therapy.** Historical classifications depended chiefly on clinical appearance, before identification of [Treponema pallidum](../../../organism.md#treponema-pallidum) and modern [serology](../../../biology.md#serology).

For a primary indurated [chancre](../../../biology.md#chancre), period treatment combined cleanliness, protection from friction and irritant discharges, and, in mercurial practice, a prolonged course of a [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) preparation. Administration could be by mouth or by rubbing an ointment into the [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin); some practitioners used fumigation. Local cauterisation of a sore could not be assumed to remove infection already spread beyond it. Painful soft sores and suppurating groin swellings were often discussed among venereal diseases, but modern [chancroid](../../../biology.md#chancroid) is distinct from [syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis); an 1865 “variety” cannot automatically be assigned the same cause or treatment.[discussion of infecting sores, soft sores, buboes, mercurial and non-mercurial practice](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#health-of-the-navy-home-station-report-1864)

In secondary disease, with generalised eruptions and mucosal lesions, [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) was used in carefully watched courses, with food, hygiene and local care. Blue pill, grey powder and other mercurial compounds were alternative forms, not interchangeable quantities. Stomatitis, excessive salivation, bowel disturbance and constitutional deterioration were reasons to interrupt treatment, not desirable signs of cure. Relapse after apparent healing was recognised. For later [gummatous syphilis](../../../biology.md#gummatous-syphilis), painful periosteal swellings, ulceration and some nervous manifestations, [potassium iodide](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-iodide), sometimes combined with a mercurial, was a characteristic period choice. Apparent symptomatic improvement did not demonstrate eradication of the [infection](../../../biology.md#infection).[pp. 17–23, secondary disease, mercurial effects and uncertainty](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#remarks-on-syphilis-and-its-treatment-1864)[3 September, p. 263, late tertiary lesions and iodide practice](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#clinical-commentaries-on-late-syphilis-in-the-british-medical-journal-1864)

Congenital disease also received mercurial treatment in the period, together with attention to feeding and the mother. That history must be separated from present management: [congenital syphilis](../../../biology.md#congenital-syphilis) results from maternal [infection](../../../biology.md#infection), not an inherited constitutional taint. Maternal treatment, infant examination and laboratory evaluation determine modern management; an affected infant can require a full course of parenteral [penicillin](../../../biology.md#penicillin) even without obvious external lesions.[evaluation and scenario-specific regimens](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#congenital-syphilis-treatment-guidance-by-cdc)

Modern early acquired [syphilis](../../../biology.md#syphilis), including primary, secondary and early latent disease, is ordinarily treated with **benzathine [penicillin G](../../../biology.md#penicillin-g), 2.4 million units intramuscularly once** in adults. Late latent disease or disease of unknown duration ordinarily requires **three weekly 2.4-million-unit injections**. [Neurosyphilis](../../../biology.md#neurosyphilis), ocular disease and otic disease require a regimen with appropriate tissue penetration, ordinarily **aqueous crystalline [penicillin G](../../../biology.md#penicillin-g), 18–24 million units per day intravenously for 10–14 days**, rather than the single early-disease injection. Pregnancy, allergy, congenital disease and other special circumstances need the corresponding specialist pathway. Clinical and quantitative [serological](../../../biology.md#serology) follow-up, partner management and assessment for coexisting [infections](../../../biology.md#infection) are part of treatment. The stage labels guide management, but nervous, ocular or auditory involvement can occur at more than one stage.[recommended adult regimen](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#primary-and-secondary-syphilis-treatment-guidance-by-cdc)[early and late latent regimens](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#latent-syphilis-treatment-guidance-by-cdc)[recommended regimen and ocular/otic management](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neurosyphilis-ocular-syphilis-and-otosyphilis-treatment-guidance-by-cdc)

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

What indications for treatment do you gather from microscopical examination of the urine ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**[Urine microscopy](../../../biology.md#urine-microscopy) can identify the kind and possible site of urinary disease, directing further investigation and cause-specific treatment; a sediment finding alone is seldom a prescription.** This is a general methods question. No particular specimen, microscopic field or patient result is supplied.

Examine a fresh, properly collected sample, noting collection and storage conditions. After gentle mixing, examine a standardised sediment with a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope): a low-power objective helps find [urinary casts](../../../biology.md#urinary-cast), while higher power characterises [cells](../../../biological-cell.md), [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria) and [crystals](../../../thermodynamics.md#crystal). A [centrifuge](../../../centrifugation.md#centrifuge) concentrates sediment when that method is used, but excessive delay or unsuitable processing can destroy [cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and casts or generate crystals after collection. Interpret findings with symptoms, urine chemistry and kidney function; a dirty container or vaginal contamination can otherwise mimic urinary disease.[urine particles, specimen stability and microscopy procedures](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#eflm-european-urinalysis-guideline-2023)

[Red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) indicate [haematuria](../../../biology.md#haematuria), whose source may be anywhere along the tract. [Red blood cell casts](../../../biology.md#red-blood-cell-cast) or convincing dysmorphic [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) support a glomerular origin and justify assessment for [glomerulonephritis](../../../biology.md#glomerulonephritis), including renal function, [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria) and appropriate referral. They do not establish a particular immunological cause. Isomorphic [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) with pain may suggest a stone; persistent unexplained [haematuria](../../../biology.md#haematuria) also requires assessment for other urinary pathology. A historically tempting order to give an [astringent](../../../pharmacology.md#astringent) would not address these different mechanisms.[initial evaluation, suspected medical renal disease and risk-based urinary evaluation](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#microhematuria-guideline-by-aua-and-sufu-2025)

Many [white blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte), with compatible symptoms and [bacteria](../../../organism.md#bacteria), support [urinary tract infection](../../../biology.md#urinary-tract-infection); obtain a suitable [bacterial culture](../../../biology.md#bacterial-culture) when indicated and select an [antibiotic](../../../biology.md#antibiotic) according to the clinical syndrome and susceptibility. Contamination or asymptomatic bacteriuria is not equivalent to symptomatic [infection](../../../biology.md#infection). [White blood cell casts](../../../biology.md#white-blood-cell-cast) favour an intrarenal process such as [pyelonephritis](../../../anatomy.md#pyelonephritis) or [interstitial nephritis](../../../biology.md#interstitial-nephritis), so the treatment cannot be inferred from pus alone. Numerous squamous [epithelial cells](../../../biological-cell.md#epithelial-cell) instead raise concern about collection quality.[urine testing, pyuria, bacteriuria and casts](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bacterial-urinary-tract-infections-by-merck-manual)

Renal tubular [epithelial cells](../../../biological-cell.md#epithelial-cell) and granular casts can support [tubular injury](../../../biology.md#tubular-injury); fatty elements together with marked [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria) suggest a nephrotic process. These findings direct assessment of kidney disease, fluid balance and potentially nephrotoxic exposures. A few hyaline casts may occur without a specific kidney disease. Casts are moulds formed within renal tubules; that origin is why they can localise a finding more effectively than free cells.[table, tubular, granular, fatty and hyaline casts](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#urinary-casts-by-merck-manual)

[Urinary crystals](../../../biology.md#urinary-crystal) must be interpreted with urine acidity, concentration and the time after collection. [Uric acid](../../../metabolism.md#uric-acid) or [calcium oxalate](../../../biology.md#calcium-oxalate) crystals can suggest a stone-forming environment but do not prove an obstructing stone or justify an unrestricted alkali prescription. Hexagonal [cystine](../../../chemical-substance.md#cystine) crystals raise a different metabolic question. Infection-associated phosphate stones require attention to the associated [infection](../../../biology.md#infection) as well as stone management. Modern decisions combine stone analysis, imaging and metabolic assessment; hydration, urinary alkalinisation or other measures depend on the confirmed disorder. Microscopy does not by itself measure [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose), albumin loss or renal filtration, so these require separate tests.[metabolic evaluation and recurrence prevention: uric acid, cystine, calcium oxalate and infection stones](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#eau-guidelines-on-urolithiasis)

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the counter irritants contained in the British Pharmacopœia, and state under what circumstances you would have recourse to the use of each.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**The 1864 [British Pharmacopoeia](../../../pharmacology.md#british-pharmacopoeia) supplied mild rubefacients, blistering preparations and agents producing pustulation; these different degrees of injury were grouped under historical [counterirritation](../../../biology.md#counterirritation).** Redness of the [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) demonstrates a local effect, not removal of inflammation from a distant organ. The following enumeration uses the preparations current for the 1865 examination.

- [Mustard](../../../pharmacology.md#mustard), as [Sinapis](../../../pharmacology.md#mustard) and [Cataplasma Sinapis](../../../pharmacology.md#cataplasma-sinapis), was a rapidly acting [rubefacient](../../../biology.md#rubefacient), used historically for transient painful or spasmodic conditions and as a supposed revulsive application in internal disease. Its duration had to be limited because prolonged contact caused a [burn](../../../biology.md#burn). The later compound mustard liniment and mustard paper should not be silently inserted into this 1864 list.[Cruciferae, mustard and its official cataplasm](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)
- [Ammonia liniment](../../../pharmacology.md#ammonia-liniment), [Linimentum Ammoniae](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-ammoniae), and the ammoniacal [Linimentum Camphorae Compositum](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-camphorae-compositum) gave relatively prompt irritation. They were used for superficial muscular or rheumatic pains when a short rubefacient effect was desired. Simple [camphor liniment](../../../pharmacology.md#camphor-liniment) and [soap liniment](../../../pharmacology.md#soap-liniment) were milder stimulating embrocations for stiff painful parts; the compound [camphor liniment](../../../pharmacology.md#camphor-liniment) owed much of its greater irritation to [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia).[strong solution of ammonia; Lauraceae, camphor preparations](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)
- [Turpentine](../../../chemical-substance.md#turpentine), in [Linimentum Terebinthinae](../../../chemical-substance.md#linimentum-terebinthinae) or [Linimentum Terebinthinae Aceticum](../../../chemical-substance.md#linimentum-terebinthinae-aceticum), and in the official [turpentine ointment](../../../pharmacology.md#turpentine-ointment), was used as a stimulating application. Historical indications included muscular pain, neuralgic pain and chest or abdominal applications intended to act on deeper disease. The acetic preparation was the more irritating form. This does not establish that either could cure [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia) or intestinal disease.[Coniferae, turpentine liniments and ointment](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)
- [Capsicum](../../../pharmacology.md#capsicum) and its [tincture](../../../pharmacology.md#tincture) supplied a pungent local stimulant, used in diluted embrocations for chronic painful conditions. [Burgundy pitch](../../../pharmacology.md#burgundy-pitch), chiefly [Emplastrum Picis](../../../pharmacology.md#emplastrum-picis), supplied milder sustained stimulation, historically chosen for chronic chest, back or rheumatic discomfort. An adhesive plaster could itself cause dermatitis.[Solanaceae, capsicum; Coniferae continued, pitch plaster](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)
- [Cantharides](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharides) supplied [Emplastrum Cantharidis](../../../pharmacology.md#emplastrum-cantharidis), [Linimentum Cantharidis](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-cantharidis), [Emplastrum Calefaciens](../../../pharmacology.md#emplastrum-calefaciens) and [Unguentum Cantharidis](../../../pharmacology.md#unguentum-cantharidis). The warming plaster aimed at a milder prolonged effect; the ordinary plaster and liniment were vesicants, while the ointment could maintain an already blistered surface. Period indications included [pleurisy](../../../biology.md#pleurisy), [pericarditis](../../../biology.md#pericarditis), chronic painful [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint) and [neuralgia](../../../neurology.md#neuralgia). These were intended remote effects, not demonstrated cures. The 1864 name is [Linimentum Cantharidis](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-cantharidis); its later renaming as [Liquor Epispasticus](../../../pharmacology.md#liquor-epispasticus) must not erase the edition difference. Cantharides vinegar and official blistering paper belong to the later additions, rather than this list.[Coleoptera, British preparations and distinction from the old London vinegar](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)[prefatory lists of additions and altered names, pp. xvii–xx](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- [Iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine), as [Linimentum Iodi](../../../chemical-substance.md#linimentum-iodi), [Tinctura Iodi](../../../chemical-substance.md#tinctura-iodi) and [Unguentum Iodi Compositum](../../../chemical-substance.md#unguentum-iodi-compositum), was painted or rubbed over chronic swellings, indurations and diseased [anatomical joints](../../../anatomy.md#anatomical-joint). The strong liniment could blister; the intended historical effect was alteration of local action or promotion of absorption. Such an indication was not equivalent to proof that a swelling was benign or that it would resolve.[iodine, official preparations and external therapeutic uses](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)
- [Croton oil](../../../pharmacology.md#croton-oil), especially [Linimentum Crotonis](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-crotonis), and [tartar emetic](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-antimony-tartrate), in [Unguentum Antimonii Tartarati](../../../chemical-substance.md#unguentum-antimonii-tartarati), produced more persistent irritation and a pustular eruption. They were employed for prolonged counterirritation in chronic pulmonary or other deep-seated complaints when a temporary rubefacient was considered insufficient. Ulceration, scarring and systemic toxicity were substantial disadvantages, not evidence of effective elimination of disease.[Euphorbiaceae, croton liniment; tartarated antimony and its ointment](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

Other official substances could destroy tissue, but a [caustic](../../../geometrical-optics.md#wave-caustic) used to destroy a particular lesion is conceptually different from a counterirritant applied to influence another site. Likewise [belladonna](../../../pharmacology.md#belladonna) and [aconite](../../../pharmacology.md#aconite) applications were principally intended to relieve pain, not simply to create a competing skin inflammation. Modern treatment should address the diagnosed condition; a historical claim of revulsion cannot justify blistering over a diseased [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) or [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung). The period literature itself recognised absorption, urinary irritation and sloughing from [cantharides](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharides). No present-day use of a particular regulated skin preparation makes these broad historical indications valid.[Coleoptera, precautions and toxic effects](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the various modes in which water may be used externally as a Therapeutical agent, and name the cases to which each is suited.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**External [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) treatment can clean, transfer [heat](../../../heat.md), provide moisture and apply mechanical pressure; the useful method depends on the object, not on a general claim that water expels disease.** Historical classifications distinguished general and local bathing, cold and warm applications, affusion, showers, compresses, packing and vapour baths.

Cold applications included sponging, pouring water over the body, immersion, a local wet compress and a cold shower or douche. A renewed wet cloth removes [heat](../../../heat.md) by contact and [evaporation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#evaporation); a wrapping allowed to warm and then covered is a different intervention. In 1865 these methods were used for febrile heat, local pain or swelling, and sometimes to obtain a supposed stimulating reaction in chronic debility. A cold douche also adds mechanical impact and an abrupt circulatory stimulus. Historical wet-sheet packing enclosed the body in a wet sheet with outer coverings and was either renewed to sustain cooling or left to warm. The contemporary description distinguishes these uses; the claims about stimulation and cure are historical claims.[wet-sheet packing, renewed cooling compresses and warming compresses](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#hydrotherapy-in-the-edinburgh-medical-journal-1865-review)

Tepid and warm immersion, a warm local bath or sitz bath, and warm moist fomentations were used to soothe painful or spasmodic conditions, support cleansing and soften crusts or secretions. Local applications avoid heating the whole body. The apparatus might be a bath, basin, irrigating vessel or repeatedly renewed cloth; a warm moist dressing must not become a source of sustained scalding. Historically, hot immersion and vapour baths were also used to induce sweating in rheumatic complaints and supposed retention of harmful material. Heat-induced sweating is real, but removal of a disease-specific poison by that route is a separate claim requiring evidence. Showering and douching similarly cannot be treated as dose-equivalent to quiet immersion.[water and mineral waters, external thermal-bath uses](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

Current examples show why the distinction matters. [Heatstroke](../../../biology.md#heatstroke) requires rapid cooling and emergency care: cold-water immersion or other active cooling is used to reduce dangerous body temperature, not to provoke a later “reaction”. In contrast, routine tepid sponging is not recommended for [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) in children under five. Febrile [thermoregulation](../../../physiology.md#thermoregulation) can provoke shivering and distress with external cooling, so the treatment of ordinary [fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) should not be inferred from the management of [hyperthermia](../../../biology.md#hyperthermia).[heatstroke, emergency response and rapid cooling](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#heat-related-illnesses-by-cdc-and-niosh)[NG143, recommendations 1.6.2–1.6.6](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#fever-in-under-5s-by-nice)

For a thermal [burn](../../../biology.md#burn), current first aid uses **cool running [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) for 20 minutes**, with the rest of the person kept warm; ice or an ice-water application is not the appropriate substitute. Cleansing or irrigation has a further purpose independent of temperature: physically removing contamination, with the fluid and technique chosen for the tissue and injury. A steam application to the face is not a harmless version of warm bathing: spilled hot water and steam cause [scalds](../../../biology.md#scald). The historical use of inhalation therefore does not validate leaning over boiling water as a general respiratory treatment. These examples specify an actual therapeutic objective without endorsing the broad claims of nineteenth-century hydropathy.[first aid and avoidance of inappropriate applications](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#burns-and-scalds-by-nhs)[lack of strong evidence for treating colds and risk of scald injury](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#steam-inhalation-warning-by-nhs-burns-specialists)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Therapeutics](therapeutics.md)

**Original question.**

What are the doses of the Pharmacopœial preparations of Arsenic, Copper, Opium, Belladonna, Strychnia, and Cinchona ?

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**For December 1865 the relevant official preparations are those of the 1864 [British Pharmacopoeia](../../../pharmacology.md#british-pharmacopoeia); dose ranges must come from contemporary prescribing evidence, not be borrowed from later editions.** The first edition specified identities and preparations without supplying a universal clinical dose for every article. The following are historical adult quantities from [The Essentials of Materia Medica and Therapeutics by Alfred Baring Garrod (1865)](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865), using its British entries rather than bracketed United States formulas. They are not current prescribing instructions or established safe limits. “Grain” is a unit of [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass); “minim”, fluid drachm and fluid ounce are units of [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume). **One Imperial fluid drachm contains 60 minims, and one fluid ounce contains eight fluid drachms.** A minim is not an arbitrary drop.[introduction, British weights and measures; posological table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

For [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic), distinguish the elemental substance from its medicinal compounds. The relevant series comprises [Acidum Arseniosum](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide), [Liquor Arsenicalis](../../../pharmacology.md#arsenical-solution), [Sodae Arsenias](../../../pharmacology.md#sodium-arsenate), [Liquor Sodae Arseniatis](../../../pharmacology.md#liquor-sodae-arseniatis) and [Ferri Arsenias](../../../pharmacology.md#ferrous-arsenate). The contemporary ranges are **[arsenic trioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic-trioxide) about 1/60–1/12 grain; [arsenical solution](../../../pharmacology.md#arsenical-solution) 2–5 minims, occasionally 10; crystalline [sodium arsenate](../../../pharmacology.md#sodium-arsenate) 1/12–1/2 grain, or its dried salt 1/20–1/4 grain; its official solution 5–30 minims; [ferrous arsenate](../../../pharmacology.md#ferrous-arsenate) 1/10–1/8 grain**. Hydrated and dried salts cannot be exchanged grain for grain. The two official arsenical solutions each contain four grains of their specified ingredient per fluid ounce, but the ingredients are different. The solution of arsenic and mercury discussed alongside them was non-official, and [Liquor Arsenici Hydrochloricus](../../../chemical-substance.md#liquor-arsenici-hydrochloricus) was an addition in 1867: neither belongs in an unqualified list of 1864 preparations.[arsenious acid, sodium arsenate, ferrous arsenate; posological table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)[prefatory additions, p. xvii](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)

For [copper](../../../chemical-substance.md#copper), the medicinal article is purified [Cupri Sulphas](../../../pharmacology.md#copper-sulfate), [copper sulfate](../../../pharmacology.md#copper-sulfate): **1/4–2 grains as a historical astringent or tonic, 5–8 grains as a historical emetic**. An external solution was described as **1–10 grains or more per fluid ounce**, depending on the intended local action. The anhydrous test reagent and copper compounds listed among analytical materials are not additional ordinary internal preparations. Neither the historical emetic use nor an external concentration authorises modern oral use.[purified copper sulfate, medicinal doses and distinction from analytical copper salts](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

For [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium), the contemporary monograph gives **powdered [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) and [Extractum Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#extractum-opii), 1/4–3 grains or more; [Extractum Opii Liquidum](../../../pharmacology.md#extractum-opii-liquidum), [Tinctura Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#opium-tincture) and [Vinum Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#vinum-opii), 4–40 minims or more; [Tinctura Camphorae cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#tinctura-camphorae-cum-opio), 1–2 fluid drachms**. These are the author's reported ranges, not maxima. His summary table differs, listing 3–30 minims for liquid extract and 1/2–4 grains for powdered [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium); that internal variation should remain visible rather than being converted into an invented single official limit.[Action of the other constituents of opium, Dose; compare Posological table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

The compound oral [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) preparations require their own quantities: **[Pilula Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#pilula-opii), 2 1/2 grains upwards; [Pilula Plumbi cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#pilula-plumbi-cum-opio), 4–8 grains; [Pulvis Cretae Aromaticus cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#pulvis-cretae-aromaticus-cum-opio), 10–60 grains; [Pulvis Ipecacuanhae cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#pulvis-ipecacuanhae-cum-opio), a reference quantity of 5 grains upwards; [Pulvis Kino cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#pulvis-kino-cum-opio), 10 grains upwards; [Trochisci Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#trochisci-opii), one to four lozenges**. In the same order, the pill masses and powders contain approximately one part [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) in 5, 8, 40, 10 and 20; each opium lozenge contains 1/10 grain. “Upwards” in the historical source does not establish a safe upper boundary: the required [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) content, other ingredients and circumstances controlled the quantity. The lead-containing pill has an additional toxic constituent. [Enema Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#enema-opii) supplies about **one grain of [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) in the official two-fluid-ounce enema**; this is a rectal preparation, not an oral measure.[opium preparations; posological table and table of ingredient proportions](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

The external [opium](../../../pharmacology.md#opium) preparations, [Emplastrum Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#emplastrum-opii), [Linimentum Opii](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-opii) and [Unguentum Gallae cum Opio](../../../pharmacology.md#unguentum-gallae-cum-opio), have no ordinary swallowed dose. For completeness the official isolated alkaloid series was [Morphiae Hydrochloras](../../../pharmacology.md#morphine-hydrochloride), [Liquor Morphiae Hydrochloratis](../../../pharmacology.md#liquor-morphiae-hydrochloratis), [Suppositoria Morphiae](../../../pharmacology.md#suppositoria-morphiae), [Trochisci Morphiae](../../../pharmacology.md#trochisci-morphiae) and [Trochisci Morphiae et Ipecacuanhae](../../../pharmacology.md#trochisci-morphiae-et-ipecacuanhae). The reported historical quantities are **[morphine hydrochloride](../../../pharmacology.md#morphine-hydrochloride), 1/8–1 grain; its solution, 20 minims to two fluid drachms in the monograph; one suppository containing 1/4 grain; either kind of morphine lozenge, one to four, each containing 1/36 grain**. The summary table gives a narrower upper quantity of one fluid drachm for the solution. These large historical ranges must not be interpreted as safe for a person without [opioid tolerance](../../../pharmacology.md#opioid-tolerance). The solution contains four grains per fluid ounce. Neither a morphine-acetate preparation added later nor a non-official compound confection should be substituted for this 1864 series.[opium, morphine preparations, doses and ingredient proportions](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

For [belladonna](../../../pharmacology.md#belladonna), **[Extractum Belladonnae](../../../pharmacology.md#extractum-belladonnae), 1/6–1 grain; [Tinctura Belladonnae](../../../pharmacology.md#tinctura-belladonnae), 5–30 minims**. [Emplastrum Belladonnae](../../../pharmacology.md#emplastrum-belladonnae), [Linimentum Belladonnae](../../../pharmacology.md#linimentum-belladonnae) and [Unguentum Belladonnae](../../../pharmacology.md#unguentum-belladonnae) are external preparations, not additional oral doses. The associated [Atropia](../../../pharmacology.md#atropine), [Liquor Atropiae](../../../pharmacology.md#liquor-atropiae) and [Unguentum Atropiae](../../../pharmacology.md#unguentum-atropiae) likewise require their route to be stated: this author specifically discourages routine internal [atropine](../../../pharmacology.md#atropine) administration and describes ophthalmic use of the solution. Its strength is **four grains per fluid ounce**; the ointment contains **eight grains per ounce**. These are historical formulation strengths, not a modern eye-drop or oral prescription. The atropine-sulfate series introduced in 1867 is not the 1864 series.[Solanaceae and continuation; Belladonna and Atropia in the ingredient table](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

For [Strychnia](../../../biology.md#strychnine), [strychnine](../../../biology.md#strychnine), the historical range was **1/32–1/8 grain; [Liquor Strychniae](../../../biology.md#liquor-strychniae), 5–15 minims**, the solution containing four grains per fluid ounce. If the botanical preparations yielding the alkaloid are included, **powdered [nux vomica](../../../pharmacology.md#nux-vomica), 1–3 grains; [Extractum Nucis Vomicae](../../../pharmacology.md#extractum-nucis-vomicae), 1/4–2 grains; [Tinctura Nucis Vomicae](../../../pharmacology.md#tinctura-nucis-vomicae), 10–30 minims**. The American editor already warned that the two-grain extract quantity was very large and that more than half a grain commonly risked adverse effects. Keeping that qualification is more faithful than presenting the printed upper range as routine or safe.[Loganiaceae, solution, doses and editorial footnote](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

For [cinchona bark](../../../pharmacology.md#cinchona-bark), **powdered yellow, pale or red bark, 10–60 grains; [Decoctum Cinchonae Flavae](../../../pharmacology.md#decoctum-cinchonae-flavae) and [Infusum Cinchonae Flavae](../../../pharmacology.md#infusum-cinchonae-flavae), 1–2 fluid ounces; [Extractum Cinchonae Flavae Liquidum](../../../pharmacology.md#extractum-cinchonae-flavae-liquidum), 10–60 minims; [Tinctura Cinchonae Flavae](../../../pharmacology.md#tinctura-cinchonae-flavae) and [Tinctura Cinchonae Composita](../../../pharmacology.md#tinctura-cinchonae-composita), 1–2 fluid drachms**. The associated [Tinctura Quiniae Composita](../../../chemical-substance.md#tinctura-quiniae-composita) was given at **1–2 fluid drachms**. The isolated official [quinine sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine-sulfate), [Quiniae Sulphas](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine-sulfate), was listed at **1–10 grains**, with larger quantities discussed for particular intermittent fevers. [Ferri et Quiniae Citras](../../../pharmacology.md#ferri-et-quiniae-citras), the official iron-and-quinine preparation, was listed at **5–20 grains**. Other cinchona alkaloids discussed as possible remedies do not thereby become official preparations. Strength in bark equivalents also does not guarantee a fixed amount of extracted [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine).[Cinchonaceae Part 4; Action of the different kinds of cinchona barks, Dose; posological table, citrate of iron and quinine](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#the-essentials-of-materia-medica-and-therapeutics-by-alfred-baring-garrod-1865)

These figures answer a historical [materia medica](../../../biology.md#materia-medica) question. Current [drug safety](../../../pharmacology.md#drug-safety) requires the exact modern product, route, indication and patient factors; poisonous nineteenth-century “tonics” and emetics are not revived by accurate transcription of their period doses. [Strychnine](../../../biology.md#strychnine) can cause severe painful convulsions; [opioids](../../../pharmacology.md#opioid) can depress breathing; and inorganic [arsenic](../../../chemical-substance.md#arsenic) is a toxic and carcinogenic exposure. Those facts undermine any reading of the historical numbers as a self-treatment schedule.[clinical effects and emergency treatment](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#strychnine-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention)[respiratory depression and overdose](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#opioid-overdose-by-who)[acute effects, chronic exposure and carcinogenicity](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#arsenic-by-who)
