## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

Enumerate the principal pathological conditions in which the urine is found to be albuminous, and account for the phenomenon in each case.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**An albuminous [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine) specimen can reflect renal leakage, altered renal function, or added [blood plasma](../../../physiology.md#blood-plasma) [proteins](../../../protein.md) below the [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney).** Finding [serum albumin](../../../protein.md#serum-albumin) in [urine](../../../physiology.md#urine) identifies a phenomenon, not a single [disease](../../../biology.md#disease).

In the historical setting, the important group was the renal disorders gathered under [Bright's disease](../../../biology.md#bright-s-disease): acute inflammatory disease, including that following [scarlet fever](../../../biology.md#scarlet-fever), and chronic diseased or contracted [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney). In current terms, [glomerulonephritis](../../../biology.md#glomerulonephritis) damages the [glomerular filtration barrier](../../../anatomy.md#glomerular-filtration-barrier), permitting increased passage of [serum albumin](../../../protein.md#serum-albumin) into the filtrate. [Red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) and [urinary casts](../../../biology.md#urinary-cast) may accompany the loss. Different modern [glomerular diseases](../../../biology.md#glomerular-disease) can produce the same finding; the nineteenth-century umbrella does not identify one modern microscopic diagnosis.[History, the association of dropsy, albuminuria and renal disease](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#bright-s-disease-by-the-university-of-leeds-library)

In [renal amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#renal-amyloidosis), deposition in the [glomeruli](../../../anatomy.md#glomerulus) disrupts their filtering structure; heavy [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) and [nephrotic syndrome](../../../biology.md#nephrotic-syndrome) may result. [Diabetic kidney disease](../../../biology.md#diabetic-kidney-disease) and [hypertensive kidney disease](../../../biology.md#hypertensive-kidney-disease) provide further modern examples of chronic injury to the filtering apparatus. These modern classifications distinguish mechanisms.[Etiology, glomerular causes including amyloidosis, diabetic nephropathy and postinfectious glomerulonephritis](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#proteinuria-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure) group includes renal venous [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion) and impaired effective [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney) [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion). Altered pressures and injury to the filtering apparatus can increase [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria); an albuminous specimen does not by itself prove destructive [nephritis](../../../biology.md#nephritis). [Fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) and severe acute illness can also produce transient [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria) through reversible changes in renal function. In [pre-eclampsia](../../../biology.md#pre-eclampsia), injury to the filtering apparatus, including [glomerular endotheliosis](../../../anatomy.md#glomerular-endotheliosis), can cause [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria).[Abstract, endothelial swelling and disruption of the filtration apparatus](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#the-glomerular-injury-of-preeclampsia) Pregnancy alone does not make [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) normal, and pressure from a gravid [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus) is not an adequate general explanation of this disorder.[Etiology, preeclampsia; Pathophysiology, functional and tubular proteinuria](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#proteinuria-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Finally distinguish renal loss from addition below the filtering apparatus. [Haematuria](../../../biology.md#haematuria), [pyelonephritis](../../../anatomy.md#pyelonephritis), [cystitis](../../../anatomy.md#cystitis) and contamination by inflammatory or genital material can add [blood plasma](../../../physiology.md#blood-plasma) [proteins](../../../protein.md), including [serum albumin](../../../protein.md#serum-albumin), to a collected specimen. [Microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) and a clean sample help distinguish these possibilities. [Tubulointerstitial nephritis](../../../biology.md#tubulointerstitial-nephritis) reduces [proximal tubule](../../../anatomy.md#proximal-tubule) recovery of filtered [proteins](../../../protein.md), but predominantly causes loss of smaller [proteins](../../../protein.md). Likewise, overflow of [immunoglobulin light chains](../../../protein.md#immunoglobulin-light-chain) in [multiple myeloma](../../../biology.md#multiple-myeloma) is [proteinuria](../../../biology.md#proteinuria), not necessarily [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria). [Haematuria](../../../biology.md#haematuria) alone can contribute [blood plasma](../../../physiology.md#blood-plasma) [proteins](../../../protein.md), so modest protein positivity need not locate the bleeding in the [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney).[Clinical Significance, serum proteins accompanying haematuria](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#hematuria-in-clinical-methods) The distinction matters because an old protein-precipitation test did not identify the molecular species.[Definition and Technique, heat and acid precipitation; Basic Science, glomerular, tubular and overflow proteins; Clinical Significance, urinary sediment](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#proteinuria-in-clinical-methods)

A useful diagnostic comparison is repeated [urinalysis](../../../biology.md#urinalysis), with [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) of the sediment and a modern [urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio](../../../biology.md#urine-albumin-to-creatinine-ratio). Persistence, [red blood cell casts](../../../biology.md#red-blood-cell-cast), and the clinical setting distinguish sustained [glomerular disease](../../../biology.md#glomerular-disease) from a transient change or lower-tract addition. A positive protein test alone cannot make that distinction.[How is albuminuria detected?, urine albumin and creatinine measurement](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#albuminuria-albumin-in-the-urine-by-niddk)

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the chief morbid changes found in epileptics after death ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**There is no single constant anatomical lesion of [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy).** A sound [autopsy](../../../biology.md#autopsy) answer separates an underlying [brain lesion](../../../neuroscience.md#brain-lesion), changes associated with longstanding [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy), and the consequences of the final [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure) or death.

The potentially causal group includes healed traumatic scars, old [cerebral infarctions](../../../neurology.md#cerebral-infarction) or [intracranial haemorrhages](../../../biology.md#intracranial-haemorrhage), developmental abnormalities of the [cerebral cortex](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-cortex), [brain tumours](../../../biology.md#brain-tumour), and inflammatory or infectious destruction of [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology). Such abnormalities can leave an enduring network capable of generating recurrent [seizures](../../../neurology.md#seizure). Their importance depends on location and the lifetime history; an incidental abnormality is not automatically the cause. A modern acute symptomatic [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure) during a transient metabolic disturbance should also not automatically be called [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy).[Etiology of Seizure Disorders, structural, infectious and metabolic causes](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#seizure-disorders-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

In longstanding cases one may find [atrophy](../../../biology.md#atrophy) or scarring of particular [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) regions. [Hippocampal sclerosis](../../../neurology.md#hippocampal-sclerosis) is an important modern example: loss of [neurons](../../../neuron.md) with [gliosis](../../../biology.md#gliosis) in the [hippocampus](../../../neuroscience.md#hippocampus), associated especially with temporal-lobe [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy). Association does not settle whether a particular lesion preceded the first [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure), resulted from repeated [seizures](../../../neurology.md#seizure), or shares another cause with them. Conversely, genetically determined disturbances of [neuronal excitability](../../../neuron.md#neuronal-excitability) can produce recurrent [seizures](../../../neurology.md#seizure) without a grossly visible destructive lesion.[Causes of epilepsy; Types of epilepsy, temporal lobe epilepsy and hippocampal scarring](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#epilepsy-and-seizures-by-the-national-institute-of-neurological-disorders-and-stroke)

For the historical question, vascular redness or [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion) and alterations around the [medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience.md#medulla-oblongata) deserve mention as contemporary observations rather than established universal causes. A January 1864 report describes microscopic examination of fourteen longstanding cases, including dilated [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) and altered nervous material in that region. Its proposed localization of [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy) to the [medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience.md#medulla-oblongata) is a historical theory; modern [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy) is not defined by that site or by those vascular appearances.[Opening discussion of fourteen cases and the medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#report-on-epilepsy-in-the-buffalo-medical-and-surgical-journal-january-1864)

The terminal group includes [cerebral oedema](../../../biology.md#cerebral-oedema), [hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury](../../../neuroscience.md#hypoxic-ischaemic-brain-injury), and [pulmonary oedema](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-oedema) or [aspiration](../../../physiology.md#aspiration) in some deaths. [Congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion) is nonspecific and may be terminal; injuries can result from a fall during a [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure). None of these alone establishes that [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy) was the original cause. A modern [autopsy](../../../biology.md#autopsy) study of sudden deaths in people with [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy) found both structural abnormalities and nonspecific changes; exclusion of another anatomical or toxicological cause remains central to classifying [sudden unexpected death in epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#sudden-unexpected-death-in-epilepsy).[Results, neuropathological and pulmonary findings; Discussion, exclusion of other causes](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#sudden-unexpected-death-in-epilepsy-a-retrospective-autopsy-study-of-112-epileptic-patients)

The appropriate investigation is therefore inspection followed by systematic [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology), especially of the [cerebral cortex](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-cortex) and [hippocampus](../../../neuroscience.md#hippocampus) on both sides, interpreted alongside the recorded [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure) history and examination of other organs. **A normal-looking [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) does not exclude [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy), and a congested one does not prove it.**

**Original question.**

What is meant by ‘Amyloid disease,’ and in which of the viscera is it found ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**Historical “amyloid disease” denotes the waxy or lardaceous visceral change, especially in the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney); modern [amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#amyloidosis) denotes deposition of misfolded [protein](../../../protein.md) fibrils.** The old descriptive appearance and the modern chemical explanation must be kept distinct.

In nineteenth-century usage the affected organs could be enlarged, firm and unusually translucent or wax-like on section. The name [amyloid](../../../protein.md#amyloid) means starch-like. The historical chemical argument relied on colour reactions with [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) and [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid), suggesting affinity with [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch) or [cellulose](../../../chemical-substance.md#cellulose). Such a reaction was a clue to recognition, not a modern identification of the deposited substance. [Rudolf Virchow](../../../physician.md#rudolf-virchow)'s 1856 account explicitly discusses the waxy change of the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney), and its occurrence with longstanding destructive disease.[1856 English abstract, pp. 380–383, opening account of visceral sites and chronic disease](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#on-the-course-of-the-amyloid-degeneration-by-rudolf-virchow)

The three principal visceral examples can be described more precisely:

- In the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), deposits in the [splenic white pulp](../../../physiology.md#splenic-white-pulp) may produce translucent granules, the so-called [sago spleen](../../../biology.md#sago-spleen). More diffuse involvement gives a widespread lardaceous appearance.
- In the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver), deposits along the [hepatic sinusoids](../../../physiology.md#hepatic-sinusoid) can compress adjacent [hepatocytes](../../../physiology.md#hepatocyte); extensive deposition may produce a large firm [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver).[Abstract, autopsy examination of 286 livers; sinusoidal and portal patterns](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#hepatic-amyloidosis-morphologic-differences-between-systemic-al-and-aa-types)
- In the [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney), deposits in [glomeruli](../../../anatomy.md#glomerulus), [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) and sometimes the interstitium interfere with function. Involvement of the [glomeruli](../../../anatomy.md#glomerulus) explains the [albuminuria](../../../biology.md#albuminuria) of Question 1; it may progress to [nephrotic syndrome](../../../biology.md#nephrotic-syndrome) and [kidney failure](../../../biology.md#kidney-failure).

The distribution is not confined to those three organs. [Amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#amyloidosis) can also affect the [gastrointestinal tract](../../../physiology.md#gastrointestinal-tract), [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) and other sites; the pattern depends on the precursor [protein](../../../protein.md). Modern examples include [AA amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#aa-amyloidosis) associated with sustained [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation), [AL amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#al-amyloidosis) involving [immunoglobulin light chains](../../../protein.md#immunoglobulin-light-chain), and [transthyretin amyloidosis](../../../biology.md#transthyretin-amyloidosis). This classification is a present-day explanation, not a list of molecular diagnoses asserted for unnamed 1867 patients.[Introduction; Etiology, systemic types and common sites of deposition](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#amyloidosis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Modern confirmation uses [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology) with [Congo red](../../../biology.md#congo-red) and examination with a [polarizing microscope](../../../optical-instrument.md#polarizing-microscope), followed by identification of the deposited [protein](../../../protein.md), for example with [mass spectrometry](../../../chemistry.md#mass-spectrometry). The characteristic birefringence supports identification of [amyloid](../../../protein.md#amyloid); typing distinguishes biologically different diseases. These are later laboratory methods, not apparatus available to the candidate in 1867.[§82.2 Diagnosis, Congo red, polarized light and amyloid subtyping](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#systemic-light-chain-amyloidosis-in-the-ebmt-handbook)

Thus an adequate historical list starts with **[spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen), [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney)**, while the modern explanation is extracellular [protein](../../../protein.md) deposition rather than conversion of an organ into [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch).

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the causes of Vomiting, and in what way do they produce that phenomenon ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**[Vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting) is a coordinated nervous motor act that can be initiated from the [digestive system](../../../physiology.md#digestive-system), circulating emetic substances, the [vestibular system](../../../neuroscience.md#vestibular-system), or higher and lower [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) pathways.** A cause need not lie in the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach).

Local causes include irritant food or poisons, [gastritis](../../../physiology.md#gastritis), [gastroenteritis](../../../biology.md#gastroenteritis), [gastric outlet obstruction](../../../physiology.md#gastric-outlet-obstruction) and [intestinal obstruction](../../../physiology.md#intestinal-obstruction). Chemical irritation, [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) or distension activates visceral sensory pathways, notably afferents of the [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve), which relay to the [medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience.md#medulla-oblongata). In obstruction, retention and distension provide continuing stimulation; merely seeing food return does not imply excessive gastric contractile strength. [Peritonitis](../../../biology.md#peritonitis) and painful disease elsewhere in the abdomen can also recruit visceral afferent pathways.[Etiology; table Some Causes of Nausea and Vomiting, gastrointestinal disorders](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#nausea-and-vomiting-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Circulating emetic agents, including some drugs and metabolic products accumulating in [kidney failure](../../../biology.md#kidney-failure), act partly through the [area postrema](../../../neuroscience.md#area-postrema), a chemosensitive region with unusually direct access to substances in the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). Gastrointestinal and blood-borne inputs converge through the [nucleus tractus solitarius](../../../neuroscience.md#nucleus-tractus-solitarius) and connected medullary networks. It is more accurate to describe interacting groups of [neurons](../../../neuron.md) than one isolated “vomiting centre”.[Abstract, area postrema, abdominal vagal afferents, nucleus tractus solitarius and distributed pattern generation](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#central-neurocircuitry-associated-with-emesis)

Other important groups are [motion sickness](../../../neuroscience.md#motion-sickness), where information from the [vestibular system](../../../neuroscience.md#vestibular-system) conflicts with other sensory information; [migraine](../../../neurology.md#migraine) and intracranial disease, including [raised intracranial pressure](../../../biology.md#raised-intracranial-pressure) from a [brain tumour](../../../biology.md#brain-tumour) or [intracranial haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#intracranial-haemorrhage); and higher-centre influences such as a repulsive sight, smell, fear or learned association. [Pregnancy-associated vomiting](../../../physiology.md#pregnancy-associated-vomiting) belongs in the differential, with endocrine and central contributions rather than an explanation that it is simply mechanical pressure by the [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus) upon the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach). These causes engage different inputs to an overlapping final motor programme.[Etiology; table Some Causes of Nausea and Vomiting, CNS and systemic causes](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#nausea-and-vomiting-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The act itself requires several coordinated changes. The proximal [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach) and [lower oesophageal sphincter](../../../physiology.md#lower-oesophageal-sphincter) relax; retrograde activity can bring contents of the upper [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine) into the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach); the [diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#diaphragm) and abdominal [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) generate a pressure gradient that expels the contents. During expulsion the upper passage opens while coordinated laryngeal closure helps protect the [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway). [Retching](../../../physiology.md#retching) and actual expulsion are related stages, and neither should be confused with effortless [regurgitation](../../../physiology.md#regurgitation).[Digestive tract correlates and neurophysiology; relaxation, retrograde giant contraction, retching and expulsion](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#physiology-of-the-digestive-tract-correlates-of-vomiting)

This mechanism explains the diversity of causes: **many sensory and chemical inputs can recruit the same coordinated output**. A pressure recording and observation of gastrointestinal movements during an emetic response can distinguish the patterned act from a simple spontaneous return of contents; they would not, without further evidence, identify the original cause in an individual.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the modes and causes of sudden Death ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**The chief physiological routes are abrupt failure of [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation), failure of [pulmonary ventilation](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) or [gas exchange](../../../physiology.md#gas-exchange), or catastrophic failure of the central control of those functions.** Historical clinical accounts used labels such as [syncope](../../../biology.md#syncope), [asphyxia](../../../biology.md#asphyxia) and [medical coma](../../../neurology.md#coma-medicine) in explaining deaths; these terms describe broad physiological failures rather than identifying the underlying [diseases](../../../biology.md#disease).[February 25, p. 142; March 10, pp. 181–183, contrasting cardiac failure, obstructed circulation and respiratory failure](../../../biology-bibliography.md#hospital-reports-and-lectures-on-modes-of-death-in-the-british-medical-journal-1860) A brief faint or ordinary [medical coma](../../../neurology.md#coma-medicine) is not itself necessarily fatal.

In primary circulatory failure, effective delivery of [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) to the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) stops. Modern examples include [ventricular fibrillation](../../../biology.md#ventricular-fibrillation), pulseless [ventricular tachycardia](../../../biology.md#ventricular-tachycardia), severe [bradyarrhythmia](../../../biology.md#bradyarrhythmia) and [asystole](../../../biology.md#asystole). They may arise in [coronary artery disease](../../../biology.md#coronary-artery-disease), after [myocardial infarction](../../../biology.md#myocardial-infarction), in [cardiomyopathy](../../../biology.md#cardiomyopathy), or from inherited [cardiac ion channel disorders](../../../biology.md#cardiac-ion-channel-disorder). An electrical cause may leave little distinctive gross evidence at [autopsy](../../../biology.md#autopsy). These modern mechanisms refine the broad historical description of cardiac [syncope](../../../biology.md#syncope).[Dangerous heart rhythms, scarred or thickened myocardium, and inherited electrical abnormalities](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#what-causes-cardiac-arrest-by-the-american-heart-association)

Mechanical interruption of [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation) gives another group: massive [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), rupture of an [aortic aneurysm](../../../physiology.md#aortic-aneurysm), [cardiac tamponade](../../../biology.md#cardiac-tamponade), and large [pulmonary embolism](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-embolism). The immediate deficit differs in each. [Haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage) removes circulating volume; [cardiac tamponade](../../../biology.md#cardiac-tamponade) prevents adequate filling; [pulmonary embolism](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-embolism) obstructs flow through the [pulmonary circulation](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-circulation). All can end with critically low [cardiac output](../../../physiology.md#cardiac-output) even if some electrical activity persists.[Etiology, noncardiac shock including pulmonary embolism and hemorrhage; Pathophysiology and reversible causes](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#cardiac-arrest-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

Respiratory failure includes obstruction of the [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway) by a foreign body, [laryngeal oedema](../../../biology.md#laryngeal-oedema) or [aspiration](../../../physiology.md#aspiration); drowning; and depression of [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) by drugs or major [brainstem](../../../neuroscience.md#brainstem) injury. [Oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) delivery then fails and secondary [cardiac arrest](../../../biology.md#cardiac-arrest) follows. [Carbon monoxide poisoning](../../../biology.md#carbon-monoxide-poisoning) and [cyanide poisoning](../../../biology.md#cyanide-poisoning) illustrate related failure of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) transport or use: not every hypoxic death is caused by physical obstruction of an [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway).[Overview, impaired oxygen delivery and cellular oxygen utilization](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#clinical-guidance-for-carbon-monoxide-poisoning-by-cdc)[Background, effects on the body's ability to use oxygen](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#cyanide-chemical-fact-sheet-by-cdc)[Other medical conditions and events, respiratory arrest, poisoning and drug overdose](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#cardiac-arrest-causes-and-risk-factors-by-the-national-heart-lung-and-blood-institute)

A major [intracranial haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#intracranial-haemorrhage) or rapidly developing [brain herniation](../../../neuroscience.md#brain-herniation) can destroy or compress the pathways maintaining [consciousness](../../../psychology.md#consciousness) and [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). A severe disturbance of [electrolytes](../../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte), especially [potassium](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium), may instead act directly on the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart)'s electrical function. A fatal [seizure](../../../neurology.md#seizure) can involve respiratory and circulatory mechanisms; [sudden unexpected death in epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#sudden-unexpected-death-in-epilepsy) remains a diagnosis requiring exclusion of other causes, not merely recognition of a history of [epilepsy](../../../neurology.md#epilepsy).[Heart problems; Other medical conditions and events, electrolytes and seizure disorders](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#cardiac-arrest-causes-and-risk-factors-by-the-national-heart-lung-and-blood-institute)

The practical distinction is between **mode, immediate mechanism and underlying cause**. For example, rupture of an [aneurysm](../../../physiology.md#aneurysm) can cause [haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#haemorrhage), then circulatory failure, then irreversible [hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury](../../../neuroscience.md#hypoxic-ischaemic-brain-injury). A complete explanation identifies that chain. Inspection, [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology), examination of the [airway](../../../physiology.md#airway) and [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), and appropriately interpreted [toxicology](../../../biology.md#toxicology) may establish it; an unexplained sudden death cannot be assigned a precise cause from its suddenness alone.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

By what process are new Blood Vessels formed in a Wounded Part.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**New [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) in a healing wound arise chiefly by [angiogenesis](../../../developmental-biology.md#angiogenesis): sprouts grow from surviving [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel), unite into patent channels, and mature within the [granulation tissue](../../../physiology.md#granulation-tissue).** [Blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) does not simply excavate permanent channels in a shapeless clot.

The initial wound contains a provisional [extracellular matrix](../../../anatomy.md#extracellular-matrix), including [fibrin](../../../protein.md#fibrin), with inflammatory [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) and later [fibroblasts](../../../biological-cell.md#fibroblast). Local [hypoxia](../../../biology.md#hypoxia) and signals from injured [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) and inflammatory [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) stimulate surviving [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium). In modern molecular terms, [vascular endothelial growth factor](../../../developmental-biology.md#vascular-endothelial-growth-factor) is an important stimulus. This molecular account explains the process today; it is not evidence that the examiner expected the named signalling molecule in 1867.[Chapter 22, Blood Vessels and Endothelial Cells, Angiogenesis Is Controlled by Factors Released by the Surrounding Tissues; Figure 22-27](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others)

The sequence is as follows:

- Portions of the old vascular [basement membrane](../../../anatomy.md#basement-membrane) and surrounding [extracellular matrix](../../../anatomy.md#extracellular-matrix) are remodelled. Activated [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) extend and migrate towards the wound.
- [Endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) [cell proliferation](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-proliferation) and [cell migration](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-migration) lengthen the sprouts. Their organization establishes a lumen rather than a solid cord remaining indefinitely.
- Sprouts meet one another or an existing [blood vessel](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel), forming an [anastomosis](../../../physiology.md#anastomosis). A connected route with inflow and outflow can then carry [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). A blind sprout is not yet a useful circulating loop.
- Junctions between [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md), a renewed [basement membrane](../../../anatomy.md#basement-membrane), and supporting [pericytes](../../../biological-cell.md#pericyte) stabilize the small [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel). Larger channels acquire further mural support as appropriate.
- During remodelling, many surplus [capillaries](../../../physiology.md#capillary) regress. The vascular, red [granulation tissue](../../../physiology.md#granulation-tissue) becomes a less vascular mature scar.

This sequence separates growth of the cellular lining, formation of the channel, establishment of flow, and stabilization. These are different experimental observations, even though they overlap in time.[Chapter 6, initiation by endothelial activation, matrix degradation, migration, proliferation and maturation](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#angiogenesis-in-inflammation-and-the-microcirculation)

An instructive anatomical investigation follows the wound at successive stages. Thin sections show solid sprouts, lumina lined by [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) and their relation to the old [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel). Injection of a suitable casting material into the supplying [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel), followed by examination of the cast, reveals which channels are continuous with the circulation. The corneal wound example in the modern textbook uses resin injection and [scanning electron microscopy](../../../optics.md#scanning-electron-microscope) of the resulting casts. Seeing an cord of [endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#endothelium) and demonstrating a perfused loop therefore answer different questions.[Chapter 22, Blood Vessels and Endothelial Cells, Figure 22-27 caption, vascular casts after corneal wounding](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others)

**The essential repair process is sprouting, lumen formation, connection and maturation.** The contrast with embryonic [vasculogenesis](../../../developmental-biology.md#vasculogenesis), assembly from vascular precursors, helps define [angiogenesis](../../../developmental-biology.md#angiogenesis); it does not require assuming that every new [blood vessel](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) in an adult wound has an identical cellular history.

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What minute structural changes are classed under the term Hypertrophy? Give illustrations of Hypertrophy in various organs in early and in later stages.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**Modern [hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#hypertrophy) means enlargement of individual [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md); [hyperplasia](../../../biology.md#hyperplasia) means an increase in their number.** Both can enlarge an organ, and both can occur together. The historical question requires care because nineteenth-century authors sometimes included “numerical hypertrophy” within the broader discussion of growth. [Rudolf Virchow](../../../physician.md#rudolf-virchow)'s 1860 English edition explicitly distinguishes enlargement of existing elements from multiplication of elements, using simple and numerical [hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#hypertrophy) for these different processes.[Lecture III, pp. 93–95, Hypertrophy and Hyperplasy and Degenerative New Formations](../../../biology-bibliography.md#cellular-pathology-by-rudolf-virchow-1860)

At the minute structural level, true [hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#hypertrophy) entails increased cellular substance and, in a contractile [biological cell](../../../biological-cell.md), more contractile apparatus. Enlarged [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) can accompany it, but a large [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) alone does not prove a proportional increase in functional [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology). Increased cell number belongs to [hyperplasia](../../../biology.md#hyperplasia). Apparent organ enlargement from [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema), fatty infiltration, [amyloid](../../../protein.md#amyloid) or a [tumour](../../../biology.md#tumour) must be distinguished from a coordinated increase of the organ's working [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology).[Hypertrophy and Hyperplasia; morphological distinction and increased contractile protein in pressure-loaded myocardium](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#cellular-pathology-in-pathology-the-big-picture)

The clearest early-to-late examples compare adaptation with the possible effects of continued overload:

- In the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), sustained [hypertension](../../../biology.md#hypertension) or [aortic stenosis](../../../biology.md#aortic-stenosis) makes the [left ventricle](../../../physiology.md#left-ventricle) work against greater resistance. Initially its [cardiomyocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#cardiomyocyte) enlarge and the wall thickens, with enlarged [cell nuclei](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) and increased contractile [proteins](../../../protein.md). With prolonged pathological stress there may be [fibrosis](../../../anatomy.md#fibrosis), loss of [cardiomyocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#cardiomyocyte), ventricular dilatation and [heart failure](../../../biology.md#heart-failure). The late enlarged failing [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) need not have a uniformly thick wall; [hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#hypertrophy) and dilatation describe different structural changes.[Cardiac hypertrophy, physiological adaptation versus pathological hypertrophy and progression to heart failure](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#stress-kinase-signaling-in-cardiac-myocytes)
- In the [urinary bladder](../../../anatomy.md#urinary-bladder), persistent outflow obstruction increases the work of the [detrusor muscle](../../../anatomy.md#detrusor-muscle). Enlargement of [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) bundles thickens the wall and can produce [bladder trabeculation](../../../anatomy.md#bladder-trabeculation). With prolonged obstruction, [fibrosis](../../../anatomy.md#fibrosis), dilatation and incomplete emptying can coexist with the initial growth. This is a progression possible under sustained pathological load. Human studies describe early [hypertrophy](../../../biology.md#hypertrophy) and subsequent compensation or decompensation; progression is not an inevitable fate of all enlarged [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle).[Results; Discussion, proposed three-stage model and its evidential limits](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#progressive-bladder-remodeling-due-to-bladder-outlet-obstruction)
- In [skeletal muscle](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle), increased use enlarges existing fibres through additional contractile material. Later development of this physiological response produces larger, stronger fibres; it does not inherently require fatty degeneration. If an apparent muscular enlargement is mainly fat or [connective tissue](../../../anatomy.md#connective-tissue), that is [pseudohypertrophy](../../../biology.md#pseudohypertrophy).

These illustrations compare different outcomes rather than proposing a universal stage in which every enlarged organ degenerates.[Adaptive responses to increased demand; physiological hypertrophy and hyperplasia](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#hypertrophy-and-hyperplasia-in-underwood-s-pathology-a-clinical-approach)[Hypertrophy, physiological and pathological examples](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#cellular-pathology-in-pathology-the-big-picture)

A second set illustrates growth in organs whose [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) can also divide. The pregnant [uterus](../../../anatomy.md#uterus) combines marked enlargement of [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) [biological cells](../../../biological-cell.md) with an increase in their number; after delivery it undergoes [involution](../../../group-theory.md#involution). An enlarged remaining [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney) after loss of its partner exemplifies compensatory growth. In a modern rat experiment comparing neonatal, weanling and adult animals after removal of one [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney), the younger groups showed greater compensatory growth; that experimental comparison does not make an age-independent prediction for every human [kidney](../../../anatomy.md#kidney).[Abstract, comparison of neonatal, weanling and young adult rats](../../../reproductive-and-urinary-medicine-bibliography.md#age-dependent-characteristics-of-compensatory-renal-growth) Thus **organ size alone cannot distinguish the cellular processes**: compare cell dimensions, numbers and [tissue](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) composition by [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology).[Physiological hypertrophy and hyperplasia, uterine growth; compensatory growth](../../../cell-biology-bibliography.md#hypertrophy-and-hyperplasia-in-underwood-s-pathology-a-clinical-approach)

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What is understood by ‘Atelectasis pulmonum,’ and with what other affections might it be confounded ?

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**“Atelectasis pulmonum” means incomplete expansion or collapse of [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) air spaces; in the newborn it classically describes portions that have not expanded adequately after birth.** Modern [atelectasis](../../../biology.md#atelectasis) also includes collapse of previously aerated [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung). The older distinction between primary non-expansion and secondary collapse is useful when reading this paper.

The affected [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) is airless, often dark and firm, with reduced volume, a depressed surface and little or no crepitation. Failure of inspiratory effort, obstruction of a [bronchus](../../../anatomy.md#bronchus) by secretions, and external compression are different routes to this appearance. Modern physiology adds the role of [pulmonary surfactant](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-surfactant) in opposing alveolar collapse; deficiency in premature infants belongs to [neonatal respiratory distress syndrome](../../../biology.md#neonatal-respiratory-distress-syndrome), not an explanation the 1867 question itself supplies.[Definition; factors maintaining alveolar expansion; Etiology](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#atelectasis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The main historical confusion was with [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia). A pre-1867 review of [Charles West](../../../physician.md#charles-west)'s work distinguishes primary non-expansion from secondary collapse, and contrasts both with pneumonic [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization). A collapsed region loses air and volume; inflammatory [pulmonary consolidation](../../../anatomy.md#pulmonary-consolidation) fills air spaces with [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate). Both can look solid.[pp. 109–112, primary atelectasis, secondary collapse and confusion with lobular pneumonia](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#lectures-on-the-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-review-1855)

The classical experiment introduces air through the [trachea](../../../anatomy.md#trachea) or an appropriate [bronchus](../../../anatomy.md#bronchus) while the affected region is observed. Uncomplicated collapsed lobules can expand and regain buoyancy; inflation does not simply remove inflammatory [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate). The historical review describes this distinction.[pp. 109–111, artificial inflation and buoyancy](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#lectures-on-the-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-review-1855) The experiment's interpretation requires more than one observation: whether a region takes air, whether its volume changes, and whether its contents show [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) are different measurements. Cut-surface inspection and [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology) test the last point directly. Floating or sinking alone cannot distinguish all mixtures of collapse, fluid and [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia), and cannot reliably establish whether an infant was born alive.

Other potential confusions include [pulmonary oedema](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-oedema), [pulmonary haemorrhage](../../../biology.md#pulmonary-haemorrhage), and compressed [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) beside a [pleural effusion](../../../biology.md#pleural-effusion) or [pneumothorax](../../../biology.md#pneumothorax). Fluid or [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) in the air spaces differs from collapse alone, although the conditions can coexist; external compression can actually cause [atelectasis](../../../biology.md#atelectasis). [Pulmonary hypoplasia](../../../developmental-biology.md#pulmonary-hypoplasia) is different again: an underdeveloped organ is not merely an otherwise normally formed organ awaiting inflation.[Etiology; Differential Diagnosis](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#atelectasis-in-statpearls)

**Reduced aeration is the observation; non-expansion, obstruction, compression and inflammatory consolidation are competing or coexisting explanations.** Gross inspection, controlled inflation and [histology](../../../anatomy.md#histology) help discriminate them.

## 9

↑ **Parent:** [Pathology](pathology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the peculiarities of Pneumonia in the infant as distinguished from the same disease in the adult ?

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**In infants, [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia) often has a patchy airway-centred pattern and less typical localizing symptoms, while collapse and systemic illness complicate its recognition.** The classical comparison is with an adult's acute [lobar pneumonia](../../../biology.md#lobar-pneumonia), but age does not impose an absolute anatomical division.

The adult reference pattern is relatively continuous consolidation of a lobe. In the traditional pathological sequence, [congestion](../../../biology.md#congestion) is followed by red [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), with [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell), [neutrophils](../../../biological-cell.md#neutrophil) and [fibrin](../../../protein.md#fibrin) in the [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus); later breakdown of the [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) contributes to grey [hepatization](../../../biology.md#hepatization), followed by clearance or organization. These stages are a descriptive model, can overlap, and are not a mandatory timetable for every adult infection.[Histopathology, lobar pneumonia and the four classical stages](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pneumonia-pathology-in-statpearls)

By comparison, an infant may have [bronchopneumonia](../../../biology.md#bronchopneumonia): separate foci centred on small [bronchi](../../../anatomy.md#bronchus) and [bronchioles](../../../anatomy.md#bronchiole), extending into adjacent [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus), sometimes in both [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung). Airway secretions and inflammatory material coexist with patches of [atelectasis](../../../biology.md#atelectasis); lesions of different ages can be present together. Coalescent foci can eventually resemble a larger area of consolidation. The distinction is anatomical rather than proof of one specific infecting organism.[Bronchopneumonia, Pathologic Features, airway-centred exudation and coalescence](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pulmonary-infections-in-the-normal-host)

Question 8 supplies an essential historical qualification: some lesions labelled infantile “lobular pneumonia” were actually collapse. Distinguishing [atelectasis](../../../biology.md#atelectasis) from inflammatory [exudate](../../../biology.md#exudate) prevents that diagnostic inflation. The two conditions can nevertheless coexist.[pp. 111–112, secondary collapse formerly described as lobular pneumonia](../../../clinical-medicine-bibliography.md#lectures-on-the-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-review-1855)

Clinically, an infant cannot report pleuritic chest pain and usually swallows rather than expectorates sputum. The adult combination of rigors, chest pain and conspicuous rusty sputum is therefore a poor required checklist. More useful infant signs include rapid [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation), lower chest indrawing, nasal flaring, grunting, poor feeding, reduced activity and sometimes [cyanosis](../../../biology.md#cyanosis). [Fever](../../../physiology.md#fever) can be present, but the youngest infants may instead show temperature instability and nonspecific illness. Physical signs can be scattered when the lesions are scattered; absence of one large area of dullness does not exclude [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia).[Infant symptoms and signs of breathing difficulty](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#pneumonia-symptoms-by-the-national-heart-lung-and-blood-institute)[Symptoms and Signs, respiratory distress and temperature instability](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#neonatal-pneumonia-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

In current practice, the age group also changes the differential. Early neonatal [pneumonia](../../../biology.md#pneumonia) can be part of generalized [sepsis](../../../biology.md#sepsis) acquired around birth; later infant infections include both viral and bacterial causes. Assessment integrates the illness history, [oxygen saturation](../../../physiology.md#oxygen-saturation), examination and selected laboratory or imaging evidence rather than assigning a diagnosis from age alone. A positive airway culture can reflect colonization, so the organism must be interpreted with the clinical and inflammatory evidence.[Etiology; Diagnosis, blood cultures, tracheal aspirates and colonization](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#neonatal-pneumonia-by-merck-manual-professional-edition)

The decisive comparison is therefore **patchy and mixed pathology, greater diagnostic difficulty, and frequent systemic presentation in infancy**, with [lobar pneumonia](../../../biology.md#lobar-pneumonia) possible in children and [bronchopneumonia](../../../biology.md#bronchopneumonia) possible in adults.
