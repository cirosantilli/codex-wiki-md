**Modern [hypertrophy](../../../../../hypertrophy.md) means enlargement of individual [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md); [hyperplasia](../../../../../hyperplasia.md) means an increase in their number.** Both can enlarge an organ, and both can occur together. The historical question requires care because nineteenth-century authors sometimes included “numerical hypertrophy” within the broader discussion of growth. [Rudolf Virchow](../../../../../rudolf-virchow.md)'s 1860 English edition explicitly distinguishes enlargement of existing elements from multiplication of elements, using simple and numerical [hypertrophy](../../../../../hypertrophy.md) for these different processes.[Lecture III, pp. 93–95, Hypertrophy and Hyperplasy and Degenerative New Formations](../../../../../cellular-pathology-by-rudolf-virchow-1860.md)

At the minute structural level, true [hypertrophy](../../../../../hypertrophy.md) entails increased cellular substance and, in a contractile [biological cell](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md), more contractile apparatus. Enlarged [cell nuclei](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md) can accompany it, but a large [cell nucleus](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md) alone does not prove a proportional increase in functional [tissue](../../../../../tissue-biology.md). Increased cell number belongs to [hyperplasia](../../../../../hyperplasia.md). Apparent organ enlargement from [oedema](../../../../../oedema.md), fatty infiltration, [amyloid](../../../../../amyloid.md) or a [tumour](../../../../../tumour.md) must be distinguished from a coordinated increase of the organ's working [tissue](../../../../../tissue-biology.md).[Hypertrophy and Hyperplasia; morphological distinction and increased contractile protein in pressure-loaded myocardium](../../../../../cellular-pathology-in-pathology-the-big-picture.md)

The clearest early-to-late examples compare adaptation with the possible effects of continued overload:

- In the [heart](../../../../../heart.md), sustained [hypertension](../../../../../hypertension.md) or [aortic stenosis](../../../../../aortic-stenosis.md) makes the [left ventricle](../../../../../left-ventricle.md) work against greater resistance. Initially its [cardiomyocytes](../../../../../cardiomyocyte.md) enlarge and the wall thickens, with enlarged [cell nuclei](../../../../../cell-nucleus.md) and increased contractile [proteins](../../../../../protein-split.md). With prolonged pathological stress there may be [fibrosis](../../../../../fibrosis.md), loss of [cardiomyocytes](../../../../../cardiomyocyte.md), ventricular dilatation and [heart failure](../../../../../heart-failure.md). The late enlarged failing [heart](../../../../../heart.md) need not have a uniformly thick wall; [hypertrophy](../../../../../hypertrophy.md) and dilatation describe different structural changes.[Cardiac hypertrophy, physiological adaptation versus pathological hypertrophy and progression to heart failure](../../../../../stress-kinase-signaling-in-cardiac-myocytes.md)
- In the [urinary bladder](../../../../../urinary-bladder.md), persistent outflow obstruction increases the work of the [detrusor muscle](../../../../../detrusor-muscle.md). Enlargement of [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md) bundles thickens the wall and can produce [bladder trabeculation](../../../../../bladder-trabeculation.md). With prolonged obstruction, [fibrosis](../../../../../fibrosis.md), dilatation and incomplete emptying can coexist with the initial growth. This is a progression possible under sustained pathological load. Human studies describe early [hypertrophy](../../../../../hypertrophy.md) and subsequent compensation or decompensation; progression is not an inevitable fate of all enlarged [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md).[Results; Discussion, proposed three-stage model and its evidential limits](../../../../../progressive-bladder-remodeling-due-to-bladder-outlet-obstruction.md)
- In [skeletal muscle](../../../../../skeletal-muscle.md), increased use enlarges existing fibres through additional contractile material. Later development of this physiological response produces larger, stronger fibres; it does not inherently require fatty degeneration. If an apparent muscular enlargement is mainly fat or [connective tissue](../../../../../connective-tissue.md), that is [pseudohypertrophy](../../../../../pseudohypertrophy.md).

These illustrations compare different outcomes rather than proposing a universal stage in which every enlarged organ degenerates.[Adaptive responses to increased demand; physiological hypertrophy and hyperplasia](../../../../../hypertrophy-and-hyperplasia-in-underwood-s-pathology-a-clinical-approach.md)[Hypertrophy, physiological and pathological examples](../../../../../cellular-pathology-in-pathology-the-big-picture.md)

A second set illustrates growth in organs whose [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) can also divide. The pregnant [uterus](../../../../../uterus.md) combines marked enlargement of [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md) [biological cells](../../../../../biological-cell-split.md) with an increase in their number; after delivery it undergoes [involution](../../../../../involution.md). An enlarged remaining [kidney](../../../../../kidney.md) after loss of its partner exemplifies compensatory growth. In a modern rat experiment comparing neonatal, weanling and adult animals after removal of one [kidney](../../../../../kidney.md), the younger groups showed greater compensatory growth; that experimental comparison does not make an age-independent prediction for every human [kidney](../../../../../kidney.md).[Abstract, comparison of neonatal, weanling and young adult rats](../../../../../age-dependent-characteristics-of-compensatory-renal-growth.md) Thus **organ size alone cannot distinguish the cellular processes**: compare cell dimensions, numbers and [tissue](../../../../../tissue-biology.md) composition by [histology](../../../../../histology.md).[Physiological hypertrophy and hyperplasia, uterine growth; compensatory growth](../../../../../hypertrophy-and-hyperplasia-in-underwood-s-pathology-a-clinical-approach.md)
