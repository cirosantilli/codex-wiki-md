<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

**“Atelectasis pulmonum” means incomplete expansion or collapse of [lung](../../../../../lung.md) air spaces; in the newborn it classically describes portions that have not expanded adequately after birth.** Modern [atelectasis](../../../../../atelectasis.md) also includes collapse of previously aerated [lung](../../../../../lung.md). The older distinction between primary non-expansion and secondary collapse is useful when reading this paper.

The affected [lung](../../../../../lung.md) is airless, often dark and firm, with reduced volume, a depressed surface and little or no crepitation. Failure of inspiratory effort, obstruction of a [bronchus](../../../../../bronchus.md) by secretions, and external compression are different routes to this appearance. Modern physiology adds the role of [pulmonary surfactant](../../../../../pulmonary-surfactant.md) in opposing alveolar collapse; deficiency in premature infants belongs to [neonatal respiratory distress syndrome](../../../../../neonatal-respiratory-distress-syndrome.md), not an explanation the 1867 question itself supplies.[Definition; factors maintaining alveolar expansion; Etiology](../../../../../atelectasis-by-merck-manual-professional-edition.md)

The main historical confusion was with [pneumonia](../../../../../pneumonia.md). A pre-1867 review of [Charles West](../../../../../charles-west.md)'s work distinguishes primary non-expansion from secondary collapse, and contrasts both with pneumonic [hepatization](../../../../../hepatization.md). A collapsed region loses air and volume; inflammatory [pulmonary consolidation](../../../../../pulmonary-consolidation.md) fills air spaces with [exudate](../../../../../exudate.md). Both can look solid.[pp. 109–112, primary atelectasis, secondary collapse and confusion with lobular pneumonia](../../../../../lectures-on-the-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-review-1855.md)

The classical experiment introduces air through the [trachea](../../../../../trachea.md) or an appropriate [bronchus](../../../../../bronchus.md) while the affected region is observed. Uncomplicated collapsed lobules can expand and regain buoyancy; inflation does not simply remove inflammatory [exudate](../../../../../exudate.md). The historical review describes this distinction.[pp. 109–111, artificial inflation and buoyancy](../../../../../lectures-on-the-diseases-of-infancy-and-childhood-review-1855.md) The experiment's interpretation requires more than one observation: whether a region takes air, whether its volume changes, and whether its contents show [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) are different measurements. Cut-surface inspection and [histology](../../../../../histology.md) test the last point directly. Floating or sinking alone cannot distinguish all mixtures of collapse, fluid and [pneumonia](../../../../../pneumonia.md), and cannot reliably establish whether an infant was born alive.

Other potential confusions include [pulmonary oedema](../../../../../pulmonary-oedema.md), [pulmonary haemorrhage](../../../../../pulmonary-haemorrhage.md), and compressed [lung](../../../../../lung.md) beside a [pleural effusion](../../../../../pleural-effusion.md) or [pneumothorax](../../../../../pneumothorax.md). Fluid or [blood](../../../../../blood.md) in the air spaces differs from collapse alone, although the conditions can coexist; external compression can actually cause [atelectasis](../../../../../atelectasis.md). [Pulmonary hypoplasia](../../../../../pulmonary-hypoplasia.md) is different again: an underdeveloped organ is not merely an otherwise normally formed organ awaiting inflation.[Etiology; Differential Diagnosis](../../../../../atelectasis-in-statpearls.md)

**Reduced aeration is the observation; non-expansion, obstruction, compression and inflammatory consolidation are competing or coexisting explanations.** Gross inspection, controlled inflation and [histology](../../../../../histology.md) help discriminate them.
