<h1 id="history-of-bloodletting/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [History of bloodletting](../history-of-bloodletting.md)

**By 1867 the central change was from confidence in [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) as a general remedy to a disputed, increasingly restricted practice tested against clinical outcomes.** [Bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) includes opening a [vein](../../../../../vein.md), local [wet cupping](../../../../../wet-cupping.md), and the application of [medicinal leeches](../../../../../medicinal-leech.md). The history must explain both the theories that supported it and why clinical comparisons weakened that support.

**Ancient explanations and qualifications.** In the [Hippocratic Corpus](../../../../../hippocratic-corpus.md), [disease](../../../../../disease.md) could be explained through disturbances of bodily fluids. The four-fluid account underlying [humorism](../../../../../humorism.md) named [blood](../../../../../blood.md), [humoral phlegm](../../../../../humoral-phlegm.md), yellow [bile](../../../../../bile.md) and [black bile](../../../../../black-bile.md); restoring their proper mixture offered a rationale for evacuation. This was a historical explanatory system, not a modern description of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) composition. Nor was ancient practice uniformly indiscriminate. [Aulus Cornelius Celsus](../../../../../aulus-cornelius-celsus.md) describes [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) as a powerful intervention whose suitability depends on bodily strength and whether the condition represents excess rather than exhaustion. [Galen](../../../../../galen.md) likewise distinguishes whether evacuation is needed, whether it should be by [venesection](../../../../../venesection.md), and whether the patient can tolerate it; he discusses the site, quantity and repetition. Thus the ancient debate already included selection and restraint. [Selection 1, The Elements of Life, paragraphs 1–2: four distinct fluids; translated primary excerpt](../../../../../on-the-nature-of-man-in-the-hippocratic-corpus.md) [II.10.1–8, strength, depletion and the risks of bloodletting](../../../../../on-medicine-by-aulus-cornelius-celsus.md) [§1, successive questions governing evacuation, tolerance, site and amount](../../../../../on-treatment-by-bloodletting-by-galen.md)

**Medieval continuity and the instruments.** The [Canon of Medicine by Ibn Sina](../../../../../canon-of-medicine-by-ibn-sina.md) preserves a carefully qualified doctrine of [venesection](../../../../../venesection.md), including cases where removing good [blood](../../../../../blood.md) would leave harmful fluids behind. Its reasoning belongs to [humorism](../../../../../humorism.md); its distinctions should not be mistaken for verified modern indications. In late medieval Europe, [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) was also organized by calendars: a surviving Augsburg calendar for 1472 specifies supposedly favourable times according to the patient and the [Moon](../../../../../moon.md). This documents an additional astrological framework, not evidence of therapeutic efficacy. [Book I, translation §§1019–1022, contraindications, timing and choice of vessels](../../../../../canon-of-medicine-by-ibn-sina.md) [Princeton copy and catalogue description: days selected by lunar phases, patient age, affected part and temperament](../../../../../bloodletting-calendar-for-augsburg-1472.md)

The material distinction between general and local removal mattered. [Venesection](../../../../../venesection.md) opened a [vein](../../../../../vein.md) with a sharp instrument. [Wet cupping](../../../../../wet-cupping.md) combined superficial cuts in the [skin](../../../../../skin.md) with suction under a cup; [Celsus](../../../../../aulus-cornelius-celsus.md) describes bronze cups in which burning material reduced the enclosed air's [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md), and horn cups from which air was sucked through a small opening. [Dry cupping](../../../../../dry-cupping.md) did not include the cuts and therefore did not itself remove [blood](../../../../../blood.md). [Medicinal leeches](../../../../../medicinal-leech.md) provided another means of local removal. These instruments made depletion visibly demonstrable, but the visible removal of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) did not establish that a [disease](../../../../../disease.md) had been removed. [II.11.1–6, bronze and horn cups, scarified versus intact skin, and local versus general depletion](../../../../../on-medicine-by-aulus-cornelius-celsus.md)

**New [physiology](../../../../../physiology-split.md) did not immediately settle [treatment](../../../../../treatment.md).** [William Harvey](../../../../../william-harvey.md)'s 1628 account of [blood circulation](../../../../../blood-circulation.md) undermined the older picture of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) simply being supplied and consumed in the parts. His observations of [venous valves](../../../../../venous-valve.md) and experiments with arm ligatures showed directional movement: filling below a ligature and the behaviour of compressed [veins](../../../../../vein.md) helped demonstrate return towards the [heart](../../../../../heart.md). Nevertheless, a circulating [blood](../../../../../blood.md) supply could still be invoked to explain relief through depletion or redistribution. Establishing [blood circulation](../../../../../blood-circulation.md) and establishing the benefit of [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) were different questions. [chapters 8–9, quantitative argument for circulation; chapter 13, ligatures and venous valves](../../../../../on-the-motion-of-the-heart-and-blood-in-animals-by-william-harvey.md)

In the early nineteenth century, [François-Joseph-Victor Broussais](../../../../../francois-joseph-victor-broussais.md)'s emphasis on local irritation and [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) encouraged extensive use of [medicinal leeches](../../../../../medicinal-leech.md). This illustrates why changing theory could increase [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md): a local inflammatory explanation supplied a fresh reason to remove [blood](../../../../../blood.md) near the supposed seat of [disease](../../../../../disease.md). The accompanying claims of efficacy still needed comparison with what happened under other [treatments](../../../../../treatment.md). [section discussing Broussais, local irritation, leeching, and the claim that leeches offered a relatively gentle intervention](../../../../../re-imagining-bleeders-by-robert-g-w-kirk-and-neil-pemberton.md)

**Comparative evidence weakened general claims.** In 1835 [Pierre Charles Alexandre Louis](../../../../../pierre-charles-alexandre-louis.md) asked whether comparable patients with [pneumonia](../../../../../pneumonia.md) did better when first subjected to [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md) during days 1–4 than when first bled later. He explicitly considered age and other differences. This [numerical method in medicine](../../../../../numerical-method-in-medicine.md) shifted attention from selected recoveries to outcomes in defined groups. His work restricted confidence in a large, general benefit; it should not be presented as a [randomized controlled trial](../../../../../randomized-controlled-trial.md) proving that every use was ineffective. In modern terms, these were [observational studies](../../../../../observational-study.md): [confounding by indication](../../../../../confounding-by-indication.md) could make the earlier-treated group differ in severity, and surviving long enough to enter the later-treated group could introduce [selection bias](../../../../../selection-bias.md). Counting cases was an advance, but did not automatically remove these problems. [1835, pp. 70–76; primary passages reproduced by the James Lind Library on timing, age and comparisons between groups](../../../../../research-on-the-effects-of-bloodletting-by-pierre-charles-alexandre-louis.md)

**The dispute still alive in 1867.** [William Pulteney Alison](../../../../../william-alison.md) defended earlier experience of benefit, while [John Hughes Bennett](../../../../../john-hughes-bennett.md) opposed routine depletion and developed a restorative approach. One defence of the altered practice was the [change-of-type theory of disease](../../../../../change-of-type-theory-of-disease.md): formerly vigorous or “sthenic” inflammatory illness was said to have become weak or “asthenic”, so that measures once borne well were now inappropriate. The rival explanation was that understanding of [pathology](../../pathology-split.md) and the evidence about [treatment](../../../../../treatment.md) had improved. [Alison](../../../../../william-alison.md)'s 1856 argument and the contemporary 1858 review show that this was an active disagreement, not a completed unanimous rejection. [1856, pp. 769–788, opening response to Bennett and defence of previous clinical experience](../../../../../reflections-on-the-results-of-experience-as-to-the-symptoms-of-internal-inflammations-by-william-pulteney-alison.md) [opening pp. 1–3, contrasting altered disease type with altered pathological understanding](../../../../../the-bloodletting-controversy-1858-review.md)

[Bennett](../../../../../john-hughes-bennett.md)'s 1864 discussion made the theoretical contrast explicit: an account centred on altered [blood](../../../../../blood.md) and vessels favoured depletion, whereas his account of tissue irritation and [inflammatory exudation](../../../../../inflammatory-exudate.md) led him towards the opposite practice. That historical account of [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md) should itself be evaluated historically, rather than adopted as current [pathology](../../pathology-split.md). The durable methodological lesson is that recovery after [treatment](../../../../../treatment.md) is insufficient evidence of recovery because of it: [acute disease](../../../../../acute-disease.md) may resolve naturally, as even the Latin extract acknowledges. Changes in theory, recognition of the patient's limited reserve, and comparative [clinical observation](../../../../../clinical-observation.md) together explain the retreat from routine [bloodletting](../../../../../bloodletting.md). [1864, p. 142, opening comparison of vascular and extravascular accounts of inflammation and their therapeutic consequences](../../../../../treatment-of-inflammation-by-john-hughes-bennett.md)

**Present-day distinction.** Contemporary [venesection](../../../../../venesection.md) has specific indications: in [haemochromatosis](../../../../../haemochromatosis.md), removal of [red blood cells](../../../../../red-blood-cell.md) helps reduce excess [iron](../../../../../iron.md); in [polycythaemia vera](../../../../../polycythaemia-vera.md), it can reduce an excessive [red blood cell](../../../../../red-blood-cell.md) burden. Those uses concern defined abnormalities, rather than correction of historical [humorism](../../../../../humorism.md) or a general remedy for [fever](../../../../../fever.md) and [inflammation](../../../../../inflammation.md). They are a modern explanatory coda, not knowledge attributed to the 1867 candidate.
