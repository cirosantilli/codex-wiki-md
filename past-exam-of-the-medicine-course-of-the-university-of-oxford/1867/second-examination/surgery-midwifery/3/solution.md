**An historically appropriate classification distinguishes the hard, soft, gelatinous, pigmented and epithelial malignant growths, together with recurrent and destructive growths whose place in “cancer” was disputed. Modern classification instead distinguishes [tumours](../../../../../tumour.md) by cellular lineage and biological behaviour.**

The quotation marks around “Malignant” matter. In 1867 the clinical grouping did not have a single settled boundary in [histology](../../../../../histology.md). Infiltration, destructive growth, return after removal, spread to distant sites and progressive constitutional illness were warning features; hardness or rapid growth alone was not a sufficient definition. A contemporary review of an 1866 textbook explicitly disputed confused descriptions of hard and soft [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md), illustrating that this was already a live diagnostic controversy.[Discussion under “tumours”, contrasting scirrhus with encephaloid, melanotic and colloid growths and rejecting an indiscriminate use of “cancer”](../../../../../review-of-demonstrations-of-microscopic-anatomy-1866-book.md)

A useful period classification, without pretending that every contemporary author drew the same boundaries, is:

- **[Scirrhous carcinoma](../../../../../scirrhous-carcinoma.md), or hard [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md).** Abundant firm fibrous supporting tissue makes a hard, often contracted growth, classically in the [breast](../../../../../breast.md). Retraction and fixation reflect invasion and contraction of that supporting tissue. “Hard” describes the gross appearance; it is not a separate modern cellular lineage.
- **[Encephaloid cancer](../../../../../encephaloid-cancer.md), or medullary/soft [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md).** The name refers to soft, brain-like tissue, often richly cellular and prone to rapid growth, breakdown and [haemorrhage](../../../../../haemorrhage.md). The historical label could include more than one modern diagnosis; it does not mean a [tumour](../../../../../tumour.md) of the [brain](../../../../../brain.md).
- **[Colloid cancer](../../../../../colloid-cancer.md).** A gelatinous appearance associated with abundant [mucin](../../../../../mucin.md). Some such lesions would now be classified as [mucinous carcinomas](../../../../../mucinous-carcinoma.md); appearance alone cannot retrospectively determine their modern identity.
- **[Melanotic tumour](../../../../../melanotic-tumour.md).** Dark pigment supplied a gross category. Many malignant examples belong to modern [melanoma](../../../../../melanoma.md), arising from [melanocytes](../../../../../melanocyte.md), but pigmentation is neither necessary nor sufficient for [melanoma](../../../../../melanoma.md). Pigmented examples could also be treated as varieties within other period categories.
- **[Epithelioma](../../../../../epithelioma.md), or epithelial [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md).** A growth exhibiting epithelial structure, especially at [skin](../../../../../skin.md) and [mucous membrane](../../../../../mucous-membrane.md) surfaces. Many examples correspond to [squamous cell carcinoma](../../../../../squamous-cell-carcinoma.md). A locally destructive [rodent ulcer](../../../../../rodent-ulcer.md), now generally [basal cell carcinoma](../../../../../basal-cell-carcinoma.md), was sometimes separated because distant spread was unusual.
- **[Recurrent fibroid tumour](../../../../../recurrent-fibroid-tumour.md) and related “fibroplastic” or other [sarcomas](../../../../../sarcoma.md).** These could return repeatedly and invade locally despite differing from the period's typical [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md). They were sometimes called semi-malignant. A reported 1866 [recurrent fibroid tumour](../../../../../recurrent-fibroid-tumour.md) developed distant deposits after apparently successful local treatment: a vivid reason that a purely local category could fail.[Case of recurrent fibroid tumour of the thigh, subsequent constitutional disease and post-mortem deposits; terminology is the report's historical terminology](../../../../../st-george-s-hospital-reports-1866.md)

Those are overlapping historical descriptive groups, not six mutually exclusive modern diagnoses. Nor does every growth historically called “sarcoma” meet the modern definition of [sarcoma](../../../../../sarcoma.md).

The modern organising principle asks what cells are malignant. [Carcinoma](../../../../../carcinoma.md) arises from [epithelium](../../../../../epithelium.md); [sarcoma](../../../../../sarcoma.md) arises in [bone](../../../../../bone.md) or [soft tissue](../../../../../soft-tissue.md); [melanoma](../../../../../melanoma.md) arises from [melanocytes](../../../../../melanocyte.md); [lymphoma](../../../../../lymphoma.md) and [leukaemia](../../../../../leukaemia.md) concern blood-forming or immune cells. Other families include central [nervous system](../../../../../nervous-system.md) and [germ cell tumours](../../../../../germ-cell-tumour.md). A solid lump is not essential for [cancer](../../../../../cancer.md). Within a family, [histology](../../../../../histology.md), [immunohistochemistry](../../../../../immunohistochemistry.md) and appropriate [molecular diagnostics](../../../../../molecular-diagnostics.md) refine the diagnosis. [Tumour grade](../../../../../tumour-grade.md) describes features of aggressiveness, whereas [tumour stage](../../../../../tumour-stage.md) describes extent of disease.[“Types of Cancer”, especially carcinoma, sarcoma, leukaemia, lymphoma, melanoma and other tumour types](../../../../../what-is-cancer-by-the-national-cancer-institute.md)

This also corrects the old inference that recurrence must reflect a mysterious constitutional deposit: local residual disease and [metastasis](../../../../../metastasis.md) are distinct mechanisms, and an apparently circumscribed growth may already have disseminated.
