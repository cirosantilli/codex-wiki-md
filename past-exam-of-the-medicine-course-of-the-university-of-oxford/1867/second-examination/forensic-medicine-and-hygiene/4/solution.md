**[Strychnine poisoning](../../../../../strychnine-poisoning.md) characteristically produces rapidly recurring, intensely painful generalized [muscle spasms](../../../../../muscle-spasm.md), precipitated by slight sensory stimuli, with consciousness initially preserved.** “Strychnia” is the historical name for [strychnine](../../../../../strychnine.md).

Following ingestion, manifestations commonly begin within roughly 15–60 minutes, although exposure route and amount alter the time course. Early features include apprehension, restlessness, heightened sensitivity and stiffness or twitching of the [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md), especially about the neck and back. The patient can then develop violent, generalized [muscle spasms](../../../../../muscle-spasm.md) with rigid limbs and backward arching of the trunk, termed [opisthotonus](../../../../../opisthotonus.md). Jaw closure and contraction of the facial [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md) can produce [trismus](../../../../../trismus.md) and [risus sardonicus](../../../../../risus-sardonicus.md). The [respiratory muscles](../../../../../respiratory-muscle.md) may become fixed, causing difficulty breathing and [cyanosis](../../../../../cyanosis.md).[“How it works” and “Signs and symptoms”: rapid onset, painful spasms, arching and initial awareness](../../../../../strychnine-by-the-centers-for-disease-control-and-prevention.md)

Attacks may be triggered by touch, a noise, light or attempts to move the patient. During the intervals the [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md) may relax substantially, and the patient may remain lucid and fearful of the next attack. Repeated contractions cause exhaustion, sweating and [hyperthermia](../../../../../hyperthermia.md); severe cases develop [acidosis](../../../../../acidosis.md), [rhabdomyolysis](../../../../../rhabdomyolysis.md) and [acute kidney injury](../../../../../acute-kidney-injury.md). Death can result from [respiratory failure](../../../../../respiratory-failure.md). Consciousness can be lost late through [hypoxia](../../../../../hypoxia.md), so preserved awareness is a characteristic early clue, not an invariable requirement.[“Signs/Symptoms”, ingestion: stimulus-sensitive spasms, lucid intervals, opisthotonus and systemic complications](../../../../../strychnine-biotoxin-by-the-national-institute-for-occupational-safety-and-health.md)

This pattern helps distinguish the condition from many [epileptic seizures](../../../../../epileptic-seizure.md), in which impaired awareness and a [postictal state](../../../../../postictal-state.md) are common, and from [tetanus](../../../../../tetanus.md), whose evolution is usually slower and whose rigidity tends to persist between attacks. The distinction is not absolute and symptoms alone do not prove the particular [poison](../../../../../poison.md). A convincing forensic diagnosis combines the clinical sequence and exposure history with [toxicological analysis](../../../../../toxicological-analysis.md).

The modern explanation is blockade of inhibitory [glycine receptors](../../../../../glycine-receptor.md) in the [spinal cord](../../../../../spinal-cord.md) and [brainstem](../../../../../brainstem.md), leaving reflex motor activity excessively excitable. This explains why trivial stimulation can cause a massive contraction. The receptor explanation is a later interpretation of the recognizable nineteenth-century syndrome.[“Discussion of case questions and key points”, questions 1–3: glycine action, maintained alertness and comparison with tetanus](../../../../../strychnine-poisoning-by-the-california-poison-control-system.md)
