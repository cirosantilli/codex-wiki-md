↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**English law in 1867 fixed no maximum period of proved non-intercourse compatible with legitimacy; an exceptional interval had to be assessed on the evidence of possible [gestation](../../../../../gestation.md) in the particular case.** The relevant interval runs from the last possible intercourse between the spouses to the child's birth. Proof of subsequent non-intercourse establishes that interval; it does not itself establish how long [pregnancy](../../../../../pregnancy.md) could have lasted.

The contemporary eighth edition of the [Encyclopædia Britannica](../../../../../encyclopaedia-britannica.md), published in 1853–1860, expressly distinguished the English position from numerical limits in continental codes. Its article on [forensic medicine](../../../../../forensic-medicine.md) states: “The law of England at present has no definite limit”; it then says that a case beyond the usual term would go to a jury. This directly answers the legal question: there was no prescribed number of days that resolved every case. Medical evidence about the credibility of the claimed [gestation](../../../../../gestation.md) informed the tribunal's decision about the child's status.[Part I, Section I, VI.3, “Limits of Utero-gestation”: English law has no definite limit; a case beyond the usual term would go to a jury](../../../../../medical-jurisprudence-in-the-eighth-edition-of-encyclopaedia-britannica.md)

To see what “consistent” means, distinguish the ordinary duration of [pregnancy](../../../../../pregnancy.md), the range alleged in exceptional medical reports, and a legal decision about a particular child. An accepted exceptional interval would show that the ordinary duration was not an inflexible rule; it would not establish a maximum for every subsequent case. For illustration, the later historical account in [Legal medicine by Charles Meymott Tidy (volume 3)](../../../../../legal-medicine-by-charles-meymott-tidy-volume-3.md) reports an English decision, dated there to 1834, allowing legitimacy after about 42 weeks, or 294 days, from last intercourse. This is a retrospective textbook report, not independent verification of the original judgment or proof of a longest accepted interval. The legal answer rests on the contemporary statement of the English rule, rather than on that numerical example.[pp.59–60, tabulated 294-day interval and case 112 (1834); retrospective account published in 1884](../../../../../legal-medicine-by-charles-meymott-tidy-volume-3.md)

The medical evidence also requires care over the starting event. A period counted from the last [menstruation](../../../../../menstruation.md) is not the same measurement as a period counted from intercourse, and neither directly observes the instant of [fertilization](../../../../../fertilization.md). Modern [gestational age](../../../../../gestational-age.md) conventionally counts from the first day of the last menstrual period; early [ultrasonography](../../../../../ultrasonography.md) provides a more accurate check than menstrual recall alone. It would be misleading to combine historical figures with these different starting points into a single supposedly proved maximum.[Committee Opinion No.700, “Clinical Considerations in the First Trimester” and “Last Menstrual Period”](../../../../../methods-for-estimating-the-due-date-by-the-american-college-of-obstetricians-and-gynecologists.md)

The absence of a predetermined English maximum did not make every length of separation legally acceptable or biologically possible. A claimed prolonged [pregnancy](../../../../../pregnancy.md) still required credible evidence, and a nineteenth-century finding about legitimacy does not validate an extraordinary [pregnancy](../../../../../pregnancy.md) length as a modern scientific observation. In [historical legitimacy and prolonged gestation](../../../../../historical-legitimacy-and-prolonged-gestation.md), the legal rule, the determination in an individual case, and the observed biological interval must be kept distinct. Establishing a unique longest reported case is a separate historical inquiry; it is not necessary to answer that English law prescribed no fixed maximum.
