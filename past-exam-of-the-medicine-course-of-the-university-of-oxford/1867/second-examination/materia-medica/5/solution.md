<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

**The assertion is too absolute: a remedy can cause a distant response through a [reflex](../../../../../reflex.md) or another intervening physiological change without first being absorbed into the [blood](../../../../../blood.md).** It is substantially right for the direct action of a circulating [drug](../../../../../drug.md) on a distant target, but does not describe every remote effect of treatment.

Three mechanisms should be distinguished:

- **Direct action after [drug absorption](../../../../../drug-absorption.md).** An orally administered [drug](../../../../../drug.md) that acts directly in the [brain](../../../../../brain.md) must gain access to that tissue. Absorption from the [gastrointestinal tract](../../../../../gastrointestinal-tract.md), transport in the [blood](../../../../../blood.md), and passage to the target explain such a systemic action. Changing the route changes the first step: a subcutaneous medicine must leave its local depot, whereas an intravenous medicine is introduced directly into the circulation. The literal word “absorbed” is consequently too restrictive even before considering [reflexes](../../../../../reflex.md).
- **Local action followed by a neural response.** Stimulation of [sensory nerves](../../../../../sensory-nerve.md) at one surface can cause a response elsewhere through the [nervous system](../../../../../nervous-system.md). Gastric stimulation can evoke [vomiting](../../../../../vomiting.md), including coordinated movement of respiratory and abdominal [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md); sensory input from the [gastrointestinal tract](../../../../../gastrointestinal-tract.md) reaches brainstem circuits through [vagal afferents](../../../../../vagal-afferent.md). No requirement follows that the original gastric irritant must itself circulate to every contracting [muscle](../../../../../muscle.md). A swallowed emetic can have more than one mechanism, however: an observed instance of [vomiting](../../../../../vomiting.md) alone does not prove that absorption played no part.[“Pathophysiology of N&V”; visceral afferent inputs and output to abdominal musculature and respiratory centres](../../../../../nausea-and-vomiting-related-to-cancer-treatment-by-the-national-cancer-institute.md)
- **Local action followed by a change in the body's contents.** As a present-day illustration, an osmotic [laxative](../../../../../laxative.md) can retain [water](../../../../../water.md) in the intestinal lumen and promote evacuation with limited systemic absorption. Any secondary change in fluid balance or symptoms is then a consequence of the local intestinal action, not proof that the laxative has directly acted on a remote organ. The [polyethylene glycol 3350](../../../../../polyethylene-glycol-3350.md) information from MedlinePlus describes retention of [water](../../../../../water.md) in stool; the regulatory monograph records minimal absorption.[“Why is this medication prescribed?”; retention of water in stool](../../../../../polyethylene-glycol-3350-by-medlineplus.md)[“Action and clinical pharmacology”, p. 9; mechanism and pharmacokinetics of PEG 3350](../../../../../peglyte-prescribing-information.md)

The distinction is between transport of the medicinal substance and transmission of its consequences. A [reflex](../../../../../reflex.md) transmits activity in [neurons](../../../../../neuron-split.md); a locally acting intestinal agent changes a physical condition; a circulating [drug](../../../../../drug.md) delivers molecules to its distant target. The same remedy may combine these routes, and an apparently rapid effect does not by itself settle which route caused it.

A useful experimental design would therefore compare remote responses with measurements of the agent in [blood](../../../../../blood.md), and would test the proposed neural pathway separately. In an appropriate physiological preparation, recording the response while interrupting a relevant afferent pathway can test whether that pathway is necessary; preserving the circulation while interrupting nerve conduction distinguishes neural transmission from vascular transport. Conversely, a remote effect after vascular transfer supports a circulating agent, provided contamination and other transferred mediators are controlled. Failure to detect a [drug](../../../../../drug.md) is only as strong as the analytical detection limit.

**Absorption is one route to remote action, not a universal prerequisite for every distant physiological consequence.** The historical language “animal economy” means the functioning organism; it should not conceal the distinction between a direct [pharmacological effect](../../../../../pharmacological-effect.md) and a secondary [physiological response](../../../../../physiological-response.md).
