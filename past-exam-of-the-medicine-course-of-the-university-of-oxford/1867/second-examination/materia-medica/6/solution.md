A valid examination of the missing materials would first record each label separately and describe the actual material: whole or powdered form; size and shape; external and cut surfaces; texture and fracture; and any diagnostic [plant](../../../../../plant.md) organ or animal structure. For a botanical material, [microscopy](../../../../../microscopy.md) could distinguish [bark](../../../../../bark.md), [wood](../../../../../wood.md), [root](../../../../../root-botany.md), [leaf](../../../../../leaf.md) and [seed](../../../../../seed.md) tissues, with suitable reference material and the historical [pharmacopoeia](../../../../../pharmacopoeia.md) definition used for comparison. A putative chemical preparation would instead require appropriate [solubility](../../../../../solubility.md) and reagent tests. No colour, smell, section, chemical reaction, botanical origin or medicinal constituent is asserted for an unseen sample. Historical descriptions involving tasting are documentary evidence, not a method to apply to unknown potentially poisonous materials.
