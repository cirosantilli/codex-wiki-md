## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Materia medica](materia-medica.md)

**Original question.**

What are the most commonly used preparations of Iron, and in what form does the metal exist in them ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**The main distinction is between metallic [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron), [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) preparations, [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) preparations, and mixed [iron oxides](../../../chemistry.md#iron-oxide).** The old prefixes “proto-” and “per-” ordinarily distinguish the lower and higher [oxidation states](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) here. “Peroxide of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)” means the historical [iron(III) oxide](../../../chemistry.md#iron-iii-oxide) preparation, not a modern [peroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#peroxide) containing an oxygen–oxygen bond.

The principal medicinal preparations can be grouped by the chemical form supplied:

- **[Iron(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-sulfate)**: _Ferri sulphas_, its granulated form, and its dried form supply [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii). The ordinary and granulated [salts](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry) are represented as $\mathrm{FeSO_4\cdot7H_2O}$; the 1867 dried preparation is represented as $\mathrm{FeSO_4\cdot H_2O}$. Drying principally changes the [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization), rather than converting [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) into [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii).
- **[Iron(II) carbonate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-carbonate)**: _Ferri carbonas saccharata_, the carbonate pill, and the compound [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) mixture contain freshly formed [iron(II) carbonate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-carbonate), $\mathrm{FeCO_3}$. Exposure to air introduces higher [iron oxides](../../../chemistry.md#iron-oxide); [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose) helps preserve the lower [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state). Thus the saccharated material is a protected preparation rather than a pure, permanently stable [salt](../../../ionic-bonding.md#salt-chemistry).
- **[Iron(II) iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-iodide)**: _Ferri iodidum_, its syrup, and its pill supply mainly $\mathrm{FeI_2}$. The historical solid includes [water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization) and some [iron oxide](../../../chemistry.md#iron-oxide); the syrup again employs [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose) to retard deterioration.
- **[Iron(II) phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-phosphate)**: _Ferri phosphas_ and the corresponding syrup supply the lower [iron phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-phosphate), conventionally $\mathrm{Fe_3(PO_4)_2}$. The official precipitated material is partly oxidized, so describing the actual powder as chemically pure [iron(II) phosphate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-phosphate) would be too strong.
- **[Iron(III) chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii-chloride)**: the solution and tincture of “perchloride of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)” contain the higher [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride), $\mathrm{FeCl_3}$, in an acidic liquid. This historical “perchloride” is unrelated to a [perchlorate](../../../chemical-substance.md#perchlorate).
- **[Iron(III) nitrate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii-nitrate)**: solution of “pernitrate of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)” contains the higher [nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#nitrate), $\mathrm{Fe(NO_3)_3}$, again in an acidic liquid. The solution of “persulphate” used to manufacture several other preparations supplies [iron(III) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii-sulfate), $\mathrm{Fe_2(SO_4)_3}$; it is useful to distinguish this manufacturing reagent from the widely administered [iron(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-sulfate).
- **[Iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) with organic acids**: citrate of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) and [ammonium](../../../chemistry.md#ammonium), and tartarated [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron), contain the higher [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) in citrate or tartrate combinations. Citrate of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) and [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) is prepared from a higher [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) compound, but should not be assumed to contain exclusively [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii): the 1867 monograph reports blue reactions with both [potassium ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-ferrocyanide) and [potassium ferricyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-ferricyanide), consistent with an [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) component as well. The tartarated preparation also contains [potassium](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium); citrate of [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) and [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) is made with [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia). They should be described as soluble pharmaceutical combinations, without pretending that every historical batch had a single simple molecular formula. The acetate tincture similarly contains the higher [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) combined with [acetic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#acetic-acid).
- **[Iron oxides](../../../chemistry.md#iron-oxide)**: _Ferri peroxidum hydratum_ (dried hydrated “peroxide”) and _Ferri peroxidum humidum_ (the moist material) contain [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) as [iron(III) oxide](../../../chemistry.md#iron-iii-oxide) or hydrated [iron(III) oxide](../../../chemistry.md#iron-iii-oxide). The magnetic oxide is a mixed [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii)–[iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) oxide preparation; $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$ expresses the ideal mixed oxide, rather than a claim that the historical hydrated powder was pure crystalline [magnetite](../../../chemical-substance.md#magnetite).
- **Metallic [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)**: _Ferrum redactum_, used also in lozenges, contains finely divided [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) in [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) zero together with magnetic oxide. The official preparation is therefore not all elemental [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron). [Iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) wine made by contacting wire with wine obtains a dissolved tartrate-containing [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) preparation from reaction with the wine's acids; the wire placed in the vessel should not be confused with the chemical form in the liquid ultimately dispensed.

These identifications follow the historical monographs, rather than the names of present commercial supplements.[“Ferri carbonas saccharata” through “Ferrum tartaratum”, pp. 130–141; the iron solutions, pp. 186–189; “Vinum ferri”, p. 367](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867) A green [iron(II) sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii-sulfate) solution gives the characteristic blue reaction with [potassium ferricyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-ferricyanide), whereas the higher [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) solutions give blue with [potassium ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#potassium-ferrocyanide). Those diagnostic reactions explain why the official tests distinguish the two [oxidation states](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state); the historical colour test alone does not establish every detail of a complex preparation's composition.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Materia medica](materia-medica.md)

**Original question.**

How is Sulphate of Quinia prepared, and what are the tests of its purity given in the British Pharmacopœia ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**Prepare [quinine sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine-sulfate) by acidic extraction of [cinchona bark](../../../pharmacology.md#cinchona-bark), alkaline precipitation of the [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) base, conversion into the [sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate), and purification by [crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#crystallization).** “[Quinia](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine)” is the historical name for [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine).

The 1867 process starts with one pound of yellow [cinchona bark](../../../pharmacology.md#cinchona-bark) in coarse powder, three fluid ounces of [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid), four pints of solution of [sodium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium-hydroxide), and sufficient [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water) and diluted [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid). The [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid) is diluted with ten pints of [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water). The bark is first moistened with diluted [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid), macerated for twenty-four hours with occasional stirring, and then exhausted in a [percolator](../../../pharmacology.md#percolator) with the diluted [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid). The historical endpoint is disappearance of the bitter character of the emerging liquid. This describes the period's test, not an instruction to taste an unidentified medicinal material.[“Quiniæ sulphas”, pp. 268–269, ingredients and extraction](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)

The soluble [alkaloid](../../../chemical-substance.md#alkaloid) salts enter the percolate. Adding solution of [sodium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#sodium-hydroxide) liberates the much less water-soluble [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) base. After settling, the liquid is decanted and the precipitate collected on a filter and washed with cold [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water) until the washings cease to be coloured. The precipitate is then treated warm with [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water) and diluted [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid), added gradually to obtain a neutral liquid with nearly all the precipitate dissolved. The hot solution is filtered through paper, the filter washed with [distilled water](../../../chemical-substance.md#distilled-water), and the filtrate concentrated until a surface film appears. It is then set aside for [crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#crystallization); the crystals are dried on filter paper without applied heat. This is a separation of a naturally occurring [alkaloid](../../../chemical-substance.md#alkaloid), not a synthesis of the [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) skeleton.

Writing $B$ for the [quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) base, the central changes are:

$$
\mathrm{BH^+ + OH^- \longrightarrow B\downarrow + H_2O},\qquad
\mathrm{2B+H_2SO_4\longrightarrow (BH)_2SO_4}.
$$

The successive changes in [solubility](../../../ionic-bonding.md#solubility) explain the apparatus: a [percolator](../../../pharmacology.md#percolator) washes soluble salts out of the powder; a filter retains the liberated base; a heated porcelain vessel dissolves the crude base as its [sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate); cooling then recovers the less soluble crystals. Other [cinchona alkaloids](../../../chemical-substance.md#cinchona-alkaloid) can accompany the product, which is why appearance and bitterness are insufficient evidence of purity.

The 1867 “Characters and Tests” combine identity and purity checks. They must be read as a group:

- **Appearance and [solubility](../../../ionic-bonding.md#solubility)**: filiform, silky, white crystals, sparingly soluble in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), with the characteristic bluish appearance of the solution. A little dilute acid increases [solubility](../../../ionic-bonding.md#solubility). The visible blue phenomenon is associated with [quinine fluorescence](../../../fluorescence.md#quinine-fluorescence).
- **[Sulfate](../../../chemistry.md#sulfate) identity**: [barium chloride](../../../chemical-substance.md#barium-chloride) gives a white precipitate insoluble in [nitric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitric-acid). The precipitate is [barium sulfate](../../../chemical-substance.md#barium-sulfate).
- **[Quinine](../../../chemical-substance.md#quinine) identity**: treating the solution first with chlorine water and then with [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) gives an emerald-green colour, the [thalleioquin test](../../../chemical-substance.md#thalleioquin-test). A positive colour reaction establishes a characteristic chemical response, rather than proving that no other [alkaloid](../../../chemical-substance.md#alkaloid) is present.
- **Strong-acid colour test**: dissolution in pure [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) gives only a faint yellowish tint, with no further colour change on gentle warming. Marked colouring suggests foreign organic matter, such as [sucrose](../../../chemical-substance.md#sucrose) or other readily altered material.
- **Acid dissolution and liberation of the base**: ten grains with ten minims of diluted [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) and half a fluid ounce of [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) form a complete solution; [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) then gives a white precipitate. Material insoluble under the acid conditions would fail the dissolution requirement.
- **Ether separation**: the precipitated base must pass into [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) on shaking the whole with half a fluid ounce of [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether), without crystalline matter remaining at the boundary between the two liquid layers. Less ether-soluble [cinchona alkaloids](../../../chemical-substance.md#cinchona-alkaloid), particularly [cinchonine](../../../chemical-substance.md#cinchonine), can leave material at this boundary. This is a period screening test, not a modern quantitative determination of every related impurity.
- **[Water of crystallization](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#water-of-crystallization)**: twenty-five grains lose 3.6 grains on drying at $212\,{}^\circ\mathrm{F}$, equivalent to $100\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. This checks excessive moisture or a materially different degree of hydration.

The historical quantity and endpoint requirements belong to the 1867 monograph.[“Quiniæ sulphas”, pp. 268–269, “Characters and Tests”](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867) Its formula represents the then-described heptahydrate; the current [“Quinine sulfate”](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#international-pharmacopoeia-by-the-world-health-organization) instead specifies a dihydrate and modern tests for related [cinchona alkaloids](../../../chemical-substance.md#cinchona-alkaloid). The modern specification explains that distinction; the exam's historical tests remain those listed above.[12th edition, 2024, “Quinine sulfate (Quinini sulfas)”, formula, loss on drying and related cinchona alkaloids](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#international-pharmacopoeia-by-the-world-health-organization)

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Materia medica](materia-medica.md)

**Original question.**

What parts of the following plants are directed by the British Pharmacopœia to be used; viz., _Sambucus, Scilla, Rosa Gallica, Pterocarpus, Physostigma venenosum, Theobroma Cacoa, Croton Tiglium, Taraxacum._

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**The required sequence is fresh [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower); sliced and dried [bulb](../../../organism.md#bulb); unexpanded [petals](../../../organism.md#petal); [heartwood](../../../organism.md#heartwood); [seeds](../../../organism.md#seed); expressed seed [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat); expressed seed [oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#oil); and fresh and dried [root](../../../organism.md#root-botany).** The [plant](../../../organism.md#plant) organ, its state, and the actual official material must all be specified.

- **[Sambucus nigra](../../../organism.md#sambucus-nigra): fresh [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower).** The official article is _Sambuci flores_, elder flowers, gathered from the native [plant](../../../organism.md#plant). The flower stalks are separated when making elder-flower water; the preparation also allows an equivalent quantity of flowers preserved fresh with common salt. Neither elder berries nor elder bark answers the botanical part asked here.[“Sambuci flores”, p. 274; “Aqua sambuci”, p. 45](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Squill](../../../pharmacology.md#squill): the sliced and dried [bulb](../../../organism.md#bulb).** The historical botanical source is _Urginea scilla_, also identified there as _Scilla maritima_. The medicinal slices consist of the fleshy bulb scales; this is an underground shoot structure, not a [root](../../../organism.md#root-botany). The official article is _Scilla_.[“Scilla”, pp. 279–280](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Rosa gallica](../../../organism.md#rosa-gallica): the fresh and dried unexpanded [petals](../../../organism.md#petal).** These are _Rosæ gallicæ petala_, the red-rose material. “Unexpanded” refers to collection before the flower has opened fully. The [petals](../../../organism.md#petal) must be distinguished from the [rose hip](../../../organism.md#rose-hip) used for a different official preparation.[“Rosæ gallicæ petala”, red-rose monograph](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Pterocarpus santalinus](../../../organism.md#pterocarpus-santalinus): the sliced or rasped [heartwood](../../../organism.md#heartwood).** This is _Pterocarpi lignum_, red sandalwood. The answer is the wood, rather than [bark](../../../organism.md#bark) or the exudate known as [kino](../../../pharmacology.md#kino). Botanical relationship between two medicinal materials does not make their official definitions interchangeable.[“Pterocarpi lignum”, p. 261](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Physostigma venenosum](../../../organism.md#physostigma-venenosum): the [seed](../../../organism.md#seed).** _Physostigmatis faba_, the [Calabar bean](../../../pharmacology.md#calabar-bean), is botanically a [seed](../../../organism.md#seed), not the entire pod. Its two large [cotyledons](../../../organism.md#cotyledon) lie inside a hard seed coat. The official alcoholic extract is made from the powdered [seed](../../../organism.md#seed).[“Physostigmatis faba”, pp. 233–234; “Extractum physostigmatis”, p. 126](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Theobroma cacao](../../../organism.md#theobroma-cacao): the concrete [oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#oil) obtained by expression and heat from the ground [seeds](../../../organism.md#seed).** The official article is _Oleum theobromæ_, [cocoa butter](../../../pharmacology.md#cocoa-butter). Naming the [seed](../../../organism.md#seed) gives its source organ, but omits the important fact that the official pharmaceutical material is the separated [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat). It is distinct from [coconut oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#coconut-oil).[“Oleum theobromæ”, p. 229](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Croton tiglium](../../../organism.md#croton-tiglium): the [oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#oil) expressed from the [seeds](../../../organism.md#seed).** The official material is _Oleum crotonis_, [croton oil](../../../pharmacology.md#croton-oil), rather than the whole [plant](../../../organism.md#plant) or an unspecified [leaf](../../../organism.md#leaf) preparation. The [seeds](../../../organism.md#seed) are its source; the expressed [oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#oil) is the article used in the official formulations.[“Oleum crotonis”, p. 222](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)
- **[Taraxacum officinale](../../../organism.md#taraxacum-officinale): the fresh and dried [root](../../../organism.md#root-botany).** The official article is _Taraxaci radix_, dandelion root, called _Taraxacum dens leonis_ in the 1867 standard, gathered between September and February from British meadows and pastures. The collection season and fresh/dried distinction belong to the historical standard; the answer is not the [leaves](../../../organism.md#leaf) or flowers.[“Taraxaci radix”, p. 317](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)

The names in the paper sometimes use the shorter terminology of the first [British Pharmacopoeia](../../../pharmacology.md#british-pharmacopoeia), whereas the 1867 edition explicitly renamed many entries to identify the medicinal organ. Its alteration list, for example, maps _Sambucus_ to _Sambuci flores_, _Pterocarpus_ to _Pterocarpi lignum_, and _Taraxacum_ to _Taraxaci radix_.[“Articles the names of which have been altered”, pp. xix–xxi](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867) That is a nomenclature change, not permission to choose any part of the named [plant](../../../organism.md#plant).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Materia medica](materia-medica.md)

**Original question.**

What is the active agent in the various means of producing blisters on the skin, and by what physiological processes do they act beneficially ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**Ordinary cantharides blisters owe their activity to [cantharidin](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharidin); other blistering methods act through different chemical irritants or through physical injury.** There is no single substance common to every way of raising a [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister).

In the standard historical preparations, the dried [blister beetle](../../../organism.md#blister-beetle) supplies [cantharidin](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharidin). A plaster, blistering paper, blistering liquid, or cantharides vinegar is a different vehicle for delivering the same [vesicant](../../../pharmacology.md#vesicant) to the [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin). Extracting the beetle into a liquid does not make the solvent the specific active principle. The 1867 [British Pharmacopoeia](../../../pharmacology.md#british-pharmacopoeia) includes _Emplastrum cantharidis_, _Charta epispastica_, _Liquor epispasticus_ and _Acetum cantharidis_.[“Cantharis”; “Acetum cantharidis”; “Charta epispastica”; “Emplastrum cantharidis”; “Liquor epispasticus”](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867)

Other historical means need separate explanations. Moist [mustard](../../../pharmacology.md#mustard) develops an irritant volatile product, principally [allyl isothiocyanate](../../../chemical-substance.md#allyl-isothiocyanate) from black mustard; its usual intended effect is reddening, with prolonged exposure capable of more destructive injury. This differs from the fixed [mustard](../../../pharmacology.md#mustard) seed [oil](../../../chemical-substance.md#oil): the irritant volatile product develops after moistening the powdered [seeds](../../../organism.md#seed).[“Sinapis” and “Oleum sinapis”; mustard seed preparations and production of the volatile oil](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-by-the-general-medical-council-1867) Strong [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) acts as an alkaline irritant. [Croton oil](../../../pharmacology.md#croton-oil) can produce an inflammatory and pustular eruption, which is not the same lesion as a clean cantharides [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister). A sufficiently hot object or hot liquid raises a [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister) through thermal damage, without a medicinal active molecule. These differences matter because [erythema](../../../physiology.md#erythema), a fluid-filled [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister), a [pustule](../../../physiology.md#pustule), and tissue destruction are not interchangeable endpoints.

For [cantharidin](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharidin), the modern tissue explanation involves loss of adhesion between [epidermal cells](../../../physiology.md#epidermal-cell), including disruption of [desmosomes](../../../biological-cell.md#desmosome), with separation within the [epidermis](../../../physiology.md#epidermis) and accumulation of fluid. The [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister) is therefore a local lesion of cell adhesion accompanied by [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation), rather than a pocket containing disease drawn from a remote organ. The FDA review describes this [acantholysis](../../../physiology.md#acantholysis) and distinguishes it from the incompletely understood mechanism of clinical benefit in a particular modern indication.[NDA 212905, §5.3, “Primary pharmacology”, p. 32](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#fda-multidisciplinary-review-of-ycanth)

A nineteenth-century answer would usually describe two supposed routes to benefit:

- **[Counterirritation](../../../biology.md#counterirritation).** A new superficial irritation was believed to relieve a deeper painful or inflamed part by altering nervous activity and the distribution of [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). A physiologically intelligible component is that input from [sensory nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#sensory-nerve) can change [pain](../../../neuroscience.md#pain) processing and [reflex](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex) output. That does not establish the stronger historical claim that creating an artificial [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation) cures an unrelated internal [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation).
- **[Serous drainage](../../../biology.md#serous-drainage) or “depletion”.** Fluid escaping from the raised [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister) was thought to remove fluid from the circulation and reduce local fullness. Fluid does indeed leave damaged [blood vessels](../../../physiology.md#blood-vessel) and collect in the [blister](../../../physiology.md#blister), but the observation alone does not demonstrate beneficial selective removal of a disease-producing substance. Maintaining a draining sore was a historical practice, not a consequence required by current [physiology](../../../physiology.md).

Thus **the demonstrable processes are local injury, [inflammation](../../../biology.md#inflammation), fluid exudation, and sensory stimulation; the broad historical claims of remote benefit require separate evidence.** Current approved topical [cantharidin](../../../pharmacology.md#cantharidin) use for [molluscum contagiosum](../../../physiology.md#molluscum-contagiosum) does not validate nineteenth-century blistering for diseases of internal organs. The modern label explicitly states that the mechanism of action in [molluscum contagiosum](../../../physiology.md#molluscum-contagiosum) is unknown and documents local [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin) reactions.[§§5.2 and 12.1–12.3; vesicant action, local reactions, unknown mechanism and measured exposure](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#ycanth-prescribing-information)

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Materia medica](materia-medica.md)

**Original question.**

‘A remedy must be absorbed before it can exert any remote action on the Animal Economy.’

Is this true ? Illustrate the statement.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**The assertion is too absolute: a remedy can cause a distant response through a [reflex](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex) or another intervening physiological change without first being absorbed into the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood).** It is substantially right for the direct action of a circulating [drug](../../../pharmacology.md#drug) on a distant target, but does not describe every remote effect of treatment.

Three mechanisms should be distinguished:

- **Direct action after [drug absorption](../../../pharmacology.md#drug-absorption).** An orally administered [drug](../../../pharmacology.md#drug) that acts directly in the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) must gain access to that tissue. Absorption from the [gastrointestinal tract](../../../physiology.md#gastrointestinal-tract), transport in the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), and passage to the target explain such a systemic action. Changing the route changes the first step: a subcutaneous medicine must leave its local depot, whereas an intravenous medicine is introduced directly into the circulation. The literal word “absorbed” is consequently too restrictive even before considering [reflexes](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex).
- **Local action followed by a neural response.** Stimulation of [sensory nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#sensory-nerve) at one surface can cause a response elsewhere through the [nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#nervous-system). Gastric stimulation can evoke [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting), including coordinated movement of respiratory and abdominal [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle); sensory input from the [gastrointestinal tract](../../../physiology.md#gastrointestinal-tract) reaches brainstem circuits through [vagal afferents](../../../neuroscience.md#vagal-afferent). No requirement follows that the original gastric irritant must itself circulate to every contracting [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle). A swallowed emetic can have more than one mechanism, however: an observed instance of [vomiting](../../../physiology.md#vomiting) alone does not prove that absorption played no part.[“Pathophysiology of N&V”; visceral afferent inputs and output to abdominal musculature and respiratory centres](../../../oncology-bibliography.md#nausea-and-vomiting-related-to-cancer-treatment-by-the-national-cancer-institute)
- **Local action followed by a change in the body's contents.** As a present-day illustration, an osmotic [laxative](../../../pharmacology.md#laxative) can retain [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) in the intestinal lumen and promote evacuation with limited systemic absorption. Any secondary change in fluid balance or symptoms is then a consequence of the local intestinal action, not proof that the laxative has directly acted on a remote organ. The [polyethylene glycol 3350](../../../pharmacology.md#polyethylene-glycol-3350) information from MedlinePlus describes retention of [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) in stool; the regulatory monograph records minimal absorption.[“Why is this medication prescribed?”; retention of water in stool](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#polyethylene-glycol-3350-by-medlineplus)[“Action and clinical pharmacology”, p. 9; mechanism and pharmacokinetics of PEG 3350](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#peglyte-prescribing-information)

The distinction is between transport of the medicinal substance and transmission of its consequences. A [reflex](../../../neuroscience.md#reflex) transmits activity in [neurons](../../../neuron.md); a locally acting intestinal agent changes a physical condition; a circulating [drug](../../../pharmacology.md#drug) delivers molecules to its distant target. The same remedy may combine these routes, and an apparently rapid effect does not by itself settle which route caused it.

A useful experimental design would therefore compare remote responses with measurements of the agent in [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), and would test the proposed neural pathway separately. In an appropriate physiological preparation, recording the response while interrupting a relevant afferent pathway can test whether that pathway is necessary; preserving the circulation while interrupting nerve conduction distinguishes neural transmission from vascular transport. Conversely, a remote effect after vascular transfer supports a circulating agent, provided contamination and other transferred mediators are controlled. Failure to detect a [drug](../../../pharmacology.md#drug) is only as strong as the analytical detection limit.

**Absorption is one route to remote action, not a universal prerequisite for every distant physiological consequence.** The historical language “animal economy” means the functioning organism; it should not conceal the distinction between a direct [pharmacological effect](../../../pharmacology.md#pharmacological-effect) and a secondary [physiological response](../../../physiology.md#physiological-response).
