## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Describe the movements of the Thorax during ordinary respiration. Mention the muscles concerned in producing them, describing the part which each takes in effecting the general result.

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**Ordinary inspiration is active enlargement of the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax); ordinary expiration is chiefly passive recoil after the inspiratory [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) relax.** The movements increase and decrease the vertical, anteroposterior and transverse dimensions together. Their relative prominence varies with posture and the individual; there is no exclusive division into abdominal and costal kinds of [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation).

The principal inspiratory [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) is the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm). Its peripheral fibres shorten towards the central tendon, lowering and flattening its domes. This increases the vertical dimension of the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax) and displaces the abdominal contents downwards and forwards, making the relaxed abdominal wall project. Its costal attachments also act on the lower [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib): with abdominal support opposing unlimited descent, shortening can lift and expand the lower costal margin. Thus the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) does more than pull down a piston. Its motor supply is through the [phrenic nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#phrenic-nerve), principally from cervical segments C3–C5.

The [external intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#external-intercostal-muscle) elevate the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) and help keep the intercostal spaces from being drawn inwards as [pleural pressure](../../../physiology.md#pleural-pressure) falls. The interchondral, or parasternal, portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#internal-intercostal-muscle) also contribute to inspiration. It would therefore be inaccurate to label every part of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#internal-intercostal-muscle) expiratory. Coordinated activity braces the [rib cage](../../../anatomy.md#rib-cage) so that the shortening of one layer moves the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) rather than simply deforming the spaces between them.

Rotation about the posterior articulations raises the anterior ends of the upper [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) and the [sternum](../../../anatomy.md#sternum), increasing the anteroposterior diameter: the usual pump-handle description. The more laterally directed portions of the lower [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) rise and move outwards, increasing the transverse diameter: the bucket-handle description. These are descriptions of components of an oblique movement, not two independent sets of hinges. The [scalene muscles](../../../anatomy.md#scalene-muscle) can stabilize and elevate the uppermost [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib), with recruitment varying in quiet [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). The [sternocleidomastoid muscle](../../../anatomy.md#sternocleidomastoid-muscle), when the head is fixed, is an accessory elevator of the [sternum](../../../anatomy.md#sternum) in greater inspiratory effort; it is not required for an ordinary quiet breath.

The [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) follow the expanding chest wall because their surfaces remain coupled to it through the thin fluid-filled [pleural cavity](../../../anatomy.md#pleural-cavity). More negative [pleural pressure](../../../physiology.md#pleural-pressure) increases the distending [transpulmonary pressure](../../../physiology.md#transpulmonary-pressure); enlargement of the [alveoli](../../../anatomy.md#alveolus) briefly makes [alveolar pressure](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-pressure) lower than [atmospheric pressure](../../../pressure.md#atmospheric-pressure), so air enters. At the end of inspiration, airflow stops when these last two [pressures](../../../pressure.md) equalize. During quiet expiration, relaxation of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) and inspiratory [intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#intercostal-muscle) allows the stretched [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) and chest-wall system to recoil towards their resting balance. The domes rise, the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib) and [sternum](../../../anatomy.md#sternum) descend, and [alveolar pressure](../../../physiology.md#alveolar-pressure) becomes slightly positive relative to [atmospheric pressure](../../../pressure.md#atmospheric-pressure) while air leaves. Active [abdominal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#abdominal-muscle) and the interosseous portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#internal-intercostal-muscle) become important in forced expiration, rather than supplying the main force in quiet expiration.[Mechanisms of Breathing and Pulmonary Ventilation; Figures 22.16–22.17](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/22-3-the-process-of-breathing)

A simple mechanical demonstration uses two elastic bags in an airtight transparent vessel, connected through its stopper to the air, with a movable rubber sheet forming the base. Pulling the sheet down expands the vessel and inflates the bags; letting it return allows deflation. A side-tube [manometer](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#manometer) can show the associated [pressure](../../../pressure.md) change. This illustrates expansion by lowering the floor, but its rigid vessel cannot demonstrate the movements of the [ribs](../../../anatomy.md#rib), the action of the [intercostal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#intercostal-muscle), or the real geometry of the [pleural cavity](../../../anatomy.md#pleural-cavity). Observing lower-chest widening and abdominal displacement during quiet [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) supplies those missing observations without confusing the model with the anatomy.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the origin, course, and connexions of the Pneumogastric nerves. What results have been observed to follow section of these nerves ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The two [pneumogastric nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) are the right and left [vagus nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve), the tenth [cranial nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#cranial-nerve): mixed sensory, branchial motor and [parasympathetic](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic) pathways from the [medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience.md#medulla-oblongata) to the neck, [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax) and abdomen.** A useful description separates their apparent origin at the surface of the [brain](../../../neuroscience.md#brain) from the origins and terminations of their component fibres.

Rootlets emerge in the groove behind the [inferior olive](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-olive), between the [glossopharyngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#glossopharyngeal-nerve) above and the accessory rootlets below. Branchial motor fibres arise chiefly in the [nucleus ambiguus](../../../neuroscience.md#nucleus-ambiguus); preganglionic [parasympathetic](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic) fibres arise in the [dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus](../../../neuroscience.md#dorsal-motor-nucleus-of-the-vagus) and, particularly for cardiac control, the [nucleus ambiguus](../../../neuroscience.md#nucleus-ambiguus). Visceral sensory fibres have their cell bodies chiefly in the [inferior vagal ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-vagal-ganglion) and terminate centrally in the [nucleus of the solitary tract](../../../neuroscience.md#nucleus-of-the-solitary-tract); somatic sensory fibres associated with the [superior vagal ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-vagal-ganglion) project to the [spinal trigeminal nucleus](../../../neuroscience.md#spinal-trigeminal-nucleus). Consequently a sensory fibre is not accurately described as originating in a motor nucleus simply because its [peripheral nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#peripheral-nerve) emerges nearby.[Introduction and Structure and Function: branchial motor output and cardiac vagal control](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neuroanatomy-nucleus-ambiguus-statpearls)

Each [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) leaves the [skull](../../../anatomy.md#skull) through the [jugular foramen](../../../anatomy.md#jugular-foramen), with the [glossopharyngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#glossopharyngeal-nerve) and [accessory nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#accessory-nerve). It bears the small [superior vagal ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-vagal-ganglion) at the foramen and the larger [inferior vagal ganglion](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-vagal-ganglion) below it. Connections include communications with adjacent [cranial nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#cranial-nerve) and the cervical [sympathetic nervous system](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic-nervous-system). Traditional descriptions assign fibres joining the [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) from the cranial part of the [accessory nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#accessory-nerve) to the latter [peripheral nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#peripheral-nerve); their modern classification is more nuanced and does not change the peripheral branches being described. In the neck the [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) descends in the [carotid sheath](../../../anatomy.md#carotid-sheath), behind and between the [internal jugular vein](../../../anatomy.md#internal-jugular-vein) and the internal, then common, [carotid artery](../../../anatomy.md#carotid-artery).

Its meningeal and auricular branches supply small sensory territories of the cranial covering and external ear. Pharyngeal branches contribute to the [pharyngeal plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#pharyngeal-plexus), supplying much of the [soft palate](../../../anatomy.md#soft-palate) and [pharynx](../../../anatomy.md#pharynx); the [stylopharyngeus muscle](../../../anatomy.md#stylopharyngeus-muscle) belongs to the [glossopharyngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#glossopharyngeal-nerve) and the [tensor veli palatini muscle](../../../anatomy.md#tensor-veli-palatini-muscle) to the mandibular division of the [trigeminal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#trigeminal-nerve). The internal branch of the [superior laryngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-laryngeal-nerve) supplies sensation above the [vocal folds](../../../anatomy.md#vocal-fold); its external branch supplies the [cricothyroid muscle](../../../anatomy.md#cricothyroid-muscle). The [recurrent laryngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#recurrent-laryngeal-nerve) supplies the other intrinsic [laryngeal muscles](../../../anatomy.md#laryngeal-muscle) and sensation below the [vocal folds](../../../anatomy.md#vocal-fold). The right [recurrent laryngeal nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#recurrent-laryngeal-nerve) loops beneath the [right subclavian artery](../../../anatomy.md#right-subclavian-artery); the left loops under the [aortic arch](../../../physiology.md#aortic-arch) beside the [ligamentum arteriosum](../../../physiology.md#ligamentum-arteriosum). Both return towards the [larynx](../../../anatomy.md#larynx) near the groove between [trachea](../../../anatomy.md#trachea) and [oesophagus](../../../anatomy.md#oesophagus).[Structure and Function; Vagus Nerve Branches; Muscles](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neuroanatomy-cranial-nerve-10-vagus-nerve-statpearls)

In the [thorax](../../../anatomy.md#thorax), both [vagus nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) pass behind the roots of the [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung), contrasting with the anterior course of the [phrenic nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#phrenic-nerve). Cardiac branches join the [cardiac plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#cardiac-plexus); pulmonary branches join the [pulmonary plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#pulmonary-plexus). The trunks break up into the [oesophageal plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#oesophageal-plexus), then collect principally as an anterior trunk derived from the left [vagus nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) and a posterior trunk derived from the right. Through the [oesophageal hiatus](../../../anatomy.md#oesophageal-hiatus) they reach the [stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach); hepatic and coeliac branches distribute to abdominal viscera. Their [parasympathetic](../../../neuroscience.md#parasympathetic) territory includes foregut and midgut derivatives, extending into the proximal [colon](../../../physiology.md#colon), rather than every abdominal and pelvic organ.[Structure and Function: thoracic course and oesophageal plexus](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neuroanatomy-cranial-nerve-10-vagus-nerve-statpearls) This extensive distribution explains why the level of experimental section is indispensable information.

The main consequences of section can be organized by the interrupted functions:

- [Heart](../../../physiology.md#heart): cutting both cervical [vagus nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) removes tonic inhibition and usually increases [heart rate](../../../physiology.md#heart-rate). The [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) continues beating because its rhythm is intrinsic. Electrical stimulation of the peripheral cut end can slow the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart), whereas stimulation of the central end tests reflex effects through the [brainstem](../../../neuroscience.md#brainstem). Cutting a pathway and stimulating its intact output are different experiments.
- [Breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation): bilateral cervical section commonly produces slower, deeper breaths in experimental mammals. Sensory information from the [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) has been interrupted; the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) is not directly paralysed, because its motor [peripheral nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#peripheral-nerve) are the [phrenic nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#phrenic-nerve). Persistence of [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation) establishes that the [vagus nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#vagus-nerve) modify the respiratory rhythm rather than being its sole generator. The subsequently named [Hering-Breuer inflation reflex](../../../physiology.md#hering-breuer-inflation-reflex) provides a later explanation of one component of this feedback; its formal description belongs to 1868, after this examination.[Discussion of the 1868 lung inflation and deflation experiments](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#reflexes-from-the-lungs-and-airways-historical-perspective)
- [Larynx](../../../anatomy.md#larynx) and [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung): section proximal to the [recurrent laryngeal nerves](../../../neuroscience.md#recurrent-laryngeal-nerve) can impair opening and closure of the [vocal folds](../../../anatomy.md#vocal-fold), causing altered voice, obstruction and impaired airway protection. Classical bilateral cervical experiments sometimes ended in death with pulmonary congestion, [oedema](../../../biology.md#oedema) or [aspiration](../../../physiology.md#aspiration) changes. Such outcomes must be distinguished from an immediate necessary arrest of [breathing](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation): species, age, lesion level and airway management affect them.
- [Stomach](../../../physiology.md#stomach): bilateral section can reduce vagally driven [gastric acid secretion](../../../physiology.md#gastric-acid-secretion) and disturb gastric motor coordination and emptying. Nevertheless [digestion](../../../physiology.md#digestion) and [nutrient absorption](../../../physiology.md#nutrient-absorption) are not abolished as chemical possibilities: [enteric nervous system](../../../neuroscience.md#enteric-nervous-system) activity, local stimuli and [hormones](../../../signal-transduction.md#hormone) remain. Likewise, interruption of gastric sensory fibres does not establish that the entire experience of hunger originates in them.

A discriminating historical experiment would record respiratory movements and [heart rate](../../../physiology.md#heart-rate) before and after section, distinguish unilateral from bilateral section, and compare an intact airway with an airway bypass. A [tracheal cannula](../../../physiology.md#tracheal-cannula) can separate obstruction at the [glottis](../../../anatomy.md#glottis) from changes in the respiratory rhythm, while the central-versus-peripheral stimulation comparison separates incoming reflex traffic from outgoing motor effects. Later separate-lung recordings confirmed that a change following unilateral section could affect both [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung), supporting a central reflex interpretation rather than simple local paralysis.[Summary and Conclusions, points 1–4; separate recordings of the two lungs, 1927](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#a-study-of-the-hering-breuer-reflex) The gastric distinction is also borne out by measurements of persistent acidity after surgical [vagotomy](../../../neuroscience.md#vagotomy), despite major reductions in stimulated [gastric acid secretion](../../../physiology.md#gastric-acid-secretion).[Abstract: hourly intragastric sampling, seven patients, four years after operation](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#twenty-four-hour-gastric-acidity-after-vagotomy)

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

‘In the vegetable kingdom solar force is absorbed in the production of our food ; in the animal kingdom it is liberated by the eremacausis of our fat and glands and muscle.’ Odling, _Lect. on Animal Chemistry._ Explain and illustrate these statements.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**The defensible core of [William Odling](../../../chemistry.md#william-odling)'s statement is a transfer of [energy](../../../energy.md): sunlight helps build reduced food material, and its subsequent [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) can supply animal [heat](../../../heat.md) and [work](../../../energy.md#work).** Here solar force means solar [energy](../../../energy.md), and [eremacausis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#eremacausis) means slow [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation), rather than a special vital agent. The historical wording, from his 1866 lectures, must be qualified where it identifies the fuel with the substance of the working [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) or contrasts plant and animal [metabolism](../../../metabolism.md) too absolutely.[Lecture IV, discussion of solar energy and animal oxidation; see also the concluding recapitulation](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#lectures-on-animal-chemistry-by-william-odling)

In [oxygenic photosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#oxygenic-photosynthesis), light absorbed by [chlorophyll](../../../metabolism.md#chlorophyll) supports conversion of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) into reduced organic material, with release of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). An overall account, written for one [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) unit, is

$$
6\mathrm{CO_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}+\text{light energy}
\longrightarrow \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\mathrm{O_2}.
$$

This summarizes several stages, rather than describing a single elementary [chemical reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md). The evolved [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) comes from [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). The product can furnish [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch), other [carbohydrates](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbohydrate) and precursors for [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) and [protein](../../../protein.md); the latter additionally requires a source of [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen). A human obtains these materials directly from plants or through animals that have eaten plants. Thus a grain meal and the flesh of a grain-fed animal can trace much of their chemical [energy](../../../energy.md) to the same original sunlight.[Main Structures and Summary of Photosynthesis; photosynthetic products and their passage through food chains](../../../biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/8-1-overview-of-photosynthesis)

The reverse overall balance for complete aerobic utilization of [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) is

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}+6\mathrm{O_2}
\longrightarrow 6\mathrm{CO_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}+\text{released energy}.
$$

Similarly a representative [fatty acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#fatty-acid), [palmitic acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#palmitic-acid), has the complete [oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) balance

$$
\mathrm{C_{16}H_{32}O_2}+23\mathrm{O_2}
\longrightarrow16\mathrm{CO_2}+16\mathrm{H_2O}+\text{released energy}.
$$

Both equations conserve each chemical element. The relevant [energy](../../../energy.md) difference belongs to the complete set of reactants and products, including [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen); merely breaking a bond does not supply net [energy](../../../energy.md). In modern terms, controlled [cellular respiration](../../../metabolism.md#cellular-respiration) couples part of the available chemical [free energy](../../../thermodynamic-potential.md#thermodynamic-free-energy) to [ATP](../../../molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) formation. [ATP hydrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#atp-hydrolysis) can then support [muscle contraction](../../../anatomy.md#muscle-contraction), active transport and [biosynthesis](../../../metabolism.md#biosynthesis); some [energy](../../../energy.md) is transferred to [heat](../../../heat.md). The process occurs through enzyme-mediated steps, without a flame, although complete combustion in a [calorimeter](../../../calorimetry.md#calorimeter) can help measure the corresponding chemical [energy](../../../energy.md) change.

[Glands](../../../anatomy.md#gland) and [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) continually replace constituents, and their [proteins](../../../protein.md) can be degraded. [Amino acids](../../../chemical-substance.md#amino-acid) can contribute carbon skeletons to [energy metabolism](../../../biochemistry.md#energy-metabolism), while their [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) is disposed of largely as [urea](../../../chemical-substance.md#urea). But ordinary [muscle contraction](../../../anatomy.md#muscle-contraction) does not require proportional destruction and rebuilding of the contractile [protein](../../../protein.md) at every stroke. [Carbohydrates](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbohydrate) and [fats](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) are major fuels for [muscle](../../../anatomy.md#muscle), and a [gland](../../../anatomy.md#gland) can use those fuels to support secretion. The historical picture of burning the organ's own substance is therefore an inadequate general explanation.[Protein as metabolic fuel; amino-acid carbon skeletons and the urea cycle](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/24-4-protein-metabolism)

This correction was already an experimental issue before the exam. [Edward Frankland](../../../chemistry.md#edward-frankland)'s 1866 discussion compared the mechanical [work](../../../energy.md#work) of climbing with the [energy](../../../energy.md) that could be assigned to [protein](../../../protein.md) destruction estimated from urinary [nitrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen). The latter was insufficient to account for all the [work](../../../energy.md#work). His combustion measurements also distinguished the [heat](../../../heat.md) from burning a sample completely from the smaller amount available when nitrogenous material leaves the animal as still-combustible [urea](../../../chemical-substance.md#urea). Measuring external [work](../../../energy.md#work), urinary products and calorific values gives the hypothesis a quantitative test; it is more informative than simply pointing out that an active animal gets warm.[pp. 394–395, criticism of tissue-consumption doctrine; pp. 401–406, calorific measurements and comparison with climbing work, 1866](../../../biochemistry-and-biophysics-bibliography.md#on-the-source-of-muscular-power-by-edward-frankland)

Finally, plants themselves carry out [cellular respiration](../../../metabolism.md#cellular-respiration), and not every ecosystem obtains its input from sunlight: [chemolithotrophy](../../../metabolism.md#chemolithotrophy) is an alternative. The plant-animal contrast is useful for following [energy](../../../energy.md) through an ordinary food chain, but it is not an exhaustive division of biochemical capabilities. [Conservation of energy](../../../mathematical-physics.md#conservation-of-energy) also requires the bookkeeping form

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm food}=W_{\rm external}+Q_{\rm outward}+\Delta E_{\rm body}}
$$

for an idealized interval in which $E_{\rm food}$ is the metabolizable food input, with energy lost in excreta already subtracted and other material-energy exchanges neglected. Here $W_{\rm external}$ is external [work](../../../energy.md#work), $Q_{\rm outward}$ is net outward [heat transfer](../../../heat.md#heat-transfer), and $\Delta E_{\rm body}$ includes changes in stored chemical and other [energy](../../../energy.md). The living body transforms [energy](../../../energy.md); it does not create it.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What are the functions of the Liver, and of its secretion ?

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

**The [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) is both a metabolic processing organ and the producer of [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile).** These functions must be distinguished: much of what the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) contributes to the body enters the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), whereas [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) is its secretion into the [digestive system](../../../physiology.md#digestive-system).

[Portal venous blood](../../../physiology.md#portal-venous-blood) brings absorbed materials from the alimentary organs to the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver), while the [hepatic artery](../../../physiology.md#hepatic-artery) supplies oxygenated [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood). Within the [hepatic sinusoids](../../../physiology.md#hepatic-sinusoid), the incoming [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) encounters [hepatocytes](../../../physiology.md#hepatocyte) and scavenging [Kupffer cells](../../../physiology.md#kupffer-cell). [Hepatocytes](../../../physiology.md#hepatocyte) take up, transform, store and release substances; their [bile canaliculi](../../../physiology.md#bile-canaliculi) conduct [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) towards the [bile ducts](../../../physiology.md#bile-duct). The organ's position therefore makes it a useful regulator between absorption from a meal and supply to the rest of the body.

Its main non-biliary functions include the following:

- [Carbohydrate metabolism](../../../metabolism.md#carbohydrate-metabolism): after a meal, [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose) can be stored as [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen) by [glycogenesis](../../../metabolism.md#glycogenesis); during fasting, [glycogenolysis](../../../metabolism.md#glycogenolysis) and [gluconeogenesis](../../../metabolism.md#gluconeogenesis) help maintain [blood glucose](../../../physiology.md#blood-sugar). The [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) therefore both removes and supplies [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose), depending on the physiological state.
- [Lipid metabolism](../../../metabolism.md#lipid-metabolism): the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) handles [fatty acids](../../../chemical-substance.md#fatty-acid), synthesizes and exports [triglycerides](../../../chemical-substance.md#triglyceride) in [lipoproteins](../../../metabolism.md#lipoprotein), makes [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol), and can produce [ketone bodies](../../../metabolism.md#ketone-body) during fasting. Storage of [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) is not its sole function in this domain.
- [Amino acid metabolism](../../../metabolism.md#amino-acid-metabolism): [transamination](../../../metabolism.md#transamination) and [deamination](../../../metabolism.md#deamination) redistribute or remove amino groups; the [urea cycle](../../../metabolism.md#urea-cycle) converts much potentially toxic [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) nitrogen into [urea](../../../chemical-substance.md#urea) for excretion by the [kidneys](../../../anatomy.md#kidney). The [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) synthesizes major [plasma proteins](../../../physiology.md#plasma-protein), including [albumin](../../../physiology.md#albumin) and many [coagulation factors](../../../physiology.md#coagulation-factor).
- Storage and regulation: stores include [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen), several [vitamins](../../../biology.md#vitamin) and [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron). The [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) also modifies many [hormones](../../../signal-transduction.md#hormone) and administered chemicals. Such [biotransformation](../../../metabolism.md#biotransformation) can aid elimination, but the word detoxification must not imply that every product is less toxic than its precursor.
- Clearance and development: [Kupffer cells](../../../physiology.md#kupffer-cell) remove material from the incoming [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) by [phagocytosis](../../../cell-membrane.md#phagocytosis). The fetal [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) is an important site of [haematopoiesis](../../../physiology.md#haematopoiesis); this is a developmental function, rather than the ordinary principal source of adult [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell).

This modern list extends beyond the knowledge and biochemical vocabulary of 1867. It is consistent with the organ's combined metabolic, storage and clearance roles, rather than treating [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) production as the whole explanation.[Introduction; Mechanism; Related Testing: metabolic, synthetic, storage and clearance functions](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#physiology-liver-statpearls)

[Bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) contains [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), [electrolytes](../../../electrochemistry.md#electrolyte), [bile salts](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt), [phospholipids](../../../chemical-substance.md#phospholipid), [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) and [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin) conjugates. [Hepatocytes](../../../physiology.md#hepatocyte) form the primary secretion; ductal [epithelium](../../../anatomy.md#epithelium) modifies it. The [gallbladder](../../../physiology.md#gallbladder) stores and concentrates [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) between meals and releases it into the [duodenum](../../../physiology.md#duodenum). Production of [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) must therefore be distinguished from its intermittent delivery to a meal.

Its two central functions are **assistance with [lipid digestion](../../../physiology.md#lipid-digestion) and [nutrient absorption](../../../physiology.md#nutrient-absorption), and excretion of selected waste substances**. Amphipathic [bile salts](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt) help disperse dietary [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) and form [mixed micelles](../../../physiology.md#mixed-micelle) carrying products of [lipid digestion](../../../physiology.md#lipid-digestion) to the absorptive surface of the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine). They thereby promote uptake of [fatty acids](../../../chemical-substance.md#fatty-acid), [monoglycerides](../../../chemical-substance.md#monoglyceride), [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) and [fat-soluble vitamins](../../../biology.md#fat-soluble-vitamin). [Bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) is not itself a source of [pancreatic lipase](../../../enzyme.md#pancreatic-lipase), nor does emulsification chemically hydrolyse [triglycerides](../../../chemical-substance.md#triglyceride). Biliary [bilirubin](../../../physiology.md#bilirubin), produced through [haem](../../../chemical-substance.md#haem) breakdown, provides an excretory route; [cholesterol](../../../chemical-substance.md#cholesterol) and selected other substances also leave in [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile). Most secreted [bile salts](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt) are recovered, especially in the terminal [ileum](../../../physiology.md#ileum), and return through the [portal vein](../../../physiology.md#portal-vein): the [enterohepatic circulation](../../../physiology.md#enterohepatic-circulation) conserves the useful detergent.[The Liver; Composition of Bile; The Gallbladder](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/23-6-accessory-organs-in-digestion-the-liver-pancreas-and-gallbladder)

Two comparisons reveal why both sorts of function matter. First, compare a fresh [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) extract with an extract prepared after an appropriate controlled interval: testing for reducing sugar and isolating [glycogen](../../../metabolism.md#glycogen) distinguishes a stored precursor from newly liberated [glucose](../../../chemical-substance.md#glucose). A reliable interpretation needs prompt sampling and controls because post-mortem [glycogenolysis](../../../metabolism.md#glycogenolysis) can change the result after the animal has died; detecting sugar in an organ alone does not prove where all of it was made. Secondly, in classical [biliary fistula](../../../physiology.md#biliary-fistula) observations, diverting [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) away from the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine) while collecting it separately allowed its output to be measured and the loss of normal [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) assimilation to be examined. Comparing faecal [fat](../../../chemical-substance.md#fat) with and without delivery of [bile](../../../physiology.md#bile) distinguishes a digestive contribution from a merely excretory one. The modern explanation is that loss of intestinal [bile salts](../../../physiology.md#bile-salt) impairs [mixed micelle](../../../physiology.md#mixed-micelle) formation, while [jaundice](../../../biology.md#jaundice) from retained biliary pigment is a separate consequence of failed pigment elimination.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Write an accurate description of the fourth Ventricle of the Brain.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**The [fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#fourth-ventricle) is the cavity of the hindbrain between the dorsal surfaces of the [pons](../../../neuroscience.md#pons) and upper [medulla oblongata](../../../neuroscience.md#medulla-oblongata) anteriorly and the [cerebellum](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebellum) posteriorly.** It is continuous above with the [cerebral aqueduct](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-aqueduct) and below, at the [obex](../../../neuroscience.md#obex), with the [central canal of the spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#central-canal-of-the-spinal-cord). Its floor is diamond-shaped when viewed from behind, while its roof rises dorsally towards the [fastigium](../../../neuroscience.md#fastigium).

The floor, the [rhomboid fossa](../../../neuroscience.md#rhomboid-fossa), consists of an upper pontine triangle and a lower medullary triangle. Its upper apex points towards the [cerebral aqueduct](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-aqueduct); its lower apex is the [obex](../../../neuroscience.md#obex); the two lateral angles continue into the [lateral recesses of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#lateral-recesses-of-the-fourth-ventricle). A median groove divides it into right and left halves. On each side, the [medial eminence](../../../neuroscience.md#medial-eminence) lies medial to the [sulcus limitans](../../../neuroscience.md#sulcus-limitans), which separates predominantly motor territories medially from sensory territories laterally. The [striae medullares of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#striae-medullares-of-the-fourth-ventricle) cross the floor near the junction of its pontine and medullary portions; their extent varies.

Several surface features identify deeper relations. In the pontine part, the [facial colliculus](../../../neuroscience.md#facial-colliculus) overlies the [abducens nucleus](../../../neuroscience.md#abducens-nucleus) and the internal looping course of fibres of the [facial nerve](../../../neuroscience.md#facial-nerve); it is not the exposed [facial motor nucleus](../../../neuroscience.md#facial-motor-nucleus). The upper part of the [sulcus limitans](../../../neuroscience.md#sulcus-limitans) has the [superior fovea](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-fovea), and near its rostral lateral region lies the [locus coeruleus](../../../neuroscience.md#locus-coeruleus). Laterally, the [vestibular area](../../../neuroscience.md#vestibular-area) overlies the [vestibular nuclei](../../../neuroscience.md#vestibular-nucleus). In the lower triangle, the [hypoglossal trigone](../../../neuroscience.md#hypoglossal-trigone) overlies the [hypoglossal nucleus](../../../neuroscience.md#hypoglossal-nucleus), and the [vagal trigone](../../../neuroscience.md#vagal-trigone), lateral to it, overlies the [dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus](../../../neuroscience.md#dorsal-motor-nucleus-of-the-vagus). The [area postrema](../../../neuroscience.md#area-postrema) lies still farther caudolaterally, separated from the [vagal trigone](../../../neuroscience.md#vagal-trigone) by the [funiculus separans](../../../neuroscience.md#funiculus-separans). These are landmarks and underlying relations, not claims that the [cranial nerve nuclei](../../../neuroscience.md#cranial-nerve-nucleus) lie loose within the cavity.[Introduction; Structure and Function; Surgical Considerations: floor and relations](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#neuroanatomy-fourth-ventricle-statpearls)

The upper lateral boundaries are formed by the [superior cerebellar peduncles](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-cerebellar-peduncle). The lower lateral boundaries relate to the [inferior cerebellar peduncles](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-cerebellar-peduncle) and adjacent dorsal medullary margins. The lateral recess on each side curves around the [inferior cerebellar peduncle](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-cerebellar-peduncle) towards its external opening near the [flocculus](../../../neuroscience.md#flocculus).

The roof has superior and inferior slopes. Superiorly the thin [superior medullary velum](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-medullary-velum) spans the [superior cerebellar peduncles](../../../neuroscience.md#superior-cerebellar-peduncle), with the [lingula of the cerebellum](../../../neuroscience.md#lingula-of-the-cerebellum) on its dorsal surface. Inferiorly, the roof relates to the [inferior medullary velum](../../../neuroscience.md#inferior-medullary-velum), [nodule of the cerebellum](../../../neuroscience.md#nodule-of-the-cerebellum) and thin [tela choroidea](../../../neuroscience.md#tela-choroidea). Vascular folds of the [tela choroidea](../../../neuroscience.md#tela-choroidea) carry the [choroid plexus of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#choroid-plexus-of-the-fourth-ventricle), with median and lateral extensions. The cavity has an [ependymal](../../../neuroscience.md#ependyma) lining; the [choroid plexus](../../../neuroscience.md#choroid-plexus) has its specialized epithelial covering and produces [cerebrospinal fluid](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebrospinal-fluid).

There are three important exits to the [subarachnoid space](../../../anatomy.md#subarachnoid-space): one [median aperture of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#median-aperture-of-the-fourth-ventricle) through the inferior roof, opening towards the [cisterna magna](../../../anatomy.md#cisterna-magna), and two [lateral apertures of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#lateral-apertures-of-the-fourth-ventricle) at the ends of the [lateral recesses of the fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#lateral-recesses-of-the-fourth-ventricle). These must be distinguished from the inlet through the [cerebral aqueduct](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebral-aqueduct) and the inferior continuity with the [central canal of the spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#central-canal-of-the-spinal-cord). Most [cerebrospinal fluid](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebrospinal-fluid) leaving the [fourth ventricle](../../../neuroscience.md#fourth-ventricle) reaches the [subarachnoid space](../../../anatomy.md#subarachnoid-space), not the narrow adult [central canal of the spinal cord](../../../neuroscience.md#central-canal-of-the-spinal-cord).

The three-dimensional arrangement explains the method of examination: reflecting the [cerebellum](../../../neuroscience.md#cerebellum) and opening the delicate roof exposes the [rhomboid fossa](../../../neuroscience.md#rhomboid-fossa), but destroys part of the structure one is trying to describe. Studying an intact roof, then the exposed floor, and using a median section to locate the [fastigium](../../../neuroscience.md#fastigium) avoids interpreting a dissected cavity as naturally open behind. The modern nomenclature is useful here; not every fine landmark or physiological interpretation in this account should be read as an assertion about what an 1867 examiner required.[Gross-dissection study: dorsal brainstem, rhomboid fossa and fourth-ventricular approaches](../../../neuroscience-bibliography.md#morphometric-assesment-of-the-external-anatomy-of-fourth-ventricle-and-dorsal-brainstem-in-fresh-cadavers)

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

Examine into the nature of the forces which assist the Heart's action in maintaining the circulation of the blood.

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**The [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart) supplies the principal continuing mechanical input to [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation); arterial recoil, respiratory movements and the action of [skeletal muscles](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle) redistribute and supplement that input.** They have different sources of [energy](../../../energy.md) and should not all be called independent propulsive powers. The [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient) remaining after passage through the [capillaries](../../../physiology.md#capillary) is still a consequence of the upstream pump.

**Arterial elasticity.** During ventricular [systole](../../../physiology.md#systole), ejected [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) expands the [aorta](../../../physiology.md#aorta) and large [arteries](../../../physiology.md#artery), storing [elastic potential energy](../../../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy) in their walls. During [diastole](../../../physiology.md#diastole), recoil continues to drive [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) into the peripheral vessels while closure of the [aortic valve](../../../physiology.md#aortic-valve) limits return towards the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart). This reduces the intermittency of flow downstream. The recoil is a temporary store of cardiac [work](../../../energy.md#work), rather than a fresh source of [energy](../../../energy.md). A vessel that is too rigid provides less of this smoothing; a compliant vessel with an open backward outlet would not provide the same useful result.

**The skeletal-muscle pump.** Contracting [skeletal muscles](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle) compress adjacent [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein). [Venous valves](../../../physiology.md#venous-valve) make the displacement preferentially towards the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart); when compression ceases, refilling permits another stroke. Walking therefore aids return from dependent limbs. The source of the added [work](../../../energy.md#work) is [muscle contraction](../../../anatomy.md#muscle-contraction), and the valves guide it without producing [energy](../../../energy.md). Merely possessing valves cannot make a stationary column circulate.

**The respiratory pump.** During inspiration, falling intrathoracic [pressure](../../../pressure.md) and displacement of the abdominal contents favour a [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient) from abdominal and peripheral [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) towards the thoracic [veins](../../../physiology.md#vein) and right side of the [heart](../../../physiology.md#heart). The [respiratory diaphragm](../../../anatomy.md#respiratory-diaphragm) thus participates indirectly in [venous return](../../../physiology.md#venous-return) as well as [pulmonary ventilation](../../../physiology.md#pulmonary-ventilation). The exact flow varies with posture, vessel collapse and the phase of the respiratory cycle; positive-pressure inflation does not reproduce the same mechanical circumstances as spontaneous inspiration.[Venous System; Skeletal Muscle Pump; Respiratory Pump](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-2-blood-flow-blood-pressure-and-resistance)

**Venous tone and cardiac filling.** [Sympathetic](../../../physiology.md#sympathetic) contraction of venous [smooth muscle](../../../physiology.md#smooth-muscle) can reduce venous storage capacity and transfer [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) towards the central circulation. This alters the volume available for filling rather than requiring a wave of venous peristalsis. Cardiac relaxation, recoil and movement of the atrioventricular plane also affect filling [pressure](../../../pressure.md). Some traditionally described cardiac suction is therefore a feature of the cardiac cycle and mechanical coupling, not a separate organ pulling [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) through every vessel.[Neural Regulation of Blood Flow; sympathetic effects on vessels and venous return](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-4-homeostatic-regulation-of-the-vascular-system)

**Gravity and supposed capillary attraction.** [Gravity](../../../physics.md#gravity) assists a downward column but opposes an upward one. In a complete circuit returning to its original height it supplies no net gravitational [work](../../../energy.md#work); its important practical effects include hydrostatic [pressure](../../../pressure.md) differences and dependent venous pooling. Ordinary [capillary action](../../../surface-tension.md#capillary-action) in an open narrow tube, driven by a liquid meniscus, is not a general pump in a fluid-filled [capillary](../../../physiology.md#capillary) network. Nor does a vague attraction of [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) by [tissues](../../../anatomy.md#tissue-biology) replace a measurable [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient). Local [vasodilation](../../../physiology.md#vasodilation) can increase [perfusion](../../../physiology.md#perfusion) by lowering [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance), but that change does not manufacture the necessary [pressure](../../../pressure.md) source.

For a simple mean-flow description, with $Q$ the volume flow rate, $\Delta P$ the driving [pressure gradient](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#pressure-gradient) and $R$ the effective [vascular resistance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-resistance),

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{\Delta P}{R}}.
$$

This relation distinguishes a source of driving [pressure](../../../pressure.md) from a change of resistance. In a lumped compliant arterial reservoir with [vascular compliance](../../../physiology.md#vascular-compliance) $C$ and no inflow between beats, volume balance gives

$$
C\frac{dP}{dt}=-\frac{P-P_v}{R},\qquad
P(t)-P_v=(P(0)-P_v)e^{-t/(RC)},
$$

where $P_v$ is the downstream [pressure](../../../pressure.md). The [exponential decay](../../../analysis.md#exponential-decay) follows from stored volume leaving through the resistance. It explains continued flow during [diastole](../../../physiology.md#diastole) without claiming that the idealization describes every reflected arterial wave.

A physical demonstration can compare a pulsatile pump feeding a narrow outlet through rigid tubing with the same pump feeding it through an elastic reservoir. Collecting the outflow over the pumping and resting phases reveals the reservoir's smoothing action; a side-arm [manometer](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#manometer) records its falling [pressure](../../../pressure.md). Separately compressing a valved flexible tube shows the role of a peripheral [muscle pump](../../../physiology.md#muscle-pump). Keeping pump stroke and outlet resistance comparable prevents the apparatus from conflating a smoother flow with creation of extra [energy](../../../energy.md).

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

What views are held with regard to the development of blood cells ; (1) in early embryonic, (2) in later life ?

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**An answer appropriate to 1867 must distinguish the early nucleated embryonic [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) from later populations, and report uncertainty about their origin.** Simply stating the modern [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) account would miss the historical force of the request for the views then held.

**Early embryonic life.** In the pre-exam account of [William Senhouse Kirkes](../../../physiology.md#william-senhouse-kirkes), a first generation of red corpuscles arose from embryonic cells in the early vascular territory before or around the establishment of [blood circulation](../../../physiology.md#blood-circulation). The initially colourless cellular material acquired colour, and the early corpuscles were relatively large and nucleated. Division of these early nucleated forms was among the processes considered capable of increasing their number. A second generation subsequently replaced the first. His embryological terminology, including the vegetative layer, should not be silently treated as an exact statement of modern [germ layer](../../../developmental-biology.md#germ-layer) derivation.[p. 69, development of two sets of corpuscles and embryonic origin of the first set; Figure 6](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#handbook-of-physiology-by-william-senhouse-kirkes-5th-edition)

**Later fetal and adult life.** A widely taught hypothesis made pale corpuscles in [lymph](../../../physiology.md#lymph) and [chyle](../../../physiology.md#chyle) precursors of the later [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell). In this scheme the pale cells entered the [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood), acquired colouring matter and underwent changes associated with loss or alteration of the [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus). [Lymph nodes](../../../physiology.md#lymph-node), the [spleen](../../../physiology.md#spleen) and, during development, the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) were consequently central candidates for sites of renewal. Other opinions proposed multiplication of pre-existing red corpuscles by division or segmentation. These were competing interpretations of microscopic appearances, not demonstrations that every adult [leukocyte](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) can become a [red blood cell](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell). The pre-exam historical record explicitly documents both the pale-corpuscle hypothesis and the alternative multiplication hypotheses.[p. 132, Development of the Blood: rival explanations of post-embryonic corpuscle formation](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#recent-advances-in-the-physiology-of-motion-the-senses-generation-and-development-by-william-baly-and-william-senhouse-kirkes)

The modern correction is **successive developmental waves of [haematopoiesis](../../../physiology.md#haematopoiesis), followed chiefly by [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) production after birth**, with distinct committed precursors. Primitive [erythropoiesis](../../../physiology.md#erythropoiesis) begins in extra-embryonic [yolk sac](../../../developmental-biology.md#yolk-sac) blood islands. Definitive blood-forming populations emerge through additional embryonic processes, including [haemogenic endothelium](../../../anatomy.md#haemogenic-endothelium) in the [aorta-gonad-mesonephros region](../../../developmental-biology.md#aorta-gonad-mesonephros-region), and expand in the fetal [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver). [Bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) becomes a major site later in fetal development and is the normal main adult site. The sequence is not a single already mature cell population merely migrating unchanged from organ to organ.[Abstract: organ culture distinguishes autonomous generation from interchange through the circulation, mouse study, 1996](../../../developmental-biology-bibliography.md#definitive-hematopoiesis-is-autonomously-initiated-by-the-agm-region)[Abstract and Introduction: endothelial-to-haematopoietic transition; human stem-cell-derived experimental populations, 2018](../../../developmental-biology-bibliography.md#single-cell-resolution-of-human-hemato-endothelial-cells-defines-transcriptional-signatures-of-hemogenic-endothelium)[Sites of Hemopoiesis; Differentiation of Formed Elements from Stem Cells](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/18-2-production-of-the-formed-elements)

During definitive [erythropoiesis](../../../physiology.md#erythropoiesis), [erythroblasts](../../../physiology.md#erythroblast) divide and accumulate [haemoglobin](../../../protein.md#hemoglobin); the late precursor extrudes its [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus), producing a [reticulocyte](../../../physiology.md#reticulocyte), which matures into a [red blood cell](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell). The large primitive mammalian [erythroblasts](../../../physiology.md#erythroblast) also ultimately undergo enucleation; their transient nucleation does not make them adult [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte).[Abstract: mouse primitive erythroblast maturation and nuclear extrusion, 2008](../../../developmental-biology-bibliography.md#enucleation-of-primitive-erythroid-cells-generates-a-transient-population-of-pyrenocytes-in-the-mammalian-fetus) A mature human [red blood cell](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell) does not normally divide. Adult [haematopoietic stem cells](../../../biological-cell.md#haematopoietic-stem-cell) maintain progenitor populations giving rise to [red blood cells](../../../biological-cell.md#red-blood-cell), [leukocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte) and [megakaryocytes](../../../physiology.md#megakaryocyte); [platelets](../../../physiology.md#platelet) are fragments produced by the last of these, not equivalent nucleated cells. [Lymphocytes](../../../biological-cell.md#lymphocyte) have their own differentiation and maturation pathways, including development in [lymphoid organs](../../../physiology.md#lymphoid-organ). Thus a precursor's pale or lymphoid appearance under a nineteenth-century [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope) is insufficient to identify it as an ordinary mature circulating [leukocyte](../../../biological-cell.md#leukocyte).

Two observations explain both the attraction and the weakness of the older theories. Comparing embryonic and adult [blood](../../../physiology.md#blood) under a [microscope](../../../physics.md#microscope) reveals genuine differences in corpuscle size, colour and nuclear appearance. But a series of different-looking cells in separate samples is not direct observation of one changing into another. Mixing developmental stages, species or preparations can create a false apparent sequence; reagents that reveal or damage the [cell nucleus](../../../biological-cell.md#cell-nucleus) add another complication. A stronger test samples the proposed blood-forming organs and identifies proliferating precursors there, then connects them experimentally to their descendants. The explicit identification of adult [bone marrow](../../../anatomy.md#bone-marrow) as a blood-forming organ in 1868 is therefore a subsequent development, not established background to the December 1867 paper.[Part I, Blood-Circulation, item 1: report of the 1868 preliminary communication on marrow and corpuscle formation](../../../anatomy-and-physiology-bibliography.md#chronicle-of-physiology-january-1869)

↑ **Parent:** [Anatomy and physiology](anatomy-and-physiology.md)

**Original question.**

To what subdivisions of the animal kingdom are the Entozoa referred ? Mention their leading peculiarities of structure and development. Enumerate the species most commonly found in man.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**[Entozoa](../../../ecology.md#entozoa) is a historical grouping of internal animal [parasites](../../../ecology.md#parasite), especially parasitic worms; it is not a single natural modern [clade](../../../evolution.md#clade).** The core human examples belong principally to the [flatworms](../../../organism.md#flatworm), including [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda) and [Trematoda](../../../organism.md#trematoda), and to the [roundworms](../../../organism.md#nematoda), or [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda). The distinction is structural and developmental, not merely between inhabitants of the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine) and inhabitants of other organs.

The nineteenth-century higher arrangement was unsettled. Schemes placed these animals within [Vermes](../../../organism.md#vermes), or within annuloid assemblages that included other supposedly related simple invertebrates. For a specifically pre-exam example, the 1865 geological-museum classification put [Entozoa](../../../ecology.md#entozoa) in [Annuloida](../../../organism.md#annuloida), within [Annulosa](../../../organism.md#annulosa). These historical groups must not be equated with the modern [Annelida](../../../organism.md#annelida): an [Ascaris lumbricoides](../../../organism.md#ascaris-lumbricoides) is not an earthworm. A more useful subdivision for this answer is the four orders treated in [Thomas Spencer Cobbold](../../../physician.md#thomas-spencer-cobbold)'s 1864 [Entozoa, an Introduction to the Study of Helminthology by Thomas Spencer Cobbold](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#entozoa-an-introduction-to-the-study-of-helminthology-by-thomas-spencer-cobbold): [Trematoda](../../../organism.md#trematoda), [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda), [Acanthocephala](../../../organism.md#acanthocephala) and [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda).[p. lxxvi, classification table: Annulosa, Annuloida, Entozoa](../../../ecology-and-evolution-bibliography.md#catalogue-of-the-collection-of-fossils-in-the-museum-of-practical-geology-1865)[contents and order headings: Trematoda p. 14, Nematoda, Acanthocephala p. 97, Cestoda p. 104](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#entozoa-an-introduction-to-the-study-of-helminthology-by-thomas-spencer-cobbold)

The leading distinctions are as follows:

- [Cestoda](../../../organism.md#cestoda), the [tapeworms](../../../organism.md#cestoda), have a flattened adult body with an attachment [scolex](../../../organism.md#scolex) and, in the familiar human forms, a chain of reproductive [proglottids](../../../organism.md#proglottid). They lack an alimentary canal and absorb nutrients across the [tegument](../../../organism.md#tegument). The [proglottids](../../../organism.md#proglottid) are repeated reproductive units, not annelid segments. Typical development passes through an egg and one or more larval stages, commonly in an [intermediate host](../../../ecology.md#intermediate-host), before the adult reaches a [definitive host](../../../ecology.md#definitive-host). A bladder-like [cysticercus](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) and its adult [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda) are stages of one life cycle.
- [Trematoda](../../../organism.md#trematoda), the [flukes](../../../organism.md#trematoda), have flattened unsegmented bodies, attachment suckers and a mouth leading to a usually branched, blind-ending [digestive system](../../../physiology.md#digestive-system). Most are [hermaphrodites](../../../reproduction.md#hermaphrodite), but [Schistosoma](../../../organism.md#schistosoma) has separate sexes. Typical human-infecting [flukes](../../../organism.md#trematoda) use a molluscan [intermediate host](../../../ecology.md#intermediate-host), undergo multiplication in larval stages there, and produce dispersing [cercariae](../../../organism.md#cercaria). The route from that stage into the human varies by species; there is no universal requirement to swallow an adult worm.
- [Nematoda](../../../organism.md#nematoda) have cylindrical unsegmented bodies, a resistant [cuticle](../../../anatomy.md#cuticle), a fluid-filled [pseudocoelom](../../../anatomy.md#pseudocoelom), longitudinal [muscles](../../../anatomy.md#muscle) and a complete alimentary canal with mouth and anus. The sexes are usually separate. Development includes [moults](../../../developmental-biology.md#moult); some life cycles are direct, while others need a vector or another host. Human infection may follow swallowing infective eggs, penetration by larvae, ingestion of tissue larvae or transmission by a vector, depending on the species.
- [Acanthocephala](../../../organism.md#acanthocephala), the thorny-headed worms, possess a retractile hooked [proboscis](../../../anatomy.md#proboscis) and lack an alimentary canal. Their development normally involves an arthropod [intermediate host](../../../ecology.md#intermediate-host). They belong in a general survey of historical [Entozoa](../../../ecology.md#entozoa), but are not among the usual common human intestinal worms.

For a British medical answer of this period, the ordinary intestinal list should begin with the following species; the modern life-cycle details clarify their identities rather than claiming that every step was already established in 1867:

- [Ascaris lumbricoides](../../../organism.md#ascaris-lumbricoides), the large intestinal [roundworm](../../../organism.md#nematoda). Adults inhabit the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine); infective eggs are acquired from the environment, and larvae migrate through the [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) and [lungs](../../../anatomy.md#lung) before returning to mature in the [intestine](../../../physiology.md#intestine).[Causal Agents; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-ascariasis)
- [Enterobius vermicularis](../../../organism.md#enterobius-vermicularis), historically commonly named [Oxyuris vermicularis](../../../organism.md#enterobius-vermicularis), the small pinworm or threadworm. Adults chiefly occupy the caecal region; females deposit eggs on the perianal [skin](../../../physiology.md#skin), explaining spread through contaminated hands and objects and the usefulness of perianal sampling rather than reliance on ordinary stool samples.[Causal Agent; Life Cycle; Laboratory Diagnosis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-enterobiasis)
- [Trichuris trichiura](../../../organism.md#trichuris-trichiura), historically [Trichocephalus dispar](../../../organism.md#trichuris-trichiura), the whipworm. The thin anterior end is embedded in the caecal or colonic mucosa; eggs passed in faeces must develop outside the host before becoming infective.[Causal Agent; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-trichuriasis)
- [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium), the pork [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda), with an armed [scolex](../../../organism.md#scolex), and [Taenia saginata](../../../organism.md#taenia-saginata), the beef [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda), historically often [Taenia mediocanellata](../../../organism.md#taenia-saginata), with an unarmed [scolex](../../../organism.md#scolex). Intestinal adults follow ingestion of tissue [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) in infected meat. Their eggs alone cannot reliably distinguish the two species by ordinary [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy).[Causal Agents; Life Cycle; Taenia spp. scoleces; Laboratory Diagnosis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-taeniasis)
- The broad fish [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda), historically [Bothriocephalus latus](../../../organism.md#dibothriocephalus-latus), later [Diphyllobothrium latum](../../../organism.md#dibothriocephalus-latus), now [Dibothriocephalus latus](../../../organism.md#dibothriocephalus-latus). It is especially relevant in freshwater-fish-eating populations and has a life cycle involving a copepod and fish before the human definitive stage. Its attachment organs are longitudinal grooves, rather than the four rounded suckers typical of [Taenia](../../../organism.md#taenia).[Causal Agents; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-diphyllobothriasis)

Important tissue examples extend the answer beyond intestinal adults. The historical [Trichina spiralis](../../../organism.md#trichinella-spiralis) is [Trichinella spiralis](../../../organism.md#trichinella-spiralis): ingested infected meat carries larvae, adults develop in the [small intestine](../../../physiology.md#small-intestine), and new larvae migrate to [skeletal muscle](../../../physiology.md#skeletal-muscle). This is a distinct developmental pattern from the egg-acquired intestinal [Ascaris lumbricoides](../../../organism.md#ascaris-lumbricoides) infection.[Causal Agents; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-trichinellosis) Human hydatids are the larval [hydatid cysts](../../../organism.md#hydatid-cyst) of [Echinococcus](../../../organism.md#echinococcus), classically the [Echinococcus granulosus](../../../organism.md#echinococcus-granulosus) complex; the small adult [tapeworm](../../../organism.md#cestoda) is in a canid, while humans accidentally occupy the [intermediate host](../../../ecology.md#intermediate-host) role, commonly with [liver](../../../physiology.md#liver) or [lung](../../../anatomy.md#lung) cysts.[Causal Agents; Life Cycle, Cystic Echinococcosis](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-echinococcosis)

A crucial distinction is that **human [Taenia solium](../../../organism.md#taenia-solium) [cysticercosis](../../../biology.md#cysticercosis) follows ingestion of eggs; eating pork containing [cysticerci](../../../organism.md#cysticercus) produces intestinal [taeniasis](../../../biology.md#taeniasis)**. The historical name [Cysticercus cellulosae](../../../organism.md#cysticercus-cellulosae) designates a larval form, rather than an additional independent species to add to the list.[Causal Agent; Life Cycle](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-cysticercosis)

Geographical qualification matters: [Ancylostoma duodenale](../../../organism.md#ancylostoma-duodenale), [Dracunculus medinensis](../../../organism.md#dracunculus-medinensis) and [Schistosoma haematobium](../../../organism.md#schistosoma-haematobium) are important in their endemic settings; [Fasciola hepatica](../../../organism.md#fasciola-hepatica) is a familiar liver [fluke](../../../organism.md#trematoda) but is not an ordinary common human worm in Britain. An enumeration should not turn this into a claim that all named species are equally common in every population.[Causal Agents; Geographic Distribution](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-intestinal-hookworm)[Causal Agent; Geographic Distribution](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-dracunculiasis)[Causal Agents; Geographic Distribution](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-schistosomiasis-infection)[Causal Agents; Geographic Distribution](../../../microbiology-and-infectious-disease-bibliography.md#dpdx-fascioliasis) Conversely, calling only the familiar intestinal adults [Entozoa](../../../ecology.md#entozoa) would omit the instructive tissue stages and their transmission.

The decisive experimental insight is continuity of development. Comparing eggs, tissue larvae and adults, and tracing transfer between appropriate hosts, demonstrates a life cycle rather than spontaneous generation within a patient. In diagnostic material, an adult attachment organ, a [proglottid](../../../organism.md#proglottid), an egg and a tissue cyst answer different identification questions. Identification therefore depends on the stage and the material actually supplied.
