**Ordinary inspiration is active enlargement of the [thorax](../../../../../thorax.md); ordinary expiration is chiefly passive recoil after the inspiratory [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md) relax.** The movements increase and decrease the vertical, anteroposterior and transverse dimensions together. Their relative prominence varies with posture and the individual; there is no exclusive division into abdominal and costal kinds of [breathing](../../../../../pulmonary-ventilation.md).

The principal inspiratory [muscle](../../../../../muscle.md) is the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md). Its peripheral fibres shorten towards the central tendon, lowering and flattening its domes. This increases the vertical dimension of the [thorax](../../../../../thorax.md) and displaces the abdominal contents downwards and forwards, making the relaxed abdominal wall project. Its costal attachments also act on the lower [ribs](../../../../../rib.md): with abdominal support opposing unlimited descent, shortening can lift and expand the lower costal margin. Thus the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md) does more than pull down a piston. Its motor supply is through the [phrenic nerves](../../../../../phrenic-nerve.md), principally from cervical segments C3–C5.

The [external intercostal muscles](../../../../../external-intercostal-muscle.md) elevate the [ribs](../../../../../rib.md) and help keep the intercostal spaces from being drawn inwards as [pleural pressure](../../../../../pleural-pressure.md) falls. The interchondral, or parasternal, portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../../../internal-intercostal-muscle.md) also contribute to inspiration. It would therefore be inaccurate to label every part of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../../../internal-intercostal-muscle.md) expiratory. Coordinated activity braces the [rib cage](../../../../../rib-cage.md) so that the shortening of one layer moves the [ribs](../../../../../rib.md) rather than simply deforming the spaces between them.

Rotation about the posterior articulations raises the anterior ends of the upper [ribs](../../../../../rib.md) and the [sternum](../../../../../sternum.md), increasing the anteroposterior diameter: the usual pump-handle description. The more laterally directed portions of the lower [ribs](../../../../../rib.md) rise and move outwards, increasing the transverse diameter: the bucket-handle description. These are descriptions of components of an oblique movement, not two independent sets of hinges. The [scalene muscles](../../../../../scalene-muscle.md) can stabilize and elevate the uppermost [ribs](../../../../../rib.md), with recruitment varying in quiet [breathing](../../../../../pulmonary-ventilation.md). The [sternocleidomastoid muscle](../../../../../sternocleidomastoid-muscle.md), when the head is fixed, is an accessory elevator of the [sternum](../../../../../sternum.md) in greater inspiratory effort; it is not required for an ordinary quiet breath.

The [lungs](../../../../../lung.md) follow the expanding chest wall because their surfaces remain coupled to it through the thin fluid-filled [pleural cavity](../../../../../pleural-cavity.md). More negative [pleural pressure](../../../../../pleural-pressure.md) increases the distending [transpulmonary pressure](../../../../../transpulmonary-pressure.md); enlargement of the [alveoli](../../../../../alveolus.md) briefly makes [alveolar pressure](../../../../../alveolar-pressure.md) lower than [atmospheric pressure](../../../../../atmospheric-pressure.md), so air enters. At the end of inspiration, airflow stops when these last two [pressures](../../../../../pressure-split.md) equalize. During quiet expiration, relaxation of the [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md) and inspiratory [intercostal muscles](../../../../../intercostal-muscle.md) allows the stretched [lungs](../../../../../lung.md) and chest-wall system to recoil towards their resting balance. The domes rise, the [ribs](../../../../../rib.md) and [sternum](../../../../../sternum.md) descend, and [alveolar pressure](../../../../../alveolar-pressure.md) becomes slightly positive relative to [atmospheric pressure](../../../../../atmospheric-pressure.md) while air leaves. Active [abdominal muscles](../../../../../abdominal-muscle.md) and the interosseous portions of the [internal intercostal muscles](../../../../../internal-intercostal-muscle.md) become important in forced expiration, rather than supplying the main force in quiet expiration.[Mechanisms of Breathing and Pulmonary Ventilation; Figures 22.16–22.17](../../../../../anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/22-3-the-process-of-breathing.md)

A simple mechanical demonstration uses two elastic bags in an airtight transparent vessel, connected through its stopper to the air, with a movable rubber sheet forming the base. Pulling the sheet down expands the vessel and inflates the bags; letting it return allows deflation. A side-tube [manometer](../../../../../manometer.md) can show the associated [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) change. This illustrates expansion by lowering the floor, but its rigid vessel cannot demonstrate the movements of the [ribs](../../../../../rib.md), the action of the [intercostal muscles](../../../../../intercostal-muscle.md), or the real geometry of the [pleural cavity](../../../../../pleural-cavity.md). Observing lower-chest widening and abdominal displacement during quiet [breathing](../../../../../pulmonary-ventilation.md) supplies those missing observations without confusing the model with the anatomy.
