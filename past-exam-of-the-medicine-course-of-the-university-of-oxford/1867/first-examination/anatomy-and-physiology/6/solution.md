↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**The [heart](../../../../../heart.md) supplies the principal continuing mechanical input to [blood circulation](../../../../../blood-circulation.md); arterial recoil, respiratory movements and the action of [skeletal muscles](../../../../../skeletal-muscle.md) redistribute and supplement that input.** They have different sources of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and should not all be called independent propulsive powers. The [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) remaining after passage through the [capillaries](../../../../../capillary.md) is still a consequence of the upstream pump.

**Arterial elasticity.** During ventricular [systole](../../../../../systole.md), ejected [blood](../../../../../blood.md) expands the [aorta](../../../../../aorta.md) and large [arteries](../../../../../artery.md), storing [elastic potential energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md) in their walls. During [diastole](../../../../../diastole.md), recoil continues to drive [blood](../../../../../blood.md) into the peripheral vessels while closure of the [aortic valve](../../../../../aortic-valve.md) limits return towards the [heart](../../../../../heart.md). This reduces the intermittency of flow downstream. The recoil is a temporary store of cardiac [work](../../../../../work.md), rather than a fresh source of [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). A vessel that is too rigid provides less of this smoothing; a compliant vessel with an open backward outlet would not provide the same useful result.

**The skeletal-muscle pump.** Contracting [skeletal muscles](../../../../../skeletal-muscle.md) compress adjacent [veins](../../../../../vein.md). [Venous valves](../../../../../venous-valve.md) make the displacement preferentially towards the [heart](../../../../../heart.md); when compression ceases, refilling permits another stroke. Walking therefore aids return from dependent limbs. The source of the added [work](../../../../../work.md) is [muscle contraction](../../../../../muscle-contraction.md), and the valves guide it without producing [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Merely possessing valves cannot make a stationary column circulate.

**The respiratory pump.** During inspiration, falling intrathoracic [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and displacement of the abdominal contents favour a [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) from abdominal and peripheral [veins](../../../../../vein.md) towards the thoracic [veins](../../../../../vein.md) and right side of the [heart](../../../../../heart.md). The [respiratory diaphragm](../../../../../respiratory-diaphragm.md) thus participates indirectly in [venous return](../../../../../venous-return.md) as well as [pulmonary ventilation](../../../../../pulmonary-ventilation.md). The exact flow varies with posture, vessel collapse and the phase of the respiratory cycle; positive-pressure inflation does not reproduce the same mechanical circumstances as spontaneous inspiration.[Venous System; Skeletal Muscle Pump; Respiratory Pump](../../../../../anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-2-blood-flow-blood-pressure-and-resistance.md)

**Venous tone and cardiac filling.** [Sympathetic](../../../../../sympathetic.md) contraction of venous [smooth muscle](../../../../../smooth-muscle.md) can reduce venous storage capacity and transfer [blood](../../../../../blood.md) towards the central circulation. This alters the volume available for filling rather than requiring a wave of venous peristalsis. Cardiac relaxation, recoil and movement of the atrioventricular plane also affect filling [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Some traditionally described cardiac suction is therefore a feature of the cardiac cycle and mechanical coupling, not a separate organ pulling [blood](../../../../../blood.md) through every vessel.[Neural Regulation of Blood Flow; sympathetic effects on vessels and venous return](../../../../../anatomy-and-physiology-2e-by-j-gordon-betts-and-others/20-4-homeostatic-regulation-of-the-vascular-system.md)

**Gravity and supposed capillary attraction.** [Gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) assists a downward column but opposes an upward one. In a complete circuit returning to its original height it supplies no net gravitational [work](../../../../../work.md); its important practical effects include hydrostatic [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) differences and dependent venous pooling. Ordinary [capillary action](../../../../../capillary-action.md) in an open narrow tube, driven by a liquid meniscus, is not a general pump in a fluid-filled [capillary](../../../../../capillary.md) network. Nor does a vague attraction of [blood](../../../../../blood.md) by [tissues](../../../../../tissue-biology.md) replace a measurable [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md). Local [vasodilation](../../../../../vasodilation.md) can increase [perfusion](../../../../../perfusion.md) by lowering [vascular resistance](../../../../../vascular-resistance.md), but that change does not manufacture the necessary [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) source.

For a simple mean-flow description, with $Q$ the volume flow rate, $\Delta P$ the driving [pressure gradient](../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) and $R$ the effective [vascular resistance](../../../../../vascular-resistance.md),

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{\Delta P}{R}}.
$$

This relation distinguishes a source of driving [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) from a change of resistance. In a lumped compliant arterial reservoir with [vascular compliance](../../../../../vascular-compliance.md) $C$ and no inflow between beats, volume balance gives

$$
C\frac{dP}{dt}=-\frac{P-P_v}{R},\qquad
P(t)-P_v=(P(0)-P_v)e^{-t/(RC)},
$$

where $P_v$ is the downstream [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). The [exponential decay](../../../../../exponential-decay.md) follows from stored volume leaving through the resistance. It explains continued flow during [diastole](../../../../../diastole.md) without claiming that the idealization describes every reflected arterial wave.

A physical demonstration can compare a pulsatile pump feeding a narrow outlet through rigid tubing with the same pump feeding it through an elastic reservoir. Collecting the outflow over the pumping and resting phases reveals the reservoir's smoothing action; a side-arm [manometer](../../../../../manometer.md) records its falling [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). Separately compressing a valved flexible tube shows the role of a peripheral [muscle pump](../../../../../muscle-pump.md). Keeping pump stroke and outlet resistance comparable prevents the apparatus from conflating a smoother flow with creation of extra [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md).
