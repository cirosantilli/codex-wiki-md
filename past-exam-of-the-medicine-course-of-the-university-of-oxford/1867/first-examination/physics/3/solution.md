<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Take downward [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) as positive and [gravitational acceleration](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) $g$ as constant. In a vacuum, $m\,dv/dt=mg$, so with initial downward [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) $u$,

$$
\boxed{v=u+gt,\qquad s=ut+\tfrac12gt^2.}
$$

For release from rest these become $v=gt$ and $s=gt^2/2$.

A raindrop encounters an increasing upward [drag force](../../../../../drag-physics.md) as its [speed](../../../../../speed.md) grows. At [terminal velocity](../../../../../terminal-velocity.md), [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md) is balanced by [drag force](../../../../../drag-physics.md) and the small [buoyancy](../../../../../buoyancy.md) contribution:

$$
\boxed{F_{\rm net}=mg-F_{\rm drag}-F_{\rm buoyancy}=0,\qquad a=0.}
$$

The drop remains in motion: zero [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) does not imply zero [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). In a quadratic [drag force](../../../../../drag-physics.md) model, $F_{\rm drag}=\rho_{\rm air}C_DAv^2/2$, giving $v_t=\sqrt{2(m-\rho_{\rm air}V)g/(\rho_{\rm air}C_DA)}$. That formula assumes a fixed effective area and [drag coefficient](../../../../../drag-coefficient.md); sufficiently small drops or deforming large drops need an appropriate model. The question's “effective force” at settled [terminal velocity](../../../../../terminal-velocity.md) is the approximately zero [resultant force](../../../../../resultant-force.md), not its nonzero [gravitational weight](../../../../../weight-physics.md).
