<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For constant [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $\mathbf F$ and [displacement](../../../../../displacement.md) $\mathbf s$, the [work](../../../../../work.md) is

$$
\boxed{W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf s=Fs\cos\theta.}
$$

For a variable [force](../../../../../force-split.md), $W=\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$. The [work-energy theorem](../../../../../work-energy-theorem.md) explains this choice: [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) for fixed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) gives $\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=m\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf v\,dt=d(mv^2/2)$. The component along the motion changes [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md); a perpendicular [force](../../../../../force-split.md) can change direction without doing [work](../../../../../work.md). Exerting a [force](../../../../../force-split.md) on a stationary object likewise does no [work](../../../../../work.md) on it.

Walking on level ground still requires muscular [work](../../../../../work.md). The [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) rises and falls during each step; the limbs gain and lose [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md); and [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md) perform positive [work](../../../../../work.md) that replaces [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) dissipated in impacts and tissue motion. Air resistance can add another cost. Returning to the same height makes the net change of [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) zero, not every intermediate [work](../../../../../work.md) contribution.

At ideal no-slip contact with an immovable road, the contact point is instantaneously stationary, so the road's [force](../../../../../force-split.md) does no [work](../../../../../work.md) through that point. The [muscles](../../../../../muscle.md) convert internal chemical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) into motion. **Zero net gain of height is compatible with positive muscular work and energy expenditure.**
