## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What is meant by a combustible substance ?

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

**A [combustible substance](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustible-substance) can sustain a sufficiently rapid, heat-producing [chemical reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md) with a suitable [oxidizing agent](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidizing-agent).** In ordinary [air](../../../mixture.md#air) this usually means combination with [molecular oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-oxygen), producing enough [heat](../../../heat.md) to maintain the reaction after ignition. A visible flame is not essential: [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) can glow as it undergoes [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion).

[Combustibility](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustibility) depends on the surroundings and experimental conditions. A cold block of [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) does not ignite merely because it meets [air](../../../mixture.md#air): heating must first provide combustible vapour and overcome the kinetic barrier to reaction. Finely divided [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) burns much more readily than a massive bar because more surface is exposed per unit [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass). Nor is [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) universally required: [molecular hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-hydrogen) can undergo [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) in [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine). The expression “supports [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion)” identifies the surrounding reactant in a particular experiment; it does not establish an absolute division between two kinds of [chemical substance](../../../chemical-substance.md).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Give an account of the physical and chemical changes which constitute the burning of a candle.

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

**The [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick) supplies [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) by [capillary action](../../../surface-tension.md#capillary-action); [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) vapour reacts with atmospheric [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen), releasing the [heat](../../../heat.md) that keeps the supply going.** The [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick) is a delivery system, rather than the principal [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel).

First, an external flame warms the [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick) and adjacent [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax). [Melting](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) produces a small [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) reservoir, and [capillary action](../../../surface-tension.md#capillary-action) draws it into the spaces between the [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick) fibres. Near the hot tip, [evaporation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#evaporation) and some [pyrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#pyrolysis) supply gaseous [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel). [Melting](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#melting) and [evaporation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#evaporation) are physical changes; [pyrolysis](../../../chemical-reaction.md#pyrolysis) and [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) change the [chemical substances](../../../chemical-substance.md) present. Once established, the [candle flame](../../../chemical-reaction.md#candle-flame) transfers enough [heat](../../../heat.md) back to the [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) to maintain this sequence.[Lecture I, melting reservoir, [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick), capillary attraction and combustible vapour](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#the-chemical-history-of-a-candle-by-michael-faraday)

The [candle flame](../../../chemical-reaction.md#candle-flame) is a [diffusion flame](../../../chemical-reaction.md#diffusion-flame). Its relatively dark inner region contains newly supplied [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel) with limited access to [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). In the yellow luminous region, incomplete conversion of the [hydrocarbons](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrocarbon) produces small [soot](../../../chemical-substance.md#soot) particles, which become incandescent. Farther out, mixing supplies enough [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) to consume most of the remaining [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel), [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) and [soot](../../../chemical-substance.md#soot). Thus the yellow light is largely thermal emission from hot particles, rather than a yellow [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas).[Lecture II, deposition of [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) on a cold surface and the luminosity of heated [solid](../../../thermodynamics.md#solid) particles](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#the-chemical-history-of-a-candle-by-michael-faraday)

For a representative [alkane](../../../chemical-substance.md#alkane) component of [paraffin wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#paraffin-wax), complete [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) has the [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation)

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2}+\frac{3n+1}{2}\mathrm{O}_2\longrightarrow n\mathrm{CO}_2+(n+1)\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}.}
$$

Real candle materials are mixtures, and some, such as fatty materials, already contain [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen); the displayed [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) is an illustrative [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) model. The principal products of complete [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) are [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [water vapour](../../../chemical-substance.md#water-vapor). [Convection](../../../convection.md) carries the hot [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) upward and brings fresh [air](../../../mixture.md#air) into the flame. A cold collector can reveal condensed [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water); collecting the [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) and passing them into [limewater](../../../chemical-substance.md#limewater) gives a [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate) cloudiness from [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide).

Removing the [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) supply, cooling the reaction zone, or cutting off [wax](../../../chemical-substance.md#wax) delivery extinguishes the flame. The smoking [wick](../../../chemical-reaction.md#wick) immediately afterwards can still emit combustible vapour; without sufficient [heat](../../../heat.md) at the mixing zone, that vapour need not sustain a flame.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How is bleaching-powder made ? What are its chief properties ?

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

**[Bleaching powder](../../../chemistry.md#bleaching-powder) is made by passing [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine) over [slaked lime](../../../chemical-substance.md#slaked-lime), exposing a large surface of the powder to the [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas).** The contemporary name “chlorinated lime” is more informative than treating it as pure [calcium chloride](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-chloride). The 1867 [British Pharmacopoeia (1867)](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-1867) describes this manufacture and a dull-white, partly [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water)-soluble powder with bleaching and disinfecting properties.[Calx Chlorata, printed p. 65](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-1867)

An idealized overall [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{Ca(OH)}_2+2\mathrm{Cl}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ca(OCl)}_2+\mathrm{CaCl}_2+2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}.}
$$

The [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine) undergoes [disproportionation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#disproportionation): one part becomes [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride), with [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) $-1$, and another becomes [hypochlorite](../../../chemistry.md#hypochlorite), with [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) $+1$. Commercial [bleaching powder](../../../chemistry.md#bleaching-powder) contains residual [calcium hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-hydroxide), [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) and chlorine-bearing [solid](../../../thermodynamics.md#solid) phases; the [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) is a useful composition balance, not a claim that the powder is one pure molecular substance or simply a fully characterized mechanical mixture. The old formula $\mathrm{CaOCl}_2$ is an abbreviated representation, not the formula of pure [calcium hypochlorite](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-hypochlorite), $\mathrm{Ca(OCl)}_2$.

Its chief properties follow from the [hypochlorite](../../../chemistry.md#hypochlorite) chemistry. The aqueous extract is alkaline and oxidizing. The [chemical equilibrium](../../../chemical-potential.md#chemical-equilibrium)

$$
\mathrm{OCl}^-+\mathrm{H}^+\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{HOCl}
$$

produces [hypochlorous acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#hypochlorous-acid) on lowering the [pH](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#ph). Oxidation of coloured [organic compounds](../../../chemical-substance.md#organic-compound) can destroy the [chromophores](../../../molecule.md#chromophore) responsible for their colour. This is irreversible chemical alteration of a dye, rather than merely washing it away. The historical description that bleaching works through “nascent [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen)” is not a necessary modern mechanism: [hypochlorous acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#hypochlorous-acid) and related reactive chlorine species themselves react with substrates.

With sufficient [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid), the [hypochlorite](../../../chemistry.md#hypochlorite) and [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride) can generate [chlorine](../../../chemical-substance.md#chlorine):

$$
\mathrm{OCl}^-+\mathrm{Cl}^-+2\mathrm{H}^+\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cl}_2+\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}.
$$

This explains acid-induced [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) evolution and why acidification is not an innocuous way to use a bleaching material. Exposure, moisture and heating can reduce the stored oxidizing strength. Its useful strength is measured as [available chlorine](../../../chemistry.md#available-chlorine), rather than inferred from the powder's total [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) or its smell; inert material and residual [lime](../../../chemistry.md#lime) do not provide the same bleaching capacity.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Write equations expressing the changes which take place when chlorate of potash, nitrate of ammonia, and carbonate of lime, are decomposed by heat ; also when zinc, magnesia, and alumina, are dissolved in hydrochloric acid.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

The historical names identify [potassium chlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-chlorate), [ammonium nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#ammonium-nitrate) and [calcium carbonate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#calcium-carbonate). Their expected net [thermal decomposition](../../../chemical-reaction.md#thermal-decomposition) [chemical equations](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation), written in modern formulae, are

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm{KClO}_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}2\mathrm{KCl}+3\mathrm{O}_2,}
$$



$$
\boxed{\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{NO}_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{O}+2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O},}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CaCO}_3\xrightarrow{\Delta}\mathrm{CaO}+\mathrm{CO}_2.}
$$

The first gives [potassium chloride](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-chloride) and [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen). A [potassium perchlorate](../../../chemistry.md#potassium-perchlorate) intermediate can occur during uncatalysed heating; the first [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) gives the final overall balance. The second represents the familiar [nitrous oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrous-oxide) pathway, with hot [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) initially as vapour. It does not mean that [ammonium nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#ammonium-nitrate) has only one decomposition pathway: conditions, impurities and confinement can produce different [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) and violent decomposition.[Decomposition Chemistry: dependence on heating, contamination and confinement](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#chemical-advisory-on-ammonium-nitrate-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)

The third is [calcination](../../../engineering.md#calcination) of [limestone](../../../planetary-science.md#limestone) to [quicklime](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-oxide). Its extent depends on [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and the [partial pressure](../../../ideal-gas.md#partial-pressure) of [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide); removing [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) favours further decomposition. Reversing the process gives carbonation of [calcium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-oxide).

For dissolution in [hydrochloric acid](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#hydrochloric-acid), the corresponding balances are

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Zn}+2\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow\mathrm{ZnCl}_2+\mathrm{H}_2,}
$$



$$
\boxed{\mathrm{MgO}+2\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow\mathrm{MgCl}_2+\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O},}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{O}_3+6\mathrm{HCl}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{AlCl}_3+3\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}.}
$$

“Magnesia” here means [magnesium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#magnesium-oxide), and “alumina” means [aluminium oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium-oxide). [Zinc](../../../chemical-substance.md#zinc) undergoes an [oxidation-reduction reaction](../../../chemical-reaction.md#redox), reducing [hydrogen ions](../../../chemistry.md#hydrogen-ion) to [molecular hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-hydrogen). The oxides instead undergo [acid-base reactions](../../../acid-base-chemistry.md#acid-base-reaction): [hydrogen ions](../../../chemistry.md#hydrogen-ion) convert oxide into [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), without [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) evolution. The chlorides are dissolved species in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), rather than anhydrous molecular salts necessarily existing intact in the solution. Strongly ignited [aluminium oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#aluminium-oxide) may dissolve slowly; the balance does not determine the rate.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How is the atomic theory related to the laws of definite and of multiple proportions ?

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**[Atomic theory](../../../chemistry.md#atomic-theory) explains fixed composition by fixed numbers of [chemical atoms](../../../physics.md#atom-physics), and [multiple proportions](../../../chemistry.md#law-of-multiple-proportions) by different small whole-number combinations of the same [chemical atoms](../../../physics.md#atom-physics).** The two laws are empirical regularities which the theory explains; they are not themselves direct observations of individual [chemical atoms](../../../physics.md#atom-physics).

For the [law of definite proportions](../../../chemistry.md#law-of-definite-proportions), suppose a pure [chemical compound](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-compound) has composition $\mathrm A_x\mathrm B_y$. If the [atomic masses](../../../chemical-substance.md#atomic-mass) are $m_A$ and $m_B$, every formula unit contains masses $xm_A$ and $ym_B$. Enlarging the sample multiplies both by the same number, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{\text{mass of B}}{\text{mass of A}}=\frac{ym_B}{xm_A}.}
$$

The [mass ratio](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass-ratio) is therefore independent of sample size or preparation route, provided the same pure stoichiometric [chemical compound](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-compound) is obtained. Ordinary [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) supplies an example: its [hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen)-to-[oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [mass ratio](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass-ratio) is approximately $1:8$.

For the [law of multiple proportions](../../../chemistry.md#law-of-multiple-proportions), compare $\mathrm A_x\mathrm B_y$ and $\mathrm A_u\mathrm B_v$. At a fixed [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) of A, the masses of B are in the ratio

$$
\boxed{\frac{y/x}{v/u}=\frac{yu}{vx}.}
$$

This is a ratio of integers, and is often a ratio of small integers for simple [chemical compounds](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-compound). With [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) and [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide), about $12$ [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) units of [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) combine with about $16$ and $32$ of [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen): the [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) masses are in the ratio $1:2$. The [chemical formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-formula) explain why adding a second [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) [chemical atom](../../../physics.md#atom-physics) doubles the [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) contribution without changing the [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) contribution.

The laws alone do not uniquely determine the correct [chemical formulas](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-formula) or the [atomic weights](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass); independent information is needed to distinguish, for example, one formula unit from a multiple of it. Modern qualifications include variations in [isotopic composition](../../../chemical-substance.md#isotopic-composition) and [non-stoichiometric compounds](../../../chemical-substance.md#non-stoichiometric-compound). These refine the simple nineteenth-century formulation without removing its usefulness for pure compounds of fixed composition.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What are the relative specific gravities of carbonic acid, carbonic oxide, nitrous oxide, air, ammonia, olefiant gas, and the vapours of alcohol and ether ?

<h3 id="6/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

**Take dry [air](../../../mixture.md#air) as unity, and compare each [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) at the same [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and [pressure](../../../pressure.md).** In the [ideal gas](../../../ideal-gas.md) approximation, $\rho=pM/(RT)$, so the [relative density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#relative-density) is

$$
\boxed{d_{\rm air}=\frac{\rho_{\rm gas}}{\rho_{\rm air}}\simeq\frac{M_{\rm gas}}{28.97\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}}.}
$$

Here $M$ is [molar mass](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass). The following values use modern approximate [molar masses](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass); rounding and historical [atomic weight](../../../chemical-substance.md#relative-atomic-mass) conventions can shift the last figures.

| Historical name and modern identification | Formula | $M/(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}})$ | [Relative density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#relative-density), [air](../../../mixture.md#air) $=1$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbonic acid: [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) | $\mathrm{CO}_2$ | 44.01 | **1.519** |
| Carbonic oxide: [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) | $\mathrm{CO}$ | 28.01 | **0.967** |
| [Nitrous oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrous-oxide) | $\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{O}$ | 44.01 | **1.519** |
| Dry [air](../../../mixture.md#air) | Mixture | 28.97 | **1.000** |
| [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) | $\mathrm{NH}_3$ | 17.03 | **0.588** |
| Olefiant [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas): [Ethylene](../../../polymer.md#ethylene) | $\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_4$ | 28.05 | **0.968** |
| Alcohol vapour: [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) | $\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_6\mathrm{O}$ | 46.07 | **1.590** |
| Ether vapour: [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) | $\mathrm{C}_4\mathrm{H}_{10}\mathrm{O}$ | 74.12 | **2.559** |

“Olefiant [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas)” is confirmed as a historical synonym of [Ethylene](../../../polymer.md#ethylene) in the [NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#nist-chemistry-webbook-srd-69). In this [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) list, “carbonic acid” means [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide), not an isolated vapour of aqueous $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{CO}_3$. “Alcohol” and “ether” refer to [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) and [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether), rather than their entire modern chemical classes.[Ethylene entry, Formula, Molecular weight and Other names; [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) entry, Formula and Molecular weight](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#nist-chemistry-webbook-srd-69)

The order is therefore [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia), then [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) and [Ethylene](../../../polymer.md#ethylene) nearly tied, then [air](../../../mixture.md#air), then [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) and [nitrous oxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrous-oxide) nearly tied, then [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) vapour, then [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) vapour. Nearly equal [molar masses](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass) do not imply equal chemical behaviour.

If instead [molecular hydrogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#molecular-hydrogen) is chosen as unity, divide each [molar mass](../../../chemistry.md#molar-mass) by about $2.016\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ and state that convention. In the older nominal $\mathrm H_2=2$ approximation, the corresponding values are about $22,14,22,14.5,8.5,14,23,37$ in the source order. These are a different reference scale, not different substances.

The vapour comparisons are not comparisons of the [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [densities](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#density). Pure [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) and [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) must be considered at conditions where they remain gaseous, or by a low-[pressure](../../../pressure.md) [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) approximation. Their [liquids](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) can be lighter than [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) while their vapours are heavier than [air](../../../mixture.md#air).

## 7

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

Name some fluids that are heavier and some that are lighter than water.

<h3 id="7/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#7)

**Examples heavier than [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) are [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet), [glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol) and concentrated [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid); examples lighter than [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) are [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) and [diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether).** “Heavier” here means greater [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) for the same [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume), rather than greater total [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) in arbitrarily sized samples.

Representative room-[temperature](../../../temperature.md) [relative densities](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#relative-density), with [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) as unity, are

| [Liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) | Approximate [relative density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#relative-density) |
| --- | --- |
| [Mercury](../../../planetary-science.md#mercury-planet) | 13.5 |
| Concentrated [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid) | 1.84 |
| [Glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol) | 1.26 |
| [Water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) | 1.00 |
| [Ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) | 0.79 |
| [Diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) | 0.71 |

These are illustrative values, not exact constants independent of [temperature](../../../temperature.md) or composition. Dilution changes the [density](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) of [sulfuric acid](../../../chemical-substance.md#sulfuric-acid), [glycerol](../../../chemical-substance.md#glycerol) and [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) solutions.[Glycerin, Sulfuric acid, Ethyl alcohol and Ethyl ether entries, Specific Gravity fields](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#niosh-pocket-guide-to-chemical-hazards)[Agent Properties, [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) density at 25 °C](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#mercury-agent-properties-by-niosh)

A distinction from simply observing a floating layer is essential: [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) is miscible with [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), so it mixes rather than remaining as a permanent upper layer. [Diethyl ether](../../../chemical-substance.md#diethyl-ether) has limited [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) solubility and can form an upper layer. More broadly, [gases](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) are also [fluids](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#fluid), but the natural comparison intended here is between [liquids](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid); [air](../../../mixture.md#air) at ordinary conditions is much less dense than [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water).

## 8

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

What is steel? Give a short account of the processes by which it is obtained from an ore of iron.

<h3 id="8/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#8)

**[Steel](../../../chemical-substance.md#steel) is an [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)-based [alloy](../../../chemical-substance.md#alloy) with controlled [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) content and impurities; its manufacture requires extracting [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) from an [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore) and then adjusting the composition.** In a modern plain-[carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) classification, [steel](../../../chemical-substance.md#steel) generally contains less than about $2\%$ [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) by [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass), whereas [pig iron](../../../engineering.md#pig-iron) is more [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-rich. The historical practical contrast was with low-[carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) [wrought iron](../../../engineering.md#wrought-iron) and readily cast but relatively brittle [cast iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#cast-iron). Hardening by quenching is characteristic of suitable [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-rich steels, but not every grade hardens in the same way.

Begin, for example, with an [iron oxide](../../../chemistry.md#iron-oxide) [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore). Preparation removes some earthy matter and, where appropriate, roasting expels moisture or converts a carbonate-bearing [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore) to oxide. A [blast furnace](../../../engineering.md#blast-furnace) receives the [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore), [coke](../../../chemical-substance.md#coke) and a flux such as [limestone](../../../planetary-science.md#limestone). The hot [air](../../../mixture.md#air) blast sustains [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) of [coke](../../../chemical-substance.md#coke), generating [heat](../../../heat.md) and the [carbon monoxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monoxide) needed for much of the oxide [reduction](../../../chemical-reaction.md#reduction-chemistry). Representative overall balances are

$$
\mathrm C+\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{CO}_2,\qquad
\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm C\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CO},
$$



$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm O_3+3\mathrm{CO}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Fe}+3\mathrm{CO}_2.}
$$

[Calcination](../../../engineering.md#calcination) of [limestone](../../../planetary-science.md#limestone) provides [calcium oxide](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-oxide). Combination with acidic gangue, represented by [silica](../../../planetary-science.md#silica), gives a [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) [slag](../../../engineering.md#slag):

$$
\mathrm{CaCO}_3\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaO}+\mathrm{CO}_2,\qquad
\mathrm{CaO}+\mathrm{SiO}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaSiO}_3.
$$

Actual [slag](../../../engineering.md#slag) is a multicomponent melt, rather than necessarily pure [calcium silicate](../../../chemistry.md#calcium-silicate). The descending [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) picks up [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and other impurities and collects as molten [pig iron](../../../engineering.md#pig-iron). This first extraction product is not already finished [steel](../../../chemical-substance.md#steel).

Two important routes available in 1867 proceed from this stage. In the older indirect route, refining and [puddling](../../../engineering.md#puddling) oxidize much of the [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and remove slag-associated impurities to obtain [wrought iron](../../../engineering.md#wrought-iron). Bars packed with [charcoal](../../../chemical-substance.md#charcoal) are then heated for a prolonged period in the [cementation process](../../../engineering.md#cementation-process). [Carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) enters the bars, giving [blister steel](../../../engineering.md#blister-steel); forging and welding can make [shear steel](../../../engineering.md#shear-steel), while melting suitable stock in covered crucibles produces more uniform [crucible steel](../../../engineering.md#crucible-steel). The bar can acquire [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) through [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas)-mediated surface reactions and [diffusion](../../../thermodynamics.md#diffusion); the process should not be imagined as intact charcoal grains travelling through the [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron).

In the [Bessemer process](../../../engineering.md#bessemer-process), an [air](../../../mixture.md#air) blast passes through molten [pig iron](../../../engineering.md#pig-iron) in a converter. [Oxidation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation) of [silicon](../../../chemical-substance.md#silicon), [manganese](../../../chemical-substance.md#manganese) and [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) supplies [heat](../../../heat.md), while lowering the [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) content. Composition can be corrected by adding a controlled [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)- and manganese-bearing material such as [spiegeleisen](../../../engineering.md#spiegeleisen). [Henry Bessemer](../../../engineering.md#henry-bessemer)'s 1856 patent describes the central idea of maintaining the conversion by oxidizing constituents of the molten metal, without [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel) for reheating during that stage.[US16082A, specification and final claim, 11 November 1856](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#improvement-in-the-manufacture-of-iron-and-steel-by-henry-bessemer)

The early acidic converter demanded suitable feed: it did not solve removal of [phosphorus](../../../chemical-substance.md#phosphorus) from every [ore](../../../engineering.md#ore). Later basic converter practice must not be silently imported into a December 1867 account. Final casting, working and [heat treatment](../../../engineering.md#heat-treatment) determine the [steel microstructure](../../../chemical-substance.md#steel-microstructure) and properties as well as composition. The useful manufacturing chain is therefore **[ore](../../../engineering.md#ore) → extracted [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-rich [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) → controlled composition → worked and appropriately heat-treated [steel](../../../chemical-substance.md#steel)**.

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

How would you examine a sample of water suspected of sewage contamination ?

<h3 id="9/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9](#9)

**Combine investigation of the source with several independent analyses; a clear appearance or a single negative chemical test cannot exclude sewage pollution.** No sample results are supplied, so the answer is an examination scheme rather than a finding about an actual [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) sample.

Record where and when the [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water) was collected, recent rainfall, proximity of drains and cesspits, and possible routes from sewage to the supply. Compare with a credible uncontaminated sample from the same geological setting. Use clean, suitably closed vessels and examine promptly; contamination introduced during sampling and chemical changes during storage can both mislead. Separate portions are needed when one analysis alters the sample for another.

For an 1867 investigation, inspect colour, turbidity and sediment, and examine concentrated sediment by [microscopy](../../../optics.md#microscopy) for suspicious organic debris. These observations can motivate further work but cannot identify all disease-causing agents. Determine residue after evaporating a measured [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume); carefully heating that residue may reveal charring or a volatile fraction. Loss on ignition is not a specific measure of sewage: mineral constituents can also change on heating, and some organic material can be lost during evaporation itself.

Test a separate aliquot for [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride), for example by [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate) [precipitation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry), and compare its quantity with local background. Elevated [chloride](../../../chemistry.md#chloride) can accompany sewage but also comes from natural salts. Examine [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) or [ammonium](../../../chemistry.md#ammonium) and oxidized nitrogen, [nitrite](../../../chemical-substance.md#nitrite) and [nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#nitrate). Organic nitrogen can be converted during decomposition and [nitrification](../../../biology.md#nitrification), so an old contamination may have little free [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia). Conversely, a nitrogen signal alone does not establish its origin.

An oxidant-consumption experiment with a known amount of [potassium permanganate](../../../ionic-bonding.md#potassium-permanganate), under controlled reaction time, [temperature](../../../temperature.md) and acidity, supplies another comparison. Residual oxidant is measured against a reagent blank. A large consumption indicates oxidizable material, but reducing inorganic constituents also consume oxidant, while some organic materials react incompletely. It is therefore an operational test, not a direct count of sewage organisms.

A method actually reported in June–July 1867 first distils off free [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia), then uses alkaline [permanganate](../../../chemistry.md#permanganate) to generate further [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) from some organic nitrogen. A boiling flask, condenser and successive receiving vessels separate the fractions, whose [Ammonia](../../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) can be compared by colour with standards using [Nessler's reagent](../../../chemistry.md#nessler-s-reagent). The later fraction became known as [albuminoid ammonia](../../../biology.md#albuminoid-ammonia). It is neither proof of the presence of albumen specifically nor complete conversion of every organic nitrogen compound. Contemporary discussion already disputed how different substances behaved in the preliminary distillation.[Vol. 16, printed p. 7, Chemical Society meeting of 20 June, method and discussion of gelatin and urea](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#water-analysis-report-in-the-chemical-news-5-july-1867)

A present-day assessment adds validated [microbiological water testing](../../../biology.md#microbiological-water-testing), including a specific [Escherichia coli](../../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) test using culture or an accepted equivalent, together with [quality control](../../../chemistry.md#quality-control) and a [sanitary survey](../../../biology.md#sanitary-survey). [Escherichia coli](../../../organism.md#escherichia-coli) is an indicator of faecal contamination, not a label for all sewage-derived pathogens. The Australian [drinking water](../../../biology.md#drinking-water) guideline specifies none detected in a $100\ \mathrm{mL}$ sample and notes that the indicator does not reliably represent all enteric [viruses](../../../biology.md#virus) or [protozoa](../../../organism.md#protozoan).[Guideline; Indicator value and application in practice](../../../public-health-and-forensic-medicine-bibliography.md#escherichia-coli-in-the-australian-drinking-water-guidelines)

The justified conclusion comes from the combined evidence and the detection limits. **Chemical indicators can support a sewage-contamination hypothesis, but chemical clarity or a negative result in one aliquot is not a certificate of microbiological safety.** This modern distinction should not be attributed to an examiner as a fully developed 1867 laboratory protocol.

**Original question.**

Describe iodine. Whence is it derived ?

<h3 id="10/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10](#10)

**[Iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) is a dark, lustrous crystalline [halogen](../../../chemical-substance.md#halogen) which gives a violet vapour; historically, an important source was the ash of [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine)-rich seaweeds.** Elemental [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) commonly occurs as diatomic $\mathrm I_2$. It melts at about $114\ ^\circ\mathrm C$ and boils at about $184\ ^\circ\mathrm C$ at ordinary atmospheric [pressure](../../../pressure.md). It also undergoes [sublimation](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#sublimation) below its melting point, depositing crystals again on a cooler surface.[Fact box; Appearance; Natural abundance](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/iodine-element-information-properties-and-uses)

[Iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) is sparingly soluble in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), giving a brownish solution, and dissolves more freely in [ethanol](../../../metabolism.md#ethanol) and in an aqueous [iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodide) solution. In the latter case the [chemical equilibrium](../../../chemical-potential.md#chemical-equilibrium)

$$
\mathrm I_2+\mathrm I^-\rightleftharpoons\mathrm I_3^-
$$

forms [triiodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#triiodide). [Iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) with [starch](../../../chemical-substance.md#starch), under suitable conditions, gives a sensitive dark-blue colour; this is a useful recognition test, but oxidants that liberate [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) from added [iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodide) must be distinguished from [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) initially present. Reaction with reactive [metals](../../../chemical-substance.md#metal) gives [iodides](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodide); in such compounds the [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) usually has [oxidation state](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidation-state) $-1$.

For the historical seaweed route, collect and burn [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine)-accumulating seaweeds to produce [kelp ash](../../../chemical-substance.md#kelp-ash), then extract the soluble salts with [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water). Crystallization removes much of the common salt content, leaving an iodide-enriched mother liquor. Oxidation releases [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine), which can be distilled or sublimed and collected on a cool surface. One representative net [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) for liberation from [iodide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodide) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm I^-+\mathrm{MnO}_2+4\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow\mathrm I_2+\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}+2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

The [manganese dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#manganese-dioxide) is the [oxidizing agent](../../../chemical-reaction.md#oxidizing-agent); the seaweed does not contain a supply of ready-made violet [iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) crystals. This describes the chemical principle of a historical extraction, rather than prescribing one unique factory recipe.

[Iodine](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodine) also occurs in natural brines and in [iodate](../../../chemical-substance.md#iodate)-bearing deposits associated with nitrate ores. Modern industrial supply relies heavily on brines and iodate ores; it should not be equated with the seaweed source emphasized in nineteenth-century teaching.[Natural abundance, iodide in seawater and modern brine/iodate sources](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/iodine-element-information-properties-and-uses)

## 11

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry.md)

**Original question.**

In what forms does carbon appear ?

<h3 id="11/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [11](#11)

**The conventional nineteenth-century answer is [diamond](../../../chemical-substance.md#diamond), [graphite](../../../chemical-substance.md#graphite) and so-called [amorphous carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#amorphous-carbon).** These exhibit strikingly different physical properties while sharing the same [chemical element](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-element), [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon).

[Diamond](../../../chemical-substance.md#diamond) is a crystalline, usually transparent form, very hard and ordinarily an electrical insulator. Its three-dimensional [covalent bond](../../../covalent-bond.md) network joins each [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) [chemical atom](../../../physics.md#atom-physics) to four neighbours. [Graphite](../../../chemical-substance.md#graphite) is a dark, soft, crystalline form with layered bonding; easy shear between layers makes it useful for marking and lubrication. Its electronic structure permits much greater [electrical conductivity](../../../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductivity) than that of ordinary [diamond](../../../chemical-substance.md#diamond).

The historical “amorphous” class includes [charcoal](../../../chemical-substance.md#charcoal), [coke](../../../chemical-substance.md#coke), lampblack and [soot](../../../chemical-substance.md#soot), as well as [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-rich materials such as [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas)-retort [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon). Such names describe preparation and texture as well as structure: restricted-[air](../../../mixture.md#air) heating of wood gives [charcoal](../../../chemical-substance.md#charcoal), destructive distillation of coal gives [coke](../../../chemical-substance.md#coke), and incomplete [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) of a [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-bearing [fuel](../../../chemical-reaction.md#fuel) produces [soot](../../../chemical-substance.md#soot). Modern structural examination shows that these materials can contain small, imperfect graphitic regions. They are not necessarily one distinct pure [allotrope](../../../chemical-substance.md#allotrope). [Coal](../../../chemical-substance.md#coal) itself is a chemically complex, impure [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon)-rich material, rather than a pure form of the [chemical element](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-element).

A common chemical connection is that pure samples of these [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) forms can yield [carbon dioxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-dioxide) upon complete [combustion](../../../chemical-reaction.md#combustion) in excess [oxygen](../../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm C+\mathrm O_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{CO}_2.}
$$

Their different [hardness](../../../engineering.md#hardness), appearance and [electrical conductivity](../../../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductivity) arise from structure and impurities, rather than from being different [chemical elements](../../../chemical-substance.md#chemical-element). This is the explanatory role of [allotropy](../../../chemical-substance.md#allotropy).

Modern lists also include [fullerenes](../../../chemical-substance.md#fullerene), [carbon nanotubes](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-nanotube) and [graphene](../../../chemical-substance.md#graphene). These are useful present-day extensions, not discoveries to credit to the 1867 candidate.[Appearance; Uses, charcoal and coke as impure [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon); discussion of modern [carbon](../../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) forms](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/carbon-element-information-properties-and-uses)

**Original question.**

How may a liquid be tested for hydrocyanic acid, and the quantity present be estimated ?

<h3 id="12/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12](#12)

**Confirm [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) by chemical tests on a suitable separated fraction, then estimate its amount with a defined quantitative assay.** Here “hydrocyanic acid” means $\mathrm{HCN}$ in [water](../../../chemical-substance.md#water), also historically called prussic acid. Smelling or tasting is not an analytical test: [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) is acutely poisonous, and its characteristic odour is not reliably perceived.[Agent characteristics; odor warning and exposure hazards](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#hydrogen-cyanide-emergency-response-information-by-niosh)

A coloured or complex [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) can mask the tests or contain interfering ions. Historically, controlled [distillation](../../../chemistry.md#distillation) separated volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) from many nonvolatile constituents; a receiving solution captures the analyte. In a modern analytical apparatus, the closed transfer system includes a condenser and an alkaline [gas](../../../thermodynamics.md#gas) absorber. The receiver converts volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) into less volatile [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide):

$$
\mathrm{HCN}+\mathrm{OH}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{CN}^-+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.
$$

The measured fraction must be defined: free [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) in the original [liquid](../../../thermodynamics.md#liquid) is not automatically identical to “total [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide)” released from complexes by a more aggressive preparation. A blank, a known positive control and recovery checks help distinguish a negative sample from failed separation or reagent failure.[§§1–4, scope and interferences; §§8–9, sample handling and quality control](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)

For the historical [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) test, make a separate test portion alkaline, introduce fresh [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii) salt together with an [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) source, and finally acidify as specified by the historical procedure. [Cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) first forms [ferrocyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#ferrocyanide) with [iron(II)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-ii); [iron(III)](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-iii) then gives the characteristic [Prussian blue](../../../chemistry.md#prussian-blue) precipitate. Idealized balances explaining the recognition chemistry are

$$
\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}+6\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-},
$$



$$
4\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}+3[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe}_4[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]_3\downarrow.
$$

Real [Prussian blue](../../../chemistry.md#prussian-blue) can be hydrated and nonstoichiometric; the second [chemical equation](../../../chemical-reaction.md#chemical-equation) is a useful idealization. The acid stage also removes obscuring [iron hydroxide](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron-hydroxide) precipitates. These steps explain the old mixed [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron)-salt test, rather than assuming that [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) simply reacts directly with any [iron](../../../chemical-substance.md#iron) salt to give blue material.[Acidum Hydrocyanicum Dilutum, printed p. 14, mixed ferrous/ferric salt, potash and acid sequence](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-1867)

A second aliquot can be examined with [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate). [Silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide) is white, but whiteness alone is not specific because [silver chloride](../../../optics.md#silver-chloride) and other precipitates can interfere:

$$
\mathrm{Ag}^++\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{AgCN}\downarrow.
$$

For a purified aliquot, precipitate the [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide) quantitatively with [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate), separate the [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide), wash it free of soluble salts, dry it to constant [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) and weigh it. The precipitation conditions must avoid losses as volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide) and dissolution as [dicyanoargentate](../../../chemical-substance.md#dicyanoargentate); interfering precipitates must be excluded. For complete collection of pure, dry [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide), [gravimetric analysis](../../../chemistry.md#gravimetric-analysis) gives

$$
\boxed{m(\mathrm{HCN})=\frac{27.03}{133.89}\,m(\mathrm{AgCN})\simeq0.2019\,m(\mathrm{AgCN}).}
$$

Thus $10$ [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) units of [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide) correspond to about $2.02$ [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) units of [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide). The 1867 pharmacopoeial check was $10$ grains of dry [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide) from $100$ grains of the diluted acid, corresponding approximately to its stated $2\%$ strength by [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass).[Acidum Hydrocyanicum Dilutum, printed pp. 13–14, identity, strength and silver-precipitation assay](../../../pharmacology-and-toxicology-bibliography.md#british-pharmacopoeia-1867) A fraction or dilution of a sample requires the corresponding scaling back to the original quantity.

Alternatively, an alkaline [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate) [titration](../../../chemistry.md#titration) depends on soluble [dicyanoargentate](../../../chemical-substance.md#dicyanoargentate) formation:

$$
\mathrm{Ag}^++2\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Ag(CN)}_2]^-.
$$

Before the endpoint, added [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide) redissolves in excess [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide). In the classical version, the first persistent turbidity marks the end of this clear-solution stage. For a clean sample, a standardized [silver nitrate](../../../chemistry.md#silver-nitrate) [molar concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#molar-concentration) $c$, a blank-corrected endpoint [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) $V$, and an aliquot fraction $f$ of the total recovered sample,

$$
\boxed{n(\mathrm{HCN})_{\rm original}=\frac{2cV}{f},\qquad m(\mathrm{HCN})_{\rm original}=\frac{2cV}{f}(27.03\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}).}
$$

Here $V$ is in litres if $c$ is in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Divide by the original sample [mass](../../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) for a [mass fraction](../../../mixture.md#mass-fraction), or by its original [volume](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#volume) for a [mass concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#mass-concentration). No numerical sample amount is supplied, so no particular measured [concentration](../../../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) can be inferred.

**Keep the two assay ratios separate: precipitation gravimetry gives one mole of [silver cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver-cyanide) per mole of [hydrogen cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen-cyanide); alkaline complex-formation [titration](../../../chemistry.md#titration) uses one mole of [silver](../../../chemical-substance.md#silver) per two moles of [cyanide](../../../chemical-substance.md#cyanide).** A modern version of the latter uses a rhodanine indicator and a blank, as in EPA Method 9014; that indicator procedure is a modern comparison, not a detail assigned to 1867.[§11.3, silver-to-cyanide reaction, rhodanine endpoint and blank; §12.2, calculation](../../../chemistry-bibliography.md#cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency)
