The following definitions use current terminology in [plant morphology](../../../../../plant-morphology.md); the examples illustrate the structures named in the historical paper.

**A [rhizome](../../../../../rhizome.md) is a persistent [plant stem](../../../../../plant-stem.md), usually growing horizontally at or beneath the ground surface.** Like other [plant stems](../../../../../plant-stem.md), a [rhizome](../../../../../rhizome.md) has [stem nodes](../../../../../stem-node.md) separated by [internodes](../../../../../internode.md), with [buds](../../../../../bud.md) and often reduced, scale-like [leaves](../../../../../leaf.md). It can produce upward-growing [shoots](../../../../../shoot.md) and [adventitious roots](../../../../../adventitious-root.md). These features identify it as a [plant stem](../../../../../plant-stem.md), even though it is underground: a [root](../../../../../root-botany.md) does not bear [leaves](../../../../../leaf.md) at [stem nodes](../../../../../stem-node.md). The fleshy, branched part of [ginger](../../../../../ginger.md) used as a spice is a [rhizome](../../../../../rhizome.md), rather than a [root](../../../../../root-botany.md).[Q–R, entries “rhizome” and “root”; I–L, entry “internode”](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[Plant description: branched underground stem and annual growth from it](../../../../../ginger-by-the-royal-botanic-gardens-kew.md)

**A [spathe](../../../../../spathe.md) is an enlarged, sheathing [bract](../../../../../bract.md) that encloses, at least initially, a [flower](../../../../../flower.md) or [inflorescence](../../../../../inflorescence.md).** A [bract](../../../../../bract.md) is a modified [leaf](../../../../../leaf.md) associated with a [flower](../../../../../flower.md) or [inflorescence](../../../../../inflorescence.md). A familiar example is the pale green hood surrounding the [spadix](../../../../../spadix.md) in [Arum maculatum](../../../../../arum-maculatum.md), or [cuckoo-pint](../../../../../arum-maculatum.md). The [spadix](../../../../../spadix.md) is the thickened axis bearing the small [flowers](../../../../../flower.md); the surrounding hood is the [spathe](../../../../../spathe.md). The [spathe](../../../../../spathe.md) therefore belongs to the covering of the [inflorescence](../../../../../inflorescence.md), rather than being a large [petal](../../../../../petal.md) of one [flower](../../../../../flower.md).[S, entries “spathe” and “spadix”](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[CATE Araceae description: spathe tube, spathe limb and flower-bearing spadix](../../../../../arum-maculatum-in-plants-of-the-world-online.md)

**The [perianth](../../../../../perianth.md) is the floral envelope: the [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) and [corolla](../../../../../corolla.md) considered together, or whichever of these is present.** The [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) comprises the [sepals](../../../../../sepal.md), and the [corolla](../../../../../corolla.md) comprises the [petals](../../../../../petal.md). When the two sets look alike and are not differentiated into a recognizable [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) and [corolla](../../../../../corolla.md), their individual members are called [tepals](../../../../../tepal.md). A [lily](../../../../../lilium.md) provides an example: its six conspicuous [tepals](../../../../../tepal.md) form the [perianth](../../../../../perianth.md). The word is especially useful for such an undifferentiated envelope, but its collective meaning also includes a distinct [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) and [corolla](../../../../../corolla.md). The [stamens](../../../../../stamen.md) and [carpels](../../../../../carpel.md) are separate reproductive organs and are excluded from the [perianth](../../../../../perianth.md).[P, entry “perianth”; T–U, entry “tepal”](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[Generic description: six free tepals, followed separately by stamens and ovary](../../../../../lilium-in-new-south-wales-flora-online.md)

**An [involucre](../../../../../involucre.md) is a ring, or several rings, of [bracts](../../../../../bract.md) surrounding a [flower](../../../../../flower.md) or [inflorescence](../../../../../inflorescence.md).** In the [common daisy](../../../../../bellis-perennis.md), the small green [bracts](../../../../../bract.md) underneath and around the base of the [flower head](../../../../../flower-head.md) together form its [involucre](../../../../../involucre.md). That apparent single blossom is actually a [flower head](../../../../../flower-head.md) containing many small [flowers](../../../../../flower.md), called [florets](../../../../../floret.md). Its [involucre](../../../../../involucre.md) surrounds this entire cluster; it is distinct from the [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) belonging to an individual [flower](../../../../../flower.md).[I–L, entry “involucre”, flowering-plant sense](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[Description: involucral bracts, white ray florets and yellow disc florets](../../../../../bellis-perennis-in-new-south-wales-flora-online.md)

**A [sepal](../../../../../sepal.md) is one of the parts making up the [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md), the outer floral envelope.** [Sepals](../../../../../sepal.md) are commonly green and protect the other parts of a [flower](../../../../../flower.md) while it is in [bud](../../../../../bud.md). They may remain separate or be joined into a [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) with free lobes or teeth. In a typical [buttercup](../../../../../ranunculus.md), the five outer [sepals](../../../../../sepal.md) surround the inner, usually yellow [petals](../../../../../petal.md). Thus one part is a [sepal](../../../../../sepal.md), the whole outer set is the [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md), and the [calyx](../../../../../calyx.md) plus [corolla](../../../../../corolla.md) is the [perianth](../../../../../perianth.md).[S, entries “sepal” and “segment”; C, entry “calyx”](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[Generic description: usually five sepals and five or more yellow or white petals](../../../../../ranunculus-in-new-south-wales-flora-online.md)

**An [umbel](../../../../../umbel.md) is an [inflorescence](../../../../../inflorescence.md) in which the [flower](../../../../../flower.md) stalks arise together from one point at the top of a common stalk.** The stalk of each individual [flower](../../../../../flower.md) is a [pedicel](../../../../../pedicel.md); the common supporting stalk is the [peduncle](../../../../../peduncle.md). The [pedicels](../../../../../pedicel.md) spread out like umbrella ribs and are often of roughly equal length, producing a rounded or relatively level cluster. A flowering [onion](../../../../../allium-cepa.md) is an example of a simple [umbel](../../../../../umbel.md), with individual [flowers](../../../../../flower.md) at the ends of its [pedicels](../../../../../pedicel.md). In a [compound umbel](../../../../../compound-umbel.md), the primary branches instead end in smaller [umbels](../../../../../umbel.md), called [umbellets](../../../../../umbellet.md), each carrying its own [flowers](../../../../../flower.md). The defining feature is where the stalks join: a rounded outline alone does not establish that a cluster is an [umbel](../../../../../umbel.md).[T–U, entry “umbel”, figure 17 J; P, entries “pedicel” and “peduncle”](../../../../../glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet.md)[Generic description: umbellate inflorescence; cultivated examples include onion, Allium cepa](../../../../../allium-in-new-south-wales-flora-online.md)[Entries “ray” and “umbellet”; figure 14, compound umbel](../../../../../glossary-of-the-flora-of-newfoundland-and-labrador.md)
