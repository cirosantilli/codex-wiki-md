↑ **Parent:** [12](../12.md)

**Confirm [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) by chemical tests on a suitable separated fraction, then estimate its amount with a defined quantitative assay.** Here “hydrocyanic acid” means $\mathrm{HCN}$ in [water](../../../../../water.md), also historically called prussic acid. Smelling or tasting is not an analytical test: [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) is acutely poisonous, and its characteristic odour is not reliably perceived.[Agent characteristics; odor warning and exposure hazards](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide-emergency-response-information-by-niosh.md)

A coloured or complex [liquid](../../../../../liquid.md) can mask the tests or contain interfering ions. Historically, controlled [distillation](../../../../../distillation.md) separated volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) from many nonvolatile constituents; a receiving solution captures the analyte. In a modern analytical apparatus, the closed transfer system includes a condenser and an alkaline [gas](../../../../../gas.md) absorber. The receiver converts volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) into less volatile [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md):

$$
\mathrm{HCN}+\mathrm{OH}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{CN}^-+\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.
$$

The measured fraction must be defined: free [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) in the original [liquid](../../../../../liquid.md) is not automatically identical to “total [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md)” released from complexes by a more aggressive preparation. A blank, a known positive control and recovery checks help distinguish a negative sample from failed separation or reagent failure.[§§1–4, scope and interferences; §§8–9, sample handling and quality control](../../../../../cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency.md)

For the historical [iron](../../../../../iron.md) test, make a separate test portion alkaline, introduce fresh [iron(II)](../../../../../iron-ii.md) salt together with an [iron(III)](../../../../../iron-iii.md) source, and finally acidify as specified by the historical procedure. [Cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md) first forms [ferrocyanide](../../../../../ferrocyanide.md) with [iron(II)](../../../../../iron-ii.md); [iron(III)](../../../../../iron-iii.md) then gives the characteristic [Prussian blue](../../../../../prussian-blue.md) precipitate. Idealized balances explaining the recognition chemistry are

$$
\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}+6\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-},
$$



$$
4\mathrm{Fe}^{3+}+3[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Fe}_4[\mathrm{Fe(CN)}_6]_3\downarrow.
$$

Real [Prussian blue](../../../../../prussian-blue.md) can be hydrated and nonstoichiometric; the second [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md) is a useful idealization. The acid stage also removes obscuring [iron hydroxide](../../../../../iron-hydroxide.md) precipitates. These steps explain the old mixed [iron](../../../../../iron.md)-salt test, rather than assuming that [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md) simply reacts directly with any [iron](../../../../../iron.md) salt to give blue material.[Acidum Hydrocyanicum Dilutum, printed p. 14, mixed ferrous/ferric salt, potash and acid sequence](../../../../../british-pharmacopoeia-1867.md)

A second aliquot can be examined with [silver nitrate](../../../../../silver-nitrate.md). [Silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md) is white, but whiteness alone is not specific because [silver chloride](../../../../../silver-chloride.md) and other precipitates can interfere:

$$
\mathrm{Ag}^++\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{AgCN}\downarrow.
$$

For a purified aliquot, precipitate the [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md) quantitatively with [silver nitrate](../../../../../silver-nitrate.md), separate the [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md), wash it free of soluble salts, dry it to constant [mass](../../../../../mass.md) and weigh it. The precipitation conditions must avoid losses as volatile [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) and dissolution as [dicyanoargentate](../../../../../dicyanoargentate.md); interfering precipitates must be excluded. For complete collection of pure, dry [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md), [gravimetric analysis](../../../../../gravimetric-analysis.md) gives

$$
\boxed{m(\mathrm{HCN})=\frac{27.03}{133.89}\,m(\mathrm{AgCN})\simeq0.2019\,m(\mathrm{AgCN}).}
$$

Thus $10$ [mass](../../../../../mass.md) units of [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md) correspond to about $2.02$ [mass](../../../../../mass.md) units of [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md). The 1867 pharmacopoeial check was $10$ grains of dry [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md) from $100$ grains of the diluted acid, corresponding approximately to its stated $2\%$ strength by [mass](../../../../../mass.md).[Acidum Hydrocyanicum Dilutum, printed pp. 13–14, identity, strength and silver-precipitation assay](../../../../../british-pharmacopoeia-1867.md) A fraction or dilution of a sample requires the corresponding scaling back to the original quantity.

Alternatively, an alkaline [silver nitrate](../../../../../silver-nitrate.md) [titration](../../../../../titration.md) depends on soluble [dicyanoargentate](../../../../../dicyanoargentate.md) formation:

$$
\mathrm{Ag}^++2\mathrm{CN}^-\longrightarrow[\mathrm{Ag(CN)}_2]^-.
$$

Before the endpoint, added [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md) redissolves in excess [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md). In the classical version, the first persistent turbidity marks the end of this clear-solution stage. For a clean sample, a standardized [silver nitrate](../../../../../silver-nitrate.md) [molar concentration](../../../../../molar-concentration.md) $c$, a blank-corrected endpoint [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $V$, and an aliquot fraction $f$ of the total recovered sample,

$$
\boxed{n(\mathrm{HCN})_{\rm original}=\frac{2cV}{f},\qquad m(\mathrm{HCN})_{\rm original}=\frac{2cV}{f}(27.03\ \mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}).}
$$

Here $V$ is in litres if $c$ is in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Divide by the original sample [mass](../../../../../mass.md) for a [mass fraction](../../../../../mass-fraction.md), or by its original [volume](../../../../../volume.md) for a [mass concentration](../../../../../mass-concentration.md). No numerical sample amount is supplied, so no particular measured [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) can be inferred.

**Keep the two assay ratios separate: precipitation gravimetry gives one mole of [silver cyanide](../../../../../silver-cyanide.md) per mole of [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md); alkaline complex-formation [titration](../../../../../titration.md) uses one mole of [silver](../../../../../silver.md) per two moles of [cyanide](../../../../../cyanide.md).** A modern version of the latter uses a rhodanine indicator and a blank, as in EPA Method 9014; that indicator procedure is a modern comparison, not a detail assigned to 1867.[§11.3, silver-to-cyanide reaction, rhodanine endpoint and blank; §12.2, calculation](../../../../../cyanide-in-waters-and-extracts-method-9014-by-united-states-environmental-protection-agency.md)
