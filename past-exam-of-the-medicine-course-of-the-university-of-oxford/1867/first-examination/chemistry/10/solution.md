<h1 id="10/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10](../10.md)

**[Iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) is a dark, lustrous crystalline [halogen](../../../../../halogen.md) which gives a violet vapour; historically, an important source was the ash of [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md)-rich seaweeds.** Elemental [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) commonly occurs as diatomic $\mathrm I_2$. It melts at about $114\ ^\circ\mathrm C$ and boils at about $184\ ^\circ\mathrm C$ at ordinary atmospheric [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md). It also undergoes [sublimation](../../../../../sublimation.md) below its melting point, depositing crystals again on a cooler surface.[Fact box; Appearance; Natural abundance](../../../../../periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/iodine-element-information-properties-and-uses.md)

[Iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) is sparingly soluble in [water](../../../../../water.md), giving a brownish solution, and dissolves more freely in [ethanol](../../../../../ethanol.md) and in an aqueous [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) solution. In the latter case the [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md)

$$
\mathrm I_2+\mathrm I^-\rightleftharpoons\mathrm I_3^-
$$

forms [triiodide](../../../../../triiodide.md). [Iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) with [starch](../../../../../starch.md), under suitable conditions, gives a sensitive dark-blue colour; this is a useful recognition test, but oxidants that liberate [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) from added [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) must be distinguished from [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) initially present. Reaction with reactive [metals](../../../../../metal.md) gives [iodides](../../../../../iodide.md); in such compounds the [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) usually has [oxidation state](../../../../../oxidation-state.md) $-1$.

For the historical seaweed route, collect and burn [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md)-accumulating seaweeds to produce [kelp ash](../../../../../kelp-ash.md), then extract the soluble salts with [water](../../../../../water.md). Crystallization removes much of the common salt content, leaving an iodide-enriched mother liquor. Oxidation releases [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md), which can be distilled or sublimed and collected on a cool surface. One representative net [chemical equation](../../../../../chemical-equation.md) for liberation from [iodide](../../../../../iodide.md) is

$$
\boxed{2\mathrm I^-+\mathrm{MnO}_2+4\mathrm H^+\longrightarrow\mathrm I_2+\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}+2\mathrm H_2\mathrm O.}
$$

The [manganese dioxide](../../../../../manganese-dioxide.md) is the [oxidizing agent](../../../../../oxidizing-agent.md); the seaweed does not contain a supply of ready-made violet [iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) crystals. This describes the chemical principle of a historical extraction, rather than prescribing one unique factory recipe.

[Iodine](../../../../../iodine.md) also occurs in natural brines and in [iodate](../../../../../iodate.md)-bearing deposits associated with nitrate ores. Modern industrial supply relies heavily on brines and iodate ores; it should not be equated with the seaweed source emphasized in nineteenth-century teaching.[Natural abundance, iodide in seawater and modern brine/iodate sources](../../../../../periodic-table-by-royal-society-of-chemistry/iodine-element-information-properties-and-uses.md)
