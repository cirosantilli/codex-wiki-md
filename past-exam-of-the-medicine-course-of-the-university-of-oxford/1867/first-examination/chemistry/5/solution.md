**[Atomic theory](../../../../../atomic-theory.md) explains fixed composition by fixed numbers of [chemical atoms](../../../../../atom-physics.md), and [multiple proportions](../../../../../law-of-multiple-proportions.md) by different small whole-number combinations of the same [chemical atoms](../../../../../atom-physics.md).** The two laws are empirical regularities which the theory explains; they are not themselves direct observations of individual [chemical atoms](../../../../../atom-physics.md).

For the [law of definite proportions](../../../../../law-of-definite-proportions.md), suppose a pure [chemical compound](../../../../../chemical-compound.md) has composition $\mathrm A_x\mathrm B_y$. If the [atomic masses](../../../../../atomic-mass.md) are $m_A$ and $m_B$, every formula unit contains masses $xm_A$ and $ym_B$. Enlarging the sample multiplies both by the same number, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{\text{mass of B}}{\text{mass of A}}=\frac{ym_B}{xm_A}.}
$$

The [mass ratio](../../../../../mass-ratio.md) is therefore independent of sample size or preparation route, provided the same pure stoichiometric [chemical compound](../../../../../chemical-compound.md) is obtained. Ordinary [water](../../../../../water.md) supplies an example: its [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md)-to-[oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [mass ratio](../../../../../mass-ratio.md) is approximately $1:8$.

For the [law of multiple proportions](../../../../../law-of-multiple-proportions.md), compare $\mathrm A_x\mathrm B_y$ and $\mathrm A_u\mathrm B_v$. At a fixed [mass](../../../../../mass.md) of A, the masses of B are in the ratio

$$
\boxed{\frac{y/x}{v/u}=\frac{yu}{vx}.}
$$

This is a ratio of integers, and is often a ratio of small integers for simple [chemical compounds](../../../../../chemical-compound.md). With [carbon monoxide](../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) and [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md), about $12$ [mass](../../../../../mass.md) units of [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) combine with about $16$ and $32$ of [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md): the [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) masses are in the ratio $1:2$. The [chemical formulas](../../../../../chemical-formula.md) explain why adding a second [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) [chemical atom](../../../../../atom-physics.md) doubles the [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) contribution without changing the [carbon](../../../../../carbon.md) contribution.

The laws alone do not uniquely determine the correct [chemical formulas](../../../../../chemical-formula.md) or the [atomic weights](../../../../../relative-atomic-mass.md); independent information is needed to distinguish, for example, one formula unit from a multiple of it. Modern qualifications include variations in [isotopic composition](../../../../../isotopic-composition.md) and [non-stoichiometric compounds](../../../../../non-stoichiometric-compound.md). These refine the simple nineteenth-century formulation without removing its usefulness for pure compounds of fixed composition.
