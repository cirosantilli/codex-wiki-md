## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Botany](botany.md)

**Original question.**

What is the meaning of the terms :—Rhizome—Spathe—Perianth—Involucre—Sepal—Umbel ?

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

The following definitions use current terminology in [plant morphology](../../../botany.md#plant-morphology); the examples illustrate the structures named in the historical paper.

**A [rhizome](../../../botany.md#rhizome) is a persistent [plant stem](../../../botany.md#plant-stem), usually growing horizontally at or beneath the ground surface.** Like other [plant stems](../../../botany.md#plant-stem), a [rhizome](../../../botany.md#rhizome) has [stem nodes](../../../botany.md#stem-node) separated by [internodes](../../../botany.md#internode), with [buds](../../../botany.md#bud) and often reduced, scale-like [leaves](../../../organism.md#leaf). It can produce upward-growing [shoots](../../../botany.md#shoot) and [adventitious roots](../../../organism.md#adventitious-root). These features identify it as a [plant stem](../../../botany.md#plant-stem), even though it is underground: a [root](../../../organism.md#root-botany) does not bear [leaves](../../../organism.md#leaf) at [stem nodes](../../../botany.md#stem-node). The fleshy, branched part of [ginger](../../../organism.md#ginger) used as a spice is a [rhizome](../../../botany.md#rhizome), rather than a [root](../../../organism.md#root-botany).[Q–R, entries “rhizome” and “root”; I–L, entry “internode”](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[Plant description: branched underground stem and annual growth from it](../../../botany.md#ginger-by-the-royal-botanic-gardens-kew)

**A [spathe](../../../botany.md#spathe) is an enlarged, sheathing [bract](../../../botany.md#bract) that encloses, at least initially, a [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) or [inflorescence](../../../botany.md#inflorescence).** A [bract](../../../botany.md#bract) is a modified [leaf](../../../organism.md#leaf) associated with a [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) or [inflorescence](../../../botany.md#inflorescence). A familiar example is the pale green hood surrounding the [spadix](../../../botany.md#spadix) in [Arum maculatum](../../../organism.md#arum-maculatum), or [cuckoo-pint](../../../organism.md#arum-maculatum). The [spadix](../../../botany.md#spadix) is the thickened axis bearing the small [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower); the surrounding hood is the [spathe](../../../botany.md#spathe). The [spathe](../../../botany.md#spathe) therefore belongs to the covering of the [inflorescence](../../../botany.md#inflorescence), rather than being a large [petal](../../../organism.md#petal) of one [flower](../../../organism.md#flower).[S, entries “spathe” and “spadix”](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[CATE Araceae description: spathe tube, spathe limb and flower-bearing spadix](../../../botany.md#arum-maculatum-in-plants-of-the-world-online)

**The [perianth](../../../organism.md#perianth) is the floral envelope: the [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) and [corolla](../../../organism.md#corolla) considered together, or whichever of these is present.** The [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) comprises the [sepals](../../../organism.md#sepal), and the [corolla](../../../organism.md#corolla) comprises the [petals](../../../organism.md#petal). When the two sets look alike and are not differentiated into a recognizable [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) and [corolla](../../../organism.md#corolla), their individual members are called [tepals](../../../organism.md#tepal). A [lily](../../../organism.md#lilium) provides an example: its six conspicuous [tepals](../../../organism.md#tepal) form the [perianth](../../../organism.md#perianth). The word is especially useful for such an undifferentiated envelope, but its collective meaning also includes a distinct [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) and [corolla](../../../organism.md#corolla). The [stamens](../../../organism.md#stamen) and [carpels](../../../organism.md#carpel) are separate reproductive organs and are excluded from the [perianth](../../../organism.md#perianth).[P, entry “perianth”; T–U, entry “tepal”](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[Generic description: six free tepals, followed separately by stamens and ovary](../../../botany.md#lilium-in-new-south-wales-flora-online)

**An [involucre](../../../botany.md#involucre) is a ring, or several rings, of [bracts](../../../botany.md#bract) surrounding a [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) or [inflorescence](../../../botany.md#inflorescence).** In the [common daisy](../../../organism.md#bellis-perennis), the small green [bracts](../../../botany.md#bract) underneath and around the base of the [flower head](../../../botany.md#flower-head) together form its [involucre](../../../botany.md#involucre). That apparent single blossom is actually a [flower head](../../../botany.md#flower-head) containing many small [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower), called [florets](../../../organism.md#floret). Its [involucre](../../../botany.md#involucre) surrounds this entire cluster; it is distinct from the [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) belonging to an individual [flower](../../../organism.md#flower).[I–L, entry “involucre”, flowering-plant sense](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[Description: involucral bracts, white ray florets and yellow disc florets](../../../botany.md#bellis-perennis-in-new-south-wales-flora-online)

**A [sepal](../../../organism.md#sepal) is one of the parts making up the [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx), the outer floral envelope.** [Sepals](../../../organism.md#sepal) are commonly green and protect the other parts of a [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) while it is in [bud](../../../botany.md#bud). They may remain separate or be joined into a [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) with free lobes or teeth. In a typical [buttercup](../../../organism.md#ranunculus), the five outer [sepals](../../../organism.md#sepal) surround the inner, usually yellow [petals](../../../organism.md#petal). Thus one part is a [sepal](../../../organism.md#sepal), the whole outer set is the [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx), and the [calyx](../../../organism.md#calyx) plus [corolla](../../../organism.md#corolla) is the [perianth](../../../organism.md#perianth).[S, entries “sepal” and “segment”; C, entry “calyx”](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[Generic description: usually five sepals and five or more yellow or white petals](../../../botany.md#ranunculus-in-new-south-wales-flora-online)

**An [umbel](../../../botany.md#umbel) is an [inflorescence](../../../botany.md#inflorescence) in which the [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) stalks arise together from one point at the top of a common stalk.** The stalk of each individual [flower](../../../organism.md#flower) is a [pedicel](../../../botany.md#pedicel); the common supporting stalk is the [peduncle](../../../botany.md#peduncle). The [pedicels](../../../botany.md#pedicel) spread out like umbrella ribs and are often of roughly equal length, producing a rounded or relatively level cluster. A flowering [onion](../../../organism.md#allium-cepa) is an example of a simple [umbel](../../../botany.md#umbel), with individual [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower) at the ends of its [pedicels](../../../botany.md#pedicel). In a [compound umbel](../../../botany.md#compound-umbel), the primary branches instead end in smaller [umbels](../../../botany.md#umbel), called [umbellets](../../../botany.md#umbellet), each carrying its own [flowers](../../../organism.md#flower). The defining feature is where the stalks join: a rounded outline alone does not establish that a cluster is an [umbel](../../../botany.md#umbel).[T–U, entry “umbel”, figure 17 J; P, entries “pedicel” and “peduncle”](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-botanical-terms-by-plantnet)[Generic description: umbellate inflorescence; cultivated examples include onion, Allium cepa](../../../botany.md#allium-in-new-south-wales-flora-online)[Entries “ray” and “umbellet”; figure 14, compound umbel](../../../botany.md#glossary-of-the-flora-of-newfoundland-and-labrador)
