<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Clark–Wells model](../../../../clark-wells-model-of-social-anxiety-disorder.md) is a useful, partly experimentally supported account of [adolescent](../../../../adolescent.md) [social anxiety](../../../../social-anxiety.md), but its applicability must be assessed at the level of particular processes. It proposes that feared evaluation activates negative assumptions, [self-focused attention](../../../../self-focused-attention.md) and an internally generated impression of how one appears. [safety behaviors](../../../../safety-behavior.md) can interfere with the interaction or make a satisfactory outcome seem dependent on protection. Anticipatory and [post-event processing](../../../../post-event-processing.md) can perpetuate the threat interpretation. Its adult origins do not establish that all these processes operate identically at every developmental stage.[Clinical Child and Family Psychology Review 21, 388–414; cognitive model and developmental qualifications](../../../../understanding-social-anxiety-disorder-in-adolescents-and-improving-treatment-outcomes-applying-the-cognitive-model-of-clark-and-wells-1995-by-eleanor-leigh-and-david-m-clark.md)

For an [adolescent](../../../../adolescent.md), the account gives testable examples. A pupil who believes that visible hesitation will lead to rejection may monitor their voice, mentally rehearse every sentence and speak very little. Monitoring consumes [attention](../../../../attention.md) that could be used to follow the conversation; silence changes how the interaction proceeds; afterwards, the pupil may treat their felt [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) as evidence that their performance looked disastrous. A [behavioral experiment](../../../../behavioral-experiment.md) should test the feared prediction and the effects of these strategies, rather than merely reassure the pupil that peers will approve. Actual bullying or exclusion also matters: a clinically useful account must include the social environment, not classify every negative expectation as an error.

[Eleanor Leigh](../../../../eleanor-leigh.md) and colleagues provided an experimental test in 57 high- and low-social-anxiety [adolescents](../../../../adolescent.md). Participants held counterbalanced conversations while either focusing internally and using [safety behaviors](../../../../safety-behavior.md), or focusing externally and dropping those behaviours. The internal-focus/safety-behaviour condition increased experienced and apparent [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) and undermined performance, using self, conversation-partner and independent ratings. This gives stronger causal evidence than a [correlation](../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) between [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) and reported strategies. But the two processes were manipulated together, participants were selected by symptom scores rather than all having a [clinical diagnosis](../../../../clinical-diagnosis.md), and brief instructed behaviour need not reproduce the chronic disorder.[PLOS ONE 16, e0247703; counterbalanced conversations, 57 participants, partner and independent ratings](../../../../self-focused-attention-and-safety-behaviours-maintain-social-anxiety-in-adolescents-an-experimental-study-by-eleanor-leigh-and-others.md)

Treatment evidence is consistent with clinical utility but tests a package. [Jo Magne Ingul](../../../../jo-magne-ingul.md) and colleagues [randomised](../../../../randomization.md) 57 thirteen- to sixteen-year-olds with primary [social phobia](../../../../social-anxiety-disorder.md) to individual [cognitive therapy](../../../../cognitive-therapy.md), group [CBT](../../../../cognitive-behavioral-therapy.md) or attentional [placebo](../../../../placebo.md). Individual [cognitive therapy](../../../../cognitive-therapy.md) produced greater improvement in symptoms and [impairment](../../../../functional-impairment.md), with benefits assessed through twelve months. Differences in format and delivery, however, accompany the difference in formulation. Improvement cannot establish which proposed process was necessary, or exclude explanations involving individual [attention](../../../../attention.md), [exposure](../../../../exposure-therapy.md) opportunities or treatment competence.[Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics 83, 54–61; 57 adolescents aged 13–16, randomised treatments and twelve-month follow-up](../../../../a-randomized-controlled-trial-of-individual-cognitive-therapy-group-cognitive-behaviour-therapy-and-attentional-placebo-for-adolescent-social-phobia-by-jo-magne-ingul-and-others.md)

Developmental qualifications are substantive. [Adolescents](../../../../adolescent.md)' models of other minds, self-description and ability to notice a mental image are still changing. A failure to articulate a distorted self-image may reflect the measure or interview demands, rather than its absence; conversely, an eloquent account elicited by an interviewer is not proof that the image drives [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) in daily life. Peer evaluation can be particularly consequential because school interactions are repeated and difficult to leave. Parents may arrange or prevent social opportunities, while online interactions introduce different opportunities for rehearsal and concealment. These issues suggest adapting assessment and delivery while testing, rather than presuming, the proposed mechanisms.

An informative new experiment would separate the bundled processes. Recruit a multisite sample of [adolescents](../../../../adolescent.md) with diagnosed [social anxiety disorder](../../../../social-anxiety-disorder.md), alongside an age-matched comparison sample. Within each group, [randomise](../../../../randomization.md) participants to a two-by-two design: internal versus external [attention](../../../../attention.md), and use versus reduction of individually identified [safety behaviors](../../../../safety-behavior.md) during one standardised conversation. A between-participant design avoids [learning](../../../../learning.md) and carry-over across four conversations; the required sample should be [powered](../../../../statistical-power.md) for prespecified interactions and meaningful effects, using pilot variability and plausible smallest effects rather than adult [effect sizes](../../../../effect-size.md) alone. Diagnostic group and age are observed characteristics, so their interactions would moderate the experimental effect without being [randomised](../../../../randomization.md) causes themselves.

The conversation should use a trained, age-appropriate partner following a common script and unaware of allocation. Instructions must be comprehensible, equally credible and checked before the task. Independent coders, [masked](../../../../blinding.md) to condition and diagnostic status, would rate video-recorded performance. Record baseline [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) and predicted evaluation before the task, [self-focused attention](../../../../self-focused-attention.md) and strategy use as [manipulation checks](../../../../manipulation-check.md), [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) as the [primary outcome](../../../../primary-outcome.md), and partner evaluations, self–observer discrepancies and [post-event processing](../../../../post-event-processing.md) as secondary outcomes. A physiological measure such as [heart rate](../../../../heart-rate.md) can characterise [arousal](../../../../physiological-arousal.md), but should not be treated as a direct measurement of what the [adolescent](../../../../adolescent.md) believes others think.

The model predicts that manipulating the proposed maintaining processes should alter [anxiety](../../../../anxiety.md) and, potentially, interaction quality. Independent effects would support separating the two processes; an interaction would indicate that one changes the consequences of the other. Effects in the comparison group would not refute the model, because the clinical claim can concern habitual use and persistent feedback rather than a uniquely pathological response to the instruction. A convincing result would relate the manipulation to the specific predicted threat and self–observer gap, rather than to general distress alone.

Several controls sharpen the inference. Measure baseline social competence and verbal ability, [randomise](../../../../randomization.md) the partner/topic allocation, and use [blinded](../../../../blinding.md) coding with prespecified reliability checks. Avoid asking the partner to provide genuine humiliation: the experimental contrast concerns [attention](../../../../attention.md) and strategies in an ordinary interaction. Obtain [informed consent](../../../../informed-consent.md) and assent appropriate to the participants, agree withdrawal procedures and explain the purpose afterwards. Follow participants with a new, uncoached interaction and a brief delayed assessment to examine whether any [learning](../../../../learning.md) extends beyond immediate compliance. Prespecify exclusions, primary contrasts, missing-data handling and all subgroup analyses.

A null result is informative only if the manipulation actually changes [attention](../../../../attention.md) and strategies, measurement is reliable, and [confidence intervals](../../../../confidence-interval.md) rule out effects worth detecting. If only immediate [self-report](../../../../self-report.md) changes, demand characteristics remain a serious alternative. If [masked](../../../../blinding.md) performance and delayed threat [beliefs](../../../../belief.md) change in the predicted directions, support for an [adolescent](../../../../adolescent.md) maintaining mechanism becomes stronger, though not a full explanation of onset. By 2022, the model justified focused assessment and promising treatment development; the proposed factorial study would improve on treating a successful adult model or an effective treatment package as a complete developmental validation.
