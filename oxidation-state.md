# Oxidation state

↑ **Parent:** [Oxidation](oxidation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation_state)

An [oxidation state](oxidation-state.md) is an [Electron](electron.md)-bookkeeping assignment to a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md): its [electric charge](electric-charge.md) number after the [ionic approximation of bonds](ionic-approximation-of-bonds.md). It helps identify [oxidation](oxidation.md) and [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) and determine [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md), even for [covalent bonds](covalent-bond.md) without separate charged [ions](ion.md). In [water](water.md), each [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) has [oxidation state](oxidation-state.md) $+1$ and [oxygen](oxygen.md) has $-2$; this does not assert that a [water](water.md) [molecule](molecule.md) contains two free [protons](proton.md) and a free oxide [ion](ion.md).[Karen, McArdle and Takats, Comprehensive definition of oxidation state (IUPAC Recommendations 2016), §2, pp. 832–833: definition and equal splitting of same-element bonds](comprehensive-definition-of-oxidation-state-iupac-recommendations-2016-by-pavel-karen-and-others.md)

Three conventions answer different questions. [Formal charge](formal-charge.md) divides each bonding [electron pair](electron-pair.md) equally in a chosen [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md). [Oxidation state](oxidation-state.md) uses the [ionic approximation of bonds](ionic-approximation-of-bonds.md). [Partial atomic charge](partial-atomic-charge.md) instead partitions the actual [electron density](electron-density.md) and depends on that partition. In the ordinary [water](water.md) [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md), every [formal charge](formal-charge.md) is zero although the [oxidation states](oxidation-state.md) are not. Neither bookkeeping convention supplies numerical [partial atomic charges](partial-atomic-charge.md).
