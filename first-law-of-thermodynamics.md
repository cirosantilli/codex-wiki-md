# First law of thermodynamics

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_law_of_thermodynamics)

The first law applies [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) to a thermodynamic system. For a [closed system](closed-system.md) with unchanged bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](potential-energy.md), write

$$
dU=\delta Q-\delta W,\qquad \Delta U=Q-W.
$$

Here $U$ is [internal energy](internal-energy.md), $Q>0$ is [heat](heat.md) entering the system and $W>0$ is [work](work.md) done by the system on its surroundings. Compression therefore gives negative $W$. Some references define [work](work.md) positive when done on the system; their plus sign represents the same balance with a different convention. If bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) or [potential energy](potential-energy.md) changes, include it in the total [energy](energy.md) balance. [MIT, First law and work sign convention](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node15.html)

Supplying $1000\,\mathrm J$ of [heat](heat.md) while a gas does $300\,\mathrm J$ of [work](work.md) increases its [internal energy](internal-energy.md) by $700\,\mathrm J$. Compressing it with $200\,\mathrm J$ of input [work](work.md) in an [adiabatic process](adiabatic-process.md) instead gives $Q=0$, $W=-200\,\mathrm J$ and $\Delta U=200\,\mathrm J$.

The distinction between $d$ and $\delta$ records state versus path: [internal energy](internal-energy.md) is a [state function](state-function.md), whereas [heat](heat.md) and [work](work.md) depend on the process. For an [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) expanding reversibly at constant [temperature](temperature.md), $W=Nk_BT\log(V_2/V_1)$ and $Q=W$, since [internal energy of an ideal gas](internal-energy-of-an-ideal-gas.md) depends only on [temperature](temperature.md). Choose $Nk_BT=1000\,\mathrm J$ and $V_2=2V_1$: then $Q=W=693.15\,\mathrm J$. An insulated [free expansion](free-expansion.md) into vacuum between the same equilibrium endpoints has $Q=W=0$. Both have $\Delta U=0$, yet their transfers differ. Their common [entropy](entropy.md) increase is $Nk_B\log2$. [MIT, Ideal-gas entropy differences](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node40.html)

For a simple compressible equilibrium system of fixed composition, combining the laws gives

$$
dU=T\,dS-p\,dV.
$$

Here $p$ is [pressure](pressure.md) and $V$ is [volume](volume.md). This identity relates neighboring equilibrium states. Identifying $T\,dS$ with actual [heat](heat.md) and $p\,dV$ with actual [work](work.md) requires a suitable [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md); an irreversible piston calculation uses the external resisting [pressure](pressure.md) for boundary [work](work.md). This distinction prevents applying a [reversible ideal-gas adiabat](reversible-ideal-gas-adiabat.md) to an [irreversible adiabatic piston compression](irreversible-adiabatic-piston-compression.md).
