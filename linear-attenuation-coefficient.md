# Linear attenuation coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray attenuation](x-ray-attenuation.md)

The [linear attenuation coefficient](linear-attenuation-coefficient.md) is the infinitesimal probability of removal from the uncollided beam per unit path length. Its units are inverse [length](length.md). In the homogeneous monoenergetic model, $\mu=-d\ln I/dx$; $1/\mu$ is the thickness leaving fraction $e^{-1}$ uncollided. The [mass attenuation coefficient](mass-attenuation-coefficient.md) is $\mu/\rho$ for material [mass density](density.md) $\rho$. Removing a [photon](photon.md) by [scattering of light](scattering-of-light.md) does not imply deposition of all its [energy](energy-split.md) at that point; attenuation and [energy](energy-split.md) absorption are different quantities.[Tables of X-Ray Mass Attenuation Coefficients and Mass Energy-Absorption Coefficients by John H. Hubbell and Stephen M. Seltzer; NIST §2, mass thickness, Eq. (1), and total interaction cross section; distinction from the mass energy-absorption coefficient](tables-of-x-ray-mass-attenuation-coefficients-and-mass-energy-absorption-coefficients-by-john-h-hubbell-and-stephen-m-seltzer/2-the-mass-attenuation-coefficient.md)
