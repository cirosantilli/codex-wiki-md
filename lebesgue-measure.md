# Lebesgue measure

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-finite uniqueness theorem for measures](sigma-finite-uniqueness-theorem-for-measures.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_measure)

Lebesgue measure on the [real numbers](real-number.md) extends interval length: $\lambda([a,b])=b-a$ for $a\leq b$. Its restriction to the [Borel sigma-algebra](borel-sigma-algebra.md) is the unique translation-invariant [sigma-finite measure](sigma-finite-measure.md) on that [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) normalized by $\lambda((0,1])=1$. The full [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) is the [completion of a measure](completion-of-a-measure.md) of this restriction; its domain is the [Lebesgue measurable sets](lebesgue-measurable-set.md). Translation invariance follows by comparing a translated [Borel measure](borel-measure.md) on half-open intervals and applying the [uniqueness theorem for measures](sigma-finite-uniqueness-theorem-for-measures.md). For the construction and interval properties, see [MIT lecture 8](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-102-introduction-to-functional-analysis-spring-2021/1bf275c0667d3e903e4de22b9fb4481b_MIT18_102s21_lec8.pdf).

Every [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md) has [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) zero: $\{x\}\subseteq[x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon]$ gives $0\leq\lambda(\{x\})\leq2\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Enumerating the [countable set](countable-set.md) $\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]$ and applying [countable additivity](countable-additivity.md) yields

$$
\lambda(\mathbb Q\cap[0,1])=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda(\{q_n\})=0,
\qquad\lambda([0,1])=1.
$$

Thus a [dense set](dense-set.md) can be a [null set](null-set.md), and a [union](set-union.md) of [null sets](null-set.md) indexed by an [uncountable set](uncountable-set.md) can have positive [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md). The distinction explains both the [Dirichlet function](dirichlet-function.md) example and why [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) is weaker than everywhere.
