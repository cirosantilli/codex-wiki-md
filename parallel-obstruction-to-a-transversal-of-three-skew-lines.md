# Parallel obstruction to a transversal of three skew lines

↑ **Parent:** [Skew lines](skew-lines.md)

For three [skew lines](skew-lines.md) $L_i$ and a [point](point-geometry.md) $\mathbf y$ outside them, let $\Pi_i$ be the [plane](plane.md) through $\mathbf y$ and $L_i$. A [line](straight-line.md) through $\mathbf y$ meeting all three $L_i$ must lie in all three $\Pi_i$. Therefore the [matrix](matrix.md) of their [normal vectors](normal-vector.md) must have zero [determinant](determinant.md). This necessary condition can fail to be sufficient: the common [line](straight-line.md) of the [planes](plane.md) can be parallel to, and disjoint from, one of the original [lines](straight-line.md).

For example, take $L_1=(s,0,1)$, $L_2=(1,s,0)$, $L_3=(0,1,s)$ and $\mathbf y=(2,1,0)$. The corresponding [planes](plane.md) have equations $r_2+r_3=1$, $r_3=0$, $r_2=1$. Their common [line](straight-line.md) is $(s,1,0)$, which never meets $L_1$. This illustrates why a [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) condition for a common direction must be supplemented by intersection checks.
