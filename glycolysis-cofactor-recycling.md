# Glycolysis cofactor recycling

↑ **Parent:** [Glycolysis](glycolysis.md)

In the payoff stage of [glycolysis](glycolysis.md), [oxidation](oxidation.md) of [glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate](glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.md) is coupled to [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) of [NAD+](nad-plus.md). Each [NADH](nadh.md) carries a two-[electron](electron.md) reducing equivalent. The formal half-[chemical equation](chemical-equation.md)

$$
\mathrm{NAD}^{+}+\mathrm H^{+}+2e^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{NADH}
$$

makes the [electron](electron.md) count explicit: two [NADH](nadh.md) per [glucose](glucose.md) carry four [electrons](electron.md). This is [redox](redox.md) bookkeeping, not a claim that free [electrons](electron.md) travel through the [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) or that [NADH](nadh.md) is itself [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md).

[Fermentation](fermentation.md) can return [NADH](nadh.md) to [NAD+](nad-plus.md) by reducing an organic [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md); reduction of [pyruvate](pyruvate.md) to [lactate](lactate.md) is one example. In aerobic [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md), reducing equivalents reach an [electron transport chain](electron-transport-chain.md), reducing molecular [oxygen](oxygen.md). Other forms of [cellular respiration](cellular-respiration.md) use other [electron acceptors](electron-acceptor.md), so absence of [oxygen](oxygen.md) does not uniquely imply [fermentation](fermentation.md).[§7.2, step 6 and its NAD+ regeneration paragraph; §7.5, opening comparison of fermentation and anaerobic respiration, Lactic Acid Fermentation and Figure 7.16 caption](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)

Original pool-and-process schematic, not an anatomical map. [Fermentation](fermentation.md) and respiratory [oxidation](oxidation.md) are alternative routes for recycling reducing equivalents, not simultaneous obligatory reactions. The respiratory box groups transport and [electron transport chain](electron-transport-chain.md) steps. The lower ledger shows two net [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) per [glucose](glucose.md); separate [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) use can return [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md). Recycling material carriers does not supply an independent source of [energy](energy-split.md).

The need for [NAD+](nad-plus.md) regeneration follows from a finite-pool argument. In a hypothetical closed preparation with $12\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of initially oxidized [NAD+](nad-plus.md) and no regeneration, at most $6\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [glucose](glucose.md) can complete the two [NAD+](nad-plus.md)-consuming conversions per [glucose](glucose.md). This is an upper bound even with excess [glucose](glucose.md), not a predicted stopping time: other limitations can stop the preparation earlier. With an intact fixed [nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide](nicotinamide-adenine-dinucleotide.md) pool and no side reactions, converting [NAD+](nad-plus.md) into [NADH](nadh.md) preserves their sum but depletes the usable oxidized form.

[ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) recycling imposes a second condition. [Glycolysis](glycolysis.md) needs some initial [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) for investment and consumes two net [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and two [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md) per completed [glucose](glucose.md). In a closed ideal preparation with only $8\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of initial [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md), no other source of [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md), and enough initial [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) and [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md), at most $4\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [glucose](glucose.md) can finish. This bound follows from the net [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) consumption, even though investment temporarily produces additional [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md). Initial investment also means that adequate final inventories alone cannot guarantee the route can start.

For continued operation at fixed carrier inventories, other [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md) must match the two net [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) formed per [glucose](glucose.md) with [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) use that restores [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) and [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md), or exchange must supply those [reactants](reactant.md). [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md) coupled to cellular work is one route. Thus [glycolysis](glycolysis.md) can sustain useful [energy coupling](energy-coupling.md) while neither its [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) inventory nor its [NADH](nadh.md) inventory grows indefinitely; matter cycles while [glucose](glucose.md) supplies fresh chemical [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md).
