# Light cone

↑ **Parent:** [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cone)

The [light cone](light-cone.md) of a [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) $O$ consists of $O$ together with the [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) having [null separation](null-separation.md) from it. In the [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) of an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) centered on $O$, it obeys

$$
|\mathbf x|^2=c^2t^2.
$$

For $t>0$ it traces an expanding spherical flash of light, with radius $ct$ at time $t$; for $t<0$ it describes possible incoming light rays. A [spacetime diagram](spacetime-diagram.md) with one spatial coordinate shows the two lines $x=\pm ct$. The apparent $45^\circ$ angle uses equal scales for $x$ and $ct$ and is not a separate physical angle.

<a id="image-light-cone-boundaries-separate-timelike-null-and-spacelike-events-relative-to-the-origin"></a>
![](minkowski-light-cone-events.png)

**[Figure 12](#image-light-cone-boundaries-separate-timelike-null-and-spacelike-events-relative-to-the-origin). Light-cone boundaries separate timelike, null and spacelike events relative to the origin**.

The [light cone](light-cone.md) is the boundary, while its interior has [timelike separation](timelike-separation.md) from $O$. An outgoing light ray follows the boundary; a future-directed massive observer's [world line](world-line.md) remains inside it. The exterior has [spacelike separation](spacelike-separation.md): no signal travelling at or below the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) can connect its [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) directly to $O$. This separates possible causal influence from merely being assigned a later coordinate time.

In the diagram, $A=(4\,\mathrm s,3\,\mathrm{ls})$ is inside the future boundary, $N=(3\,\mathrm s,3\,\mathrm{ls})$ is on it, and $B=(2\,\mathrm s,3\,\mathrm{ls})$ is outside. Their squared [Minkowski intervals](minkowski-interval.md) are respectively $-7,0,5$ in $\mathrm{ls}^2$. Thus $B$ occurs later than $O$ in the displayed [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) while still being unable to receive a light-speed-or-slower signal sent from $O$.

The usefulness is immediate for an experiment: a detector's reading can depend on a source event only if a causal influence can reach it. A [Lorentz boost](lorentz-boost.md) changes [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) but preserves this classification. For a boost with $\beta=0.8$ and [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md) $\gamma=5/3$, applying the [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) gives, in [light-second](light-second.md) length units,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(ct,x)_A=(4,3)&\longmapsto (ct',x')_A=(8/3,-1/3),\\
(ct,x)_N=(3,3)&\longmapsto (ct',x')_N=(1,1),\\
(ct,x)_B=(2,3)&\longmapsto (ct',x')_B=(-2/3,7/3).
\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding squared [Minkowski intervals](minkowski-interval.md) remain $-7,0,5$. In particular $B$ is now earlier than $O$, which is allowed for [spacelike separation](spacelike-separation.md); $A$ remains later.
