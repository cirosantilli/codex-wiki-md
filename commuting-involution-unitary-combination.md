# Commuting-involution unitary combination

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary operator](unitary-operator.md)

If A and B are commuting [self-adjoint operators](self-adjoint-operator.md) with $A^2=B^2=I$, then the displayed combination is a [unitary operator](unitary-operator.md). Indeed,

$$
U^\dagger U=\tfrac12(A-iB)(A+iB)=\tfrac12(A^2+B^2+iAB-iBA)=I,
$$

and the reverse product is also I. The factors are [involutions](involution.md), and their commutation is essential to cancel the cross terms. Operators $A=U_A\otimes I$ and $B=I\otimes U_B$ on separate tensor factors provide a natural example. More generally, $\cos\theta\,A+i\sin\theta\,B$ is unitary for real $\theta$ by the same proof. Replacing i by a real coefficient usually loses this property.
