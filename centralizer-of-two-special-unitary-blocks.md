# Centralizer of two special unitary blocks

↑ **Parent:** [Centralizer](centralizer.md)

For the block embedding $SU(m)\times SU(n)\subset SU(m+n)$ with $m,n\geq2$, the [centralizer](centralizer.md) is the block-scalar [subgroup](subgroup.md) $C=\{\operatorname{diag}(zI_m,wI_n):|z|=|w|=1,\ z^mw^n=1\}$. [Schur lemma](schur-s-lemma.md) makes the diagonal blocks scalar and kills the off-diagonal [intertwiners](intertwiner.md), since the two blocks are inequivalent [representations](group-representation.md) of the product [group](group-split.md).

Put $d=\gcd(m,n)$, $a=m/d$, $b=n/d$. The map $(z,w)\mapsto z^aw^b$ has image the $d$th [roots of unity](root-of-unity.md) and connected [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) $(z,w)=(e^{ib\theta},e^{-ia\theta})$. A [Bezout identity](bezout-s-identity.md) completes $(a,b)$ to an invertible integer coordinate change on the two-dimensional torus, proving $C\cong U(1)\times\mathbb Z_d$. Thus the full [centralizer](centralizer.md) is a [circle group](circle-group.md) exactly when $m,n$ are coprime. For $m=n=2$, $(zw)^2=1$ gives two circles, a useful counterexample to the unrestricted connectedness claim.
