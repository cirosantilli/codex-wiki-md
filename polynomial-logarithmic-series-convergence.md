# Polynomial-logarithmic series convergence

↑ **Parent:** [Series (mathematics)](series-mathematics.md)

For $p,q>0$, the positive [series](series-mathematics.md) converges precisely when $p>1$, or $p=1$ and $q>1$. For $p>1$ use comparison with a convergent [p-series](p-series.md); for $0<p<1$, the bound $(\log n)^q<n^{(1-p)/2}$ gives a lower comparison with a divergent [p-series](p-series.md). At $p=1$, the [integral test for convergence](integral-test-for-convergence.md) and $u=\log x$ reduce the question to $\int u^{-q}\,du$. In contrast, $\sum_{n\geq3}[n(\log\log n)^r]^{-1}$ diverges for every finite $r>0$, since $(\log\log n)^r<\log n$ eventually and $\sum[n\log n]^{-1}$ diverges.
