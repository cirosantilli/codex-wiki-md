# Parsec

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsec)

The parsec is a distance unit defined by the exact convention

$$
1\,\mathrm{pc}=\frac{648000}{\pi}\,\mathrm{au}\simeq3.085677581\times10^{16}\,\mathrm m.
$$

The [astronomical unit](astronomical-unit.md) is exactly $149597870700\,\mathrm m$; one [arcsecond](arcsecond.md) is exactly $\pi/648000$ [radians](radian.md). The IAU resolution explicitly records the [parsec](parsec.md) convention. A megaparsec is one million [parsecs](parsec.md). [IAU 2015 Resolution B2, note 4: Exact parsec convention](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06262). [ESA: Astronomical-unit length](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2013/06/Measuring_stellar_distances_by_parallax).

The familiar description "the distance giving one [arcsecond](arcsecond.md) of [stellar parallax](stellar-parallax.md)" uses the [small-angle approximation](small-angle-approximation.md). Exact [right triangle](right-triangle.md) geometry instead gives $\mathrm{au}/\tan(\pi/648000)$, slightly shorter than the conventional [parsec](parsec.md). The relative difference is approximately $(\pi/648000)^2/3\simeq7.83\times10^{-12}$. This is negligible for the examples here, but distinguishing an exact unit convention from an approximate geometric interpretation prevents a misleading equality.
