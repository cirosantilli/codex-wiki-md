# Phosphor-converted white light-emitting diode

↑ **Parent:** [White light-emitting diode](white-light-emitting-diode.md)

To isolate conversion losses, suppose a converter receives 100 blue [photons](photon.md) at $\lambda_b=450\,\mathrm{nm}$, absorbs a fraction $a=0.60$, and has [fluorescence quantum yield](fluorescence-quantum-yield.md) $\Phi=0.90$: 0.90 converted [photons](photon.md) per absorbed blue [photon](photon.md). Stipulate that every converted [photon](photon.md) has $\lambda_c=560\,\mathrm{nm}$, that unabsorbed blue is transmitted, and that both outputs escape without further losses. The output is then 40 blue and 54 converted [photons](photon.md). The total photon yield is $0.94$, while the retained optical [energy](energy-split.md) fraction is

$$
\frac{P_{\rm after}}{P_{\rm before}}
=(1-a)+a\Phi\frac{\lambda_b}{\lambda_c}
=0.40+0.60\times0.90\times\frac{450}{560}
=0.833929.
$$

The lower [energy](energy-split.md) of each converted [photon](photon.md) matters even if $\Phi=1$. Combining this stipulated converter with the preceding chosen [light-emitting diode](light-emitting-diode.md) gives $P_{\rm after}=16.5430\,\mathrm{mW}$ and an overall optical/electrical [power](power.md) ratio $0.258485$. These chosen narrow lines and photon counts illustrate conversion accounting; they do not establish a white appearance, color quality or [luminous efficacy](luminous-efficacy.md). Those require the full [spectrum](spectrum-functional-analysis.md) and a model of human visual sensitivity.
