# Four-terminal sensing

↑ **Parent:** [Resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity](resistance-versus-temperature-experiment-for-superconductivity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-terminal_sensing)

[Four-terminal sensing](four-terminal-sensing.md) uses one pair of contacts to carry [electric current](electric-current.md) and a separate pair to sense [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). The sensing instrument draws negligible [electric current](electric-current.md), so the voltage drops along its own leads are negligible, and the current-contact drops outside the sensing interval are excluded. A measurement using just two shared leads includes their [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) and contact drops, which can overwhelm the small sample signal in a [resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity](resistance-versus-temperature-experiment-for-superconductivity.md). Four contacts do not automatically remove offsets from the [thermoelectric effect](thermoelectric-effect-split.md). [Tektronix, Low-Voltage Measurement Techniques, four-lead arrangement and thermoelectric offsets](https://www.tek.com/en/documents/technical-article/low-voltage-measurement-techniques)

For a stable offset $V_0$, let $I>0$ denote the magnitude of the reversed [electric current](electric-current.md). The measured [electric potential differences](electric-potential-difference.md) are $V(+I)=IR+V_0$ and $V(-I)=-IR+V_0$, so subtraction gives the displayed [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md). For example, with stipulated $I=1\,\mathrm{mA}$, $R=0.2\,\mathrm{m\Omega}$ and $V_0=3\,\mathrm{\mu V}$, the two readings are $3.2$ and $2.8\,\mathrm{\mu V}$; their difference correctly recovers $R$. If the offset changes between readings, its change leaves a residual [measurement error](measurement-error.md); the sample and measuring circuit must remain sufficiently stable during reversal.
