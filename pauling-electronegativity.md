# Pauling electronegativity

↑ **Parent:** [Electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md)

[Pauling electronegativity](pauling-electronegativity.md) is a relative, [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md) scale motivated by [bond-dissociation energies](bond-dissociation-energy.md). The interesting observation is that a [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md) between unlike [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) can be stronger than a simple baseline inferred from the corresponding like-atom [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md). An empirical excess [energy](energy-split.md) can then be associated with an [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) difference. The 1932 paper [The Nature of the Chemical Bond. IV. The Energy of Single Bonds and the Relative Electronegativity of Atoms](the-nature-of-the-chemical-bond-iv-the-energy-of-single-bonds-and-the-relative-electronegativity-of-atoms-by-linus-pauling.md) built such a map; its original coordinates used [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) as zero, so those coordinates should not be mistaken for the modern tabulated [Pauling electronegativities](pauling-electronegativity.md).[pp. 3576–3578, equation (1), Table III: H 0.00, Cl 0.94](the-nature-of-the-chemical-bond-iv-the-energy-of-single-bonds-and-the-relative-electronegativity-of-atoms-by-linus-pauling.md)

One arithmetic-baseline version of the [Pauling scale](pauling-electronegativity.md) relation is

$$
\Delta_D=D_{AB}-\frac{D_{AA}+D_{BB}}{2},\qquad
\left|\chi_{\rm P}(A)-\chi_{\rm P}(B)\right|\approx\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_D}{1\,\mathrm{eV}}}.
$$

Here $D_{AB}$, $D_{AA}$ and $D_{BB}$ are consistently defined [bond-dissociation energies](bond-dissociation-energy.md) per [molecule](molecule-split.md), and the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) is the chosen baseline. With molar [energies](energy-split.md) instead, write $\sqrt{\Delta_D/(96.485\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})}$, approximately $0.102\sqrt{\Delta_D/(1\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})}$. The denominator makes the result [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md); the factor $0.102$ is not a unit-independent magic constant. This is an empirical relation with a specified baseline, not a definition valid for every [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md).[§1 equation (1), coefficients for eV, kcal/mol and kJ/mol; §2.2, dependence on other bond properties](energy-electronegativity-and-chemical-bonding-by-stepan-s-batsanov.md)

For an explicitly synthetic example, suppose $D_{AA}=D_{BB}=300\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and $D_{AB}=396.485\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ with the same temperature and dissociation convention. Then $\Delta_D=96.485\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and the model gives an [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) difference of $1$. Replacing $D_{AB}$ by $300\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ gives zero; a negative excess would make this real square-root model inapplicable, not produce a negative [electronegativity](electronegativity-split.md) difference. These invented inputs illustrate the formula and are not measurements of named [chemical substances](chemical-substance-split.md).

Only differences appear, so adding the same constant to every [Pauling electronegativity](pauling-electronegativity.md) leaves this relation unchanged. A reference value must fix the origin. The [bond-dissociation energies](bond-dissociation-energy.md) alone also do not tell which endpoint attracts [electron density](electron-density.md) more strongly, because interchanging $A$ and $B$ leaves the formula unchanged. Chemical comparisons fix that direction. Thus [Pauling electronegativity](pauling-electronegativity.md) is useful as a compact ordering, while the underlying measured [energies](energy-split.md) and approximations remain essential.
