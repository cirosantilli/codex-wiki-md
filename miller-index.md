# Miller index

↑ **Parent:** [Lattice plane](lattice-plane.md)

Three [Miller indices](miller-index.md) $(hkl)$ specify a [lattice plane](lattice-plane.md) orientation in chosen [unit cell](unit-cell.md) axes. Choose a parallel plane away from the origin, express its intercepts in axis units, take reciprocals, and clear fractions. An infinite intercept gives index zero; a negative intercept gives a negative index, written with an overbar.[Online Dictionary of Crystallography by International Union of Crystallography; Definition, Direct space: reciprocal intercepts; Definition, Reciprocal space: normal to the plane family; primitive versus centered-cell qualifications](online-dictionary-of-crystallography-by-international-union-of-crystallography/miller-indices.md)

For example, intercepts $(2a,3b,\infty)$ have reciprocal ratios $(1/2,1/3,0)$, so the orientation is $(320)$ after multiplying by six. Relative to the same axes, intercepts $(a,-b,\infty)$ give $(1\bar10)$. Choosing a different parallel member changes the common intercept scale, not the orientation.

Primitive axes require relatively prime [Miller indices](miller-index.md) for complete families. Centered conventional [unit cells](unit-cell.md) need not, as the [FCC lattice](face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) $(200)$ example shows. Reflection labels can also retain higher [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md). Use the [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) for the [normal vector](normal-vector.md) with oblique axes; the direct vector $ha_1+ka_2+la_3$ need not be perpendicular.
