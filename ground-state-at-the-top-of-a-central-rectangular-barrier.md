# Ground state at the top of a central rectangular barrier

↑ **Parent:** [Infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md)

Consider hard walls at $x=0,3L$, zero [potential energy](potential-energy.md) on $0<x<L$ and $2L<x<3L$, and a central rectangular barrier whose height equals the [ground state](ground-state.md) energy $E$. The [time-independent Schrödinger equation](time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md) gives $\psi''=0$ on the central interval. Reflection symmetry and the [nodeless theorem for a one-dimensional ground state](nodeless-theorem-for-a-one-dimensional-ground-state.md) require an even, positive state about $3L/2$, hence a constant central segment. [Wavefunction matching at a finite potential step](wavefunction-matching-at-a-finite-potential-step.md) makes the derivatives vanish at $L,2L$. The side segments are proportional to $\sin(\pi x/(2L))$ and $\sin(\pi(3L-x)/(2L))$. Their common plateau value is $1/\sqrt{2L}$ after [wavefunction normalization](wavefunction-normalization.md), and $E=\pi^2\hbar^2/(8mL^2)$. Equality of barrier height and energy is essential: a higher barrier gives positive curvature of a positive wavefunction in the middle, while a lower barrier gives negative curvature.
