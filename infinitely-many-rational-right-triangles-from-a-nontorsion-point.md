# Infinitely many rational right triangles from a nontorsion point

↑ **Parent:** [Congruent number](congruent-number.md)

For $D>0$, a nontorsion [rational point](rational-point.md) on the [congruent number elliptic curve](congruent-number-elliptic-curve.md) $y^2=x^3-D^2x$ gives infinitely many positive rational [right triangles](right-triangle.md) of area $D$. Apply the formulas $a=|(x^2-D^2)/y|$, $b=|2Dx/y|$, $c=|(x^2+D^2)/y|$ to its multiples. The map is finite-to-one: if $r=c/b$ is fixed, then $|x|$ satisfies $X^2-2DrX+D^2=0$, leaving only finitely many coordinate signs. Thus infinitely many points give infinitely many distinct triangles.
