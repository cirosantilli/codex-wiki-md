<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a regular thermal $f_0$ and noise at $k\lambda_{\mathrm{De}}\gg1$, the susceptibility $\epsilon-1$ is small at thermal [phase velocities](../../../../phase-velocity.md): its scale is $\omega_{pe}^2/(k^2v_{\mathrm{the}}^2)$. To leading order the electrons cannot appreciably screen such short-scale fields, and [b](../b.md) becomes independent of [velocity](../../../../velocity.md):

$$
D(v)\simeq D_0=\frac{e^2}{m_e^2}\sum_k k^2A_k.
$$

The electron [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) per unit volume is $\mathcal K=(m_e/2)\int v^2f_0\,dv$. Assume $v^2D f_0'$ and $vf_0$ vanish at infinity. One [integration by parts](../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives the general heating formula

$$
\frac{d\mathcal K}{dt}=-m_e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}vD(v)f_0'(v)\,dv.
$$

For constant $D_0$, a second [integration by parts](../../../../integration-by-parts.md) and $\int f_0\,dv=n_e$ give

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\mathcal K}{dt}=m_en_eD_0=\frac{e^2n_e}{m_e}\sum_k k^2A_k.}
$$

This is the leading short-[wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) result. With vanishing boundary flux, the [diffusion equation](../../../../diffusion-equation-split.md) also conserves particle number; constant $D_0$ conserves the mean [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) while increasing the random [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md).
