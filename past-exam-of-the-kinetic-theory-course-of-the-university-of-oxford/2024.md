<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Put $z_i=(\mathbf x_i,\mathbf v_i)$ and $dz_i=d^3x_i\,d^3v_i$. Normalize the symmetric $N$-particle [phase-space distribution function](../statistical-physics.md#phase-space-distribution-function) $D_N$ to one. Use factorial normalization for its one-particle marginal and [two-particle distribution function](../statistical-physics.md#two-particle-distribution-function):

$$
f(z_1)=N\int D_N\,dz_2\cdots dz_N,\qquad f_2(z_1,z_2)=N(N-1)\int D_N\,dz_3\cdots dz_N.
$$

Thus $\int f\,dz=N$ and $\int f_2(z_1,z_2)\,dz_2=(N-1)f(z_1)$. For unit particle [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass), $f$ also carries [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density). These conventions explain why no extra factor $N-1$ occurs in the result.

Separate the [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian) as $H=H_1+H_{\mathrm{rest}}+H_{\mathrm{int}}$, where

$$
H_1=\frac12|\mathbf v_1|^2,\qquad H_{\mathrm{int}}=\sum_{j=2}^N\phi(|\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_j|),
$$

and $H_{\mathrm{rest}}$ contains all terms involving only particles $2,\ldots,N$. The stated [Poisson bracket](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket) gives [Hamilton's equations](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamilton-s-equations)

$$
\dot{\mathbf x}_i=\mathbf v_i,\qquad \dot{\mathbf v}_i=-\sum_{j\ne i}\nabla_{\mathbf x_i}\phi(|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j|).
$$

Conservation of probability along the [Hamiltonian flow](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian-flow) gives the [Hamiltonian Liouville equation](../kinetic-theory.md#hamiltonian-liouville-equation)

$$
\partial_tD_N+\sum_{i=1}^N\left[\mathbf v_i\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x_i}D_N-\sum_{j\ne i}\nabla_{\mathbf x_i}\phi_{ij}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v_i}D_N\right]=0.
$$

Assume decay of the required [velocity moments](../kinetic-theory.md#velocity-moment) and either decay or periodic [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) in position. Integrating over $z_2,\ldots,z_N$ eliminates the $H_{\mathrm{rest}}$ terms by [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts). The terms in $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ differentiating an integrated [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) also vanish. Indistinguishability makes the remaining $N-1$ terms equal, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tf+\mathbf v_1\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x_1}f
&=N\sum_{j=2}^N\int\nabla_{\mathbf x_1}\phi_{1j}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v_1}D_N\,dz_2\cdots dz_N\\
&=\int dz_2\,\nabla_{\mathbf x_1}\phi_{12}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v_1}f_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Relabeling the two particles gives the first [BBGKY hierarchy](../kinetic-theory.md#bbgky-hierarchy) equation:

$$
\boxed{\partial_tf+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x}f=\int d^3x_\star\,d^3v_\star\,\nabla_{\mathbf v}f_2\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x}\phi(|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_\star|).}
$$

This is exact for a differentiable [pair potential](../statistical-physics.md#pair-potential); no independent-particle closure has been made.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Take the zeroth [velocity moment](../kinetic-theory.md#velocity-moment) of [a](#1/a). The right side is a [velocity space](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#velocity-space) divergence with vanishing boundary contribution, giving the [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation)

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf u)=0,\qquad \rho=\int f\,d^3v,\qquad \rho\mathbf u=\int\mathbf v f\,d^3v.}
$$

For the first [velocity moment](../kinetic-theory.md#velocity-moment), use [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) componentwise:

$$
\int d^3v\,v_i\partial_{v_j}f_2=-\delta_{ij}\int d^3v\,f_2.
$$

Write $\mathbf c=\mathbf v-\mathbf u$; then $\int\mathbf c f\,d^3v=0$, and the [momentum flux](../mathematical-physics.md#momentum-flux) splits into bulk transport and the [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor):

$$
\int v_iv_jf\,d^3v=\rho u_iu_j+P_{ij},\qquad \boxed{P_{ij}=\int (v_i-u_i)(v_j-u_j)f\,d^3v.}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\partial_t(\rho u_i)+\partial_{x_j}(\rho u_iu_j+P_{ij})=-\int d^3x_\star\,n_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf x_\star,t)\partial_{x_i}\phi(|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_\star|),}
$$

where repeated Cartesian indices are summed and $n_2=\int f_2\,d^3v\,d^3v_\star$. The negative sign is the attractive or repulsive [force](../force.md) $-\nabla\phi$, rather than an additional sign convention for the [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For fixed $\mathbf R$ and $t$, define $h(\mathbf x,s)=n_2(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,t)$ with $\mathbf a=\mathbf x+(s-1)\mathbf R$ and $\mathbf b=\mathbf x+s\mathbf R$. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives

$$
\partial_sh=\mathbf R\cdot(\nabla_{\mathbf a}+\nabla_{\mathbf b})n_2,\qquad \nabla_{\mathbf x}h=(\nabla_{\mathbf a}+\nabla_{\mathbf b})n_2,
$$

so $\boxed{\partial_sh=\mathbf R\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x}h}$. The derivative with respect to $\mathbf x$ here translates _both_ arguments of the [two-particle number density](../statistical-physics.md#two-particle-number-density); it is not a derivative of its first argument alone.

Take the spatial divergence of the proposed [interaction pressure tensor](../pressure.md#interaction-pressure-tensor). Its [pair potential](../statistical-physics.md#pair-potential) factor is independent of $\mathbf x$, so the preceding identity gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{x_j}P^\phi_{ij}
&=-\frac12\int d^3R\,\frac{R_i\phi'(R)}R\int_0^1\partial_sh\,ds\\
&=-\frac12\int d^3R\,\frac{R_i\phi'(R)}R\left[n_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf x+\mathbf R)-n_2(\mathbf x-\mathbf R,\mathbf x)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Exchange symmetry of the [two-particle distribution function](../statistical-physics.md#two-particle-distribution-function) implies $n_2(\mathbf x-\mathbf R,\mathbf x)=n_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf x-\mathbf R)$. In the second term, substitute $\mathbf R\mapsto-\mathbf R$. Since $R_i\phi'(R)/R$ is odd, both terms become equal:

$$
\partial_{x_j}P^\phi_{ij}=-\int d^3R\,\frac{R_i\phi'(R)}R\,n_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf x+\mathbf R).
$$

On the other hand, at fixed $\mathbf x_\star=\mathbf x+\mathbf R$, the first-argument derivative of the [pair potential](../statistical-physics.md#pair-potential) is $\nabla_{\mathbf x}\phi=-\mathbf R\phi'(R)/R$. Thus the interaction [force density](../force.md#force-density) in [b](#1/b) is $-\partial_{x_j}P^\phi_{ij}$. Moving it to the left yields local [conservation of momentum](../momentum.md#momentum-conservation):

$$
\boxed{\partial_t(\rho\mathbf u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf u\mathbf u+\mathbf P+\mathbf P^\phi)=0.}
$$

The integral over $s$ distributes each pair's [momentum flux](../mathematical-physics.md#momentum-flux) along the straight segment joining the particles. The factor $1/2$ removes the double counting of ordered pairs. Central [forces](../force.md) also make $P^\phi_{ij}=P^\phi_{ji}$.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

[Spatial homogeneity](../cosmology.md#spatial-homogeneity) makes $\rho$ independent of position and makes the [two-particle number density](../statistical-physics.md#two-particle-number-density) depend only on the separation. [Isotropy](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) removes its directional dependence. Consequently

$$
n_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf x_\star,t)=\rho^2g_2(|\mathbf x_\star-\mathbf x|,t).
$$

Here $g_2$ is a [radial distribution function](../thermodynamics.md#radial-distribution-function), with the finite-$N$ normalization inherited from [a](#1/a); statistical independence is unnecessary. In particular, the translated pair in [c](#1/c) has separation $\mathbf R$ for every $s$, so its $s$ integral is simply $\rho^2g_2(R,t)$.

[Isotropy](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) of the random [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $\mathbf c=\mathbf v-\mathbf u$ makes its second [velocity moment](../kinetic-theory.md#velocity-moment) proportional to the identity. Taking its trace fixes the proportionality constant:

$$
\boxed{\theta=\frac1{3\rho}\int|\mathbf v-\mathbf u|^2f\,d^3v,\qquad P_{ij}=\rho\theta\delta_{ij}.}
$$

Thus $\theta$ is the one-component random [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) variance; for unit [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) in [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md), $\theta=k_BT$. A [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](../quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md#maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution) is not required for the [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor) to be isotropic.

For the interaction contribution, the angular integral is $\int d\Omega\,\widehat R_i\widehat R_j=(4\pi/3)\delta_{ij}$, by [isotropy](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) and its trace. With $d^3R=R^2\,dR\,d\Omega$, [c](#1/c) becomes

$$
P^\phi_{ij}=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\rho^2\delta_{ij}\int_0^\infty R^3\phi'(R)g_2(R,t)\,dR.
$$

Combining the two [pressure tensors](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor) gives the [virial pressure from pair correlations](../thermodynamics.md#virial-pressure-from-pair-correlations):

$$
\boxed{\mathbf P+\mathbf P^\phi=p\mathbf I,\qquad p=\rho\theta-\frac{2\pi}{3}\rho^2\int_0^\infty R^3\phi'(R)g_2(R,t)\,dR.}
$$

For a repulsive [pair potential](../statistical-physics.md#pair-potential), the outward radial [force](../force.md) is $-\phi'(R)\geq0$. Since $g_2\geq0$, the second term in $p$ is **nonnegative**, and positive when occupied separations feel nonzero repulsion. It expresses additional [momentum transfer](../quantum-scattering.md#momentum-transfer) through interactions. The displayed integral assumes a differentiable [pair potential](../statistical-physics.md#pair-potential) and convergence; a hard core requires a contact formula or a limiting argument.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use the electron [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) $-e$, so $E=-\partial_x\varphi$ gives acceleration $+(e/m_e)\partial_x\varphi$. The [Vlasov equation](../kinetic-theory.md#vlasov-equation), its average, and its linearized fluctuating part are

$$
\begin{gathered}
\partial_tf+v\partial_xf+\frac e{m_e}\partial_x\varphi\,\partial_vf=0,\\
\partial_tf_0=-\frac e{m_e}\partial_v\langle\partial_x\varphi\,\delta f\rangle,\qquad
(\partial_t+ikv)\delta f_k=-\frac{ike}{m_e}\varphi_k f_0'.
\end{gathered}
$$

The [quasilinear approximation](../kinetic-theory.md#quasilinear-approximation) retains the averaged quadratic term but drops products of fluctuations from the fluctuating equation. It freezes the slowly evolving $f_0$ during the fast response calculation. A real [electrostatic potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) has $\varphi_{-k}=\varphi_k^*$, and the sum includes both signs of every nonzero [wavenumber](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber). Spatial averaging selects the opposite [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) harmonics; statistical homogeneity supplies the corresponding diagonal noise covariance.

Let $\widehat g(p)=\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}g(t)\,dt$. Zero initial $\delta f_k$ gives

$$
\widehat{\delta f}_k(p,v)=-\frac{ike}{m_e}\frac{f_0'(v)}{p+ikv}\widehat\varphi_k(p).
$$

Insert this into the electrostatic self-consistency relation. Using $\omega_{pe}^2=4\pi e^2n_e/m_e$ and $p+ikv=ik(v-ip/k)$ gives

$$
\epsilon(p,k)\widehat\varphi_k=\widehat\chi_k,\qquad
\epsilon(p,k)=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{k^2n_e}\int_{C_L}\frac{f_0'(v')}{v'-ip/k}\,dv'.
$$

This derives the stated [dielectric function](../quantum-mechanics.md#dielectric-function) with the [Landau contour](../physics.md#landau-contour). A noise-free growing collective response is a zero of $\epsilon$.

The [Laplace transform](../integral-transform.md#laplace-transform) of the [white noise](../time-series.md#white-noise) covariance is particularly simple. Writing $q=p'^*$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\widehat\chi_k(p)\widehat\chi_k(p')^*\rangle
&=2A_k\int_0^\infty ds\int_0^\infty ds'\,e^{-ps-qs'}\delta(s-s')\\
&=\frac{2A_k}{p+q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For clarity about contour signs, introduce the inverse-transform measures

$$
\int_{\mathcal B}\frac{dp}{2\pi i},\qquad
\int_{\mathcal B^*}\frac{dq}{-2\pi i}.
$$

The first [Bromwich inversion formula](../integral-transform.md#bromwich-inversion-formula) contour runs upward on $\operatorname{Re}p=\sigma$; its conjugate contour runs downward on $\operatorname{Re}q=\sigma$. Both measures therefore have the same positive orientation in their real [frequency](../wave.md#frequency) parameters. Combining the averaged equation with $\widehat{\delta f}_k$ yields

$$
\partial_tf_0=\partial_v\left[D(v,t)\partial_vf_0\right],\qquad
D=-\frac e{m_e f_0'}\sum_k(-ik)\langle\delta f_k(t,v)\varphi_k(t)^*\rangle.
$$

At a zero of $f_0'$, the last expression means its continuous coefficient obtained before cancelling $f_0'$. Substitution of the two inverse [Laplace transforms](../integral-transform.md#laplace-transform) and the transformed covariance gives

$$
\boxed{D(v,t)=\frac{e^2}{m_e^2}\sum_k\frac{k^2A_k}{2\pi^2}\int_{\mathcal B}dp\int_{\mathcal B^*}dp'^*\,\frac{e^{(p+p'^*)t}}{(p+ikv)(p+p'^*)\epsilon(p,k)\epsilon(p',k)^*}.}
$$

This is the paper's expression, with the orientation of $dp'^*$ made explicit. Reality follows by pairing $k$ and $-k$. The double integral retains switch-on transients; replacing it by a time-independent [diffusion coefficient](../brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient) requires the additional long-time limit in [b](#2/b).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

A linearly stable $f_0$ has no zeros of the causal [dielectric function](../quantum-mechanics.md#dielectric-function) in $\operatorname{Re}p>0$. Hence the inverse [Laplace transform](../integral-transform.md#laplace-transform) contours can approach $\sigma=0^+$, retaining the causal boundary value at $p=-i\omega+0^+$. To obtain a stationary forced response, further assume that the excited collective perturbations actually damp. This is the decay condition in the question's long-time instruction.

A transparent evaluation of the double integral uses the stationary [power spectral density](../electrical-engineering.md#power-spectral-density). The [white noise](../time-series.md#white-noise) covariance $2A_k\delta(t-t')$ has temporal spectrum $2A_k$. Linear filtering by $1/\epsilon$ therefore gives

$$
S_{\varphi,k}(\omega)=\frac{2A_k}{|\epsilon(-i\omega+0^+,k)|^2},\qquad
\langle\varphi_k(t-\tau)\varphi_k(t)^*\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,S_{\varphi,k}(\omega)e^{i\omega\tau}.
$$

The causal solution for $\delta f_k$ in [a](#2/a) gives, after the initial transients have decayed,

$$
D(v)=\frac{e^2}{m_e^2}\sum_k k^2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\infty d\tau\,e^{-ikv\tau}\langle\varphi_k(t-\tau)\varphi_k(t)^*\rangle.
$$

The real part can be taken because the full $k$ sum pairs conjugates. Using the [distribution theory](../distribution-theory.md) identity $\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\infty e^{ia\tau}\,d\tau=\pi\delta(a)$, with a positive vanishing convergence factor, samples the temporal spectrum at $\omega=kv$:

$$
\boxed{D(v)=\frac{e^2}{m_e^2}\sum_k\frac{k^2A_k}{|\epsilon(-ikv+0^+,k)|^2}.}
$$

The factor $1/2$ from the one-sided time integral cancels the $2$ in the noise covariance. The result is nonnegative, as a stationary [velocity-space diffusion](../kinetic-theory.md#velocity-space-diffusion) coefficient should be.

**Qualification of stability.** Absence of growing modes alone does not guarantee this stationary limit. For example, the electron [waterbag distribution](../statistical-physics.md#waterbag-distribution) $f_0(v)=n_e\mathbf1_{|v|<V}/(2V)$ has

$$
\epsilon(-i\omega,k)=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2-k^2V^2},\qquad \omega^2=k^2V^2+\omega_{pe}^2
$$

as undamped collective roots. [White noise](../time-series.md#white-noise) drives their field variance to grow instead of settling. Thus the formula requires decay of excited modes, not merely absence of exponentially growing ones. For weak [Landau damping](../physics.md#landau-damping), the waiting time can be much longer than $\omega_{pe}^{-1}$, and must still be short compared with evolution of $f_0$ for the frozen-background [quasilinear approximation](../kinetic-theory.md#quasilinear-approximation) to apply.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For a regular thermal $f_0$ and noise at $k\lambda_{\mathrm{De}}\gg1$, the susceptibility $\epsilon-1$ is small at thermal [phase velocities](../dispersion-relation.md#phase-velocity): its scale is $\omega_{pe}^2/(k^2v_{\mathrm{the}}^2)$. To leading order the electrons cannot appreciably screen such short-scale fields, and [b](#2/b) becomes independent of [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity):

$$
D(v)\simeq D_0=\frac{e^2}{m_e^2}\sum_k k^2A_k.
$$

The electron [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy) per unit volume is $\mathcal K=(m_e/2)\int v^2f_0\,dv$. Assume $v^2D f_0'$ and $vf_0$ vanish at infinity. One [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives the general heating formula

$$
\frac{d\mathcal K}{dt}=-m_e\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}vD(v)f_0'(v)\,dv.
$$

For constant $D_0$, a second [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) and $\int f_0\,dv=n_e$ give

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\mathcal K}{dt}=m_en_eD_0=\frac{e^2n_e}{m_e}\sum_k k^2A_k.}
$$

This is the leading short-[wavelength](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) result. With vanishing boundary flux, the [diffusion equation](../diffusion-equation.md) also conserves particle number; constant $D_0$ conserves the mean [momentum](../momentum.md) while increasing the random [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Set $\kappa=|k|$ and first work with a representative positive $k=\kappa$. For $\kappa\lambda_{\mathrm{De}}\ll1$, the thermal core is strongly screened. However, the causal [dielectric function](../quantum-mechanics.md#dielectric-function) has weakly damped [Langmuir wave](../physics.md#langmuir-wave) roots $\omega_s+i\gamma_s$, with $s=\pm1$, $\omega_s\simeq s\omega_{pe}$ and $\gamma_s<0$. Their [phase velocities](../dispersion-relation.md#phase-velocity) $v_s=\omega_s/\kappa$ are in the tail. Both roots must be retained for each spatial [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) harmonic.

Write $\epsilon(-i\omega+0^+,\kappa)=\epsilon_R(\omega)+i\epsilon_I(\omega)$. The supplied approximation gives

$$
\epsilon_R\simeq1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2},\qquad \epsilon_I\simeq-\frac{\pi\omega_{pe}^2}{\kappa^2n_e}f_0'(\omega/\kappa),\qquad \epsilon_R'(\omega_s)\simeq\frac2{\omega_s}.
$$

Expanding $\epsilon(\omega_s+i\gamma_s)=0$ to first order gives the [Landau damping](../physics.md#landau-damping) rate

$$
\gamma_s=-\frac{\epsilon_I(\omega_s)}{\epsilon_R'(\omega_s)}\simeq\frac{\pi\omega_s\omega_{pe}^2}{2\kappa^2n_e}f_0'(v_s).
$$

In particular, stable damping means $\omega_sf_0'(v_s)<0$. Near either root,

$$
|\epsilon(-i\kappa v+0^+,\kappa)|^2\simeq\frac4{\omega_{pe}^2}\left[(\kappa v-\omega_s)^2+\gamma_s^2\right].
$$

The narrow [Lorentzian resonance approximation](../distribution-theory.md#lorentzian-resonance-approximation) then gives the leading resonant contribution

$$
\frac1{|\epsilon(-i\kappa v+0^+,\kappa)|^2}\simeq\frac{\pi\omega_{pe}^2}{4}\sum_{s=\pm1}\frac{\delta(\kappa v-\omega_s)}{|\gamma_s|}.
$$

This is a formula under the [integral](../integral.md) over [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity); it is not a pointwise approximation away from the [resonances](../dynamical-systems.md#resonance). Insert it into the heating formula in [c](#2/c). For one spatial harmonic, the contribution from one root is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{k,s}
&=-\frac{e^2}{m_e}k^2A_k\frac{\pi\omega_{pe}^2}{4\kappa}\frac{v_sf_0'(v_s)}{|\gamma_s|}\\
&=\frac{e^2n_e}{2m_e}k^2A_k,
\end{aligned}
$$

because

$$
\frac{v_sf_0'(v_s)}{|\gamma_s|}=-\frac{2\kappa n_e}{\pi\omega_{pe}^2}.
$$

Thus the tiny tail slope cancels the tiny [Landau damping](../physics.md#landau-damping) rate: weak absorption is compensated by a large noise-driven wave intensity. The two temporal roots supply two equal halves of the answer. Negative $k$ gives the same result by the conjugate response of a real [electric field](../electric-field.md), without altering the original convention that the spatial sum includes both signs. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\mathcal K}{dt}=\sum_k\sum_{s=\pm1}Q_{k,s}\simeq\frac{e^2n_e}{m_e}\sum_k k^2A_k.}
$$

As in [c](#2/c), equality means the leading result in the stated asymptotic limit. This calculation assumes nonzero damping, observation times $t\gg\max_s|\gamma_s|^{-1}$, and forcing weak enough that $f_0$ changes negligibly during that relaxation. Taking zero damping before the long-time limit invalidates the stationary calculation.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

The long-[wavelength](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) limit in [d](#2/d) is **[resonant heating](../physics.md#resonant-heating)**. Most electrons screen the slowly varying spatial field. The noise nevertheless excites collective [Langmuir waves](../physics.md#langmuir-wave), whose sharp temporal spectrum lies near $\omega=\pm\omega_{pe}$. Electrons with $v\simeq\omega/k$ remain in phase with a wave and exchange [energy](../energy.md) with it. A stable tail absorbs net wave [energy](../energy.md) through [Landau damping](../physics.md#landau-damping). The heating is initially deposited in narrow tail regions of [velocity space](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#velocity-space), even though the volume-integrated rate equals that in [c](#2/c).

The short-[wavelength](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) limit in [c](#2/c) is **[stochastic heating](../physics.md#stochastic-heating)** in the terminology intended here. With $\epsilon\simeq1$, the imposed [electric field](../electric-field.md) is essentially unscreened. Its temporally uncorrelated kicks cause a random walk of electron [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity), so $\langle(\Delta v)^2\rangle=2D_0\Delta t$. Electrons throughout the thermal population contribute to the resulting [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy) increase; no narrow collective wave peak selects a small tail population.

Both mechanisms are driven by [white noise](../time-series.md#white-noise), and the formal condition $\omega=kv$ enters the stationary [quasilinear diffusion](../kinetic-theory.md#quasilinear-diffusion) calculation in both cases. The physical distinction is the sharp collective [resonance](../dynamical-systems.md#resonance) in [d](#2/d) versus the broad direct random forcing in [c](#2/c), rather than the mere presence of randomness. In collisionless theory this heating is an increase of the averaged [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy); subsequent [collisions](../classical-mechanics.md#collision) can establish [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2024](2024.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For an integrable or approximately integrable background [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian), [action-angle variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-angle-variables) turn an otherwise complicated [stellar orbit](../celestial-mechanics.md#stellar-orbit) into $\dot{\mathbf J}=0$ and $\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}=\boldsymbol\Omega(\mathbf J)$. This has two useful consequences. First, a phase-mixed equilibrium can be represented directly as $f_0(\mathbf J)$: the [action variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-variable) label the unperturbed [orbits](../dynamical-systems.md#orbit-dynamical-system), while [angle variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#angle-variable) describe position along them. Second, the angles are periodic, so [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) reduce unperturbed transport to multiplication by $i\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega$ and make [orbital resonances](../celestial-mechanics.md#orbital-resonance) explicit. The [action variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-variable) also separate rapid orbital motion from slow secular changes. These advantages apply on regular orbital regions; a globally chaotic background need not admit global [action-angle variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-angle-variables).

The transformation is canonical, preserving the [phase space](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#phase-space) measure and the stated total [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M=\int f\,d^3\theta\,d^3J$. The collisionless [Vlasov equation](../kinetic-theory.md#vlasov-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\partial_tf+\{f,H\}_{\theta,J}=\partial_tf+\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mathbf J}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\boldsymbol\theta}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf J}=0.}
$$

Here $H$ is the single-star specific [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian); the normalization of $f$ by [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) changes neither the [Poisson bracket](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket) nor the transport equation.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Since $H_0$ and $f_0$ depend only on the [action variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-variable), $\{f_0,H_0\}=0$. Drop the quadratic term $\{\delta f,\delta\Phi^{\mathrm{tot}}\}$ from the [Vlasov equation](../kinetic-theory.md#vlasov-equation) in [a](#3/a). Defining the orbital [frequency](../wave.md#frequency) vector by $\boldsymbol\Omega(\mathbf J)=\partial H_0/\partial\mathbf J$, the linearized equation is

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\delta f+\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\partial_{\boldsymbol\theta}\delta f-\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial\mathbf J}\cdot\partial_{\boldsymbol\theta}\delta\Phi^{\mathrm{tot}}=0.}
$$

The sign of the driving term follows from the second term in the canonical [Poisson bracket](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#poisson-bracket), because the background has no angle dependence.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use the stated [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) normalization, so $g(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf J,t)=\sum_{\mathbf n}g_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf J,t)e^{i\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\theta}$. Then [b](#3/b) gives

$$
\partial_t\delta f_{\mathbf n}+i\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega\,\delta f_{\mathbf n}-i\mathbf n\cdot\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial\mathbf J}\,\delta\Phi_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{tot}}=0.
$$

For the [Laplace transform](../integral-transform.md#laplace-transform) with kernel $e^{i\omega t}$, [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) yields

$$
\int_0^\infty e^{i\omega t}\partial_t\delta f_{\mathbf n}\,dt=-\delta f_{\mathbf n}(0)-i\omega\widetilde{\delta f}_{\mathbf n}.
$$

The upper boundary vanishes for sufficiently large $\operatorname{Im}\omega>0$, and the initial term is zero by hypothesis. Thus

$$
i(\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega-\omega)\widetilde{\delta f}_{\mathbf n}=i\mathbf n\cdot\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial\mathbf J}\,\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{tot}},
$$

or

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\delta f}_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf J,\omega)=-\mathbf n\cdot\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial\mathbf J}\frac{\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf n}^{\mathrm{tot}}(\mathbf J,\omega)}{\omega-\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega(\mathbf J)},\qquad \boldsymbol\Omega=\frac{\partial H_0}{\partial\mathbf J}.}
$$

The denominator identifies the [orbital resonance](../celestial-mechanics.md#orbital-resonance) between the perturbation and unperturbed orbital [frequencies](../wave.md#frequency). The zero harmonic has no driving term and remains zero with these initial data.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

In the periodic cube, $H_0=|\mathbf v|^2/2=(2\pi/L)^2|\mathbf J|^2/2$, so $\boldsymbol\Omega=(2\pi/L)\mathbf v$. The supplied [action-angle variables](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#action-angle-variables) are canonical: $d^3\theta\,d^3J=d^3x\,d^3v$. For $\mathbf k=2\pi\mathbf n/L$,

$$
\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\Omega=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v,\qquad \mathbf n\cdot\partial_{\mathbf J}f_0=\mathbf k\cdot\partial_{\mathbf v}f_0.
$$

Consequently [c](#3/c) becomes

$$
\widetilde{\delta f}_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf v,\omega)=\frac{\mathbf k\cdot\partial_{\mathbf v}f_0}{\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v-\omega}\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}(\omega).
$$

For each nonzero [wavenumber](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber), the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity) gives

$$
-k^2\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}=4\pi G\int\widetilde{\delta f}_{\mathbf k}\,d^3v.
$$

Substitute the preceding response and add the externally imposed [Newtonian gravitational potential](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-gravitational-potential):

$$
\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}=\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}-\frac{4\pi G}{k^2}\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}\int d^3v\,\frac{\mathbf k\cdot\partial_{\mathbf v}f_0}{\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v-\omega}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}}{\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\omega)},\qquad \epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\omega)=1+\frac{4\pi G}{k^2}\int d^3v\,\frac{\mathbf k\cdot\partial_{\mathbf v}f_0}{\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v-\omega}.}
$$

This [gravitational dielectric function](../galaxy.md#gravitational-dielectric-function) has the opposite susceptibility sign to the electron [dielectric function](../quantum-mechanics.md#dielectric-function), reflecting attractive rather than repulsive collective interactions. The homogeneous mean field is explicitly subtracted in this fictitious periodic model: the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity) acts on density fluctuations, and the $\mathbf k=0$ mode is omitted. Otherwise a nonzero uniform positive [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) would be incompatible with a periodic potential. This is the convention often called the [Jeans swindle](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#jeans-swindle).

For [analytic continuation](../holomorphic-function.md#analytic-continuation), resolve [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) parallel to $\mathbf k$: let $u=\widehat{\mathbf k}\cdot\mathbf v$ and $F(u)=\int f_0(u,\mathbf v_\perp)\,d^2v_\perp$. Then

$$
\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\omega)=1+\frac{4\pi G}{k^2}\int\frac{F'(u)}{u-\omega/k}\,du,\qquad k=|\mathbf k|.
$$

Start at $\operatorname{Im}\omega>0$ and deform the [Landau contour](../physics.md#landau-contour) continuously below the moving pole. At real $\omega$, the integral is

$$
\operatorname{PV}\int\frac{F'(u)}{u-\omega/k}\,du+i\pi F'(\omega/k).
$$

For $\operatorname{Im}\omega<0$, this causal continuation equals the undeformed real-axis integral plus $2\pi iF'(\omega/k)$ wherever $F'$ has the necessary analytic extension. It must not be replaced by the undeformed integral alone.

Zeros of $\epsilon_{\mathbf k}$ are self-consistent collective [Landau modes](../physics.md#landau-mode): with no external forcing, a nonzero [Newtonian gravitational potential](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-gravitational-potential) is possible at such a root. For time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$, upper-half-plane roots grow, lower-half-plane roots describe [Landau damping](../physics.md#landau-damping), and real roots are neutral. Damped [Landau modes](../physics.md#landau-mode) are poles of the continued response; they need not be regular discrete eigenfunctions of the original collisionless operator.

**Regularity qualification.** Continuation throughout the complex plane presupposes suitable analytic $F$. Arbitrary smooth $F$ need not allow it. For example, $F(u)=C\exp[-1/(1-u^2)]$ for $|u|<1$ and zero elsewhere is smooth but not analytic at $u=\pm1$; its Cauchy integral cannot be holomorphically continued across those endpoints. The [Landau contour](../physics.md#landau-contour) gives the standard causal continuation on the domains where the equilibrium permits it, with possible singularities retained.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

Represent the perturber by the periodic [Dirac delta function](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) at $\mathbf x_p+\mathbf v_pt$. With spatial [Fourier series](../fourier-series.md) coefficient normalized by $L^{-3}$, its nonzero density coefficients are

$$
\rho_{p,\mathbf k}(t)=\frac{m_p}{L^3}e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x_p}e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_pt},\qquad t\geq0.
$$

The mean density is subtracted as in [d](#3/d). The [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity) gives

$$
\delta\Phi_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)=C_{\mathbf k}e^{-iat},\qquad C_{\mathbf k}=-\frac{4\pi Gm_p}{L^3k^2}e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x_p},\qquad a=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_p.
$$

The perturbation is zero before switch-on. For $\operatorname{Im}\omega>0$, its [Laplace transform](../integral-transform.md#laplace-transform) is

$$
\int_0^\infty e^{i(\omega-a)t}\,dt=-\frac1{i(\omega-a)}=\frac1{i(a-\omega)}.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\delta\Phi}_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}(\omega)=-\frac{4\pi Gm_p}{L^3k^2}e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x_p}\frac1{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_p-\omega)}.}
$$

The switch-on creates a forcing pole at the real [frequency](../wave.md#frequency) $a$, approached causally from the original upper inversion contour.

<h3 id="3/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#3/f)

With $C_{\mathbf k}$ and $a$ from [e](#3/e), inversion gives

$$
\delta\Phi_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Im}\omega=\eta>0}d\omega\,e^{-i\omega t}\frac{iC_{\mathbf k}}{(\omega-a)\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\omega)}.
$$

For $t>0$, deform the contour downwards. Closing below is clockwise, so the [residue theorem](../analysis.md#residue-theorem) multiplies the sum of enclosed residues by $-i$. The forcing pole has residue $iC_{\mathbf k}e^{-iat}/\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(a+i0^+)$ and therefore contributes

$$
\frac{C_{\mathbf k}e^{-iat}}{\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(a+i0^+)}.
$$

A simple collective root $\omega_j$ of the continued [gravitational dielectric function](../galaxy.md#gravitational-dielectric-function) contributes

$$
\frac{C_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\omega_jt}}{(\omega_j-a)\epsilon_{\mathbf k}'(\omega_j)}.
$$

These are the [Landau mode](../physics.md#landau-mode) transients excited by introducing the mass abruptly. For a strictly damped background they decay, together with any remaining phase-mixing contour contribution under the usual regularity and decay assumptions. Thus

$$
\boxed{\delta\Phi_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{tot}}(t)\sim\frac{\delta\Phi_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}(t)}{\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_p+i0^+)},\qquad t\to\infty.}
$$

The causal boundary value is what the paper abbreviates by $\epsilon_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v_p)$. Physically, the moving mass polarizes the stellar population and carries a [gravitational wake](../planetary-science.md#gravitational-wake). Each spatial harmonic is changed in amplitude and phase by $1/\epsilon_{\mathbf k}$ at the forcing [frequency](../wave.md#frequency). [Self-gravity](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#self-gravity) can amplify the wake. The induced part alone is $(1/\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-1)\delta\Phi_{\mathbf k}^{\mathrm{ext}}$; the wake's back-reaction on the perturber produces [dynamical friction](../planetary-science.md#dynamical-friction).

The waiting time is controlled by the **slowest excited collective damping**, not just an orbital or crossing time. If $\omega_j=\omega_{rj}-i\Gamma_j$ with $\Gamma_j>0$, then a modal amplitude is suppressed by $e^{-\Gamma_jt}$, and one needs $t\Gamma_j\gg1$ for every appreciably excited mode, together with decay of the phase-mixing remainder. Large transient prefactors can require additional time. Near marginal stability, a relevant $\Gamma_j$ tends to zero and the required waiting time diverges; the stationary wake approximation is then not uniform in the stability parameter.[Sections 2.1–2.2: forcing-pole and Landau-mode contributions, and the nonuniform late-time limit](../physics-bibliography.md#the-linear-response-of-stellar-systems-does-not-diverge-at-marginal-stability-by-chris-hamilton-and-tobias-heinemann)

**Qualification of stability.** If stable means only non-growing, a persistent neutral mode invalidates the asserted asymptote. A concrete example is a homogeneous [waterbag distribution](../statistical-physics.md#waterbag-distribution) with parallel marginal $F(u)=\rho\mathbf1_{|u|<V}/(2V)$. Its [gravitational dielectric function](../galaxy.md#gravitational-dielectric-function) is

$$
\epsilon_k(\omega)=1-\frac{4\pi G\rho}{k^2V^2-\omega^2}.
$$

For $k^2V^2>4\pi G\rho$, the roots $\omega=\pm\sqrt{k^2V^2-4\pi G\rho}$ are real, so the switch-on transients persist although that harmonic has no growing mode. Taking the smallest box [wavenumber](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber) to satisfy the same inequality makes the whole waterbag model free of growing harmonics. The claimed late-time formula therefore requires damping of all excited modes and $\epsilon_k(a)\ne0$; exact forcing at an undamped root instead produces a secular response.
