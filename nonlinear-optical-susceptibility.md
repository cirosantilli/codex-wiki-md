# Nonlinear optical susceptibility

↑ **Parent:** [Nonlinear optics](nonlinear-optics.md)

Choose one driving [electric field](electric-field-split.md) component and one responding [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) component. A schematic local, instantaneous expansion is

$$
P=\epsilon_0\left(\chi^{(1)}E+\chi^{(2)}E^2+\chi^{(3)}E^3+\cdots\right).
$$

Here $\epsilon_0$ is the [vacuum permittivity](vacuum-permittivity.md), $\chi^{(1)}$ is the linear [electric susceptibility](electric-susceptibility.md), and the higher coefficients describe nonlinear response. In SI, $P$ has units $\mathrm{C\,m^{-2}}$, $E$ has units $\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$, and $\chi^{(2)}$ has units $\mathrm{m\,V^{-1}}$. The selected scalar coefficient depends on [polarization of waves](polarization-waves.md) and [crystal](crystal.md) orientation; it is not a universal material number independent of geometry.

For a chosen [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md) $E(t)=E_0\cos\omega t$, the quadratic contribution is exactly

$$
P^{(2)}(t)=\frac{\epsilon_0\chi^{(2)}E_0^2}{2}\left(1+\cos2\omega t\right).
$$

The [cosine](cosine.md) identity separates a constant contribution, associated with [optical rectification](optical-rectification.md), from an oscillation at twice the [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md), the source for [second-harmonic generation](second-harmonic-generation.md). A term oscillating at $2\omega$ is a local driving source; efficient outgoing [light](light.md) additionally requires propagation and [phase matching](phase-matching.md).

For a material with [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md), the scalar instantaneous expansion is only a teaching model. The actual quadratic response is a [tensor](tensor.md) with output and input [frequency](frequency.md) arguments: $\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}(2\omega;\omega,\omega)$ describes the harmonic, whereas $\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}(0;\omega,-\omega)$ describes the constant response. Their coefficients need not coincide. The [cosine](cosine.md) calculation establishes which [frequencies](frequency.md) are available, without assigning measured coefficients to any [crystal](crystal.md).
