# Converse implication

↑ **Parent:** [Logical implication](logical-implication.md)

The converse implication of $A\to B$ is $B\to A$. It exchanges hypothesis and conclusion without [logical negation](negation.md). It need not follow from the original [logical implication](logical-implication.md): $n=2$ is an [even number](even-number.md) but is not divisible by four. Thus “being an [even number](even-number.md) implies [divisibility](divisibility-split.md) by four” is false, although the original implication holds for all [natural numbers](natural-number.md). Proving both directions establishes an “if and only if” statement; proving a [contrapositive](contrapositive.md) establishes the original implication classically, without establishing its [converse implication](converse-implication.md).
