# Prime-power contraction under pth powers

↑ **Parent:** [Modular congruence](modular-congruence.md)

For a prime $p$ and $k\geq1$, write $a=b+p^kh$. The [binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md) shows $a^p-b^p$ is divisible by $p^{k+1}$: the linear term contains the extra factor $p$ and every higher term contains at least $p^{2k}$. Iteration gives the displayed [modular congruence](modular-congruence.md) for every $r\geq0$, including $p=2$. Applied to [Fermat's little theorem](fermat-little-theorem.md), it yields $a^{p^n}\equiv a^{p^{n-1}}\pmod{p^n}$.
