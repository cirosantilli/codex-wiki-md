# Strict polynomial error decrease from nonvanishing derivatives

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial approximation](polynomial-approximation.md)

If $n\ge1$, $f$ is real and $C^n$ on an interval, and $f^{(n)}$ is nowhere zero there, the displayed inequality holds. If the errors were equal, a best degree-at-most-$n-1$ polynomial would also be best at degree $n$. The [Chebyshev alternation theorem](equioscillation-theorem.md) and [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md) would give $n+1$ zeros of the error. Applying [Rolle's theorem](rolle-theorem.md) $n$ times would force a zero of $f^{(n)}$, a contradiction. The zero-error case already contradicts $f^{(n)}\ne0$. In particular this proves strict improvement for the [exponential function](exponential-function.md) at every positive degree step.
