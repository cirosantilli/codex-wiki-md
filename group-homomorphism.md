# Group homomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Group theory](group-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homomorphism)

A [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) is a [map](function-class.md) $\varphi:G\to H$ preserving the [group operation](group-operation.md): $\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)\varphi(h)$. It also preserves the [identity element](identity-element.md) and [inverse elements](inverse-element.md). In an additive [group](group-split.md), the same requirement reads $\varphi(g+h)=\varphi(g)+\varphi(h)$. A [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) preserves a way of combining inputs, even when several inputs have the same output.

Reduction modulo three from $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ is a [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md): reducing a [sum](sum.md) agrees with adding the reduced residues. For example, $2+5=1$ modulo six, and both sides reduce to one modulo three; the outputs $2$ and $2$ also add to one modulo three. This [map](function-class.md) is [surjective](surjective-function.md) but not [injective](injective-function.md), since zero and three both map to zero.

The [sign of a permutation](sign-of-a-permutation.md) gives another example, from the [symmetric group](symmetric-group.md) $S_3$ to the multiplicative [group](group-split.md) $\{1,-1\}$. A product of two odd [permutations](permutation.md) is even, matching $(-1)(-1)=1$. Its [group kernel](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is $A_3$, the [alternating group](alternating-group.md) of even [permutations](permutation.md). This shows how a [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) can retain a useful feature while forgetting other details.
