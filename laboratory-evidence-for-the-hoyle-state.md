# Laboratory evidence for the Hoyle state

↑ **Parent:** [Fowler nuclear astrophysics](fowler-nuclear-astrophysics.md)

In 1953, [David Noel Ferguson Dunbar](david-noel-ferguson-dunbar.md) and collaborators used accelerated [deuterons](deuteron.md) on [nitrogen-14](nitrogen-14.md), populating [carbon-12](carbon-12.md) through ${}^{14}\mathrm N(d,\alpha){}^{12}\mathrm C$. Magnetic analysis resolved outgoing [alpha particle](alpha-particle.md) groups. At $620\,\mathrm{keV}$ incident [energy](energy-split.md) and laboratory angle $90$ degrees, they reported excitation $7.68\pm0.03\,\mathrm{MeV}$, with yield $6\%$ of the $4.43\,\mathrm{MeV}$ group's yield. The stronger group supplied an internal comparison. The paper's opening explicitly relates the search to [Fred Hoyle](fred-hoyle.md)'s abundance argument. This is evidence for a nuclear level from a different entrance reaction than stellar fusion.[1953 paper, p. 649, abstract and opening paragraphs](the-7-68-mev-state-in-c12-by-david-noel-ferguson-dunbar-and-others.md)

The inference uses [energy](energy-split.md) and [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md): at fixed incident [energy](energy-split.md) and observation angle, more [energy](energy-split.md) retained as [carbon-12](carbon-12.md) excitation leaves less outgoing [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). A resolved particle group therefore identifies a nuclear level. This laboratory nuclear line supports an input required by a stellar synthesis model. It does not reproduce [core helium burning](core-helium-burning.md), measure a complete stellar [carbon](carbon.md) yield, or by itself determine the stabilizing decay probability.
