# Best response

↑ **Parent:** [Game theory](game-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_response)

A [best response](best-response.md) maximizes a player's [expected payoff](expected-payoff.md) while the other players' strategies are held fixed. In a [finite game](finite-game.md), write the full [set](set-split.md) of [mixed strategy](mixed-strategy.md) [best responses](best-response.md) as

$$
\operatorname{BR}_i(\sigma_{-i})=
\left\{\tau_i\in\Delta(S_i):U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})=
\max_{\rho_i\in\Delta(S_i)}U_i(\rho_i,\sigma_{-i})\right\}.
$$

This is generally a [set-valued mapping](set-valued-mapping.md), since several choices may tie. Every mixture supported on maximizing [pure strategies](pure-strategy.md) is a [best response](best-response.md), and mixing in a strictly worse choice reduces [expected payoff](expected-payoff.md).

For example, against $q=3/4$ in [matching pennies](matching-pennies.md), the row player's heads and tails [expected payoffs](expected-payoff.md) are $1/2$ and $-1/2$, so heads is the unique [best response](best-response.md). Against $q=1/2$, all mixtures are [best responses](best-response.md). In the [asymmetric coordination example](asymmetric-coordination-example.md), against $q=3/4$ the row player gets $9/4$ from $A$ and $1/2$ from $B$, so $A$ is optimal. A [Nash equilibrium](nash-equilibrium.md) requires everyone to be a [best response](best-response.md) simultaneously; one optimal reply alone does not establish it.
