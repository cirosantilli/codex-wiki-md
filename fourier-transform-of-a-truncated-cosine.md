# Fourier transform of a truncated cosine

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md)

For $f(x)=\cos x$ on $[-a,a]$ and zero outside, the angular-frequency [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) follows by writing the cosine as two [complex exponentials](complex-exponential-function.md). Combining fractions gives $[2k\cos a\sin(ak)-2\sin a\cos(ak)]/(k^2-1)$. The apparent singularities at $k=\pm1$ are removable, with value $a+\sin(2a)/2$. If $\cos a\ne0$, jumps at the endpoints give an oscillatory $O(|k|^{-1})$ envelope. If $\cos a=0$, the function is continuous at the endpoints but its first [derivative](derivative.md) jumps, giving an $O(|k|^{-2})$ envelope. This is an explicit instance of [Fourier decay from a derivative jump](fourier-decay-from-a-derivative-jump.md).
