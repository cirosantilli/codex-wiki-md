<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write

$$
q:(X\times I)\sqcup Y\longrightarrow M(f)
$$

for the [quotient map](../../../../../quotient-map.md) defining the [mapping cylinder](../../../../../mapping-cylinder.md), and put $j(x)=q(x,0)$. Since the [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) only attaches points at height $1$ to $Y$, distinct points at height $0$ remain distinct. Thus $j$ is an [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md) [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md).

Define the [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy-split.md)

$$
H(x,s)=q(x,1-s).
$$

It is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md), with $H(x,0)=q(x,1)=i(f(x))$ and $H(x,1)=j(x)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{i\circ f\simeq j,\qquad j\text{ injective}.}
$$

Even if $f$ identifies many points, the free end of the [mapping cylinder](../../../../../mapping-cylinder.md) retains a separate copy of every point of $X$.
