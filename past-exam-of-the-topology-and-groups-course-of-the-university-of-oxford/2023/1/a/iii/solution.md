<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $i:A\hookrightarrow X$ be the [inclusion map](../../../../../inclusion-map.md) and $r:X\to A$ the [retraction](../../../../../retraction.md). Since $X$ is [contractible](../../../../../contractible-space.md), choose a [contraction of a topological space](../../../../../contraction-of-a-topological-space.md)

$$
H:X\times I\longrightarrow X,\qquad H(x,0)=x,\quad H(x,1)=x_0.
$$

The [function composition](../../../../../function-composition.md)

$$
K:A\times I\longrightarrow A,\qquad K(a,t)=r(H(i(a),t))
$$

is a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md). Its endpoints satisfy

$$
K(a,0)=ri(a)=a,\qquad K(a,1)=r(x_0).
$$

Consequently $K$ is a [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy-split.md) from the [identity map](../../../../../identity-function.md) of $A$ to a [constant function](../../../../../constant-function.md). This proves

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is contractible}.}
$$

The contracting point of $X$ need not belong to $A$: applying the [retraction](../../../../../retraction.md) sends it to the contracting point $r(x_0)$ of $A$. This is the [retract of a contractible space](../../../../../retract-of-a-contractible-space.md) principle.
