<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume $p$ has a common finite [degree of a covering map](../../../../../degree-of-a-covering-map.md) $d$. Declare the lifted [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) to be $p^{-1}(V(X))$, so there are exactly $dv(X)$ of them. Each open [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is [homeomorphic](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) to $(0,1)$, a [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). The restriction of the [covering map](../../../../../covering-space.md) above it is a disjoint union of $d$ copies of that open [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md).

These copies acquire endpoints as follows. Lift the parametrizing [path](../../../../../continuous-path.md) $[0,1]\to X$ of each oriented [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), starting at each of the $d$ points over its initial [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). The [path lifting theorem](../../../../../path-lifting-theorem.md) gives a unique lift all the way to $1$. Its endpoint lies over the terminal [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md), and the interiors of these lifted [paths](../../../../../continuous-path.md) are exactly the $d$ open [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) already described. This also handles a [graph loop](../../../../../loop-graph-theory.md), whose two endpoints may coincide downstairs or upstairs. Local [covering sheets](../../../../../covering-sheet.md) above a small star at a [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) show that the lifted incidence relations have precisely the original [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md). Thus the [graph structure lifted through a covering map](../../../../../graph-structure-lifted-through-a-covering-map.md) is a finite [topological graph](../../../../../graph-topology.md) with

$$
v(\widetilde X)=dv(X),\qquad e(\widetilde X)=de(X).
$$

Subtracting gives the [Euler characteristic under a finite covering](../../../../../euler-characteristic-under-a-finite-covering.md) formula

$$
\boxed{\chi(\widetilde X)=d\,\chi(X).}
$$

The formula holds even when $\widetilde X$ is disconnected. If a disconnected base is allowed to have degrees $d_C$ that vary with its [connected components](../../../../../connected-component.md) $C$, the precise replacement is

$$
\chi(\widetilde X)=\sum_C d_C\chi(C).
$$

The printed formula with a single $d$ uses the constant-degree convention explained in part (a)(ii).
