<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

Two [continuous maps](../topology.md#continuous-map) $f_0,f_1:X\to Y$ are [homotopic](../homotopy.md) if there is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map)

$$
H:X\times I\longrightarrow Y,\qquad H(x,0)=f_0(x),\quad H(x,1)=f_1(x).
$$

Here the [Cartesian product](../set-theory.md#cartesian-product) has the [product topology](../geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology). Such an $H$ is a [homotopy](../homotopy.md), written $f_0\simeq f_1$.

The [topological spaces](../topology.md#topological-space) $X,Y$ are [homotopy equivalent](../homotopy.md#homotopy-inverse) if there are [continuous maps](../topology.md#continuous-map) $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to X$ such that

$$
\boxed{gf\simeq\operatorname{id}_X,\qquad fg\simeq\operatorname{id}_Y.}
$$

Each is then a [homotopy inverse](../homotopy.md#homotopy-inverse) of the other.

For $A\subseteq X$ with its [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology) with [inclusion map](../topology.md#inclusion-map) $i:A\hookrightarrow X$, a [homotopy retract](../homotopy.md#homotopy-retract), in the course's convention, is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $r:X\to A$ with

$$
\boxed{ri=\operatorname{id}_A,\qquad ir\simeq\operatorname{id}_X.}
$$

Thus $r$ is an actual [retraction](../topology.md#retraction), and $i,r$ give a [homotopy equivalence](../homotopy.md#homotopy-equivalence). Requiring the latter [homotopy](../homotopy.md) to fix $A$ pointwise at all times gives the stronger condition of a [deformation retraction](../homotopy.md#deformation-retraction) as used in this wiki. The course's definition of [homotopy retract](../homotopy.md#homotopy-retract) requires no such restriction on that [homotopy](../homotopy.md).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

**Yes.** Take $X=S^1$, the [circle](../topology.md#circle), and $A=\{1\}\subset S^1$. The [constant function](../function.md#constant-function) $r(z)=1$ is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) and satisfies $ri=\operatorname{id}_A$, so $A$ is a [retract](../homotopy.md#retract) of $X$.

However, their [fundamental groups](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) are

$$
\pi_1(S^1,1)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad \pi_1(\{1\},1)=1.
$$

A [homotopy equivalence](../homotopy.md#homotopy-equivalence) between [path-connected spaces](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space) induces a [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism) of their [fundamental groups](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group), allowing the usual change of [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point). The [infinite cyclic group](../group.md#infinite-cyclic-group) is not the [trivial group](../group.md#trivial-group). Therefore **a [retract](../homotopy.md#retract) need not have the [homotopy type](../homotopy.md#homotopy-type) of its ambient space**.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Let $i:A\hookrightarrow X$ be the [inclusion map](../topology.md#inclusion-map) and $r:X\to A$ the [retraction](../topology.md#retraction). Since $X$ is [contractible](../homotopy.md#contractible-space), choose a [contraction of a topological space](../homotopy.md#contraction-of-a-topological-space)

$$
H:X\times I\longrightarrow X,\qquad H(x,0)=x,\quad H(x,1)=x_0.
$$

The [function composition](../function.md#function-composition)

$$
K:A\times I\longrightarrow A,\qquad K(a,t)=r(H(i(a),t))
$$

is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map). Its endpoints satisfy

$$
K(a,0)=ri(a)=a,\qquad K(a,1)=r(x_0).
$$

Consequently $K$ is a [homotopy](../homotopy.md) from the [identity map](../function.md#identity-function) of $A$ to a [constant function](../function.md#constant-function). This proves

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is contractible}.}
$$

The contracting point of $X$ need not belong to $A$: applying the [retraction](../topology.md#retraction) sends it to the contracting point $r(x_0)$ of $A$. This is the [retract of a contractible space](../homotopy.md#retract-of-a-contractible-space) principle.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Write

$$
q:(X\times I)\sqcup Y\longrightarrow M(f)
$$

for the [quotient map](../topology.md#quotient-map) defining the [mapping cylinder](../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-cylinder), and put $j(x)=q(x,0)$. Since the [equivalence relation](../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation) only attaches points at height $1$ to $Y$, distinct points at height $0$ remain distinct. Thus $j$ is an [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map).

Define the [homotopy](../homotopy.md)

$$
H(x,s)=q(x,1-s).
$$

It is a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map), with $H(x,0)=q(x,1)=i(f(x))$ and $H(x,1)=j(x)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{i\circ f\simeq j,\qquad j\text{ injective}.}
$$

Even if $f$ identifies many points, the free end of the [mapping cylinder](../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-cylinder) retains a separate copy of every point of $X$.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Define

$$
r:M(f)\longrightarrow Y,\qquad r(q(x,t))=f(x),\quad r(i(y))=y.
$$

The two formulas agree on the attached end, so the [universal property of the quotient topology](../topology.md#universal-property-of-the-quotient-topology) makes $r$ a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map). Also $ri=\operatorname{id}_Y$.

Slide each cylinder point towards its attached end using

$$
K(q(x,t),s)=q(x,t+s(1-t)),\qquad K(i(y),s)=i(y).
$$

At $t=1$ these formulas both give $i(f(x))$, so they define a [homotopy](../homotopy.md) on the [mapping cylinder](../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-cylinder). For continuity, the [product of a quotient map with the unit interval](../topology.md#product-of-a-quotient-map-with-the-unit-interval) is a [quotient map](../topology.md#quotient-map); the formulas before taking the [quotient topology](../topology.md#quotient-topology) are [continuous maps](../topology.md#continuous-map) and agree on every identified pair. Thus

$$
K(-,0)=\operatorname{id}_{M(f)},\qquad K(-,1)=ir.
$$

Moreover $Y$ stays fixed throughout. We have obtained the stronger conclusion

$$
\boxed{M(f)\text{ deformation retracts onto }Y.}
$$

In particular $r$ is the requested [homotopy retract](../homotopy.md#homotopy-retract).

<h4 id="1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/b/iii)

Identify the bottom $j(X)=q(X\times\{0\})$ with $X$; its natural map $j$ is a [topological embedding](../topology.md#topological-embedding). To verify this directly, an [open subset](../topology.md#open-set) $O\subseteq X$ gives the saturated [open subset](../topology.md#open-set) $O\times[0,1/2)$ of the space before taking the [quotient topology](../topology.md#quotient-topology); its image is open in $M(f)$ and meets the bottom in $j(O)$.

Suppose first that there is a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction) $R:M(f)\to j(X)$. Define the [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map)

$$
g=j^{-1}Ri:Y\longrightarrow X.
$$

Then

$$
L(x,t)=j^{-1}R(q(x,t))
$$

is a [homotopy](../homotopy.md) with $L(x,0)=x$ and $L(x,1)=g(f(x))$. Hence $gf\simeq\operatorname{id}_X$.

Conversely, suppose a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $g:Y\to X$ has $gf\simeq\operatorname{id}_X$. Reverse the parameter if necessary to choose a [homotopy](../homotopy.md) $L$ with $L(x,0)=x$ and $L(x,1)=gf(x)$. Set

$$
R(q(x,t))=j(L(x,t)),\qquad R(i(y))=j(g(y)).
$$

At the glued end, $j(L(x,1))=j(gf(x))$, exactly the value prescribed at $i(f(x))$. The [universal property of the quotient topology](../topology.md#universal-property-of-the-quotient-topology) therefore gives a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $R:M(f)\to j(X)$. At the bottom, $R(j(x))=j(x)$, proving that $R$ is a [retraction](../topology.md#retraction). We have established the [mapping-cylinder bottom retraction criterion](../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-cylinder-bottom-retraction-criterion):

$$
\boxed{j(X)\text{ is a retract of }M(f)\iff\exists g:Y\to X\text{ continuous with }gf\simeq\operatorname{id}_X.}
$$

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

A [group presentation](../geometric-group-theory.md#group-presentation) of $G$ consists of a [set](../set.md) $S$ of symbols and a [subset](../set.md#subset) $R\subseteq F(S)$ of [relators](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator), together with a [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism)

$$
\boxed{G\cong\langle S\mid R\rangle:=F(S)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle.}
$$

Here $F(S)$ is the [free group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) on $S$, and $\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle$ is the [normal closure](../group-theory.md#normal-closure) of $R$. The images of $S$ form a [generating set of a group](../group.md#generating-set-of-a-group); the [relators](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator) prescribe which words become the [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element).

For the [canonical presentation of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#canonical-presentation-of-a-group), use one symbol $s_g$ for every [group element](../group.md#group-element) $g\in G$. The [universal property of a free group](../geometric-group-theory.md#universal-property-of-a-free-group) gives a [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism)

$$
\varepsilon:F(\{s_g:g\in G\})\longrightarrow G,\qquad s_g\longmapsto g.
$$

Let $R(G)=\ker\varepsilon$, namely all words whose evaluation in $G$ is the [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element). Then

$$
\boxed{G\cong\langle s_g\ (g\in G)\mid R(G)\rangle.}
$$

Indeed $R(G)$ is already a [normal subgroup](../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup), so its [normal closure](../group-theory.md#normal-closure) is itself, and the [first isomorphism theorem](../group-theory.md#first-isomorphism-theorem) identifies the resulting [quotient group](../group-theory.md#quotient-group) with $G$. This is the course's [canonical presentation of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#canonical-presentation-of-a-group).

An equivalent, smaller collection of [relators](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator) is $s_gs_hs_{gh}^{-1}$ for all $g,h\in G$. These encode the multiplication table. They imply $s_1=1$ and $s_g^{-1}=s_{g^{-1}}$, so every word reduces to the single symbol recording its product. This proves directly that they normally generate $R(G)$, although the canonical collection above consists of every word in that [group homomorphism kernel](../group-theory.md#kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism).

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Take the [free product](../algebraic-topology.md#free-product) $F=G_1*G_2$, with its natural [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $k_j:G_j\to F$, and form the [normal closure](../group-theory.md#normal-closure)

$$
N=\left\langle\!\left\langle
k_1(\phi_1(h))\,k_2(\phi_2(h))^{-1}:h\in G_0
\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.
$$

The [pushout of groups](../category.md#pushout-of-groups) is the [quotient group](../group-theory.md#quotient-group)

$$
\boxed{P=(G_1*G_2)/N,}
$$

equipped with the [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $j_1,j_2$ obtained by composing $k_1,k_2$ with the [quotient map of groups](../group-theory.md#quotient-map-of-groups). The defining [relators](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator) ensure

$$
j_1\phi_1=j_2\phi_2.
$$

Thus the two prescribed images of every [group element](../group.md#group-element) of $G_0$ agree in $P$. This construction works for arbitrary [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $\phi_1,\phi_2$; no [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function) is assumed, and $j_1,j_2$ need not be [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). Its [universal property](../category-theory.md#universal-property) is proved in the next part.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

The [universal property of a pushout of groups](../category.md#universal-property-of-a-pushout-of-groups) says: for every [group](../group.md) $H$ and [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $u_j:G_j\to H$ satisfying $u_1\phi_1=u_2\phi_2$, there is exactly one [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $u:P\to H$ such that

$$
\boxed{uj_1=u_1,\qquad uj_2=u_2.}
$$

To prove existence, the [universal property of a free product](../algebraic-topology.md#universal-property-of-a-free-product) supplies a unique [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $v:G_1*G_2\to H$ with $vk_j=u_j$. Every defining [relator](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator) of $N$ maps to

$$
u_1(\phi_1(h))\,u_2(\phi_2(h))^{-1}=1.
$$

Since $\ker v$ is a [normal subgroup](../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup), it contains the entire [normal closure](../group-theory.md#normal-closure) $N$. The [universal property of a quotient group](../group-theory.md#universal-property-of-a-quotient-group) therefore gives a [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $u:P\to H$ through which $v$ factors. Its restrictions are the required $u_j$.

For uniqueness, $j_1(G_1)$ and $j_2(G_2)$ together generate $P$: their preimages already generate the [free product](../algebraic-topology.md#free-product). Consequently a [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) out of $P$ is determined by its restrictions to these two [subgroups](../group.md#subgroup). Any two candidates for $u$ therefore coincide.

This also proves that the [pushout of groups](../category.md#pushout-of-groups) is unique up to a unique compatible [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism). Given another such object $P'$, apply the [universal property](../category-theory.md#universal-property) in both directions. Both composites restrict to the original maps from $G_1,G_2$, so uniqueness makes them the respective [identity maps](../function.md#identity-function).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Put $A=T^2\setminus B^2$ and let $E=\partial A=S^1$ denote the attaching [circle](../topology.md#circle). The version of the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem) used here states that if $X=U\cup V$ is an [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) with $U,V,U\cap V$ [path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space) and a common [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) in $U\cap V$, then

$$
\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(U)*_{\pi_1(U\cap V)}\pi_1(V).
$$

The expression on the right means the [pushout of groups](../category.md#pushout-of-groups) of the two inclusion-induced [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism); they need not be [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function).

Choose a thin [collar neighbourhood](../smooth-manifold.md#collar-neighbourhood) $C_A$ of $E$ inside $A$, and a thin equatorial band $C_S$ inside $S^2$. For example, before the square's side identifications take $C_A=\{v:1\leq|v|<1+\epsilon\}$ with $0<\epsilon<1/2$, and take $C_S=\{(x,y,z)\in S^2:|z|<\epsilon\}$. In the glued [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) set

$$
U=A\cup C_S,\qquad V=S^2\cup C_A.
$$

These are [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set): their preimages in the two pieces are open and respect the gluing. They cover $X$, and the three [deformation retractions](../homotopy.md#deformation-retraction)

$$
U\simeq A,\qquad V\simeq S^2,\qquad U\cap V=C_A\cup_E C_S\simeq E
$$

fix $E$. Thus all three spaces are [path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space), and a [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) $b\in E$ is preserved.

For completeness, the [fundamental group of a once-punctured torus](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group-of-a-once-punctured-torus) is $F(a,b)$, and its [boundary loop of a once-punctured torus](../algebraic-topology.md#boundary-loop-of-a-once-punctured-torus) represents $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, up to [orientation](../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) and a change of [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point). Here is the geometry behind both facts. In the square model, move each point of $A$ radially outwards to the square boundary:

$$
v\longmapsto\left((1-t)+\frac{2t}{\max(|v_1|,|v_2|)}\right)v,\qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
$$

The denominator never vanishes on $A$. The [homotopy](../homotopy.md) stays outside the missing [open disk](../topology.md#open-disk), stays inside the square, and fixes the square boundary, so it descends through the side identifications. The resulting [retract](../homotopy.md#retract) is a [wedge of two circles](../topology.md#wedge-of-two-circles). Its [fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) is the [free group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) on the two oriented side [based loops](../algebraic-topology.md#based-loop) $a,b$. The inner [boundary loop](../algebraic-topology.md#boundary-loop) travels around the square once under this [deformation retraction](../homotopy.md#deformation-retraction); its successive sides read $a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}$.

The [sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere) $S^2$ is [simply connected](../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space). Hence the [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) from $\pi_1(E)\cong\mathbb Z$ to $\pi_1(V)$ is trivial, while its map to $\pi_1(U)$ sends a [group generator](../group.md#generator-of-a-group) to $[a,b]$. Applying the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem) gives

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(X,b)\cong F(a,b)/\langle\!\langle[a,b]\rangle\!\rangle
=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2.}
$$

The final [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism) follows because the single [relator](../geometric-group-theory.md#relator) makes $a,b$ commute, leaving the [free abelian group](../group-theory.md#free-abelian-group) on two [group generators](../group.md#generator-of-a-group). The two hemispheres kill the same [boundary loop](../algebraic-topology.md#boundary-loop); they do not create another [group generator](../group.md#generator-of-a-group).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Let $n=(0,0,1)$ and use the [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover)

$$
U=Y\setminus\{n\},\qquad V=\{(x,y,z)\in S^2:z>1/2\}.
$$

The cap $V$ is open in $Y$: it equals $Y\cap\{z>1/2\}$ because the attached chord has $z=0$. The cap is [homeomorphic](../topology.md#homeomorphism) to an [open disk](../topology.md#open-disk), and $U\cap V=V\setminus\{n\}$ is a punctured [open disk](../topology.md#open-disk), hence [homotopy equivalent](../homotopy.md#homotopy-inverse) to a [circle](../topology.md#circle). Both sets and their [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) are [path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space). Choose the [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) $b$ in that [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection).

We first calculate $\pi_1(U,b)$. A [stereographic projection](../riemann-surfaces.md#stereographic-projection) identifies $S^2\setminus\{n\}$ with $\mathbb R^2$ and sends the two chord endpoints to $(-1,0),(1,0)$. Thus $U$ becomes the [Euclidean plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#euclidean-plane) with an extra [closed interval](../real-analysis.md#closed-real-interval) attached at those two points. This description is a [homeomorphism](../topology.md#homeomorphism): gluing the two closed pieces along their endpoints gives exactly the original [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology).

The [Euclidean plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#euclidean-plane) has a [deformation retraction](../homotopy.md#deformation-retraction) onto $L=[-1,1]\times\{0\}$ while fixing its endpoints. An explicit [homotopy](../homotopy.md) is

$$
D((u,v),t)=\big((1-t)u+t\,c(u),(1-t)v\big),\qquad
c(u)=\max(-1,\min(1,u)).
$$

Extend it by the [identity map](../function.md#identity-function) on the extra chord. The [pasting lemma for closed subspaces](../topology.md#pasting-lemma-for-closed-subspaces) gives a [deformation retraction](../homotopy.md#deformation-retraction) of $U$ onto two distinct [closed intervals](../real-analysis.md#closed-real-interval) sharing just their endpoints, a [circle](../topology.md#circle). Consequently $\pi_1(U,b)\cong\mathbb Z$, with the routine [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) transport if the [retraction](../topology.md#retraction) moves $b$.

The inclusion-induced [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $\pi_1(U\cap V,b)\to\pi_1(U,b)$ is trivial: it factors through $S^2\setminus\{n\}\cong\mathbb R^2$, which is [contractible](../homotopy.md#contractible-space). Also $\pi_1(V,b)=1$. All hypotheses of the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem) are now checked, and it gives the [fundamental group of a sphere with an attached chord](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group-of-a-sphere-with-an-attached-chord):

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(Y,b)\cong\mathbb Z*_{\mathbb Z}1\cong\mathbb Z,}
$$

where both maps from the amalgamating [infinite cyclic group](../group.md#infinite-cyclic-group) are trivial. A [group generator](../group.md#generator-of-a-group) follows the chord from one endpoint to the other and returns along any [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) on the [sphere](../euclidean-geometry.md#sphere), with a connecting [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) from the chosen [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point).

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

A [covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) is a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) [surjection](../algebra.md#surjective-function) $p:\widetilde X\to X$ such that every $x\in X$ has an [open](../topology.md#open-set) [neighbourhood](../topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) $U$ for which

$$
\boxed{p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in A}U_\alpha,\qquad
p|_{U_\alpha}:U_\alpha\longrightarrow U\text{ a homeomorphism},}
$$

where the $U_\alpha$ are pairwise disjoint [open subsets](../topology.md#open-set) of $\widetilde X$. Such a $U$ is an [evenly covered neighbourhood](../algebraic-topology.md#evenly-covered-neighbourhood), and the sets $U_\alpha$ are its [covering sheets](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-sheet). The index [set](../set.md) may be infinite. The requirement that every sheet map onto all of $U$ is part of the definition.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

For a [covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) over a nonempty [connected space](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), the [degree of a covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#degree-of-a-covering-map) is

$$
\boxed{\deg p=|p^{-1}(x)|,}
$$

the [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of any [fibre of a function](../function.md#fiber-of-a-function). To show independence of $x$, choose an [evenly covered neighbourhood](../algebraic-topology.md#evenly-covered-neighbourhood) $U$. Each [covering sheet](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-sheet) contains exactly one point over every $y\in U$, so $|p^{-1}(y)|$ equals the number of [covering sheets](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-sheet) over $U$. The [fibre of a function](../function.md#fiber-of-a-function) cardinality is therefore [locally constant](../calculus.md#locally-constant-function). For a fixed [cardinal number](../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa$, the points with that cardinality form an [open subset](../topology.md#open-set), and their complement is also open. [Connectedness](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) forces the value to be constant everywhere.

For a [path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space) base there is also an explicit [fibre bijection by path lifting](../algebraic-topology.md#fibre-bijection-by-path-lifting): lift a [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) from $x$ to $y$ starting at each point over $x$ and take its endpoint. The [path lifting theorem](../algebraic-topology.md#path-lifting-theorem) gives existence and uniqueness; [path reversal](../algebraic-topology.md#path-reversal) gives the inverse [bijection](../function.md#bijection). The bijection may depend on the [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path), but the resulting equality of [cardinalities](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) does not.

**A [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) base is needed for one global degree.** Without it, use a degree on each [connected component](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-component) on which the cardinality is constant. For example, a [covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) of a discrete two-point [topological space](../topology.md#topological-space) can have one point over the first point and two over the second. Thus the unqualified printed definition needs the usual connected-base convention, or the explicit assumption that every [fibre of a function](../function.md#fiber-of-a-function) has the same cardinality.

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

**With [local path connectedness](../geometry-and-topology.md#locally-path-connected-space), the intended conclusion holds.** Choose a [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) $x_0$ and the standard [covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space)

$$
p:\mathbb R\longrightarrow S^1,\qquad p(t)=e^{2\pi it}.
$$

The [fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) of the [circle](../topology.md#circle) is $\mathbb Z$. Since the image of a [finite group](../group.md#finite-group) under a [group homomorphism](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) is finite and $\mathbb Z$ has no nontrivial finite [subgroups](../group.md#subgroup),

$$
f_*:\pi_1(X,x_0)\longrightarrow\pi_1(S^1,f(x_0))\cong\mathbb Z
$$

is trivial. If $X$ is also [locally path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#locally-path-connected-space), the [lifting criterion for a covering space](../algebraic-topology.md#lifting-criterion-for-a-covering-space) supplies a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $\widetilde f:X\to\mathbb R$ with $p\widetilde f=f$, because $p_*\pi_1(\mathbb R)=0$. The [homotopy](../homotopy.md)

$$
\boxed{H(x,s)=p\big((1-s)\widetilde f(x)+s\widetilde f(x_0)\big)}
$$

runs from $f$ to the [constant function](../function.md#constant-function) $f(x_0)$. This proves the claimed [null-homotopy](../homotopy.md#null-homotopy) under that additional hypothesis, in particular for [CW complexes](../algebraic-topology.md#cw-complex) and the [topological graphs](../algebraic-topology.md#graph-topology) considered later in the paper.

**For arbitrary [path-connected spaces](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space), the printed assertion is false.** Here is an explicit [Warsaw circle](../topology.md#warsaw-circle) counterexample, including the obstruction that the usual lifting argument misses. In the [Euclidean plane](../geometry-and-topology.md#euclidean-plane) take

$$
G=\{(x,\sin(\pi/x)):0<x\leq1\},\qquad
L=\{0\}\times[-1,1].
$$

Join $P=(1,0)$ to $Q=(0,-1)$ by the polygonal [arc](../geometry-and-topology.md#arc-topology) $A$ passing successively through $(2,0),(2,-2),(0,-2)$, and put $W=G\cup L\cup A$ with its [subspace topology](../topology.md#subspace-topology). The [arc](../geometry-and-topology.md#arc-topology) meets $G\cup L$ only at $P,Q$. The [topologist's sine curve](../geometry-and-topology.md#topologist-s-sine-curve) $G\cup L$ has two [path components](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-component), but the added [arc](../geometry-and-topology.md#arc-topology) joins them, making $W$ [path-connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space).

Every [loop](../algebraic-topology.md#loop-topology) in $W$ is contained in

$$
T_\delta=L\cup A\cup\{(x,\sin(\pi/x)):\delta\leq x\leq1\}
$$

for some $\delta>0$. To justify the assertion, a [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) cannot reach $L$ along the oscillating tail: the [intermediate value theorem](../calculus.md#intermediate-value-theorem) for its $x$-coordinate would force its $y$-coordinate to attain both $1$ and $-1$ arbitrarily close to the supposed arrival time, contradicting [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function). The only alternative route from the tail to $L$ uses $P$ and the joining [arc](../geometry-and-topology.md#arc-topology). If a [loop](../algebraic-topology.md#loop-topology) visited tail points with $x\to0$, a convergent subsequence of their parameter values would approach a time at which the [loop](../algebraic-topology.md#loop-topology) lies in $L$. On a sufficiently small parameter [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics) around that time its image avoids $P$. That would give a forbidden [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) from $L$ into the tail without passing through $P$. Thus those tail coordinates have a positive lower bound. The space $T_\delta$ is a finite [tree](../tree-graph-theory.md), in fact a single [arc](../geometry-and-topology.md#arc-topology), so every such [loop](../algebraic-topology.md#loop-topology) contracts. Consequently

$$
\pi_1(W)=1.
$$

Now define a [continuous map](../topology.md#continuous-map) $h:W\to S^1$ to be $1$ on $G\cup L$, and to make one full turn on $A$: for a parametrization $a:[0,1]\to A$ from $P$ to $Q$, set $h(a(t))=e^{2\pi it}$. The pieces $G\cup L$ and $A$ are closed in $W$, and the formulas agree at $P,Q$, so the [pasting lemma for closed subspaces](../topology.md#pasting-lemma-for-closed-subspaces) proves [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function).

There is no [continuous lift](../topology.md#continuous-lift) $\widetilde h:W\to\mathbb R$. Any such lift is [integer](../integer.md)-valued on $G\cup L$, so it is constant on each of the [connected spaces](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) $G,L$. Since $G$ accumulates on $L$, [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) forces those two constants to be the same. But along $A$ a lift of $e^{2\pi it}$ has the form $t+k$, giving values at $Q$ and $P$ differing by $1$, a contradiction. Finally, if $h$ had a [null-homotopy](../homotopy.md#null-homotopy), the [homotopy lifting theorem for a covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-lifting-theorem-for-a-covering-map), starting with a lift of the final [constant function](../function.md#constant-function) and reversing time, would produce a [continuous lift](../topology.md#continuous-lift) of $h$. This theorem needs no [local path connectedness](../geometry-and-topology.md#locally-path-connected-space) of the parameter space. Hence $h$ is not [null-homotopic](../homotopy.md#null-homotopic-map), although $W$ has finite, indeed trivial, [fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group).

The native PDF explicitly assumes only [path connectedness](../geometry-and-topology.md#path-connected-space) here. The missing [local path connectedness](../geometry-and-topology.md#locally-path-connected-space) cannot be inferred from the finiteness of the [fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

A finite [tree](../tree-graph-theory.md) is a nonempty [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph) with no [graph cycles](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph). We show by [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on its number $v$ of [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) that it has $v-1$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). For $v=1$ there are no [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), so the assertion holds.

If $v>1$, take a [path in a graph](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) of maximal length among those without repeated [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). Each endpoint is a [leaf of a tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#leaf-of-a-tree): an additional incident [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) would either extend the [path in a graph](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) or create a [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph). Delete one such [leaf of a tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#leaf-of-a-tree) and its unique incident [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). The remaining [graph](../graph.md) stays [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), since a [path in a graph](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) between remaining [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) never needs that terminal [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), and it has no [graph cycles](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph). It is therefore a [tree](../tree-graph-theory.md) with $v-1$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) and, by [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction), $v-2$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). Restoring the deleted [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) gives $e=v-1$.

Thus its [Euler characteristic](../homology.md#euler-characteristic) is

$$
\boxed{\chi(X)=v-e=v-(v-1)=1.}
$$

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

Choose a [spanning tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#spanning-tree) $T$ of the finite [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph) $X$. It contains every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $X$ and has $v(X)-1$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) by part (i). Collapsing $T$ to a point is a [homotopy equivalence](../homotopy.md#homotopy-equivalence): $T$ is a [contractible](../homotopy.md#contractible-space) [subcomplex](../algebraic-topology.md#simplicial-subcomplex), and [collapsing a contractible cofibration](../homotopy.md#collapsing-a-contractible-cofibration) in a [CW complex](../algebraic-topology.md#cw-complex) preserves [homotopy type](../homotopy.md#homotopy-type).

Every [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) outside $T$ then becomes a [circle](../topology.md#circle) attached to the common point. Hence the [quotient space](../topology.md#quotient-topological-space) $X/T$ is a [bouquet of circles](../algebraic-topology.md#bouquet-of-circles) with

$$
r=e(X)-e(T)=e(X)-v(X)+1
$$

petals. The [fundamental group of a bouquet of circles](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group-of-a-bouquet-of-circles) is the [free group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) with one member of a [free basis of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-basis-of-a-group) for each petal. Therefore the [rank of the fundamental group of a finite connected graph](../algebraic-topology.md#rank-of-the-fundamental-group-of-a-finite-connected-graph) is

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(X)\cong F_{\,1-\chi(X)},\qquad r=1-\chi(X).}
$$

More concretely, choose a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) as [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point); follow the unique [path in a graph](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) in $T$ to one end of a non-tree [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), traverse it, and return through $T$. These [based loops](../algebraic-topology.md#based-loop) give the [free basis of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-basis-of-a-group). Thus $r$ is the number of free [group generators](../group.md#generator-of-a-group), equivalently the minimum size of a [generating set of a group](../group.md#generating-set-of-a-group), rather than the size of every possible redundant [generating set of a group](../group.md#generating-set-of-a-group). Minimality also follows by [abelianization](../group-theory.md#abelianization), since $F_r$ has [abelianization](../group-theory.md#abelianization) $\mathbb Z^r$.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

Assume $p$ has a common finite [degree of a covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#degree-of-a-covering-map) $d$. Declare the lifted [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) to be $p^{-1}(V(X))$, so there are exactly $dv(X)$ of them. Each open [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) is [homeomorphic](../topology.md#homeomorphism) to $(0,1)$, a [simply connected](../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space) [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics). The restriction of the [covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) above it is a disjoint union of $d$ copies of that open [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph).

These copies acquire endpoints as follows. Lift the parametrizing [path](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) $[0,1]\to X$ of each oriented [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), starting at each of the $d$ points over its initial [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). The [path lifting theorem](../algebraic-topology.md#path-lifting-theorem) gives a unique lift all the way to $1$. Its endpoint lies over the terminal [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), and the interiors of these lifted [paths](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) are exactly the $d$ open [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) already described. This also handles a [graph loop](../graph.md#loop-graph-theory), whose two endpoints may coincide downstairs or upstairs. Local [covering sheets](../algebraic-topology.md#covering-sheet) above a small star at a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) show that the lifted incidence relations have precisely the original [topology](../topology.md). Thus the [graph structure lifted through a covering map](../algebraic-topology.md#graph-structure-lifted-through-a-covering-map) is a finite [topological graph](../algebraic-topology.md#graph-topology) with

$$
v(\widetilde X)=dv(X),\qquad e(\widetilde X)=de(X).
$$

Subtracting gives the [Euler characteristic under a finite covering](../homology.md#euler-characteristic-under-a-finite-covering) formula

$$
\boxed{\chi(\widetilde X)=d\,\chi(X).}
$$

The formula holds even when $\widetilde X$ is disconnected. If a disconnected base is allowed to have degrees $d_C$ that vary with its [connected components](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-component) $C$, the precise replacement is

$$
\chi(\widetilde X)=\sum_C d_C\chi(C).
$$

The printed formula with a single $d$ uses the constant-degree convention explained in part (a)(ii).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use a [topological graph](../algebraic-topology.md#graph-topology) consisting of the ray $[0,\infty)$ with [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $v_n=n$ and one oriented [graph loop](../graph.md#loop-graph-theory) $\ell_n$ attached at each $v_n$, for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$. Set the [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point) to $b=v_0$. This is a [connected](../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), [locally finite graph](../graph-theory.md#locally-finite-graph); it is deliberately infinite.

Define [continuous maps](../topology.md#continuous-map) $f,g:X\to X$ by

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(t)&=t+1&&\text{on the ray},&
f(\ell_n(s))&=\ell_{n+1}(s),\\
g(t)&=\max(t-1,0)&&\text{on the ray},&
g(\ell_n(s))&=\begin{cases}b,&n=0,\\\ell_{n-1}(s),&n\geq1.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

Here each [graph loop](../graph.md#loop-graph-theory) is parametrized by $s\in[0,1]$ with its endpoints at the attaching [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). The formulas agree at every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), and their restrictions to every closed [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) are [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function). The [weak topology of a CW complex](../algebraic-topology.md#weak-topology-of-a-cw-complex) therefore makes $f,g$ [continuous maps](../topology.md#continuous-map). Directly on the ray and on each [graph loop](../graph.md#loop-graph-theory),

$$
\boxed{gf=\operatorname{id}_X.}
$$

We check the two [fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) maps at exactly the specified [base point](../algebraic-topology.md#base-point)s. The ray is a [spanning tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#spanning-tree). At $b=v_0$ a [free basis of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-basis-of-a-group) consists of $a_n$, obtained by going along the ray to $v_n$, following $\ell_n$, and returning. At $f(b)=v_1$, use the corresponding [free basis of a group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-basis-of-a-group) $c_n$ with connecting [paths](../geometry-and-topology.md#continuous-path) starting at $v_1$; in particular $c_0$ travels backwards along the first [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) before following $\ell_0$. The standard [spanning tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#spanning-tree) description applies to this infinite [topological graph](../algebraic-topology.md#graph-topology) as well: every [loop](../algebraic-topology.md#loop-topology) and every [homotopy](../homotopy.md) between [based loops](../algebraic-topology.md#based-loop) has [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [image](../set-theory.md#image-of-a-function), so lies in a finite [subcomplex](../algebraic-topology.md#simplicial-subcomplex).

The induced [group homomorphisms](../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) are

$$
\boxed{f_*(a_n)=c_{n+1},\qquad
g_*(c_0)=1,\quad g_*(c_n)=a_{n-1}\ (n\geq1).}
$$

Thus $f_*$ misses $c_0$: words in $c_1,c_2,\ldots$ cannot equal $c_0$ by the [normal form for free groups and free product of groups](../algebraic-topology.md#normal-form-for-free-groups-and-free-product-of-groups). Consequently $f_*$ is not [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function). Meanwhile $c_0$ is a nontrivial [free group](../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) element killed by $g_*$, so $g_*$ is not [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). **Neither induced map is a [group isomorphism](../algebra.md#group-isomorphism)**, while [functoriality of the fundamental group](../algebraic-topology.md#functoriality-of-the-fundamental-group) correctly gives $g_*f_*=\operatorname{id}$.
