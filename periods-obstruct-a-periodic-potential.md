# Periods obstruct a periodic potential

↑ **Parent:** [Conservative vector field](conservative-vector-field.md)

If a periodic [vector field](vector-field.md) has a periodic [potential of a conservative vector field](potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md), the [line integral](line-integral.md) between points separated by a period vector must vanish, since it is the potential difference. A periodic gradient field need not satisfy this: the constant field $(1,2)$ on the plane has potential $x+2y$, but its integrals over the two unit translations are $1$ and $2$. On the periodic quotient these translation integrals are nonzero periods, obstructing a single-valued potential there.
