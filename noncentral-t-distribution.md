# Noncentral t-distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Student's t-distribution](student-s-t-distribution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncentral_t-distribution)

If $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $U\sim\chi^2_\nu$ are [independent](independent-random-variables.md), then $(Z+\delta)/\sqrt{U/\nu}$ has the [noncentral t-distribution](noncentral-t-distribution.md) with $\nu>0$ [statistical degrees of freedom](statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md) and noncentrality $\delta$. It describes the [test statistic](test-statistic.md) of a [Student's t-test](student-s-t-test.md) under a shifted [alternative hypothesis](alternative-hypothesis.md). At $\delta=0$ it reduces to the [Student's t-distribution](student-s-t-distribution.md). This is different from adding a constant to a central [Student t-distribution](student-s-t-distribution.md): here the shift is inside the random denominator. For a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) sample tested against [null hypothesis](null-hypothesis.md) $\mu=\mu_0$, the noncentrality is $\delta=\sqrt n(\mu-\mu_0)/\sigma$.
