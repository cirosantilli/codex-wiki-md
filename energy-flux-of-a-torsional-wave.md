# Energy flux of a torsional wave

↑ **Parent:** [Torsional waves in a solid circular rod](torsional-waves-in-a-solid-circular-rod.md)

The local [elastic strain energy](elastic-energy.md) and [rotational kinetic energy](rotational-kinetic-energy.md) per length are $u_s=GJ_p\theta_x^2/2$ and $u_k=\rho J_p\theta_t^2/2$. Multiplying the [torsional wave equation in a circular shaft](torsional-wave-equation-in-a-circular-shaft.md) by $\rho J_p\theta_t$ and integrating the product derivative gives $\partial_t(u_s+u_k)+\partial_x\mathcal P=0$. Thus the [energy flux](energy-flux.md) is torque times angular speed, with the sign set by the positive direction. The inequality $|\mathcal P|=2\sqrt{G/\rho}\sqrt{u_su_k}\le\sqrt{G/\rho}(u_s+u_k)$ follows from a square. A one-way travelling wave has $u_s=u_k$; a steadily rotating twisted shaft need not. Stored energy divided by transit time is not a separate bound for each energy component in a general superposition.
