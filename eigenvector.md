# Eigenvector

↑ **Parent:** [Eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector)

A nonzero [vector](vector.md) $v$ is an eigenvector of a [linear operator](linear-operator.md) $A$ when $Av=\lambda v$ for some [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$. For a real [vector space](vector-space-split.md) and $\lambda\ne0$, the line through $v$ is preserved: the image can point forward or backward, but cannot turn away from that line. When $\lambda=0$, the image is zero and has no direction. The zero [vector](vector.md) is excluded because it satisfies the equation for every [scalar](scalar.md) and identifies no special direction.

Every nonzero [scalar multiple](scalar-multiple.md) of an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) is an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) for the same [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md), since $A(cv)=cAv=\lambda(cv)$. Thus there is usually no preferred length or sign; choosing a [unit vector](unit-vector.md) is a convenient normalization. [Eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) for a fixed [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md), together with zero, form its [eigenspace](eigenspace.md).

For a fully computed example, consider the real [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md)

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad A\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2x+y\\x+2y\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [characteristic polynomial](characteristic-polynomial.md) is

$$
p_A(\lambda)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\lambda-2&-1\\-1&\lambda-2\end{pmatrix}=(\lambda-2)^2-1=(\lambda-3)(\lambda-1).
$$

For $\lambda=3$, $(A-3I)v=0$ gives $-x+y=0$, so $v_3=(1,1)^T$ is an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md). For $\lambda=1$, $(A-I)v=0$ gives $x+y=0$, so $v_1=(1,-1)^T$ is an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md). Direct multiplication checks both:

$$
Av_3=\begin{pmatrix}3\\3\end{pmatrix}=3v_3,\qquad Av_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}=v_1.
$$

The map stretches the line $y=x$ threefold and fixes the line $y=-x$ pointwise. These [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) are [orthogonal](orthogonal-vectors.md) because $v_3^Tv_1=0$; dividing each by $\sqrt2$ gives an [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md). A general [vector](vector.md) turns because its components along these two lines receive different scalings. For example, $(1,0)^T$ maps to $(2,1)^T$ and is not an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md).

A contrasting example is the [planar rotation](planar-rotation.md) through $90$ degrees:

$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad R\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-y\\x\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Every nonzero real [vector](vector.md) turns to a perpendicular direction, so there are no real [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md). Its [characteristic polynomial](characteristic-polynomial.md) is $\lambda^2+1$, which has no real roots. Over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md), however, $R(1,-i)^T=i(1,-i)^T$ and $R(1,i)^T=-i(1,i)^T$. The same [matrix](matrix.md) therefore has an [eigenbasis](eigenbasis.md) in $\mathbb C^2$ even though it has none in $\mathbb R^2$: the choice of [field](field.md) matters.
