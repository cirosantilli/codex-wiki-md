<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define $\Phi:R\to R/I\times R/J$ by $\Phi(r)=(r+I,r+J)$. It is a [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) with

$$
\ker\Phi=I\cap J.
$$

Since $I+J=R$, choose $i\in I,j\in J$ such that $i+j=1$. For arbitrary $a,b\in R$, the element $r=aj+bi$ satisfies $r-a=i(b-a)\in I$ and $r-b=j(a-b)\in J$. Hence $\Phi(r)=(a+I,b+J)$, proving [surjectivity](../../../../surjective-function.md). The first [isomorphism](../../../../isomorphism.md) theorem now gives

$$
\boxed{R/(I\cap J)\cong R/I\times R/J.}
$$

This is the two-ideal [Chinese remainder theorem for ideals](../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem-for-ideals.md). The hypothesis is comaximality of the two [ideals](../../../../ideal.md); no assertion that every pair of [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) has this property is needed.
