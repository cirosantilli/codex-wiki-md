↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [first isomorphism theorem for rings](../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem-for-rings.md) states that the map

$$
\boxed{R/\ker f\longrightarrow\operatorname{im}f,\qquad r+\ker f\longmapsto f(r)}
$$

is a [ring isomorphism](../../../../ring-isomorphism.md). It is well defined because two representatives differ by an element of the [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) exactly when their images agree. It preserves addition and multiplication, is onto its stated codomain, and has trivial [kernel](../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md). In particular, if $f$ is [surjective](../../../../surjective-function.md) then $R/\ker f\cong S$.
