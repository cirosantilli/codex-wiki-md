<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Because $g,h$ are nonconstant, their classes in $\mathbb Q[x]/(gh)$ are nonzero: each has smaller [degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) than $gh$, so neither is a multiple of $gh$. Their product is zero. Thus **the [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) has [zero divisors](../../../../zero-divisor.md)**.

If both factors have [degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) one, each [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) by one factor is [isomorphic](../../../../isomorphism.md) to $\mathbb Q$, by evaluation at its rational [root](../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md). [Coprimality](../../../../coprime-integers.md) ensures the two roots are distinct. Part (c) therefore identifies the ring with $\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q$. If $(r,s)^2=(0,0)$, then $r^2=s^2=0$ in a [field](../../../../field.md), so $r=s=0$.

$$
\boxed{\text{There is no nonzero square-zero element when both factors have degree one.}}
$$

A ring may therefore have [zero divisors](../../../../zero-divisor.md) without having nonzero [nilpotent](../../../../nilpotent.md) elements.
