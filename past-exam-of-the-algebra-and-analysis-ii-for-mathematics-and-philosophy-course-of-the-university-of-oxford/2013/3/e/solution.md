<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Factorization over $\mathbb Q$ gives

$$
R_1\cong\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q,\qquad
R_2\cong\mathbb Q(i),\qquad
R_3\cong\mathbb Q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),\quad\varepsilon=x-1.
$$

For $R_1$, the factors $x-1$ and $x+1$ are coprime. The nonzero elements $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ multiply to zero, so the ring is neither an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) nor a [field](../../../../field.md). Squaring coordinatewise shows it has no nonzero square-zero element.

For $R_2$, the [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) $x^2+1$ has no rational [root](../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) and is [irreducible](../../../../irreducible-representation.md) of [degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) two over $\mathbb Q$. The [quotient](../../../../quotient.md) is consequently a [field](../../../../field.md), hence an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md), and has no nonzero square-zero element.

For $R_3$, the class $\varepsilon$ is nonzero by its [degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md), but $\varepsilon^2=0$. Thus this ring is neither an [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md) nor a [field](../../../../field.md), and does have a nonzero square-zero element.

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|ccc}&\text{integral domain}&\text{field}&\exists u\neq0:\ u^2=0\\\hline
R_1&\text{no}&\text{no}&\text{no}\\
R_2&\text{yes}&\text{yes}&\text{no}\\
R_3&\text{no}&\text{no}&\text{yes}
\end{array}}
$$

**No two are [isomorphic](../../../../isomorphism.md).** Being a [field](../../../../field.md) separates $R_2$ from the other two; existence of a nonzero square-zero element separates $R_1$ from $R_3$. Both properties are preserved by [ring isomorphisms](../../../../ring-isomorphism.md).
