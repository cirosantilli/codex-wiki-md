<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an [isolated singularity](../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) at $a$, write the [Laurent expansion](../../../../laurent-series.md) in a punctured disk as $f(z)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_k(z-a)^k$. Its [residue](../../../../residue.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{res}(f;a)=c_{-1}.}
$$

The [sine](../../../../sine.md) in the denominator has simple zeros precisely at the integers, and the numerator is nonzero there. Consequently $g(z)=\pi\cot(\pi z)$ has [simple poles](../../../../simple-pole.md) exactly at $n\in\mathbb Z$, with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{res}(g;n)=\frac{\pi\cos(\pi n)}{\pi\cos(\pi n)}=1.}
$$

There are no other finite [singularities](../../../../complex-singularity.md).
