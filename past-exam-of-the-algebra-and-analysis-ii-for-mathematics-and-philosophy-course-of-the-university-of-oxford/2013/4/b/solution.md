<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Cauchy's integral theorem](../../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) says that if $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on an [open set](../../../../open-set.md) $U$ and a closed, [piecewise smooth](../../../../piecewise-smooth-curve.md) [contour](../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) $\gamma$ is [null-homotopic](../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) in $U$, then

$$
\boxed{\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0.}
$$

In particular, this holds for every closed [contour](../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) when $U$ is [simply connected](../../../../simply-connected-space.md), and for a simple closed [contour](../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) whose boundary and enclosed region lie in $U$. For the later arguments it suffices to use the triangular version: the [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) around a [triangle](../../../../triangle.md) is zero if the [triangle](../../../../triangle.md) and its interior lie in $U$ and $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) there.

Both the holomorphicity condition and a suitable condition excluding holes inside the [contour](../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) matter. [Continuity](../../../../continuous-function.md) alone, which is the starting hypothesis for this question, does not imply the theorem's conclusion; nor does holomorphicity on an arbitrary punctured [open set](../../../../open-set.md) imply zero [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) around every loop.
