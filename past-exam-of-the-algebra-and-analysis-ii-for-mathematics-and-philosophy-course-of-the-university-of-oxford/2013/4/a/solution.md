<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A function is [holomorphic](../../../../holomorphic-function-split.md) on an [open set](../../../../open-set.md) when it is [complex differentiable](../../../../complex-differentiability.md) at every point of that set. Explicitly, for every $z\in U$, the limit

$$
\boxed{f'(z)=\lim_{h\to0,\ h\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}}\frac{f(z+h)-f(z)}h}
$$

exists as a finite [complex number](../../../../complex-number.md). The limit is independent of the direction in which the complex increment approaches zero; existence of [derivatives](../../../../derivative.md) only along the real axis would not suffice.
