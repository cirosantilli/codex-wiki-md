# Torsion element of a module

↑ **Parent:** [Torsion submodule](torsion-submodule.md)

For a [module](module-mathematics.md) $M$ over an [integral domain](integral-domain.md) $R$, a torsion element is an $m\in M$ with $rm=0$ for some $0\ne r\in R$. This includes zero. Equivalently, its [annihilator](annihilator-ring-theory.md) $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)=\{r\in R:rm=0\}$ is a nonzero [ideal](ideal.md). The [torsion elements of a module](torsion-element-of-a-module.md) form the [torsion submodule](torsion-submodule.md).

For $M=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, computed [annihilators](annihilator-ring-theory.md) are

$$
\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb Z}(\overline1)=6\mathbb Z,\qquad \operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb Z}(\overline2)=3\mathbb Z,\qquad \operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb Z}(\overline3)=2\mathbb Z.
$$

For these three elements, the additive [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md) is respectively $6$, $3$, and $2$: these are the smallest positive [integers](integer-split.md) killing them. Torsion over $\mathbb Z$ agrees exactly with finite additive [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md). Over a general [integral domain](integral-domain.md), torsion concerns the chosen scalars instead; the [polynomial module from a linear operator](polynomial-module-from-a-linear-operator.md) gives a torsion example whose nonzero elements have infinite additive [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md).
