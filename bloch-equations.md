# Bloch equations

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear magnetic resonance](nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch_equations)

The [Bloch equations](bloch-equations.md) describe an ensemble [magnetization](magnetization.md) $\mathbf M$ with [Larmor precession](larmor-precession.md) plus phenomenological recovery and decay:

$$
\frac{d\mathbf M}{dt}=\gamma\mathbf M\times\mathbf B
-\frac{M_x\hat{\mathbf x}+M_y\hat{\mathbf y}}{T_2}
-\frac{M_z-M_0}{T_1}\hat{\mathbf z}.
$$

Here $T_1,T_2>0$ are model parameters, not a microscopic explanation of relaxation. The precession term follows from the torque on a [magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md); the two dissipative terms prescribe [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md). Bloch explicitly introduced this macroscopic relaxation description as a simplifying approximation.[§4, pp. 467–468, equations (32), (37) and (38): longitudinal recovery and assumed exponential transverse decay](nuclear-induction-by-felix-bloch.md)

For positive $\gamma$ and no transverse drive, $M_x+iM_y$ rotates as $e^{-i\gamma B_0t}$: its signed rotation is negative about $z$, although the resonance [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\omega_0=\gamma B_0$ is positive. This distinction prevents a hidden sign change when choosing a [rotating frame](rotating-reference-frame.md).
