# Doppler cooling

↑ **Parent:** [Laser cooling](laser-cooling.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_cooling)

Consider equal opposing [laser](laser-split.md) beams along the $x$ axis and an atom with [velocity](velocity.md) $v$. Let $\omega_L$ be their [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md), $\omega_0$ the atomic resonance [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md), $k=2\pi/\lambda>0$ their [wavenumber](wavenumber.md), and $\delta=\omega_L-\omega_0$ the [laser detuning](laser-detuning.md). Red detuning means $\delta<0$. To first order in $v/c$, the atom sees

$$
\omega'_+=\omega_L-kv,\qquad \omega'_- =\omega_L+kv,\qquad
\delta_+=\delta-kv,\qquad \delta_- =\delta+kv.
$$

For small positive $v$, the beam travelling towards negative $x$ is closer to resonance and causes more [photon absorption](photon-absorption.md); its [momentum](momentum-split.md) kicks point against $v$. The beam travelling towards positive $x$ is further from resonance. Reversing $v$ exchanges the roles of the two beams. This is a [Doppler effect](doppler-effect.md) on the absorption rates, rather than a need to follow each atom with a moving beam.

An absorbed [photon](photon.md) gives the atom [momentum](momentum-split.md) $\pm\hbar k$, where $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$ is the reduced [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). Subsequent [spontaneous emission](spontaneous-emission.md) gives a recoil opposite to the emitted [photon](photon.md). For an emission pattern symmetric under reversal of direction, that recoil has zero [expected value](expected-value-split.md), but nonzero [variance](variance-split.md). Its mean therefore leaves the directional absorption [force](force-split.md) intact, while its randomness adds [recoil energy](recoil-energy.md). Random absorption times and choices of beam add further [momentum](momentum-split.md) fluctuations. Zero mean recoil does not mean zero heating.
