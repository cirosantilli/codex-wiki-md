# Titrant standardization

↑ **Parent:** [Acid-base titration](acid-base-titration.md)

[Titrant standardization](titrant-standardization.md) determines the actual [titrant](titrant.md) [concentration](concentration.md) by reaction with a known inventory, often a [primary standard](primary-standard.md). It is distinct from calibrating the delivered [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) or a [glass electrode](glass-electrode.md)'s response. For a known [monoprotic acid](monoprotic-acid.md) inventory $n_s$ and a suitable blank-corrected endpoint,

$$
c_B=\frac{n_s}{V_{\mathrm{end}}-V_{\mathrm{blank}}}.
$$

An original illustrative standard of $0.980\,\mathrm{mmol}$ requiring $9.85\,\mathrm{mL}$, with matched blank $0.05\,\mathrm{mL}$, gives $c_B=0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. The measured quantities here are stipulated examples. Independent [solution preparation and volumetric calibration](solution-preparation-and-volumetric-calibration.md) tests the [pipette](pipette.md), [burette](burette.md), and [volumetric flask](volumetric-flask.md); [titrant standardization](titrant-standardization.md) tests the reacting inventory per delivered [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md).

The useful chain is known [mass](mass.md), known composition, known [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), and then a measured [titre](titre.md) through a specified [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md). If each standard unit consumes $r$ units of [titrant](titrant.md), the general inventory equation is $c_BV=r n_s$, where $V$ is the corrected [titre](titre.md). The one-to-one formula above has $r=1$. A [titration endpoint](titration-endpoint.md) supplies an observable stopping rule; whether it represents the [equivalence point](equivalence-point.md) accurately remains a separate question. [Standardization endpoint and blank bias](standardization-endpoint-and-blank-bias.md) makes the direction of the resulting error explicit.
