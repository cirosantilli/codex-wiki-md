# Steady temperature of a uniformly heated cylindrical wire

↑ **Parent:** [Heat equation](heat-equation.md)

For a long homogeneous circular wire of radius $a$, uniform volumetric heating $H$, constant [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) $\kappa$ and negligible end losses, the steady [heat equation](heat-equation.md) is $\kappa\rho^{-1}(\rho T')'+H=0$. Regularity at the axis excludes a [logarithm](logarithm.md) term, giving $T=T_s+H(a^2-\rho^2)/(4\kappa)$. If the surface obeys [Newton's law of cooling](newton-s-law-of-cooling.md), $-\kappa T'(a)=\gamma(T_s-T_{\rm air})$, then $T_s=T_{\rm air}+Ha/(2\gamma)$. Here $\gamma$ has units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-1}}$ and $H$ has units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}$. For uniform [electric current](electric-current.md) $I$ and constant [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma$, [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) gives $H=I^2/(\sigma\pi^2a^4)$.
