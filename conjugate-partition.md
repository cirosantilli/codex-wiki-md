# Conjugate partition

↑ **Parent:** [Young diagram](young-diagram.md)

The conjugate partition exchanges rows and columns in a [Young diagram](young-diagram.md): the cell $(i,j)$ becomes $(j,i)$. Equivalently,

$$
\lambda'_j=\#\{i:\lambda_i\geq j\},\qquad
Y(\lambda')=\{(j,i):(i,j)\in Y(\lambda)\}.
$$

For $\lambda=(3,2)$, the three column lengths are $2,2,1$, so $\lambda'=(2,2,1)$, as shown in [Young diagram](young-diagram.md). Conjugating twice returns the original [partition of an integer](partition-of-an-integer.md), and the number of cells stays five. Transposition also preserves [hook lengths](hook-length.md), with $h_{j,i}(\lambda')=h_{i,j}(\lambda)$; consequently the [hook-length formula](hook-length-formula.md) gives equal [dimensions](dimension-vector-space.md) for the two [Specht modules](specht-module.md) over the [complex numbers](complex-number.md). This row-column symmetry is already used in §1 of [Frame, Robinson and Thrall's 1954 paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1954-030-1).
