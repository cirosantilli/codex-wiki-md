<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $\mu=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ and choose cofinal [ordinals](../../../../ordinal-split.md) $\alpha_i<\kappa$, $i<\mu$. With $a_i=|\alpha_i|$, the same union comparison and [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../../../../infinite-cardinal-arithmetic.md) give $\sum_{i<\mu}a_i=\kappa$. Since $a_i<\kappa$, their [successor cardinals](../../../../successor-cardinal.md) satisfy $a_i^+\leq\kappa$. The [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../../konig-s-theorem-set-theory.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\sum_{i<\mu}a_i<\prod_{i<\mu}a_i^+\leq\kappa^\mu=\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}.}
$$

This [König bound for the Gimel function](../../../../konig-bound-for-the-gimel-function.md) applies to regular and [singular cardinals](../../../../singular-cardinal.md) alike; choosing cofinal [ordinals](../../../../ordinal-split.md) also covers successor [cardinal numbers](../../../../cardinal-number-split.md).
