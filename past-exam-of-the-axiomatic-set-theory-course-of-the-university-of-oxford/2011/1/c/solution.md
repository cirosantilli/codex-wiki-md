<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Every [finite ordinal](../../../../finite-ordinal.md) belongs to $V_\omega$, so $\omega\subseteq V_\omega$ and $\omega\in\mathcal P(V_\omega)=V_{\omega+1}$. However, $\omega\notin V_\omega$, whose elements are the [hereditarily finite sets](../../../../hereditarily-finite-set.md).

Suppose $y\in V_{\omega+1}$ were an internal [power set](../../../../power-set.md) of $\omega$. Internally $\omega\subseteq\omega$, and $\omega$ is an available element of the structure. Thus the [Axiom of power set](../../../../axiom-of-power-set.md) would require $\omega\in y$. But $y\in\mathcal P(V_\omega)$ means $y\subseteq V_\omega$, which would put $\omega\in V_\omega$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(V_{\omega+1},\in)\not\models\mathrm{PowerSet}.}
$$

This proves [power-set failure at the first rank above V omega](../../../../power-set-failure-at-the-first-rank-above-v-omega.md) by an internal obstruction, including the subset $\omega$ itself.
