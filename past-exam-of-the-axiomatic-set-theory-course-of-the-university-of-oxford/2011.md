<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

The [cumulative hierarchy](../set-theory.md#cumulative-hierarchy) is the [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion)

$$
\boxed{V_0=\varnothing,\qquad V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal P(V_\alpha),\qquad V_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_\alpha\quad(\lambda\text{ a nonzero limit ordinal}).}
$$

Here $\mathcal P$ is the [power set](../set.md#power-set). The class $V=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}V_\alpha$ consists of all sets in [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory), by the [Axiom of foundation](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-regularity) and [Axiom schema of replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Two facts about a [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) $A$ drive the [transfinite induction](../ordinal.md#transfinite-induction). First, $A\subseteq\mathcal P(A)$: every $x\in A$ is a subset of $A$. Second, $\mathcal P(A)$ is a [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set): if $x\in y\in\mathcal P(A)$, then $x\in A$, hence $x\subseteq A$, hence $x\in\mathcal P(A)$.

Starting with $V_0=\varnothing$, these facts prove the successor step. At a [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\lambda$, the union of the earlier increasing [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) is transitive: if $x\in y\in V_\lambda$, some $\alpha<\lambda$ contains $y$ in $V_\alpha$, and transitivity puts $x$ there too. The union contains every earlier stage. Thus the induction gives both transitivity and $V_\beta\subseteq V_\alpha$ whenever $\beta\leq\alpha$.

Finally $V_\alpha\subseteq V_\alpha$, so $V_\alpha\in\mathcal P(V_\alpha)=V_{\alpha+1}$. The [Axiom of foundation](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-regularity) gives $V_\alpha\notin V_\alpha$. This witnesses strictness:

$$
\boxed{V_\alpha\text{ is transitive},\qquad V_\alpha\subsetneq V_{\alpha+1}.}
$$

This is the [transitivity and strict growth of the cumulative hierarchy](../set-theory.md#transitivity-and-strict-growth-of-the-cumulative-hierarchy).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Fix $x\in V_\delta$. Since $\delta$ is a [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal), choose $\beta<\delta$ with $x\in V_\beta$. Transitivity gives $x\subseteq V_\beta$. Every subset $u\subseteq x$ therefore belongs to $V_{\beta+1}=\mathcal P(V_\beta)$, and consequently

$$
\mathcal P(x)\subseteq V_{\beta+1},\qquad\mathcal P(x)\in V_{\beta+2}\subseteq V_\delta.
$$

The last inclusion uses $\beta+2<\delta$, since a finite number of successor steps remains below a [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal). In particular every subset of $x$ is also an element of the [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) $V_\delta$.

Take $y=\mathcal P(x)$. For any $u\in V_\delta$, transitivity ensures that the internal statement $u\subseteq x$ agrees with the external one. Hence

$$
(V_\delta,\in)\models\forall u\,(u\in y\leftrightarrow u\subseteq x).
$$

Such a $y\in V_\delta$ exists for every $x\in V_\delta$, so **$(V_\delta,\in)$ satisfies the [Axiom of power set](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-power-set)**. This [power-set axiom at limit ranks](../set-theory.md#power-set-axiom-at-limit-ranks) actually holds for every nonzero [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal); the stated restriction $\delta>\omega$ is sufficient.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Every [finite ordinal](../ordinal.md#finite-ordinal) belongs to $V_\omega$, so $\omega\subseteq V_\omega$ and $\omega\in\mathcal P(V_\omega)=V_{\omega+1}$. However, $\omega\notin V_\omega$, whose elements are the [hereditarily finite sets](../set-theory.md#hereditarily-finite-set).

Suppose $y\in V_{\omega+1}$ were an internal [power set](../set.md#power-set) of $\omega$. Internally $\omega\subseteq\omega$, and $\omega$ is an available element of the structure. Thus the [Axiom of power set](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-power-set) would require $\omega\in y$. But $y\in\mathcal P(V_\omega)$ means $y\subseteq V_\omega$, which would put $\omega\in V_\omega$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(V_{\omega+1},\in)\not\models\mathrm{PowerSet}.}
$$

This proves [power-set failure at the first rank above V omega](../set-theory.md#power-set-failure-at-the-first-rank-above-v-omega) by an internal obstruction, including the subset $\omega$ itself.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

A [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory), or $\Sigma_0$ formula in the [Lévy hierarchy](../set-theory.md#levy-hierarchy), uses only [bounded quantifiers](../computability-theory.md#bounded-quantifier) $\exists u\in v$ and $\forall u\in v$, with the usual Boolean connectives. A [Sigma-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory) has the form

$$
\exists u_1\cdots\exists u_m\,\theta(\mathbf u,\mathbf x),
$$

where $\theta$ is a [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory); harmless logically equivalent rearrangements are allowed. A [Pi-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#pi-one-formula-in-set-theory) is defined with a universal unbounded block instead.

A [Delta-one formula modulo ZF](../set-theory.md#delta-one-formula-modulo-zf) is a [first-order formula](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) $\varphi(\mathbf x)$ for which there are such $\Sigma_1$ and $\Pi_1$ formulas $\sigma,\pi$ with

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{ZF}\vdash\forall\mathbf x\,(\varphi\leftrightarrow\sigma),\qquad\mathrm{ZF}\vdash\forall\mathbf x\,(\varphi\leftrightarrow\pi).}
$$

Equivalently, both $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ have [Sigma-one formulas in set theory](../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory) provably equivalent to them in [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory). These are levels of the [Lévy hierarchy](../set-theory.md#levy-hierarchy), with bounds by sets.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Begin with [bounded formulas in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory). Atomic membership and equality have the same interpretation on shared elements of $U_1\subseteq U_2$, each a [transitive class](../set-theory.md#transitive-class). Boolean connectives preserve agreement. For a bounded quantifier with bound $a\in U_1$, every member of $a$ lies in $U_1$ by transitivity. Its quantifier range is therefore exactly the same in both structures. [Structural induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#structural-induction) proves [set-theoretic absoluteness](../set-theory.md#set-theoretic-absoluteness) for every [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory).

A [Sigma-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory) is upward absolute: witnesses in $U_1$ are still witnesses in $U_2$, and the bounded matrix agrees. A [Pi-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#pi-one-formula-in-set-theory) is downward absolute, by negation or by restricting its universal quantifiers.

For the given [Delta-one formula modulo ZF](../set-theory.md#delta-one-formula-modulo-zf) $\varphi$, select a $\Sigma_1$ equivalent $\sigma$ and a $\Pi_1$ equivalent $\pi$. Both structures satisfy [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory), so both equivalences hold in both structures. Upward absoluteness of $\sigma$ proves the forward implication, and downward absoluteness of $\pi$ proves the reverse implication. Thus [Delta-one absoluteness](../set-theory.md#delta-one-absoluteness) gives

$$
\boxed{U_1\models\varphi(\mathbf a)\iff U_2\models\varphi(\mathbf a)\qquad(\mathbf a\in U_1^n).}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

There is a [bounded definition of omega](../set-theory.md#bounded-definition-of-omega). Transitivity has the bounded definition $\operatorname{Trans}(x)\equiv\forall u\in x\,\forall v\in u\,(v\in x)$. An [ordinal](../ordinal.md) is a [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) of [transitive sets](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) on which membership is a strict total order; this too has a [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory), since [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) supplies well-foundedness by the [Axiom of foundation](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-regularity).

Define the bounded predicate

$$
L(x)\equiv\operatorname{Ord}(x)\land x\ne\varnothing\land\forall y\in x\,\exists z\in x\,(y\in z).
$$

For an [ordinal](../ordinal.md), this says that it is a nonzero [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal). It follows in [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) that

$$
\boxed{x=\omega\iff L(x)\land\forall y\in x\,\neg L(y),}
$$

since $\omega$ is the least nonzero [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal). The right side is a [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory); consequently it is absolute between the specified [transitive models](../set-theory.md#transitive-model). This also shows directly that those models have the same $\omega$.

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

Use the convention that the [transitive closure](../set-theory.md#transitive-closure) $\operatorname{TC}(x)$ is the least [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) containing $x$ as a subset. In [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory), [transfinite recursion](../ordinal.md#transfinite-recursion) and [Axiom schema of replacement](../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) construct

$$
x_0=x,\qquad x_{n+1}=\bigcup x_n,\qquad t=\bigcup_{n<\omega}x_n.
$$

Then $x\subseteq t$. If $a\in b\in t$, some $n$ has $b\in x_n$, whence $a\in x_{n+1}\subseteq t$. Thus $t$ is transitive. If another [transitive set](../set-theory.md#transitive-set) $s$ contains $x$, induction gives $x_n\subseteq s$ for every $n$, and hence $t\subseteq s$. This proves existence and uniqueness of the [transitive closure](../set-theory.md#transitive-closure).

Let $W(w)$ be the [bounded definition of omega](../set-theory.md#bounded-definition-of-omega) above. The displayed construction can be expressed as

$$
\exists w\,\exists f\,\exists r\,\bigl[W(w)\land f:w\to r\land\operatorname{ran}(f)=r\land f(0)=x\land\forall n\in w\,(f(n+1)=\bigcup f(n))\land y=\bigcup r\bigr].
$$

Here the brackets abbreviate a [bounded formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory): use the permitted bounded function and union predicates, bound the values of $f$ by $r$, and bound each successor index by $w$. Ordered-pair graph access is bounded; $\operatorname{ran}(f)=r$ says every value in $r$ occurs at an index in $w$. Any auxiliary sets used for the graph coding may be put in the initial existential block. Thus this is a [Sigma-one formula in set theory](../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory) defining $y=\operatorname{TC}(x)$.

Denote this formula by $T(x,y)$. Existence and uniqueness give the [ZF](../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) equivalence

$$
\neg T(x,y)\iff\exists z\,(T(x,z)\land z\ne y).
$$

The right side is again $\Sigma_1$, after combining existential blocks. Consequently $T$ is a [Delta-one formula modulo ZF](../set-theory.md#delta-one-formula-modulo-zf), so [Delta-one absoluteness](../set-theory.md#delta-one-absoluteness) applies. Therefore **[transitive closure is Delta-one definable](../set-theory.md#transitive-closure-is-delta-one-definable) and is ZF-absolute**. If closure is instead required to contain $x$ as an element, apply exactly this construction to $\{x\}$; the absoluteness conclusion is unchanged.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Use the usual [cardinal arithmetic](../cardinal-number.md#cardinal-arithmetic) with the [axiom of choice](../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice). The [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) states that for any index set $I$ and [cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$,

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i\in I}\kappa_i<\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i.}
$$

Represent the [cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md) as [initial ordinals](../cardinal-number.md#initial-ordinal). Let $A=\{(i,\alpha):i\in I,\alpha<\kappa_i\}$ and $B=\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_i$. There is an [injection](../algebra.md#injective-function) $A\to B$: send $(i,\alpha)$ to the function whose $i$th coordinate is $\alpha+1$ and whose other coordinates are zero. The unique nonzero coordinate identifies $i$, and its value identifies $\alpha$. All values are allowed because $\alpha+1\leq\kappa_i<\lambda_i$.

For any proposed [surjection](../algebra.md#surjective-function) $F:A\to B$, its $i$th row has at most $\kappa_i$ elements, so

$$
\{F(i,\alpha)(i):\alpha<\kappa_i\}\ne\lambda_i.
$$

Choose $b_i$ to be the least omitted [ordinal](../ordinal.md). The function $b=(b_i)_{i\in I}$ belongs to $B$ and differs from $F(i,\alpha)$ at coordinate $i$, for every $(i,\alpha)\in A$. Thus $F$ is not surjective. Cardinal comparison now gives the strict inequality. If $I=\varnothing$, the conclusion is $0<1$, also valid.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For an infinite [cardinal number](../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa$, its [cofinality](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality) is

$$
\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\min\{|A|:A\subseteq\kappa,\ \sup A=\kappa\},
$$

viewing $\kappa$ as an [initial ordinal](../cardinal-number.md#initial-ordinal). Equivalently it is the smallest [cardinal number](../cardinal-number.md) which is the domain size of a [cofinal map](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-map) into $\kappa$.

Put $\lambda=2^\kappa$ and $\mu=\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$. Suppose $\mu\leq\kappa$, and choose cofinal [ordinals](../ordinal.md) $\alpha_i<\lambda$ for $i<\mu$. Put $a_i=|\alpha_i|<\lambda$. Their union is $\lambda$, so [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../cardinal-number.md#infinite-cardinal-arithmetic) gives

$$
\lambda\leq\sum_{i<\mu}a_i\leq\lambda\mu=\lambda.
$$

Apply the [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) to $a_i<a_i^+$. Each [successor cardinal](../cardinal-number.md#successor-cardinal) $a_i^+$ is at most $\lambda$, whence

$$
\lambda=\sum_{i<\mu}a_i<\prod_{i<\mu}a_i^+\leq\lambda^\mu\leq(2^\kappa)^\kappa=2^{\kappa\cdot\kappa}=2^\kappa=\lambda.
$$

Here the last equalities use the [currying law for cardinal exponentiation](../cardinal-number.md#currying-law-for-cardinal-exponentiation) and [square of an infinite cardinal](../cardinal-number.md#square-of-an-infinite-cardinal). This contradiction proves the [cofinality of a power-set cardinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality-of-a-power-set-cardinal) bound

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(2^\kappa)>\kappa.}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Put $\mu=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ and choose cofinal [ordinals](../ordinal.md) $\alpha_i<\kappa$, $i<\mu$. With $a_i=|\alpha_i|$, the same union comparison and [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../cardinal-number.md#infinite-cardinal-arithmetic) give $\sum_{i<\mu}a_i=\kappa$. Since $a_i<\kappa$, their [successor cardinals](../cardinal-number.md#successor-cardinal) satisfy $a_i^+\leq\kappa$. The [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\sum_{i<\mu}a_i<\prod_{i<\mu}a_i^+\leq\kappa^\mu=\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}.}
$$

This [König bound for the Gimel function](../cardinal-number.md#konig-bound-for-the-gimel-function) applies to regular and [singular cardinals](../cardinal-number.md#singular-cardinal) alike; choosing cofinal [ordinals](../ordinal.md) also covers successor [cardinal numbers](../cardinal-number.md).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Take the [Beth number](../cardinal-number.md#beth-number)

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\beth_\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}\beth_n,\qquad\beth_0=\aleph_0,\quad\beth_{n+1}=2^{\beth_n}.}
$$

[Cantor's theorem](../set.md#cantor-s-theorem) makes this sequence strictly increasing. It is a countable cofinal sequence in $\kappa$, while no finite set is cofinal in the [limit ordinal](../ordinal.md#limit-ordinal) $\kappa$. Hence $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\aleph_0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=2^{\aleph_0}=\beth_1<\beth_\omega=\kappa.}
$$

Thus [Beth omega is a singular strong limit above the continuum](../cardinal-number.md#beth-omega-is-a-singular-strong-limit-above-the-continuum). No [Continuum hypothesis](../cardinal-number.md#continuum-hypothesis) is needed for the example.
