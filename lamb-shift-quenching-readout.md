# Lamb shift quenching readout

↑ **Parent:** [Lamb-Retherford experiment](lamb-retherford-experiment.md)

For a simple original detector model, let $\Phi_S$ be the incident rate of surviving $2S$ atoms, $e$ the positive [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md), $\eta$ the mean number of collected [electrons](electron.md) per incident $2S$ atom, and $I_{\rm bg}$ a stable background current. Take current magnitudes as positive. If [microwaves](microwave.md) remove a fraction $p$ before the target,

$$
I_{\rm off}=I_{\rm bg}+e\eta\Phi_S,\qquad
I_{\rm on}=I_{\rm bg}+e\eta\Phi_S(1-p),\qquad Q=p.
$$

The equality needs the same beam flux, background and collection efficiency in both measurements. It is an ideal interpretation of a normalized current loss, not a claim that an actual galvanometer directly counts individual transitions. Varying microwave amplitude tests whether the drive distorts the resonance; a field calibration is separately needed to turn a current-versus-field minimum into an [energy](energy-split.md) difference.

The [metastable atomic level](metastable-atomic-level.md) matters because an [electric-dipole selection rule](electric-dipole-selection-rule.md) suppresses the direct $2S\to1S$ [electric-dipole interaction](electric-dipole-interaction.md), whereas the opposite-parity $2S\leftrightarrow2P$ transition can be driven. A long lifetime can store the prepared population until interrogation. After transfer to $2P$, a short lifetime supplies the loss mechanism and a broad [natural linewidth](natural-linewidth.md). A forbidden strong decay does not mean absolute stability: weaker decay channels and unwanted [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) still matter.
