# Average power

↑ **Parent:** [Power](power.md)

[Average power](average-power.md) divides the [work](work.md) over a nonzero interval by the elapsed [time](time-in-physics.md). For the same $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ [point mass](point-mass.md) driven from rest through $4\,\mathrm m$ by a net $3\,\mathrm N$ [force](force.md), the [displacement](displacement.md) is $x(t)=at^2/2$. Thus

$$
t_f=\sqrt{\frac{2(4\,\mathrm m)}{1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}}
=\sqrt{16/3}\,\mathrm s\approx2.309401\,\mathrm s,
\qquad \overline P=\frac{12\,\mathrm J}{t_f}
\approx5.19615\,\mathrm W.
$$

This [average power](average-power.md) is half the final [instantaneous power](instantaneous-power.md) because $P(t)$ is a [linear function](linear-function.md) of [time](time-in-physics.md), starting at zero. Its [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md) bounds a [triangle](triangle.md) of area $P(t_f)t_f/2=12\,\mathrm J$; the horizontal line at [average power](average-power.md) encloses a [rectangle](rectangle.md) with the same area. In general, an endpoint value of [instantaneous power](instantaneous-power.md) does not determine [average power](average-power.md). [OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.4: Average and instantaneous power](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-4-power)
