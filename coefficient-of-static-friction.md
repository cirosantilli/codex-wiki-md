# Coefficient of static friction

↑ **Parent:** [Static friction](static-friction.md)

The [coefficient of static friction](coefficient-of-static-friction.md) is the nonnegative [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md) $\mu_s=|f_s|_{\max}/N$, with $N>0$, in the ideal dry-contact model. It characterizes the pair of surfaces under specified conditions. It describes the largest supported tangential [force](force-split.md), whereas the [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) describes sliding. It is an empirical approximation, not a universal material constant.[Equations 6.1–6.2; unitless coefficient and dependence on both surfaces; empirical-law limitations immediately before Example 6.10](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/6-2-friction.md)

In the horizontal $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ example, an observed state balancing a $2\,\mathrm N$ pull would only establish $\mu_s\ge2/19.6\simeq0.10204$ under the model assumptions. It would not establish $\mu_s=0.10204$: the actual [static friction](static-friction.md) is below its limit. Identifying the coefficient needs an onset measurement, such as [inclined-plane measurement of static friction](inclined-plane-measurement-of-static-friction.md), or the starting peak in a suitably controlled horizontal pull. The sustained sliding part of [constant-speed measurement of kinetic friction](constant-speed-measurement-of-kinetic-friction.md) measures a different coefficient.

In a simple grain model, the existing name [granular friction coefficient](coefficient-of-static-friction.md) represents resistance to downstream motion. That model excludes lift and detailed contact [torques](torque.md). Its coefficient should not automatically be identified with a bulk pile's [angle of repose](angle-of-repose.md): individual contact geometry and collective rearrangement can matter.
