# Equivalence principle

↑ **Parent:** [General relativity](general-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_principle)

The equivalence principle states that gravity couples universally to energy-momentum and that a freely falling local frame removes uniform gravitational acceleration at a point. Tidal curvature remains measurable over a finite region.

Imagine releasing a ball inside a small falling elevator. Relative to the cabin it initially floats, because cabin and ball share the same [free fall](free-fall.md). The [weak equivalence principle](weak-equivalence-principle.md) says this trajectory is independent of the [test particle](test-particle.md)'s composition for identical initial conditions. The [Einstein equivalence principle](einstein-equivalence-principle.md) further identifies local nongravitational experiments with those of [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md). It is a statement about experiments in a [local inertial frame](local-inertial-frame.md), not just equality of two masses.

The word local is essential. Coordinates can make the [metric tensor](metric-tensor.md) equal to the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) and its first [derivatives](derivative.md) vanish at one event. They generally cannot erase the [Riemann curvature tensor](riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md), which involves second [derivatives](derivative.md). Two separated balls can therefore approach or recede even inside the falling cabin. A sufficiently extended experiment detects these [tidal forces](tidal-force.md); a long observation can accumulate them even when the cabin is small. The [equivalence principle](equivalence-principle.md) does not identify an entire planetary [gravitational field](gravitational-field.md) with acceleration in flat [spacetime](spacetime.md). See [Tong's discussion of normal coordinates and local equivalence](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S3.html).
