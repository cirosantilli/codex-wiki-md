# Leaking bucket under constant tension

↑ **Parent:** [Variable-mass system](variable-mass-system.md)

Let a massless bucket approach a wall under constant [tension](tension.md) $T>0$, with remaining distance $x$ and remaining sand [mass](mass.md) $m=Mx/L$. Assume [zero-relative-velocity mass shedding](zero-relative-velocity-mass-shedding.md) horizontally. Then $m\ddot x=-T$ and, from rest at $x=L$, the [speed](speed.md) satisfies $u^2=(2TL/M)\ln(L/x)$. Its [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is $K=Tx\ln(L/x)$ and its [momentum](momentum-split.md) magnitude is $p=x\sqrt{(2MT/L)\ln(L/x)}$. Differentiating gives $x_K=L/e$ and $x_p=L/\sqrt e$: the [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) peaks later, closer to the wall, because its dependence on the increasing [speed](speed.md) is stronger. Both tend to zero as the remaining [mass](mass.md) vanishes, despite divergent ideal [speed](speed.md).
