<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On the extension in part (i), the [hypersurface](../../../../../hypersurface.md) $\mathcal N$ has nonzero normal [covector](../../../../../covector.md) $n_a=\partial_a\lambda$. Its squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is

$$
g^{ab}n_an_b=g^{\lambda\lambda}=\frac{\lambda^2}{M^2}.
$$

This vanishes on $\mathcal N$, which is therefore a [null hypersurface](../../../../../null-hypersurface.md). Raising the index gives

$$
n^a=-\frac1M(\partial_v)^a+\frac{\lambda^2}{M^2}(\partial_\lambda)^a.
$$

Since $\partial_t=\partial_v$ at fixed $\lambda$, the original [Killing vector field](../../../../../killing-vector-field-split.md) becomes the smooth nonzero field $K=\partial_v$. The transformed [metric tensor](../../../../../metric-tensor.md) is independent of $v$, so $K$ remains a [Killing vector field](../../../../../killing-vector-field-split.md), and

$$
\left.n^a\right|_{\mathcal N}=-\frac1M K^a,\qquad
\left.g(K,K)\right|_{\mathcal N}=0.
$$

Thus $K$ is both tangent and normal to $\mathcal N$. This is exactly a [Killing horizon](../../../../../killing-horizon.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathcal N=\{\lambda=0\}\text{ is a Killing horizon of }\partial_t=\partial_v.}
$$

The [Killing vector field](../../../../../killing-vector-field-split.md) itself does not vanish there; a vanishing squared [norm](../../../../../norm.md) means nullness, not a zero vector.
