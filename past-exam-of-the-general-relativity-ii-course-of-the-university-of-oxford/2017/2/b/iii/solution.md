<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [Killing equation](../../../../../killing-equation.md) implies

$$
K^a\nabla_aK^b=-\frac12\nabla^b(K^aK_a).
$$

Here $K^2=-\lambda^2$, so in the regular coordinates

$$
K^a\nabla_aK^b
=\lambda\,g^{b\lambda}
=-\frac{\lambda}{M}(\partial_v)^b
+\frac{\lambda^3}{M^2}(\partial_\lambda)^b.
$$

This vector vanishes at $\lambda=0$. The [Killing vector field](../../../../../killing-vector-field-split.md) $K=\partial_v$ is nonzero there, so comparison with the definition of [surface gravity](../../../../../surface-gravity.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\kappa=0.}
$$

Hence this is a [degenerate Killing horizon](../../../../../degenerate-killing-horizon.md). The argument uses the vanishing gradient of $K^2$ on the horizon; the condition $K^2=0$ by itself would not force zero [surface gravity](../../../../../surface-gravity.md).
