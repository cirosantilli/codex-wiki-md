# Factorial-moment criterion for Poisson convergence

↑ **Parent:** [Poisson factorial moments](poisson-factorial-moments.md)

If nonnegative integer-valued [random variables](random-variable-split.md) $X_n$ have all fixed [factorial moments](factorial-moment.md) converging to $\lambda^k$, then their [convergence in distribution](convergence-in-distribution.md) is to the [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md) with mean $\lambda$. The first-moment bound gives [uniform tightness](uniform-tightness.md), and bounds on higher moments give [uniform integrability](uniform-integrability.md) of each fixed lower moment. Every subsequential limit therefore has the indicated [factorial moments](factorial-moment.md). For such a limit $X$ and $t\geq0$, the nonnegative binomial expansion and the [monotone convergence theorem](monotone-convergence-theorem.md) give $\mathbb E(1+t)^X=\sum_{k\geq0}t^k\lambda^k/k!=e^{\lambda t}$. This identifies its [moment-generating function](moment-generating-function.md) as that of the [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md), uniquely determining the limit.
