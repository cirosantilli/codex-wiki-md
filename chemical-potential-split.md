# Chemical potential

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_potential)

The [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) measures how a [thermodynamic potential](thermodynamic-potential-split.md) changes when matter is added under specified constraints. It plays for matter exchange the role that [temperature](temperature-split.md) plays for [heat](heat-split.md) exchange: a difference supplies a direction, and equality characterizes [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) when the corresponding exchange is allowed. This connects [diffusion](diffusion.md), [phase coexistence](phase-coexistence.md) and [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) through one quantity.

For a single component, the particle convention is

$$
\mu_{\rm particle}=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial N}\right)_{S,V},
$$

where $U$ is [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md), $N$ counts particles, $S$ is [entropy](entropy-split.md) and $V$ is [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). This [partial derivative](partial-derivative.md) holds [entropy](entropy-split.md) and [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) fixed; it measures an incremental change rather than the average [energy](energy-split.md) per particle. See [David Tong, Statistical Physics, section 1.4](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/statistical-physics/statphys1.pdf).

In [chemistry](chemistry-split.md) the molar convention instead uses [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) $n$, measured in [moles](mole-unit.md):

$$
\mu_{\rm m}=\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial n}\right)_{T,p},
\qquad n=\frac{N}{N_{\rm A}},
\qquad \mu_{\rm m}=N_{\rm A}\mu_{\rm particle}.
$$

Here $G$ is [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md), $T$ is [temperature](temperature-split.md), $p$ is [pressure](pressure-split.md) and $N_{\rm A}$ is the [Avogadro constant](avogadro-constant.md). The units are $\mathrm J$ per particle versus $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$. For several [chemical species](chemical-species.md), define each [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) by varying only its own particle count or [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), with all other components' counts or amounts fixed. Many texts use $\mu$ for either convention, so the units and the variable being differentiated must identify it.

For a [simple compressible system](simple-compressible-system.md) with variable component inventories, the fundamental differentials are

$$
dU=T\,dS-p\,dV+\sum_i\mu_{{\rm particle},i}\,dN_i,
\qquad
dG=-S\,dT+V\,dp+\sum_i\mu_{{\rm m},i}\,dn_i.
$$

They describe changes between states in [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md) with only [pressure](pressure-split.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) [work](work.md). If every component inventory is fixed, the sums vanish. The [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) form follows by differentiating $G=U-TS+pV$ and using $dN_i=N_{\rm A}\,dn_i$; this is why the two definitions describe the same [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) in different units.

A [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) can be negative. Holding [entropy](entropy-split.md) fixed while adding matter need not increase [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md); Tong explains this explicitly for a classical [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) in [Statistical Physics, section 2.2.4](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/statistical-physics/statphys2.pdf). Its absolute sign also depends on the reference [energy](energy-split.md): replacing $U$ by $U+\epsilon_0N$ shifts $\mu_{\rm particle}$ by $\epsilon_0$. A common reference shift cancels from differences for the same [chemical species](chemical-species.md), which are what determine exchange. Thus “energy cost” means a signed increment, not an always positive amount that must be supplied.
