# Focal length

↑ **Parent:** [Lens (optics)](lens-optics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focal_length)

The [focal length](focal-length.md) $f$ specifies the signed distance from an ideal [thin lens](thin-lens.md) to its [optical focus](focus-optics.md) for incident [light rays](light-ray.md) parallel to the [optical axis](optical-axis.md). A converging [thin lens](thin-lens.md) has $f>0$, with a rear [optical focus](focus-optics.md) beyond the [thin lens](thin-lens.md); a diverging [thin lens](thin-lens.md) has $f<0$, with a virtual [optical focus](focus-optics.md) on the incoming side. The [focal length](focal-length.md) is a property of the [optical lens](lens-optics.md) in its specified surroundings, not the image distance for every object. For example, a converging [thin lens](thin-lens.md) with $f=1\,\mathrm m$ images an object $3\,\mathrm m$ away at $1.5\,\mathrm m$, as computed below. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics I, §§27-3–27-4](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_27.html).
