# Continuous periodic functions have a least positive period

↑ **Parent:** [Fundamental period of a function](fundamental-period-of-a-function.md)

For a real-valued [continuous function](continuous-function.md), the [period group of a function](period-group-of-a-function.md) is a [closed subgroup](closed-subgroup.md): if periods $T_n$ tend to $T$, [continuity](continuous-function.md) passes $f(x+T_n)=f(x)$ to the limit. If positive periods could tend to zero, integer multiples of those periods would approach any prescribed displacement $y-x$, forcing $f(y)=f(x)$. A [nonconstant function](nonconstant-function.md) therefore has a positive lower bound for its positive periods. Their [infimum](infimum.md) $T_0$ is itself a period by closedness. Subtracting an integer multiple of $T_0$ from any other period leaves a period in $[0,T_0)$, which must be zero. This proves the stated structure without assuming [differentiability](differentiability.md).
