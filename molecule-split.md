# Molecule

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule)

A [molecule](molecule-split.md), using the [International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry](international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md) (IUPAC) convention, is an electrically neutral entity containing two or more [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Its constituents form a bound unit through [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md); merely drawing a boundary around neighboring particles does not make a [molecule](molecule-split.md). The neutrality requirement concerns total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), not the absence of internal charge separation: [water](water.md) is a [polar molecule](polar-molecule.md) with zero total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md).[Gold Book, fifth edition, molecule: neutral entity with more than one atom; bound-state qualification](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/molecule.md)

One [water](water.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md), $\mathrm{H_2O}$, contains two [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) and one [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md). One [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md), $\mathrm{O_2}$, contains two [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md); a [molecule](molecule-split.md) need not contain different [chemical elements](chemical-element.md). One [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md), $\mathrm{CO_2}$, contains one [carbon](carbon.md) and two [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). The [molecular formula](molecular-formula.md) records these inventories, while [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) describes their arrangement. [Water](water.md) and [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) are [chemical compounds](chemical-compound.md); [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) is a form of one [chemical element](chemical-element.md).

Ordinary crystalline [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), $\mathrm{NaCl}$, contains an extended arrangement of [sodium](sodium.md) [cations](cation.md) $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and [chlorine](chlorine.md) [anions](anion.md) $\mathrm{Cl^-}$. Its [formula unit](formula-unit.md) specifies one of each for compositional accounting, without selecting a unique isolated pair. Bulk neutrality therefore does not imply a material consists of neutral [molecules](molecule-split.md). This statement concerns the [ionic crystal](ionic-crystal.md); the physical state and conditions matter when deciding what particles a [chemical formula](chemical-formula.md) represents.[Ionic Compounds: NaCl composition and the absence of discrete molecules in the ordinary crystal](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/2-6-ionic-and-molecular-compounds.md)

A [polyatomic ion](polyatomic-ion.md) is a charged bound group of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), such as [hydronium](hydronium.md), $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$. A [molecular ion](molecular-ion.md) formed by removing an [electron](electron.md) from intact [water](water.md), $\mathrm{H_2O^{\bullet+}}$, retains the same atom inventory but has net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $+e$. Neither satisfies the neutral-[molecule](molecule-split.md) definition. Distinguishing these entities makes [molecular count and amount of substance](molecular-count-and-amount-of-substance.md) and [mass spectrometry](mass-spectrometry.md) interpretable: say whether the count is of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), neutral [molecules](molecule-split.md), [ions](ion.md), or [formula units](formula-unit.md).

The useful surprise is that a [molecule](molecule-split.md) can be treated as one particle for motion and counting while retaining internal structure. Its [molar mass](molar-mass.md) connects a particle inventory to bulk [mass](mass.md); [molecular rotation](molecular-rotation.md) and [molecular vibration](molecular-vibration.md) reveal internal organization. The [continuum approximation](continuum-approximation.md) uses bulk fields in place of individual molecular positions and motions.
