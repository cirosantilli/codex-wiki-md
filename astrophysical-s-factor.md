# Astrophysical S factor

↑ **Parent:** [Thermonuclear reaction rate](thermonuclear-reaction-rate.md)

The astrophysical S factor separates the dominant [Coulomb barrier](coulomb-barrier.md) suppression from a charged-particle [nuclear reaction cross-section](nuclear-reaction-cross-section.md) $\sigma(E)$:

$$
\sigma(E)=\frac{S(E)}{E}e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}},\qquad E_G=2\mu c^2(\pi\alpha Z_1Z_2)^2.
$$

Here $E$ is relative [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), $\mu$ is [reduced mass](reduced-mass.md), $\alpha$ is the [fine-structure constant](fine-structure-constant.md), and $Z_1,Z_2$ are [atomic numbers](atomic-number.md). The astrophysical S factor has units of [energy](energy-split.md) times area. Factoring out a barrier does not remove all nuclear physics: nuclear levels and reaction mechanisms can still make $S(E)$ vary strongly.

For a dilute nonrelativistic [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution.md) of relative motion, multiplying the relative-energy density by speed and [nuclear reaction cross-section](nuclear-reaction-cross-section.md) cancels the explicit factors of $E$, giving

$$
\langle\sigma v\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\mu}}(k_BT)^{-3/2}\int_0^\infty S(E)e^{-\Phi(E)}\,dE,\qquad \Phi(E)=\frac{E}{k_BT}+\sqrt{\frac{E_G}{E}}.
$$

Thus the [Gamow peak](gamow-peak.md) describes a rate-integral kernel, not a histogram of reacting [stars](star.md) or even the unweighted particle population. [Plasma screening](plasma-screening.md) and departures from the assumed [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution.md) require additional physics.
