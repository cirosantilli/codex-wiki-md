# High-temperature superconductivity

↑ **Parent:** [Superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-temperature_superconductivity)

[High-temperature superconductivity](high-temperature-superconductivity.md) is a historical name for [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md) above earlier metallic benchmarks. The original [Bednorz-Müller experiment](bednorz-muller-experiment.md) operated at tens of [kelvins](kelvin.md), requiring deep cooling; [cuprate superconductors](cuprate-superconductor.md) opened a new materials family.[Part 1, p.426, historical comparison](perovskite-type-oxides-the-new-approach-to-high-tc-superconductivity-by-johannes-georg-bednorz-and-karl-alexander-muller.md)

A higher [superconducting transition temperature](superconducting-transition-temperature.md) enlarges the available operating range, but useful transport also needs a sufficient [superconducting critical current](superconducting-critical-current.md) at the operating [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). In a [ceramic](ceramic.md), connections between grains can limit the whole specimen even when individual grains support [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md). This connects the materials problem to [superconducting weak links](superconducting-weak-link.md) and the [Josephson effect](josephson-effect.md). The [parallel-path model of a superconducting ceramic](parallel-path-model-of-a-superconducting-ceramic.md) below isolates why connectivity matters.
