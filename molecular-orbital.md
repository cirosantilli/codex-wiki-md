# Molecular orbital

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular orbital theory](molecular-orbital-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital)

A [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md) is a one-[electron](electron.md) spatial [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) for the combined [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) of a [molecule](molecule-split.md). For a normalized [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md), the [Born rule](born-rule.md) gives the position [probability density](probability-density.md) $|\psi(\mathbf r)|^2$, with $\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\psi|^2\,d^3\mathbf r=1$. The [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md) is the amplitude, rather than its squared [modulus](modulus.md) or a classical trajectory.

In the independent-orbital approximation, a doubly occupied [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md) contributes $2|\psi|^2$ to the [electron](electron.md) number density, whose [integral](integral-split.md) is two. An empty [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md) contributes zero, even though its shape is still part of the model. The [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) example therefore uses one occupied spatial [molecular orbital](molecular-orbital.md) for two [electrons](electron.md), distinguished by their [spins](spin.md). A negative real [probability amplitude](probability-amplitude.md) means a relative [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) of $\pi$, not positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md); multiplying the whole [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) by $-1$ changes only its [global phase](global-phase.md).
