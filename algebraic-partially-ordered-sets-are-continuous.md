# Algebraic partially ordered sets are continuous

↑ **Parent:** [Algebraic partially ordered set](algebraic-partially-ordered-set.md)

Let $K_x$ be the [directed set](directed-set.md) of [compact elements](compact-element.md) below $x$, with [least upper bound](least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $x$, and put $A_x=\{y:y\ll x\}$. A [compact element](compact-element.md) $k\leq x$ belongs to $A_x$: a [directed set](directed-set.md) whose [least upper bound](least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is at least $x$ also has [least upper bound](least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) at least $k$, so the definition of $k\ll k$ supplies an element above $k$. Hence $K_x\subseteq A_x$.

If $y_1,y_2\in A_x$, apply the [way-below relation](way-below-relation.md) to $K_x$ to find $k_1,k_2\in K_x$ above them. Since $K_x$ is [directed](directed-set.md), it contains a common [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $k$ for $k_1,k_2$. This $k$ belongs to $A_x$, proving that $A_x$ is [directed](directed-set.md). Every member of $A_x$ is below $x$, while $K_x\subseteq A_x$ has [least upper bound](least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $x$. Therefore $x$ is the [least upper bound](least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $A_x$, which is the defining property of a [continuous poset](continuous-poset.md).
