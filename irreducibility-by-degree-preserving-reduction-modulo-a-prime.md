# Irreducibility by degree-preserving reduction modulo a prime

↑ **Parent:** [Irreducible polynomial](irreducible-polynomial.md)

Let $f\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be [primitive polynomial](primitive-polynomial.md) and let $p$ be a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) not dividing its leading coefficient. If its coefficientwise reduction is irreducible in $\mathbb F_p[X]$, then $f$ is irreducible in $\mathbb Q[X]$. Indeed, a rational factorization would give an integer factorization by [Gauss's lemma](gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry.md). The leading coefficients of both factors remain nonzero modulo $p$, so reduction preserves their positive degrees, contradicting irreducibility of the reduction. For monic polynomials the leading-coefficient condition is automatic.
