# Compact group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_group)

A [compact group](compact-group.md) is a [topological group](topological-group-split.md) whose underlying [topological space](topological-space.md) is a [compact Hausdorff space](compact-hausdorff-space.md). The [circle group](circle-group.md) is a [compact group](compact-group.md): the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) is a closed bounded subset of the [complex plane](complex-plane.md), so the [Heine-Borel theorem](heine-borel-theorem.md) gives [compactness](compact-space.md). The additive [group](group-split.md) $(\mathbb R,+)$ is a [locally compact group](locally-compact-group.md) and a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md), but is not a [compact group](compact-group.md): the [open cover](open-cover.md) $\{(-n,n):n\geq1\}$ has no finite subcover. A [finite group](finite-group.md) with the [discrete topology](discrete-space.md) is another [compact group](compact-group.md); [compactness](compact-space.md) does not require a smooth [manifold](topological-manifold-split.md) structure.

The great advantage of a [compact group](compact-group.md) is that one can average over every element without choosing a preferred origin. Precisely, every [locally compact group](locally-compact-group.md) that is a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md) admits a nonzero left [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) $\mu$, a regular [Borel measure](borel-measure.md) finite on [compact sets](compact-space.md), unique up to a positive scalar. Left invariance means $\mu(gA)=\mu(A)$ for every [Borel set](borel-set.md) $A$ and every $g\in G$. There is also a right [Haar measure](haar-measure.md); in general left and right [Haar measures](haar-measure.md) can differ. On a [compact group](compact-group.md), $0<\mu(G)<\infty$, and normalizing to $\mu(G)=1$ gives a unique regular [Borel probability measure](borel-probability-measure.md) invariant under both left and right translation. See [Tornier, Haar Measures, Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10956v1#S2) for existence and uniqueness, and [Ram's notes on left and right Haar measure](https://math.soimeme.org/~arunram/Teaching/1996padicGroupsSydney/TopologicalGroupsAndHaarMeasure.html) for the distinction.

For a [finite group](finite-group.md) $G$, normalized [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) assigns each element mass $1/|G|$, so [integration](integral-split.md) is simply $|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}f(g)$. For the [circle group](circle-group.md), the corresponding [integral](integral-split.md) is uniform angle averaging, computed below. On $(\mathbb R,+)$, [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) is a [Haar measure](haar-measure.md), but its total mass is infinite: no positive scalar multiple makes it a [probability measure](probability-measure.md). Thus the existence of [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) does not make every [topological group](topological-group-split.md) a [compact group](compact-group.md).

Historically, Alfréd Haar introduced the general invariant-measure construction in [Der Massbegriff in der Theorie der kontinuierlichen Gruppen, Annals of Mathematics 34 (1933), pp. 147-169](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968346). This bibliographic identification is also recorded in [André Weil's archived seminar notes, Mesure de Haar, reference Haa33](https://proceedings.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/mbk.21/). The conceptual step is to let [group](group-split.md) symmetry itself determine the weights used in [integration](integral-split.md), rather than relying on coordinates in an ambient [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md).
