# Uniform integrability gives uniform compactness of primitives

↑ **Parent:** [Dunford-Pettis theorem](dunford-pettis-theorem.md)

For a [uniformly integrable](uniform-integrability.md) bounded sequence of derivatives on a compact interval and a fixed initial value, its primitives are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. The increment over any short interval is bounded by the integral of the absolute derivative on that interval. The [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](arzela-ascoli-theorem.md) gives relative uniform [compactness](compact-space.md). If the derivatives converge weakly in $L^1$, tests by interval indicators identify every pointwise limit with $u(x)=\alpha+\int_a^xv$. Hence every convergent subsequence has this same limit and the primitives converge uniformly. The limit belongs to $W^{1,1}$ and retains any common terminal value. [Uniform integrability](uniform-integrability.md) alone gives no essential-supremum bound on its derivative, so this [compactness](compact-space.md) statement does not assert a Lipschitz limit.
