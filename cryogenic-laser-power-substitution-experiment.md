# Cryogenic laser-power substitution experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Cryogenic radiometer](cryogenic-radiometer.md)

In NIST's 2001 comparison, the Laser-Optimized Cryogenic Radiometer held its cavity near $5.4\,\mathrm K$ and controlled the heat sink separately. Opening the beam reduced heater [power](power.md) at fixed cavity [temperature](temperature-split.md); an AC bridge read the [temperature](temperature-split.md). Tests used stabilized, spatially filtered [lasers](laser-split.md) at $488$, $514$ and $633\,\mathrm{nm}$, with horizontal input and a movable [photodiode](photodiode.md) transfer detector in the cavity's optical plane. The paper contrasts this with the High Accuracy Cryogenic Radiometer's optical heating followed by two electrical heating cycles and interpolation.[§§2–3, printed pp. 642–643: apparatus, wavelengths, readout and the two substitution protocols](comparison-of-two-cryogenic-radiometers-at-nist-by-jeanne-m-houston-and-others.md)

The two laboratories compared calibrations of the same transfer detectors, and checked their spatial response. Alignment differences and transported dust limited that comparison; its agreement did not prove universal equivalence. Its correction tables retain window transmission, cavity [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md), alignment, electrical readout and [optical-electrical heating non-equivalence](optical-electrical-heating-non-equivalence.md). The heat sink was controlled, but §3 does not specify its numerical setpoint: the $5.0\,\mathrm K$ reference in the preceding example is stipulated.[§§3–5, printed pp. 643–646, especially Tables 2–3 and spatial-uniformity checks](comparison-of-two-cryogenic-radiometers-at-nist-by-jeanne-m-houston-and-others.md)

For a reproducible substitution measurement, record the beam's [wavelength](wavelength.md) and entrance boundary, receiver and reference [temperatures](temperature-split.md), heater terminal voltage and [electric current](electric-current.md), dark readings before and after illumination, and the settling rule. Check beam collection at the [aperture](aperture.md), window transmission, [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md), deposited lead heating, and sensitivity to beam position. Repeatability alone misses a stable correction error. [Photometric calibration](photometric-calibration.md) can subsequently use this [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) scale, but [photometry](photometry-optics.md) still needs the appropriate spectral visual weighting; the cold receiver does not itself measure [luminous flux](luminous-flux.md).
