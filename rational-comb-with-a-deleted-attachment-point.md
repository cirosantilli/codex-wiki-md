# Rational comb with a deleted attachment point

↑ **Parent:** [Path-connected space](path-connected-space.md)

The displayed subspace of the [Euclidean plane](euclidean-plane.md) is [path-connected](path-connected-space.md): each vertical rational tooth joins the horizontal line at height one. Delete $(r,1)$ for rational $r$. The residual tooth $V=\{r\}\times[0,1)$ cannot be joined by a [path](continuous-path.md) to the top line. Before a path first reaches height one, its first coordinate is a continuous rational-valued function on an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) and must therefore be constant; it would reach the deleted point.

The punctured space remains [connected](connected-space.md). Its portions $L,R$ on either side of $x=r$ are [path-connected](path-connected-space.md), and $V\subseteq\overline L\cap\overline R$ by the [density of the rational numbers](density-of-the-rational-numbers.md). Adjoining [limit points](limit-point.md) to a [connected set](connected-set.md) preserves connectedness: a separation would place the connected subset on one side and prevent the other side from containing its limit points. Hence $L\cup V$ and $R\cup V$ are connected and intersect in $V$. Their union is connected. This example distinguishes [connectedness](connected-space.md) from [path-connected](path-connected-space.md) using the loss of a single attachment point.
