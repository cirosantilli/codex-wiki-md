# X-ray

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray)

[X-rays](x-ray.md) are high-[frequency](frequency.md) [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md), conventionally with [wavelengths](wavelength.md) of roughly $0.01$ to $10\,\mathrm{nm}$. The precise [wavelength](wavelength.md) convention varies with context. Hot accreting [gas](gas.md) and energetic electronic processes can emit [X-rays](x-ray.md), whose short [wavelengths](wavelength.md) make them useful probes of compact objects and [electron shells](electron-shell.md).

For a [photon](photon.md), $E=h\nu=hc/\lambda$, where $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md) and $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). As a calculated example, $\lambda=0.10\,\mathrm{nm}$ gives $E\simeq12.4\,\mathrm{keV}$, using $hc\simeq1.23984\,\mathrm{keV\,nm}$. The same [wavelength](wavelength.md) can describe different production mechanisms: [bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md) produces a continuous [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md), whereas [Characteristic X-rays](characteristic-x-ray.md) arise from discrete electronic changes in [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Measuring transmitted [X-rays](x-ray.md) gives [radiography](radiography.md); measuring their [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) gives [spectroscopy](spectroscopy.md).
