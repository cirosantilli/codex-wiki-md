# Bridge obstruction to regular edge colouring

↑ **Parent:** [Edge chromatic number](edge-chromatic-number.md)

An $r$-[regular graph](regular-graph.md) with $r\geq2$ and a [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) separating an odd number of [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) from the rest cannot have an $r$-colour [proper edge colouring](proper-edge-coloring.md). Such a colouring would partition the [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) into $r$ [perfect matchings](perfect-matching.md). Each [perfect matching](perfect-matching.md) must cross the odd-sided [cut of a graph](cut-graph-theory.md), so each would have to contain its unique crossing [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md), the [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md). An [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) cannot belong to two colour classes.

For odd $r$, every [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) in an $r$-[regular graph](regular-graph.md) automatically has odd-sized sides: if $S$ is either resulting [connected component of a graph](component-graph-theory.md), [double counting](double-counting-proof-technique.md) gives $r|S|=2|E(S)|+1$, so [parity](parity.md) forces $|S|$ odd. Thus an odd-$r$ [regular graph](regular-graph.md) with a [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) is a [class two regular graph](class-two-regular-graph.md).
