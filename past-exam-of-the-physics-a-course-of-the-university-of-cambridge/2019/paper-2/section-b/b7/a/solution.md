Here $x(t)$ is displacement from equilibrium, in metres; $m>0$ is the oscillating [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), in kilograms; $b\geq0$ is the coefficient of linear viscous [damping](../../../../../../damping.md), in $\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$; and $k>0$ is the spring constant, in $\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$. The terms $m\ddot x$, $b\dot x$ and $kx$ are respectively the inertial term, the opposing velocity-dependent damping term and the opposing displacement-dependent restoring term when moved to the [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) side of [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md).

$F_0$ is a possibly complex driving-force [complex amplitude](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md), measured in newtons. $\omega$ is the driving [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md) in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, and taking the real part of $F_0e^{i\omega t}$ gives the physical sinusoidal [force](../../../../../../force-split.md). Its [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) in hertz is $f=\omega/(2\pi)$. The natural undamped [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md) and damping rate are

$$
\boxed{\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m},\qquad\gamma=b/(2m).}
$$

This [damped harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../damped-harmonic-oscillator.md) model assumes a linear restoring [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) and a linear, instantaneous viscous damping law.
