<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $C_R=4R/(1-R)^2$, keeping this coefficient distinct from the [finesse](../../../../../../finesse.md) $\mathcal F$. The transmission has maxima at $\delta=2\pi m$. At a displacement $\epsilon$ from a maximum, its ratio to peak transmission is $[1+C_R\sin^2(\epsilon/2)]^{-1}$. Half maximum therefore requires

$$
\sin^2(\delta_{1/2}/2)=\frac{(1-R)^2}{4R},\qquad
\boxed{\delta_{1/2}=2\arcsin\!\left(\frac{1-R}{2\sqrt R}\right).}
$$

This is the half-width in [wave phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md); the [full width at half maximum](../../../../../../full-width-at-half-maximum.md) is $2\delta_{1/2}$. Successive resonances are $2\pi$ apart, so the exact ideal-model result is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal F=\frac{\pi}{2\arcsin[(1-R)/(2\sqrt R)]}.}
$$

For narrow peaks, $(1-R)/(2\sqrt R)\ll1$, the [small-angle approximation](../../../../../../small-angle-approximation.md) to the inverse sine gives

$$
\delta_{1/2}\simeq\frac{1-R}{\sqrt R},\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal F\simeq\frac{\pi\sqrt R}{1-R}.}
$$

Thus the formula requested in the paper is the high-reflectivity approximation, not an exact identity at arbitrary $R$. Ordinary half-maximum peaks exist only when $(1-R)/(2\sqrt R)\leq1$, or $R\geq3-2\sqrt2$; below that threshold the transmission minimum never reaches half the peak. The prefactor $T^2/(1-R)^2$ cancels from this relative-width calculation.
