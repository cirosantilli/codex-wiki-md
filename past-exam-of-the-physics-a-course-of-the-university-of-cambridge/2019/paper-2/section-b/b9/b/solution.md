<h1 id="section-b/b9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the trial displacement $z=\sin(qy)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$, direct differentiation gives

$$
\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial x^2}=-k^2z,\qquad
\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial y^2}=-q^2z,\qquad
\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial t^2}=-\omega^2z.
$$

The two-dimensional [wave equation](../../../../../../wave-equation-split.md) is therefore satisfied if

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=\frac T\sigma(k^2+q^2)=c_m^2(k^2+q^2),\qquad c_m=\sqrt{T/\sigma}.}
$$

Here $k$ is the longitudinal [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md), $q$ the transverse [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md), and $c_m$ the speed of an unconstrained membrane wave. The physical displacement is the real part, multiplied by an arbitrary displacement amplitude. $T$ has units $\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ and $\sigma$ is [surface mass density](../../../../../../projected-surface-mass-density.md) in $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}$, so $T/\sigma$ has units $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$ as required.
