<h1 id="section-b/b9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The fixed edge at $y=0$ is automatically satisfied by $\sin(qy)$. At $y=a$, the displacement must vanish at every $x,t$, so $\sin(qa)=0$. Thus the allowed transverse [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) have

$$
\boxed{q_n=\frac{n\pi}{a},\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots.}
$$

The case $n=0$ gives zero displacement for this sine solution and is not a physical propagating mode. Negative $n$ only reverses the amplitude sign and does not create a distinct transverse mode. This discrete transverse spectrum is a consequence of the [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md), rather than quantization of the longitudinal [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md) in an indefinitely long guide.
