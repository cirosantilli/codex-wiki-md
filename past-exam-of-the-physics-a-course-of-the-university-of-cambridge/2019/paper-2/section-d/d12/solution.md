<h1 id="section-d/d12/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D12](../d12.md)

A [wave](../../../../../wave-split.md) propagates an organized disturbance through space. What oscillates depends on the medium: displacement in a string or solid, pressure and density in a fluid, [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and [magnetic fields](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) in an [electromagnetic wave](../../../../../electromagnetic-wave-split.md), or a quantum [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md). The propagation law follows from the restoring mechanism, inertia and relevant [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md). A common linear equation is $u_{tt}=c^2\nabla^2u$, whose [plane waves](../../../../../plane-wave.md) obey $\omega=c|\mathbf k|$. The resulting [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) and [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) coincide, so a narrow [wave packet](../../../../../wave-packet.md) can travel without dispersive spreading. More generally the material supplies a [dispersion relation](../../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) $\omega(\mathbf k)$; the envelope velocity is $\nabla_{\mathbf k}\omega$, while constant phase surfaces move at the corresponding [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md).

Mechanical waves make the balance of restoring [force](../../../../../force-split.md) and inertia particularly visible. A flexible stretched string has $c=\sqrt{T/\mu}$: greater [tension](../../../../../tension.md) stiffens the string, whereas greater [linear mass density](../../../../../linear-mass-density.md) slows its transverse motion. Small-amplitude [acoustic waves](../../../../../acoustic-wave.md) in a uniform fluid are longitudinal, with $c_s=\sqrt{K_s/\rho}$, where $K_s$ is the adiabatic [bulk modulus](../../../../../bulk-modulus.md). For an [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), $c_s=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$. Pressure changes in rapid sound oscillations are approximately adiabatic because little heat crosses a [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) in one period. Fluids do not support ordinary bulk shear waves, whereas an elastic solid supports both longitudinal and transverse waves. In an isotropic solid their speeds are $c_L=\sqrt{(K+4G/3)/\rho}$ and $c_T=\sqrt{G/\rho}$, where $G$ is the [shear modulus](../../../../../shear-modulus.md).

A material can be dispersive even when its equilibrium properties are uniform. In a liquid with a free surface, [gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) and [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md) act as restoring mechanisms. A simple inviscid [capillary-gravity wave](../../../../../gravity-capillary-wave.md) has

$$
\omega^2=\left(gk+\frac{\gamma_s}{\rho}k^3\right)\tanh(kh),
$$

where $h$ is depth and $\gamma_s$ is [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md). Long shallow gravity waves have approximately constant speed $\sqrt{gh}$; deep-water gravity waves have $\omega=\sqrt{gk}$ and [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) half their [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md). Short capillary waves have a different dispersion dominated by [surface tension](../../../../../surface-tension-split.md). Bending stiffness similarly changes short-wavelength propagation in a string or plate, giving a contribution proportional to $k^4$ in $\omega^2$. Different restoring mechanisms can therefore control different [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) ranges of the same medium.

[Electromagnetic waves](../../../../../electromagnetic-wave-split.md) propagate in [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) without material displacement, because [Maxwell equations](../../../../../maxwell-equations-split.md) couple changing electric and magnetic fields. They are transverse and travel at $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon_0\mu_0}$. In a simple transparent isotropic material, the phase speed is $1/\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}=c/n$, where $n$ is the [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md). Real material polarization depends on [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md). A [Lorentz oscillator model](../../../../../lorentz-oscillator-model.md) for bound charges explains why [permittivity](../../../../../permittivity-split.md) and [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) become strongly [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) dependent near material resonances, accompanied by absorption. In a birefringent medium, different field polarizations have different propagation constants, permitting [wave plates](../../../../../waveplate.md) and mode-dependent delays. A [electrical conductor](../../../../../electrical-conductor.md) additionally supports dissipative currents; its complex [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) gives attenuation and, for a good conductor, a [skin depth](../../../../../skin-depth.md) approximately $\sqrt{2/(\mu\sigma\omega)}$. [Energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) removed from the wave becomes heat rather than disappearing.

At an interface, [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) is conserved for stationary media, and tangential [wave phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) must match. These conditions give [Snell's law](../../../../../snell-s-law.md) for refraction and constrain reflected and transmitted directions. Amplitudes follow from continuity of the appropriate fields or mechanical forces. On strings the relevant [mechanical impedance](../../../../../mechanical-impedance.md) is $\sqrt{T\mu}$; for sound it is related to $\rho c_s$, and for electromagnetic waves to $\sqrt{\mu/\epsilon}$. A mismatch gives [wave reflection](../../../../../wave-reflection.md), and a lossless boundary conserves the total incident, reflected and transmitted [power](../../../../../power.md). Multiple reflections can reinforce or cancel, as in a [Fabry–Pérot etalon](../../../../../fabry-perot-etalon.md). Total internal reflection and other boundary conditions confine waves in [waveguides](../../../../../waveguide.md). Transverse modes then become discrete, and even an otherwise nondispersive material develops longitudinal cutoffs and [wave dispersion](../../../../../wave-dispersion.md). A membrane strip, for example, obeys $\omega_n^2=c_m^2[k^2+(n\pi/a)^2]$.

Propagation also involves transport and loss. The [Poynting vector](../../../../../poynting-vector.md) gives electromagnetic [energy flux](../../../../../energy-flux.md); mechanical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transport is [force](../../../../../force-split.md) or pressure times velocity, averaged over a cycle. [Wave absorption](../../../../../wave-absorption.md), viscosity, [electrical resistance](../../../../../electrical-resistance.md) and imperfect boundaries dissipate [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Scattering redistributes [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) into other directions and modes, while geometric spreading reduces local intensity without necessarily dissipating anything. These mechanisms should be distinguished when interpreting a decreasing measured amplitude. In strongly absorbing or rapidly varying dispersive regimes, the derivative called [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) is not automatically the speed of a sharp signal front; pulse reshaping and the medium's causal response must be considered.

Finally, quantum [matter waves](../../../../../matter-wave.md) illustrate how the same propagation mathematics can describe a different physical quantity. For a free nonrelativistic particle, $\omega=\hbar k^2/(2m)$, so the [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) $\hbar k/m$ equals its particle velocity and differs from the phase speed $\hbar k/(2m)$. In a periodic crystal, [Bloch's theorem](../../../../../bloch-s-theorem-split.md) replaces the free dispersion by [energy bands](../../../../../energy-band.md). Their slope controls wave-packet velocity, $v_g=\hbar^{-1}dE/dk$, and their curvature gives [band effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md). Thus mechanical confinement, material response and microscopic periodicity all reshape propagation through their allowed modes and dispersion, while the physical interpretation of the wave and its transported quantities remains essential.
