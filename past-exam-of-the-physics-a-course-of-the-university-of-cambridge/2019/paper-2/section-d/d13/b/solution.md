<h1 id="section-d/d13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Semiconductor doping](../../../../../../semiconductor-doping.md) introduces controlled impurities to alter equilibrium carrier populations and [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md). In an [intrinsic semiconductor](../../../../../../intrinsic-semiconductor.md), thermally excited [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) in the [conduction band](../../../../../../conduction-band.md) and [electron holes](../../../../../../electron-hole.md) in the [valence band](../../../../../../valence-band.md) have equal densities $n=p=n_i$. A shallow [donor impurity](../../../../../../donor-impurity.md), such as phosphorus in silicon, supplies an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) from an impurity level just below the conduction-band edge. Once ionized it is positively charged, and the material is n type. An [acceptor impurity](../../../../../../acceptor-impurity.md), such as boron in silicon, accepts a valence [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) into a level just above the valence-band edge, leaving a mobile hole and a negatively charged ionized impurity; the material is p type.

With dopant densities $N_D,N_A$, charge neutrality requires

$$
p+N_D^+=n+N_A^-.
$$

For fully ionized, nondegenerate dopants at equilibrium, the [semiconductor mass-action law](../../../../../../semiconductor-mass-action-law.md) gives $np=n_i^2$. In a substantially n-type sample, $N_D-N_A\gg n_i$, so

$$
\boxed{n\simeq N_D-N_A,\qquad p\simeq\frac{n_i^2}{N_D-N_A}.}
$$

The corresponding p-type result is $p\simeq N_A-N_D$ and $n\simeq n_i^2/(N_A-N_D)$. The majority population grows while the minority population decreases; adding donors does not simply add equal numbers of free [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and holes.

The [Fermi energy](../../../../../../fermi-energy.md) shifts toward the conduction band for n-type material and toward the valence band for p-type material. In the nondegenerate approximation,

$$
n=N_Ce^{-(E_C-E_F)/(k_BT)},\qquad p=N_Ve^{-(E_F-E_V)/(k_BT)},
$$

where $N_C,N_V$ are effective band-edge state densities. At very low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), dopants may not be ionized and carrier freeze-out occurs. At intermediate temperatures, ionized dopants dominate the carrier density; at sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), intrinsic excitation can dominate. Heavy doping can produce degenerate carrier statistics, invalidating these Boltzmann expressions.

The [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) is $\boxed{\sigma=e(n\mu_e+p\mu_h)}$. Doping usually raises the majority carrier density, but ionized impurity scattering can lower mobility, so conductivity need not scale indefinitely with dopant concentration. Joining p-type and n-type regions creates a [p-n junction](../../../../../../p-n-junction.md), with a [depletion region](../../../../../../depletion-region.md) and built-in [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) established by carrier diffusion and compensating drift. The fixed ionized dopants, not an absence of all charge, account for the depleted region's net [charge density](../../../../../../charge-density.md).
