<h1 id="section-d/d13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Hall effect](../../../../../../hall-effect.md) is a transverse electric response to a magnetic [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) on moving [charge carriers](../../../../../../charge-carrier.md). Take conventional [current density](../../../../../../current-density.md) $\mathbf J=J_x\widehat{\mathbf x}$ and [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B=B_z\widehat{\mathbf z}$ in a conducting slab. For one carrier type of [number density](../../../../../../number-density.md) $n$ and signed [electric charge](../../../../../../electric-charge-split.md) $q$, the [drift velocity](../../../../../../drift-velocity.md) is $\mathbf v_d=\mathbf J/(nq)$. Carriers initially deflect sideways until a Hall [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) balances the magnetic [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md):

$$
q(\mathbf E_H+\mathbf v_d\times\mathbf B)=0,\qquad
E_y=\frac{J_xB_z}{nq}.
$$

The [Hall coefficient](../../../../../../hall-coefficient.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{R_H=\frac{E_y}{J_xB_z}=\frac1{nq}.}
$$

For [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) it is $-1/(ne)$, whereas a simple hole-dominated conductor gives $+1/(pe)$. The sign reveals the effective carrier sign; the magnitude estimates their density in the one-carrier approximation.

For width $w$ along $y$ and thickness $t$ along $z$, $I=J_xwt$. Define the signed [Hall voltage](../../../../../../hall-voltage.md) by $V_H=E_yw$, namely the electric potential at the lower-$y$ edge minus that at the higher-$y$ edge. Then

$$
\boxed{V_H=\frac{IB_z}{nqt},\qquad n=\frac{|IB_z|}{e t|V_H|}\quad(|q|=e).}
$$

This explicit contact convention fixes the voltage sign. A [Hall bar](../../../../../../hall-bar.md) with longitudinal and transverse contacts measures both [electrical resistivity](../../../../../../electrical-resistivity.md) and the Hall response. Reverse the field and take the part of the transverse voltage odd in $B_z$ to suppress longitudinal-voltage pickup from misaligned contacts; reversing current provides another useful check.

A Hall measurement is useful for carrier identification, semiconductor doping measurements and magnetic-field sensing. Combining a one-carrier $R_H$ with [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) gives a mobility magnitude $\mu=\sigma|R_H|$. However, mixed [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) and hole conduction is not described by a single density: in weak fields,

$$
R_H=\frac{p\mu_h^2-n\mu_e^2}{e(p\mu_h+n\mu_e)^2}.
$$

The response weights mobilities differently from ordinary conductivity. Complicated [energy bands](../../../../../../energy-band.md), anisotropic [Fermi surfaces](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) and multiple bands can likewise change the simple interpretation in metals; a positive Hall coefficient does not mean that the material contains positively charged fundamental [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md).
