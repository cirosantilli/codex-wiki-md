A [Bravais lattice](../../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is the set of positions generated by integer combinations of independent primitive translation vectors, $\mathbf R=n_1\mathbf a_1+n_2\mathbf a_2+n_3\mathbf a_3$. Every lattice point has the same translational environment. A crystal may additionally have a basis of several atoms attached to each lattice point; those atoms are not automatically separate points of its [Bravais lattice](../../../../../../bravais-lattice.md).

A [unit cell](../../../../../../unit-cell.md) is a region whose translated copies fill the crystal without gaps or overlaps. It can be chosen for convenient symmetry, and a conventional cell may contain several lattice points. A [primitive unit cell](../../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) is a smallest such repeating cell, containing exactly one lattice point when boundary sharing is counted. Its shape is not unique, but its [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) $\Omega=|\mathbf a_1\cdot(\mathbf a_2\times\mathbf a_3)|$ is fixed for a given lattice. For a monatomic Bravais crystal, each primitive cell contains one atom.
