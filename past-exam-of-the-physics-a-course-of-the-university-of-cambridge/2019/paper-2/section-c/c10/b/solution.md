<h1 id="section-c/c10/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Interpret the sphere as an atom of [iron](../../../../../../iron.md), and assume neighbouring atoms touch without overlap. Its radius is $r=0.124\,\mathrm{nm}=1.24\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm m$, and its [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is $m_{\rm Fe}=56u\simeq9.30\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}$, using atomic mass unit $u=1.66054\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

In the [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md), atoms touch along a face diagonal: $\sqrt2a_F=4r$, so $a_F=2\sqrt2r=0.3507\,\mathrm{nm}$. Four atoms occupy a conventional cube. The [hard-sphere atomic volume in cubic lattices](../../../../../../hard-sphere-atomic-volume-in-cubic-lattices.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\Omega_F=\frac{a_F^3}{4}=4\sqrt2r^3=1.08\times10^{-29}\,\mathrm{m^3},\qquad
\rho_F=\frac{56u}{\Omega_F}=8.62\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

In the [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md), atoms touch along the body diagonal: $\sqrt3a_B=4r$, so $a_B=4r/\sqrt3=0.2864\,\mathrm{nm}$. There are two atoms per conventional cube, giving

$$
\boxed{\Omega_B=\frac{a_B^3}{2}=\frac{32r^3}{3\sqrt3}=1.17\times10^{-29}\,\mathrm{m^3},\qquad
\rho_B=7.92\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.}
$$

These are [primitive unit cell](../../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) volumes, not the volumes of the conventional cubes or of the atomic spheres. The denser FCC contact packing gives the higher [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) in this fixed-radius model. Actual hot phases have temperature-dependent dimensions and bonding, so these numbers should not be treated as exact experimental phase densities.
