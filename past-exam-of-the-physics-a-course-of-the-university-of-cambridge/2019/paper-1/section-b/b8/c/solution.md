<h1 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose the two real [energy eigenstates](../../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) in part (a) and take their coefficients to be in phase initially. A different initial relative phase only shifts the oscillation in time. The normalized [quantum superposition](../../../../../../quantum-superposition.md) is

$$
\Psi(x,t)=\frac{\psi_1(x)e^{-iE_1t/\hbar}+\psi_2(x)e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

[Orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) removes the cross terms in the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md), so

$$
\boxed{\langle E\rangle=\frac{E_1+E_2}{2}=\frac{5\pi^2\hbar^2}{4mL^2}.}
$$

For [position](../../../../../../position.md), the diagonal [matrix elements](../../../../../../matrix-element.md) equal $L/2$ by reflection symmetry, but the off-diagonal element is nonzero. Using the product-to-sum identity and $\int_0^Lx\cos(j\pi x/L)\,dx=L^2[(-1)^j-1]/(j^2\pi^2)$ gives

$$
x_{12}=\frac2L\int_0^Lx\sin\frac{\pi x}L\sin\frac{2\pi x}L\,dx=-\frac{16L}{9\pi^2}.
$$

Therefore the [equal superposition of the first two box states](../../../../../../equal-superposition-of-the-first-two-box-states.md) has

$$
\boxed{\langle x\rangle=\frac L2-\frac{16L}{9\pi^2}\cos\Omega t,\quad \Omega=\frac{E_2-E_1}{\hbar}=\frac{3\pi^2\hbar}{2mL^2}.}
$$

The [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is $\boxed{16L/(9\pi^2)\simeq0.1801L}$, and the ordinary [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) is $\boxed{f=\Omega/(2\pi)=3h/(8mL^2)}$. The motion of the mean [position](../../../../../../position.md) is an interference effect between distinct energies, while their populations, and hence the mean [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), remain fixed.
