<h1 id="section-b/b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) of finding the electron in a spherical shell is $P(r)\,dr=4\pi r^2|\psi(r)|^2dr$, so

$$
P(r)=4\beta^3r^2e^{-2\beta r}.
$$

This [radial probability density](../../../../../../radial-probability-density.md), rather than the density per unit volume, determines the most probable radius. Its [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md) is

$$
\langle r\rangle=4\beta^3\int_0^\infty r^3e^{-2\beta r}\,dr=\boxed{\frac{3}{2\beta}=\frac{3a_0}{2Z}.}
$$

For its interior maximum, differentiate $\log P=\text{constant}+2\log r-2\beta r$. The stationary condition $2/r-2\beta=0$ gives

$$
\boxed{r_{\mathrm{mode}}=\frac1\beta=\frac{a_0}{Z}.}
$$

The density per unit volume $|\psi|^2$ is instead largest at the nucleus; including the spherical-shell volume factor is essential to the requested radial [mode](../../../../../../mode-statistics.md).
