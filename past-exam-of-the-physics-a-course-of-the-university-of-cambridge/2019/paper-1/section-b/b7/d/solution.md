<h1 id="section-b/b7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The nuclear [beta decay](../../../../../../beta-decay.md) is fast compared with the electronic orbital timescale. In the [sudden approximation](../../../../../../sudden-approximation.md), the electronic [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) does not have time to change during the increase from $Z_i=1$ to $Z_f=2$. The subsequent [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) of a particular daughter [energy eigenstate](../../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) is therefore its squared overlap with the initial state.

For the final $1s$ state, write $\beta_i=1/a_0$ and $\beta_f=2/a_0$. The [sudden hydrogenic charge change](../../../../../../sudden-hydrogenic-charge-change.md) gives the [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md)

$$
A_{1s}=\int\psi_f^*\psi_i\,d^3r=4(\beta_i\beta_f)^{3/2}\int_0^\infty r^2e^{-(\beta_i+\beta_f)r}\,dr=\frac{8(\beta_i\beta_f)^{3/2}}{(\beta_i+\beta_f)^3}=\frac{16\sqrt2}{27}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{P_{1s}=|A_{1s}|^2=\frac{512}{729}\simeq0.702.}
$$

The final $2p$ states have [spherical harmonics](../../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) $Y_{1m}$, whereas the initial state is proportional to $Y_{00}$. Their angular overlap $\int Y_{1m}^*Y_{00}\,d\Omega$ vanishes by [orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md). Accordingly $\boxed{P_{2p}=0}$ in the ideal spherically symmetric sudden-change model. Nuclear recoil or interactions with the emitted particles can produce small corrections. This is an overlap [selection rule](../../../../../../selection-rule.md) for a sudden change, not a photon-induced [electric dipole transition](../../../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md).
