<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The rotational [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $K=L^2/(2I)$. For a three-dimensional [linear rigid rotor](../../../../../linear-rigid-rotor.md), [angular momentum operator](../../../../../angular-momentum-operator-split.md) replaces the squared magnitude by $\hbar^2l(l+1)$, where $l=0,1,2,\ldots$. Hence

$$
E_l=\frac{\hbar^2}{2I}l(l+1),\qquad \frac{\hbar^2}{I}=1.1121\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm J=6.941\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

The three lowest distinct [energy levels](../../../../../energy-level.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{E_0=0,\qquad E_1=6.94\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad E_2=2.08\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$

Their magnetic [quantum degeneracy](../../../../../degenerate-energy-levels.md) values are $1,3,5$. A single moment of inertia specifies this [rigid rotor](../../../../../rigid-rotor.md) model; an arbitrary asymmetric body would require its other principal moments.
