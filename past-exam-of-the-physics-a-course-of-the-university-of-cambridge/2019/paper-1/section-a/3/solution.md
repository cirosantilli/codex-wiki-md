<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Inside the barrier, the stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) gives an exponentially decaying [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) with inverse penetration length

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m_e(V_0-E)}}\hbar=7.245\times10^9\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}.
$$

The [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) falls approximately as $e^{-\kappa a}$, so the leading exponential estimate for the [transmission probability](../../../../../transmission-probability.md) is

$$
\boxed{T\sim e^{-2\kappa a}=e^{-14.49}=5.09\times10^{-7}.}
$$

For comparison, [wavefunction matching at a finite potential step](../../../../../wavefunction-matching-at-a-finite-potential-step.md) at both edges gives the [rectangular-barrier tunnelling probability](../../../../../rectangular-barrier-tunnelling-probability.md)

$$
T=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}=1.30\times10^{-6}.
$$

The difference is the interface prefactor: for an opaque barrier it is $16E(V_0-E)/V_0^2=2.56$. Both give a transmission of order $10^{-6}$; the first is the requested exponential estimate.
