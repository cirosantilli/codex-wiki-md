<h1 id="section-d/d12/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [ideal operational amplifier](../../../../../../ideal-operational-amplifier.md) has infinite differential [open-loop gain](../../../../../../open-loop-gain.md), infinite [input impedance](../../../../../../input-impedance.md), zero [output impedance](../../../../../../output-impedance.md), unlimited [bandwidth](../../../../../../bandwidth-signal-processing.md) and [slew rate](../../../../../../slew-rate.md), and zero [input offset voltage](../../../../../../input-offset-voltage.md), [input bias current](../../../../../../input-bias-current.md) and intrinsic [noise](../../../../../../noise-signal-processing.md). The idealized model has no finite output-swing or output-current limit.

The usual circuit rules are

$$
\boxed{i_+=i_-=0,\qquad v_+=v_-\ \text{under stable, unsaturated negative feedback}.}
$$

The first follows from infinite [input impedance](../../../../../../input-impedance.md). The second follows because a finite output requires $v_+-v_-=v_o/A\to0$ as the [open-loop gain](../../../../../../open-loop-gain.md) tends to infinity. It is a virtual equality of potentials, not a conducting connection between the inputs. It cannot be imposed for open-loop operation or after an amplifier reaches its supply rails.
