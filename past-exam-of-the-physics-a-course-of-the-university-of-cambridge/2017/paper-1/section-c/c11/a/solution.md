<h1 id="section-c/c11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Bohr model](../../../../../../bohr-model.md) combines classical circular motion in the [Coulomb potential](../../../../../../coulomb-potential-energy.md) with explicitly quantum postulates. A one-electron atom of nuclear charge $+Ze$ has electron–nucleus [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$. With $C=Ze^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$, the relative circular orbit obeys

$$
\frac{\mu v^2}{r}=\frac C{r^2},\qquad L=\mu vr.
$$

Bohr postulates permitted nonradiating stationary orbits with $L=n\hbar$, for $n=1,2,\ldots$. These assumptions give

$$
\boxed{r_n=\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{\mu C}
=\frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2}{\mu Ze^2}n^2.}
$$

For hydrogen with the nucleus taken infinitely heavy, the $n=1$ radius is the [Bohr radius](../../../../../../bohr-radius.md), $a_0\simeq5.29\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m$. The finite [reduced mass](../../../../../../reduced-mass.md) gives the small nuclear-motion correction, and increasing $Z$ contracts the one-electron orbit.

The circular-motion equation gives $T=C/(2r)$ while the [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) is $-C/r$, so the total [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is $-C/(2r)$. Substituting the quantized radius yields

$$
\boxed{E_n=-\frac{\mu C^2}{2\hbar^2n^2}
=-\frac{\mu Z^2e^4}{2(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^2\hbar^2n^2}.}
$$

For the heavy-nucleus hydrogen approximation, $E_n\simeq-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}/n^2$. The ionization threshold is zero [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), so removing the ground-state electron requires about $13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$.

The model further postulates that radiation is emitted or absorbed only in transitions between stationary orbits, with [photon energy](../../../../../../photon-energy.md) $h\nu=|E_i-E_f|$. Emission from $n_i>n_f$ gives

$$
\frac1\lambda=R_\mu Z^2\left(\frac1{n_f^2}-\frac1{n_i^2}\right),\qquad
R_\mu=\frac{\mu e^4}{8\varepsilon_0^2h^3c}.
$$

Thus it reproduces the principal [hydrogen spectral series](../../../../../../hydrogen-spectral-series.md), their limiting [wavelengths](../../../../../../wavelength.md), and the gross hydrogenic [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) levels. For example, $n=3\to2$ in hydrogen has $\Delta E\simeq1.89\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\lambda\simeq656\,\mathrm{nm}$, a visible line. Absorption reverses the transition when the photon matches the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gap.

The achievement is that quantized energies explain discrete spectra, but the model's stationary orbits are postulates rather than consequences of classical electrodynamics: an accelerating classical charge would radiate. It does not provide a general calculation of line intensities or transition selection rules and fails as a quantitative theory of many-electron atoms. [Spin](../../../../../../spin.md), [fine structure](../../../../../../fine-structure.md) and the full [Zeeman effect](../../../../../../zeeman-splitting.md) require further physics. Modern [quantum mechanics](../../../../../../quantum-mechanics-split.md) replaces definite electron orbits by [wavefunctions](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) and quantum probabilities. In particular, the true hydrogen [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) has $\ell=0$, so its [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) vanishes; the model's rule $L=n\hbar$ should not be identified with the modern orbital [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) rule $L^2=\ell(\ell+1)\hbar^2$. **Bohr's [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) quantization remains a successful hydrogenic approximation, while its literal orbital picture does not.**
