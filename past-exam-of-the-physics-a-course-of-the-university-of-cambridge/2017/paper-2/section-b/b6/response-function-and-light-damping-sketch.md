<h1 id="section-b/b6/response-function-and-light-damping-sketch">Response function and light-damping sketch</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Use the $e^{i\omega t}$ convention for the harmonic [force](../../../../../force-split.md) and displacement [complex amplitudes](../../../../../complex-amplitude.md). Substitution in the [forced harmonic oscillator](../../../../../forced-harmonic-oscillator.md) equation gives

$$
(k-m\omega^2+ib\omega)x_0=F_0,\qquad
\boxed{R(\omega)=\frac1{k-m\omega^2+ib\omega}}.
$$

Its magnitude and [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) relative to the [force](../../../../../force-split.md) are

$$
|R|=\frac1{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+b^2\omega^2}},\qquad
\phi=-\operatorname{atan2}(b\omega,k-m\omega^2).
$$

At zero [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), $|R|=1/k$ and $\phi=0$. At $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$, $\phi=-\pi/2$; at high [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) the [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) approaches $-\pi$. For light [viscous damping](../../../../../viscous-damping.md), the [quality factor](../../../../../q-factor.md) $Q=m\omega_0/b$ is large and there is a tall narrow displacement peak close to $\omega_0$.
