<h1 id="section-b/b7/which-wavelength-enters-the-derivative-formula">Which wavelength enters the derivative formula</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

For the _wavelength in the medium_, $\lambda_m=2\pi/k$, differentiate $\omega=kv_p(k)$:

$$
v_g=v_p+k\frac{dv_p}{dk}
=v_p+k\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda_m}\left(-\frac{\lambda_m}{k}\right),\qquad
\boxed{v_g=v_p-\lambda_m\frac{dv_p}{d\lambda_m}}.
$$

The distinction in [group velocity in material and vacuum wavelength coordinates](../../../../../group-velocity-in-material-and-vacuum-wavelength-coordinates.md) is essential for the numerical data. For incident _vacuum_ wavelength $\lambda_0=2\pi c/\omega$, differentiation of $k=\omega/v_p(\lambda_0)$ instead gives

$$
\boxed{v_g=\frac{v_p}{1+(\lambda_0/v_p)\,dv_p/d\lambda_0}
=\frac{c}{n-\lambda_0\,dn/d\lambda_0}}.
$$

Thus the printed derivative identity is valid, but its wavelength coordinate cannot be carried unchanged into data specified before refraction.
