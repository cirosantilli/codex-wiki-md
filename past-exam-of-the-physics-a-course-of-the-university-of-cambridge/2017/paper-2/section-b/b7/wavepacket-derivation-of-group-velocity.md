<h1 id="section-b/b7/wavepacket-derivation-of-group-velocity">Wavepacket derivation of group velocity</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Superpose two nearby harmonic waves. The identity

$$
\cos(k_1x-\omega_1t)+\cos(k_2x-\omega_2t)
=2\cos\!\left(\frac{\Delta k\,x-\Delta\omega\,t}{2}\right)
\cos(\bar kx-\bar\omega t)
$$

separates a rapid carrier from a slow beat envelope. A constant point of the envelope moves at $\Delta\omega/\Delta k$, which tends to

$$
\boxed{v_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}}
$$

as the components approach one another. Equivalently, expand a narrow [wave packet](../../../../../wave-packet.md) spectrum around $k_0$: $\omega(k)=\omega_0+v_g(k-k_0)+\cdots$. Factoring the carrier out of its [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) representation leaves an envelope depending on $x-v_gt$. This interpretation requires a narrow spectrum and little attenuation or distortion; higher derivatives of the [dispersion relation](../../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) can change the envelope shape. The carrier [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) is $v_p=\omega/k$ and need not equal $v_g$.
