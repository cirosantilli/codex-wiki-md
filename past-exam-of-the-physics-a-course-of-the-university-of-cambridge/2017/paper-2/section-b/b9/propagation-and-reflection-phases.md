<h1 id="section-b/b9/propagation-and-reflection-phases">Propagation and reflection phases</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Let the first ray reflect at the upper surface and the second traverse the film, reflect below, and emerge at a laterally displaced point. Its internal round-trip geometric length is $2d/\cos\theta$, but the external path to a common outgoing [wavefront](../../../../../wavefront.md) must also be included. The lateral separation is $2d\tan\theta$, whose projection along the external ray is $2d\tan\theta\sin\theta_i$. The net [optical path difference](../../../../../optical-path-difference.md) is therefore

$$
\Delta\ell=\frac{2nd}{\cos\theta}-2d\tan\theta\sin\theta_i
=\frac{2nd(1-\sin^2\theta)}{\cos\theta}=2nd\cos\theta,
$$

using [Snell's law](../../../../../snell-s-law.md) $\sin\theta_i=n\sin\theta$. Multiplying by the vacuum [wavenumber](../../../../../wavenumber.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\delta=\frac{4\pi nd}{\lambda}\cos\theta}.
$$

For the usual index ordering $1<n<n_2$, including the stated later value $n=1.2$, both reflections are towards higher [refractive index](../../../../../refractive-index.md) and both reverse the electric-field sign. Their relative reflection [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is therefore zero; $\delta$ is also their total relative [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) modulo $2\pi$. A different index ordering or polarization-dependent reflection [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) would require that additional term.
