<h1 id="section-b/b9/nonuniform-oil-film-in-monochromatic-light">Nonuniform oil film in monochromatic light</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

At normal incidence, the propagation [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) is $4\pi n_{\rm oil}d(x,y)/\lambda$. Different film thicknesses therefore produce bright and dark contours of equal thickness, with adjacent same-type fringes differing by $\boxed{\Delta d=\lambda/(2n_{\rm oil})}$. Under monochromatic illumination these are intensity variations of the incident colour, rather than a rainbow.

For the usual case $n_{\rm oil}>n_{\rm water}>1$, reflection at air-oil reverses [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) while reflection at oil-water does not. The additional relative $\pi$ makes reflected minima occur at $2n_{\rm oil}d=m\lambda$ and maxima at $2n_{\rm oil}d=(m+1/2)\lambda$. If the oil index were below the water index, the phase-reversal pattern would change and these conditions would interchange. The thinnest regions need not be completely dark because the two reflected amplitudes need not match; at vanishing thickness the residual reflection approaches that of the air-water boundary. Surface motion changes the thickness contours and moves the fringes.
