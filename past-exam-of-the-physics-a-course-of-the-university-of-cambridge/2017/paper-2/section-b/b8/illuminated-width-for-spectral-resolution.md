<h1 id="section-b/b8/illuminated-width-for-spectral-resolution">Illuminated width for spectral resolution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Near order $m$, the first angular zero from a principal maximum is $\delta\theta=\lambda/(ND\cos\theta)$. Two wavelengths have peak separation $\Delta\theta=m\Delta\lambda/(D\cos\theta)$. Equating them applies the [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) and gives the [resolving power of a diffraction grating](../../../../../resolving-power-of-a-diffraction-grating.md),

$$
\boxed{\frac\lambda{\Delta\lambda}=mN}.
$$

Here the required resolving [power](../../../../../power.md) is $400/0.2=2000$, so in second order $N\geq1000$. The illuminated width is therefore

$$
\boxed{W\simeq ND=2.00\,\mathrm{mm}}.
$$

Whether one counts the full illuminated window or the first-to-last slit-centre distance changes the width by one pitch and is immaterial at this precision. The result assumes negligible aberrations and adequate slit illumination and coherence.
