<h1 id="section-b/b8/detector-pixels-and-optical-layout">Detector pixels and optical layout</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

The angular dispersion at the chosen order is

$$
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}=\frac m{D\cos\theta},\qquad
\Delta\theta=2.182\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$

For a collecting lens with its axis directed along the selected second-order beam, the local focal-plane separation is

$$
\Delta x\simeq f\Delta\theta=\boxed{54.6\,\mathrm{\mu m}}.
$$

A basic one-pixel separation criterion is therefore $\boxed{t\lesssim55\,\mathrm{\mu m}}$. A more reliable two-pixels-per-resolution-interval choice is $\boxed{t\lesssim27\,\mathrm{\mu m}}$. Finite pixels integrate the pattern, so sampling and pixel response should not degrade the grating's optical resolution.

This [pixel sampling of a grating spectrum](../../../../../pixel-sampling-of-a-grating-spectrum.md) depends on the layout and criterion, neither of which is fully specified. If the detector is instead described by $x=f\tan\theta$ relative to the incident-axis coordinate, the derivative contains an additional $\sec^2\theta$ and gives $65.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ separation, or about $32\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ for two-pixel sampling. The more approximate paraxial mapping $x\simeq fm\lambda/D$ gives $50\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. These are stated alternatives rather than one falsely precise universal pixel width.
