<h1 id="section-d/d13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [band effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) describes the acceleration of an electronic wavepacket in a periodic lattice through the local [band curvature](../../../../../../band-curvature.md). It incorporates the periodic lattice potential into a useful transport parameter without changing the bare [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). For a [Bloch state](../../../../../../bloch-state.md) in an isolated band,

$$
\mathbf v_g=\frac1\hbar\nabla_{\mathbf k}\epsilon(\mathbf k),\qquad
\hbar\dot{\mathbf k}=\mathbf F.
$$

Differentiation gives

$$
a_i=\sum_j(m^{*-1})_{ij}F_j,\qquad
\boxed{(m^{*-1})_{ij}=\frac1{\hbar^2}\frac{\partial^2\epsilon}{\partial k_i\partial k_j}}.
$$

In one dimension this becomes $\boxed{m^*=\hbar^2/(d^2\epsilon/dk^2)}$. A locally parabolic isotropic band minimum has $\epsilon\simeq\epsilon_c+\hbar^2|\mathbf k-\mathbf k_c|^2/(2m^*)$ with positive $m^*$. A flatter band has a larger [effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md). At a band maximum the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) curvature is negative, so the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) accelerates oppositely to the applied [force](../../../../../../force-split.md). Describing the missing [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) as a positive-charge [electron hole](../../../../../../electron-hole.md) with positive [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is often simpler.

In the scalar relaxation-time approximation, a conduction [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) in an electric field reaches $\mathbf v_d=-e\tau\mathbf E/m^*$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mu_e=\frac{e\tau}{m^*}},\qquad
\boxed{\sigma=ne\mu_e=\frac{ne^2\tau}{m^*}}.
$$

For a crystal with different directional curvatures, conductivity is a tensor and involves the occupied states and their scattering. Contributions from [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and holes add: $\sigma=e(n\mu_e+p\mu_h)$ in an isotropic two-carrier description. A filled band contributes no ordinary longitudinal current because opposite group velocities cancel; a partially filled band or holes permit a current response.

[Effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) therefore connects measured electrical transport to [electronic band structure](../../../../../../electronic-band-structure.md), but [carrier density](../../../../../../carrier-density.md) and [momentum relaxation time](../../../../../../momentum-relaxation-time.md) matter as much as curvature. Ionised impurities and [phonons](../../../../../../phonon.md) alter $\tau$. A smaller [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) does not guarantee a higher conductivity if carrier population or scattering changes. Nor are all reported masses identical: density-of-states, cyclotron and conductivity masses weight anisotropic bands differently. The simple semiclassical formula assumes an isolated band, slowly varying weak fields and negligible interband transitions; near degeneracies or strong fields a single constant scalar [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) is inadequate.
