<h1 id="section-c/c10/acoustic-and-optical-branches">Acoustic and optical branches</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

The minus solution is the [acoustic phonon branch](../../../../../acoustic-phonon-branch.md). As $q\to0$, $\omega_-$ tends to zero and $U=V$: adjacent atoms move together in a long-wavelength compression. The plus solution is the [optical phonon branch](../../../../../optical-phonon-branch.md), with nonzero zone-centre [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) and $m_AU+m_BV=0$: the two sublattices move against one another with fixed cell centre of [mass](../../../../../mass.md). For oppositely charged ions, this relative displacement can couple to an electric field, motivating the optical name.

At the zone boundary the frequencies are $\sqrt{2\alpha/m_{\rm heavy}}$ and $\sqrt{2\alpha/m_{\rm light}}$. Different masses produce a gap; equal masses make the two branches meet, recovering a folded monatomic dispersion.
