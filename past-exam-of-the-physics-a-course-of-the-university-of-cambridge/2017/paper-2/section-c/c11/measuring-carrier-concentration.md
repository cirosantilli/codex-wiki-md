<h1 id="section-c/c11/measuring-carrier-concentration">Measuring carrier concentration</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

Use a [Hall effect](../../../../../hall-effect.md) measurement. Pass a known current $I$ through a thin rectangular specimen, apply a perpendicular magnetic field $B$, and measure the transverse [Hall voltage](../../../../../hall-voltage.md) $V_H$. For a uniform single-carrier sample of thickness $t$,

$$
R_H=\frac{V_Ht}{IB},\qquad
\boxed{n=\frac1{e|R_H|}=\frac{IB}{et|V_H|}}.
$$

The Hall-voltage sign identifies the carrier sign. Reverse $B$ and the current to separate the field-odd Hall signal from voltage-probe misalignment and offsets, and measure the specimen thickness accurately. The extraction assumes a one-carrier Hall factor near one. In intrinsic or mixed-carrier material, the [two-carrier Hall coefficient](../../../../../two-carrier-hall-coefficient.md) is mobility weighted, so Hall voltage and conductivity need a two-carrier interpretation rather than this simple inverse formula.
