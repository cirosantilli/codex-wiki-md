<h1 id="section-c/c11/donor-binding-energy-size-and-thermal-ionisation">Donor binding energy, size and thermal ionisation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Solution](solution.md)

For a [hydrogenic donor state](../../../../../hydrogenic-donor-state.md), replace the bare [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md) by $m^*=0.1m_e$ and the Coulomb permittivity by $\epsilon_r\epsilon_0$ with $\epsilon_r=16$. Rescaling the [Bohr model](../../../../../bohr-model.md) gives the positive ground-state binding magnitude

$$
\boxed{E_B=13.6057\,\mathrm{eV}\frac{0.1}{16^2}=5.31\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV}=5.31\,\mathrm{meV}}.
$$

The radius is

$$
\boxed{a_B^*=a_0\frac{16}{0.1}=8.47\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm m=8.47\,\mathrm{nm}},
$$

using the [Bohr radius](../../../../../bohr-radius.md) $a_0=5.29177\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm m$. The supplied positive hydrogen “[energy](../../../../../energy-split.md)” formula should be interpreted as a binding magnitude; the bound level's [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) relative to the conduction-band edge is $-E_B$.

At $300\,\mathrm K$, $k_BT=25.9\,\mathrm{meV}$, about five times the binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Thus **a substantial fraction, ordinarily most of the shallow donors in the dilute nondegenerate regime, will be ionised**. An exact fraction also depends on the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md), donor concentration and degeneracies; comparing $E_B$ with $k_BT$ alone does not determine it exactly. The large radius explains why a weakly bound donor extends across many unit cells.
