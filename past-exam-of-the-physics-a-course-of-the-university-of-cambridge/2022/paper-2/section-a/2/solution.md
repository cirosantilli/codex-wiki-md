<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The components are in [phase quadrature](../../../../../phase-quadrature.md) when the axes of the [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) coincide with the coordinate axes. Since the $x$ [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is one and the $y$ [wave amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) is two, the [Jones vector](../../../../../jones-vector.md) must have $A=\pm2i$.

A handedness convention is essential. Adopt the optical convention in which right-handed [elliptical polarization](../../../../../elliptical-polarization.md) rotates clockwise when viewed toward the source, from the positive-$z$ side. With the paper's $e^{+i\omega t}$ [phase convention for complex amplitudes](../../../../../phase-convention-for-complex-amplitudes.md), this gives

$$
\boxed{A=+2i,\qquad \boldsymbol\Psi(t)=(\cos\omega t,-2\sin\omega t).}
$$

The tip follows $\Psi_x^2+\Psi_y^2/4=1$. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\Psi(0)=(1,0),\qquad \boldsymbol\Psi(\pi/(2\omega))=(0,-2).}
$$

**[Figure 1](#section-a/2/image-an-original-elliptical-polarization-trajectory-and-the-two-requested-vectors-using-a-plus-2i). An original elliptical polarization trajectory and the two requested vectors, using $A=+2i$**.

The figure states the viewing direction and shows increasing time. Under the alternative convention that calls rotation from $+x$ toward $+y$ right-handed when looking along propagation, the equally consistent answer is $A=-2i$ and the second [vector](../../../../../vector.md) is $(0,+2)$. The original paper does not state a handedness convention, so its wording alone cannot select between these labels; the real components and viewing direction remove the ambiguity.
