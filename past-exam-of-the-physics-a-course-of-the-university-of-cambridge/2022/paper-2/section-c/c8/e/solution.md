<h1 id="section-c/c8/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The length, area and volume of a first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) are $(2\pi/a)^d$. The corresponding occupied $k$-space measures are

$$
2k_F^{(1)}=\frac\pi a=\frac12\frac{2\pi}a,
$$



$$
\pi(k_F^{(2)})^2=\frac{2\pi^2}{a^2}=\frac12\left(\frac{2\pi}a\right)^2,
$$



$$
\frac{4\pi}3(k_F^{(3)})^3=\frac{4\pi^3}{a^3}=\frac12\left(\frac{2\pi}a\right)^3.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{\text{occupied }k\text{-space measure}}{\text{one Brillouin-zone measure}}=\frac12\quad\text{in all three dimensions}.}
$$

This is state counting, independent of the interval/disk/ball geometry. One [Brillouin zone](../../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) contains one spatial state per [unit cell](../../../../../../unit-cell.md) in a single [energy band](../../../../../../energy-band.md), and that band can hold two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) per cell because of [spin](../../../../../../spin.md). One [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per cell therefore occupies half of it. The “volume enclosed” is the occupied [Fermi sea](../../../../../../fermi-sea.md), not the lower-dimensional area of the [Fermi surface](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) itself. Its half-volume does not mean half the distance to the zone boundary in two or three dimensions.
