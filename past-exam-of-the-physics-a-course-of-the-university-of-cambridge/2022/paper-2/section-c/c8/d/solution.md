<h1 id="section-c/c8/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For each primitive linear, square or cubic [Bravais lattice](../../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) of spacing $a$, the [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) has spacing $2\pi/a$. The first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) is consequently the interval, square or cube with faces at components $k_i=\pm\pi/a$. The nearest zone boundary is a distance $k_B=\pi/a$ from the centre. The ratios are

$$
\boxed{\frac{k_F^{(1)}}{k_B}=\frac12,\qquad
\frac{k_F^{(2)}}{k_B}=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}=0.7979,\qquad
\frac{k_F^{(3)}}{k_B}=\left(\frac3\pi\right)^{1/3}=0.9847.}
$$

All are below one: the ideal free-electron [Fermi surface](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) lies wholly inside the first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../../brillouin-zone.md). Its nearest approach to the boundary is in three dimensions, where it lies only about $1.53\%$ short of the nearest face. A lattice-induced [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md) at a zone face therefore does not by itself make these half-filled systems insulating in the stated free-electron model. Interactions, structural changes and actual [electronic band structure](../../../../../../electronic-band-structure.md) can alter that model prediction.
