<h1 id="section-c/c8/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In two dimensions the [density of states](../../../../../../../density-of-states.md) in $k$ space per [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) species is $(L/2\pi)^2$. A [annulus](../../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) of radius $k$ and thickness $dk$ has area $2\pi k\,dk$. Therefore

$$
g_2^{(1)}(\epsilon)=\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^2 2\pi k\frac{dk}{d\epsilon}
=\boxed{\frac{L^2m}{2\pi\hbar^2}.}
$$

The [two-dimensional free-electron density of states](../../../../../../../two-dimensional-free-electron-density-of-states.md) is constant in [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md), $g_2^{(1)}\propto\epsilon^0$, above the band bottom. Including both [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) states gives $g_2=L^2m/(\pi\hbar^2)$. Dividing by $L^2$ gives the density per unit physical area, rather than the total specimen's density.
