<h1 id="section-c/c8/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

To preserve the convention of part (a), first count one [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) species. Define $g_d^{(1)}(\epsilon)\,d\epsilon$ as the number of spatial states in the whole specimen in that [energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md) interval. Put

$$
k=\frac{\sqrt{2m\epsilon}}{\hbar},\qquad
\frac{dk}{d\epsilon}=\frac{m}{\hbar^2k}.
$$

In one dimension there are intervals near both $+k$ and $-k$, so the [one-dimensional free-electron density of states](../../../../../../../one-dimensional-free-electron-density-of-states.md) is

$$
g_1^{(1)}(\epsilon)=\frac{L}{2\pi}\,2\frac{dk}{d\epsilon}
=\boxed{\frac{L\sqrt{2m}}{2\pi\hbar}\epsilon^{-1/2}.}
$$

Thus $g_1^{(1)}\propto\epsilon^{-1/2}$. The factor of two here comes from opposite [momenta](../../../../../../../momentum-split.md), not [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md). For physical [electrons](../../../../../../../electron.md) with two [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) states, the [spin-summed density of states](../../../../../../../spin-summed-density-of-states.md) is $g_1=2g_1^{(1)}=L\sqrt{2m}\,\epsilon^{-1/2}/(\pi\hbar)$. These expressions are continuum approximations away from individual discrete levels.
