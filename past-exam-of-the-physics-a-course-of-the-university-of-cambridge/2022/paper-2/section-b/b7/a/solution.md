<h1 id="section-b/b7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the normally incident [plane wave](../../../../../../plane-wave.md), the incident [complex amplitude](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) and [phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) are uniform across the aperture. Treat the field using scalar [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md), with the aperture represented by its prescribed [amplitude transmittance](../../../../../../amplitude-transmittance.md) $h(x,y)$. Let $R$ be the distance from the aperture centre to the observation point and let $\ell_x,\ell_y$ be the transverse direction cosines of that point.

Across an aperture of characteristic size $a$, the [Fraunhofer diffraction](../../../../../../fraunhofer-diffraction.md) assumptions are that $a/R\ll1$, so $1/r\simeq1/R$ and the [obliquity factor](../../../../../../obliquity-factor.md) is nearly constant, and that the quadratic contribution to the propagation [phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) is negligible:

$$
\frac{ka^2}{2R}\ll1,\qquad\text{or, in order of magnitude,}\quad \frac{a^2}{\lambda R}\ll1.
$$

The remaining path-length dependence is linear:

$$
r\simeq R-\ell_xx-\ell_yy.
$$

A coherent [monochromatic wave](../../../../../../monochromatic-wave.md) and an aperture large enough relative to [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) for the scalar aperture model complete the usual physical assumptions. The [complex amplitude](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) becomes

$$
\boxed{\psi_P=C\iint h(x,y)e^{-ik(\ell_xx+\ell_yy)}\,dx\,dy,}
$$

where $C$ includes the common propagation [phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md), distance factor and nearly constant [obliquity factor](../../../../../../obliquity-factor.md). For the central part of a planar screen in the [paraxial approximation](../../../../../../paraxial-approximation.md), $\ell_x\simeq X/R$ and $\ell_y\simeq Y/R$. Small observation angles are useful for that screen-coordinate formula; a general far-field [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) can instead use the exact direction cosines. A lens can also produce this pattern in its focal plane without requiring a physically very distant screen.
