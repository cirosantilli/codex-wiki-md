<h1 id="section-b/b7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For $N$ equally spaced illuminated slits, with $N\simeq W/D$, summing a [geometric series](../../../../../../geometric-series.md) of [complex amplitudes](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) gives

$$
I(\theta)=I_1(\theta)\left[\frac{\sin(N\beta)}{\sin\beta}\right]^2,\qquad
\beta=\frac{\pi D\sin\theta}{\lambda}.
$$

Here $I_1(\theta)$ is the one-slit [single-slit diffraction](../../../../../../single-slit-diffraction.md) [wave envelope](../../../../../../envelope-waves.md). The [grating equation](../../../../../../grating-equation.md) for principal maxima is

$$
D\sin\theta_m=m\lambda,
$$

with height $N^2I_1(\theta_m)$ and much weaker subsidiary maxima between orders. The first zeros around order $m$ are $\beta=m\pi\pm\pi/N$, so the angular distance from a maximum to its first minimum is, for large $N$,

$$
\delta\theta_{\rm zero}\simeq\frac{\lambda}{ND\cos\theta_m}
\simeq\frac{\lambda}{W\cos\theta_m}.
$$

Changing [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) by $\delta\lambda$ moves the same [diffraction order](../../../../../../diffraction-order.md) by

$$
\delta\theta_\lambda\simeq\left|\frac{d\theta_m}{d\lambda}\right|\delta\lambda
=\frac{|m|\delta\lambda}{D\cos\theta_m}.
$$

The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) places the maximum for one wavelength at the first zero for its neighbour. Equating these separations gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\lambda}{\delta\lambda}=|m|N\simeq\frac{|m|W}{D}.}
$$

This is the ideal [resolving power of a diffraction grating](../../../../../../resolving-power-of-a-diffraction-grating.md), for an illuminated and transmitted order with sufficiently narrow source and instrumental broadening. The zeroth order has no [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) separation and therefore no useful spectral resolving power. The distinction between $W=ND$ and the span $(N-1)D$ of the outer slit centres is a small edge convention; the exact state count in the resolving-power formula is $N$.
