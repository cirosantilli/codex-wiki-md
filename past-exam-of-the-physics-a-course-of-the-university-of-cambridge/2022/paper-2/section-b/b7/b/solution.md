↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The far-field [complex amplitude](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) is the two-dimensional [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) of the aperture [amplitude transmittance](../../../../../../amplitude-transmittance.md), evaluated at transverse [spatial frequencies](../../../../../../spatial-frequency.md) $q_x=k\ell_x$, $q_y=k\ell_y$. The [light intensity](../../../../../../light-intensity.md) is proportional to the squared magnitude of this transform.

Use [linearity](../../../../../../linearity.md) to add the transforms of simpler aperture components, preserving their relative [phases](../../../../../../phase-waves.md). A translated component acquires a [complex exponential](../../../../../../complex-exponential-function.md) factor. Repeated identical openings can be expressed as the [convolution](../../../../../../convolution.md) of one opening with a sum of [Dirac delta functions](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md); the [convolution theorem](../../../../../../convolution-theorem.md) then factors the field into a single-opening transform and an array factor. Multiplying aperture functions instead convolves their transforms. These operations simplify complex apertures without repeatedly evaluating the full [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) integral. It is the [complex amplitudes](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md), rather than their [wave intensities](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md), that are added for coherent illumination.
