<h1 id="section-b/b7/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The required [resolving power of a diffraction grating](../../../../../../resolving-power-of-a-diffraction-grating.md) is

$$
\mathcal R_{\rm req}=\frac{589.3\,\mathrm{nm}}{0.6\,\mathrm{nm}}=982.2.
$$

For $N=260$ illuminated slits, $\mathcal R=260|m|$. In orders $1,2,3,4$, the ideal resolvable [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) differences are respectively $2.267$, $1.133$, $0.756$ and $0.567\,\mathrm{nm}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{The doublet is unresolved in orders 1--3; order 4 is the first potentially resolving order.}}
$$

Fourth order gives $\mathcal R=1040>982.2$, only a modest margin. It must also exist: for normal incidence the [grating equation](../../../../../../grating-equation.md) requires $D\geq4\lambda\simeq2.36\,\mu\mathrm m$, with $D>4\lambda$ needed to avoid an exactly grazing order. The relevant order must transmit appreciable [light intensity](../../../../../../light-intensity.md) rather than lie at a single-slit [diffraction](../../../../../../diffraction.md) zero, and the source and detector widths must preserve this resolution.

The original question gives only the slit count, not the line spacing, slit width, usable [diffraction order](../../../../../../diffraction-order.md) or instrumental broadening. It therefore supports the conditional conclusion “yes in fourth or higher usable order”, but not an unconditional historical claim that this particular instrument resolved the [sodium](../../../../../../sodium.md) [absorption line](../../../../../../absorption-line.md) doublet in its observed configuration.
