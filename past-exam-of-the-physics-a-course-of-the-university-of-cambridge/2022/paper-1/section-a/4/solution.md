<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Let $M$ denote an incident [muon](../../../../../muon.md) and $S$ a detector signal. The selected sample is conditioned on $S$. The [law of total probability](../../../../../law-of-total-probability.md) gives

$$
P(S)=P(S\mid M)P(M)+P(S\mid M^c)P(M^c)
=(0.90)(0.95)+(0.20)(0.05)=0.865.
$$

By [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../bayes-theorem.md), the contamination, namely the [conditional probability](../../../../../conditional-probability.md) that a selected particle is not a [muon](../../../../../muon.md), is

$$
\boxed{P(M^c\mid S)=\frac{(0.20)(0.05)}{0.865}
=0.01156\simeq1.16\%.}
$$

For example, per $10\,000$ incident particles the expected selected counts are $8550$ true [muons](../../../../../muon.md) and $100$ other particles. The denominator is the $8650$ selected particles; the rejected [muons](../../../../../muon.md) are outside this sample.
