<h1 id="section-b/b7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $\epsilon=\hbar^2/(2mR^2)$. The two-particle product [energy eigenstates](../../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) are

$$
u_{n_1}(\phi_1)u_{n_2}(\phi_2),\qquad E=\epsilon(n_1^2+n_2^2).
$$

Because the particles are distinguishable, $(n_1,n_2)$ and $(n_2,n_1)$ are distinct when $n_1\ne n_2$. Also, the signs of nonzero integers distinguish opposite [angular momenta](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md). The [quantum degeneracy](../../../../../../degenerate-energy-levels.md) is therefore the number of ordered integer pairs at each sum of squares.

The first distinct [energy levels](../../../../../../energy-level.md) are $0,\epsilon,2\epsilon,4\epsilon,5\epsilon,\ldots$. This ordering matters: $4\epsilon$ is the third excited level, while $5\epsilon$ is the fourth excited level.

- At $E=0$, only $(0,0)$ occurs: **$g_0=1$**.
- At the first excited level, $E=\epsilon$, the pairs are $(\pm1,0)$ and $(0,\pm1)$: **$g_1=4$**.
- At the fourth excited level, $E=5\epsilon$, the pairs are $(\pm1,\pm2)$ and $(\pm2,\pm1)$, with independent signs: **$g_4=8$**.
- At $E=25\epsilon$, the pairs are $(\pm5,0),(0,\pm5)$, contributing four, and $(\pm3,\pm4),(\pm4,\pm3)$, contributing eight: **$g_{25}=12$**.

These counts concern [energy eigenspaces](../../../../../../energy-eigenspace.md); arbitrary [quantum superpositions](../../../../../../quantum-superposition.md) within each one do not create additional independent states.
