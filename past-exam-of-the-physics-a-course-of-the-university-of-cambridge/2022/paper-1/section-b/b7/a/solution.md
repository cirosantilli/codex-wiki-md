<h1 id="section-b/b7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [particle in a ring](../../../../../../particle-in-a-ring.md), the arc-length coordinate is $s=R\phi$. The tangential [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is

$$
H=\frac{p_s^2}{2m}=\frac{L_z^2}{2mR^2}.
$$

The constraint removes radial motion; the constant [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) along the ring can be chosen as zero. The tangential [momentum operator](../../../../../../momentum-operator.md) is $\hat p_s=-i\hbar\partial_s$, or equivalently the [angular momentum operator](../../../../../../angular-momentum-operator-split.md) is $\hat L_z=-i\hbar\partial_\phi$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\hat H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2mR^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}.}
$$

Its physical domain has periodic [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md), $\psi(\phi+2\pi)=\psi(\phi)$ and $\psi'(\phi+2\pi)=\psi'(\phi)$. These make the [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) [self-adjoint](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) and reflect that $\phi$ and $\phi+2\pi$ describe the same point. No magnetic flux or additional angle-dependent [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) is present.
