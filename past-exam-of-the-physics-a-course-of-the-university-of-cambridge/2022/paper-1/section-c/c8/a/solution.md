<h1 id="section-c/c8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The original apparatus has two small masses at distance $L/2$ from the suspension axis. Neglecting the rod's [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), their total [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is $I=2m(L/2)^2=mL^2/2$, consistent with the supplied [torsion pendulum](../../../../../../torsion-pendulum.md) period. Treat $m$ and $L$ as exact because no uncertainties are specified. Rearranging the period relation gives

$$
\kappa=\frac{2\pi^2mL^2}{T^2}
=\frac{2\pi^2(0.038\,\mathrm{kg})(0.100\,\mathrm m)^2}{(498.2\,\mathrm s)^2}
=3.02208\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m\,rad^{-1}}.
$$

The [torsion constant](../../../../../../torsion-constant.md) is a restoring [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) per unit [angle](../../../../../../angle.md). In dimensional calculations the radian is dimensionless.

Using first-order [propagation of uncertainty](../../../../../../propagation-of-uncertainty.md), with the quoted period uncertainty interpreted as a [standard uncertainty](../../../../../../standard-uncertainty.md),

$$
\sigma_\kappa=\left|\frac{d\kappa}{dT}\right|\sigma_T
=\frac{2\kappa}{T}\sigma_T,
\qquad \frac{\sigma_\kappa}{\kappa}=\frac{2(6.0)}{498.2}=0.02409.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\kappa=(3.022\pm0.073)\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m\,rad^{-1}}.}
$$
