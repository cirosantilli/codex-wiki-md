[Quantum tunnelling](../../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) is transmission through a finite classically forbidden region, where $E<V(x)$. The stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) permits an evanescent [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) inside such a barrier rather than forcing it to vanish at its entrance. Matching to propagating states beyond a barrier of finite width gives a nonzero transmitted [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md).

For a rectangular barrier of height $V_0$ and width $a$, with equal exterior potentials and $0<E<V_0$, write $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. The exact [rectangular-barrier tunnelling probability](../../../../../../rectangular-barrier-tunnelling-probability.md) is

$$
T=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}.
$$

For an opaque barrier its dominant dependence is $e^{-2\kappa a}$. For a slowly varying barrier, the [WKB approximation](../../../../../../wkb-approximation.md) generalizes this to $T\sim\exp[-(2/\hbar)\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\sqrt{2m(V(x)-E)}\,dx]$, apart from matching prefactors. Barrier width, particle [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) and [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) therefore have a strong influence. [Energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is conserved in a static barrier: tunnelling does not require the particle to borrow [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) or acquire a classical path with negative [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md).

In [alpha decay](../../../../../../alpha-decay.md), a nuclear alpha-particle configuration is confined by the short-range nuclear interaction but separated from the exterior by a [Coulomb barrier](../../../../../../coulomb-barrier.md). Its [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) can be positive relative to infinity while still lying below the barrier maximum. Escape by tunnelling produces alpha particles at that conserved [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), with an exponentially small decay [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) per nuclear encounter. The pronounced dependence of half-life on alpha [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is a characteristic consequence of the barrier exponent, unlike a simple classical escape threshold.

In [scanning tunneling microscopy](../../../../../../scanning-tunneling-microscopy.md), a conducting tip is brought within a nanometer-scale vacuum gap of a conducting surface and biased weakly. [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) tunnelling gives a current that depends approximately exponentially on separation, $I\propto Ve^{-2\kappa s}$ in a simple low-bias model. Feedback adjusts the tip height to keep current fixed while scanning, producing a surface map sensitive to both geometry and electronic states. Finite current through the non-contact gap, and its steep dependence on separation, are the direct tunnelling signatures; current is not a direct measurement of topographic height alone.

A [Josephson junction](../../../../../../josephson-junction.md) provides a collective superconducting example. Two superconductors separated by a thin barrier can carry a coherent pair current $I=I_c\sin\varphi$ even at zero [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md). At constant [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) the [AC Josephson effect](../../../../../../ac-josephson-effect.md) gives $d\varphi/dt=2eV/\hbar$ and oscillation [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) $2eV/h$. The coupled condensate [quantum phases](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) and pair transfer distinguish this from independent normal-electron transmission. These examples involve different microscopic barriers, but all require quantum coupling across a region that suppresses classical passage. Comparing weak-temperature tunnelling behavior with thermally activated escape helps distinguish the mechanisms experimentally.
