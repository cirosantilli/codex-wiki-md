↑ **Parent:** [C10](../c10.md)

The [postulates of quantum mechanics](../../../../../postulates-of-quantum-mechanics.md) give a framework for predicting observations from a prepared state. The experiments below motivate different features of that framework; no one experiment proves the whole set of postulates or specifies every [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md).

A pure [quantum state](../../../../../quantum-state.md) is a normalized vector in a complex [Hilbert space](../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), with vectors differing by an overall [quantum phase](../../../../../quantum-phase.md) representing the same physical preparation. In [position](../../../../../position.md) representation, $\int|\psi(\mathbf r)|^2d^3r=1$. The linear state space permits [quantum superposition](../../../../../quantum-superposition.md), while the [Born rule](../../../../../born-rule.md) assigns [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $\int_\Omega|\psi|^2d^3r$ to a [position](../../../../../position.md) outcome in a region $\Omega$. The [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is a [probability amplitude](../../../../../probability-amplitude.md); it is not a classical distribution of definite particle positions and momenta.

An observable is represented by a [self-adjoint operator](../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md). If $Q$ has spectral projectors $P_q$, its possible measurement outcomes are its spectral values and $\Pr(q)=\langle\psi|P_q|\psi\rangle$. The mean is $\langle Q\rangle=\langle\psi|Q|\psi\rangle$. For an ideal repeatable [projective measurement](../../../../../projective-measurement.md), obtaining $q$ leaves the normalized conditional state $P_q\psi/\sqrt{\Pr(q)}$. This projects onto an entire eigenspace if the outcome is degenerate. General detectors need more general [measurement in quantum mechanics](../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) models; the projective update is an idealization. Noncommuting observables need not have simultaneous sharp values, as the [canonical commutation relation](../../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) and [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](../../../../../heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) illustrate.

Between measurements, an isolated system has [unitary time evolution](../../../../../unitary-time-evolution.md) generated by its [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md):

$$
i\hbar\frac{\partial|\psi\rangle}{\partial t}=H|\psi\rangle,\qquad U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}\quad\text{for fixed }H.
$$

This preserves normalization and superpositions. Composite systems use a [tensor product](../../../../../tensor-product.md) of state spaces, allowing entangled states as well as product states; identical particles additionally require the bosonic or fermionic exchange symmetry appropriate to their species. Statistical mixtures are described by a [density matrix](../../../../../density-matrix-split.md), with $\Pr(q)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho P_q)$. These rules separate preparation, coherent evolution and measurement.

The [photoelectric effect](../../../../../photoelectric-effect.md) exposes discrete [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transfer by light. Illuminate a clean metal cathode in an evacuated tube and measure the collected [electron](../../../../../electron.md) current while varying the light [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), intensity and a retarding [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md). The [stopping potential](../../../../../stopping-potential.md) $V_s$ probes the largest emitted [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), whereas the [photocurrent](../../../../../photocurrent.md) measures the rate at which charge is collected. In the ordinary single-photon regime there is a threshold [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md); above it the maximum [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) grows with [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), rather than with intensity. Changing intensity at fixed [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) mainly changes the number of emitted electrons. These observations are summarized by the [Einstein photoelectric equation](../../../../../einstein-photoelectric-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{K_{\max}=h\nu-\Phi=eV_s,\qquad \nu_0=\Phi/h.}
$$

Here $\Phi$ is the surface [work function](../../../../../work-function.md), and the stopping [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) is understood after any [photoelectric contact-potential correction](../../../../../photoelectric-contact-potential-correction.md). A [photon](../../../../../photon.md) delivers $h\nu$ in an absorption event; emission is allowed only when that [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) can pay the escape cost. This accounts for the [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) threshold and the lack of the classical waiting time associated with slowly accumulating [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) in a single [electron](../../../../../electron.md). The experiment supports [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) quantization of light; it does not imply that every incident [photon](../../../../../photon.md) emits an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) or that every emitted [electron](../../../../../electron.md) has the maximum [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). Surface losses and collection geometry affect the full current-voltage curve.

The [Davisson–Germer experiment](../../../../../davisson-germer-experiment.md) demonstrates a complementary wave property of matter. An [electron](../../../../../electron.md) gun uses a heated emitter, accelerating [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) and apertures to send a narrow beam onto a nickel single crystal in vacuum. A movable collector measures scattered [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) versus direction; rotating the crystal also tests its azimuthal symmetry. The crystal gives sharp angular beams whose positions depend on [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) and crystal orientation. A prominent feature occurs near $54\,\mathrm V$ accelerating [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) and roughly $50^\circ$ detector colatitude. The nonrelativistic [de Broglie wavelength](../../../../../de-broglie-wavelength.md) predicted at that [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) is

$$
\lambda=\frac h{\sqrt{2m_e eU}}\simeq0.167\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$

[Diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) wavelengths inferred using the known crystal structure agree with this [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) dependence. These angular peaks arise from coherent addition of scattering amplitudes from the crystal, not from classical specular reflection of independent point projectiles. [Bragg's law](../../../../../bragg-s-law.md) is $2d\sin\theta_B=r\lambda$ for lattice-plane spacing $d$ and angle to those planes; a detector colatitude is a different angle and must not be substituted without its geometry. The historical readout was current, not a photograph of individual [electron](../../../../../electron.md) trajectories.[1927, apparatus pp. 707–710 and angular/[voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) scans pp. 715–719](../../../../../diffraction-of-electrons-by-a-crystal-of-nickel-by-c-davisson-and-l-h-germer.md)

The [double-slit experiment](../../../../../double-slit-experiment.md) makes the role of amplitudes especially direct. A coherent source illuminates two narrow slits; an observation screen a distance $D$ away records the pattern. For slit spacing $d$ and small screen displacement $y$, the path difference is approximately $dy/D$. The two amplitudes add before probabilities are formed:

$$
\boxed{P(y)=|\psi_1(y)+\psi_2(y)|^2=|\psi_1|^2+|\psi_2|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\psi_1^*\psi_2).}
$$

For equal coherent illumination the [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) term varies as $\cos(2\pi dy/(\lambda D))$, giving fringe spacing $\lambda D/d$ beneath the single-slit [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) envelope. Blocking one slit removes that cross term. Young's optical experiment established wave [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md); later particle-resolved realizations with photons or electrons show localized detection events whose accumulated distribution has the [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) pattern. Low arrival rates demonstrate that this pattern does not require different incident particles to collide with one another.

If the paths become correlated with distinguishable detector states $|D_1\rangle$ and $|D_2\rangle$, the cross term acquires their overlap $\langle D_2|D_1\rangle$. Perfectly distinguishable path records make that overlap zero, suppressing [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) even if the spatial beams still overlap. This connects [quantum entanglement](../../../../../entangled-state.md), measurement and loss of observable coherence. Without a path measurement, the two alternatives are amplitudes for one preparation, rather than classical mutually exclusive events whose probabilities may simply be added.

Together these experiments motivate a description in which [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transfer and detector outcomes can be discrete, yet propagation retains complex [quantum phases](../../../../../quantum-phase.md) and [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md). The [Born rule](../../../../../born-rule.md) connects those amplitudes to repeatable frequencies of outcomes. The [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) supplies the dynamics once a [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) is specified, while observable operators, state preparation and the measurement model specify what the apparatus can read.
