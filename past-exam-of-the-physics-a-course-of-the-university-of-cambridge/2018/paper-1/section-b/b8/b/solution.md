<h1 id="section-b/b8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Expand the initial [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) in the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md) using $\cos^2\phi=1/2+(e^{2i\phi}+e^{-2i\phi})/4$. With the normalized $u_m$ from part (a), this gives

$$
\boxed{|\Psi(0)\rangle=\sqrt{\frac23}|m=0\rangle+\frac1{\sqrt6}|m=2\rangle+\frac1{\sqrt6}|m=-2\rangle.}
$$

The coefficient squares sum to one, also verifying the stated normalization. The [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md) therefore gives [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) outcomes

$$
\boxed{L_z=0,\ +2\hbar,\ -2\hbar\quad\text{with probabilities}\quad\frac23,\ \frac16,\ \frac16.}
$$

For an [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) measurement the two distinct $m=\pm2$ states belong to the same [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) eigenspace. Sum their probabilities, rather than adding their amplitudes, to obtain

$$
\boxed{E=0\ \text{with probability }\frac23,\qquad E=\frac{2\hbar^2}{I}\ \text{with probability }\frac13.}
$$

Their relative probabilities are $4:1:1$ for the angular momenta and $2:1$ for the energies. In particular, zero mean [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) does not imply zero rotational [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md).
