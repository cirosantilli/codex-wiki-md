<h1 id="section-b/b8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each [energy eigenstate](../../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) evolves by its own [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) $e^{-iE_mt/\hbar}$. The two $m=\pm2$ components have the same [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), while the $m=0$ [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) stays one. Thus

$$
|\Psi(t)\rangle=\sqrt{\frac23}|0\rangle+\frac{e^{-2i\hbar t/I}}{\sqrt6}(|2\rangle+|-2\rangle).
$$

In angle representation this is

$$
\boxed{\Psi(\phi,t)=\frac1{\sqrt{3\pi}}\left[1+e^{-2i\hbar t/I}\cos2\phi\right].}
$$

At $t=0$, $1+\cos2\phi=2\cos^2\phi$ recovers the initial state. The fixed coefficient magnitudes keep the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) probabilities constant even though the density in angle changes.
