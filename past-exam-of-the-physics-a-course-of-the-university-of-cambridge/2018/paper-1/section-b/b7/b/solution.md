<h1 id="section-b/b7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume $a>0$ and define $\omega=\sqrt{2a/m}$ so that the potential is that of a [quantum harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md). The two trial forms have derivatives

$$
\Psi_1''=(4C^2x^2-2C)\Psi_1,\qquad \Psi_2''=(4C^2x^2-6C)\Psi_2.
$$

Substitution into the stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) cancels the $x^2$ terms precisely when $a=2\hbar^2C^2/m$. Normalizability selects the positive root,

$$
\boxed{C=\sqrt{\frac{ma}{2\hbar^2}}=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}.}
$$

The remaining constants give

$$
\boxed{E_{\Psi_1}=\frac{\hbar^2C}{m}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2},\qquad E_{\Psi_2}=\frac{3\hbar^2C}{m}=\frac{3\hbar\omega}{2}.}
$$

For later overlaps choose positive normalization constants. The [Gaussian integral](../../../../../../gaussian-integral.md) gives $A=(2C/\pi)^{1/4}$, while its second-moment [integral](../../../../../../integral-split.md) gives $B=2\sqrt C\,A$. Thus both wavefunctions have unit [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) and are [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) by their opposite [parity](../../../../../../parity.md).

The real Gaussian is positive everywhere and has no nodes; the odd state has exactly one node at $x=0$. The [Sturm oscillation theorem](../../../../../../sturm-oscillation-theorem.md) orders one-dimensional bound states by their node count. Consequently these are the [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) and first excited state, respectively, also agreeing with the oscillator spectrum $E_n=(n+1/2)\hbar\omega$.
