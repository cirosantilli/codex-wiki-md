<h1 id="section-b/b6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set

$$
k_0=\frac{\sqrt{2mE}}\hbar,\qquad k_1=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E-V_1)}}\hbar,\qquad k_2=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E-V_2)}}\hbar.
$$

All are real and $k_0>k_1>k_2>0$. Suppressing a common time [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, choose

$$
\boxed{\psi_{\mathrm L}=Ae^{ik_0x}+Be^{-ik_0x},\qquad \psi_{\mathrm M}=Ce^{ik_1x}+De^{-ik_1x},\qquad \psi_{\mathrm R}=Fe^{ik_2(x-a)}.}
$$

The rightmost region has no left-moving term because no wave is incident from the right. The shifted [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) convention makes $F$ the transmitted [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) at the second interface.

For finite potential steps and equal [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), [wavefunction matching at a finite potential step](../../../../../../wavefunction-matching-at-a-finite-potential-step.md) requires continuity of both $\psi$ and $\partial_x\psi$. Explicitly,

$$
A+B=C+D,\quad k_0(A-B)=k_1(C-D),
$$



$$
Ce^{ik_1a}+De^{-ik_1a}=F,\quad k_1(Ce^{ik_1a}-De^{-ik_1a})=k_2F.
$$

A discontinuous [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) would introduce an inadmissible singular kinetic term, while integrating the stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) across a finite step shows that the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) cannot jump. These conditions also keep the [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md) continuous, so a finite interface neither creates nor destroys [probability](../../../../../../probability.md). The [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) rule would need modification for a delta-function potential or a different effective [mass](../../../../../../mass.md); neither occurs here.
