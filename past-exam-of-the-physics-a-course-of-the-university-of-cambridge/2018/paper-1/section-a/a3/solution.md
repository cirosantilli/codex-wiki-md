<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

In the [orthonormal](../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) $S_z$ [eigenbasis](../../../../../eigenbasis.md) $(\alpha,\beta)$, the [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md) operator is $S_x=(\hbar/2)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. An eigenvector $a\alpha+b\beta$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $s_x$ obeys $b=(2s_x/\hbar)a$ and $a=(2s_x/\hbar)b$. A nonzero vector requires $(2s_x/\hbar)^2=1$. Choosing normalized [quantum phases](../../../../../quantum-phase.md) gives

$$
\boxed{s_x=+\frac\hbar2,\quad \chi_+=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt2};\qquad s_x=-\frac\hbar2,\quad \chi_-=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt2}.}
$$

These [spin eigenstates](../../../../../spin-eigenstate.md) are [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) and have unit [norm](../../../../../norm.md) because $\alpha$ and $\beta$ do.
