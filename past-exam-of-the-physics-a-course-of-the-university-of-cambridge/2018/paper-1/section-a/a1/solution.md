<h1 id="section-a/a1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](../a1.md)

For a neutral atom, count both the nucleons and the electrons. Protons, neutrons and electrons are all [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md), and exchanging two identical intact atoms produces the sign $(-1)^{N_f}$ from exchanging their fermionic constituents. An odd number gives a composite [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md); an even number gives a composite [boson](../../../../../boson.md), consistently with the [Spin-statistics theorem](../../../../../spin-statistics-theorem.md).

A lithium atom has three protons and three electrons. Lithium-6 also has three neutrons, giving $3+3+3=9$ fermionic constituents; lithium-7 has four neutrons, giving $3+4+3=10$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{{}^6\mathrm{Li}\text{ atoms are fermions},\qquad {}^7\mathrm{Li}\text{ atoms are bosons}.}
$$

The [composite statistics of neutral atoms](../../../../../composite-statistics-of-neutral-atoms.md) cannot be inferred from the [mass](../../../../../mass.md) number alone: counting only nucleons would classify the nuclei and give the opposite answers here. The atom's total [spin](../../../../../spin.md) includes its electrons as well as its nucleus.
