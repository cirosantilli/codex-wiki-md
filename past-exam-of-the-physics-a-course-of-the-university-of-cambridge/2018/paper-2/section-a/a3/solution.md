<h1 id="section-a/a3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](../a3.md)

At normal incidence between lossless media with real positive [acoustic impedances](../../../../../acoustic-impedance.md) $Z_1,Z_2$, continuity of pressure and normal particle [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) gives the pressure transmission amplitude $t_p=2Z_2/(Z_1+Z_2)$. Since the mean [acoustic intensity](../../../../../acoustic-energy-flux.md) is $|p|^2/(2Z)$, the fraction of incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transmitted is

$$
\mathcal T=\frac{Z_1}{Z_2}|t_p|^2=\frac{4Z_1Z_2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2},\qquad
\mathcal R=\left(\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1}\right)^2=1-\mathcal T.
$$

This is [acoustic reflection and transmission at an interface](../../../../../acoustic-reflection-and-transmission-at-an-interface.md). With the supplied [acoustic impedances](../../../../../acoustic-impedance.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathcal T_{\rm air\to body}=9.81\times10^{-4}=0.0981\%},\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal T_{\rm water\to body}=0.99767=99.77\%}.
$$

A thin water-based gel layer excludes air gaps and approximately matches the body's [acoustic impedance](../../../../../acoustic-impedance.md). About $99.90\%$ of incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) would otherwise reflect at the air-body interface. The coupling is also needed on the return journey, so it strongly improves the detectable echo. These figures concern interface transmission, before absorption within tissue; the gel is approximated by the stated water value.
