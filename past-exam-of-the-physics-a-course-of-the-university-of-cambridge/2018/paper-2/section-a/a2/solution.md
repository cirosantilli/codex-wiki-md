<h1 id="section-a/a2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](../a2.md)

Both the incoming and outgoing spherical [wavefronts](../../../../../wavefront.md) contribute to the path excess. At [aperture](../../../../../aperture.md) radius $u$, with axial distances $s$ before and $r$ after the [aperture](../../../../../aperture.md),

$$
\Delta\ell=\sqrt{s^2+u^2}+\sqrt{r^2+u^2}-(s+r)
\simeq\frac{u^2}{2}\left(\frac1s+\frac1r\right).
$$

The outer edge of the $n$th [Fresnel half-period zone](../../../../../fresnel-half-period-zone.md) has $\Delta\ell=n\lambda/2$. Hence

$$
u_n^2=\frac{n\lambda sr}{s+r},\qquad
u_2=\sqrt{\frac{2(400\times10^{-9})(0.5)(0.5)}{1.0}}.
$$

Therefore **the outer radius is** $\boxed{u_2=4.47\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm m=0.447\,\mathrm{mm}}$. The [paraxial approximation](../../../../../paraxial-approximation.md) is excellent because $u_2\ll s,r$. Treating the illumination as a [plane wave](../../../../../plane-wave.md) would incorrectly omit the source-side path curvature.
