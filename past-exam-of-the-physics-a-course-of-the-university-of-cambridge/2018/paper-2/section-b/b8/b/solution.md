The beam splitter sends transmitted and reflected portions into perpendicular arms. Each mirror returns its beam to the splitter, where the two returning fields recombine towards the detector. A collimating [lens](../../../../../../lens-optics.md) provides approximately plane illumination. A compensating plate of the same glass thickness as the beam splitter can equalise the dispersive glass paths, which matters especially for broadband light.

If the arm lengths are $\ell_1,\ell_2$, the geometric [optical path difference](../../../../../../optical-path-difference.md) in air is $x=2(\ell_1-\ell_2)$, up to a chosen constant and any additional material paths. Moving one mirror by $d$ changes $x$ by $2d$. Fixed reflection [phases](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) can be absorbed into the reference position of a bright or dark fringe.
