<h1 id="section-b/b6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The capacitor-charge [complex amplitude](../../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) is

$$
q_0=\frac{V_0/L}{1/(LC)-\omega^2+i(R/L)\omega}.
$$

For fixed driving-voltage [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md), maximizing $|q_0|$ minimizes

$$
D(\omega)=\left(\omega_0^2-\omega^2\right)^2+\gamma^2\omega^2,\qquad
\omega_0^2=\frac1{LC},\quad\gamma=\frac RL.
$$

Differentiate:

$$
\frac{dD}{d\omega}=2\omega[-2(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)+\gamma^2].
$$

The positive-frequency minimum therefore has

$$
\boxed{\omega_{\rm res}=\sqrt{\frac1{LC}-\frac{R^2}{2L^2}}},\qquad
R^2<\frac{2L}{C}.
$$

This is the [resonance](../../../../../../resonance.md) of charge, or capacitor voltage. If the stated inequality fails, their maximum is at zero [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md), and no positive displacement-resonance peak exists. The [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md) has the additional factor $\omega$, and instead peaks exactly at $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ for every $R>0$.
