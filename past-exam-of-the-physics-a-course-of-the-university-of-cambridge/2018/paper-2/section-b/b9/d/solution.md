<h1 id="section-b/b9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At an ordinary point or a massless join, the string is continuous, so

$$
\boxed{\Psi(x_0^-,t)=\Psi(x_0^+,t)}.
$$

With no concentrated transverse [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) or point [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), transverse [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) balance requires

$$
\boxed{T_1\Psi_x(x_0^-,t)=T_2\Psi_x(x_0^+,t)}.
$$

For the common [tension](../../../../../../tension.md) in this problem, the slope $\partial\Psi/\partial x$ is therefore continuous as well. A jump in [linear mass density](../../../../../../linear-mass-density.md) changes [wave speed](../../../../../../wave-speed.md), but introduces no finite inertial [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) at a zero-length join. A point [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), external support [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), or unequal tensions would require modified [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md).
