<h1 id="section-b/b9/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A characteristic [wave impedance](../../../../../../wave-impedance.md) relates an effort-like quantity to its conjugate flow-like quantity in a single travelling wave: for a mechanical wave these are [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) and [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md), and their product is [power](../../../../../../power.md). The sign depends on the propagation and [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) conventions. Use the transverse [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) transmitted towards increasing $x$, $F=-T\Psi_x$. A rightgoing [travelling wave](../../../../../../travelling-wave.md) $\Psi=f(x-vt)$ has transverse [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $u=\Psi_t=-v\Psi_x$, so

$$
\boxed{Z=F/u=T/v=\sqrt{T\rho}},\qquad [Z]=\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}.
$$

This is the positive [characteristic impedance of a stretched string](../../../../../../characteristic-impedance-of-a-stretched-string.md). A leftgoing component has $F/u=-Z$ with the same [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) orientation. Its units differ from pressure-based [acoustic impedance](../../../../../../acoustic-impedance.md) because it uses [force](../../../../../../force-split.md), rather than [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) per area.
