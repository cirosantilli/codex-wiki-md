<h1 id="section-b/b9/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The reflected and transmitted mean-power fractions are

$$
\mathcal R=\left(\frac{Z_1-Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2}\right)^2,\qquad
\mathcal T=\frac{Z_2}{Z_1}\left(\frac{2Z_1}{Z_1+Z_2}\right)^2
=\frac{4Z_1Z_2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2},\quad \mathcal R+\mathcal T=1.
$$

For strictly positive finite [linear mass densities](../../../../../../../linear-mass-density.md), $\mathcal T>0$; a finite density step cannot produce exact total reflection. The ideal limits are

$$
\boxed{\rho_2/\rho_1\to\infty:\ r\to-1,\ \mathcal R\to1},\qquad
\boxed{\rho_2/\rho_1\to0:\ r\to+1,\ \mathcal R\to1}.
$$

The first behaves as a fixed end; the second as a free end. In the free-end limit the transmitted displacement tends to $2A$, but its [mechanical impedance](../../../../../../../mechanical-impedance.md) tends to zero, so it carries vanishing [power](../../../../../../../power.md). Thus total reflected _[energy](../../../../../../../energy-split.md)_ does not require a vanishing boundary displacement. “Entirely reflected” at a merely large density contrast is an approximation.
