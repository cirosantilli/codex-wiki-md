<h1 id="section-c/c10/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The occupation probability of an individual one-electron state is the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md),

$$
\boxed{f(\epsilon,T)=\frac1{e^{(\epsilon-\mu(T))/(k_BT)}+1}}.
$$

At $T=0$, it is a step: one below $E_F$ and zero above it. Heating rounds the step over an [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) range of order a few $k_BT$, creating empty states below and occupied states above the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md). At every positive [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), $f(\mu,T)=1/2$.

For fixed $N,V$ in the three-dimensional [free electron model](../../../../../../free-electron-model.md), the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) is determined by $N=\int g(\epsilon)f(\epsilon,T)d\epsilon$; it is not exactly constant. In the degenerate regime $T\ll T_F$, the [Sommerfeld expansion](../../../../../../sommerfeld-expansion.md) gives

$$
\mu(T)=E_F\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac{T}{T_F}\right)^2+\cdots\right].
$$

It is therefore reasonable in a simple low-temperature sketch to set $\mu\simeq E_F$, provided this approximation is stated. The figure includes the leading small shift. At much higher [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) the fixed-$E_F$ approximation fails and the distribution approaches the classical dilute limit. The plotted occupation is $f$, rather than the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) distribution $g f$.
