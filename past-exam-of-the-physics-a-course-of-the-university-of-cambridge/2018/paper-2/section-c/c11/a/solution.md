<h1 id="section-c/c11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Bloch state](../../../../../../bloch-state.md) in a crystal experiences a periodic lattice potential. Its band [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation-split.md) $\epsilon(k)$ encodes the lattice response, so an [effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) allows a wavepacket's acceleration under a weak applied [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) to be described without explicitly following the microscopic periodic [forces](../../../../../../force-split.md).

In one dimension the wavepacket [group velocity](../../../../../../group-velocity.md) and semiclassical equation for [crystal momentum](../../../../../../crystal-momentum.md) are

$$
v_g=\frac1\hbar\frac{d\epsilon}{dk},\qquad \hbar\dot k=F.
$$

Differentiating the first equation with respect to time gives

$$
a=\frac1\hbar\frac{d^2\epsilon}{dk^2}\dot k
=\frac{F}{\hbar^2}\frac{d^2\epsilon}{dk^2}.
$$

Defining $F=m^*a$ yields

$$
\boxed{m^*=\hbar^2\left(\frac{d^2\epsilon}{dk^2}\right)^{-1}}.
$$

This [band effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) is controlled by [band curvature](../../../../../../band-curvature.md), not the slope. A band minimum has positive $m^*$; a band maximum has negative [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) $m^*$ and is often more conveniently described with positive-mass [electron holes](../../../../../../electron-hole.md). In three dimensions the inverse [effective mass](../../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) is generally a tensor, $(m^{*-1})_{ij}=\hbar^{-2}\partial_{k_i}\partial_{k_j}\epsilon$. The scalar formula assumes motion along a specified direction in an isolated band, weak fields and negligible interband transitions; the bare [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) has not physically changed.
