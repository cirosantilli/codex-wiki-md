<h1 id="section-c/c11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Interpret “3 mm wire” as a circular wire of diameter $3\,\mathrm{mm}$, and take the current density as uniform. One conduction [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per atom gives

$$
n=\frac{8940}{0.0635}N_A=8.48\times10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}},\qquad
A=\pi(1.5\times10^{-3})^2=7.07\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

Using $I=neA|v_d|$, the [drift velocity](../../../../../../drift-velocity.md) corresponding to a current magnitude of $13\,\mathrm A$ is

$$
\boxed{|v_d|=\frac{13}{neA}=1.35\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}.
$$

It points opposite to conventional [electric current](../../../../../../electric-current.md). A simple distance scale over the $1/50=0.020\,\mathrm s$ cycle is $\boxed{|v_d|/f=2.71\,\mathrm{\mu m}}$, but this treats the magnitude as constant. For sinusoidal mains, [alternating-current drift excursion](../../../../../../alternating-current-drift-excursion.md) gives zero net displacement after a full cycle.

If $13\,\mathrm A$ is the usual [root mean square](../../../../../../root-mean-square.md) current, $v_{\rm pk}=\sqrt2|v_d|$ and the displacement amplitude and total absolute drift distance per cycle are

$$
\boxed{x_{\rm pk}=\frac{\sqrt2|v_d|}{2\pi f}=0.609\,\mathrm{\mu m}},\qquad
\boxed{s_{\rm cycle}=4x_{\rm pk}=2.44\,\mathrm{\mu m}}.
$$

If $13\,\mathrm A$ instead denotes peak current, these become $0.431\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and $1.72\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. The paper does not state peak versus RMS; the characteristic drift is the boxed $1.35\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ on whichever current convention is chosen. It also does not explicitly say diameter; taking $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ as the radius would reduce all these velocities and drift distances by four. None of these distances is the microscopic path travelled at roughly the [Fermi velocity](../../../../../../fermi-velocity.md) between scattering events; that path cannot be obtained from the supplied current alone.
