<h1 id="section-b/b7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](../b7.md)

For [observables](../../../../../observable.md) with finite [variances](../../../../../variance-split.md), the [Heisenberg uncertainty principle](../../../../../heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) gives $\Delta A\Delta B\geq|\langle[A,B]\rangle|/2$; in particular $\Delta x\Delta p\geq\hbar/2$. It concerns the spreads of measurements in a prepared state, rather than only disturbance by a measuring device. Identical integer-spin particles have symmetric full states and [Bose-Einstein statistics](../../../../../bose-einstein-statistics.md); half-integer-spin particles have antisymmetric full states and [Fermi-Dirac statistics](../../../../../fermi-dirac-statistics.md). The latter imply Pauli exclusion from one complete single-particle state, including [spin](../../../../../spin.md).

Inside an [infinite square well](../../../../../infinite-square-well.md), solutions satisfying both zero boundary conditions are

$$
\boxed{\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad
E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,
$$

with zero [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) outside. For the [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md), direct integration gives

$$
\langle x\rangle=\frac L2,\quad
\langle x^2\rangle=L^2\left(\frac13-\frac1{2\pi^2}\right),\quad
\langle p\rangle=0,\quad\langle p^2\rangle=\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{L^2}.
$$

The [infinite-well ground-state uncertainty product](../../../../../infinite-well-ground-state-uncertainty-product.md) follows:

$$
\boxed{\Delta x=L\sqrt{\frac1{12}-\frac1{2\pi^2}},\quad
\Delta p=\frac{\pi\hbar}{L},\quad
\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2}{12}-\frac12}\simeq0.568\hbar>\frac\hbar2}.
$$

The [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) is not a minimum-uncertainty Gaussian. Here [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) is the usual kinetic-momentum second moment of the confined state; the hard-wall [momentum operator](../../../../../momentum-operator.md) has domain subtleties that do not alter this kinetic-energy result.

For [spin degeneracy in filling a one-dimensional well](../../../../../spin-degeneracy-in-filling-a-one-dimensional-well.md), let $E_1=\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$ and assume the [spin](../../../../../spin.md) levels are degenerate and available. Four spin-zero [bosons](../../../../../boson.md) all occupy the lowest orbital, giving **$4E_1$**. Four spin-$1/2$ [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) fill two [spin](../../../../../spin.md) states in each of $n=1,2$, giving **$10E_1$**. Spin-$3/2$ [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) have four [spin](../../../../../spin.md) projections, so all four can occupy $n=1$ in different complete states, giving **$4E_1$**. These fillings do not impose full [spin](../../../../../spin.md) polarization.
