<h1 id="section-c/c11/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a finite potential barrier with $E<V$, the stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) has exponentially decaying and growing solutions, with local decay constant $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V-E)}/\hbar$. Matching the [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) and its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) at finite steps produces a transmitted [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md) even though a classical particle at that [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) could not cross. [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) remains conserved. For a smooth thick barrier, the leading WKB transmission factor is

$$
\boxed{T\sim\exp\left[-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{\sqrt{2m[V(x)-E]}}\hbar\,dx\right]}.
$$

For a rectangular barrier of height $V_0$, width $d$ and $0<E<V_0$, exact matching gives $T=[1+V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa d)/(4E(V_0-E))]^{-1}$. Thickness and [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) therefore affect transmission exponentially in the opaque limit; prefactors and near-turning-point corrections require more than the leading exponent.

[alpha decay](../../../../../../alpha-decay.md) illustrates escape from a nuclear well through a [Coulomb barrier](../../../../../../coulomb-barrier.md). A rough decay rate is an internal encounter [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) times barrier penetrability, multiplied by the [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) of forming the outgoing cluster; penetrability alone is not a complete nuclear lifetime calculation. In a [scanning tunnelling microscope](../../../../../../scanning-tunneling-microscope.md), [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) transmission through the vacuum gap gives an approximately exponential distance dependence $I\propto e^{-2\kappa d}$ at fixed conditions. A feedback loop adjusts tip height to maintain current, providing surface information. Bias, tip electronic structure and local electronic density of states also affect the current, so constant-current height is not purely geometric topography. Both applications exploit the same sensitivity of transmission to a classically forbidden region.
