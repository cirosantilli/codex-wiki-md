[angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) generates rotations. Its components obey $[J_i,J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$, and $J^2$ commutes with each component. A compatible basis has $J^2=\hbar^2j(j+1)$ and $J_z=m\hbar$, where $m=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$. [ladder operators](../../../../../ladder-operator.md) change $m$ by one, terminate at $\pm j$, and give $2j+1$ magnetic substates. Different Cartesian components generally cannot all be sharp simultaneously.

[orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) is $\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p=-i\hbar\mathbf r\times\nabla$. Its angular eigenfunctions are [spherical harmonics](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md), with integer $\ell$ arising from their spatial rotation properties and single-valuedness. [spin](../../../../../spin.md) is intrinsic [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md), not literal rotation of a finite charged sphere. It may be half-integer; [spin](../../../../../spin.md) $1/2$ is represented by $\mathbf S=\hbar\boldsymbol\sigma/2$. For several contributions, $\mathbf J=\mathbf L+\mathbf S$ obeys the same algebra. Coupling angular momenta $j_1,j_2$ permits $J=|j_1-j_2|,\ldots,j_1+j_2$, with [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](../../../../../clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md) relating product and coupled bases. [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) can distinguish these total-angular-momentum sectors.

The Stern-Gerlach apparatus sends a collimated neutral atomic beam through an inhomogeneous [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) and records its deflection. A [magnetic moment](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) experiences $F_z\simeq\mu_z\,\partial B_z/\partial z$; field uniformity would produce precession but no analogous net separating force. A classically continuous distribution of moment orientations would broaden the beam continuously. Gerlach and Stern instead observed separated silver-beam components. Reversing or removing the field and comparing the undeflected profile controls the magnetic origin; collimation, velocity spread and finite resolution affect the spots. The original experiment demonstrated space quantization; its modern silver interpretation uses the unpaired [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [spin](../../../../../spin.md) with nearly zero [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md), rather than a direct measurement of a free [electron](../../../../../electron.md)'s trajectory.[Experimental setup, recorded silver deposits and field-on/off comparison](../../../../../der-experimentelle-nachweis-der-richtungsquantelung-im-magnetfeld-by-walther-gerlach-and-otto-stern.md)

For two spin-$1/2$ particles, the [triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) sector has $S=1$ and symmetric [spin](../../../../../spin.md) states

$$
|1,1\rangle=|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,\quad
|1,0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad
|1,-1\rangle=|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle.
$$

The [spin singlet state](../../../../../spin-singlet-state.md) has $S=0$ and antisymmetric [spin](../../../../../spin.md) state

$$
\boxed{|0,0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}}.
$$

Their total $S^2$ values are $2\hbar^2$ and zero, while $\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2$ has values $\hbar^2/4$ and $-3\hbar^2/4$. The singlet is rotationally invariant and gives anticorrelated [spin](../../../../../spin.md) measurements along a common axis. For identical [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) the complete state is antisymmetric: a symmetric spatial state pairs with a singlet [spin](../../../../../spin.md) state, whereas an antisymmetric spatial state pairs with triplet [spin](../../../../../spin.md). **Orbital, [spin](../../../../../spin.md) and coupled angular momenta share an algebra, but their physical origins and allowed representations differ.**
