<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For [elliptical polarization from circular and linear components](../../../../../elliptical-polarization-from-circular-and-linear-components.md), use coherent waves of the same [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md), propagation direction and fixed relative phase. Let the circular component be $A(\widehat{\mathbf x}\cos\psi+\widehat{\mathbf y}\sin\psi)$ and the linear component $B\widehat{\mathbf x}\cos\psi$, with $A,B>0$. Their sum has $E_x=(A+B)\cos\psi$ and $E_y=A\sin\psi$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{E_x^2}{(A+B)^2}+\frac{E_y^2}{A^2}=1}.
$$

The tip of the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) traces an ellipse with unequal semiaxes $A+B$ and $A$. Coherence is necessary: averaging unrelated phases does not create this fixed [polarization ellipse](../../../../../polarization-ellipse.md).
