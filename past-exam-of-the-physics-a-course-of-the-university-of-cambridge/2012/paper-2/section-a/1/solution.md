<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [Arago spot](../../../../../arago-spot.md) is a bright axial point inside the geometrical shadow of a circular opaque object. Under coherent illumination, contributions from points around its circular edge have equal optical path to an axial observation point and interfere constructively. It is a [Fresnel diffraction](../../../../../fresnel-diffraction.md) effect, so geometrical rays alone miss it. In the ideal paraxial plane-wave model, [Babinet's principle](../../../../../babinet-s-principle.md) gives $U_{\rm disk}=U_0-U_{\rm aperture}=U_0e^{ika^2/(2z)}$ on axis for disk radius $a$; thus **the axial intensity equals the unobstructed intensity** in that model. The whole shadow is not bright: off-axis phases differ and form a localized central feature. Finite source size, poor coherence or an imperfect edge reduce its visibility.
