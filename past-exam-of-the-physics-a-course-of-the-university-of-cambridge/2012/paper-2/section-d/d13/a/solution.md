<h1 id="section-d/d13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A three-phonon normal process conserves crystal [wavevector](../../../../../../wavevector.md) without a reciprocal-lattice shift, $\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2=\mathbf k_3$. An [Umklapp process](../../../../../../umklapp-scattering.md) instead has

$$
\boxed{\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2=\mathbf k_3+\mathbf G,\qquad \mathbf G\ne0}.
$$

The crystal can absorb the reciprocal-lattice [momentum](../../../../../../momentum-split.md); after reduction to the first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) the [phonon](../../../../../../phonon.md) drift transporting heat is relaxed. A normal process redistributes [phonons](../../../../../../phonon.md) while preserving their total [crystal momentum](../../../../../../crystal-momentum.md), so it is not by itself the same resistive mechanism. [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) must also be conserved in either process.

At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), large-wavevector [phonons](../../../../../../phonon.md) are rare, suppressing [Umklapp scattering](../../../../../../umklapp-scattering.md). Boundary and defect scattering then limit the [mean free path](../../../../../../mean-free-path.md); with a nearly constant boundary-limited path, $\kappa_{\rm ph}\simeq(C_{\rm ph}/V)v\ell/3$ grows as $T^3$. As [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) increases, [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) rises but [Umklapp scattering](../../../../../../umklapp-scattering.md) shortens $\ell$, often producing a [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) maximum. At sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) a simple anharmonic model has $C_{\rm ph}$ nearly constant and $\tau_U^{-1}\propto T$, giving $\kappa_{\rm ph}\propto T^{-1}$. These are regime estimates, not a universal law for every material or size.
