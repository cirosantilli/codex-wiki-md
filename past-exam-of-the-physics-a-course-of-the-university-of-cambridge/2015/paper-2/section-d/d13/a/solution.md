<h1 id="section-d/d13/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a small longitudinal disturbance, let the [equilibrium](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) pressure and [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) be $p_0,\rho_0$, their perturbations be $p',\rho'$, and the fluid [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) be $v$. Linearizing [conservation of mass](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) and the inviscid momentum equation in one dimension gives

$$
\partial_t\rho'=-\rho_0\partial_xv,\qquad
\rho_0\partial_tv=-\partial_xp'.
$$

When compression is adiabatic, the [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) gives $p'=c_s^2\rho'$ with $c_s^2=(\partial p/\partial\rho)_s$. Differentiating and eliminating $v$ yields

$$
\partial_t^2\rho'=c_s^2\partial_x^2\rho',\qquad
\boxed{c_s=\sqrt{\gamma p_0/\rho_0}=\sqrt{\gamma RT/M_{\rm mol}}}
$$

for an [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) with heat-capacity ratio $\gamma=C_p/C_v$ and molar [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $M_{\rm mol}$. Compression raises [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) as well as density, explaining the factor $\gamma$ compared with the isothermal value $\sqrt{p_0/\rho_0}$.

A rightgoing plane [sound wave](../../../../../../acoustic-wave.md) has pressure and particle [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) in phase, $p'=\rho_0c_sv$, and mean [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) $\langle p'v\rangle=\widehat p^{\,2}/(2\rho_0c_s)=\rho_0c_s\widehat v^{\,2}/2$ for peak amplitudes. Particle [displacement](../../../../../../displacement.md) is a quarter-cycle out of phase with particle [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md): particles oscillate locally while the compression pattern transports [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md). This continuum linear description requires [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) much larger than the [mean free path](../../../../../../mean-free-path.md), small pressure perturbations and negligible viscous attenuation. Adiabatic propagation requires heat exchange across the compressed region to be slow on the oscillation time; strong thermal coupling would instead favour an isothermal response.
