<h1 id="section-d/d13/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For propagation along $z$, a [transverse wave](../../../../../../transverse-wave.md) has two perpendicular [displacement](../../../../../../displacement.md) components. With $\phi=kz-\omega t$, write $u_x=A\cos\phi$, $u_y=B\cos(\phi+\delta)$. Their trajectory at a fixed point obeys

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{u_x}{A}\right)^2+\left(\frac{u_y}{B}\right)^2
-2\frac{u_xu_y}{AB}\cos\delta=\sin^2\delta.}
$$

The general nondegenerate trajectory is an ellipse: [elliptical polarization](../../../../../../elliptical-polarization.md). For $\delta=0$ or $\pi$, the components remain in phase or antiphase and the trajectory collapses to a line. A single nonzero component is also linearly polarized. Equal amplitudes $A=B$ with $\delta=\pm\pi/2$ give [circular polarization](../../../../../../circular-polarization.md). Unequal quadrature amplitudes give an ellipse aligned with the chosen axes, while general [phase differences](../../../../../../phase-difference.md) rotate its axes.

![](../../../../../../past-exam-of-the-physics-a-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2015/paper-2-polarization.png)

**[Figure 6](#section-d/d13/c/image-linear-circular-and-elliptical-transverse-wave-trajectories-at-a-fixed-spatial-point). Linear, circular and elliptical transverse-wave trajectories at a fixed spatial point**.

For the displayed convention and $\delta=+\pi/2$, at $z=0$ the vector starts at $+x$ and moves towards $+y$ as time increases. Reversing $\delta$ reverses its rotation. Right- and left-handed labels require a stated viewing and phase convention, so rotation is defined explicitly here. A fixed ellipse is a coherent relationship between transverse components; unpolarized light instead has fluctuating orientation and phase relationships on the measurement timescale.

A linear polarizer transmits a linearly polarized beam with [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) proportional to $\cos^2\theta$, allowing its direction to be measured by an analyser. [circular polarization](../../../../../../circular-polarization.md) transmits equal [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) through any linear analyser, but that test alone does not distinguish it from unpolarized light. A [quarter-wave plate](../../../../../../quarter-wave-plate.md) converts a suitable coherent circular state to [linear polarization](../../../../../../linear-polarization.md), allowing the combination to discriminate them. For light the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) defines the usual polarization; the [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) remains perpendicular to it and to propagation. Longitudinal sound in a homogeneous gas lacks the two transverse degrees of freedom needed for these polarization states.
