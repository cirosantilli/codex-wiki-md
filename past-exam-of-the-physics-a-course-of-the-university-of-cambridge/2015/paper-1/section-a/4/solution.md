<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a fixed [magnitude zero point](../../../../../magnitude-zero-point.md), [photometric magnitude uncertainty](../../../../../photometric-magnitude-uncertainty.md) follows from [propagation of uncertainty](../../../../../propagation-of-uncertainty.md)

$$
\frac{dm}{df}=-\frac{2.5}{f\ln10},\qquad
\sigma_m=\frac{2.5}{\ln10}\frac{\sigma_f}{f}
=\boxed{0.1086\ \mathrm{mag}}.
$$

This is the absolute [standard uncertainty](../../../../../standard-uncertainty.md) of the [apparent magnitude](../../../../../apparent-magnitude.md). The requested fractional uncertainty is instead

$$
\boxed{\frac{\sigma_m}{|m|}=\frac{0.1086}{|m|}
=\frac{0.1}{|\ln(f/f_0)|}}.
$$

This is the requested fractional [standard uncertainty](../../../../../standard-uncertainty.md) as a function of $m$; **there is no single numerical fractional error independent of the magnitude**. At $m=0$ it is undefined. Moreover, its value changes when the [magnitude zero point](../../../../../magnitude-zero-point.md) changes; the absolute magnitude uncertainty is the useful invariant of such a zero-point shift. Treating the stated percentage as a finite perturbation rather than a [standard uncertainty](../../../../../standard-uncertainty.md) gives changes $-0.1035$ mag for $f\mapsto1.1f$ and $+0.1144$ mag for $f\mapsto0.9f$, consistent with the linear estimate.
