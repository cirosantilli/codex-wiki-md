<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In the [position representation in quantum mechanics](../../../../../position-representation-in-quantum-mechanics.md), $\hat{\mathbf p}=-i\hbar\nabla$. Therefore the [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) operator gives

$$
\hat L_z=\hat x\hat p_y-\hat y\hat p_x=-i\hbar(x\partial_y-y\partial_x).
$$

At fixed $r,\theta$, the [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) obey $\partial_\phi x=-y$, $\partial_\phi y=x$, and $\partial_\phi z=0$. The [chain rule](../../../../../chain-rule.md) consequently gives $\partial_\phi=-y\partial_x+x\partial_y$, hence

$$
\boxed{\hat L_z=-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}}.
$$

The derivative holds the other two [spherical coordinates](../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) fixed; it is not a derivative along an arbitrary spatial curve.
