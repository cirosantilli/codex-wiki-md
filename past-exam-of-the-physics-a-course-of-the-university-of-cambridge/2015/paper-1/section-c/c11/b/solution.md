<h1 id="section-c/c11/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) generates spatial rotations and is $\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p$. With $\mathbf p=-i\hbar\nabla$, its components satisfy

$$
[L_i,L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}L_k,\qquad [L^2,L_i]=0.
$$

Thus $L^2$ and one component, conventionally $L_z$, have simultaneous [eigenstates](../../../../../../eigenstate.md), while all three components cannot generally have sharp values. On ordinary single-valued spatial [wavefunctions](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md),

$$
L^2Y_{\ell m}=\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)Y_{\ell m},\qquad
L_zY_{\ell m}=\hbar mY_{\ell m},
\quad\ell=0,1,\ldots,\quad m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell.
$$

The [spherical harmonics](../../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) form an [orthonormal](../../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) angular [basis](../../../../../../basis.md). The integer restriction is specific to orbital [wavefunctions](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md); half-integer spin is an intrinsic degree of freedom, not the angular dependence of a scalar spatial [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md).

The [angular momentum ladder operators](../../../../../../angular-momentum-ladder-operator.md) obey

$$
L_\pm Y_{\ell m}=\hbar\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)-m(m\pm1)}\,Y_{\ell,m\pm1}.
$$

The ladder ends at $m=\pm\ell$, producing $2\ell+1$ states. A state with specified $m$ has a sharp projection $\hbar m$, but the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) magnitude is $\hbar\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}$, not $\hbar\ell$. No classical picture of a vector with simultaneously fixed Cartesian components reproduces all these measurement properties.

In a [central potential](../../../../../../central-potential.md), [rotational symmetry](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md) gives $[H,L_i]=0$. [separation of variables](../../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) yields $\psi(\mathbf r)=R_\ell(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$. The radial [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) includes a [centrifugal potential](../../../../../../centrifugal-potential.md) $\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2mr^2)$, which suppresses small-radius amplitude for nonzero $\ell$. The [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is independent of $m$ when [rotational symmetry](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md) is intact, but need not be independent of $\ell$. A [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) can break that degeneracy. Spatial inversion gives $Y_{\ell m}(-\widehat{\mathbf r})=(-1)^\ell Y_{\ell m}(\widehat{\mathbf r})$, connecting [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) to [parity](../../../../../../parity.md) and to the even/odd rotational restriction for the [hydrogen](../../../../../../hydrogen.md) spin isomers.
