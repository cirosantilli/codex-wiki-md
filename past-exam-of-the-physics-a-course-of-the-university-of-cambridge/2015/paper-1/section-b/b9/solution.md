<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

**Exchange constraint.** Exchange of two [identical particles](../../../../../indistinguishable-particles.md) cannot change physical predictions. For the usual three-dimensional bosonic or fermionic state space, the [exchange operator](../../../../../exchange-operator.md) obeys $P_{12}^2=1$, so its allowed [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $\pm1$. [bosons](../../../../../boson.md) use the symmetric overall [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), $P_{12}\Psi=+\Psi$; [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) use the antisymmetric overall [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md), $P_{12}\Psi=-\Psi$. Exchange includes the spatial coordinates and all internal labels. The identity $P_{12}^2=1$ alone does not prove which particle species has which statistics; that assignment is the [Spin-statistics theorem](../../../../../spin-statistics-theorem.md).

**Factored [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) states.** If $\Psi=\psi_{\rm space}\chi_{\rm spin}$ and both factors have definite exchange symmetry, their exchange signs must multiply to $-1$. Thus a symmetric spatial factor requires an antisymmetric spin factor, and an antisymmetric spatial factor requires a symmetric spin factor. For two spin-one-half particles, the former spin factor is the singlet $(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$; the latter factors are the three [triplet states](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). A more general entangled spin-space state need not factor, but must still be antisymmetric overall.

**[hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) spin isomers.** The two [protons](../../../../../proton.md) are identical spin-one-half [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md). [parahydrogen](../../../../../parahydrogen.md) has total [nuclear spin](../../../../../nuclear-spin.md) $I_{\rm n}=0$, the antisymmetric singlet; [orthohydrogen](../../../../../orthohydrogen.md) has $I_{\rm n}=1$, the symmetric triplet. Here $I_{\rm n}$ is a spin quantum number, distinct from the [moment of inertia](../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) used below.

**Rotational restriction.** In the electronic and vibrational [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md), these two factors are symmetric under nuclear exchange. Exchanging the [protons](../../../../../proton.md) reverses the internuclear axis, $(\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\pi-\theta,\phi+\pi)$. The [spherical harmonic](../../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) rotational state contributes the exchange sign $(-1)^\ell$. Overall antisymmetry therefore requires

$$
(-1)^\ell(-1)=-1\quad\hbox{for <parahydrogen>},\qquad
(-1)^\ell(+1)=-1\quad\hbox{for <orthohydrogen>}.
$$

Hence **[parahydrogen](../../../../../parahydrogen.md) has even $\ell$; [orthohydrogen](../../../../../orthohydrogen.md) has odd $\ell$**. The $\ell$ here labels [molecular rotation](../../../../../molecular-rotation.md), rather than the [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) obtained by also coupling the [nuclear spins](../../../../../nuclear-spin.md).

**Conversion [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md).** The [ortho-para conversion heat](../../../../../ortho-para-conversion-heat.md) follows from the [linear rigid rotor](../../../../../linear-rigid-rotor.md) approximation, $E_\ell=\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2I)$ with $I=\mu r^2\simeq m_pr^2/2$. Thus conversion of one [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md) from $\ell=1$ to $\ell=0$ releases

$$
\Delta E=\frac{\hbar^2}{I}=\frac{2\hbar^2}{m_pr^2}
\simeq2.43\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm J.
$$

Neglecting the small [electron](../../../../../electron.md) contribution to the molecular mass, a sample of mass $M$ contains $M/(2m_p)$ [molecules](../../../../../molecule-split.md). Only the initial [orthohydrogen](../../../../../orthohydrogen.md) fraction releases this [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), giving

$$
Q=\frac34\frac{M}{2m_p}\frac{2\hbar^2}{m_pr^2}
=\frac{3M\hbar^2}{4m_p^2r^2}
=\boxed{5.44\times10^5\ \mathrm J\quad(M=1\,\mathrm{kg})}.
$$

The estimate uses $r=74$ pm, $m_p\simeq1.67262\times10^{-27}$ kg and $\hbar\simeq1.05457\times10^{-34}$ J s. Handbook-level rounding and the [rigid rotor](../../../../../rigid-rotor.md) approximation justify about three significant figures, not a high-precision thermochemical prediction.[recommended-value entries: proton mass and reduced Planck constant](../../../../../codata-recommended-values-of-the-fundamental-physical-constants-2022-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)

**Storage consequence.** The internal conversion is a delayed [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) source even in a well insulated container, causing warming or additional evaporation of the liquid. Insulation reduces external [heat transfer](../../../../../heat-transfer.md) but does not remove this source. Converting to predominantly [parahydrogen](../../../../../parahydrogen.md) during liquefaction, while removing the released [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md), reduces the subsequent storage [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) load. No evaporation fraction can be calculated without a [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md) and a model for how the [heat](../../../../../heat-split.md) is removed.
