<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

The three [primitive lattice translation vectors](../../../../../primitive-lattice-translation-vector.md) join a cube corner to the three adjacent face centres. Thus the [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is a [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md), with conventional cubic side $a$.

Write the given [primitive lattice translation vectors](../../../../../primitive-lattice-translation-vector.md) as $\mathbf a_1,\mathbf a_2,\mathbf a_3$. Their [primitive unit cell](../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) [volume](../../../../../volume.md) is

$$
\Omega=\mathbf a_1\cdot(\mathbf a_2\times\mathbf a_3)=\frac{a^3}{4},\qquad
\mathbf a_2\times\mathbf a_3=\frac{a^2}{4}(-1,1,1).
$$

The [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) convention $\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}$ then gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbf b_1&=\frac{2\pi}{a}(-1,1,1),\\
\mathbf b_2&=\frac{2\pi}{a}(1,-1,1),\\
\mathbf b_3&=\frac{2\pi}{a}(1,1,-1).
\end{aligned}}
$$

Each [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) vector has units $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ when $a$ is in metres. Integer combinations generate a [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) of conventional cubic side $4\pi/a$; the point $(2\pi/a)(1,1,1)$ is its body centre. Directly taking the nine [dot products](../../../../../dot-product.md) checks the signs and the $2\pi$ normalization.
