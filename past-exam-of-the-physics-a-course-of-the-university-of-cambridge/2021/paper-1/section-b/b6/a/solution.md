<h1 id="section-b/b6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The total [angular momentum operator](../../../../../../angular-momentum-operator-split.md) is $\mathbf J=\mathbf L+\mathbf S$. Since orbital and spin operators act on different factors of the [tensor product](../../../../../../tensor-product.md), they commute, and expanding $J^2$ gives

$$
\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S=\frac12(J^2-L^2-S^2).
$$

A coupled [angular momentum eigenstate](../../../../../../angular-momentum-eigenstate.md) $|l,s;j,m_j\rangle$ is a simultaneous [eigenstate](../../../../../../eigenstate.md) of these three squared operators, with [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\hbar^2j(j+1)$, $\hbar^2l(l+1)$ and $\hbar^2s(s+1)$. Thus the angular factor in the [spin-orbit interaction](../../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) is the number

$$
\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S\longmapsto
\frac{\hbar^2}{2}[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)].
$$

The other factor, $r^{-1}dV/dr$, is radial. It commutes with the angular operators, and a central-potential [energy eigenstate](../../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) separates into a radial [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) and an angular spinor. Taking its radial [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\langle H_1\rangle=\frac{\hbar^2}{4m^2c^2}
[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)]
\left\langle\frac1r\frac{dV}{dr}\right\rangle.}
$$

This [spin-orbit energy shift in a central potential](../../../../../../spin-orbit-energy-shift-in-a-central-potential.md) is independent of $m_j$, so rotational symmetry preserves the $2j+1$ magnetic degeneracy within a fixed $j$ multiplet. It is a first-order [fine structure](../../../../../../fine-structure.md) correction; the given coefficient already includes the relativistic factor needed for the electron's spin-orbit coupling.
