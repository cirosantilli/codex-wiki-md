<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [Stern-Gerlach experiment](../../../../../stern-gerlach-experiment.md) sends a collimated beam through an inhomogeneous [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) and then measures its transverse position after a drift region. The [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) is electrically neutral but has a nonzero [magnetic moment](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) proportional to its [spin](../../../../../spin.md), $\boldsymbol\mu=\gamma_n\mathbf S$. A predominantly $z$-directed field gives the interaction [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) and transverse [force](../../../../../force-split.md)

$$
H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B,\qquad
F_z=-\partial_zH_{\mathrm{int}}\simeq\mu_z\frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}.
$$

Since a neutron has [spin one-half](../../../../../spin-one-half.md), its spin component along the local field has only two [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md), $S_z=\pm\hbar/2$. Hence there are two opposite force branches, corresponding to $\mu_z=\pm\gamma_n\hbar/2$, and a beam containing both spin components gives two displaced detector spots. This is [neutron Stern-Gerlach splitting](../../../../../neutron-stern-gerlach-splitting.md), not an electric-charge [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md). A uniform [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) can cause [Larmor precession](../../../../../larmor-precession.md) but does not provide the required separating [magnetic field gradient](../../../../../magnetic-field-gradient.md).

**[Figure 1](#section-a/3/image-an-ideal-neutron-stern-gerlach-arrangement-separates-two-magnetic-moment-projections). An ideal neutron Stern-Gerlach arrangement separates two magnetic-moment projections**. Original functional schematic: collimating slits, a region with a [magnetic field gradient](../../../../../magnetic-field-gradient.md), and a position-sensitive neutron detector. The two curves represent the two allowed magnetic-moment branches, not a continuous distribution of classical spin orientations. Geometry and deflections are illustrative.

The neutron's [gyromagnetic ratio](../../../../../gyromagnetic-ratio.md) is negative, so its [magnetic moment](../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md) points opposite to its [spin](../../../../../spin.md); this reverses which spin label goes to which spot without changing the two-way splitting. Equal populations occur for an initially unpolarized beam. A beam prepared entirely in one field-axis [spin eigenstate](../../../../../spin-eigenstate.md) gives only its corresponding branch, explaining the qualification “in general”.
