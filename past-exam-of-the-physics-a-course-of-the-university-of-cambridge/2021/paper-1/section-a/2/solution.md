<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Inside the [rectangular potential barrier](../../../../../rectangular-potential-barrier.md), the electron's [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) is evanescent, with decay constant

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m_e(V_0-E)}}\hbar
=\frac{\sqrt{2m_e(2\,\mathrm{eV})}}\hbar
\simeq7.25\,\mathrm{nm}^{-1}.
$$

For width $a=1\,\mathrm{nm}$, the squared exponential suppression in the [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) [probability](../../../../../probability.md) is

$$
\boxed{T\sim e^{-2\kappa a}=e^{-14.49}\simeq5.1\times10^{-7}.}
$$

This is the usual leading-exponential estimate. The [rectangular-barrier tunnelling probability](../../../../../rectangular-barrier-tunnelling-probability.md), retaining the interface-matching prefactor for equal potentials on the two sides, is

$$
T=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}
\simeq\frac{16E(V_0-E)}{V_0^2}e^{-2\kappa a}.
$$

Here that prefactor is $16(8)(2)/10^2=2.56$, so the more accurate result is $\boxed{T\simeq1.30\times10^{-6}}$. Both estimates show that transmission is of order one part in a million. A classical particle with this [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) cannot cross the barrier at all.
