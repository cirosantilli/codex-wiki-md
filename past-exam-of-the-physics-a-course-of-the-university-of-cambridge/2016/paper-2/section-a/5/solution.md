<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The [effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) follows from the curvature of the [electronic band structure](../../../../../electronic-band-structure.md). Differentiating twice gives

$$
\frac{dE}{dk}=\frac{\hbar^2}{m_ea}\sin ka,\qquad
\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}=\frac{\hbar^2}{m_e}\cos ka,
\qquad m_e^*(k)=\frac{m_e}{\cos ka}.
$$

At either first [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md) boundary $k=\pm\pi/a$,

$$
\boxed{m_e^*=-m_e.}
$$

The boundary is a band maximum, with negative curvature and zero [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md). In the semiclassical single-band description, an applied [force](../../../../../force-split.md) changes $\hbar k$; the resulting [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) is opposite the [force](../../../../../force-split.md) near that maximum. This is a band-response coefficient, not a negative microscopic [electron](../../../../../electron.md) rest mass. A nearly filled band is often described more conveniently through positively charged [electron holes](../../../../../electron-hole.md) near its maximum.
