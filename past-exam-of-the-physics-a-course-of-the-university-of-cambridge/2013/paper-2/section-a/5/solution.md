<h1 id="section-a/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

In the [Hall effect](../../../../../hall-effect.md), the [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) deflects drifting [charge carriers](../../../../../charge-carrier.md) until a transverse [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) balances it: $\mathbf E_H=-\mathbf v_d\times\mathbf B$. Choose current along $x$, field along $z$ and define $R_H=E_y/(j_xB_z)$. Since $j_x=nqv_x$,

$$
\boxed{R_H=\frac1{nq},\qquad n=\frac1{e|R_H|}\quad\text{for electron carriers}}.
$$

The sign is negative for [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) with this convention. If the sample's [mass density](../../../../../density.md) is $\rho_m$ and its [molar mass](../../../../../molar-mass.md) is $M$, the atomic [number density](../../../../../number-density.md) is $n_{\rm at}=\rho_mN_A/M$, so

$$
\boxed{z_{\rm free}=\frac n{n_{\rm at}}=\frac{M}{e|R_H|\rho_mN_A}}.
$$

Measure the transverse voltage, specimen thickness, current and [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md); reversing field and current helps remove longitudinal-voltage pickup and offsets. This [free-electron count from a Hall coefficient](../../../../../free-electron-count-from-a-hall-coefficient.md) assumes one approximately free carrier band. Multiple bands, unequal mobilities or a complicated [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) can make a [Hall coefficient](../../../../../hall-coefficient.md) differ from $-1/(ne)$.
