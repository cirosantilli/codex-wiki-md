<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For conventional cubic cell side $a$, the [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) has corner and face-centre sites, giving four lattice points per cell; the [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) has corners and a body centre, giving two. Their [primitive unit cells](../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) each contain one lattice point. With reciprocal vectors written $\mathbf G=(2\pi/a)(h,k,l)$, [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) allows $h,k,l$ all even or all odd, while [body-centred cubic](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) allows $h+k+l$ even. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{fcc}\longleftrightarrow\text{bcc in reciprocal space},\qquad a^*=4\pi/a}.
$$

Here $a^*$ is the side of the [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md)'s conventional cubic cell. The parity conditions describe the reciprocal point sets; reciprocal space contains [wavevectors](../../../../../wavevector.md), not a second arrangement of physical atoms.
