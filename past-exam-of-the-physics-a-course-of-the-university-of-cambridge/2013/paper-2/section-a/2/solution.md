<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A thin [antireflection coating](../../../../../antireflection-coating.md) makes [reflections](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) from its two interfaces interfere destructively near a chosen [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md), often in the green part of the visible spectrum. For a single lossless layer at [normal incidence](../../../../../normal-incidence.md), $n_fd=\lambda_0/4$ and ideally $n_f=\sqrt{n_0n_s}$ minimize [reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) at $\lambda_0$. [reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) is less completely cancelled in the red and blue, so **the residual reflected light can appear purple**. This is primarily a [reflection](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) colour, not a claim that the transmitted beam is strongly purple. Multilayer coatings can broaden the low-reflectance band.
