<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Sound compressions in air are approximately adiabatic, giving $c_s^2=(\partial p/\partial\rho)_s=\gamma p/\rho$. With $p=\rho RT/M$,

$$
\boxed{T=\frac{Mc_s^2}{\gamma R}\simeq255\ \mathrm K\simeq-18\ ^\circ\mathrm C}.
$$

Here dry air is approximated as a diatomic [ideal gas](../../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) with $\gamma=7/5$ and [molar mass](../../../../../molar-mass.md) $M\simeq0.029\ \mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$. The ideal-gas law alone is insufficient without this adiabatic response and mean molecular [mass](../../../../../mass.md); humidity and composition affect a more precise inference.
