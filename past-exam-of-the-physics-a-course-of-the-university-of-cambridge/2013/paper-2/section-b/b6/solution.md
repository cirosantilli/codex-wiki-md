<h1 id="section-b/b6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B6](../b6.md)

**[time average of harmonic power](../../../../../time-average-of-harmonic-power.md).** Expand the two [real parts](../../../../../real-part.md):

$$
\langle\operatorname{Re}F\operatorname{Re}u\rangle
=\frac14\langle Fu+Fu^*+F^*u+F^*u^*\rangle
=\boxed{\frac12\operatorname{Re}(Fu^*)}.
$$

The terms proportional to $e^{\pm2i\omega t}$ average to zero over complete periods, while $Fu^*$ is time-independent. For real peak amplitudes this is $F_0u_0\cos\phi/2$.

**String dynamics.** A short element of length $dx$ experiences transverse [force](../../../../../force-split.md) $T[\psi_x(x+dx)-\psi_x(x)]$. [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives $\rho\,dx\,\psi_{tt}=T\,dx\,\psi_{xx}$, or

$$
\boxed{\psi_{xx}=\frac1{v^2}\psi_{tt},\qquad v=\sqrt{T/\rho}}.
$$

The approximation is a flexible, lossless string with small slope and constant [tension](../../../../../tension.md); $\rho$ is its [linear mass density](../../../../../linear-mass-density.md).

**[energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) transport.** Across a cut toward increasing $x$, the transverse [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is $F_y=-T\psi_x$ and the transverse velocity is $u=\psi_t$. For a rightgoing wave these have [complex amplitudes](../../../../../complex-amplitude.md) $ikTA$ and $i\omega A$. Hence

$$
\boxed{Z=\frac Tv=\rho v=\sqrt{T\rho},\qquad
\langle P\rangle=\frac12Z\omega^2|A_0|^2}.
$$

This real [mechanical impedance](../../../../../mechanical-impedance.md) is [force](../../../../../force-split.md) divided by transverse velocity for a [travelling wave](../../../../../travelling-wave.md), with units $\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$.

**Matching and [power](../../../../../power.md) ratios.** At an ordinary massless junction without an external transverse [force](../../../../../force-split.md), displacement is continuous and transverse [tension](../../../../../tension.md) is balanced: $\psi_1=\psi_2$, $T_1\psi_{1x}=T_2\psi_{2x}$. For the same [tension](../../../../../tension.md), the slope is therefore continuous even if density changes. With real positive impedances,

$$
r_\psi=\frac{Z_1-Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2},\quad t_\psi=\frac{2Z_1}{Z_1+Z_2},
\qquad
\boxed{P_r=\frac{(Z_1-Z_2)^2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2}P_i,\quad
P_t=\frac{4Z_1Z_2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2}P_i}.
$$

Their sum equals $P_i$; the transmitted [power](../../../../../power.md) includes the impedance ratio and is not simply $t_\psi^2P_i$.
