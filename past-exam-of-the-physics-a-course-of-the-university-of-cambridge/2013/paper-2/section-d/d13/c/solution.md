<h1 id="section-d/d13/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $N$ uniformly illuminated identical slits of a [diffraction grating](../../../../../../diffraction-grating.md), with width $b$ and period $d$, the far-field amplitude is a single-slit transform times a finite phasor sum. At [normal incidence](../../../../../../normal-incidence.md), write $\beta=\pi d\sin\theta/\lambda$. Then

$$
\boxed{I(\theta)=I_{1,0}\left[\frac{\sin(\pi b\sin\theta/\lambda)}{\pi b\sin\theta/\lambda}\right]^2
\left(\frac{\sin N\beta}{\sin\beta}\right)^2}.
$$

Principal maxima obey $d\sin\theta=m\lambda$ and have $N^2$ times the single-slit intensity at that angle. Nearby first minima are separated from a principal maximum by $\delta\theta\simeq\lambda/(Nd\cos\theta_m)$, so more illuminated slits sharpen each order. The broad single-slit envelope suppresses high angles; an order is missing if its principal maximum coincides with an envelope zero.

Differentiating the [grating equation](../../../../../../grating-equation.md) gives angular dispersion $d\theta/d\lambda=m/(d\cos\theta_m)$. Comparing the separation of nearby [wavelengths](../../../../../../wavelength.md) with the first-minimum width yields the Rayleigh resolving [power](../../../../../../power.md)

$$
\boxed{\lambda/\delta\lambda=|m|N}.
$$

Only propagating orders with $|m\lambda|\leq d$ exist. Different spectral orders can overlap, and finite source-slit width, limited coherence, aberrations and grating errors worsen ideal resolution. A [spectrometer](../../../../../../spectrometer.md) therefore collimates light from an entrance slit and focuses each diffracted direction onto a detector; the slit image width and illuminated grating width must both support the desired spectral separation.
