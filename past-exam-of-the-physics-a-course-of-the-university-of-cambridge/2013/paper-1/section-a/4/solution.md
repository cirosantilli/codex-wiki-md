<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For the [squared discrepancy of a digit frequency](../../../../../squared-discrepancy-of-a-digit-frequency.md), write $d_N^2=(f_0-0.1)^2$. The plotted ordinates are $10^{-2}$, $4.00\times10^{-4}$, $1.69\times10^{-4}$, $4.90\times10^{-5}$, $3.60\times10^{-5}$ and $9.00\times10^{-6}$ in increasing $N$. Under an independent, equiprobable-digit model, the zero count is binomial and

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[d_N^2]=\operatorname{Var}(f_0)=\frac{0.1(0.9)}N=\frac{0.09}N}.
$$

Thus the expected log-log slope is **$-1$**. If “relative” means division by the expected fraction before squaring, every ordinate is multiplied by 100 and the prediction becomes $9/N$; the slope is unchanged. These are nested prefixes of one digit sequence, so the points are correlated, rounded observations, not independent [variance](../../../../../variance-split.md) estimates. Their rough trend supports a reciprocal-power scale but does not prove independence or that $\pi$ is a [normal number](../../../../../normal-number.md).
