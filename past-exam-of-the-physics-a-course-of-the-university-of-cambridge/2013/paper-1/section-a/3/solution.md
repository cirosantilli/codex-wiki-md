<h1 id="section-a/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [mass dependence of fixed-barrier tunnelling](../../../../../mass-dependence-of-fixed-barrier-tunnelling.md) follows by holding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and the potential-energy barrier fixed: [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) has the leading [WKB approximation](../../../../../wkb-approximation.md)

$$
T\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac2\hbar\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\sqrt{2m(V-E)}\,dx\right].
$$

The alpha-particle [mass](../../../../../mass.md) is about $7.3\times10^3$ [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [masses](../../../../../mass.md), so its decay exponent is about **85 times larger** for the same $V-E$ profile. Exponential suppression therefore makes transmission much smaller. This comparison holds the [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) and incident [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) fixed: the same electrostatic potential is not the same potential-energy barrier for charges of different magnitude and sign.
