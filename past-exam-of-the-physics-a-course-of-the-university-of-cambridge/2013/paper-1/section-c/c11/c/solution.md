<h1 id="section-c/c11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A black body absorbs all incident radiation; a small opening into an equilibrated opaque cavity approximates it because internal reflections strongly suppress escape without absorption. Equilibrium radiation has zero [photon chemical potential](../../../../../../photon-chemical-potential.md). Counting electromagnetic modes and assigning [mean](../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) occupation $(e^{h\nu/(k_BT)}-1)^{-1}$ gives the spectral [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) density

$$
\boxed{u_\nu(T)=\frac{8\pi h\nu^3}{c^3}\frac1{e^{h\nu/(k_BT)}-1}}.
$$

At low [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) this becomes the [Rayleigh-Jeans law](../../../../../../rayleigh-jeans-law.md), $u_\nu\simeq8\pi k_BT\nu^2/c^3$; using that limit at all [frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md) gives the [ultraviolet catastrophe](../../../../../../ultraviolet-catastrophe.md). At high [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) the exponential suppresses the spectrum. Quantized mode [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) resolves the classical divergence.

Integration gives $u=a_{\rm rad}T^4$. An isotropic radiation field sends outward flux $cu/4$ through a small aperture, so

$$
\boxed{M=\sigma T^4,\qquad\sigma=\frac{2\pi^5k_B^4}{15h^3c^2}}.
$$

Differentiating the [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) spectrum gives $\lambda_{\max}T\simeq2.90\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,K}$; the frequency-spectrum maximum is a different transformed peak, not simply $c/\lambda_{\max}$. A real surface has [emissivity](../../../../../../emissivity.md) less than one and possibly dependent on [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md). Cavity [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) uniformity, aperture geometry, calibrated detector response and background subtraction are necessary when testing the spectrum or inferring [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) from it.
