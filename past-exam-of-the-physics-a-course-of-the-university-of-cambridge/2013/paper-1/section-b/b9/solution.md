<h1 id="section-b/b9/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B9](../b9.md)

The [anisotropic quantum harmonic oscillator](../../../../../anisotropic-quantum-harmonic-oscillator.md) is a sum of three commuting one-coordinate oscillator [Hamiltonians](../../../../../hamiltonian.md). Substituting $\psi=X(x)Y(y)Z(z)$ and dividing by $XYZ$ gives a sum of three functions of independent coordinates, so each equals a separation constant. Consequently

$$
\psi_{n_xn_yn_z}=X_{n_x}Y_{n_y}Z_{n_z},\qquad
E=\hbar\sum_{i=x,y,z}\omega_i(n_i+1/2).
$$

For a time-independent operator, conservation for arbitrary states follows from $[H,Q]=0$, since $d\langle Q\rangle/dt=(i/\hbar)\langle[H,Q]\rangle$. The first labelled operator is $iL_z/\hbar$; the second is $-L^2/\hbar^2$. The [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is rotationally invariant. The potential is invariant under [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) about $z$ precisely when **(i) $\omega_x^2=\omega_y^2$**; full rotational invariance requires **(ii) $\omega_x^2=\omega_y^2=\omega_z^2$**. For positive oscillator [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) these are equality of the [frequencies](../../../../../frequency.md) themselves. Acting with the [commutators](../../../../../commutator.md) on arbitrary test functions excludes anisotropic potentials in case (ii).

In the isotropic case let $b=m\omega/\hbar$. Then

$$
\boxed{E_N=\hbar\omega(N+3/2),\quad N=n_x+n_y+n_z},\qquad
\psi_{100}=\sqrt2\,\frac{b^{5/4}}{\pi^{3/4}}\,x e^{-br^2/2},\qquad
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}.
$$

The polynomial $x$ is the first excited oscillator factor and the other two factors are ground-state [Gaussians](../../../../../normal-distribution.md). The first excited spatial level has three independent choices, proportional to $x,y,z$. Odd [parity](../../../../../parity.md) gives $\langle x\rangle=0$, and [Gaussian](../../../../../normal-distribution.md) integration yields

$$
\langle x^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2b},\qquad
\boxed{\Delta x=\sqrt{\frac{3\hbar}{2m\omega}}}.
$$

For [oscillator uncertainty and allocation of classical energy](../../../../../oscillator-uncertainty-and-allocation-of-classical-energy.md), the $x$ mode has [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $3\hbar\omega/2$, whose classical amplitude is $A_x=\sqrt{3\hbar/(m\omega)}$, giving $\Delta x=A_x/\sqrt2$. If “same [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md)” instead means assigning the entire three-dimensional [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $5\hbar\omega/2$ to one classical coordinate, its amplitude is $\sqrt{5\hbar/(m\omega)}$ and the ratio is $\sqrt{3/10}$. A general three-dimensional classical orbit's $x$ amplitude is not fixed by total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) without specifying its allocation among modes.

The [capacity of an isotropic oscillator shell](../../../../../capacity-of-an-isotropic-oscillator-shell.md), including the two [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [spin](../../../../../spin.md) states, is $(N+1)(N+2)$ [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). Nine non-interacting [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) occupy shells as $2,6,1$ for $N=0,1,2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{E_g=\left[2\frac32+6\frac52+\frac72\right]\hbar\omega=\frac{43}{2}\hbar\omega,\qquad
E_{\rm first\ higher}=\frac{45}{2}\hbar\omega}.
$$

The least positive excitation promotes an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) by one oscillator quantum. Rearrangements within the partly filled degenerate shell remain [ground states](../../../../../ground-state.md) and cost no [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md).
