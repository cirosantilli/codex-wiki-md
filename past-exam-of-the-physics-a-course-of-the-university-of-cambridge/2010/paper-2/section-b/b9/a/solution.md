<h1 id="section-b/b9/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Both particles can occupy $l=0$ with opposite [spins](../../../../../../spin.md), giving a unique [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md) at **$E=0$, [quantum degeneracy](../../../../../../degenerate-energy-levels.md) 1**. At the next distinct [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) $\epsilon_R$, one orbital is $l=0$ and the other is $l=+1$ or $-1$. For each orbital choice there is one symmetric spatial state paired with the [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md), and one antisymmetric spatial state paired with three [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) projections. Hence

$$
\boxed{g(E=\epsilon_R)=2(1+3)=8}.
$$

Swapping particle labels adds no independent state after the required exchange symmetry is imposed.
