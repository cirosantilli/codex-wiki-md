<h1 id="section-b/b9/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At coincidence $\phi_1=\phi_2$, the [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) spatial factor vanishes, while the lowest [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md) has finite, uniform pair [probability](../../../../../../probability.md). Consequently a weak short-range repulsion raises the [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md) [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) more strongly: **antisymmetry suppresses close encounters in the [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md)**. In the contact-interaction limit the [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) contribution is exactly zero, whereas the [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md) shift is positive. A finite-range interaction gives a nonzero but suppressed [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) shift. This explains a relative [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) increase; it does not by itself establish a crossing or justify using unchanged noninteracting [wavefunctions](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) for arbitrarily strong repulsion.
