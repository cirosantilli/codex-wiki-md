<h1 id="section-a/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

As an estimate, model the confinement by an [infinite square well](../../../../../infinite-square-well.md) of width $L$ and use the stipulated [electron](../../../../../electron.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md). Its [energy eigenvalues](../../../../../energy-eigenvalue-split.md) are $E_j=j^2\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$, $j=1,2,\ldots$, so the gap controlling [thermal occupation of a quantum well](../../../../../thermal-occupation-of-a-quantum-well.md) is

$$
\Delta E=E_2-E_1=\frac{3\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}
=1.81\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm J=11.3\,\mathrm{meV},
\qquad \boxed{\frac{\Delta E}{k_B}\simeq131\,\mathrm K}.
$$

Thus **$T\ll130\,\mathrm K$** suppresses excitation: $p_2/p_1=e^{-\Delta E/(k_BT)}$. For example $T\lesssim28\,\mathrm K$ makes this ratio below one percent in this model. There is no sharp cutoff. The [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) itself corresponds to about $44\,\mathrm K$, but it is the excitation gap that enters the [Boltzmann factor](../../../../../boltzmann-factor.md). Finite barrier height and a different [effective mass](../../../../../band-effective-mass.md) would change the numerical scale; neither is supplied.
