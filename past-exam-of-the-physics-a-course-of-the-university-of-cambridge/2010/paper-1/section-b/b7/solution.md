<h1 id="section-b/b7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B7](../b7.md)

A [dispersive wave](../../../../../dispersive-wave.md) has phase speed depending on frequency. Its [phase velocity](../../../../../phase-velocity.md) is $v_p=\omega/k$, whereas the [group velocity](../../../../../group-velocity.md) $v_g=d\omega/dk$ describes a narrow wave packet in a lossless single branch. Examples are deep-water gravity waves, $\omega^2=gk$, and electromagnetic waves in a cold plasma, $\omega^2=\omega_p^2+c^2k^2$.

The fixed-edge [membrane waveguide](../../../../../membrane-waveguide.md) has transverse profiles $\sin(n\pi y/b)$ with

$$
\boxed{k_y=n\pi/b,\quad n=1,2,\ldots,\qquad
\omega_n^2=v^2[k_x^2+(n\pi/b)^2].}
$$

There is no nonzero n=0 displacement satisfying both fixed-edge conditions. Even though the unconfined membrane is nondispersive, transverse confinement makes propagation along x dispersive. The [membrane-guide phase and energy speeds](../../../../../membrane-guide-phase-and-energy-speeds.md) are, for a propagating mode,

$$
\boxed{v_{p,x}=\frac\omega{k_x},\quad v_{g,x}=\frac{v^2k_x}{\omega},\quad
v_{p,x}v_{g,x}=v^2,\qquad f_{c,n}=\frac{nv}{2b}.}
$$

For positive $k_x$, the phase speed exceeds v while group speed is below v. At cutoff $k_x=0$ the formal phase speed diverges, but group speed vanishes; no finite longitudinal energy flux is implied.

With the supplied width and speed, $f_{c,n}=10n\,\mathrm{kHz}$. At15kHz only n=1 propagates:

$$
\boxed{k_x=\sqrt{(30\pi)^2-(20\pi)^2}=10\pi\sqrt5=70.2\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.}
$$

The negative value represents travel in the opposite direction; higher transverse modes are evanescent.
