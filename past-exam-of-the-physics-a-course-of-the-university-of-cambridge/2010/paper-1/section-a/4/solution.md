<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For hard spheres of radius r in a [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md), contact occurs along a face diagonal: $4r=\sqrt2a$. Four spheres belong to a conventional cell, so the [hard-sphere cubic packing fractions](../../../../../hard-sphere-cubic-packing-fractions.md) give

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\rm FCC}=\frac{4(4\pi r^3/3)}{a^3}=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2}=0.7405.}
$$

For a [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md), contact occurs along a body diagonal, $4r=\sqrt3a$, and there are two spheres per conventional cell:

$$
\boxed{\eta_{\rm BCC}=\frac{2(4\pi r^3/3)}{a^3}=\frac{\sqrt3\pi}{8}=0.6802.}
$$

Their ratio $8/(3\sqrt6)=1.0887>1$ proves that FCC packs more densely. Coordination alone would not establish the occupied volume fraction.
