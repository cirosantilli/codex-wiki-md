<h1 id="section-a/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

For the [adjacent reflected minima of an air-film-air layer](../../../../../adjacent-reflected-minima-of-an-air-film-air-layer.md) in [thin-film interference](../../../../../thin-film-interference.md), reflection at the front surface acquires a phase reversal, while reflection at the rear does not. Near normal incidence the reflected minima therefore obey $2nt=m\lambda$ for integral m. A single intermediate maximum means that the two stated minima are consecutive orders:

$$
\frac{2nt}{500\,\mathrm{nm}}-\frac{2nt}{600\,\mathrm{nm}}=1.
$$

Thus $2nt=3000\,\mathrm{nm}$, with orders6 and5, and

$$
\boxed{t=\frac{3000}{2(1.3)}\,\mathrm{nm}=1.15\,\mu\mathrm m.}
$$

The intermediate reflected maximum is at $\lambda=3000/5.5\simeq545\,\mathrm{nm}$. This assumes negligible dispersion of the supplied refractive index and an air environment on both sides.
