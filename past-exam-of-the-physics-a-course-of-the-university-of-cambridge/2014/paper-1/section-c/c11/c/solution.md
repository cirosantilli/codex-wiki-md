<h1 id="section-c/c11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[electron spin](../../../../../../electron-spin.md) is intrinsic [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md), independent of an orbital [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md)'s $\mathbf r\times\mathbf p$. It has $s=1/2$, so

$$
\boxed{S^2=\frac34\hbar^2,\qquad S_z=\pm\frac\hbar2,\qquad
S_i=\frac\hbar2\sigma_i,\qquad[S_i,S_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}S_k.}
$$

A spatial orbital therefore carries a two-component [spinor](../../../../../../spinor.md) degree of freedom. Noncommuting components do not describe an ordinary [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) with simultaneously definite projections; measurements along one axis give only the two allowed values. Even a spherically symmetric $l=0$ orbital retains [electron spin](../../../../../../electron-spin.md).

The associated magnetic moment is $\boldsymbol\mu=-g_e e\mathbf S/(2m_e)$ with $e>0$ and $g_e\simeq2$. A [spinor](../../../../../../spinor.md) changes sign under a $2\pi$ [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) and returns under $4\pi$; that sign is observable only relative to another coherent [amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md), not as an isolated [global phase](../../../../../../global-phase.md). A literal classically rotating charged sphere does not account for this fixed half-integer [representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md), component algebra and [spinor](../../../../../../spinor.md) [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) law. Classical [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) provides useful analogies for torque and precession, but no ordinary classical orientation reproduces the full intrinsic quantum [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) structure.
