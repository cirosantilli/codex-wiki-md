<h1 id="section-a/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Let $D$ denote detection. Weight each incident fraction by its [detection efficiency](../../../../../detection-efficiency.md), not by its loss [probability](../../../../../probability.md). [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../bayes-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{P(H\mid D)=\frac{0.05(1-0.50)}{0.65(1-0.05)+0.30(1-0.20)+0.05(1-0.50)}
=\frac{10}{353}\simeq0.0283.}
$$

Thus about **2.83%** of detected particles are in the high-energy group. Energy-dependent [detector dead time](../../../../../detector-dead-time.md) changes the composition of the observed sample.
