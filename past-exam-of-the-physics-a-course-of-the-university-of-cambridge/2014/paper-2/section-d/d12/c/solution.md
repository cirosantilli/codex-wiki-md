<h1 id="section-d/d12/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [Michelson interferometer](../../../../../../michelson-interferometer.md) divides light at a [beam splitter](../../../../../../beam-splitter.md), sends it to two mirrors, and recombines the returned beams. A compensating plate can equalize [glass](../../../../../../glass.md) traversal. For approximately axial rays the [optical path difference](../../../../../../optical-path-difference.md) is $\Delta=2(n_1L_1-n_2L_2)$, plus fixed instrument phase, and

$$
I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos(2\pi\Delta/\lambda_0+\phi_0)
$$

for coherent, mode-matched fields. Moving one mirror by $\delta L$ in air changes the round-trip path by $2\delta L$, so **one fringe corresponds to $\lambda_0/2$ mirror motion**. It measures displacement, [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) and refractive-index changes; scanning the delay enables Fourier-transform spectroscopy. Mirror alignment, [polarization](../../../../../../polarization-waves.md) overlap, vibration and source coherence control visibility. A finite coherence length limits useful arm mismatch.
