<h1 id="section-c/c11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C11](../c11.md)

For two [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) per primitive cell,

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_F=\frac{\hbar^2k_F^2}{2m_e}
=\frac{2\pi\hbar^2}{m_ea^2}
\simeq2.99\ \mathrm{eV}}.
$$

Set $A=\hbar^2\pi^2/(2m_ea^2)=2.350\ \mathrm{eV}$. In the [indirect gap in a uniformly split zone-boundary model](../../../../../indirect-gap-in-a-uniformly-split-zone-boundary-model.md) approximation stipulated here, the free boundary [energies](../../../../../energy-split.md) are $A$ along $[10]$ at $X=(\pi/a,0)$ and $2A$ along $[11]$ at $M=(\pi/a,\pi/a)$. Opening a symmetric gap gives

$$
\boxed{E_{X,\pm}=A\pm E_g/2=1.85,\ 2.85\ \mathrm{eV}},
\qquad
\boxed{E_{M,\pm}=2A\pm E_g/2=4.20,\ 5.20\ \mathrm{eV}}.
$$

The branches bend away from the free-electron crossings and flatten at their boundary extrema. Importantly, the upper band near $X$ lies below the lower band's corner [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md):

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm indirect}\simeq E_{X,+}-E_{M,-}=E_g-A=-1.35\ \mathrm{eV}<0}.
$$

Therefore **the crystal is metallic despite its positive boundary gaps**; [electron](../../../../../electron.md) and [hole](../../../../../electron-hole.md) occupations redistribute across overlapping bands. [positive direct gaps can coexist with band overlap](../../../../../positive-direct-gaps-can-coexist-with-band-overlap.md).

A microscopic square-lattice corner has additional plane-wave degeneracy. The quoted symmetric boundary numbers use the question's directional gap model, not an unspecified four-state corner Hamiltonian. For the [indirect gap of a separable square cosine potential](../../../../../indirect-gap-of-a-separable-square-cosine-potential.md), a separable weak potential with the same face gap gives the lowest corner [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $2A-E_g=3.70\ \mathrm{eV}$, still above the $2.85\ \mathrm{eV}$ upper-band face [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). This refinement preserves the metallic conclusion.
