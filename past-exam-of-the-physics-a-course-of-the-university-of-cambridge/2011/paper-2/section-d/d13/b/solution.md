<h1 id="section-d/d13/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [open-loop gain](../../../../../../open-loop-gain.md) is large and roughly constant at low [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md), then falls as internal [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) become important. A dominant-pole approximation is

$$
\boxed{A(f)\simeq\frac{A_0}{1+if/f_p}}.
$$

Above $f_p$, but before higher [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) dominate, $|A|\propto f^{-1}$, a slope of $-20\,\mathrm{dB}$ per decade, with increasing [phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) lag. The approximate unity-gain [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) is $f_T=A_0f_p$. A 741-class amplifier has typical low-frequency gain about $2\times10^5$; this is the [open-loop gain](../../../../../../open-loop-gain.md), distinct from the feedback-set signal gain. The finite-gain formula at the parent level explains why the closed-loop response eventually rolls off.[§6.5, p. 5, large-signal voltage-gain row](../../../../../../lm741-operational-amplifier-by-texas-instruments.md)
