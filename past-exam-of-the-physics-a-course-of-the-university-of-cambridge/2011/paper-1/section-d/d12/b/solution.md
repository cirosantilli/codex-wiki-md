<h1 id="section-d/d12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A finite [optical pupil](../../../../../../optical-pupil.md) spreads a point into a [point spread function](../../../../../../point-spread-function-split.md). In [incoherent imaging](../../../../../../incoherent-imaging.md) an extended [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) distribution is convolved with that function; its [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform-split.md) is the [optical transfer function](../../../../../../optical-transfer-function.md), which attenuates and ultimately removes high spatial [frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md). For an ideal circular pupil the point response is an [Airy disk](../../../../../../airy-disk.md). The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) for two equally bright incoherent points is

$$
\boxed{\theta_R\simeq1.22\lambda/D},\qquad \Delta x_R\simeq1.22\lambda f/D.
$$

A microscope's object-space scale is of order $\lambda/(2\,\mathrm{NA})$, with the numerical factor depending on illumination and the criterion; $\mathrm{NA}=n\sin\alpha$ measures the accepted ray cone. A larger pupil or shorter [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) improves the diffraction scale. Aberrations, [defocus](../../../../../../defocus-aberration.md), detector pixel sampling, motion, background and [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) noise can worsen practical performance. Merely magnifying the same blurred image adds no information. Coherent sources require amplitude [superposition](../../../../../../superposition-principle.md) instead of the incoherent [intensity](../../../../../../intensity-physics.md) rule. Rayleigh resolution is a convention for separating a pair, distinct from the precision of localizing one isolated point; information does not abruptly vanish below it.
