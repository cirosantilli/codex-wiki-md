<h1 id="section-a/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [free-electron Fermi circle in a square lattice](../../../../../../free-electron-fermi-circle-in-a-square-lattice.md), include two spin states per [wavevector](../../../../../../wavevector.md). A square cell of side $a$ supplies $z/a^2$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) per unit area; counting the disk of occupied states gives

$$
\frac{z}{a^2}=\frac{2\pi k_F^2}{(2\pi)^2},\qquad k_F=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi z}}a.
$$

The [first Brillouin zone](../../../../../../first-brillouin-zone.md) is $|k_x|,|k_y|\leq\pi/a$. For $z=1$, **$k_F=2.507/a$**, so the circular [Fermi surface](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) lies wholly inside this square and encloses half its area. The figure uses the stated free-electron approximation; the lattice symmetry alone does not prescribe an exact circular surface.
