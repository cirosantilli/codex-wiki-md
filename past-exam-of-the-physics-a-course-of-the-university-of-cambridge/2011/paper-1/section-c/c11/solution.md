<h1 id="section-c/c11/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C11](../c11.md)

For the [electronic and lattice heat-capacity crossover in a monovalent metal](../../../../../electronic-and-lattice-heat-capacity-crossover-in-a-monovalent-metal.md), to obtain a numerical estimate from $E_F$ alone, assume monovalent potassium is a three-dimensional [free electron gas](../../../../../free-electron-model.md) with a parabolic band. Including both spins, the molar [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) is $g(E_F)=3N_A/(2E_F)$. Using the supplied approximate coefficient $3$, rather than silently replacing it by $\pi^2/3$, gives

$$
C_{el}=\frac92R\frac{k_B T}{E_F}.
$$

Equating it to the stated low-temperature [Debye heat capacity](../../../../../debye-heat-capacity.md) yields the nonzero crossover

$$
\boxed{T=\left[\frac{15}{8\pi^4}\frac{k_B}{E_F}\theta_D^3\right]^{1/2}=0.911\,\mathrm K.}
$$

We used $k_B=8.61733\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV\,K^{-1}}$ and $E_F=2\,\mathrm{eV}$. This is self-consistently far below $\theta_D=100\,\mathrm K$ and the [Fermi temperature](../../../../../fermi-temperature.md). Using the exact Sommerfeld coefficient instead gives $0.954\,\mathrm K$; that is a change of approximation, not a discrepancy in the algebra. Without a band/DOS assumption, the supplied [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md) does not by itself determine $g(E_F)$.
