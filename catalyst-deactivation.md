# Catalyst deactivation

↑ **Parent:** [Catalysis](catalysis.md)

[Catalyst deactivation](catalyst-deactivation.md) is loss of catalytic activity during use. Regeneration in an ideal [catalytic cycle](catalytic-cycle.md) does not rule out a separate damaging [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md), blocking of a surface, or physical loss of [catalyst](catalyst.md) from the apparatus. A route can also be slowed by [diffusion](diffusion.md) to the surface or by accumulation of a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md). Such effects limit the usefulness of a low schematic [reaction energy barrier](reaction-energy-barrier.md): measured throughput depends on the whole [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) and on transport. Demonstrating regeneration requires more evidence than observing a faster first run. For a recovered solid [catalyst](catalyst.md), a useful further comparison is a second run with fresh [reactants](reactant.md), matched recovered amount, and the same mixing and [temperature](temperature-split.md), alongside a fresh-solid run and a blank. Loss during recovery or residual [reactants](reactant.md) can otherwise imitate [catalyst deactivation](catalyst-deactivation.md) or apparent reuse. This is a proposed control, not an observation reported for the [catalysed peroxide comparison](catalysed-peroxide-comparison.md).
