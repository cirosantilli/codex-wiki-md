# Convex function

↑ **Parent:** [Real analysis](real-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_function)

A [function](function-split.md) $f:C\to\mathbb R$ on a [convex set](convex-set-split.md) $C$ is a [convex function](convex-function.md) if, for every $x,y\in C$ and $t\in[0,1]$,

$$
f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y).
$$

Geometrically, a [line segment](line-segment.md) joining two points of its [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md) lies on or above that [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md). The [convex set](convex-set-split.md) condition ensures that the intermediate input stays in the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). An [affine function](affine-function.md) satisfies the defining [inequality](inequality-mathematics.md) with equality.

For the [quadratic function](quadratic-function.md) $f(x)=x^2$, the inputs $1$ and $3$ have equally weighted [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) $2$. Evaluating at the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) gives $f(2)=4$, whereas averaging the outputs gives $(f(1)+f(3))/2=5$. **Averaging first saves one unit in this example.** More generally, direct expansion gives

$$
tx^2+(1-t)y^2-[tx+(1-t)y]^2=t(1-t)(x-y)^2\geq0.
$$

This identity proves [convexity](convex-function.md) and also shows exactly when its defining [inequality](inequality-mathematics.md) is strict.

<a id="image-the-graph-of-a-convex-quadratic-lies-below-its-endpoint-interpolation-and-above-its-supporting-tangent"></a>
![](convex-chord-and-tangent.png)

**[Figure 24](#image-the-graph-of-a-convex-quadratic-lies-below-its-endpoint-interpolation-and-above-its-supporting-tangent). The graph of a convex quadratic lies below its endpoint interpolation and above its supporting tangent**. For $f(x)=x^2$, the [line segment](line-segment.md) through $(1,1)$ and $(3,9)$ has height $5$ at $x=2$, while the [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md) has height $4$. The [tangent line](tangent-line.md) $y=4x-4$ is an [affine minorant](affine-minorant.md): $x^2-(4x-4)=(x-2)^2\geq0$ everywhere.

For a [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) [convex function](convex-function.md), the [tangent line](tangent-line.md) in one dimension, or the corresponding supporting [affine function](affine-function.md) in several dimensions, lies below the entire [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md). This is the [subgradient inequality](subgradient-inequality.md). The equivalent geometric statement is that the [epigraph](epigraph.md), consisting of points on or above the [graph of a function](graph-of-a-function.md), is a [convex set](convex-set-split.md). These characterizations are developed in [Boyd and Vandenberghe's convex-function slides, sections 3.1–3.4](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides.pdf).

[Convexity](convex-function.md) does not require [differentiability](differentiability.md): the [absolute value function](absolute-value.md) $|x|$ is a [convex function](convex-function.md) with a corner at $0$. When a [function](function-split.md) is twice continuously [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) on an open [interval](interval-mathematics.md), it is a [convex function](convex-function.md) exactly when its [second derivative](second-derivative.md) is nonnegative throughout that [interval](interval-mathematics.md). In several dimensions, the corresponding criterion on an open [convex set](convex-set-split.md) is that its [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) is [positive semidefinite](positive-semidefinite-matrix.md) everywhere. The [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) matters: $x^3$ is a [convex function](convex-function.md) on $[0,\infty)$, but not on all of $\mathbb R$. A strictly positive [second derivative](second-derivative.md) is sufficient for [strict convexity](strictly-convex-function.md), but is not necessary: $x^4$ is a [strictly convex function](strictly-convex-function.md) although its [second derivative](second-derivative.md) vanishes at $0$.

The usefulness of [convexity](convex-function.md) is that local information can certify a global conclusion. Every [local minimum](local-minimum.md) of a [convex function](convex-function.md) on a [convex set](convex-set-split.md) is a [global minimum](global-minimum.md): if a lower value existed elsewhere, the defining [inequality](inequality-mathematics.md) would produce lower values arbitrarily close to the proposed [local minimum](local-minimum.md). For a [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) [convex function](convex-function.md) on an open [convex set](convex-set-split.md), a zero [gradient](gradient.md) therefore certifies a [global minimum](global-minimum.md). [Strict convexity](strictly-convex-function.md) gives uniqueness when a [global minimum](global-minimum.md) exists; it does not supply existence by itself. These facts explain the role of [convex functions](convex-function.md) in [convex optimization](convex-optimization-split.md).
