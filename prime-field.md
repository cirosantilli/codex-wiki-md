# Prime field

↑ **Parent:** [Field](field.md)

The [prime field](prime-field.md) of a [field](field.md) $K$ is its smallest [subfield](subfield.md): the [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) forced upon us by the [identity element](identity-element.md) $1_K$. Every [subfield](subfield.md) must contain $1_K$, its repeated sums, their [additive inverses](additive-inverse.md), and the [multiplicative inverses](multiplicative-inverse.md) of nonzero results. In [characteristic zero](characteristic-zero.md) these operations produce a copy of the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) $\mathbb Q$. In positive [characteristic of a field](characteristic-of-a-field.md) $p$, they produce a copy of $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$, with [addition](addition.md) and [multiplication](multiplication.md) given by [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md).

For example, inside the [field with four elements](field-with-four-elements.md), repeated [addition](addition.md) of $1$ gives $0,1,0,1,\ldots$. Thus its [prime field](prime-field.md) is $\{0,1\}$, and the other two elements cannot be reached by repeatedly adding $1$. This is why a [finite field](finite-field.md) need not consist entirely of ordinary [integer](integer.md) residues: a larger [finite field](finite-field.md) extends the [arithmetic](arithmetic-split.md) of its [prime field](prime-field.md) with additional elements.

The positive [characteristic of a field](characteristic-of-a-field.md) must be a [prime number](prime-number.md). If its least vanishing multiple of $1_K$ were $rs$ with $1<r,s<rs$, then $(r1_K)(s1_K)=0$ would exhibit [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md). The absence of [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md) rules this out. The [prime field](prime-field.md) supplies the [scalars](scalar.md) when viewing $K$ as a [vector space](vector-space-split.md); its [basis](basis.md) then explains the possible sizes of a [finite field](finite-field.md).
