# Mathematical optimization

↑ **Parent:** [Area of mathematics](area-of-mathematics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_optimization)

[Mathematical optimization](mathematical-optimization-split.md) turns a preference into a precise question: among the allowed decisions, which gives the best outcome? A model separates the [objective function](objective-function.md), which measures quality, from the [optimization constraints](optimization-constraint.md), which determine the [feasible set](feasible-region.md). This language unifies finding a cheap plan for a [transportation problem](transportation-problem.md), efficient [scheduling](scheduling.md), or an accurate model in [linear regression](linear-regression-split.md). The separation is useful because changing a preference and changing what is possible are different modeling decisions. See [Boyd and Vandenberghe, introductory optimization models](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides.pdf#page=4).

For an [objective function](objective-function.md) $f:F\to\mathbb R$, a [maximization problem](maximization-problem.md) asks for a [feasible point](feasible-point.md) $z_*$ satisfying $f(z_*)\geq f(z)$ for every $z\in F$; a [minimization problem](minimization-problem.md) reverses the inequality. This is a global [extremum](maximum-and-minimum.md). A [local maximum](local-maximum.md) or [local minimum](local-minimum.md) compares only nearby [feasible points](feasible-point.md), so finding one does not settle the global question.

Consider allocating ten units between two complementary inputs:

$$
\max xy\qquad\text{subject to }x+y=10,\quad x,y\geq0.
$$

The [budget constraint](budget-constraint.md) forces a tradeoff: an extra unit of $x$ costs a unit of $y$. The best allocation is $(5,5)$, with [objective function](objective-function.md) value $25$. Without the [budget constraint](budget-constraint.md), $x=y=t$ gives $xy=t^2\to\infty$: there is no [global maximum](global-maximum.md). [Constrained optimization](constrained-optimization.md) can therefore have a finite answer even when the corresponding problem with fewer [optimization constraints](optimization-constraint.md) does not. Its beauty is that geometry, [calculus](calculus-split.md), and an [arithmetic-geometric mean inequality](arithmetic-geometric-mean-inequality.md) can certify the same answer.
