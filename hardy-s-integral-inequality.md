<h1 id="hardy-s-integral-inequality">Hardy's integral inequality</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Hardy's inequality](hardy-s-inequality.md)

Let $1<p<\infty$ and let $f$ be a real or complex [measurable function](measurable-function.md) in [Lp space](lp-space.md) $L^p((0,\infty),dx)$, with [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) $dx$. The [Hardy operator](hardy-operator.md), denoted here by $H$, takes the running average

$$
Hf(x)=\frac1x\int_0^x f(t)\,dt,\qquad x>0.
$$

[Hölder's inequality](holder-s-inequality.md) ensures $\int_0^x|f(t)|\,dt\leq x^{1-1/p}\|f\|_p<\infty$, so every such average exists. [Hardy's integral inequality](hardy-s-integral-inequality.md) states

$$
\int_0^\infty |Hf(x)|^p\,dx\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^p\int_0^\infty |f(x)|^p\,dx.
$$

The real signed and complex extensions of the nonnegative statement follow from the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) $|Hf|\leq H|f|$. Changing $f$ on a [null set](null-set.md) changes neither average nor [Lp norm](lp-norm.md).[Inequalities, second edition, §9.8, Theorem 327, equation (9.8.2), printed p. 240](inequalities-by-godfrey-harold-hardy-and-others.md)

Averaging over an expanding [interval](interval-mathematics.md) spreads a localized input across the whole positive half-line. [Hardy's integral inequality](hardy-s-integral-inequality.md) bounds the total [Lp norm](lp-norm.md) of that spread. The [Hardy operator](hardy-operator.md) is consequently a [bounded linear operator](continuous-linear-operator.md) on this [Lp space](lp-space.md). The factor $p/(p-1)$ bounds its [operator norm](operator-norm.md); its $p$th power is the constant in the displayed [integral](integral-split.md) bound. These are different constants: at $p=2$, the [L2 norm](l2-norm.md) bound is two and the squared [L2 norm](l2-norm.md) bound is four.
