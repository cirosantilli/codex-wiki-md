<h1 id="galilean-covariance-of-newton-s-second-law">Galilean covariance of Newton's second law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Galilean invariance](galilean-invariance.md)

For a [Galilean transformation](galilean-transformation.md) $t'=t+b$, $\mathbf x'=R\mathbf x-\mathbf U t+\mathbf a$, with constant rotation $R$ and boost $\mathbf U$, [velocity](velocity.md) transforms as $\mathbf v'=R\mathbf v-\mathbf U$ and [acceleration](acceleration.md) as $\mathbf a'=R\mathbf a$. With invariant inertial [mass](mass.md), [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) retains its form only if the actual interaction laws and their physical background data transform to give $\mathbf F'=R\mathbf F$. Acceleration invariance alone does not prove covariance of an arbitrary force law. For example, resistance depends on velocity relative to a medium, whose [velocity](velocity.md) must transform too.
