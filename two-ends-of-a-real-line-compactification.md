# Two ends of a real-line compactification

↑ **Parent:** [Hausdorff compactification](hausdorff-compactification.md)

For a [Hausdorff compactification](hausdorff-compactification.md) $h:\mathbb R\to Y$, let $C^\pm$ be the intersections of the closures of the positive and negative tails, respectively. Each is nonempty and [connected](connected-space.md) by the closed-filter-base theorem. Their [union](set-union.md) equals the remainder: an image point has a bounded-interval [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) excluding sufficiently long tails, whereas every [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of a remainder point meets tails by density and [compactness](compact-space.md) of bounded intervals. If the remainder is finite, each $C^\pm$ must be a [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md), so there can be only one or two remainder points. In particular there is no three-point compactification of the [real line](real-line.md).
