# Lorentz transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_transformation)

A [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) preserves the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md). For a [Lorentz boost](lorentz-boost.md), let $S'$ move at constant [velocity](velocity.md) $+v$ along the $x$-axis of $S$, with parallel spatial axes and coincident origins at $t=t'=0$. With $\beta=v/c$ and the [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md) $\gamma$, the coordinates of the same [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) obey

$$
ct'=\gamma(ct-\beta x),\qquad x'=\gamma(x-\beta ct),\qquad y'=y,\qquad z'=z.
$$

The inverse [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) replaces $v$ by $-v$. In particular, $x'=0$ traces the moving origin $x=vt$, fixing the sign convention. The time formula is $t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2)$; dropping its position term would lose the [relativity of simultaneity](relativity-of-simultaneity.md). [Einstein (1905), section 3: transformation of coordinates and times](https://fourmilab.ch/etexts/einstein/specrel/www/).

For the moving-clock example in [time dilation](time-dilation.md), the second tick has $(t,x)=(5\,\mu\mathrm s,3c\,\mu\mathrm s)$. At $v=0.6c$, substitution gives $t'=1.25(5-0.6\times3)\,\mu\mathrm s=4\,\mu\mathrm s$ and $x'=1.25(3-0.6\times5)c\,\mu\mathrm s=0$. The clock stays at its own origin, and its coordinate time there equals its [proper time](proper-time.md).
