# Slotted-line waveguide measurement

↑ **Parent:** [Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide](rectangular-electromagnetic-waveguide.md)

MIT's documented [slotted-line waveguide measurement](slotted-line-waveguide-measurement.md) shorts one end of a [rectangular electromagnetic waveguide](rectangular-electromagnetic-waveguide.md) and excites it through a probe coupled by [capacitance](capacitance.md). A second such probe moves through a longitudinal slot, aligned with the wall [electric current](electric-current.md) to reduce disturbance. Its output locates axial [standing wave](standing-wave.md) nulls. The book's standing-wave setup chooses a [frequency](frequency.md) above the first [waveguide cutoff frequency](waveguide-cutoff-frequency.md) and below higher-mode cutoffs. Lowering it below the first cutoff instead reveals spatial decay away from the source. The text supplies apparatus and an observable comparison, rather than a table of measured null positions.[Haus and Melcher, Electromagnetic Fields and Energy, §13.4, Example 13.4.1, Demonstration 13.4.1 and Fig. 13.4.5; chapter PDF printed pp. 31–33](electromagnetic-fields-and-energy-by-hermann-a-haus-and-james-r-melcher/rectangular-waveguide-modes.md)

The inference can be checked without assuming that detector output is proportional to [electric field](electric-field-split.md) amplitude. At fixed measured [frequency](frequency.md), record several minima at coordinates $z_0,\ldots,z_N$; then estimate $\lambda_g=2(z_N-z_0)/N$ when the minima are consecutive. This uses positions alone. Compare the result with the [guided wavelength](guided-wavelength.md) prediction using independently measured $a$ and $f$. The $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ spacing above is a synthetic example, not a reading from the MIT apparatus.

Additional proposed controls are to keep probe insertion fixed, repeat the scan in both directions, recheck source [frequency](frequency.md), and compare shallow probe settings for shifts in the minima. These test probe loading, mechanical backlash and drift. A deliberately reflecting termination supplies the [standing wave](standing-wave.md); varying its position should translate the pattern without changing adjacent-null spacing in a uniform single-mode region. Source-adjacent [evanescent waves](evanescent-wave.md), unintended reflections, additional propagating [normal modes](normal-mode.md), detector noise and finite positional resolution can spoil that inference. A disappearing remote signal alone does not distinguish below-cutoff decay from loss or poor excitation, and a scan at one [frequency](frequency.md) does not measure a pulse's [group velocity](group-velocity.md).
