# Deterministic claims maximize the adjustment coefficient at fixed mean and loading

↑ **Parent:** [Adjustment coefficient](adjustment-coefficient.md)

Fix positive claim [expected value](expected-value.md) $\mu$ and [relative safety loading](relative-safety-loading.md) $\theta>0$ in a [classical risk model](classical-risk-model.md). Assume the random claim law has an [adjustment coefficient](adjustment-coefficient.md) $R_X>0$. By [Jensen inequality](jensen-s-inequality.md), $M_X(r)\ge e^{\mu r}$. Thus $e^{\mu R_X}-1-(1+\theta)\mu R_X\le0$. The expression on the left is strictly [convex](convex-function.md), has negative derivative at zero and tends to infinity, so its unique positive zero $R_\mu$ satisfies $R_X\le R_\mu$. If the claim size is not almost surely constant, strict [Jensen inequality](jensen-s-inequality.md) gives $R_X<R_\mu$. Consequently the [Lundberg inequality](lundberg-inequality.md) upper bound $e^{-R_\mu u}$ for deterministic claims is smaller for $u>0$. Comparing these upper bounds alone does not establish an ordering of actual [ultimate ruin probabilities](ultimate-ruin-probability.md).
