# Stationary inflection point

↑ **Parent:** [Inflection point](inflection-point.md)

An [inflection point](inflection-point.md) of the graph of a differentiable real [function](function-split.md) at which its [derivative](derivative.md) is zero. It has a horizontal tangent and a change of concavity, but need not be a local [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) or [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). For a three-times continuously differentiable [function](function-split.md), $f'(a)=f''(a)=0$ and $f'''(a)\ne0$ suffice: the expansion $f''(a+h)=f'''(a)h+o(h)$ gives opposite signs on the two sides. The elementary example is $f(x)=x^3$ at zero. The word “saddle” is sometimes used for this one-dimensional situation in elementary examination papers, but the usual [saddle point of a scalar function](saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md) has a multivariable meaning; do not merge the two concepts.
