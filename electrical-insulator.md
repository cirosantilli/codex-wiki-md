# Electrical insulator

↑ **Parent:** [Dielectric](dielectric.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulator_(electricity))

An [electrical insulator](electrical-insulator.md) has sufficiently small transport of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) for its operating conditions. Insulation is a practical requirement involving [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), [temperature](temperature-split.md), geometry and duration, rather than a universal claim of zero [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md). [Electric polarization](polarization-density.md) can be substantial in an [electrical insulator](electrical-insulator.md); [dielectric breakdown](dielectric-breakdown.md) can end the insulating regime.

For an idealized uniform slab with small constant [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma$, the [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md) model predicts leakage [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) $R=d/(\sigma A)$ in parallel with [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C=\epsilon A/d$. An isolated [capacitor](capacitor.md) then has

$$
\frac{dQ}{dt}=-\frac{Q}{RC},\qquad
Q(t)=Q(0)e^{-t/(RC)},\qquad RC=\frac{\epsilon}{\sigma}.
$$

This is a derived model of slow discharge, not an assertion that every leakage mechanism obeys [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md). Its time scale matches [charge relaxation](charge-relaxation.md). The fixed-charge comparison in [dielectric capacitor response](dielectric-capacitor-response.md) needs an observation time short compared with $RC$, but long enough for [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) to settle. During a maintained voltage, leakage consumes [power](power.md) $V^2/R$ as [Joule heating](joule-heating.md); delayed [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) can add [dielectric loss](dielectric-loss.md) under changing [electric fields](electric-field-split.md).[Overview: real and imaginary permittivity, storage and dissipated loss](materials-measurement-dielectric-materials.md)

Large [relative permittivity](relative-permittivity.md) alone does not rule out either mechanism.[§8.5, paragraph following Table 8.1: high dielectric constant does not ensure a good insulator; mobile ions permit conduction](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/8-5-molecular-model-of-a-dielectric.md)
