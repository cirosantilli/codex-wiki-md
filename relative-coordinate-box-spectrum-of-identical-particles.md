# Relative-coordinate box spectrum of identical particles

↑ **Parent:** [Indistinguishable particles](indistinguishable-particles.md)

For two equal-mass particles with orthonormal coordinates $y_\pm=(x_1\pm x_2)/\sqrt2$ confined to $|y_\pm|<a$, the [Hamiltonian](hamiltonian.md) is the sum of two width-$2a$ [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) Hamiltonians. Exchange preserves $y_+$ and reverses $y_-$. The relative-coordinate box mode is even for odd index $s$ and odd for even index $s$. Pure spatial [bosonic exchange symmetry](bosonic-exchange-symmetry.md) therefore allows odd $s$, while pure spatial [fermionic exchange symmetry](fermi-dirac-statistics.md) allows even $s$.

The bosonic ground mode is $(r,s)=(1,1)$, while its first excited mode $(2,1)$ is degenerate in energy with the fermionic ground mode $(1,2)$. Angular spin degrees of freedom, if present, must be included in the full exchange symmetry rather than assigned the spatial rule automatically. The mean relative distance is $\langle|y_-|\rangle=a(1/2-2/\pi^2)$ in the lowest even mode and $a/2$ in the lowest odd mode, obtained by integrating the normalized squared cosine and sine. Multiplication by $\sqrt2$ gives the particle separation.
