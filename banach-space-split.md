# Banach space

↑ **Parent:** [Normed vector space](normed-vector-space-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_space)

A Banach space is a [normed vector space](normed-vector-space-split.md) over the [real numbers](real-number.md) or [complex numbers](complex-number.md) that is [complete](completeness.md) for the [metric](metric.md) $d(x,y)=\|x-y\|$. Explicitly, whenever a [sequence](sequence.md) $(x_n)$ satisfies

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists N\;\forall m,n\geq N:\quad\|x_m-x_n\|<\varepsilon,
$$

there is an element $x$ of the same space with $\|x_n-x\|\to0$. The hypothesis says that the approximations eventually agree to any requested accuracy; [completeness](completeness.md) says that they describe an actual object in the space. The [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) condition can be checked before anyone knows what that object is.

This makes a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md) a natural setting for constructing solutions by successive improvements. If corrections $v_k$ have $\sum_k\|v_k\|<\infty$, their [partial sums](partial-sum.md) form a [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) by the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md), and [completeness](completeness.md) supplies their sum. In fact, convergence of every such absolutely summable vector [series](series-mathematics.md) characterizes a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md). The [contraction mapping theorem](contraction-mapping-theorem.md) uses precisely this mechanism: each correction is smaller than the preceding one by a fixed factor.[Richard Melrose, Functional Analysis, chapter 1, section 3, Proposition 1.2: completeness and summable series](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-102-introduction-to-functional-analysis-spring-2021/3d4cc88026d44a01f936cd6a0aa995cb_MIT18_102s20_lec_FA.pdf)

Every [normed vector space](normed-vector-space-split.md) of finite [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) is a [Banach space](banach-space-split.md). Infinite-dimensional examples include the [absolutely summable sequence space](absolutely-summable-sequence-space.md) and the [l-infinity sequence space](l-infinity-sequence-space.md); the choice of [norm](norm.md) remains part of the specification. A collection of increasingly accurate finite descriptions can require an infinite description for its limit, as the [finitely supported sequence](finitely-supported-sequence.md) example below demonstrates. [Completion of a normed space](completion-of-a-normed-space.md) adds these missing limits while preserving all original distances.
