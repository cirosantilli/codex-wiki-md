# Twin paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Time dilation](time-dilation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_paradox)

The twin paradox compares [proper time](proper-time.md) along the different [world lines](world-line.md) of a travelling observer and a stay-at-home observer who reunite. Reciprocal inertial [time dilation](time-dilation.md) produces an apparent contradiction only if the traveller's change of [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) is ignored.

The decisive experiment is local: put two clocks together at departure, separate them, then compare their readings together at reunion. Everyone agrees on those two [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) and on the final difference. The [twin paradox](twin-paradox.md) asks why two clocks can accumulate different [proper times](proper-time.md) between the same [spacetime events](event-relativity.md), despite reciprocal [time dilation](time-dilation.md) during uniform motion. Its answer is the geometry of their complete [world lines](world-line.md), together with the [relativity of simultaneity](relativity-of-simultaneity.md) when comparing distant clocks.
