# Prime exponential-sum estimate from Vaughan identity

↑ **Parent:** [Vaughan's identity](vaughan-s-identity.md)

For reduced $a/q$ with $|\alpha-a/q|\leq q^{-2}$ and $1\leq q\leq N$, the displayed bound follows by splitting [Vaughan identity](vaughan-s-identity.md) with parameters $U=V=N^{2/5}$. [Type I sums](type-i-sum.md) use short geometric-sum bounds; [type II sums](type-ii-sum.md) use the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) and rational spacing to control bilinear cancellation. A [badly approximable number](badly-approximable-number.md) has a Dirichlet approximant with $q\asymp N^{1/2}$, yielding $O(N^{4/5}\log^4N)$ and hence $O(N^{9/10})$.
