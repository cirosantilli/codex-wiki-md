↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the first year's increase, the forcing increment is

$$
\Delta F_1=5.4\ln\!\left(\frac{417.5}{415}\right)
=0.0324325\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}.
$$

Using linear superposition of the supplied one-year [aerosol](../../../../../../aerosol.md) calibration,

$$
\boxed{m_1=\frac{0.0324325}{1.37\times10^{-5}}
=2.367\times10^3\ \mathrm{tons\ of\ SO_2}.}
$$

This compensates the year-end forcing increment for a one-year [aerosol](../../../../../../aerosol.md) effect. It is not a one-time permanent cancellation of the continuing carbon-dioxide increase. Treating the rising [carbon dioxide](../../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) [concentration](../../../../../../concentration.md) as a uniform-in-time forcing during that first year would be an additional time-averaging model, rather than the end-of-year comparison used here.
