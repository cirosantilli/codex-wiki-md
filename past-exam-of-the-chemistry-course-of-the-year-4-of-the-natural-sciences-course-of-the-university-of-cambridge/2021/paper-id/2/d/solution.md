<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the [tropospheric OH budget](../../../../../tropospheric-oh-budget.md), let $P$ be [hydroxyl radical](../../../../../hydroxyl-radical.md) production per unit volume and $K$ its total first-order loss frequency. The present [hydroxyl radical](../../../../../hydroxyl-radical.md) steady state has

$$
P=\frac{10^{-6}(2.5\times10^{19})}{1\ \mathrm{yr}}
=7.922\times10^5\ \mathrm{molecules\,cm^{-3}\,s^{-1}},
\qquad[\mathrm{OH}]_0=\frac P{1.3}=6.094\times10^5\ \mathrm{molecules\,cm^{-3}}.
$$

Here one year is $365.25$ days; using it consistently makes the reported lifetimes in years independent of that conversion convention. [Methane](../../../../../methane.md)'s chemical lifetime is $\tau_0=1/(k_2[\mathrm{OH}]_0)$, giving

$$
\boxed{\tau_0=8.254\ \mathrm{yr}.}
$$

The current combined loss frequency already includes existing hydrogen, so **add only the incremental hydrogen sink**:

$$
\Delta K=k_1(2.0-0.5)10^{-6}[M]=0.25125\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},
\qquad K_1=1.55125\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.
$$

Holding production and the other losses fixed then gives $[\mathrm{OH}]_1=5.107\times10^5\ \mathrm{molecules\,cm^{-3}}$ and

$$
\boxed{\tau_1=9.849\ \mathrm{yr},\qquad\tau_1/\tau_0=1.19327.}
$$

The rapid [hydroxyl radical](../../../../../hydroxyl-radical.md) adjustment time, $1/K$ of order a second, supports its [steady-state approximation](../../../../../steady-state-approximation.md) in this box model. Subsequent [methane](../../../../../methane.md) growth would itself change [hydroxyl radical](../../../../../hydroxyl-radical.md) consumption, so the computed increase is not a fully coupled long-term atmospheric prediction. Detailed [molecular hydrogen](../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) modelling treats changes in [methane](../../../../../methane.md), [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) and stratospheric [water](../../../../../water.md) alongside reductions in other precursor emissions.[Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics 23 (2023), §3.1 and Abstract: indirect methane, ozone and water-vapor responses](../../../../../atmospheric-composition-and-climate-impacts-of-a-future-hydrogen-economy-by-nicola-j-warwick-and-others.md)
