<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $D=[\mathrm{Cl_2O_2}]$ and $c=[\mathrm{ClO}]$. In the [steady-state approximation](../../../../../../steady-state-approximation.md) for the dimer, [chlorine peroxide](../../../../../../chlorine-peroxide.md) [chlorine peroxide](../../../../../../chlorine-peroxide.md) formation is balanced by its two competing removal channels:

$$
\frac{dD}{dt}=k_1[M]c^2-\{J_2+k_3[M]\}D\simeq0,
\qquad D=\frac{k_1[M]c^2}{J_2+k_3[M]}.
$$

The association event rate is $k_1[M]c^2$; the associated disappearance rate of [chlorine monoxide](../../../../../../chlorine-monoxide.md) is twice that value. Each successful [photodissociation](../../../../../../photodissociation.md) event produces two [chlorine](../../../../../../chlorine.md) atoms after the subsequent fast fragmentation, and their return to [chlorine monoxide](../../../../../../chlorine-monoxide.md) consumes two [ozone](../../../../../../ozone.md) [molecules](../../../../../../molecule-split.md). [Thermal dissociation](../../../../../../thermal-dissociation.md) instead returns the dimer to two [chlorine monoxide](../../../../../../chlorine-monoxide.md) [radicals](../../../../../../radical-chemistry.md) without destroying [ozone](../../../../../../ozone.md). Thus the [chemical branching fraction](../../../../../../chemical-branching-fraction.md) for an ozone-destroying event is $f=J_2/(J_2+k_3[M])=0.7$, giving

$$
\boxed{-\frac{d[\mathrm O_3]}{dt}=2J_2D
=2f k_1[M][\mathrm{ClO}]^2=1.4k_1[M][\mathrm{ClO}]^2.}
$$

This is the sunlit [chlorine monoxide dimer cycle](../../../../../../chlorine-monoxide-dimer-cycle.md), with net conversion $2\mathrm O_3\to3\mathrm O_2$. It assumes that the dimer relaxes rapidly, downstream fragmentation and [chlorine](../../../../../../chlorine.md)–[ozone](../../../../../../ozone.md) reactions are sufficiently fast, and competing [chlorine](../../../../../../chlorine.md) sinks can be neglected. Counting the formation event twice and then multiplying by two again would overestimate loss by a factor of two.
