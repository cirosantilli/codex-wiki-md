<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a gas whose lifetime is much less than the selected horizon, the numerator of its [global warming potential](../../../../../global-warming-potential.md) has nearly saturated: $\int_0^H a_i m_i(t)\,dt\simeq a_i m_i(0)\tau_i$. The carbon-dioxide denominator continues to accumulate because of its long-lived response. Consequently **a shorter horizon usually gives a larger GWP for this short-lived substitute; a longer horizon gives a smaller GWP**. If the horizon becomes comparable to, or shorter than, the substitute's lifetime, the numerator also depends strongly on the horizon and cannot be replaced by its infinite-time limit.

The horizon is a value choice, and integrated forcing is not the same as [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) at a chosen future date. Equal-GWP pulses can therefore have different near-term warming and long-term [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) paths. The result also depends on [greenhouse gas radiative efficiency](../../../../../greenhouse-gas-radiative-efficiency.md), background [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md), lifetime estimates and which indirect chemical effects or feedbacks are included. A pulse-based metric does not by itself describe a continuing emissions trajectory, nor does it measure [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) depletion or local air-pollution impacts. Comparisons should state the horizon, the emission scenario and the chosen climate outcome.[IPCC AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 7, section 7.6.1: physical emissions metrics and time-horizon dependence](../../../../../the-earth-s-energy-budget-climate-feedbacks-and-climate-sensitivity-by-piers-forster-and-others.md)
