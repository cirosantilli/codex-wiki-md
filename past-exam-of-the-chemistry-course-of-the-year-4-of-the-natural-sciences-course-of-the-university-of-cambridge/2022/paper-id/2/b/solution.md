<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Convert the [molar mass](../../../../../molar-mass.md) to $0.0288\ \mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$ before substitution:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dT}{dz}=-\frac{(0.0288)(9.81)}{29}
=-9.742\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{K\,m^{-1}}
\simeq-9.74\ \mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}.}
$$

The often-used environmental value near $-6.5\ \mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$ is less steep because rising moist air can condense [water vapor](../../../../../water-vapor.md), releasing [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md) and offsetting some expansion cooling. A saturated parcel therefore follows a smaller-magnitude [moist adiabatic lapse rate](../../../../../moist-adiabatic-lapse-rate.md), which varies with [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and [water](../../../../../water.md) content. Real environmental profiles also reflect radiation, mixing and weather systems; $6.5\ \mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$ is a representative profile value, not a universal parcel law.
