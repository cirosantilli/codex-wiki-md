<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Much stratospheric heating is supplied by absorption of solar [ultraviolet](../../../../../ultraviolet.md) radiation by [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md), whereas [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) is an important emitter of thermal infrared radiation. In the usual stratospheric balance, increasing [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) enhances net infrared cooling at the original [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) without supplying a matching increase in the solar heating. [Temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) must fall until infrared emission and absorption again balance the heating.

A useful optically thin schematic is $Q_{\rm solar}=c_{\rm CO_2}K[B(T)-\overline J]$, where $B(T)$ represents the relevant thermal source function and $\overline J$ the incident infrared radiation field. With positive fixed solar heating and an approximately fixed radiation field, increasing $c_{\rm CO_2}$ lowers the required $B(T)-\overline J$, hence lowers $T$. This explains why the surface greenhouse argument cannot simply be applied locally to the [stratosphere](../../../../../stratosphere.md). Spectral blocking of upwelling radiation contributes too; the real response depends on altitude, [ozone](../../../../../ozone.md) heating and [radiative transfer](../../../../../radiative-transfer.md), rather than emissivity alone.[Earth System Dynamics 7 (2016), abstract and sections on the blocking and indirect solar effects](../../../../../why-co2-cools-the-middle-atmosphere-a-consolidating-model-perspective-by-helge-f-goessling-and-sebastian-bathiany.md)
