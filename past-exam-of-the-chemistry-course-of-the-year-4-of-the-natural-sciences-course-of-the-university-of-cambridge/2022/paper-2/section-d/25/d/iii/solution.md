<h1 id="section-d/25/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [depletion force](../../../../../../../depletion-force.md) requires **mobile nonadsorbing [polymers](../../../../../../../polymer-split.md) excluded from the gap**, rather than grafted chains alone. If their depletion layers overlap when the plate separation is $h<2\delta$, the overlap restores accessible solution volume and increases [polymer](../../../../../../../polymer-split.md) [entropy](../../../../../../../entropy-split.md). For ideal, dilute depletants of bulk osmotic pressure $\Pi$, an idealized interaction free energy per plate area is

$$
\boxed{W(h)\simeq-\Pi(2\delta-h)\quad(0<h<2\delta),\qquad W(h)\simeq0\quad(h\geq2\delta).}
$$

Its attractive pressure is $-\Pi$ in the overlap interval. The layer thickness is of order the ideal-coil [radius of gyration](../../../../../../../radius-of-gyration.md), $\delta\sim R_g=b_0\sqrt{N/6}$, so **the range scales as $N^{1/2}$**. For the supplied chain parameters, $R_g\simeq81.6$ nm and an order-of-magnitude two-layer range is 163 nm. Grafted brushes instead oppose compression through stretching and osmotic penalties; they can coexist with depletion attraction if free depletants are present, but the mere existence of a grafted layer does not create a reservoir of depleting chains. The source does not specify that reservoir's concentration, so its attraction magnitude cannot be calculated.
