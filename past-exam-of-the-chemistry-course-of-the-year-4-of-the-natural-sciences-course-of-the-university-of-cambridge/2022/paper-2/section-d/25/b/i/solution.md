<h1 id="section-d/25/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In [electrophoresis](../../../../../../../electrophoresis.md), an applied [electric field](../../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) drives a charged particle and its associated diffuse ionic atmosphere relative to the liquid. Electric forcing is balanced by viscous stresses, giving a steady velocity $\mathbf v=\mu_e\mathbf E$ in the linear regime. The relevant electrokinetic potential is the [zeta potential](../../../../../../../zeta-potential.md) at the slipping plane, rather than necessarily the solid surface potential.

For a thin [electric double layer](../../../../../../../electric-double-layer.md), the surrounding layer develops an electro-osmotic flow and the [Smoluchowski electrophoretic mobility](../../../../../../../smoluchowski-electrophoretic-mobility.md) is $\mu_e=\epsilon\zeta/\eta$. The sign of $\zeta$ fixes the direction. This transport is different from [dielectrophoresis](../../../../../../../dielectrophoresis.md), which can move a neutral polarizable particle in a nonuniform field.
