↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Using the [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) values above and accounting for **two metal ions per formula unit**,

$$
\boxed{g_{\rm Fe}=\sqrt{7.4/1.5}=2.22,\quad g_{\rm Co}=\sqrt{5.2/0.9375}=2.36,\quad g_{\rm Ni}=\sqrt{3.4/0.5}=2.61.}
$$

The deviations from 2 indicate [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) and incomplete quenching of [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md). The partly filled $e''$ manifold in Fe and $e'$ manifold in Ni permit orbital degeneracy in the ideal geometry. Co's high-spin orbital occupation is more orbitally balanced, but mixing with excited ligand-field states still produces a substantial effective $g$ shift. These fitted scalar values summarize a thermally averaged response; they do not determine the individual components of an anisotropic [g tensor](../../../../../../../g-tensor.md).
