<h1 id="section-b/20/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For two independent identical ions at sufficiently high temperature, [Curie's law](../../../../../../../curie-s-law.md) gives $\chi T=2(N_A\mu_B^2/3k_B)g^2S(S+1)$. In the cgs units used here, $N_A\mu_B^2/3k_B\simeq0.125\ \mathrm{cm^3\,K\,mol^{-1}}$.

**Fe(II), Co(II) and Ni(II) have $S=2$, $3/2$ and 1, respectively.** The corresponding high-spin occupancies, ordered as $(e'',e',a_1')$, are

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fe^{II}}:d^6=(3,2,1),\qquad\mathrm{Co^{II}}:d^7=(4,2,1),\qquad\mathrm{Ni^{II}}:d^8=(4,3,1).}
$$

Hund filling puts one electron in each orbital before additional pairing. Spin-only dimer values at $g=2$ would be 6.0, 3.75 and 2.0; the observed larger values anticipate orbital contributions. The [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) assignments require the chemically appropriate high-spin states, rather than rounding the measured $\chi T$ directly to a spin-only integer.
