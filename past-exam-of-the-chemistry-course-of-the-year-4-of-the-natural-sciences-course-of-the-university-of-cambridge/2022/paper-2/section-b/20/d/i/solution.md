<h1 id="section-b/20/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For an axial [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) Hamiltonian $\mathcal H_D=D S_z^2$ with $D/k_B=-25$ K, the minima are $m_S=\pm5/2$. The highest levels along the reversal ladder are the $m_S=\pm1/2$ doublet. Hence

$$
\boxed{U/k_B=|D|\{(5/2)^2-(1/2)^2\}=25(6)=150\ \mathrm K,}
$$

about 1.25 kJ mol$^{-1}$ or 12.9 meV per molecule. Using $|D|S^2$ would incorrectly put a half-integer [spin](../../../../../../../spin.md) at a nonexistent $m_S=0$ level. Transverse anisotropy and [quantum tunnelling of magnetization](../../../../../../../quantum-tunnelling-of-magnetization.md) can create faster relaxation pathways, so this axial ladder barrier need not equal an experimentally fitted effective barrier.
