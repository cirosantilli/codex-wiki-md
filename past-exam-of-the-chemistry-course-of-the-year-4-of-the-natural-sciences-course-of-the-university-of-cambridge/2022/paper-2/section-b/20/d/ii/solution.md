↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Frequency-dependent ac susceptibility** probes relaxation: a peak in the out-of-phase component occurs approximately when $\omega\tau=1$. Measuring its displacement with frequency determines $\tau(T)$; an activated region may fit $\tau=\tau_0\exp(U_{\rm eff}/k_BT)$. A low-temperature departure can reveal tunnelling or other relaxation channels.

**Low-temperature, sweep-rate-dependent [magnetization](../../../../../../../magnetization.md) [hysteresis](../../../../../../../hysteresis.md)** tests whether reversal is slow on the measurement timescale. A loop opening below the blocking temperature, variation with sweep rate and possible tunnelling steps provide complementary evidence. Isothermal remanent [magnetization](../../../../../../../magnetization.md) decay after field removal directly measures the associated relaxation time. A single open loop alone would not distinguish isolated-molecule blocking from collective ordering or impurities.
