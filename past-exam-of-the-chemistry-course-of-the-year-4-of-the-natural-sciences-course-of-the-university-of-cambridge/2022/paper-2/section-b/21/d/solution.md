<h1 id="section-b/21/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use $C=0.125g^2S(S+1)$ for **one** ion per formula unit, with high-spin $S=5/2,2,3/2,1$ for Mn, Fe, Co, Ni. Then

$$
\boxed{g\simeq(2.04,\ 2.20,\ 2.83,\ 2.47).}
$$

High-spin Mn(II), with an approximately orbitally nondegenerate $d^5$ [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md), stays closest to the free-electron value. Fe, Co and Ni have stronger orbital admixture, particularly Co in this effective Curie-law comparison. [Spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md) creates [magnetic anisotropy](../../../../../../magnetic-anisotropy.md), which can make reversal of the weak-ferromagnetic domains slower and enhance field-cooled/zero-field-cooled separation. A field-cooled sample selects domains as it orders; a zero-field-cooled sample initially has competing domains and can remain pinned when a small measuring field is applied.

This is a qualitative rationalization, not a strict proportionality between $g-2$ and the [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) separation. The anisotropy tensor, domain-wall pinning and experimental timescale also matter; scalar $g$ values alone do not quantitatively fix the divergence.
