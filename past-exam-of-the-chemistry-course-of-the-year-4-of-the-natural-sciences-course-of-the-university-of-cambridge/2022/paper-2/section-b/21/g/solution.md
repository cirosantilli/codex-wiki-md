<h1 id="section-b/21/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Below the ordering temperature, coherent [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) correlations produce **magnetic Bragg intensity**. Depending on the magnetic propagation vector, this can appear as additional peaks or as added intensity at positions already occupied by nuclear reflections. A magnetic unit cell larger than the chemical one gives extra reciprocal-space positions; a zero-propagation-vector canted structure need not do so.

Magnetic intensity generally decreases at large scattering vector through the [magnetic form factor](../../../../../../magnetic-form-factor.md) and depends on moment components perpendicular to the scattering vector. Above the transition the long-range magnetic Bragg contribution disappears, though short-range correlations can leave diffuse scattering. The nuclear pattern remains approximately unchanged unless magnetic ordering also drives a structural distortion.
