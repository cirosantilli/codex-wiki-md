<h1 id="section-h/33/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A shell with angular momentum $l$ holds $2(2l+1)$ electrons. Successively filling the shells listed in the source gives the closed-shell totals

$$
\boxed{2,8,18,20,34,40,58,68,90,92,106,112,138.}
$$

For $\mathrm{Al_4Cp^*_4}$ the delocalized count is **$4(3)-4=8$**, matching $1S^21P^6$. The tetrahedral aluminium core is consequently consistent with the [jellium model](../../../../../../../jellium-model.md). Counting the same eight electrons as though the tetrahedron were an ordinary closo Wade cluster would fail: a four-vertex closo framework would require $2n+2=10$ skeletal electrons. The competing models use different physical assumptions and should not be interchanged just because both involve a polyhedron.
