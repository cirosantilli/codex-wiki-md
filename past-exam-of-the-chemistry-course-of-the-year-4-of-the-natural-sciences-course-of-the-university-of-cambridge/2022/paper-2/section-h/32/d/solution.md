<h1 id="section-h/32/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Stoichiometry gives **H = $\mathrm{Ph(TMP)_3CuLi_3}$**, formally F plus two LiTMP units. A compatible ring connectivity is $\mathrm{Ph-Cu-N_1-Li-N_2-Li-N_3-Li}$, closed through a lithium–phenyl interaction. The TMP attached to copper, N$_1$, remains chemically distinct; N$_2$ and N$_3$ belong to the more labile lithium-amide part.

Reversible detachment and reattachment of a symmetric $\mathrm{(LiTMP)_2}$ segment can exchange its two amides and reassign which Li occupies the phenyl-contacting or nitrogen-bridging positions. It can therefore **exchange all three Li sites and the two Li-associated TMP groups while retaining the Cu-bound TMP group**. Fast TMP-ring conformational inversion can further average methyl environments without exchanging entire ligands. These are different kinds of motion; simple ring inversion alone cannot explain exchange of the lithium sites. Such a dissociative interpretation is consistent with the experimentally studied lithium-rich aggregate, but the exchange pattern does not determine a unique [transition state](../../../../../../transition-state.md) or prove that every bond breaks in one concerted event.
