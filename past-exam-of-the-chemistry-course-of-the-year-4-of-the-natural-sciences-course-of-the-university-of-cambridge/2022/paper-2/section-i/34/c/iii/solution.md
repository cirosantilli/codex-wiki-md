<h1 id="section-i/34/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

KOH hydrolyses the [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) ethyl [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) to a carboxylate; acidification gives the acid. Silver(I) then activates the neighbouring alkyne towards **intramolecular six-membered O-cyclization**. The carboxylate oxygen attacks the alkyne carbon farther from the [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md), so the new ring follows carbonyl C–O–distal alkyne C–proximal alkyne C–[thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) C4–[thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) C5. A vinyl-silver intermediate is protonated to give the fused pyrone G.

The remote terminal carboxylic acid survives. [Ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) hydrolysis changes E's formula by $-\mathrm{C_2H_4}$ to $\mathrm{C_{16}H_{12}FNO_4S}$; the subsequent addition across the alkyne changes connectivity without altering that formula. This distinguishes the **lactonizing carboxyl group at [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) C5** from the side-chain acid, which is not the group closing the observed fused ring.
