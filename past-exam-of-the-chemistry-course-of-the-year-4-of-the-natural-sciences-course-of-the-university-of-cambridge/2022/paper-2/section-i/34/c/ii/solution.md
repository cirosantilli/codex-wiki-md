<h1 id="section-i/34/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The acidic ethanolic step converts the terminal carboxylic acid to its ethyl [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md), leaving the pre-existing [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md). Azide then adds across the alkyne by [1,3-dipolar cycloaddition](../../../../../../../1-3-dipolar-cycloaddition.md), followed by protonation at work-up. **F contains an NH-1,2,3-triazole replacing the alkyne**, with the [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md) and $\mathrm{(CH_2)_3CO_2Et}$ attached to its two carbon atoms. The [thiazole](../../../../../../../thiazole.md)'s own CO$_2$Et group remains.

The formula check is decisive:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{C_{18}H_{16}FNO_4S+C_2H_4+HN_3=C_{20}H_{21}FN_4O_4S}.}
$$

One can represent the [cycloaddition](../../../../../../../cycloaddition.md) with cyclic movement of the azide resonance-form electrons and the alkyne $\pi$ electrons into the two new C–N bonds. Alkali azide initially gives a triazolide; aqueous work-up provides the NH. Interchanging formal 4/5 labels for the carbon substituents in an N-unsubstituted triazole does not establish a separate permanently fixed N-substituted regioisomer.
