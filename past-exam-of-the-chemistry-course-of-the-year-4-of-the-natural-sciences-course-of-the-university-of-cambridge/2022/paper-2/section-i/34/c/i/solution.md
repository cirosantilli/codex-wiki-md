<h1 id="section-i/34/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The thioamide first undergoes sulfur alkylation at the bromomalonate carbon, displacing bromide. Intramolecular attack of nitrogen on one [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) carbonyl and ethanol loss produce a five-membered thiazolinone-type intermediate. Tautomerization gives **ethyl 2-(3-fluorophenyl)-4-hydroxythiazole-5-carboxylate**. For unambiguous atom assignment, write the ring in order as S1–C2(aryl)–N3–C4(OH)–C5(CO$_2$Et): the thioamide carbon becomes C2, its nitrogen becomes N3, the attacked [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) carbonyl becomes C4 and the original bromomalonate methine becomes C5.

Thus the correctly numbered intermediate is **ethyl 2-(3-fluorophenyl)-4-hydroxythiazole-5-carboxylate**. Triflic anhydride activates the hydroxyl oxygen; triethylamine removes its proton to give **D = ethyl 2-(3-fluorophenyl)-4-(trifluoromethanesulfonyloxy)thiazole-5-carboxylate**. This aryl triflate undergoes the subsequent palladium-catalysed alkynyl coupling at C4. The explicit ring atom mapping prevents interchanging the 4-hydroxy and 5-ester positions.
