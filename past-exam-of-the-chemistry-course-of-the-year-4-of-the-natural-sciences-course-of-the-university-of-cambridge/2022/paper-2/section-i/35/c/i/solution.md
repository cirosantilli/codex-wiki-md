<h1 id="section-i/35/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**K is ethyl 4-(4-methylbenzoyl)-1H-pyrrole-2-carboxylate.** AlCl$_3$ activates the aroyl chloride to an acylium-like [electrophile](../../../../../../../electrophile.md). Pyrrole attacks through C4, forming a sigma complex; deprotonation restores [aromaticity](../../../../../../../aromaticity.md) and work-up releases the [ketone](../../../../../../../ketone.md) from its Lewis-acid complex.

Unsubstituted pyrrole normally favours an $\alpha$ position, but the [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) at C2 changes the regioselectivity. Attack at C5, or at C3, permits important sigma-complex contributors with positive charge at the ester-bearing C2, which is strongly destabilized by the electron-withdrawing [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md). C4 substitution avoids that particularly unfavourable contributor. **The C2 [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) therefore directs the acylation towards C4**, overriding the simple unsubstituted-pyrrole rule. Combining the starting [ester](../../../../../../../ester.md) with a p-toluoyl group while replacing a ring hydrogen gives $\mathrm{C_{15}H_{15}NO_3}$, as required.
