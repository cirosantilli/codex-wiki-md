<h1 id="section-a/18/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A sufficiently phase-separated [block copolymer](../../../../../../block-copolymer.md) can show **two [glass transitions](../../../../../../glass-transition.md), associated approximately with its two microdomains**. Simultaneous feeding instead tends to distribute the [monomers](../../../../../../monomer.md) along each chain, giving a [statistical copolymer](../../../../../../statistical-copolymer.md) and usually one composition-dependent [glass transition](../../../../../../glass-transition.md). Different [monomer](../../../../../../monomer.md) reactivities could produce a gradient rather than a perfectly random sequence.

For a homogeneous mixture an approximate interpolation is the [Fox equation](../../../../../../fox-equation.md), $T_g^{-1}=w_1/T_{g1}+w_2/T_{g2}$, with temperatures in kelvin. Two transitions are not guaranteed merely by block architecture: short blocks, miscibility or insufficient experimental resolution can give one broad transition. The contrast is between domain-specific segmental environments and a more uniform mixed environment.
