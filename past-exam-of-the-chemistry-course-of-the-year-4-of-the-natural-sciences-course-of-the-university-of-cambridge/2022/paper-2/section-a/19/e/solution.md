<h1 id="section-a/19/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Replacing lactide by ethylene oxide gives

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{HO-[CH_2CH_2O]_{50}-CH_2CH(Ph)-[CH_2CH(Ph)]_{100}-O-TEMPO}.}
$$

The first chain grows by alkoxide attack on ethylene oxide, followed by protonation at work-up; the alkoxyamine subsequently controls styrene [polymerization](../../../../../../polymerization.md). The final material is an [amphiphilic block copolymer](../../../../../../amphiphilic-block-copolymer.md). In water, [polystyrene](../../../../../../polystyrene.md) avoids solvent exposure while the poly(ethylene oxide) block is hydrated. Above a concentration-dependent assembly threshold, **[micelles](../../../../../../micelle.md) with a [polystyrene](../../../../../../polystyrene.md) core and hydrated poly(ethylene oxide) corona** are a reasonable morphology. Unimers remain in equilibrium with aggregates. Core–corona assembly lowers unfavourable water contact but costs chain-stretching and translational [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md). Block volume fractions, solvent history and concentration can instead favour cylinders or vesicles, so the chemical structure alone does not uniquely establish spherical [micelles](../../../../../../micelle.md).
