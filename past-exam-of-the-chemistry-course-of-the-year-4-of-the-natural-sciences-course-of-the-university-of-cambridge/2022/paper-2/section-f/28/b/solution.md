<h1 id="section-f/28/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Dibutylboron triflate coordinates the imide carbonyl and triethylamine removes the propionyl $\alpha$ proton, giving predominantly the **Z dibutylboron [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md)**. Its connectivity is $\mathrm{Aux-N-C(O-BBu_2)=CHMe}$; the imide auxiliary remains attached, rather than being displaced during enolization. Coordination of cinnamaldehyde's oxygen to boron organizes a six-membered [Zimmerman–Traxler transition state](../../../../../../zimmerman-traxler-transition-state.md).

A chair with the [aldehyde](../../../../../../aldehyde.md)'s styryl group pseudoequatorial avoids the auxiliary's bulky benzyl side. Formation of the C–C bond and transfer of the [aldehyde](../../../../../../aldehyde.md) oxygen into the boron coordination sphere give the boron aldolate, which hydrolyses to D. **A Z [enolate](../../../../../../enolate.md) gives the syn aldol relationship.** In the printed product zigzag, the labelled $\alpha$-methyl bond should therefore be a solid wedge, on the same stereochemical face as the neighbouring solid-wedge hydroxyl bond. With the stated connectivity, the labelled carbon is **R**: priority is imide carbonyl, then $\mathrm{CH(OH)CH=CHPh}$, then methyl, then hydrogen. The adjacent hydroxyl centre is S. These assignments refer to the actual auxiliary and product orientation, not an arbitrary choice of an otherwise equivalent chair.
