<h1 id="section-f/28/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**A hydroxyl-directed [Evans–Saksena reduction](../../../../../../evans-saksena-reduction.md) gives the required anti-1,3-diol.** A suitable reagent is tetramethylammonium triacetoxyborohydride in an acidic medium, for example acetic acid/acetonitrile. Ligand exchange between the pre-existing [alcohol](../../../../../../alcohol.md) and an acetoxyborohydride group tethers the reducing reagent to the substrate. Intramolecular hydride delivery to the [ketone](../../../../../../ketone.md) then proceeds through a six-membered arrangement, converting C=O to an alkoxide while conserving all existing [stereocentres](../../../../../../stereocentre.md). Hydrolysis releases the second hydroxyl group.

The preferred directed transition-state conformation minimizes crowding by the larger carbon-chain substituents. It delivers the new C–H bond on the face that leaves the two hydroxyl groups **anti**, as in B: with the original hydroxyl drawn as a wedge in the printed zigzag, the new hydroxyl is dashed. The intervening methyl [stereocentre](../../../../../../stereocentre.md) is retained. Choosing an undirected borohydride reduction would not by itself rationalize this strong substrate-controlled relationship.
