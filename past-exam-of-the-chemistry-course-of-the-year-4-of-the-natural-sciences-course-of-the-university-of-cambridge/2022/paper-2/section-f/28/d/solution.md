<h1 id="section-f/28/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The acetylide attacks the less substituted terminal [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) carbon by [SN2](../../../../../../sn2-reaction.md) ring opening. The C–O bond at that carbon breaks, leaving the oxygen attached to the original substituted [stereocentre](../../../../../../stereocentre.md). Protonation gives $\mathrm{BnOCH_2CH(OH)CH_2C\equiv CMe}$. Dissolving-metal reduction then gives the E [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) and also cleaves the benzyl ether.

Thus **H is $\mathrm{HOCH_2CH(OH)CH_2CH=CHMe}$ with an E double bond**, and its original epoxide-derived [stereocentre](../../../../../../stereocentre.md) is retained geometrically. No new tetrahedral [stereocentre](../../../../../../stereocentre.md) is formed: the attacked [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) carbon becomes CH$_2$. The new stereochemical information is the **E [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) geometry**. Calling its secondary hydroxyl carbon “newly generated” would confuse changing its functional environment with creating a new [stereocentre](../../../../../../stereocentre.md).
