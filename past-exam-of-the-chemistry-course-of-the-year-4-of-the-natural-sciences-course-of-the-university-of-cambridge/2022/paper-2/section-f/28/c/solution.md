<h1 id="section-f/28/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The free allylic hydroxyl can hydrogen-bond to the peracid and accelerate directed oxygen transfer to the nearby double bond. The remote styryl double bond lacks this directing contact. **Epoxidation of the hydroxyl-adjacent [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) is therefore favoured**, even though both [alkenes](../../../../../../alkene.md) are in principle oxidizable. [Peracid epoxidation](../../../../../../peracid-epoxidation.md) is a concerted oxygen transfer: [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) $\pi$ donation to terminal peroxide oxygen, O–O cleavage, proton transfer and carbonyl re-formation occur through a cyclic transition-state arrangement. The [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md)'s E geometry is retained as a trans relationship between its substituents in the [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md).

The two [alkene](../../../../../../alkene.md) faces are diastereotopic because the molecule already contains [stereocentres](../../../../../../stereocentre.md). A plausible substrate-controlled conformation minimizes allylic strain and presents the face opposite the bulky OTBS-bearing part of the chain to the oxidant. **Predict [epoxide](../../../../../../epoxide.md) oxygen on the face opposite OTBS**, retaining the pre-existing methyl and OTBS configurations. Hydrogen-bond direction, steric approach and conformational population compete; one cannot deduce an exact diastereomeric ratio from the flat drawing alone. The same facial preference has been discussed for this synthetic series.[2007, synthesis section and Scheme 2, epoxidation opposite OTBS](../../../../../../recent-results-toward-the-stereoselective-synthesis-of-biologically-active-natural-products-by-luiz-c-dias-and-others.md)
