<h1 id="13/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

One standard description of sodium-rich NaCl is removal of lattice chloride into a NaCl reservoir, leaving a positively charged chloride vacancy and an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Na(s)+Cl_{Cl}^{\times}\longrightarrow
NaCl(s)+V_{Cl}^{\bullet}+e'.}}
$$

This [Kröger–Vink notation](../../../../../../kroger-vink-notation.md) equation conserves atoms, chloride sites and effective charge. The [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) can be trapped in the vacancy, forming an electrically neutral [F center](../../../../../../f-center.md):

$$
\mathrm{V_{Cl}^{\bullet}+e'\rightleftharpoons
(V_{Cl}^{\bullet}e')^{\times}}.
$$

Thus sodium excess introduces electronic defects and can give coloration. **It does not imply that every added [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) is a mobile conduction [electron](../../../../../../electron.md)**: electronic transport depends on trapping and thermal release. Chloride vacancies can also assist anion transport. An alternative microscopic incorporation route is a sodium interstitial plus an [electron](../../../../../../electron.md), but the vacancy–[electron](../../../../../../electron.md) model describes the usual alkali-metal-colored halide.
