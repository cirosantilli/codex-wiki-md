<h1 id="13/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The trivalent dopant is comparable in radius to Na$^+$ and is naturally accommodated on a sodium site rather than a chloride site. One charge-balanced incorporation reaction is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{YCl_3\longrightarrow
Y_{Na}^{\bullet\bullet}+2V_{Na}'+3Cl_{Cl}^{\times}}.}
$$

Three cation sites are filled by one Y ion and two vacancies; the three chloride ions occupy the corresponding anion sites. The effective $+2$ substitutional charge is balanced by two singly negative sodium vacancies.

**YCl$_3$ doping primarily increases sodium-vacancy-mediated ionic conductivity**, provided the vacancies remain mobile rather than strongly bound to the dopant. In contrast, sodium-rich doping introduces [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and anion vacancies or interstitial-related defects. Neither mechanism requires holes as its compensating carrier, and an increase in vacancy number need not translate proportionally into conductivity if defect association reduces mobility.
