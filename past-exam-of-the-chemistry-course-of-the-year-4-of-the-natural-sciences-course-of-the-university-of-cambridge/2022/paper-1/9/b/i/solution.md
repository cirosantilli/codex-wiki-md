<h1 id="9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Label the ynone carbons $\mathrm{Ar(OH)-CO-C_\alpha\equiv C_\beta-Ph}$. The N-oxide oxygen adds to the electrophilic $C_\beta$, giving an O-vinylated quinolinium species with an enolate-like nucleophilic $C_\alpha$.

The chain $C_\alpha-C_\beta-O-N-C2_{\rm quinoline}$ is now arranged for rearrangement: form a bond between $C_\alpha$ and quinoline C2 while cleaving N–O and forming the $C_\beta=O$ bond. This can be represented through a cyclic addition/rearrangement intermediate, followed by [proton transfer](../../../../../../proton-transfer.md) and rearomatization. The resulting carbon skeleton is the heteroarylated 1,3-dicarbonyl system

$$
\mathrm{Ar(OH)-CO-CH(quinolin\!-\!2\!-\!yl)-CO-Ph}.
$$

Finally, the ortho phenolic oxygen attacks the distal [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md). [Cyclodehydration](../../../../../../cyclodehydration.md) forms the chromone ring and eliminates water. **The product is 2-phenyl-3-(quinolin-2-yl)chromen-4-one**. The original ynone [carbonyl](../../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) becomes the chromone 4-one, the phenyl-bearing alkyne carbon becomes C2, and the other alkyne carbon becomes C3 carrying the heteroaryl substituent.

This atom mapping explains both the new C–C bond and the loss of the N-oxide oxygen from the heteroarene; it is not a direct substitution on the phenyl ring.
