<h1 id="9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**A is 1-propyl-1,2,4-triazole**, with propyl on the nitrogen that is adjacent to one N and one C in the triazole ring, rather than on the nitrogen between the two carbon atoms.

[DBU](../../../../../../dbu.md) removes the ring NH [proton](../../../../../../proton.md), producing a resonance-stabilized [triazolide](../../../../../../triazolide.md) anion. Nitrogen attacks the primary bromide carbon by an [SN2 reaction](../../../../../../sn2-reaction.md), displacing bromide and giving the N-propyl product. Alkylation at the equivalent tautomer-related N1/N2 positions gives the same constitutional product, while N4 alkylation would give a different isomer. Charge distribution in the triazolide and the accessibility of its nitrogen lone pairs favor the N1 product under the stated conditions. This is kinetic [regioselectivity](../../../../../../regioselectivity.md), not an assertion that the unalkylated tautomer has a permanently localized negative charge.
