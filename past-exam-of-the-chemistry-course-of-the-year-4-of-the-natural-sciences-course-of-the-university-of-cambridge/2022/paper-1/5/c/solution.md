↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**There is no universal ranking from the information supplied.** The [depletion force](../../../../../depletion-force.md) scales with [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) [osmotic pressure](../../../../../osmotic-pressure.md), hence with $\rho$ in the ideal model. [London dispersion forces](../../../../../london-dispersion-force.md) instead depend on the material-dependent [Hamaker constant](../../../../../hamaker-constant.md), the intervening solvent, the closest approach and the contact geometry.

Close face contact can give a strong dispersion contribution; the ideal continuum expression even becomes singular without a molecular-distance cutoff. Nevertheless, refractive-index matching can greatly reduce that contribution, and increasing [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) concentration can strengthen [depletion force](../../../../../depletion-force.md). A quantitative comparison requires those missing material and concentration scales. The two attractive contributions may act together.
