<h1 id="12/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [sydnone](../../../../../sydnone.md) is a mesoionic [1,3-dipole](../../../../../1-3-dipole.md). Its two [cycloaddition](../../../../../cycloaddition.md) termini add across the alkyne to give a bicyclic adduct. Subsequent retrocycloaddition expels the ring oxygen and [carbonyl](../../../../../carbonyl-group.md) carbon together as carbon dioxide, leaving an aromatic [pyrazole](../../../../../pyrazole.md).

**The product is 1-$R_1$-4,5-di-$R$-pyrazole, plus CO$_2$.** The [sydnone](../../../../../sydnone.md) nitrogen bearing $R_1$ becomes [pyrazole](../../../../../pyrazole.md) N1; the adjacent nitrogen becomes N2; the [sydnone](../../../../../sydnone.md) CH becomes C3; and the two alkyne carbons become C4 and C5. Since both alkyne substituents are the same, reversing its orientation gives the same constitutional product.

Formation of the aromatic [pyrazole](../../../../../pyrazole.md) and release of CO$_2$ favor the fragmentation. The net transformation can be understood entirely as a [cycloaddition](../../../../../cycloaddition.md)–[cycloreversion](../../../../../cycloreversion.md) sequence; no biological application is needed.
