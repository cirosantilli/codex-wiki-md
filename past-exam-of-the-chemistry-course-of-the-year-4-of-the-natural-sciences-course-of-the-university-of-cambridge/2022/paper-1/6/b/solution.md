<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**A is the kinetic, less-hindered-side [silyl enol ether](../../../../../silyl-enol-ether.md).** Its double bond lies away from the methyl substituent. Forming B requires removal of a [proton](../../../../../proton.md) from the more hindered $\alpha$ position adjacent to the methyl-bearing carbon.

The base strengths alone do not determine the ratios. [trimethylsilyl triflate](../../../../../trimethylsilyl-triflate.md) is strongly electrophilic: oxygen silylation activates the [ketone](../../../../../ketone.md) towards deprotonation, and irreversible trapping of the resulting [silyl enol ether](../../../../../silyl-enol-ether.md) can make the product ratio reflect competing deprotonation rates. A weak [amine](../../../../../amine.md) therefore need not first produce a substantial equilibrium concentration of the free [ketone](../../../../../ketone.md) [enolate](../../../../../enolate.md).

DBU is the strongest and comparatively accessible base, and distinguishes the two sites least strongly, giving the smallest A:B ratio. Triethylamine gives greater preference for the less-hindered site. The more sterically hindered diisopropylethylamine discriminates most strongly and gives the largest proportion of A. Its slightly higher conjugate-acid $pK_a$ than that of triethylamine does not reverse this trend, because **steric accessibility and the coupled silylation/deprotonation mechanism matter alongside basicity**. The data do not establish a simple thermodynamic [enolate](../../../../../enolate.md) distribution.
