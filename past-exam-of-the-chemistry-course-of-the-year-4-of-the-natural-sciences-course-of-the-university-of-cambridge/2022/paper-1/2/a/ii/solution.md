<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At high [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), $\chi T$ tends towards the nearly constant [Curie constant](../../../../../../curie-constant.md) of weakly correlated $S=\tfrac12$ centers. On cooling, $\chi T$ falls because neighboring [spins](../../../../../../spin.md) increasingly correlate antiparallel. The broad maximum in $\chi$ marks the crossover from the high-temperature Curie-like regime to substantial [short-range order](../../../../../../short-range-order.md).

At still lower [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), the [Magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) becomes small because the antiferromagnetic ladder favors a singlet-like ground state and has few thermally accessible magnetic excitations. An even-leg [spin ladder](../../../../../../spin-ladder.md) can have a [spin gap](../../../../../../spin-gap.md), which reinforces this suppression. **A broad [Magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) maximum and falling $\chi T$ support short-range antiferromagnetic correlations; they do not establish a three-dimensional Néel transition.** Such a transition would require independent evidence, rather than identifying the broad maximum itself as the ordering [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).
