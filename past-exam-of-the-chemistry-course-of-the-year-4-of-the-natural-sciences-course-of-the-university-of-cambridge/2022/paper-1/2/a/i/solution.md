<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The four shortest contacts are the three Cu–N bonds and Cu–OR bond. They define an approximately square equatorial set. The two remaining Cu–O contacts are longer and define a strongly elongated axial direction, as expected for the [Jahn-Teller effect](../../../../../../jahn-teller-effect.md) of $d^9$ Cu(II).

Choose $z$ along the long contacts and $x,y$ towards the short equatorial donors. The singly occupied, predominantly metal [magnetic orbital](../../../../../../magnetic-orbital.md) is then **$d_{x^2-y^2}$**, with substantial [Ligand in coordination chemistry](../../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) admixture. All three pyrazine nitrogen donors lie in this equatorial set, so both ladder directions have strong metal–[Ligand in coordination chemistry](../../../../../../ligand-in-coordination-chemistry.md) [orbital overlap](../../../../../../orbital-overlap.md). Neighboring copper centers communicate through comparable pyrazine [superexchange](../../../../../../superexchange.md) pathways. Virtual [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) transfer between half-filled orbitals stabilizes the antiparallel arrangement, giving **antiferromagnetic interactions in both directions with comparable magnitudes**. The axial oxygen donors are much less effective exchange routes.
