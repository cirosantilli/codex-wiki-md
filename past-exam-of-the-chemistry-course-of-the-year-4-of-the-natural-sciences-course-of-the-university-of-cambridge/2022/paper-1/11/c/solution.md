<h1 id="11/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Nitrogen-down [adsorption](../../../../../adsorption.md) places the positive hydrogen end farther from the metal, producing an outward molecular [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) and the observed decrease in [work function](../../../../../work-function.md). Its maximum normal component, however, is only $1.42$ D. Tilting alone can only reduce that component; it cannot explain an effective $2.20$ D.

The adsorbed [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md) and metal therefore undergo appreciable [electron density](../../../../../electron-density.md). Lone-pair donation into the metal, polarization of the substrate, image response and distortion of the adsorbate can change the effective interface dipole. The inferred increase relative to an upright isolated [molecule](../../../../../molecule-split.md) is about **$0.78$ D**. For scale, transferring about $0.16$ elementary charges over $1$ Å would produce that dipole increment:

$$
\frac{0.78(3.33564\times10^{-30})}{10^{-10}}
\simeq0.16e.
$$

This scale illustrates a plausible interfacial polarization; the work-function measurement alone does not uniquely determine a transferred charge or [adsorption](../../../../../adsorption.md) geometry.
