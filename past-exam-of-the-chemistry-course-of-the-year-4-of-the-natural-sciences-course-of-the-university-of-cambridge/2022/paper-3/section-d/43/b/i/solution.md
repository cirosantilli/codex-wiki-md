Here $\mathbf j$ is electric [current density](../../../../../../../current-density.md), $n$ the [number density](../../../../../../../number-density.md) of independently mobile [ions](../../../../../../../ion.md), $Q$ their charge, $T$ absolute temperature and $k_B$ the [Boltzmann constant](../../../../../../../boltzmann-constant.md). The sum runs over all nearest-neighbour destinations $j$ of site $i$, and $\mathbf r_{ij}$ is the displacement from $i$ to $j$. The applied field is $\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}$, $\omega_0$ is an attempt [frequency](../../../../../../../frequency.md), and $\Delta E_{ij}$ is the zero-field [reaction energy barrier](../../../../../../../reaction-energy-barrier.md) for the indicated jump. With the normalization of the supplied equation,

$$
\boxed{f(\omega_0,\Delta E_{ij},T)=\omega_0\exp[-\Delta E_{ij}/(k_BT)]}.
$$

It has units s$^{-1}$; it is not an equilibrium occupation [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md). The current formula is the linear-response result of a small field-induced asymmetry in forward and reverse rates, requiring $|Q\mathbf r_{ij}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}|\ll k_BT$. Use its explicit factor $1/2$ consistently rather than absorbing it into a redefined jump rate.
