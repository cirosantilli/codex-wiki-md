<h1 id="section-e/44/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The acid initially protonates 1 and switches the system into the lithium-bound reporter state, so **512 nm intensity rises rapidly**. The resulting carboxylate then decarboxylates irreversibly. Its [carbanion](../../../../../../carbanion.md) removes the proton from 1, giving neutral waste 5 and restoring the original [zinc](../../../../../../zinc.md)/[lithium](../../../../../../lithium.md) distribution. Thus the 512 nm signal is a **transient pulse followed by recovery to its initial low baseline**; no repeated oscillations follow from one fuel addition.

For first-order [decarboxylation](../../../../../../decarboxylation.md) the surviving fuel-derived carboxylate fraction is $e^{-kt}$ with $k=\ln2/(75\ \mathrm{min})$. If proton exchange and metal redistribution are fast, background is subtracted, and the population of the switched state is proportional to remaining protonated 1, the illustrative recovery is

$$
\boxed{I_{512}(t)=I_{\rm low}+(I_{\rm high}-I_{\rm low})2^{-t/(75\,\mathrm{min})}},\qquad t>0.
$$

Half the initial excess signal remains after 75 min and one quarter after 150 min in that limiting model. Actual binding equilibria can make the intensity nonlinear in remaining fuel; the supplied [half-life](../../../../../../half-life.md) fixes the fuel decay, not an exact reporter-intensity law. Monitor $^1$H [NMR](../../../../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md) fuel consumption and waste formation alongside the optical trace to distinguish dissipation-driven recovery from [photobleaching](../../../../../../photobleaching.md) or simple dilution.
