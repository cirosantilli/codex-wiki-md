<h1 id="section-e/45/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The six diphosphines bind the six Pd centres, occupying two adjacent sites on each and displacing two pyridines per Pd. Reassembly therefore favours the two-connected Pd nodes of the bipyramid instead of the cube's four-connected nodes. The balanced transformation is

$$
\boxed{[\mathrm{Fe}_8\mathrm{Pd}_6L_8]^{28+}+6\,\mathrm{dppp}\longrightarrow2[\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{Pd}_3L_2(\mathrm{dppp})_3]^{10+}+4[\mathrm{Fe}L]^{2+}}.
$$

Thus **two cages of type 4 and four free [metalloligands](../../../../../../metalloligand.md) of type 3** form per cage 5. [iron](../../../../../../iron.md), [palladium](../../../../../../palladium.md), ligand and charge balances all close. Strong phosphine [chelation](../../../../../../chelation.md) drives the donor replacement, while the release of additional discrete species can favour entropy. The analogous conversion is observed experimentally in the cited heterobimetallic system.[Scheme 3 and discussion of six-equivalent dppp addition](../../../../../../dynamic-complex-to-complex-transformations-of-heterobimetallic-systems-influence-the-cage-structure-or-spin-state-of-iron-ii-ions-by-matthias-hardy-and-others.md)
