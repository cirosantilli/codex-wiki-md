<h1 id="section-b/39/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

When $|eV|$ reaches a vibrational quantum, a tunnelling [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) can lose energy by exciting that vibration. Opening an inelastic channel changes $dI/dV$ at both voltage polarities. If the conductance increases in each direction, $d^2I/dV^2$ has a positive feature at positive threshold and a negative feature at the corresponding negative threshold. Approximate antisymmetry follows from approximate symmetry of the conductance; unequal tip and sample electronic structures can make amplitudes unequal.

The three energy scales are **8, 48 and 375 meV**, corresponding to about **65, 387 and 3025 cm$^{-1}$**. A plausible assignment is a low-energy frustrated lateral translation or rotation, an [adsorbate](../../../../../../adsorbate.md)–metal stretch or hindered molecular mode at 48 meV, and a C–H stretching vibration at 375 meV. The terminal [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) of methyl isocyanide supplies a plausible [adsorption](../../../../../../adsorption.md) contact, but exact mode assignment requires calculated [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) or independent vibrational measurements. In particular the 48 meV band should not be labelled a C–N stretching vibration simply because the molecule contains that bond: its [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) is far too low for the usual internal stretching mode.
