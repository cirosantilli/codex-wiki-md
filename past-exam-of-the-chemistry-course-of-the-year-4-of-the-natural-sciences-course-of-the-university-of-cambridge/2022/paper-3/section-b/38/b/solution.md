<h1 id="section-b/38/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The hemispherical component needs 100.0 eV [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md), whereas [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) arrive at its entrance optics with 111.4 eV. The optics must therefore remove **11.4 eV**, requiring a **retarding-potential magnitude of 11.4 V**. For a negative [electron](../../../../../../electron.md), moving to a more negative electrostatic potential increases potential energy, so the retarding region is 11.4 V negative relative to the analyser entrance. Equivalently the hemisphere entrance is 11.8 V negative relative to the sample vacuum reference after including the 0.4 V contact step. Reporting 11.8 V as the additional instrument retardation would count the contact correction twice.
