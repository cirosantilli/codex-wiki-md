<h1 id="section-b/38/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The initial [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) environment loses intensity on heating, and a lower-binding-energy component becomes prominent near the highest temperatures. **A change of surface chemical species accompanies loss or conversion of the original [adsorbate](../../../../../../adsorbate.md).** Plausible processes include thermally activated decomposition with nitrogen-containing fragments remaining on [copper](../../../../../../copper.md), [desorption](../../../../../../desorption.md) of intact material or fragments, and rearrangement of the coordination environment. The extra components at intermediate temperatures suggest coexistence rather than a rigid, uniform shift of every molecule.

The individual curves have vertical offsets, so their baseline height is not a coverage measurement. Integrated, sensitivity-corrected N and C peak areas, a clean heated substrate control, and evolved-gas [mass spectrometry](../../../../../../mass-spectrometry.md) would help distinguish loss of material from chemical conversion. [binding energy](../../../../../../binding-energy.md) alone cannot establish a unique fragment structure: local charge, metallic screening and coordination all affect it.
