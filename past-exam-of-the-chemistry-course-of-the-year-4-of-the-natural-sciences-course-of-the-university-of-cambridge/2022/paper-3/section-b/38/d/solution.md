<h1 id="section-b/38/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Define the retard ratio using the [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) at the analyser entrance before the deliberate retardation: $R=K_a/E_p$. Initially $R=111.4/100.0=1.114$. With the original excitation, a [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) [photoelectron](../../../../../../photoelectron.md) arrives with $K_{a,C}=515.0-284.8-4.4=225.8\ \mathrm{eV}$. Thus

$$
E_{p,C}=\frac{225.8}{1.114}=\boxed{202.69\ \mathrm{eV}}.
$$

The new deliberate retardation is $225.8-202.69=23.11\ \mathrm{eV}$. For fixed slits, angular acceptance and hemisphere radii, the geometrical fractional energy width is approximately constant, so the absolute analyser width grows in proportion to pass energy: **about 2.03 times poorer absolute energy resolution**. Counts may improve, but closely separated [chemical shifts](../../../../../../chemical-shift.md) become harder to resolve. An alternative definition $(K_a-E_p)/E_p$ gives 0.114 rather than 1.114 for the same physical setting and leads to the same final pass energy when used consistently.
