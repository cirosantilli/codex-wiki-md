<h1 id="section-b/38/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The large resonance at the smallest field-to-normal angle identifies an unoccupied orbital whose transition dipole is mainly normal to the surface. An N 1s initial state is approximately spherical; the dipole [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) element for a $1s\to\pi^*$ transition is directed along the normal to the [adsorbate](../../../../../../adsorbate.md)'s aromatic plane. Consequently **the [adsorbate](../../../../../../adsorbate.md) rings lie predominantly parallel to the [copper](../../../../../../copper.md) surface**. For perfectly flat molecules the resonance strength scales as $|\mathbf E\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}|^2\propto\cos^2\theta$, which decreases strongly from $17^\circ$ to $67^\circ$ as observed.

For azimuthally disordered molecular normals tilted by $\alpha$ from the surface normal, averaging gives $I(\theta)\propto\cos^2\theta\cos^2\alpha+\tfrac12\sin^2\theta\sin^2\alpha$. Strong dichroism therefore implies a large orientational order parameter, but the plotted qualitative curves alone do not determine a unique tilt angle or distribution. Background subtraction, incident [polarization](../../../../../../polarization-waves.md) and intensity normalization would be needed for such a fit. This experiment identifies the orientation of the pi system; it does not by itself identify a particular [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md)–[copper](../../../../../../copper.md) bonding topology.
