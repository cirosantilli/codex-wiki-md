<h1 id="section-f/46/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose a [reaction coordinate](../../../../../../../reaction-coordinate.md) $s(q)$ that is negative in the reactant region and positive in the product region; $s=0$ defines a [dividing surface](../../../../../../../dividing-surface.md). With unnormalized canonical phase-space averaging, define

$$
C_{fs}(t)=\frac1{(2\pi\hbar)^f}\int dq\,dp\,e^{-\beta H(q,p)}\,\delta[s(q)]\dot s(q,p)\Theta[s(q_t)].
$$

The signed initial flux $\delta(s)\dot s$ counts both directions, while the later side function tests product commitment. The rate is **$k=C_{fs}(t_{\rm pl})/Q_r$** on a plateau after microscopic recrossings have died away but before reactant depletion and global equilibration become important. The $t\to0^+$ limit instead gives the transition-state-theory positive flux and can overcount trajectories that later return. A suitable [dividing surface](../../../../../../../dividing-surface.md) changes that early flux but not a correctly evaluated committed plateau rate. This correlation formulation provides the dynamical correction to a transition-state estimate.[Journal of Chemical Physics 68, pp. 2959–2970: rate correlation functions and recrossing](../../../../../../../statistical-mechanics-of-isomerization-dynamics-in-liquids-and-the-transition-state-approximation-by-david-chandler.md)
