# Aliasing

↑ **Parent:** [Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem](nyquist-shannon-sampling-theorem.md)

[Aliasing](aliasing.md) is the inability to distinguish different continuous [frequencies](frequency.md) from sampled values. The identity above says that [frequencies](frequency.md) separated by an integer multiple of the sampling rate $f_s$ give identical samples of a complex [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md). For real [cosines](cosine.md), changing the sign of a [frequency](frequency.md) also gives the same waveform, explaining the $2$-versus-$8$ example at rate $10$. The [Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem](nyquist-shannon-sampling-theorem.md) prevents this ambiguity within its specified class of [bandlimited functions](bandlimited-function.md). Before sampling, unwanted higher [frequencies](frequency.md) must be suppressed if they would violate that assumption; once two waveforms give the same samples, those samples cannot identify which was present.
