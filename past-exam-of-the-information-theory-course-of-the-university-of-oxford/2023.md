<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

**The inequality is true** for ordinary addition, and also for addition in a [group](../group.md). For any fixed $y$, the translation $x\mapsto x+y$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function), so it merely relabels the [probability masses](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass) in the [conditional distribution](../probability-theory.md#conditional-distribution). Since $X,Y$ are [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables),

$$
H(X+Y\mid Y=y)=H(X\mid Y=y)=H(X).
$$

Averaging over $y$ and using [conditioning reduces entropy](../information-theory.md#conditioning-reduces-entropy) proves [entropy monotonicity under independent addition](../information-entropy.md#entropy-monotonicity-under-independent-addition):

$$
\boxed{H(X+Y)\geq H(X+Y\mid Y)=H(X).}
$$

There is no requirement that $X+Y$ remain in the original [finite alphabet](../information-theory.md#finite-alphabet). The proof also gives $H(X+Y)\geq H(Y)$ by symmetry. Independence matters: for a nonconstant $X$ and $Y=-X$, the sum is constant while $H(X)>0$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Let $N=|\mathcal X|\geq2$ and let $E=\mathbf1_{\{X\ne Y\}}$ be the error [indicator random variable](../expected-value.md#indicator-random-variable). Because $E$ is determined by $(X,Y)$, the [chain rule for conditional entropy](../information-theory.md#chain-rule-for-conditional-entropy) in its two orders gives

$$
H(X\mid Y)=H(X,E\mid Y)=H(E\mid Y)+H(X\mid E,Y).
$$

By [conditioning reduces entropy](../information-theory.md#conditioning-reduces-entropy), $H(E\mid Y)\leq H(E)$. On $E=0$, knowing $Y$ determines $X$, so the corresponding [conditional entropy](../information-theory.md#conditional-entropy) is zero. On $E=1$, at most $N-1$ symbols remain possible for $X$. The maximum [Shannon entropy](../information-entropy.md) of a [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) on at most $N-1$ symbols is $\log(N-1)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{H(X\mid Y)\leq H(\mathbf1_{\{X\ne Y\}})+\Pr(X\ne Y)\log(N-1).}
$$

For an arbitrary [function](../function.md) $g:\mathcal X\to\mathcal X$, repeat the argument with $E_g=\mathbf1_{\{X\ne g(Y)\}}$. This is still determined by $(X,Y)$; when it is zero, $X=g(Y)$, and when it is one, $X$ has at most $N-1$ possibilities for each $Y$. Thus [Fano's inequality](../information-theory.md#fano-s-inequality) gives

$$
\boxed{H(X\mid Y)\leq H(\mathbf1_{\{X\ne g(Y)\}})+\Pr(X\ne g(Y))\log(N-1).}
$$

Events of zero [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) contribute zero to the conditional average, covering the error [probabilities](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero and one. If $N=1$, the left side and error [probabilities](../probability-theory.md#probability) are zero; the trivial conclusion is handled separately instead of writing $0\log0$ as an ordinary finite product.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For the [entropy summand](../information-entropy.md#entropy-summand) $f(x)=-x\log x$, differentiation on $(0,1)$ gives

$$
f'(x)=-\frac{\ln x+1}{\ln2},\qquad f''(x)=-\frac1{x\ln2}<0.
$$

Since $f(x)\to0$ as $x\downarrow0$, its continuous extension to $[0,1]$ is a [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function).

Fix $0<v<1$ and define the increment $D_v(t)=f(t+v)-f(t)$ on $[0,1-v]$. In its interior,

$$
D_v'(t)=f'(t+v)-f'(t)=-\log\frac{t+v}{t}<0.
$$

Thus the increment is decreasing, and its endpoint values are $D_v(0)=f(v)$ and $D_v(1-v)=-f(1-v)$. Therefore

$$
-f(1-v)\leq f(t+v)-f(t)\leq f(v),
\qquad
\boxed{|f(t+v)-f(t)|\leq\max\{f(v),f(1-v)\}.}
$$

The cases $v=0,1$ follow directly from the endpoint values.

To obtain the sharper half-interval bound, put $G(v)=f(v)-f(1-v)$. It has $G(0)=G(1/2)=0$, while

$$
G''(v)=\frac{2v-1}{v(1-v)\ln2}\leq0\qquad(0<v\leq1/2).
$$

Hence $G$ is a [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) on $[0,1/2]$ and lies above the chord joining its zero endpoints. Thus $f(v)\geq f(1-v)$ there, proving

$$
\boxed{|f(t+v)-f(t)|\leq f(v)\qquad(0\leq v\leq1/2).}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Set $N=|\mathcal X|$, $d_i=|p_i-q_i|$ and $d=\sum_i d_i=\|p-q\|_1$. If $d=0$, the two [probability mass functions](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass-function) agree and the bound is zero. Otherwise every $d_i\leq d\leq1/2$. The half-interval [entropy summand](../information-entropy.md#entropy-summand) bound, with $t=\min(p_i,q_i)$ and $v=d_i$, gives

$$
|f(p_i)-f(q_i)|\leq f(d_i).
$$

By the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality), followed by [Jensen's inequality](../real-analysis.md#jensen-s-inequality) for the [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) $f$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|H(p)-H(q)|
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^N|f(p_i)-f(q_i)|
\leq\sum_{i=1}^Nf(d_i)\\
&\leq Nf\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^Nd_i\right)
=-d\log\frac dN.
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves the requested [finite-alphabet entropy continuity bound](../information-entropy.md#finite-alphabet-entropy-continuity-bound):

$$
\boxed{|H(p)-H(q)|\leq-\|p-q\|_1\log\left(\frac{\|p-q\|_1}{|\mathcal X|}\right).}
$$

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) hypothesis factors the [joint probability mass function](../random-variable.md#joint-probability-mass-function):

$$
p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\prod_{i=1}^np(X_i),\qquad
\frac1n\log p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\log p(X_i).
$$

Symbols outside the [support of a probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md#support-of-a-probability-distribution) $p$ never occur, almost surely. On its finite positive [probability support](../probability-distribution.md#support-of-a-probability-distribution), $\log p(X_i)$ is bounded, so the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) applies:

$$
\boxed{\frac1n\log p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\longrightarrow
\sum_{x:p(x)>0}p(x)\log p(x)=-H(p)\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

This is the [asymptotic equipartition property](../information-theory.md#asymptotic-equipartition-property) with the sign appropriate to a [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) of a [probability mass](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For a fixed estimated [probability mass function](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass-function) $q$, the estimated [joint probability mass function](../random-variable.md#joint-probability-mass-function) is $q_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\prod_iq(x_i)$. If $q(x)>0$ whenever $p(x)>0$, the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) gives the [mismatched asymptotic equipartition property](../information-entropy.md#mismatched-asymptotic-equipartition-property):

$$
\boxed{\frac1n\log q_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\longrightarrow
\sum_{x:p(x)>0}p(x)\log q(x)
=-H(p,q)=-H(p)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\Vert q)
\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Here $H(p,q)$ is [cross-entropy](../information-entropy.md#cross-entropy), and the last equality is [cross-entropy equals entropy plus relative entropy](../information-entropy.md#cross-entropy-equals-entropy-plus-relative-entropy). The [Kullback-Leibler divergence](../f-divergence.md#kullback-leibler-divergence) measures the extra average [logarithmic loss](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#logarithmic-loss) from the estimated law.

If $q(x)=0$ for some $x$ with $p(x)>0$, let $a=\sum_{x:q(x)=0}p(x)>0$. The [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) that none of the first $n$ observations belongs to this set is $(1-a)^n\to0$. Almost surely such a symbol eventually occurs, after which $q_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=0$ forever. Thus the limit is **$-\infty$**, consistent with infinite [cross-entropy](../information-entropy.md#cross-entropy) and infinite [Kullback-Leibler divergence](../f-divergence.md#kullback-leibler-divergence).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

For every $x^n\in A^{n,\varepsilon}$, the defining [typical set](../information-theory.md#typical-set) constraint implies

$$
2^{-n(H(p)+\varepsilon)}\leq p_n(x^n)\leq2^{-n(H(p)-\varepsilon)}.
$$

Let $S_n=A^{n,\varepsilon}\cap B^{n,\varepsilon}$. Summing the lower [probability mass](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass) bound over $S_n$ gives

$$
1\geq\Pr(X^n\in S_n)
=\sum_{x^n\in S_n}p_n(x^n)
\geq |S_n|2^{-n(H(p)+\varepsilon)}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{|A^{n,\varepsilon}\cap B^{n,\varepsilon}|\leq2^{n(H(p)+\varepsilon)}.}
$$

The [sample mean](../variance.md#sample-mean) constraint can only shrink the [typical set](../information-theory.md#typical-set), so this [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) upper bound holds for every positive integer $n$.

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the [asymptotic equipartition property](../information-theory.md#asymptotic-equipartition-property) gives $\Pr(X^n\notin A^{n,\varepsilon})\to0$. The [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) applied to $X_i$, which are bounded because their numerical [alphabet](../information-theory.md#alphabet) is finite, gives $\Pr(X^n\notin B^{n,\varepsilon})\to0$. The [union bound](../probability-inequality.md#boole-s-inequality) therefore yields

$$
\Pr(X^n\in S_n)\geq1-\Pr(X^n\notin A^{n,\varepsilon})-\Pr(X^n\notin B^{n,\varepsilon})\longrightarrow1.
$$

Since $\delta>0$, eventually $\Pr(X^n\in S_n)\geq2^{-\delta}$. Each word in $S_n$ has [probability mass](../probability-distribution.md#probability-mass) at most $2^{-n(H(p)-\varepsilon)}$, so

$$
2^{-\delta}\leq\Pr(X^n\in S_n)
\leq |S_n|2^{-n(H(p)-\varepsilon)}.
$$

Rearranging proves

$$
\boxed{|A^{n,\varepsilon}\cap B^{n,\varepsilon}|\geq2^{n(H(p)-\varepsilon)-\delta}}
$$

for sufficiently large $n$. The key is that the factor $2^{-\delta}$ is a fixed number below one, however small $\delta$ is. This is an instance of [typical sets with an additional high-probability constraint](../information-theory.md#typical-sets-with-an-additional-high-probability-constraint).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

First, the binary translation of a quaternary [prefix code](../coding-theory.md#prefix-code) is still a [prefix code](../coding-theory.md#prefix-code): every translated [codeword length](../coding-theory.md#codeword-length) is even, and a binary prefix relation would split into complete two-[bit](../information-theory.md#bit) blocks, giving a prefix relation between the original quaternary [codewords](../coding-theory.md#codeword). Binary [Huffman coding](../information-theory.md#huffman-coding) is optimal among binary [prefix codes](../coding-theory.md#prefix-code), so

$$
\mathbb E|c_B(X_1)|\leq\mathbb E|c_{QB}(X_1)|.
$$

For the other direction, let $\ell_i=|c_B(x_i)|$ and round these binary [codeword lengths](../coding-theory.md#codeword-length) to quaternary lengths $k_i=\lceil\ell_i/2\rceil$. The [Kraft inequality](../coding-theory.md#kraft-mcmillan-inequality) gives

$$
\sum_i4^{-k_i}=\sum_i2^{-2\lceil\ell_i/2\rceil}
\leq\sum_i2^{-\ell_i}\leq1.
$$

The quaternary converse of the [Kraft inequality](../coding-theory.md#kraft-mcmillan-inequality) supplies a quaternary [prefix code](../coding-theory.md#prefix-code) with lengths $k_i$. Optimality of the [quaternary Huffman code](../information-theory.md#quaternary-huffman-coding) then implies

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|c_{QB}(X_1)|
&=2\mathbb E|c_Q(X_1)|
\leq2\sum_i p_i\left\lceil\frac{\ell_i}2\right\rceil\\
&\leq\sum_i p_i(\ell_i+1)
=\mathbb E|c_B(X_1)|+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus **binary translation of an optimal quaternary code costs at most one extra bit per source symbol**:

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E|c_B(X_1)|\leq\mathbb E|c_{QB}(X_1)|\leq\mathbb E|c_B(X_1)|+1.}
$$

The [quaternary Huffman code](../information-theory.md#quaternary-huffman-coding) uses the usual adjustment of adding zero-weight dummy leaves when needed. If the [alphabet](../information-theory.md#alphabet) has one symbol, both optimal codes may use the empty [codeword](../coding-theory.md#codeword), giving the trivial endpoint.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The specified transition law is a [binary erasure channel](../coding-theory.md#binary-erasure-channel). For an input [bit](../information-theory.md#bit) with $\Pr(X=1)=a$, its [Shannon entropy](../information-entropy.md) is the [binary entropy](../information-entropy.md#binary-entropy) $h_2(a)$. A non-erased output determines $X$, whereas the erasure symbol leaves its [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) unchanged because the erasure event is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $X$. Thus

$$
H(X\mid Y)=qH(X),\qquad I(X;Y)=(1-q)H(X)=(1-q)h_2(a).
$$

Maximizing the [mutual information](../information-theory.md#mutual-information) over the input [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), $h_2(a)\leq1$ with equality at $a=1/2$, gives

$$
\boxed{C=1-q\quad\text{bits per channel use}.}
$$

This includes $q=0$, a noiseless binary [channel](../information-theory.md#communication-channel), and $q=1$, a completely erased [channel](../information-theory.md#communication-channel) with zero [channel capacity](../information-theory.md#channel-capacity).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Use the standard deterministic [channel encoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-encoder) and [channel decoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-decoder), with $m$ message labels and [code rate](../coding-theory.md#code-rate) $R=(\log m)/n$. The [Fano's inequality](../information-theory.md#fano-s-inequality) hint printed here replaces $\log(m-1)$ by $\log m-1$, which is false in general: take $X$ uniform on $\{0,1\}$ and $Y=2$ in the common [alphabet](../information-theory.md#alphabet) $\{0,1,2\}$. The error [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is one and its [binary entropy](../information-entropy.md#binary-entropy) is zero, so the proposed bound asserts $1\leq\log3-1<1$. The correct [Fano's inequality](../information-theory.md#fano-s-inequality) alone gives $(1-P_e)\log m\leq n(1-q)+h_2(P_e)$, which does not immediately give the requested additive $1/n$. The following [erasure-pattern counting bound for maximal decoding error](../coding-theory.md#erasure-pattern-counting-bound-for-maximal-decoding-error) proves the displayed claim directly.

The case $m\leq2$ is immediate because $R\leq1/n$. Suppose $m>2$, put $L=\log m-1>0$, and let $w_0$ be the message that the [channel decoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-decoder) returns on the all-erased output. Choose $W$ uniformly among the other $m-1$ messages. Every one of these messages is decoded correctly with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) at least $1-\delta$, by the assumed [maximal decoding error probability](../coding-theory.md#maximal-decoding-error-probability) bound.

Let $S$ be the random set of unerased coordinates and $K=|S|$. For each fixed $S$ with $K=k\geq1$, at most $2^k$ output words are possible. A deterministic [channel decoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-decoder) can correctly identify at most one message per output word, so its conditional success [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability), averaged over these $m-1$ messages, is at most $\min\{1,2^k/(m-1)\}$. When $K=0$, success is zero because $w_0$ was excluded.

For every integer $k\geq1$,

$$
\min\left\{1,\frac{2^k}{m-1}\right\}\leq\frac{k}{L}.
$$

If $k\geq L$, this is immediate. If $k<L$, write $r=m/2^k>2$. The elementary inequality $\log r\leq r-1$ for $r\geq2$ gives

$$
L=k-1+\log r\leq k+r-2\leq k(r-1)<\frac{k(m-1)}{2^k},
$$

where the middle inequality is $(k-1)(r-2)\geq0$. This proves the needed scalar bound.

Taking the [expectation](../expected-value.md) over erasure patterns, and using $\mathbb E K=n(1-q)$, yields

$$
1-\delta\leq\Pr(\widehat W=W)\leq\frac{\mathbb E K}{L}
=\frac{n(1-q)}{\log m-1}.
$$

Consequently the requested bound is valid:

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{\log m}{n}\leq\frac{1-q}{1-\delta}+\frac1n.}
$$

The use of [maximal decoding error probability](../coding-theory.md#maximal-decoding-error-probability) matters: excluding one message preserves the lower bound on success. The deterministic [channel decoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-decoder) convention also matters; allowing independent randomized guessing would invalidate this particular finite-length claim at $q=1$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

For $0\leq q<1$, use [repeat-until-success coding over an erasure channel](../coding-theory.md#repeat-until-success-coding-over-an-erasure-channel): **delete every erasure symbol from the received sequence and retain the non-erased bits in order**. Each message [bit](../information-theory.md#bit) contributes a string of erasures followed by exactly one correct non-erased copy. The [channel encoder](../coding-theory.md#channel-encoder) advances to the next [bit](../information-theory.md#bit) only after this successful copy, so the retained [bits](../information-theory.md#bit) are precisely $W_1,\ldots,W_n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{d(Y)=W,\qquad\Pr(d(Y)\ne W)=0.}
$$

Indeed, $\Pr(M_j>k)=q^k\to0$, so each stage and the finite collection of $n$ stages terminate [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The protocol uses [channel feedback](../information-theory.md#channel-feedback): Alice must know whether an erasure occurred before deciding whether to repeat. This feedback is implicit in the stated algorithm and is not counted as a forward [channel](../information-theory.md#communication-channel) use.

At $q=1$, no stage ever finishes, so there is **no terminating zero-error decoder for arbitrary messages**. This endpoint is excluded from the operational claim above.

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

For $0\leq q<1$, the number of attempts has the [geometric distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#geometric-distribution) with success [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1-q$:

$$
\Pr(M_1=k)=q^{k-1}(1-q),\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.
$$

The [tail-sum formula for expectation](../probability-theory.md#tail-sum-formula-for-expectation) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E M_1=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Pr(M_1>k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^k=\frac1{1-q}.}
$$

At $q=0$, $M_1=1$ deterministically. At $q=1$, $M_1=+\infty$ and its [expectation](../expected-value.md) is $+\infty$, rather than a finite waiting time.

<h4 id="3/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/c/iii)

For $0\leq q<1$, the total number of forward [channel](../information-theory.md#communication-channel) uses is $T_n=\sum_{j=1}^nM_j$, so the random [code rate](../coding-theory.md#code-rate) of a message with $n$ [bits](../information-theory.md#bit) is

$$
R_n=\frac{n}{T_n}=\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nM_j\right)^{-1}.
$$

The [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) $M_j$ have finite [expectation](../expected-value.md) $1/(1-q)$. By the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers),

$$
\boxed{R_n\longrightarrow1-q\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Thus the protocol reaches the [binary erasure channel](../coding-theory.md#binary-erasure-channel) [channel capacity](../information-theory.md#channel-capacity) in its asymptotic forward throughput while guaranteeing zero error on every completed message. Its random transmission length and use of [channel feedback](../information-theory.md#channel-feedback) distinguish it from the fixed-length [channel codes](../coding-theory.md#channel-code) in part (b). Independent erasure indicators suffice for this calculation; the message [bits](../information-theory.md#bit) themselves need not be [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) or uniformly distributed. At $q=1$, the algorithm never produces a completed transmission, so $R_n$ as a completed-code rate is undefined; the physical throughput is zero.
