[Oral language](../../../oral-language.md) shapes [written language](../../../written-language.md) development by supplying the phonological, lexical, grammatical and discourse knowledge that print must connect with. The influence is not unidirectional. [Learning](../../../learning.md) to read and write can make speech structure more explicit, expand [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) and alter the processing of spoken words. The strength and direction of a particular relation depend on age, writing system, instruction and the aspect of [language](../../../language.md) being measured.

At the word level, alphabetic [reading](../../../reading.md) links [graphemes](../../../grapheme.md) with speech sounds and familiar spoken forms. [Phonological awareness](../../../phonological-awareness.md) can help a [child](../../../child.md) analyse a word and use letter–sound relations, but it is not identical to speech comprehension or to the ability to pronounce a word. A [child](../../../child.md) may understand a spoken word without being able to segment its [phonemes](../../../phoneme.md) deliberately. [Reading instruction](../../../reading-instruction.md) and [metalinguistic awareness](../../../metalinguistic-awareness.md) can consequently develop together rather than one being wholly completed before the other starts.

Longitudinal evidence separates some routes. [Valerie Muter](../../../valerie-muter.md) and colleagues found that letter knowledge and [phoneme](../../../phoneme.md) sensitivity predicted early [word recognition](../../../word-recognition.md), while [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) and [grammar](../../../grammar.md) contributed to comprehension alongside [word recognition](../../../word-recognition.md). The result supports a differentiated [oral language](../../../oral-language.md) foundation. It does not imply that [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) is irrelevant to every unfamiliar-word problem or that the observed predictors alone specify a complete teaching programme.[Developmental Psychology 40, 665–681; ninety school entrants, two-year follow-up and distinct predictors](../../../phonemes-rimes-vocabulary-and-grammatical-skills-as-foundations-of-early-reading-development-evidence-from-a-longitudinal-study-by-valerie-muter-and-others.md)

For text comprehension, oral [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) supplies meanings and [grammar](../../../grammar.md) supports interpretation of relations and reference. Inferencing and discourse knowledge connect information beyond a sentence. [Listening comprehension](../../../listening-comprehension.md) offers a useful comparison when [decoding](../../../word-decoding.md) is suspected to be the bottleneck, but it is not a demand-free measure of [language](../../../language.md). Speech unfolds over time and can tax [attention](../../../attention.md) and [memory](../../../memory-in-animals.md); written text can be reread. These differences should be considered before interpreting a written–spoken discrepancy as a pure [decoding](../../../word-decoding.md) deficit.

[Oral language](../../../oral-language.md) also contributes to producing [written language](../../../written-language.md). Choosing words, constructing sentences and organising a narrative draw on [language](../../../language.md) knowledge, while [spelling](../../../spelling.md) and transcription add constraints. [Julie E. Dockrell](../../../julie-e-dockrell.md) and colleagues followed 64 [children](../../../child.md) with a history of [language](../../../language.md) impairment, assessing them at eight and their written text at ten. The writing showed difficulties in sentence structure and organisation; concurrent [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) and [reading](../../../reading.md) helped explain variation. The associations support a broad profile, not proof that an isolated [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) treatment would resolve composition difficulties.[Exceptional Children 73, 147–164; 64 children with language-impairment history, measures at eight and written text at ten](../../../constraints-in-the-production-of-written-text-in-children-with-specific-language-impairments-by-julie-e-dockrell-and-others.md)

Intervention gives stronger leverage on one direction. [Paula J. Clarke](../../../paula-j-clarke.md) and colleagues' [oral language](../../../oral-language.md) programme improved [reading comprehension](../../../reading-comprehension.md) in [children](../../../child.md) who could already read accurately, with gains extending beyond the intervention period. This supports [oral language](../../../oral-language.md) as a modifiable contributor, while the multicomponent programme and untreated comparator leave its precise ingredients partly open. An implication is that improving [word recognition](../../../word-recognition.md) alone may leave a language-level limitation unresolved.[Psychological Science 21, 1106–1116; text/oral/combined interventions, untreated comparison and eleven-month follow-up](../../../ameliorating-children-s-reading-comprehension-difficulties-a-randomized-controlled-trial-by-paula-j-clarke-and-others.md)

Home experiences provide potentially different inputs. [Monique Sénéchal](../../../monique-senechal.md) and [Jo-Anne LeFevre](../../../jo-anne-lefevre.md) linked book exposure with [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) and [listening comprehension](../../../listening-comprehension.md), and direct parental teaching with early literacy in a five-year study. These routes help explain why 'home literacy' is not one uniform activity. The observational, relatively advantaged sample limits causal and population inference; shared [reading](../../../reading.md) can involve both spoken interaction and print exposure, making it difficult to assign its effects exclusively to either medium.[Child Development 73, 445–460; 168 middle/upper-middle-class children, home activities and distinct language/literacy pathways](../../../parental-involvement-in-the-development-of-children-s-reading-skill-a-five-year-longitudinal-study-by-monique-senechal-and-jo-anne-lefevre.md)

Evidence for the reverse direction has a long history. [José Morais](../../../jose-morais.md) and colleagues compared adults who remained illiterate with people who had learned to read in adulthood. The former had difficulty deliberately adding or deleting a speech phone, whereas adult readers performed the tasks more readily. The contrast suggests that explicit access to phonemic structure is not an automatic consequence of mature spoken [language](../../../language.md). Because literacy was not randomly assigned, schooling and selection remain alternative contributors; the result concerns metalinguistic performance rather than absence of ordinary speech perception.[Cognition 7, 323–331; illiterate and adult-literate comparisons on phone addition/deletion](../../../does-awareness-of-speech-as-a-sequence-of-phones-arise-spontaneously-by-jose-morais-and-others.md)

[Written language](../../../written-language.md) also supplies new lexical knowledge. [William E. Nagy](../../../william-e-nagy.md) and colleagues found small but reliable gains in knowledge of unfamiliar words encountered in natural text. Repeated [reading](../../../reading.md) can therefore contribute to [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) growth, rather than [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md) always being an independent precursor. The amount learned from a passage depends on prior knowledge, context and assessment sensitivity, so tentative population extrapolations should not be confused with a guaranteed [learning](../../../learning.md) rate for every [child](../../../child.md).[Reading Research Quarterly 20, 233–253; incidental word knowledge after reading natural text and limited extrapolation](../../../learning-words-from-context-by-william-e-nagy-and-others.md)

Neuroscience provides converging evidence of reorganisation. [Stanislas Dehaene](../../../stanislas-dehaene.md) and colleagues compared adults with different literacy histories using [fMRI](../../../functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) responses to written and spoken material. Literacy was associated with changed visual responses to writing, stronger phonological responses to speech and activation of orthographic information from spoken input. The inclusion of adult learners shows that relevant differences are not restricted to childhood schooling, but the groups were not randomly assigned. The imaging does not by itself identify a unique instructional cause or show that larger activation is always better.[Science 330, 1359–1364; 10 illiterate/22 adult-literate/31 childhood-literate adults, visual and spoken-language fMRI](../../../how-learning-to-read-changes-the-cortical-networks-for-vision-and-language-by-stanislas-dehaene-and-others.md)

The developmental relation can thus be recursive. Better [language](../../../language.md) knowledge helps a [child](../../../child.md) understand print; successful [reading](../../../reading.md) permits more independent encounters with words and structures; those encounters can support later [oral language](../../../oral-language.md) performance. Difficulty at either stage may restrict opportunities at the next. Such a loop is a hypothesis about mechanisms and experience, not a reason to assume that all observed growth differences are self-amplifying or irreversible.

A strong test would combine repeated measures before and after instruction with controlled changes to oral or written experience. One study could compare [learning](../../../learning.md) the same unfamiliar meanings through listening, independent [reading](../../../reading.md) or supported [reading](../../../reading.md) while matching content and exposure. Test both spoken understanding and written use after a delay, alongside [decoding](../../../word-decoding.md) and prior [vocabulary](../../../vocabulary.md). Another could vary print support during [phoneme](../../../phoneme.md) instruction while assessing untrained speech-segmentation tasks. These designs can distinguish medium, content, instruction and transfer more clearly than a concurrent [correlation](../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md).

Writing outcomes should remain part of that programme. Oral retelling, dictated composition and independent writing can help distinguish [language](../../../language.md) formulation from [spelling](../../../spelling.md) and transcription demands, though the tasks still differ in support and planning. Cross-language and multilingual studies can test whether reciprocity depends on script or experience rather than assuming that an English alphabetic result is universal.

[Oral language](../../../oral-language.md) is therefore a major foundation for [reading](../../../reading.md) and writing, but literacy is also an experience that reshapes [language](../../../language.md) knowledge and access. The most useful account specifies the component and timescale in each direction. It treats experimental, longitudinal, intervention and imaging evidence as complementary constraints on a reciprocal developmental system.
