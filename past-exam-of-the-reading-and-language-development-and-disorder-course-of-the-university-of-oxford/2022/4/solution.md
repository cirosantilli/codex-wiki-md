↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

[Visual word recognition](../../../visual-word-recognition.md) develops through sensitivity to statistical structure at several levels: visual letter patterns, relations between print and sound, and relations between word forms and meanings. Calling it [statistical learning](../../../distributional-learning.md) is meaningful when it generates predictions about which experiences change performance and what transfers to new items. It is less informative if it merely redescribes every consequence of practice, or implies that [children](../../../child.md) learn to read without explicit instruction.

An alphabetic writing system contains systematic but imperfect correspondences. A printed unit can map to different sounds depending on its neighbours, while several [spellings](../../../spelling.md) can express a similar sound. Frequency makes some patterns more available than others; consistency affects how strongly a local cue predicts an outcome. Whole-word familiarity, sublexical generalisation and [semantic](../../../semantics.md) support can therefore develop together. Recognising a trained item does not by itself establish what unit or relation was learned.

[David L. Share](../../../david-l-share.md)'s self-teaching experiment provides a concrete developmental mechanism. Second-grade readers encountered novel written forms while [reading](../../../reading.md) short texts aloud. Three days later they showed preferential [learning](../../../learning.md) of the encountered [spelling](../../../spelling.md) over a homophonic alternative on [spelling](../../../spelling.md) choice, naming and [spelling](../../../spelling.md) tasks. Conditions limiting phonological processing reduced [learning](../../../learning.md), with additional controls addressing visual-exposure explanations. This supports a role for successful recoding in acquiring word-specific representations, rather than treating [orthographic learning](../../../orthographic-learning.md) as a purely visual accumulation of letter shapes.[Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 72, 95–129; second-grade pseudoword reading, delayed spelling-choice/naming/spelling and recoding controls](../../../phonological-recoding-and-orthographic-learning-a-direct-test-of-the-self-teaching-hypothesis-by-david-l-share.md)

Self-teaching also clarifies what the learner's input is. A [child](../../../child.md) does not receive an unlimited sequence of perfectly labelled examples. They must attempt a pronunciation, connect it with a known spoken form or a context, and sometimes correct an initial interpretation. [Reading](../../../reading.md) skill changes which texts are encountered and which mappings are successfully extracted. [Statistical learning](../../../distributional-learning.md) therefore occurs within an actively selected developmental environment, supported by prior [language](../../../language.md) knowledge and instruction.

[Artificial orthographies](../../../artificial-orthography.md) make some of these influences experimentally separable. [Joanne S. H. Taylor](../../../joanne-s-h-taylor.md) and colleagues trained adults on novel written forms and manipulated frequency, consistency and prior phonological or [semantic](../../../semantics.md) knowledge. Participants learned trained items and generalised to new ones, showing sensitivity to more than isolated visual tokens. Pretraining could favour [item-specific learning](../../../item-specific-learning.md) while reducing generalisation. This dissociation demonstrates why [learning](../../../learning.md) many familiar words and abstracting reusable mappings should be measured separately.[Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition 37, 60–76; sixteen/32 adults, novel script, frequency/consistency and pretraining](../../../the-influence-of-consistency-frequency-and-semantics-on-learning-to-read-an-artificial-orthography-paradigm-by-joanne-s-h-taylor-and-others.md)

The apparatus in such studies is comparatively simple but powerful: unfamiliar characters and controlled pronunciations allow the experimenter to define the [learning](../../../learning.md) history. Training and test items can be chosen so that memorising the trained strings will not solve the transfer test. Naming, lexical decisions and generalisation can then constrain what was acquired. Adult participants, short training and artificial scripts limit extrapolation to [children](../../../child.md)'s years of classroom [learning](../../../learning.md), and successful transfer does not uniquely identify a particular [neural network](../../../neural-network.md) or rule-learning algorithm.

[Child](../../../child.md) evidence strengthens the link to ordinary instruction. [Keith S. Apfelbaum](../../../keith-s-apfelbaum.md) and colleagues trained 224 first graders on vowel letter–sound relations embedded in words with either varied or similar consonants. Greater variation in the irrelevant consonants benefited [learning](../../../learning.md) and transfer across words and tasks. The result shows that the distribution of examples matters, not merely their total number. It does not establish one optimal curriculum for every [spelling](../../../spelling.md) system or learner.[Developmental Psychology 49, 1348–1365; 224 first graders, vowel mappings with varied/similar consonants and transfer tasks](../../../statistical-learning-in-reading-variability-in-irrelevant-letters-helps-children-learn-phonics-skills-by-keith-s-apfelbaum-and-others.md)

The idea is intuitive: if one vowel pattern occurs with many different consonants, the invariant mapping is easier to distinguish from accidental properties of a few whole words. This is a hypothesis about cue selection and generalisation. A competing possibility is that highly similar examples force a fine-grained contrast. Experiments can adjudicate these principles by matching exposure and manipulating irrelevant variation, rather than assuming that either maximum variety or maximum similarity must always be best.

Explicit teaching is compatible with this account. [Joanne S. H. Taylor](../../../joanne-s-h-taylor.md) and colleagues taught 24 adults two unfamiliar scripts, contrasting emphasis on print-to-sound and print-to-meaning relations. Print-to-sound emphasis benefited naming, generalisation and single-word comprehension. Their behavioural and imaging results constrain how experience is organised, but do not constitute a classroom trial in beginning readers. The study shows why a statistical account should include what instructions direct the learner to notice.[Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 146, 826–858; 24 adults, two novel scripts, eight-day training, generalisation and fMRI](../../../comparing-and-validating-methods-of-reading-instruction-using-behavioural-and-neural-findings-in-an-artificial-orthography-by-joanne-s-h-taylor-and-others.md)

Several levels of explanation must remain distinct. An observed frequency effect identifies a property of performance. A [learning](../../../learning.md) model must specify representations, [learning](../../../learning.md) rules and the input needed to produce it. A developmental theory must additionally explain changes in prior [language](../../../language.md), [attention](../../../attention.md), instruction and [reading](../../../reading.md) experience. A computational system fitted to skilled performance may not reproduce acquisition, and a model that learns successfully from an unrealistic input has not established how [children](../../../child.md) solve the problem.

The [word recognition](../../../word-recognition.md) task also extends beyond an isolated spelling-to-sound mapping. Efficient recognition must tolerate changes in font and case, distinguish similar forms, access meaning and operate in context. Different scripts make different statistical units useful. Evidence from one English-trained adult sample should not be presented as proving an identical grain size across [languages](../../../language.md). [Statistical learning](../../../distributional-learning.md) can be a common principle while the representations and instructional demands differ.

A decisive developmental test would hold total exposures and teaching time constant while varying a specified distribution, measure both trained and untrained forms, and assess retention after a delay. Pre-existing [decoding](../../../word-decoding.md) and [oral language](../../../oral-language.md) skills should be measured before allocation, with prespecified tests of moderation. Eye movements or response times can supplement accuracy, but each needs an explicit model linking the measurement to the [learning](../../../learning.md) hypothesis. A classroom replication should then test whether the predicted advantage survives ordinary teaching and broader text demands.

[Visual word recognition](../../../visual-word-recognition.md) is thus a feat of [statistical learning](../../../distributional-learning.md) in a substantive sense: readers extract useful relations from a structured, partly inconsistent environment and generalise beyond particular experiences. The claim becomes explanatory when it specifies the input, units, [learning](../../../learning.md) process and transfer predictions. It remains compatible with [phonological recoding](../../../phonological-recoding.md), [semantic](../../../semantics.md) knowledge and explicit instruction, and it requires more than observing that frequently encountered words are read quickly.
