# Facial boundary walk

↑ **Parent:** [Face of a plane graph](face-of-a-plane-graph.md)

For a finite [connected graph](connected-graph.md) embedded as a [plane graph](plane-graph.md), follow the [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) around a [face of a plane graph](face-of-a-plane-graph.md) to obtain a [closed walk](closed-walk.md). Count traversals, not distinct [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md): a [bridge in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md) occurs twice along one [facial boundary walk](facial-boundary-walk.md). Each [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) contributes two sides in total.[Mathematics for Computer Science, §12.4, Lemma 12.4.1, printed p. 485](mathematics-for-computer-science-by-eric-lehman-and-others.md)

For the three-[vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md) $A-B-C$, the only [facial boundary walk](facial-boundary-walk.md) is $A,B,C,B,A$, of length four. Both [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) are [bridges in a graph](bridge-graph-theory.md); neither is a [graph cycle](cycle-in-a-graph.md). Thus a [facial boundary walk](facial-boundary-walk.md) need not be a [graph cycle](cycle-in-a-graph.md).
