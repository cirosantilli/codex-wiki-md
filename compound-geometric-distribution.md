# Compound geometric distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Random sum of independent claims](random-sum-of-independent-claims.md)

Take an independent [geometric distribution](geometric-distribution.md) count with $\mathbb P(N=k)=p(1-p)^k$, $k\ge0$, and sum its independent positive claims. Its [moment-generating function](moment-generating-function.md) is the displayed composition wherever finite. With $q=1-p$ and severity [moments](moment.md) $m_j$, the third [cumulant](cumulant.md) is $(q/p)m_3+3(q/p)^2m_1m_2+2(q/p)^3m_1^3$, which is strictly positive for nonzero positive claims and $0<p<1$. The law has an atom of mass $p$ at zero. Its positive-count convention would instead add a factor $M_X(t)$ to the numerator, as in the [geometric-sum moment-generating function](geometric-sum-moment-generating-function.md).
