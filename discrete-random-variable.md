# Discrete random variable

↑ **Parent:** [Random variable](random-variable-split.md)

A [random variable](random-variable-split.md) is discrete when some finite or [countable set](countable-set.md) $D\subseteq\mathbb R$ has $\mathbb P(X\in D)=1$. Its [probability mass function](probability-mass-function.md) is $p_X(x)=\mathbb P(X=x)$; these nonnegative masses sum to one. An [event](event.md) determined by the value has [probability](probability.md) $\mathbb P(X\in A)=\sum_{x\in A\cap D}p_X(x)$. Values outside $D$ may still occur on a [zero-probability event](zero-probability-event.md) without changing this definition.

Countable support need not consist of [integers](integer-split.md): a random variable constantly equal to $3/2$ is discrete. Integer-indexed [tail-sum formulas](tail-sum-formula.md) therefore require a separate integer-valued hypothesis. For the dice sum in [random variable](random-variable-split.md), the [expected value](expected-value-split.md) is $\sum_{s=2}^{12}s\,p_S(s)=7$ and the [variance](variance-split.md) is $\sum_{s=2}^{12}(s-7)^2p_S(s)=35/6$; the same answer follows by adding the [variances](variance-split.md) of its [independent](independent-random-variables.md) summands.
