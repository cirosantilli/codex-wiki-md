# First ascent in independent continuous observations

↑ **Parent:** [Order symmetry of independent random variables](order-symmetry-of-independent-random-variables.md)

For [independent and identically distributed random variables](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with a [continuous probability distribution](continuous-probability-distribution-split.md), let $N\geq2$ be the index of the first increase. The event $N\geq n$ requires the first $n-1$ observations to be decreasing; symmetry among their $(n-1)!$ possible strict orders gives probability $1/(n-1)!$ for $n\geq2$. Subtracting successive tails gives the displayed mass function for $r\geq2$. The tails tend to zero, so $N$ is finite almost surely. The [tail-sum formula for expectation](tail-sum-formula-for-expectation.md) gives $\mathbb EN=1+\sum_{j\geq1}1/j!=e$, independently of the continuous sampling law.
