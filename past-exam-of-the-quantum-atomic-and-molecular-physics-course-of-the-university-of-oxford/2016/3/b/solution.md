<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $A=A_{21}$ and $r=g_2/g_1$. Before [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) and [stimulated absorption](../../../../stimulated-absorption.md) are added, the source and decay terms give

$$
\dot N_2=R_2-AN_2,\qquad
\dot N_1=AN_2-\frac{N_1}{\tau_1}.
$$

The upper level is fed at a specified rate of addition to the [number density](../../../../number-density.md), its spontaneous decay feeds the lower level, and lower-level removal leads to states outside this [two-level system](../../../../two-level-system.md). This is therefore an open pumped system, not a closed [two-level system](../../../../two-level-system.md) with conserved population.

At steady state,

$$
N_2^{(0)}=\frac{R_2}{A},\qquad N_1^{(0)}=R_2\tau_1,
\qquad\boxed{N_0^*=R_2\left(\frac1A-r\tau_1\right).}
$$

The degeneracy-weighted [population inversion](../../../../population-inversion.md) compares population per [magnetic sublevel](../../../../magnetic-sublevel.md). It is positive only if $rA\tau_1<1$; a positive pump rate alone cannot guarantee [optical gain](../../../../optical-gain.md) if lower-level removal is too slow. A short lower-state lifetime is consequently helpful even when the upper-state pumping is unchanged.

The equations assume finite positive $A$ and $\tau_1$, approximately constant pumping and no other specified sources, sinks or branching channels. A finite steady state would not exist for continuous pumping if the lower level had no escape route. The fixed source rate also presumes access to an external reservoir; it does not imply that the two populations can grow beyond all available active atoms in a real finite medium.
