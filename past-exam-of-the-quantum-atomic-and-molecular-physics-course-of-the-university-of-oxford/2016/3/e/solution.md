<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For the second transition, $r=3$ and the much longer lower-level lifetime changes the sign of the available [population inversion](../../../../population-inversion.md):

$$
A^{-1}-r\tau_1=\frac1{3\times10^6}-3(3\times10^{-7})
=-5.667\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm s,
\qquad D_0=-2.833\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}.
$$

If its [emission cross section](../../../../emission-cross-section.md) equals the first transition's value, the estimate is

$$
\boxed{\alpha_0\simeq-0.1133\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}=-11.33\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}.}
$$

The word “comparable” does not specify exact equality, so the more general answer is $\alpha_0=-(0.1133\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}})[\sigma_2/(4\times10^{-15}\ \mathrm{cm^2})]$. The quoted numerical value is an explicitly equal-cross-section estimate, while the negative sign is robust for any positive comparable [emission cross section](../../../../emission-cross-section.md).

In this case $rA\tau_1=2.7>1$. At fixed pump, $N_1=1.50\times10^{13}$ cm$^{-3}$, so its degeneracy-weighted contribution exceeds the upper population. The medium therefore absorbs a weak beam at this transition. Increasing the radiation saturates the [photon absorption](../../../../photon-absorption.md) magnitude toward zero according to [c](../c.md); it does not convert this fixed-pump model into an [optical amplifier](../../../../optical-amplifier.md) with positive gain. Simply inserting the magnitude $|\alpha_0|$ into the propagation law for positive [optical gain](../../../../optical-gain.md) would discard the physically important population balance.
