<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Keep the given [emission cross section](../../../../emission-cross-section.md) in square centimetres so that the density remains per cubic centimetre. For the first transition, $r=5/3$ and

$$
A^{-1}-r\tau_1=\frac1{3\times10^6}-\frac53(2\times10^{-8})
=3.00\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm s.
$$

Thus

$$
D_0=(5\times10^{19})(3.00\times10^{-7})=1.50\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}},
\qquad\boxed{\alpha_0=(4\times10^{-15})D_0=0.0600\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}=6.00\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}.}
$$

The separate populations are $N_2^{(0)}=1.667\times10^{13}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $N_1^{(0)}=1.00\times10^{12}$ cm$^{-3}$. Subtracting $5N_1/3$, rather than $N_1$ alone, reproduces the [population inversion](../../../../population-inversion.md) used above.

Here $rA\tau_1=0.10<1$, so upper-state pumping and sufficiently rapid lower-level removal produce positive small-signal [optical gain](../../../../optical-gain.md). This coefficient is an [intensity](../../../../intensity-physics.md) [optical gain](../../../../optical-gain.md); a weak field amplitude would grow with half this exponent. A positive single-pass coefficient also does not by itself establish [laser oscillation](../../../../laser-oscillation.md) without a cavity-loss budget. No transition [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) was supplied for this numerical example, so a numerical [saturation intensity](../../../../saturation-intensity.md) is not needed or invented to calculate $\alpha_0$.
