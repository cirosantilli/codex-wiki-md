<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Potassium](../../../../potassium.md) has a closed 18-electron core and one [valence electron](../../../../valence-electron.md). In $3d\,{}^2D_{5/2}$ the orbital label specifies [principal quantum number](../../../../principal-quantum-number.md) $n=3$ and single-electron [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) $l=2$. The term letter D specifies total [orbital angular momentum](../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) $L=2$, the superscript $2S+1=2$ specifies $S=1/2$, and the subscript is total electronic [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) $J=5/2$. The $5p\,{}^2P_{3/2}$ state instead has $n=5$, $l=L=1$, the same [spin](../../../../spin.md), and $J=3/2$. The [closed electron shell](../../../../closed-electron-shell.md) that forms the core contributes no net $L$ or $S$ in this alkali-like approximation. The dash identifies a pair of levels rather than by itself specifying the direction of emission.

The leading [electric dipole transition](../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md) rules for one active [electron](../../../../electron.md) are

$$
\boxed{\Delta l=\pm1,\quad\Delta S=0,\quad
\Delta J=0,\pm1\ \text{except }J=0\leftrightarrow0,\quad
\Delta m_J=0,\pm1.}
$$

The electronic [parity](../../../../parity.md) must change, since the [electric dipole operator](../../../../electric-dipole-operator.md) is odd under [spatial reflection](../../../../spatial-reflection.md). The [electric-dipole selection rule](../../../../electric-dipole-selection-rule.md) imposes no general restriction on changing $n$ by more than one. The rule for [magnetic sublevels](../../../../magnetic-sublevel.md) depends on the radiation's [polarization](../../../../polarization-waves.md) relative to the [quantization axis](../../../../quantization-axis.md): the $q=0$ component couples equal $m_J$, whereas $q=\pm1$ supplies the other changes. In a many-electron description using [LS coupling](../../../../ls-coupling.md) the total orbital rule is $\Delta L=0,\pm1$, excluding $L=0\leftrightarrow0$; for a single active [electron](../../../../electron.md) the stronger $\Delta l=\pm1$ applies. Thus the displayed D–P pair satisfies the leading rules. Relativistic mixing and higher-multipole transitions can permit weaker lines outside the approximation of [electric dipole transitions](../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md).
