<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

First make the units explicit. Use [angular momentum operators](../../../../angular-momentum-operator-split.md) in units of $\hbar$, so their squared [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $J(J+1)$ rather than $\hbar^2J(J+1)$; the coefficient $A$ then has units of [energy](../../../../energy-split.md). The operator identity

$$
\mathbf J^2=(\mathbf J_1+\mathbf J_2)^2
=\mathbf J_1^2+\mathbf J_2^2+2\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2
$$

gives, at fixed $J_1,J_2$,

$$
E_J=\frac A2[J(J+1)-J_1(J_1+1)-J_2(J_2+1)].
$$

Subtracting the expression for the adjacent allowed value $J-1$ leaves

$$
\boxed{E_J-E_{J-1}=\frac A2[J(J+1)-(J-1)J]=AJ.}
$$

This is the [Landé interval rule](../../../../lande-interval-rule.md). Its content is that consecutive gaps grow in proportion to the higher total-angular-momentum quantum number, with the same coefficient within an isolated coupled multiplet. A negative $A$ reverses the ordering of the levels, while positive observed transition [frequencies](../../../../frequency.md) alone measure gap magnitudes.

If the source symbols instead denote physical angular momenta with units of action, the [Hamiltonian](../../../../hamiltonian.md) written literally as $A\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2$ gives the gap $A\hbar^2J$. Recovering the printed form then requires absorbing $\hbar^2$ into the coefficient or writing $H=(A/\hbar^2)\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2$. The convention is essential to dimensional consistency rather than an extra dynamical assumption.
