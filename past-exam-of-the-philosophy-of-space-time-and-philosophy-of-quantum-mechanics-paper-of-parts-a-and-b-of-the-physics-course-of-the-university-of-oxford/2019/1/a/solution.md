<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**There is no unconditional logical dependence of [Newton's first law](../../../../newton-s-first-law.md) on [Newton's third law](../../../../newton-s-third-law.md).** There is, however, a dependence if one applies the first to an extended, isolated composite and interprets its [motion](../../../../motion.md) as the [motion](../../../../motion.md) of its [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md). The difference is between the behaviour of an elementary body with zero total [force](../../../../force-split.md) and a theorem about cancellation of internal [forces](../../../../force-split.md). [Isaac Newton](../../../../isaac-newton.md)'s own first-law discussion includes projectiles and spinning bodies, so the composite-body issue is physically significant.[Axioms, Laws I–III and Corollary III; distinguish impressed forces and the common centre of gravity](../../../../the-mathematical-principles-of-natural-philosophy-by-isaac-newton-1846-edition.md)

For a constant positive [mass](../../../../mass.md) in an [inertial frame](../../../../inertial-frame.md), [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) gives $m\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F$. Setting the total [force](../../../../force-split.md) to zero yields uniform rectilinear [motion](../../../../motion.md); it uses no action–reaction assumption. The first law additionally identifies a common class of [inertial frames](../../../../inertial-frame.md) in which such a statement applies. One cannot establish that class by arbitrarily straightening a single [trajectory](../../../../trajectory.md), and one cannot test the claim by defining every observed [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) to be an unspecified [force](../../../../force-split.md).

A concrete countermodel separates these issues. Suppose two bodies obey the second-law equations with internal [forces](../../../../force-split.md) $\mathbf F_{12}=\mathbf f$ and $\mathbf F_{21}=-2\mathbf f$, with nonzero constant $\mathbf f$. Every elementary body with zero total [force](../../../../force-split.md) still has constant [velocity](../../../../velocity.md), but the two-body [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) satisfies

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf p_1+\mathbf p_2)=-\mathbf f.
$$

Thus the elementary-body first law is compatible with failure of the third. Calling the pair isolated does not make its internal total [force](../../../../force-split.md) zero: that is precisely what has not been assumed. This countermodel establishes logical independence, not a proposal for a successful fundamental interaction.

Now strengthen the first-law claim: every isolated composite has an inertially moving [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) whenever the total [external force](../../../../external-force.md) vanishes. With $M=\sum_i m_i$, [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) gives

$$
M\ddot{\mathbf R}=\sum_i\mathbf F_i^{\rm ext}
+\sum_{i<j}(\mathbf F_{ij}+\mathbf F_{ji}).
$$

For an isolated pair, the strengthened claim requires $\mathbf F_{12}+\mathbf F_{21}=0$. This is the weak, equal-and-opposite form of [Newton's third law](../../../../newton-s-third-law.md). It does not by itself require collinearity with the bodies' separation; the central-force condition is stronger and bears on [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md). For many bodies, cancellation of the sum does not alone imply pairwise cancellation without assumptions about the decomposition into interactions.

The right conclusion is therefore conditional. A first law for individual force-free bodies does not presuppose the third law. A first law for all isolated material composites, combined with the usual [centre of mass](../../../../center-of-mass.md) and [Newton's second law](../../../../newton-s-second-law.md), imports an internal-[momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md)-balance condition. In theories with dynamical [fields](../../../../field.md), material bodies can exchange [momentum](../../../../momentum-split.md) with those [fields](../../../../field.md); [conservation of momentum](../../../../momentum-conservation.md) then concerns the combined system and need not imply instantaneous pairwise action–reaction between particles.
