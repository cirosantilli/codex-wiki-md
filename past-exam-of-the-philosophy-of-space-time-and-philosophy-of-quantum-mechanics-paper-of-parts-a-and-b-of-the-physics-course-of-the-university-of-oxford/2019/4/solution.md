<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**The ideal [slow clock transport synchronization](../../../slow-clock-transport-synchronization.md) procedure reproduces [Einstein synchronisation](../../../einstein-synchronisation.md) in an [inertial frame](../../../inertial-frame.md), as a limiting result about [proper time](../../../proper-time.md).** It does not determine distant simultaneity without assumptions about [clocks](../../../clock.md) and their [dynamics](../../../dynamics-mechanics.md). Begin with identical ideal [clocks](../../../clock.md) together at $x=0$, with matching rates and readings. Leave one there and transport the other to a laboratory position $L$ at small constant [speed](../../../speed.md) $u$, neglecting [gravity](../../../gravity.md). Use Einstein-synchronised laboratory coordinates to calculate the result, rather than assuming that the transported [clock](../../../clock.md) already displays coordinate [time](../../../time-in-physics.md).

The journey takes laboratory [time](../../../time-in-physics.md) $T=L/u$. The transported [clock](../../../clock.md)'s [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) is $\tau=T\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}$, giving the lag

$$
\delta=T-\tau=\frac{L}{u}\left(1-\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}\right)
=\frac{Lu}{c^2\left(1+\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}\right)}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\delta\sim\frac{Lu}{2c^2}\longrightarrow0\quad(u\longrightarrow0).}
$$

The small [time dilation](../../../time-dilation.md) accumulates over a long journey, but its quadratic dependence on $u$ dominates the journey's inverse dependence on $u$. Saying that the journey is arbitrarily long is therefore not an objection to this limit. For a nonuniform journey with $|v|\leq u$, $\delta=\int(1-\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2})dt$; a simple monotonic path of fixed [length](../../../length.md) has the same vanishing bound. Acceleration stages can be arranged with vanishing displacement and [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) deficit, assuming the [clock hypothesis](../../../clock-hypothesis.md).[§4, transported clocks and the leading quadratic retardation](../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md)

This procedure establishes synchronization operationally for stationary laboratory [clocks](../../../clock.md), once ideal-clock behaviour has empirical support. It does not show that every actual device is insensitive to transport: thermal shifts, [acceleration](../../../acceleration.md)-dependent mechanisms or differing [gravitational potentials](../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) can spoil the idealization. Nor does the same construction define global synchronization for rotating laboratories, where path dependence is significant.

The conventionality question is separate. Resynchronize the same laboratory with $t_\kappa=t+\kappa x/c$, where $|\kappa|<1$. At the arrival event the transported [clock](../../../clock.md) approaches the reading $t$, whereas a stationary [clock](../../../clock.md) displaying $t_\kappa$ reads $t+\kappa L/c$. Slow transport has not eliminated that difference. The transformed [metric tensor](../../../metric-tensor.md) has an off-diagonal term, and the [proper time](../../../proper-time.md) calculation still gives the same physical [clock](../../../clock.md) reading: changing labels cannot change its ticks. This is the [invariance of reunion ages under clock resynchronization](../../../invariance-of-reunion-ages-under-clock-resynchronization.md), applied to transport comparisons.

[Slow clock transport synchronization](../../../slow-clock-transport-synchronization.md) is thus a useful physical realization of a preferred conventional coordinate choice given [Lorentz-invariant dynamics](../../../lorentz-invariant-dynamics.md) and ideal [clocks](../../../clock.md). It does not independently prove that the one-way [speed of light](../../../speed-of-light.md) must be isotropic in every admissible synchronization. It supplies a reproducible convention with a strong physical rationale, rather than a convention-free observation of an absolute present.
