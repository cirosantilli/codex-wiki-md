<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**One can derive a family containing the [Lorentz transformations](../../../lorentz-transformation.md) without assuming anything about [light](../../../light.md), but the [Einstein's principle of relativity](../../../einstein-s-principle-of-relativity.md) alone does not select that family member over the [Galilean transformation](../../../galilean-transformation.md).** Extra assumptions must be stated: spatial and temporal [translational symmetry](../../../translational-symmetry.md), [isotropy](../../../isotropy.md) of [SPACE](../../../deterministic-space-complexity-class.md), continuous transformations between equivalent [inertial frames](../../../inertial-frame.md), reciprocity, and closure under composition. These are substantive restrictions, rather than consequences of the bare assertion that physical laws agree between [inertial frames](../../../inertial-frame.md).

Here is the algebraic content. [Translational symmetry](../../../translational-symmetry.md) makes the transformation between coincident origins a [linear transformation](../../../linear-map.md). In one spatial direction the moving origin $x=vt$ fixes $x'=a(v)(x-vt)$. [Isotropy](../../../isotropy.md) and the inverse transformation at $-v$ allow the standard calibrated form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}t'\\x'\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-Kv^2}}
\begin{pmatrix}1&-Kv\\-v&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}t\\x\end{pmatrix}.
$$

To see why $K$ is universal, temporarily write the upper-right entry as $-b(v)$, leaving the lower-left entry $-v$. Equality of the two diagonal entries in a composite boost gives $b(u)v=u b(v)$, so $b(v)/v=K$ is constant for nonzero boosts. Inversion fixes $a(v)^2(1-Kv^2)=1$, with the positive branch continuous at zero. This is the [universality of an invariant speed under boost composition](../../../universality-of-an-invariant-speed-under-boost-composition.md).

The resulting [velocity-addition formula](../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) law and invariant quadratic expression are

$$
w=\frac{u+v}{1+Kuv},\qquad dt'^2-K\,dx'^2=dt^2-K\,dx^2.
$$

For $K>0$, $C=K^{-1/2}$ is an invariant limiting [speed](../../../speed.md); setting the empirically determined $C$ equal to the vacuum [speed of light](../../../speed-of-light.md) gives the standard [Lorentz transformation](../../../lorentz-transformation.md). For $K=0$, $t'=t$ and $x'=x-vt$: an entirely consistent [Galilean transformation](../../../galilean-transformation.md) satisfying the stated [Einstein's principle of relativity](../../../einstein-s-principle-of-relativity.md). Thus the general assumptions do not establish that an invariant finite [speed](../../../speed.md) exists, much less identify it with [light](../../../light.md).

There is also a formal negative-$K$ branch, which has a rotation-like geometry in the time–space plane. Standard derivations exclude it using additional global assumptions, such as a preserved [time orientation](../../../time-orientation.md) and unrestricted composition of admissible boosts. Without identifying those assumptions it is misleading to claim a two-way classification from the [Einstein's principle of relativity](../../../einstein-s-principle-of-relativity.md) alone. In three spatial dimensions [isotropy](../../../isotropy.md) requires the same constant in every direction.

The [light](../../../light.md) postulate is therefore dispensable as the starting point for a conditional kinematical derivation; empirical input remains indispensable in identifying our world's value and sign of $K$. [Albert Einstein](../../../albert-einstein.md)'s original construction uses the [light](../../../light.md) postulate and [Einstein synchronisation](../../../einstein-synchronisation.md), making that empirical connection explicit.[§§1–3, synchronization and the derivation from the two postulates](../../../on-the-electrodynamics-of-moving-bodies-by-albert-einstein.md) Replacing the postulate by an independently established finite invariant [speed](../../../speed.md) changes the route, rather than deriving all of [special relativity](../../../special-relativity-split.md) from relativity alone.
