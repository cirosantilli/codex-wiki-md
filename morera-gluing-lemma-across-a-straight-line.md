# Morera gluing lemma across a straight line

↑ **Parent:** [Morera's theorem](morera-s-theorem.md)

Let $U\subseteq\mathbb C$ be [open](open-set.md), let $L$ be a straight line, and let $f:U\to\mathbb C$ be [continuous](continuous-function.md) on $U$ and [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) on $U\setminus L$. Then $f$ is [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) on all of $U$. Near a point of $L$, work in a disk whose closure lies in $U$. Split a small filled triangle along $L$ and triangulate the two resulting convex polygons. Translate each triangle slightly into its corresponding open half-plane, keeping it in $U$. Its [contour integral](contour-integral.md) is zero by [Cauchy's integral theorem](cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md). On each segment the [integral](integral-split.md) is the endpoint difference times the integral of $f$ along the unit-interval parametrization; [uniform continuity](uniform-continuity.md) on a containing [compact](compact-space.md) disk therefore makes the [contour integral](contour-integral.md) continuous as the vertices move back. Each untranslated piece consequently has zero integral. Internal oriented edges cancel when the pieces are added, so every small triangle has zero integral. [Morera's theorem](morera-s-theorem.md) proves the claim locally along the line. The continuity across $L$ is essential; holomorphicity on the two sides alone is insufficient.
