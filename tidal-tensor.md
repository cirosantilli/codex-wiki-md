# Tidal tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md)

The acceleration tidal tensor is $T_{jk}=\partial_k g_j=-\partial_j\partial_k\Phi$ for [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) $\mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi$. It describes differential acceleration $\Delta\mathbf g\simeq T\Delta\mathbf r$. Some authors instead call the positive [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) $\partial_j\partial_k\Phi$ the tidal tensor, reversing this sign convention. For a point mass and the positive potential convention $\mathbf g=\nabla\psi$, the tensor is $Gm(3\mathbf R\mathbf R^{\mathsf T}/R^5-\mathbf1/R^3)$.

The [tidal tensor](tidal-tensor.md) is the [Jacobian matrix](jacobian-matrix.md) of the [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md), rather than the [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) itself. Its entries have units $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$: multiplying by a [displacement](displacement.md) produces an [acceleration](acceleration.md). For a smooth [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md), the [tidal tensor](tidal-tensor.md) is a [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md), since mixed [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) commute. A spatially uniform [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) has zero [tidal tensor](tidal-tensor.md), however large that [acceleration](acceleration.md) is.
