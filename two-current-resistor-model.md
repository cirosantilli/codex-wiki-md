# Two-current resistor model

↑ **Parent:** [Current-perpendicular-to-plane magnetoresistance](current-perpendicular-to-plane-magnetoresistance.md)

Consider two identical magnetic layers separated by a nonmagnetic layer, between reservoirs that impose the same terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) on both [spin](spin.md) channels. Neglect [spin-flip scattering](spin-flip-scattering.md) and assume each layer obeys [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md). In a magnetic layer, define positive channel [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md) $a,b>0$. Let the spacer contribute $r\geq0$ to **each** channel. These are circuit parameters, not measured [iron](iron.md) or [chromium](chromium.md) resistivities.

For parallel layer [magnetizations](magnetization.md), one channel meets $a,r,a$ and the other $b,r,b$. For opposite layer [magnetizations](magnetization.md), each meets one $a$, one $b$, and the spacer. The two channels share the same terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) and run in parallel. Writing $x\parallel y=xy/(x+y)$ gives

$$
R_P=\frac{(2a+r)(2b+r)}{2(a+b+r)},\qquad
R_{AP}=\frac{a+b+r}{2},\qquad
R_{AP}-R_P=\frac{(a-b)^2}{2(a+b+r)}\geq0.
$$

The inequality is algebra, given the specified network. It shows how a difference between spin-channel responses can yield a difference between magnetic configurations even though all the constituent [resistors](resistor.md) are ohmic.

For chosen $a=1\,\Omega$, $b=4\,\Omega$, $r=1\,\Omega$, the parallel channels have total [electrical resistances](electrical-resistance.md) $3$ and $9\,\Omega$, while the opposite-alignment channels each have $6\,\Omega$. Thus **$R_P=2.25\,\Omega$ and $R_{AP}=3.00\,\Omega$**. At a common terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $1\,\mathrm V$, the total [electric currents](electric-current.md) are $4/9\,\mathrm A$ and $1/3\,\mathrm A$, respectively.
