# Function composition

↑ **Parent:** [Function](function-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_composition)

For [functions](function-split.md) $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$, their [function composition](function-composition.md) is $g\circ f:X\to Z$, defined by $(g\circ f)(x)=g(f(x))$. The rightmost [function](function-split.md) is applied first. Matching the intermediate [codomain](codomain.md) and [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) makes the expression well-defined.

For [self-maps](self-map.md) of the [real numbers](real-number.md), take $f(x)=x+1$ and $g(x)=2x$. Then $(g\circ f)(x)=2x+2$, whereas $(f\circ g)(x)=2x+1$. Thus [function composition](function-composition.md) need not be [commutative](commutativity.md). It does have the [associative property](associative-property.md): both $(h\circ g)\circ f$ and $h\circ(g\circ f)$ evaluate to $h(g(f(x)))$ whenever each intermediate [codomain](codomain.md) matches the next [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). The [identity function](identity-function.md) leaves composition unchanged, making [self-maps](self-map.md) particularly useful as a [monoid](monoid.md) of transformations.
