# Wedge product of differential forms

↑ **Parent:** [Differential form](differential-form-split.md)

The wedge product combines a degree-$p$ [differential form](differential-form-split.md) and a degree-$q$ [differential form](differential-form-split.md) into one of degree $p+q$. It is [associative](associative-property.md) and graded-commutative:

$$
\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{pq}\beta\wedge\alpha.
$$

In particular, $dx\wedge dy=-dy\wedge dx$ and $dx\wedge dx=0$. For planar [vectors](vector.md) $v,w$,

$$
(dx\wedge dy)(v,w)=v_xw_y-v_yw_x.
$$

This [determinant](determinant.md) is the signed area of their [parallelogram](parallelogram.md). Exchanging the edges reverses the sign; parallel edges give zero. Thus antisymmetry records the geometry of oriented area rather than adding an arbitrary algebraic convention. The [wedge product of differential forms](wedge-product-of-differential-forms.md) requires no [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md) to define it. It obeys the [graded Leibniz rule](graded-leibniz-rule.md) under the [exterior derivative](exterior-derivative.md).
