# Antipodal-free closed cover from simplex Voronoi cells

↑ **Parent:** [Lusternik–Schnirelmann theorem](lusternik-schnirelmann-theorem.md)

Let $v_0,\ldots,v_{n+1}$ be the vertices of a regular simplex centered at zero in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. The displayed spherical Voronoi cells are a [closed cover](closed-cover.md) by $n+2$ sets. If $x$ and $-x$ belonged to the same cell, all their scalar products with the vertices would coincide. Their sum is zero and the vertices span the ambient [vector space](vector-space-split.md), so this would force $x=0$, a contradiction. Thus each cell contains no [antipodal pair](antipodal-pair.md). Together with the [Lusternik-Schnirelmann-Borsuk theorem](lusternik-schnirelmann-theorem.md), this shows $n+2$ is the minimum number of antipodal-free members of a finite [closed cover](closed-cover.md).
