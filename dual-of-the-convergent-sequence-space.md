# Dual of the convergent sequence space

↑ **Parent:** [Duality of sequence spaces](duality-of-sequence-spaces.md)

For the [Banach space](banach-space-split.md) $c$ of convergent complex sequences with the [supremum norm](supremum-norm.md), every bounded complex-[linear functional](linear-functional.md) has the unique representation

$$
F(x)=a_\infty\lim_jx_j+\sum_{j\geq1}a_jx_j,\qquad \|F\|=|a_\infty|+\sum_j|a_j|.
$$

Restrict $F$ to $c_0$ and use coordinate [duality of sequence spaces](duality-of-sequence-spaces.md) to obtain $(a_j)\in\ell^1$. Splitting $x$ into its constant limit sequence and a zero-limit remainder forces $a_\infty=F(\mathbf1)-\sum_ja_j$. For the [norm](norm.md) lower bound choose the first $N$ coordinates of $x$ with phases maximizing $a_jx_j$ and make its tail the constant phase maximizing $a_\infty\lim x$. The finite prefix and limit contributions attain their absolute values; the remaining contribution is bounded by the coefficient tail, which tends to zero. The opposite [norm](norm.md) inequality is the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md). The index at infinity is an independent coordinate of the dual [norm](norm.md), rather than part of the sum of the finite-coordinate coefficients.
