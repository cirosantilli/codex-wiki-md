# Sum of two linear projections

↑ **Parent:** [Projection (linear algebra)](projection-linear-algebra.md)

For [linear projections](projection-linear-algebra.md) $E,F$, expanding the square proves that their sum is a [linear projection](projection-linear-algebra.md) exactly when $EF=-FE$. Multiplying this equality on the left and right by $E$ gives $EF=-EFE$ and $EFE=-FE=EF$, hence $2EF=0$. If the [field](field.md) has [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) different from two, this forces $EF=FE=0$, and those conditions also suffice. In [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) two, the condition instead reduces to commutation; $E=F=I$ has sum zero but nonzero product. For three commuting [linear projections](projection-linear-algebra.md), the square-minus-sum is $2(EF+EG+FG)$, so vanishing of the triple product alone is not sufficient.
