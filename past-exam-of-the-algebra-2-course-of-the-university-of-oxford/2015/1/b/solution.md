<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For two factors, the source's $R_1\oplus R_2$ is the [direct product of rings](../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md) with componentwise operations. Its [principal ideals](../../../../principal-ideal.md) satisfy

$$
\bigl((a_1,a_2)\bigr)=(a_1)\times(a_2),
$$

One inclusion follows by projection to the factors. For the reverse inclusion, multiplication by $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ puts $(a_1,0)$ and $(0,a_2)$ in the generated [ideal](../../../../ideal.md); taking [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) combinations separately produces every pair in $(a_1)\times(a_2)$.

Suppose $(a_1,a_2)$ and $(b_1,b_2)$ generate the same [principal ideal](../../../../principal-ideal.md). Projecting to the factors gives $(a_i)=(b_i)$ for $i=1,2$. Since each factor is an [associator ring](../../../../associator-ring.md), there are [units](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $u_i\in R_i$ with $a_i=u_ib_i$. The pair $(u_1,u_2)$ is a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) of the [direct product of rings](../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md), with inverse $(u_1^{-1},u_2^{-1})$, and

$$
(a_1,a_2)=(u_1,u_2)(b_1,b_2).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{R_1\oplus R_2\text{ is an associator ring}.}
$$

Repeating this argument proves the same assertion for every finite [direct product of rings](../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md).
