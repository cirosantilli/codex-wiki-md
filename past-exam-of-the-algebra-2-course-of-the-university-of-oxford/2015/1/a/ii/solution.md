<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $R$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) and $(a)=(b)$. If $a=0$, then $(b)=0$, so $b=0$ and $a=1\cdot b$.

Otherwise membership in the equal [principal ideals](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) supplies $r,s\in R$ with $a=rb$ and $b=sa$. Substituting gives $a=rsa$, or $(1-rs)a=0$. There are no nonzero [zero divisors](../../../../../zero-divisor.md) in an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md), and $a\ne0$, so $rs=1$. Hence $r$ is a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) with inverse $s$, and $a=rb$ makes $a$ and $b$ [associate elements](../../../../../associate-element.md). We have proved

$$
\boxed{\text{Every integral domain is an associator ring.}}
$$
