<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [ideal correspondence theorem](../../../../../correspondence-theorem-for-ideals.md) identifies the [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) of $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ with the [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) of $\mathbb Z$ containing $4\mathbb Z$. The possible positive generators divide $4$, giving exactly

$$
\boxed{\{\overline0\},\qquad
(\overline2)=\{\overline0,\overline2\},\qquad
\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.}
$$

The [units](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) are $\overline1$ and $\overline3$. The generators of the three displayed [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) are, respectively,

$$
\{\overline0\},\qquad\{\overline2\},\qquad
\{\overline1,\overline3\}.
$$

Within the last pair, multiplication by the [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $\overline3$ exchanges the generators. The other two sets are singletons, whose members are [associate elements](../../../../../associate-element.md) to themselves by multiplication by $\overline1$. Thus equal [principal ideals](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) always have associate generators: this is an [associator ring](../../../../../associator-ring.md).

However, $\overline2\ne\overline0$ and $\overline2^2=\overline0$, so $\overline2$ is a nonzero [zero divisor](../../../../../zero-divisor.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\text{ is an associator ring but not an integral domain}.}
$$
