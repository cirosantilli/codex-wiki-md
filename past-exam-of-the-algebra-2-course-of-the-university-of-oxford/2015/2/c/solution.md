<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Using the common denominator $70$, the three given generators become

$$
\frac25=\frac{28}{70},\qquad
\frac37=\frac{30}{70},\qquad
\frac12=\frac{35}{70}.
$$

Thus their [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) is contained in $(1/70)\mathbb Z$. For the reverse inclusion, the following explicit [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) linear combination supplies the missing generator:

$$
2\cdot28-3\cdot30+35=1,
$$

so

$$
2\cdot\frac25-3\cdot\frac37+\frac12=\frac1{70}.
$$

Therefore $1/70$ belongs to the generated [submodule](../../../../submodule.md), and all its [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) multiples belong as well. We have established equality in both directions:

$$
M=\frac1{70}\mathbb Z.
$$

Finally, $k/70=0$ for an [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $k$ forces $k=0$, so the single generator is linearly independent. Hence the required [basis of a module](../../../../basis-of-a-module.md) is

$$
\boxed{\left\{\frac1{70}\right\}.}
$$
