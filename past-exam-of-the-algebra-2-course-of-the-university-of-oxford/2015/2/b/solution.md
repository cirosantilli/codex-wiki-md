<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $x,y\in\mathbb Q$. Choose a positive [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $D$ clearing their denominators and write $x=m/D$, $y=n/D$ with $m,n\in\mathbb Z$. If $x$ and $y$ are not both zero, then

$$
nx-my=0
$$

is a relation with [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) coefficients not both zero. If both vanish, use $1\cdot x+0\cdot y=0$. Thus every pair is [linearly dependent](../../../../linear-dependence.md) over $\mathbb Z$.

A [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) $M\subseteq\mathbb Q$ is a [torsion-free module](../../../../torsion-free-module.md): if $kz=0$ for a nonzero [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) $k$ and a [rational number](../../../../rational-number.md) $z$, then $z=0$. If $M$ is also a [finitely generated module](../../../../finitely-generated-module.md), part (a), applied to the [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) $\mathbb Z$, makes it a [free module](../../../../free-module.md). Its [rank](../../../../rank-of-a-module-over-an-integral-domain.md) cannot exceed one, since two different members of a [basis of a module](../../../../basis-of-a-module.md) would be linearly independent, contradicting the relation just proved. Since $M\ne0$, its rank cannot be zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}M=1,\qquad M\cong\mathbb Z.}
$$

The denominator-and-[greatest common divisor](../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md) construction in part (c) gives an explicit version of this result for a [finitely generated subgroup of the rational additive group](../../../../finitely-generated-subgroup-of-the-rational-additive-group.md).
