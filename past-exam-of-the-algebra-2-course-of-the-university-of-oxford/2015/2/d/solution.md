<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose a finite set of [rational numbers](../../../../rational-number.md) $q_1,\ldots,q_m$ generated $\mathbb Q$ as a [module](../../../../module-mathematics.md) over $\mathbb Z$. A positive common denominator $D$ makes every $Dq_i$ an [integer](../../../../integer-split.md). For every [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) linear combination, therefore,

$$
D\sum_i n_iq_i\in\mathbb Z.
$$

The entire generated [submodule](../../../../submodule.md) is contained in $(1/D)\mathbb Z$. But $1/(2D)$ is a [rational number](../../../../rational-number.md) outside that set: $1/(2D)=k/D$ would require the noninteger $k=1/2$. This contradicts generation of all of $\mathbb Q$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q\text{ is not finitely generated as a }\mathbb Z\text{-module}.}
$$

Each finite set of generators imposes a common denominator; the full [rational number](../../../../rational-number.md) system has no such bound.
