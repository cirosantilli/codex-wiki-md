<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../../../../structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) applies to a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) because every [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md). In invariant-factor form, every [finitely generated module](../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) $M$ has a decomposition

$$
\boxed{M\cong R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_s),\qquad
 d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_s,}
$$

where $r\geq0$, and each $d_i$ is a nonzero [nonunit](../../../../nonunit.md). The [rank of a module](../../../../rank-of-a-module-over-an-integral-domain.md) $r$ is unique and the [invariant factors of a finitely generated module](../../../../invariant-factor-of-a-finitely-generated-module.md) $d_i$ are unique up to multiplication by units. The zero module is allowed by taking $r=s=0$.

Every [abelian group](../../../../abelian-group.md) is a [module](../../../../module-mathematics.md) over $\mathbb Z$, and a [finite abelian group](../../../../finite-abelian-group.md) is finitely generated. Since $\mathbb Z$ is a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md), the theorem applies. A nonzero [free module](../../../../free-module.md) summand would be infinite, so a finite group has $r=0$. Choosing positive integer representatives gives the finite abelian classification

$$
\boxed{A\cong\mathbb Z/d_1\mathbb Z\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Z/d_s\mathbb Z,
\qquad 2\leq d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_s.}
$$

The ordered integers $d_i$ are uniquely determined. Splitting each $d_i$ into prime powers using the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) gives the equivalent elementary-divisor form as a direct sum of cyclic prime-power groups.
