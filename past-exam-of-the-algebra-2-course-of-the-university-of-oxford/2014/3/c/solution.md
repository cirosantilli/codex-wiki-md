<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

We prove the [unique-subgroup criterion for a finite abelian group to be cyclic](../../../../unique-subgroup-criterion-for-a-finite-abelian-group-to-be-cyclic.md) using the invariant-factor classification from part (a). If $A$ is nontrivial, write

$$
A\cong C_{d_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{d_s},\qquad 2\leq d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_s,
$$

where $C_m$ denotes a [cyclic group](../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order $m$. If $s\geq2$, choose a [prime number](../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$ dividing $d_1$; then $p$ also divides $d_2$. In the first cyclic factor, the element represented by $d_1/p$ has [order of a group element](../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $p$, and the corresponding element in the second factor has order $p$ as well.

The two elements supported in their separate coordinates generate distinct [subgroups](../../../../subgroup.md) of order $p$: one has zero second coordinate, and the other has zero first coordinate. Both are subgroups of $A$, and $p\mid|A|$. This contradicts the assumed uniqueness for that divisor. Hence $s\leq1$, and

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is cyclic}.}
$$

If $A$ is the trivial group, it is already cyclic, generated by its identity. Thus the argument covers every finite order.
