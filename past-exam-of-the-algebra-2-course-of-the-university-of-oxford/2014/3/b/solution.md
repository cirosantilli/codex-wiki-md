<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) to separate relatively prime factors:

$$
\mathbb Z/60\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\mathbb Z/21\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z.
$$

These five cyclic prime-power summands are the elementary-divisor form. Leave one $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$ summand aside and combine the factors of orders $4,3,5,7$, which are pairwise coprime. Their [direct sum of modules](../../../../direct-sum-of-modules.md) is cyclic of order $420$. The invariant-factor canonical form is therefore

$$
\boxed{A\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/420\mathbb Z,\qquad3\mid420.}
$$

Its [group order](../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) is $3\cdot420=1260=60\cdot21$, and its [exponent of a finite group](../../../../exponent-of-a-finite-group.md) is $420=\operatorname{lcm}(60,21)$, providing independent checks on the decomposition. Since its exponent is smaller than its order, $A$ itself is not cyclic.
