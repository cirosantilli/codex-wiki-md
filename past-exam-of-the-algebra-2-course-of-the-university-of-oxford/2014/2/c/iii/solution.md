<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [cubic polynomial](../../../../../cubic-polynomial.md) over a [field](../../../../../field.md) is reducible if and only if it has a root in that field: a nontrivial factorization of degree three has a [linear polynomial](../../../../../linear-polynomial.md) factor, and conversely a root supplies one by the [factor theorem](../../../../../factor-theorem.md).

The [rational root theorem](../../../../../rational-root-theorem.md), or part (b), shows that a rational root of this monic polynomial must be an integer. Such an integer must divide the constant coefficient $4$, so the only candidates are $\pm1,\pm2,\pm4$. Direct evaluation gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
a&1&-1&2&-2&4&-4\\\hline
h(a)&9&-1&20&-12&84&-76
\end{array}
$$

None vanishes. Hence there is no rational root, proving

$$
\boxed{t^3+4t+4\text{ is irreducible over }\mathbb Q.}
$$

The same no-root argument alone would not decide a [quartic polynomial](../../../../../quartic-polynomial.md), which could factor as two [quadratic polynomials](../../../../../quadratic-polynomial.md); its sufficiency here uses degree three.
