# Sinc fourth-power integral

↑ **Parent:** [Sinc function](sinc-function.md)

The [triangular function](triangular-function.md) $f(x)=(1-|x|)$ on $|x|<1$, zero elsewhere, has [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) $\widehat f(k)=[\sin(k/2)/(k/2)]^2$ in the angular-frequency convention. Its squared [L2 norm](l2-norm.md) is $2/3$. [Parseval's identity](parseval-identity.md) therefore gives

$$
\frac23=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sin(k/2)}{k/2}\right)^4dk=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{\sin u}u\right)^4du.
$$

This proves the displayed value. The removable value at zero is one, and the fourth-power decay bounds the tail. The [sinc-squared integral](sinc-squared-integral.md) follows similarly from a rectangular function rather than a triangle.
