# Torsion-free module

↑ **Parent:** [Module theory](module-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_module)

A [module](module-mathematics.md) $M$ over an [integral domain](integral-domain.md) $R$ is torsion-free when $rm=0$ with $0\ne r\in R$ implies $m=0$, equivalently when its [torsion submodule](torsion-submodule.md) is zero. Every [free module](free-module.md) over an [integral domain](integral-domain.md) is torsion-free, as one can check in each coefficient. Every [vector space](vector-space-split.md) is torsion-free over its scalar [field](field.md).

The converse requires hypotheses. A [finitely generated module](finitely-generated-module.md) over a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md) is free if it is torsion-free, but the [module](module-mathematics.md) $\mathbb Q$ over $\mathbb Z$ is torsion-free and not free. Any two nonzero [rational numbers](rational-number.md) have a nontrivial [integer](integer.md) relation, so a possible [basis of a module](basis-of-a-module.md) could have at most one element; no single $q$ generates all [rational numbers](rational-number.md), since $q/2\notin\mathbb Zq$ for $q\ne0$. The finite-generation hypothesis cannot be dropped. See [Keith Conrad's Modules over a PID, Corollary 2.6](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/linmultialg/modulesoverPID.pdf).
