# Apparent weight

↑ **Parent:** [Weight (physics)](weight-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_weight)

Apparent weight measures support rather than the gravitational [force](force.md) itself. In an ideal scale model, it is the magnitude $N$ of the [normal force](normal-force.md) exerted on the body. A scale calibrated to display [mass](mass.md) divides that support force by its calibration value of $g$.

Take upward as positive in a constant downward [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) of magnitude $g$. If a body stays in contact with a horizontal scale and accelerates upward at $a$, [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) gives $N-mg=ma$, hence $N=m(g+a)$. At rest, $N=mg$. In common [free fall](free-fall.md), $a=-g$, so $N=0$ despite the nonzero [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) $mg$. If the formula would require $N<0$, ordinary supporting contact cannot realize it: the body loses contact instead.
