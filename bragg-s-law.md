<h1 id="bragg-s-law">Bragg's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Elastic Bragg scattering condition](elastic-bragg-scattering-condition.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bragg's_law)

[Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md) selects directions of [constructive interference](constructive-interference.md) in [coherent elastic scattering](coherent-elastic-scattering.md) from equally spaced [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md):

$$
n\lambda=2d\sin\theta,\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots.
$$

Here $d>0$ is the perpendicular [interplanar spacing](interplanar-spacing.md), $\lambda>0$ the incident [wavelength](wavelength.md), $n$ the positive integer [diffraction order](diffraction-order.md), and $\theta$ the [Bragg angle](bragg-angle.md), measured between the incoming beam and the [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md). The scattering deflection, between incoming and outgoing propagation directions, is $2\theta$. Thus a plotted detector angle $2\theta$ must be halved before insertion into [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md). The $\theta$ in the parent [elastic Bragg scattering condition](elastic-bragg-scattering-condition.md) denotes the full deflection; the present $\theta$ denotes half of it. [Online Dictionary of Crystallography, Bragg's law, Definition: spacing, wavelength, glancing angle and reflection order](online-dictionary-of-crystallography-by-international-union-of-crystallography/bragg-s-law.md)

The [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md) organize [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) throughout a [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md); they are not literal metal mirrors or separate reflecting surfaces. The picture assumes [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md), hence an unchanged [wavelength](wavelength.md), and sufficiently stable relative [wave phases](phase-waves.md) across the contributing [lattice planes](lattice-plane.md). [X-ray](x-ray.md) [wavelengths](wavelength.md) comparable with atomic [interplanar spacings](interplanar-spacing.md) make these directions experimentally accessible. The [sine](sine.md) cannot exceed one: an order exists geometrically only if $n\lambda/(2d)\leq1$. For $\lambda>2d$, even the first [diffraction order](diffraction-order.md) is inaccessible. A geometrically possible direction need not have nonzero [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md); the [crystal lattice structure factor](crystal-lattice-structure-factor.md) and [crystal basis structure factor](crystal-basis-structure-factor.md) also matter. [Online Dictionary of Crystallography by International Union of Crystallography; Definition and Derivation: coherent addition of the atoms within the unit cell](online-dictionary-of-crystallography-by-international-union-of-crystallography/structure-factor.md)
