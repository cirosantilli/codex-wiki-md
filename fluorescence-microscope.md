# Fluorescence microscope

↑ **Parent:** [Fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence_microscope)

A [fluorescence microscope](fluorescence-microscope.md) maps [photon emission](photon-emission.md) to an image. Excitation [light](light.md) passes through a [fluorescence excitation filter](fluorescence-excitation-filter.md) and reflects from a wavelength-selective [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) into the objective [optical lens](lens-optics.md). Returning [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) passes through that [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) and a [fluorescence emission filter](fluorescence-emission-filter.md) to the [photodetector](photodetector.md). Rejecting excitation makes faint emission visible.[§1.2.1, Figure 5 and excitation/emission-filter paragraphs](advanced-fluorescence-microscopy-techniques-frap-flip-flap-fret-and-flim-by-hellen-c-ishikawa-ankerhold-and-others.md)

An ordinary image records emission-band counts at each [pixel](pixel.md). Adding pulse-delay measurements gives a [fluorescence lifetime](fluorescence-lifetime.md) map; scanning emission [wavelength](wavelength.md) gives spectral information instead. [Confocal microscopy](confocal-microscopy.md) adds spatial rejection and [confocal optical sectioning](confocal-optical-sectioning.md), as developed in its own branch. An unlabeled specimen under the same illumination reveals intrinsic [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) and excitation leakage; excitation-blocked frames reveal detector background. A blank cannot perfectly predict background in a differently prepared specimen.
