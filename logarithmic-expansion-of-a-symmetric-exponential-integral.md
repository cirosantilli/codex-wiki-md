# Logarithmic expansion of a symmetric exponential integral

↑ **Parent:** [Exponential integral](exponential-integral.md)

For $J(\varepsilon)=\int_0^\infty e^{-x-\varepsilon/x}dx/x$, reciprocity $x\mapsto\varepsilon/x$ and the [small-argument expansion of the exponential integral](small-argument-expansion-of-the-exponential-integral.md) give $J=-\log\varepsilon-2\gamma+o(1)$. Differentiation and [integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md) give $\varepsilon J''+J'=J$. Its [Frobenius method](frobenius-method.md) yields

$$
J=-\log\varepsilon-2\gamma+\varepsilon[-\log\varepsilon+2-2\gamma]+O(\varepsilon^2|\log\varepsilon|).
$$

The logarithm is a singular contribution that a naive termwise expansion at $x=0$ misses.
