# Rotational Raman spectroscopy

↑ **Parent:** [Rotational spectroscopy](rotational-spectroscopy.md)

[Rotational Raman spectroscopy](rotational-raman-spectroscopy.md) observes a [frequency](frequency.md) change of scattered light associated with [molecular rotation](molecular-rotation.md). The coupling uses the induced [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md), $\mathbf d_{\rm ind}=\boldsymbol\alpha\mathbf E$, where $\boldsymbol\alpha$ is the [electric polarizability](electric-polarizability.md) tensor. For a [linear molecule](linear-molecule.md), the useful anisotropy is $\alpha_\parallel-\alpha_\perp$: the response differs for an [electric field](electric-field-split.md) parallel or perpendicular to the molecular axis. A permanent [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) is not required.[Andrei Tokmakoff, §6.2, printed pp. 6-7–6-8, equations (6.40)–(6.44): induced dipole, polarizability tensor and rotational Raman response](absorption-lineshapes-by-andrei-tokmakoff.md)

For the spin-free [linear rigid rotor](linear-rigid-rotor.md) in one internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md), the anisotropic tensor depends quadratically on the molecular direction. Its angular part has even parity and degree two in [spherical harmonics](spherical-harmonic.md). The [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) for coupled [angular momenta](angular-momentum.md) permits $|\Delta J|\leq2$, and even parity removes odd $\Delta J$, leaving $\Delta J=0,\pm2$. The $\Delta J=0$ contribution is unshifted light; shifted rotational lines have $\Delta J=\pm2$. Consequently the rigid-model Stokes shifts, in ordinary [frequency](frequency.md) units, are

$$
\Delta\nu_J=\frac{E_{J+2}-E_J}{h}=B(4J+6),\qquad J=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

Successive allowed shifts are separated by $4B$, rather than the $2B$ absorption spacing of [pure rotational electric-dipole selection rules](pure-rotational-electric-dipole-selection-rules.md). In [spectroscopic wavenumber](spectroscopic-wavenumber.md) units replace $B$ by $\widetilde B=B/c$ and $\Delta\nu$ by $\Delta\widetilde\nu=\Delta\nu/c$, using the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) in $\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$ for $\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.

A [homonuclear diatomic molecule](homonuclear-diatomic-molecule.md) such as [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md) has no permanent [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) in its symmetric internal state, so it lacks the ordinary electric-dipole microwave ladder. Its anisotropic [electric polarizability](electric-polarizability.md) can nevertheless give [rotational Raman spectroscopy](rotational-raman-spectroscopy.md) lines. Nuclear-[spin](spin.md) symmetry can change the populations of even and odd $J$ levels, so the spin-free population example above must not be reused unchanged for [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md). The method of coupling, as well as the [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md), determines what the experiment can see.
