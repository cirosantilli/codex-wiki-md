# Equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation

↑ **Parent:** [Robertson uncertainty principle](robertson-uncertainty-principle.md)

Equality in the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) requires the centred [vectors](vector.md) $x'\psi$ and $p'\psi$ to be imaginary [scalar multiples](scalar-multiple.md), where $x'=x-x_0I$, $p'=p-p_0I$ and $x_0,p_0$ are the [expectation values](expectation-value.md). Thus, for some $s>0$,

$$
(p-p_0)\psi=is(x-x_0)\psi.
$$

In the [position](position.md) representation, using the [momentum operator](momentum-operator.md) turns this into a first-order [differential equation](differential-equation-split.md), whose normalizable solutions are

$$
\psi(x)=C\exp\left(\frac{ip_0x}{\hbar}-\frac{s(x-x_0)^2}{2\hbar}\right).
$$

Hence the [quantum states](quantum-state.md) saturating the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) are these [Gaussian wave packets](gaussian-wave-packet.md), with arbitrary [position](position.md) and [momentum](momentum.md) means and [global phase](global-phase.md). Their width is $\sigma^2=\hbar/(2s)$. A [Gaussian wave packet](gaussian-wave-packet.md) with an additional quadratic [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) need not saturate this product bound.
