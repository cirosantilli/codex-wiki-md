# Hydrodynamic torque

↑ **Parent:** [Traction](traction.md)

The hydrodynamic torque on a body about a reference point is $T=\int_S r\times(\sigma\cdot n)dS$, with $r$ measured from that point and $n$ pointing from the body into the fluid. The [stress](stress.md) includes pressure and viscous contributions, and $\sigma\cdot n$ is the fluid [traction](traction.md) on the body. Changing the reference point by $d$ changes the [torque](torque.md) by $-d\times F$, where $F=\int_S\sigma\cdot n dS$ is the fluid force. A [torque-free](torque-free.md) body has zero resultant torque, including any external torque.
