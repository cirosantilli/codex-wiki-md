<h1 id="2/c/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The beam energy is a **total** [pion](../../../../../pion.md) energy, so the momentum follows the relativistic relation $p^2c^2=E_\pi^2-m_\pi^2c^4$. Taking the standard charged-pion rest energy $m_\pi c^2=139.57$ MeV,[page 1, charged-pion mass table](../../../../../charged-pion-particle-listing-by-particle-data-group.md)

$$
pc=\sqrt{249^2-139.57^2}\ \mathrm{MeV}=206.21\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

For motion perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field, the [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) supplies the transverse momentum change and gives $r=p/(qB)$. With the stipulated $qB=1$ eV s m$^{-2}$,

$$
\boxed{r=\frac{206.21\times10^6\ \mathrm{eV}/c}{1\ \mathrm{eV\,s\,m^{-2}}}
\simeq0.688\ \mathrm m.}
$$

The unit cancellation leaves length after dividing by $c$ in metres per second. Equivalently, the usual magnetic-rigidity conversion is $p[\mathrm{GeV}/c]\simeq0.2998\,B[\mathrm T]r[\mathrm m]$ for unit charge. The sign of the [pion](../../../../../pion.md) charge chooses the bending direction, while the radius uses its magnitude. Treating the [pion](../../../../../pion.md) as massless would give about $0.831$ m and is not a good approximation at this modest total energy. The production target does not determine the downstream orbit once the [pion](../../../../../pion.md) momentum and magnetic field are specified.
