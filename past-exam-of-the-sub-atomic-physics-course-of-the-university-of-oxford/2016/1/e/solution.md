<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For exponential decay with [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md) $\tau$, the state amplitude evolves as $\exp[-i(E_0-i\Gamma/2)t/\hbar]$ and its survival probability is $e^{-\Gamma t/\hbar}$. Hence the energy [decay width](../../../../decay-width.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\frac\hbar\tau=
\frac{6.58212\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm{eV\,s}}{15\times60\ \mathrm s}
=7.31\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{eV}.}
$$

The exceptionally small energy width expresses the long lifetime of this weak decay. Use $\hbar$, not $h$, because $\Gamma$ is an energy width in the complex-energy convention; changing to a frequency convention introduces the appropriate factor of $2\pi$. The quoted lifetime is treated as the [mean lifetime](../../../../mean-lifetime-physics.md). If a half-life were supplied instead, one would first use $\tau=t_{1/2}/\ln2$. No extra logarithm is needed for the given mean-lifetime interpretation. This calculation uses the source's approximate 15-minute value rather than silently replacing it with a more precise contemporary neutron-lifetime measurement.
