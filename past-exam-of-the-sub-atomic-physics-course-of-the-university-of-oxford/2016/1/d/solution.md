<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Work first in units $c=1$. In the [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md) rest frame, let $s_{p\nu}=(p_p+p_\nu)^2$. From $p_n-p_e=p_p+p_\nu$,

$$
s_{p\nu}=m_n^2+m_e^2-2m_nE_e,
\qquad E_e=\frac{m_n^2+m_e^2-s_{p\nu}}{2m_n}.
$$

The [electron](../../../../electron.md)'s minimum possible total energy is its rest energy. This configuration has the [electron](../../../../electron.md) at rest while the [proton](../../../../proton.md) and massless [antineutrino](../../../../antineutrino.md) move back to back with equal momentum magnitudes. Their common momentum satisfies a two-body energy balance for the remaining mass $m_n-m_e$:

$$
\boxed{E_{e,\min}=m_ec^2,\qquad
|\mathbf p_p|=|\mathbf p_\nu|=
\frac{(m_n-m_e)^2-m_p^2}{2(m_n-m_e)}c,\quad\mathbf p_p=-\mathbf p_\nu.}
$$

For the maximum [electron](../../../../electron.md) energy, minimize $s_{p\nu}$. Since the massless [antineutrino](../../../../antineutrino.md) has nonnegative energy, $s_{p\nu}\geq m_p^2$, with the boundary reached as its energy tends to zero. Then

$$
\boxed{E_{e,\max}=\frac{m_n^2+m_e^2-m_p^2}{2m_n}c^2.}
$$

At this limiting endpoint the [electron](../../../../electron.md) and [proton](../../../../proton.md) have equal and opposite momenta. The [proton](../../../../proton.md) recoils; it is **not** at rest. A massless [neutrino](../../../../neutrino.md) has no rest frame, so “[neutrino](../../../../neutrino.md) at rest” must be replaced by the precise soft-energy limit $E_\nu\to0$. The [electron](../../../../electron.md)'s maximum [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is the displayed total energy minus $m_ec^2$, rather than the same number. These endpoint configurations lie on phase-space boundaries; their kinematic existence does not imply a nonzero event density exactly at the boundary.
