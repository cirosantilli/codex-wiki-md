<h1 id="1/b/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Adding the three characteristic [quark](../../../../../quark.md) energies from [b](../b.md) gives

$$
\boxed{m_n^{\rm estimate}c^2\sim3E_q=255.2\ \mathrm{MeV},\qquad
\frac{m_n^{\rm true}}{m_n^{\rm estimate}}\sim\frac{940}{255.2}=3.68.}
$$

The estimate recovers a sub-GeV scale but underestimates the stated [neutron](../../../../../neutron-split.md) mass by a factor of a few. This is consistent with its deliberately crude uncertainty and geometry assumptions; it is not a precision mass prediction.

A better model needs the actual spatial and momentum correlations, a confining interaction, and the energy in the [quark](../../../../../quark.md) and [gluon](../../../../../gluon.md) fields. In [Quantum chromodynamics](../../../../../quantum-chromodynamics.md), hadron masses are not simply the sum of three freely moving particles with tiny masses. The supplied $5$ MeV rest-energy scale resembles a light current-quark scale; a phenomenological constituent-quark mass includes substantial dressing and is a different parameter. Color, spin correlations, relativistic dynamics and sea components all matter when defining a more realistic bound-state [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) or performing a [lattice QCD](../../../../../lattice-qcd.md) calculation. The prescribed uniform-sphere model supplies none of those extra dynamical inputs, so its discrepancy should not be repaired by an unannounced change of the given radius or mass.
