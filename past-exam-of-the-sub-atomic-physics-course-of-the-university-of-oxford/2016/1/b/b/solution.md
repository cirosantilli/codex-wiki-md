<h1 id="1/b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Adopt the lower-bound scale of the [Heisenberg uncertainty principle](../../../../../heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md), $\Delta r\Delta p\sim\hbar/2$, with the prescribed radial scale from [a](../a.md). Using $\hbar c=197.327$ MeV fm,

$$
\boxed{\Delta p\,c\sim\frac{\hbar c}{2r_{\rm rms}}=84.92\ \mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

For a one-sided [exponential distribution](../../../../../exponential-distribution.md) of a positive momentum magnitude, $f(p)=p_0^{-1}e^{-p/p_0}$, both its mean and its [standard deviation](../../../../../standard-deviation.md) are $p_0$. Under the additional identification made in this estimate, set the characteristic momentum to $\Delta p$. This identification concerns a positive magnitude; the mean of a signed momentum component in an isotropic stationary state would instead vanish.

Use the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md), not a nonrelativistic kinetic-energy approximation, because the momentum-energy scale is much larger than the stipulated [quark](../../../../../quark.md) rest energy:

$$
\boxed{E_q\sim\sqrt{(\Delta p\,c)^2+(m_qc^2)^2}
=\sqrt{(84.92)^2+5^2}\ \mathrm{MeV}=85.06\ \mathrm{MeV}.}
$$

This is a characteristic energy estimated at the characteristic momentum. It is not an exact evaluation of $\langle\sqrt{p^2c^2+m_q^2c^4}\rangle$ over a fully specified three-dimensional wavefunction. An order-of-magnitude convention $\Delta p\sim\hbar/\Delta r$ would give an answer about twice as large; the uncertainty principle by itself specifies a bound, not a unique confinement energy.
