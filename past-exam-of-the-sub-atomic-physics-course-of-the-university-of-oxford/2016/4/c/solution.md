<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

An extra active generation with the same [weak neutral current](../../../../neutral-current.md) couplings would increase the [Z boson](../../../../z-boson.md) [total decay width](../../../../total-decay-width.md) if any new particle–antiparticle pair were light enough. A simple sufficient kinematic condition for closing each such two-body channel is

$$
\boxed{m_T,m_B,m_\sigma,m_N\gtrsim\frac{m_Z}{2}\simeq45.6\ \mathrm{GeV}/c^2.}
$$

The corresponding within-generation [W boson](../../../../w-boson.md) channels close when

$$
\boxed{m_T+m_B\gtrsim m_W,\qquad m_\sigma+m_N\gtrsim m_W,\qquad
m_W\simeq80.4\ \mathrm{GeV}/c^2.}
$$

If both members of each doublet already exceed $m_Z/2$, these two sum constraints follow automatically. Mixing with lighter generations can also open charged-current channels involving one heavy and one light particle, depending on the mixing amplitudes; that information is not specified.

These are threshold estimates, not precise confidence limits. Close to threshold, [two-body decay phase space](../../../../two-body-decay-phase-space.md) suppresses a new [partial decay width](../../../../partial-decay-width.md), and a quantitative lower mass bound requires the experimental width uncertainties and the fermion's couplings. The given observations yield no upper mass bound. A sterile [neutrino](../../../../neutrino.md) with no standard active coupling would not obey the stated $Z$-width inference, whereas the question specifies the same quantum numbers as the existing generations.
