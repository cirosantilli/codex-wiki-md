<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For a measurement of the cross-section ratio with [standard deviation](../../../../standard-deviation.md) $0.019R$, the separation of the fourth-lepton prediction from the ordinary prediction is

$$
\boxed{n_{\rm ratio}=\frac{|R'-R|}{0.019R}
=\frac{0.0191107}{0.019}=1.006\simeq1.0.}
$$

Thus the peak-ratio model predicts only about a one-standard-deviation shift in this observable. In contrast, the direct [Z boson](../../../../z-boson.md) width changes by $167+84=251$ MeV, giving

$$
\boxed{n_{\Gamma_Z}=\frac{251}{2.3}=109.1.}
$$

Under these assumptions the direct width measurement is overwhelmingly more sensitive. These are separate expected displacements, using the supplied experimental uncertainties and fixed theoretical inputs; they are not a combined statistical significance. The $501/3$ single-neutrino normalization and the peak-energy assumption are the same as in [e](../e.md). If the alternative literal $501$ MeV per [neutrino](../../../../neutrino.md) premise were used, the corresponding displacements would instead be $12.3$ and $254$ standard deviations, illustrating why that normalization must be stated.
