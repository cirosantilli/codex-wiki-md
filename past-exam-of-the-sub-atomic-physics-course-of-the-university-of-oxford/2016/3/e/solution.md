<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The supplied average retained-energy fraction in one [elastic collision](../../../../elastic-collision.md) is

$$
f=\frac{11.18^2+0.94^2}{(11.18+0.94)^2}=0.8569149.
$$

The [mean-energy neutron moderation count](../../../../mean-energy-neutron-moderation-count.md) follows by iterating the stated mean-energy model for [neutron moderation](../../../../neutron-moderation.md), giving $E_n=f^nE_0$. Equivalently, take logarithms to solve the geometric decrease:

$$
n=\frac{\ln(E_n/E_0)}{\ln f}
=\frac{\ln[0.025/(2\times10^6)]}{\ln(0.8569149)}
=117.847.
$$

Hence the estimated integer count is

$$
\boxed{n=118\ \text{collisions}.}
$$

After $117$ collisions the predicted mean energy is about $0.0285$ eV; after $118$ it is about $0.0244$ eV, bracketing the target. This is a mean-energy estimate using the given average retention factor. Individual scattering angles and energy losses fluctuate, so a specified single [neutron](../../../../neutron-split.md) is not guaranteed to reach the target on exactly its $118$th collision. An estimate based on the average logarithmic energy decrement is a different statistical convention.
