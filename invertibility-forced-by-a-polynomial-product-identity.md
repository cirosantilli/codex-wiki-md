# Invertibility forced by a polynomial product identity

↑ **Parent:** [Invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md)

Let $A,B$ be finite square [matrices](matrix.md) over a [field](field.md) and write $p(t)=c+tq(t)$ with $c\ne0$. If $AB=p(A)$, then $A(B-q(A))=cI$. Taking [determinants](determinant.md) proves $A$ is an [invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md), and rearrangement gives $B=q(A)+cA^{-1}$. Thus $B$ commutes with $A$. If $Av=\lambda v$, then $\lambda\ne0$ and $Bv=(p(\lambda)/\lambda)v$, proving an explicit [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) transfer. The argument avoids assuming commutation before proving it; it depends on square finite-dimensional [matrices](matrix.md), where a right inverse is two-sided.
