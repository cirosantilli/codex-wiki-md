# Group velocity and polarization of an inertial wave

↑ **Parent:** [Inertial wave](inertial-wave.md)

For $\omega=\sigma2\Omega k_\parallel/k$, differentiating the [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation-split.md) gives the displayed [group velocity](group-velocity.md). It is perpendicular to the [wavevector](wavevector.md). If $|k_\parallel|\ll k_\perp$, [energy](energy-split.md) propagates almost along the rotation axis while the [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) is almost horizontal. In an orthonormal basis $\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2$ normal to $\mathbf k$, with $\mathbf e_2=\widehat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf e_1$, the complex [velocity](velocity.md) is $A(\mathbf e_1-\sigma i\mathbf e_2)$ and its real oscillation is circular. In the anisotropic limit the vertical and transverse-horizontal [velocity](velocity.md) amplitudes are comparable, while the horizontal component parallel to $\mathbf k_\perp$ is smaller by $|k_\parallel|/k_\perp$.
