# Bounded-degree extensions with restricted ramification

↑ **Parent:** [Hermite–Minkowski theorem](hermite-minkowski-theorem.md)

Fix a [number field](number-field.md) $K$, a positive integer $d$, and a finite set $S$ of its finite places. There are finitely many [field extensions](field-extension.md) $L/K$ of degree at most $d$ unramified outside $S$, inside a fixed [algebraic closure](algebraic-closure.md). To see this, view them as extensions of the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) of bounded degree. Their ramified rational primes belong to a fixed finite set. At each of these primes, the [different exponent](different-exponent.md) is bounded in terms of the local degree and ramification index, giving a bound for the absolute [field discriminant](field-discriminant.md). The [Hermite–Minkowski theorem](hermite-minkowski-theorem.md) gives finitely many fields up to isomorphism, and each has only finitely many embeddings into the fixed closure. This useful form of arithmetic compactness is stated and proved for $K=\mathbb Q$ in [Andrew Sutherland's number-theory notes, Theorem 14.25](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2018fa/LectureNotes14.pdf); the preceding reduction gives the general case.
