# Sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Sequence and series](sequence-and-series.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence)

A sequence assigns a value to each position in an ordered index list. For an infinite [sequence](sequence.md) in a [set](set-split.md) $X$, one standard convention is a [function](function-split.md) $x:\mathbb N_{>0}\to X$, written $(x_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ with $x_n=x(n)$. Some applications start the index at zero, and a finite [sequence](sequence.md) has only finitely many positions. Values may repeat; both order and repetition matter. [Lebl, Basic Analysis I, §2.1: sequences as functions and their limits](basic-analysis-introduction-to-real-analysis-volume-i-by-jiri-lebl/2-1-sequences-and-limits.md).

For example, $a_n=(-1)^n$ begins $-1,1,-1,1,\ldots$, whereas $b_n=(-1)^{n+1}$ begins $1,-1,1,-1,\ldots$. Their [sets](set-split.md) of values are both $\{-1,1\}$, but these are different [sequences](sequence.md). A [subsequence](subsequence.md) keeps selected positions in increasing order; the [subsequence](subsequence.md) of $a_n$ at its [even integers](even-number.md) is the constant [sequence](sequence.md) $1,1,1,\ldots$. A [tail of a sequence](tail-of-a-sequence.md) retains all positions from one index onward.

[Sequences](sequence.md) express successive approximations, repeated observations, and the iterates of a [recurrence relation](recurrence-relation.md). A [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) describes where its terms eventually concentrate. Finite observations alone cannot establish that behaviour: for any positive [integer](integer.md) $K$, the all-zero [sequence](sequence.md) and the [sequence](sequence.md) with $u_n=0$ for $n\leq K$ and $u_n=1$ for $n>K$ agree through index $K$, yet have different [limits of sequences](limit-of-a-sequence.md). A formula or bound controlling the entire future, such as the [tail diameter of a sequence](tail-diameter-of-a-sequence.md) in the [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) condition, supplies information that a finite list cannot.
