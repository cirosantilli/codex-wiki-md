# Von Neumann-Morgenstern utility theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Expected utility hypothesis](expected-utility-hypothesis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann–Morgenstern_utility_theorem)

For a finite outcome space, [complete lottery preferences](complete-lottery-preferences.md), a [transitive relation](transitive-relation.md), [continuity of lottery preferences](continuity-of-lottery-preferences.md) and the [independence axiom for lottery preferences](independence-axiom-for-lottery-preferences.md) are equivalent to an [expected utility](expected-utility.md) representation of the displayed form. The representing outcome [utility function](utility-function-split.md) is unique up to a positive [affine function](affine-function.md). The theorem is associated with [John von Neumann](john-von-neumann.md) and [Oskar Morgenstern](oskar-morgenstern.md).

A useful proof construction selects a best outcome $a$ and a worst outcome $b$. If all outcomes are indifferent, a constant [utility function](utility-function-split.md) suffices. Otherwise assign $u(a)=1$, $u(b)=0$. [Continuity of lottery preferences](continuity-of-lottery-preferences.md) makes each outcome indifferent to a mixture $u(\omega)a+(1-u(\omega))b$, and [mixture monotonicity](mixture-monotonicity.md) makes the coefficient unique. [Substitution of indifferent lotteries](substitution-of-indifferent-lotteries.md) and [reduction of compound lotteries](reduction-of-compound-lotteries.md) turn any [lottery](lottery.md) into a best–worst mixture with best-outcome [probability](probability.md) $\sum_\omega L(\omega)u(\omega)$. [Mixture monotonicity](mixture-monotonicity.md) then ranks all such mixtures. The reverse direction follows by taking [expected values](expected-value-split.md); positive [affine functions](affine-function.md) preserve every comparison.[“The Expected Utility Property”; preceding axioms](expected-utility-theory-by-econport.md)
