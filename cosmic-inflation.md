## Graceful exit from inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

A [graceful exit from inflation](#graceful-exit-from-inflation) ends sustained [accelerating expansion](cosmology.md#accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe) and connects it to a hot [radiation-dominated universe](cosmology.md#radiation-dominated-universe). Crossing $\epsilon_H=1$ ends acceleration in a homogeneous expanding model; [reheating](#reheating) requires an additional mechanism to transfer [inflaton](#inflaton) energy into other species. Pure [exponential-potential power-law inflation](#exponential-potential-power-law-inflation) keeps $\epsilon_H$ constant and its [inflaton potential](#inflaton-potential) has no finite minimum, so neither an automatic endpoint nor oscillatory [reheating](#reheating) follows from that potential alone.

## Power-law inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

[Power-law inflation](#power-law-inflation) is [cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md) with a [scale factor](cosmology.md#scale-factor-cosmology) proportional to $t^p$ for constant $p>1$. Its [Hubble parameter](cosmology.md#hubble-parameter) is $H=p/t$ and its [first Hubble slow-roll parameter](#first-hubble-slow-roll-parameter) is $\epsilon_H=1/p$. The [comoving Hubble radius](cosmology.md#comoving-hubble-radius) decreases as $t^{1-p}$. A canonical [inflaton](#inflaton) with an exponential [inflaton potential](#inflaton-potential) admits an exact example. Because $\epsilon_H$ is constant, an indefinitely maintained power law has no [graceful exit from inflation](#graceful-exit-from-inflation).

### Exponential-potential power-law inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Power-law inflation](#power-law-inflation)

Use [natural units](physics.md#natural-units), the unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) $m_P=G^{-1/2}$, $V_0>0$ and $c\ne0$. In a flat universe dominated by a canonical [inflaton](#inflaton), $\phi=(2m_P/c)\log(\beta t)$ and $a\propto t^p$ solve the [inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion) and [Friedmann equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) exactly when $p=16\pi/c^2$ and $\beta^2=V_0c^2/[2m_P^2(3p-1)]$. The positive-potential scaling solution requires $p>1/3$, while [cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md) requires $p>1$. Its constant [equation-of-state parameter](cosmology.md#equation-of-state-parameter) is $w=-1+2/(3p)$. In terms of the [reduced Planck mass](physics.md#reduced-planck-mass) $M=m_P/\sqrt{8\pi}$, the exponential slope is $\lambda=c/\sqrt{8\pi}$ and the same exponent is $p=2/\lambda^2$.

#### Slow-roll normalization for exponential inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Exponential-potential power-law inflation](#exponential-potential-power-law-inflation)

For $V=V_0e^{-A\phi/m_{\rm pl}}$, neglecting acceleration of the field and [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) gives $3H\dot\phi=AV/m_{\rm pl}$ and $H^2=8\pi V/(3m_{\rm pl}^2)$. Integration yields the displayed field with $a\propto(t+b)^{16\pi/A^2}$. The exact flat scalar-dominated solution has the same power but $C^2=A^4V_0/[2m_{\rm pl}^2(48\pi-A^2)]$. The two normalizations agree at leading slow-roll order. Inflation requires $A^2<16\pi$; the stronger slow-roll conditions require $A^2/(8\pi)\ll1$.

## F-term hybrid inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

The bilinear hybrid [superpotential](supersymmetry.md#superpotential) couples an inflaton $S$ to two waterfall fields $u,v$. In global [supersymmetry](supersymmetry.md), $V=|g|^2(|uv-\zeta|^2+|S|^2(|u|^2+|v|^2))$. The branch $u=v=0$ breaks supersymmetry and is transversely stable for $|S|^2>|\zeta|$, while the zero-energy branch $S=0$, $uv=\zeta$ is supersymmetric. A canonical [Kähler potential](supersymmetry.md#kahler-potential) in [supergravity](supersymmetry.md#supergravity) slightly lifts the inflationary valley. It must not be described as two isolated stationary minima of the full potential.

### Supersymmetric vacuum manifold of a bilinear hybrid superpotential

↑ **Parent:** [F-term hybrid inflation](#f-term-hybrid-inflation)

For nonzero $g$ and $\zeta$, these field values give $W=0$ and all [Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential](supersymmetry.md#kahler-covariant-derivative-of-a-superpotential) zero, hence zero [supergravity auxiliary fields](supersymmetry.md#supergravity-auxiliary-field) and zero vacuum energy. With only the supplied chiral fields, the product condition gives a complex vacuum manifold rather than one isolated point. A specified gauged opposite-charge symmetry can add a [D-flatness](supersymmetry.md#d-flatness) condition and a gauge quotient, but no such gauge sector should be invented from the field labels alone.

### Waterfall instability in F-term hybrid inflation

↑ **Parent:** [F-term hybrid inflation](#f-term-hybrid-inflation)

In the small-field global approximation, the waterfall fluctuations on $u=v=0$ have the displayed masses. One becomes negative when $|S|^2<|\zeta|$, driving the fields towards the [supersymmetric vacuum](supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-vacuum) branch. In the canonical supergravity model, the exact masses follow from expanding the exponential and Kähler-covariant derivatives; their Planck-suppressed corrections should not be discarded unless the corresponding field and symmetry-breaking scales are small.

<h3 id="canonical-kahler-correction-to-an-f-term-hybrid-valley">Canonical-Kähler correction to an F-term hybrid valley</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [F-term hybrid inflation](#f-term-hybrid-inflation)

The [supergravity F-term potential](supersymmetry.md#supergravity-f-term-potential) on the inflationary branch has the displayed exact form. Its expansion is $|g\zeta|^2(1+x^2/2+O(x^3))$, so the inflaton quadratic mass cancels at tree level for this canonical model. For $x>0$ its derivative is $|g\zeta|^2e^xx(1+x)>0$; the branch is therefore a transverse valley, not a stationary supersymmetry-breaking vacuum.

## de Sitter approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

Approximately constant positive [Hubble parameter](cosmology.md#hubble-parameter) gives a nearly [de Sitter spacetime](general-relativity.md#de-sitter-spacetime) expansion in flat slicing. Its scale factor can be approximated by $a=-1/(H\tau)$ with negative [conformal time](cosmology.md#conformal-time) increasing toward zero. Slow variation of the expansion rate limits the accuracy of this approximation.

## Reheating

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

Reheating transfers the [inflaton](#inflaton) energy into other particles after [cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md), eventually establishing a hot thermal bath. It requires interactions in addition to a background [scalar potential](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-potential). The duration and averaged [equation-of-state parameter](cosmology.md#equation-of-state-parameter) of reheating affect the [number of e-folds](#number-of-e-folds) between a chosen scale's horizon exit and the end of inflation.

### Reheating temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Reheating](#reheating)

The reheating temperature characterizes the thermal radiation bath after energy from a nonthermal component has been transferred and thermalized. For [sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic](#sudden-decay-of-a-dominant-nonrelativistic-relic), $T_R\simeq[3M_{\rm Pl}^2\Gamma^2/(c_2g_\star)]^{1/4}$. It differs from the residual radiation temperature immediately before decay.

#### Quartic-inflation prompt-reheating estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Reheating temperature](#reheating-temperature)

For a quartic [inflaton](#inflaton) potential, an endpoint field of order the unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) leaves density of order $\lambda M_P^4$. If prompt, efficient [reheating](#reheating) converts it to equilibrium radiation, $\rho=(\pi^2/30)g_*T_R^4$ gives the displayed scaling. With the potential-only endpoint estimate $\phi_e=M_P/\sqrt\pi$, the coefficient is $[15/(2\pi^4)]^{1/4}$. End-of-inflation [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) changes that coefficient by order one, and delayed transfer or incomplete thermalization requires a reheating model rather than the potential alone.

### Sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic

↑ **Parent:** [Reheating](#reheating)

The sudden-decay approximation converts a dominant cold relic's rest-mass density into radiation at $H\simeq\Gamma$, neglecting expansion during the conversion. Its pre-decay radiation temperature obeys $T_-^3\simeq3M_{\rm Pl}^2\Gamma^2/(c_1g_\star Ym)$, while the [reheating temperature](#reheating-temperature) is set by the post-decay radiation energy density. Constant relativistic counts and prompt thermalization are additional assumptions.

#### Entropy production by decay of a dominant relic

↑ **Parent:** [Sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic](#sudden-decay-of-a-dominant-nonrelativistic-relic)

At fixed [scale factor](cosmology.md#scale-factor-cosmology) and unchanged relativistic counts, instantaneous conversion of a relic into thermal radiation gives the displayed entropy-density enhancement. It exceeds one because the gas receives energy from outside its prior equilibrium sector. This does not contradict [cosmological entropy conservation](cosmology.md#cosmological-entropy-conservation), whose source-free reversible assumptions fail during decay and thermalization.

## Number of e-folds

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

The number of e-folds during an interval of expansion is the logarithmic growth of the [scale factor](cosmology.md#scale-factor-cosmology):

$$
N=\log\frac{a_f}{a_i}=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}H\,dt.
$$

## Primordial perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

A primordial perturbation is an early-universe fluctuation that seeds later cosmic microwave background anisotropy and large-scale structure. Inflation generates scalar curvature perturbations and tensor perturbations from quantum fluctuations.

### Dimensionless cosmological power spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial perturbation](#primordial-perturbation)

The dimensionless spectrum is the contribution to a statistically isotropic field variance per logarithmic [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) interval: $\langle X^2\rangle=\int d\log k\,\Delta_X(k)$, with any necessary smoothing or regulators. Some conventions denote this same quantity $\Delta_X^2$. The definition, not the superscript convention, fixes its meaning.

#### Dimensional and dimensionless matter spectra across equality

↑ **Parent:** [Dimensionless cosmological power spectrum](#dimensionless-cosmological-power-spectrum)

The dimensionless spectrum is $\mathscr P(k)=k^3P(k)/(2\pi^2)$. If it rises as $k^4$ below equality and as $\ln^2k$ above equality, then the dimensional spectrum rises as $k$ and falls as $k^{-3}\ln^2k$. Thus the dimensional spectrum has a turnover while the dimensionless spectrum only bends to a much slower rise. A schematic transition must smoothly join the two asymptotic regimes.

## Inflaton

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflaton)

A canonical homogeneous inflaton $\phi(t)$ has

$$
\rho=\frac12\dot\phi^2+V(\phi),
\qquad
P=\frac12\dot\phi^2-V(\phi),
$$

and obeys $\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V'(\phi)=0$.

### Inflaton potential

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton](#inflaton)

The [inflaton potential](#inflaton-potential) is the [potential energy](classical-mechanics.md#potential-energy) density of an [inflaton](#inflaton) as a function of its field value. Its derivative enters the [inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion), while its slope and curvature enter the [potential slow-roll parameters](#potential-slow-roll-parameter).

### Vacuum domination by a scalar field

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton](#inflaton)

A homogeneous canonical [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) with positive [scalar potential](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-potential) has [energy density](statistical-physics.md#energy-density) $\rho_\phi=\dot\phi^2/2+V$ and [pressure](thermodynamics.md#pressure) $P_\phi=\dot\phi^2/2-V$. When its [potential energy](classical-mechanics.md#potential-energy) dominates its [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy), the [equation-of-state parameter](cosmology.md#equation-of-state-parameter) approaches $-1$. If other components and spatial curvature are negligible, the [Friedmann acceleration equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-acceleration-equation) gives [accelerating expansion](cosmology.md#accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe). Small initial [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) is not enough to ensure long [cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md); the field must also remain on a sufficiently flat part of the [scalar potential](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-potential), as measured by the [potential slow-roll parameters](#potential-slow-roll-parameter).

### Inflaton equation of motion

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton](#inflaton)

For a homogeneous canonical inflaton in an expanding [FLRW metric](cosmology.md#friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric),

$$
\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V'(\phi)=0.
$$

The expansion contributes the damping term called [Hubble friction](cosmology.md#hubble-friction).

#### Frozen scalar fields and the Friedmann constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion)

For a homogeneous canonical [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field), the [cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation](cosmology.md#cosmological-perfect-fluid-continuity-equation) reduces to $\dot\phi(\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V')=0$. It implies the [inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion) wherever $\dot\phi\ne0$, and by continuity at isolated turning points. It cannot by itself exclude a frozen field on a time interval. Indeed, choose $\phi=\phi_0$ with $V(\phi_0)>0$ but $V'(\phi_0)\ne0$; a [de Sitter scale factor in flat slicing](general-relativity.md#de-sitter-scale-factor-in-flat-slicing) with $H^2=V(\phi_0)/(3M_{\rm Pl}^2)$ satisfies the homogeneous [Friedmann equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) and [Friedmann acceleration equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-acceleration-equation), but not the scalar equation. Varying the [inflaton action in an expanding universe](#inflaton-action-in-an-expanding-universe) supplies the missing equation also for a frozen field, requiring $V'(\phi_0)=0$.

### Exact hyperbolic-sine scalar-field cosmology

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton](#inflaton)

In a flat universe containing a homogeneous canonical [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field), the ansatz

$$
\phi(t)=\phi_0\log\tanh(\lambda t)
$$

has the exact solution

$$
H=\lambda\frac{\phi_0^2}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\coth(2\lambda t),
\qquad
a=a_0[\sinh(2\lambda t)]^{\phi_0^2/(2M_{\rm Pl}^2)}.
$$

It interpolates between decelerating power-law expansion at early times and exponential accelerated expansion at late times when $2/3<\phi_0^2/M_{\rm Pl}^2<2$.

### Inflaton action in an expanding universe

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton](#inflaton)

For a canonical scalar field in a spatially flat expanding universe,

$$
S[\phi]=\int d^3x\,dt\,a^3
\left[
\frac12\dot\phi^2
-\frac{c^2}{2a^2}|\nabla\phi|^2
-V(\phi)
\right].
$$

The field [Euler-Lagrange equation](analysis.md#euler-lagrange-equation) is

$$
\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi-\frac{c^2}{a^2}\nabla^2\phi+V'(\phi)=0.
$$

#### Inflationary scalar Fourier mode

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton action in an expanding universe](#inflaton-action-in-an-expanding-universe)

For nearly constant inflaton potential and constant $H_{\rm inf}$, the spatial [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) obeys

$$
\ddot{\widehat\phi}_{\mathbf k}
+3H_{\rm inf}\dot{\widehat\phi}_{\mathbf k}
+\frac{c^2k^2}{a^2}\widehat\phi_{\mathbf k}=0.
$$

##### Canonically rescaled de Sitter scalar mode

↑ **Parent:** [Inflationary scalar Fourier mode](#inflationary-scalar-fourier-mode)

For $a=-1/(H_{\rm inf}\tau)$, the rescaled mode

$$
\widetilde\phi_{\mathbf k}=a\widehat\phi_{\mathbf k}
$$

satisfies

$$
\widetilde\phi_{\mathbf k}''
+\left(c^2k^2-\frac2{\tau^2}\right)
\widetilde\phi_{\mathbf k}=0.
$$

###### Conformal-time quadratic action for an inflaton perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Canonically rescaled de Sitter scalar mode](#canonically-rescaled-de-sitter-scalar-mode)

Neglecting metric perturbations, the rescaling $f=a\delta\phi$ removes the expansion friction from the scalar perturbation action after a boundary term is discarded. Its canonical momentum is $f'$, and each real Fourier oscillator has time-dependent squared frequency $k^2+a^2V''-a''/a$. The oscillator can become inverted on [superhorizon scales](#superhorizon-scale); the canonical commutation relations still apply.

###### Bunch-Davies vacuum

↑ **Parent:** [Canonically rescaled de Sitter scalar mode](#canonically-rescaled-de-sitter-scalar-mode)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunch–Davies_vacuum)

The Bunch-Davies vacuum selects the mode that approaches the positive-frequency ground-state mode of angular frequency $ck$ in the far past $\tau\to-\infty$.

###### Bunch-Davies mode normalization in a finite comoving volume

↑ **Parent:** [Bunch-Davies vacuum](#bunch-davies-vacuum)

For a [minimally coupled massless scalar field](scalar-field-theory.md#minimally-coupled-massless-scalar-field) in flat [de Sitter spacetime](general-relativity.md#de-sitter-spacetime), $a=-1/(H\tau)$ and the [Canonically rescaled de Sitter scalar mode](#canonically-rescaled-de-sitter-scalar-mode) is $u_k=af_k$. The equation $u_k''+(k^2-2/\tau^2)u_k=0$ and the positive-frequency condition $u_k\sim e^{-ik\tau}/\sqrt{2k\mathcal V}$ select the displayed mode. Direct differentiation verifies its [Wronskian](differential-equation.md#wronskian) normalization, which ensures the equal-time [canonical commutation relations](quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation) with box-normalized creation and annihilation operators. A conformally coupled massless scalar has a different mode equation.

###### Cosmological horizon exit

↑ **Parent:** [Bunch-Davies vacuum](#bunch-davies-vacuum)

A Fourier mode exits the Hubble horizon during inflation when $k=aH$. On superhorizon scales $k\ll aH$, the growing mode of a sufficiently light scalar perturbation approaches a constant.

###### Superhorizon scale

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmological horizon exit](#cosmological-horizon-exit)

A cosmological mode is on a superhorizon scale when its physical wavenumber is smaller than the Hubble expansion rate, $k/a\ll H$, equivalently $k\ll aH$.

###### Scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Bunch-Davies vacuum](#bunch-davies-vacuum)

A Fourier-space variance $P_k$ in three dimensions is scale invariant when the dimensionless power per logarithmic wavenumber interval,

$$
\Delta^2(k)=\frac{k^3P_k}{2\pi^2},
$$

is independent of $k$. For a massless inflaton mode at late times,

$$
P_k\longrightarrow\frac{\hbar H_{\rm inf}^2}{2c^3k^3},
$$

so $\Delta^2(k)$ is constant.

###### Infrared qualification of a scale-invariant covariance

↑ **Parent:** [Scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum](#scale-invariant-inflationary-power-spectrum)

An exact $P(k)=A/k^3$ has divergent unsubtracted point [variance](variance.md). Its finite isotropic [covariance](variance.md#covariance) difference is the displayed logarithm, unchanged by simultaneous dilation of $r,r_0$. Thus the nonzero-mode scaling statement has a natural subtraction interpretation. Exact dilation invariance of a continuous finite-variance isotropic [covariance](variance.md#covariance) itself would instead force a constant [covariance](variance.md#covariance), corresponding only to a zero-mode spectral measure. Cutoffs or a zero-mode subtraction must accompany the nontrivial spectrum.

###### Equal-time two-point function of a de Sitter scalar

↑ **Parent:** [Scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum](#scale-invariant-inflationary-power-spectrum)

For a light scalar in the [Bunch-Davies vacuum](#bunch-davies-vacuum) with metric perturbations neglected, the mode $e^{-ik\tau}(1-i/(k\tau))/\sqrt{2k}$ gives the displayed equal-time spectrum. The dimensionless power tends to $H^2/(4\pi^2)$ on [superhorizon scales](#superhorizon-scale). The exact massless infinite-volume correlation has an infrared divergence; a finite-volume or finite-duration infrared regulator, and smearing for coincident products, makes the correlators well defined.

###### Frozen contribution to a de Sitter scalar spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Equal-time two-point function of a de Sitter scalar](#equal-time-two-point-function-of-a-de-sitter-scalar)

The second term in the [equal-time two-point function of a de Sitter scalar](#equal-time-two-point-function-of-a-de-sitter-scalar) is independent of time for constant $H$. It dominates on [superhorizon scales](#superhorizon-scale) and has constant dimensionless [power spectrum](probability-and-statistics.md#power-spectrum), giving a [scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum](#scale-invariant-inflationary-power-spectrum). The [infrared qualification of a scale-invariant covariance](#infrared-qualification-of-a-scale-invariant-covariance) still applies to the formally infinite-volume coincident variance.

###### Subhorizon vacuum contribution to a de Sitter scalar spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Equal-time two-point function of a de Sitter scalar](#equal-time-two-point-function-of-a-de-sitter-scalar)

The [equal-time two-point function of a de Sitter scalar](#equal-time-two-point-function-of-a-de-sitter-scalar) has a contribution $P_{\rm vac}=1/(2ka^2)$, giving the displayed dimensionless [power spectrum](probability-and-statistics.md#power-spectrum) per logarithmic [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber). At fixed comoving [wavenumber](wave-equation.md#wavenumber) it decreases as $a^{-2}$; on short physical scales it reproduces the vacuum fluctuations of a [massless scalar field](scalar-field-theory.md#massless-scalar-field) in [Minkowski spacetime](special-relativity.md#minkowski-spacetime). Its coincident integral is ultraviolet divergent, so the fixed-mode decrease cannot be interchanged with an unregulated integral over all modes.

###### Primordial scalar amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum](#scale-invariant-inflationary-power-spectrum)

At leading slow-roll order, the dimensionless primordial curvature-perturbation amplitude at horizon exit is

$$
A_s=\frac{H^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon_VM_{\rm Pl}^2}
=\frac{V}{24\pi^2M_{\rm Pl}^4\epsilon_V}.
$$

###### Scalar spectral index

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial scalar amplitude](#primordial-scalar-amplitude)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_spectral_index)

The scalar spectral index describes the departure of the primordial scalar spectrum from scale invariance. To first order in potential slow-roll parameters,

$$
n_s-1\simeq-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V.
$$

###### Running of the scalar spectral index

↑ **Parent:** [Scalar spectral index](#scalar-spectral-index)

The running describes the scale dependence of the [scalar spectral index](#scalar-spectral-index). Differentiating $n_s-1\simeq-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V$ with the [potential slow-roll flow equations](#potential-slow-roll-flow-equations) gives the displayed second-order slow-roll result. It includes the [third potential slow-roll parameter](#third-potential-slow-roll-parameter) and is generally smaller than the leading spectral tilt.

###### Scalar spectral index in potential slow-roll parameters

↑ **Parent:** [Scalar spectral index](#scalar-spectral-index)

The leading scalar spectrum is proportional to $V^3/V'^2$. Along a slow-roll trajectory, $d\phi/d\ln a\simeq-M_{\rm pl}^2V'/V$ and $d\ln k/d\ln a=1-\epsilon_H$. Differentiating at horizon exit and keeping first order gives $n_s-1=-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V$. The derivative-factor correction contributes at second order.

## Slow-roll approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow-roll_approximation)

For a canonical scalar field with potential $V(\phi)$, slow roll reduces the field and Friedmann equations to

$$
H^2\simeq\frac{V}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2},
\qquad
3H\dot\phi\simeq-V'.
$$

### Relaxation to the slow-roll attractor

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

With approximately constant positive $H$ and potential slope $V'$, a homogeneous [scalar field](quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) initially at rest solves $\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi=-V'$ with the displayed [velocity](classical-mechanics.md#velocity). The transient decays on time $1/(3H)$, leaving the [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation) $3H\dot\phi\simeq-V'$. The fractional potential change over a [Hubble time](cosmology.md#hubble-time) is of order $M_{\rm Pl}^2(V'/V)^2$. Sustained slow roll normally uses both a small slope parameter and a small absolute curvature parameter $|M_{\rm Pl}^2V''/V|$. Local derivative bounds must hold over the traversed field interval; initial small [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) alone is not a global inflation-duration theorem.

### Horizon-exit logarithmic derivative in slow roll

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

Along [cosmological horizon exit](#cosmological-horizon-exit) $k\propto aH$, differentiation gives $d\log k/d\log a=1-\epsilon_H$, where $\epsilon_H=-\dot H/H^2$. The [chain rule](calculus.md#chain-rule) gives the first expression. In [reduced Planck units](physics.md#reduced-planck-units), the [slow-roll equations](#slow-roll-approximation) $3H\dot\phi\simeq-V'$ and $3H^2\simeq V$ give the second expression to leading order. Applied to a quantity already of slow-roll order, the omitted factor changes the answer only at second order.

### Single-field slow-roll inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

Single-field slow-roll inflation uses one canonical scalar clock whose potential evolves slowly enough for the slow-roll parameters to remain small. Its adiabatic long mode produces the [single-field inflation squeezed-limit consistency relation](cosmology.md#single-field-inflation-squeezed-limit-consistency-relation).

### Starobinsky inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starobinsky_inflation)

Starobinsky inflation is an inflationary model whose Einstein-frame scalar potential can be written

$$
V(\phi)=V_0\left(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\,\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^2.
$$

The exponentially flat large-field plateau supports [slow-roll inflation](#slow-roll-approximation), while the potential is quadratic near its minimum.

### Hubble slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

A Hubble slow-roll parameter measures the fractional change of the Hubble rate or of a preceding slow-roll parameter per e-fold. Small values make the inflationary background evolve adiabatically.

#### Potential and Hubble slow-roll parameter relation

↑ **Parent:** [Hubble slow-roll parameter](#hubble-slow-roll-parameter)

For a canonical [inflaton](#inflaton) under the [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation), $\dot\phi/H\simeq-M_{\rm Pl}^2V'/V$ and $\epsilon\simeq\epsilon_V=(M_{\rm Pl}^2/2)(V'/V)^2$. Differentiating $\log\epsilon_V$ along this trajectory gives

$$
\eta\simeq2\left(\frac{V''}{V'}-\frac{V'}V\right)\frac{\dot\phi}{H}=4\epsilon_V-2\eta_V.
$$

Here $\eta=d\log\epsilon/d\log a$, not the alternate convention $-\ddot\phi/(H\dot\phi)$. The logarithmic definition requires $\epsilon>0$; exact de Sitter expansion is treated as a limit.

#### First Hubble slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Hubble slow-roll parameter](#hubble-slow-roll-parameter)

The first Hubble slow-roll parameter is

$$
\epsilon=-\frac{\dot H}{H^2}.
$$

For a canonical inflaton, $\epsilon=\dot\phi^2/(2M_{\rm Pl}^2H^2)\geq0$.

#### Second Hubble slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Hubble slow-roll parameter](#hubble-slow-roll-parameter)

Using the e-fold variable $N=\log a$, define

$$
\eta=\frac{d\log\epsilon}{dN}.
$$

### Comoving curvature perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

The comoving curvature perturbation is the scalar curvature perturbation on hypersurfaces orthogonal to the total matter flow. For single-field adiabatic inflation it is conserved on superhorizon scales.

#### Gradient evolution identity for comoving curvature

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

For adiabatic scalar perturbations without anisotropic stress, define $A=4\pi Ga^2(\bar\rho+\bar P)=\mathcal H^2-\mathcal H'$ and $c_a^2=\bar P'/\bar\rho'$. In [conformal Newtonian gauge](linear-cosmological-perturbation-theory.md#newtonian-gauge), $\mathcal R=-\phi-\mathcal H(\phi'+\mathcal H\phi)/A$. Background conservation gives $A'/A=-\mathcal H(1+3c_a^2)$. Differentiating the expression for $\mathcal R$ and using the scalar-potential equation gives the displayed identity. For regular adiabatic modes with nonzero background enthalpy, evolution is suppressed on scales $k\ll\mathcal H$.

#### Mukhanov-Sasaki variable

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

For single-field adiabatic inflation, the Mukhanov-Sasaki variable rescales the [comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation) by $z=a\sqrt{2\epsilon}M_{\rm Pl}$, where $M_{\rm Pl}$ is the [reduced Planck mass](physics.md#reduced-planck-mass). Its quadratic bulk [action](classical-mechanics.md#action) is $\tfrac12\int[(v')^2-(\nabla v)^2+(z''/z)v^2]$, after a boundary term is removed. A [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) mode obeys $v_k''+(k^2-z''/z)v_k=0$ and has canonical [Wronskian normalization](quantum-field-theory.md#wronskian-normalization). The mass scale and sound-speed factors depend on the underlying scalar theory; this definition assumes the canonical single-field action and positive slow-roll kinetic coefficient.

#### Superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

For an adiabatic single-field attractor, gradient terms are negligible well outside the [Hubble radius](cosmology.md#hubble-radius) and the growing comoving-curvature mode is constant. The separate-universe interpretation identifies it with the difference of local expansion histories. The conservation statement can fail for a non-attractor phase or an independent entropy mode.

#### Inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

On a single-field attractor, a field fluctuation changes the local point along the background trajectory. The corresponding time shift has magnitude $|\delta\phi/\dot\phi|$, and local expansion converts it into curvature of magnitude $H|\delta\phi/\dot\phi|$. On spatially flat slices a standard sign convention gives $\mathcal R=H\delta\phi_{\rm flat}/\dot\phi$. The vacuum mode amplitude $H/(2\pi)$ then determines the scalar curvature spectrum.

#### De Sitter curvature mode function

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

For approximately constant $H$ and $\epsilon$, a vacuum-normalized curvature mode is $u_k(\tau)=H(1+ik\tau)e^{-ik\tau}/\sqrt{4\epsilon M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2k^3}$. Its derivative is $u_k^\prime=Hk^2\tau e^{-ik\tau}/\sqrt{4\epsilon M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2k^3}$. Ordered unequal-time [Wick contractions](perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#wick-contraction) use the complex conjugate at the earlier vertex, consistently with the [vacuum prescription for inflationary in-in integrals](quantum-field-theory.md#vacuum-prescription-for-inflationary-in-in-integrals).

#### Non-adiabatic pressure perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

The non-adiabatic pressure perturbation is

$$
\delta P_{\rm nad}=\delta P-\frac{\dot{\bar P}}{\dot{\bar\rho}}\delta\rho.
$$

On [superhorizon scales](#superhorizon-scale) it sources the evolution of the [comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation) through a convention-dependent signed relation proportional to $H\delta P_{\rm nad}/(\bar\rho+\bar P)$.

##### Cosmological entropy perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Non-adiabatic pressure perturbation](#non-adiabatic-pressure-perturbation)

A cosmological entropy perturbation changes the relative composition of cosmic fluids while leaving the total curvature initially unchanged. For [cold dark matter](cosmology.md#cold-dark-matter) and photons, a common relative entropy variable is

$$
S_{c\gamma}=\delta_c-\frac34\delta_\gamma.
$$

#### Slow-roll curvature power spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Comoving curvature perturbation](#comoving-curvature-perturbation)

At leading order in canonical single-field slow roll and evaluated at horizon exit $k=aH$,

$$
\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2(k)=\frac1{2\epsilon M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^2.
$$

##### Inflationary potential normalization and matter power

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll curvature power spectrum](#slow-roll-curvature-power-spectrum)

At a fixed horizon-exit e-fold count and with the later cosmology fixed, rescaling a canonical single-field inflationary potential by a positive constant leaves its potential slow-roll parameters and field solution unchanged. The [slow-roll curvature power spectrum](#slow-roll-curvature-power-spectrum) is proportional to $V/\epsilon_V$, so the [matter power spectrum](linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#matter-power-spectrum) scales by the same factor while its leading tilt and transfer-function shape remain unchanged. A changed specified reheating history can also shift the horizon-exit count assigned to a fixed physical wavenumber.

##### Scalar spectral index in Hubble slow-roll parameters

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll curvature power spectrum](#slow-roll-curvature-power-spectrum)

Differentiating the curvature spectrum at horizon exit gives

$$
n_s-1=\frac{d\log\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2}{d\log k}
=-2\epsilon-\eta
$$

to first order in Hubble slow-roll parameters.

### Primordial tensor power spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

For the two graviton polarizations in canonical single-field inflation,

$$
\Delta_t^2(k)=\frac8{M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^2
$$

at horizon exit.

#### Graviton power spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial tensor power spectrum](#primordial-tensor-power-spectrum)

The graviton power spectrum is the two-point spectrum of a tensor metric perturbation. A convention may quote it per polarization or summed over both polarizations, so that choice must accompany its normalization.

#### Adiabatic tensor mode

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial tensor power spectrum](#primordial-tensor-power-spectrum)

An adiabatic tensor mode is a long-wavelength transverse-traceless metric perturbation whose leading local effect can be produced by a [large spatial diffeomorphism](geometry-and-topology.md#large-spatial-diffeomorphism). Its effect on short modes is therefore constrained by a Ward identity.

##### Cosmological soft-graviton consistency relation

↑ **Parent:** [Adiabatic tensor mode](#adiabatic-tensor-mode)

For a single adiabatic long tensor mode, the squeezed scalar-scalar-graviton bispectrum is the anisotropic momentum response of the scalar power spectrum:

$$
\lim_{q\to0}\frac{\langle\gamma^s_{\mathbf q}\varphi_{\mathbf k}
\varphi_{-\mathbf k-\mathbf q}\rangle'}{P_\gamma(q)}
=-\frac12\epsilon_{ij}^s k_i\partial_{k_j}P_\varphi(k).
$$

#### Tensor spectral index

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial tensor power spectrum](#primordial-tensor-power-spectrum)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_spectral_index)

The tensor spectral index is the logarithmic slope $n_T=d\log\Delta_t^2/d\log k$. Canonical slow-roll inflation gives $n_T=-2\epsilon$ to first order.

#### Tensor-to-scalar ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial tensor power spectrum](#primordial-tensor-power-spectrum)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor-to-scalar_ratio)

The tensor-to-scalar ratio is $r=\Delta_t^2/\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2$. Canonical single-field slow roll gives $r=16\epsilon$.

##### Single-field inflation consistency relation

↑ **Parent:** [Tensor-to-scalar ratio](#tensor-to-scalar-ratio)

Combining $r=16\epsilon$ with $n_T=-2\epsilon$ gives the leading-order consistency relation

$$
r=-8n_T.
$$

### Blue primordial spectrum

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

A primordial power spectrum is blue when its dimensionless power increases with wavenumber. Canonical single-field slow roll cannot produce a blue tensor spectrum because $\epsilon\geq0$ implies $n_T\leq0$, but its scalar spectrum can be blue when $\eta<-2\epsilon$.

### Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

For $V(\phi)=m^2\phi^2$ in units where $3H^2\simeq V$ and for positive $\phi$,

$$
\phi(t)=\phi_i-\frac{2m}{\sqrt3}t,
$$

and

$$
a(t)=a_i\exp\left(
\frac{m\phi_i}{\sqrt3}t-\frac{m^2t^2}{3}
\right)
=a_i\exp\left(\frac{\phi_i^2-\phi(t)^2}{4}\right).
$$

#### Quadratic-inflation expansion from Planck density

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution](#quadratic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

For $V=m^2\phi^2/2$ and unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass), the [slow-roll e-fold count](#slow-roll-e-fold-count) is $N=2\pi(\phi_i^2-\phi_f^2)/m_P^2$. A formal initial potential $V_i\sim m_P^4$ gives $\phi_i^2\sim2m_P^4/m^2$, hence the displayed large-field estimate and expansion factor $e^N$. The final-field contribution is order one in $N$. Without specifying $m/m_P$ there is no unique numerical expansion factor. The extrapolation to Planck density is a classical estimate, rather than a controlled treatment of quantum-gravity corrections.

#### Quadratic-inflation endpoint estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution](#quadratic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

For $V=m^2\phi^2/2$ and unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) $m_P=G^{-1/2}$, the [potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter) is $\epsilon_V=m_P^2(V'/V)^2/(16\pi)=m_P^2/(4\pi\phi^2)$. Its conventional value one gives the displayed endpoint estimate. Exact accelerated expansion ends at $\epsilon_H=1$, or $\dot\phi^2=V$ for a canonical scalar alone. Substitution of slow-roll formulas into this exact condition gives a different order-one coefficient because slow roll is failing there; a precise endpoint requires integrating the full [inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion) and [Friedmann equation](cosmology.md#friedmann-equations). The large-field e-fold estimate is insensitive to this order-one endpoint uncertainty.

#### Quadratic-inflation field interval from a wavenumber interval

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution](#quadratic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

For a quadratic [inflaton](#inflaton) potential ending at the leading estimate $\epsilon_V=1$, $\Phi/M_{\rm Pl}=\sqrt{4N+2}$. [Cosmological horizon exit](#cosmological-horizon-exit) gives $d\log k/dN=-1+\epsilon_H$. A narrow logarithmic interval therefore probes a field span $2\Delta\log k/(\sqrt{4N+2}(1-\epsilon_H))$, to the accuracy of the [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation). At sixty e-folds, five logarithmic wavenumber units probe about $0.64M_{\rm Pl}$ around a background field near $15.6M_{\rm Pl}$.

#### Quadratic-inflation tilt-tensor relation

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution](#quadratic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

The quadratic potential has equal [potential slow-roll parameters](#potential-slow-roll-parameter), $\epsilon_V=\eta_V=1/(2N+1)$. Consequently $n_s=1-4/(2N+1)$ and $r=16/(2N+1)$ at leading order. The relation $r=4(1-n_s)$ is independent of the [inflaton](#inflaton) mass, so a satisfactory scalar tilt does not by itself guarantee a satisfactory tensor prediction.

#### Quadratic-inflation amplitude calibration

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic-potential slow-roll solution](#quadratic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

For a quadratic potential with the end set by $\epsilon_V=1$, a mode leaving $N$ e-folds earlier has $\phi_*^2=(4N+2)M_{\rm pl}^2$. Its curvature amplitude is $(m/M_{\rm pl})^2(2N+1)^2/(24\pi^2)$. Thus the scalar amplitude fixes the [inflaton](#inflaton) mass while the tilt and tensor ratio depend only on the exit e-fold count at this order.

### Quartic-potential slow-roll solution

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

For $V(\phi)=\lambda\phi^4/4$ with $\lambda>0$, use [natural units](physics.md#natural-units) and the unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) $M_P=G^{-1/2}$. The [slow-roll equations](#slow-roll-approximation) give $H\simeq\sqrt{2\pi\lambda/3}\,\phi^2/M_P$ and $\dot\phi\simeq-M_P\sqrt{\lambda/(6\pi)}\,\phi$. Integrating gives the displayed exponential field decay, valid on either sign branch, and

$$
\frac{a(t)}{a_i}\simeq\exp\left[\frac{\pi}{M_P^2}(\phi_i^2-\phi(t)^2)\right].
$$

The [potential slow-roll parameters](#potential-slow-roll-parameter) require $\phi^2\gg M_P^2$. The expression is valid only during [slow-roll inflation](#slow-roll-approximation); its formal late-time saturation does not describe the subsequent [reheating](#reheating) or radiation era.

#### Quartic-inflation expansion from Planck density

↑ **Parent:** [Quartic-potential slow-roll solution](#quartic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

With unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) $M_P$ and initial potential-dominated density $\lambda\phi_i^4/4\sim M_P^4$, one has $\phi_i^2\sim2M_P^2/\sqrt\lambda$. The [slow-roll e-fold count](#slow-roll-e-fold-count) is $N\simeq\pi(\phi_i^2-\phi_e^2)/M_P^2$. Thus small positive $\lambda$ produces a very large expansion factor $e^N$ in this classical quartic model. The order-one endpoint correction is negligible compared with $2\pi/\sqrt\lambda$; initial [kinetic energy](classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy), spatial curvature and the model's validity at trans-Planckian field values are independent assumptions.

#### Quartic-inflation endpoint estimate

↑ **Parent:** [Quartic-potential slow-roll solution](#quartic-potential-slow-roll-solution)

For $V=\lambda\phi^4/4$ and unreduced [Planck mass](physics.md#planck-mass) $M_P$, the [potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter) is $\epsilon_V=M_P^2/(\pi\phi^2)$. Setting it to one gives the displayed conventional endpoint. The exact end of scalar-dominated [accelerating expansion](cosmology.md#accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe) is $\epsilon_H=1$, equivalently $\dot\phi^2=V$. Since [slow-roll](#slow-roll-approximation) is failing near that point, a precise endpoint requires evolving the full [inflaton equation of motion](#inflaton-equation-of-motion); potential and kinetic criteria evaluated with a failing approximation need not give the same order-one coefficient.

### Potential slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

The potential slow-roll parameter is

$$
\epsilon_V=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}
\left(\frac{V'}V\right)^2.
$$

Under the slow-roll equations, $\dot\phi^2/(2V)\simeq\epsilon_V/3$.

#### Potential slow-roll flow equations

↑ **Parent:** [Potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter)

At horizon exit during single-field [slow-roll inflation](#slow-roll-approximation), $d/d\log k\simeq-M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2(V'/V)d/d\Phi$. Applying this operator to the [potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter) and the [second potential slow-roll parameter](#second-potential-slow-roll-parameter) yields the displayed leading-order flow equations. Corrections to the horizon-exit derivative contribute only at higher slow-roll order here.

#### Third potential slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter)

Despite its notation, $\xi_V^2$ need not be nonnegative. It is the signed dimensionless derivative combination entering the leading [running of the scalar spectral index](#running-of-the-scalar-spectral-index).

#### Second potential slow-roll parameter

↑ **Parent:** [Potential slow-roll parameter](#potential-slow-roll-parameter)

The second potential slow-roll parameter is

$$
\eta_V=M_{\rm Pl}^2\frac{V''}{V}.
$$

Slow roll usually requires both $\epsilon_V\ll1$ and $|\eta_V|\ll1$.

### Effective equation of state of an oscillating scalar field

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

Suppose a homogeneous scalar field oscillates much faster than the [Hubble parameter](cosmology.md#hubble-parameter) in a monomial potential $V(\phi)=\lambda|\phi|^n$. The [virial theorem](classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem) averaged over one oscillation gives $\langle\dot\phi^2\rangle=n\langle V\rangle$, and hence

$$
\langle w\rangle=\frac{\langle P\rangle}{\langle\rho\rangle}=\frac{n-2}{n+2},
\qquad
\rho\propto a^{-6n/(n+2)}.
$$

A quadratic minimum therefore behaves like [pressureless matter](cosmology.md#pressureless-matter), whereas a quartic minimum behaves like [radiation in cosmology](cosmology.md#radiation-in-cosmology).

### Hilltop inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilltop_inflation)

Hilltop inflation begins near a local maximum of the inflaton potential. For

$$
V(\phi)=\Lambda^4-\frac12m^2\phi^2,
$$

a positive field rolls away from the maximum at $\phi=0$.

#### Quartic hilltop inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Hilltop inflation](#hilltop-inflation)

Near a flat quartic maximum, $V\simeq\alpha\sigma^4-3\alpha\phi^4$. With [reduced Planck mass](physics.md#reduced-planck-mass) $M$, the [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation) gives $N\simeq\sigma^4/(24M^2\phi_N^2)$, $\eta_V\simeq-3/(2N)$ and $\epsilon_V\simeq\sigma^4/(192M^4N^3)$. Hence $n_s\simeq1-3/N$, $n_T\simeq-2\epsilon_V$, and $\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}\simeq8\alpha N^3/\pi^2$. The result is dominated by the near-maximum part of the trajectory; extending slow roll to a distant minimum is unnecessary.

// Target: cosmic-inflation.bigb

##### Low-multipole tensor fraction in quartic hilltop inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Quartic hilltop inflation](#quartic-hilltop-inflation)

Using the approximate large-angle CMB relation $T/S\simeq-7n_T$ gives $T/S\simeq7\sigma^4/(96M^4N^3)$ in [quartic hilltop inflation](#quartic-hilltop-inflation). It is suppressed by the fourth power of the small-field scale. This large-angle temperature ratio is not the primordial [tensor-to-scalar ratio](#tensor-to-scalar-ratio) $r=16\epsilon_V$; their transfer functions and normalization differ.

// Target: cosmology.bigb

##### COBE normalization of quartic hilltop inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Quartic hilltop inflation](#quartic-hilltop-inflation)

For a negligible tensor contribution, the historical COBE horizon amplitude $\delta_H\sim2\times10^{-5}$ corresponds to curvature power $\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}\sim25\delta_H^2/4$. In [quartic hilltop inflation](#quartic-hilltop-inflation), $\alpha=\pi^2\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}/(8N^3)$ is of order $10^{-14}$ at $N=50$, independent of the small symmetry-breaking scale at this order. The exact inferred amplitude depends on the late cosmology and tilt used in the COBE transfer calculation.

#### Quadratic hilltop slow-roll prediction

↑ **Parent:** [Hilltop inflation](#hilltop-inflation)

For $V(\phi)=V_0-m^2\phi^2/2$ with $V_0$ dominant,

$$
\eta_V\simeq-\frac{m^2M_{\rm Pl}^2}{V_0},
\qquad
\epsilon_V\simeq\frac12\eta_V^2\frac{\phi^2}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.
$$

If $\widetilde N$ e-folds remain and $n_s-1\simeq2\eta_V$, then

$$
\phi=\phi_e e^{(n_s-1)\widetilde N/2},
\qquad
r\simeq2(n_s-1)^2\left(\frac{\phi_e}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^2e^{(n_s-1)\widetilde N}.
$$

### Monomial inflation potential

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

A monomial inflation potential is a positive power of the inflaton. Its potential slow-roll parameter is $\epsilon_V=\alpha^2M_{\rm Pl}^2/(2\phi^2)$.

#### Monomial-inflation curvature amplitude

↑ **Parent:** [Monomial inflation potential](#monomial-inflation-potential)

For $V=C_p(\Phi/M_{\rm Pl})^p$ and the leading endpoint estimate $\epsilon_V=1$, the [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation) gives $\Phi_N^2/M_{\rm Pl}^2=2pN+p^2/2$ and $\epsilon_V=p/(4N+p)$. Thus $\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}=C_p(2pN+p^2/2)^{p/2}(4N+p)/(24\pi^2M_{\rm Pl}^4p)$ and the [scalar spectral index](#scalar-spectral-index) obeys $n_s-1=-2(p+2)/(4N+p)$. The coefficient sets the amplitude, whereas the monomial power and exit e-fold count set the leading tilt. Changing the power without specifying the coefficient leaves the overall normalization undetermined.

#### Monomial inflation with a prescribed endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Monomial inflation potential](#monomial-inflation-potential)

For $V=\lambda\phi^n$ on $\phi>0$, [slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation) gives $\epsilon\simeq n^2M_{\rm Pl}^2/(2\phi^2)$ and $\eta\simeq2nM_{\rm Pl}^2/\phi^2$. The [slow-roll e-fold count](#slow-roll-e-fold-count) integrates to $N\simeq(\phi_N^2-\phi_e^2)/(2nM_{\rm Pl}^2)$. For $n=2,3,4$, the loose order-one conditions $\epsilon\leq1$ and $|\eta|\leq1$ imply $\phi^2\geq2nM_{\rm Pl}^2$ in this approximation. Genuine slow roll requires a much stronger hierarchy. A prescribed endpoint $\phi_e^2=2nM_{\rm Pl}^2$ gives $\phi_N^2=2n(N+1)M_{\rm Pl}^2$ and

$$
n_s\simeq1-\frac{n+2}{2(N+1)},\qquad r\simeq\frac{4n}{N+1}.
$$

The actual end criterion for accelerated expansion is $\epsilon=1$, whose leading potential estimate instead gives $\phi_e^2\simeq n^2M_{\rm Pl}^2/2$. The prescribed endpoint is a convention, not an exact slow-roll or inflation boundary.

#### Quartic-inflation tilt-tensor relation

↑ **Parent:** [Monomial inflation potential](#monomial-inflation-potential)

For canonical slow-roll inflation with $V(\phi)=\lambda\phi^4$, the leading [scalar spectral index](#scalar-spectral-index) and [tensor-to-scalar ratio](#tensor-to-scalar-ratio) obey

$$
n_s-1=-\frac3{16}r.
$$

It is a parameter-free consistency check of the quartic potential at leading slow-roll order.

### Slow-roll e-fold count

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)

The number of e-folds between field values $\phi_i$ and $\phi_f$ is

$$
N\simeq\frac1{\sqrt2M_{\rm Pl}}
\int_{\phi_f}^{\phi_i}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_V(\phi)}}
$$

when the field rolls monotonically down the potential.

#### Monomial slow-roll e-fold count

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll e-fold count](#slow-roll-e-fold-count)

For $V(\phi)=\lambda\phi^n/n$ under the stated Cambridge Tripos normalization,

$$
N_e=\frac{4\pi G}{c^4n}
(\phi_i^2-\phi_f^2).
$$

If slow roll ends by kinetic-potential equality, then

$$
\phi_f^2\simeq\frac{n^2c^4}{48\pi G},
\qquad
N_e\simeq\frac n{12}
\left[\left(\frac{\phi_i}{\phi_f}\right)^2-1\right].
$$

### Natural inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Slow-roll approximation](#slow-roll-approximation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_inflation)

Natural inflation uses a periodic potential

$$
V(\phi)=V_0[1+\cos(\phi/f)].
$$

Its potential slow-roll parameter obeys

$$
\sqrt{\epsilon_V}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}}{\sqrt2f}
\tan\frac{\phi}{2f}
$$

on the branch where the tangent is positive.

#### E-fold count for the natural-inflation cosine potential

↑ **Parent:** [Natural inflation](#natural-inflation)

For the cosine potential,

$$
N=\frac{2f^2}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\left[
\log\sin\frac{\phi_i}{2f}
-\log\sin\frac{\phi_f}{2f}
\right].
$$

## Ultra-slow-roll inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)

Ultra-slow-roll inflation occurs on an exceptionally flat potential. The homogeneous field velocity decays as $\dot\phi\propto a^{-3}$, so $\epsilon\propto a^{-6}$ and the second Hubble slow-roll parameter approaches $-6$.

### Growing curvature perturbation in ultra-slow-roll inflation

↑ **Parent:** [Ultra-slow-roll inflation](#ultra-slow-roll-inflation)

In ultra-slow-roll inflation, $z=a\sqrt{2\epsilon}\propto a^{-2}\propto\eta^2$. The comoving curvature mode equation is

$$
\mathcal R_k''+\frac4\eta\mathcal R_k'+k^2\mathcal R_k=0,
$$

and its Bunch-Davies mode is proportional to $(1+ik\eta)e^{-ik\eta}/\eta^3$. Unlike ordinary slow roll, the superhorizon curvature perturbation grows as $a^3$.

#### Classicality parameter of a cosmological perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Growing curvature perturbation in ultra-slow-roll inflation](#growing-curvature-perturbation-in-ultra-slow-roll-inflation)

For a perturbation $X$, a normalized commutator

$$
\mathcal C=
\frac{|\langle[X,\dot X]\rangle|}
{\sqrt{\langle XX\rangle\langle\dot X\dot X\rangle}}
$$

compares its quantum commutator with its root-mean-square phase-space fluctuations. A limit $\mathcal C\to0$ signals effective classicality.

## Curvaton

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmic inflation](cosmic-inflation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvaton)

A curvaton is a light spectator scalar field whose energy density is negligible during inflation but whose fluctuations can later generate the primordial curvature perturbation.

### Curvaton mechanism

↑ **Parent:** [Curvaton](#curvaton)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvaton_mechanism)

In the curvaton mechanism, the curvaton's relative energy density grows after inflation and its initially isocurvature fluctuation becomes an adiabatic curvature fluctuation when the curvaton decays.
