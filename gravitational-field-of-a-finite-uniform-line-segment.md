# Gravitational field of a finite uniform line segment

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md)

Spread [mass](mass.md) $M$ uniformly along $-a<z<a$ on an axis, with [linear mass density](linear-mass-density.md) $\lambda=M/(2a)$. In its perpendicular bisector plane, axial field components cancel, and the radial component is

$$
g_r=-G\lambda r\int_{-a}^a\frac{dz}{(r^2+z^2)^{3/2}}=-\frac{GM}{r\sqrt{r^2+a^2}}.
$$

This is an exact [line mass](line-mass.md) model and a thin-rod approximation for a physical cylinder. A centred [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) in this symmetry plane has $v^2=GM/\sqrt{R^2+a^2}$. Thus $v\to\sqrt{GM/a}=\sqrt{2G\lambda}$ for $R/a\to0$, and $v\sim\sqrt{GM/R}$ for $R/a\to\infty$. For a finite-width rod, the first limit is useful only while its transverse size remains much smaller than $R$.
