# Gold-iron alloy susceptibility experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Spin glass](spin-glass.md)

[V. Cannella](vincent-cannella.md) and [J. A. Mydosh](john-a-mydosh.md) measured low-field [magnetic susceptibility](magnetic-susceptibility.md) of [gold](gold.md)-[iron](iron.md) [alloys](alloy.md) using a [mutual inductance](mutual-inductance.md) bridge. Moving samples into and out of the pickup arrangement separated their signal from the background. Cooling revealed sharp susceptibility cusps; changing concentration, sample shape and applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) tested their interpretation. Larger fields rounded the peaks. Their 1972 paper interpreted low-concentration ordering in antiferromagnetic terms: the cusp alone did not demonstrate Parisi's later theory.[§III, p. 4224, mutual-inductance method; §V, pp. 4228–4230, figures 9–12 and field/shape comparisons; abstract for the authors' contemporary interpretation](magnetic-ordering-in-gold-iron-alloys-by-vincent-cannella-and-john-a-mydosh.md)

This experiment measures a response to a small applied perturbation, rather than directly photographing frozen [spins](spin.md). Comparing field strengths is essential because the probing [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) itself changes the response.[§V.A, pp. 4229–4230, weak-field cusp rounding](magnetic-ordering-in-gold-iron-alloys-by-vincent-cannella-and-john-a-mydosh.md)
