# Eccentricity of a conic

↑ **Parent:** [Conic section](conic-section.md)

The parameter $e\geq0$ in a focus-centred [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md) equation $r=\ell/(1-e\cos\theta)$ for a [conic section](conic-section.md). It distinguishes an [ellipse](ellipse.md) ($e<1$), a [parabola](parabola.md) ($e=1$), and a [hyperbola](hyperbola.md) ($e>1$).

For a noncircular [conic section](conic-section.md), the [eccentricity of a conic](eccentricity-of-a-conic.md) is the constant ratio

$$
e=\frac{\operatorname{dist}(X,F)}{\operatorname{dist}(X,L)},
$$

where $F$ is a [focus of a conic](focus-of-a-conic.md), $L$ the corresponding [directrix of a conic](directrix-of-a-conic.md), and $X$ a point on the curve. For an [ellipse](ellipse.md) with [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md) $a$ and distance $c$ from the focal [midpoint](midpoint.md) to either [focus of a conic](focus-of-a-conic.md), $e=c/a=\sqrt{1-b^2/a^2}$. Its [circle](circle.md) case has $e=0$; a finite directrix does not give that circle through the displayed ratio. The circle is the limit as $c\to0$ and its [directrices of a conic](directrix-of-a-conic.md) move arbitrarily far away.

The [eccentricity of a conic](eccentricity-of-a-conic.md) is intrinsic to the cutting [plane](plane.md), and is not generally its slope in a chosen three-dimensional drawing. For the family $z=1+mx$ in [conic section](conic-section.md), with $0<m<1$, completing the square gives

$$
(1-m^2)\left(x-\frac{m}{1-m^2}\right)^2+y^2=\frac1{1-m^2}.
$$

A [displacement](displacement.md) $dx$ along this [plane](plane.md) has [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) $\sqrt{1+m^2}|dx|$. Thus the intrinsic semiaxis ratio satisfies $b^2/a^2=(1-m^2)/(1+m^2)$, giving

$$
e=\frac{\sqrt2m}{\sqrt{1+m^2}}.
$$

For $m=3/5$, this is $3/\sqrt{17}$, rather than $3/5$. This normalization separates the curve's geometry from a distorted projection of it onto the $xy$ plane.
