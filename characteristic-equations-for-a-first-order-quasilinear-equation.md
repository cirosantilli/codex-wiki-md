# Characteristic equations for a first-order quasilinear equation

↑ **Parent:** [Method of characteristics](method-of-characteristics.md)

For the first-order [quasilinear partial differential equation](quasilinear-partial-differential-equation.md) $P(x,y,z)z_x+Q(x,y,z)z_y=R(x,y,z)$, the displayed [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md) trace curves tangent to every classical [solution surface](solution-surface.md). Indeed, if $z=u(x,y)$ solves the equation and $x_s=P$, $y_s=Q$, then the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) gives $z_s=u_xP+u_yQ=R$. Conversely a surface swept out by these curves solves the equation wherever its projection to $(x,y)$ is invertible and hence represents a differentiable graph. The [coefficients](coefficient.md) can depend on the unknown $z$, which distinguishes these equations from characteristic equations for a prescribed linear [transport equation](transport-equation-split.md).
