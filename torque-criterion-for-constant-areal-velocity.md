# Torque criterion for constant areal velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Areal velocity](areal-velocity.md)

Assume a constant positive [mass](mass.md) $m$, a twice differentiable trajectory in the fixed [plane](plane.md), a fixed origin in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), and [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) for the net [force](force-split.md). The [product rule](product-rule.md) gives the short proof

$$
2\ddot A=\mathbf n\cdot\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf r\times\mathbf v)
=\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf r\times\mathbf a)
=\frac{\tau_n}{m},\qquad
\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}.
$$

The term $\mathbf v\times\mathbf v$ vanishes. This is the planar [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) balance written in [area](surface-area.md) language.[Section 11.2, equations 11.5-11.6: particle angular momentum and its torque balance](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others.md)

On a connected [time](time-in-physics.md) interval, [areal velocity](areal-velocity.md) is constant if and only if $\tau_n=0$ throughout. Since both [position](position.md) and [acceleration](acceleration.md) lie in the fixed [plane](plane.md), the net [torque](torque.md) has only that normal component. For $r\ne0$, zero net [torque](torque.md) is therefore equivalent to net [force](force-split.md) parallel to $\mathbf r$: its instantaneous direction is central, possibly inward, outward or zero. This diagnoses the net [force](force-split.md) along this motion, not its dependence on [position](position.md) or [time](time-in-physics.md) elsewhere. Opposing transverse [forces](force-split.md) may cancel.

A [central force](central-force.md) about a fixed origin gives [conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md), irrespective of its radial dependence: an [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md) is sufficient but unnecessary. With nonzero conserved [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md), $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf L=0$ fixes the [orbital plane](orbital-plane.md). Zero [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) gives radial motion and zero [areal velocity](areal-velocity.md); it does not select a unique [orbital plane](orbital-plane.md). The converse needs the fixed-plane assumption: a constant projected [areal velocity](areal-velocity.md) in three dimensions only constrains one [torque](torque.md) component. Also, $r=0$ makes the instantaneous [torque](torque.md) vanish regardless of [force](force-split.md) and makes [plane polar coordinates](plane-polar-coordinates.md) singular. A moving origin or changing plane requires additional terms.
