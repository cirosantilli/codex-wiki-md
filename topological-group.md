## Locally profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

A locally profinite group is a Hausdorff, locally compact, totally disconnected [topological group](topological-group.md). It has a neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting of [compact-open subgroups](#compact-open-subgroup). In smooth representation theory this is also called an $\ell$-group; that terminology is unrelated to a lattice-ordered group.

### Compact-open subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Locally profinite group](#locally-profinite-group)

A [compact-open subgroup](#compact-open-subgroup) is both a [compact subset](topology.md#compact-space) and an [open subgroup](#open-subgroup). Its coset space is discrete, and compact subsets meet only finitely many cosets. Finite intersections of [compact-open subgroups](#compact-open-subgroup) are again compact-open, making them useful common stabilizers for finitely many vectors.

## Continuous unitary character

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

A continuous unitary character is a continuous [group homomorphism](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) from a [topological group](topological-group.md) to the [unit circle](complex-analysis.md#complex-unit-circle) with multiplication. For Abelian groups, multiplication of characters is pointwise. Such characters must be distinguished from the traces of higher-dimensional group representations and from [characters of an algebra](banach-algebra.md#character-of-an-algebra).

## Right-invariant group metric

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

Right translations are isometries for a right-invariant group metric. Given a compatible [left-invariant group metric](#left-invariant-group-metric) $d$, the formula $d_R(x,y)=d(x^{-1},y^{-1})$ defines a compatible right-invariant group metric, since inversion is a homeomorphism and $(xa)^{-1}=a^{-1}x^{-1}$. Compatible invariant metrics need not be Lipschitz equivalent; on the additive group $\mathbb R$, both $|x-y|$ and $\sqrt{|x-y|}$ are compatible invariant distances.

## Left-invariant group metric

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

This is a distance function satisfying the displayed identity, rather than a [Riemannian metric](differential-geometry.md#riemannian-metric) tensor. When it induces the group's given topology, it is a compatible left-invariant group metric. Left translations are isometries.

### Bi-invariant group metric

↑ **Parent:** [Left-invariant group metric](#left-invariant-group-metric)

A group distance is bi-invariant when both left and right translations are [isometries](riemannian-geometry.md#isometry). [Conjugation](group-theory.md#conjugation) then fixes the [identity element](group.md#identity-element) and is an [isometry](riemannian-geometry.md#isometry), so each [metric](topological-analysis.md#metric) ball there is invariant under every inner automorphism. A compatible [bi-invariant group metric](#bi-invariant-group-metric) therefore requires arbitrarily small conjugation-invariant [neighbourhoods](topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics). This concerns a distance function, not merely a [bi-invariant Riemannian metric](lie-theory.md#bi-invariant-riemannian-metric) tensor.

#### Affine conjugation obstruction to a bi-invariant group metric

↑ **Parent:** [Bi-invariant group metric](#bi-invariant-group-metric)

In the [orientation-preserving affine group of the real line](lie-theory.md#orientation-preserving-affine-group-of-the-real-line), translations $T_{1/n}$ converge to the [identity element](group.md#identity-element), but [conjugation](group-theory.md#conjugation) by dilation $D_n$ sends each one to $T_1$. A compatible [bi-invariant group metric](#bi-invariant-group-metric) would give $d(e,T_{1/n})=d(e,T_1)>0$ for every $n$, contradicting convergence. The group is nonetheless metrizable in its ordinary two real coordinates.

### Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Left-invariant group metric](#left-invariant-group-metric)

A Hausdorff [topological group](topological-group.md) admits a compatible [left-invariant group metric](#left-invariant-group-metric) exactly when it has a countable [neighbourhood basis](topology.md#neighbourhood-basis) at the [identity element](group.md#identity-element). Choose symmetric [neighbourhoods](topology.md#neighbourhood-mathematics) of the [identity element](group.md#identity-element) $U_n$ with $U_{n+1}^3\subset U_n$ and trivial intersection. Assign a step in $U_n$ cost $2^{-n}$, and take the infimum of total costs of chains joining two points. The [dyadic product estimate for group neighbourhoods](#dyadic-product-estimate-for-group-neighbourhoods) makes this a genuine compatible [metric](topological-analysis.md#metric). The Hausdorff assumption separates distinct points; without it the same construction can give only a pseudometric.

#### Dyadic product estimate for group neighbourhoods

↑ **Parent:** [Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem](#birkhoff-kakutani-theorem)

Let $U_0=G$, and let the $U_n$ be decreasing symmetric sets containing the [identity element](group.md#identity-element) with $U_n^3\subset U_{n-1}$. If $g_j\in U_{n_j}$ and their dyadic costs sum to at most $2^{-n}$ for $n\ge1$, split the ordered word at an edge crossing its half-cost point. The words on either side each have cost at most $2^{-n-1}$. Induction on word length puts both side words in $U_n$, while the middle edge also belongs to $U_n$. Their threefold product lies in $U_{n-1}$. This controls the short-chain collapse that would otherwise make a path-distance construction degenerate.

## Closed subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

A closed subgroup is a [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) which is closed in the ambient topology. It is a [topological group](topological-group.md) in its [subspace topology](topology.md#subspace-topology), but need not be an [open subgroup](#open-subgroup).

## Open subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

An open [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) is also closed, since its complement is the union of its other open [cosets](group-theory.md#coset). Conversely a closed subgroup need not be open, as $\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb R$ shows.

### Open normal subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Open subgroup](#open-subgroup)

An [open normal subgroup](#open-normal-subgroup) $N$ of a [topological group](topological-group.md) is simultaneously open in its [topology](topology.md) and invariant under [conjugation](group-theory.md#conjugation). For a [profinite group](#profinite-group), the quotient $G/N$ is a finite discrete [group](group.md): its [cosets](group-theory.md#coset) form a disjoint [open cover](topology.md#open-cover), so [compactness](topology.md#compact-space) gives finitely many. Conversely the [group homomorphism kernel](group-theory.md#kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism) of a continuous [homomorphism](algebra.md#homomorphism) to a finite discrete [group](group.md) is open and normal. Such [subgroups](group.md#subgroup) form an identity-neighbourhood [basis](vector-space.md#basis) of every [profinite group](#profinite-group).

## Compactly generated group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_generated_group)

A topological group is compactly generated in the algebraic sense when a compact subset generates its underlying group. Adjoining its inverse and the identity gives a compact symmetric generating set $K$, and $G=\bigcup_{n\geq1}K^n$ is a [sigma-compact space](topology.md#sigma-compact-space). Some conventions restrict the term to locally compact groups or require a compact generating neighbourhood; those stronger conventions supply local compactness. Compact generation by an arbitrary compact subset alone does not.

### Compact generation does not imply local compactness

↑ **Parent:** [Compactly generated group](#compactly-generated-group)

The compact set $K=\{0\}\cup\{1/n!:n\geq1\}$ generates the additive group $\mathbb Q$, since every positive integer denominator divides some factorial. With its usual topology, $\mathbb Q$ is metrizable and is not locally compact. Nor can it have a nonzero [Haar measure](measure-theory.md#haar-measure) finite on compact sets: invariance gives equal masses to all singletons; a positive singleton mass makes $K$ have infinite mass, whereas zero singleton masses make the countable group have zero total mass.

## Locally compact group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_group)

A locally compact group is a [topological group](topological-group.md) whose underlying space is a [locally compact space](topology.md#locally-compact-space). In [Haar measure](measure-theory.md#haar-measure) and [Fourier transform](analysis.md#fourier-transform) theory one normally also assumes it is a [Hausdorff space](topology.md#hausdorff-space). Local compactness supplies compact neighbourhoods, not just compact sets which algebraically generate the group.

### Sigma-compact open subgroup of a locally compact group

↑ **Parent:** [Locally compact group](#locally-compact-group)

A compact identity neighbourhood $K$ produces a symmetric compact neighbourhood $S=K\cup K^{-1}\cup\{e\}$. The subgroup $\bigcup_{n\geq1}S^n$ is open because it contains an identity neighbourhood, and is sigma-compact because each finite product $S^n$ is compact. This construction does not require the original group itself to be sigma-compact.

## Neighbourhood criterion for a topological group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

A topology on a [group](group.md) makes it a [topological group](topological-group.md) precisely when its [neighbourhood bases](topology.md#neighbourhood-basis) satisfy two local tests: every neighbourhood $U$ of $xy$ contains $VW$ for some neighbourhoods $V$ of $x$ and $W$ of $y$, and every neighbourhood of $x^{-1}$ contains $V^{-1}$ for some neighbourhood $V$ of $x$. These are exactly continuity of multiplication in the [product topology](geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology) and continuity of inversion. Equivalently, bases can be chosen as left translates of a basis at the identity, with arbitrarily small squares, inverses and conjugates inside each prescribed identity neighbourhood.

## Discrete subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

A subgroup $G$ of a [topological group](topological-group.md) is discrete when the identity has a neighbourhood containing no other element of $G$. Translation then isolates every group element. In a Hausdorff [topological group](topological-group.md), such a subgroup is closed: any accumulation at a point would make differences of distinct nearby group elements approach the identity. Consequently its intersection with a [compact set](topology.md#compact-space) is finite. For isometries of [hyperbolic space](geometry-and-topology.md#hyperbolic-space), this converts compactness of the set of isometries moving one point a bounded distance into a [properly discontinuous group action](geometric-group-theory.md#properly-discontinuous-group-action).

### Discrete additive subgroup of a real vector space

↑ **Parent:** [Discrete subgroup](#discrete-subgroup)

A discrete additive [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) of $\mathbb R^n$ is generated by real-linearly independent vectors. Indeed, isolation of zero gives uniform separation, so compact sets meet the [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) finitely. Choose a real basis from the [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) for its real span. Its [integer](number-theory.md#integer) span has finite index, since reduction into a compact fundamental parallelepiped gives finitely many [coset](group-theory.md#coset) representatives. The [structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups](group.md#fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups) then gives a free [integer](number-theory.md#integer) basis, whose size is the dimension of the real span. Thus the [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) is a [Euclidean lattice](fourier-analysis.md#euclidean-lattice) in its span, although it need not have full rank in the ambient space.

### Planar discrete-subgroup basis

↑ **Parent:** [Discrete subgroup](#discrete-subgroup)

A nonzero discrete additive subgroup of $\mathbb R^2$ has a basis of one or two real-linearly independent vectors. Choose a shortest nonzero vector $w_1$. If the subgroup is not contained in its line, reduce parallel components modulo $w_1$ and choose a vector $w_2$ of least positive perpendicular height. Such a height exists: heights tending to zero would give infinitely many reduced points in a bounded region, contrary to discreteness. Reducing any group vector modulo $w_2$ leaves height smaller than this minimum, hence height zero, and reducing along $w_1$ then gives an integer multiple of $w_1$.

### Kleinian group

↑ **Parent:** [Discrete subgroup](#discrete-subgroup)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleinian_group)

A discrete subgroup of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$, acting by [Möbius transformations](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) on the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere) or orientation-preserving isometries of [hyperbolic space](geometry-and-topology.md#hyperbolic-space) $\mathbb H^3$. A group may be Kleinian even when some elements fix points; freeness and absence of torsion are additional conditions.

#### Gaussian Bianchi group

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

The Gaussian Bianchi group is the [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group) of determinant-one two-by-two matrices over the [Gaussian integers](commutative-algebra.md#gaussian-integer), modulo $\{\pm I\}$. Its matrix entries lie in a discrete subring of $\mathbb C$, so it is a discrete subgroup of $PSL_2(\mathbb C)$. The [Borromean link group](knot-theory.md#borromean-link-group) is a torsion-free subgroup of index 24.

<h4 id="jorgensen-s-inequality">Jørgensen's inequality</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgensen's_inequality)

For a non-elementary [discrete subgroup](#discrete-subgroup) generated by $A,B\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$, the displayed inequality is a necessary trace constraint. Failure of it proves nondiscreteness when non-elementarity has independently been checked. It is not a sufficient condition for discreteness and must not be applied to an elementary pair without its hypothesis.

#### Schottky group

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottky_group)

A [Schottky group](#schottky-group) is a free, discrete group of [Möbius transformations](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) constructed by pairing disjoint Jordan curves on the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere), taking the exterior of one bounded disk to the interior of its partner. For round circles it is classical. Strictly separated defining disks give a [Cantor set](geometry-and-topology.md#cantor-set) as the [limit set of a Kleinian group](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group). The tangent-disk extension permits parabolic elements and is often called a noded or T-Schottky group; its [Kleinian limit set](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group) need not be a [Cantor set](geometry-and-topology.md#cantor-set).

##### Tangent-disk Schottky group

↑ **Parent:** [Schottky group](#schottky-group)

A [Tangent-disk Schottky group](#tangent-disk-schottky-group) uses side-pairing disks with disjoint interiors but permits boundaries to meet. This convention must be distinguished from the strictly separated, purely loxodromic [Schottky group](#schottky-group) convention. For example, the maps $A(z)=z+2$ and $B(z)=z/(iz+1)$ pair the half-plane disks $\Re z<-1,\Re z>1$ and the disks $|z-i|<1,|z+i|<1$. The [ping-pong lemma](geometric-group-theory.md#ping-pong-lemma) gives a free discrete group. With $C=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}$, the subgroup generated by $B,C$ is conjugate by $M(z)=iz/(2-z)$ to the principal level-two modular group, generated by $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Thus its [Kleinian limit set](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group) contains the circle $|z-1|=1$ and its translate $|z-3|=1$. Its [Hausdorff dimension](measure-theory.md#hausdorff-dimension) is at least one and it cannot be [homeomorphic](topology.md#local-homeomorphism) to a single circle.

<h4 id="rank-one-convergence-of-divergent-mobius-transformations">Rank-one convergence of divergent Möbius transformations</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

A divergent sequence of [Möbius transformations](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) has a [subsequence](real-analysis.md#subsequence) converging uniformly on compact subsets of the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere) outside one exceptional point to a constant. Represent the transformations by determinant-one $2\times2$ [matrices](vector-space.md#matrix) and divide each by its matrix norm. A convergent subsequence has a nonzero rank-one limit: its determinant tends to zero because the original norms tend to infinity. The kernel line is the exceptional point, and the image line is the constant limit. This converts the [limit set of a Kleinian group](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group) into a tractable boundary dynamics problem.

#### Elliptic stabilizer obstruction to a hyperbolic manifold quotient

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

A nontrivial finite-order [elliptic Möbius transformation](geometry-and-topology.md#elliptic-element-of-psl2-r) fixes an axis in [hyperbolic space](geometry-and-topology.md#hyperbolic-space). The quotient metric has cone angle $2\pi/m$ along that axis for a stabilizer of order $m$, so it is locally an [orbifold](geometry-and-topology.md#orbifold) metric rather than a smooth hyperbolic-manifold metric. The underlying topological quotient may nevertheless be a manifold.

#### Non-elementary Kleinian group

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

A [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group) whose [Kleinian limit set](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group) contains at least three points. A discrete group fixing a common boundary point has at most two limit points and is therefore elementary.

#### Limit set of a Kleinian group

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

For a discrete isometry group of [hyperbolic space](geometry-and-topology.md#hyperbolic-space), its limit set is the boundary accumulation set of one interior orbit. All interior base points give the same set: corresponding orbit points stay a fixed hyperbolic distance apart, and bounded-distance sequences approaching the boundary have the same endpoint. This definition does not require finite generation.

##### Minimality of the Kleinian limit set

↑ **Parent:** [Limit set of a Kleinian group](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group)

Every nonempty closed boundary subset invariant under a [non-elementary Kleinian group](#non-elementary-kleinian-group) contains the [Kleinian limit set](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group). Such a subset cannot be a singleton. Its hyperbolic convex hull contains an interior point, whose orbit lies in the hull; in the Klein ball model the boundary of this hull is contained in the original closed boundary subset.

###### Limit set of an infinite normal Kleinian subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Minimality of the Kleinian limit set](#minimality-of-the-kleinian-limit-set)

The [Kleinian limit set](#limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group) of an infinite subgroup is nonempty. If it is normal, its limit set is invariant under the ambient group by base-point independence and conjugation. [Minimality of the Kleinian limit set](#minimality-of-the-kleinian-limit-set) gives one inclusion, and subgroup containment gives the other. Neither properness nor finite generation of the subgroup is needed.

#### Gaussian-integer parabolic construction

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

For $n\in\mathbb Z[i]$ and a nonzero integer $r$, the displayed determinant-one matrix has trace two and represents a nonidentity [parabolic Möbius transformation](geometry-and-topology.md#parabolic-element-of-psl2-r) fixing $n/r$. Its fixed-point equation is $(rz-n)^2=0$. These parabolic fixed points are dense in the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere), proving that the Gaussian-integer determinant-one [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group) has full limit set.

#### A free Kleinian group generated by two parabolic transformations

↑ **Parent:** [Kleinian group](#kleinian-group)

The integer matrices $A=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix})$ and $B=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{smallmatrix})$ generate a discrete subgroup. For every nonzero integer $m$, $A^m$ sends $|x|<1$ into $|x|>1$, while $B^m$ sends $|x|>1$, including infinity, into $|x|<1$. The [ping-pong lemma](geometric-group-theory.md#ping-pong-lemma) identifies their subgroup as the [free product](algebraic-topology.md#free-product) of two infinite cyclic groups. This separates the proof of discreteness from the proof of freeness.

## Compact group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_group)

A [compact group](#compact-group) is a [topological group](topological-group.md) whose underlying [topological space](topology.md#topological-space) is compact and Hausdorff.

### Compact groups with a descending chain condition are Lie groups

↑ **Parent:** [Compact group](#compact-group)

The [Peter-Weyl theorem](representation-theory.md#peter-weyl-theorem) makes finite-dimensional [unitary representations](representation-theory.md#unitary-representation) separate elements of a [compact group](#compact-group). Intersecting their closed kernels gives the identity [subgroup](group.md#subgroup). If the [descending chain condition](algebra.md#descending-chain-condition) holds for closed [subgroups](group.md#subgroup), choose finitely many representations whose kernel intersection is already trivial: otherwise repeatedly exclude a surviving nonidentity element to obtain an infinite strict chain. Their direct sum is a [faithful representation](representation-theory.md#faithful-representation) into a finite-dimensional [unitary group](#unitary-group). Compactness makes its image closed, and the [closed-subgroup theorem](lie-theory.md#closed-subgroup-theorem) gives the original [group](group.md) a compatible [Lie group](lie-theory.md#lie-group) structure.

### Closed subsemigroup of a compact group

↑ **Parent:** [Compact group](#compact-group)

A nonempty closed [subsemigroup](algebra.md#subsemigroup) $S$ of a [compact group](#compact-group) is a subgroup. For $x\in S$ and a neighbourhood $U$ of the identity, choose $V$ with $V^{-1}V\subseteq U$ and cover the compact group by finitely many translates of $V$. Two of sufficiently many powers $1,x,x^2,\ldots$ lie in the same translate, so some positive power of $x$ belongs to $U$. Hence the identity is a limit of positive powers and lies in $S$. The preceding powers converge to $x^{-1}$, which also lies in $S$. Applied to the closure of a positive-word monoid, this proves that it equals the closed subgroup generated by its generators, without assuming they already include their inverses.

## Profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_group)

A profinite group is an [inverse limit](module-theory.md#inverse-limit) of finite discrete groups. Equivalently, it is a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected [topological group](topological-group.md).

### Just-infinite profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

An infinite [profinite group](#profinite-group) is just infinite if every nontrivial closed [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) has finite index, equivalently is open. Requiring closedness is part of the topological convention. This property says that every proper [Hausdorff](topology.md#hausdorff-space) quotient is finite.

#### Hereditarily just-infinite profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Just-infinite profinite group](#just-infinite-profinite-group)

An infinite [profinite group](#profinite-group) is [hereditarily just infinite](#hereditarily-just-infinite-profinite-group) if every [open subgroup](#open-subgroup) is a [just-infinite profinite group](#just-infinite-profinite-group). To prove this, it suffices to show that every nonidentity element of every [open subgroup](#open-subgroup) has open closed [normal closure](group-theory.md#normal-closure): any nontrivial closed [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) then contains that [normal closure](group-theory.md#normal-closure). The [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group) is an example. In odd characteristic its depth-commutator formula constructs nonzero elements of each sufficiently high layer inside any such [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup), and successive coefficient correction fills the entire tail.

### Rank of a profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

The rank of a [profinite group](#profinite-group) is $\operatorname{rk}(G)=\sup_{K\le_cG}d(K)$, where $K$ runs through closed [subgroups](group.md#subgroup) and $d(K)$ is the least number of topological generators. If an [open subgroup](#open-subgroup) $H$ has rank $r$, its [normal core](group-theory.md#core-group-theory) $C$ is open and has rank at most $r$. Every closed $K\le G$ has a [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) $K\cap C$ generated by at most $r$ elements, while $K/(K\cap C)$ has order at most $[G:C]$. A [finite group](group.md#finite-group) of order at most $M$ is generated by at most $\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$ elements, since each necessary new generator at least doubles the generated [subgroup](group.md#subgroup). Lifting generators yields $d(K)\le r+\lfloor\log_2[G:C]\rfloor$. Thus finite rank is preserved in both directions under passage to an [open subgroup](#open-subgroup).

### Procyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

A [profinite group](#profinite-group) $G$ is procyclic if every quotient by an [open normal subgroup](#open-normal-subgroup) is cyclic. Equivalently $G=\overline{\langle g\rangle}$ for one element $g$. For the forward implication, the sets of lifts of generators of $G/N$ are nonempty [clopen](topology.md#clopen-set) sets. Any finite intersection contains a lift of a generator of the cyclic quotient by the intersection of those [open normal subgroups](#open-normal-subgroup). [Compactness](topology.md#compact-space) supplies one element generating every finite quotient, and hence generating densely. The reverse implication follows by projecting that generator. For a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group), two distinct maximal [open subgroups](#open-subgroup) would give a quotient $C_p^2$, so a nontrivial [procyclic group](#procyclic-group) has a unique maximal [open subgroup](#open-subgroup) $M$. Its [profinite Frattini subgroup](#profinite-frattini-subgroup) is $M$, and any $g\notin M$ generates topologically: otherwise the proper [closed subgroup](#closed-subgroup) it generates lies in a maximal [open subgroup](#open-subgroup). The additive [group](group.md) of [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer) is an infinite example, generated topologically by $1$.

### Open normal subgroup basis of a profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

Every identity neighbourhood of a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected [topological group](topological-group.md) contains an open [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup). Choose a [clopen set](topology.md#clopen-set) $U$ containing the identity inside that neighbourhood. Compactness and continuity give a symmetric identity neighbourhood $V$ with $VU\subseteq U$, hence $VU=U$. The setwise stabilizer $H=\{g:gU=U\}$ is an open [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) contained in $U$. Its index is finite, so its [normal core](group-theory.md#core-group-theory) is an open [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) contained in $U$. These subgroups recover the [profinite group](#profinite-group) as the [inverse limit](module-theory.md#inverse-limit) of its finite quotients.

### Profinite Frattini subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

The intersection of the maximal proper open [subgroups](group.md#subgroup) of a [profinite group](#profinite-group). A subset topologically generates the [profinite group](#profinite-group) exactly when its image topologically generates its quotient by this intersection: a proper closed generated [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) is contained in a maximal open [subgroup](group.md#subgroup), obtained from a suitable finite quotient. For a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group), $\Phi(G)=\overline{G^p[G,G]}$, where the bar denotes [closure](topology.md#closure-topology) of the algebraically generated power and [commutator subgroups](group-theory.md#commutator-subgroup). Closedness cannot simply be discarded in infinite rank.

### Profinite abelian group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

A profinite abelian group is an inverse limit of finite [abelian groups](group.md#abelian-group). Its topology is compact and totally disconnected. An abelian [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) acquires scalar multiplication by [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer) by taking limits of integer multiples.

### Pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

A pro-$p$ group is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups. Every continuous action on a finite set or finite module factors through a finite $p$-group. In particular its action on $\mu_p$ is trivial when the field characteristic differs from $p$, because $\operatorname{Aut}(\mu_p)$ has order $p-1$.

#### Pro-p subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

A closed [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) of a [profinite group](#profinite-group) that is a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) with the induced topology. Its images in finite continuous quotients are [p-groups](finite-group-theory.md#p-group).

#### R-analytic pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

For a [pro-p ring](commutative-algebra.md#pro-p-ring) $R$, an $R$-analytic [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) is a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) equipped with an analytic manifold structure locally modelled on open subsets of a finite free $R$-module, such that multiplication and inversion are given locally by convergent [power series](real-analysis.md#power-series). The number of local coordinates is its analytic [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space). The [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group) admits no such structure over any pro-$p$ ring: its hereditary just infiniteness, unbounded finite cyclic torsion and sub-base-three derived-series growth exclude both alternatives in the analytic just-infinite structure theorem.

#### Nottingham group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_group)

Over $k=\mathbb F_p$, the [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group) is $\mathcal N(k)=\{f(t)=t+\sum_{i\ge2}a_it^i:a_i\in k\}$ with composition and the $t$-adic [topology](topology.md). Equivalently it is the [group](group.md) of continuous $k$-automorphisms of the [Laurent series field](arithmetic.md#laurent-series-field) $k((t))$ that act trivially on $(t)/(t^2)$. With ordinary series composition, the [isomorphism](algebra.md#isomorphism) sends $f$ to the automorphism $h(t)\mapsto h(f^{-1}(t))$; this inverse avoids reversing the [group](group.md) product. It is a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group), recovered from its finite truncations, and contains elements of arbitrarily large finite $p$-power order.

##### Finite p-group embedding in the Nottingham group

↑ **Parent:** [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group)

Realize a [finite p-group](finite-group-theory.md#finite-p-group) $P$ as the [Galois group](galois-theory.md#galois-group) of a [totally ramified extension](arithmetic.md#totally-ramified-extension) $E/\mathbb F_p((t))$, using [Artin-Schreier embedding problems over a characteristic-p field](galois-theory.md#artin-schreier-embedding-problems-over-a-characteristic-p-field). A [uniformizer](commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer) $u$ of $E$ gives $E\cong\mathbb F_p((u))$: every element has a unique convergent Laurent expansion with coefficients in the [residue field](commutative-algebra.md#residue-field). An automorphism has $u\mapsto a_1u+a_2u^2+\cdots$, and $a_1$ defines a [homomorphism](algebra.md#homomorphism) $P\to\mathbb F_p^\times$. Its image is trivial because $|P|$ is a $p$-power and $p\nmid|\mathbb F_p^\times|$. Thus the faithful Galois action lies in the [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group), embedding $P$.

##### Depth filtration of the Nottingham group

↑ **Parent:** [Nottingham group](#nottingham-group)

Put $\mathcal N_m=\{f:f(t)\equiv t\pmod{t^{m+1}}\}$ for $m\ge1$. These are [open normal subgroups](#open-normal-subgroup) and $\mathcal N_m/\mathcal N_{m+1}\cong(\mathbb F_p,+)$ via the coefficient of $t^{m+1}$. Thus $[\mathcal N:\mathcal N_m]=p^{m-1}$. The depth of a nonidentity series is $\operatorname{ord}_t(f(t)-t)-1$. If $f=t+at^{r+1}+\cdots$ and $g=t+bt^{s+1}+\cdots$, expansion of substitution gives

$$
[f,g](t)=t+ab(r-s)t^{r+s+1}+O(t^{r+s+2}).
$$

Here $[f,g]=f^{-1}\circ g^{-1}\circ f\circ g$. This formula controls [commutator subgroups](group-theory.md#commutator-subgroup) and [normal subgroups](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup).

###### Derived-series growth of the Nottingham group

↑ **Parent:** [Depth filtration of the Nottingham group](#depth-filtration-of-the-nottingham-group)

For odd $p$, [group commutator](group.md#group-commutator) collection in the depth filtration gives $[\mathcal N_m,\mathcal N_m]=\mathcal N_{2m+1}$. Every degree $k\ge2m+1$ occurs as a leading [group commutator](group.md#group-commutator) degree by choosing $r,s\ge m$, $r+s=k$, and $r\not\equiv s\pmod p$; changing $r$ by one changes $r-s$ by two. Closed successive coefficient correction then fills the tail. Therefore $\mathcal N^{(n)}=\mathcal N_{2^{n+1}-1}$ and $\log_p[\mathcal N:\mathcal N^{(n)}]=2^{n+1}-2$. In characteristic two the sharper collection theorem gives $\log_2[\mathcal N:\mathcal N^{(n)}]\le C\kappa^n$ for a constant $C$, with $\kappa=(1+\sqrt{17})/2<3$. In both cases the exponential base is strictly smaller than three.

#### Golod-Shafarevich infinitude criterion for pro-p groups

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

If a pro-$p$ presentation on $d$ generators and possibly infinitely many relators $r_i$ satisfies

$$
1-d\tau+\sum_i\tau^{D(r_i)}<0\qquad(0<\tau<1),
$$

with the sum convergent and $D$ the [Magnus degree of a free pro-p group element](#magnus-degree-of-a-free-pro-p-group-element), then the presented [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) is infinite. The Golod-Shafarevich inequality forces its completed group algebra to be infinite-dimensional, which would be impossible for a finite group. This formulation applies to relators whose degrees tend to infinity and is suitable for enumerated power-relation constructions.

#### Powerful pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

A [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) whose closed [commutator subgroup](group-theory.md#commutator-subgroup) lies in its closed power subgroup $G^{p^\epsilon}$, with $\epsilon=1$ for odd $p$ and $\epsilon=2$ for $p=2$. This is the profinite analogue of a [powerful p-group](finite-group-theory.md#powerful-p-group). Finitely generated torsion-free examples are exactly the [uniform pro-p groups](#uniform-pro-p-group). The additive [group](group.md) of a finite product of [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer) is an abelian example.

#### Pro-p completion

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

The [inverse limit](module-theory.md#inverse-limit) of the quotients of an abstract [group](group.md) by its [normal subgroups](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) of finite $p$-power index. Maps to [pro-p groups](#pro-p-group) factor uniquely through the canonical dense image in this completion. Unlike [profinite completion](#profinite-completion), only [finite p-groups](finite-group-theory.md#finite-p-group) are used.

#### Free pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

For finite $X$, this is the [pro-p completion](#pro-p-completion) of the [free group](geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) on $X$. Every function from $X$ to a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) extends uniquely to a continuous [group homomorphism](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism). More generally one can require a basis tending to the identity and impose the universal property for maps with that convergence condition; alternatively the pro-$p$ completion of the abstract free group represents unrestricted maps from an arbitrary set. These infinite-rank conventions should not be conflated when interpreting generator cardinalities.

##### Magnus degree of a free pro-p group element

↑ **Parent:** [Free pro-p group](#free-pro-p-group)

Embed a [free pro-p group](#free-pro-p-group) on $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ into the units of the noncommutative power-series algebra $\mathbb F_p\langle\!\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle\!\rangle$ by $x_i\mapsto1+X_i$. Its Magnus degree is the lowest total degree occurring in $\iota(w)-1$, with $D(1)=\infty$. Since $(1+v)^{p^e}=1+v^{p^e}$ in characteristic $p$, one has $D(w^{p^e})\ge p^eD(w)$. High powers therefore impose relations with small weight at any fixed parameter $0<\tau<1$.

##### Finite pro-p presentation

↑ **Parent:** [Free pro-p group](#free-pro-p-group)

A finite generating set and finitely many relators in a [free pro-p group](#free-pro-p-group), divided out by their closed [normal closure](group-theory.md#normal-closure). Relators are elements of the [free pro-p group](#free-pro-p-group); they need not be finite ordinary words. A [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) with such a presentation is finitely presented in the pro-$p$ category.

###### Finite extensions preserve finite pro-p presentations

↑ **Parent:** [Finite pro-p presentation](#finite-pro-p-presentation)

Suppose $K$ is an open [normal subgroup](group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) of a [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) $G$ and has a [finite pro-p presentation](#finite-pro-p-presentation). Choose lifts of generators of the finite quotient $G/K$. Add finitely many relators giving their conjugation and inverse conjugation on the generators of $K$, and replace each quotient relator by its value in $K$. The resulting finite [finite pro-p presentation](#finite-pro-p-presentation) maps onto $G$. Its closed subgroup generated by the kernel generators is isomorphic to $K$, and its quotient by that subgroup is exactly $G/K$, so the map is also injective.

#### Generator number and first cohomology of a finitely generated pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

For a finitely generated [pro-p group](#pro-p-group), its [profinite Frattini subgroup](#profinite-frattini-subgroup) has finite [elementary abelian](group.md#elementary-abelian-group) quotient. Continuous [group homomorphisms](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) to $\mathbb F_p$ are precisely the linear functionals on this quotient. The topological generation criterion identifies a minimal generating set with a basis, giving the displayed equality. Finite generation is essential when generator number means the least cardinality of an unrestricted dense generating family.

##### Infinite-rank failure of the first-cohomology generator formula

↑ **Parent:** [Generator number and first cohomology of a finitely generated pro-p group](#generator-number-and-first-cohomology-of-a-finitely-generated-pro-p-group)

Let $I$ be the set of infinite binary sequences and $G=\mathbb F_p^I$ with its [product topology](geometry-and-topology.md#product-topology). Index the finite binary strings by the positive integers. For each $n$, take the element whose $r$ coordinate is one exactly when the $n$th string is a prefix of $r$. These countably many elements generate a dense [subgroup](group.md#subgroup): given finitely many distinct sequences, sufficiently long prefixes distinguish them individually. No finite family generates $G$, so its topological generator number is countable. Every continuous linear functional depends on finitely many coordinates, however, so $H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$ has a basis of size $|I|=2^{\aleph_0}$. Thus the arbitrary-rank identity is false for unrestricted topological generating sets.

#### Uniform pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

A finitely generated [powerful p-group](finite-group-theory.md#powerful-p-group) in the category of [pro-p groups](#pro-p-group) is uniform if all successive indices in its [lower p-series](finite-group-theory.md#lower-p-series) are equal. Equivalently it is torsion-free. If its generator number is $d$, the successive indices are $p^d$, the power map carries $G^{p^n}$ bijectively onto $G^{p^{n+1}}$, and an ordered minimal generating set gives unique coordinates $g_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots g_d^{\lambda_d}$ with $\lambda_i$ in the [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer). For $p=2$, powerfulness means $[G,G]\leq G^4$.

##### Odd-prime principal p-adic congruence subgroup is uniform

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

For an odd prime $p$, the [matrix logarithm](vector-space.md#matrix-logarithm) and [matrix exponential](linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) are inverse maps between $1+pM_d(\mathbb Z_p)$ and $pM_d(\mathbb Z_p)$. They imply that the $p$th-power map carries $U_m$ bijectively onto $U_{m+1}$. Direct multiplication gives $[U_i,U_j]\subseteq U_{i+j}$, so the [group](group.md) is a [powerful pro-p group](#powerful-pro-p-group). The quotient $U_m/U_{m+1}$ is the additive [matrix](vector-space.md#matrix) space over $\mathbb F_p$, with constant index $p^{d^2}$. The $d^2$ [matrices](vector-space.md#matrix) $1+pE_{ij}$ topologically generate, by successive approximation through these quotients. The [lower p-series](finite-group-theory.md#lower-p-series) is exactly $U_m$, so the [group](group.md) is a [uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group) and a [torsion-free group](group.md#torsion-free-group). At $p=2$ the first congruence subgroup contains $-I$ and is not uniform.

##### Root correction criterion for uniformity

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

For odd $p$ and a finitely generated [powerful pro-p group](#powerful-pro-p-group), put $U_j=G^{p^j}$. The displayed [group homomorphisms](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) are surjective. If one has a nonzero [kernel](linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map), choose $x\in U_j\setminus U_{j+1}$ with $x^p\in U_{j+2}$. Successive corrections $x\mapsto xu_r$, with $u_r\in U_r$, cancel its $p$-th power one layer at a time, using $(xu_r)^p\equiv x^pu_r^p\pmod {U_{r+2}}$. [Compactness](topology.md#compact-space) and the separated power filtration give a nonidentity limit of order $p$. Thus a [torsion-free group](group.md#torsion-free-group) has constant layer indices and is a [uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group). Conversely constant indices make every displayed map injective; comparison at the first differing power layer proves that $x\mapsto x^p$ is injective, so there is no nontrivial torsion.

##### Linearity of a uniform pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

Every [uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group) embeds continuously in $\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z_p)$ for some finite $n$. Apply the [Ado theorem](lie-algebra.md#ado-s-theorem) to $\mathbb Q_p\otimes L_G$ and choose a deep power lattice whose representing [matrices](vector-space.md#matrix) lie in $p\operatorname{End}(\Lambda)$, or $4\operatorname{End}(\Lambda)$ at $p=2$. The [matrix exponential](linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) and [logarithm](calculus.md#logarithm) give a [faithful representation](representation-theory.md#faithful-representation) of the corresponding open normal power [subgroup](group.md#subgroup). Induce that representation to the whole [group](group.md) using its finitely many [cosets](group-theory.md#coset). An element outside the [open subgroup](#open-subgroup) permutes the [coset](group-theory.md#coset) blocks nontrivially; an element inside it is detected by the original representation. Thus the [induced representation](representation-theory.md#induced-representation) is faithful. All block [matrices](vector-space.md#matrix) preserve a finite free $\mathbb Z_p$-module, giving the integral embedding.

##### Power congruences in a uniform pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

For a [uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group) with $U_i=G^{p^i}$, the map $x\mapsto x^{p^a}$ transports congruences by

$$
x\equiv y\pmod{U_b}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{p^a}\equiv y^{p^a}\pmod{U_{a+b}}.
$$

[Commutator](lie-algebra.md#commutator) collection gives $(yu)^p\equiv y^pu^p\pmod{U_{b+2}}$ for $u\in U_b$, and the power map is an [isomorphism](algebra.md#isomorphism) $U_b/U_{b+1}\to U_{b+1}/U_{b+2}$. Thus a nontrivial first difference moves down exactly one layer when taking a $p$th power. Iterating gives the equivalence; completeness supplies unique roots when the congruence is read in reverse.

##### Transported addition on a uniform pro-p group

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

For a [uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group), the power map $x\mapsto x^{p^n}$ is a [homeomorphism](topology.md#homeomorphism) onto $G^{p^n}$. Transport multiplication back to $G$ by

$$
x+_ny=(x^{p^n}y^{p^n})^{p^{-n}}.
$$

Unique roots make this an associative [group](group.md) operation. For odd $p$, [group commutator](group.md#group-commutator) collection gives $x+_ny\equiv y+_nx\pmod{G^{p^{n+1}}}$ and $x+_ny\equiv x+_{n-1}y\pmod{G^{p^n}}$. The operations converge to addition in the [Lie algebra of a uniform pro-p group](lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-of-a-uniform-pro-p-group).

##### Uniform pro-p group and powerful Lie lattice correspondence

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

The [Lie algebra of a uniform pro-p group](lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-of-a-uniform-pro-p-group) construction and [BCH convergence on a powerful Lie lattice](lie-algebra.md#bch-convergence-on-a-powerful-lie-lattice) are mutually inverse on objects and morphisms. On the group side morphisms are continuous [group homomorphisms](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism); on the lattice side they are linear [Lie algebra homomorphisms](lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-homomorphism) over the [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer). A group homomorphism respects powers, roots and limits, and a Lie homomorphism respects every convergent [Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula](linear-operator-theory.md#baker-campbell-hausdorff-formula) term.

##### Uniform group and powerful Lie lattice correspondence

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform pro-p group](#uniform-pro-p-group)

The [Lie algebra of a uniform pro-p group](lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-of-a-uniform-pro-p-group) construction and [BCH convergence on a powerful Lie lattice](lie-algebra.md#bch-convergence-on-a-powerful-lie-lattice) are mutually inverse on objects and morphisms. On the group side morphisms are continuous [group homomorphisms](group-theory.md#group-homomorphism); on the lattice side they are linear [Lie algebra homomorphisms](lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-homomorphism) over the [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer). A group homomorphism respects powers, roots and limits, and a Lie homomorphism respects every convergent [Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula](linear-operator-theory.md#baker-campbell-hausdorff-formula) term.

#### Strong completeness of finitely generated pro-p groups

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

Every abstract [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) of finite index in a finitely generated [pro-p group](#pro-p-group) is open. [Fixed-generator commutator collection in nilpotent groups](group-theory.md#fixed-generator-commutator-collection-in-nilpotent-groups) makes the abstract [commutator subgroup](group-theory.md#commutator-subgroup) closed. The resulting abelian quotient is a quotient of a finite power of the [p-adic integers](number-theory.md#p-adic-integer), so its subgroup of $p$th powers is closed and of finite index. Thus the algebraic $G^p[G,G]$ is open and every abstract index-$p$ [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) is open. An arbitrary finite quotient is a [finite p-group](finite-group-theory.md#finite-p-group); induction on its order, using a normal subgroup of index $p$ and the [Schreier lemma](geometric-group-theory.md#reidemeister-schreier-theorem), gives the assertion. The [normal core](group-theory.md#core-group-theory) reduces nonnormal finite-index [subgroups](group.md#subgroup) to this case.

#### Profinite Sylow subgroup

↑ **Parent:** [Pro-p group](#pro-p-group)

A Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup of a [profinite group](#profinite-group) is a closed pro-$p$ subgroup whose images are Sylow $p$-subgroups in every finite quotient. Such subgroups exist and are conjugate. Every open subgroup containing one has index prime to $p$. They allow prime-to-$p$ finite extensions to detect $p$-primary [Galois cohomology](galois-theory.md#galois-cohomology).

### Profinite topology

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

The profinite topology on a group takes the cosets of finite-index normal subgroups as a neighborhood basis. A profinite group carries the inverse-limit topology from its finite quotients.

#### Profinite topology on the integers is not compact

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite topology](#profinite-topology)

The [profinite topology](#profinite-topology) on $\mathbb Z$ has basis the [residue classes](number-theory.md#residue-class) $a+k\mathbb Z$, $k\geq1$. These classes are both open and closed, since their complements are finite unions of other [residue classes](number-theory.md#residue-class). Set $F_n=(4^n-1)/3+4^n\mathbb Z$ for $n\geq1$. Then $F_{n+1}\subseteq F_n$ and every $F_n$ is nonempty. An integer $x$ in their intersection would make $3x+1$ divisible by every $4^n$, forcing $3x+1=0$, impossible. Thus the open complements $\mathbb Z\setminus F_n$ form an increasing cover with no finite subcover. The [topology](topology.md) is [Hausdorff](topology.md#hausdorff-space) because distinct integers are distinct modulo a sufficiently large integer, but is not compact.

#### Inverse-limit topology

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite topology](#profinite-topology)

An inverse limit carries the coarsest topology making every coordinate [projection map](function.md#projection-map) continuous. It is the subspace topology inherited from the product of the component spaces.

### Profinite completion

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_completion)

The profinite completion of a group $G$ is the [inverse limit](module-theory.md#inverse-limit) of its finite quotient groups:

$$
\widehat G=\varprojlim_{N\triangleleft_fG}G/N.
$$

The canonical map $G\to\widehat G$ has dense image and is injective exactly when $G$ is [residually finite](group-theory.md#residually-finite-group).

#### Universal property of profinite completion

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite completion](#profinite-completion)

Every homomorphism from a group $G$ to a [profinite group](#profinite-group) $P$ extends uniquely to a continuous homomorphism $\widehat G\to P$. Construct the extension on every finite quotient of $P$ and invoke the [universal property of an inverse limit](module-theory.md#universal-property-of-an-inverse-limit); uniqueness follows from the density of $G$ in $\widehat G$.

### Topological generating set

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

A subset $S$ of a [topological group](topological-group.md) topologically generates it when the ordinary subgroup $\langle S\rangle$ is dense.

#### Topological generator

↑ **Parent:** [Topological generating set](#topological-generating-set)

An element of a [topological generating set](#topological-generating-set) of a [topological group](topological-group.md). A finite tuple consists of topological generators when the abstract [subgroup](group.md#subgroup) it generates is dense in the group; it need not equal that abstract subgroup.

#### Topologically finitely generated group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological generating set](#topological-generating-set)

A topological group is topologically finitely generated when it has a finite [topological generating set](#topological-generating-set).

#### Finite-quotient criterion for topological generation

↑ **Parent:** [Topological generating set](#topological-generating-set)

For a [profinite group](#profinite-group) $G=\varprojlim_jG_j$, a subset $S\subseteq G$ is a [topological generating set](#topological-generating-set) exactly when $\langle p_j(S)\rangle=p_j(G)$ in every finite quotient. Indeed, a subgroup of a profinite group is dense exactly when its image in every finite continuous quotient is surjective.

### Finite-quotient criterion for conjugacy in a profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

Elements $g,h$ of a profinite group $G=\varprojlim_jG_j$ are conjugate exactly when their images are conjugate in every $G_j$. For the nontrivial direction, the finite nonempty sets of conjugators in the $G_j$ form an inverse system, whose inverse limit supplies a conjugator in $G$.

### Topological Hopf property

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

A topological group has the topological Hopf property when every continuous surjective endomorphism is a topological automorphism.

#### Hopf property of a topologically finitely generated profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological Hopf property](#topological-hopf-property)

Every [topologically finitely generated group](#topologically-finitely-generated-group) that is profinite has the [topological Hopf property](#topological-hopf-property). There are only finitely many open subgroups of each given index, and inverse image under a surjective endomorphism permutes them. The endomorphism kernel consequently lies in every open normal subgroup and is trivial.

### Finite-quotient criterion for roots in a profinite group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](#profinite-group)

For $G=\varprojlim_jG_j$, the equation $x^m=g$ has a solution in $G$ exactly when it has a solution after projection to every $G_j$. The finite nonempty sets of roots form an inverse system, and a compatible family is a root in $G$.

## Fundamental group of a topological group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)

For loops based at the identity of a topological group, pointwise multiplication and concatenation obey the interchange law and have the same identity. The Eckmann-Hilton argument therefore makes the fundamental group abelian.

### Eckmann-Hilton argument

↑ **Parent:** [Fundamental group of a topological group](#fundamental-group-of-a-topological-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckmann–Hilton_argument)

If two unital operations on one set share their identity and satisfy $(a*b)\cdot(c*d)=(a\cdot c)*(b\cdot d)$, then the operations coincide and are commutative.

#### Homotopy-group addition in a topological group

↑ **Parent:** [Eckmann-Hilton argument](#eckmann-hilton-argument)

For $n\ge1$, pointwise multiplication of based cube maps into a [topological group](topological-group.md) induces the ordinary addition in its [homotopy group](algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) $\pi_n(G,e)$. Concatenation and pointwise multiplication share an identity and satisfy the interchange rule, so the [Eckmann-Hilton argument](#eckmann-hilton-argument) identifies the two operations. In particular, the $m$-th power map induces multiplication by $m$ on every [homotopy group](algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group), for all integers $m$.

## Unitary group

↑ **Parent:** [Topological group](topological-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_group)

The unitary group $U(n)$ consists of complex matrices preserving the standard Hermitian inner product.

### Unitary sphere quotient and stability range

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)

The last-column map has fibre the subgroup fixing the last standard unit vector. Unitary completion gives surjectivity, and [compactness](topology.md#compact-space) gives the quotient [homeomorphism](topology.md#homeomorphism). Projecting the other standard vectors onto the [orthogonal complement](hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-complement) of a nearby unit vector and applying Gram-Schmidt gives a local section, so the projection is a fiber bundle. The bundle $U(n)\to U(n+1)\to S^{2n+1}$ then gives $\pi_iU(n)\cong\pi_iU(n+1)$ for $i\le2n-1$ because both adjacent [sphere](geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) [homotopy groups](algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) vanish.

### Unitary group as a regular level set

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)

The smooth map $F(A)=A^*A$ from complex matrices, regarded as a real vector space, to Hermitian matrices has derivative $DF_A(K)=A^*K+K^*A$. At a unitary $A$, taking $K=AH/2$ gives any prescribed Hermitian $H$. Thus $I$ is a [regular value](differential-geometry.md#regular-value) and $F^{-1}(I)=U(n)$ is a [smooth manifold](differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold) of real dimension $n^2$.

### Restricted unitary group

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)

For an orthogonal projection $P$ on a complex [Hilbert space](hilbert-space.md), this is the group of unitary operators whose off-diagonal blocks relative to $PH\oplus(I-P)H$ are [Hilbert-Schmidt operators](compact-operator.md#hilbert-schmidt-operator). Its usual topology combines strong operator convergence with Hilbert-Schmidt convergence of $[P,u]$. It is the group implementable in the corresponding [polarized fermionic Fock space](quantum-mechanics.md#polarized-fermionic-fock-space).

### Projective unitary group

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_unitary_group)

The quotient identifies unitary operators differing by a scalar phase. A continuous [projective unitary representation](quantum-mechanics.md#projective-unitary-representation) takes values in this quotient, with the quotient of the strong operator topology. A local continuous section fixes the phase by requiring a chosen nonzero matrix coefficient to be positive real.

#### Circle projective representations lift to unitary representations

↑ **Parent:** [Projective unitary group](#projective-unitary-group)

The pullback of $U(H)\to PU(H)$ along a continuous circle homomorphism is a compact central extension of the circle. Its lifts commute: a scalar commutator is a continuous alternating bicharacter, which vanishes on a dense cyclic subgroup and hence everywhere. Average a rank-one positive operator over this compact extension. The resulting nonzero compact operator has a finite-dimensional invariant eigenspace, and commuting unitaries have a common eigenvector there. Its continuous character has central weight one; dividing the lifts by that character yields an honest continuous circle representation.

### Compact symplectic group

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)

The subgroup $\{U\in U(2n):UJU^T=J\}$, with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix}$, is the compact symplectic group, often denoted $Sp(n)$ or $USp(2n)$. It preserves both a positive Hermitian metric and a nondegenerate complex alternating form.

#### Logarithm charts for the compact symplectic group

↑ **Parent:** [Compact symplectic group](#compact-symplectic-group)

The [matrix logarithm](vector-space.md#matrix-logarithm) near the identity maps $Sp(n)=\{A\in U(2n):AJA^t=J\}$ onto an open set of its real [compact symplectic Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#compact-symplectic-lie-algebra) $\{X:X^*=-X,\ XJ+JX^t=0\}$. Its inverse is the [matrix exponential](linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential). These statements follow by applying the logarithm to $A^*=A^{-1}$ and $A^t=J^{-1}A^{-1}J$, and differentiating $e^{tX}Je^{tX^t}$. Left translations provide [manifold charts](differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) at every group element. In block form $X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P&Q\\-\bar Q&\bar P\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $P^*=-P$ and $Q^t=Q$, giving real dimension $n^2+n(n+1)=n(2n+1)$.

### Special unitary group

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_unitary_group)

The special unitary group is $SU(n)=\{U\in U(n):\det U=1\}$.

#### Logarithm charts for the special unitary group

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

Near the identity, the [matrix logarithm](vector-space.md#matrix-logarithm) of a unitary matrix is skew-Hermitian. Restrict the logarithm's operator norm below $\pi/n$; determinant one then forces its trace to be zero, since $\det U=\exp(\operatorname{tr}\log U)$ and no nonzero multiple of $2\pi i$ fits this bound. The [matrix exponential](linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) gives the inverse chart on a ball in the displayed real [Lie algebra](lie-algebra.md), of dimension $n^2-1$. Left translation supplies compatible charts on the whole [special unitary group](#special-unitary-group).

<h4 id="su-4-group">SU(4) group</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

The four-by-four unitary [matrices](vector-space.md#matrix) of [determinant](linear-algebra.md#determinant) one form this [special unitary group](#special-unitary-group). Its [two-index antisymmetric representation](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#two-index-antisymmetric-representation) has complex dimension six and the [real structure of the SU4 exterior square](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#real-structure-of-the-su4-exterior-square) realizes it on a six-dimensional real space.

#### Unit sphere orbit of the defining special unitary action

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

For $n\geq2$, the [special unitary group](#special-unitary-group) acts transitively on the complex unit sphere. Extend any unit vector to an orthonormal basis, then adjust the phase of a remaining column to give determinant one. The [stabilizer subgroup](group-theory.md#stabilizer-subgroup) of the first vector consists of special unitary transformations of its orthogonal complement. Thus the sphere is a homogeneous quotient, not just a set with a norm-preserving action.

<h4 id="su-3-group">SU(3) group</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

The [SU(3) group](#su-3-group) consists of unitary complex three-by-three matrices of determinant one. Its defining action on $\mathbb C^3$ preserves the Hermitian norm. It contains block-diagonal copies of [SU(2)](#su-2-group) and the real [SO(3) group](linear-algebra.md#so-3-group). Its [Lie algebra](lie-algebra.md) consists of traceless anti-Hermitian matrices.

<h5 id="triality-su-3">Triality (SU(3))</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [SU(3) group](#su-3-group)

The central element $\omega I$ of [SU(3)](#su-3-group), with $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, acts on an irreducible representation of [Dynkin labels](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) $(a,b)$ as $\omega^{a+2b}$. This residue is its triality. In a tensor realization the representation has $a+2b$ fundamental boxes, and a complete column of three is a singlet; removing such columns preserves the residue. Tensor products add trialities and complex conjugation reverses them.

<h5 id="mixed-su-3-tensor-representation">Mixed SU(3) tensor representation</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [SU(3) group](#su-3-group)

A mixed [tensor](linear-algebra.md#tensor) has $k$ defining indices and $l$ dual indices. The [SU(3)](#su-3-group) action applies $U$ to every upper index and $U^{-1}$ to every lower index. [Young symmetrizers](representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-symmetrizer) select permutation types, while invariant [Kronecker deltas](linear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta) take [traces](linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) and invariant alternating [tensors](linear-algebra.md#tensor) convert an antisymmetric pair of defining indices into a dual index, or conversely. Repeated fundamental and dual [tensor](linear-algebra.md#tensor) rules decompose it with multiplicities: $(p,q)\otimes(1,0)=(p+1,q)\oplus(p-1,q+1)\oplus(p,q-1)$ and the conjugate rule, omitting negative labels.

###### Explicit cubic SU3 tensor projections

↑ **Parent:** [Mixed SU(3) tensor representation](#mixed-su-3-tensor-representation)

For $T^{abc}$, split its first pair into symmetric and antisymmetric parts. The fully symmetric part $S^{abc}$ is a [symmetric cubic representation of SU(3)](#symmetric-cubic-representation-of-su-3). Write $M^{abc}=T^{(ab)c}-S^{abc}$ and $O^a{}_d=\epsilon_{dbc}M^{abc}$. Then $O$ is traceless and $M^{abc}=(\epsilon^{bcd}O^a{}_d+\epsilon^{acd}O^b{}_d)/3$. The antisymmetric part is $T^{[ab]c}=\epsilon^{abd}N^c{}_d$, with $N^c{}_d=\epsilon_{dab}T^{abc}/2$. Splitting $N$ into its traceless part and trace gives the second [adjoint representation of SU(3)](lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-su-3) and the singlet. These maps use [invariant tensors](representation-theory.md#invariant-tensor), so they commute with the group action and explicitly realize the four irreducible summands.

<h6 id="symmetric-traceless-su-3-tensor-representation">Symmetric traceless SU(3) tensor representation</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Mixed SU(3) tensor representation](#mixed-su-3-tensor-representation)

Symmetrize the $p$ upper and $q$ lower indices separately, and remove every upper-lower [trace](linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace). The resulting representation has [highest weight](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) $p\omega_1+q\omega_2$. Its [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is

$$
\binom{p+2}{2}\binom{q+2}{2}-\binom{p+1}{2}\binom{q+1}{2}
=\frac{(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)}2.
$$

The subtraction is valid because contraction is onto: in the polynomial realization its adjoint, for the monomial-factorial [inner product](linear-algebra.md#inner-product), is multiplication by the nonzero polynomial $\sum_ix_iy_i$, an injective map. The resulting kernel contains the highest-weight constituent and has precisely its [Weyl dimension formula](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-dimension-formula) [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space), hence is irreducible. This highest-weight constituent is not always the largest-dimensional constituent of the full mixed [tensor](linear-algebra.md#tensor).

<h6 id="symmetric-cubic-representation-of-su-3">Symmetric cubic representation of SU(3)</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric traceless SU(3) tensor representation](#symmetric-traceless-su-3-tensor-representation)

The degree-three monomials $z_1^az_2^bz_3^c$, with $a+b+c=3$, form a basis of this ten-dimensional [highest-weight representation](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-representation). Its highest monomial is $z_1^3$, and $E_{21}=z_2\partial_{z_1}$ and $E_{32}=z_3\partial_{z_2}$ generate every basis monomial. Its [Dynkin labels](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#dynkin-label) are $(3,0)$, and the weight of a monomial is $(a-b,b-c)$. In coordinates $(h_1,(h_1+2h_2)/\sqrt3)$, the ten weights form four horizontal rows of lengths $4,3,2,1$, all with [weight multiplicity](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-multiplicity) one. The [baryon decuplet](standard-model.md#baryon-decuplet) realizes this representation of approximate [flavor symmetry](standard-model.md#flavor-symmetry).

<h6 id="maximal-dimension-in-mixed-su-3-tensor-powers">Maximal dimension in mixed SU(3) tensor powers</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric traceless SU(3) tensor representation](#symmetric-traceless-su-3-tensor-representation)

For $d(p,q)=(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2$, the exact largest constituent [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) of a [mixed SU(3) tensor representation](#mixed-su-3-tensor-representation) is

$$
D_{\max}(k,l)=\max\left\{\max_{0\le a\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor}d(k-2a,l+a),\ \max_{0\le b\le\lfloor l/2\rfloor}d(k+b,l-2b)\right\}.
$$

Every constituent [highest weight](semisimple-lie-algebra.md#highest-weight-of-a-representation) lies below $k\omega_1+l\omega_2$, so its labels have the form $(p,q)=(k-2a+b,l+a-2b)$ for nonnegative integers $a,b$. If both are positive, reducing each by their minimum increases both $p,q$ and strictly increases the [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space). The resulting boundary labels occur: antisymmetrize disjoint upper pairs into lower indices, or lower pairs into upper indices, then take the symmetric traceless component. This proves the maximum formula. For $(k,l)=(5,0)$, the fully symmetric [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is 21 but $(3,1)$ has [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) 24. For $k=l$, the fully symmetric traceless [dimension](vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $(k+1)^3$ is the maximum.

<h4 id="su-2-group">SU(2) group</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

The SU(2) group is the group of two-by-two unitary matrices of determinant one. It is a compact three-dimensional [Lie group](lie-theory.md#lie-group) whose underlying manifold is a three-sphere.

<h5 id="su-2-matrix">SU(2) matrix</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [SU(2) group](#su-2-group)

An SU(2) matrix is a two-by-two complex [unitary matrix](linear-operator-theory.md#unitary-matrix) with [determinant](linear-algebra.md#determinant) one. It has the form $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-\bar b&\bar a\end{pmatrix}$ with $|a|^2+|b|^2=1$. Its action on the [Riemann sphere](complex-analysis.md#riemann-sphere) is a rotational [Möbius transformation](group-theory.md#mobius-transformation); opposite matrices induce the same sphere rotation.

<h4 id="su-2-as-the-three-sphere">SU(2) as the three-sphere</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Special unitary group](#special-unitary-group)

Every element of $SU(2)$ has the unique form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}z_1&-\overline z_2\\z_2&\overline z_1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad |z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1.
$$

Thus $SU(2)$ is diffeomorphic to the unit three-sphere in $\mathbb C^2$ and is a [parallelizable manifold](differential-geometry.md#parallelizable-manifold).

### Determinant detects the order of a unitary loop

↑ **Parent:** [Unitary group](#unitary-group)

The determinant $U(n)\to S^1$ sends a unitary loop to a circle-valued loop. A nonzero determinant winding number proves that the original loop class has infinite order.
