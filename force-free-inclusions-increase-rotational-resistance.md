# Force-free inclusions increase rotational resistance

↑ **Parent:** [Extra dissipation due to a rigid inclusion](extra-dissipation-due-to-a-rigid-inclusion.md)

At fixed sphere rotation in an annular [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md), add rigid particles with zero net [force](force.md) and [torque](torque.md). Extend their actual rigid velocities through their interiors. The extension is an admissible incompressible competitor in the particle-free annulus, with zero interior [rate-of-strain tensor](strain-rate-tensor.md). The [Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow](minimum-dissipation-theorem-for-stokes-flow.md) gives $D\ge D_0$. The boundary-work identity gives $D=\boldsymbol\Omega\cdot\mathbf G$, because the stationary outer wall and the freely moving particles contribute no power. Equality would require the extension to equal the unique particle-free flow, whose strain cannot vanish on a finite particle volume when $\Omega\ne0$. Thus the resistance component along the imposed rotation strictly increases.
