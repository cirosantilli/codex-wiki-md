# A p-value does not determine the type II error probability

↑ **Parent:** [Power function of a statistical test](power-function-of-a-statistical-test.md)

Consider a two-sided test rejecting when $|Z|>c$, with $Z$ having a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) of mean $\delta$ and variance one. Here $\Phi$ is the [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) of the [standard normal distribution](standard-normal-distribution.md). Under a nonzero alternative $\delta$, the [type II error](type-i-and-type-ii-errors.md) probability is the displayed function. It tends to the nonrejection probability under the null as $\delta\to0$, and to zero as $\delta\to+\infty$. A particular observed $Z=z$ has positive density under all these alternatives. Its null-calibrated [p-value](p-value.md) therefore does not select one value of $\beta$. The [power function](power-function-of-a-statistical-test.md) describes repeated-sampling performance at specified alternatives.
