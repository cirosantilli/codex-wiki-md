# Electromagnetism

↑ **Parent:** [Branches of physics](branches-of-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism)

Electromagnetism describes how [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) and [electric current](electric-current.md) interact through the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) $\mathbf E$ and [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $\mathbf B$. The [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations-split.md) describe the fields and their sources; the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) describes the response of a particle of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q$ and [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v$:

$$
\mathbf F=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B),\qquad
\frac{d\mathcal E_{\mathrm{particle}}}{dt}=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

The [electric field](electric-field-split.md) can change the particle's [energy](energy-split.md). The magnetic part of the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) is perpendicular to [velocity](velocity.md), so [magnetic forces do no work](magnetic-forces-do-no-work.md); they can bend a trajectory. These are local interactions: a particle responds to the [electromagnetic field](electromagnetic-field.md) at its position. [Feynman lectures, II.1: fields and forces](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_01.html).

The remarkable unification is that the same [Maxwell equations](maxwell-equations-split.md) connect [Coulomb's law](coulomb-s-law.md), [Faraday's law of induction](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md), and freely propagating [electromagnetic waves](electromagnetic-wave-split.md). A changing [electric field](electric-field-split.md) contributes to magnetic circulation through [displacement current](displacement-current.md); together with [Faraday's law](faraday-s-law-of-induction.md), this permits waves in vacuum without conducting matter. The [Poynting theorem](poynting-theorem.md) then explains how the fields store and transport [energy](energy-split.md). This connects the force on a charge to the energy carried by light.

In his 1865 paper, [James Clerk Maxwell](james-clerk-maxwell.md) derived transverse disturbances and compared the propagation speed inferred from electrical measurements with independently measured light speeds. Part VI, especially §§95–97, argues that their agreement supports identifying light as an [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave-split.md). This was a comparison with finite experimental accuracy, rather than a numerical identity between the historical measurements. [Maxwell, A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field, Philosophical Transactions 155 (1865), 459–512](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstl.1865.0008); [transcription of Maxwell's original Part VI](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dynamical_Theory_of_the_Electromagnetic_Field/Part_VI).
