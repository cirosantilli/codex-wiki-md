# Fundamental theorem for line integrals

↑ **Parent:** [Vector calculus](vector-calculus.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_for_line_integrals)

For a continuously differentiable [function](function-split.md) $\Phi$ and a piecewise smooth [parametric curve](parametric-curve.md) $\gamma$ from $a$ to $b$,

$$
\int_\gamma\nabla\Phi\cdot d\mathbf x=\Phi(b)-\Phi(a).
$$

The [chain rule](chain-rule.md) converts the integrand into $d(\Phi(\gamma(t)))/dt$, then the [fundamental theorem of calculus](fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) supplies the endpoint difference. This is why a [conservative vector field](conservative-vector-field.md) has a [path independent](path-independence.md) [line integral](line-integral.md). For $\Phi(x,y)=x^2+y^2$, every path from $(0,0)$ to $(1,2)$ gives $5$, provided it lies in the domain where the potential is defined.
