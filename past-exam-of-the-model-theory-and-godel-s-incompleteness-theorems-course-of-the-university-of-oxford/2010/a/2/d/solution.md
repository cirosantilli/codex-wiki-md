<h1 id="a/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**Assume additionally that the internal modus-ponens condition requested in part (c) holds for this $R$.** If [positive introspection](../../../../../positive-introspection.md) also held for every sentence, then $R$ would satisfy all three [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../../../../../hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions.md): external [necessitation rule](../../../../../necessitation-rule.md) was proved in part (c), [modus ponens rule](../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) is the added condition, and introspection is the supposition.

The diagonal lemma applies to $R$ just as to any [arithmetic](../../../../../arithmetic-split.md) formula. The usual [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) for a predicate with these conditions would then imply

$$
PA\nvdash\neg R(\ulcorner\bot\urcorner)
$$

when $PA$ is consistent. Part (b) proves the opposite. Therefore the three conditions cannot all hold, and under the added modus-ponens hypothesis there must be some sentence $X$ such that

$$
\boxed{PA\nvdash R(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\to R(\ulcorner R(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\urcorner).}
$$

This is the intended contradiction argument and requires no identification of a particular $X$.

Without that extra hypothesis, the valid conclusion is only that **at least one of internal [modus ponens rule](../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md) and [positive introspection](../../../../../positive-introspection.md) fails**. Rosser predicates can be constructed that satisfy [positive introspection](../../../../../positive-introspection.md), so a failure of that condition cannot be inferred from the printed general assumptions alone. [Rosser provability and the second incompleteness theorem: constructions with different derivability conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06863). The distinction concerns proof coding, not a missing page of the exam.
