<h1 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $\Box A$ for the [arithmetical provability predicate](../../../../../arithmetical-provability-predicate.md) applied to the [Gödel number](../../../../../godel-number.md) of $A$. Use the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../../../../../hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions.md) that belong to the course meaning of a provability predicate: external [necessitation rule](../../../../../necessitation-rule.md), internal [modus ponens rule](../../../../../implication-elimination-rule.md), and [positive introspection](../../../../../positive-introspection.md). No incompleteness theorem is needed here.

If $S\vdash G$, [necessitation rule](../../../../../necessitation-rule.md) gives $S\vdash\Box G$. The displayed [Gödel sentence](../../../../../godel-sentence.md) equivalence also gives $S\vdash\neg\Box G$. These two theorems contradict the assumption that $S$ is a [consistent first-order theory](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ consistent}\ \Longrightarrow\ S\nvdash G.}
$$

For this part alone, the external [necessitation rule](../../../../../necessitation-rule.md) condition suffices; the stronger internal conditions are used in the next part.
