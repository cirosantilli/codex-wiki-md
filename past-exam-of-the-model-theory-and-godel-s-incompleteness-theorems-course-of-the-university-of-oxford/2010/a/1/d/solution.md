<h1 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

By part (c), [one-consistency](../../../../../one-consistency.md) gives [Sigma-1 soundness](../../../../../sigma-1-soundness.md) and hence ordinary consistency. First, $S$ cannot prove $\neg G$: if it did, the diagonal equivalence would give $S\vdash\Box G$. This is a $\Sigma_1$ sentence, so soundness would make it true. The stipulated standard interpretation of the [arithmetical provability predicate](../../../../../arithmetical-provability-predicate.md) would then supply an actual proof of $G$, contradicting consistency.

Now suppose $S\vdash\Box\neg G$. This too is a $\Sigma_1$ theorem. Soundness and the standard correctness of the predicate would imply that there is an actual $S$-proof of $\neg G$, which was just excluded. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\text{ one-consistent}\ \Longrightarrow\ S\nvdash\Box\neg G.}
$$

The two applications of soundness concern the provability assertions, not the truth of arbitrary [arithmetic](../../../../../arithmetic-split.md) sentences.
