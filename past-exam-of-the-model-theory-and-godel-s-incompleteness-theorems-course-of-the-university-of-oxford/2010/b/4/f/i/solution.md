**No.** In the standard [successor structure](../../../../../../successor-structure.md), every element other than the named zero has a predecessor:

$$
\forall x\bigl(x=0\ \vee\ \exists y\,s(y)=x\bigr).
$$

The proposed structure fails this [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) at the integer $-1$. Its nonnegative elements map to positive elements, while every negative element maps to an integer at most $-2$; hence no element maps to $-1$. This gives a direct first-order distinction, without treating the negative branch as a two-sided successor chain.
