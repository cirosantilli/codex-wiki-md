<h1 id="b/4/f/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Consider the [partial type](../../../../../../partial-type.md)

$$
p_\infty(x)=\{x\ne s^n(0):n\in\mathbb N\}.
$$

Each finite fragment is realized by a sufficiently large standard [natural number](../../../../../../natural-number.md), so it is consistent. It is omitted in the standard [successor structure](../../../../../../successor-structure.md), since each of its elements equals one of the named iterates.

There is exactly one [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) extending this [partial type](../../../../../../partial-type.md). By [classification of models of the successor theory](../../../../../../classification-of-models-of-the-successor-theory.md) and its quantifier-elimination property, a parameter-free one-variable formula is equivalent to a Boolean combination of finitely many equalities $x=s^n(0)$ and sentences whose truth is fixed by the theory. For a point on an extra integer chain every displayed equality is false, so all such points have the same [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tp}(z/\varnothing)\text{ for a point }z\text{ on an added integer chain}}
$$

is the required omitted [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md). More formally take all formulas true at such a point; $p_\infty$ axiomatizes that type modulo the theory, and its deductive closure is complete.
