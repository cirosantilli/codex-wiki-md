<h1 id="b/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An $n$-type over the empty set is a set $p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ of formulas of the language, with no additional parameters, jointly consistent with $T$. Equivalently every [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) is simultaneously satisfiable with $T$. A [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) decides every formula in these variables: for each $\varphi$, exactly one of $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ belongs to $p$.

A principal type has an isolating formula $\theta(x)$ consistent with $T$, such that $T\models\forall x(\theta(x)\to\varphi(x))$ for every $\varphi\in p$. For a [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) this is an [isolated type](../../../../../isolated-type.md); its isolator determines the entire type. A principal [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) may be weaker than a [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md), so its isolator need not decide all formulas.

A tuple $a\in A^n$ realizes $p$ if $\mathcal A\models\varphi(a)$ for every formula in $p$. The model omits $p$ if it contains no such tuple. Thus omission is a simultaneous failure of realizing the whole type, even though each finite fragment can be realized.
