<h1 id="b/4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Add constants $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ and form $T\cup\{\varphi(c):\varphi\in p\}$. Every [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) has a model by consistency of the type. The [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) gives a model $\mathcal M$ realizing the whole expanded theory, so $c^{\mathcal M}$ realizes $p$.

Apply the downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) to the [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) expanded language, retaining these finitely many named elements. The resulting [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [elementary substructure](../../../../../elementary-substructure.md) still satisfies every formula of $p$ at the same tuple. Its reduct to the original language is a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) model of $T$ realizing $p$. Since $T$ has no finite models, this model is countably infinite.

$$
\boxed{\text{Every finite-arity type has a realization in a countable model of }T.}
$$
