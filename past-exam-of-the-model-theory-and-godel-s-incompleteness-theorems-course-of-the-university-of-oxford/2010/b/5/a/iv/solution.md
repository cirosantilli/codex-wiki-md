<h1 id="b/5/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A [prime model](../../../../../../prime-model.md) admits an [elementary embedding](../../../../../../elementary-embedding.md) into every model of its [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md). In the [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) setting a prime model is atomic. If a tuple in the prime model had a nonisolated [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md), the [omitting types theorem](../../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md) would give a model omitting that type; an [elementary embedding](../../../../../../elementary-embedding.md) of the prime model into it would realize the omitted type, a contradiction.

Combining the four parts, all positive implications are exactly those generated by

$$
\boxed{(\mathrm i)\ \Longrightarrow\ (\mathrm{ii})\ \Longrightarrow\ (\mathrm{iii})\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\mathrm{iv}).}
$$

The structure $(\mathbb N,<,0)$ refutes (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). The structure $(\mathbb Q,<,0)$ is a [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) atomic and hence prime model of its [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md), while failing both (i) and (ii); it refutes every remaining reverse arrow, including (iv)$\Rightarrow$(i) and (iv)$\Rightarrow$(ii). The counterexamples therefore cover all ordered pairs of distinct properties not forced by the boxed chain.
