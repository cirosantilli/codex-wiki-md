<h1 id="b/5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An element $a$ is parameter-free definable if some formula $\theta(x)$ of the original language has $a$ as its unique solution in the structure. Saying this for every element makes the model [pointwise definable](../../../../../../pointwise-definable-model.md).

If a finite tuple has individually defining formulas, their conjunction uniquely defines that tuple. Its [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) is isolated by this conjunction: for each formula $\varphi$, the sentence saying the unique tuple does or does not satisfy $\varphi$ is decided by the [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{pointwise definable}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{atomic}.}
$$

It follows that the [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) model is also prime. The converse implication to property (i) fails. In $(\mathbb N,<,0)$ every element is defined by having exactly $n$ predecessors, but the only [closed term](../../../../../../closed-term.md) in this language is the constant $0$. This infinite model is pointwise definable and is not term-generated.
