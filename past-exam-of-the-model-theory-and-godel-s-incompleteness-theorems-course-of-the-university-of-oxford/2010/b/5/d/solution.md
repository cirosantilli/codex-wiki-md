<h1 id="b/5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose $T$ is an [omega-categorical theory](../../../../../omega-categorical-theory.md). By the [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](../../../../../ryll-nardzewski-theorem.md), every finite-arity [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) over the empty set is isolated. Let $p(x,a)$ be a [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) over a finite tuple in the unique [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) model $M$, and take an [elementary extension](../../../../../elementary-extension.md) realizing it at $b$. The joint type of $(b,a)$ is isolated by a formula $\theta(x,y)$.

The [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) of $a$ contains $\exists x\,\theta(x,y)$, since this formula is true of $a$ in the extension. Hence it is true of $a$ in $M$, and some $b'\in M$ satisfies $\theta(b',a)$. Isolation of the joint type implies that $b'$ realizes $p$. Thus the [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) model is an [omega-saturated model](../../../../../omega-saturated-model.md).

**The converse fails.** The complete [successor theory](../../../../../theory-of-successor.md) from question 4 has a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) model

$$
M=\mathcal N\ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{j\in\mathbb N}\mathbb Z_j
$$

with countably infinitely many two-sided successor components. It is omega-saturated. Over finitely many parameters, a complete one-variable type either fixes a finite successor/predecessor translate of a parameter or zero, or asks for a point in no component represented by those parameters and zero. The first kind is realized in the named component, and the second in an unused integer component. The quantifier-elimination description of [classification of models of the successor theory](../../../../../classification-of-models-of-the-successor-theory.md) shows there are no other cases.

Nevertheless this theory is not omega-categorical: $M$ and its standard model $\mathcal N$ are [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) and not isomorphic. Thus existence of a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) saturated model is strictly weaker than [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) categoricity.
