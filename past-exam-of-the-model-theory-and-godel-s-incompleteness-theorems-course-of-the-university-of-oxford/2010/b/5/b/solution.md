<h1 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An [omega-saturated model](../../../../../omega-saturated-model.md) realizes every [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) of a finite tuple over every [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) of its domain. It suffices to require this for one-variable types: realize the coordinates successively, enlarging the finite parameter set at each step.

**Every [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) here has such a model, with no countability requirement on its size.** Start with a model of size at most $\lambda=2^{\aleph_0}$. There are at most $\lambda$ types over its [finite sets](../../../../../finite-set.md), because the language is [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) and each such type is a set of formulas in a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) collection. By the [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) and the [elementary diagram](../../../../../elementary-diagram-of-a-structure.md) construction, pass to an [elementary extension](../../../../../elementary-extension.md) realizing all these types, keeping size at most $\lambda$ by the [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md).

Repeat this for stages $0,1,2,\ldots$ and take the union of the elementary chain. Any finite parameter set in the union lies in some stage, and every type over it is realized at the next stage. The union is therefore omega-saturated. [Countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) language does not by itself guarantee that a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) saturated model exists.
