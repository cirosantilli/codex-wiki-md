<h1 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Skolem's paradox](../../../../../skolem-s-paradox.md) is the apparent conflict between a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) model of a sufficiently strong [set theory](../../../../../set-theory-split.md) and that model's theorem asserting the existence of [uncountable](../../../../../uncountable-set.md) sets. Downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) gives a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) elementary model whenever an appropriate infinite model exists; internally the same model still proves, for example, that its real-number set is [uncountable](../../../../../uncountable-set.md).

The two uses of “[countable](../../../../../countable-set.md)” quantify over different collections of functions. Externally the whole model, and therefore the elements it regards as members of its real-number set, can be enumerated. Internally a set is [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) only if an enumerating function exists among the sets of the model. The external enumeration need not be an element of the model.

Thus there is no contradiction: the model's assertion is that **no function it contains** is the required enumeration. [Cantor's theorem](../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) remains valid inside the model. The model need not be transitive or have the actual standard [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) for this explanation; the essential issue is the restriction of internal quantifiers to its own domain.
