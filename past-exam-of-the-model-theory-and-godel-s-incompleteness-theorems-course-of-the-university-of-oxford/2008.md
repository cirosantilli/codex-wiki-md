<h4 id="a/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

An [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is $\Sigma_0$, or bounded, if all its [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) are bounded by arithmetical [first-order terms](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term). A $\Sigma_1$ [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has an initial block of [existential quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-quantification) followed by a bounded matrix, up to the usual logical transformations. [Soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) at a given level means that every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) of that level proved by the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is true in the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). Here $\Sigma_0$-completeness means that every true [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) is provable.

A [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) if there is no [bounded arithmetic formula](../computability-theory.md#arithmetic-bounded-formula) $A(x)$ for which it proves $\exists xA(x)$ while proving $\neg A(\bar n)$ for every standard [natural number](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) $n$. This is a restricted form of [omega-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#omega-consistency-of-an-arithmetic-theory). In particular [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) implies [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), since an inconsistent [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) proves every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) and its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation).

Suppose a $\Sigma_0$-complete [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $S$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). If it proved a false [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $\theta$, the true [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $\neg\theta$ would also be provable by completeness, contradicting [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). Thus $S$ is $\Sigma_0$-sound. Conversely $\Sigma_0$-soundness excludes inconsistency because an inconsistent [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) proves the false [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $0=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Sigma_0\text{-complete}:\quad\operatorname{Con}(S)\iff\Sigma_0\text{-soundness}.}
$$

If $S$ is [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) but proved a false [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) $\exists xA(x)$ with bounded matrix, every standard instance $\neg A(\bar n)$ would be true and hence provable. This violates [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency). Thus it is $\Sigma_1$-sound. Conversely, $\Sigma_1$-soundness implies [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) by the previous argument, since [bounded arithmetic sentences](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) are included at the existential level. If $S$ proved $\exists xA(x)$, [soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) would give a standard witness $n$ with $A(n)$ true. [Bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) would then give $S\vdash A(\bar n)$, so [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) prevents simultaneous [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of all the negated instances. Hence

$$
\boxed{\Sigma_0\text{-complete}:\quad1\text{-consistency}\iff\Sigma_1\text{-soundness}.}
$$

Multiple existential [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) can be treated as a finite [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) of witnesses; the same reasoning applies.

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

The definition of $a$ makes the diagonal instance $E_a[\bar a]$ exactly $G_S$. The representing [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) therefore has the standard interpretation

$$
\mathbb N\models\exists v_2A(\bar a,v_2)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\vdash G_S.
$$

If $S\vdash G_S$, choose an actual witness $m$ to the left-hand existential statement. The [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $A(\bar a,\bar m)$ is true and hence provable by $\Sigma_0$-completeness. But $G_S$ also gives $S\vdash\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)$, a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Thus

$$
\boxed{S\text{ consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\nvdash G_S.}
$$

Now assume [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency). It implies [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), so no [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $G_S$ exists and $A(a,m)$ is false for each standard $m$. Each [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)$ is consequently true and provable. A [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\neg G_S$ would give a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\exists v_2A(\bar a,v_2)$ while every standard witness instance is refuted, contrary to [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency). Hence

$$
\boxed{S\text{ one-consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\nvdash\neg G_S.}
$$

The two hypotheses play different roles: ordinary [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) excludes the positive [Gödel sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-sentence), while the stronger hypothesis also excludes its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation).

By the standard interpretation of the representing [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula),

$$
\mathbb N\models G_S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\nvdash G_S.
$$

If $S$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), part (b) supplies the right-hand side, so $G_S$ is true. If $S$ is inconsistent, explosion gives $S\vdash G_S$, so the representing existential statement is true and $G_S$ is false. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb N\models G_S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

This is an external truth assertion about the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). Formalizing that assertion inside a [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) requires the additional facts explicitly supplied in part (d).

Put $T=S\cup\{\neg G_S\}$. The first supplied equivalence, retained in $T$, combines with its new axiom to give

$$
T\vdash\neg\operatorname{Con}(S).
$$

The supplied implication $\operatorname{Con}(T)\to\operatorname{Con}(S)$ then yields, by contraposition,

$$
T\vdash\neg\operatorname{Con}(T).
$$

Finally the second supplied equivalence is $T\vdash G_T\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(T)$, so propositional reasoning gives

$$
\boxed{T\vdash\neg G_T.}
$$

No extra [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) assumption is needed for this formal derivation. If $S$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), part (b) shows that adding $\neg G_S$ preserves [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), because inconsistency of the extension would imply $S\vdash G_S$. Such a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) extension therefore proves the [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) of its own true [Gödel sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-sentence), illustrating why [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) alone does not imply [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency).

Write $\rho=\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}^*(\ulcorner R\urcorner)$. The supplied fixed-point equivalence gives $PA\vdash R\leftrightarrow\neg\rho$.

If PA proved $R$, the first assumed property would give $PA\vdash\rho$, while the equivalence would give $PA\vdash\neg\rho$. This contradicts [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). If PA proved $\neg R$, the second assumed property would give $PA\vdash\neg\rho$, and the reverse implication of the equivalence would give $PA\vdash R$, again a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Hence

$$
\boxed{PA\text{ consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad PA\nvdash R\text{ and }PA\nvdash\neg R.}
$$

This is the Rosser improvement: its comparison of [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) codes permits ordinary [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) to exclude both alternatives, without assuming [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency).

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

If PA is inconsistent, every desired conclusion is already a theorem, so it suffices to analyze the [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) case. Use $Q(u,v)$ for the bounded [arithmetical proof relation](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-proof-relation) and $\operatorname{neg}(u)$ for the code of the [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) of the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) coded by $u$.

Suppose $PA\vdash E_n$ and let $m$ be the code of an actual [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof). By [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) there is no [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\neg E_n$, in particular none with code at most $m$. Thus

$$
Q(\bar n,\bar m)\wedge(\forall z\le\bar m)\neg Q(\overline{\operatorname{neg}(n)},z)
$$

is a true [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence). [Bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) proves it in PA, and existential introduction gives $PA\vdash\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}^*(\bar n)$.

Conversely suppose $PA\vdash\neg E_n$ and choose an actual [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) code $k$ of that [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). [First-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) excludes every [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $E_n$, so each of the finitely many statements $\neg Q(\bar n,\bar j)$ for $j<k$ is true and provable. The supplied finite-case theorem permits PA to combine them into the assertion that no [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) code below $k$ proves $E_n$.

PA also proves $Q(\overline{\operatorname{neg}(n)},\bar k)$. For any candidate [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) code $v\ge\bar k$, this one code already refutes the universal no-earlier-negation condition in the Rosser [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol). For $v<\bar k$, the finite verification refutes its positive [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) condition. The supplied order dichotomy and finite-case expansion cover all $v$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{PA\vdash\neg\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}^*(\bar n).}
$$

The argument uses bounded verification of a finite initial segment and one fixed counter-witness beyond it; it does not infer that PA proves the absence of all ordinary [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) merely from [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency).

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

<h5 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/2/c)

If PA is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) and already proves $\neg X$, it cannot also prove $X$. By the correctness of the ordinary arithmetized [arithmetical proof relation](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-proof-relation), $\mathbb N\models\neg\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}(\ulcorner X\urcorner)$.

If PA is inconsistent, it proves $X$ by explosion, so an actual [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) code witnesses $\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}(\ulcorner X\urcorner)$ in the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). Thus

$$
\boxed{PA\text{ consistent}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb N\models\neg\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}(\ulcorner X\urcorner).}
$$

The hypothesis that $X$ is refutable is essential to this [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) characterization for an arbitrary fixed [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence).

Let $C$ denote the addition-commutativity [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) and put $d=\ulcorner\neg C\urcorner$. In the ordinary checker for PA derivations, replace the axiom test $\operatorname{Ax}_{PA}(u)$ by

$$
\operatorname{Ax}_S(u)\equiv\operatorname{Ax}_{PA}(u)\vee u=\bar d.
$$

Keep all tests for sequence formation, earlier lines, logical axiom schemes and [inference rules](../mathematical-logic.md#inference-rule) unchanged. This gives a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) checker $Q_S(u,w)$ which recognizes exactly ordinary $S$-derivations. The finite syntactic modification is arithmetizable by the same bounded coding apparatus as the original checker.

For the proof-order comparison in the next part, make the choice of coding explicit. Reserve code $0$ for the one-line derivation of the newly added axiom $\neg C$, and give an ordinary derivation with code $w$ the new code $w+1$. A corresponding bounded relation is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Prov}_S(u,v)\equiv
(v=0\wedge u=\bar d)\vee(\exists w\le v)(v=w+1\wedge Q_S(u,w)).}
$$

Every code has a specified decoding, every genuine [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) is represented, and no false [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) is admitted. The reserved code duplicates one genuine derivation, which is harmless. This is one explicit arithmetized [arithmetical proof relation](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-proof-relation) obtainable from the PA checker by the requested axiom change and a simple renumbering. Stating the proof-order convention matters because a Rosser [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) uses code order, not merely the existence of [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof).

<h5 id="a/2/d/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/2/d)

<h6 id="a/2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/2/d/ii)

Use the [arithmetical proof relation](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-proof-relation) and coding just constructed. A [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $C$ cannot have code $0$, since that code was reserved for $\neg C$. Every candidate code $v$ for a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $C$ is therefore positive. But code $0\le v$ is already a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\neg C$, so the no-earlier-negation condition fails for every candidate. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb N\models\neg\operatorname{Pr}_S^*(\ulcorner C\urcorner).}
$$

PA proves $C$ by [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction), while $S$ has $\neg C$ as an added axiom. Thus $S$ is inconsistent and its ordinary [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) is true of every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), including $C$. The displayed negative Rosser assertion can nevertheless be true, because the Rosser condition compares the order of opposite [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) rather than asking for their mere existence.

There is an important coding qualification. The PDF refers to a course [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) coding but does not specify its numerical ordering. The truth of this Rosser assertion for an inconsistent [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is not coding-invariant, so [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) length alone cannot establish it for an unspecified fixed coding. For example, a different legitimate numbering could reserve code $0$ for a known PA [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $C$, code $1$ for the new axiom $\neg C$, and higher codes for all other derivations. Then code $0$ would witness $\operatorname{Pr}_S^*(\ulcorner C\urcorner)$ instead. The explicit allowed construction in part (i) supplies a convention under which the requested assertion holds; interpreting it as a claim about some externally fixed course numbering requires that numbering's corresponding [order property](../model-theory.md#order-property).

Use the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) for an [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate). Abbreviate $P_S(\ulcorner A\urcorner)$ by $P A$. The conditions needed below are

$$
S\vdash A\Longrightarrow S\vdash P A,\qquad
S\vdash P(A\to B)\to(P A\to P B),\qquad
S\vdash P A\to P P A.
$$

These are the usual theoremhood, distribution and positive-introspection conditions intended by a standard [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate); arbitrary [predicate symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) merely agreeing with provability externally need not satisfy them.

Since $S\vdash\neg Y$, it proves $Y\to G$. The first two conditions give $S\vdash P Y\to P G$. For the converse, the fixed-point equivalence gives $S\vdash G\to(P G\to Y)$. Applying theoremhood and distribution gives

$$
S\vdash P G\to P(P G\to Y).
$$

[Positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) also gives $P G\to P P G$, and distribution combines these to yield $S\vdash P G\to P Y$. Thus $S\vdash P G\leftrightarrow P Y$. Combining with $G\leftrightarrow\neg P G$ proves

$$
\boxed{S\vdash G\leftrightarrow\neg P Y.}
$$

If $S$ proved $\neg P Y$, it would prove $G$. The theoremhood condition would then give $P G$, while the fixed-point equivalence gives $\neg P G$. Therefore [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) implies

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash\neg P Y.}
$$

<h4 id="a/3/b">b</h4>

<h5 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/3/b)

Put $T=S\cup\{\neg X\}$ and assume $S\vdash P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\to X$. In $T$, contraposition against its extra axiom gives $\neg P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner)$.

Classical logic proves $(\neg X\to X)\to X$. By the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions),

$$
S\vdash P_S(\ulcorner\neg X\to X\urcorner)\to P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner).
$$

Compose this with the supplied formalized deduction implication from $P_T(\ulcorner X\urcorner)$ to its left-hand side. Then $T$ proves

$$
P_T(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\to P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner),
$$

and therefore

$$
T\vdash\neg P_T(\ulcorner X\urcorner).
$$

The other supplied assumption says this is impossible if $T$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). Thus $T$ is inconsistent. By the deduction theorem, inconsistency after adjoining $\neg X$ yields $S\vdash\neg\neg X$, hence

$$
\boxed{S\vdash X.}
$$

This establishes Löb's [inference rule](../mathematical-logic.md#inference-rule) using precisely the strengthened unprovability assumption and the formalized deduction property supplied in the question.

<h4 id="a/3/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/3/c)

Take a [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $Y$ refutable in $S$ and suppose, toward [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction), that a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) $S$ proved $\neg P_S(\ulcorner Y\urcorner)$. [Propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-logic) would then give

$$
S\vdash P_S(\ulcorner Y\urcorner)\to Y.
$$

Löb's inference, applied with $X=Y$, would give $S\vdash Y$, contradicting the existing [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\neg Y$. Hence [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) excludes the proposed [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of non-provability.

Thus the familiar unprovability of the [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) statement associated with a refutable [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) is a special consequence of the general rule

$$
\boxed{S\vdash(P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\to X)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash X.}
$$

The general rule applies to every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), not just a chosen false or refutable one.

The index restriction on [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) has been respected even when $i=0$: the chain begins at $F_{i+1}$, which already is a provability assertion.

<h4 id="a/4/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#a/4)

<h5 id="a/4/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/4/d)

Under the arithmetical interpretation $\square A=P_S(\ulcorner A\urcorner)$, the Löb axiom says that a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of the local reflection implication for $A$ already entails a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $A$:

$$
P_S(\ulcorner P_S(\ulcorner A\urcorner)\to A\urcorner)\to P_S(\ulcorner A\urcorner).
$$

It is a formalized constraint on self-verification of a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) system. Externally, [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) says that if the system itself proves its own local reflection implication for $A$, it proves $A$. Distribution reflects closure of [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) under implication, necessitation reflects that a theorem has an arithmetically recognizable [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof), and [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) reflects [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) verification inside [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md).

Taking $A$ to be a refutable [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), especially a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction), recovers the obstruction behind the second incompleteness theorem: a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) sufficiently strong system cannot prove the corresponding claim that no [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of that [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) exists. This does not identify provability with truth, nor grant the unrestricted factivity principle $\square A\to A$ as a theorem of the system. The Löb axiom is the [characteristic](../algebra.md#characteristic-of-a-field) additional scheme of Gödel–Löb [provability logic](../mathematical-logic.md#provability-logic).

<h2 id="b">B</h2>

<h3 id="b/5">5</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/5/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/5/i)

An $L$-structure has a nonempty underlying [set](../set.md) $A$. It assigns to each [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) $c_k$ an element $c_k^{\mathcal A}\in A$, to each $n$-ary [function symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol) $f_j$ a [function](../function.md) $A^n\to A$, and to each $n$-ary [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) $P_i$ a relation contained in $A^n$. [Logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality), when included as [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality), is interpreted as actual identity.

The arities belong to the specification of the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). An assignment of [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) to elements of $A$ then evaluates [first-order terms](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) recursively and determines satisfaction of [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) through the [logical connectives](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-connective) and [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). A [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) has no [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable), so its truth in the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) is independent of the assignment.

<h4 id="b/5/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/ii)

The [set](../set.md) $\Sigma$ is [satisfiable](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfiable-first-order-theory) if there is one $L$-structure $\mathcal A$ satisfying every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) in $\Sigma$ simultaneously. It is finitely [satisfiable](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfiable-first-order-theory) if each finite [subset](../set.md#subset) $\Sigma_0\subseteq\Sigma$ has a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory); that [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is allowed to depend on the chosen finite [subset](../set.md#subset).

Thus the two statements have different [quantifier orders](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier-order):

$$
\exists\mathcal A\ \forall\sigma\in\Sigma\ (\mathcal A\models\sigma),
\qquad
\forall\Sigma_0\subseteq_{\rm finite}\Sigma\ \exists\mathcal A\ (\mathcal A\models\Sigma_0).
$$

Satisfiability immediately implies finite satisfiability by using the same [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) for every finite [subset](../set.md#subset). The converse is the substantive content of [compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem).

<h4 id="b/5/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/5/iii)

The [compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) states that a [set](../set.md) of [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) has a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) if and only if every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) has a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

For the nontrivial direction suppose every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) of $\Sigma$ is [satisfiable](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfiable-first-order-theory). If $\Sigma$ were inconsistent, a finite formal derivation of a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) would use only finitely many premises $\Sigma_0\subseteq\Sigma$. [Soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) would then exclude a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $\Sigma_0$, contradicting finite satisfiability. Therefore $\Sigma$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). The completeness theorem, proved by adjoining witnesses and constructing a Henkin [term model](../mathematical-logic.md#term-model), supplies a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $\Sigma$.

The reverse direction is immediate by restriction of the list of required [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). The key reason [compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) follows from syntactic completeness is that **every [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) uses only finitely many premises**, even when the whole [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is infinite.

<h4 id="b/5/iv">iv</h4>

<h5 id="b/5/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

Expand the [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) by a new constant $c$ and consider

$$
\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z)\cup\{c\ne0\}\cup
\{\exists y\ (\bar n\cdot y=c):n=1,2,3,\ldots\}.
$$

Every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is [satisfiable](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfiable-first-order-theory) in the [integer](../integer.md) [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) itself. Only finitely many [divisibility](../divisibility.md) conditions occur; interpret $c$ as a nonzero common multiple of their positive [integer](../integer.md) indices, using $1$ if none occur. All [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) from $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z)$ are automatically true there.

[Compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) therefore gives a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Its reduct $R$ to the original [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) satisfies the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) of $\mathbb Z$, so $R\equiv\mathbb Z$. Its element $c^R$ is nonzero and divisible by every positive standard [integer](../integer.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{R\equiv\mathbb Z,\qquad c^R\ne0,\qquad
(\forall n\in\mathbb N_{>0})\ R\models\exists y\ (\bar n y=c^R).}
$$

Such an element cannot be an ordinary nonzero [integer](../integer.md), since an [integer](../integer.md) of absolute value less than $n$ cannot be a nonzero multiple of $n$. The statement concerns each standard [integer](../integer.md) index separately; it does not assert [divisibility](../divisibility.md) by every internally nonzero element of $R$.

<h4 id="b/6/i">i</h4>

<h5 id="b/6/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/i)

Let $L_A$ expand $L$ by a constant $c_a$ for each $a\in A$, and let $\mathcal A_A$ be the canonical expansion interpreting $c_a$ as $a$. Its [diagram of a structure](../model-theory.md#diagram-mathematical-logic) is the [set](../set.md) of all atomic and negated atomic $L_A$-sentences true in $\mathcal A_A$. Its [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure) is the [set](../set.md) of all $L_A$-sentences true there:

$$
\operatorname{Diag}(\mathcal A)=\{\theta:\theta\text{ atomic or negated atomic},\ \mathcal A_A\models\theta\},
$$



$$
\operatorname{CDiag}(\mathcal A)=\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal A_A).
$$

A [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) inclusion $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B$ means that $A\subseteq B$, constants have the same interpretations, the operations of $\mathcal B$ restrict to those of $\mathcal A$, and each relation restricts to the corresponding relation on $A$.

An [elementary substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#elementary-substructure) additionally preserves the truth of every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with parameters from $A$:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A\preccurlyeq\mathcal B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal A\models\phi(\vec a)\iff\mathcal B\models\phi(\vec a)
\text{ for every }\phi,\vec a\in A.}
$$

Here the right-hand condition includes the [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) requirements by applying it to atomic [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and their [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation).

<h4 id="b/6/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/ii)

If $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B$, interpret each new constant $c_a$ in $\mathcal B$ as the same element $a$. Atomic [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and their [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) then have exactly their [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) in $\mathcal A_A$, so the expansion satisfies the [diagram of a structure](../model-theory.md#diagram-mathematical-logic). If the inclusion is elementary, every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with those named parameters has the same [truth value](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value), so the expansion satisfies the [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure).

Conversely, from any expansion $\mathcal B^+$ satisfying the [diagram of a structure](../model-theory.md#diagram-mathematical-logic) define

$$
e:A\to B,\qquad e(a)=c_a^{\mathcal B^+}.
$$

The [diagram of a structure](../model-theory.md#diagram-mathematical-logic) contains $c_a\ne c_{a'}$ whenever $a\ne a'$, so $e$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). It contains every true function-value equation, so $e$ commutes with all operations and constants. Positive and negative relation instances ensure both preservation and reflection of the relations. Thus $e$ is an [embedding of first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#embedding-of-first-order-structures). If the expansion satisfies the [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure), for every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\phi$ and [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec a$ it also gives

$$
\mathcal A\models\phi(\vec a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal B\models\phi(e(\vec a)),
$$

so $e$ is an elementary [embedding of first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#embedding-of-first-order-structures).

**The precise conclusion for an arbitrary expansion is an [embedding of first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#embedding-of-first-order-structures), rather than a literal inclusion of the original carriers.** The source's inclusion notation is correct when one uses the customary identification of $\mathcal A$ with its embedded copy, or requires the expansion to interpret $c_a$ as the actual element $a$ of a carrier already containing $A$. With that naming convention, $e$ is the inclusion and the requested two equivalences follow exactly.

Without the convention the literal wording fails, even in the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) of [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality): singleton [first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) with carriers $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$ have a diagram-satisfying expansion by sending the first [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure)'s named point to $1$, but the first carrier is not a [subset](../set.md#subset) of the second. The [embedding of first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#embedding-of-first-order-structures) formulation proved above states the invariant result and makes the intended inclusion formulation precise.

<h4 id="b/6/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

The [Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#downward-lowenheim-skolem-theorem) says that for an $L$-structure $M$ and a [subset](../set.md#subset) $S\subseteq M$, there is an [elementary substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#elementary-substructure) $N\preccurlyeq M$ containing $S$ with

$$
\boxed{|N|\le\max(|S|,|L|,\aleph_0).}
$$

If an infinite cardinal $\kappa$ satisfies $\max(|L|,\aleph_0)\le\kappa\le|M|$, one can in particular obtain size exactly $\kappa$ by starting with a [subset](../set.md#subset) of that size.

Choose a default element $a_0\in M$. For every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\exists x\phi(x,\vec y)$ choose a [Skolem function](../mathematical-logic.md#skolem-function) $F_\phi$ which returns a witness in $M$ when one exists, and returns $a_0$ otherwise. Begin with $S$, the interpretations of the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language)'s constants, and $a_0$. Successively adjoin the values of all the original [functions](../function.md) and these [Skolem functions](../mathematical-logic.md#skolem-function) on finite [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) already chosen. Let $N$ be the [union](../set.md#set-union) of the resulting [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) sequence of closure stages.

There are at most $\max(|L|,\aleph_0)$ [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula), and each [function](../function.md) has finite arity. With $\kappa=\max(|S|,|L|,\aleph_0)$, each closure stage has size at most $\kappa$, and so does their [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [union](../set.md#set-union). Closure under the original [functions](../function.md) makes $N$ an $L$-substructure. Whenever $M\models\exists x\phi(x,\vec a)$ for parameters in $N$, its chosen Skolem witness belongs to $N$, since the finitely many parameters lie in some common stage. The Tarski–Vaught test therefore gives $N\preccurlyeq M$.

For the exact-cardinality version, choose an initial [set](../set.md) of $\kappa$ elements, enlarged to contain any prescribed [subset](../set.md#subset) of size at most $\kappa$. The construction gives an upper bound $\kappa$ while containment gives the matching lower bound.

<h4 id="b/6/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/6/iv)

Add a [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) $P_\theta$ for each $L$-formula $\theta(\vec x)$, of arity equal to its displayed free-variable [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple). Let $T^+$ consist of $T$ together with all defining axioms

$$
\forall\vec x\ (P_\theta(\vec x)\leftrightarrow\theta(\vec x)).
$$

Every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$ has a unique such expansion, interpreting the new [predicate symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) by their defining [sets](../set.md). Conversely the reduct of every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T^+$ satisfies $T$.

Given an arbitrary $L^+$-formula $\psi(\vec x)$, replace each new atomic [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) by its original $L$-definition, renaming [bound variables](../mathematical-logic.md#bound-variable) to avoid capture. This produces an $L$-formula $\psi^-(\vec x)$ with

$$
T^+\models\forall\vec x\ (\psi(\vec x)\leftrightarrow\psi^-(\vec x)).
$$

The defining axiom for that particular [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) now gives an atomic replacement:

$$
\boxed{T^+\models\forall\vec x\ (\psi(\vec x)\leftrightarrow P_{\psi^-}(\vec x)).}
$$

Thus the expanded [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) eliminates [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). For a [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), the same translation is an $L$-sentence, decided by completeness of $T$; hence every expanded [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) is decided and $T^+$ is complete. Empty free-variable [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) can be handled by zero-ary [predicate symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol), or by the usual [logical truth](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-truth) and falsity constants. This definitional expansion is called [Morleyization](../model-theory.md#morleyization).

[Prime elements](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-element) are first-order definable in the [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). For instance let $\operatorname{Unit}(u)$ mean $\exists v\ (uv=1)$ and use

$$
\operatorname{Prime}(x)\equiv x\ne0\wedge\neg\operatorname{Unit}(x)\wedge
\forall u\forall v\ (uv=x\to\operatorname{Unit}(u)\vee\operatorname{Unit}(v)).
$$

In the [integer](../integer.md) [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) this defines [prime elements](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-element) up to the harmless choice of sign. Expand the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) by names for every [integer](../integer.md) and a new constant $c$. Take the [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure) of $\mathcal Z$ together with

$$
\operatorname{Prime}(c),\qquad c\ne\bar a\quad(a\in\mathbb Z).
$$

Every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is [satisfiable](../mathematical-logic.md#satisfiable-first-order-theory) in $\mathcal Z$: choose a positive prime [integer](../integer.md) outside the finitely many excluded values, using the [Euclid's theorem](../prime-number.md#euclid-s-theorem). [Compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) gives a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of the entire [set](../set.md). The complete-diagram argument embeds $\mathcal Z$ elementarily into its reduct; identify that copy with the original [integer](../integer.md) [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring).

The resulting [elementary extension](../model-theory.md#elementary-extension) contains $c$, a [prime element](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-element) unequal to every named standard [integer](../integer.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z\preccurlyeq\mathcal Z^*,\qquad
\mathcal Z^*\models\operatorname{Prime}(c),\qquad c\notin\mathbb Z.}
$$

The construction rules out all standard [integers](../integer.md), not merely the finite list appearing in any one approximation.

<h4 id="b/7/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/7/ii)

Apply downward Löwenheim-Skolem to the extension from part (i), keeping all standard [integers](../integer.md) and the new prime $c$. The [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) and this prescribed [subset](../set.md#subset) are [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set), so there is a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [elementary substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#elementary-substructure) $N$ containing them. In particular $N\equiv\mathcal Z$ and contains an element outside the standard copy of $\mathbb Z$.

No [ring isomorphism](../algebra.md#ring-isomorphism) $N\to\mathcal Z$ can exist. Such an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) fixes $0$ and $1$, hence every nonnegative [integer](../integer.md) numeral. It also fixes each negative [integer](../integer.md), which is the unique solution of $x+\bar n=0$ for the corresponding positive $n$. Consequently it fixes the whole standard copy. The image of $c$ would be some standard [integer](../integer.md) $a$, already the image of that same named standard element, violating [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function).

Thus $N$ and $\mathcal Z$ are two nonisomorphic countably infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$, so

$$
\boxed{T\text{ is not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

<h4 id="b/7/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/7/iii)

Every [integer](../integer.md) is definable without parameters in the [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). For $a\ge0$, use $x=\bar a$; for $a=-m<0$, use $x+\bar m=0$. For a finite [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec a$, the [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) $\delta_{\vec a}(\vec x)$ of these coordinate [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) uniquely defines that [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) in $\mathcal Z$.

If $\phi(\vec x)$ belongs to its [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type), the [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence)

$$
\forall\vec x\ (\delta_{\vec a}(\vec x)\to\phi(\vec x))
$$

is true in $\mathcal Z$, since there is only the specified [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) to check. It therefore belongs to $T=\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal Z)$. The [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\delta_{\vec a}$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) with $T$, since it is realized in $\mathcal Z$, and implies every member of the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) modulo $T$. Hence **every [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) realized in $\mathcal Z$ is a [principal complete type](../model-theory.md#principal-complete-type)**, isolated by its tuple-defining [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula).

<h4 id="b/7/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/7/iv)

Take the parameter-free [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type)

$$
p(x)=\operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal Z^*}(c/\varnothing)
$$

of the nonstandard prime constructed in part (i). It is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) and complete because it consists of exactly the [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) true of an actual element in a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$. It contains $x\ne\bar a$ for every [integer](../integer.md) $a$, and consequently no element of $\mathcal Z$ realizes it. Thus

$$
\boxed{p\text{ is a complete type over }T\text{ omitted in }\mathcal Z.}
$$

It is necessarily a [nonprincipal complete type](../model-theory.md#nonprincipal-complete-type). If a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) isolated it, completeness of $T$ would force the existential closure of that [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) to hold in every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$, including $\mathcal Z$; a witness there would realize the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type), a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). This connects the explicit omission with the hypothesis of the [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem).

<h4 id="b/7/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#b/7/v)

Enumerate the standard positive primes as $(p_n)_{n<\omega}$. For each [subset](../set.md#subset) $A\subseteq\omega$ consider the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type)

$$
q_A(x)=\{x\ne0\}\cup\{\bar p_n\mid x:n\in A\}\cup
\{\neg(\bar p_n\mid x):n\notin A\},
$$

where [divisibility](../divisibility.md) is the [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\exists y\ (\bar p_ny=x)$.

Every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is realized in $\mathbb Z$: take the product of the finitely many primes required to divide $x$, or $1$ if there are none. This is nonzero and is not divisible by any of the distinct finitely many primes required not to divide it. [Compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) supplies a [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) extending $q_A$.

Distinct [subsets](../set.md#subset) $A,B$ yield distinct complete extensions, because at an index in their symmetric difference they require opposite [divisibility](../divisibility.md) statements. There are $2^{\aleph_0}$ such [subsets](../set.md#subset), so there are at least that many [complete one-types](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type). There are at most that many because the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) has only countably many [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and a [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) is a [set](../set.md) of them. Hence

$$
\boxed{|S_1(T)|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

In particular the number of [complete one-types](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type) is uncountable, despite every [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) realized in the particular [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $\mathcal Z$ being a [principal complete type](../model-theory.md#principal-complete-type).

<h3 id="b/8">8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/8/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/8/i)

The [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](../model-theory.md#ryll-nardzewski-theorem) says that for a [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) $T$ in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) with infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory), the following are equivalent: $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical; for every positive [integer](../integer.md) $n$, the [set](../set.md) $S_n(T)$ of complete parameter-free $n$-types is finite; every such finite-arity [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) is isolated by a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). The countable-language hypothesis and the requirement for every finite arity are important.

The [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem) says that, for a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language), a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) family of [nonprincipal partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#nonprincipal-partial-type) in finite [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) of [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) can all be omitted in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Here nonprincipal means that no [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) with the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) implies every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) of the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type). Omission means that no [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) in the constructed [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) satisfies all [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) of that [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type). The [complete one-type](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type) version is the special case of one [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) in one [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable).

<h4 id="b/8/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/ii/a">a</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/a/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/8/ii/a)

Use the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) of [dense linear orders without endpoints](../model-theory.md#dense-linear-order-without-endpoints) in the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) $\{<\}$. It eliminates [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier): existential conditions on one new point reduce to finitely many [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) constraints and interval bounds, and density and absence of endpoints decide whether a suitable point exists. Its countably infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) are all [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism) to the rational order by the usual [back-and-forth method](../model-theory.md#back-and-forth-method), extending finite order-preserving maps alternately along enumerations of the two [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Thus it is $\aleph_0$-categorical.

To distinguish [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of size $\aleph_1$, let

$$
M_1=\sum_{\alpha<\omega_1}(\mathbb Q,<),\qquad M_2=M_1+(\mathbb Q,<),
$$

with ordered sums. Both are dense without endpoints and have [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\aleph_1$. The first has [cofinality](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality) $\aleph_1$: countably many points lie in a bounded [set](../set.md) of block indices and cannot be [cofinal](../cardinal-number.md#cofinal-subset). The second has [cofinality](../cardinal-number.md#cofinality) $\aleph_0$, supplied by its final rational block. [Cofinality of a linear order](../set.md#cofinality-of-a-linear-order) is preserved by [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism), so they are not [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{DLO is }\aleph_0\text{-categorical but not }\aleph_1\text{-categorical}.}
$$

For Ryll-Nardzewski, its $n$-types are precisely the finitely many equality-and-order patterns on $n$ coordinates. Each is isolated by the [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) specifying that pattern, agreeing with the theorem's prediction.

<h5 id="b/8/ii/b">b</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/b/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/8/ii/b)

Use $ACF_0$, the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) of [algebraically closed fields](../algebra.md#algebraically-closed-field) of [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). Its [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) is a standard characterization of this [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory). [Algebraically closed fields](../algebra.md#algebraically-closed-field) of a fixed [characteristic](../algebra.md#characteristic-of-a-field) are classified up to [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) by [transcendence degree](../algebra.md#transcendence-degree) over their [prime field](../algebra.md#prime-field).

For such a [field](../algebra.md#field) $K$,

$$
|K|=\max(\aleph_0,\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}K)
$$

when the [transcendence degree](../algebra.md#transcendence-degree) is infinite, while finite [transcendence degree](../algebra.md#transcendence-degree) also gives a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [field](../algebra.md#field). This follows by counting [rational functions](../isolated-singularity.md#rational-function) in a [transcendence basis](../algebra.md#transcendence-basis) and then algebraic elements over that [field](../algebra.md#field). Thus every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\aleph_1$ has [transcendence degree](../algebra.md#transcendence-degree) $\aleph_1$, and the classification gives a unique such [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

On the other hand $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and an [algebraic closure](../algebra.md#algebraic-closure) of $\mathbb Q(t)$ are countably infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) with [transcendence degrees](../algebra.md#transcendence-degree) zero and one, so are not [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism). Hence

$$
\boxed{ACF_0\text{ is }\aleph_1\text{-categorical but not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

The transcendental [complete one-type](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type) consists of $f(x)\ne0$ for every nonzero [polynomial](../polynomial.md) over $\mathbb Q$. [Quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) makes it complete. It is nonprincipal: any parameter-free one-variable [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) containing a transcendental element defines a cofinite [set](../set.md), hence contains algebraic elements as well and cannot isolate this [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type). The [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ omits it, concretely illustrating the [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem). The existence of infinitely many algebraic [complete one-types](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type), together with this [nonprincipal complete type](../model-theory.md#nonprincipal-complete-type), also agrees with the failure of the Ryll-Nardzewski finiteness condition.

<h5 id="b/8/ii/c">c</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/c/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/8/ii/c)

Take the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) of an [infinite set](../set.md#infinite-set) in the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) of [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) alone, with an axiom asserting at least $n$ distinct elements for each positive [integer](../integer.md) $n$. It is complete and eliminates [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier): a new [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) either equals a specified old [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) or is required to avoid only finitely many existing values, which an infinite domain permits.

For every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, any two [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of size $\kappa$ are [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism) because every [bijection](../function.md#bijection) between their underlying [sets](../set.md) preserves [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality), the whole [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). Thus

$$
\boxed{T_{\infty}\text{ is categorical in every infinite cardinality}.}
$$

Its complete $n$-types record only the partition of coordinate indices according to [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality). There are finitely many such partitions, and each is isolated by the corresponding [logical equalities](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) and inequalities. Ryll-Nardzewski therefore recovers [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) categoricity; there are no nonprincipal complete finite-arity [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) to omit in this example.

<h5 id="b/8/ii/d">d</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/d/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/8/ii/d)

The same [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) $T_\infty$ of an infinite pure [set](../set.md) provides this property directly. For $n$ [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), each [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) specifies exactly which [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) are equal. [First-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) requires an [equivalence relation](../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation) on the finite index [set](../set.md), and infinitude ensures every such partition is realizable. Hence

$$
\boxed{|S_n(T_\infty)|=B_n<\infty,}
$$

where $B_n$ is the $n$th [Bell number](../combinatorics.md#bell-number), counting partitions of an $n$-element [set](../set.md). For example the first values are $B_1=1$, $B_2=2$ and $B_3=5$. The isolating [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) lists [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) within blocks and inequality between blocks. This explicitly verifies the hypothesis which Ryll-Nardzewski identifies with $\aleph_0$-categoricity.

<h5 id="b/8/ii/e">e</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/e/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#b/8/ii/e)

Use the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $T_{\mathbb Z}$ of a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) $f$ with named inverse $g$ and no finite cycles. Its axioms are

$$
\forall x\ f(g(x))=x,\qquad\forall x\ g(f(x))=x,\qquad
\forall x\ f^n(x)\ne x\quad(n=1,2,3,\ldots).
$$

Every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is a disjoint [union](../set.md#set-union) of free two-sided [integer](../integer.md) orbits. Write $f^k$ for positive powers of $f$ and for powers of $g$ when $k$ is negative.

This [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is complete and eliminates [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). Every [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) reduces to $f^k(x_i)$, and atomic [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) reduce to statements $x_j=f^k(x_i)$. To eliminate an existential [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) in a [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) of literals, first remove self-equalities using the no-finite-cycle axioms. If an equation fixes that [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) as an [integer](../integer.md) translate of another [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), substitute that value. If no such equation remains, only finitely many points are excluded by the remaining inequalities, so some value exists in the infinite domain. Apply this to [disjunctive normal form](../theoretical-computer-science.md#disjunctive-normal-form) and then induct on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). The resulting parameter-free [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) are decided, proving completeness.

For each fixed finite $n$, a [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) records a partition of the [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) into common orbits and finitely many [integer](../integer.md) offsets within each block. There are countably many possibilities. [Countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) with one orbit and with two orbits are not [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism), so the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is not $\aleph_0$-categorical.

Thus this example has the source's explicitly defined countable-type property. In fact it also satisfies ordinary [omega-stability](../model-theory.md#omega-stability-of-a-theory): over a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) parameter [set](../set.md), an element is either a specified [integer](../integer.md) translate of a parameter, giving countably many possibilities, or lies in an entirely new orbit, giving one further [complete one-type](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type). The same finite-orbit-pattern description handles finite [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple). Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_{\mathbb Z}\text{ has countably many }n\text{-types for every finite }n,
\text{ but is not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

The source prints the label “$0$-stable”; the construction meets its stated definition as well as the usual stronger [omega-stability](../model-theory.md#omega-stability-of-a-theory) condition. The difference between countably many and finitely many [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) is exactly why Ryll-Nardzewski does not yield categoricity here.

<h5 id="b/8/ii/f">f</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

<h6 id="b/8/ii/f/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#b/8/ii/f)

Reuse the complete [free-bijection theory](../model-theory.md#theory-of-a-free-bijection) from part (e). [Quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) shows that every one-variable atomic statement is already decided by the no-finite-cycle axioms. Thus there is exactly one [complete one-type](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type), isolated by $x=x$.

For two [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable), each [integer](../integer.md) $k$ gives a different [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) isolated by

$$
y=f^k(x).
$$

There is also the [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type)

$$
p_\infty(x,y)=\{y\ne f^k(x):k\in\mathbb Z\},
$$

saying that the two elements lie in different orbits. These exhaust the possibilities by [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination). In particular

$$
\boxed{|S_1(T_{\mathbb Z})|=1,\qquad |S_2(T_{\mathbb Z})|=\aleph_0.}
$$

The last [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) is a [nonprincipal complete type](../model-theory.md#nonprincipal-complete-type). Any [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) uses only finitely many [integer](../integer.md) offsets after [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination); a pair in a single orbit with a sufficiently different offset agrees with a different-orbit pair on those tests. Hence no single [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) can isolate $p_\infty$.

The one-orbit [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $(\mathbb Z,f(n)=n+1,g(n)=n-1)$ omits $p_\infty$, while a two-orbit [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) realizes it. This illustrates the [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem) and shows concretely why finitely many [complete one-types](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type) alone cannot replace the all-arities condition in Ryll-Nardzewski.
