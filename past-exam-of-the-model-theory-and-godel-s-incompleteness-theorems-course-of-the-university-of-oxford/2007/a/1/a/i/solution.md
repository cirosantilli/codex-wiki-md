<h1 id="a/1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../../../../../../numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic.md) convention in the [first-order theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md): membership supplies a standard stage with a provable enumerating instance, while nonmembership makes every standard-stage instance refutable. Put $D=\{n:P\vdash E_n(\bar n)\}$. By the definition of $a$, the diagonal instance $E_a(\bar a)$ is exactly $G$.

If $P\vdash G$, then $a\in D$. [Numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../../../../../../numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic.md) supplies a [natural number](../../../../../../natural-number.md) $m$ such that $P\vdash A(\bar a,\bar m)$. But universal instantiation of $G$ gives $P\vdash\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)$. This contradicts [first-order consistency](../../../../../../first-order-consistency.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{P\text{ consistent}\Longrightarrow P\nvdash G.}
$$

This uses the [formal proof](../../../../../../formal-proof.md) obligations in [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../../../../../../numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic.md) in $P$, rather than merely the external assertion that a [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) has a particular complexity.
