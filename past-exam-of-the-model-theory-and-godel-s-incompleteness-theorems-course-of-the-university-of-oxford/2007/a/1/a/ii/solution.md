<h1 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[One-consistency](../../../../../../one-consistency.md) says that no [bounded arithmetic formula](../../../../../../arithmetic-bounded-formula.md) $F(x)$ has a provable existential closure while each of its standard numeral instances is refutable. It implies [first-order consistency](../../../../../../first-order-consistency.md), since an inconsistent classical [first-order theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) proves every [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md).

If $P$ is [one-consistent](../../../../../../one-consistency.md), (i) gives $P\nvdash G$, so $a\notin D$. [Numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../../../../../../numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic.md) therefore gives

$$
P\vdash\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)\qquad\text{for every standard }m.
$$

If $P\vdash\neg G$, classical logic gives $P\vdash\exists v_2 A(\bar a,v_2)$. The matrix is bounded, and the preceding displayed derivations refute every standard instance. This violates [one-consistency](../../../../../../one-consistency.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{P\text{ one-consistent}\Longrightarrow P\nvdash\neg G.}
$$

Together with (i), this yields undecidability of this [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) under the stronger hypothesis, without asserting that ordinary [first-order consistency](../../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) alone suffices for its negative half.
