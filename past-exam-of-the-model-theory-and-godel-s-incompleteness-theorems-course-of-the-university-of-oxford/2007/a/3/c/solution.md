<h1 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

If $S\vdash G$, theoremhood gives $S\vdash\Box G$, while the fixed-point equivalence also gives $S\vdash\neg\Box G$. Therefore [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) implies $S\nvdash G$.

If $S\cup\{\neg G\}$ were inconsistent, the deduction theorem and [classical propositional logic](../../../../../classical-propositional-logic.md) would give $S\vdash\neg\neg G$, hence $S\vdash G$. This is impossible by the preceding argument. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Con}(S)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(S+\neg G).}
$$

This is a syntactic [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) result. It does not claim that the extra axiom is true in the standard [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) or that the extended [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is [sound](../../../../../soundness.md).
