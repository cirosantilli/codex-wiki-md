<h1 id="b/5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [prime model](../../../../../prime-model.md) of a [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) $T$ is a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) admitting an elementary [embedding of first-order structures](../../../../../embedding-of-first-order-structures.md) into every [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$. Let $\mathcal A\models T$ be canonical and $\mathcal B\models T$. For each [closed term](../../../../../closed-term.md) $t$, define

$$
e(t^{\mathcal A})=t^{\mathcal B}.
$$

Every element of $A$ has a name, so this defines the desired map on all of $A$. If two [first-order terms](../../../../../first-order-term.md) agree in $\mathcal A$, their [logical equality](../../../../../logical-equality.md) [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) holds in one [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of the [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) and therefore in every [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of it; they agree in $\mathcal B$. This proves well-definedness. If they are distinct in $\mathcal A$, their inequality holds throughout the [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) as well, proving [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md).

For every [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) $\phi(\vec x)$ and named [tuple](../../../../../finite-tuple.md) $\vec t^{\mathcal A}$, the substituted [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) $\phi(\vec t)$ has the same [truth value](../../../../../truth-value.md) in the two [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) by completeness of $T$. Consequently

$$
\mathcal A\models\phi(\vec t^{\mathcal A})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal B\models\phi(\vec t^{\mathcal B}).
$$

Thus $e$ is elementary. Since $\mathcal B$ was arbitrary, **every such [canonical first-order structure](../../../../../canonical-first-order-structure.md) is prime**. This [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) also handles closed-term generation when [function symbols](../../../../../function-symbol.md) are present; under the constant-name convention one simply uses constants as the [first-order terms](../../../../../first-order-term.md).
