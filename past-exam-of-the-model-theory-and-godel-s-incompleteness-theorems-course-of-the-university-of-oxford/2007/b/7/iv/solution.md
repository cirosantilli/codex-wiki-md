<h1 id="b/7/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Use the [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) with one [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) $E$. Let $\mathcal A$ have exactly two [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md), one countably infinite and one uncountable, with no further [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md). Its [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) says that there are exactly two classes and both are infinite.

This [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) has [quantifier elimination](../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md). To eliminate one existential [logical variable](../../../../../logical-variable.md) from a [conjunction](../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) of [atomic formulas](../../../../../atomic-formula.md) and negated [atomic formulas](../../../../../atomic-formula.md), a [logical equality](../../../../../logical-equality.md) either fixes its value, or finitely many inequalities exclude finitely many points. Its required equivalence-class membership is determined by the finitely many existing classes represented among the parameters. A required unused class exists exactly when fewer than two classes have already been used. Once its class is possible, infinitude supplies a point avoiding the finite exclusions. These tests are quantifier-free [logical equality](../../../../../logical-equality.md) and equivalence conditions on the parameters. [Disjunctive normal form](../../../../../disjunctive-normal-form.md) and [induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) eliminate all [quantifiers](../../../../../quantifier.md) and also show completeness.

Thus a finite [tuple](../../../../../finite-tuple.md)'s [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) is isolated by its finite equality-and-equivalence pattern. Every realized finite-tuple [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) is principal, so $\mathcal A$ is atomic. In particular all individual elements have the same [complete one-type](../../../../../complete-one-type.md), isolated by $x=x$.

Choose $a$ in the [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) class and $b$ in the uncountable class. Any automorphism taking $a$ to $b$ would give a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) from their whole [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md), impossible because their cardinalities differ. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A\text{ is atomic but not homogeneous}.}
$$

Its uncountability is precisely what avoids the conclusion of (iii).
