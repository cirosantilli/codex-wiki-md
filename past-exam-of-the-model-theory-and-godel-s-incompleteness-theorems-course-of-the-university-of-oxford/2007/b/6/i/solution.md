↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) in a fixed [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md), often identified with its deductive closure. It is $\kappa$-categorical if it has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) $\kappa$ and all [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of that [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) are [isomorphic](../../../../../isomorphism.md) as [first-order structures](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) in that same [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md). In particular, an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) must preserve the named constants as well as the order.

A [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is complete if it is [first-order consistent](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) and decides every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md): for each $\sigma$ it entails either $\sigma$ or $\neg\sigma$. Equivalently all its [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) satisfy exactly the same [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md). [First-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) is included to exclude the vacuous [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) with no [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). The distinctions matter here: naming infinitely many ordered points changes [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) categoricity without destroying completeness.
