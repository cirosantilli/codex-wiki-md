<h1 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use two expansions of the rational order. In the first interpret $c_n=n$, so the named sequence is unbounded above. In the second interpret $c_n=-1/n$, so the named sequence is strictly increasing but has upper bounds in the rational order. Both expansions are countably infinite [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of the specified [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md).

An [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) preserving each $c_n$ would preserve the existence of an element greater than all the named constants, since any such element in one [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) would map to such an element in the other. The first [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) has none and the second has, for instance, $0$. They cannot be [isomorphic](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_\omega\text{ is not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

The property of lying above all $c_n$ is an infinite [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) rather than a single [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md). This is why its realization can distinguish the two [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) without contradicting the completeness established in (iv).
