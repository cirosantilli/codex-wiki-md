<h1 id="b/6/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Use the completeness of the [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) of [dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md) and its [quantifier elimination](../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md). After naming finitely many increasing constants, [quantifier elimination](../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) still holds in the expanded [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md): treat these constants as fixed parameters when eliminating [quantifiers](../../../../../quantifier.md). Every [quantifier-free sentence](../../../../../quantifier-free-sentence.md) is a Boolean combination of [logical equalities](../../../../../logical-equality.md) and order comparisons of the named constants, all decided by their stated strict increasing order. Hence $\mathrm{DLO}_n$ is complete.

An alternative [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) of this finite case uses the stated general theorem that a [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) in a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) with only infinite [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) and a unique [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) is complete: if [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) disagreed on a [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md), downward Löwenheim-Skolem would give disagreeing [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). Part (iii) supplies the uniqueness.

Every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) of $L_\omega$ mentions only finitely many [constant symbols](../../../../../constant-symbol.md), hence belongs to some $L_n$. Its truth is decided by $\mathrm{DLO}_n$, and every [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $\mathrm{DLO}_\omega$ has a reduct satisfying that finite [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md). Thus the larger [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) also decides every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md). The rational-order examples in (ii) establish [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_n\text{ and }\mathrm{DLO}_\omega\text{ are complete}.}
$$

There is no [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md) with (ii): completeness is agreement on individual finite [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md), while categoricity requires a global [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) respecting all constants simultaneously.
