<h1 id="b/6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md), the finitely many named constants split the order into the open intervals

$$
(-\infty,c_1),\ (c_1,c_2),\ldots,(c_{n-1},c_n),\ (c_n,\infty),
$$

together with the $n$ named points. Each interval is a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [dense linear order without endpoints](../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md) in its induced order. It is nonempty and infinite by density and the absence of endpoints.

Use Cantor's classification of [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md): each such interval is [isomorphic](../../../../../isomorphism.md) to the rational order. It follows by a [back-and-forth method](../../../../../back-and-forth-method.md) extending finite increasing partial [bijections](../../../../../bijection.md) alternately along enumerations of the two orders. Given two [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $\mathrm{DLO}_n$, choose [isomorphisms](../../../../../isomorphism.md) between their corresponding intervals and send each named point to its counterpart. Their [union](../../../../../set-union.md) is an increasing [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) preserving all constants, hence an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) in $L_n$.

There are [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md), for example any finite increasing list in the rational order. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_n\text{ is }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

For $n=0$, the same argument is just the classification theorem for the whole order.
