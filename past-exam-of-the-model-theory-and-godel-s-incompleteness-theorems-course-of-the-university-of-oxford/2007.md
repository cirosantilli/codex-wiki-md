<h5 id="a/1/a/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

<h6 id="a/1/a/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a/1/a/i)

Use the [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) convention in the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory): membership supplies a standard stage with a provable enumerating instance, while nonmembership makes every standard-stage instance refutable. Put $D=\{n:P\vdash E_n(\bar n)\}$. By the definition of $a$, the diagonal instance $E_a(\bar a)$ is exactly $G$.

If $P\vdash G$, then $a\in D$. [Numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) supplies a [natural number](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) $m$ such that $P\vdash A(\bar a,\bar m)$. But universal instantiation of $G$ gives $P\vdash\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)$. This contradicts [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). Therefore

$$
\boxed{P\text{ consistent}\Longrightarrow P\nvdash G.}
$$

This uses the [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) obligations in [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) in $P$, rather than merely the external assertion that a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) has a particular complexity.

<h5 id="a/1/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

<h6 id="a/1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a/1/a/ii)

[One-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) says that no [bounded arithmetic formula](../computability-theory.md#arithmetic-bounded-formula) $F(x)$ has a provable existential closure while each of its standard numeral instances is refutable. It implies [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), since an inconsistent classical [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) proves every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula).

If $P$ is [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency), (i) gives $P\nvdash G$, so $a\notin D$. [Numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) therefore gives

$$
P\vdash\neg A(\bar a,\bar m)\qquad\text{for every standard }m.
$$

If $P\vdash\neg G$, classical logic gives $P\vdash\exists v_2 A(\bar a,v_2)$. The matrix is bounded, and the preceding displayed derivations refute every standard instance. This violates [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency). Consequently

$$
\boxed{P\text{ one-consistent}\Longrightarrow P\nvdash\neg G.}
$$

Together with (i), this yields undecidability of this [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) under the stronger hypothesis, without asserting that ordinary [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) alone suffices for its negative half.

<h4 id="a/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

[Bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) means that every bounded [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) true in the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) is provable in $S$. If $S$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) and proves a false [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $B$, [bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) also proves its true bounded [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation), a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Conversely, if every bounded theorem is true, $S$ cannot be inconsistent, because inconsistency would prove the false [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) $0=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Con}(S)\iff\Sigma_0\text{-soundness}.}
$$

If $S$ is [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) and proves $\exists xB(x)$ with bounded matrix, that [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) cannot be false: otherwise every $\neg B(\bar n)$ would be a true [bounded arithmetic sentence](../computability-theory.md#bounded-arithmetic-sentence) and hence provable, contrary to [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency). Thus $S$ has [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness).

Conversely, [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness) excludes inconsistency. If $S\vdash\exists xB(x)$, it supplies a standard witness $n$ with $B(n)$ true. [Bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) proves $B(\bar n)$, so [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) forbids a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of $\neg B(\bar n)$. Hence there cannot simultaneously be refutations of all standard instances. Therefore

$$
\boxed{1\text{-consistency}\iff\Sigma_1\text{-soundness}.}
$$

Finite blocks of existential [logical variables](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) are treated as finite [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) of witnesses, with the same argument.

<h4 id="a/1/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c)

[Omega-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#omega-consistency-of-an-arithmetic-theory) forbids a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $B(x)$ of any complexity for which $S\vdash\exists xB(x)$ and $S\vdash\neg B(\bar n)$ for every standard $n$. The difference from [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) is that the matrix is unrestricted. It implies [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency).

For a complete [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) classical [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), theorem membership obeys the ordinary Boolean [truth tables](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-table). For example, it proves a [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) exactly when it proves both conjuncts; completeness and [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) also make theoremhood of a [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) the complement of theoremhood of the [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). [Omega-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#omega-consistency-of-an-arithmetic-theory) additionally gives the witness property

$$
S\vdash\exists xB(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\vdash B(\bar n)\text{ for some standard }n.
$$

The reverse implication is existential introduction. For the forward implication, if no standard instance were provable, completeness would give a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of each negated instance, violating [omega-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#omega-consistency-of-an-arithmetic-theory). This works also after replacing additional [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable) by numerals.

Now induct on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with their remaining [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable) replaced by standard numerals. At the atomic level, the equivalence between theoremhood and standard truth follows from [bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) and bounded [soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) from (b). The Boolean steps use the truth-table facts just proved. The existential step uses the witness property and the [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) hypothesis on its matrix; [universal quantification](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-quantification) follows by [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). Hence

$$
\boxed{S\vdash X\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb N\models X}
$$

for every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $X$. Completeness ensures it decides each [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), and this equivalence ensures every decision is correct.

<h4 id="a/1/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/1/d)

Start with the supplied bounded-complete, [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $T$ proving a false [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $F$. Replace it by its deductive closure, which preserves these properties and contains $F$. Enumerate the [arithmetic](../arithmetic.md) [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) as $X_0,X_1,\ldots$. Recursively extend the current [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) by either $X_n$ or $\neg X_n$, choosing an extension which remains [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) as permitted by the supplied lemma, and then take its deductive closure. Call the resulting increasing sequence $T_n$, with $T_0$ the initial [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), and put $S=\bigcup_nT_n$.

Every stage is still bounded-complete. By (b), every stage has [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness). If $S$ proved a false Sigma-1 [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), the finite [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) would use assumptions from one stage $T_n$, contradicting its [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness). Thus $S$ has [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness) and is bounded-complete, so (b) makes it [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency).

This [soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) argument is essential: the definition of [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) involves infinitely many standard refutations, so it would be insufficient merely to say that a violation has a finite [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof). What has a finite [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) is the false [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence), and [bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) is what turns [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) into the needed [soundness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) condition.

The [union](../set.md#set-union) is deductively closed because each [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) has finitely many assumptions. It contains either $X$ or $\neg X$ for every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), by the [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic), and contains the false [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $F$. If it were omega-consistent, (c) would make it correct on all [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), contradicting $F$. We have therefore constructed

$$
\boxed{S\text{ complete and one-consistent, but not omega-consistent}.}
$$

[Omega-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#omega-consistency-of-an-arithmetic-theory) always implies [one-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) by restricting its matrices. This counterexample proves that the converse is strictly false.

A [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) separates $A$ from $B$ in $S$ if it is provable at the standard numeral [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) of every member of $A$ and refutable at that of every member of $B$:

$$
\vec n\in A\Longrightarrow S\vdash F(\bar{\vec n}),\qquad
\vec n\in B\Longrightarrow S\vdash\neg F(\bar{\vec n}).
$$

There is no obligation on [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) outside the two [sets](../set.md). In a [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) these two obligations make $A$ and $B$ disjoint.

Let $h$ be the [Gödel number](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-number) of $H(v_1)$ and take the literal diagonal [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $R=H(\bar h)$. If $S\vdash R$, then $h$ belongs to the second source [set](../set.md), so separation gives $S\vdash\neg H(\bar h)$, a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). If $S\vdash\neg R$, then $h$ belongs to the first source [set](../set.md), so separation gives $S\vdash H(\bar h)$, again a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Hence

$$
\boxed{S\text{ consistent}\Longrightarrow S\nvdash R\text{ and }S\nvdash\neg R.}
$$

The direction of separation matters: here $H$ is provable on refutable diagonal codes and refutable on provable diagonal codes.

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

Write $H(\vec v)$ for the proposed stage-comparison [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and fix a standard [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec a$. If $\vec a\in A$, [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) supplies a standard $m$ with $S\vdash F(\bar{\vec a},\bar m)$. Disjointness gives $\vec a\notin B$, so $S\vdash\neg G(\bar{\vec a},\bar j)$ for every standard $j$, in particular $j=0,\ldots,m$. The source's finite initial-segment axiom combines this finite list into

$$
S\vdash\forall y\le\bar m\,\neg G(\bar{\vec a},y).
$$

Use $\bar m$ as the [existential witness](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-witness) to obtain $S\vdash H(\bar{\vec a})$.

If instead $\vec a\in B$, choose $n$ with $S\vdash G(\bar{\vec a},\bar n)$. Disjointness gives refutations of $F(\bar{\vec a},\bar j)$ for all $j$, so the finitely many $j\le n$ give

$$
S\vdash\forall x\le\bar n\,\neg F(\bar{\vec a},x).
$$

For an arbitrary $x$, the source's comparison axiom gives $x\le\bar n$ or $\bar n\le x$. In the first case the first conjunct of the proposed witness condition is false. In the second case $y=\bar n$ witnesses failure of its bounded universal conjunct, since $G(\bar{\vec a},\bar n)$ is provable. Generalization over $x$ therefore gives $S\vdash\neg H(\bar{\vec a})$.

Thus **the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) separates $A$ from $B$**. The [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) uses the enumerators' numeralwise positive and negative [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) properties and the two explicit order principles supplied in the source; it does not assume the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) can decide arbitrary unbounded existential [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula).

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/2/c)

A [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $D(\vec x,y)$ weakly defines a total numerical [function](../function.md) $f$ in $S$ if, for every standard input [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec n$ and $m=f(\vec n)$,

$$
S\vdash D(\bar{\vec n},\bar m),\qquad
S\vdash\neg D(\bar{\vec n},\bar r)\quad(r\ne m).
$$

[Strong definability of a numerical function](../mathematical-logic.md#strong-definability-of-a-numerical-function) additionally requires the internal uniqueness statement

$$
S\vdash\forall y\,(D(\bar{\vec n},y)\to y=\bar m).
$$

Together with the positive instance, this is equivalent to proving $\forall y(D(\bar{\vec n},y)\leftrightarrow y=\bar m)$ for each standard input. The [universal quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-quantification) is the extra strength: it covers nonstandard potential outputs in [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $S$. These are the weak and strong [function](../function.md) conventions used here. [Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems, printed page99, definition of weak and [strong definability of a numerical function](../mathematical-logic.md#strong-definability-of-a-numerical-function) of [functions](../function.md)](https://www.brucewatkins.org/math/docs/Smullyan.pdf)

Suppose the graph of the total [function](../function.md) is Sigma-1. Its complement is also Sigma-1, since

$$
f(\vec x)\ne y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists z\,(f(\vec x)=z\wedge z\ne y).
$$

Replace the graph assertion on the right by its Sigma-1 definition; [existential quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-quantification) can be collected into one block. [Bounded completeness of an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) supplies numeralwise stage enumerators for each of these two disjoint complementary [sets](../set.md). Apply (b) to them. Its separating [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) is provable exactly at the required graph [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) in the positive direction and refutable at all the other standard [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple), proving [weak definability of a numerical function](../mathematical-logic.md#weak-definability-of-a-numerical-function).

Using the graph [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) alone would only supply positive instances from true existential witnesses; the separation construction is what supplies the negative-instance [formal proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) without assuming [Sigma-1 soundness](../mathematical-logic.md#sigma-1-soundness) or completeness for false Sigma-1 statements.

Let $d(n)$ be the [Gödel number](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-number) of the diagonal [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) obtained by substituting $\bar n$ into the one-free-variable [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with code $n$, with an arbitrary default value on noncodes. By the assumptions and (c), this total [function](../function.md) has a strong defining [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $D(x,y)$.

For the specified $F$, form the one-variable [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula)

$$
H(x)=\exists y\,(D(x,y)\wedge F(y)).
$$

Let $h$ be the [Gödel number](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-number) of $H$ and put $C=H(\bar h)$. Then $d(h)=\ulcorner C\urcorner$. [Strong definability of a numerical function](../mathematical-logic.md#strong-definability-of-a-numerical-function) at this particular standard input gives

$$
S\vdash\forall y\,[D(\bar h,y)\leftrightarrow y=\overline{\ulcorner C\urcorner}].
$$

Substitution into the existential definition yields

$$
\boxed{S\vdash C\leftrightarrow F(\overline{\ulcorner C\urcorner}).}
$$

The permitted equivalence between the source's two substitution notations identifies this with its requested fixed point. [Bound variables](../mathematical-logic.md#bound-variable) are renamed when necessary before forming $H$, so the substitution is capture-free. This constructs one particular [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) from the code of the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) which applies $F$ to its own diagonal output.

<h4 id="a/3/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/3/a)

Write $\Box A$ for $P(\ulcorner A\urcorner)$. A standard [arithmetical provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) obeys the [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions):

$$
S\vdash A\Longrightarrow S\vdash\Box A,\qquad
S\vdash\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B),\qquad
S\vdash\Box A\to\Box\Box A.
$$

The first condition is a rule about theorems; the latter two are statements proved within the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory). When the terminology also includes external adequacy, require that $P(\bar n)$ be true in the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) exactly when the [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with code $n$ has an $S$-proof.

For [Peano arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#peano-arithmetic), code finite strings, [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) and [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) sequences by [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). [Formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) checking is effective and has an arithmetized bounded checking relation with its computation data. Existentially assert the existence of a correct [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) code and any required checking data. The resulting [predicate symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#predicate-symbol) is Sigma-1, and its standard truth means exactly that an actual finite PA [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) exists. Effective operations on [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) codes combine an implication [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) with an antecedent [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof), and turn a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) into a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) that its code is a [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof); PA verifies those constructions. They give distribution and [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection), while an actual theorem's finite [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) gives its internal provability assertion. This is the requested rough construction, with the three internal [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) properties distinguished from external agreement.

Hence an externally [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) PA does not prove its own [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency).

<h3 id="b/5">5</h3>

<h4 id="b/5/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/5/i)

A [canonical first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#canonical-first-order-structure) is generated by its names: every element is the value of a [closed term](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term). In the function-free setting permitted here, this means every element is the interpretation of a [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol). Equivalently, the natural map from [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) modulo [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) in the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) onto its carrier is surjective. When canonical is defined specifically using constant names, the same definition and all the arguments below apply in this function-free setting.

A [full set of first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#full-set-of-first-order-sentences) has the witness property in its own [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language): whenever it proves an [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) $\exists x\phi(x)$, some [closed term](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) $t$ satisfies that the [set](../set.md) proves $\phi(t)$. In a deductively closed [set](../set.md), one can equivalently state this using membership of the [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). In the function-free setting the witness [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) is a [constant symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol). This is the [Henkin witness property](../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-witness-property); fullness does not by itself replace the separate requirements of [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) and completeness.

<h4 id="b/5/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/ii)

A sufficient condition is that $T$ be [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency), complete, and full in its own [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). Work in the allowed function-free setting. Since $T$ proves the logically valid [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $\exists x(x=x)$, fullness guarantees at least one constant and a nonempty collection of names.

Form the carrier from [equivalence classes](../set-theory.md#equivalence-class) $[c]$ of [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol) under

$$
c\sim d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\vdash c=d.
$$

[Logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) logic makes this an [equivalence relation](../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation). Interpret a constant by its class and interpret each relation symbol by

$$
R([c_1],\ldots,[c_n])\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T\vdash R(c_1,\ldots,c_n).
$$

Substitution of equals makes this independent of representatives. This defines a [canonical first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#canonical-first-order-structure) because every carrier element is named by a constant.

The truth lemma, proved by [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) with constants substituted for their [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable), says

$$
\mathcal M\models\phi([\vec c])\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\vdash\phi(\vec c).
$$

[Atomic formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula) hold by construction; completeness and [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) give the Boolean steps. For the existential step, a witness in the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) is a named class, so its truth gives a proved instance and hence a proved [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence). Conversely a proved [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) has a constant witness by fullness, and the [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) hypothesis realizes that instance in the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure). The universal case follows by [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). Thus $\mathcal M\models T$, proving

$$
\boxed{\text{Consistent complete full theories have canonical first-order structures}.}
$$

The witness condition is needed in the original [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language); adding new constants produces a [canonical first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#canonical-first-order-structure) in an expanded [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language), which is a different assertion.

<h4 id="b/5/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/5/iii)

A [prime model](../model-theory.md#prime-model) of a [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) $T$ is a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) admitting an elementary [embedding of first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#embedding-of-first-order-structures) into every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$. Let $\mathcal A\models T$ be canonical and $\mathcal B\models T$. For each [closed term](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) $t$, define

$$
e(t^{\mathcal A})=t^{\mathcal B}.
$$

Every element of $A$ has a name, so this defines the desired map on all of $A$. If two [first-order terms](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) agree in $\mathcal A$, their [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) holds in one [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) and therefore in every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of it; they agree in $\mathcal B$. This proves well-definedness. If they are distinct in $\mathcal A$, their inequality holds throughout the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) as well, proving [injectivity](../algebra.md#injective-function).

For every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\phi(\vec x)$ and named [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec t^{\mathcal A}$, the substituted [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) $\phi(\vec t)$ has the same [truth value](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value) in the two [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) by completeness of $T$. Consequently

$$
\mathcal A\models\phi(\vec t^{\mathcal A})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal B\models\phi(\vec t^{\mathcal B}).
$$

Thus $e$ is elementary. Since $\mathcal B$ was arbitrary, **every such [canonical first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#canonical-first-order-structure) is prime**. This [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) also handles closed-term generation when [function symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol) are present; under the constant-name convention one simply uses constants as the [first-order terms](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term).

<h3 id="b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/6/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/i)

A [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is a [set](../set.md) of [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) in a fixed [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language), often identified with its deductive closure. It is $\kappa$-categorical if it has a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$ and all [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of that [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) are [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism) as [first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) in that same [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language). In particular, an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) must preserve the named constants as well as the order.

A [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is complete if it is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) and decides every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence): for each $\sigma$ it entails either $\sigma$ or $\neg\sigma$. Equivalently all its [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) satisfy exactly the same [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). [First-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) is included to exclude the vacuous [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) with no [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). The distinctions matter here: naming infinitely many ordered points changes [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) categoricity without destroying completeness.

<h4 id="b/6/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/ii)

Use two expansions of the rational order. In the first interpret $c_n=n$, so the named sequence is unbounded above. In the second interpret $c_n=-1/n$, so the named sequence is strictly increasing but has upper bounds in the rational order. Both expansions are countably infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of the specified [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory).

An [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) preserving each $c_n$ would preserve the existence of an element greater than all the named constants, since any such element in one [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) would map to such an element in the other. The first [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) has none and the second has, for instance, $0$. They cannot be [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_\omega\text{ is not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

The property of lying above all $c_n$ is an infinite [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) rather than a single [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). This is why its realization can distinguish the two [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) without contradicting the completeness established in (iv).

<h4 id="b/6/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/iii)

In a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory), the finitely many named constants split the order into the open intervals

$$
(-\infty,c_1),\ (c_1,c_2),\ldots,(c_{n-1},c_n),\ (c_n,\infty),
$$

together with the $n$ named points. Each interval is a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense linear order without endpoints](../model-theory.md#dense-linear-order-without-endpoints) in its induced order. It is nonempty and infinite by density and the absence of endpoints.

Use Cantor's classification of [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [dense linear orders without endpoints](../model-theory.md#dense-linear-order-without-endpoints): each such interval is [isomorphic](../algebra.md#isomorphism) to the rational order. It follows by a [back-and-forth method](../model-theory.md#back-and-forth-method) extending finite increasing partial [bijections](../function.md#bijection) alternately along enumerations of the two orders. Given two [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $\mathrm{DLO}_n$, choose [isomorphisms](../algebra.md#isomorphism) between their corresponding intervals and send each named point to its counterpart. Their [union](../set.md#set-union) is an increasing [bijection](../function.md#bijection) preserving all constants, hence an [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) in $L_n$.

There are [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory), for example any finite increasing list in the rational order. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_n\text{ is }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

For $n=0$, the same argument is just the classification theorem for the whole order.

<h4 id="b/6/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/6/iv)

Use the completeness of the [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) of [dense linear orders without endpoints](../model-theory.md#dense-linear-order-without-endpoints) and its [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination). After naming finitely many increasing constants, [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) still holds in the expanded [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language): treat these constants as fixed parameters when eliminating [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier). Every [quantifier-free sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier-free-sentence) is a Boolean combination of [logical equalities](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) and order comparisons of the named constants, all decided by their stated strict increasing order. Hence $\mathrm{DLO}_n$ is complete.

An alternative [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof) of this finite case uses the stated general theorem that a [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) with only infinite [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) and a unique [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is complete: if [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) disagreed on a [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), downward Löwenheim-Skolem would give disagreeing [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Part (iii) supplies the uniqueness.

Every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) of $L_\omega$ mentions only finitely many [constant symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#constant-symbol), hence belongs to some $L_n$. Its truth is decided by $\mathrm{DLO}_n$, and every [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $\mathrm{DLO}_\omega$ has a reduct satisfying that finite [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory). Thus the larger [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) also decides every [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence). The rational-order examples in (ii) establish [first-order consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DLO}_n\text{ and }\mathrm{DLO}_\omega\text{ are complete}.}
$$

There is no [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) with (ii): completeness is agreement on individual finite [first-order sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence), while categoricity requires a global [isomorphism](../algebra.md#isomorphism) respecting all constants simultaneously.

<h3 id="b/7">7</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/7/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/7/i)

An $n$-type over a [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $T$ is a [set](../set.md) of [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) in the displayed $n$ [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable) [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) with $T$. A [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) chooses exactly one of $\phi$ and $\neg\phi$ for each such [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). Equivalently it is a [maximal consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#maximal-consistent-set-of-first-order-sentences) such [set](../set.md). For parameter-free [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) the [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) use the original [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language); [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) over parameters use additional names for those parameters.

A [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) $p(\vec x)$ is principal if some [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta(\vec x)$ [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) with $T$ implies every member of $p$ modulo $T$:

$$
T\models\forall\vec x\,(\theta(\vec x)\to\phi(\vec x))\qquad(\phi\in p).
$$

Such a $\theta$ is a [principal formula of a type](../mathematical-logic.md#principal-formula-of-a-type) of the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type). For a [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) it isolates that [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type), so [principal complete types](../model-theory.md#principal-complete-type) are exactly [isolated types](../model-theory.md#isolated-type).

For a [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec a$ in a [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) $\mathcal A$, its parameter-free [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal A}(\vec a)=
\{\phi(\vec x):\mathcal A\models\phi(\vec a)\}.}
$$

It is a [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) over $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal A)$ because the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) decides the truth of every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) at that [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple).

<h4 id="b/7/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/7/ii)

Let $\vec a\in A^n$, with $A$ finite, and put $T=\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal A)$. There are only finitely many competing [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec b\in A^n$. For each whose [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) differs from that of $\vec a$, choose a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\phi_{\vec b}$ true at $\vec a$ and false at $\vec b$. Such a [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) exists by the inequality of their [complete types](../model-theory.md#complete-type), negating a distinguishing [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) if necessary.

Let $\theta$ be the [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) of this finite list, or [logical truth](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-truth) if the list is empty. It is true at $\vec a$, and every [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) satisfying it has the same [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) as $\vec a$. For each $\psi\in\operatorname{tp}(\vec a)$, the [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence)

$$
\forall\vec x\,(\theta(\vec x)\to\psi(\vec x))
$$

is therefore true in $\mathcal A$ and belongs to $T$. The [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) $\theta$ is [first-order consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-consistency) with $T$ because it is realized at $\vec a$. It is consequently a [principal formula of a type](../mathematical-logic.md#principal-formula-of-a-type) of the [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type), proving **every [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) realized in a finite [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) is principal**. Finiteness of the [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure), rather than finiteness of the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language), is what makes the [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) finite.

<h4 id="b/7/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/7/iii)

Every automorphism preserves all [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) by [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on their construction, so an automorphism taking $\vec a$ to $\vec b$ necessarily gives equal [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type).

For the converse, first note that a finite [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) is atomic by (ii). Thus in both cases every realized finite-tuple [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) has an isolating [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula). Suppose two finite [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec u,\vec v$ have the same [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type), and choose a new element $x$ to extend the first [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple). Let $\theta(\vec z,w)$ isolate the realized [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) of $(\vec u,x)$. Since

$$
\mathcal A\models\exists w\,\theta(\vec u,w),
$$

[logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) of [partial types](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) gives $\mathcal A\models\exists w\,\theta(\vec v,w)$. Choose a witness $y$. Isolation ensures $(\vec u,x)$ and $(\vec v,y)$ have the same [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type). In particular, if $x$ is distinct from the entries already used, their inequality [logical formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) ensure $y$ is distinct from its corresponding entries. The resulting finite map is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) and partial elementary.

Start from $\vec a\mapsto\vec b$, which is well-defined even for repeated coordinates because their [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) patterns agree. Enumerate the carrier and extend alternately to include the next missing element in the domain and the next missing element in the range. For a range step apply the same extension argument to the inverse finite map. The [union](../set.md#set-union) is an elementary [bijection](../function.md#bijection) from $A$ onto itself: every finite [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) and every [logical formula](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-formula) involving it is handled at some stage. It is therefore an automorphism taking $\vec a$ to $\vec b$. In the finite case the construction terminates once all elements have been included.

Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tp}(\vec a)=\operatorname{tp}(\vec b)
\iff\exists\pi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A),\ \pi(\vec a)=\vec b.}
$$

Countability makes the back-and-forth [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) exhaust the entire [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure); it cannot simply be dropped.

<h4 id="b/7/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [7](#b/7)

<h5 id="b/7/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/7/iv)

Use the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) with one [equivalence relation](../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation) $E$. Let $\mathcal A$ have exactly two [equivalence classes](../set-theory.md#equivalence-class), one countably infinite and one uncountable, with no further [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure). Its [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) says that there are exactly two classes and both are infinite.

This [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) has [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination). To eliminate one existential [logical variable](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-variable) from a [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) of [atomic formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula) and negated [atomic formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula), a [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) either fixes its value, or finitely many inequalities exclude finitely many points. Its required equivalence-class membership is determined by the finitely many existing classes represented among the parameters. A required unused class exists exactly when fewer than two classes have already been used. Once its class is possible, infinitude supplies a point avoiding the finite exclusions. These tests are quantifier-free [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality) and equivalence conditions on the parameters. [Disjunctive normal form](../theoretical-computer-science.md#disjunctive-normal-form) and [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) eliminate all [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) and also show completeness.

Thus a finite [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple)'s [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) is isolated by its finite equality-and-equivalence pattern. Every realized finite-tuple [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) is principal, so $\mathcal A$ is atomic. In particular all individual elements have the same [complete one-type](../model-theory.md#complete-one-type), isolated by $x=x$.

Choose $a$ in the [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) class and $b$ in the uncountable class. Any automorphism taking $a$ to $b$ would give a [bijection](../function.md#bijection) from their whole [equivalence classes](../set-theory.md#equivalence-class), impossible because their cardinalities differ. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A\text{ is atomic but not homogeneous}.}
$$

Its uncountability is precisely what avoids the conclusion of (iii).

<h3 id="b/8">8</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/8/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/8/i)

Here $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A$ means a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure), so the smaller carrier contains all named constants, is closed under every [function symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol), and has each relation restricted from the larger [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure). [First-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) evaluation on [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) from $B$ agrees in the two [first-order structures](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure). Consequently [atomic formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#atomic-formula) agree, and [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on Boolean connectives makes every [quantifier-free formula](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier-free-formula) agree as well.

Write an arbitrary [universal sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-sentence) of $T$ as $\forall\vec x\,\theta(\vec x)$ with quantifier-free matrix. Every [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) from $B$ is also a [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) from $A$, so $\theta$ holds at that [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) in $\mathcal A$. Quantifier-free agreement transfers it to $\mathcal B$. Since the [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) was arbitrary, $\mathcal B$ satisfies the [universal sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-sentence). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A\models T,\ \mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A
\Longrightarrow\mathcal B\models T.}
$$

An arbitrary [subset](../set.md#subset) without the [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) operations and relation restrictions would not be the hypothesis used by this [formal proof](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-proof).

<h4 id="b/8/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/8/ii)

The [set](../set.md) of closed-term values is nonempty because the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language) has a constant. It contains the value of every constant. If $f$ is an $r$-ary [function symbol](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol) and $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ are [closed terms](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term), then

$$
f^{\mathcal A}(t_1^{\mathcal A},\ldots,t_r^{\mathcal A})
=(f(t_1,\ldots,t_r))^{\mathcal A},
$$

which is another closed-term value. Hence the [set](../set.md) is closed under every operation. Restrict the operations and relations of $\mathcal A$ to it; this defines a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) $\mathcal B$ whose carrier is exactly the specified [set](../set.md).

Part (i) now applies to the universal [first-order theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), giving

$$
\boxed{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A,\qquad\mathcal B\models T.}
$$

It is the [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) generated by the [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language)'s constants, often called the [ground-term substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#ground-term-substructure). The source's constant-symbol assumption ensures its nonemptiness, even when no other elements can be generated.

<h4 id="b/8/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [8](#b/8)

<h5 id="b/8/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/8/iii)

Adjoin to $T$ the [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) of all closed-term instances of $\theta$. If the resulting [set](../set.md) had a [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $\mathcal A$, its [ground-term substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#ground-term-substructure) $\mathcal B$ from (ii) would still [first-order model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $T$. The assumed existential consequence therefore supplies a [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) $\vec b$ in $\mathcal B$ satisfying $\theta$. Each $b_j$ is the value of a [closed term](../mathematical-logic.md#closed-term) $t_j$.

Quantifier-free agreement between a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) and its ambient [first-order structure](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-structure) gives

$$
\mathcal A\models\theta(t_1,\ldots,t_k),
$$

contradicting the adjoined [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) of this very ground instance. Thus

$$
T\cup\{\neg\theta(t_1,\ldots,t_k):t_1,\ldots,t_k\text{ closed terms}\}
$$

is unsatisfiable. [Compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) gives a finite unsatisfiable [subset](../set.md#subset). Its finitely many ground [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) imply that, for some finite list of [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term) [tuples](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple),

$$
\boxed{T\models\bigvee_{i=1}^m\theta(t_{i1},\ldots,t_{ik}).}
$$

If the finite [subset](../set.md#subset) already shows $T$ itself inconsistent and supplies no ground [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation), choose any one closed-term [tuple](../set-theory.md#finite-tuple) using the available constant; then any [first-order sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) is a consequence of $T$, so $m=1$ suffices. Otherwise the selected list is nonempty. This also explains why the conclusion contains a finite disjunction rather than necessarily one universal choice of a witness [first-order term](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-term).
