<h1 id="b/5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the vector-space [axioms](../../../../../../axiom.md): addition is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) operation with identity zero; $\lambda_1(x)=x$; $\lambda_a\lambda_b(x)=\lambda_{ab}(x)$; $\lambda_a(x+y)=\lambda_a(x)+\lambda_a(y)$; and $\lambda_{a+b}(x)=\lambda_a(x)+\lambda_b(x)$, with $\lambda_0(x)=0$. [Group](../../../../../../group-split.md) inverses may be expressed by $\lambda_{-1}$, so no extra inverse symbol is needed.

Add the dimension-unbounded scheme

$$
\boxed{D_n:\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_n\operatorname{LI}_n(\bar x)\qquad(n\ge1).}
$$

Over a [finite field](../../../../../../finite-field.md), a finite-dimensional space has finitely many [Elements](../../../../../../euclid-s-elements.md), while every infinite-dimensional space is infinite. Hence this scheme axiomatizes exactly the infinite underlying [vector spaces](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) requested. In this finite-field case the vector-space [axiom](../../../../../../axiom.md) list itself is finite; the infinitude of the additional [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) scheme is substantive.
