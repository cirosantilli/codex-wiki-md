<h1 id="b/5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For each fixed finite tuple length $n$, use the finite [conjunction](../../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{LI}_n(\bar x)=\bigwedge_{\bar a\in K^n\setminus\{0\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{a_j}(x_j)\ne0\right).}
$$

It asserts that no nontrivial [scalar](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) combination vanishes, exactly [linear independence](../../../../../../linear-independence.md). It is an $L$-formula because the [field](../../../../../../field.md) is finite and every [scalar multiplication](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) is named. [Scalar coefficients](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) are indices of [function symbols](../../../../../../function-symbol.md), not variables ranging over a separate [field](../../../../../../field.md) sort. There is no single finite formula with arbitrarily many [free variables](../../../../../../free-variable.md); the assertion is a formula for each prescribed finite $n$.
