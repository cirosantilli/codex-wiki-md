<h1 id="b/5/c/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

For [quantifier elimination for infinite underlying vector spaces](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination-for-infinite-underlying-vector-spaces.md), we first justify that formulas cannot make finer distinctions than their linear-equation data. In any infinite underlying [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md), eliminate an existential variable from a disjunction of [conjunctions](../../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) of [linear equations](../../../../../../linear-equation.md) and inequations. If an equation has a nonzero [coefficient](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) of that variable, solve for it and substitute. If none does, the inequations forbid only finitely many values, leaving an available value in the infinite domain; the remaining equations concern only the other variables. Terms are [linear combinations](../../../../../../linear-combination.md), so induction gives [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) for $T$ and completeness, since closed terms reduce to zero.

For one variable there are two possible [complete types](../../../../../../complete-type.md), zero and nonzero. Both are isolated, and every definable [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is their [union](../../../../../../set-union.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{F_1/E_1(T)\cong\mathcal P(\{1,2\}),\qquad |F_1/E_1(T)|=4.}
$$

For [types of pairs of vectors over a finite field](../../../../../../types-of-pairs-of-vectors-over-a-finite-field.md), the [linear relation](../../../../../../linear-relation.md) kernel $\{(a,b)\in K^2:ax+by=0\}$ determines the [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md). If $q=|K|$, the possibilities are one rank-zero pair, $q+1$ rank-one directions, and one rank-two independent type. Concretely these are $(0,0)$; $x\ne0$ with $y=\lambda_a(x)$ for each $a\in K$; $x=0,y\ne0$; and the independent pairs. Their isolating formulas are finite because $K$ is finite. Hence

$$
\boxed{F_2/E_2(T)\cong\mathcal P(\{1,\ldots,q+3\}),\qquad |F_2/E_2(T)|=2^{q+3}.}
$$

Every class is the [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) of a selection of these [atoms](../../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md). [Basis extension](../../../../../../basis-extension.md) also shows that pairs with the same relation kernel are conjugate by a [linear automorphism](../../../../../../linear-isomorphism.md) within any infinite-dimensional [model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md).
