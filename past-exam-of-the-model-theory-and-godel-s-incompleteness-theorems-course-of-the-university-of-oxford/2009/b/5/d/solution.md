<h1 id="b/5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [cardinality of a vector space](../../../../../cardinality-of-a-vector-space.md) is central: here “infinite” must continue to refer to the underlying [set](../../../../../set-split.md). Over a countably infinite [field](../../../../../field.md), every nonzero finite-dimensional space is already infinite. Thus the [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) class now consists of all nonzero $K$-vector spaces, not only infinite-dimensional ones.

Since [independence of a pair is not definable over an infinite field](../../../../../independence-of-a-pair-is-not-definable-over-an-infinite-field.md), for (i), independence of a single vector is still $x\ne0$, but independence of tuples of length at least two is not definable by one formula in this one-sorted language. [Quantifier elimination](../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) follows from the linear-equation argument in the preceding part for all these infinite domains. Any candidate two-variable formula therefore involves only finitely many [linear equations](../../../../../linear-equation.md). Take an independent pair, and compare it with $(x,\lambda_a x)$, where $x\ne0$ and $a\in K$ avoids the finitely many ratios making one of those nontrivial equations vanish. Both pairs satisfy the same listed atomic formulas, contradicting a proposed definition of independence. Longer tuple independence would define pair independence by existentially completing a pair inside an infinite-dimensional [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md), giving the same obstruction.

For (ii), the vector-space [axiom](../../../../../axiom.md) schema together with $\exists x(x\ne0)$ axiomatizes the infinite underlying spaces. For (iii), there is again no universal axiomatization, because the zero subspace is a [substructure](../../../../../substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md).

For [finite axiomatizability with infinitely many scalar symbols](../../../../../finite-axiomatizability-with-infinitely-many-scalar-symbols.md), in (iv) there is no [finite axiomatization](../../../../../finite-axiomatizability.md) in the full language: any finite [axiom](../../../../../axiom.md) [set](../../../../../set-split.md) mentions only finitely many [scalar](../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) symbols. In a nonzero [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) alter an unused nonzero $\lambda_a$ to send zero to a nonzero vector, retaining all mentioned interpretations. The finite sentences keep their [truth values](../../../../../truth-value.md), but a vector-space [axiom](../../../../../axiom.md) is now false. **Relative to the already imposed vector-space [axioms](../../../../../axiom.md)**, the extra condition is finitely axiomatized by the single nonzero-vector sentence. These absolute and relative answers must be distinguished.

For (v), the one-variable algebra still has two [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md) and four [Elements](../../../../../euclid-s-elements.md). In two variables the isolated [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md) are $(0,0)$ and the countably many rank-one directions, represented by $x\ne0,y=\lambda_a x$ and by $x=0,y\ne0$. There is also a nonisolated [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) of an independent pair. A formula using finitely many [linear equations](../../../../../linear-equation.md) either picks finitely many isolated [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md), or contains that independent type and all but finitely many isolated [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md). Therefore **$F_2/E_2(T)$ is the [finite-cofinite Boolean algebra](../../../../../finite-cofinite-boolean-algebra.md) on a countably [infinite set](../../../../../infinite-set.md) of [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md)**, with the independent type the extra nonisolated point in its Stone space. In particular this algebra is countably infinite, rather than the full [power set](../../../../../power-set.md) of its [atoms](../../../../../atom-of-a-boolean-algebra.md).

For (vi), [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) can be $1,2,3,\ldots$ or $\aleph_0$ while the domain is [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md), producing pairwise nonisomorphic [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). Thus $T$ is not countably categorical. For an uncountable $\kappa$, a [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) over this [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [field](../../../../../field.md) has [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) $\kappa$ exactly when its [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) is $\kappa$, since in general a nonzero space has size $\max(|K|,\dim V)$ when that maximum is infinite. [Basis](../../../../../basis.md) [bijections](../../../../../bijection.md) then give **categoricity in every uncountable cardinal**. [Quantifier elimination](../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) also shows that these different positive [dimensions](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) are [elementarily equivalent](../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md); independence of an arbitrarily long tuple is not being silently treated as definable over this infinite [field](../../../../../field.md).
