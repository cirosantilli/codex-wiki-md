<h1 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is $\kappa$-categorical if it has a [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) $\kappa$ and any two [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of that [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) are isomorphic. The existence clause prevents vacuous categoricity at cardinalities where there is no [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md).

To show that [infinite-cardinal categoricity forces completeness](../../../../../infinite-cardinal-categoricity-forces-completeness.md), suppose $T$ were incomplete. Then some sentence $\sigma$ has both $T+\sigma$ and $T+\neg\sigma$ satisfiable, by completeness of [first-order logic](../../../../../first-order-logic.md). Their [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) must be infinite because $T$ has no finite [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). For an infinite $\kappa\ge|L|$, the upward and downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorems](../../../../../lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) produce a [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of each extension of [cardinality](../../../../../cardinality.md) exactly $\kappa$: pass to an elementary submodel of size at most $\max(|L|,\aleph_0)$ and then to the desired size, or reduce a larger [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) to that size. Categoricity makes these two [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) isomorphic, but one satisfies $\sigma$ and the other its [negation](../../../../../negation.md). [Contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md). Therefore **$T$ is complete**. If language size is defined without automatically including $\aleph_0$, the infinite-cardinal qualification is explicit; the assumed existence of a $\kappa$-sized [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) and absence of finite [models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) already force it here.
