<h1 id="b/6/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For [cut types over the named rational order](../../../../../../cut-types-over-the-named-rational-order.md), choose an irrational real cut, for example $r=\sqrt2$, and form

$$
p_r(x)=\{c_q<x:q\in\mathbb Q,\ q<r\}\cup\{x<c_q:q\in\mathbb Q,\ q>r\}.
$$

Every finite [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) is realized by a rational between its finite lower and [upper bounds](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), so compactness makes it a [consistent](../../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) [partial type](../../../../../../partial-type.md); [quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination-for-dense-linear-orders-without-endpoints.md) makes these comparisons determine a [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) in the language naming all rationals. No rational realizes the cut, so $Q^+$ omits it.

Add a new constant realizing this type and apply compactness, followed by downward Löwenheim-Skolem in the [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) expanded language. This gives a [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) [model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$ realizing it. An [isomorphism](../../../../../../structure-isomorphism.md) in $L_{\mathbb Q}$ fixes all named rationals and preserves realization of the cut, so it cannot be isomorphic to $Q^+$. Thus **$T$ is not countably categorical**. Omission by itself would need this additional realization-and-countable-model argument to establish noncategoricity.
