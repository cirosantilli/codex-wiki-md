↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A complete $n$-type over $T$ is a maximal [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $p(\bar x)$ of formulas in $n$ variables [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) with $T$; it decides each such formula or its [negation](../../../../../negation.md). A [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) drops maximality. A tuple realizes the type if it satisfies every member. A [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) is principal, or isolated, if some formula $\theta(\bar x)$ [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) with $T$ implies every member of the type modulo $T$; then $\theta$ itself belongs to that type.

The [omitting types theorem](../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md) says that in a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md), a [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) has a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) omitting a specified nonprincipal type; more generally a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) family of nonprincipal types can be omitted simultaneously. In the present complete-theory setting it applies directly to complete nonisolated types. “Omitted” means that no tuple in the [model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) realizes all formulas of the type, even though each finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) is compatible with the [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md).
