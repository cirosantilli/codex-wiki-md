<h1 id="b/4/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The natural-number successor [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) embeds into the [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) successor [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) by ordinary inclusion. Thus $\mathbb Z$ witnesses the existential joint satisfiability in the first assertion. The two [chains](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) are not [elementarily equivalent](../../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md): every [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) has a predecessor, whereas zero has none in the natural-number [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md).

For the universal assertion, construct an [elementary extension](../../../../../../elementary-extension.md) of the natural-number [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) containing a two-sided successor [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md). To its [elementary diagram](../../../../../../elementary-diagram-of-a-structure.md) add distinct constants $d_j$ for $j\in\mathbb Z$ with $s(d_j)=d_{j+1}$ and require them to differ from all named [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md). Every finite [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) is realized in the natural-number [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md): place the finitely many required consecutive blocks sufficiently far to the right, away from the finitely many forbidden named [Elements](../../../../../../euclid-s-elements.md). Compactness yields an [elementary extension](../../../../../../elementary-extension.md) $A^*$ with the required two-sided [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md). That [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) is a [substructure](../../../../../../substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md) isomorphic to the [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) successor structure and hence is a [model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of its [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md).

Consequently both joint [theories](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) in the two assertions are satisfiable, though the [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) cannot embed into the natural-number [chain](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) itself: repeated predecessors would give an infinite descending [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) of [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md). The second assertion correctly uses an [elementarily equivalent](../../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md) ambient structure rather than the original one.
