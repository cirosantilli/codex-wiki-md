↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The relation $A\subseteq B$ means that the domain of $A$ is a [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) of the domain of $B$ and the inclusion map is an $L$-embedding. Thus it contains all named constants, is closed under every named function, and uses the restrictions of the ambient relations.

The stronger relation $A\preccurlyeq B$ means that for every [first-order formula](../../../../../../first-order-formula.md) $\phi(\bar x)$ and tuple $\bar a$ from $A$,

$$
\boxed{A\models\phi(\bar a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\models\phi(\bar a).}
$$

This includes formulas with [quantifiers](../../../../../../quantifier.md) ranging over the respective domains. Elementary inclusion implies ordinary [substructure](../../../../../../substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md) inclusion, but the latter need only preserve quantifier-free truth. In particular an existential witness available in $B$ need not lie in an arbitrary [substructure](../../../../../../substructure-of-a-first-order-structure.md).
