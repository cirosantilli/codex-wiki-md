↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Under the deductively closed convention, an $L$-theory is a [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) of $L$-sentences containing all its first-order logical consequences: if $T\models\phi$, then $\phi\in T$. By completeness this is equivalent to closure under provability. An [axiom](../../../../../../axiom.md) [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is often called a [theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) too, with its deductive closure understood. Neither convention by itself requires completeness or satisfiability; an [inconsistent](../../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) deductively closed [theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) contains every sentence.
