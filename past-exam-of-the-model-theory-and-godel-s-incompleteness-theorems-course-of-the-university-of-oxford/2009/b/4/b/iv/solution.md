A complete satisfiable [theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) has a [model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) and decides every $L$-sentence: for each $\sigma$, exactly one of $\sigma$ and $\neg\sigma$ is a consequence. Under the closed convention, exactly one belongs to $T$. Satisfiability excludes the possibility of both. Equivalently all [models](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of the [theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) are [elementarily equivalent](../../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md), even though they need not be isomorphic or have equal [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md).
