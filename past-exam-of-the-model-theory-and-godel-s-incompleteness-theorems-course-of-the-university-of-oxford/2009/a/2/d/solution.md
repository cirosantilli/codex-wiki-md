<h1 id="a/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the two formal [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) equivalences expressly supplied for this part. If $S\vdash G\leftrightarrow R$, the [derivability conditions](../../../../../hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions.md) give $S\vdash\Box\neg G\leftrightarrow\Box\neg R$. Taking [negations](../../../../../negation.md) and applying those supplied equivalences yields

$$
S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_{S+\operatorname{Con}_S}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}_S.
$$

[One-consistency](../../../../../one-consistency.md) implies $\Sigma_1$ soundness under [bounded completeness](../../../../../bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory.md). Hence $S+\operatorname{Con}_S$ is [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md): if not, $S$ would prove $\neg\operatorname{Con}_S$, a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence. But in that [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) extension the last equivalence and its [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) [axiom](../../../../../axiom.md) prove its own [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) $\operatorname{Con}_{S+\operatorname{Con}_S}$. [Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) rules this out. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash G\leftrightarrow R.}
$$

This establishes the requested unprovability using the explicit formal hypotheses, without inferring internal provability from the semantic equivalence discussed in the preceding part. The hypotheses for the second theorem are the standard effective arithmetic presentation and adequate [provability predicates](../../../../../arithmetical-provability-predicate.md) stipulated here.
