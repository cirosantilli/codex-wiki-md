<h1 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The literal semantic hypothesis is insufficient:** [a semantic Gödel fixed point need not be canonical](../../../../../a-semantic-godel-fixed-point-need-not-be-canonical.md). A sentence can be false and provable in a [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md), unsound [arithmetic theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md), in which case both sides of the stipulated semantic biconditional can be false.

For a concrete counterexample, assume PA is [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) and [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $S=\mathrm{PA}+\neg\operatorname{Con}_{\mathrm{PA}}$. [Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) makes this extension [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md): otherwise PA would prove its own [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md). It is still [bounded-complete](../../../../../bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory.md). Use ordinary numeralwise proof and refutation enumerators for this recursively axiomatized [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md), and take $G=\neg\operatorname{Con}_{\mathrm{PA}}$. This $G$ is false in the standard [natural numbers](../../../../../natural-number.md) but is an [axiom](../../../../../axiom.md) of $S$. Since it is provable, $\exists yP(\ulcorner G\urcorner,y)$ is true; its [negation](../../../../../negation.md) is false. Thus the semantic [fixed-point](../../../../../logical-fixed-point.md) biconditional required in this part is true, with both sides false.

Choose $R$ by the usual canonical Rosser construction for these proof matrices. Its undecidability follows from the preceding parts, and it is a true $\Pi_1$ sentence: if it were false, its true $\Sigma_1$ [negation](../../../../../negation.md) would be provable in this [bounded-complete](../../../../../bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory.md) [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md), contradicting undecidability. Hence $G$ is false and $R$ true, so their proposed semantic equivalence is false. This counterexample meets the literal assumptions and does not exploit a missing source page.

Under the intended canonical $\Pi_1$ Gödel/Rosser convention, the expected answer does hold. [Consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) plus [bounded completeness](../../../../../bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory.md) implies $\Pi_1$ soundness: a false $\Pi_1$ theorem has a standard bounded counterexample whose [negation](../../../../../negation.md) the [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) proves. If canonical $G$ were false, its truth [fixed point](../../../../../logical-fixed-point.md) would make it provable, contradicting this soundness; thus $G$ is true. Canonical $R$ is true by the preceding $\Sigma_1$-negation argument, so **both canonical sentences are true and their equivalence is true**. This last conclusion needs that canonical construction or an equivalent additional restriction; truth of a biconditional alone does not supply it.
