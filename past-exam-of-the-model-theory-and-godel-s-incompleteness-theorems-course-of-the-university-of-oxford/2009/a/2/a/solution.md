<h1 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For [separation of number sets in an arithmetic theory](../../../../../separation-of-number-sets-in-an-arithmetic-theory.md), a formula separates $A$ from $B$ in $S$ if $n\in A$ implies $S\vdash F(\overline n)$, and $n\in B$ implies $S\vdash\neg F(\overline n)$. No demand is made for numbers outside their [union](../../../../../set-union.md). [Consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) forces disjointness when such a separator exists.

Let $r$ be the code of the [fixed-point](../../../../../logical-fixed-point.md) sentence. If $S\vdash R$, then $r$ is in the provable [set](../../../../../set-split.md), so separation gives $S\vdash H(\overline r)$. The [fixed-point equivalence](../../../../../diagonal-lemma.md) gives its [negation](../../../../../negation.md), a [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md). If $S\vdash\neg R$, then $r$ is in the refutable [set](../../../../../set-split.md), so separation gives $S\vdash\neg H(\overline r)$, and the [fixed-point equivalence](../../../../../diagonal-lemma.md) gives $S\vdash R$, again a [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash R\quad\text{and}\quad S\nvdash\neg R.}
$$

The argument is syntactic: it needs only the separating implications and [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md), not omega-consistency or general soundness.
