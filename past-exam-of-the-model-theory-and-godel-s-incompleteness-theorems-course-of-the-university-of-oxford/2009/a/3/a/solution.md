↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) says that a [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) effectively presented [arithmetic theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) with an adequate proof predicate cannot prove its [formal consistency statement](../../../../../formal-consistency-statement.md). The background strength must support the [diagonal lemma](../../../../../diagonal-lemma.md) and the [derivability conditions](../../../../../hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions.md), as in the present arithmetic setting.

[Löb's theorem](../../../../../lob-s-theorem.md) says

$$
\boxed{S\vdash\Box A\to A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash A.}
$$

It is a theorem about derivability for every sentence $A$, not merely an external soundness assertion about the interpretation of $\Box$. Taking $A=\bot$, a proof of [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) gives $S\vdash\Box\bot\to\bot$; Löb then gives $S\vdash\bot$. Thus a [consistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) $S$ cannot prove [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md). This is how the [second incompleteness theorem](../../../../../godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md) is the contradiction-sentence instance of Löb's more general result.
