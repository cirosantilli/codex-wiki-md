<h1 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $\Box A$ for the [arithmetical sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) asserting $S$-provability of $A$. If $S\vdash G$, the first derivability condition gives $S\vdash\Box G$. The stipulated [fixed-point equivalence](../../../../../diagonal-lemma.md) also gives $S\vdash\neg\Box G$. This contradicts [consistency](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md). Conversely an [inconsistent](../../../../../consistent-first-order-theory.md) classical [theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) proves every sentence, including $G$. Thus

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

Only the direction from an actual proof to its formalized provability is used here. There is no inference from a theorem $\Box G$ to a theorem $G$, and no assumption that every theorem of $S$ is true.
