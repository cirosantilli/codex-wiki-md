<h1 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $\rho=\operatorname{Pr}_{PA}^*(\ulcorner R\urcorner)$. The supplied fixed-point equivalence gives $PA\vdash R\leftrightarrow\neg\rho$.

If PA proved $R$, the first assumed property would give $PA\vdash\rho$, while the equivalence would give $PA\vdash\neg\rho$. This contradicts [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md). If PA proved $\neg R$, the second assumed property would give $PA\vdash\neg\rho$, and the reverse implication of the equivalence would give $PA\vdash R$, again a [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{PA\text{ consistent}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad PA\nvdash R\text{ and }PA\nvdash\neg R.}
$$

This is the Rosser improvement: its comparison of [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) codes permits ordinary [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) to exclude both alternatives, without assuming [one-consistency](../../../../../one-consistency.md).
