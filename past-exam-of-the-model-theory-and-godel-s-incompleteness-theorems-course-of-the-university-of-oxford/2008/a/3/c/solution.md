<h1 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take a [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) $Y$ refutable in $S$ and suppose, toward [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md), that a [first-order consistent](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) $S$ proved $\neg P_S(\ulcorner Y\urcorner)$. [Propositional logic](../../../../../propositional-logic.md) would then give

$$
S\vdash P_S(\ulcorner Y\urcorner)\to Y.
$$

Löb's inference, applied with $X=Y$, would give $S\vdash Y$, contradicting the existing [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) of $\neg Y$. Hence [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) excludes the proposed [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) of non-provability.

Thus the familiar unprovability of the [first-order consistency](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) statement associated with a refutable [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) is a special consequence of the general rule

$$
\boxed{S\vdash(P_S(\ulcorner X\urcorner)\to X)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash X.}
$$

The general rule applies to every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md), not just a chosen false or refutable one.
