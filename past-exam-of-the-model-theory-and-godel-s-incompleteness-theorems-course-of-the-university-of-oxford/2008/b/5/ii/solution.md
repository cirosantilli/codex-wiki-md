<h1 id="b/5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $\Sigma$ is [satisfiable](../../../../../satisfiable-first-order-theory.md) if there is one $L$-structure $\mathcal A$ satisfying every [first-order sentence](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) in $\Sigma$ simultaneously. It is finitely [satisfiable](../../../../../satisfiable-first-order-theory.md) if each finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) $\Sigma_0\subseteq\Sigma$ has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md); that [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) is allowed to depend on the chosen finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md).

Thus the two statements have different [quantifier orders](../../../../../quantifier-order.md):

$$
\exists\mathcal A\ \forall\sigma\in\Sigma\ (\mathcal A\models\sigma),
\qquad
\forall\Sigma_0\subseteq_{\rm finite}\Sigma\ \exists\mathcal A\ (\mathcal A\models\Sigma_0).
$$

Satisfiability immediately implies finite satisfiability by using the same [first-order structure](../../../../../first-order-structure.md) for every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md). The converse is the substantive content of [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md).
