<h1 id="b/5/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Expand the [ring](../../../../../ring.md) [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) by a new constant $c$ and consider

$$
\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z)\cup\{c\ne0\}\cup
\{\exists y\ (\bar n\cdot y=c):n=1,2,3,\ldots\}.
$$

Every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) is [satisfiable](../../../../../satisfiable-first-order-theory.md) in the [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) [ring](../../../../../ring.md) itself. Only finitely many [divisibility](../../../../../divisibility-split.md) conditions occur; interpret $c$ as a nonzero common multiple of their positive [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) indices, using $1$ if none occur. All [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) from $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z)$ are automatically true there.

[Compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) therefore gives a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). Its reduct $R$ to the original [ring](../../../../../ring.md) [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) satisfies the [complete theory](../../../../../complete-theory.md) of $\mathbb Z$, so $R\equiv\mathbb Z$. Its element $c^R$ is nonzero and divisible by every positive standard [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{R\equiv\mathbb Z,\qquad c^R\ne0,\qquad
(\forall n\in\mathbb N_{>0})\ R\models\exists y\ (\bar n y=c^R).}
$$

Such an element cannot be an ordinary nonzero [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md), since an [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) of absolute value less than $n$ cannot be a nonzero multiple of $n$. The statement concerns each standard [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) index separately; it does not assert [divisibility](../../../../../divisibility-split.md) by every internally nonzero element of $R$.
