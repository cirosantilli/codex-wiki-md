<h1 id="b/5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) states that a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) if and only if every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) has a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md).

For the nontrivial direction suppose every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) of $\Sigma$ is [satisfiable](../../../../../satisfiable-first-order-theory.md). If $\Sigma$ were inconsistent, a finite formal derivation of a [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md) would use only finitely many premises $\Sigma_0\subseteq\Sigma$. [Soundness of an arithmetic theory](../../../../../soundness-of-an-arithmetic-theory.md) would then exclude a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $\Sigma_0$, contradicting finite satisfiability. Therefore $\Sigma$ is [first-order consistent](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md). The completeness theorem, proved by adjoining witnesses and constructing a Henkin [term model](../../../../../term-model.md), supplies a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $\Sigma$.

The reverse direction is immediate by restriction of the list of required [first-order sentences](../../../../../first-order-sentence.md). The key reason [compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) follows from syntactic completeness is that **every [formal proof](../../../../../formal-proof.md) uses only finitely many premises**, even when the whole [first-order theory](../../../../../first-order-theory.md) is infinite.
