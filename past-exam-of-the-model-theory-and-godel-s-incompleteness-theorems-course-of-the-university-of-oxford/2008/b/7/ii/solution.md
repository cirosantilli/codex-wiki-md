<h1 id="b/7/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Apply downward Löwenheim-Skolem to the extension from part (i), keeping all standard [integers](../../../../../integer-split.md) and the new prime $c$. The [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) and this prescribed [subset](../../../../../subset.md) are [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md), so there is a [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [elementary substructure](../../../../../elementary-substructure.md) $N$ containing them. In particular $N\equiv\mathcal Z$ and contains an element outside the standard copy of $\mathbb Z$.

No [ring isomorphism](../../../../../ring-isomorphism.md) $N\to\mathcal Z$ can exist. Such an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) fixes $0$ and $1$, hence every nonnegative [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) numeral. It also fixes each negative [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md), which is the unique solution of $x+\bar n=0$ for the corresponding positive $n$. Consequently it fixes the whole standard copy. The image of $c$ would be some standard [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $a$, already the image of that same named standard element, violating [injectivity](../../../../../injective-function.md).

Thus $N$ and $\mathcal Z$ are two nonisomorphic countably infinite [first-order models](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$, so

$$
\boxed{T\text{ is not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$
