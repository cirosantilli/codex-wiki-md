<h1 id="b/7/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Take the parameter-free [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md)

$$
p(x)=\operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal Z^*}(c/\varnothing)
$$

of the nonstandard prime constructed in part (i). It is [first-order consistent](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) and complete because it consists of exactly the [logical formulas](../../../../../logical-formula.md) true of an actual element in a [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$. It contains $x\ne\bar a$ for every [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md) $a$, and consequently no element of $\mathcal Z$ realizes it. Thus

$$
\boxed{p\text{ is a complete type over }T\text{ omitted in }\mathcal Z.}
$$

It is necessarily a [nonprincipal complete type](../../../../../nonprincipal-complete-type.md). If a [first-order consistent](../../../../../first-order-consistency.md) [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) isolated it, completeness of $T$ would force the existential closure of that [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) to hold in every [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of $T$, including $\mathcal Z$; a witness there would realize the [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md), a [contradiction](../../../../../contradiction.md). This connects the explicit omission with the hypothesis of the [omitting types theorem](../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md).
