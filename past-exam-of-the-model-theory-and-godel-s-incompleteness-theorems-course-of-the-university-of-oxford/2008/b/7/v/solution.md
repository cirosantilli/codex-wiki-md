<h1 id="b/7/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Enumerate the standard positive primes as $(p_n)_{n<\omega}$. For each [subset](../../../../../subset.md) $A\subseteq\omega$ consider the [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md)

$$
q_A(x)=\{x\ne0\}\cup\{\bar p_n\mid x:n\in A\}\cup
\{\neg(\bar p_n\mid x):n\notin A\},
$$

where [divisibility](../../../../../divisibility-split.md) is the [ring](../../../../../ring.md) [logical formula](../../../../../logical-formula.md) $\exists y\ (\bar p_ny=x)$.

Every finite [subset](../../../../../subset.md) is realized in $\mathbb Z$: take the product of the finitely many primes required to divide $x$, or $1$ if there are none. This is nonzero and is not divisible by any of the distinct finitely many primes required not to divide it. [Compactness theorem](../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) supplies a [complete type](../../../../../complete-type.md) extending $q_A$.

Distinct [subsets](../../../../../subset.md) $A,B$ yield distinct complete extensions, because at an index in their symmetric difference they require opposite [divisibility](../../../../../divisibility-split.md) statements. There are $2^{\aleph_0}$ such [subsets](../../../../../subset.md), so there are at least that many [complete one-types](../../../../../complete-one-type.md). There are at most that many because the [first-order language](../../../../../first-order-language.md) has only countably many [logical formulas](../../../../../logical-formula.md) and a [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) is a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of them. Hence

$$
\boxed{|S_1(T)|=2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

In particular the number of [complete one-types](../../../../../complete-one-type.md) is uncountable, despite every [partial type](../../../../../partial-type.md) realized in the particular [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md) [first-order model](../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) $\mathcal Z$ being a [principal complete type](../../../../../principal-complete-type.md).
