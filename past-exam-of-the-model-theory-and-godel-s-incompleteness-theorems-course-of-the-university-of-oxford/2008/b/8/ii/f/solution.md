<h1 id="b/8/ii/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Reuse the complete [free-bijection theory](../../../../../../theory-of-a-free-bijection.md) from part (e). [Quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) shows that every one-variable atomic statement is already decided by the no-finite-cycle axioms. Thus there is exactly one [complete one-type](../../../../../../complete-one-type.md), isolated by $x=x$.

For two [logical variables](../../../../../../logical-variable.md), each [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $k$ gives a different [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) isolated by

$$
y=f^k(x).
$$

There is also the [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md)

$$
p_\infty(x,y)=\{y\ne f^k(x):k\in\mathbb Z\},
$$

saying that the two elements lie in different orbits. These exhaust the possibilities by [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md). In particular

$$
\boxed{|S_1(T_{\mathbb Z})|=1,\qquad |S_2(T_{\mathbb Z})|=\aleph_0.}
$$

The last [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) is a [nonprincipal complete type](../../../../../../nonprincipal-complete-type.md). Any [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) uses only finitely many [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) offsets after [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md); a pair in a single orbit with a sufficiently different offset agrees with a different-orbit pair on those tests. Hence no single [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) can isolate $p_\infty$.

The one-orbit [first-order model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) $(\mathbb Z,f(n)=n+1,g(n)=n-1)$ omits $p_\infty$, while a two-orbit [first-order model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) realizes it. This illustrates the [omitting types theorem](../../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md) and shows concretely why finitely many [complete one-types](../../../../../../complete-one-type.md) alone cannot replace the all-arities condition in Ryll-Nardzewski.
