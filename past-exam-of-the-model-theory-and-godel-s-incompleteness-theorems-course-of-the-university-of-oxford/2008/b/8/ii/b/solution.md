<h1 id="b/8/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use $ACF_0$, the [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md) of [algebraically closed fields](../../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) of [characteristic zero](../../../../../../characteristic-zero.md). Its [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) is a standard characterization of this [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md). [Algebraically closed fields](../../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) of a fixed [characteristic](../../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) are classified up to [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) by [transcendence degree](../../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) over their [prime field](../../../../../../prime-field.md).

For such a [field](../../../../../../field.md) $K$,

$$
|K|=\max(\aleph_0,\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}K)
$$

when the [transcendence degree](../../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) is infinite, while finite [transcendence degree](../../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) also gives a [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) [field](../../../../../../field.md). This follows by counting [rational functions](../../../../../../rational-function.md) in a [transcendence basis](../../../../../../transcendence-basis.md) and then algebraic elements over that [field](../../../../../../field.md). Thus every [first-order model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) $\aleph_1$ has [transcendence degree](../../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) $\aleph_1$, and the classification gives a unique such [first-order model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md).

On the other hand $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and an [algebraic closure](../../../../../../algebraic-closure.md) of $\mathbb Q(t)$ are countably infinite [first-order models](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) with [transcendence degrees](../../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) zero and one, so are not [isomorphic](../../../../../../isomorphism.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{ACF_0\text{ is }\aleph_1\text{-categorical but not }\aleph_0\text{-categorical}.}
$$

The transcendental [complete one-type](../../../../../../complete-one-type.md) consists of $f(x)\ne0$ for every nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) over $\mathbb Q$. [Quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md) makes it complete. It is nonprincipal: any parameter-free one-variable [logical formula](../../../../../../logical-formula.md) containing a transcendental element defines a cofinite [set](../../../../../../set-split.md), hence contains algebraic elements as well and cannot isolate this [partial type](../../../../../../partial-type.md). The [first-order model](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ omits it, concretely illustrating the [omitting types theorem](../../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md). The existence of infinitely many algebraic [complete one-types](../../../../../../complete-one-type.md), together with this [nonprincipal complete type](../../../../../../nonprincipal-complete-type.md), also agrees with the failure of the Ryll-Nardzewski finiteness condition.
