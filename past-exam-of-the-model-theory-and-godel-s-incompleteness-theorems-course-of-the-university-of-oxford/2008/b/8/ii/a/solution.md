<h1 id="b/8/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md) of [dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md) in the [first-order language](../../../../../../first-order-language.md) $\{<\}$. It eliminates [quantifiers](../../../../../../quantifier.md): existential conditions on one new point reduce to finitely many [logical equality](../../../../../../logical-equality.md) constraints and interval bounds, and density and absence of endpoints decide whether a suitable point exists. Its countably infinite [first-order models](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) are all [isomorphic](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) to the rational order by the usual [back-and-forth method](../../../../../../back-and-forth-method.md), extending finite order-preserving maps alternately along enumerations of the two [first-order models](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md). Thus it is $\aleph_0$-categorical.

To distinguish [first-order models](../../../../../../model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) of size $\aleph_1$, let

$$
M_1=\sum_{\alpha<\omega_1}(\mathbb Q,<),\qquad M_2=M_1+(\mathbb Q,<),
$$

with ordered sums. Both are dense without endpoints and have [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) $\aleph_1$. The first has [cofinality](../../../../../../cofinality.md) $\aleph_1$: countably many points lie in a bounded [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) of block indices and cannot be [cofinal](../../../../../../cofinal-subset.md). The second has [cofinality](../../../../../../cofinality.md) $\aleph_0$, supplied by its final rational block. [Cofinality of a linear order](../../../../../../cofinality-of-a-linear-order.md) is preserved by [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md), so they are not [isomorphic](../../../../../../isomorphism.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{DLO is }\aleph_0\text{-categorical but not }\aleph_1\text{-categorical}.}
$$

For Ryll-Nardzewski, its $n$-types are precisely the finitely many equality-and-order patterns on $n$ coordinates. Each is isolated by the [conjunction](../../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) specifying that pattern, agreeing with the theorem's prediction.
