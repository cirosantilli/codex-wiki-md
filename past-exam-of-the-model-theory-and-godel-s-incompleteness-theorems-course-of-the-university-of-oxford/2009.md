<h4 id="a/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

Write $\Box A$ for the [arithmetical sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-sentence) asserting $S$-provability of $A$. If $S\vdash G$, the first derivability condition gives $S\vdash\Box G$. The stipulated [fixed-point equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#diagonal-lemma) also gives $S\vdash\neg\Box G$. This contradicts [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory). Conversely an [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) classical [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) proves every sentence, including $G$. Thus

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\text{ is consistent}.}
$$

Only the direction from an actual proof to its formalized provability is used here. There is no inference from a theorem $\Box G$ to a theorem $G$, and no assumption that every theorem of $S$ is true.

<h4 id="a/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

The [Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) are: if $S\vdash A$ then $S\vdash\Box A$; $S\vdash\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B)$; and $S\vdash\Box A\to\Box\Box A$. The first is an external rule about provability; the latter two are internal theorems. Standard numerical correctness and the stipulated $\Sigma_1$ form accompany the predicate in this question. These conditions do not include [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent) $\Box A\to A$.

Choose a fixed refutable sentence $\bot$ and identify $\operatorname{Con}_S$ with $\neg\Box\bot$. Different fixed refutable choices have provably equivalent [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) sentences, because $S$ proves their equivalence to a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). From $\bot\to G$, [necessitation](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) and [distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#normal-modal-distribution-axiom) give $\Box\bot\to\Box G$. Since $G\to\neg\Box G$, this yields $G\to\operatorname{Con}_S$.

For the converse, necessitate $G\to\neg\Box G$ and distribute to get $\Box G\to\Box\neg\Box G$. [Positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) also gives $\Box G\to\Box\Box G$. The two boxed contradictories imply $\Box\bot$ by formalized [propositional reasoning](../mathematical-logic.md#classical-propositional-logic). Thus $\Box G\to\Box\bot$, and contraposition gives $\operatorname{Con}_S\to\neg\Box G\to G$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{S\vdash G\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}_S.}
$$

Finally the [deduction theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-first-order-logic) says that $S+G$ is [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) exactly when $S\vdash\neg G$. The internal equivalence identifies $S+G$ and $S+\operatorname{Con}_S$ in their deductive consequences. Hence **$S\nvdash\neg G$ exactly when $S+\operatorname{Con}_S$ is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory)**. [Consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) of $S$ alone need not ensure this stronger [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) condition.

<h4 id="a/1/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/1/c)

For [consistency of a negated-consistency extension](../mathematical-logic.md#consistency-of-a-negated-consistency-extension), put $A=S+\neg\operatorname{Con}_S$. The [arithmetized deduction theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetized-deduction-theorem), together with double-negation elimination and formalized proof transformations, gives

$$
S\vdash\Box_A\bot\leftrightarrow\Box_S\operatorname{Con}_S,
\qquad S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_A\leftrightarrow\neg\Box_S\operatorname{Con}_S.
$$

From the preceding proof, $S\vdash\Box_SG\to\Box_S\bot$. Necessitating the equivalence between $G$ and $\operatorname{Con}_S$ and using [distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#normal-modal-distribution-axiom) therefore gives $S\vdash\Box_S\operatorname{Con}_S\to\Box_S\bot$. Its contrapositive is

$$
\boxed{S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_S\to\operatorname{Con}_{S+\neg\operatorname{Con}_S}.}
$$

This is an internal conditional statement. It does not assert that $S$ proves its own [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) or permits the [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) premise to be discarded.

<h4 id="a/1/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/1/d)

For [unrestricted consistency self-strengthening is inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#unrestricted-consistency-self-strengthening-is-inconsistent), again take $A=S+\neg\operatorname{Con}_S$. In $S^{++}$ the [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) $\operatorname{Con}_S$ and the preceding implication prove $\operatorname{Con}_A$. The additional rule then proves $\operatorname{Con}_{A+\operatorname{Con}_A}$.

However [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) of an extension implies [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) of its subtheory by the [formalized inclusion of proofs](../mathematical-logic.md#monotonicity-of-inconsistency): $S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_A\to\operatorname{Con}_S$. Thus the ordinary [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $A+\operatorname{Con}_A$ contains $\neg\operatorname{Con}_S$ and derives $\operatorname{Con}_S$, so it has a finite [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) proof. Checking that concrete proof in arithmetic gives $S\vdash\neg\operatorname{Con}_{A+\operatorname{Con}_A}$. This theorem is also present in $S^{++}$, contradicting the result of the new rule. Therefore **$S^{++}$ is [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory)**.

The error in the added rule is its unrestricted internal use on arbitrary arithmetically presented systems, including systems containing false [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) assertions. A true external [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) progression for a specified sound system is not this unrestricted [inference rule](../mathematical-logic.md#inference-rule).

<h5 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h5>

For [separation of number sets in an arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#separation-of-number-sets-in-an-arithmetic-theory), a formula separates $A$ from $B$ in $S$ if $n\in A$ implies $S\vdash F(\overline n)$, and $n\in B$ implies $S\vdash\neg F(\overline n)$. No demand is made for numbers outside their [union](../set.md#set-union). [Consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) forces disjointness when such a separator exists.

Let $r$ be the code of the [fixed-point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) sentence. If $S\vdash R$, then $r$ is in the provable [set](../set.md), so separation gives $S\vdash H(\overline r)$. The [fixed-point equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#diagonal-lemma) gives its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation), a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). If $S\vdash\neg R$, then $r$ is in the refutable [set](../set.md), so separation gives $S\vdash\neg H(\overline r)$, and the [fixed-point equivalence](../mathematical-logic.md#diagonal-lemma) gives $S\vdash R$, again a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Thus

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash R\quad\text{and}\quad S\nvdash\neg R.}
$$

The argument is syntactic: it needs only the separating implications and [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory), not omega-consistency or general soundness.

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

For [Rosser separation by a bounded stage comparison](../mathematical-logic.md#rosser-separation-by-a-bounded-stage-comparison), use the [numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic](../mathematical-logic.md#numeralwise-enumeration-in-arithmetic) properties meant by enumeration in $S$: membership supplies some standard stage with a provable positive instance, while nonmembership makes every standard-stage instance refutable. These are stronger than merely attaching a $\Sigma_1$ syntactic label to an arbitrary formula. The finite-initial-segment [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) lets us combine finitely many numeralwise refutations into a bounded universal assertion.

If $n$ is provable, choose a standard stage $p$ with $S\vdash P(\overline n,\overline p)$. [Consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) means $n$ is not refutable. Therefore $S\vdash\neg R(\overline n,\overline j)$ for each $j\le p$. Using the displayed finite-segment [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) and equality substitution, combine these proofs to get $S\vdash\forall z\le\overline p\,\neg R(\overline n,z)$. [Existential introduction](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-quantifier-introduction-rule) with stage $p$ proves the separator at $n$.

If $n$ is refutable, choose a standard stage $q$ with $S\vdash R(\overline n,\overline q)$. [Consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) implies that $n$ is not provable, so all standard instances of $P(\overline n,\cdot)$ are refutable. In particular their finitely many instances through $q$ give $S\vdash\forall z\le\overline q\,\neg P(\overline n,z)$. For an arbitrary proposed witness $z$, the total-comparison [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) gives $z\le\overline q$ or $\overline q\le z$. In the first case its $P$ conjunct fails; in the second case the known $R$ instance at $q$ contradicts its bounded no-refutation conjunct. [Generalize](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-generalization-rule) on $z$ to refute the existential separator. Hence **the proposed first-proof-before-refutation formula separates the two [sets](../set.md)**.

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/2/c)

**The literal semantic hypothesis is insufficient:** [a semantic Gödel fixed point need not be canonical](../mathematical-logic.md#a-semantic-godel-fixed-point-need-not-be-canonical). A sentence can be false and provable in a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory), unsound [arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), in which case both sides of the stipulated semantic biconditional can be false.

For a concrete counterexample, assume PA is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) and [set](../set.md) $S=\mathrm{PA}+\neg\operatorname{Con}_{\mathrm{PA}}$. [Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) makes this extension [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory): otherwise PA would prove its own [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory). It is still [bounded-complete](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory). Use ordinary numeralwise proof and refutation enumerators for this recursively axiomatized [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), and take $G=\neg\operatorname{Con}_{\mathrm{PA}}$. This $G$ is false in the standard [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number) but is an [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) of $S$. Since it is provable, $\exists yP(\ulcorner G\urcorner,y)$ is true; its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) is false. Thus the semantic [fixed-point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) biconditional required in this part is true, with both sides false.

Choose $R$ by the usual canonical Rosser construction for these proof matrices. Its undecidability follows from the preceding parts, and it is a true $\Pi_1$ sentence: if it were false, its true $\Sigma_1$ [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) would be provable in this [bounded-complete](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory), contradicting undecidability. Hence $G$ is false and $R$ true, so their proposed semantic equivalence is false. This counterexample meets the literal assumptions and does not exploit a missing source page.

Under the intended canonical $\Pi_1$ Gödel/Rosser convention, the expected answer does hold. [Consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) plus [bounded completeness](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory) implies $\Pi_1$ soundness: a false $\Pi_1$ theorem has a standard bounded counterexample whose [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) the [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) proves. If canonical $G$ were false, its truth [fixed point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) would make it provable, contradicting this soundness; thus $G$ is true. Canonical $R$ is true by the preceding $\Sigma_1$-negation argument, so **both canonical sentences are true and their equivalence is true**. This last conclusion needs that canonical construction or an equivalent additional restriction; truth of a biconditional alone does not supply it.

<h4 id="a/2/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/2/d)

Use the two formal [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) equivalences expressly supplied for this part. If $S\vdash G\leftrightarrow R$, the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) give $S\vdash\Box\neg G\leftrightarrow\Box\neg R$. Taking [negations](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) and applying those supplied equivalences yields

$$
S\vdash\operatorname{Con}_{S+\operatorname{Con}_S}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}_S.
$$

[One-consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency) implies $\Sigma_1$ soundness under [bounded completeness](../mathematical-logic.md#bounded-completeness-of-an-arithmetic-theory). Hence $S+\operatorname{Con}_S$ is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory): if not, $S$ would prove $\neg\operatorname{Con}_S$, a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence. But in that [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) extension the last equivalence and its [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) prove its own [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) $\operatorname{Con}_{S+\operatorname{Con}_S}$. [Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) rules this out. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S\nvdash G\leftrightarrow R.}
$$

This establishes the requested unprovability using the explicit formal hypotheses, without inferring internal provability from the semantic equivalence discussed in the preceding part. The hypotheses for the second theorem are the standard effective arithmetic presentation and adequate [provability predicates](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) stipulated here.

<h3 id="a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/3/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/3/a)

[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) says that a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) effectively presented [arithmetic theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) with an adequate proof predicate cannot prove its [formal consistency statement](../mathematical-logic.md#formal-consistency-statement). The background strength must support the [diagonal lemma](../mathematical-logic.md#diagonal-lemma) and the [derivability conditions](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions), as in the present arithmetic setting.

[Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) says

$$
\boxed{S\vdash\Box A\to A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\vdash A.}
$$

It is a theorem about derivability for every sentence $A$, not merely an external soundness assertion about the interpretation of $\Box$. Taking $A=\bot$, a proof of [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) gives $S\vdash\Box\bot\to\bot$; Löb then gives $S\vdash\bot$. Thus a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) $S$ cannot prove [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory). This is how the [second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem) is the contradiction-sentence instance of Löb's more general result.

<h4 id="a/3/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/3/b)

Suppose $S+A$ proves every specified [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent) instance. Use only the instance with $X=\neg A$. The [deduction theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#deduction-theorem-for-first-order-logic) gives

$$
S\vdash A\to(\Box\neg A\to\neg A).
$$

[Classical propositional reasoning](../mathematical-logic.md#classical-propositional-logic) turns this into $S\vdash\Box\neg A\to\neg A$: if $\neg A$ already holds the implication is true, while otherwise $A$ holds and the displayed theorem supplies it. [Löb's theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lob-s-theorem) now yields $S\vdash\neg A$, making $S+A$ [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory). Therefore **no sentence [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with $S$ can supply all the local [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent) instances in a one-sentence extension**. The proof uses just one well-chosen instance, and never applies [necessitation](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) to a derivation with the extra assumption still open.

<h4 id="a/3/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/3/c)

[GL](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-lob-logic) has all classical propositional [tautologies](../mathematical-logic.md#tautology), the normal [distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#normal-modal-distribution-axiom) [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) $\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B)$ and the Löb [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) $\Box(\Box A\to A)\to\Box A$, closed under uniform substitution, modus ponens and [necessitation](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) of theorems. We use its derived positive-introspection theorem $\Box A\to\Box\Box A$, normal boxed reasoning, and the boxed replacement principle supplied in the question.

For completeness, [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) follows from these [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom). Put $C=A\land\Box A$. [Distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#hilbert-bernays-lob-derivability-conditions) gives $\Box C\to\Box A$, so [propositional logic](../mathematical-logic.md#classical-propositional-logic) proves $A\to(\Box C\to C)$. Necessitate and distribute to get $\Box A\to\Box(\Box C\to C)$; Löb's [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) gives $\Box A\to\Box C$, and boxing $C\to\Box A$ finishes $\Box A\to\Box\Box A$.

For a [GL fixed point by truth substitution](../mathematical-logic.md#gl-fixed-point-by-truth-substitution), [set](../set.md) $\top=(\bot\to\bot)$, $U=\Box Y(\top)$ and $V=\Box Y(U)$. From the [tautology](../mathematical-logic.md#tautology) $U\to(\top\leftrightarrow U)$, boxed reasoning gives $\Box U\to\Box(\top\leftrightarrow U)$. The supplied replacement principle and [distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#normal-modal-distribution-axiom) then give

$$
\mathrm{GL}\vdash\Box U\to(U\leftrightarrow V).
$$

Since $U$ is itself boxed, [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) gives $U\to\Box U$, and hence $U\to V$.

For the reverse direction, the displayed implication entails the theorem $V\to(\Box U\to U)$. Necessitate it and distribute to obtain $\Box V\to\Box(\Box U\to U)$. Since $V$ is boxed, $V\to\Box V$ holds. The Löb [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) therefore yields $V\to\Box U$, and the same displayed implication then yields $V\to U$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{GL}\vdash U\leftrightarrow\Box Y(U),\qquad U=\Box Y(\top).}
$$

All occurrences of the unknown letter have been removed from $U$ by substitution of truth; any other parameter letters remain available. The proof does not assume the invalid [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent) principle $\Box U\to U$ as an unconditional [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom).

<h4 id="a/3/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/3/d)

The [fixed point of boxed negated provability](../mathematical-logic.md#fixed-point-of-boxed-negated-provability) gives, in fact, that [GL](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-lob-logic) proves $F\leftrightarrow\Box\bot$. Here is a direct derivation. Since $F$ is provably equivalent to a boxed formula, [positive introspection](../mathematical-logic.md#positive-introspection) and [necessitation](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) of that equivalence give $F\to\Box F$. Another introspection step gives $F\to\Box\Box F$. The stipulated [fixed point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) also gives $F\to\Box\neg\Box F$. Combining these two boxed contradictories yields $F\to\Box\bot$. Conversely $\Box\bot\to\Box\neg\Box F$ follows by normal boxed explosion, and the [fixed point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) then yields $\Box\bot\to F$.

[Arithmetical soundness of GL](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-soundness-of-gl), proved by translating [distribution](../mathematical-logic.md#normal-modal-distribution-axiom) into the second derivability condition, [necessitation](../mathematical-logic.md#necessitation-rule) into the first, and Löb's [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) into its internal arithmetic theorem, now gives

$$
\mathrm{PA}\vdash F^*\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Pr}_{\mathrm{PA}}(\ulcorner\bot\urcorner).
$$

Any remaining propositional parameters may be assigned arbitrary arithmetic sentences; this equivalence is uniform in those assignments. If PA is [one-consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#one-consistency), it is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) and $\Sigma_1$ sound. Its standard [provability predicate](../mathematical-logic.md#arithmetical-provability-predicate) for a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) is then a false $\Sigma_1$ sentence and cannot be a theorem. Hence **$F^*$ is not provable in PA**. Moreover its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) is equivalent to PA's [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) statement and is unprovable by the [second incompleteness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#godel-second-incompleteness-theorem). Thus this [fixed point](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-fixed-point) is, under the stated hypothesis, undecidable rather than an unboxed [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent) principle.

An $L$-embedding $\pi:A\to B$ is an [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) map preserving the interpretations of all constants and functions and preserving and reflecting every relation. Explicitly $\pi(c^A)=c^B$, $\pi(f^A(\bar a))=f^B(\pi\bar a)$, and $A\models R(\bar a)$ if and only if $B\models R(\pi\bar a)$. Injectivity handles [logical equality](../mathematical-logic.md#logical-equality). Equivalently it is an [isomorphism](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-isomorphism) onto an $L$-substructure of $B$, or a map preserving the truth of all [quantifier-free formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier-free-formula). Preserving relations in only one direction would instead give a weaker homomorphism notion, not an [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding).

<h5 id="b/4/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/4/a)

<h6 id="b/4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/a/ii)

The relation $A\subseteq B$ means that the domain of $A$ is a [subset](../set.md#subset) of the domain of $B$ and the inclusion map is an $L$-embedding. Thus it contains all named constants, is closed under every named function, and uses the restrictions of the ambient relations.

The stronger relation $A\preccurlyeq B$ means that for every [first-order formula](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) $\phi(\bar x)$ and tuple $\bar a$ from $A$,

$$
\boxed{A\models\phi(\bar a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\models\phi(\bar a).}
$$

This includes formulas with [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) ranging over the respective domains. Elementary inclusion implies ordinary [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) inclusion, but the latter need only preserve quantifier-free truth. In particular an existential witness available in $B$ need not lie in an arbitrary [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure).

<h5 id="b/4/a/iii">iii</h5>

<h6 id="b/4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/4/a/iii)

The [Tarski-Vaught test](../mathematical-logic.md#tarski-vaught-test) says that a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) $A\subseteq B$ is elementary exactly when for every formula $\phi(x,\bar y)$ and tuple $\bar a\in A$, if $B\models\exists x\phi(x,\bar a)$, some $b\in A$ satisfies $B\models\phi(b,\bar a)$. Thus **every existential assertion with parameters from $A$ that has a witness in $B$ already has a witness in $A$**. The matrix may contain [quantifiers](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier); restricting it to [quantifier-free formulas](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier-free-formula) would not state the full test.

[Finite satisfiability](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) means that every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) $T_0\subseteq T$ has an $L$-structure satisfying all its sentences. Different finite [subsets](../set.md#subset) may have different witnessing structures. The first-order [compactness theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) says this condition is equivalent to satisfiability of the entire [set](../set.md), but [finite satisfiability](../mathematical-logic.md#compactness-theorem) itself is defined by the finite-subset condition.

<h5 id="b/4/b/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/b/ii)

Satisfiability means that there exists one nonempty-domain $L$-structure $M$ such that $M\models\phi$ for every $\phi\in T$. It is this single simultaneous [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) that is required. By the soundness and completeness theorems, satisfiability is equivalent to syntactic [consistency](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) in classical [first-order logic](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-logic).

<h5 id="b/4/b/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/4/b/iii)

Under the deductively closed convention, an $L$-theory is a [set](../set.md) of $L$-sentences containing all its first-order logical consequences: if $T\models\phi$, then $\phi\in T$. By completeness this is equivalent to closure under provability. An [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) [set](../set.md) is often called a [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) too, with its deductive closure understood. Neither convention by itself requires completeness or satisfiability; an [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) deductively closed [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) contains every sentence.

<h5 id="b/4/b/iv">iv</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/iv/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/4/b/iv)

A complete satisfiable [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) has a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) and decides every $L$-sentence: for each $\sigma$, exactly one of $\sigma$ and $\neg\sigma$ is a consequence. Under the closed convention, exactly one belongs to $T$. Satisfiability excludes the possibility of both. Equivalently all [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of the [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) are [elementarily equivalent](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence), even though they need not be isomorphic or have equal [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality).

<h4 id="b/4/c">c</h4>

<h5 id="b/4/c/i">i</h5>

<h6 id="b/4/c/i/solution">Solution</h6>

The [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) $\operatorname{Th}(A)$ is the [set](../set.md) of all $L$-sentences true in $A$. The [existential theory of a structure](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-theory-of-a-structure) $\operatorname{Th}_\exists(A)$ consists of those true sentences of the form $\exists\bar x\,\theta(\bar x)$ with quantifier-free $\theta$. The universal [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $\operatorname{Th}_\forall(A)$ uses $\forall\bar x\,\theta(\bar x)$ with quantifier-free matrix. Allow equivalent formulas and empty [quantifier](../mathematical-logic.md#quantifier) blocks, or insert dummy variables in nonempty domains. These are sentences in the original language, distinct from diagrams, which add constants naming [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements) of the structure.

<h5 id="b/4/c/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/4/c)

<h6 id="b/4/c/ii/alpha">α</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/c/ii)

<h6 id="b/4/c/ii/alpha/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Α](#b/4/c/ii/alpha)

If a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $B$ of $T$ contains $A$ as a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure), every [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) true in $A$ remains true in $B$: use the same witness tuple and preservation of quantifier-free truth. Hence $B$ satisfies the required joint [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory).

For the [existential-theory embedding criterion](../model-theory.md#existential-theory-embedding-criterion), conversely suppose that joint [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) has a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Expand the language by a constant $c_a$ for each $a\in A$, and add the atomic and negated [atomic diagram](../model-theory.md#diagram-mathematical-logic) $D(A)$ to $T$. Any finite collection of its literals is a quantifier-free [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) $\theta(\bar c)$ true of the corresponding tuple in $A$. Replacing those named constants by variables and existentially closing gives a sentence in $\operatorname{Th}_\exists(A)$. A [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of the assumed joint [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) can supply its witnesses, and hence an expansion satisfying those finitely many diagram requirements and any finite [subset](../set.md#subset) of $T$.

Compactness therefore gives a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T+D(A)$. Its named [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements) define an [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding) of $A$ into its $L$-reduct: inequalities ensure injectivity, and all function and relation literals ensure preservation and [reflection](../mathematical-logic.md#full-local-reflection-in-a-one-sentence-extension-is-inconsistent). Replace the ambient [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) by an isomorphic copy identifying this image with $A$ itself. Thus **a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$ containing $A$ exists**. The diagram step explains how parameter-free existential information nevertheless yields an [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding) of the whole structure.

<h6 id="b/4/c/ii/beta">β</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/c/ii)

<h6 id="b/4/c/ii/beta/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Β](#b/4/c/ii/beta)

If $B\models T$ and $B\subseteq A^*\equiv A$, every [universal sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-sentence) of $A$ holds in $A^*$ and is preserved by passage to the [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) $B$. Thus $B$ witnesses satisfiability of the joint [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory).

For [universal theory and substructures of elementarily equivalent models](../model-theory.md#universal-theory-and-substructures-of-elementarily-equivalent-models), conversely let $B$ satisfy $T$ together with the universal [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) of $A$. Every [existential sentence](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) true in $B$ must be true in $A$: if an existential $\sigma$ were false in $A$, its universal [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) would belong to $\operatorname{Th}_\forall(A)$ and hold in $B$, a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Hence $\operatorname{Th}_\exists(B)\subseteq\operatorname{Th}(A)$. In particular $\operatorname{Th}(A)\cup\operatorname{Th}_\exists(B)$ is satisfiable, with [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $A$. Apply the preceding [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding) result to the [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) $\operatorname{Th}(A)$ and structure $B$. It supplies a structure $A^*\models\operatorname{Th}(A)$ containing $B$. Therefore **$A^*\equiv A$ and $B\subseteq A^*$**, as required. [Elementary equivalence](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence) here does not assert that $A$ itself contains a copy of $B$.

The natural-number successor [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) embeds into the [integer](../integer.md) successor [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) by ordinary inclusion. Thus $\mathbb Z$ witnesses the existential joint satisfiability in the first assertion. The two [chains](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) are not [elementarily equivalent](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence): every [integer](../integer.md) has a predecessor, whereas zero has none in the natural-number [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order).

For the universal assertion, construct an [elementary extension](../model-theory.md#elementary-extension) of the natural-number [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) containing a two-sided successor [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order). To its [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure) add distinct constants $d_j$ for $j\in\mathbb Z$ with $s(d_j)=d_{j+1}$ and require them to differ from all named [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). Every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is realized in the natural-number [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order): place the finitely many required consecutive blocks sufficiently far to the right, away from the finitely many forbidden named [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements). Compactness yields an [elementary extension](../model-theory.md#elementary-extension) $A^*$ with the required two-sided [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order). That [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) is a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure) isomorphic to the [integer](../integer.md) successor structure and hence is a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of its [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory).

Consequently both joint [theories](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) in the two assertions are satisfiable, though the [integer](../integer.md) [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) cannot embed into the natural-number [chain](../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) itself: repeated predecessors would give an infinite descending [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) of [natural numbers](../arithmetic.md#natural-number). The second assertion correctly uses an [elementarily equivalent](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence) ambient structure rather than the original one.

<h5 id="b/4/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/4/d)

[Existential sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-sentence) are preserved under extensions: witnesses remain present and quantifier-free truth is unchanged by an [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding). Therefore any existential axiomatization implies the specified extension-closure property.

For [existential axiomatization from extension closure](../mathematical-logic.md#existential-axiomatization-from-extension-closure), let $E$ be all existential consequences of $T$, and suppose $A\models E$. We claim that $T\cup\operatorname{Th}_\forall(A)$ is satisfiable. Otherwise compactness supplies finitely many [universal sentences](../mathematical-logic.md#universal-sentence) $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m$ true in $A$ such that $T\models\neg(\theta_1\land\cdots\land\theta_m)$. The finite [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) is universal after renaming [bound variables](../mathematical-logic.md#bound-variable), so its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation) is existential. It belongs to $E$ but is false in $A$, a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction).

The preceding universal-theory [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding) result now yields $B\models T$ and $A^*\equiv A$ with $B\subseteq A^*$. By the assumed extension closure, $A^*\models T$; [elementary equivalence](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence) implies $A\models T$. Thus $E$ axiomatizes exactly the [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$, proving **existential axiomatizability is equivalent to closure under extensions**. This includes the [inconsistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) case, whose empty [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) class is axiomatized by an existential [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction).

<h4 id="b/5/a">a</h4>

<h5 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/5/a)

A [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is $\kappa$-categorical if it has a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$ and any two [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of that [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) are isomorphic. The existence clause prevents vacuous categoricity at cardinalities where there is no [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

To show that [infinite-cardinal categoricity forces completeness](../model-theory.md#infinite-cardinal-categoricity-forces-completeness), suppose $T$ were incomplete. Then some sentence $\sigma$ has both $T+\sigma$ and $T+\neg\sigma$ satisfiable, by completeness of [first-order logic](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-logic). Their [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) must be infinite because $T$ has no finite [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). For an infinite $\kappa\ge|L|$, the upward and downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorems](../mathematical-logic.md#lowenheim-skolem-theorem) produce a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of each extension of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) exactly $\kappa$: pass to an elementary submodel of size at most $\max(|L|,\aleph_0)$ and then to the desired size, or reduce a larger [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) to that size. Categoricity makes these two [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) isomorphic, but one satisfies $\sigma$ and the other its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). [Contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Therefore **$T$ is complete**. If language size is defined without automatically including $\aleph_0$, the infinite-cardinal qualification is explicit; the assumed existence of a $\kappa$-sized [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) and absence of finite [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) already force it here.

<h4 id="b/5/b">b</h4>

<h5 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h5>

Let $F_n$ be the formulas whose [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable) are among $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. Define $\phi\,E_n(T)\,\psi$ when $T\models\forall\bar x(\phi\leftrightarrow\psi)$. The [n-variable Lindenbaum algebra](../mathematical-logic.md#n-variable-lindenbaum-algebra) quotient is a [Boolean algebra](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-algebra), with meet, join and complement induced by [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy), disjunction and [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation); its zero and one are [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) and truth classes. Its order is $[\phi]\le[\psi]$ exactly when $T\models\forall\bar x(\phi\to\psi)$.

The [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](../model-theory.md#ryll-nardzewski-theorem) states that for a [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) with infinite [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) language, the following are equivalent: the [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) is countably categorical; for every finite $n$ there are only finitely many complete $n$-types over the empty [set](../set.md); and every [Boolean algebra](../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-algebra) $F_n/E_n(T)$ is finite. Equivalently each such type is isolated. The countable-language and completeness hypotheses are essential parts of this formulation.

For each fixed finite tuple length $n$, use the finite [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{LI}_n(\bar x)=\bigwedge_{\bar a\in K^n\setminus\{0\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{a_j}(x_j)\ne0\right).}
$$

It asserts that no nontrivial [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar-multiplication) combination vanishes, exactly [linear independence](../vector-space.md#linear-independence). It is an $L$-formula because the [field](../algebra.md#field) is finite and every [scalar multiplication](../vector-space.md#scalar-multiplication) is named. [Scalar coefficients](../vector-space.md#scalar-multiplication) are indices of [function symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#function-symbol), not variables ranging over a separate [field](../algebra.md#field) sort. There is no single finite formula with arbitrarily many [free variables](../mathematical-logic.md#free-variable); the assertion is a formula for each prescribed finite $n$.

<h5 id="b/5/c/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/5/c)

<h6 id="b/5/c/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/c/ii)

Use the vector-space [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom): addition is an [abelian group](../group.md#abelian-group) operation with identity zero; $\lambda_1(x)=x$; $\lambda_a\lambda_b(x)=\lambda_{ab}(x)$; $\lambda_a(x+y)=\lambda_a(x)+\lambda_a(y)$; and $\lambda_{a+b}(x)=\lambda_a(x)+\lambda_b(x)$, with $\lambda_0(x)=0$. [Group](../group.md) inverses may be expressed by $\lambda_{-1}$, so no extra inverse symbol is needed.

Add the dimension-unbounded scheme

$$
\boxed{D_n:\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_n\operatorname{LI}_n(\bar x)\qquad(n\ge1).}
$$

Over a [finite field](../algebra.md#finite-field), a finite-dimensional space has finitely many [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements), while every infinite-dimensional space is infinite. Hence this scheme axiomatizes exactly the infinite underlying [vector spaces](../vector-space.md) requested. In this finite-field case the vector-space [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) list itself is finite; the infinitude of the additional [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) scheme is substantive.

<h6 id="b/5/c/iv/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/5/c/iv)

**No.** If a finite [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) $\phi$ axiomatized the infinite spaces, it would follow from the vector-space [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) together with all the $D_n$. This [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction) proves failure of [finite axiomatizability](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-axiomatizability), including [finite axiomatizability](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-axiomatizability) relative to [vector spaces](../vector-space.md).

<h5 id="b/5/c/v">v</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/5/c)

<h6 id="b/5/c/v/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#b/5/c/v)

For [quantifier elimination for infinite underlying vector spaces](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination-for-infinite-underlying-vector-spaces), we first justify that formulas cannot make finer distinctions than their linear-equation data. In any infinite underlying [vector space](../vector-space.md), eliminate an existential variable from a disjunction of [conjunctions](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) of [linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation) and inequations. If an equation has a nonzero [coefficient](../vector-space.md#scalar-multiplication) of that variable, solve for it and substitute. If none does, the inequations forbid only finitely many values, leaving an available value in the infinite domain; the remaining equations concern only the other variables. Terms are [linear combinations](../vector-space.md#linear-combination), so induction gives [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) for $T$ and completeness, since closed terms reduce to zero.

For one variable there are two possible [complete types](../model-theory.md#complete-type), zero and nonzero. Both are isolated, and every definable [set](../set.md) is their [union](../set.md#set-union). Thus

$$
\boxed{F_1/E_1(T)\cong\mathcal P(\{1,2\}),\qquad |F_1/E_1(T)|=4.}
$$

For [types of pairs of vectors over a finite field](../vector-space.md#types-of-pairs-of-vectors-over-a-finite-field), the [linear relation](../vector-space.md#linear-relation) kernel $\{(a,b)\in K^2:ax+by=0\}$ determines the [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type). If $q=|K|$, the possibilities are one rank-zero pair, $q+1$ rank-one directions, and one rank-two independent type. Concretely these are $(0,0)$; $x\ne0$ with $y=\lambda_a(x)$ for each $a\in K$; $x=0,y\ne0$; and the independent pairs. Their isolating formulas are finite because $K$ is finite. Hence

$$
\boxed{F_2/E_2(T)\cong\mathcal P(\{1,\ldots,q+3\}),\qquad |F_2/E_2(T)|=2^{q+3}.}
$$

Every class is the [union](../set.md#set-union) of a selection of these [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra). [Basis extension](../vector-space.md#basis-extension) also shows that pairs with the same relation kernel are conjugate by a [linear automorphism](../vector-space.md#linear-isomorphism) within any infinite-dimensional [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

<h5 id="b/5/c/vi">vi</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/5/c)

<h6 id="b/5/c/vi/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#b/5/c/vi)

Every [vector space](../vector-space.md) has a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) and is classified up to [linear isomorphism](../vector-space.md#linear-isomorphism) by its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space). Over a [finite field](../algebra.md#finite-field), a vector is a finite [linear combination](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) of [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements). If the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) has infinite [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\lambda$, the [set](../set.md) of finite such combinations has [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\lambda$: there are at least $\lambda$ [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector) and at most a [countable union](../set.md#countable-union) of finite powers of $\lambda$, each of size $\lambda$. Thus the underlying [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) of an infinite space equals its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space).

Any two [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa\ge\aleph_0$ therefore have [bases](../vector-space.md#basis) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$. A [bijection](../function.md#bijection) between their [bases](../vector-space.md#basis) extends uniquely to an $L$-isomorphism. A space with that [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) size exists, giving the required existence clause as well. Hence **$T$ is categorical in every infinite cardinal**.

<h4 id="b/5/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/5/d)

The [cardinality of a vector space](../vector-space.md#cardinality-of-a-vector-space) is central: here “infinite” must continue to refer to the underlying [set](../set.md). Over a countably infinite [field](../algebra.md#field), every nonzero finite-dimensional space is already infinite. Thus the [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) class now consists of all nonzero $K$-vector spaces, not only infinite-dimensional ones.

Since [independence of a pair is not definable over an infinite field](../vector-space.md#independence-of-a-pair-is-not-definable-over-an-infinite-field), for (i), independence of a single vector is still $x\ne0$, but independence of tuples of length at least two is not definable by one formula in this one-sorted language. [Quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) follows from the linear-equation argument in the preceding part for all these infinite domains. Any candidate two-variable formula therefore involves only finitely many [linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation). Take an independent pair, and compare it with $(x,\lambda_a x)$, where $x\ne0$ and $a\in K$ avoids the finitely many ratios making one of those nontrivial equations vanish. Both pairs satisfy the same listed atomic formulas, contradicting a proposed definition of independence. Longer tuple independence would define pair independence by existentially completing a pair inside an infinite-dimensional [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory), giving the same obstruction.

For (ii), the vector-space [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) schema together with $\exists x(x\ne0)$ axiomatizes the infinite underlying spaces. For (iii), there is again no universal axiomatization, because the zero subspace is a [substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#substructure-of-a-first-order-structure).

For [finite axiomatizability with infinitely many scalar symbols](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-axiomatizability-with-infinitely-many-scalar-symbols), in (iv) there is no [finite axiomatization](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-axiomatizability) in the full language: any finite [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) [set](../set.md) mentions only finitely many [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar-multiplication) symbols. In a nonzero [vector space](../vector-space.md) alter an unused nonzero $\lambda_a$ to send zero to a nonzero vector, retaining all mentioned interpretations. The finite sentences keep their [truth values](../mathematical-logic.md#truth-value), but a vector-space [axiom](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom) is now false. **Relative to the already imposed vector-space [axioms](../mathematical-logic.md#axiom)**, the extra condition is finitely axiomatized by the single nonzero-vector sentence. These absolute and relative answers must be distinguished.

For (v), the one-variable algebra still has two [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra) and four [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements). In two variables the isolated [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra) are $(0,0)$ and the countably many rank-one directions, represented by $x\ne0,y=\lambda_a x$ and by $x=0,y\ne0$. There is also a nonisolated [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) of an independent pair. A formula using finitely many [linear equations](../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation) either picks finitely many isolated [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra), or contains that independent type and all but finitely many isolated [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra). Therefore **$F_2/E_2(T)$ is the [finite-cofinite Boolean algebra](../mathematical-logic.md#finite-cofinite-boolean-algebra) on a countably [infinite set](../set.md#infinite-set) of [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra)**, with the independent type the extra nonisolated point in its Stone space. In particular this algebra is countably infinite, rather than the full [power set](../set.md#power-set) of its [atoms](../mathematical-logic.md#atom-of-a-boolean-algebra).

For (vi), [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) can be $1,2,3,\ldots$ or $\aleph_0$ while the domain is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set), producing pairwise nonisomorphic [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory). Thus $T$ is not countably categorical. For an uncountable $\kappa$, a [vector space](../vector-space.md) over this [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [field](../algebra.md#field) has [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$ exactly when its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is $\kappa$, since in general a nonzero space has size $\max(|K|,\dim V)$ when that maximum is infinite. [Basis](../vector-space.md#basis) [bijections](../function.md#bijection) then give **categoricity in every uncountable cardinal**. [Quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination) also shows that these different positive [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) are [elementarily equivalent](../model-theory.md#elementary-equivalence); independence of an arbitrarily long tuple is not being silently treated as definable over this infinite [field](../algebra.md#field).

<h3 id="b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/6/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

A complete $n$-type over $T$ is a maximal [set](../set.md) $p(\bar x)$ of formulas in $n$ variables [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with $T$; it decides each such formula or its [negation](../theoretical-computer-science.md#negation). A [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type) drops maximality. A tuple realizes the type if it satisfies every member. A [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) is principal, or isolated, if some formula $\theta(\bar x)$ [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with $T$ implies every member of the type modulo $T$; then $\theta$ itself belongs to that type.

The [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem) says that in a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [first-order language](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-language), a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) has a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) omitting a specified nonprincipal type; more generally a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) family of nonprincipal types can be omitted simultaneously. In the present complete-theory setting it applies directly to complete nonisolated types. “Omitted” means that no tuple in the [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) realizes all formulas of the type, even though each finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is compatible with the [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory).

<h4 id="b/6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

A [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is prime if it has an [elementary embedding](../set-theory.md#elementary-embedding) into every [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$. A [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) is atomic if every finite tuple realizes an isolated [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) over the empty [set](../set.md).

If $A$ is prime, the downward [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](../mathematical-logic.md#lowenheim-skolem-theorem) gives a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $M$ of $T$, and an [elementary embedding](../set-theory.md#elementary-embedding) $A\to M$ makes $A$ [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set). If a tuple of $A$ realized a nonisolated type, the [omitting types theorem](../model-theory.md#omitting-types-theorem) would give a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$ omitting that type. The prime [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding) into it would preserve the tuple's full type and realize the [omitted type](../model-theory.md#omitted-type), a [contradiction](../mathematical-logic.md#contradiction). Thus a [prime model](../model-theory.md#prime-model) is [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) and atomic.

Conversely enumerate a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [atomic model](../model-theory.md#atomic-model) as $a_0,a_1,\ldots$, and take any $B\models T$. Build finite [partial elementary maps](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-elementary-map) into $B$. Suppose the current tuple $\bar a$ maps to $\bar b$. Choose a formula $\theta(\bar x,y)$ isolating the type of $(\bar a,a_n)$. The assertion $\exists y\theta(\bar a,y)$ holds in $A$, so its counterpart holds at $\bar b$ in $B$ by partial elementarity. Choose a witness $b_n$. Since $\theta$ isolates the entire extended tuple type, the extended map is elementary on its finite domain; its equality formulas ensure distinct [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements) remain distinct. The [union](../set.md#set-union) is a total [elementary embedding](../set-theory.md#elementary-embedding) $A\to B$. The empty initial tuple works because $T$ is complete. Hence

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is prime}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\text{ is countable and atomic}.}
$$

<h4 id="b/6/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/6/c)

An $\aleph_0$-saturated [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) realizes every [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) in finitely many variables over every finite parameter [subset](../set.md#subset) of itself. It suffices equivalently to require one-variable types and then realize finite tuples successively. The definition puts no countability restriction on the size of the [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory).

For any [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $M$, collect all [complete types](../model-theory.md#complete-type) over its finite parameter [sets](../set.md). Add to its [elementary diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure), for each such type, a new tuple of constants satisfying all that type's formulas. Any finite selection of these requirements is realizable in $M$: each finite type fragment has its existential closure true there, since it is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with the [complete theory](../model-theory.md#complete-theory) of the corresponding named finite tuple. Distinct new tuples have no additional mutual restrictions. Compactness gives an [elementary extension](../model-theory.md#elementary-extension) realizing all these types simultaneously.

Start from any [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) $M_0\models T$ and apply this construction repeatedly to obtain $M_0\preccurlyeq M_1\preccurlyeq\cdots$, with each successor realizing all types over finite [subsets](../set.md#subset) of its predecessor. The [elementary-chain theorem](../model-theory.md#elementary-chain-theorem) makes $M_\omega=\bigcup_nM_n$ a [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$ with every stage elementary in it. A finite parameter [set](../set.md) $A\subseteq M_\omega$ lies in some $M_n$. Every type over $A$ [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with $M_\omega$ is [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) with $M_n$, since the expanded complete [theories](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory) at those parameters agree by elementarity. It is realized in $M_{n+1}$, hence in the [union](../set.md#set-union). Therefore **an $\aleph_0$-saturated [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) always exists**, possibly with uncountable [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality).

For [cut types over the named rational order](../model-theory.md#cut-types-over-the-named-rational-order), choose an irrational real cut, for example $r=\sqrt2$, and form

$$
p_r(x)=\{c_q<x:q\in\mathbb Q,\ q<r\}\cup\{x<c_q:q\in\mathbb Q,\ q>r\}.
$$

Every finite [subset](../set.md#subset) is realized by a rational between its finite lower and [upper bounds](../set.md#upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set), so compactness makes it a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) [partial type](../mathematical-logic.md#partial-type); [quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination-for-dense-linear-orders-without-endpoints) makes these comparisons determine a [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) in the language naming all rationals. No rational realizes the cut, so $Q^+$ omits it.

Add a new constant realizing this type and apply compactness, followed by downward Löwenheim-Skolem in the [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) expanded language. This gives a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$ realizing it. An [isomorphism](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-isomorphism) in $L_{\mathbb Q}$ fixes all named rationals and preserves realization of the cut, so it cannot be isomorphic to $Q^+$. Thus **$T$ is not countably categorical**. Omission by itself would need this additional realization-and-countable-model argument to establish noncategoricity.

<h5 id="b/6/d/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/6/d)

<h6 id="b/6/d/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/d/ii)

**No infinite cardinal is categorical for this [theory](../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-theory)**, as described by [no categorical cardinal for the complete named rational diagram](../model-theory.md#no-categorical-cardinal-for-the-complete-named-rational-diagram). For an arbitrary infinite $\kappa$, choose a dense linear order $I_\kappa$ without endpoints of size $\kappa$; for example the [lexicographic order](../extremal-set-theory.md#lexicographic-order) on $\kappa\times\mathbb Q$ works, since every rational block is dense and has no endpoints.

Construct one order by inserting $I_\kappa$ into a fixed irrational cut of the named rational order. Every element of this order is bounded above by some named rational. Construct a second order by putting an $I_\kappa$ tail above all named rationals, as the ordered sum $\mathbb Q+I_\kappa$. Both are dense linear orders without endpoints, both have [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $\kappa$, and both contain the named rational copy as an [elementary substructure](../mathematical-logic.md#elementary-substructure) by [quantifier elimination](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination). Hence both satisfy its [complete diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure) $T$.

They are not isomorphic over the named constants: the second has an element greater than every named rational, and the first has none. This property need not be expressible by a single sentence in the infinite language to be an [isomorphism](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-isomorphism) invariant. Finite cardinalities have no [models](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) at all because the constants name infinitely many distinct [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements), so the existence clause excludes categoricity there too.

<h5 id="b/6/d/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/6/d)

<h6 id="b/6/d/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/d/iii)

**Yes: $Q^+$ is prime.** In any [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of its [complete diagram](../model-theory.md#elementary-diagram-of-a-structure), map each rational to the interpretation of its named constant. The diagram ensures this is an [embedding](../mathematical-logic.md#structure-embedding), and [quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints](../model-theory.md#quantifier-elimination-for-dense-linear-orders-without-endpoints) makes it elementary. Alternatively every finite tuple of $Q^+$ has its [complete type](../model-theory.md#complete-type) isolated by the [conjunction](../planetary-science.md#conjunction-astronomy) equating its coordinates to their named rational constants. Thus it is a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [atomic model](../model-theory.md#atomic-model), and the earlier prime-model equivalence applies. A [prime model](../model-theory.md#prime-model) may omit many types; primeness is not saturation.

<h5 id="b/6/d/iv">iv</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#b/6/d)

<h6 id="b/6/d/iv/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#b/6/d/iv)

There are $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct irrational cuts of the named rational order. Each defines a [consistent](../mathematical-logic.md#consistent-first-order-theory) complete one-type as above, already over the empty additional parameter [set](../set.md), since the rational constants are part of the language. Different cuts cannot be realized by the same element: a named rational between two cuts gives contradictory required inequalities.

An $\aleph_0$-saturated [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) must realize every one of these types, so it has at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ [Elements](../euclidean-geometry.md#euclid-s-elements). Consequently **there is no [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) $\aleph_0$-saturated [model](../mathematical-logic.md#model-of-a-first-order-theory) of $T$**. This does not contradict existence of a [saturated model](../model-theory.md#saturated-model) from the preceding construction, whose size was unrestricted, or existence of the [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) [prime model](../model-theory.md#prime-model), whose requirement is different. No continuum-hypothesis assumption is involved.
