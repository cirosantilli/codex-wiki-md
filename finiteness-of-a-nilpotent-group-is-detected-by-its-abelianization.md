# Finiteness of a nilpotent group is detected by its abelianization

↑ **Parent:** [Nilpotent group](nilpotent-group.md)

A [nilpotent group](nilpotent-group.md) is finite exactly when its [abelianization](abelianization.md) is finite; finite generation is not a separate hypothesis. Induct on the nilpotency class $c$. The abelian case is immediate. Otherwise $G/\gamma_c(G)$ has smaller class and the same abelianization, so is finite. Let $H=G$, $K=\gamma_{c-1}(G)$. Their [commutator subgroup](commutator-subgroup.md) $[H,K]=\gamma_c(G)$ is central. Hence its commutator map is a homomorphism in each variable. Both centralizer quotients are finite because their kernels contain $\gamma_c(G)$. Finitely many pairs of coset representatives therefore generate the abelian group $[H,K]$, and its exponent divides the exponent of $H/C_H(K)$. It is finite, completing the induction.
