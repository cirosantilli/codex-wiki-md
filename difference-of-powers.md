# Difference of powers

↑ **Parent:** [Factorization of polynomials](factorization-of-polynomials.md)

For $n\geq1$ and $a,b$ in a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md), multiplying out the displayed [polynomial factorization](factorization-of-polynomials.md) cancels all intermediate terms, leaving $a^n-b^n$. The case $n=2$ is the [difference of squares](difference-of-two-squares.md). Unlike division by $a-b$, this identity also applies when $a=b$.

For real $1\leq b\leq a$, every summand lies between one and $a^{n-1}$. Consequently $n(a-b)\leq a^n-b^n\leq n(a-b)a^{n-1}$. This turns a one-step discrepancy into a bound after repeated multiplication, for example when estimating the [global error](global-discretization-error.md) of a [one-step method](one-step-method.md) on $y'=y$.
