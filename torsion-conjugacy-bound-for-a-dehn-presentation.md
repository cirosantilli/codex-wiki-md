# Torsion conjugacy bound for a Dehn presentation

↑ **Parent:** [Dehn presentation](dehn-presentation.md)

In a finite [Dehn presentation](dehn-presentation.md), every nonidentity finite-order element has a [shortest conjugacy representative](shortest-conjugacy-representative.md) of length at most half the length of some [relator](relator.md). Indeed a positive power contains a Dehn segment. If the representative had length at least $k=\lfloor|r|/2\rfloor+1$, the first $k$ letters of that segment would fit into a cyclic rotation and could be replaced by fewer letters, contradicting minimality. Bounded [relator](relator.md) lengths and a finite alphabet therefore give finitely many [conjugacy classes](conjugacy-class.md) of finite-order elements. With no [relators](relator.md) the group is free and torsion-free, so only the identity class remains.
