# Spin isomers of hydrogen

↑ **Parent:** [Homonuclear diatomic molecule](homonuclear-diatomic-molecule.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_isomers_of_hydrogen)

[Spin isomers of hydrogen](spin-isomers-of-hydrogen.md) are two forms of [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md), $^{1}\mathrm H_2$, distinguished by the combined [nuclear spin](nuclear-spin.md) of their two [protons](proton.md). They have the same [chemical formula](chemical-formula.md) and electronic [ground state](ground-state.md), but different allowed [molecular rotation](molecular-rotation.md) states. [Parahydrogen](parahydrogen.md) has total nuclear [spin](spin.md) zero and even rotational [quantum number](quantum-number.md) $J$; [orthohydrogen](orthohydrogen.md) has total nuclear [spin](spin.md) one and odd $J$. The difference makes [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) a probe of [fermionic exchange symmetry](fermi-dirac-statistics.md), and makes cooling history matter for a tank of [molecular hydrogen](molecular-hydrogen.md).

Assume the electronic [ground state](ground-state.md) $X\,{}^1\Sigma_g^+$ and the vibrational [ground state](ground-state.md) of a free [molecule](molecule-split.md). The electronic factor is symmetric under exchanging the two [protons](proton.md), and the vibrational factor depends only on their separation, so it is also symmetric. These assumptions concern the exchange of [protons](proton.md); the electronic [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) separately obeys [fermionic exchange symmetry](fermi-dirac-statistics.md) under exchange of [electrons](electron.md). Neglect electronic excitation, vibrational excitation, external fields, and small interactions involving [nuclear spin](nuclear-spin.md) when calculating rotational [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md).

The two [protons](proton.md) are [identical particles](indistinguishable-particles.md) with [spin](spin.md) $1/2$. Their complete [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) must change sign under their [particle exchange operator](particle-exchange-operator.md). Interchanging the two ends reverses the internuclear direction $\widehat{\mathbf r}$, and the [spherical harmonic](spherical-harmonic.md) rotational factor satisfies

$$
Y_{JM}(-\widehat{\mathbf r})=(-1)^JY_{JM}(\widehat{\mathbf r}).
$$

The antisymmetric [spin-one-half singlet state](spin-one-half-singlet-state.md) therefore requires symmetric, even-$J$ rotation; the symmetric [spin-one-half triplet state](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) requires antisymmetric, odd-$J$ rotation. This is [exchange symmetry of two identical spin-one-half fermions](exchange-symmetry-of-two-identical-spin-one-half-fermions.md) applied inside a [diatomic molecule](diatomic-molecule.md), not an extra rotational [force](force-split.md). A [linear rigid rotor](linear-rigid-rotor.md) may omit spin-dependent splittings while still requiring this restriction on its allowed [quantum states](quantum-state.md).
