# Unit-circle attracting limit cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Limit cycle](limit-cycle.md)

The planar system $\dot x=y+(1-x^2-y^2)x$, $\dot y=-x+(1-x^2-y^2)y$ has [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md) $\dot r=r(1-r^2)$, $\dot\theta=-1$. Every nonzero [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) approaches the clockwise unit-circle [limit cycle](limit-cycle.md), of period $2\pi$. For $r(t_0)=r_0>0$,

$$
r^2(t)=\frac1{1+(r_0^{-2}-1)e^{-2(t-t_0)}}.
$$

Interior [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) approach the origin backward in time, but exterior [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) have backward [finite-time blow-up of an ordinary differential equation](finite-time-blow-up-of-an-ordinary-differential-equation.md) at $t=t_0+\tfrac12\log(1-r_0^{-2})$. Thus forward convergence to a [limit cycle](limit-cycle.md) does not imply existence for all negative times.
