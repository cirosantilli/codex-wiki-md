# Intrinsic nuclear quadrupole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear deformation](nuclear-deformation.md)

The axial [electric quadrupole moment](electric-quadrupole-moment.md) of an intrinsic [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md), with the origin at its center and the $z$ axis along its symmetry axis:

$$
Q_0=\int \rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf r)(2z^2-x^2-y^2)\,d^3r.
$$

This convention includes [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) in $Q_0$; a tabulated moment divided by the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) $e$ instead has units of area. For the uniform [spheroid](spheroid.md), mapping a uniform unit ball to $(ax,ay,cz)$ gives $\langle x^2\rangle=a^2/5$ and $\langle z^2\rangle=c^2/5$, so

$$
Q_0=\frac25Ze(c^2-a^2),\qquad
\frac{Q_0}{ZeR^2}=\frac25\left(\eta^{4/3}-\eta^{-2/3}\right).
$$

The formula explains the signs in the figure. It measures an anisotropy of the [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md), rather than counting individual [nucleons](nucleon.md) on a surface. An illustrative choice $\eta=\sqrt8$ gives $a=R/\sqrt2$, $c=2R$ and $Q_0=7ZeR^2/5$, while preserving $a^2c=R^3$ exactly.

[James Rainwater](james-rainwater.md)'s lecture contrasts measured laboratory [electric quadrupole moments](electric-quadrupole-moment.md) with intrinsic moments inferred through [Coulomb excitation](coulomb-excitation.md), including [even-even nuclei](even-even-nucleus.md). Extracting intrinsic shape therefore uses a nuclear model, rather than equating every measured moment to $Q_0$.[p. 266, Fig. 4 and preceding Coulomb-excitation discussion](background-for-the-spheroidal-nuclear-model-proposal-by-james-rainwater.md)
