# Subdivision invariance of Euler characteristic

↑ **Parent:** [Euler characteristic](euler-characteristic.md)

In the illustrated [simplicial subdivision](simplicial-subdivision.md), inserting the three [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) midpoints turns one filled [triangle](triangle.md) into four. The counts change from $(V,E,F)=(3,3,1)$ to $(6,9,4)$, but $6-9+4=1$. Splitting a single [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) adds one [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) and one [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md), so its net contribution is $1-1=0$. Adding a diagonal inside a polygonal face adds one [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) and one face, giving $-1+1=0$.

These local accounts explain common refinements. For arbitrary finite [simplicial subdivisions](simplicial-subdivision.md), including [barycentric subdivision](barycentric-subdivision.md) in any [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md), the underlying [geometric realization](geometric-realization-of-a-simplicial-complex.md) is unchanged. The [topological invariance of Euler characteristic](topological-invariance-of-euler-characteristic.md) therefore guarantees the same alternating count, even when the individual counts change substantially.
