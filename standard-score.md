# Standard score

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_score)

A [standard score](standard-score.md) expresses an outcome $x$ as a signed number of [standard deviations](standard-deviation.md) from a [mean](expected-value-split.md) $\mu$, assuming $0<\sigma<\infty$. It is a [dimensionless quantity](dimensionless-quantity.md). For a [random variable](random-variable-split.md) with finite positive [variance](variance-split.md) $\sigma^2$, $(X-\mu)/\sigma$ has [mean](expected-value-split.md) zero and [variance](variance-split.md) one; it has a [standard normal distribution](standard-normal-distribution.md) when $X$ has a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md), but centering and scaling alone do not make other [probability distributions](probability-distribution-split.md) normal. [MIT 18.05, Reading 6b, §5.1: standardization](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/mit18_05_s22_class06-prep-b.pdf).
