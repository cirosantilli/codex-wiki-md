# Trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_pyramidal_molecular_geometry)

In [trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry](trigonal-pyramidal-molecular-geometry.md), three neighbouring [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) form a triangular base and the central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) lies outside its plane. The pyramid has four [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus.md); its central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) is the apex, not the centre of the base. [Ammonia](ammonia.md), $\mathrm{NH}_3$, has this arrangement. The [NIST CCCBDB experimental ammonia entry, geometric data, citing Herzberg (1966)](https://cccbdb.nist.gov/exp2x.asp?casno=7664417&charge=0) lists an H–N–H [bond angle](bond-angle.md) of $106.67^\circ$, commonly rounded to $106.7^\circ$ or about $107^\circ$.

The [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md) of [ammonia](ammonia.md) allocates eight [valence electrons](valence-electron.md) as three [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) and one [lone pair](lone-pair.md). Four [electron domains](electron-domain.md) therefore suggest tetrahedral [electron pair geometry](electron-pair-geometry.md); removing the [lone pair](lone-pair.md) marker leaves three [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) below [nitrogen](nitrogen.md). The [VSEPR model](valence-shell-electron-pair-repulsion-theory.md) treats the [lone pair](lone-pair.md) as more space-demanding and predicts compression relative to $109.4712^\circ$, but it does not calculate the precise $106.67^\circ$ value. See [Chemistry 2e, §7.6: lone-pair effects](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-6-molecular-structure-and-polarity).

Three equal bond-dipole contributions in this pyramid cancel sideways, but retain a component along its axis. By contrast, three equal contributions lying in one plane at $120^\circ$ would cancel completely. This shows how a small change in [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) can permit a permanent [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md). [Ammonia](ammonia.md) is a [polar molecule](polar-molecule.md); NIST lists a measured magnitude of about $1.476$ debye in the same [experimental entry, electric dipole moment, citing Shimizu (1970)](https://cccbdb.nist.gov/exp2x.asp?casno=7664417&charge=0). The vector argument predicts allowed direction and cancellation, not the full measured magnitude.
