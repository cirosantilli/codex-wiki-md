<h1 id="1/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Take positive [circulation](../../../../circulation-physics.md) to mean counterclockwise in the $(x,z)$ diagram, with $x$ right and $z$ up. The upper surface is then traversed right to left. To first order in $a/b$,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\oint\mathbf u\cdot d\mathbf l
=\int_{-b/2}^{b/2}(U_0-u_s)\,dx=-2U_0a.}
$$

Thus the bound [circulation](../../../../circulation-physics.md) is clockwise. Any exterior contour enclosing the [hydrofoil](../../../../hydrofoil.md) has the same [circulation](../../../../circulation-physics.md) in an [irrotational flow](../../../../irrotational-flow.md); a globally completed two-dimensional flow must contain a circulation component decaying as $|\Gamma|/(2\pi r)$ at large distance. The simple local upper/lower velocity prescription cannot describe the entire exterior. For a start from rest, [Kelvin's circulation theorem](../../../../kelvin-s-circulation-theorem.md) requires compensating shed [vorticity](../../../../vorticity-split.md), represented by a starting vortex in a physical wake.

Numerically,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma=-0.40\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}},\qquad
L'_{\rm dynamic}=8.0\times10^2\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}.}
$$

If the question's fluid [force](../../../../force-split.md) includes [buoyancy](../../../../buoyancy.md), $B'\simeq1.25\times10^3\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ and the total upward fluid [force](../../../../force-split.md) is about $2.05\times10^3\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, using $g=9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Weight of the [hydrofoil](../../../../hydrofoil.md) is not specified and is not part of this fluid [force](../../../../force-split.md). The motion-induced [lift](../../../../lift-force-split.md) also agrees with the [Kutta–Joukowski theorem](../../../../kutta-joukowski-theorem.md), $L'=-\rho U_0\Gamma$ in this orientation.

The chord-based [Reynolds number](../../../../reynolds-number.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Re}=\frac{U_0b}{\nu}\simeq2.7\times10^6.}
$$

At this large [Reynolds number](../../../../reynolds-number.md), smooth [laminar flow](../../../../laminar-flow.md) over the whole body is unlikely in ordinary conditions; wall layers may undergo transition and separate. A thin-layer [inviscid flow](../../../../inviscid-flow.md) approximation outside them can remain useful, but does not justify globally [laminar flow](../../../../laminar-flow.md).
