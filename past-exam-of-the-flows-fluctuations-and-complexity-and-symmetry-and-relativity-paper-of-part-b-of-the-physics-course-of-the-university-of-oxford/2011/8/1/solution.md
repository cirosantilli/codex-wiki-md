**Covered by the official notes:** definitions of four-force and four-velocity and the vanishing antisymmetric contraction (PDF p. 15; handwritten p. 10).[PDF p. 15; handwritten p. 10: definitions of four-force and four-velocity and the vanishing antisymmetric contraction](../../../../symmetry-and-relativity-2011-exam-solution-notes.md)

To make the necessity statement precise, mass preservation must hold **for every allowed timelike four-velocity**, with the same field [tensor](../../../../tensor.md) and nonzero charge. It is not necessary for one prescribed trajectory alone. Use [metric signature](../../../../metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$, so $U_aF^a=-c^2dm/d\tau$. Put $H^{ab}=(\mathcal F^{ab}+\mathcal F^{ba})/2$. The mass-preservation condition requires the [quadratic form](../../../../quadratic-form-split.md) $H^{ab}U_aU_b=0$ for every timelike $U$. Every timelike vector can be rescaled to a physical [four-velocity](../../../../four-velocity.md); by homogeneity the quadratic form vanishes on the open timelike cone. A polynomial vanishing on an open set is identically zero, so $H^{ab}=0$. Thus the [electromagnetic field tensor](../../../../electromagnetic-field-tensor.md) must be antisymmetric under this universal quantifier. For a single velocity, the scalar contraction can vanish even for a nonzero symmetric part, which is why the quantifier is essential.
