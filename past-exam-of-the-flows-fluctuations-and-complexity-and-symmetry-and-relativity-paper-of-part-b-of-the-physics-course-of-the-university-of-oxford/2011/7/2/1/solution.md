**Covered by the official notes:** definition of a pure force via constant rest mass (PDF p. 13; handwritten p. 8).[PDF p. 13; handwritten p. 8: definition of a pure force via constant rest mass](../../../../../symmetry-and-relativity-2011-exam-solution-notes.md)

The page labels $dp^\mu/d\tau$ as a proper force; that is the [four-force](../../../../../four-force.md). The requested [proper force](../../../../../proper-force.md) is the **three-force measured in the instantaneous rest frame of the object**. For a [pure force](../../../../../pure-force.md) its magnitude is $m_0$ times the [proper acceleration](../../../../../proper-acceleration.md), and in that frame the [four-force](../../../../../four-force.md) has components $(0,\mathbf f_0)$.
