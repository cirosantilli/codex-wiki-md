<h1 id="2/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Put $A=2\mu aF_0k/(cf)$. In the specified near-wall limit the outer [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) is $T-T_0\simeq A/(bz)$, so

$$
q_C\simeq\frac{2\kappa A}{bz^3},\qquad
\frac{|q_C|}{q_R}\simeq\frac{4\kappa\mu a}{\rho cfbz^3}
=\frac{4\kappa\nu a}{cfbz^3},\qquad
\chi=\frac{\kappa}{\rho c}.
$$

Thus the [near-wall radiative heating layer](../../../../near-wall-radiative-heating-layer.md) has scale

$$
\boxed{0<z\lesssim\delta_C,\qquad
\delta_C=\left(\frac{4\kappa\mu a}{\rho cfb}\right)^{1/3}
=\left(\frac{4\kappa\nu a}{cfb}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

The comparison is an estimate of the breakdown of the outer solution, not a new inner [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) solution. It requires $\delta_C\ll b$ and $k\rho\delta_C\ll1$; neglecting longitudinal relative to transverse [thermal conduction](../../../../thermal-conduction.md) additionally requires $\delta_C\ll a$ in the region to which the estimate is applied.
