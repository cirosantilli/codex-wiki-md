# Monomial inflation with a prescribed endpoint

↑ **Parent:** [Monomial inflation potential](monomial-inflation-potential.md)

For $V=\lambda\phi^n$ on $\phi>0$, [slow-roll approximation](slow-roll-approximation.md) gives $\epsilon\simeq n^2M_{\rm Pl}^2/(2\phi^2)$ and $\eta\simeq2nM_{\rm Pl}^2/\phi^2$. The [slow-roll e-fold count](slow-roll-e-fold-count.md) integrates to $N\simeq(\phi_N^2-\phi_e^2)/(2nM_{\rm Pl}^2)$. For $n=2,3,4$, the loose order-one conditions $\epsilon\leq1$ and $|\eta|\leq1$ imply $\phi^2\geq2nM_{\rm Pl}^2$ in this approximation. Genuine slow roll requires a much stronger hierarchy. A prescribed endpoint $\phi_e^2=2nM_{\rm Pl}^2$ gives $\phi_N^2=2n(N+1)M_{\rm Pl}^2$ and

$$
n_s\simeq1-\frac{n+2}{2(N+1)},\qquad r\simeq\frac{4n}{N+1}.
$$

The actual end criterion for accelerated expansion is $\epsilon=1$, whose leading potential estimate instead gives $\phi_e^2\simeq n^2M_{\rm Pl}^2/2$. The prescribed endpoint is a convention, not an exact slow-roll or inflation boundary.
