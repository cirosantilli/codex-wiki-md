# Neutron half-life effect on primordial helium

↑ **Parent:** [Primordial helium mass fraction](primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md)

After [cosmological weak freeze-out](cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md), the surviving [neutron](neutron.md) fraction is $x_n=r_f(1+r_f)^{-1}e^{-\Delta t/\tau_n}$, where $r_f$ is the initial [neutron-to-proton ratio](neutron-to-proton-ratio.md) and $\tau_n=t_{1/2}/\ln2$ is the mean lifetime. [Baryon number](baryon-number.md) is conserved because a decaying [neutron](neutron.md) becomes a [proton](proton.md). If almost all surviving neutrons enter helium-4, [neutron-limited helium synthesis](neutron-limited-helium-synthesis.md) gives the displayed [primordial helium mass fraction](primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md). Increasing the [half-life](half-life.md) increases survival. If the longer lifetime also represents uniformly weaker [weak interactions](weak-interaction.md), it raises the temperature of [cosmological weak freeze-out](cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md) and the initial neutron fraction, reinforcing the helium increase. That last conclusion requires specifying how the weak rates change, rather than treating lifetime alone as a complete microscopic model.
