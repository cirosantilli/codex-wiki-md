# Settling speed of a nearly occluding sphere without through-flow

↑ **Parent:** [Lubrication resistance integrals for a nearly occluding sphere](lubrication-resistance-integrals-for-a-nearly-occluding-sphere.md)

A [sphere](sphere.md) of radius $R$ settling in a stationary cylindrical tube of radius $R+d$, with $d\ll R$ and zero net through-flow, has leading speed

$$
U\sim\frac{8\sqrt2}{27\pi}\frac{\Delta\rho g d^2}{\mu}\sqrt{\frac dR}.
$$

The neck gap is $H(z)=d+z^2/(2R)$. In the [sphere](sphere.md) frame, leading bypass flux per circumferential length is $q\simeq UR/2$; wall-motion transport is smaller by $d/R$. The [pressure-driven flux through a parabolic lubrication gap](pressure-driven-flux-through-a-parabolic-lubrication-gap.md) gives $\Delta p=9\pi\mu UR\sqrt{2R}/(4d^{5/2})$. [Pressure](pressure.md) dominates the [sphere](sphere.md) drag, $F\simeq\pi R^2\Delta p$, and balancing $F$ against $4\pi R^3\Delta\rho g/3$ proves the speed. Tube end conditions allowing appreciable net flow change the bypass balance and can give other drag regimes.
