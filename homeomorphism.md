# Homeomorphism

↑ **Parent:** [Topology](topology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphism)

A homeomorphism is a [bijection](bijection.md) between [topological spaces](topological-space.md) such that both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are [continuous maps](continuous-map.md). Equivalently, for every [subset](subset.md) $U\subseteq X$, $U$ is an [open set](open-set.md) if and only if $f(U)$ is an [open set](open-set.md) in $Y$. A [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md) therefore transports the entire [topology](topology-split.md), including [connectedness](connected-space.md) and [compactness](compact-space.md). It need not preserve numerical [metric](metric.md) values, and a [bounded set](bounded-set.md) need not map to a [bounded set](bounded-set.md).

For example, an explicit [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md) between an [open interval](open-interval.md) and the [real line](real-line.md) is

$$
f:(0,1)\to\mathbb R,\qquad f(x)=\tan\bigl(\pi(x-1/2)\bigr),\qquad
f^{-1}(y)=\frac12+\frac{\arctan y}{\pi}.
$$

The [tangent function](tangent-function.md) on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ is strictly increasing and takes every real value; the displayed [arctangent](inverse-tangent.md) inverse verifies the [bijection](bijection.md). Both formulas define [continuous maps](continuous-map.md) on their stated domains. Computed values are $f(1/4)=-1$, $f(1/2)=0$, and $f(3/4)=1$. Points approach infinity as $x$ approaches an excluded endpoint. Thus a [bounded set](bounded-set.md) can have the same [topology](topology-split.md) as an unbounded one; neither of these [topological spaces](topological-space.md) is [compact](compact-space.md).

A useful shortcut is the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md): a [continuous](continuous-function.md) [bijection](bijection.md) from a [compact space](compact-space.md) to a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md) is a [homeomorphism](homeomorphism.md). Its images of [closed sets](closed-set.md) are [compact sets](compact-space.md), hence [closed sets](closed-set.md) in the target, which proves [continuity](continuous-function.md) of the inverse. The [indiscrete topology](indiscrete-topology.md) example shows why the [Hausdorff](hausdorff-space.md) assumption matters.
