# Circle integral of a logarithm with a radial branch cut

↑ **Parent:** [Complex logarithm](complex-logarithm.md)

Take a [complex logarithm](complex-logarithm.md) whose radial [branch cut](branch-cut.md) has angle $\beta$. On a positively oriented circle, use the one-sided branch values on either side of its crossing with the cut. Parametrize from $\theta=\beta$ to $\beta+2\pi$; adding an integer multiple of $2\pi i$ to the branch does not change the integral because the integral of $dz$ is zero. Along the open arc, a primitive is $z\operatorname{Log}z-z$. Its endpoint difference is $2\pi i$ times the cut-crossing point $Re^{i\beta}$, proving the displayed formula. The circle crosses the cut, so this is a piecewise contour integral of boundary values, not the integral of a single holomorphic logarithm on a neighborhood of a closed circle. The [Cauchy integral theorem](cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md) therefore does not make it zero.
