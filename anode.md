# Anode

↑ **Parent:** [Electrode](electrode.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anode)

The [anode](anode.md) is the [electrode](electrode.md) at which the net [electrode](electrode.md) [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) is [oxidation](oxidation.md): the reacting [chemical species](chemical-species.md) loses [electrons](electron.md). This definition is independent of terminal sign. See [IUPAC Gold Book: anode](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00370).

During [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) discharge, a [zinc](zinc.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) becomes a $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ [cation](cation.md), leaving two [electrons](electron.md) in the [electrode](electrode.md); those [electrons](electron.md) leave through the external [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md). The [zinc](zinc.md) [anode](anode.md) is the negative terminal. In an externally driven [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md), [electrons](electron.md) instead leave the [anode](anode.md) through the lead to the source's positive terminal. [Conventional current](conventional-current.md) in that lead points toward the [anode](anode.md), opposite to [electron](electron.md) motion. The [anode](anode.md) can therefore be positive while still being the site of [oxidation](oxidation.md). See [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) for the balanced [half-reaction](half-reaction.md) and [electrode](electrode.md) for the operating-mode sign conventions.
