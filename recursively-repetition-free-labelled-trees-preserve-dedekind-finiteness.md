# Recursively repetition-free labelled trees preserve Dedekind-finiteness

↑ **Parent:** [Recursively repetition-free labelled tree](recursively-repetition-free-labelled-tree.md)

If $D$ is an [infinite Dedekind-finite set](infinite-dedekind-finite-set.md), then $\mathcal T(D)$ is another such [set](set-split.md). Single-vertex [rooted trees](rooted-tree.md) inject $D$ into it. A countable [sequence](sequence.md) of distinct [rooted trees](rooted-tree.md) gives finite lists of labels by the [depth-first traversal of a tree](depth-first-traversal-of-a-tree.md) in the prescribed child order. By [countable union of explicitly ordered finite lists without choice](countable-union-of-explicitly-ordered-finite-lists-without-choice.md), an infinite union of labels contradicts Dedekind-finiteness. A finite union $F$ is equally impossible, since every [rooted tree](rooted-tree.md) then lies in the [finite set](finite-set.md) $\mathcal T(F)$, by [mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md) on $|F|$ using the recursive child rule. Thus no countably infinite subset of [rooted trees](rooted-tree.md) exists.
