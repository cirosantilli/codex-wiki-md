# Discrete metric

↑ **Parent:** [Metric](metric.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_metric)

The [discrete metric](discrete-metric.md) sets $d(x,x)=0$ and $d(x,y)=1$ for distinct points. It induces the [discrete topology](discrete-space.md), and its [Cauchy sequences](cauchy-sequence.md) are exactly the eventually constant [sequences](sequence.md).

Every nonempty [set](set-split.md) equipped with the [discrete metric](discrete-metric.md) is a [bounded set](bounded-set.md), even if it is an [infinite set](infinite-set.md). For $0<r<1$, both the [open ball](open-ball.md) and [closed ball](closed-ball.md) about $a$ are the [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md) $\{a\}$. At $r=1$, the [open ball](open-ball.md) is still $\{a\}$, but the [closed ball](closed-ball.md) is the whole space; for $r>1$, both are the whole space. Every [subset](subset.md) is both an [open set](open-set.md) and a [closed set](closed-set.md). Consequently the [boundary](boundary-of-a-set.md) of the radius-one [closed ball](closed-ball.md) is empty, even though every point other than $a$ is at distance exactly one. Also, the [closure](closure-topology.md) of $B_d(a,1)$ is $\{a\}$, rather than the radius-one [closed ball](closed-ball.md).
