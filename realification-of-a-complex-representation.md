# Realification of a complex representation

↑ **Parent:** [Real representation](real-representation.md)

A complex [group representation](group-representation.md) $\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb C)$ defines a real representation on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ by viewing each complex coordinate as its real and imaginary parts. In grouped coordinates its matrices are the [realification of a complex matrix](realification-of-a-complex-matrix.md) shown in the title. Direct block multiplication gives $\mathcal R(AB)=\mathcal R(A)\mathcal R(B)$ and $\mathcal R(I_n)=I_{2n}$, proving the representation property and preservation of invertibility.

Taking the [trace](matrix-trace.md) gives its [character](character-of-a-representation.md) $\chi_\mathbb R(g)=2\operatorname{Re}\chi_\mathbb C(g)$. After [complexification of a real vector space](complexification-of-a-real-vector-space.md), this realification splits as the original representation and its conjugate. Realification need not preserve irreducibility: the answer depends on whether the irreducible complex representation has real, complex or [quaternionic representation](pseudoreal-representation.md) type. The standard two-dimensional complex representation of the [quaternion group](quaternion-group.md) gives a four-dimensional real example with character four at the identity, minus four at its central negative identity, and zero at the other six elements.
