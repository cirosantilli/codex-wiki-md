# Subsequential limit

↑ **Parent:** [Subsequence](subsequence.md)

A subsequential limit of a [sequence](sequence.md) $(a_n)$ is a value $L$ approached by some [subsequence](subsequence.md) $(a_{n_k})$ with strictly increasing indices $n_k$. For $a_n=(-1)^n(1+1/n)$, the [subsequences](subsequence.md) at [even integers](even-number.md) and [odd integers](odd-integer.md) give [subsequential limits](subsequential-limit.md) one and minus one. A finite value appearing among only finitely many initial terms does not become a [subsequential limit](subsequential-limit.md) merely by being a member of the [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md) represented by the [sequence](sequence.md).

For a [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md) of [real numbers](real-number.md), its [subsequential limits](subsequential-limit.md) form a nonempty [bounded set](bounded-set.md) that is also a [closed set](closed-set.md). The [Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem](bolzano-weierstrass-theorem.md) guarantees nonemptiness, and its extreme values are the [limit inferior](limit-inferior.md) and [limit superior](limit-superior.md). The [sequence](sequence.md) in [sparse peaks can determine a limit superior](sparse-peaks-can-determine-a-limit-superior.md) has the two [subsequential limits](subsequential-limit.md) zero and one, despite almost all indices in the [natural density](natural-density.md) sense carrying zero.
