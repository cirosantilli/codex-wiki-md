# Constant matrix perturbation of a Weyl Hamiltonian

↑ **Parent:** [Weyl Hamiltonian](weyl-hamiltonian.md)

Adding a spatially constant [Hermitian matrix](hermitian-operator.md) $V=dI+\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ to an isotropic [Weyl Hamiltonian](weyl-hamiltonian.md) preserves each momentum sector. By the [Hermitian two-by-two matrix in the Pauli basis](hermitian-two-by-two-matrix-in-the-pauli-basis.md) decomposition, the exact energies are $d\pm|\hbar v\mathbf k+\mathbf b|$. The touching point moves to $\mathbf k_*=-\mathbf b/(\hbar v)$ at energy $d$, without opening a gap in three-dimensional momentum space.

For a fixed $k_z=0$ slice, however,

$$
E_\pm=d\pm\sqrt{(\hbar vk_x+b_x)^2+(\hbar vk_y+b_y)^2+b_z^2}.
$$

This slice has minimum branch separation $2|b_z|$ if $b_z\ne0$, because it misses the shifted node. A one-dimensional cut can likewise look gapped while a crossing exists elsewhere. No nonzero two-by-two matrix anticommutes with all three [Pauli matrices](pauli-matrices.md), which is another way to see why an independent constant mass matrix is unavailable for this isolated three-dimensional two-component model.
