<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Take the wind to blow toward positive $x$, so $U_0\geq0$. In [subsonic flow](../../../../subsonic-flow.md), both stationary observers record the source [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\omega$ and, for the imposed density boundary data, the same [acoustic density perturbation](../../../../acoustic-density-perturbation.md) amplitude $\rho_0|A|$. Their spatial records distinguish the [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md) and directions:

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\mathrm{down}}=\frac{2\pi(c_0+U_0)}{\omega},\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}=\frac{2\pi(c_0-U_0)}{\omega}.}
$$

The downstream pattern moves at $c_0+U_0$ and the upstream pattern at signed [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $U_0-c_0$. Thus the downstream observer sees longer [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md) while the upstream observer sees shorter ones. The interval containing multiple [wavelengths](../../../../wavelength.md) lets each measure the spatial spacing and its direction of travel. The arrival delays at $\pm L$ are $L/(c_0+U_0)$ and $L/(c_0-U_0)$ respectively; after arrival their temporal traces differ by these propagation [phases](../../../../phase-waves.md), not by temporal [frequency](../../../../frequency.md).

In [supersonic flow](../../../../supersonic-flow.md), both [characteristic curves](../../../../characteristic-curve.md) have positive slopes $dx/dt=U_0\pm c_0$. With an initially undisturbed upstream region, **the observer at $-L$ sees no source-generated disturbance**. The observer at $+L$ can see two downstream [travelling waves](../../../../travelling-wave.md), with

$$
k_+=\frac{\omega}{U_0+c_0},\qquad k_-=\frac{\omega}{U_0-c_0},\qquad
\rho_1=\rho_0\operatorname{Re}\left[e^{-i\omega t}\left(A_+e^{ik_+x}+A_-e^{ik_-x}\right)\right].
$$

The faster signal arrives first, at $L/(U_0+c_0)$; the slower one arrives at $L/(U_0-c_0)$. Once both are present, their different [wavenumbers](../../../../wavenumber.md) can produce spatial modulation, while the time dependence at a fixed position remains harmonic with [angular frequency](../../../../angular-frequency.md) $\omega$.

There is a qualification to the source data: the single prescribed value of $\rho_1(0^+,t)$ fixes only $A_++A_-=A$. It does not determine the two [complex amplitudes](../../../../complex-amplitude.md) separately. A second source condition, such as its downstream [acoustic particle velocity](../../../../acoustic-particle-velocity.md) with amplitude $c_0(A_+-A_-)$, or a specified mass/force source model, is needed. Consequently two downstream branches are allowed; the stated density data alone do not require both to have nonzero amplitude. Extending a formally upstream-going subsonic formula into $x<0$ when $U_0>c_0$ would impose a signal arriving from upstream infinity and violate the causal source interpretation.

As $U_0\to c_0$, one laboratory [phase velocity](../../../../phase-velocity.md) tends to zero. Its [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) tends to zero, its [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md) diverges, and its arrival time diverges; a finite-time experiment never attains the assumed established upstream field at fixed $L$. Finite source size, [viscosity](../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md), thermal losses and eventually molecular scales invalidate this singular [inviscid flow](../../../../inviscid-flow.md) limit. With effective acoustic diffusivity $\nu_a$, a loss estimate is $\nu_a k^2/\omega\sim\nu_a\omega/(U_0-c_0)^2$, which diverges. Moreover the finite-amplitude change in the characteristic [speed](../../../../speed.md) is of order $c_0|A|$, so uniform [linearization](../../../../linearization.md) requires $|U_0-c_0|\gg c_0|A|$. At exactly sonic background [speed](../../../../speed.md), the zero-speed branch cannot support a nonzero-$\omega$ harmonic solution. [Nonlinear acoustics](../../../../nonlinear-acoustics.md) and losses must resolve the near-sonic regime; an unlimited spatial compression of the ideal wave is not physical.
