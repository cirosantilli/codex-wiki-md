<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At a fixed point beside a river, the [Eulerian coordinate](../../../../eulerian-coordinate.md) description measures $\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t)$. Its local [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) $\partial_t\mathbf u$ measures change at that fixed point. Following a floating parcel gives a [Lagrangian trajectory](../../../../lagrangian-trajectory.md) $\mathbf X(t)$ satisfying $\dot{\mathbf X}=\mathbf u(\mathbf X,t)$. The [chain rule](../../../../chain-rule.md) gives its physical [material acceleration](../../../../material-acceleration.md):

$$
\boxed{\ddot{\mathbf X}=\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt}
=\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u.}
$$

The extra term is the [convective acceleration](../../../../convective-acceleration.md). For a river that narrows steadily, an observer at one fixed position measures a constant [velocity](../../../../velocity.md), yet a parcel speeds up as it reaches the narrowing. A parcel following a steady bend also experiences a [centripetal acceleration](../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md) even when its speed is constant. An Eulerian calculation of the physical [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) must include the same [convective acceleration](../../../../convective-acceleration.md); the two descriptions do not predict different forces.
