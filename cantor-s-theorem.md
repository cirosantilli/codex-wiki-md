<h1 id="cantor-s-theorem">Cantor's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Power set](power-set.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_theorem)

There is no surjection from a set $X$ onto its [power set](power-set.md) $\mathcal P(X)$. Consequently $\mathcal P(X)$ has strictly greater cardinality than $X$.

For any candidate [function](function-split.md) $f:X\to\mathcal P(X)$, form $D=\{x\in X:x\notin f(x)\}$. This is a [subset](subset.md) of $X$ by [set comprehension](restricted-set-comprehension.md). If $f(d)=D$, then $d\in D$ holds exactly when $d\notin D$, a [contradiction](contradiction.md). Thus $D$ is missing from the image of $f$. On the other hand, $x\mapsto\{x\}$ is an [injection](injective-function.md) from $X$ into its [power set](power-set.md), establishing the strict comparison of [cardinalities](cardinality.md). For $X=\mathbb N$, the omitted selection is the subset version of [Cantor's diagonal argument](cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md).
