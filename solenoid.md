# Solenoid

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic field](magnetic-field.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoid)

A [solenoid](solenoid.md) is a coil of [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md), usually wire wound into a [helix](helix.md) around a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md). The same [electric current](electric-current.md) $I$ flows through its successive turns. Each turn contributes a [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md); the [superposition principle](superposition-principle.md) makes their axial contributions reinforce inside the coil. Curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of the [electric current](electric-current.md) around the turns: the thumb gives the interior [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) direction by the [right-hand rule](right-hand-rule.md). Throughout this branch, $N$ is the total number of turns, $\ell$ is the coil [length](length.md), and $n=N/\ell$ is the [solenoid turn density](solenoid-turn-density.md). [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II.13–5: the field of a long coil](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_13.html#Ch13-S5).

The attraction of a long [solenoid](solenoid.md) is a controllable, nearly uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) over an interior working region: change the [electric current](electric-current.md) to tune its strength, or reverse the [electric current](electric-current.md) to reverse its direction. This makes it a useful setting for studying the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md), measuring [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md), and constructing an [inductor](inductor.md). Its geometry turns the many contributions from individual turns into the simple [long-solenoid approximation](long-solenoid-approximation.md). A real finite coil has end fields and leakage; its exterior [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md) is not zero. The [on-axis magnetic field of a finite solenoid](on-axis-magnetic-field-of-a-finite-solenoid.md) below quantifies that difference.

Historically, the coil helped connect [magnetic fields](magnetic-field.md) to [electric currents](electric-current.md). In his memoir on the mathematical theory of electrodynamic phenomena, Ampère modeled the interactions of magnets using the ends of solenoids in place of microscopic magnetic constituents. This was a route toward explaining magnetism by [electric currents](electric-current.md), rather than a modern microscopic account of [magnetization](magnetization.md). [Ampère, Mémoire sur la théorie mathématique des phénomènes électro-dynamiques, concluding discussion on p. 289](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/download/pdf/1038813). The historical setting is documented by the [Ampère archives, investigations of current interactions in 1820–1826](https://ampere-archives.fr/histoire-electricite/parcours-historique/lois-courants/ampere-loi).
