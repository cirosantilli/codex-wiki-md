<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Since the [general linear Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#general-linear-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak{gl}_1(k)$ has zero [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket), identify it with $kz$. A [central extension of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra) is a [short exact sequence](../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence)

$$
0\longrightarrow kz\xrightarrow{\iota}\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathfrak g\longrightarrow0
$$

of [Lie algebra homomorphisms](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-homomorphism) with $\iota(kz)$ contained in the [center of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#center-of-a-lie-algebra) $Z(\widetilde{\mathfrak g})$. Thus $\pi$ is surjective, its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is precisely the indicated line, and that line commutes with all of $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$.

Choose a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $s:\mathfrak g\to\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ with $\pi s=\operatorname{id}$. Such a section exists by lifting a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). Identify $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ with the [direct sum of vector spaces](../vector-space.md#direct-sum) $\mathfrak g\oplus kz$ by $(x,tz)\mapsto s(x)+t\iota(z)$. The difference $[s(x),s(y)]-s([x,y])$ belongs to the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of $\pi$, so there is a unique [bilinear map](../multilinear-algebra.md#bilinear-map) $\alpha:\mathfrak g\times\mathfrak g\to k$ such that

$$
[s(x),s(y)]-s([x,y])=\alpha(x,y)\iota(z).
$$

It is an [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form): $\alpha(x,x)=0$, equivalently $\alpha(x,y)=-\alpha(y,x)$ in [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). Taking the central component of the [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) gives the [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) condition

$$
\alpha([x,y],w)+\alpha([y,w],x)+\alpha([w,x],y)=0\qquad(x,y,w\in\mathfrak g).
$$

Conversely, any [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form) satisfying this identity defines a [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) by

$$
\boxed{[(x,tz),(y,uz)]=([x,y],\alpha(x,y)z).}
$$

Indeed its [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) is an [alternating multilinear map](../multilinear-algebra.md#alternating-multilinear-map) and a [bilinear map](../multilinear-algebra.md#bilinear-map), and the two components of its [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) are respectively the [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) in $\mathfrak g$ and the displayed [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) condition. The line $kz$ is central, and projection to $\mathfrak g$ gives the required [central extension of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra).

For later use, replacing $s$ by $s'(x)=s(x)+t(x)\iota(z)$, where $t\in\mathfrak g^*$ is in the [dual space](../dual-space.md), changes the [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) to

$$
\boxed{\alpha'(x,y)=\alpha(x,y)-t([x,y]).}
$$

Thus changes of section add a [Lie algebra two-coboundary](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-coboundary), up to the conventional sign. A section that is a [Lie algebra homomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-homomorphism) has $\alpha'=0$ and gives a [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Choose a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $x,y$ of the two-dimensional [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak a$, and lifts with the same names. Every [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form) is determined by $c=\alpha(x,y)$, and every such form is a [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) because every [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) in $\mathfrak a$ is zero. Thus every [central extension of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra) has a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $x,y,z$ with

$$
[x,y]=cz,\qquad [x,z]=[y,z]=0.
$$

If $c=0$, all [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) vanish, so this is the [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras) $\mathfrak a\oplus kz\cong k^3$. If $c\ne0$, replace the central [basis vector](../vector-space.md#basis-vector) by $z'=cz$. Then $[x,y]=z'$, which is the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#heisenberg-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak h_3(k)$. This change of [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) shows that every nonzero $c$ gives the same [Lie algebra isomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-isomorphism) class.

These two [Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md) cannot be isomorphic: their [derived algebras](../lie-algebra.md#derived-algebra) have [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) zero and one, respectively, and a [Lie algebra isomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-isomorphism) preserves the [derived algebra](../lie-algebra.md#derived-algebra). Moreover, the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#heisenberg-lie-algebra) cannot be a [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras) $kz\oplus\mathfrak a$, since that [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras) would have zero [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\mathfrak a}\cong k^3\quad\text{or}\quad\mathfrak h_3(k).}
$$

Here “up to isomorphism” means the [Lie algebra isomorphism](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-isomorphism) class of the middle [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md), allowing a change of the identifications at the ends of the [short exact sequence](../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence). If instead [equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#equivalence-of-central-extensions-of-a-lie-algebra) must induce the identity on both $kz$ and $\mathfrak a$, the parameter $c$ cannot change: the formula in part (a) has $t([x,y])=0$. That stricter notion has one class for each $c\in k$, rather than two. This distinction explains the convention required by the question.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

First put the nonabelian two-dimensional [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) in a convenient form. For any [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $p,q$, let $u=[p,q]=ap+bq\ne0$. Then $[p,u]=bu$ and $[q,u]=-au$. Since $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, some [linear combination](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) $x$ of $p,q$ has $[x,u]=u$. In particular, $x,u$ are [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence). Taking $y=u$ gives a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) with

$$
[x,y]=y.
$$

This is the [affine Lie algebra of the line](../lie-algebra.md#affine-lie-algebra-of-the-line).

Every [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form) on this [vector space](../vector-space.md) is

$$
\alpha_c(rx+sy,tx+uy)=c(ru-st)\qquad(c\in k).
$$

For any [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form) $\alpha$, the expression

$$
J_\alpha(v,w,t)=\alpha([v,w],t)+\alpha([w,t],v)+\alpha([t,v],w)
$$

is an [alternating trilinear form](../multilinear-algebra.md#alternating-trilinear-form): it is trilinear, and setting any two arguments equal makes the terms cancel. Every [alternating trilinear form](../multilinear-algebra.md#alternating-trilinear-form) on a two-dimensional [vector space](../vector-space.md) vanishes, since any triple is [linearly dependent](../vector-space.md#linear-dependence). Hence every $\alpha_c$ satisfies the [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) condition, and there are no others:

$$
\boxed{Z^2(\mathfrak s,k)=\{\alpha_c:c\in k\}\cong k,\qquad\alpha_c(x,y)=c.}
$$

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

For the [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) $\alpha_c$ of part (i), the [central extension of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra) has a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $x,y,z$ and [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket)

$$
[x,y]=y+cz,\qquad[x,z]=[y,z]=0.
$$

Put $y'=y+cz$. This is still a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) together with $x,z$, and

$$
[x,y']=[x,y]+c[x,z]=y+cz=y'.
$$

Consequently $\langle x,y'\rangle$ is a [Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#lie-subalgebra) isomorphic to the [affine Lie algebra of the line](../lie-algebra.md#affine-lie-algebra-of-the-line). It commutes with $kz$ and has zero intersection with $kz$, so both summands are [ideals of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) and

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\mathfrak s}\cong\mathfrak s\oplus kz:\quad\text{exactly one class, which is trivial}.}
$$

This also holds for the stricter [equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#equivalence-of-central-extensions-of-a-lie-algebra). Indeed, choose the [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) $t$ with $t(x)=0$, $t(y)=c$. Since $[x,y]=y$ in $\mathfrak s$, the change of section in part (a) gives $\alpha'_c(x,y)=c-t(y)=0$. Thus every [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-cocycle) here is a [Lie algebra two-coboundary](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-two-coboundary), so $H^2(\mathfrak s,k)=0$ in [Lie algebra cohomology](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-cohomology). The explicit map from the split [central extension of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra) sends $x$ to $x$, $y$ to $y+cz$, and $z$ to $z$; it fixes the central line and induces the identity on the [quotient Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#quotient-lie-algebra).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Let $A$ be a three-dimensional [vector space](../vector-space.md) with [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $p,q,z$. Define a [bilinear map](../multilinear-algebra.md#bilinear-map) $\bullet:A\times A\to A$ by

$$
p\bullet q=z,\qquad q\bullet p=-z,
$$

with all other products of [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector) zero. Equivalently,

$$
(ap+bq+cz)\bullet(a'p+b'q+c'z)=(ab'-ba')z.
$$

Every product belongs to $kz$, and every product with an element of $kz$ is zero. Hence both $(u\bullet v)\bullet w$ and $u\bullet(v\bullet w)$ vanish for every $u,v,w\in A$. This proves [associativity](../algebraic-operation.md#associative-property), so $A$ is an [associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md). The product is an [alternating multilinear map](../multilinear-algebra.md#alternating-multilinear-map), and each nested product in the [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) is zero, so the same product is a [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket). It is nonzero because $p\bullet q=z\ne0$.

There is no multiplicative [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element): any candidate $1_A$ would satisfy $1_A\bullet z=0$, whereas an [identity element](../algebraic-operation.md#identity-element) would require $1_A\bullet z=z$. Thus

$$
\boxed{A=kp\oplus kq\oplus kz,\quad p\bullet q=z=-q\bullet p,\quad A^2=kz,\quad A^3=0.}
$$

This is an [associative Lie product](../associative-algebra.md#associative-lie-product); its underlying [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) is the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#heisenberg-lie-algebra).

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For a [perfect Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#perfect-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak s$, every element is a sum of [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket). The [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) satisfies $\operatorname{ad}[x,y]=[\operatorname{ad}x,\operatorname{ad}y]$, and the [trace of a commutator](../linear-algebra.md#trace-of-a-commutator) is zero. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}x)=0\qquad(x\in\mathfrak s).
$$

Some $\operatorname{ad}x$ must have a nonzero [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue). Otherwise, over the [algebraically closed field](../algebra.md#algebraically-closed-field) $k$, every $\operatorname{ad}x$ would be a [nilpotent linear map](../linear-operator-theory.md#nilpotent-linear-map), and the [Engel theorem](../lie-algebra.md#engel-s-theorem) would imply that $\mathfrak s$ is a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#nilpotent-lie-algebra). This contradicts perfection: the [lower central series of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#lower-central-series-of-a-lie-algebra) $\gamma_1=\mathfrak s$, $\gamma_{r+1}=[\mathfrak s,\gamma_r]$ would equal $\mathfrak s\ne0$ at every step instead of terminating.

Choose such an $x$. Since $[x,x]=0$, zero is an [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $\operatorname{ad}x$. Its [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) is zero and its [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) is three, so the remaining [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $\lambda,-\lambda$ with $\lambda\ne0$. These three [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are distinct in [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero), hence $\operatorname{ad}x$ is [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix). Put $h=2x/\lambda$, and choose nonzero [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) $e,f$ with

$$
[h,e]=2e,\qquad[h,f]=-2f.
$$

The zero [eigenspace](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) is $kh$, so $h,e,f$ form a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). Applying the [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) gives

$$
[h,[e,f]]=[[h,e],f]+[e,[h,f]]=2[e,f]-2[e,f]=0.
$$

Thus $[e,f]=ch$ for some $c\in k$. If $c=0$, all [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) of [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector) lie in $\langle e,f\rangle$, contradicting $[\mathfrak s,\mathfrak s]=\mathfrak s$. Therefore $c\ne0$, and replacing $f$ by $f/c$ gives $[e,f]=h$.

We have constructed an [sl2 triple](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-triple) that is a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis). The [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) to the [sl2 Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-lie-algebra) sending

$$
e\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad h\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad f\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

preserves all [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) of [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector) and is bijective. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak s\cong\mathfrak{sl}_2(k).}
$$

This is the [classification of three-dimensional perfect Lie algebras](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#classification-of-three-dimensional-perfect-lie-algebras) without appealing to a classification of all [simple Lie algebras](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#simple-lie-algebra).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Take the [semidirect product of a Lie algebra and a module](../lie-algebra.md#semidirect-product-of-a-lie-algebra-and-a-module)

$$
L=\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\ltimes k^2,\qquad[(X,u),(Y,v)]=([X,Y],Xv-Yu),
$$

where $k^2$ is the [defining representation](../lie-algebra.md#defining-representation-of-a-matrix-lie-algebra) of the [sl2 Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-lie-algebra). To see directly that this is a [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md), realize $(X,u)$ as the block [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X&u\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. These [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) are closed under the [operator commutator](../vector-space.md#operator-commutator), whose [Jacobi identity](../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) holds in every [associative algebra](../associative-algebra.md).

Using the standard [sl2 triple](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-triple) $e,h,f$, its [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) show $[\mathfrak{sl}_2,\mathfrak{sl}_2]=\mathfrak{sl}_2$: indeed $h=[e,f]$, $e=[h,e]/2$, and $f=-[h,f]/2$. Also $h=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ is invertible on $k^2$. For any $u\in k^2$, put $v=h^{-1}u$; then

$$
[(h,0),(0,v)]=(0,u).
$$

It follows that the [derived algebra](../lie-algebra.md#derived-algebra) contains both $\mathfrak{sl}_2\oplus0$ and $0\oplus k^2$, so $[L,L]=L$: this is a [perfect Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#perfect-lie-algebra).

However, $0\oplus k^2$ is a nonzero [ideal of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#ideal-of-a-lie-algebra) with zero [Lie bracket](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket). It is therefore a [solvable Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#solvable-lie-algebra) and is contained in the [solvable radical](../lie-algebra.md#radical-of-a-lie-algebra) of $L$. A [semisimple Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md) has zero [solvable radical](../lie-algebra.md#radical-of-a-lie-algebra), so

$$
\boxed{[L,L]=L,\qquad0\ne k^2\subseteq\operatorname{rad}L,\qquad\dim L=5.}
$$

This illustrates the [perfect semidirect product criterion](../lie-algebra.md#perfect-semidirect-product-criterion): the [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) internal to the first summand generate it, while its action generates the second summand.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

**There is a sign error in the printed assertion under the standard [sl2 triple](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-triple) convention.** Work first with the operators exactly as printed. Since the $v_i$ form a [Hamel basis](../vector-space.md#basis), their prescribed images extend to [linear maps](../vector-space.md#linear-map) on $V$; each vector is a finite [linear combination](../vector-space.md#linear-combination) of [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector).

For $i\geq1$, the [operator commutators](../vector-space.md#operator-commutator) with $h$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
[h,e]v_i&=\bigl(-2(i-1)(i-1)+2i(i-1)\bigr)v_{i-1}=2ev_i,\\
[h,f]v_i&=\bigl(-2(i+1)^2+2i(i+1)\bigr)v_{i+1}=-2fv_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

At $i=0$, the first identity holds because both sides vanish; the second holds because $hf(v_0)=-2v_1$ and $fh(v_0)=0$. On the other hand, for $i\geq1$,

$$
[e,f]v_i=i(i+1)v_i-i(i-1)v_i=2iv_i=-hv_i.
$$

At $i=0$, both $ef(v_0)=e(v_1)=0$ and $fe(v_0)=0$, so this identity also holds. Thus the actual relations are

$$
\boxed{[h,e]=2e,\qquad[h,f]=-2f,\qquad[e,f]=-h.}
$$

In particular, $[e,f]v_1=2v_1\ne-2v_1=hv_1$ in [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero); the requested equality cannot hold as printed. Setting

$$
\boxed{E=-e,\qquad H=h,\qquad F=f.}
$$

corrects the sign and gives $[H,E]=2E$, $[H,F]=-2F$, $[E,F]=H$. These are the standard [sl2 triple](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-triple) relations, so the standard [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of the [sl2 Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-lie-algebra) acts on $V$ by $E,H,F$, giving a [Lie algebra representation](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-representation).

For completeness, these operators are [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence): applying $aE+bH+cF=0$ to $v_0$ gives $c=0$; applying it to $v_1$ then gives $b=0$; applying it to $v_2$ gives $a=0$. Hence the printed operators span a [Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#lie-subalgebra) isomorphic to the [sl2 Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#sl2-lie-algebra), even though their printed normalization fails. Replacing the coefficient $i-1$ in the printed definition of $e(v_i)$ by $1-i$ is one equivalent way to repair the statement.

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

Use the corrected [Lie algebra representation](../lie-algebra.md#lie-algebra-representation) $E=-e$, $H=h$, $F=f$ from part (i). A [vector subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) is invariant under $E,H,F$ exactly when it is invariant under the printed $e,h,f$, since changing the sign of a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) does not change its [invariant subspaces](../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace). Define

$$
W=\bigoplus_{i\geq1}kv_i.
$$

This is a nonzero proper [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule): $H$ preserves each $kv_i$, $F$ increases the index, $E(v_1)=0$, and $E(v_i)=-(i-1)v_{i-1}\in W$ for $i\geq2$.

We must rule out [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule) consisting only of mixtures of [weight vectors](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector). Let $U\ne0$ be any [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule), and choose $u=\sum_{j\in S}a_jv_j\in U$ with $S$ finite and every $a_j\ne0$. The [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $-2j$ of $H$ are distinct. For $i\in S$, the [Lagrange interpolation polynomial](../interpolation.md#lagrange-polynomial)

$$
p_i(t)=\prod_{\substack{j\in S\\j\ne i}}\frac{t+2j}{-2i+2j}
$$

satisfies $p_i(H)u=a_iv_i$. Since $U$ is invariant under $H$, it is invariant under every [polynomial](../polynomial.md) in $H$, so $v_i\in U$.

If $i=0$, repeated applications of $F$ give $F^mv_0=m!v_m$, and $U=V$. If $i\geq1$, repeated applications of $E$ give a nonzero multiple of $v_1$, because the successive coefficients $-(i-1),\ldots,-1$ are nonzero in [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). Repeated applications of $F$ to $v_1$ then give every $v_j$ with $j\geq1$, so $W\subseteq U$.

Finally, if such a $U$ contains any vector outside $W$, that vector has a nonzero coefficient of $v_0$; the same [polynomial interpolation](../interpolation.md#polynomial-interpolation) extracts $v_0$, forcing $U=V$. Hence the complete list of [submodules](../module-theory.md#submodule) is $0,W,V$. In particular, $W$ is a [simple module](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module). The [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $V/W$ has [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $\overline v_0$; $E\overline v_0=H\overline v_0=0$ and $F\overline v_0=\overline v_1=0$. It is the one-dimensional [trivial Lie algebra representation](../lie-algebra.md#trivial-lie-algebra-representation).

The [module extension](../module-theory.md#module-extension) does not split. If $V=W\oplus U$ for a [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) $U$, then $U$ would be a one-dimensional [trivial Lie algebra representation](../lie-algebra.md#trivial-lie-algebra-representation), since it maps isomorphically to $V/W$. But $\ker H=kv_0$ and $Fv_0=v_1\ne0$, so no such $U$ exists. Thus

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}W=\bigoplus_{i\geq1}kv_i,\qquad V/W\cong k_{\mathrm{triv}},\\\text{Submodules: }0,W,V;\qquad V\text{ is not semisimple}.\end{gathered}}
$$

Here [semisimple module](../module-theory.md#semisimple-module) means a [direct sum of modules](../module-theory.md#direct-sum-of-modules) that are [simple modules](../module-theory.md#irreducible-module), equivalently a module in which every [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule) has a complementary [submodule](../module-theory.md#submodule). This is the [nonsplit zero-weight Verma extension for sl2](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#nonsplit-zero-weight-verma-extension-for-sl2): rescaling to $w_i=i!v_i$ gives $Fw_i=w_{i+1}$ and $Ew_i=i(1-i)w_{i-1}$. Its [infinite-dimensional vector space](../vector-space.md#infinite-dimensional-vector-space) is outside the hypotheses of the [Weyl complete reducibility theorem](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-complete-reducibility-theorem).

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

The [normalizer of a Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#normalizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra) and the [centralizer of a Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#centralizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra) are

$$
N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a)=\{x:[x,\mathfrak a]\subseteq\mathfrak a\},\qquad C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a)=\{x:[x,\mathfrak a]=0\}.
$$

The second is always contained in the first. Conversely, let $x\in N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a)$ and $a\in\mathfrak a$. Then $[a,x]\in\mathfrak a$, and, because $\mathfrak a$ is an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra),

$$
(\operatorname{ad}a)^2x=[a,[a,x]]=0.
$$

By the definition of a [toral subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#toral-subalgebra), $\operatorname{ad}a$ is [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix). A [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix) [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $T$ satisfies $\ker T^2=\ker T$: in an [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis), both [kernels](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) consist of exactly the [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) whose [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) is zero. Thus $[a,x]=0$. This holds for every $a\in\mathfrak a$, so $x\in C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a)$ and

$$
\boxed{N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a)=C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak a).}
$$

This is the [normalizer-centralizer equality for a toral abelian subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#normalizer-centralizer-equality-for-a-toral-abelian-subalgebra); it does not require $\mathfrak g$ to be a [semisimple Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Use the general definition of a [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra): a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#nilpotent-lie-algebra) that is a [Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#lie-subalgebra) and equals its [normalizer of a Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#normalizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra). In a [semisimple Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md), the stated structural fact says every [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra) $\mathfrak h$ is a [toral subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#toral-subalgebra) and an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra). Part (i) therefore gives

$$
C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h.
$$

Conversely, suppose $\mathfrak h$ is a [toral subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#toral-subalgebra) and an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra) with $C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$. An [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra) is a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#nilpotent-lie-algebra), because its [lower central series of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#lower-central-series-of-a-lie-algebra) vanishes at the second term. Part (i) gives $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$. Thus $\mathfrak h$ satisfies the defining conditions of a [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra), proving

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak h\text{ is Cartan}\iff\begin{gathered}\mathfrak h\text{ is toral and abelian},\\C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h.\end{gathered}}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

We use the supplied [weight decomposition](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-decomposition) $V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Psi(\mathfrak a)}V_\lambda$. There is a small distinction to justify: the definition of a [toral subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#toral-subalgebra) uses the [Adjoint representation](../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra), while these [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space) use the [defining representation](../lie-algebra.md#defining-representation-of-a-matrix-lie-algebra). For the [symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-lie-algebra), [adjoint semisimplicity in the symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#adjoint-semisimplicity-in-the-symplectic-lie-algebra) implies that each $a\in\mathfrak a$ is [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix) on $V$ as well. Here is a direct verification.

Define the [symplectic adjoint](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-adjoint) $a^*$ by $\omega(av,w)=\omega(v,a^*w)$. The [symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-lie-algebra) condition is $a^*=-a$. Write the [Jordan–Chevalley decomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-chevalley-decomposition) in $\operatorname{End}(V)$ as $a=S+N$, with $S$ [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix), $N$ a [nilpotent linear map](../linear-operator-theory.md#nilpotent-linear-map), and $SN=NS$. Uniqueness of the [Jordan–Chevalley decomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-chevalley-decomposition), applied to $a^*=-a$, gives $S^*=-S$ and $N^*=-N$. Thus $S,N\in\mathfrak{sp}(V)$. On $\operatorname{End}(V)$, $\operatorname{ad}S$ is [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix) by using a [matrix unit](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) in an [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) of $S$, and $\operatorname{ad}N$ is a [nilpotent linear map](../linear-operator-theory.md#nilpotent-linear-map), since

$$
(\operatorname{ad}N)^m(T)=\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^j\binom mjN^{m-j}TN^j
$$

vanishes for large $m$. These operators commute and preserve $\mathfrak{sp}(V)$, so their restrictions are the [Jordan–Chevalley decomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-chevalley-decomposition) of $\operatorname{ad}a$. Because $\operatorname{ad}a$ is [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix), its nilpotent part is zero: $\operatorname{ad}N=0$ on $\mathfrak{sp}(V)$.

To conclude that $N=0$ without an assumption on the [center of a Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#center-of-a-lie-algebra), observe that each map $T_v(w)=\omega(v,w)v$ lies in $\mathfrak{sp}(V)$. If $v\ne0$, its image is $kv$, because $\omega$ is a [nondegenerate bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#nondegenerate-bilinear-form). Since $N$ commutes with $T_v$, it preserves $kv$. Hence $Nv\in kv$ for every $v$, forcing $N$ to be a scalar [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map): compare its action on two [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) vectors and their sum. A scalar [nilpotent linear map](../linear-operator-theory.md#nilpotent-linear-map) is zero. For $V=0$ the assertion is immediate. This proves the needed [diagonalizability](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix). Commuting [diagonalizable](../linear-operator-theory.md#diagonalizable-matrix) [linear maps](../vector-space.md#linear-map) admit [simultaneous diagonalization](../mathematics.md#simultaneous-diagonalization), giving the displayed [weight decomposition](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-decomposition).

Now let $v\in V_\lambda$ and $w\in V_\mu$. For every $a\in\mathfrak a$, preservation of the [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form) gives

$$
0=\omega(av,w)+\omega(v,aw)=(\lambda(a)+\mu(a))\omega(v,w).
$$

If $\lambda+\mu\ne0$ as a [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional), choose $a$ on which it is nonzero; then $\omega(v,w)=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\omega(V_\lambda,V_\mu)=0\quad\text{unless }\mu=-\lambda.}
$$

For $v\ne0$ in $V_\lambda$, the fact that $\omega$ is a [nondegenerate bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#nondegenerate-bilinear-form) supplies a $w\in V$ with $\omega(v,w)\ne0$. Decomposing $w$ into [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space), only its $V_{-\lambda}$ component can contribute. Thus $V_{-\lambda}\ne0$, and $\Psi(\mathfrak a)=-\Psi(\mathfrak a)$. The same argument on both sides shows that $\omega:V_\lambda\times V_{-\lambda}\to k$ is a [perfect pairing](../dual-space.md#perfect-pairing), so $\dim V_\lambda=\dim V_{-\lambda}$. On $V_0$, the restriction of $\omega$ is a [nondegenerate bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#nondegenerate-bilinear-form) that is an [alternating bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#alternating-bilinear-form), so $\dim V_0$ is even.

Choose one representative $\lambda_i$ from each pair of nonzero [weights of a representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation), for $1\leq i\leq r$. Here

$$
r=\frac{|\Psi(\mathfrak a)\setminus\{0\}|}{2}.
$$

The [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $\mathfrak a\to k^r$ sending $a$ to $(\lambda_1(a),\ldots,\lambda_r(a))$ is injective: an element in its [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) acts by zero on every nonzero [weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space), and also on $V_0$, so it is the zero element of $\mathfrak a\subseteq\operatorname{End}(V)$. Taking [dimensions](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) yields

$$
\boxed{\dim\mathfrak a\leq r=\frac{|\Psi(\mathfrak a)\setminus\{0\}|}{2}\leq n.}
$$

The final inequality follows because each pair contributes at least two to $\dim V=2n$. Equivalently, the nonzero [weights of a representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) span the [dual space](../dual-space.md) $\mathfrak a^*$; they need not be [linearly independent](../vector-space.md#linear-independence) at this stage.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Choose representatives $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ as in part (b). We use the [symplectic centralizer decomposition](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-centralizer-decomposition)

$$
\boxed{C_{\mathfrak{sp}(V)}(\mathfrak a)\cong\mathfrak{sp}(V_0)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathfrak{gl}(V_{\lambda_i}).}
$$

To prove it, a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) commuting with every $a\in\mathfrak a$ must preserve every [weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space): if $v\in V_\lambda$, then $a(Tv)=T(av)=\lambda(a)Tv$. Conversely, preserving the [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space) makes a [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) commute with the scalar action of every $a$ on each space.

On $V_0$, the [symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-lie-algebra) condition says exactly that the restriction belongs to $\mathfrak{sp}(V_0)$. On $V_{\lambda_i}\oplus V_{-\lambda_i}$, use the [perfect pairing](../dual-space.md#perfect-pairing) from part (b) to identify $V_{-\lambda_i}$ with the [dual space](../dual-space.md) $V_{\lambda_i}^*$. The action $A_i$ on $V_{\lambda_i}$ can be arbitrary, but its action on $V_{-\lambda_i}$ must be $-A_i^*$, where $A_i^*$ is the [dual map](../dual-space.md#transpose-of-a-linear-map). Indeed,

$$
\omega(A_iv,w)+\omega(v,B_iw)=0\quad\text{for all }v,w
$$

determines $B_i=-A_i^*$ uniquely. There are no additional conditions between different pairs because $\omega$ vanishes between different pairs. Every choice of these blocks therefore gives an element of the [centralizer of a Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#centralizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra). The block correspondence preserves [Lie brackets](../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket), proving the claimed [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras).

The [general linear Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#general-linear-lie-algebra) $\mathfrak{gl}_m(k)$ is an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra) exactly when $m\leq1$: for $m\geq2$, the [matrix units](../vector-space.md#matrix-unit) satisfy $[E_{11},E_{12}]=E_{12}\ne0$. A nonzero [symplectic vector space](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-vector-space) has a [symplectic basis](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-basis) and contains a two-dimensional [symplectic subspace](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-subspace). The [symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-lie-algebra) acting on that plane, extended by zero on its [symplectic orthogonal complement](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-orthogonal-complement), contains the [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and $E=E_{12}$ with $[H,E]=2E\ne0$. Thus $\mathfrak{sp}(V_0)$ is an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra) exactly when $V_0=0$.

Since different summands in a [direct sum of Lie algebras](../lie-algebra.md#direct-sum-of-lie-algebras) commute, we obtain the precise criterion

$$
\boxed{C_{\mathfrak{sp}(V)}(\mathfrak a)\text{ is abelian}\iff\begin{gathered}V_0=0,\\\dim V_\lambda=1\quad(0\ne\lambda\in\Psi(\mathfrak a)).\end{gathered}}
$$

Equivalently, there are $2n$ distinct nonzero [weights of a representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation), each with a one-dimensional [weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space). The condition concerns multiplicities in the [defining representation](../lie-algebra.md#defining-representation-of-a-matrix-lie-algebra); it does not assert that $\dim\mathfrak a=n$ for every such $\mathfrak a$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Use the standard structural fact that $\mathfrak{sp}(V)$ is a [semisimple Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md) over an [algebraically closed field](../algebra.md#algebraically-closed-field) of [characteristic zero](../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). If $\mathfrak h$ is a [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra), part (a)(ii) gives $C_{\mathfrak{sp}(V)}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$, and $\mathfrak h$ is an [abelian Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md#abelian-lie-algebra). Part (c) therefore shows that its [weight spaces](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space) occur as $n$ pairs of one-dimensional spaces, with no zero [weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space).

Choose representatives $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$ of its nonzero [weights of a representation](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation). Pick $p_i\ne0$ in $V_{\lambda_i}$ and $q_i\ne0$ in $V_{-\lambda_i}$, scaling $q_i$ so that $\omega(p_i,q_i)=1$. Part (b) shows that all other pairings among different pairs are zero. Each individual [weight space](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-space) is one-dimensional and the [bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md) is alternating, so the ordered [basis](../vector-space.md#basis)

$$
\mathcal B=(p_1,\ldots,p_n,q_1,\ldots,q_n)
$$

is a [symplectic basis](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-basis). Every $h\in\mathfrak h$ is a [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix) in this [basis](../vector-space.md#basis), with entries $\lambda_1(h),\ldots,\lambda_n(h),-\lambda_1(h),\ldots,-\lambda_n(h)$.

Let $D_{\mathcal B}$ be the [Lie subalgebra](../lie-algebra.md#lie-subalgebra) consisting of all [diagonal matrices](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix)

$$
D_{\mathcal B}=\{\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_n,-t_1,\ldots,-t_n):t_i\in k\}.
$$

Each such [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) lies in the [symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#symplectic-lie-algebra) and commutes with $\mathfrak h$. Hence

$$
\mathfrak h\subseteq D_{\mathcal B}\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{sp}(V)}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h,
$$

and $\mathfrak h=D_{\mathcal B}$. In particular, every [Cartan subalgebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-subalgebra) has [dimension](../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) $n$; the $\lambda_i$ are a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $\mathfrak h^*$.

Carry out the same construction for $\mathfrak h'$, obtaining a [symplectic basis](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-basis) $\mathcal B'=(p'_1,\ldots,p'_n,q'_1,\ldots,q'_n)$ with $\mathfrak h'=D_{\mathcal B'}$. The unique [linear map](../vector-space.md#linear-map) $g$ sending $p_i$ to $p'_i$ and $q_i$ to $q'_i$ is invertible and preserves all pairings of [basis vectors](../vector-space.md#basis-vector). By [bilinearity](../multilinear-algebra.md#bilinearity), it preserves $\omega$ on all of $V$, so $g$ belongs to the [symplectic group](../symplectic-geometry.md#symplectic-group) $\operatorname{Sp}(V)$. The [change of basis](../linear-algebra.md#change-of-basis) gives

$$
\boxed{g\mathfrak h g^{-1}=gD_{\mathcal B}g^{-1}=D_{\mathcal B'}=\mathfrak h',\qquad g\in\operatorname{Sp}(V).}
$$

Thus [conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras of the symplectic Lie algebra](../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#conjugacy-of-cartan-subalgebras-of-the-symplectic-lie-algebra) follows by explicitly matching their [symplectic bases](../bilinear-form.md#symplectic-basis). If $n=0$, the [Lie algebra](../lie-algebra.md) is zero and the same conclusion holds with $g$ the identity on the zero [vector space](../vector-space.md).
