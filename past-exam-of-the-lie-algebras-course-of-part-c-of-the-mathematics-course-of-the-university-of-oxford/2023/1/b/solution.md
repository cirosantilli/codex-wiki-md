<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose a [basis](../../../../basis.md) $x,y$ of the two-dimensional [abelian Lie algebra](../../../../abelian-lie-algebra.md) $\mathfrak a$, and lifts with the same names. Every [alternating bilinear form](../../../../alternating-bilinear-form.md) is determined by $c=\alpha(x,y)$, and every such form is a [Lie algebra two-cocycle](../../../../lie-algebra-two-cocycle.md) because every [Lie bracket](../../../../lie-bracket.md) in $\mathfrak a$ is zero. Thus every [central extension of a Lie algebra](../../../../central-extension-of-a-lie-algebra.md) has a [basis](../../../../basis.md) $x,y,z$ with

$$
[x,y]=cz,\qquad [x,z]=[y,z]=0.
$$

If $c=0$, all [Lie brackets](../../../../lie-bracket.md) vanish, so this is the [direct sum of Lie algebras](../../../../direct-sum-of-lie-algebras.md) $\mathfrak a\oplus kz\cong k^3$. If $c\ne0$, replace the central [basis vector](../../../../basis-vector.md) by $z'=cz$. Then $[x,y]=z'$, which is the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../../../../heisenberg-lie-algebra.md) $\mathfrak h_3(k)$. This change of [basis](../../../../basis.md) shows that every nonzero $c$ gives the same [Lie algebra isomorphism](../../../../lie-algebra-isomorphism.md) class.

These two [Lie algebras](../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) cannot be isomorphic: their [derived algebras](../../../../derived-algebra.md) have [dimensions](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) zero and one, respectively, and a [Lie algebra isomorphism](../../../../lie-algebra-isomorphism.md) preserves the [derived algebra](../../../../derived-algebra.md). Moreover, the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../../../../heisenberg-lie-algebra.md) cannot be a [direct sum of Lie algebras](../../../../direct-sum-of-lie-algebras.md) $kz\oplus\mathfrak a$, since that [direct sum of Lie algebras](../../../../direct-sum-of-lie-algebras.md) would have zero [Lie bracket](../../../../lie-bracket.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\mathfrak a}\cong k^3\quad\text{or}\quad\mathfrak h_3(k).}
$$

Here “up to isomorphism” means the [Lie algebra isomorphism](../../../../lie-algebra-isomorphism.md) class of the middle [Lie algebra](../../../../lie-algebra-split.md), allowing a change of the identifications at the ends of the [short exact sequence](../../../../short-exact-sequence.md). If instead [equivalence of central extensions of a Lie algebra](../../../../equivalence-of-central-extensions-of-a-lie-algebra.md) must induce the identity on both $kz$ and $\mathfrak a$, the parameter $c$ cannot change: the formula in part (a) has $t([x,y])=0$. That stricter notion has one class for each $c\in k$, rather than two. This distinction explains the convention required by the question.
