# Light-crossing size bound for a variable source

↑ **Parent:** [Pulsar](pulsar.md)

If a single approximately stationary emitting region changes coherently through a causal disturbance, a disturbance travelling no faster than the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) takes at least $L/c$ to coordinate a scale $L$. Light-travel differences also smear simultaneous emission from different depths. Under those assumptions, observed intrinsic variability on a time scale $\Delta t$ suggests $L\lesssim c\Delta t$.

For a chosen example $\Delta t=0.010\,\mathrm{s}$, multiplication by $c=299{,}792.458\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ gives $L\lesssim2998\,\mathrm{km}$. This is an order-of-magnitude [causality](causality.md) bound on a coordinated emitting scale. It is not a measured stellar radius, and even the relation between $L$ and a diameter depends on geometry. A [pulsar](pulsar.md) beam crossing is a geometric clock, and [pulsar dispersion delay](pulsar-dispersion-delay.md), scattering and instrumental response can broaden its observed pulse. The recurrence [period](period-of-a-function.md), observed pulse width and intrinsic variability time must therefore be distinguished before applying the bound. Relativistically moving emitters require further arrival-time corrections.
