# Order of a direct-product element

↑ **Parent:** [Direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md)

If the [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md) is $m$ for $g$ and $n$ for $h$, with both finite, then

$$
(g,h)^k=(g^k,h^k)=(e_G,e_H)
$$

exactly when both $m$ and $n$ divide $k$. The smallest positive such $k$ is their [least common multiple](least-common-multiple.md), giving

$$
\operatorname{ord}(g,h)=\operatorname{lcm}(m,n).
$$

This concerns the orders of the selected elements, which need not equal the orders of their entire [groups](group-split.md). If either component has infinite [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md), no positive power can return the pair to the [identity element](identity-element.md), so the pair also has infinite order.

For example, in $C_4\times C_6$, the additive element $(2,3)$ has component orders $2,2$, hence pair order $2$. The element $(1,1)$ instead has order $\operatorname{lcm}(4,6)=12$. The product has $24$ elements, so even this latter element does not generate it. This distinguishes [group order](order-of-a-finite-group.md) from [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md) while exposing the simultaneous-return interpretation.
