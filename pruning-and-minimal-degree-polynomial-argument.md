# Pruning and minimal-degree polynomial argument

↑ **Parent:** [Joints theorem](joints-theorem.md)

Let a line collection have $M$ [joints of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md) and put $D=\lceil nM^{1/n}\rceil$. If $M>LD$, deleting each line with at most $D$ current [joints of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md), together with those [joints of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md), leaves a nonempty configuration in which every line has more than $D$ [joints of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md). A nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $D$ vanishes on the retained [joints of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md) by [rank-nullity theorem](rank-nullity-theorem.md). Choose one of minimum degree. Its restriction vanishes identically on every retained line, so its [gradient](gradient.md) is orthogonal to a spanning set of directions at every [joint of a line collection](joint-of-a-line-collection.md) and hence vanishes there. Each nonzero [partial derivative](partial-derivative.md) would be a lower-degree [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) with the same zeros, contradicting minimality. In characteristic zero, all derivatives vanishing forces a constant [polynomial](polynomial-split.md), another contradiction. Therefore $M\le LD$, giving the [joints theorem](joints-theorem.md). The argument's characteristic-zero assumption is essential to its derivative step.
