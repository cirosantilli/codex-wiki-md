# Apparent regular singular point

↑ **Parent:** [Regular singular point](regular-singular-point.md)

A [regular singular point](regular-singular-point.md) with a full set of analytic local solutions is an apparent singularity of the differential equation. Singular normalized coefficients do not by themselves force singular solutions. For example, $xy''-y'=0$ has normalized coefficient $-1/x$, but its fundamental solutions $1,x^2$ are both analytic through zero. Their [Wronskian](wronskian.md) is $2x$, which vanishes at the singular point while being nonzero on each adjacent ordinary interval. The nonvanishing-Wronskian criterion for a fundamental set applies on intervals where the normalized equation has regular coefficients.
