# Electrical impedance

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_impedance)

Electrical impedance is the ratio of voltage and current [complex amplitudes](complex-amplitude.md) for sinusoidal steady response at a specified [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md). With time dependence $e^{\mathrm i\omega t}$, an ideal [resistor](resistor.md), [inductor](inductor.md) and [capacitor](capacitor.md) have $Z_R=R$, $Z_L=\mathrm i\omega L$ and $Z_C=1/(\mathrm i\omega C)$. Their [electrical impedances](electrical-impedance.md) add in a [series RLC circuit](series-rlc-circuit.md), giving $Z=R+\mathrm i(\omega L-1/(\omega C))$. The current [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) is $V_0/|Z|$, so cancellation of the imaginary terms explains the [resonant series RLC response](resonant-series-rlc-response.md). [OpenStax University Physics 2, §15.3: impedance and series resonance](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/15-3-rlc-series-circuits-with-ac).
