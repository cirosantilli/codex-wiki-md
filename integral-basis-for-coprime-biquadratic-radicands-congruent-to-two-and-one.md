# Integral basis for coprime biquadratic radicands congruent to two and one

↑ **Parent:** [Integral basis](integral-basis.md)

Let $m,n$ be coprime [square-free integers](square-free-integer.md), $m\equiv2\pmod4$ and $n\equiv1\pmod4$, with $n\ne1$. Put $\alpha^2=m$, $\beta^2=n$, and $\omega=(1+\beta)/2$. Then $1,\alpha,\omega,\alpha\omega$ is an [integral basis](integral-basis.md) of $\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta)$. All four entries are [algebraic integers](algebraic-integer.md). For spanning, [field traces](field-trace.md) to the three quadratic subfields show that an arbitrary [algebraic integer](algebraic-integer.md) has form $(a+b\alpha+c\beta+d\alpha\beta)/2$ with $a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z$. Its [field norm](field-norm.md) to $\mathbb Q(\beta)$ is $A/4+(ac-mbd)\beta/2$, where $A=a^2+nc^2-mb^2-mnd^2$. The [ring of integers of a quadratic field](ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field.md) formula forces $A$ even and $A\equiv2ac-2mbd\pmod4$. These give $a\equiv c\pmod2$ and then $b\equiv d\pmod2$. The element is consequently $(a-c)/2+((b-d)/2)\alpha+c\omega+d\alpha\omega$. The coefficient [determinant](determinant.md) $1/4$ proves rational [linear independence](linear-independence.md).
