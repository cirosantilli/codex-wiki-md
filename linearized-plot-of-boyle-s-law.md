<h1 id="linearized-plot-of-boyle-s-law">Linearized plot of Boyle's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Boyle's law](boyle-s-law.md)

A [linearized plot of Boyle's law](linearized-plot-of-boyle-s-law.md) uses $x=1/V$ as its horizontal coordinate. Then [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md) satisfies $p=Cx$: the [slope](slope.md) is $C=nRT$, and the ideal intercept is zero. In the figure, $(V,p)=(100,100)$ and $(50,200)$ in $\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $\mathrm{kPa}$ become $(x,p)=(0.010,100)$ and $(0.020,200)$ in $\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ and $\mathrm{kPa}$. Both give [slope](slope.md) $10\,000\,\mathrm{kPa\,cm^3}$. The Vernier experiment includes this reciprocal-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) graph as its extension.[Experiment 6, printed p. 6–4, Extension steps 1–2: pressure versus reciprocal total volume and linear fitting](chemistry-with-vernier/6-boyle-s-law-pressure-volume-relationship-in-gases.md)

At constant [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md), [gauge pressure](gauge-pressure.md) instead obeys $p_g=Cx-p_{\rm atm}$, with the same [slope](slope.md) and a negative intercept. A nonzero fitted intercept in an intended [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md) plot can indicate [sensor bias](sensor-bias.md) or an incorrect [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) correction, but is not by itself a unique diagnosis. The origin is a mathematical extrapolation to infinite [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) and zero [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md), rather than an additional measured state.
