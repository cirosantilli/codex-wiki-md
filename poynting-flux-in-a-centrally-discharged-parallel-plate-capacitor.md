# Poynting flux in a centrally discharged parallel-plate capacitor

↑ **Parent:** [Poynting theorem](poynting-theorem.md)

For circular plates of radius $a$, separation $d\ll a$, and a thin axial resistor $R$, the quasistatic [capacitance](capacitance.md) is $C=\varepsilon_0\pi a^2/d$. During discharge, $I=V/R=-C\dot V$. A circular [Ampere-Maxwell law](ampere-s-circuital-law.md) contour between the plates encloses wire current $I$ and opposing [displacement current](displacement-current.md) $-Ir^2/a^2$, giving $H=I(1-r^2/a^2)/(2\pi r)$. The [electric field](electric-field-split.md) has magnitude $V/d$, so the [Poynting vector](poynting-vector.md) points radially inward with magnitude $V^2(1-r^2/a^2)/(2\pi rdR)$. Its flux through the cylindrical side is the displayed inward power. The field inside that cylinder releases $V^2r^2/(Ra^2)$ per unit time; adding the incoming flux gives the resistor's full [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) rate $V^2/R$. End fringing, wire radius and magnetic-energy corrections are neglected.
