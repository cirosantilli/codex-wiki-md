# Magnitude difference

↑ **Parent:** [Astronomical magnitude](astronomical-magnitude.md)

For two positive measurements $F_1,F_2$ in the same [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md), using the same [magnitude zero point](magnitude-zero-point.md), units, and weighting convention, their [apparent magnitudes](apparent-magnitude.md) obey

$$
\Delta m=m_2-m_1=-2.5\log_{10}\left(\frac{F_2}{F_1}\right),\qquad \frac{F_2}{F_1}=10^{-0.4\Delta m}.
$$

The [logarithm](logarithm.md) takes a dimensionless ratio. An [energy](energy.md)-weighted measurement must be compared with another [energy](energy.md)-weighted measurement, and a [photon](photon.md)-counting measurement with another consistently calibrated [photon](photon.md)-counting measurement. Changing [photometric passband](photometric-passband.md) or [magnitude zero point](magnitude-zero-point.md) requires the corresponding calibration, as in a [colour index](color-index.md). [IAU 2015 Resolution B2: Energy-flux magnitude convention](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06262). [ESA XMM-Newton: Filter-specific count-rate calibration](https://xmm-tools.cosmos.esa.int/external/xmm_calibration/calib/documentation/CALHB/node597.html).

For example, $F_2=100F_1$ gives $\Delta m=-5$: source 2 is brighter. Conversely, $\Delta m=+5$ gives $F_2/F_1=0.01$, and $\Delta m=+1$ gives $F_2/F_1\simeq0.398107$. Even fractional steps work: $\Delta m=+0.1$ gives $F_2/F_1\simeq0.912011$, an approximately $8.80\%$ decrease. These are illustrative ratios, independent of any particular [star](star.md) catalogue.
