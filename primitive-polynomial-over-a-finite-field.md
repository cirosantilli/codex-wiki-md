# Primitive polynomial over a finite field

↑ **Parent:** [Primitive element of a finite field](primitive-element-of-a-finite-field.md)

A [monic polynomial](monic-polynomial.md) of degree $m$ over $\mathbb F_q$ that is an [irreducible polynomial](irreducible-polynomial.md) is primitive in the finite-field sense when one of its roots generates the [multiplicative group of a finite field](multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field.md) $\mathbb F_{q^m}^{\times}$. All its roots are Frobenius conjugates and then have the same order $q^m-1$. This notion differs from a [primitive polynomial](primitive-polynomial.md) over an integral domain, which concerns the common divisor of its coefficients. Primitive finite-field [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) provide explicit [field](field.md) representations and [primitive cyclic Reed-Solomon codes](primitive-cyclic-reed-solomon-code.md) or [BCH codes](bch-code.md).
