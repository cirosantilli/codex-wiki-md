# Neutron activation

↑ **Parent:** [Neutron](neutron-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_activation)

[Neutron activation](neutron-activation.md) is the production of [radioactivity](radioactive-decay.md) by irradiating a material with [neutrons](neutron-split.md). One route is [neutron capture](neutron-capture.md) producing an unstable [isotope](isotope.md); other [nuclear reactions](nuclear-reaction.md) can also produce unstable [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md). A specimen's later [radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md) provides a way to detect that a transformation occurred during irradiation. It does not by itself identify the product or reconstruct the incoming [neutron](neutron-split.md) [energy](energy-split.md) distribution.

This makes [neutron activation](neutron-activation.md) a useful bridge between an invisible radiation field and a delayed measurement. The [neutron flux](neutron-flux.md) and [collision cross-section](collision-cross-section.md) determine production; [radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md) and [detection efficiency](detection-efficiency.md) determine what remains observable later. [Neutron activation buildup and decay](neutron-activation-buildup-and-decay.md) keeps these stages separate.
