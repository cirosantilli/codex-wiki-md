# Harris-Kesten theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Percolation critical probability](percolation-critical-probability.md)

The [percolation critical probability](percolation-critical-probability.md) of independent [bond percolation](bond-percolation-split.md) on the infinite [square lattice](square-lattice.md) is exactly

$$
\boxed{p_c(\mathbb Z^2)=\frac12.}
$$

In this model, $\theta(p)=0$ for $p\leq1/2$ and $\theta(p)>0$ for $p>1/2$. The particular [graph](graph-split.md), independent [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) decisions, and distinction between [bond percolation](bond-percolation-split.md) and [site percolation](site-percolation-split.md) matter: this value is not a universal threshold for arbitrary random networks. The equality of open and closed [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) [probabilities](probability.md) at $1/2$ suggests a relation to [dual bond percolation](dual-bond-percolation.md), but that symmetry alone is not a proof of long-distance connectivity.

Harris established the lower bound, including absence of an [infinite percolation cluster](infinite-percolation-cluster.md) at $p=1/2$, in 1960. Kesten completed the equality in 1980. His original paper states the threshold result as Theorem 1 and the positive/zero [percolation probability](percolation-probability.md) alternatives as Theorem 2: [Harry Kesten, The critical probability of bond percolation on the square lattice equals 1/2](https://math.bme.hu/~balint/oktatas/perkolacio/percolation_papers/kesten_1.pdf). These are claims about the infinite [square lattice](square-lattice.md), rather than deductions from a simulated finite sample.
