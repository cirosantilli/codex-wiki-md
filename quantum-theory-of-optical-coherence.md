# Quantum theory of optical coherence

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum optics](quantum-optics-split.md)

Fix a detected position and [polarization of waves](polarization-waves.md). Write the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) operator as $\widehat E(t)=\widehat E^{(+)}(t)+\widehat E^{(-)}(t)$, where the positive-frequency part contains [annihilation operators](annihilation-operator.md) and $\widehat E^{(-)}=(\widehat E^{(+)})^\dagger$. An ideal absorbing [photodetector](photodetector.md) responds to

$$
G^{(1)}(t,t')=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho\widehat E^{(-)}(t)\widehat E^{(+)}(t')\right],
$$

with [density matrix](density-matrix-split.md) $\rho$. The diagonal gives the single-count rate up to the detector coupling. Multiple absorptions require higher, [normal-ordered](normal-ordering.md) field products. [Roy J. Glauber](roy-jay-glauber.md) used these correlations to develop the quantum account of [optical coherence](optical-coherence.md).[1963, §II, equations (2.1)–(2.4), pp. 2766–2767; Universidade Estadual de Campinas mirror](coherent-and-incoherent-states-of-the-radiation-field-by-roy-jay-glauber.md)

The ordering follows from a detection amplitude. If absorption takes an initial [pure state](pure-state.md) $|i\rangle$ to an unobserved final state $|f\rangle$, its field factor is $\langle f|\widehat E^{(+)}|i\rangle$. Squaring its [complex modulus](complex-modulus.md) and summing over a complete [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md) of final states gives $\langle i|\widehat E^{(-)}\widehat E^{(+)}|i\rangle$. Averaging over initial preparations replaces this expression by a [trace](matrix-trace.md). Thus a detector that absorbs cannot count the [Fock vacuum](fock-vacuum.md), although the vacuum has [quantum fluctuations](quantum-fluctuation.md).

For one selected [normal mode](normal-mode.md), $\widehat E^{(+)}(t)=\mathcal E u(t)a$. All [Fock states](fock-state.md) with nonzero occupation, [coherent states](coherent-state.md), and [thermal light](thermal-light.md) in this same mode have

$$
\frac{G^{(1)}(t,t')}{\sqrt{G^{(1)}(t,t)G^{(1)}(t',t')}}
=\frac{u^*(t)u(t')}{|u(t)||u(t')|}
$$

where both denominators are nonzero. Its magnitude is one even when the [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of the field vanishes. Perfect [first-order optical coherence](first-order-optical-coherence.md) therefore does not identify a [coherent state](coherent-state.md). Pair detection supplies additional information.
