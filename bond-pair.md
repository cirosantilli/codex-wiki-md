# Bond pair

↑ **Parent:** [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md)

A [bond pair](bond-pair.md) is an [electron pair](electron-pair.md) allocated to a connection between two [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) in a [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md). A single line stands for one [bond pair](bond-pair.md), or two [electrons](electron.md). A double connection contains two [bond pairs](bond-pair.md), or four [electrons](electron.md); a triple connection contains three [bond pairs](bond-pair.md), or six [electrons](electron.md). A [bond pair](bond-pair.md) is shared bookkeeping for the combined arrangement, rather than two distinguishable [electrons](electron.md) each permanently owned by one endpoint.

In [water](water.md), two [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) use $2\times2=4$ of the eight [valence electrons](valence-electron.md). In [ammonia](ammonia.md), three [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) use $3\times2=6$ of the eight [valence electrons](valence-electron.md). In $\mathrm{O=C=O}$, each C=O connection contains two [bond pairs](bond-pair.md), so there are four [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) in the entire [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md). Counting connections and counting [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) therefore give different answers for multiple connections.

The same [bond pair](bond-pair.md) is treated differently for different questions: both endpoints count its two [electrons](electron.md) for the [octet rule](octet-rule.md), whereas each endpoint receives just one [electron](electron.md) for [formal charge](formal-charge.md). Neither convention asserts equal physical [electron density](electron-density.md) at the endpoints. For electronic shapes, occupancy, and [energy](energy-split.md), see [molecular orbital theory](molecular-orbital-theory.md) rather than treating a [bond pair](bond-pair.md) line as an entire [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md).
