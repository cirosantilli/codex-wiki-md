# Degeneracy of principal moments of inertia

↑ **Parent:** [Principal moment of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md)

If two [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) coincide, for example $I_1=I_2\ne I_3$, every direction in their two-dimensional [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) is a [principal axis](principal-axis.md). The perpendicular axis is determined, but there is no privileged orthogonal pair within that plane. Rotating the pair in the plane leaves the diagonal [inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md) unchanged. If all three [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) coincide, $\mathbf I_O=I_0\mathbf1$ and every axis through $O$ is principal. This follows from the [eigenvalue multiplicity](eigenvalue-multiplicity.md) and [eigenspace](eigenspace.md), rather than from a failure of [diagonalization](diagonalization-of-a-matrix.md). [Matrix Eigenvalue Theory](newtonian-dynamics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/matrix-eigenvalue-theory.md)

Equal [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) do not prove that a body's outline has continuous rotational symmetry. For example, six equal [point masses](point-mass.md) at $(\pm a,0,0)$, $(0,\pm a,0)$ and $(0,0,\pm a)$ have $\mathbf I_O=4ma^2\mathbf1$. This discrete layout has the same axial [moment of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) in every direction, although its actual [mass](mass.md) distribution changes under most rotations. Conversely, even when all three [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md) are distinct, [principal axes](principal-axis.md) are unoriented lines: reversing an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md)'s sign gives the same axis. Near repeated [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md), small measurement errors can rotate fitted [principal axes](principal-axis.md) substantially within the nearly degenerate [eigenspace](eigenspace.md), so quoted directions need an uncertainty assessment as well as quoted [principal moments of inertia](principal-moment-of-inertia.md).
