# Power

↑ **Parent:** [Work](work.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_(physics))

[Power](power.md) describes how quickly [energy](energy.md) is transferred. For mechanical [work](work.md), the instantaneous [power](power.md) supplied by a [force](force.md) is

$$
P(t)=\frac{dW}{dt}=\mathbf F(t)\cdot\mathbf v(t),
\qquad W=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}P(t)\,dt.
$$

The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is $\mathrm W=\mathrm{J/s}=\mathrm{N\,m/s}$, distinct from the [velocity](velocity.md) unit $\mathrm{m/s}$. The italic symbol $W$ for [work](work.md) and the upright unit $\mathrm W$ for [power](power.md) mean different things. Two processes can transfer the same [energy](energy.md) while requiring different [powers](power.md): doing $12\,\mathrm J$ of [work](work.md) in $2\,\mathrm s$ requires an average $6\,\mathrm W$, whereas doing it in $4\,\mathrm s$ requires an average $3\,\mathrm W$. [OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.4: Power](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-4-power)
