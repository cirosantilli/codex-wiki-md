# Vertical mass coordinate of a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Vertical structure of an astrophysical disk](vertical-structure-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md)

In a reflection-symmetric [astrophysical disk](astrophysical-disk.md), the vertical mass coordinate measures [mass](mass.md) per unit area above the midplane. It satisfies $dm=\rho\,dz$, so $\partial_m=\rho^{-1}\partial_z$ inside the material. If the total [surface density](surface-density-of-a-disk.md) is $\Sigma$, the upper face is $m=\Sigma/2$; the normalized coordinate $\zeta=2m/\Sigma$ ranges from zero to one. Integrals weighted by [mass density](density.md) become ordinary integrals in $m$, which is especially useful for [radiative diffusion](radiative-diffusion.md) and the [density-weighted mean kinematic viscosity](density-weighted-mean-kinematic-viscosity.md).
