# Rooted tree

↑ **Parent:** [Tree (graph theory)](tree-graph-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooted_tree)

A [rooted tree](rooted-tree.md) is a [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md) with a distinguished [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md), its [root of a tree](root-of-a-tree.md) $r$. Choosing $r$ turns connections into a hierarchy: every other [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) has a unique [parent in a rooted tree](parent-in-a-rooted-tree.md), the preceding [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) on its [graph path](path-in-a-graph.md) from $r$. Conversely, the [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) whose [parent in a rooted tree](parent-in-a-rooted-tree.md) is $v$ are the [children in a rooted tree](child-in-a-rooted-tree.md) of $v$. Choosing the [root of a tree](root-of-a-tree.md) changes this description, while the underlying [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) remain the same. Arrows towards the [root of a tree](root-of-a-tree.md) can encode the relationship, as in MIT's counting notes; the undirected [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md) itself needs no arrows. [MIT, Peter Shor's counting notes, pp. 3–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-200-principles-of-discrete-applied-mathematics-spring-2024/mit18_200_s24_lec05-06.pdf)

In the seven-[vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) [tree](tree-graph-theory-split.md) above, choose $r=1$. The levels, grouped by [graph distance](distance-graph-theory.md) from the [root of a tree](root-of-a-tree.md), are $\{1\}$, $\{2,3\}$, $\{4,5,6\}$, $\{7\}$. The [children in a rooted tree](child-in-a-rooted-tree.md) of $2$ are $4,5$, and the [parent in a rooted tree](parent-in-a-rooted-tree.md) of $7$ is $6$. Choosing $r=7$ instead makes the levels $\{7\}$, $\{6\}$, $\{3\}$, $\{1\}$, $\{2\}$, $\{4,5\}$: now $6$ has [parent in a rooted tree](parent-in-a-rooted-tree.md) $7$, reversing their relationship. All six [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) survive.

This hierarchy supports [structural recursion](structural-recursion.md): process the smaller [rooted trees](rooted-tree.md) below each [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md), then combine their results. A [depth-first traversal of a tree](depth-first-traversal-of-a-tree.md) explores one branch before returning; [breadth-first search](breadth-first-search.md) processes successive levels. The [lowest common ancestor](lowest-common-ancestor.md) identifies where two routes from the [root of a tree](root-of-a-tree.md) last coincide. For $4,5$ it is $2$ when $r=1$. A [binary tree](binary-tree.md) imposes an additional restriction on [children in a rooted tree](child-in-a-rooted-tree.md); a general [rooted tree](rooted-tree.md) has no such bound.
