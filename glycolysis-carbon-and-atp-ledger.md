# Glycolysis carbon and ATP ledger

↑ **Parent:** [Glycolysis](glycolysis.md)

Follow one [glucose](glucose.md) through canonical [glycolysis](glycolysis.md), with no diversion of intermediates into other [metabolic pathways](metabolic-pathway.md). The investment stage uses two [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) to prepare the six-[carbon](carbon.md) [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md). Cleavage and rearrangement provide two [glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate](glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md), each containing three [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). Each then yields one three-[carbon](carbon.md) [pyruvate](pyruvate.md), one [NADH](nadh.md), and two [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) in the payoff stage.[§7.2, steps 3–7 and 10; Figures 7.8–7.9 captions: investment, two glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate molecules and duplicated payoff](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)

Glycolysis preserves six carbon chemical atoms while four gross ATP repay a two-ATP investment**. Original aggregate-stage schematic per [glucose](glucose.md), not a complete [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) map or measured data. Dots count [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), without assigning their positions in the [molecules](molecule-split.md). Both three-[carbon](carbon.md) routes perform the same payoff sequence. The running [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) ledger starts at zero relative to the initial inventory; negative two means expenditure from an existing pool.

The material check is $6=2\times3$: all six [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) remain in the two [pyruvates](pyruvate.md). No [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) has been released within this boundary. In the [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) ledger, each three-[carbon](carbon.md) route contains two [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md)-forming transfers, so

$$
\text{gross ATP}=2\times2=4,\qquad
\text{net ATP}=4-2=2.
$$

The first pair of payoff transfers only restores the initial [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) inventory; the second pair creates the net gain. Four newly formed [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) is therefore compatible with only two additional [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) remaining.

For an original scaled example, stipulate complete conversion of $5\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [glucose](glucose.md). The initial $30\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) becomes $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [pyruvate](pyruvate.md), still containing $30\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). The pathway spends $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md), forms $20\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md), and adds $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ to the [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) inventory before any other [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) use. It also reduces $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol}$ of [NAD+](nad-plus.md). These are calculated ideal amounts, not observed yields; side routes or incomplete conversion change the outcome.

A common biochemical net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{glucose}+2\,\mathrm{ADP}+2\,\mathrm P_i+2\,\mathrm{NAD}^{+}
\longrightarrow {}&2\,\mathrm{pyruvate}+2\,\mathrm{ATP}\\
&+2\,\mathrm{NADH}+2\,\mathrm H^{+}+2\,\mathrm{H_2O}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\mathrm P_i$ is [inorganic phosphate](inorganic-phosphate.md). The names group biochemical [protonation](protonation.md) and metal-binding states; the displayed [proton](proton.md) and [water](water.md) terms use a common shorthand rather than a unique specification of every [chemical species](chemical-species.md). The robust ledger records two net [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md)-to-[ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) conversions and two [NAD+](nad-plus.md)-to-[NADH](nadh.md) conversions. Summing the individual [chemical equations](chemical-equation.md) cancels two invested [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) against four formed [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) and cancels the internal [substrates](substrate-biochemistry.md), giving those coefficients.
