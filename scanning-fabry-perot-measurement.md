<h1 id="scanning-fabry-perot-measurement">Scanning Fabry–Pérot measurement</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Fabry–Pérot interferometer](fabry-perot-interferometer.md)

An actual teaching experiment in LM-0300 Fabry Perot Spectrum Analyser by [Walter Luhs](walter-luhs.md), §§3.1.1–3.1.2, prepares a green [laser](laser-split.md) beam by aligning it near and far along a rail, then centring back-reflected [light](light.md) from adjustable curved mirrors. An actuator using [piezoelectricity](piezoelectricity.md) moves one mirror. A screen first displays [interference fringes](interference-fringe.md); a focusing [optical lens](lens-optics.md) and [photodiode](photodiode.md) then replace the viewing arrangement. The [oscilloscope](oscilloscope.md) records the photodiode signal and the piezo drive reference on separate channels, using the latter as its trigger. Alignment and mirror separation are adjusted to suppress extra transverse [normal modes](normal-mode.md); source [temperature](temperature-split.md) and drive [electric current](electric-current.md) are controlled. Repeated peaks calibrate the scan using the appropriate [free spectral range](free-spectral-range.md), and their spacing divided by their [full width at half maximum](full-width-at-half-maximum.md) estimates [finesse](finesse.md).[LM-0300 Fabry Perot Spectrum Analyser, §1.0 pp. 3–4; §§3.1.1–3.1.2 pp. 20–21, figures 19–23](lm-0300-fabry-perot-spectrum-analyser-by-walter-luhs.md)

This experiment uses a confocal curved-mirror cavity, whose repeated peak families can have spacing $c/(4d)$ in air; it must not be calibrated blindly with the parallel-mirror $c/(2d)$. Extra transverse [normal modes](normal-mode.md) can mimic source features. A finite measured peak width bounds source sharpness only after instrumental broadening is considered; it does not determine the source's intrinsic width by itself.[§3.1.2 p. 20: transverse modes, confocal calibration and source-linewidth inference](lm-0300-fabry-perot-spectrum-analyser-by-walter-luhs.md)

For an independent ideal scan calculation, hold a narrow [laser](laser-split.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) fixed and vary the parallel-mirror gap $d$ at [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md). Advancing one resonance order requires $\Delta d=\lambda/(2n)$. If $d$ changes uniformly over the measured interval, the [time](time-in-physics.md) between repeated peaks divided by their [full width at half maximum](full-width-at-half-maximum.md) in [time](time-in-physics.md) equals [finesse](finesse.md). The cancellation of scan rate follows from $d(t)=d_0+vt$: both peak spacing and peak width are divided by the same $|v|$. It fails if the scan rate changes appreciably over that interval. A practical control in the University of Washington's motor-driven flat-mirror experiment is to check that successive peaks are equally spaced before using the time axis this way; its handout also warns that table strain and aggressive low-pass filtering alter the trace.[§2.2 pp. 9–11: central-spot scanning, nonuniform drive, table strain and filtering; §2.3 p. 11: widths and spacings in time](fabry-perot-interferometer-university-of-washington-physics-331.md)
