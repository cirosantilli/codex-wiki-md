# Dual lattice

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean lattice](euclidean-lattice.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_lattice)

The [dual lattice](dual-lattice.md) of a full-rank [Euclidean lattice](euclidean-lattice.md) $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^n$ is

$$
\Lambda^\vee=\{y\in\mathbb R^n:\langle x,y\rangle\in\mathbb Z\text{ for every }x\in\Lambda\}.
$$

Here $\langle x,y\rangle$ is the Euclidean [inner product](inner-product-split.md). If the columns of an invertible [matrix](matrix.md) $B$ form a [basis](basis.md) of $\Lambda=B\mathbb Z^n$, then

$$
\Lambda^\vee=B^{-\mathsf T}\mathbb Z^n,\qquad m(\Lambda^\vee)=|\det B|^{-1}=m(\Lambda)^{-1}.
$$

Indeed, $y$ belongs to the [dual lattice](dual-lattice.md) exactly when $B^{\mathsf T}y\in\mathbb Z^n$; $B^{-\mathsf T}$ denotes the [transpose](transpose.md) of the [matrix inverse](matrix-inverse-split.md). The reciprocal [covolume](covolume.md) follows by taking the [determinant](determinant.md). With the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) kernel $e^{-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle}$, these are precisely the [frequencies](frequency.md) of the [characters of a real torus](characters-of-a-real-torus.md): translating $x$ by any $\lambda\in\Lambda$ leaves $e^{2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle}$ unchanged. In one dimension, $(h\mathbb Z)^\vee=h^{-1}\mathbb Z$ for $h>0$: increasing the spacing in physical space decreases the spacing in [frequency](frequency.md) space.
