# Microscopic Ginzburg-Landau coefficients from BCS theory

↑ **Parent:** [Microscopic derivation of Ginzburg-Landau theory](microscopic-derivation-of-ginzburg-landau-theory.md)

For a clean isotropic three-dimensional weak-coupling s-wave model, use the energy-valued gap $\Delta$, a [density of states](density-of-states.md) $N(0)$ per spin, and free-energy density $\alpha_\Delta|\Delta|^2+\beta_\Delta|\Delta|^4/2+K_\Delta|D\Delta|^2$. Near $T_c$, $\alpha_\Delta\simeq N(0)(T-T_c)/T_c$. The quartic coefficient follows from

$$
\beta_\Delta=N(0)k_BT_c\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{d\xi}{(\omega_n^2+\xi^2)^2},\qquad\omega_n=(2n+1)\pi k_BT_c.
$$

The integral is $\pi/(2|\omega_n|^3)$ and the positive odd-integer sum is $7\zeta(3)/8$, yielding the displayed coefficient. The isotropic Fermi-velocity average gives $K_\Delta=\beta_\Delta\hbar^2v_F^2/6$. The pair charge is $q=-2e$ in $D=\nabla-iq\mathbf A/\hbar$. These values change under a rescaling of $\Delta$, different density-of-states convention or different material model; the local expansion is a near-transition approximation.[Microscopic near-transition expansion; pair charge](microscopic-derivation-of-the-ginzburg-landau-equations-in-the-theory-of-superconductivity-by-lev-gor-kov.md)
