# Electromagnetic production and decay of vector quarkonium

↑ **Parent:** [Quarkonium](quarkonium.md)

Single-photon [electron-positron annihilation](electron-positron-annihilation.md) can directly form [quarkonium](quarkonium.md) with $J^{PC}=1^{--}$. A vector S-wave [quarkonium](quarkonium.md) can annihilate back through a virtual [photon](photon.md) into a charged-lepton pair. At leading nonrelativistic order, its electronic partial [decay width](decay-width.md) is proportional to $\alpha^2Q_q^2|\psi(0)|^2/M^2$, where the wavefunction at the origin describes the quark-antiquark annihilation overlap.

Below the threshold for a pair of heavy-light [mesons](meson.md), hadronic decays require annihilation, primarily into three [gluons](gluon.md) for a color-singlet $1^{--}$ state. One gluon cannot carry a color-singlet initial state, and two gluons have the wrong charge-conjugation parity. Above the heavy-light pair threshold, a [strong interaction](strong-interaction.md) can instead separate the heavy quark and antiquark into different mesons after creating a light pair. This can greatly increase the total [decay width](decay-width.md) without comparably changing the short-distance electronic partial width. A specific example is the contrast between the first three [Upsilon mesons](upsilon-meson.md) and the $4S$ state. Higher radial states can additionally undergo hadronic or radiative transitions to lower quarkonium states.
