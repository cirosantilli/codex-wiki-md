# World line

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_line)

A [world line](world-line.md) is an object's history as a curve of [spacetime events](event-relativity.md). In an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), a particle at rest has constant spatial [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md), so its [world line](world-line.md) is vertical in a [spacetime diagram](spacetime-diagram.md) with $ct$ upward. A particle at constant [velocity](velocity.md) $v$ along one axis has $x=x_0+vt$, a straight sloping [world line](world-line.md). The coordinate representation changes with the [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), while the physical history is the same.

A massive observer follows a future-directed [timelike curve](timelike-curve.md). Along a differentiable [world line](world-line.md) with [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v(t)$, its clock accumulates

$$
d\tau=dt\sqrt{1-\frac{|\mathbf v(t)|^2}{c^2}},
\qquad
\Delta\tau=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\sqrt{1-\frac{|\mathbf v(t)|^2}{c^2}}\,dt.
$$

The [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) therefore measures clock time along the curve, not just the difference between two coordinate readings. In the straight $O$-to-$A$ example, $v=0.75c$ for $4\,\mathrm s$, giving $4\sqrt{1-0.75^2}\,\mathrm s=\sqrt7\,\mathrm s$. If the observer changes [velocity](velocity.md) along the path, use the [integral](integral-split.md) rather than assigning the straight-segment formula to the whole path.
