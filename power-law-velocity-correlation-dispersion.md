# Power-law velocity-correlation dispersion

↑ **Parent:** [Taylor turbulent dispersion](taylor-turbulent-dispersion.md)

For a zero-mean stationary [velocity](velocity.md) process of variance $\sigma_v^2$ and normalized [autocorrelation](autocorrelation.md) $(1+|s|)^{-\alpha}$, integrating velocity twice gives the displayed expression, exactly rather than just asymptotically. For $\alpha>1$ the long-time [diffusion coefficient](diffusion-coefficient.md) is $\sigma_v^2/(\alpha-1)$. At $\alpha=1$, the displacement variance is asymptotic to $2\sigma_v^2t\log t$. For $0<\alpha<1$, it is asymptotic to $2\sigma_v^2t^{2-\alpha}/[(1-\alpha)(2-\alpha)]$. Linear variance growth does not by itself imply a [central limit theorem](central-limit-theorem.md) or a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) for displacement.
