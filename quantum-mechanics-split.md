# Quantum mechanics

↑ **Parent:** [Branches of physics](branches-of-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics)

Quantum mechanics describes a [quantum system](quantum-system-split.md) using a [quantum state](quantum-state.md), with [observables](observable.md) represented by [self-adjoint operators](self-adjoint-operator.md). For a single particle in one dimension, a [pure state](pure-state.md) can be described by a [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) $\psi(x,t)$: its squared [modulus](modulus.md) gives the [probability density](probability-density.md) for position. The [Schrödinger equation](schrodinger-equation.md) predicts how this [probability amplitude](probability-amplitude.md) evolves between interventions; [measurement in quantum mechanics](quantum-measurement-split.md) connects it to experimental outcomes through the [Born rule](born-rule.md). A [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) therefore predicts a distribution of possible positions rather than a single trajectory as in [classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md).

The [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) makes three central ideas visible with elementary [sine](sine.md) functions. Confinement selects discrete [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md); [wavefunction normalization](wavefunction-normalization.md) converts a shape into quantitative [probabilities](probability.md); and a [quantum superposition](quantum-superposition.md) can have a changing [probability density](probability-density.md) even when each constituent [energy eigenstate](energy-eigenstate.md) has a stationary one. This combination explains why [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md) uses both [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) and [differential equations](differential-equation-split.md): [eigenfunctions](eigenfunction.md) describe the building blocks, while [quantum phases](quantum-phase.md) determine how they interfere. The same tools help explain the discrete energies of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) and [molecules](molecule-split.md), although their [Quantum Hamiltonians](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) require different potentials and often many particles.

In his first 1926 paper, Erwin Schrödinger formulated quantization as an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) problem for a [hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md) in [nonrelativistic quantum mechanics](nonrelativistic-quantum-mechanics.md). On its opening page he compared the emergence of integers with the number of [nodes of a standing wave](node-physics.md) on a vibrating string. The [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) below realizes that appealing idea especially transparently: the allowed integers follow from the [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md). This historical analogy concerns the origin of discrete energies; the [Born rule](born-rule.md) supplies the statistical interpretation of the modern [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md). [Schrödinger, Quantisierung als Eigenwertproblem, 1926, pp. 361–363](quantisierung-als-eigenwertproblem-erste-mitteilung-by-erwin-schrodinger.md).
