# Finite generation clears localization kernels

↑ **Parent:** [Localization of a ring](localization-of-a-ring.md)

For a [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) $P$, suppose the [kernel of a ring homomorphism](kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) $K=\ker(R\to R_P)$ is a finitely generated [ideal](ideal.md), say $(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$. Choose $s_i\notin P$ killing $a_i$ and let $f=\prod_i s_i$. Then $f\notin P$ and $fK=0$. If a fraction $a/f^n$ maps to zero in $R_P$, then $a\in K$, so $fa=0$ and the fraction was already zero in $R_f$. Thus the canonical [ring homomorphism](ring-homomorphism.md) $R_f\to R_P$ is [injective](injective-function.md). The empty product $f=1$ handles $K=0$. Every [Noetherian ring](noetherian-ring.md) satisfies the finite generation hypothesis.
