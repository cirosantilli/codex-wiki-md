# An exponential has no isolated pole

↑ **Parent:** [Isolated singularity](isolated-singularity-split.md)

If $g$ is [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) on a punctured disc about $a$, the function $e^g$ cannot have a [pole](pole.md) at $a$. Indeed, $(e^g)'/e^g=g'$, whereas a [pole](pole.md) of order $n\geq1$ would give [residue](residue.md) $-n$ for this [logarithmic derivative](logarithmic-derivative.md). The [Laurent series](laurent-series.md) derivative of $g$ has [residue](residue.md) zero, since its only candidate term comes from differentiating the constant coefficient. This contradiction rules out a [pole](pole.md), regardless of the type of the [isolated singularity](isolated-singularity-split.md) of $g$.
