# Equal-mass Coulomb circular orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Two-body problem](two-body-problem.md)

For two particles of equal [mass](mass.md) $m$ and opposite charges $\pm q$ separated by $s$, the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) lies halfway between them. In a nonradiating classical model, each radius is $s/2$ and $k_eq^2/s^2=m\omega^2s/2$. Hence $\omega^2=2k_eq^2/(ms^3)$ and $T=2\pi\sqrt{ms^3/(2k_eq^2)}$. Equivalently, relative motion has [reduced mass](reduced-mass.md) $m/2$. Radiation and quantum binding invalidate an indefinitely stable classical orbit for actual microscopic charges.
