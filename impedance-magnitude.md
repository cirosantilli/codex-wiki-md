# Impedance magnitude

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical impedance](electrical-impedance.md)

The [impedance magnitude](impedance-magnitude.md) of a linear [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md) in sinusoidal steady state is the ratio of [voltage](voltage.md) amplitude to [electric current](electric-current.md) amplitude. For [complex impedance](electrical-impedance.md) $Z=R+iX$, it is $|Z|=\sqrt{R^2+X^2}$, whereas the dissipative [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) is $\operatorname{Re}Z=R$. Measuring only amplitudes does not separately determine $R$ and $X$; a [phase shift](phase-shift.md) measurement supplies the missing information.
