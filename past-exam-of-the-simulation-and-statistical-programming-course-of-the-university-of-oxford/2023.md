↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

Generate [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ with [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) $p$, evaluate $\phi(X_i)$ for each draw, and return the [Monte Carlo estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-estimator)

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(X_i).}
$$

[Linearity of expectation](../expected-value.md#linearity-of-expectation) makes this an [unbiased estimator](../statistical-modelling.md#unbiased-estimator): $\mathbb E\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}}=\theta$. Independence and the specified [variance](../variance.md) give $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}})=1/n$. Its [Monte Carlo standard error](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-standard-error) is therefore $n^{-1/2}$, and the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) gives convergence to $\theta$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Apply [Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) to the [Monte Carlo estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-estimator):

$$
\mathbb P\!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}}-\theta\right|\geq0.01\right)
\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}})}{0.01^2}
=\frac{10^4}{n}.
$$

Thus $10^4/n\leq0.01$ suffices, giving

$$
\boxed{n=1{,}000{,}000.}
$$

This is a finite-sample [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) guarantee for every target satisfying the given [variance](../variance.md) assumption. It is a sufficient sample count supplied by the inequality, rather than a claim that every such target actually needs a million draws.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

The [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) gives $\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}}-\theta)\Rightarrow N(0,1)$, so, with $\Phi$ the [cumulative distribution function](../probability-distribution.md#cumulative-distribution-function) of the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution),

$$
\mathbb P\!\left(\left|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{MC}}-\theta\right|<0.01\right)
\approx 2\Phi(0.01\sqrt n)-1.
$$

The error has two tails. Using the supplied $2.58$ [quantile](../probability-distribution.md#quantile-function), whose two-sided coverage is approximately $0.9902$, gives

$$
0.01\sqrt n\geq2.58,
\qquad
\boxed{N:=n=\left\lceil\frac{2.58^2}{0.01^2}\right\rceil=66{,}564.}
$$

The supplied $2.33$ [quantile](../probability-distribution.md#quantile-function) would be appropriate for a one-sided $99\%$ calculation; it would give only approximately $98.02\%$ two-sided coverage. Using the exact $0.995$ [quantile](../probability-distribution.md#quantile-function) instead of $2.58$ would give $66{,}349$ draws; we consistently use the paper's rounded value $N=66{,}564$ below.

For routine [Monte Carlo integration](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-integration), the [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) calculation is preferable as a practical budget: it uses about one fifteenth as many draws as the [Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) bound. However, its coverage is approximate. Finite [variance](../variance.md) alone supplies no universal rate of convergence to the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution); an integrand with large [skewness](../expected-value.md#skewness) or a [heavy-tailed distribution](../probability-distribution.md#heavy-tailed-distribution) can make the approximation poor even at a large sample count. If an unconditional finite-sample $99\%$ guarantee is required under only the stated assumptions, use the [Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) budget.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Integrating the envelope $p\leq Mq$ shows $M\geq1$. One trial of [rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#rejection-sampling) draws $Y\sim q$ and an [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) $U$ from the [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) on $(0,1)$, and accepts $Y$ if $U\leq p(Y)/(Mq(Y))$. Repeat with fresh [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) after each rejection. Ratios need only be evaluated where $q>0$; the envelope makes the target zero wherever $q=0$.

For any [measurable set](../measure-theory.md#measurable-set) $B$, integration of the [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) of acceptance gives

$$
\mathbb P(Y\in B,\mathrm{accept})=\int_Bq(y)\frac{p(y)}{Mq(y)}\,dy=\frac1M\int_Bp(y)\,dy.
$$

Taking the whole domain gives acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/M$, and dividing gives $\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid\mathrm{accept})=\int_Bp(y)\,dy$. Fresh trials therefore supply [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) $X_1,\ldots,X_m$ with target [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) $p$. The corresponding [Monte Carlo estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-estimator) is

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_m^{\mathrm{RS}}=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\phi(X_j).}
$$

The proposal count $T$ up to and including one acceptance has the [geometric distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#geometric-distribution)

$$
\mathbb P(T=t)=(1-1/M)^{t-1}/M,\quad t=1,2,\ldots,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb ET=M.}
$$

For example, the [tail-sum formula](../expected-value.md#tail-sum-formula) gives $\mathbb ET=\sum_{t\geq0}(1-1/M)^t=M$. Thus the expected cost is $M$ proposal draws per accepted target draw.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Stop [rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#rejection-sampling) when exactly $N=66{,}564$ target draws have been accepted. The total proposal count $T_N$ is a sum of $N$ [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) waiting times with [geometric distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#geometric-distribution), equivalently a [negative binomial distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#negative-binomial-distribution) counting trials. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb ET_N=MN=66{,}564M,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(T_N)=NM(M-1).}
$$

The accepted-sample [Monte Carlo estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-estimator) then has the approximate $99\%$ precision from (a)(iii). The displayed proposal cost is an [expected value](../expected-value.md), which need not be an integer. Spending this many proposals in advance does not ensure that $N$ target draws will have been accepted.

<h4 id="1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/b/iii)

With a fixed budget of $n$ [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) proposal trials, the accepted count $K_n$ has [binomial distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#binomial-distribution) with success [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/M$. Hence

$$
\mathbb EK_n=\frac nM,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(K_n)=\frac{n(M-1)}{M^2}.
$$

For $M>1$, the [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) gives the one-sided approximation

$$
\mathbb P(K_n\geq N)\approx
\Phi\!\left(\frac{n-MN}{\sqrt{n(M-1)}}\right).
$$

Use $z=2.33$, since now only the lower tail of the accepted count matters. Requiring $n-MN\geq z\sqrt{n(M-1)}$ and solving the resulting [quadratic equation](../polynomial.md#quadratic-equation) in $t=\sqrt n$ gives the [one-sided normal approximation for a rejection proposal budget](../monte-carlo-method.md#one-sided-normal-approximation-for-a-rejection-proposal-budget)

$$
\boxed{
n_0=\left\lceil
\left(\frac{z\sqrt{M-1}+\sqrt{z^2(M-1)+4MN}}2\right)^2
\right\rceil.}
$$

Equivalently, before rounding, this is $MN+z^2(M-1)/2+(z/2)\sqrt{(M-1)[z^2(M-1)+4MN]}$. For the value in (a)(iii),

$$
\boxed{
n_0=\left\lceil
\left(\frac{2.33\sqrt{M-1}+\sqrt{5.4289(M-1)+266256M}}2\right)^2
\right\rceil.}
$$

When $M=1$, every proposal is accepted and $\boxed{n_0=N=66{,}564}$ exactly. Otherwise the positive safety margin above $MN$ allows for random fluctuations in the number accepted. The average cost in (b)(ii) describes stopping at the $N$th acceptance; this answer chooses a deterministic budget that reaches that acceptance with approximately $99\%$ [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability).

The approximation requires a sufficiently accurate [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) approximation to the [binomial distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#binomial-distribution). An exact $99\%$ budget can instead be found from its tail probabilities. Also, a $99\%$ chance of enough accepted draws and approximate $99\%$ precision conditional on obtaining them are separate claims: their intersection is not automatically a $99\%$ event.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Draw [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\sim q$ and use the [importance weight](../monte-carlo-method.md#importance-weight) $w=p/q$. The [importance sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#importance-sampling) estimator is

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nw(Y_i)\phi(Y_i).}
$$

The envelope supplies the [support condition for importance sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#support-condition-for-importance-sampling). Since $\mathbb E_q[w(Y)\phi(Y)]=\mathbb E_p\phi(X)=\theta$, this is an [unbiased estimator](../statistical-modelling.md#unbiased-estimator). Moreover, $0\leq w\leq M$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_q[w(Y)^2\phi(Y)^2]
&=\int w(x)\phi(x)^2p(x)\,dx\\
&=\mathbb E_p[w(X)\phi(X)^2]\\
&\leq M\mathbb E_p[\phi(X)^2]
=M(1+\theta^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\mathbb E_p\phi^2=\operatorname{Var}_p(\phi)+\theta^2=1+\theta^2$. Subtracting the squared [expected value](../expected-value.md) and dividing by $n$ proves the [bounded-weight importance-sampling moment bound](../monte-carlo-method.md#bounded-weight-importance-sampling-moment-bound) in the requested form:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}})
=\frac{\mathbb E_p[w\phi^2]-\theta^2}{n}
\leq\frac{M+(M-1)\theta^2}{n}.}
$$

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

The definition of $a$ and (c)(i) give $a\leq M$. Nonnegativity of the [variance](../variance.md) strengthens the lower bound to

$$
\boxed{\frac{\theta^2}{1+\theta^2}\leq a\leq M.}
$$

In fact the [variance](../variance.md) of one weighted observation is exactly

$$
v_{\mathrm{IS}}:=\operatorname{Var}_q(w(Y)\phi(Y))
=a(1+\theta^2)-\theta^2.
$$

It is finite by the envelope. For $v_{\mathrm{IS}}>0$, the [central limit theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#central-limit-theorem) gives approximate two-sided coverage $2\Phi(0.01\sqrt{n/v_{\mathrm{IS}}})-1$, so the paper's rounded [quantile](../probability-distribution.md#quantile-function) yields

$$
\boxed{n_{\mathrm{IS}}=\left\lceil66{,}564\,[a(1+\theta^2)-\theta^2]\right\rceil.}
$$

If $v_{\mathrm{IS}}=0$, the weighted observation equals $\theta$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), and one draw is sufficient. These are theoretical budgets: $a$ and $\theta$ may themselves be unknown and need preliminary estimation in an implementation.

Compare with the average [rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#rejection-sampling) cost $66{,}564M$. Ignoring integer rounding, [importance sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#importance-sampling) uses fewer proposal draws at the same approximate precision precisely when

$$
a(1+\theta^2)-\theta^2<M,
\qquad
\boxed{a<\frac{M+\theta^2}{1+\theta^2}.}
$$

For literal integer budgets, the comparison is $\lceil66{,}564v_{\mathrm{IS}}\rceil<66{,}564M$. The simpler strict inequality is the intended leading-order condition. The bound $a\leq M$ alone does not ensure an improvement when $\theta\ne0$; this is a [proposal-budget variance comparison of importance and rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#proposal-budget-variance-comparison-of-importance-and-rejection-sampling).

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

Write $w=p/q$ where $q>0$, assigning zero elsewhere. The envelope ensures $0\leq w/M\leq1$, and $\pi$ is nonnegative. Integrating its [joint probability density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#joint-probability-density) over the auxiliary coordinate gives

$$
\int_0^1\pi(x,u)\,du=Mq(x)\frac{w(x)}M=p(x).
$$

Consequently $\int_\Omega\int_0^1\pi(x,u)\,du\,dx=\int_\Omega p(x)\,dx=1$. This proves [normalization of a probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normalization-of-a-probability-distribution); the change of integration order is justified by [Tonelli's theorem](../measure-theory.md#tonelli-theorem).

The coordinates are generally dependent. For $p(x)>0$, their [conditional probability density](../probability-theory.md#conditional-density) is

$$
\pi(u\mid x)=\frac M{w(x)}\mathbf1_{[0,w(x)/M]}(u).
$$

Thus $U\mid X=x$ has a [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) on an interval whose endpoint depends on $x$. They are [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) exactly when $w(x)$ is constant for $p$-almost every $x$, since only then do these conditional laws agree. In particular, $p=q$ is an exception: $X\sim p$ and $U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1/M)$ are [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables). An unconditional answer that they can never be [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) would therefore be too strong.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Integrating the [joint probability density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#joint-probability-density) over the auxiliary coordinate, as in (i), gives the [marginal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#marginal-distribution)

$$
\boxed{\pi_X(x)=\int_0^1\pi(x,u)\,du=p(x).}
$$

In particular, augmenting with $U$ preserves the target [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) of $X$. The other [marginal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#marginal-distribution), though not needed to identify $X$, is $\pi_U(u)=M\int_\Omega q(x)\mathbf1_{\{w(x)\geq Mu\}}\,dx$ for $0<u<1$. It need not be a [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) on $(0,1)$.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

For the proposed [joint probability density](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#joint-probability-density) $\rho$, define the acceptance [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function)

$$
A_i:=\mathbf1_{\{V_i\leq w(Y_i)/M\}}.
$$

On the proposal's [support](../function.md#support) the [importance weight](../monte-carlo-method.md#importance-weight) is $\pi(Y_i,V_i)/\rho(Y_i,V_i)=MA_i$. Therefore the requested augmented-space [importance sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#importance-sampling) estimator is

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}}=\frac Mn\sum_{i=1}^nA_i\phi(Y_i).}
$$

Its [expected value](../expected-value.md) is $\theta$, provided $\mathbb E_p|\phi(X)|<\infty$. This is not the ordinary estimator $n^{-1}\sum_iw(Y_i)\phi(Y_i)$ from question 1. Taking the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) of this augmented estimator given all $Y_i$ produces that ordinary estimator, because $\mathbb E[A_i\mid Y_i]=w(Y_i)/M$. This is the [Rao-Blackwell identity for a fixed-denominator rejection estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#rao-blackwell-identity-for-a-fixed-denominator-rejection-estimator).

<h4 id="2/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/a/iv)

Use the same acceptance [indicator functions](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) and set $K_n=\sum_{i=1}^nA_i$, $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nA_i\phi(Y_i)$. The [rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#rejection-sampling) estimator based on these $n$ proposals is the mean over the accepted observations:

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{RS}}=\frac{S_n}{K_n}\quad(K_n>0).}
$$

For $K_n=0$, any fixed convention, such as zero, is harmless asymptotically. The $A_i$ are [independent and identically distributed random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) with [Bernoulli distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#bernoulli-distribution) and success [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/M$, so the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) gives $K_n/n\to1/M$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). In particular $K_n>0$ eventually [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The exact identity

$$
\boxed{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}}=\frac{MK_n}{n}\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{RS}}}
$$

then holds whenever $K_n>0$. Wherever $S_n\ne0$, it follows that

$$
\frac{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}}}{\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{RS}}}
=\frac{MK_n}{n}\longrightarrow1\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

A qualification is missing from the printed claim: the quotient must be defined eventually. A convenient sufficient assumption is $\mathbb E_p|\phi|<\infty$ and $\theta\ne0$. Indeed, another application of the [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) gives $S_n/n\to\theta/M$, ensuring that $S_n$ is eventually nonzero. This proves the stated quotient limit under that assumption.

As printed, the assertion is false as a universally defined quotient. For example, $\phi\equiv0$ makes both estimates zero for every $n$. Even excluding that example is insufficient: take $p=q$ to be the [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) on $(0,1)$, $M=1$, and $\phi(y)=1$ for $y<1/2$ and $-1$ otherwise. Then $S_n$ is a [simple symmetric random walk](../random-walk.md#simple-symmetric-random-walk), which returns to zero infinitely often [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), leaving the quotient undefined infinitely often.

With integrability but no assumption $\theta\ne0$, the exact multiplicative identity remains valid, and both estimates converge to $\theta$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). In particular $\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{IS}}-\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{RS}}\to0$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). One may alternatively define the quotient's removable $0/0$ cases as $MK_n/n$; that explicitly extended quantity always converges to one. These are valid repairs of the source's unrestricted statement.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Fix a proposal $y$ with $\widetilde q(y)>0$, and abbreviate $B=M\widetilde q(y)$, $H=h(y)$ and $P=\widetilde p(y)$. The envelope says $0\leq H\leq P\leq B$. Assuming fresh [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) draws at both stages, the [law of total probability](../probability-space.md#law-of-total-probability) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\mathrm{accept}\mid Y=y)
&=\frac HB+\left(1-\frac HB\right)\frac{P-H}{B-H}\\
&=\frac HB+\frac{P-H}{B}
=\boxed{\frac{\widetilde p(y)}{M\widetilde q(y)}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second term includes both reaching the residual test and passing it. If $H=B$, the envelope forces $P=B$ and the first test accepts with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) one; the second-stage $0/0$ ratio is never evaluated. This is [squeeze rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#squeeze-rejection-sampling).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Average the [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) in (i) over $Y\sim q=\widetilde q/Z_q$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\mathrm{accept})
&=\int_\Omega\frac{\widetilde q(y)}{Z_q}
\frac{\widetilde p(y)}{M\widetilde q(y)}\,dy\\
&=\frac1{MZ_q}\int_\Omega\widetilde p(y)\,dy
=\boxed{\frac{Z_p}{MZ_q}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is exactly the [acceptance mass for an unnormalized rejection envelope](../monte-carlo-method.md#acceptance-mass-for-an-unnormalized-rejection-envelope). In normalized form the envelope is $p\leq(MZ_q/Z_p)q$, giving the same acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability). Here $M$ bounds the ratio of the unnormalized functions, so its value need not equal the envelope constant for the normalized [probability density functions](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function).

The squeeze changes the number of expensive target evaluations, while preserving the per-proposal acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability). The expected number of proposals per accepted draw is $MZ_q/Z_p$.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

For a [measurable set](../measure-theory.md#measurable-set) $C$, (i) gives the joint acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability)

$$
\mathbb P(Y\in C,\mathrm{accept})
=\int_C\frac{\widetilde q(y)}{Z_q}
\frac{\widetilde p(y)}{M\widetilde q(y)}\,dy
=\frac1{MZ_q}\int_C\widetilde p(y)\,dy.
$$

Divide by $Z_p/(MZ_q)$ from (ii). The resulting [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(Y\in C\mid\mathrm{accept})
=\frac1{Z_p}\int_C\widetilde p(y)\,dy
=\int_Cp(y)\,dy.}
$$

Thus every accepted value has target [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) $p$. Repeating rejected trials with fresh [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) does not change this law: the contribution from acceptance on trial $j$ is multiplied by $(1-Z_p/(MZ_q))^{j-1}$, and summing that [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series) gives the same conditional law. Independent repetitions produce [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) target draws.

<h4 id="2/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/b/iv)

The proposal's [normalization of a probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normalization-of-a-probability-distribution) is

$$
\boxed{Z_q=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-|x|}\,dx=2,\qquad q(x)=\tfrac12e^{-|x|}.}
$$

This is the [Laplace distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#laplace-distribution) with location zero and scale one. For example, generate its magnitude from the [exponential distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution) with rate one and give it an [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) fair sign. The target is the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution), with $Z_p=\sqrt{2\pi}$.

To obtain the tight envelope, let $t=|x|$. By [completing the square](../polynomial.md#completing-the-square),

$$
\frac{\widetilde p(x)}{\widetilde q(x)}
=e^{-t^2/2+t}=e^{1/2}e^{-(t-1)^2/2}\leq e^{1/2}.
$$

Equality occurs at $x=\pm1$, so $\boxed{M_*=\sqrt e}$ is the smallest valid envelope constant. It maximizes the acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) for this fixed proposal. The supplied lower bound must be clipped at zero to satisfy the algorithm's nonnegativity requirement:

$$
\boxed{h(x)=\max\{0,1-x^2/2\}.}
$$

This is valid because $e^{-s}\geq1-s$ for $s\geq0$, while $\widetilde p$ is everywhere positive. Its [support](../function.md#support) is $[-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]$.

Here is the fully specified [squeeze rejection sampling](../monte-carlo-method.md#squeeze-rejection-sampling) procedure. Draw $Y$ with [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) $q(y)=e^{-|y|}/2$, independently draw $U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, and compute $H=\max(0,1-Y^2/2)$ and $B=\sqrt e\,e^{-|Y|}$. If $U\leq H/B$, return $Y$ immediately. Otherwise evaluate $P=e^{-Y^2/2}$, independently draw $V\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, and return $Y$ if $V\leq(P-H)/(B-H)$. On rejection restart with fresh draws. In this example $B>H$ everywhere, so the residual denominator is positive.

Avoiding a target evaluation means accepting at the first test. Its unconditional [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) for one proposal is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\text{no target evaluation})
&=\int_{\mathbb R}q(y)\frac{h(y)}{M\widetilde q(y)}\,dy\\
&=\frac1{2\sqrt e}\int_{-\sqrt2}^{\sqrt2}(1-y^2/2)\,dy\\
&=\boxed{\frac{2\sqrt2}{3\sqrt e}\approx0.571843.}
\end{aligned}
$$

For comparison, the overall acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is $\sqrt{2\pi}/(2\sqrt e)=\sqrt{\pi/(2e)}\approx0.760173$. The first-stage saving $0.571843$ is the fraction of proposals requiring no target evaluation, including both eventual accepted and rejected proposals in the denominator; it is not conditional on acceptance.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The following [R](../programming-language.md#r-programming-language) code implements the given [recursion](../algorithm.md#recursion-computer-science) for the [Cholesky decomposition](../quadratic-form.md#cholesky-decomposition). The input is assumed to be a real [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) that is a [positive-definite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix).

```
mychol <- function(A) {
  n <- nrow(A)
  if (n == 1L) return(matrix(sqrt(A[1L, 1L]), 1L, 1L))
  j <- 2L:n
  L <- matrix(0, n, n)
  L[1L, 1L] <- sqrt(A[1L, 1L])
  b <- A[j, 1L, drop = FALSE] / L[1L, 1L]
  L[j, 1L] <- b
  S <- A[j, j, drop = FALSE] - tcrossprod(b)
  L[j, j] <- mychol(S)
  L
}
```

Here `tcrossprod(b)` is the [outer product](../vector-space.md#outer-product) $bb^T$. The `drop = FALSE` arguments preserve [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) dimensions, including the final one-by-one subproblem, and the [base case](../algorithm.md#base-case) also returns a [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix).

To verify correctness, partition $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&r^T\\r&B\end{pmatrix}$. The code sets $\ell=\sqrt a$, $b=r/\ell$ and recursively factors $S=B-bb^T$. This [Schur complement](../linear-algebra.md#schur-complement) is a [positive-definite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix): for $z\ne0$,

$$
z^TSz=
\begin{pmatrix}-r^Tz/a\\z\end{pmatrix}^{\!T}
A
\begin{pmatrix}-r^Tz/a\\z\end{pmatrix}>0.
$$

If $CC^T=S$ is the recursive answer, then the returned [lower triangular matrix](../linear-algebra.md#lower-triangular-matrix) satisfies

$$
L=\begin{pmatrix}\ell&0\\b&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
LL^T=\begin{pmatrix}a&\ell b^T\\\ell b&bb^T+CC^T\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}a&r^T\\r&B\end{pmatrix}=A.
$$

The one-dimensional identity starts this [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) proof. Each recursive call reduces the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) size by one and therefore terminates.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

An [inverse matrix](../matrix-inverse.md) exists only if the [lower triangular matrix](../linear-algebra.md#lower-triangular-matrix) has no zero [diagonal entries](../vector-space.md#diagonal-matrix-entry); this necessary assumption is implicit in the request for $L^{-1}$. Partition

$$
L=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\b&C\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Writing its inverse in the same block form and multiplying gives the [recursive inverse of a lower triangular matrix](../linear-algebra.md#recursive-inverse-of-a-lower-triangular-matrix):

$$
\boxed{L^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{-1}&0\\-C^{-1}b/a&C^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Indeed the lower-left block of the product is $b/a-C(C^{-1}b/a)=0$, and the diagonal blocks are identities. This gives an implementation with no call to `solve`:

```
ltinv <- function(L) {
  n <- nrow(L)
  if (n == 1L) return(matrix(1 / L[1L, 1L], 1L, 1L))
  j <- 2L:n
  Cinv <- ltinv(L[j, j, drop = FALSE])
  B <- matrix(0, n, n)
  B[1L, 1L] <- 1 / L[1L, 1L]
  B[j, j] <- Cinv
  B[j, 1L] <- -as.vector(Cinv %*% L[j, 1L, drop = FALSE]) / L[1L, 1L]
  B
}
```

The preserved [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) dimensions also handle $n=2$. A [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) using the block identity proves that every returned [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is the desired inverse. Applied to the [Cholesky decomposition](../quadratic-form.md#cholesky-decomposition) from (a), all [diagonal entries](../vector-space.md#diagonal-matrix-entry) are positive, so all divisions are valid.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Each state has exactly $k(n-k)$ neighbours: choose one occupied coordinate and one unoccupied coordinate to swap. The normalized [proposal distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#proposal-distribution) is therefore

$$
q(v\mid u)=\frac1{k(n-k)}\mathbf1_{\{v\sim u\}}.
$$

The swap relation is symmetric and the number of neighbours is constant, so $q(v\mid u)=q(u\mid v)$. The [discrete uniform distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#discrete-uniform-distribution) used as the [prior distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability) is also constant on $S_k$, with mass $\binom nk^{-1}$. Both factors cancel in the [Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability](../bayesian-statistics.md#metropolis-hastings-acceptance-probability).

Let $Q=\Sigma^{-1}$ and $d=v-u$. The displayed [multivariate normal distribution](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) presupposes that the [covariance matrix](../variance.md#covariance-matrix) $\Sigma$ is a [positive-definite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-definite-matrix), so $Q$ is a [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix). Expanding the difference of [quadratic forms](../quadratic-form.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{\pi(v\mid x)}{\pi(u\mid x)}
&=-\tfrac12\{(x-v)^TQ(x-v)-(x-u)^TQ(x-u)\}\\
&=d^TQ(x-u)-\tfrac12d^TQd\\
&=d^TQ\left(x-\frac{u+v}{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\log\alpha(u,v)=\min\!\left\{0,\ (v-u)^T\Sigma^{-1}\left(x-\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right\}.}
$$

The truncation at zero is essential: a log acceptance ratio can be positive, but the [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) of an acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) cannot.

For the particular proposed swap, let $a=i_1$ be the coordinate changing from zero to one and $b=i_2$ the coordinate changing from one to zero. Then $d=e_a-e_b$, and the untruncated scalar simplifies further to

$$
\boxed{(Q(x-u))_a-(Q(x-u))_b-\tfrac12(Q_{aa}+Q_{bb}-2Q_{ab}).}
$$

Only two rows and four entries of $Q$ are needed for this evaluation. This is [Metropolis sampling on fixed-cardinality subsets](../bayesian-statistics.md#metropolis-sampling-on-fixed-cardinality-subsets).

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

The source's function name suggests a log return value, and (c)(i) asks for $\log\alpha$, while the wording here and in (c)(iv) asks for the acceptance [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability). We use the literal probability-returning convention and explicitly exponentiate the result of (c)(i):

```
mylogapp <- function(x, u, v, sigma_inv) {
  d <- v - u
  log_ratio <- as.numeric(crossprod(d, sigma_inv %*% (x - (u + v) / 2)))
  log_alpha <- min(0, log_ratio)
  exp(log_alpha)
}
```

Thus `mylogapp` returns $\alpha\in[0,1]$, and (c)(iv) compares it directly with a [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) draw. Taking the minimum before exponentiating prevents overflow. The arguments $u,v$ are assumed to be valid neighbouring states. A log-returning convention would instead return `log_alpha` and compare `log(runif(1))` with it; the two conventions implement the same [Metropolis–Hastings algorithm](../bayesian-statistics.md#metropolis-hastings-algorithm), provided the caller uses the matching comparison.

<h4 id="3/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/c/iii)

The [Cholesky decomposition](../quadratic-form.md#cholesky-decomposition) gives $\Sigma=LL^T$. If $B=L^{-1}$, reversal of factors in a [matrix inverse](../matrix-inverse.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\Sigma^{-1}=(L^T)^{-1}L^{-1}=B^TB.}
$$

Hence the required [R](../programming-language.md#r-programming-language) implementation is

```
invsigma <- function(sigma) {
  L <- mychol(sigma)
  B <- ltinv(L)
  crossprod(B)
}
```

Here `crossprod(B)` computes $B^TB$. Using `tcrossprod(B)` would compute $BB^T$, which generally differs from $\Sigma^{-1}$. Both helper functions preserve the one-by-one [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) case.

<h4 id="3/c/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/c/iv)

Choose $k$ distinct coordinates uniformly for the starting ones; each element of $S_k$ then has starting [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/\binom nk$. At each iteration choose one current one and one current zero uniformly and swap them. This implements the normalized [proposal distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#proposal-distribution) from (c)(i), remains in $S_k$, and needs just one precomputed [matrix inverse](../matrix-inverse.md).

```
mymcmc <- function(x, k, sigma) {
  n <- length(x)
  stopifnot(length(k) == 1L, is.finite(k), k == floor(k),
            k > 0L, k < n, is.matrix(sigma),
            all(dim(sigma) == c(n, n)))
  sigma_inv <- invsigma(sigma)
  u <- integer(n)
  u[sample.int(n, k, replace = FALSE)] <- 1L
  draws <- matrix(0, 100L, n)
  for (iteration in seq_len(100L)) {
    ones <- which(u == 1L)
    zeros <- which(u == 0L)
    i <- ones[sample.int(length(ones), 1L)]
    j <- zeros[sample.int(length(zeros), 1L)]
    v <- u
    v[i] <- 0L
    v[j] <- 1L
    alpha <- mylogapp(x, u, v, sigma_inv)
    if (runif(1L) < alpha) u <- v
    draws[iteration, ] <- u
  }
  colMeans(draws)
}
```

Sampling positions with `sample.int` and then indexing into `ones` or `zeros` also works when $k=1$ or $n-k=1$. In [R](../programming-language.md#r-programming-language), a call such as `sample(ones, 1)` would incorrectly sample from `1:ones` when `ones` is a single numeric value greater than one.

The code records the state after each of exactly $100$ transitions, including repetitions after rejection. Its answer is the vector [Monte Carlo estimator](../monte-carlo-method.md#monte-carlo-estimator)

$$
\boxed{\widehat m=\frac1{100}\sum_{t=1}^{100}U^{(t)}\approx\mathbb E[U\mid X=x].}
$$

This estimates the [posterior mean](../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-mean) coordinate by coordinate; because each coordinate is binary, its entries estimate the [posterior probabilities](../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability) of inclusion. Every stored state has coordinate sum $k$, so the estimated means lie in $[0,1]$ and sum to $k$.

For neighbouring states, the symmetry of the proposal and the [Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability](../bayesian-statistics.md#metropolis-hastings-acceptance-probability) give [detailed balance](../markov-chain.md#detailed-balance):

$$
\pi(u\mid x)q(v\mid u)\alpha(u,v)
=q(v\mid u)\min\{\pi(u\mid x),\pi(v\mid x)\}
=\pi(v\mid x)q(u\mid v)\alpha(v,u).
$$

Thus the [posterior distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#bayesian-posterior) is a [stationary distribution](../markov-chain.md#stationary-distribution). Any two states in $S_k$ can be connected by moving the ones that occupy the wrong positions, and the [multivariate normal distribution](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) gives strictly positive target masses. The resulting [Markov chain](../markov-chain.md) is therefore an [irreducible Markov chain](../markov-chain.md#irreducible-markov-chain), and its long-run state averages estimate the [posterior mean](../bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-mean).

The requested $100$ iterations are a finite approximation, without a convergence guarantee or discarded [burn-in](../bayesian-statistics.md#burn-in). A randomly chosen starting subset has the [prior distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability), rather than generally the [posterior distribution](../bayesian-statistics.md#bayesian-posterior). No [aperiodicity](../markov-chain.md#aperiodic-markov-chain) assumption is needed for the finite-state [Markov chain ergodic theorem](../markov-chain.md#markov-chain-ergodic-theorem) for averages; indeed a uniform two-state target with $n=2,k=1$ gives a period-two alternating chain. This does not invalidate either the code or the state-average estimator.
