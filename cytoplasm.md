# Cytoplasm

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm)

The [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) is the material inside a [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md) but outside the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md), when one is present. It includes the [cytosol](cytosol.md), the [organelles](organelle.md) suspended in it, and other structures; it is not just a name for the liquid. In a [prokaryote](prokaryote.md), which has no [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md), the [nucleoid](nucleoid.md) and [ribosomes](ribosome.md) are within the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). See [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 4.3: cytoplasm and cytosol](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells) and [Clark, Douglas and Choi, Biology 2e, 4.2: prokaryotic organization](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells).

**[Figure 6](#image-cell-nucleus-cytosol-and-organelles-in-a-nucleated-eukaryotic-biological-cell). Cell nucleus, cytosol and organelles in a nucleated eukaryotic biological cell**. Original schematic: the pale blue region represents [cytosol](cytosol.md), while the surrounding [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) also includes the depicted [mitochondrion](mitochondria.md) and [ribosomes](ribosome.md). The [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) and [cell nucleolus](nucleolus-biology.md) are excluded from the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). A [nuclear pore complex](nuclear-pore-complex.md) provides a route across the double [nuclear envelope](nuclear-envelope.md). Dimensions and component counts are illustrative; many structures are omitted.

Try classifying four positions in the diagram. A point in the pale blue region belongs to both [cytosol](cytosol.md) and [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). A point inside the [mitochondrion](mitochondria.md) belongs to an [organelle](organelle.md) included in the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md), but not to the surrounding [cytosol](cytosol.md). A point on a free [ribosome](ribosome.md) belongs to the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md), but the [ribosome](ribosome.md) itself is not [cytosol](cytosol.md). A point inside the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) belongs to neither [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) nor [cytosol](cytosol.md). These distinctions prevent the misleading inference that everything outside the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) shares one undivided liquid environment.

The usefulness of the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) is this combination of shared surroundings and specialized structures: [enzymes](enzyme-split.md) in the [cytosol](cytosol.md) carry out many steps of [metabolism](metabolism.md), while [organelles](organelle.md) maintain their own internal conditions. A soluble [protein](protein-split.md) made by a free [ribosome](ribosome.md) may work in the [cytosol](cytosol.md) or be transported elsewhere, including into the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). Cellular organization therefore combines separation with exchange, rather than assigning each [protein](protein-split.md) permanently to its place of synthesis. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell (2002): traffic between nucleus and cytosol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26932/).
