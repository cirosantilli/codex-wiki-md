# Selection on noisy performance can produce apparent overconfidence

↑ **Parent:** [Dunning-Kruger effect](dunning-kruger-effect.md)

Let $X=A+\varepsilon$ and $S=A+\eta$, where latent score $A$ has [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma_A^2$, and the centred normal errors are independent of each other and $A$. Since $\operatorname{Cov}(S,X)=\sigma_A^2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sigma_A^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2$, [Gaussian conditional expectation](gaussian-conditional-expectation.md) gives $\mathbb E[S\mid X=x]=\mu+\lambda(x-\mu)$ with $\lambda=\sigma_A^2/(\sigma_A^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$. Subtracting $x$ proves the formula. For $0<\lambda<1$, selecting scores below the mean creates positive expected self-assessment errors relative to those observed scores. This is a mathematical possibility, not an empirical estimate of any particular study's contribution from measurement error.
