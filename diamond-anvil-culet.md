# Diamond anvil culet

↑ **Parent:** [Diamond anvil cell](diamond-anvil-cell.md)

A [diamond anvil culet](diamond-anvil-culet.md) is the small flat contact face at the tip of a [diamond](diamond.md) anvil. Its diameter $d_c$ determines its nominal [surface area](surface-area.md) $A_c$. The [gasket](gasket.md) hole has a different [surface area](surface-area.md) $A_h$; neither should be confused with the much larger drive mechanism. For an ideal static axial balance, $F$ is the compressive [force](force-split.md) on either anvil. The two equal opposing loads do not produce $2F$ through a single contact.

For an original chosen geometry, $F=1000\,\mathrm N$ and $d_c=0.300\,\mathrm{mm}$ give

$$
A_c=7.0686\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^2},\qquad
\bar q=F/A_c=14.147\,\mathrm{GPa}.
$$

Here $\bar q$ is the mean axial contact [stress](stress.md) over the culet, not a measured chamber [pressure](pressure-split.md). Using a $0.100\,\mathrm{mm}$ hole instead would give $F/A_h=127.324\,\mathrm{GPa}$, nine times larger. That substitution falsely assigns the entire applied [force](force-split.md) to the hole.

To see the missing load path, idealize the culet as a coplanar hole region plus a surrounding contact annulus. With a hydrostatic chamber [pressure](pressure-split.md) $p$ and axial resultant $F_g$ carried by the [gasket](gasket.md), its [force](force-split.md) balance is

$$
F=pA_h+F_g.
$$

If the stipulated chamber [pressure](pressure-split.md) is $5.40\,\mathrm{GPa}$, the hole carries only $42.4\,\mathrm N$ and the [gasket](gasket.md) carries $957.6\,\mathrm N$. These are model loads, not measurements. Real [gasket](gasket.md) deformation and anvil [elasticity](elasticity-physics-split.md) alter the local contact [stress](stress.md); the scalar balance cannot predict $p$ without additional information. This is why internal [pressure calibration](pressure-calibration.md) matters even when applied [force](force-split.md) is accurately known.
