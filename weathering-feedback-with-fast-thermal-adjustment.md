# Weathering feedback with fast thermal adjustment

↑ **Parent:** [Climate model](climate-model.md)

In this idealized greenhouse-[weathering](weathering.md) model, [temperature](temperature-split.md) adjusts rapidly when $\epsilon\ll1$, giving $\theta\simeq\Gamma(P-1)$. The remaining carbon balance has [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) $\mu\log P_*+\Gamma(P_*-1)=\log v$. For positive $\mu,\Gamma$, the left side has [derivative](derivative.md) $\mu/P+\Gamma>0$ and ranges from minus infinity to infinity, so there is exactly one positive [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) for every sustained positive source $v$.

[Linearization](linearization.md) of the reduced carbon equation gives [dimensionless](dimensionless-quantity.md) decay rate $v(\Gamma+\mu/P_*)$, hence dimensional relaxation time $t_C/[v(\Gamma+\mu/P_*)]$. Its [negative feedback](negative-feedback.md) is increased [weathering](weathering.md) when either [temperature](temperature-split.md) or carbon [pressure](pressure-split.md) rises. The two-variable [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) is also linearly stable: its Jacobian has negative trace and determinant $v(\mu/P_*+\Gamma)/\epsilon>0$. A fast radiative response therefore does not imply a fast carbon-cycle response. Linearized radiative coefficients must be calibrated at a consistent reference state before numerical use.
