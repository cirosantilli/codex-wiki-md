# Quaternion group modulo its centre

↑ **Parent:** [Quaternion group](quaternion-group.md)

In the [quaternion group](quaternion-group.md), $-1=i^2=j^2=k^2$ commutes with each generator because any element commutes with its powers. Hence $N=\{1,-1\}$ is central and is a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md). The reverse products satisfy $ji=-k$, $kj=-i$, $ik=-j$, so none of $\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$ is central. Therefore $Z(Q_8)=N$.

The four [cosets](coset.md) are $N,iN,jN,kN$. Each nonidentity coset squares to $N$, and $(iN)(jN)=kN$. Thus the [quotient group](quotient-group.md) is the [Klein four-group](klein-four-group.md). Explicitly, mapping $\pm1$ to its identity and mapping $\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$ to its three different nonidentity elements is a surjective [group homomorphism](group-homomorphism.md) with [group kernel](kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) $N$. The [first isomorphism theorem](first-isomorphism-theorem.md) gives the title result.
