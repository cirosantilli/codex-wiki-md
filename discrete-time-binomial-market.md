# Discrete-time binomial market

↑ **Parent:** [Binomial options pricing model](binomial-options-pricing-model.md)

At each node a positive [stock](stock.md) price moves to $uS_t$ or $dS_t$, with $0<d<u$, while a [bank account](bank-account.md) grows by a fixed gross factor $R>0$. Assume both successors have positive physical [probability](probability.md), no dividends, a [frictionless market](frictionless-market.md) with no trading costs, divisible holdings, unrestricted [short selling](short-finance.md), and borrowing and lending at the same fixed $R$. A [self-financing portfolio](self-financing-portfolio.md) receives no outside cash after its initial funding; subsequent rebalancing uses its own proceeds.

This [binomial market](discrete-time-binomial-market.md) excludes [arbitrage](arbitrage.md) exactly when $d<R<u$. If $R\leq d$, borrowing $S_0$ to buy one share gives terminal gains $S_0(u-R)$ or $S_0(d-R)$; these are nonnegative and one is positive. If $R\geq u$, [short selling](short-finance.md) one share and lending its proceeds gives the analogous [arbitrage](arbitrage.md). Strict inequalities allow a positive [risk-neutral probability](risk-neutral-probability.md) for each state. The two traded payoffs also span both successor states, giving [market completeness](complete-market.md) relative to the information generated by this tree.
