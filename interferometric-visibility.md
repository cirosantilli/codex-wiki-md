# Interferometric visibility

↑ **Parent:** [Interference (wave propagation)](interference-wave-propagation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interferometric_visibility)

[Interferometric visibility](interferometric-visibility.md) measures the contrast of a scanned [interference fringe](interference-fringe.md). For the mutually coherent, equally polarized two-beam model in [wave interference](interference-wave-propagation.md), with fixed individual [wave intensities](intensity-physics.md) and $I_1+I_2>0$, the [constructive interference intensity](constructive-interference-intensity.md) and [destructive interference intensity](destructive-interference-intensity.md) give

$$
\mathcal V=\frac{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}+I_{\min}}=\frac{2\sqrt{I_1I_2}}{I_1+I_2}.
$$

Equal nonzero [wave intensities](intensity-physics.md) give $\mathcal V=1$; $I_1=1,I_2=0.25$ give $\mathcal V=0.8$. Unequal [wave intensities](intensity-physics.md) can therefore reduce [interferometric visibility](interferometric-visibility.md) even with perfect mutual [optical coherence](optical-coherence.md). Random [phase differences](phase-difference.md) or distinguishable [wave polarizations](polarization-waves.md) can reduce it further; zero contrast alone does not identify the cause. Both intensities zero leave the ratio undefined.

For normalized paths with [complex amplitudes](complex-amplitude.md) $\alpha,\beta$ and identical internal states, the output [probability](probability.md) is $1/2+\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^*\beta e^{i\theta})$, so $\mathcal V=2|\alpha\beta|$. A [photon polarization](photon-polarization.md) fringe alone determines transverse [quantum coherence](quantum-coherence-in-a-specified-basis.md) but not the sign of the population difference.
