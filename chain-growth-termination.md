# Chain-growth termination

↑ **Parent:** [Chain-growth polymerization](chain-growth-polymerization.md)

[Chain-growth termination](chain-growth-termination.md) removes the ability of a reactive chain carrier to continue the succession of growth steps.[termination, T06274, definition; source recommendation p. 1173](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md) Two illustrative outcomes have different molecule counts:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_j^*+P_k^*&\longrightarrow P_{j+k}, &&\Delta(C,A,S)=(-1,-2,0),\\
P_j^*+P_k^*&\longrightarrow P_j+P_k, &&\Delta(C,A,S)=(0,-2,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first joins the two chains into one inactive [polymer](polymer-split.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md). The second leaves two inactive [polymer](polymer-split.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md), as can occur when one chain end transfers [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) to the other instead of their ends joining. End-group chemistry is suppressed in these unit-count symbols; they are not complete [molecular formulas](molecular-formula.md). Losing two [polymerization-active sites](polymerization-active-site.md) does not mean losing two chains or any incorporated [monomeric units](monomeric-unit.md). A temporarily inactive, reactivatable site must not be counted as irreversible [chain-growth termination](chain-growth-termination.md).
