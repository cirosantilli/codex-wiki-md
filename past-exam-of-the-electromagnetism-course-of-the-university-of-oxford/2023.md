<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

The [image charge for a grounded conducting sphere](../electric-field.md#image-charge-for-a-grounded-conducting-sphere) is obtained by the [Kelvin transform](../partial-differential-equation.md#kelvin-transform):

$$
\boxed{q^\star=-\frac{a}{r_0}q,\qquad \mathbf r^\star=\frac{a^2}{r_0^2}\mathbf r_0=\left(0,0,\frac{a^2}{r_0}\right).}
$$

It lies inside the [electrical conductor](../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductor), outside the region where it represents the physical [electric field](../electric-field.md). The [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) is

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r)=\begin{cases}
0,&r\leq a,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac q{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|}-\frac{qa/r_0}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r^\star|}\right),&r>a,\quad\mathbf r\ne\mathbf r_0.
\end{cases}}
$$

At the physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge), the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) has its usual [Coulomb's law](../electrostatics.md#coulomb-s-law) singularity. There is no physical singularity at the [image charge](../electric-field.md#image-charge): the interior expression is identically zero.

For $|\mathbf r|=a$, direct expansion of squared distances gives

$$
|\mathbf r-\mathbf r^\star|^2
=a^2+\frac{a^4}{r_0^2}-\frac{2a^2}{r_0^2}\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf r_0
=\frac{a^2}{r_0^2}|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|^2.
$$

Consequently the two terms cancel exactly, proving the zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) on the sphere. At infinity each term is $O(r^{-1})$, and more explicitly

$$
\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac{q(1-a/r_0)}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}+O(r^{-2})\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus the [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) at infinity also holds. The candidate solves the [Poisson equation](../partial-differential-equation.md#poisson-s-equation) with the one physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge); [uniqueness of Poisson equation](../analysis.md#uniqueness-of-poisson-equation), with these [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition), identifies it as the required [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Let $\mathbf n=\mathbf r/a$ be the [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal) from the [electrical conductor](../electric-charge.md#electrical-conductor) into the [vacuum](../physics.md#vacuum). The [electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor](../electrostatics.md#electrostatic-boundary-conditions-at-a-conductor), together with the vanishing interior [electric field](../electric-field.md), give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E_{\rm out}=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\mathbf n,\qquad
\sigma=\epsilon_0\mathbf E_{\rm out}\cdot\mathbf n=-\epsilon_0\left.\partial_r\phi\right|_{a^+}.}
$$

Write $u=\cos\theta$ in [spherical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system), $D=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|$ on $r=a$, and $z^\star=a^2/r_0$. Differentiating the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) at fixed angles gives

$$
\left.\partial_r\phi\right|_{a^+}
=\frac q{4\pi\epsilon_0D^3}\left[-(a-r_0u)+\frac{r_0^2}{a^2}\left(a-\frac{a^2}{r_0}u\right)\right]
=\frac q{4\pi\epsilon_0D^3}\left(\frac{r_0^2}{a}-a\right).
$$

Here the distance identity in the preceding answer was used before simplifying. Hence the induced [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(\mathbf r)=\frac{q(a-r_0^2/a)}{4\pi|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|^3},\qquad |\mathbf r|=a.}
$$

For positive $q$ this [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density) is negative everywhere, with largest magnitude nearest the [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge).

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Integrate the [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density) using the [surface integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral) in [spherical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system):

$$
Q=2\pi a^2\frac{q(a-r_0^2/a)}{4\pi}
\int_{-1}^1\frac{du}{(a^2+r_0^2-2ar_0u)^{3/2}}.
$$

Since $r_0>a$, the elementary integral is

$$
\frac1{ar_0}\left[\frac1{\sqrt{a^2+r_0^2-2ar_0u}}\right]_{-1}^1
=\frac1{ar_0}\left(\frac1{r_0-a}-\frac1{r_0+a}\right)
=\frac2{r_0(r_0^2-a^2)}.
$$

Substitution yields the total induced [electric charge](../electric-charge.md)

$$
\boxed{Q=-\frac{qa}{r_0}=q^\star.}
$$

The equality with the [image charge](../electric-field.md#image-charge) also follows by applying [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) to the exterior [electric field](../electric-field.md): the physical and fictitious descriptions give the same [electric flux](../electric-field.md#electric-flux) through a sphere enclosing the conductor but excluding the physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Put $k=b/a>1$ and $\mathbf e_z=(0,0,1)$. The complete construction of [image charges between concentric grounded spheres](../electric-field.md#image-charges-between-concentric-grounded-spheres) consists of two families, indexed by every integer:

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
q_n&=qk^n,&\mathbf r_n&=r_0k^{2n}\mathbf e_z,\\
\widetilde q_n&=-\frac{qa}{r_0}k^n,&\widetilde{\mathbf r}_n&=\frac{a^2}{r_0}k^{2n}\mathbf e_z,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The term $(q_0,\mathbf r_0)$ is the physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge); every other term is an [image charge](../electric-field.md#image-charge). In the first family $n\leq-1$ lies inside $r=a$ and $n\geq1$ lies outside $r=b$. In the second family $n\leq0$ lies inside $r=a$ and $n\geq1$ lies outside $r=b$. Thus no [image charge](../electric-field.md#image-charge) lies in the physical gap.

To derive and verify these families, reflection of an axial [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) $(Q,z)$ in a grounded sphere of radius $R$ replaces it by the [image charge](../electric-field.md#image-charge) $(-RQ/z,R^2/z)$. Reflection in $a$ sends $(q_n,\mathbf r_n)$ to $(\widetilde q_{-n},\widetilde{\mathbf r}_{-n})$; reflection in $b$ sends it to $(\widetilde q_{1-n},\widetilde{\mathbf r}_{1-n})$. Successive reflections therefore generate exactly the listed families. The [method of images](../mathematics.md#method-of-images) gives

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r)=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
\left(\frac{qk^n}{|\mathbf r-r_0k^{2n}\mathbf e_z|}
-\frac{(qa/r_0)k^n}{|\mathbf r-(a^2/r_0)k^{2n}\mathbf e_z|}\right),\qquad a<r<b.}
$$

The physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) itself is excluded when evaluating this formula. On $r=a$, pair the first-family term $n$ with the second-family term $-n$; their [electric potentials](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) cancel by the single-sphere distance identity. On $r=b$, pair $n$ with $1-n$ instead. These reindexings are justified by [absolute convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#absolute-convergence), as follows.

For $n\to-\infty$, both source positions tend to the origin while their [electric charges](../electric-charge.md) have size $O(k^n)$. On the closed gap their distances are eventually at least $a/2$, so both [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) terms are $O(k^n)$. For $n\to+\infty$, both distances are at least a positive constant times $k^{2n}$ and the [electric charges](../electric-charge.md) are $O(k^n)$, giving $O(k^{-n})$ terms. Both tails are bounded by convergent [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series). This proves [absolute convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#absolute-convergence) and [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence) of the tails on the closed gap. On any compact set avoiding the physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge), the differentiated tails obey the same type of bounds: for example, the large positive tail of the [electric field](../electric-field.md) is $O(k^{-3n})$. Thus termwise differentiation is valid and the sum is a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) except at the physical [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge), where it has the correct [Coulomb's law](../electrostatics.md#coulomb-s-law) singularity. The boundary cancellations and [uniqueness of Poisson equation](../analysis.md#uniqueness-of-poisson-equation) complete the construction.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Choose a spherical [Gaussian surface](../maxwell-equations.md#gaussian-surface) of radius $s$ with $a<s<r_0$. Its physical enclosed [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) is the total induced [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) $Q_a$ on the inner sphere. The [method of images](../mathematics.md#method-of-images) produces the same [electric field](../electric-field.md) on that [Gaussian surface](../maxwell-equations.md#gaussian-surface), so [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) identifies $Q_a$ with the sum of the enclosed [image charges](../electric-field.md#image-charge). These are the first family with $n\leq-1$ and the second family with $n\leq0$:

$$
Q_a=q\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}k^n-\frac{qa}{r_0}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}k^n
=q\frac{k^{-1}-a/r_0}{1-k^{-1}}.
$$

Summing these [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series) gives

$$
\boxed{Q_a=-q\frac{a(b-r_0)}{r_0(b-a)}.}
$$

The [electric field](../electric-field.md) in the empty central cavity vanishes by [electrostatic shielding](../electrostatics.md#electrostatic-shielding), so it contributes no additional inner-face [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density). The formula tends to $-q$ as $r_0\downarrow a$ and to zero as $r_0\uparrow b$, as expected when the [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) approaches one grounded boundary or the other.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Let $r_0=|\mathbf r_0|$ and let $\Omega_0=(\theta_0,\varphi_0)$ describe its direction in [spherical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system); write $\Omega=(\theta,\varphi)$. The given [Dirichlet Green function between concentric spheres](../green-s-function.md#dirichlet-green-function-between-concentric-spheres) has normalization $\nabla^2G=-4\pi\delta^{(3)}$, so the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) is $\phi=qG/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$. On the inner sphere the [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal) from metal into the gap is $+\mathbf e_r$, hence its induced [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density) is

$$
\sigma(a,\Omega)=-\frac q{4\pi}\left.\partial_rG(r,\Omega;\mathbf r_0)\right|_{a^+}.
$$

Near this boundary $r_<=r$ and $r_>=r_0$. The needed radial [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is

$$
\left.\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^\ell-\frac{a^{2\ell+1}}{r^{\ell+1}}\right)\right|_{r=a}
=\ell a^{\ell-1}+(\ell+1)a^{\ell-1}=(2\ell+1)a^{\ell-1}.
$$

It cancels the factor $2\ell+1$ in the [Dirichlet Green function](../green-s-function.md#dirichlet-green-function). Therefore the answer in [spherical harmonics](../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(a,\Omega)=-q\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
\frac{a^{\ell-1}\overline{Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)}Y_{\ell m}(\Omega_0)}{1-(a/b)^{2\ell+1}}
\left(\frac1{r_0^{\ell+1}}-\frac{r_0^\ell}{b^{2\ell+1}}\right).}
$$

This [spherical harmonic](../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) expansion converges on the boundary: after the angular sum, the two radial factors decay geometrically with ratios $a/r_0<1$ and $ar_0/b^2<1$, while the angular factors grow at most polynomially. This justifies the boundary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) and the subsequent [surface integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral).

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Write the constant [spherical harmonic](../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) $Y_{00}=C$. Its [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) gives $4\pi|C|^2=1$, independently of its phase convention. [Orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) to $Y_{00}$ gives

$$
\int_{S^2}\overline{Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)}\,d\Omega=0\quad(\ell>0),\qquad
\int_{S^2}\overline{Y_{00}(\Omega)}Y_{00}(\Omega_0)\,d\Omega=1.
$$

Only the $\ell=m=0$ term contributes to the [surface integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral) of the [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density). Multiplying by the surface-area factor $a^2$ gives

$$
Q_a=-qa^2\frac{a^{-1}}{1-a/b}\left(\frac1{r_0}-\frac1b\right).
$$

Thus the [spherical harmonic](../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) calculation reproduces the total induced [electric charge](../electric-charge.md):

$$
\boxed{Q_a=-q\frac{a(b-r_0)}{r_0(b-a)}.}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Translate the concentric spheres so that their centre is $(0,0,-a)$, take $b=a+d$, and keep the [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) fixed at $(0,0,h)$. Its distance from the centre is $r_0=a+h$. As $a\to\infty$, the upper portions of the inner and outer spheres approach the planes $z=0$ and $z=d$. The preceding total induced [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) becomes

$$
Q_a=-q\frac{a(d-h)}{(a+h)d}\longrightarrow-\frac{q(d-h)}d.
$$

The outer sphere has total induced [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) $Q_b=-q-Q_a$: a [Gaussian surface](../maxwell-equations.md#gaussian-surface) within its conducting material has zero [electric flux](../electric-field.md#electric-flux), and the grounded exterior is field-free by [uniqueness of Poisson equation](../analysis.md#uniqueness-of-poisson-equation). Therefore $Q_b\to-qh/d$.

One can justify the total-charge limit without assuming that local convergence of [electric fields](../electric-field.md) preserves their [surface integrals](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral). Use [induced charge as harmonic measure](../electric-field.md#induced-charge-as-harmonic-measure): if a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) $w$ is one on the selected grounded boundary and zero on the other, [Green's second identity](../partial-differential-equation.md#green-second-identity) with the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) gives

$$
Q_{\rm selected}=-q\,w(\mathbf r_0).
$$

Indeed, $\nabla^2\phi=-q\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0)/\epsilon_0$, $\phi=0$ on the boundaries, and their [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal) outward from the gap satisfies $\sigma=\epsilon_0\partial_n\phi$. For the spheres the weighting [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) is $w_a(r)=a(b-r)/(r(b-a))$, exactly the factor already obtained. For the slab the two weighting [harmonic functions](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) are $w_0=1-z/d$ and $w_d=z/d$. Applying the same identity to the decaying grounded-slab [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential), whose boundary term at lateral infinity vanishes, establishes directly

$$
\boxed{Q_0=-\frac{q(d-h)}d,\qquad Q_d=-\frac{qh}d.}
$$

Their sum is $-q$, and their relative sizes measure how close the [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) lies to each grounded plane.

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

Use the [constitutive relations](../maxwell-equations.md#constitutive-relations) $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf H=\mathbf B/\mu$, where $\mathbf D$ is the [electric displacement field](../electromagnetism.md#electric-displacement-field), $\mathbf H$ the [magnetic field strength](../magnetic-field.md#magnetic-field-strength), $\epsilon$ the [permittivity](../permittivity.md), and $\mu$ the [magnetic permeability](../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism). Let $\rho_f$ be the [charge density](../electric-charge.md#charge-density) of [free charge](../electric-charge.md#free-charge) and $\mathbf J_f$ its [current density](../electric-charge.md#current-density). In this homogeneous medium, [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) and the [magnetostatic Ampère law](../maxwell-equations.md#magnetostatic-ampere-law) are, respectively,

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho_f,\qquad
\oint_{\partial W}\mathbf D\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=Q_f(W)=\int_W\rho_f\,d^3r,}
$$



$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f,\qquad
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=I_f(S)=\int_S\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf n\,dS.}
$$

Here $W$ is a volume with closed boundary $\partial W$, and $S$ is an oriented spanning surface whose boundary orientation follows the right-hand rule. Thus the [electric field](../electric-field.md) form is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho_f/\epsilon$ and $\oint_{\partial W}\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=Q_f/\epsilon$; the [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) form is $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf J_f$ and $\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf B\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=\mu I_f$.

The remaining static [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) are

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\oint_{\partial W}\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=0,
\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf E=0,\qquad\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=0.
$$

These express [Gauss's law for magnetism](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism) and the static limit of [Faraday's law](../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction). The volume and circulation forms follow from the [divergence theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence-theorem) and [Stokes theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#stokes-theorem). The [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) and [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current) on the right of the macroscopic laws are free sources; material [electric polarization](../electromagnetism.md#polarization-density) and [magnetization](../electromagnetism.md#magnetization) are already included in $\mathbf D$ and $\mathbf H$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Allow time dependence while keeping the [constitutive relations](../maxwell-equations.md#constitutive-relations) linear, isotropic, nondispersive, and independent of time. The [Maxwell equations in matter](../maxwell-equations.md#maxwell-equations-in-matter) give

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B,\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f+\partial_t\mathbf D.
$$

For the [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector) $\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H$, the [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) identity yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}
&=\mathbf H\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf E)-\mathbf E\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf H)\\
&=-\frac1\mu\mathbf B\cdot\partial_t\mathbf B
-\epsilon\mathbf E\cdot\partial_t\mathbf E-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf J_f.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first two terms are exact time [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative). Taking the zero-field [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) as zero gives the [electromagnetic energy density](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-energy-density)

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E=\frac12\epsilon|\mathbf E|^2+\frac1{2\mu}|\mathbf B|^2
=\frac12(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D+\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H).}
$$

Consequently the [Poynting theorem](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-theorem) is the [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation)

$$
\boxed{\partial_t\mathcal E+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}=-\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E.}
$$

In integral form,

$$
\frac d{dt}\int_W\mathcal E\,d^3r+
\oint_{\partial W}\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf n\,dS
=-\int_W\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E\,d^3r.
$$

The left side is the rate of increase of stored [electromagnetic energy](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-energy) plus outward [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux). The right side accounts for work delivered to the free [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current); in an Ohmic medium $\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf E$ is the local rate of [Joule heating](../electric-charge.md#joule-heating).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Work in [cylindrical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#cylindrical-coordinate-system) and use the assumed independence of $\varphi$ and $z$. In each material region, the zero [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) of the static [electric field](../electric-field.md) gives

$$
\frac{dE_z}{d\rho}=0,\qquad
\frac{d(\rho E_\varphi)}{d\rho}=0.
$$

The tangential [electric field](../electric-field.md) is zero at a [perfect conductor](../electromagnetic-wave.md#perfect-conductor). The [dielectric-interface boundary conditions](../electromagnetic-wave.md#dielectric-interface-boundary-conditions) make its tangential components continuous at each material interface, so $E_z=E_\varphi=0$ throughout the gap. They also vanish in the empty interior and exterior for the regular solution with no applied external [electric field](../electric-field.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=E_\rho(\rho)\mathbf e_\rho.}
$$

This specifies an axis of direction: the sign of $E_\rho$ depends on the sign of $V$.

For the static [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md), [Gauss's law for magnetism](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism) implies

$$
\frac1\rho\frac{d(\rho B_\rho)}{d\rho}=0.
$$

Thus $\rho B_\rho$ is constant in each layer. Continuity of the normal [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) across every interface makes that constant global, and regularity at the axis makes it zero. Next use $\nabla\times\mathbf H=0$ away from the conducting surfaces. Its $\varphi$ component gives $dH_z/d\rho=0$. There is no azimuthal [surface current density](../electric-charge.md#surface-current-density), so $H_z$ is continuous across the conducting surfaces and the material interface. The decay of the exterior [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) at infinity therefore forces this global constant to zero. The only remaining component is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=B_\varphi(\rho)\mathbf e_\varphi.}
$$

The [magnetostatic Ampère law](../maxwell-equations.md#magnetostatic-ampere-law) fixes its sign: positive inner [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current) in the $+z$ direction produces positive $B_\varphi$ for positive [magnetic permeability](../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

For this [radially layered coaxial cable](../electrical-engineering.md#radially-layered-coaxial-cable), define

$$
L_1=\ln(c/a),\qquad L_2=\ln(b/c),\qquad
\Delta=\epsilon'L_1+\epsilon L_2,\qquad
C=\frac{V\epsilon\epsilon'}{\Delta}.
$$

Use the solution regular at the axis with bounded exterior [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential), so that the empty interior and exterior have the constant [electric potentials](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) fixed by their adjacent conducting cylinders.

There is no [free charge](../electric-charge.md#free-charge) in either [electrical insulator](../permittivity.md#electrical-insulator) or on their interface. [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) and continuity of normal [electric displacement field](../electromagnetism.md#electric-displacement-field) give $D_\rho=C/\rho$ in both layers. The [electric potential difference](../electric-field.md#electric-potential-difference) fixes the constant because

$$
V=\int_a^bE_\rho\,d\rho=C\left(\frac{L_1}{\epsilon}+\frac{L_2}{\epsilon'}\right).
$$

The [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential), including the empty regions bounded by the conducting cylinders, is therefore

$$
\boxed{\phi(\rho)=\begin{cases}
V,&0\leq\rho\leq a,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac V\Delta\left[\epsilon'\ln(c/\rho)+\epsilon\ln(b/c)\right],&a<\rho<c,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{V\epsilon}{\Delta}\ln(b/\rho),&c\leq\rho<b,\\[2pt]
0,&\rho\geq b.
\end{cases}}
$$

Both inner and outer [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) hold, and the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) is continuous at the material interface, where

$$
\boxed{\phi(c)=\frac{V\epsilon\ln(b/c)}{\epsilon'\ln(c/a)+\epsilon\ln(b/c)}.}
$$

Taking the negative [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) of the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(\rho)=\begin{cases}
0,&\rho<a\ \text{or}\ \rho>b,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{V\epsilon'}{\Delta\rho}\mathbf e_\rho,&a<\rho<c,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{V\epsilon}{\Delta\rho}\mathbf e_\rho,&c<\rho<b.
\end{cases}}
$$

The radial [electric field](../electric-field.md) generally jumps at $\rho=c$, but $\epsilon E_\rho(c^-)=\epsilon'E_\rho(c^+)$, as required by the absence of free [surface charge density](../electrostatics.md#surface-charge-density) there. The conducting cylinders carry opposite [linear charge densities](../electric-charge.md#linear-charge-density) $\lambda=2\pi C$ and $-\lambda$.

For an [Amperian loop](../maxwell-equations.md#amperian-loop) of radius $\rho$, the enclosed [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current) is zero, $I$, or zero according as $\rho<a$, $a<\rho<b$, or $\rho>b$. The [magnetostatic Ampère law](../maxwell-equations.md#magnetostatic-ampere-law) therefore gives $H_\varphi=I/(2\pi\rho)$ in the gap, and zero outside. Using the two [magnetic permeabilities](../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B(\rho)=\begin{cases}
0,&\rho<a\ \text{or}\ \rho>b,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\mu I}{2\pi\rho}\mathbf e_\varphi,&a<\rho<c,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\mu'I}{2\pi\rho}\mathbf e_\varphi,&c<\rho<b.
\end{cases}}
$$

The tangential [magnetic field strength](../magnetic-field.md#magnetic-field-strength) is continuous at the material interface, while the tangential [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) need not be. At each ideal conducting surface and at the material interface, the displayed [electric field](../electric-field.md) and [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) formulas specify one-sided limits; a single interface value is unnecessary when the limiting values differ.

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

Since $\mathbf e_\rho\times\mathbf e_\varphi=\mathbf e_z$, the [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector) is axial. The [magnetic permeability](../maxwell-equations.md#permeability-electromagnetism) cancels from $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/\mu$ in each material layer:

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}(\rho)=\begin{cases}
0,&\rho<a\ \text{or}\ \rho>b,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{VI\epsilon'}{2\pi\Delta\rho^2}\mathbf e_z,&a<\rho<c,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\frac{VI\epsilon}{2\pi\Delta\rho^2}\mathbf e_z,&c<\rho<b.
\end{cases}}
$$

The signed [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux) through the plane with [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal) $+\mathbf e_z$ is its [surface integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral) with area element $\rho\,d\rho\,d\varphi$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=2\pi\int_a^b\mathcal P_z(\rho)\rho\,d\rho\\
&=\frac{VI}{\Delta}\left[\epsilon'\ln(c/a)+\epsilon\ln(b/c)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{P=VI.}
$$

The transmitted [electromagnetic energy](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-energy) per unit time is the circuit's terminal power. For $VI>0$, the [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux) points in the direction of the inner [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current). The [electromagnetic energy](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-energy) travels through the insulating gap and reaches a load at the end of the [coaxial cable](../electrical-engineering.md#coaxial-cable). The radii and material constants redistribute the [electromagnetic energy density](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electromagnetic-energy-density) and the local [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector), while their integrated [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux) depends only on the terminal [electric potential difference](../electric-field.md#electric-potential-difference) and [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Orient the [unit normal](../differential-geometry.md#unit-normal) $\mathbf n$ from $R_1$ to $R_2$ and write $\mathbf H$ for the [magnetic field strength](../magnetic-field.md#magnetic-field-strength). For static [electric fields](../electric-field.md), $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$; the static [Ampère's law](../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law) gives $\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f$. The [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) product rule therefore gives the local [Poynting flux and terminal power](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux-and-terminal-power) identity

$$
\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}=-\nabla\phi\times\mathbf H
=\phi\mathbf J_f-\nabla\times(\phi\mathbf H).
$$

Integrate over an oriented piece $\Sigma_R$ of the separating surface and apply [Stokes theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#stokes-theorem):

$$
\int_{\Sigma_R}\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf n\,dS
=\int_{\Sigma_R}\phi\mathbf J_f\cdot\mathbf n\,dS
-\oint_{\partial\Sigma_R}\phi\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell.
$$

Once all the wires are included, the first [surface integral](../multivariable-calculus.md#surface-integral) is $\sum_k\phi_kI_k$: the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) is constant on each [perfect conductor](../electromagnetic-wave.md#perfect-conductor) and the integral of its normal [current density](../electric-charge.md#current-density) is its signed [electric current](../electric-charge.md#electric-current). Equivalently, remove the wire cross-sections and apply [Stokes theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#stokes-theorem) in the surrounding insulator. Each resulting hole has negative boundary orientation, and the [magnetostatic Ampère law](../maxwell-equations.md#magnetostatic-ampere-law) around it gives the positive contribution $\phi_kI_k$.

For a closed separating surface the boundary term is absent. For a noncompact separating surface, take $R\to\infty$ with the usual isolated-circuit condition $\oint_{\partial\Sigma_R}\phi\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell\to0$. In either case,

$$
\boxed{\int_\Sigma\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\sum_{k=1}^N\phi_kI_k.}
$$

For a steady closed circuit the signed [electric currents](../electric-charge.md#electric-current) sum to zero, so shifting the reference of [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) does not change the terminal power.

For a fully general noncompact surface, the hypotheses as printed need an additional condition at infinity. The exact result before imposing it is

$$
\boxed{P_{1\to2}=\sum_{k=1}^N\phi_kI_k-
\lim_{R\to\infty}\oint_{\partial\Sigma_R}\phi\mathbf H\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell.}
$$

Time independence alone does not remove this boundary term or ensure a finite [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux); for example, spatially constant crossed [electric fields](../electric-field.md) and [magnetic fields](../magnetic-field.md) carry a constant [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector) through an infinite plane. The stated terminal-power formula holds with the boundary condition specified above.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

With [charge density](../electric-charge.md#charge-density) $\rho$, [current density](../electric-charge.md#current-density) $\mathbf J$, and [speed of light](../special-relativity.md#speed-of-light) $c=(\epsilon_0\mu_0)^{-1/2}$, the four [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) in [vacuum](../physics.md#vacuum) are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E&=\rho/\epsilon_0,&\nabla\cdot\mathbf B&=0,\\
\nabla\times\mathbf E&=-\partial_t\mathbf B,&
\nabla\times\mathbf B&=\mu_0\mathbf J+c^{-2}\partial_t\mathbf E.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) and [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential) determine the [electric field](../electric-field.md) and [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md) by

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\mathbf A,\qquad
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.}
$$

These definitions satisfy [Gauss's law for magnetism](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law-for-magnetism) and [Faraday's law](../maxwell-equations.md#faraday-s-law-of-induction) identically. The [Lorenz gauge condition](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition) is

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf A+c^{-2}\partial_t\phi=0.}
$$

Substituting into [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) and using the [Lorenz gauge condition](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition) gives

$$
\frac\rho{\epsilon_0}=-\nabla^2\phi-\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)
=-\nabla^2\phi+c^{-2}\partial_t^2\phi.
$$

For the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law), the double-[curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) identity gives

$$
\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\nabla^2\mathbf A
=\mu_0\mathbf J-c^{-2}\nabla\partial_t\phi-c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf A.
$$

The two [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) terms cancel by the [Lorenz gauge condition](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition). Defining the [d'Alembert operator](../wave-equation.md#d-alembert-operator) with the sign convention $\square=\nabla^2-c^{-2}\partial_t^2$, the remaining equations are

$$
\boxed{\square\phi=-\rho/\epsilon_0,\qquad
\square\mathbf A=-\mu_0\mathbf J.}
$$

Conversely, if the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) and [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential) satisfy these [wave equations](../wave-equation.md) and the [Lorenz gauge condition](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition), the same substitutions in reverse give [Gauss's law](../maxwell-equations.md#gauss-s-law) and the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../maxwell-equations.md#ampere-s-circuital-law). The other two [Maxwell equations](../maxwell-equations.md) follow identically from the definitions, proving equivalence within the [Lorenz gauge](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

The moving [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) has distributional [charge density](../electric-charge.md#charge-density) and [current density](../electric-charge.md#current-density)

$$
\rho(\mathbf r',t')=q\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r'-\mathbf r_0(t')),\qquad
\mathbf J(\mathbf r',t')=q\mathbf v_0(t')\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r'-\mathbf r_0(t')).
$$

Let

$$
K(\mathbf r,t;\mathbf r',t')=
\frac{\delta(t-t'-|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|/c)}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}.
$$

The given [retarded fundamental solution](../distribution-theory.md#retarded-fundamental-solution) identity immediately implies that the [retarded potentials](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-potential)

$$
\phi=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int K\rho\,d^3r'\,dt',\qquad
\mathbf A=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int K\mathbf J\,d^3r'\,dt'
$$

solve $\square\phi=-\rho/\epsilon_0$ and $\square\mathbf A=-\mu_0\mathbf J$ in the sense of [distributions](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis). Integrating the spatial [Dirac delta function](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) first gives

$$
\phi=\frac q{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\delta(g(t'))}{R(t')}\,dt',\qquad
g(t')=t-t'-R(t')/c,
$$

and the analogous integral for $\mathbf A$ has numerator $\mu_0q\mathbf v_0(t')/(4\pi)$.

Assume the physical trajectory has [subluminal speed](../special-relativity.md#subluminal-speed) and the [retarded time](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-time) exists. At its root $t_r$, with $\mathbf n_r=\mathbf R(t_r)/R(t_r)$,

$$
\frac{dR}{dt'}=-\frac{\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf v_0}{R},\qquad
g'(t_r)=-\left(1-\frac{\mathbf n_r\cdot\mathbf v_0(t_r)}c\right).
$$

The positive factor $\kappa=1-\mathbf n_r\cdot\mathbf v_0/c$ also proves [uniqueness of retarded time for a subluminal source](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#uniqueness-of-retarded-time-for-a-subluminal-source). The [Dirac delta function](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) substitution formula gives $\delta(g(t'))=\delta(t'-t_r)/\kappa$, so

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r,t)=\frac q{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\frac1{R(t_r)-\mathbf R(t_r)\cdot\mathbf v_0(t_r)/c},\qquad
\mathbf A(\mathbf r,t)=\frac{\mathbf v_0(t_r)}{c^2}\phi(\mathbf r,t).}
$$

These are the [Liénard–Wiechert potentials](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lienard-wiechert-potential). The denominator includes the time-substitution factor, rather than just the retarded distance. Away from the trajectory the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) and [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential) are ordinary smooth functions; on the trajectory their [wave equations](../wave-equation.md) have the indicated distributional sources.

For completeness, the [charge continuity equation](../maxwell-equations.md#charge-continuity-equation) $\partial_{t'}\rho+\nabla'\cdot\mathbf J=0$ also verifies the [Lorenz gauge](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition). Using $\nabla K=-\nabla'K$ and $\partial_tK=-\partial_{t'}K$, [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) in the retarded integrals gives

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf A+c^{-2}\partial_t\phi
=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int K(\nabla'\cdot\mathbf J+\partial_{t'}\rho)\,d^3r'\,dt'=0,
$$

with the retarded boundary prescription. Thus the construction also has the required [Lorenz gauge condition](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lorenz-gauge-condition).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Take the ideal [electric dipole](../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole) limit with a fixed smooth [electric dipole moment](../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole-moment) $\mathbf p(t)$, so that $\mathbf r_0(t)=\mathbf p(t)/(2q)$; its required time [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) are bounded on the time interval under consideration. Fix $r>0$ and put $\mathbf n=\mathbf r/r$, $t_0=t-r/c$. For $s=+1$ or $-1$, the source position is $s\mathbf p(t)/(2q)$. A [Taylor expansion](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion) of the distance gives

$$
R_s(t_r^s)=\left|\mathbf r-\frac{s\mathbf p(t_r^s)}{2q}\right|
=r-\frac{s\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p(t_r^s)}{2q}+O(q^{-2}).
$$

Substitute this into the defining equation for [retarded time](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-time):

$$
t_r^s=t_0+\frac{s\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p(t_r^s)}{2qc}+O(q^{-2}).
$$

Boundedness first implies $t_r^s-t_0=O(q^{-1})$. The [Taylor expansion](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion) $\mathbf p(t_r^s)=\mathbf p(t_0)+O(q^{-1})$ then changes the displayed correction only by $O(q^{-2})$. Hence

$$
\boxed{t_r^+=t_0+\frac{\mathbf p(t_0)\cdot\mathbf r}{2qrc}+O(q^{-2}),\qquad
t_r^-=t_0-\frac{\mathbf p(t_0)\cdot\mathbf r}{2qrc}+O(q^{-2}).}
$$

The nearer positive displacement reduces the propagation distance and consequently increases the [retarded time](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-time). The [Big O notation](../real-analysis.md#big-o-notation) here is for $q\to\infty$ at a fixed observation event away from the origin. Keeping a fixed smooth $\mathbf p$ is essential: bounded amplitude alone, with arbitrarily increasing time frequency as $q$ varies, would not justify this [Taylor expansion](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion).

<h4 id="3/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/c/ii)

Use the sign $s=\pm1$ to label a [point charge](../electric-field.md#point-charge) $sq$ at $s\mathbf p/(2q)$, with [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $s\mathbf p'/(2q)$. All quantities on the right below are evaluated at $t_0=t-r/c$. To first order,

$$
\mathbf R_s=\mathbf r-\frac{s\mathbf p}{2q}+O(q^{-2}),\qquad
R_s=r-\frac{s\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p}{2q}+O(q^{-2}),\qquad
\mathbf v_s=\frac{s\mathbf p'}{2q}+O(q^{-2}).
$$

The denominator in each [Liénard–Wiechert potential](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#lienard-wiechert-potential) therefore expands as

$$
D_s=R_s-\mathbf R_s\cdot\mathbf v_s/c
=r-\frac{s}{2q}\left(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p+\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p'}c\right)+O(q^{-2}),
$$

so

$$
\frac1{D_s}=\frac1r+\frac{s}{2qr^2}
\left(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p+\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p'}c\right)+O(q^{-2}).
$$

In the sum of the two [electric potentials](../electric-field.md#electric-potential), the $q/r$ terms cancel and the finite terms add. This gives the [retarded potentials of a point electric dipole](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-potentials-of-a-point-electric-dipole):

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r,t)=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\left(\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p(t_0)}{r^3}
+\frac{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p'(t_0)}{cr^2}\right),\qquad r>0.}
$$

For the [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential), the signed [electric charge](../electric-charge.md) and signed [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) multiply to give the same sign for both contributions:

$$
\mathbf A=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0c^2}\sum_{s=\pm1}\frac{sq\,\mathbf v_s}{D_s}
\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbf p'}{4\pi\epsilon_0c^2r}.
$$

Since $\mu_0\epsilon_0c^2=1$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf A(\mathbf r,t)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r}\mathbf p'(t_0).}
$$

The $\mathbf p'$ term in the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) arises from the moving-source denominator and remains finite in the point-[electric dipole](../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole) limit; discarding it would fail to give the full retarded [electric field](../electric-field.md).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

<h5 id="3/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/d/i)

Set $K=1/(4\pi\epsilon_0)$, $t_0=t-r/c$ and $\mathbf n=\mathbf r/r$, and evaluate $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf p'$, $\mathbf p''$ at $t_0$. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives

$$
\nabla t_0=-\frac{\mathbf n}c,\qquad
\partial_i p_j=-\frac{n_i}c p'_j,\qquad
\partial_i n_j=\frac{\delta_{ij}-n_in_j}r.
$$

In particular, for $\mathbf u(t_0)$ and any positive integer $m$,

$$
\nabla\left(\frac{\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u}{r^m}\right)
=\frac{\mathbf u-(m+1)\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u)}{r^{m+1}}
-\frac{\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u')}{cr^m}.
$$

Apply this [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) identity to the [electric potential](../electric-field.md#electric-potential) $\phi=K[(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p)/r^2+(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p')/(cr)]$:

$$
\nabla\phi=K\left[
\frac{\mathbf p-3\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p)}{r^3}
+\frac{\mathbf p'-3\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p')}{cr^2}
-\frac{\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'')}{c^2r}
\right].
$$

The [magnetic vector potential](../electromagnetism.md#magnetic-vector-potential) is $\mathbf A=K\mathbf p'/(c^2r)$, with time [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) $\partial_t\mathbf A=K\mathbf p''/(c^2r)$. Thus the [electric field](../electric-field.md) $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\mathbf A$ is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=\frac1{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[
\frac{3\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p)-\mathbf p}{r^3}
+\frac{3\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p')-\mathbf p'}{cr^2}
+\frac{\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'')-\mathbf p''}{c^2r}
\right].}
$$

For the [magnetic field](../magnetic-field.md), use the [curl](../multivariable-calculus.md#curl) product rule and the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\times\left(\frac{\mathbf p'}r\right)
&=\nabla(r^{-1})\times\mathbf p'+r^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf p'\\
&=-\frac{\mathbf n\times\mathbf p'}{r^2}
-\frac{\mathbf n\times\mathbf p''}{cr}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently the [fields of a time-dependent electric dipole](../electromagnetic-wave.md#fields-of-a-time-dependent-electric-dipole) include

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\left(\frac{\mathbf p'}{r^2}
+\frac{\mathbf p''}{cr}\right)\times\mathbf n.}
$$

These formulas hold for $r>0$. The $r^{-3}$ [electric field](../electric-field.md) is the retarded [electric dipole field](../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole-field), the $r^{-2}$ terms are induction contributions, and the $r^{-1}$ terms describe [electric dipole radiation](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electric-dipole-radiation). Retaining the dependence of $t_0$ on position in every spatial [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is what produces both the induction and radiation contributions.

<h4 id="3/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

<h5 id="3/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/d/ii)

The leading [electric dipole radiation](../electromagnetic-wave.md#electric-dipole-radiation) fields are

$$
\mathbf E_{\rm rad}=\frac{\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'')-\mathbf p''}{4\pi\epsilon_0c^2r},\qquad
\mathbf B_{\rm rad}=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi cr}\mathbf p''\times\mathbf n
=\frac1c\mathbf n\times\mathbf E_{\rm rad}.
$$

The radiation [electric field](../electric-field.md) is transverse, $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf E_{\rm rad}=0$. The [vector triple product](../multivariable-calculus.md#vector-triple-product) therefore gives the leading [Poynting vector](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-vector)

$$
\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}=\frac1{\mu_0c}\mathbf E_{\rm rad}\times(\mathbf n\times\mathbf E_{\rm rad})+O(r^{-3})
=\frac{|\mathbf E_{\rm rad}|^2}{\mu_0c}\mathbf n+O(r^{-3}).
$$

If $\theta$ is the angle between $\mathbf p''(t-r/c)$ and $\mathbf n$, then

$$
|\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'')-\mathbf p''|^2
=|\mathbf p''|^2-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p'')^2
=|\mathbf p''|^2\sin^2\theta.
$$

Using $\mu_0\epsilon_0c^2=1$, the final result is

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}(\mathbf r,t)=
\frac{|\mathbf p''(t-r/c)|^2\sin^2\theta}{16\pi^2\epsilon_0c^3r^2}\mathbf n+O(r^{-3}),\qquad
|\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}|=\frac{|\mathbf p''(t-r/c)|^2\sin^2\theta}{16\pi^2\epsilon_0c^3r^2}+O(r^{-3}).}
$$

The leading [Poynting flux](../electromagnetic-wave.md#poynting-flux) is outward, vanishes along the instantaneous retarded $\mathbf p''$ axis, and is maximal in its perpendicular plane. If $\mathbf p''=0$, the radiation term is zero and no angle needs to be assigned. The large-$r$ expansion assumes bounded retarded [electric dipole moment](../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole-moment) and time [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative); it may also be read at fixed [retarded time](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#retarded-time).
