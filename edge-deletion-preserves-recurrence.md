# Edge deletion preserves recurrence

↑ **Parent:** [Recurrent graph](recurrent-graph.md)

Deleting an [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md) of a locally finite [recurrent graph](recurrent-graph.md) leaves [recurrent Markov chain](recurrent-markov-chain.md) connected components. On an infinite component choose a root away from the deleted [edge](edge-of-a-graph.md)'s endpoints, so its [vertex degree](degree-graph-theory.md) does not change. The [Rayleigh monotonicity principle](rayleigh-monotonicity-principle.md) increases resistance to wired outer boundaries, and [effective resistance to infinity](effective-resistance-to-infinity.md) therefore remains infinite. Finite components are [recurrent Markov chain](recurrent-markov-chain.md) too. This is a recurrence-type comparison; at an endpoint, changes in the [vertex degree](degree-graph-theory.md) factor prevent a blanket monotonicity claim about first-return [probabilities](probability.md).
