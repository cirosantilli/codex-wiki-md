# Continuity of fixed-choice percolation

↑ **Parent:** [Achlioptas process](achlioptas-process.md)

A fixed-choice [Achlioptas process](achlioptas-process.md) cannot have its [largest component of a graph](largest-component-of-a-graph.md) jump from $o(n)$ to a fixed positive fraction of $n$ within $o(n)$ steps, [with high probability](with-high-probability.md). The proof combines [forced merging of large components](forced-merging-of-large-components.md) and [persistence of unsampled graph components](persistence-of-unsampled-graph-components.md). An assumed jump forces positive vertex mass in each band $[k,Dk)$ slightly before the jump: insufficient smaller components can feed the final large component, while enough components above $Dk$ would merge too early. Persistence carries a fixed positive amount of each band to a common time. Taking sufficiently many disjoint bands with $k=1,D,D^2,\ldots$ then counts more than $n$ [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md). The number of offered candidates is fixed throughout this argument. Riordan and Warnke establish this obstruction and broader continuity results in [their original research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.5306).
