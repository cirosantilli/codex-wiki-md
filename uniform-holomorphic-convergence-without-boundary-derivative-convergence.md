# Uniform holomorphic convergence without boundary derivative convergence

↑ **Parent:** [Locally uniform convergence of holomorphic functions](locally-uniform-convergence-of-holomorphic-functions.md)

The [entire functions](entire-function.md) $g_n(z)=z^n/n$ converge uniformly to zero on the closed unit disc, because their supremum norm is $1/n$. Their derivatives $g_n'(z)=z^{n-1}$ instead have supremum norm one there, and equal one at $z=1$. On each smaller disc of radius $0<r<1$, the derivative supremum is $r^{n-1}\to0$. Thus the [Weierstrass convergence theorem](locally-uniform-convergence-of-holomorphic-functions.md) controls derivatives on compact subsets of an open domain, not automatically on the boundary of a closed set on which function convergence is known.
