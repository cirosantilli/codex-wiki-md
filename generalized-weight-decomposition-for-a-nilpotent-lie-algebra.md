# Generalized-weight decomposition for a nilpotent Lie algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Generalized weight space of a Lie algebra representation](generalized-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation.md)

Every finite-dimensional complex [Lie algebra representation](lie-algebra-representation.md) of a [nilpotent Lie algebra](nilpotent-lie-algebra.md) decomposes into invariant [generalized weight spaces](generalized-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation.md). The nilpotence assumption is essential: a general solvable action can have extensions between distinct weights.

Here is a proof. For $h,k\in H$, nilpotence gives $(\operatorname{ad}h)^N k=0$, hence $(\operatorname{ad}\rho(h))^N\rho(k)=0$. By the [nilpotent commutator preserves generalized eigenspaces](nilpotent-commutator-preserves-generalized-eigenspaces.md) lemma, every [generalized eigenspace](generalized-eigenspace.md) of $\rho(h)$ is invariant under every $\rho(k)$. Split successively by the acting operators of a [basis](basis.md) of $H$. Each resulting block is invariant under $H$, and each basis operator has only one [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) on it. The [Lie theorem](lie-s-theorem.md) triangularizes the action on each block; its diagonal entries therefore give one and the same [linear functional](linear-functional.md) $\lambda$ on every basis vector of that block. Every $\rho(h)-\lambda(h)I$ is strictly upper triangular there, proving that the block is $V^\lambda$. Distinct blocks have distinct weights, so this is a [direct sum](direct-sum.md). On the zero generalized [weight space](weight-space.md), every acting operator is a [nilpotent endomorphism](nilpotent-linear-map.md), so the [Engel theorem](engel-s-theorem.md) supplies a nonzero common annihilated vector whenever that space is nonzero.
