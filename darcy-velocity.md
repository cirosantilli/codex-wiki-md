# Darcy velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Darcy law](darcy-law.md)

[Darcy velocity](darcy-velocity.md), also called [Darcy flux](darcy-velocity.md) or [specific discharge](darcy-velocity.md), is [fluid](fluid.md) [volume flux](volumetric-flow-rate.md) relative to the solid framework per unit total cross-sectional area. For a uniform axial discharge $Q$ through bulk area $A$,

$$
q=\frac QA.
$$

The area includes both solid and voids. Hence [Darcy velocity](darcy-velocity.md) is a superficial flux with units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, even though no [fluid](fluid.md) travels through the solid part. For a nonuniform discharge, $Q=\int_A\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n\,dA$, with $\mathbf n$ the unit normal; $Q/A$ is the area average of the normal [Darcy flux](darcy-velocity.md).

If [porosity](porosity.md) is $\epsilon$, the pore-volume-averaged liquid [velocity](velocity.md) is $\mathbf v_l$, and the local solid framework [velocity](velocity.md) is $\mathbf v_s$, the convention used here is $\mathbf u=\epsilon(\mathbf v_l-\mathbf v_s)$. In a stationary, fully saturated framework with all pore volume participating, $\mathbf v_l=\mathbf q/\epsilon$. The notation $\phi=\epsilon$ for [porosity](porosity.md) is equally common. [Darcy flow through a homogeneous slab](darcy-flow-through-a-homogeneous-slab.md) gives $q=2.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ but [pore velocity](pore-velocity.md) $v=8.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at $\phi=0.25$. Keeping these quantities separate lets [Darcy law](darcy-law.md) predict filter discharge while [pore velocity](pore-velocity.md) predicts ideal advective travel time.[https://books.gw-project.org/hydrogeologic-properties-of-earth-materials-and-principles-of-groundwater-flow/chapter/darcys-law/](https://books.gw-project.org/hydrogeologic-properties-of-earth-materials-and-principles-of-groundwater-flow/chapter/darcys-law/)
