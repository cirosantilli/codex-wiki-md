<h1 id="kepler-s-laws-of-planetary-motion">Kepler's laws of planetary motion</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Mathematical physics](mathematical-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler's_laws_of_planetary_motion)

Kepler's laws describe three connected features of an [elliptic orbit](elliptic-orbit.md): its shape, how fast it is traversed, and how its size determines its [orbital period](orbital-period.md). [Kepler's first law](kepler-s-first-law.md) gives an [ellipse](ellipse.md) with the attracting centre at one [focus of a conic](focus-of-a-conic.md); [Kepler's second law](kepler-s-second-law.md) gives equal swept areas in equal times; [Kepler's third law](kepler-s-third-law.md) relates the squared [orbital period](orbital-period.md) to the cubed [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md). Together they turn a drawing of a [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) into a prediction of where the bodies will be and when they will return. [NASA, Basics of Space Flight: Kepler's laws](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter3-3/)

Here the [mathematical model](mathematical-model.md) is an isolated [two-body problem](two-body-problem.md) in [Newtonian gravity](gravitational-acceleration.md), with [point masses](point-mass.md) $M,m>0$ and a bound, noncolliding [elliptic orbit](elliptic-orbit.md). Let $\mathbf r=\mathbf r_m-\mathbf r_M$ be the relative displacement, $r=|\mathbf r|$ the separation, and $\mu=G(M+m)$ the [gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md), where $G$ is the [gravitational constant](gravitational-constant.md). [Newton's law of universal gravitation](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md) then gives

$$
\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\frac{\mu}{r^3}\mathbf r.
$$

The relative [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) has [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md) $a$ and [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md) $0\le e<1$. Both bodies move around their shared [astronomical barycenter](barycenter-astronomy.md), the system's [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md). In its rest frame their displacements are $\mathbf r_m=M\mathbf r/(M+m)$ and $\mathbf r_M=-m\mathbf r/(M+m)$. Their individual [semi-major axes](semi-major-axis.md) therefore add to $a$; the relative [ellipse](ellipse.md)'s attracting [focus of a conic](focus-of-a-conic.md) represents zero separation, rather than a body fixed in space.

One example will connect all three laws. After [nondimensionalization](nondimensionalization.md), choose $a=1$, $e=0.6$ and $\mu=1$. The [semi-minor axis](semi-minor-axis.md) is $b=a\sqrt{1-e^2}=0.8$, while the [periapsis distance](pericentre-distance.md) and [apoapsis distance](apocentre-distance.md) are

$$
r_{\rm p}=a(1-e)=0.4,\qquad r_{\rm a}=a(1+e)=1.6.
$$

Thus $a=(0.4+1.6)/2=1$ measures the [ellipse](ellipse.md)'s size even though the separation changes fourfold. The [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) will be $0.8$, the two endpoint [speeds](speed.md) $2$ and $0.5$, and the [orbital period](orbital-period.md) $2\pi$. These are values in the chosen scaled units, not measurements of a particular [planet](planet.md).

[Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) published [Kepler's first law](kepler-s-first-law.md) and [Kepler's second law](kepler-s-second-law.md) in _Astronomia Nova_ in 1609 and [Kepler's third law](kepler-s-third-law.md) in _Harmonices Mundi_ in 1619. These publication dates are documented by [NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, Kepler's laws](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4642). Their appeal is that three short statements organize both geometry and timing; [Newtonian gravity](gravitational-acceleration.md) supplies a common dynamical explanation.
