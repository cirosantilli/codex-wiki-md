# Apparent level crossing from a finite-interval oscillator perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [First-order energy correction](first-order-energy-correction.md)

Let $\sigma^2=\hbar/(2m\omega)$ and $g(x)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ be the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) ground-state [probability density](probability-density.md). Put $p(a)=\int_{-a}^ag(x)\,dx$. The first excited-state density is $x^2g(x)/\sigma^2$. [Integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md), using $g'=-xg/\sigma^2$, gives the first-order difference

$$
E'_1-E'_0=-\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}[p(a)-2ag(a)].
$$

The bracket grows from zero to one, since its derivative is $2a^2g(a)/\sigma^2$. Thus the first-order predicted level gap for $H_0+\lambda H'$ is $\hbar\omega[1-\lambda\{p(a)-2ag(a)\}/2]$. For every $\lambda>2$ it becomes negative at sufficiently large $a$.

For $\lambda>1$ the full potential has a central barrier and symmetric minima at $\pm a$. The unperturbed even [ground state](ground-state.md) samples the central barrier more strongly than the odd [excited state](excited-state.md), whose node reduces its shift. This explains the perturbative tendency, but the negative predicted gap is a failure diagnostic of the first-order truncation. The exact one-dimensional [ground state](ground-state.md) remains nodeless and below the first excited state; in a high symmetric barrier the actual pair has a small positive [double-well tunneling splitting](double-well-tunneling-splitting.md).
