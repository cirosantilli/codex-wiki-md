# Adverse pressure gradient

↑ **Parent:** [Pressure gradient](pressure-gradient.md)

Along a boundary-layer streamline directed by increasing $s$, an adverse pressure gradient is a rise in [pressure](pressure-split.md) in the direction of flow. The associated [pressure gradient force](pressure-gradient-force.md) opposes the [velocity](velocity.md). In a steady inviscid outer stream the [Euler equation](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) gives $U\,dU/ds=-\rho^{-1}dp/ds$, so a positive gradient decelerates a positive outer velocity. Within a viscous [boundary layer](boundary-layer.md) the slower fluid near the wall has less streamwise momentum and can reverse while the outer stream still moves forward. The onset of [flow separation](flow-separation.md) is therefore associated with vanishing and then reversed [wall shear stress](wall-shear-stress.md). A pressure rise does not by itself locate separation: the full boundary-layer solution and upstream history also matter.
