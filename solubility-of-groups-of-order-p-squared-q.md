# Solubility of groups of order p squared q

↑ **Parent:** [Solvable group](solvable-group.md)

The assertion follows using only [Sylow theorems](sylow-theorems.md). Write the order instead as $pq^2$ to separate the prime of multiplicity one. If $p<q$, the number of Sylow $q$-subgroups divides $p$ and is one modulo $q$, so one such subgroup is normal. If $p>q$ and the Sylow $p$-subgroup is not normal, its number must be $q^2$, forcing $p\mid(q-1)(q+1)$. Since $p>q$, this forces $p=q+1$ and hence $p=3,q=2$. In that order-twelve exception, four order-three subgroups account for eight nonidentity elements. The four remaining elements contain every order-four [Sylow subgroup](sylow-subgroup.md), so that subgroup is unique and normal. In every case a normal [Sylow subgroup](sylow-subgroup.md) and its quotient have prime or prime-square order, hence are [abelian groups](abelian-group.md). They provide a series with abelian factors and prove [solubility](solubility.md). Orders with the squared exponent lowered to one or zero follow by the same simpler counting.
