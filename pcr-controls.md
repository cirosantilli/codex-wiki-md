# PCR controls

↑ **Parent:** [PCR specificity](pcr-specificity.md)

[PCR controls](pcr-controls.md) are [scientific controls](scientific-control.md) that separate different explanations for a readout. A no-template reaction contains the [PCR primers](pcr-primer.md) and copying reagents but replaces the added template with [water](water.md) or sample buffer. Amplification there can indicate carried-over [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) or a [primer dimer](primer-dimer.md); a clean result does not exclude contamination confined to one sample.[FAQ: target-free control reactions and differing product sizes or melt profiles](how-can-i-distinguish-non-template-amplification-ntc-from-real-products-by-new-england-biolabs.md)

Different comparisons answer different questions. A known amplifiable template tests whether the [PCR primers](pcr-primer.md), [DNA polymerase](dna-polymerase.md) and [thermal cycler](thermal-cycler.md) can produce the expected [amplicon](amplicon.md). An extraction blank follows sample preparation without adding the sampled material. A separate amplifiable interval in the sample can test whether recovered [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) supports copying, although success there does not guarantee success at the target. A length standard calibrates [gel electrophoresis](gel-electrophoresis.md); it is not a control for the copying reaction. These comparisons are an analytical design extension to the teaching example below, not a claim that its student protocol includes every comparison.
