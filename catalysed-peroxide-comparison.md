# Catalysed peroxide comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md)

A simple motivating comparison is visible gas evolution from [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md), whose net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is

$$
2\mathrm{H_2O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{O_2}.
$$

The products are [water](water.md) and [oxygen](oxygen.md). The Royal Society of Chemistry's teaching resource uses small test tubes and droppers, [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) labelled 5–10 volumes, solutions of metal salts including [copper(II) sulfate](copper-ii-sulfate.md), and approximately $1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md). Its qualitative procedure tests additions singly and in pairs to tubes containing about $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ depth of [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md). It reports the expected classroom result that the additions individually are not effective under those conditions, whereas the solids produced by mixing metal-salt solution with [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) give effective [heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md). This is the resource's expected observation, not a newly measured ranking.[Resources and Results: small test tubes, droppers, peroxide strength, single versus paired additions, visual readout and expected precipitate effect](the-best-catalyst-to-decompose-hydrogen-peroxide-by-royal-society-of-chemistry.md)

A focused adaptation compares four matched tubes: no addition, [copper(II) sulfate](copper-ii-sulfate.md) alone, [sodium hydroxide](sodium-hydroxide.md) alone, and the pair. Use equal initial [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) amounts, identical tubes and the same starting [temperature](temperature-split.md). Record stock [concentrations](concentration.md) and added volumes; add [water](water.md) to the other tubes to match the total added liquid. Additions can first be checked without [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) to distinguish [precipitation](precipitation-chemistry.md) from gas evolution. Start a timer at contact, mix consistently, and observe gas evolution over the same interval. These matching controls are proposed improvements to the qualitative resource, not claimed details of its original trials. Follow its supervised chemical-handling requirements; the stated strengths describe its teaching protocol rather than an unrestricted recipe.

The immediate readout is relative bubbling, together with whether a solid appears. More vigorous bubbling in the paired tube supports faster conversion under the comparison conditions; it does not identify the solid's exact composition or establish a unique [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). Bubble counts are not a calibrated [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md): bubble sizes, gas retention and warming can differ. For a quantitative extension, use a freely moving [gas syringe](gas-syringe.md) to record collected [volume](volume.md) $V(t)$ and compare early slopes $dV/dt$ at matched [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [pressure](pressure-split.md); check for leaks and repeat runs. This extension is a measurement proposal, not source-reported gas traces.

The paired treatment also changes [pH](ph.md) and produces a surface, so the four tubes alone cannot assign the whole effect to one microscopic catalytic step. A filtered-liquid comparison and a separated, washed-solid comparison would help distinguish dissolved and solid contributions, although washing itself can change the active material. More gas at one time establishes neither a changed [equilibrium constant](equilibrium-constant.md) nor indefinite [catalyst](catalyst.md) reuse. The open gas-evolution experiment is especially unsuitable as a demonstration that two closed mixtures reach the same [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md): the escaping [oxygen](oxygen.md) changes the constraints. The reversible model above supplies that separate explanation. No numerical experimental [rate constants](rate-constant.md), barrier heights or gas volumes are asserted here.
