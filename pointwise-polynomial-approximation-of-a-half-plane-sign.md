# Pointwise polynomial approximation of a half-plane sign

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial Runge theorem](polynomial-runge-theorem.md)

There is a sequence of complex [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) converging pointwise on the whole plane to the sign of the imaginary part, with value zero on the real axis. For each $n$, take the [compact](compact-space.md) union of the upper rectangle $[-n,n]+i[1/n,n]$, its lower reflection and the real segment $[-n,n]$. Its complement is [connected](connected-space.md). The function equal to one, minus one and zero on disjoint [neighborhoods](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of the three components is [holomorphic](complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md). The [polynomial Runge theorem](polynomial-runge-theorem.md) provides a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) within $1/n$ of it on the union. Every fixed point lies in its corresponding component for all large $n$, proving [pointwise convergence](pointwise-convergence.md). The limit is discontinuous, so the convergence cannot be locally uniform near the real axis. This illustrates how pointwise approximation by [holomorphic functions](holomorphic-function.md) differs from locally uniform approximation.
