# Quantum information

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum information theory](quantum-information-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information)

Quantum information is information encoded in [quantum states](quantum-state.md) and processed through [quantum channels](quantum-channel.md) or [measurement in quantum measurements](quantum-measurement-split.md). A [qubit](qubit.md) supplies a two-dimensional state space; [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) and [entanglement](entangled-state.md) affect which operations and outcome statistics are possible. The [Born rule](born-rule.md) connects the state description with the [probabilities](probability.md) of classical measurement records.

The continuously varying [probability amplitudes](probability-amplitude.md) of a [qubit](qubit.md) do not supply a readable list of arbitrarily many classical [bits](bit.md). A measurement produces a classical record with state-dependent [probabilities](probability.md). [Quantum state tomography](quantum-state-tomography.md) estimates the state from many identically prepared systems. For a classical label encoded into one transmitted [qubit](qubit.md), with no additional shared resource, [Holevo's theorem](holevo-s-theorem.md) bounds the [mutual information](mutual-information.md) available from a measurement by one [bit](bit.md). This bound concerns information about the label, rather than the number of possible outcome labels a measuring device can print. [Watrous's lecture notes, Lecture 12, on accessible information and the Holevo bound](https://jhwatrous.github.io/TQI-notes.pdf#page=116)
