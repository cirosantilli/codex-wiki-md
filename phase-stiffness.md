# Phase stiffness

↑ **Parent:** [Goldstone-mode effective free energy](goldstone-mode-effective-free-energy.md)

The phase stiffness is the coefficient of $\tfrac12\int d^dx\,(\nabla\theta)^2$ in the long-distance [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md). Its renormalized value determines the [thermal phase fluctuations](thermal-phase-fluctuation.md) and algebraic [correlation function](correlation-function.md) of a two-dimensional [O(N) model](o-n-model.md) with $N=2$.

Operationally impose a small phase difference $\phi$ between opposite sides of a square sample of side $L$. The smooth minimum has $\theta=\phi x/L$, so the two-dimensional excess [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md) is

$$
F(\phi)-F(0)=\tfrac12\rho_s\phi^2+o(\phi^2),
\qquad \rho_s=\left.\frac{\partial^2F}{\partial\phi^2}\right|_{\phi=0}.
$$

The derivative concerns a prescribed twist at the stated observation scale; in $d$ dimensions its conventional size factor is $L^{d-2}$. This response definition includes thermal fluctuations and [vortices](phase-vortex.md) after equilibration, whereas a microscopic gradient coefficient need not. A finite [phase stiffness](phase-stiffness.md) requires no nonzero spatially uniform [order parameter](order-parameter.md); the [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](berezinskii-kosterlitz-thouless-transition.md) is the instructive example.
