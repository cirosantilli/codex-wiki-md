# Boundary layer thickness

↑ **Parent:** [Boundary layer](boundary-layer.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_layer_thickness)

The conventional [boundary layer thickness](boundary-layer-thickness.md) $\delta_{99}$ is the normal distance at which the streamwise [velocity](velocity.md) first reaches $0.99U$ in a monotone [velocity profile](velocity-profile.md). A [Blasius boundary layer](blasius-boundary-layer.md) approaches $U$ smoothly, so its apparent edge depends on the chosen percentage; there is no discontinuity. Solving $f'(\eta_{99})=0.99$ in the [Blasius equation](blasius-equation.md) gives $\eta_{99}\simeq4.910$, hence

$$
\delta_{99}\simeq4.910\sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}}.
$$

The factor $4.910$ belongs to this definition, whereas the [boundary-layer scaling](boundary-layer-scaling.md) specifies only order of magnitude. Near this edge the [velocity](velocity.md) changes slowly, making a threshold position sensitive to [measurement error](measurement-error.md); [displacement thickness](displacement-thickness.md) and [momentum thickness](momentum-thickness.md) summarize the whole [velocity profile](velocity-profile.md).[Boundary-layer Thickness, Skin friction, and Energy dissipation](modules-on-high-reynolds-number-flows/boundary-layer-thickness-skin-friction-and-energy-dissipation.md)
