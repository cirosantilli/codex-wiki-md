# Rigid rotor

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular rotation](molecular-rotation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_rotor)

A [rigid rotor](rigid-rotor.md) models rotational motion with fixed internal separations. Its use in the synthetic [Stark effect](stark-effect.md) figure omits vibrational motion and additional interactions in real [molecules](molecule.md).

Rigidity fixes the [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md), not the orientation, and does not require a spherical mass distribution. Remove motion of the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md), choose [principal axes](principal-axis.md) of the [inertia tensor](inertia-tensor.md), and write the classical [rotational kinetic energy](rotational-kinetic-energy.md) as

$$
T_{\rm rot}=\frac12\left(I_a\omega_a^2+I_b\omega_b^2+I_c\omega_c^2\right)
=\frac{L_a^2}{2I_a}+\frac{L_b^2}{2I_b}+\frac{L_c^2}{2I_c}.
$$

Here $I_a,I_b,I_c$ are the principal [moments of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md), $\omega_a,\omega_b,\omega_c$ components of [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md), and $L_a,L_b,L_c$ components of [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md). Quantization replaces these last components by [angular momentum operators](angular-momentum-operator.md). Different principal [moments of inertia](moment-of-inertia.md) produce different spectra: the single-constant [linear rigid rotor](linear-rigid-rotor.md) is a special case, not a formula for every [molecule](molecule.md).[printed p. 1: principal-axis energy and classification; pp. 4–5: asymmetric rotors](lecture-24-supplement-energy-levels-of-a-rigid-rotor-by-dudley-herschbach.md)
