# Gravitational lensing

↑ **Parent:** [General relativity](general-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_lensing)

Gravitational lensing is the deflection and focusing of light by spacetime curvature along the line of sight.

A useful first model is a [point-mass gravitational lens](point-mass-gravitational-lens.md): one displaced source gives [two images of a point-mass lens](two-images-of-a-point-mass-lens.md), while exact alignment gives an [Einstein ring](einstein-ring.md). The [Einstein angular radius](einstein-angular-radius.md) sets the angular scale, and [image parity in gravitational lensing](image-parity-in-gravitational-lensing.md) explains why the two images have different handedness. The [spectroscopic test of the Twin Quasar lens](spectroscopic-test-of-the-twin-quasar-lens.md) shows how [optical spectra](optical-spectrum.md) can test whether two apparent [quasars](quasar.md) represent one source.
