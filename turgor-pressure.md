# Turgor pressure

↑ **Parent:** [Cell wall](cell-wall.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turgor_pressure)

[Turgor pressure](turgor-pressure.md) is the internal [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) above the external [pressure](pressure-split.md) in a [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md). When [water](water.md) enters a walled [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) by [osmosis](osmosis.md), expansion loads the [cell wall](cell-wall.md). Rising [pressure](pressure-split.md) can oppose further net entry while the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md) retains its [selective permeability](selective-permeability.md). Mechanical support therefore does not require the [cell wall](cell-wall.md) to block [water](water.md).

As an original mechanical check, idealize a small flat patch of [cell wall](cell-wall.md) of [surface area](surface-area.md) $A$ under uniform [turgor pressure](turgor-pressure.md) $\Delta P=P_{\mathrm{in}}-P_{\mathrm{out}}$. Its outward [force](force-split.md) is $F=\Delta P A$. Doubling $A$ at fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md) doubles the load; equal internal and external [pressures](pressure-split.md) give zero net pressure load. This is a force balance, not a measured wall strength or a complete model of a curved, growing [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md). It says nothing about which [solutes](solute.md) cross the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md).
