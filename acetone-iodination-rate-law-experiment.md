# Acetone iodination rate-law experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md)

[Acetone](acetone.md) reacts with [iodine](iodine.md) to form [iodoacetone](iodoacetone.md), [iodide](iodide.md), and a [hydrogen ion](hydrogen-ion.md):

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}+\mathrm I_2\longrightarrow
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2I}+\mathrm H^++\mathrm I^-.
$$

In [water](water.md), $\mathrm H^+$ denotes a solvated [hydrogen ion](hydrogen-ion.md); added [hydrochloric acid](hydrochloric-acid.md) provides acidic conditions.

Moorpark College's protocol mixes [acetone](acetone.md), [hydrochloric acid](hydrochloric-acid.md) and [water](water.md) from burets in matched test tubes; pipet in [iodine](iodine.md), swirl and time colour loss against white paper. Use [water](water.md), then reacted solution, as references. Duplicate each mixture; keep total [volume](volume.md) $5.00\,\mathrm{mL}$ and run [temperatures](temperature-split.md) within $2\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.[Chemistry M01B Laboratory Manual, version 4.0 by Moorpark College Department of Chemistry; Version 4.0, Experiment 4, printed pp. 17–18: Procedure and Table 1](chemistry-m01b-laboratory-manual-version-4-0-by-moorpark-college-department-of-chemistry/experiment-4-chemical-kinetics-the-iodination-of-acetone.md)

The stock dilutions give $[\mathrm{acetone}]_0=0.800$, $[\mathrm H^+]_0=0.200$, and $[\mathrm I_2]_0=0.00100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. B and C separately double [acetone](acetone.md) and acid; D halves [iodine](iodine.md). The manual supplies no measured times; it states zero partial [order of reaction](order-of-reaction.md) in [iodine](iodine.md) and asks students to determine the other exponents.[Chemistry M01B Laboratory Manual, version 4.0 by Moorpark College Department of Chemistry; Printed pp. 15–16, iodine independence and equations (3)–(7); p. 18, Table 1, mixtures A–D](chemistry-m01b-laboratory-manual-version-4-0-by-moorpark-college-department-of-chemistry/experiment-4-chemical-kinetics-the-iodination-of-acetone.md)

The endpoint measurement is a time $\tau$ to an operational colour threshold, not direct observation of a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md). If the [iodine](iodine.md) [disappearance rate](disappearance-rate.md) $r$ is approximately constant until that endpoint, $r\approx[\mathrm I_2]_0/\tau$. Halving $[\mathrm I_2]_0$ at fixed other [concentrations](concentration.md) then approximately halves $\tau$ without changing $r$. A longer or shorter endpoint time alone is not a comparison of [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) unless the starting [concentration](concentration.md) is included. For an observed power-form [rate law](rate-law.md), doubling only [acetone](acetone.md) gives a rate ratio $2^m$ and hence $m=\ln(r_B/r_A)/\ln2$; doubling only acid similarly estimates its own partial [order of reaction](order-of-reaction.md). Those are inference formulas, not asserted experimental results.

A useful proposed [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) would explain how [iodine](iodine.md) is consumed after an earlier transformation sets the throughput, making the observed [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) insensitive to [iodine](iodine.md) over a tested range. The observed zero exponent would contradict treating this net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) as a single [elementary reaction](elementary-reaction.md) with directional [rate law](rate-law.md) $k[\mathrm{acetone}][\mathrm I_2]$. It would not uniquely prove which [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) or earlier step is involved. Independently detecting a candidate [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) would provide another kind of evidence.

The excess-[reactant](reactant.md) approximation has an explicit scale: the baseline iodine inventory is only $0.00100/0.800=0.125\%$ of the initial [acetone](acetone.md) inventory, and its net [hydrogen ion](hydrogen-ion.md) production is $0.00100/0.200=0.5\%$ of the initial acid inventory, assuming only the stated one-to-one [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md). These are calculated bounds on composition change, not measured reaction orders. Mixing delay, a subjective colour threshold, and [temperature](temperature-split.md) drift limit the inference. Repeats and a fixed viewing geometry help; a calibrated [absorbance](absorbance.md) trace could test linearity during conversion instead of relying on an endpoint alone. That extension would require a [reference blank](reference-blank.md) and a justified [Beer-Lambert law](beer-lambert-law.md) calibration, rather than treating visible colour as an exact [concentration](concentration.md). A [zero-order reaction](zero-order-reaction.md) approximation in [iodine](iodine.md) must fail at depletion; it cannot imply consumption after no [iodine](iodine.md) remains.
