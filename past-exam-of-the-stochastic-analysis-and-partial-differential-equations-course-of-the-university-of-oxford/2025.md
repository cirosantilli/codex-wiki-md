<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Let $Z=(Z_n)_{n\geq0}$ be a [Markov chain](../markov-chain.md) with [transition kernel](../markov-chain.md#markov-kernel) $K$, and let $N$ be an independent [Poisson process](../poisson-process.md) of constant rate $r>0$. A [Pseudo-Poisson process](../continuous-time-markov-chain.md#pseudo-poisson-process) is $X_t=Z_{N_t}$. On the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $B_b(E)$ of bounded [Borel measurable functions](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function), with the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm), write

$$
(Kf)(x)=\int_E f(y)K(x,dy),\qquad K^0=I.
$$

Its [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes), [transition semigroup](../markov-process.md#markov-semigroup), and [resolvent operator](../functional-analysis.md#resolvent-of-an-operator) are respectively

$$
\boxed{L=r(K-I),\qquad D(L)=B_b(E),}
$$



$$
\boxed{P_t=e^{tL}=e^{-rt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(rt)^n}{n!}K^n,}
$$

and, for $\lambda>0$,

$$
\boxed{R_\lambda=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_t\,dt
=\frac1{\lambda+r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac r{\lambda+r}\right)^nK^n
=(\lambda I-L)^{-1}.}
$$

The [transition semigroup](../markov-process.md#markov-semigroup) averages $K^n$ against the [Poisson distribution](../discrete-probability-distribution.md#poisson-distribution) of the number of updates. Since $K$ is a [Markov operator](../markov-process.md#markov-operator), $\|K\|\leq1$; both series converge in [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). Updates with $Z_{n+1}=Z_n$ are allowed and do not count as changes of the observed state. The degenerate rate $r=0$ gives $L=0$, $P_t=I$, and $R_\lambda=I/\lambda$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

**The displayed identity requires a correction to the process construction in the source.** In a [piecewise-deterministic Markov process](../markov-process.md#piecewise-deterministic-markov-process), the instantaneous [hazard function](../survival-analysis.md#hazard-function) is evaluated at the current flowing state $X_{u-}$, and the destination is sampled from $K_u(X_{u-},dy)$. Keeping the jump times and destinations of the original [Markov jump process](../continuous-time-markov-chain.md#markov-jump-process) $Y$ instead evaluates both quantities at its last reset state $Y_{u-}$, which generally differs from $X_{u-}$. The source PDF confirms that these original jump times and destinations were retained. Consequently its definition does not in general produce the asserted [transition kernel](../markov-chain.md#markov-kernel), or even a [Markov process](../markov-process.md) in the state variable $X$ alone.

For an explicit counterexample, take $a(t,x)=1$, $\alpha(x)=2+\tanh x$, $y_0=0$, and let every $K_t(x,dy)$ be the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution), independently of $t,x$. These are smooth coefficients with bounded positive jump rate, so [nonexplosion](../continuous-time-markov-chain.md#nonexplosion-of-a-continuous-time-markov-chain) holds. In the literal construction, the first holding time has the [exponential distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution) of rate $2$. Thus the [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) of $X_t$ has an atom of mass $e^{-2t}$ at $t$. Paths with at least one jump contribute no atom there, because the reset has a [normal density](../probability-distribution.md#normal-density). The asserted identity instead assigns to this no-jump atom the mass

$$
\exp\left(-\int_0^t(2+\tanh u)\,du\right)=\frac{e^{-2t}}{\cosh t},
$$

which differs for every $t>0$.

Here is the full derivation for the intended [piecewise-deterministic Markov process](../markov-process.md#piecewise-deterministic-markov-process). Assume [nonexplosion](../continuous-time-markov-chain.md#nonexplosion-of-a-continuous-time-markov-chain); smoothness alone does not guarantee it. Start at $(s,x)$, let $\tau$ be the first jump after $s$, and set

$$
z(u)=\varphi(s,x,u),\qquad H(u)=\int_s^u\alpha(z(r))\,dr.
$$

One precise construction draws an independent unit-rate [exponential random variable](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution) $E$ and takes $\tau=\inf\{u\geq s:H(u)\geq E\}$, with $\inf\varnothing=\infty$. This implements a [hazard function](../survival-analysis.md#hazard-function) along the [flow of a vector field](../multivariable-calculus.md#flow-of-a-vector-field), rather than a fixed exponential holding time at the last reset. Its [survival probability](../markov-chain.md#survival-probability) and jump-time [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) are

$$
S(u)=\mathbb P_{s,x}(\tau>u)=e^{-H(u)},\qquad
-\partial_u S(u)=\alpha(z(u))e^{-H(u)}.
$$

Equivalently, $S'=-\alpha(z)S$ with $S(s)=1$. On $\{\tau>t\}$, the terminal state is $z(t)$. Conditional on $\tau=u$, draw the new state $y$ from $K_u(z(u),dy)$ and restart the same construction from $(u,y)$. The [Markov property](../markov-chain.md#markov-property) and the [tower property of conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#law-of-total-expectation) therefore give the [first-jump decomposition of a piecewise-deterministic Markov process](../markov-process.md#first-jump-decomposition-of-a-piecewise-deterministic-markov-process)

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
(P_{s,t}f)(x)
&=e^{-H(t)}f(z(t))\\
&\quad+\int_s^t\alpha(z(u))e^{-H(u)}
\int_{\mathbb R}(P_{u,t}f)(y)K_u(z(u),dy)\,du.
\end{aligned}}
$$

This proves the requested identity under the corrected construction, for every bounded [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) $f$. The two contributions partition according to whether the first jump occurs before $t$; later jumps are already contained in $P_{u,t}$.

For comparison, the literal construction does have a different renewal identity when started at a reset with $X_s=Y_s=x$. If $Q_{s,t}$ denotes only these reset-start operators, the same [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) argument gives

$$
(Q_{s,t}f)(x)=e^{-\alpha(x)(t-s)}f(\varphi(s,x,t))
+\int_s^t\alpha(x)e^{-\alpha(x)(u-s)}(K_uQ_{u,t}f)(x)\,du.
$$

These $Q_{s,t}$ need not be the [transition operators](../markov-chain.md#transition-operator) conditional on $X_s=x$ at an arbitrary time: that observation does not specify the hidden reset state. This distinction explains exactly why replacing only the between-jump paths is insufficient.

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

The identity for the [killed deterministic flow](../markov-process.md#killed-deterministic-flow) follows directly from the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) and the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus), independently of the construction issue in part (b). For a continuously differentiable $f$, put $z(t)=\varphi(s,x,t)$ and $H(t)=\int_s^t\alpha(\varphi(s,x,u))\,du$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t(P^\alpha_{s,t}f)(x)
&=e^{-H(t)}\bigl[f'(z(t))\partial_tz(t)-\alpha(z(t))f(z(t))\bigr]\\
&=e^{-H(t)}\bigl[a(t,z(t))f'(z(t))-\alpha(z(t))f(z(t))\bigr]\\
&=(P^\alpha_{s,t}L_t^\alpha f)(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, on such [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function),

$$
\boxed{\partial_tP^\alpha_{s,t}=P^\alpha_{s,t}L_t^\alpha,\qquad
L_t^\alpha f=a(t,\cdot)f'-\alpha f.}
$$

The time subscript on $L_t^\alpha$ is necessary because the [vector field](../multivariable-calculus.md#vector-field) may depend on time; it is suppressed on the right-hand side of the source's displayed operator identity. The [killed deterministic flow](../markov-process.md#killed-deterministic-flow) transports an observable along the [flow of a vector field](../multivariable-calculus.md#flow-of-a-vector-field) and multiplies it by the no-jump [survival probability](../markov-chain.md#survival-probability).

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Use the corrected [piecewise-deterministic Markov process](../markov-process.md#piecewise-deterministic-markov-process) from part (b). For a smooth [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $f$, its time-dependent [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) is

$$
(L_tf)(x)=a(t,x)f'(x)+\alpha(x)\left[\int_{\mathbb R}f(y)m_t(x,y)\,dy-f(x)\right].
$$

Indeed, over an interval of length $h$, deterministic motion contributes $ha(t,x)f'(x)$, while a jump has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $h\alpha(x)+o(h)$ and changes the conditional mean of $f$ by $K_tf(x)-f(x)$. Terms involving both drift and a jump contribute only to the remainder. Thus $P_{t,t+h}f=f+hL_tf+o(h)$.

The [Dynkin formula](../stochastic-process.md#dynkin-s-formula), or this infinitesimal expansion integrated against the current [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), gives the weak evolution identity

$$
\frac d{dt}\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)p_t(x)\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R}(L_tf)(x)p_t(x)\,dx.
$$

Take $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, so [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) has no boundary contribution. Assuming the displayed jump integrals are finite, [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}a(t,x)f'(x)p_t(x)\,dx
&=-\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\partial_x\bigl(a(t,x)p_t(x)\bigr)\,dx,\\
\int_{\mathbb R}\alpha(y)p_t(y)\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)m_t(y,x)\,dx\,dy
&=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\int_{\mathbb R}\alpha(y)m_t(y,x)p_t(y)\,dy\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently the [forward equation of a piecewise-deterministic Markov process](../markov-process.md#forward-equation-of-a-piecewise-deterministic-markov-process) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\partial_tp_t(x)=(A_tp_t)(x)
&=-\partial_x\bigl(a(t,x)p_t(x)\bigr)-\alpha(x)p_t(x)\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb R}\alpha(y)m_t(y,x)p_t(y)\,dy.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Here $A_t=L_t^*$ is the formal [adjoint operator](../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator) acting on [probability density functions](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function). Its terms are transport, loss from $x$, and incoming mass from every $y$. In particular, the incoming rate is $\alpha(y)$, evaluated at the departure state. The operator denoted $A$ in the source is generally time-dependent. Because $\int m_t(y,x)\,dx=1$, the gain and loss integrals cancel; with vanishing transport flux at infinity, $\frac d{dt}\int p_t=0$.

**The deterministic initial state in part (b) does not actually yield an everywhere smooth full density.** For finite times the no-jump contribution is the positive measure

$$
\exp\left(-\int_0^t\alpha(\varphi(0,y_0,u))\,du\right)
\delta_{\varphi(0,y_0,t)},
$$

where $\delta_z$ is the [Dirac measure](../measure.md#dirac-measure) at $z$. Smooth jump destination [probability density functions](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) cannot remove this positive atom. The density equation above is therefore conditional on the extra density hypothesis, for example with a suitable random initial [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function). For the specified fixed initial state, write $\nu_t$ for the whole [probability measure](../probability-space.md#probability-measure) and use instead

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\int f\,d\nu_t=\int L_tf\,d\nu_t,\qquad \nu_0=\delta_{y_0}.}
$$

This weak measure evolution includes both the atom and any continuous component. It becomes the displayed [partial differential equation](../partial-differential-equation.md) whenever $\nu_t(dx)=p_t(x)\,dx$ with the assumed regularity. It does not describe the literal hidden-reset construction without the correction in part (b).

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For the [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) $dX_s=b(s,X_s)\,ds+\Sigma(s,X_s)\,dB_s$, the [diffusion generator](../stochastic-process.md#diffusion-generator) on smooth functions is

$$
(\mathcal L_sg)(x)=\sum_jb_j(s,x)\partial_jg(x)
+\frac12\sum_{j,k}(\Sigma\Sigma^{\mathsf T})_{jk}(s,x)\partial_{jk}g(x).
$$

One form of the [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula) is the following. Suppose the [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) is well posed and nonexplosive, $c\geq0$ is bounded, the terminal data $g$ and source $h$ are bounded, and $F$ is a [classical solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#classical-solution) bounded on finite time intervals of

$$
\partial_tF+\mathcal L_tF-cF+h=0,\qquad F(T,x)=g(x).
$$

With the regularity needed for the [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma), the [parabolic Feynman-Kac formula with a source](../stochastic-calculus.md#parabolic-feynman-kac-formula-with-a-source) states

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F(t,x)=\mathbb E_{t,x}\bigg[&e^{-\int_t^Tc(r,X_r)\,dr}g(X_T)\\
&+\int_t^Te^{-\int_t^sc(r,X_r)\,dr}h(s,X_s)\,ds\bigg].
\end{aligned}}
$$

Here $\mathbb E_{t,x}$ means expectation for the [diffusion process](../stochastic-calculus.md#markov-diffusion) started at $X_t=x$. Under hypotheses ensuring existence of a [classical solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#classical-solution), this representation also identifies that solution uniquely in the stated bounded class. In the remaining parts, $c=h=0$, so $F(t,x)=\mathbb E_{t,x}g(X_T)$ solves the terminal-value [Kolmogorov backward equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#kolmogorov-backward-equation) $F_t+\mathcal L_tF=0$. Complex terminal data are allowed by applying the real formula to their [real part](../complex-analysis.md#real-part) and [imaginary part](../complex-analysis.md#imaginary-part) separately.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The [diffusion generator](../stochastic-process.md#diffusion-generator) is $\mathcal L=\mu\partial_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial_{xx}$. By the [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula), the required [conditional characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md#conditional-characteristic-function) solves

$$
F_t+\mu F_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2F_{xx}=0,\qquad F(T,x)=e^{iux}.
$$

Use [separation of variables](../partial-differential-equation.md#separation-of-variables) in the form $F(t,x)=e^{iux}h(t)$. Since $F_x=iuF$ and $F_{xx}=-u^2F$, the temporal factor satisfies the [ordinary differential equation](../differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation)

$$
h'(t)+\left(iu\mu-\frac12\sigma^2u^2\right)h(t)=0,\qquad h(T)=1.
$$

Thus $h(t)=\exp[(iu\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2u^2)(T-t)]$, and

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[e^{iuX_T}\mid X_t=x]
=\exp\left(iu[x+\mu(T-t)]-\frac12u^2\sigma^2(T-t)\right).}
$$

This is bounded in absolute value by one, so it lies in the uniqueness class of the [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula). As an independent check, integrating the [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) gives $X_T=x+\mu(T-t)+\sigma(B_T-B_t)$. The independent [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) have the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) with mean zero and [variance](../variance.md) $T-t$, yielding exactly the same [characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md). For $\sigma=0$, the [conditional distribution](../probability-theory.md#conditional-distribution) is a point mass at $x+\mu(T-t)$, and the same boxed expression still applies.

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

Here the [diffusion generator](../stochastic-process.md#diffusion-generator) is $\mathcal L=ax\partial_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial_{xx}$. The [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula) gives

$$
F_t+axF_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2F_{xx}=0,\qquad F(T,x)=e^{iux}.
$$

Try $F(t,x)=\exp[iu\alpha(t)x+\beta(t)]$, where $\alpha(T)=1$ and $\beta(T)=0$. Substitution and collection of the coefficients of $x$ and the constant term give

$$
\alpha'(t)+a\alpha(t)=0,\qquad
\beta'(t)=\frac12\sigma^2u^2\alpha(t)^2.
$$

The solutions of these [ordinary differential equations](../differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation), with the terminal conditions imposed, are

$$
\alpha(t)=e^{a(T-t)},\qquad
\beta(t)=-\frac12\sigma^2u^2\int_t^Te^{2a(T-s)}\,ds.
$$

For $\Delta=T-t$, define the conditional [variance](../variance.md)

$$
v_\Delta=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{2a}(e^{2a\Delta}-1),&a\ne0,\\
\sigma^2\Delta,&a=0.
\end{cases}
$$

Then the required [conditional characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md#conditional-characteristic-function) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[e^{iuX_T}\mid X_t=x]
=\exp\left(iue^{a\Delta}x-\frac12u^2v_\Delta\right).}
$$

In particular, for $a\ne0$ this exponent is $iue^{a\Delta}x-\frac{\sigma^2u^2}{4a}(e^{2a\Delta}-1)$. The [variance](../variance.md) $v_\Delta$ is nonnegative for either sign of $a$, and its limit at $a=0$ is $\sigma^2\Delta$. Hence the solution is bounded in absolute value by one and the [Feynman-Kac formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula) identifies it with the required expectation. The value $u=0$ gives one directly, including that case without dividing by $u$.

For an independent check, the [integrating factor](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#integrating-factor) $e^{-as}$ and the [Itô product rule](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule) give

$$
X_T=e^{a\Delta}x+\sigma\int_t^Te^{a(T-s)}\,dB_s.
$$

The [stochastic integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) has a [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) because its integrand is deterministic; the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) gives its [variance](../variance.md) as $v_\Delta$. For $a<0$ this is the [Ornstein-Uhlenbeck transition distribution](../gaussian-process.md#ornstein-uhlenbeck-transition-distribution). The formula also covers the unstable case $a>0$, and degenerates to deterministic motion when $\sigma=0$.

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

For $a<0$, set $v_\infty=-\sigma^2/(2a)>0$. The candidate [invariant probability law of a Markov process](../markov-process.md#invariant-probability-law-of-a-markov-process) is

$$
\boxed{\pi=\mathcal N\left(0,-\frac{\sigma^2}{2a}\right),\qquad
\pi(dx)=\sqrt{\frac{-a}{\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\left(\frac{ax^2}{\sigma^2}\right)\,dx.}
$$

To prove invariance rather than merely guess a limiting [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), initialize $X_t\sim\pi$ independently of future [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment). Integrating the [conditional characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md#conditional-characteristic-function) from part (i) against this centered [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[e^{iuX_{t+\Delta}}]
&=e^{-u^2v_\Delta/2}\,\mathbb E[e^{iu e^{a\Delta}X_t}]\\
&=\exp\left[-\frac12u^2\left(v_\Delta+e^{2a\Delta}v_\infty\right)\right]
=e^{-u^2v_\infty/2},
\end{aligned}
$$

because $v_\Delta=v_\infty(1-e^{2a\Delta})$. The [uniqueness theorem for characteristic functions](../characteristic-function.md#uniqueness-theorem-for-characteristic-functions) implies $X_{t+\Delta}\sim\pi$ for every $\Delta\geq0$. This proves $\pi P_\Delta=\pi$, as required. The [time-homogeneous Markov property](../markov-chain.md#time-homogeneous-markov-property) then also makes the process with this [Ornstein-Uhlenbeck stationary initialization](../gaussian-process.md#ornstein-uhlenbeck-stationary-initialization) a [strictly stationary process](../time-series.md#strictly-stationary-process).

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Let $E$ be the underlying [Banach space](../banach-space.md). By the [Laplace-transform formula for a semigroup resolvent](../functional-analysis.md#laplace-transform-formula-for-a-semigroup-resolvent),

$$
R_\lambda f=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s}P_sf\,ds,\qquad
\|R_\lambda\|\leq\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s}\,ds=\frac1\lambda.
$$

These are [Bochner integrals](../measure-theory.md#bochner-integral), defined for every $f\in E$ and $\lambda>0$. The [contraction semigroup](../functional-analysis.md#contraction-semigroup) bound and [semigroup property](../functional-analysis.md#semigroup-property) give

$$
R_\lambda R_\mu f
=\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s-\mu t}P_{s+t}f\,dt\,ds.
$$

Interchanging and regrouping these [Bochner integrals](../measure-theory.md#bochner-integral) is justified by the integrable bound $e^{-\lambda s-\mu t}\|f\|$, whose integral is $\|f\|/(\lambda\mu)$. For $\lambda\ne\mu$, set $v=s+t$ and evaluate the inner scalar integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_\lambda R_\mu f
&=\int_0^\infty\left(\int_0^ve^{-\lambda s-\mu(v-s)}\,ds\right)P_vf\,dv\\
&=\frac1{\mu-\lambda}\int_0^\infty(e^{-\lambda v}-e^{-\mu v})P_vf\,dv\\
&=\frac{R_\lambda f-R_\mu f}{\mu-\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $f$ was arbitrary, the [resolvent identity](../banach-algebra.md#resolvent-identity) follows:

$$
\boxed{R_\lambda-R_\mu=(\mu-\lambda)R_\lambda R_\mu.}
$$

For $\lambda=\mu$, both sides vanish, so that case holds as well.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

For $\lambda\ne\mu$, apply the [resolvent identity](../banach-algebra.md#resolvent-identity) from part (i) in both orders:

$$
R_\lambda R_\mu=\frac{R_\lambda-R_\mu}{\mu-\lambda},\qquad
R_\mu R_\lambda=\frac{R_\mu-R_\lambda}{\lambda-\mu}.
$$

The two quotients are equal. For $\lambda=\mu$ the assertion is immediate. Hence

$$
\boxed{R_\lambda R_\mu=R_\mu R_\lambda\qquad(\lambda,\mu>0).}
$$

Thus [resolvent operators](../functional-analysis.md#resolvent-of-an-operator) from the same [strongly continuous semigroup](../functional-analysis.md#c0-semigroup) commute. This conclusion does not require commuting arbitrary [bounded linear operators](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-operator); it uses the fact that both arise from the same [semigroup generator](../functional-analysis.md#infinitesimal-generator-of-a-semigroup).

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

A second-order [nondivergence-form elliptic operator](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#nondivergence-form-elliptic-operator) on a domain $D\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ has the form

$$
\mathcal Af(x)=\sum_{j,k=1}^d a_{jk}(x)\partial_{jk}f(x)
+\sum_{j=1}^db_j(x)\partial_jf(x)+c(x)f(x).
$$

Only the symmetric part of $a$ affects the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) term, so one may take $a(x)$ symmetric. The operator is a [uniformly elliptic operator](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) if there is a constant $\kappa>0$, independent of $x$, such that

$$
\boxed{\sum_{j,k=1}^da_{jk}(x)\xi_j\xi_k\geq\kappa|\xi|^2
\qquad(x\in D,\ \xi\in\mathbb R^d).}
$$

A common bounded-coefficient convention additionally requires an upper bound $\sum a_{jk}(x)\xi_j\xi_k\leq\Lambda|\xi|^2$ with $\Lambda<\infty$. The lower bound says that the principal [quadratic form](../quadratic-form.md) cannot degenerate in any direction or at any location.

For an explicit stochastic example, standard $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) has [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes)

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A=\tfrac12\Delta,\qquad a_{jk}=\tfrac12\delta_{jk},\quad b=0,\quad c=0.}
$$

Its principal [quadratic form](../quadratic-form.md) is $|\xi|^2/2$, so it is [uniformly elliptic](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) with $\kappa=\Lambda=1/2$. More generally, a sufficiently regular [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) $dX_t=b(X_t)\,dt+\Sigma(X_t)\,dB_t$ has [diffusion generator](../stochastic-process.md#diffusion-generator) $b\cdot\nabla+\tfrac12(\Sigma\Sigma^{\mathsf T}):D^2$; it is [uniformly elliptic](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) when $\Sigma\Sigma^{\mathsf T}$ has a uniformly positive lower bound. A conservative [diffusion process](../stochastic-calculus.md#markov-diffusion) has no zeroth-order term, since its [infinitesimal generator](../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) annihilates constants.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

The convergence is in the [strong operator topology](../functional-analysis.md#strong-operator-topology): for each fixed $f\in E$, one must prove $\|\lambda R_\lambda f-f\|\to0$. First, the [contraction semigroup](../functional-analysis.md#contraction-semigroup) bound gives

$$
\|\lambda R_\lambda f\|
\leq\int_0^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\|P_tf\|\,dt
\leq\|f\|.
$$

Thus **$\lambda R_\lambda$ is a contraction**. This is the useful scaling of the contraction estimate in the source's hint. Since $\int_0^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\,dt=1$,

$$
\lambda R_\lambda f-f
=\int_0^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda t}(P_tf-f)\,dt.
$$

Fix $\varepsilon>0$. By [strong continuity](../functional-analysis.md#strong-continuity), choose $\delta>0$ so that $\|P_tf-f\|<\varepsilon$ for $0\leq t\leq\delta$. Split the integral there and use $\|P_tf-f\|\leq2\|f\|$ for the tail:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\lambda R_\lambda f-f\|
&\leq\varepsilon\int_0^\delta\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\,dt
+2\|f\|\int_\delta^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\,dt\\
&\leq\varepsilon+2\|f\|e^{-\lambda\delta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $\lambda\to\infty$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda R_\lambda f\longrightarrow f\text{ in }E\quad\text{for every }f\in E.}
$$

The distinction from [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm) convergence is essential. For example, the [multiplication semigroup](../functional-analysis.md#multiplication-semigroup) on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ defined by $(P_tf)_n=e^{-nt}f_n$ is a [strongly continuous semigroup](../functional-analysis.md#c0-semigroup) of contractions: coordinatewise convergence and [dominated convergence](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) applied to $\sum_n|f_n|^2$ prove [strong continuity](../functional-analysis.md#strong-continuity). Its [resolvent operator](../functional-analysis.md#resolvent-of-an-operator) is $(R_\lambda f)_n=f_n/(\lambda+n)$, and

$$
\|\lambda R_\lambda-I\|=\sup_{n\geq1}\frac n{\lambda+n}=1.
$$

Thus the claimed convergence cannot in general be read as convergence in [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Take $f$ in the [generator domain](../functional-analysis.md#generator-domain) $D(L)$. The [semigroup commutation with its unbounded generator](../functional-analysis.md#semigroup-commutation-with-its-unbounded-generator) implies $P_tf\in D(L)$ and

$$
\frac d{dt}P_tf=P_tLf=LP_tf.
$$

Indeed, applying the bounded operator $P_t$ to the difference quotient at zero gives the right derivative $P_tLf$; [strong continuity](../functional-analysis.md#strong-continuity) gives the full orbit derivative for $t>0$. Now [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) in the [Bochner integral](../measure-theory.md#bochner-integral) yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_\lambda Lf
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_tLf\,dt
=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\frac d{dt}P_tf\,dt\\
&=\left[e^{-\lambda t}P_tf\right]_0^\infty
+\lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_tf\,dt
=-f+\lambda R_\lambda f.
\end{aligned}
$$

The boundary term at infinity vanishes because $\|e^{-\lambda t}P_tf\|\leq e^{-\lambda t}\|f\|$. The derivative integral exists as a [Bochner integral](../measure-theory.md#bochner-integral), since its integrand is bounded in [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) by $e^{-\lambda t}\|Lf\|$.

Multiplying this identity by $\lambda$ gives

$$
\lambda(\lambda R_\lambda-I)f=\lambda R_\lambda(Lf).
$$

Part (b) applies to the fixed vector $Lf\in E$, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda(\lambda R_\lambda-I)f\longrightarrow Lf
\quad\text{in }E\qquad(f\in D(L)).}
$$

The bounded operators $L_\lambda=\lambda^2R_\lambda-\lambda I$ are the [Yosida approximation](../functional-analysis.md#yosida-approximation) of the [semigroup generator](../functional-analysis.md#infinitesimal-generator-of-a-semigroup). The proof only applies the unbounded operator $L$ to $f\in D(L)$; the contraction and limit arguments then act on the ordinary vector $Lf$.
