# Free surface of a Rankine vortex

↑ **Parent:** [Rankine vortex](rankine-vortex.md)

For a steady [Rankine vortex](rankine-vortex.md) in a fluid of constant density $\rho$ with gravity $-gk$, the [Euler equations](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) give $p_r=\rho u_\theta^2/r$ and $p_z=-\rho g$. Integrating on the two sides and matching pressure at $r=a$ gives

$$
p=\begin{cases}B-\rho gz-\frac12\rho\Omega^2(2a^2-r^2),&r\leq a,\\B-\rho gz-\rho\Omega^2a^4/(2r^2),&r>a.\end{cases}
$$

Setting a constant surface pressure yields

$$
z=\begin{cases}C-\Omega^2(2a^2-r^2)/(2g),&r\leq a,\\C-\Omega^2a^4/(2gr^2),&r>a.\end{cases}
$$

Thus the [free surface](free-surface.md) and its radial slope match at the core edge. The central depression is $\Omega^2a^2/g$, twice the depression at $r=a$. [Bernoulli's theorem](bernoulli-equation.md) applies throughout the exterior; the rotational core instead uses the radial momentum balance above.
