# Thermomechanical effect

↑ **Parent:** [Two-fluid model of superfluidity](two-fluid-model-of-superfluidity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_effect)

The [thermomechanical effect](thermomechanical-effect.md) couples a [temperature](temperature-split.md) difference to a [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference across a [superleak](superleak.md). For the closed-chamber model in the apparatus figure, consider neighboring equilibrium states with small [velocities](velocity.md), negligible height difference and pure [helium-4](helium-4.md). Let $\mu_m$ be [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) per unit [mass](mass.md), in $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. The [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) differential per unit [mass](mass.md) gives

$$
d\mu_m=-s\,dT+\frac{1}{\rho}\,dP.
$$

The [superleak](superleak.md) allows the [superfluid](superfluid.md) to redistribute until the two sides have equal $\mu_m$. Setting $d\mu_m=0$ therefore gives

$$
\left.\frac{dP}{dT}\right|_{\mu_m}=\rho s>0,\qquad
P_h-P_c=\int_{T_c}^{T_h}\rho(T,P(T))s(T,P(T))\,dT.
$$

The sign matters: heating one side at initially equal [pressure](pressure-split.md) lowers its $\mu_m$; inflow raises its [pressure](pressure-split.md) until chemical balance is restored. The hotter side has the higher [pressure](pressure-split.md). The [integral](integral-split.md) follows the constant-$\mu_m$ path; it need not be evaluated at fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md) when material properties vary appreciably.

For an original local example, stipulate constant $\rho=150\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $s=100\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $T_h-T_c=0.020\,\mathrm K$. The predicted [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference is $300\,\mathrm{Pa}$. These are teaching assumptions, not helium measurements. This static [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md) relation predicts neither the required heater [power](power.md) nor the filling time: those require [heat](heat-split.md) leakage, channel conductance and chamber [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md). An outlet would introduce [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and a flowing balance, so the static $300\,\mathrm{Pa}$ is not a predicted fountain height.
