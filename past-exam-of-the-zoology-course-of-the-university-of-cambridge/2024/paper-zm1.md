**A node-based name selects a common ancestor from included reference taxa; a branch-based name selects a lineage by including one reference and excluding another.** Both designate a [clade](../../biology.md#clade), so both include every descendant of the selected ancestor. The distinction concerns the rule locating that ancestor, not whether descendants are included. Neither requires its members to share a particular modern appearance.[Fig. 1 and Introduction; minimum- and maximum-clade definitions and internal/external specifiers](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-new-phylogenetic-data-standard-for-computable-clade-definitions-the-phyloreference-exchange-format-phyx-by-gaurav-vaidya-and-others)

For a [node-based clade definition](../../biology.md#node-based-clade-definition), choose internal [phylogenetic specifiers](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-specifier) $A$ and $B$. Let $m(A,B)$ be their [last common ancestor](../../biology.md#last-common-ancestor), and let $D(v)$ include ancestor $v$ and all its descendants. Then the named group is

$$
N(A,B)=D\bigl(m(A,B)\bigr).
$$

It is the smallest [clade](../../biology.md#clade) containing both references. For a [branch-based clade definition](../../biology.md#branch-based-clade-definition), choose an internal reference $A$ and an external reference $Z$. The named group is the largest [clade](../../biology.md#clade) containing $A$ but excluding $Z$: organisms sharing a more recent common ancestor with $A$ than with $Z$. On a rooted [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree), this selects the $A$-side lineage after its separation from the lineage containing $Z$. These are also called [minimum-clade definitions](../../biology.md#node-based-clade-definition) and [maximum-clade definitions](../../biology.md#branch-based-clade-definition). The names describe inclusion constraints, not distances measured from branch lengths.[Fig. 1 and Introduction; minimum- and maximum-clade definitions and internal/external specifiers](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-new-phylogenetic-data-standard-for-computable-clade-definitions-the-phyloreference-exchange-format-phyx-by-gaurav-vaidya-and-others)

An informative avian node example is **crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves)**, here anchored to living [ostrich](../../organism.md#ostrich) and [sparrow](../../organism.md#sparrow) lineages. Their [last common ancestor](../../biology.md#last-common-ancestor) is the ancestor of living [Palaeognathae](../../organism.md#palaeognathae) and [Neognathae](../../organism.md#neognathae); the name includes that ancestor and all descendants, including extinct descendants within either branch. An informative branch example is **[Panaves](../../organism.md#panaves)**, the avian [total group](../../biology.md#total-group): the largest [clade](../../biology.md#clade) including living [birds](../../organism.md#bird) but excluding living [crocodilians](../../organism.md#crocodilian). With standard [archosaur](../../organism.md#archosauria) relationships it includes [Aves](../../organism.md#aves), extinct avian stem relatives, nonavian [dinosaurs](../../organism.md#dinosaur) and [pterosaurs](../../organism.md#pterosaur). The external reference is decisive: the definition asks which side of the [bird](../../organism.md#bird)–[crocodilian](../../organism.md#crocodilian) split an organism occupies, not whether it flies. These crown and total usages were distinguished explicitly in the avian nomenclatural analysis by [Jacques A. Gauthier](../../biology.md#jacques-a-gauthier) and [Kevin de Queiroz](../../biology-author.md#kevin-de-queiroz).[pp. 9–11 and 24–28; classes of definitions, crown versus total groups, Aves and Panaves](../../biology-bibliography.md#feathered-dinosaurs-flying-dinosaurs-crown-dinosaurs-and-the-name-“aves”-by-jacques-a-gauthier-and-kevin-de-queiroz)

Consider a simplified [cladogram](../../biology.md#cladogram) in which the [crocodilian](../../organism.md#crocodilian) lineage separates first, followed on the avian side by [pterosaurs](../../organism.md#pterosaur), successive nonavian [dinosaur](../../organism.md#dinosaur) branches, [Archaeopteryx](../../organism.md#archaeopteryx), other extinct avian branches and finally the living-bird crown divergence. [Archaeopteryx](../../organism.md#archaeopteryx) belongs to [Panaves](../../organism.md#panaves) but lies outside crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves). A [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) on the [palaeognath](../../organism.md#palaeognathae) branch after the [palaeognath](../../organism.md#palaeognathae)–[neognath](../../organism.md#neognathae) divergence belongs to both. A [crocodilian](../../organism.md#crocodilian) belongs to neither. Finding a still earlier bird-line [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) would extend the known [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) history of [Panaves](../../organism.md#panaves) without automatically changing the age or known membership of crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves). This example is schematic: it illustrates how definitions operate and is not a claim that any sampled [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) is the direct ancestor of another.

Two distinctions prevent mistakes. First, a [node-based clade definition](../../biology.md#node-based-clade-definition) need not define a [crown group](../../biology.md#crown-group): internal references can be extinct, as in a deliberately broader Archaeopteryx-inclusive node definition. Likewise, a [branch-based clade definition](../../biology.md#branch-based-clade-definition) need not define an entire [total group](../../biology.md#total-group); using a nonavian [theropod dinosaur](../../organism.md#theropod-dinosaur) as the excluded reference selects a much narrower avian-lineage branch than excluding [crocodilians](../../organism.md#crocodilian). Second, a [stem group](../../biology.md#stem-group) is not synonymous with the branch-defined [total group](../../biology.md#total-group). The total includes the crown; the stem alone excludes it and normally forms a succession of extinct branches.

The definition must therefore accompany an ambiguous name. Historical uses of [Aves](../../organism.md#aves) have included the living-bird crown and more inclusive [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) assemblages; [Avialae](../../organism.md#avialae) has also received differing definitions. Discovery of [feathers](../../biology.md#feather) in a nonavian [theropod dinosaur](../../organism.md#theropod-dinosaur) changes the inferred distribution of a [phylogenetic character](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character), but does not by itself place the animal inside crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves). Conversely, loss of flight does not remove a crown descendant from its [clade](../../biology.md#clade). **A name supplies an ancestry rule; anatomical and molecular evidence supply the tree on which that rule is applied.** Uncertain [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) placement produces uncertain membership even when the definition is precise.

**The terms describe contrasting palatal architectures; their literal “old” and “new” implications do not establish which architecture was ancestral.** A [palaeognathous palate](../../biology.md#palaeognathous-palate) has extensive bracing of the palatal apparatus against the [skull](../../biology.md#skull) base, involving the [vomer](../../biology.md#vomer), [pterygoid bone](../../biology.md#pterygoid-bone), [palatine bone](../../biology.md#palatine-bone) and prominent [basipterygoid processes](../../biology.md#basipterygoid-process). In a [neognathous palate](../../biology.md#neognathous-palate), reduced bracing and a more mobile pterygoid–palatine connection permit greater relative movement. These are arrangements of [joints](../../combinatorics.md#joint-of-a-line-collection) and contacts, not simply differences in lower-jaw shape. The distinction concerns the structural basis of [cranial kinesis](../../biology.md#cranial-kinesis), movement within the [skull](../../biology.md#skull) beyond ordinary opening of the lower [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw).[Results and Discussion; vomer contacts, morphological disparity and limits of functional reconstruction](../../biology-bibliography.md#evolution-of-the-vomer-and-its-implications-for-cranial-kinesis-in-paraves-by-han-hu-and-others)

Living [Palaeognathae](../../organism.md#palaeognathae), including [ostriches](../../organism.md#ostrich) and [tinamous](../../organism.md#tinamou), and living [Neognathae](../../organism.md#neognathae), including [landfowl](../../organism.md#landfowl), [waterfowl](../../organism.md#waterfowl) and other [birds](../../organism.md#bird), form the deepest two surviving branches of crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves). Historically, the heavily braced condition was commonly interpreted as retention of a primitive nonavian configuration, followed by [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) of a more mobile [palate](../../biology.md#palate) in [Neognathae](../../organism.md#neognathae). That interpretation combined resemblance to [outgroups](../../biology.md#outgroup) with the misleading intuition that a lineage branching early must preserve the ancestral [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy). A sister lineage has had just as much time to change; its living members are not ancestors of the other branch.

The difficulty is establishing [character polarity](../../biology.md#character-polarity) when delicate palatal elements are absent, crushed or wrongly identified in [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) near the crown divergence. Comparisons of the [vomer](../../biology.md#vomer) across living and extinct [birds](../../organism.md#bird) documented meaningful differences in contact geometry but also substantial variation and repeated reduction. The 2019 analysis by [Han Hu](../../biology.md#han-hu) and colleagues emphasized how incomplete contacts restrict inferences about extinct [cranial kinesis](../../biology.md#cranial-kinesis). A single robust-looking element cannot establish an immobile [skull](../../biology.md#skull), and one potentially mobile [joint](../../combinatorics.md#joint-of-a-line-collection) cannot establish the full range of movement. Constraints elsewhere in the kinetic chain remain relevant.[Results and Discussion; vomer contacts, morphological disparity and limits of functional reconstruction](../../biology-bibliography.md#evolution-of-the-vomer-and-its-implications-for-cranial-kinesis-in-paraves-by-han-hu-and-others)

The [Ichthyornis](../../organism.md#ichthyornis) material described in 2018 helped separate components of the supposedly modern avian head. Three-dimensionally preserved [skull](../../biology.md#skull) elements combined a small beak at the [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw) tips and retained [teeth](../../biology.md#tooth) with a reconstructed kinetic apparatus, while other cranial regions remained markedly dinosaurian. Thus [tooth](../../biology.md#tooth) loss, beak expansion and acquisition of a mobile feeding apparatus did not form one indivisible evolutionary event. This example of [mosaic evolution](../../biology.md#mosaic-evolution) also weakens the argument that a toothed stem [bird](../../organism.md#bird) must have a fully rigid, palaeognath-like [palate](../../biology.md#palate). Its importance is the combination of characters, rather than an inference that every modern kinetic mechanism was already identical.[Nature 557, pp. 96–100; mosaic cranial construction and inferred kinetic apparatus](../../biology-bibliography.md#complete-ichthyornis-skull-illuminates-mosaic-assembly-of-the-avian-head-by-daniel-j-field-and-others)

The strongest recent change came from [Janavis finalidens](../../organism.md#janavis-finalidens), a toothed [bird](../../organism.md#bird) from approximately 66.7 million years ago, described in 2022. [micro-computed tomography](../../inverse-problem.md#micro-computed-tomography) exposed a well-preserved [pterygoid bone](../../biology.md#pterygoid-bone) within the specimen. Its articular construction and comparative shape resemble those of [Galloanserae](../../organism.md#galloanserae), supporting a mobile pterygoid–palatine connection. The animal was recovered outside the living-bird [crown group](../../biology.md#crown-group), close to [Ichthyornis](../../organism.md#ichthyornis), rather than inside [Neognathae](../../organism.md#neognathae). Consequently, [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy) previously treated as a [neognath](../../organism.md#neognathae) innovation occurred on an earlier branch. On that reconstruction, mobility evolved before crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves), and the tightly integrated [palaeognathous palate](../../biology.md#palaeognathous-palate) is a subsequent specialization of the [palaeognath](../../organism.md#palaeognathae) lineage.[Nature 612, pp. 100–105; Janavis pterygoid, comparative shape and ancestral-palate interpretation](../../biology-bibliography.md#cretaceous-ornithurine-supports-a-neognathous-crown-bird-ancestor-by-juan-benito-and-others)

The inference is best understood by separating observation, placement and reconstruction. The observation is the morphology of a [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) [pterygoid bone](../../biology.md#pterygoid-bone). Its placement outside crown [Aves](../../organism.md#aves) comes from a wider analysis of [phylogenetic characters](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character) using [phylogenetics](../../biology.md#phylogenetics). The inference that the crown ancestor had a relatively mobile [palate](../../biology.md#palate) combines those findings with distributions of [phylogenetic characters](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character) in living groups. None alone proves the entire history. Convergence in the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) lineage remains a logical alternative; it would require a different pattern of character acquisition from ancestral mobility followed by increased bracing. Distinguishing these alternatives requires additional palatal characters and [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) sampling, not the age of the specimen alone. A 66.7-million-year-old stem [bird](../../organism.md#bird) need not predate the crown ancestor chronologically: stem and crown lineages can coexist.

Functional and developmental caution also matters. [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) generally lack [ligaments](../../biology.md#ligament), [muscles](../../biology.md#muscle) and direct measurements of movement. Comparative [joint](../../combinatorics.md#joint-of-a-line-collection) geometry can support potential mobility, but cannot yield exact excursions or feeding performance without further assumptions. The [palaeognathous palate](../../biology.md#palaeognathous-palate) should not be reduced to total [skull](../../biology.md#skull) immobility, nor every [neognathous palate](../../biology.md#neognathous-palate) to one uniform kinetic mechanism. **The defensible revision is that important neognath-like palatal features originated before the living-bird crown split, making [palaeognath](../../organism.md#palaeognathae) specializations a plausible derived condition.** The two living [clades](../../biology.md#clade) remain valid; what changes is the inferred history of the anatomical states used to describe them.

**The [frog](../../organism.md#frog) skeleton evolved through [mosaic evolution](../../biology.md#mosaic-evolution): reorganization of the head and [pelvis](../../biology.md#pelvis) preceded completion of the compact, tailless, long-limbed jumping apparatus.** Its principal transformations are shortening and stiffening of the [axial skeleton](../../biology.md#axial-skeleton), conversion of the posterior body into a [sacrum](../../biology.md#sacrum)–[pelvis](../../biology.md#pelvis)–[urostyle](../../biology.md#urostyle) system, and specialization of the [appendicular skeleton](../../biology.md#appendicular-skeleton) for propulsion and landing. These are connected mechanically, but need not have evolved simultaneously or initially served the same function.

In the derived adult [frog](../../organism.md#frog), the trunk is short, most species having eight or nine [presacral vertebrae](../../biology.md#presacral-vertebra), and the [ribs](../../biology.md#rib) are strongly reduced or absent. The adult tail no longer consists of a long freely jointed series. Instead, the [urostyle](../../biology.md#urostyle) forms a posterior axial rod from caudal vertebral and associated developmental components. This increases the integration of the posterior body with the [pelvic girdle](../../biology.md#pelvic-girdle). The [ilium](../../biology.md#ilium) has an elongated, anteriorly directed shaft, placing much of the pelvic apparatus posterior to its sacral connection. Changes in the [sacroiliac joint](../../biology.md#sacroiliac-joint) and adjacent musculature permit transmission of bilateral [hindlimb](../../biology.md#hindlimb) thrust to the trunk. The [urostyle](../../biology.md#urostyle) is therefore more than a tail that simply shrank: it is part of a reorganized force-transmission system.

The [limbs](../../biology.md#vertebrate-limb) change in both proportion and construction. Elongate [hindlimbs](../../biology.md#hindlimb) increase the possible extension stroke, and elongated proximal ankle elements, the [tibiale](../../biology.md#tibiale) and [fibulare](../../biology.md#fibulare), add another effective segment to the propulsive chain. Union of [tibia](../../biology.md#tibia) and [fibula](../../biology.md#fibula) into a [tibiofibula](../../biology.md#tibiofibula) and of [radius bone](../../biology.md#radius-bone) and [ulna](../../biology.md#ulna) into a [radioulna](../../biology.md#radioulna) stiffens the lower [limb](../../biology.md#vertebrate-limb) segments. The [forelimbs](../../organism.md#forelimb) and [pectoral girdle](../../biology.md#pectoral-girdle) participate in support and absorption of landing forces, while the [frog](../../organism.md#frog) head is typically broad, short and flattened, with reduction and fusion of cranial elements, including integration of frontal and parietal components into a [frontoparietal bone](../../biology.md#frontoparietal-bone). These are generalizations about the characteristic skeleton, not a claim that every living [frog](../../organism.md#frog) is an obligate jumper: swimming, burrowing, climbing and walking lineages modify the inherited apparatus.

The decisive stem example is **[Triadobatrachus massinoti](../../organism.md#triadobatrachus-massinoti)**, from the Early [Triassic](../../planetary-science.md#triassic) of Madagascar, about 250 million years ago. It belongs to the [frog](../../organism.md#frog) [total group](../../biology.md#total-group), [Salientia](../../organism.md#salientia), outside crown [Anura](../../organism.md#anura). Its phylogenetic importance is the association of recognizable frog-lineage features with retained ancestral construction. An anteriorly elongated [ilium](../../biology.md#ilium) and frog-like cranial organization were already present, while a short series of separate caudal [vertebrae](../../biology.md#vertebra) persisted. The forearm and lower-leg pairs were not united into the modern [radioulna](../../biology.md#radioulna) and [tibiofibula](../../biology.md#tibiofibula), and the [hindlimbs](../../biology.md#hindlimb) lacked the full proportional specialization of powerful living jumpers. Thus pelvic reorganization did not require that all the later [limb](../../biology.md#vertebrate-limb) and tail modifications had already evolved.

The 2016 [micro-computed tomography](../../inverse-problem.md#micro-computed-tomography) redescription is especially important because it revises the evidence rather than merely repeating an iconic reconstruction. It recognized **15 [presacral vertebrae](../../biology.md#presacral-vertebra), with an [atlas vertebra](../../biology.md#atlas-vertebra)–[axis vertebra](../../biology.md#axis-vertebra) complex**, replacing the traditional interpretation of 14 with a divided atlas. Its trunk was therefore substantially less abbreviated in vertebral count than that of a typical living [frog](../../organism.md#frog). Reconstructions under alternative [amphibian](../../organism.md#amphibian) relationships suggested that some proportional trunk reduction may already have occurred before the [frog](../../organism.md#frog)–[salamander](../../organism.md#salamander) split, preceding the iliac elongation and tail shortening seen in this [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil). That inference depends on the comparative tree and model; vertebral number and actual trunk proportions are different characters. A count alone cannot demonstrate exactly how flexibly the animal moved.[Contributions to Zoology 85, pp. 201–234; revised vertebral count, axial reconstruction and trunk-length analysis](../../biology-bibliography.md#triadobatrachus-massinoti-the-earliest-known-lissamphibian-vertebrata-tetrapoda-re-examined-by-mu-ct-scan-and-the-evolution-of-trunk-length-in-batrachians-by-eduardo-ascarrunz-and-others)

Locomotor inference requires an additional test. In 2016, [Andrés I. Lires](../../biology.md#andres-i-lires) and colleagues compared major limb-bone lengths across 188 living limbed [amphibians](../../organism.md#amphibian) and [lizards](../../organism.md#lizard), relating their proportions to observed locomotor categories while considering shared ancestry. [Triadobatrachus massinoti](../../organism.md#triadobatrachus-massinoti) was better supported as using salamander-like lateral undulatory [locomotion](../../biology.md#locomotion) than as a specialized jumper. This is evidence against assuming that each frog-like skeletal feature originated as an adaptation for [saltation](../../biology.md#saltation). The limitations are that [bone](../../biology.md#bone) proportions are proxies for performance, living comparison sets may omit extinct combinations, and soft tissues and direct behaviour remain unknown. Failure to support specialized jumping does not exclude every short hop or vigorous [hindlimb](../../biology.md#hindlimb) movement.[Paleobiology 42, pp. 612–623; limb proportions of 188 living amphibians and lizards and fossil locomotor inference](../../biology-bibliography.md#walk-before-you-jump-new-insights-on-early-frog-locomotion-from-the-oldest-known-salientian-by-andres-i-lires-and-others)

Comparison with [Prosalirus bitis](../../organism.md#prosalirus-bitis), from the Early [Jurassic](../../planetary-science.md#jurassic), provides a later anatomical checkpoint. Its caudopelvic construction includes a [urostyle](../../biology.md#urostyle), rod-like iliac organization and elongate [hindlimbs](../../biology.md#hindlimb), and was interpreted as supporting jumping through changes in pelvic orientation and iliosacral mobility. This comparison reinforces a sequence in which early frog-lineage pelvic and cranial modifications preceded completion of the more specialized locomotor system. It does not turn the named [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) into a direct ancestor–descendant chain, nor require every [phylogenetic character](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character) to have followed exactly the same history in every branch.[Nature 377, pp. 49–52; Prosalirus pelvis, urostyle and reconstruction of saltation](../../biology-bibliography.md#an-early-jurassic-jumping-frog-by-neil-h-shubin-and-farish-a-jenkins-jr)

**[Triadobatrachus massinoti](../../organism.md#triadobatrachus-massinoti) separates [frog](../../organism.md#frog) ancestry from fully developed [frog](../../organism.md#frog) jumping.** It documents a combination that comparison of living [frogs](../../organism.md#frog) and [salamanders](../../organism.md#salamander) alone could not predict reliably. The appropriate evolutionary explanation allows early changes in body support or movement to become [exaptations](../../biology.md#exaptation) for later [saltation](../../biology.md#saltation), followed by stronger specialization of the [limbs](../../biology.md#vertebrate-limb) and caudopelvic apparatus. Its retained tail and incompletely fused appendicular elements demonstrate staged assembly, while the revised vertebral interpretation shows why the ordering must remain anchored to observed [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy).

**[taphonomy](../../biology.md#taphonomy) studies the transformation of biological remains from death or production into the material ultimately recovered and interpreted.** It explains how a living organism or community becomes an incomplete geological sample. For shed structures or [trace fossils](../../biology.md#trace-fossil), production of the evidence need not involve the death of the producer. Its relevance therefore extends beyond asking whether a skeleton was buried intact.

Three primary process classes are **decay; physical [disarticulation](../../biology.md#disarticulation) and transport; and burial alteration, including [diagenetic mineralization](../../biology.md#diagenetic-mineralization)**. The boundaries are operational and the mechanisms interact: [post-mortem decay](../../biology.md#post-mortem-decay) can continue after burial, and previously buried remains can be reworked and transported again. [biostratinomy](../../biology.md#biostratinomy) groups events before final burial, whereas [diagenesis](../../biology.md#diagenesis) groups subsequent physical and chemical changes. Those are stages or settings, rather than three mutually exclusive mechanisms.

First, [post-mortem decay](../../biology.md#post-mortem-decay) transforms tissues by [autolysis](../../biology.md#autolysis), microbial decomposition and associated chemical reactions. Soft tissues, [connective tissues](../../biology.md#connective-tissue) and different [phylogenetic characters](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character) disappear at different rates. The important result is not simply that hard parts survive longer: a partly decayed organism can acquire a misleading anatomical combination. Experiments on [amphioxus](../../organism.md#amphioxus) and [lamprey](../../organism.md#lamprey) larvae followed recognizable characters through decomposition. Derived diagnostic features tended to disappear before some more general chordate features, producing a directional bias. Coding every unobserved diagnostic character as ancestrally absent can push a genuinely derived [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) toward an earlier position on a [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree), the effect called [stemward slippage](../../biology.md#stemward-slippage).[Nature 463, pp. 797–800; amphioxus and ammocoete decay sequences and stemward slippage](../../biology-bibliography.md#non-random-decay-of-chordate-characters-causes-bias-in-fossil-interpretation-by-robert-s-sansom-and-others)

Experiments on adult [hagfish](../../organism.md#hagfish) and [lampreys](../../organism.md#lamprey) extended the comparison beyond larvae. They documented transformations as well as disappearance, so a residual stain or collapsed structure should not automatically be identified as an intact organ. Replicate specimens sampled through time are needed because destructive observations cannot follow every internal character in the same individual. For a new experimental test, temperature, water chemistry, microbial conditions and starting condition should be recorded and, where tested, varied independently. A laboratory sequence supplies constraints on interpretations; it is not a universal clock assigning an exact post-mortem duration to every [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil).[Results and Discussion; transformations during decay and non-random loss of diagnostic characters](../../biology-bibliography.md#decay-of-vertebrate-characters-in-hagfish-and-lamprey-cyclostomata-and-the-implications-for-the-vertebrate-fossil-record-by-robert-s-sansom-and-others)

Second, [disarticulation](../../biology.md#disarticulation), fragmentation and [taphonomic transport](../../biology.md#taphonomic-transport) alter which elements occur together and where they accumulate. Loss of [ligaments](../../biology.md#ligament) releases [bones](../../biology.md#bone), while currents or scavengers can move them. Shape, density, size, articulation and orientation influence transportability, so a concentration of robust elements need not represent the most abundant living species or the original place of death. Flow experiments and comparison of element representation, as developed in [Michael R. Voorhies](../../biology.md#michael-r-voorhies)'s [vertebrate](../../organism.md#vertebrate) assemblage study, provide a basis for testing such alternatives. A useful comparison measures elements under specified flow and [sediment](../../planetary-science.md#sediment) conditions, with articulated versus disarticulated material distinguished. [hydraulic sorting](../../biology.md#hydraulic-sorting) cannot be transferred unchanged between all animals and environments.[transport experiments and bone assemblage interpretation; transportability varies among skeletal elements](../../biology-bibliography.md#taphonomy-and-population-dynamics-of-an-early-pliocene-vertebrate-fauna-knox-county-nebraska-by-michael-r-voorhies)

The consequences include mixing habitats and ages. An apparently coherent [bone](../../biology.md#bone) bed may combine local and transported animals; [reworking of fossils](../../biology.md#reworking-of-fossils) can place older remains in younger [sediment](../../planetary-science.md#sediment); [time averaging](../../biology.md#time-averaging) can combine successive communities into one assemblage. None implies that every [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) assemblage is hopelessly mixed. Articulation, abrasion, orientation, size distributions, sedimentary context and independent age information can instead delimit the plausible history. Abrasion alone is an imperfect transport-distance meter because material properties and the transport medium also matter.

Third, burial changes both preservation and geometry. Rapid isolation can reduce [scavenging](../../biology.md#scavenging), but burial does not halt all decomposition. [sedimentary compaction](../../biology.md#sedimentary-compaction) flattens and fractures remains; [dissolution](../../ionic-bonding.md#dissolution) removes mineralized tissues; [precipitation](../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry) can infill pores or replicate tissues; [fossil recrystallization](../../biology.md#fossil-recrystallization) can erase original microscopic detail. A [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) can therefore retain an excellent outline but lack its original chemistry, or preserve [histology](../../biology.md#histology) while its overall shape has been distorted. Mineralized replacement is not the only route to [fossilization](../../biology.md#fossilization), and surviving original hard parts need not have undergone complete replacement.

Experiments by [Derek E. G. Briggs](../../biology.md#derek-e-g-briggs) and [Amanda J. Kear](../../biology.md#amanda-j-kear) illustrate constructive as well as destructive [diagenesis](../../biology.md#diagenesis). Decaying shrimps developed [diagenetic mineralization](../../biology.md#diagenetic-mineralization) by [calcium phosphate](../../chemical-substance.md#calcium-phosphate) preserving [muscle](../../biology.md#muscle) detail, particularly in oxygen-restricted systems, with the animal itself supplying [phosphate](../../chemical-substance.md#phosphate). [diagenetic mineralization](../../biology.md#diagenetic-mineralization) began on a timescale of weeks. This demonstrates a mechanism by which decay-associated chemistry can stabilize fine structures before they disappear. It does not show that low [oxygen](../../chemical-substance.md#oxygen) guarantees preservation: chemical availability, acidity, tissue composition and the relative rates of destruction and [precipitation](../../chemical-reaction.md#precipitation-chemistry) still control the outcome.[Science 259, pp. 1439–1442; shrimp decay, endogenous phosphate and early muscle phosphatization](../../biology-bibliography.md#fossilization-of-soft-tissue-in-the-laboratory-by-derek-e-g-briggs-and-amanda-j-kear)

These mechanisms are indispensable for reconstructing the history of life. A [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil)'s first occurrence is a minimum observation of a lineage, not automatically its evolutionary origin. A sudden appearance of soft-bodied organisms can reflect a new window of [exceptional preservation](../../biology.md#exceptional-preservation), while disappearance of a [phylogenetic character](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-character) can reflect its destruction rather than evolutionary loss. Relative [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) abundance reflects both biological abundance and the probabilities of production, preservation, exposure and recovery. Comparisons of diversity through time must therefore consider changes in environments, preservational pathways and sampling. **The [fossil record](../../biology.md#fossil-record) is filtered evidence whose reliability can be tested; [taphonomy](../../biology.md#taphonomy) identifies which biological conclusions that evidence can support.**

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

**Living [cartilaginous fishes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes) are specialized descendants, not a surviving anatomical stage before the origin of bony [vertebrates](../../organism.md#vertebrate).** [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) discoveries have shifted several apparently “bony-fish” features toward the ancestry of all living jawed [vertebrates](../../organism.md#vertebrate). Their absence in living [Chondrichthyes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes) can consequently represent [character loss](../../biology.md#character-loss), while other [shark](../../organism.md#shark) features remain ancestral or are new specializations. The change is a revision of [character polarity](../../biology.md#character-polarity), not a claim that every aspect of [shark](../../organism.md#shark) [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy) is reduced.

A simple comparison of living [sharks](../../organism.md#shark) with [Osteichthyes](../../organism.md#osteichthyes) can suggest a one-way sequence: an ancestor with a cartilaginous internal skeleton, followed by origin of extensive [bone](../../biology.md#bone) on the bony-vertebrate branch. That inference is insecure because the two living sister lineages can both have changed, and living jawless [outgroups](../../biology.md#outgroup) can also have lost structures. The [fossil record](../../biology.md#fossil-record) samples extinct combinations near the relevant divergences. To determine whether a structure is a gain or loss, one must place those combinations on a rooted [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree) and distinguish anatomical absence from poor preservation.

The early [gnathostome](../../organism.md#gnathostome) problem involves four familiar assemblages: [placoderms](../../organism.md#placoderm), [acanthodians](../../organism.md#acanthodian), [Chondrichthyes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes) and [Osteichthyes](../../organism.md#osteichthyes). Treating all four as separate complete [clades](../../biology.md#clade) can conceal transitions occurring along the stems of surviving groups. In particular, many newer analyses place [acanthodians](../../organism.md#acanthodian) as successive branches on the chondrichthyan stem. Their name “spiny [sharks](../../organism.md#shark)” is not itself evidence for that placement; cranial, scale, [fin](../../biology.md#fin) and other characters must support it. If this topology is correct, the large difference between living [sharks](../../organism.md#shark) and early armoured jawed fishes includes a history of reduction within the cartilaginous-fish lineage.

The 2012 redescription of the [Acanthodes](../../organism.md#acanthodes) braincase illustrates why anatomical interpretation and tree position must be kept separate. It revealed a mixture of conditions previously assigned to different living groups and argued for shark-like aspects of the [last common ancestor](../../biology.md#last-common-ancestor) of modern jawed [vertebrates](../../organism.md#vertebrate). Its analysis still placed [Acanthodes](../../organism.md#acanthodes) on the osteichthyan stem, rather than treating every [acanthodian](../../organism.md#acanthodian) as a stem chondrichthyan. Thus a resemblance initially interpreted as an osteichthyan specialization could instead be an ancestral feature retained in more than one lineage. Later hypotheses should not be projected retrospectively onto that study.[Nature 486, pp. 247–250; braincase redescription and changes in inferred character polarity](../../biology-bibliography.md#acanthodes-and-shark-like-conditions-in-the-last-common-ancestor-of-modern-gnathostomes-by-samuel-p-davis-and-others)

[Entelognathus primordialis](../../organism.md#entelognathus-primordialis), described in 2013 from the [Silurian](../../biology.md#silurian) of China, supplied a particularly important association: an armoured placoderm-like body and [skull](../../biology.md#skull) with large marginal [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw) [bones](../../biology.md#bone) identifiable as [premaxilla](../../biology.md#premaxilla), [maxilla](../../biology.md#maxilla) and [dentary](../../biology.md#dentary). Such [bones](../../biology.md#bone) had been treated as characteristic innovations of [Osteichthyes](../../organism.md#osteichthyes). Placement of this animal near the top of the jawed-vertebrate stem, together with recovery of [acanthodians](../../organism.md#acanthodian) on the chondrichthyan stem, instead supported a macromeric, extensively ossified dermal ancestor. On that reconstruction, the small-scale-dominated covering and absence of those large [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw) [bones](../../biology.md#bone) in living [Chondrichthyes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes) reflect reduction and reorganization rather than simple retention of a primitively unossified condition.[Nature 502, pp. 188–193; Entelognathus jaw bones, macromeric dermal skeleton and acanthodian placement](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-silurian-placoderm-with-osteichthyan-like-marginal-jaw-bones-by-min-zhu-and-others)

[Janusiscus schultzei](../../organism.md#janusiscus-schultzei), described in 2015 from the Early [Devonian](../../biology.md#devonian), reinforced the point using a different anatomical region. Its combination of a bony dermal [skull](../../biology.md#skull) roof and informative internal cranial conditions supported a position outside crown [gnathostomes](../../organism.md#gnathostome). Braincase [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy) therefore did not align neatly with the traditional picture of a primitive [shark](../../organism.md#shark) head versus a derived bony-fish head. Together, these [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) move apparently osteichthyan-like states closer to the common ancestry of both surviving groups. Their significance comes from character combinations and placement, not from choosing whichever living animal looks most similar.[Nature 520, pp. 82–85; Janusiscus braincase, skull roof and character-state reconstruction](../../biology-bibliography.md#osteichthyan-like-cranial-conditions-in-an-early-devonian-stem-gnathostome-by-sam-giles-and-others)

A separate question concerns internal skeletal tissue. **[dermal bone](../../biology.md#dermal-bone), [perichondral bone](../../biology.md#perichondral-bone), [endochondral bone](../../biology.md#endochondral-bone) and [calcified cartilage](../../biology.md#calcified-cartilage) must not be conflated.** [endochondral bone](../../biology.md#endochondral-bone) forms during replacement of a cartilaginous template; [perichondral bone](../../biology.md#perichondral-bone) forms around it; [calcified cartilage](../../biology.md#calcified-cartilage) remains a different tissue despite being mineralized. Therefore a bony dermal [skull](../../biology.md#skull) roof alone cannot establish endochondral [ossification](../../biology.md#ossification) in the same animal.

The 2020 description of [Minjinia turgenensis](../../organism.md#minjinia-turgenensis), from the Early [Devonian](../../biology.md#devonian) of Mongolia, addressed that distinction. [micro-computed tomography](../../inverse-problem.md#micro-computed-tomography) revealed extensive internal trabecular tissue in the braincase, interpreted as [endochondral bone](../../biology.md#endochondral-bone), rather than just an external bony shell. Its recovery close to the crown divergence permits endochondral [ossification](../../biology.md#ossification) to be reconstructed before the separation of [Chondrichthyes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes) and [Osteichthyes](../../organism.md#osteichthyes), followed by loss or marked suppression along the chondrichthyan lineage. This conclusion depends on identification of the tissue and the animal's position; the absence of a more complete skeleton and uncertainty among early jawed-vertebrate trees constrain how strongly it can be generalized.[Nature Ecology & Evolution 4, pp. 1477–1484; Minjinia internal trabeculae, tomography and ancestral-state sensitivity](../../biology-bibliography.md#endochondral-bone-in-an-early-devonian-‘placoderm’-from-mongolia-by-martin-d-brazeau-and-others)

The resulting inference is conditional but testable. If well-supported stem relatives and the bony-vertebrate branch possess a particular homologous tissue, while living cartilaginous fishes lack it, an ancestral presence followed by loss can fit better than several independent origins. Moving a crucial [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) to another branch, changing a tissue identification or allowing different transition rates can change the ancestral reconstruction. [maximum parsimony](../../biology.md#maximum-parsimony) is useful but is not proof that losses and gains have equal biological probability. Character coding should record unknown preservation as unknown, rather than use an unobserved delicate feature as evidence of absence.

**The major revision is from a ladder of increasing [ossification](../../biology.md#ossification) to a branching history of gains, retention and losses.** It concerns particular tissues and anatomical elements: reduction of large [dermal bones](../../biology.md#dermal-bone) need not coincide exactly with loss of endochondral [ossification](../../biology.md#ossification). [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) reveal that apparently simple modern [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy) can be secondarily simplified, and that major living-group differences may reflect transformations on both sides of a divergence. Continued uncertainty over [placoderm](../../organism.md#placoderm) relationships and individual tissue homologies defines the comparisons still needed; it does not restore living [sharks](../../organism.md#shark) as unchanged proxies for the jawed-vertebrate ancestor.

**The two classical anatomical hypotheses are derivation from [gill](../../biology.md#gill) arches and differentiation of continuous lateral [fin](../../biology.md#fin) folds. Developmental co-option supplies an additional explanation for shared patterning mechanisms.** The histories of the shoulder support, appendage blade and developmental programme need not be identical. Evaluating them separately allows evidence that initially appears contradictory to describe different components of the same evolutionary transition.

The **[gill-arch hypothesis of paired fin origins](../../biology.md#gill-arch-hypothesis-of-paired-fin-origins)**, associated with [Carl Gegenbaur](../../biology.md#carl-gegenbaur), proposes transformation of a posterior branchial apparatus into a girdle and projecting appendage skeleton. In living [cartilaginous fishes](../../organism.md#chondrichthyes), [branchial rays](../../biology.md#branchial-ray) project from the [gill](../../biology.md#gill) skeleton, inviting comparison with skeletal supports extending from a [pectoral girdle](../../biology.md#pectoral-girdle). The historical archipterygium interpretation treated these as evidence for a gill-to-fin transformation. Positional similarity alone is insufficient: the relevant branchial construction must be ancestral, and a plausible transformation must explain both the supporting girdle and distal [fin](../../biology.md#fin).

Developmental experiments provide stronger evidence for shared mechanisms than simple adult resemblance. Work on the little skate, [Leucoraja erinacea](../../organism.md#leucoraja-erinacea), showed interactions among [Sonic hedgehog](../../biology.md#sonic-hedgehog), [FGF signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#fgf-signalling) and [retinoic acid](../../biology.md#retinoic-acid) in branchial-ray outgrowth and patterning, resembling regulatory interactions in paired appendages. The crucial inference is reuse of a spatial patterning system; expression of the same molecule by itself would be weaker evidence. Functional perturbation helps distinguish an active requirement from a coincidental expression domain.[PNAS 106, pp. 5720–5724; retinoic acid/Shh/Fgf8 regulation of skate branchial rays](../../biology-bibliography.md#shared-developmental-mechanisms-pattern-the-vertebrate-gill-arch-and-paired-fin-skeletons-by-j-andrew-gillis-and-others)

A 2016 study used timed inhibition of [Hedgehog signalling](../../signal-transduction.md#hedgehog-signalling) with [cyclopamine](../../signal-transduction.md#cyclopamine), lineage labelling and measurements of [cell proliferation](../../biological-cell.md#cell-proliferation) in skate [embryos](../../biology.md#embryo). Earlier inhibition disrupted branchial-ray polarity, while later inhibition reduced their production or expansion. [EdU labeling](../../cell-cycle.md#dna-synthesis-labeling-with-edu) provided a proxy for proliferative activity through new [dNA synthesis](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-synthesis), and labelled precursor positions related early arch territories to later [cartilage](../../biology.md#cartilage). Solvent-treated controls are necessary because handling and drug vehicle can alter development; stage matching and general growth measurements are needed to distinguish pattern-specific effects from generalized retardation. These results support parallel roles for the signal in axial organization and progenitor expansion. They do not alone demonstrate that an ancestral [gill](../../biology.md#gill) arch physically moved to become a [fin](../../biology.md#fin).[Development 143, pp. 1313–1317; cyclopamine timing, fate mapping and EdU proliferation assays](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-shared-role-for-sonic-hedgehog-signalling-in-patterning-chondrichthyan-gill-arch-appendages-and-tetrapod-limbs-by-j-andrew-gillis-and-brian-k-hall)

The [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) argument for a pharyngeal contribution was strengthened before this examination by the 2023 analysis of [Kolymaspis sibirica](../../organism.md#kolymaspis-sibirica). [computed tomography](../../inverse-problem.md#computed-tomography) and comparative [anatomy](../../biology.md#anatomy) identified a craniothoracic relationship interpreted as connecting the shoulder to a posterior, sixth [branchial arch](../../biology.md#branchial-arch). This provides evidence for the origin of a component of the [pectoral girdle](../../biology.md#pectoral-girdle), rather than a complete [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) sequence converting [branchial rays](../../biology.md#branchial-ray) into the entire free [fin](../../biology.md#fin). Homology of the articulation and the reconstruction of the head–trunk boundary remain important assumptions. **A pharyngeal contribution to the girdle does not require a wholly pharyngeal origin for every appendicular tissue.**[Nature 623, pp. 550–554; Kolymaspis tomography, craniothoracic articulation and sixth-branchial-arch hypothesis](../../biology-bibliography.md#fossil-evidence-for-a-pharyngeal-origin-of-the-vertebrate-pectoral-girdle-by-martin-d-brazeau-and-others)

The **[lateral fin-fold hypothesis](../../biology.md#lateral-fin-fold-hypothesis)** proposes that the two localized pairs of [fins](../../biology.md#fin) arose through differentiation and regional restriction of continuous bilateral folds along the body wall. It predicts possible precursors extending through intervening pectoral and pelvic regions, with increased regional differentiation and locomotor control later. It does not require an ancestral animal to possess fully developed pectoral and [pelvic girdles](../../biology.md#pelvic-girdle) throughout the fold. A persistent developmental field capable of forming appendages could be evolutionarily restricted even if a continuous fold is not retained in living [embryos](../../biology.md#embryo).

The 2022 description of the jawless [galeaspid](../../organism.md#galeaspid) [Tujiaaspis vividus](../../organism.md#tujiaaspis-vividus) supplied a striking [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) comparison. Articulated specimens preserve continuous paired ventrolateral [fins](../../biology.md#fin) extending from the branchial region toward the tail. They occur outside the group of [osteostracans](../../organism.md#osteostracan) and jawed [vertebrates](../../organism.md#vertebrate) in the reported tree. Models using [computational fluid dynamics](../../fluid-mechanics.md#computational-fluid-dynamics) indicated that the lateral surfaces could provide passive lift. This supports an evolutionary stage with extended lateral appendages before differentiation of localized, actively controlled [pectoral fins](../../biology.md#pectoral-fin); it does not prove powered swimming by the folds or show that the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) itself was a direct ancestor. Surface reconstruction, swimming conditions and the assumed body geometry constrain the functional model.[Nature 609, pp. 959–963, Figs. 1–4; Tujiaaspis lateral fins, fluid dynamics and ancestral-state reconstruction](../../biology-bibliography.md#galeaspid-anatomy-and-the-origin-of-vertebrate-paired-appendages-by-zhikun-gai-and-others)

Other jawless stem relatives sharpen the proposed sequence. [osteostracans](../../organism.md#osteostracan) have differentiated [pectoral fins](../../biology.md#pectoral-fin) without an equivalent localized pelvic pair; jawed [vertebrates](../../organism.md#vertebrate) subsequently include both. This distribution argues against assuming simultaneous origin of the two appendage pairs. In a fin-fold interpretation, anterior differentiation can precede restriction of the remaining competent body-wall region to a pelvic position. [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) supporting that sequence still have to distinguish flexible [fins](../../biology.md#fin) from rigid body-armour extensions and demonstrate their relation to the enclosing skeleton.

The **[median-fin co-option hypothesis](../../biology.md#median-fin-co-option-hypothesis)** addresses where the developmental machinery came from. [median fins](../../biology.md#median-fin) are evolutionarily older than the paired appendages. Comparative work on [shark](../../organism.md#shark) and [lamprey](../../organism.md#lamprey) [embryos](../../biology.md#embryo) found that median-fin development uses aspects of the appendage-patterning toolkit, including regional [Hox gene](../../genetics.md#hox-gene) expression and boundary-associated regulation. In [sharks](../../organism.md#shark), the median-fin skeleton develops largely from [paraxial mesoderm](../../biology.md#paraxial-mesoderm), whereas paired appendage [mesenchyme](../../biology.md#mesenchyme) has major contributions from [lateral plate mesoderm](../../biology.md#lateral-plate-mesoderm). Similar patterning despite different tissue sources supports redeployment of an older regulatory programme. It does not imply that a dorsal [fin](../../biology.md#fin) literally split and migrated down the body wall.[Nature 442, pp. 1033–1037; shark and lamprey median fins, tissue origins and appendage-patterning genes](../../biology-bibliography.md#evidence-that-mechanisms-of-fin-development-evolved-in-the-midline-of-early-vertebrates-by-renata-freitas-and-others)

This is the distinction between [deep homology](../../biology.md#deep-homology) of mechanisms and a claim of whole-organ [biological homology](../../biology.md#biological-homology). Shared regulation could reflect inherited [serial homology](../../biology.md#serial-homology), recruitment of the same programme to a novel site, or a mixture of both. Testing alternatives requires spatial and temporal perturbations, embryonic [lineage tracing](../../biological-cell.md#lineage-tracing), comparison across jawless and jawed [outgroups](../../biology.md#outgroup), and [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) evidence locating the relevant structures. Tissue origins are informative but can change during [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution); neither a shared gene nor a different germ-layer contribution is a self-sufficient verdict.

**The strongest synthesis is component-based:** [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) lateral [fins](../../biology.md#fin) support a body-wall appendage precursor; developmental comparisons support recruitment of an older fin-patterning programme; and developmental plus 2023 [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) evidence supports a pharyngeal contribution to the shoulder. The remaining problem is to establish how those components were assembled and when regional pectoral and pelvic control evolved. This synthesis retains the testable content of the classical hypotheses without treating either a complete gill-to-fin conversion or a literal universal [fin](../../biology.md#fin) fold as already demonstrated.
