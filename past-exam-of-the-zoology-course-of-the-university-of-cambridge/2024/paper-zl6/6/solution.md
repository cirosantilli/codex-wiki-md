<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**Core polarity factors define and maintain spatial domains; tissue-specific effectors translate those domains into different patterns of membrane growth, force, adhesion and cell arrangement.** An apical–basal axis supplies a coordinate system rather than a complete geometric blueprint. Cells with the same orientation can form a flat sheet, a cyst, an elongated tube or a folded [epithelium](../../../../epithelium.md), depending on what is built and remodelled at each domain.

The conserved modules include the [PAR polarity complex](../../../../par-polarity-complex.md), with [PAR-3](../../../../par-3.md), [PAR-6](../../../../par-6.md), [Cdc42](../../../../cdc42.md)-associated regulation and [aPKC](../../../../atypical-protein-kinase-c.md); the [crumbs polarity complex](../../../../crumbs-polarity-complex.md); and basolateral regulators associated with the [scribble polarity complex](../../../../scribble-polarity-complex.md). Scaffolding, recruitment, phosphorylation and mutual antagonism help establish boundaries between domains. The components do not always form one invariant complex at one fixed position: [PAR-3](../../../../par-3.md) can occupy junctional regions differently from other partners, and tissue-specific interactions modify their localization and activity. A shared component list therefore permits different spatial and temporal organizations.

External geometry also provides orientation. Contacts with neighbouring cells and the [basement membrane](../../../../basement-membrane.md), adhesion receptors and the [extracellular matrix](../../../../extracellular-matrix.md) help define which surface should face a lumen and which should contact supporting tissue. Within the membrane plane, further anisotropy can organize elongation or neighbour exchange; [planar cell polarity](../../../../planar-cell-polarity.md) is distinct from the apical–basal axis. Thus a cell can retain [apical-basal polarity](../../../../apical-basal-polarity.md) yet change its planar orientation, dimensions or placement. Conversely, individually polarized cells can fail to align their apical surfaces into one common lumen.

Differential membrane allocation is one route from polarity to shape. Apical and basolateral surfaces can grow at different rates through [polarized membrane trafficking](../../../../polarized-membrane-trafficking.md), recycling and removal. Junction position sets the boundary between those domains, while actomyosin and adhesion alter their areas and mechanical coupling. As a simple geometric description, a cell with smaller apical than basal area can be wedge-shaped; this does not mean that its polarity factors directly encode a wedge. The resulting tissue curvature also depends on cell volume, height, neighbour arrangement and external constraints. The same apical contractile programme can bend a compliant sheet or generate tension in a constrained one.

Three-dimensional epithelial culture provides a clear mechanistic example. In [MDCK](../../../../mdck-cell.md) cyst formation, a [Rab11a](../../../../rab11a.md)/[Rabin8](../../../../rabin8.md)/[Rab8a](../../../../rab8a.md) pathway and the [exocyst](../../../../exocyst.md) coordinate apical membrane delivery with [PAR-3](../../../../par-3.md) and [Cdc42](../../../../cdc42.md) activity at an [apical membrane initiation site](../../../../apical-membrane-initiation-site.md). Perturbing components of this network disrupts formation of the organized apical surface and lumen. [Nature Cell Biology 12, pp. 1035–1045; Rab11a/Rab8a, exocyst, Par3 and Cdc42 at the lumen-initiation site](../../../../a-molecular-network-for-de-novo-generation-of-the-apical-surface-and-lumen-by-david-m-bryant-and-others.md) This connects polarity to a concrete transport process rather than treating a polarity marker as an explanation of shape. Matrix and cell-contact geometry help orient the site, while the trafficking machinery supplies the material needed to build it.

A separate output of related regulators is division orientation. In [Caco-2](../../../../caco-2.md) cysts, [Cdc42](../../../../cdc42.md) depletion disturbed orientation of the [spindle apparatus](../../../../spindle-apparatus.md) and the normal placement of apical surfaces, producing multiple lumina even though individual cells retained apical-basal organization. [Journal of Cell Biology 183, pp. 625–633; cyst polarity, spindle placement and lumen defects](../../../../cdc42-controls-spindle-orientation-to-position-the-apical-surface-during-epithelial-morphogenesis-by-aron-b-jaffe-and-others.md) The important distinction is tissue-level topology versus cell-level polarity. If daughters are placed incorrectly relative to an existing lumen, several locally polarized rosettes can form instead of one enclosure. One should therefore quantify angle of the [spindle apparatus](../../../../spindle-apparatus.md), daughter trajectories and lumen connectivity as well as the localization of a single apical marker. The [MDCK](../../../../mdck-cell.md) trafficking and [Caco-2](../../../../caco-2.md) spindle experiments are complementary models, not interchangeable proof that every [Cdc42](../../../../cdc42.md) phenotype has the same immediate cause.

Tube dimensions provide another output. In the Drosophila trachea, perturbations of [Yurt](../../../../yurt.md) and [Coracle](../../../../coracle.md) affected tube dimensions, and genetic interactions with [Crumbs](../../../../crumbs.md) linked these effects to control of the apical domain. Importantly, assays of the luminal matrix and its secretion distinguished this route from another pathway controlling tube size. [Current Biology 20, pp. 55–61; Yurt/Coracle, Crumbs suppression and luminal-matrix controls](../../../../epithelial-polarity-proteins-regulate-drosophila-tracheal-tube-size-independently-of-the-luminal-matrix-pathway-by-patrick-laprise-and-others.md) Thus a core polarity regulator can affect apical surface extent without simply abolishing epithelial identity or causing every tube phenotype through failed secretion. Cell number, cell shape, apical area and luminal matrix organization must be measured independently before elongation or widening is assigned to a particular mechanism.

An elongated tube additionally requires anisotropic growth or rearrangement. Uniform expansion of every apical surface would enlarge an enclosure but would not uniquely specify a long narrow shape. Tissue-specific regulation of actomyosin, junction turnover, division and matrix resistance can translate the same domain organization into preferential extension. Altering the balance between apical and lateral surface growth can change diameter as well as length. This is why changing the dose or distribution of a core regulator can produce a dimensional phenotype even when the basic apical–basal distinction remains detectable.

Vascular [lumen formation](../../../../lumen-formation.md) illustrates how the effector machinery changes with epithelial context. In the developing mouse aorta, imaging and perturbations connected endothelial contact organization to localization of [CD34-family sialomucin](../../../../cd34-family-sialomucin.md), [Moesin](../../../../moesin.md) and actomyosin at the prospective lumen. [VE-cadherin](../../../../ve-cadherin.md) was required for early organization of luminal components, while [VEGF-A](../../../../vegf-a.md) acted at a different step involving cytoskeletal interactions and cell-shape change. [Developmental Cell 17, pp. 505–515; endothelial polarization, sialomucins, junction remodelling and actomyosin](../../../../the-molecular-basis-of-vascular-lumen-formation-in-the-developing-mouse-aorta-by-boris-strilic-and-others.md) The lumen forms between neighbouring cells rather than being explained solely by death of central cells. This is a case in which adhesion, specialized surface composition and active mechanics convert domain organization into a space; it need not duplicate the cyst mechanism in every detail.

The shared toolkit can also have stage-specific outputs. During initial epithelial assembly, establishing a first apical site is the dominant task. During growth, orienting divisions and supplying membrane preserve an existing architecture. During folding, changing junctional mechanics may become most important. A late perturbation can therefore alter shape without preventing initial polarity, whereas an early perturbation can collapse the entire tissue and conceal more specific downstream roles. Polarity-dependent signalling can also change proliferation or differentiation, so geometry can be altered indirectly through cell composition as well as directly through mechanics.

Experiments should exploit that modularity. Use acute, mosaic or tissue-restricted perturbations; measure viability, cell number and polarity before generalized disorganization; and rescue with constructs that separate localization, catalytic and binding functions. Combine endogenous reporters with [live-cell imaging](../../../../live-cell-imaging.md) of membrane traffic, junctions, the [spindle apparatus](../../../../spindle-apparatus.md) and luminal space. Domain-targeted rescue is especially informative if it restores one geometric output while leaving another defective. Loss of an apical marker, a multiple-lumen phenotype and an abnormally wide tube are different observations and should not all be reported simply as loss of polarity.

**A limited polarity toolkit generates diverse shapes through variable localization, dosage, timing and coupling to tissue-specific effectors and boundary conditions.** Its conservation explains shared organizing principles; its modular outputs explain why conservation does not imply identical [epithelial geometry](../../../../epithelial-geometry.md).
