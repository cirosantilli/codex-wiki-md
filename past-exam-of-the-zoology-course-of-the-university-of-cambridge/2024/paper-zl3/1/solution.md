**[Animal signals](../../../../animal-signal.md) are diverse because their functions, receivers, transmission environments and evolutionary incentives differ. Similar functions can also be achieved by different historical and developmental routes.** An [animal signal](../../../../animal-signal.md) is shaped by selection for its effects on a receiver; an informative incidental feature can instead be a [biological cue](../../../../biological-cue.md). Merely finding that another animal responds to a feature does not establish why it evolved. Diversity includes differences among [signal modalities](../../../../signal-modality.md), differences among [species](../../../../species.md) using the same modality, geographical variation, and variation among individuals or contexts within a population.

Different problems demand different information. [mate choice](../../../../mate-choice.md) can favour information about identity, condition or compatibility; contests can favour assessment of fighting ability or motivation; offspring solicitation concerns need and likely returns to provisioning. Alarm communication can coordinate responses to danger, whereas recruitment can direct partners to resources. A single individual may therefore use several signals because the appropriate receiver and useful message change across activities. The efficiency of a signal is defined relative to a receiver's response and its consequences, not simply the amount of information that a human observer can extract.

Physical transmission strongly constrains [signal modality](../../../../signal-modality.md). Light can provide fine spatial patterning but depends on illumination and line of sight. Sounds can travel around obstacles, yet propagation, reverberation and background noise alter what reaches a receiver. Chemical signals can persist after a signaller leaves, which is useful for some functions and poorly suited to rapid reversal in others. Substrate vibration and electrical stimulation provide additional routes where anatomy and environment permit them. The relevant comparison is the complete communication system: production, transmission, detection and action. A brightly coloured patch is ineffective if it is hidden by posture or cannot be distinguished by the intended receiver.

The environment can consequently drive divergence in signals and sensory systems. In [cichlids](../../../../cichlid.md), variation in the light environment was associated with differences in visual pigments, male colours and female preferences, supporting a role for [sensory drive](../../../../sensory-drive.md) in divergence. [Nature 455, pp. 620–626; light gradients, visual pigments, male colours and preferences](../../../../speciation-through-sensory-drive-in-cichlid-fish-by-ole-seehausen-and-others.md) The inference requires evidence for each link; a correlation between water colour and male colour alone would not establish evolved receiver tuning or its effect on mating. Reciprocal illumination treatments, receptor measurements and preference tests help distinguish sensory mechanisms from population history. Actual mating and [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) provide further evidence about consequences beyond laboratory attraction.

Background interference also changes which signals work. [great tits](../../../../great-tit.md) in noisier urban locations sang with higher minimum frequencies in a classic field comparison. [Nature 424, p. 267, Fig. 1; song frequency and ambient urban noise](../../../../birds-sing-at-a-higher-pitch-in-urban-noise-by-hans-slabbekoorn-and-margriet-peet.md) This illustrates a plausible response to [acoustic masking](../../../../acoustic-masking.md), but the association by itself does not distinguish immediate adjustment, developmental [learning](../../../../learning.md), habitat sorting and inherited change. Playback of controlled noise to the same birds, repeated recordings, and matched quiet sites would separate some of these explanations. Signal amplitude, distance to the microphone and recording conditions also require control. Diverse responses to the same disturbance can reflect different receiver sensitivities or different costs of modifying a signal.

Selection by intended receivers can oppose selection by unintended ones. Male [guppy](../../../../guppy.md) colour patterns are associated with mating advantages but can also increase detection by [predators](../../../../predator.md). Experimental changes in [predation](../../../../predation.md) and visual background altered colour-pattern [evolution](../../../../evolution.md), showing that ecological conditions can change the balance rather than imposing a single universally optimal ornament. [Evolution 34, pp. 76–91; predation and visual-background experiments](../../../../natural-selection-on-color-patterns-in-poecilia-reticulata-by-john-a-endler.md) The intended receiver and [predator](../../../../predator.md) may have different [colour vision](../../../../color-vision.md), so conspicuousness should be modelled and tested separately for each. Increased mating attraction does not establish a net lifetime advantage if survival is substantially reduced. [signal eavesdropping](../../../../signal-eavesdropping.md) thus helps explain why closely related populations can evolve different compromises.

Receiver psychology supplies further routes to diversity. A preference may originate in another task and subsequently be exploited by a new display, a process of [sensory exploitation](../../../../sensory-exploitation.md). Alternatively, a signal and preference may coevolve through [sexual selection](../../../../sexual-selection.md). These hypotheses have different historical predictions, requiring independent phylogenetic evidence about when the preference and display arose. Learned recognition can produce local conventions, while [social learning](../../../../social-learning.md) can maintain differences without an initial genetic difference in the signal. A novel signal can be favoured when familiar ones lose effectiveness, but novelty is not always beneficial: unfamiliar warnings or mate signals may instead be ignored or mistaken for something else.

[signal honesty](../../../../signal-honesty.md) also permits several designs. Reliability can follow aligned interests, physical links between production and an advertised property, or differences in the costs and benefits of expression. An [index signal](../../../../index-signal.md) is constrained by its production mechanism; a condition-dependent signal may be easier for some individuals to sustain. In [costly signalling](../../../../costly-signalling.md), an expense is relevant when it changes incentives to exaggerate. Showing that a display consumes energy is not sufficient: an equally expensive display could still be advantageous to an unreliable signaller. Tests should manipulate both the signal and the advertised property, then measure receiver responses and the resulting fitness effects. Different information problems need not share the same honesty mechanism.

Conflict can maintain deceptive alternatives alongside informative signals. [fork-tailed drongos](../../../../fork-tailed-drongo.md) use varied alarm mimicry to obtain food from other foragers, and changes in signal type can restore responses when targets become less responsive to repetition. [Science 344, pp. 513–516; varied alarm mimicry and responses of foraging targets](../../../../deception-by-flexible-alarm-mimicry-in-an-african-bird-by-tom-p-flower-and-others.md) This demonstrates how receiver experience can favour behavioural variation rather than one permanently effective deception. To separate signal-specific [learning](../../../../learning.md) from declining general responsiveness, experiments need controlled playback order, repeated genuine alarms and measurements of target state. Deception also depends on an otherwise useful response remaining sufficiently common; a response that never benefits its receiver is unlikely to be maintained indefinitely.

Finally, developmental mechanisms and evolutionary history limit the available solutions. Related [species](../../../../species.md) inherit similar production structures and sensory systems, so some resemblance reflects [biological homology](../../../../biological-homology.md). Different lineages may reach similar functional outcomes through [convergent evolution](../../../../convergent-evolution.md). [multimodal signalling](../../../../multimodal-signalling.md) can supply redundancy under variable conditions or combine different information, but additional components also carry production and detection costs. Comparisons should therefore measure both performance and history, account for [phylogenetic nonindependence](../../../../phylogenetic-nonindependence.md), and distinguish within-individual flexibility from inherited divergence.

**Signal diversity is the outcome of several interacting filters: what benefits receivers, what signallers gain, what travels and can be perceived, and what inherited mechanisms can produce.** No single explanation such as mate attraction, environmental adaptation or honesty is expected to account for every component of that diversity.
