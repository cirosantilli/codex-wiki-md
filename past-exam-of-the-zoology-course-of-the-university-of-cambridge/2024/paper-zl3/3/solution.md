**An organism contains genetic elements and cell lineages whose transmission interests need not coincide. Conflict arises when increasing an element's representation in descendants or within the body differs from maximizing the organism's [reproductive success](../../../../reproductive-success.md).** Shared residence does not guarantee shared evolutionary interests, but neither does every difference between [genes](../../../../gene.md) constitute conflict. A convincing account identifies the competing lineages, their transmission routes, and the outcomes favoured by selection on each.

Ordinary [Mendelian segregation](../../../../mendelian-segregation.md) helps align the interests of unlinked nuclear loci: most depend on the same successful organism and receive an approximately equal chance of entering its [gametes](../../../../gamete.md). [intragenomic conflict](../../../../intragenomic-conflict.md) becomes possible when an element bypasses that shared route, biases transmission, replicates within the genome, or has a parent-specific effect on relatives. [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) supplies variation; [genetic linkage](../../../../genetic-linkage.md) can protect combinations involved in distortion, while [recombination](../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) and selection at other loci can favour suppression. The fitness currency of a [selfish genetic element](../../../../selfish-genetic-element.md) is therefore its own propagation, not the apparent health or fecundity of its carrier.

[meiotic drive](../../../../meiotic-drive.md) provides a direct example. For a rare autosomal driver with transmission probabilities $t_f$ through heterozygous females and $t_m$ through heterozygous males, suppose, for illustration, that heterozygotes have the same viability factor $1-s$ in both sexes. With random mating and otherwise equal reproductive contributions, the rare-driver approximation is:

$$
p'\simeq(1-s)(t_f+t_m)p.
$$

A rare driver can increase when the multiplier exceeds one, despite a carrier cost. This accounting also shows why the sex in which drive occurs matters: with unbiased male transmission, $t_m=1/2$, not $t_m=t_f$. The approximation omits dominance in common [genotypes](../../../../genotype.md), sex-specific fertility, linkage and suppressors; it is an explanatory invasion calculation rather than a quantitative model of every natural driver.

Asymmetric female [meiosis](../../../../meiosis.md) creates one route to distortion because some products become eggs and others are discarded. In monkeyflowers, a centromere-associated [allele](../../../../allele.md) showed a strong female transmission advantage, with male fertility costs that expose a conflict between transmission and organismal performance. [Science 322, pp. 1559–1562; female transmission distortion and male fertility costs](../../../../centromere-associated-female-meiotic-drive-entails-male-fitness-costs-in-monkeyflowers-by-lila-fishman-and-arpiar-saunders.md) Reciprocal crosses and appropriate [genetic backgrounds](../../../../genetic-background.md) are important: distorted counts among surviving offspring can result from embryonic viability differences rather than biased segregation. Direct observation or genotyping of [gametes](../../../../gamete.md) and early embryos helps identify the stage. [centromere drive](../../../../centromere-drive.md) is consequently more specific than merely observing [segregation distortion](../../../../segregation-distortion.md).

The evolutionary response can include [drive suppressors](../../../../drive-suppressor.md) at other loci. Suppression is favoured when restoring fair transmission or reducing fertility costs improves the propagation of the rest of the genome. Its effect depends on linkage: a modifier tightly associated with the driver can experience different selection from an unlinked modifier. Arms races can maintain polymorphism and alter reproductive mechanisms; conversely, a successful suppressor can conceal an earlier conflict. Present-day Mendelian ratios therefore do not establish that transmission has always been conflict-free.

[Transposable elements](../../../../transposable-element.md) exploit another route: copying or moving genetic material within a lineage. McClintock's maize experiments established mobile controlling elements through genetic and cytological observations, providing foundational evidence that genomic components need not occupy fixed positions. [PNAS 36, pp. 344–355; genetic and cytological evidence for mobile controlling elements](../../../../the-origin-and-behavior-of-mutable-loci-in-maize-by-barbara-mcclintock.md) Mobility itself is not proof that every element is harmful or selfish in every context. The conflict hypothesis requires a replication or transmission advantage alongside consequences for other genomic functions. Host silencing through mechanisms such as [RNA interference](../../../../rna-interference.md) and [DNA methylation](../../../../dna-methylation.md) can constrain propagation, while some formerly mobile sequences acquire useful regulatory functions. Current usefulness and historical origin are different claims.

[genomic imprinting](../../../../genomic-imprinting.md) illustrates conflict through expression rather than transmission distortion. Maternally and paternally inherited copies can differ in their relatedness to individuals affected by resource allocation. If a mother's future offspring are less likely to share the current father, a paternally inherited copy in the current offspring can place relatively less weight on those future maternal offspring. [kinship theory of genomic imprinting](../../../../kinship-theory-of-genomic-imprinting.md) therefore predicts disagreement over resource extraction under specified mating and life-history assumptions. It does not predict opposed interests at every locus or in every mating system. [Cell 64, pp. 1045–1046; original kinship-conflict interpretation of imprinted growth regulation](../../../../genomic-imprinting-and-the-strange-case-of-the-insulin-like-growth-factor-ii-receptor-by-david-haig-and-c-graham.md)

Targeted disruption of the mouse [insulin-like growth factor 2](../../../../insulin-like-growth-factor-2.md) [gene](../../../../gene.md) produced growth effects dependent on parental transmission, demonstrating parent-of-origin-dependent regulation of an important growth factor. [Cell 64, pp. 849–859; parental transmission of targeted Igf2 disruption and growth](../../../../parental-imprinting-of-the-mouse-insulin-like-growth-factor-ii-gene-by-thomas-m-dechiara-and-others.md) This is mechanistic evidence for imprinting. It is not, by itself, proof that parental genetic conflict caused the evolutionary origin of that regulatory pattern. Testing that explanation requires comparisons among related [species](../../../../species.md) and mating systems, tissue-specific expression, effects on maternal and offspring fitness, and alternatives such as dosage regulation or coadaptation. The genetic copy's interest must not be confused with a father's conscious preference or with a universal claim that larger offspring are always better for one parent.

The broader category also includes conflicts between distinct genomes residing in one individual. [cytoplasmic inheritance](../../../../cytoplasmic-inheritance.md) can make an organelle or microbial symbiont depend mainly on female transmission, whereas nuclear loci are ordinarily transmitted through both sexes. A maternally inherited symbiont can consequently spread through changes to reproduction that do not benefit every host. In [Nasonia](../../../../nasonia.md) wasps, reciprocal crossing, [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md) observations and antibiotic removal connected cytoplasmic microorganisms to reproductive incompatibility and abnormal paternal [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md) condensation. [Nature 346, pp. 558–560; reciprocal Nasonia crosses, chromosome condensation and antibiotic treatment](../../../../microorganisms-associated-with-chromosome-destruction-and-reproductive-isolation-between-two-insect-species-by-johannes-a-j-breeuwer-and-john-h-werren.md) This demonstrates a microbial cause of a barrier under those conditions, not necessarily the origin of every [species](../../../../species.md) difference. Antibiotic effects on host condition and other microbes require controls, and reintroduction experiments would strengthen microbial specificity.

[somatic evolution](../../../../somatic-evolution.md) is a related conflict at a different level. A mutant cell lineage can increase within a body by avoiding growth restraint or [cell death](../../../../cell-death.md) while damaging the organism. Analyses of [cancer](../../../../cancer.md) genomes and normal somatic tissues identified patterns of selection on somatic [mutations](../../../../mutation.md), showing that cellular success is an evolutionary quantity distinct from organismal success. [Cell 171, pp. 1029–1041.e21; somatic mutation spectra, driver selection and tissue comparisons](../../../../universal-patterns-of-selection-in-cancer-and-somatic-tissues-by-inigo-martincorena-and-others.md) Not all cell proliferation is selfish, and some detected [mutations](../../../../mutation.md) are passengers. Mutation-rate heterogeneity, tissue composition and sequencing ascertainment must be modelled before an excess of [mutations](../../../../mutation.md) is interpreted as selection.

Such conflicts have consequences beyond immediate harm. They can favour fair-segregation mechanisms, genome defence, regulated cell proliferation and restrictions on which cell lineages produce offspring. A [single-cell bottleneck](../../../../single-cell-bottleneck.md) and [germ-soma separation](../../../../germ-soma-separation.md) can increase alignment among cellular descendants or limit transmission of within-body competitors, although neither prevents new [mutations](../../../../mutation.md). Conflict can also affect fertility, [sex ratios](../../../../sex-ratio.md), [reproductive isolation](../../../../reproductive-isolation.md) and genome organization. Claims that it produced a particular major evolutionary change require historical and comparative evidence in addition to a plausible selective argument.

**Organismal integration is partly an evolved outcome of controlling conflict, not evidence that conflict is absent.** The strongest explanations connect a specific transmission asymmetry to an element's advantage, a measurable cost or opposed interest, and an experimentally or comparatively supported response.
