<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**Mammalian [endothermy](../../../../endothermy.md) probably emerged through several interacting changes along the synapsid stem, and its precise onset remains disputed because the [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) proxies measure different aspects of physiology.** [Endothermy](../../../../endothermy.md) concerns substantial metabolic [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) production; [homeothermy](../../../../homeothermy.md) concerns relatively constant body [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). A large animal can have relatively stable [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) without a mammalian metabolic rate, while an endothermic animal can display [heterothermy](../../../../heterothermy.md). These distinctions prevent a single [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) estimate or anatomical character from being treated as a complete diagnosis.

The physiological problem is a [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) balance. In a deliberately simplified steady-state model:

$$
M-W=G(T_b-T_a)+E,
$$

where $M$ is metabolic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) turnover, $W$ external work, $G$ effective [thermal conductance of an animal](../../../../thermal-conductance-of-an-animal.md), $T_b,T_a$ body and ambient [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), and $E$ evaporative [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) loss. Maintaining a warm body in a cool environment can involve increased [metabolism](../../../../metabolism.md), reduced conductance, behaviour, or a combination. For example, $G=0.1\,\mathrm{W\,K^{-1}}$ and a $20\,\mathrm K$ difference imply $2\,\mathrm W$ of non-evaporative [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) loss in this illustrative model. The numbers are not a reconstruction of a [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) animal.

Along the synapsid lineage, increased sustained activity would require coordinated oxygen uptake, transport and use, greater food processing and sufficient [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) assimilation. More effective locomotor construction, ventilation and circulation could support greater [aerobic capacity](../../../../aerobic-capacity.md), while insulation would reduce the cost of retaining the resulting [heat](../../../../heat-split.md). [Shivering thermogenesis](../../../../shivering-thermogenesis.md) and later forms of [non-shivering thermogenesis](../../../../non-shivering-thermogenesis.md) can contribute in living [mammals](../../../../mammal.md). [Brown adipose tissue](../../../../brown-adipose-tissue.md) in many living [placental mammals](../../../../placental-mammal.md) cannot simply be assigned to early [therapsids](../../../../therapsid.md); a modern mechanism need not have been the ancestral one. Similarly, an expanded secondary palate or an altered limb posture is compatible with increased performance but is not a direct measurement of resting [metabolism](../../../../metabolism.md).

The time sequence must be reconstructed from several lines of evidence. Early [synapsids](../../../../synapsid.md) lack the complete modern mammalian package, whereas various [therapsids](../../../../therapsid.md) and [cynodonts](../../../../cynodont.md) show changes in growth, feeding and respiratory anatomy before the mammalian crown radiation. Rapidly deposited [fibrolamellar bone](../../../../fibrolamellar-bone.md) supports rapid growth, but fast-growing ectotherms and the influence of size and environment prevent a unique inference of [endothermy](../../../../endothermy.md). Growth interruptions can reflect seasonality or resource shortage rather than a categorical physiological boundary. Nasal structures interpreted as respiratory [nasal turbinates](../../../../nasal-turbinate.md) could reduce water and [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) loss during high ventilation, but identifying their precise form and separating respiratory from olfactory roles in [fossils](../../../../fossil.md) is difficult.

Preserved [hair](../../../../hair.md) supplies direct evidence of insulation in some later [fossils](../../../../fossil.md). [Castorocauda](../../../../castorocauda.md), a Middle [Jurassic](../../../../jurassic.md) mammaliaform, preserves integument alongside an aquatic anatomical specialization. [Science 311, pp. 1123–1127; Castorocauda skeleton and preserved integument](../../../../a-swimming-mammaliaform-from-the-middle-jurassic-and-ecomorphological-diversification-of-early-mammals-by-qiang-ji-and-others.md) This establishes that a fur-bearing covering existed by that time, not when insulation first evolved or whether its original function was thermal. Sensory and protective functions can precede elaboration for [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) conservation. Direct preservation is particularly valuable, but its late appearance in the sampled record is not proof of biological absence in older lineages.

[Stable isotope analysis](../../../../stable-isotope-analysis.md) has suggested earlier elevated thermometabolism. Comparing oxygen-isotope compositions of therapsid apatite with those of coexisting tetrapods, one study inferred differences in physiology and proposed elevated thermometabolism in several Permo-Triassic therapsid groups. [eLife 6, e28589; therapsid apatite compared with coexisting tetrapods](../../../../oxygen-isotopes-suggest-elevated-thermometabolism-within-multiple-permo-triassic-therapsid-clades-by-kevin-rey-and-others.md) The comparison is stronger than analysing one isolated animal because coexisting taxa constrain aspects of the environment. Nevertheless, body water, drinking source, habitat, evaporation and [diagenesis](../../../../diagenesis.md) affect the isotopic signal. The proposed middle-to-late Permian origin is an interpretation using a physiological framework, not a thermometer reading of an observed living cynodont.

A contrasting biomechanical approach uses the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence of [endolymph](../../../../endolymph.md) properties and the dimensions of the [semicircular canals](../../../../semicircular-canal.md). Calibration against living animals and application to [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) inner ears supported a relatively abrupt transition around 233 million years ago in the Late [Triassic](../../../../triassic.md), near the origin of [mammaliamorphs](../../../../mammaliamorph.md). [Nature 607, pp. 726–731; living-species calibration and inferred transition around 233 Ma](../../../../inner-ear-biomechanics-reveals-a-late-triassic-origin-for-mammalian-endothermy-by-ricardo-araujo-and-others.md) This offers a quantitative prediction from a different anatomical system. However, body size, activity, reconstruction and the adequacy of the living calibration remain relevant. A change in a temperature-sensitive inner-ear proxy need not coincide exactly with the first increment in metabolic [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) production or the first insulating covering. The disagreement with earlier isotope-based interpretations should therefore be tested, rather than erased by choosing the preferred date.

There are several plausible selective explanations. The [aerobic capacity](../../../../aerobic-capacity.md) hypothesis proposes that selection for sustained locomotor performance raised maximum oxygen consumption, with elevated resting [metabolism](../../../../metabolism.md) and [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) production linked to that change. Comparative measurements of resting and maximum oxygen use underpin this proposal. [Science 206, pp. 649–654; resting and maximum oxygen consumption and the aerobic-capacity hypothesis](../../../../endothermy-and-activity-in-vertebrates-by-albert-f-bennett-and-others.md) A major issue is the strength and evolutionary direction of the relationship: high resting expenditure is expensive, and an association among living species does not prove which change led historically. Selection for thermal independence, nocturnal activity or performance in fluctuating climates provides related benefits, but “the climate became colder” is not sufficient unless the energetic costs and available food are considered.

A [parental care](../../../../parental-care.md) hypothesis instead emphasizes selection for controlling the developmental environment and supplying offspring. Improved provisioning endurance and more stable incubation or offspring temperatures could favour increased metabolic capacity and [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) retention. [The American Naturalist 155, pp. 326–334; parental-care hypothesis](../../../../parental-care-the-key-to-understanding-endothermy-and-other-convergent-features-in-birds-and-mammals-by-c-g-farmer.md) This gives concrete reproductive benefits that might offset energetic costs, while remaining a hypothesis about historical selective circumstances. The evidence required includes relationships among care, thermal performance and reproductive success, not merely the fact that living [mammals](../../../../mammal.md) both care for young and are endothermic. Activity, parental investment, insulation and environmental variability could reinforce one another, so the explanations need not be mutually exclusive.

**The most secure account is a mosaic physiological transition with increasingly effective [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) supply, [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) production and [heat](../../../../heat-split.md) retention. A Late [Triassic](../../../../triassic.md) onset of a more fully mammalian condition is a specific supported hypothesis, while earlier therapsid evidence indicates that parts of the transition may be older.** [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) anatomy and chemistry constrain when particular features existed; the selective reasons require functional and comparative tests and remain less directly observable.
