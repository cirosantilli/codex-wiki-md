**Introduced [mammals](../../../../mammal.md) have affected native populations through [predation](../../../../predation.md), competition, disease, habitat modification and [hybridization](../../../../hybridization-biology.md); mitigation works best when it targets the demonstrated mechanism at the geographic scale of reinvasion.** Britain and Ireland must be assessed separately, and native status is local. A mammal native to mainland Britain can be an [introduced species](../../../../introduced-species.md) on an offshore island. An introduced population is not automatically an [invasive species](../../../../invasive-species.md): establishment, spread and adverse effects need evidence.

[Grey squirrels](../../../../grey-squirrel.md), introduced in Victorian Britain and subsequently Ireland, illustrate several mechanisms. Their expansion has accompanied major contraction of [red squirrels](../../../../red-squirrel.md), involving resource competition as well as pathogen-mediated effects in some regions. Experimental infection found severe disease in [red squirrels](../../../../red-squirrel.md) and little detectable effect on grey-squirrel health, supporting a role for [squirrelpox](../../../../squirrelpox.md). [Proceedings of the Royal Society B 269, pp. 529–533; contrasting experimental susceptibility of red and grey squirrels](../../../../parapoxvirus-causes-a-deleterious-disease-in-red-squirrels-associated-with-uk-population-declines-by-daniel-m-tompkins-and-others.md) That comparison establishes differential susceptibility, not the cause of every field decline. Habitat, exposure, squirrel density and the presence of the virus must be assessed locally. [grey squirrels](../../../../grey-squirrel.md) also damage trees through bark stripping, adding forestry costs to biodiversity effects.

A response can combine surveillance, exclusion of new introductions, targeted population control and habitat management favouring [red squirrels](../../../../red-squirrel.md). Control should be coordinated across connected populations because small cleared areas can be repeatedly recolonized. Recovery of [pine martens](../../../../pine-marten.md) in Ireland has been associated with reduced grey-squirrel abundance, making predator recovery a potentially useful part of management. [Biodiversity and Conservation 23, pp. 753–774; distributional changes associated with pine marten recovery](../../../../population-crash-in-an-invasive-species-following-the-recovery-of-a-native-predator-the-case-of-the-american-grey-squirrel-and-the-european-pine-marten-in-ireland-by-emma-sheehy-and-others.md) The association is not a guarantee that every woodland will respond similarly or a reason to ignore effects on other prey. Predator recovery and direct squirrel management should be evaluated through measured outcomes and the local ecological setting.

[American mink](../../../../american-mink.md), established through twentieth-century escapes and releases associated with fur farming, have affected vulnerable prey, including [water voles](../../../../water-vole.md) and some waterbirds. Their movements along waterways make isolated trapping effort vulnerable to reinvasion from elsewhere. Landscape-scale coordinated monitoring and removal in Scotland showed how volunteer participation and adaptive deployment can operate at a much larger scale than occasional local control. [Biological Conservation 144, pp. 575–583; landscape-scale monitoring and coordinated control](../../../../turning-back-the-tide-of-american-mink-invasion-at-an-unprecedented-scale-through-community-participation-and-adaptive-management-by-rosalind-bryce-and-others.md) A trap capture is an operational measure; the conservation endpoint is improved prey survival, occupancy or reproduction. Habitat restoration remains important where bank condition, fragmentation or other pressures also limit [water voles](../../../../water-vole.md).

Introduced deer create a different set of impacts. [Sika deer](../../../../sika-deer.md) and [muntjac](../../../../muntjac.md) can contribute to heavy browsing, changes in woodland regeneration and understorey structure, damage to crops and increased collision risk. Attribution needs care because native deer and changes in their abundance can contribute to the same pressures. In an English woodland experiment, paired fenced and browsed areas of coppice provided evidence that deer exclusion altered habitat use by breeding nightingales. [Ibis 152, pp. 335–346; eight paired deer exclosures and browsed controls](../../../../experimental-evidence-that-deer-browsing-reduces-habitat-suitability-for-breeding-common-nightingales-luscinia-megarhynchos-by-chas-a-holt-and-others.md) Pairing helped separate browsing effects from much of the shared habitat setting, but the experiment did not assign all landscape-level bird decline to introduced deer alone. Deer management should therefore use observed vegetation and wildlife responses rather than a target justified solely by species origin.

[Sika deer](../../../../sika-deer.md) also hybridize with native [red deer](../../../../red-deer.md). Genetic analysis of a Scottish contact zone detected [introgression](../../../../introgression.md) even though many animals remained readily classified as red-like or sika-like. [Genetics 152, pp. 355–371; microsatellite and mitochondrial evidence of introgression](../../../../introgression-through-rare-hybridization-a-genetic-study-of-a-hybrid-zone-between-red-and-sika-deer-genus-cervus-in-argyll-scotland-by-simon-j-goodman-and-others.md) Phenotypic appearance therefore cannot reliably exclude mixed ancestry. The genetic objective of management must be stated explicitly, with appropriate sampling and markers; local removal cannot undo past [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) already distributed through a population. Preventing further releases or escapes and managing contact zones can be more tractable than claiming that every admixed individual can be identified visually.

Offshore islands show why national native status is insufficient. Introduced [hedgehogs](../../../../hedgehog.md) in the Western Isles became [predators](../../../../predator.md) of wader eggs. A removal experiment compared fenced plots with adjacent controls and found improved nest success where [hedgehogs](../../../../hedgehog.md) were removed, without evidence that native bird [predators](../../../../predator.md) compensated fully for the reduction. [Journal of Applied Ecology 38, pp. 802–812; fenced removal plots, adjacent controls and nest monitoring](../../../../experimental-removal-of-introduced-hedgehogs-improves-wader-nest-success-in-the-western-isles-scotland-by-digger-b-jackson.md) This supplies stronger causal evidence than a simple correlation between [hedgehogs](../../../../hedgehog.md) and declining birds. Exclosures and removal can protect critical sites, but fence maintenance, recolonization and the consequences of moving animals elsewhere must be considered. Transporting a problem to another sensitive site is not effective mitigation.

Ireland also has recent small-mammal invasions. The [greater white-toothed shrew](../../../../greater-white-toothed-shrew.md), first recorded there in 2007, expanded in a region previously occupied by the native [pygmy shrew](../../../../pygmy-shrew.md); field studies linked the invasion to changes in the latter's distribution. [PLoS ONE 9, e100403; first recorded in 2007, spread and native pygmy-shrew distribution](../../../../invading-and-expanding-range-dynamics-and-ecological-consequences-of-the-greater-white-toothed-shrew-crocidura-russula-invasion-in-ireland-by-allan-d-mcdevitt-and-others.md) The finding motivates monitoring invasion fronts and native populations, while recognizing that distributional association alone does not completely identify the competitive mechanism. Small species are easily moved unnoticed with materials, so pathway surveillance can be valuable even where widespread eradication is difficult. A British management priority cannot simply be copied to Ireland because the native communities and invasion histories differ.

Eradication is possible under suitable conditions. The British campaigns against [muskrats](../../../../muskrat.md) and [coypus](../../../../coypu.md), both introduced through fur farming in the 1920s, illustrate the importance of early response and sustained demographic planning. Later coypu control used population studies, trapping-effort information and coordinated deployment rather than assuming that opportunistic captures would finish the job. [Biological Journal of the Linnean Society 38, pp. 39–51; contrasting campaigns and population-based control planning](../../../../the-eradication-of-muskrats-and-coypus-from-britain-by-l-m-gosling-and-others.md) The lesson is operational as well as ecological: eradication requires resources to find the last animals, a reason for participants to continue as captures decline, and surveillance after apparent success.

Across examples, prevention and rapid response are usually more feasible than reversing a widespread established invasion. Measures include securing managed animals, identifying transport pathways, detecting new populations promptly and selecting geographically defensible management units. Where complete eradication is unrealistic, sustained suppression or protection of high-value sites may still have substantial benefits. Methods should minimize harm to non-target animals and be compared for effectiveness, feasibility and welfare consequences. Reinvasion, compensatory reproduction and interactions among [predators](../../../../predator.md) or competitors can otherwise undermine a superficially successful intervention.

Evaluation should use appropriate controls or a [before-after control-impact design](../../../../before-after-control-impact-design.md), with detection effort and habitat change recorded. Reduced invader abundance, improved native reproduction, recovered vegetation and reduced [introgression](../../../../introgression.md) are distinct outcomes and may not change together. **Successful mitigation is not simply fewer introduced animals: it is a demonstrated reduction of their harmful effects, maintained through appropriate geographic coverage, prevention of renewed introductions and continued monitoring.**
