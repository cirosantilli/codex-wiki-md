**Migration and [admixture](../../../../genetic-admixture.md) explain much of the broad genome-wide pattern, while drift, restricted mating and local demographic history generate finer structure; [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) has disproportionately strong effects at particular loci.** [Mutation](../../../../mutation.md) ultimately supplies new variation and [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) creates new combinations, but neither alone explains the major spatial and historical contrasts among present-day European [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md) populations. The relative importance depends on whether the target is genome-wide ancestry, a local population, or a specific adaptive trait.

The starting point is shared ancestry followed by differentiation, not a set of immutable European biological categories. Present populations are connected through repeated movements and reproduction, and much human variation occurs within any sampled population. Geographic clines can be real without implying sharp natural boundaries. Historical and linguistic labels identify sampling groups or hypotheses; they are not a substitute for analysing actual [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md), [allele frequencies](../../../../allele-frequency.md) and uncertainty in ancestry models.

[Ancient DNA](../../../../ancient-dna.md) changed the historical explanation by making temporal replacement and mixture directly observable. Early genomic studies modelled present-day Europeans using contributions related to western hunter-gatherers, early farmers and ancient northern Eurasian ancestry. [Nature 513, pp. 409–413; ancestry mixture models and ancient reference genomes](../../../../ancient-human-genomes-suggest-three-ancestral-populations-for-present-day-europeans-by-iosif-lazaridis-and-others.md) These are statistical ancestry relationships to sampled ancient groups, not three pure, permanent ancestral races. Subsequent sampling has resolved additional structure and [admixture](../../../../genetic-admixture.md) within those sources. In particular, an early-farmer component must not simply be renamed a hunter-gatherer component because the farmer population already carried some admixed ancestry.

The spread of farming brought substantial ancestry related to populations of Anatolia and neighbouring regions into Europe, followed by geographically variable mixing with resident hunter-gatherers. Later Neolithic and Bronze Age movements from the Pontic–Caspian steppe generated another major ancestry shift. Dated ancient [genomes](../../../../genome.md) showed that some central European groups associated with the Corded Ware horizon had substantial steppe-related ancestry. [Nature 522, pp. 207–211; dated ancient genomes and late Neolithic ancestry change](../../../../massive-migration-from-the-steppe-was-a-source-for-indo-european-languages-in-europe-by-wolfgang-haak-and-others.md) These events provide a mechanism for coordinated changes at many unlinked loci. Similar pottery or a language family does not independently prove a one-to-one population replacement; the genetic, archaeological and linguistic claims require separate evidence.

The elementary [admixture](../../../../genetic-admixture.md) calculation is:

$$
p_{\mathrm{mix}}=\alpha p_A+(1-\alpha)p_B,
$$

where $p_A,p_B$ are source [allele frequencies](../../../../allele-frequency.md) and $\alpha$ is the ancestry contribution under the stated two-source model. Many loci can move together without selection if their source frequencies differ. For an illustrative $p_A=0.8$, $p_B=0.2$ and $\alpha=0.3$, $p_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.38$. These invented values explain the logic rather than estimate any European ancestry. Further migration, drift and selection subsequently modify the mixture; imperfect ancient proxies also limit precision.

[Genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md) is particularly important after bottlenecks, colonization and prolonged small effective size. A [founder effect](../../../../founder-effect.md) samples only part of the ancestral variation, and subsequent growth can make initially unusual variants numerous without creating an adaptive advantage. Restricted movement, local mating networks and [isolation by distance](../../../../isolation-by-distance.md) generate regional structure; continued [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) generally counteracts divergence. An island, valley or relatively isolated community can therefore differ from neighbouring populations through demographic sampling rather than ecological adaptation. The relevant quantity is [effective population size](../../../../effective-population-size.md), which can differ greatly from the current census because of past size, reproductive inequality and mating structure.

[Mutation](../../../../mutation.md) supplies the [alleles](../../../../allele.md) on which these processes act. Recent population growth also changes the abundance and age distribution of rare variants, so rare-variant contrasts need not reproduce the history inferred from common variants. [Genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) reshuffles ancestry and breaks down associations among loci. It does not directly change the frequency of an [allele](../../../../allele.md) merely by exchanging chromosome segments, but its progressive shortening of ancestry blocks helps date mixture under an explicit demographic model. A single mitochondrial or Y-chromosomal genealogy gives a narrower history than the autosomal [genome](../../../../genome.md) and can be especially sensitive to sex-biased movement or lineage loss.

[Natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) is essential for some conspicuous exceptions to a purely demographic account. Ancient-genome analyses identified changes involving pigmentation, [diet](../../../../diet.md) and other traits, comparing observed frequencies with expectations given ancestry. [Nature 528, pp. 499–503; selection tests conditional on changing ancestry](../../../../genome-wide-patterns-of-selection-in-230-ancient-eurasians-by-iain-mathieson-and-others.md) A region unusually differentiated between populations is only a candidate: drift, a change of ancestry, correlated variants and the properties of the sampling procedure can all generate apparent outliers. Dated [allele](../../../../allele.md) trajectories and comparison with an appropriate demographic null are much more informative than assuming that every geographic difference is an adaptation.

[Lactase persistence](../../../../lactase-persistence.md) illustrates both strong selection and the need to distinguish competing causes. Dairying preceded the high frequency of adult milk digestion in many European populations. Archaeological dairy residues and ancient genetic time series indicate that milk use alone does not explain the [allele](../../../../allele.md)'s trajectory; hypotheses involving famine or infectious disease can change the advantage of lactose digestion. [Nature 608, pp. 336–345; dairy residues, ancient allele frequencies and competing selective explanations](../../../../dairying-diseases-and-the-evolution-of-lactase-persistence-in-europe-by-richard-p-evershed-and-others.md) This is [gene–culture coevolution](../../../../gene-culture-coevolution.md), because cultural resource use changes the selective environment. It is not evidence that all people who consumed dairy were lactase-persistent or that every aspect of dairy adaptation arose from a new [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) after farming began.

To compare the processes, I would fit demographic models to many relatively neutral regions, use ancient samples to test the timing and direction of ancestry changes, and then ask which loci deviate beyond plausible drift and sampling error. Contamination, damage, related individuals, uneven geographic coverage and imprecise dates need explicit treatment. A principal-component plot describes variation in a particular sample; it does not by itself identify an event or its cause. Likewise, an ancestry coefficient is conditional on the reference populations and model.

**There is no single defensible percentage of European genetic differentiation attributable to each process. Migration and [admixture](../../../../genetic-admixture.md) are central to broad ancestry contrasts, drift and mating geography to local structure, and selection to particular functional differences. [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) creates the variation and recombination reorganizes it; these processes interact rather than replace one another.**
