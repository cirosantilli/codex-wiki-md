**Migration and [admixture](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-admixture) explain much of the broad genome-wide pattern, while drift, restricted mating and local demographic history generate finer structure; [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) has disproportionately strong effects at particular loci.** [Mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) ultimately supplies new variation and [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) creates new combinations, but neither alone explains the major spatial and historical contrasts among present-day European [Homo sapiens](../../organism.md#homo-sapiens) populations. The relative importance depends on whether the target is genome-wide ancestry, a local population, or a specific adaptive trait.

The starting point is shared ancestry followed by differentiation, not a set of immutable European biological categories. Present populations are connected through repeated movements and reproduction, and much human variation occurs within any sampled population. Geographic clines can be real without implying sharp natural boundaries. Historical and linguistic labels identify sampling groups or hypotheses; they are not a substitute for analysing actual [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow), [allele frequencies](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency) and uncertainty in ancestry models.

[Ancient DNA](../../polymer.md#ancient-dna) changed the historical explanation by making temporal replacement and mixture directly observable. Early genomic studies modelled present-day Europeans using contributions related to western hunter-gatherers, early farmers and ancient northern Eurasian ancestry. [Nature 513, pp. 409–413; ancestry mixture models and ancient reference genomes](../../biology-bibliography.md#ancient-human-genomes-suggest-three-ancestral-populations-for-present-day-europeans-by-iosif-lazaridis-and-others) These are statistical ancestry relationships to sampled ancient groups, not three pure, permanent ancestral races. Subsequent sampling has resolved additional structure and [admixture](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-admixture) within those sources. In particular, an early-farmer component must not simply be renamed a hunter-gatherer component because the farmer population already carried some admixed ancestry.

The spread of farming brought substantial ancestry related to populations of Anatolia and neighbouring regions into Europe, followed by geographically variable mixing with resident hunter-gatherers. Later Neolithic and Bronze Age movements from the Pontic–Caspian steppe generated another major ancestry shift. Dated ancient [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) showed that some central European groups associated with the Corded Ware horizon had substantial steppe-related ancestry. [Nature 522, pp. 207–211; dated ancient genomes and late Neolithic ancestry change](../../biology-bibliography.md#massive-migration-from-the-steppe-was-a-source-for-indo-european-languages-in-europe-by-wolfgang-haak-and-others) These events provide a mechanism for coordinated changes at many unlinked loci. Similar pottery or a language family does not independently prove a one-to-one population replacement; the genetic, archaeological and linguistic claims require separate evidence.

The elementary [admixture](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-admixture) calculation is:

$$
p_{\mathrm{mix}}=\alpha p_A+(1-\alpha)p_B,
$$

where $p_A,p_B$ are source [allele frequencies](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency) and $\alpha$ is the ancestry contribution under the stated two-source model. Many loci can move together without selection if their source frequencies differ. For an illustrative $p_A=0.8$, $p_B=0.2$ and $\alpha=0.3$, $p_{\mathrm{mix}}=0.38$. These invented values explain the logic rather than estimate any European ancestry. Further migration, drift and selection subsequently modify the mixture; imperfect ancient proxies also limit precision.

[Genetic drift](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-drift) is particularly important after bottlenecks, colonization and prolonged small effective size. A [founder effect](../../population-genetics.md#founder-effect) samples only part of the ancestral variation, and subsequent growth can make initially unusual variants numerous without creating an adaptive advantage. Restricted movement, local mating networks and [isolation by distance](../../population-genetics.md#isolation-by-distance) generate regional structure; continued [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow) generally counteracts divergence. An island, valley or relatively isolated community can therefore differ from neighbouring populations through demographic sampling rather than ecological adaptation. The relevant quantity is [effective population size](../../population-genetics.md#effective-population-size), which can differ greatly from the current census because of past size, reproductive inequality and mating structure.

[Mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) supplies the [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele) on which these processes act. Recent population growth also changes the abundance and age distribution of rare variants, so rare-variant contrasts need not reproduce the history inferred from common variants. [Genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) reshuffles ancestry and breaks down associations among loci. It does not directly change the frequency of an [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) merely by exchanging chromosome segments, but its progressive shortening of ancestry blocks helps date mixture under an explicit demographic model. A single mitochondrial or Y-chromosomal genealogy gives a narrower history than the autosomal [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) and can be especially sensitive to sex-biased movement or lineage loss.

[Natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) is essential for some conspicuous exceptions to a purely demographic account. Ancient-genome analyses identified changes involving pigmentation, [diet](../../physiology.md#diet) and other traits, comparing observed frequencies with expectations given ancestry. [Nature 528, pp. 499–503; selection tests conditional on changing ancestry](../../biology-bibliography.md#genome-wide-patterns-of-selection-in-230-ancient-eurasians-by-iain-mathieson-and-others) A region unusually differentiated between populations is only a candidate: drift, a change of ancestry, correlated variants and the properties of the sampling procedure can all generate apparent outliers. Dated [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) trajectories and comparison with an appropriate demographic null are much more informative than assuming that every geographic difference is an adaptation.

[Lactase persistence](../../genetics.md#lactase-persistence) illustrates both strong selection and the need to distinguish competing causes. Dairying preceded the high frequency of adult milk digestion in many European populations. Archaeological dairy residues and ancient genetic time series indicate that milk use alone does not explain the [allele](../../genetics.md#allele)'s trajectory; hypotheses involving famine or infectious disease can change the advantage of lactose digestion. [Nature 608, pp. 336–345; dairy residues, ancient allele frequencies and competing selective explanations](../../biology-bibliography.md#dairying-diseases-and-the-evolution-of-lactase-persistence-in-europe-by-richard-p-evershed-and-others) This is [gene–culture coevolution](../../biology.md#gene-culture-coevolution), because cultural resource use changes the selective environment. It is not evidence that all people who consumed dairy were lactase-persistent or that every aspect of dairy adaptation arose from a new [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) after farming began.

To compare the processes, I would fit demographic models to many relatively neutral regions, use ancient samples to test the timing and direction of ancestry changes, and then ask which loci deviate beyond plausible drift and sampling error. Contamination, damage, related individuals, uneven geographic coverage and imprecise dates need explicit treatment. A principal-component plot describes variation in a particular sample; it does not by itself identify an event or its cause. Likewise, an ancestry coefficient is conditional on the reference populations and model.

**There is no single defensible percentage of European genetic differentiation attributable to each process. Migration and [admixture](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-admixture) are central to broad ancestry contrasts, drift and mating geography to local structure, and selection to particular functional differences. [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) creates the variation and recombination reorganizes it; these processes interact rather than replace one another.**

**Mammalian [endothermy](../../biology.md#endothermy) probably emerged through several interacting changes along the synapsid stem, and its precise onset remains disputed because the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) proxies measure different aspects of physiology.** [Endothermy](../../biology.md#endothermy) concerns substantial metabolic [heat](../../heat.md) production; [homeothermy](../../physiology.md#homeothermy) concerns relatively constant body [temperature](../../temperature.md). A large animal can have relatively stable [temperature](../../temperature.md) without a mammalian metabolic rate, while an endothermic animal can display [heterothermy](../../physiology.md#heterothermy). These distinctions prevent a single [temperature](../../temperature.md) estimate or anatomical character from being treated as a complete diagnosis.

The physiological problem is a [heat](../../heat.md) balance. In a deliberately simplified steady-state model:

$$
M-W=G(T_b-T_a)+E,
$$

where $M$ is metabolic [energy](../../energy.md) turnover, $W$ external work, $G$ effective [thermal conductance of an animal](../../physiology.md#thermal-conductance-of-an-animal), $T_b,T_a$ body and ambient [temperature](../../temperature.md), and $E$ evaporative [heat](../../heat.md) loss. Maintaining a warm body in a cool environment can involve increased [metabolism](../../biology.md#metabolism), reduced conductance, behaviour, or a combination. For example, $G=0.1\,\mathrm{W\,K^{-1}}$ and a $20\,\mathrm K$ difference imply $2\,\mathrm W$ of non-evaporative [heat](../../heat.md) loss in this illustrative model. The numbers are not a reconstruction of a [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) animal.

Along the synapsid lineage, increased sustained activity would require coordinated oxygen uptake, transport and use, greater food processing and sufficient [energy](../../energy.md) assimilation. More effective locomotor construction, ventilation and circulation could support greater [aerobic capacity](../../physiology.md#aerobic-capacity), while insulation would reduce the cost of retaining the resulting [heat](../../heat.md). [Shivering thermogenesis](../../physiology.md#shivering-thermogenesis) and later forms of [non-shivering thermogenesis](../../physiology.md#non-shivering-thermogenesis) can contribute in living [mammals](../../organism.md#mammal). [Brown adipose tissue](../../physiology.md#brown-adipose-tissue) in many living [placental mammals](../../organism.md#placental-mammal) cannot simply be assigned to early [therapsids](../../organism.md#therapsid); a modern mechanism need not have been the ancestral one. Similarly, an expanded secondary palate or an altered limb posture is compatible with increased performance but is not a direct measurement of resting [metabolism](../../biology.md#metabolism).

The time sequence must be reconstructed from several lines of evidence. Early [synapsids](../../organism.md#synapsid) lack the complete modern mammalian package, whereas various [therapsids](../../organism.md#therapsid) and [cynodonts](../../organism.md#cynodont) show changes in growth, feeding and respiratory anatomy before the mammalian crown radiation. Rapidly deposited [fibrolamellar bone](../../biology.md#fibrolamellar-bone) supports rapid growth, but fast-growing ectotherms and the influence of size and environment prevent a unique inference of [endothermy](../../biology.md#endothermy). Growth interruptions can reflect seasonality or resource shortage rather than a categorical physiological boundary. Nasal structures interpreted as respiratory [nasal turbinates](../../biology.md#nasal-turbinate) could reduce water and [heat](../../heat.md) loss during high ventilation, but identifying their precise form and separating respiratory from olfactory roles in [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) is difficult.

Preserved [hair](../../biology.md#hair) supplies direct evidence of insulation in some later [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil). [Castorocauda](../../organism.md#castorocauda), a Middle [Jurassic](../../planetary-science.md#jurassic) mammaliaform, preserves integument alongside an aquatic anatomical specialization. [Science 311, pp. 1123–1127; Castorocauda skeleton and preserved integument](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-swimming-mammaliaform-from-the-middle-jurassic-and-ecomorphological-diversification-of-early-mammals-by-qiang-ji-and-others) This establishes that a fur-bearing covering existed by that time, not when insulation first evolved or whether its original function was thermal. Sensory and protective functions can precede elaboration for [heat](../../heat.md) conservation. Direct preservation is particularly valuable, but its late appearance in the sampled record is not proof of biological absence in older lineages.

[Stable isotope analysis](../../chemical-substance.md#stable-isotope-analysis) has suggested earlier elevated thermometabolism. Comparing oxygen-isotope compositions of therapsid apatite with those of coexisting tetrapods, one study inferred differences in physiology and proposed elevated thermometabolism in several Permo-Triassic therapsid groups. [eLife 6, e28589; therapsid apatite compared with coexisting tetrapods](../../biology-bibliography.md#oxygen-isotopes-suggest-elevated-thermometabolism-within-multiple-permo-triassic-therapsid-clades-by-kevin-rey-and-others) The comparison is stronger than analysing one isolated animal because coexisting taxa constrain aspects of the environment. Nevertheless, body water, drinking source, habitat, evaporation and [diagenesis](../../biology.md#diagenesis) affect the isotopic signal. The proposed middle-to-late Permian origin is an interpretation using a physiological framework, not a thermometer reading of an observed living cynodont.

A contrasting biomechanical approach uses the [temperature](../../temperature.md) dependence of [endolymph](../../biology.md#endolymph) properties and the dimensions of the [semicircular canals](../../biology.md#semicircular-canal). Calibration against living animals and application to [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) inner ears supported a relatively abrupt transition around 233 million years ago in the Late [Triassic](../../planetary-science.md#triassic), near the origin of [mammaliamorphs](../../organism.md#mammaliamorph). [Nature 607, pp. 726–731; living-species calibration and inferred transition around 233 Ma](../../biology-bibliography.md#inner-ear-biomechanics-reveals-a-late-triassic-origin-for-mammalian-endothermy-by-ricardo-araujo-and-others) This offers a quantitative prediction from a different anatomical system. However, body size, activity, reconstruction and the adequacy of the living calibration remain relevant. A change in a temperature-sensitive inner-ear proxy need not coincide exactly with the first increment in metabolic [heat](../../heat.md) production or the first insulating covering. The disagreement with earlier isotope-based interpretations should therefore be tested, rather than erased by choosing the preferred date.

There are several plausible selective explanations. The [aerobic capacity](../../physiology.md#aerobic-capacity) hypothesis proposes that selection for sustained locomotor performance raised maximum oxygen consumption, with elevated resting [metabolism](../../biology.md#metabolism) and [heat](../../heat.md) production linked to that change. Comparative measurements of resting and maximum oxygen use underpin this proposal. [Science 206, pp. 649–654; resting and maximum oxygen consumption and the aerobic-capacity hypothesis](../../biology-bibliography.md#endothermy-and-activity-in-vertebrates-by-albert-f-bennett-and-others) A major issue is the strength and evolutionary direction of the relationship: high resting expenditure is expensive, and an association among living species does not prove which change led historically. Selection for thermal independence, nocturnal activity or performance in fluctuating climates provides related benefits, but “the climate became colder” is not sufficient unless the energetic costs and available food are considered.

A [parental care](../../biology.md#parental-care) hypothesis instead emphasizes selection for controlling the developmental environment and supplying offspring. Improved provisioning endurance and more stable incubation or offspring temperatures could favour increased metabolic capacity and [heat](../../heat.md) retention. [The American Naturalist 155, pp. 326–334; parental-care hypothesis](../../biology-bibliography.md#parental-care-the-key-to-understanding-endothermy-and-other-convergent-features-in-birds-and-mammals-by-c-g-farmer) This gives concrete reproductive benefits that might offset energetic costs, while remaining a hypothesis about historical selective circumstances. The evidence required includes relationships among care, thermal performance and reproductive success, not merely the fact that living [mammals](../../organism.md#mammal) both care for young and are endothermic. Activity, parental investment, insulation and environmental variability could reinforce one another, so the explanations need not be mutually exclusive.

**The most secure account is a mosaic physiological transition with increasingly effective [energy](../../energy.md) supply, [heat](../../heat.md) production and [heat](../../heat.md) retention. A Late [Triassic](../../planetary-science.md#triassic) onset of a more fully mammalian condition is a specific supported hypothesis, while earlier therapsid evidence indicates that parts of the transition may be older.** [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) anatomy and chemistry constrain when particular features existed; the selective reasons require functional and comparative tests and remain less directly observable.

**Skeletal anatomy can reconstruct likely resource use and test hypotheses about competition, but it cannot by itself measure the strength of an interaction between [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) populations.** [Resource partitioning](../../ecology.md#resource-partitioning) among living sympatric [primate](../../organism.md#primates) species provides a useful source of comparative predictions. It does not establish that every difference among coexisting [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) species was caused by competition: differences may predate coexistence, reflect other selective pressures, or merely determine which species can occur together.

The first requirement is to establish the relevant community. [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) from one formation may have accumulated over many thousands of years or represent animals transported from different habitats. Stratigraphy, dating, sedimentology and [taphonomy](../../biology.md#taphonomy) must therefore establish plausible coexistence at an ecologically relevant scale. Species boundaries, skeletal associations, age, sex and the number of individuals also need attention. A mixed collection of several time-separated populations cannot be treated as one living primate group, and an isolated limb should not be confidently assigned to a hominin species solely because its [teeth](../../biology.md#tooth) occur nearby.

For [diet](../../physiology.md#diet), I would measure [tooth](../../biology.md#tooth) size and proportions, crown shape, shearing edges, enamel thickness, [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw) geometry and muscle attachment regions. [Functional morphology](../../biology.md#functional-morphology) connects these observations to potential food processing: slicing leaves, crushing relatively hard items and resisting repeated chewing loads impose different demands. Body size affects both mechanics and energetic requirements, so comparisons need [allometry](../../biology.md#allometry) rather than assuming that a larger [tooth](../../biology.md#tooth) necessarily reflects a different food category. Phylogenetic relationships also matter because inherited resemblance can produce apparent ecological overlap independently of recent competitive interactions.

These measurements estimate capability better than habitual consumption. [Paranthropus boisei](../../organism.md#paranthropus-boisei) has a powerful chewing apparatus and very large postcanine [teeth](../../biology.md#tooth), yet a dental-microwear study found comparatively low surface complexity and anisotropy in the seven specimens preserving suitable surfaces. [PLoS ONE 3, e2044; seven specimens with preserved microwear and comparison with gross anatomy](../../biology-bibliography.md#dental-microwear-and-diet-of-the-plio-pleistocene-hominin-paranthropus-boisei-by-peter-s-ungar-and-others) This contradicted a straightforward inference of habitual hard-object feeding from robust anatomy alone. It is compatible with the importance of mechanically demanding [fallback foods](../../physiology.md#fallback-food), or other uses of the apparatus, without uniquely demonstrating either explanation. A morphology selected during rare food shortages need not correspond to the last meals recorded by [dental microwear](../../biology.md#dental-microwear).

Enamel chemistry provides a partially independent test. Carbon-isotope measurements indicated substantial use of C4-based resources by [Paranthropus boisei](../../organism.md#paranthropus-boisei). [PNAS 108, pp. 9337–9341; enamel carbon isotope evidence for C4-based resources](../../biology-bibliography.md#diet-of-paranthropus-boisei-in-the-early-pleistocene-of-east-africa-by-thure-e-cerling-and-others) This helps distinguish it from a consumer relying predominantly on C3-derived resources, but does not specify the plant part, exact food species or necessarily every intermediate step through a food web. Grasses and sedges can share the broad photosynthetic signature. Isotopic overlap therefore need not mean identical [diets](../../physiology.md#diet), just as different wear patterns need not imply complete separation. The strength of the reconstruction comes from combining evidence that integrates different biological timescales and different aspects of resource use.

For locomotion, I would examine joint shape, limb proportions, phalangeal curvature, grip-related structures, the shoulder, [pelvis](../../biology.md#pelvis), [vertebral column](../../biology.md#vertebral-column) and articular surfaces. These can constrain leaping, climbing, suspension, quadrupedal walking or habitual [bipedalism](../../biology.md#bipedalism) and hence access to canopy levels, support diameters and terrestrial resources. However, a locomotor category is not a [diet](../../physiology.md#diet). Animals with similar locomotor competence may choose different foods, while animals with different locomotion may compete for the same ground-level resource. Wear, preservation and reconstruction uncertainty need to be propagated rather than assigning every specimen to a single categorical lifestyle.

Hominins make this caution especially important. A bipedal lower limb need not imply complete abandonment of climbing. Comparative analysis of the juvenile scapula of [Australopithecus afarensis](../../organism.md#australopithecus-afarensis) supported retained climbing-related features and showed why ontogeny matters in comparison with living apes. [Science 338, pp. 514–517; juvenile scapula and comparative growth patterns](../../biology-bibliography.md#australopithecus-afarensis-scapular-ontogeny-function-and-the-role-of-climbing-in-human-evolution-by-david-j-green-and-others) This informs possible resource access, but does not quantify time spent climbing or competitive effects on another species. A [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) community containing an australopith, a species of [Homo](../../organism.md#homo) and other primates could consequently share some resources while partitioning others. The anatomical mosaic should be retained in the model rather than compressed into a single “terrestrial” score.

An explicit model clarifies what is missing. In one conventional competition model:

$$
\frac{1}{N_i}\frac{dN_i}{dt}=r_i\left(1-\frac{N_i+\alpha_{ij}N_j}{K_i}\right),
$$

$\alpha_{ij}$ is an [ecological competition coefficient](../../ecology.md#ecological-competition-coefficient), defined relative to the chosen density units and model assumptions. It represents the effect of another population on population $i$, not simply their morphological resemblance. Estimating it requires information about density, resource limitation and demographic responses that skeletal shape rarely provides. The same [niche overlap](../../ecology.md#niche-overlap) could accompany negligible competition when food is abundant and severe competition during a bottleneck. Conversely, strong present-day partitioning could be a legacy of past competition rather than evidence that competition was always weak.

I would therefore construct quantitative, uncertainty-aware estimates of dietary and locomotor overlap, calibrated against living primates with measured behaviour. The calibration should include species occupying similar environments but differing in abundance or competitive context, and test predictions on species excluded from model fitting. A [morphospace](../../biology.md#morphospace) can summarize measurements, but distance in it is not automatically ecological distance. Closely related species, correlated skeletal measurements and multiple [teeth](../../biology.md#tooth) from one individual cannot be counted as independent demonstrations.

To test competition more directly, compare putatively interacting species across intervals or localities with and without the competitor, while accounting for habitat, climate, ancestry and body size. Divergence specifically associated with overlap would support [ecological character displacement](../../ecology.md#ecological-character-displacement) more strongly than a difference observed at one site. Comparable shifts in abundance, resource availability or population performance would strengthen the inference, although [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) abundance itself is affected by preservation and collection. Several independent lines of evidence are needed to distinguish competition from environmental filtering or an inherited ecological difference.

**The outcome should be a ranked set of hypotheses about which resources were potentially shared, when limitation was plausible and whether divergence is associated with coexistence. Skeletal differences can support partitioning and make competition explanations testable; neither similarity nor difference alone is a direct measure of competitive strength.**

**Introduced [mammals](../../organism.md#mammal) have affected native populations through [predation](../../ecology.md#predation), competition, disease, habitat modification and [hybridization](../../genetics.md#hybridization-biology); mitigation works best when it targets the demonstrated mechanism at the geographic scale of reinvasion.** Britain and Ireland must be assessed separately, and native status is local. A mammal native to mainland Britain can be an [introduced species](../../ecology.md#introduced-species) on an offshore island. An introduced population is not automatically an [invasive species](../../ecology.md#invasive-species): establishment, spread and adverse effects need evidence.

[Grey squirrels](../../organism.md#grey-squirrel), introduced in Victorian Britain and subsequently Ireland, illustrate several mechanisms. Their expansion has accompanied major contraction of [red squirrels](../../organism.md#red-squirrel), involving resource competition as well as pathogen-mediated effects in some regions. Experimental infection found severe disease in [red squirrels](../../organism.md#red-squirrel) and little detectable effect on grey-squirrel health, supporting a role for [squirrelpox](../../biology.md#squirrelpox). [Proceedings of the Royal Society B 269, pp. 529–533; contrasting experimental susceptibility of red and grey squirrels](../../biology-bibliography.md#parapoxvirus-causes-a-deleterious-disease-in-red-squirrels-associated-with-uk-population-declines-by-daniel-m-tompkins-and-others) That comparison establishes differential susceptibility, not the cause of every field decline. Habitat, exposure, squirrel density and the presence of the virus must be assessed locally. [grey squirrels](../../organism.md#grey-squirrel) also damage trees through bark stripping, adding forestry costs to biodiversity effects.

A response can combine surveillance, exclusion of new introductions, targeted population control and habitat management favouring [red squirrels](../../organism.md#red-squirrel). Control should be coordinated across connected populations because small cleared areas can be repeatedly recolonized. Recovery of [pine martens](../../organism.md#pine-marten) in Ireland has been associated with reduced grey-squirrel abundance, making predator recovery a potentially useful part of management. [Biodiversity and Conservation 23, pp. 753–774; distributional changes associated with pine marten recovery](../../biology-bibliography.md#population-crash-in-an-invasive-species-following-the-recovery-of-a-native-predator-the-case-of-the-american-grey-squirrel-and-the-european-pine-marten-in-ireland-by-emma-sheehy-and-others) The association is not a guarantee that every woodland will respond similarly or a reason to ignore effects on other prey. Predator recovery and direct squirrel management should be evaluated through measured outcomes and the local ecological setting.

[American mink](../../organism.md#american-mink), established through twentieth-century escapes and releases associated with fur farming, have affected vulnerable prey, including [water voles](../../organism.md#water-vole) and some waterbirds. Their movements along waterways make isolated trapping effort vulnerable to reinvasion from elsewhere. Landscape-scale coordinated monitoring and removal in Scotland showed how volunteer participation and adaptive deployment can operate at a much larger scale than occasional local control. [Biological Conservation 144, pp. 575–583; landscape-scale monitoring and coordinated control](../../biology-bibliography.md#turning-back-the-tide-of-american-mink-invasion-at-an-unprecedented-scale-through-community-participation-and-adaptive-management-by-rosalind-bryce-and-others) A trap capture is an operational measure; the conservation endpoint is improved prey survival, occupancy or reproduction. Habitat restoration remains important where bank condition, fragmentation or other pressures also limit [water voles](../../organism.md#water-vole).

Introduced deer create a different set of impacts. [Sika deer](../../organism.md#sika-deer) and [muntjac](../../organism.md#muntjac) can contribute to heavy browsing, changes in woodland regeneration and understorey structure, damage to crops and increased collision risk. Attribution needs care because native deer and changes in their abundance can contribute to the same pressures. In an English woodland experiment, paired fenced and browsed areas of coppice provided evidence that deer exclusion altered habitat use by breeding nightingales. [Ibis 152, pp. 335–346; eight paired deer exclosures and browsed controls](../../biology-bibliography.md#experimental-evidence-that-deer-browsing-reduces-habitat-suitability-for-breeding-common-nightingales-luscinia-megarhynchos-by-chas-a-holt-and-others) Pairing helped separate browsing effects from much of the shared habitat setting, but the experiment did not assign all landscape-level bird decline to introduced deer alone. Deer management should therefore use observed vegetation and wildlife responses rather than a target justified solely by species origin.

[Sika deer](../../organism.md#sika-deer) also hybridize with native [red deer](../../organism.md#red-deer). Genetic analysis of a Scottish contact zone detected [introgression](../../biology.md#introgression) even though many animals remained readily classified as red-like or sika-like. [Genetics 152, pp. 355–371; microsatellite and mitochondrial evidence of introgression](../../biology-bibliography.md#introgression-through-rare-hybridization-a-genetic-study-of-a-hybrid-zone-between-red-and-sika-deer-genus-cervus-in-argyll-scotland-by-simon-j-goodman-and-others) Phenotypic appearance therefore cannot reliably exclude mixed ancestry. The genetic objective of management must be stated explicitly, with appropriate sampling and markers; local removal cannot undo past [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow) already distributed through a population. Preventing further releases or escapes and managing contact zones can be more tractable than claiming that every admixed individual can be identified visually.

Offshore islands show why national native status is insufficient. Introduced [hedgehogs](../../organism.md#hedgehog) in the Western Isles became [predators](../../ecology.md#predator) of wader eggs. A removal experiment compared fenced plots with adjacent controls and found improved nest success where [hedgehogs](../../organism.md#hedgehog) were removed, without evidence that native bird [predators](../../ecology.md#predator) compensated fully for the reduction. [Journal of Applied Ecology 38, pp. 802–812; fenced removal plots, adjacent controls and nest monitoring](../../biology-bibliography.md#experimental-removal-of-introduced-hedgehogs-improves-wader-nest-success-in-the-western-isles-scotland-by-digger-b-jackson) This supplies stronger causal evidence than a simple correlation between [hedgehogs](../../organism.md#hedgehog) and declining birds. Exclosures and removal can protect critical sites, but fence maintenance, recolonization and the consequences of moving animals elsewhere must be considered. Transporting a problem to another sensitive site is not effective mitigation.

Ireland also has recent small-mammal invasions. The [greater white-toothed shrew](../../organism.md#greater-white-toothed-shrew), first recorded there in 2007, expanded in a region previously occupied by the native [pygmy shrew](../../organism.md#pygmy-shrew); field studies linked the invasion to changes in the latter's distribution. [PLoS ONE 9, e100403; first recorded in 2007, spread and native pygmy-shrew distribution](../../biology-bibliography.md#invading-and-expanding-range-dynamics-and-ecological-consequences-of-the-greater-white-toothed-shrew-crocidura-russula-invasion-in-ireland-by-allan-d-mcdevitt-and-others) The finding motivates monitoring invasion fronts and native populations, while recognizing that distributional association alone does not completely identify the competitive mechanism. Small species are easily moved unnoticed with materials, so pathway surveillance can be valuable even where widespread eradication is difficult. A British management priority cannot simply be copied to Ireland because the native communities and invasion histories differ.

Eradication is possible under suitable conditions. The British campaigns against [muskrats](../../organism.md#muskrat) and [coypus](../../organism.md#coypu), both introduced through fur farming in the 1920s, illustrate the importance of early response and sustained demographic planning. Later coypu control used population studies, trapping-effort information and coordinated deployment rather than assuming that opportunistic captures would finish the job. [Biological Journal of the Linnean Society 38, pp. 39–51; contrasting campaigns and population-based control planning](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-eradication-of-muskrats-and-coypus-from-britain-by-l-m-gosling-and-others) The lesson is operational as well as ecological: eradication requires resources to find the last animals, a reason for participants to continue as captures decline, and surveillance after apparent success.

Across examples, prevention and rapid response are usually more feasible than reversing a widespread established invasion. Measures include securing managed animals, identifying transport pathways, detecting new populations promptly and selecting geographically defensible management units. Where complete eradication is unrealistic, sustained suppression or protection of high-value sites may still have substantial benefits. Methods should minimize harm to non-target animals and be compared for effectiveness, feasibility and welfare consequences. Reinvasion, compensatory reproduction and interactions among [predators](../../ecology.md#predator) or competitors can otherwise undermine a superficially successful intervention.

Evaluation should use appropriate controls or a [before-after control-impact design](../../design-of-experiments.md#before-after-control-impact-design), with detection effort and habitat change recorded. Reduced invader abundance, improved native reproduction, recovered vegetation and reduced [introgression](../../biology.md#introgression) are distinct outcomes and may not change together. **Successful mitigation is not simply fewer introduced animals: it is a demonstrated reduction of their harmful effects, maintained through appropriate geographic coverage, prevention of renewed introductions and continued monitoring.**

**“[missing link](../../biology.md#missing-link)” is useful when it names a specific, testable gap in an evolutionary reconstruction; it is misleading when it implies a single required intermediate in a linear chain.** Modern [phylogenetics](../../biology.md#phylogenetics) predicts branching relationships, shared derived characters and sequences of anatomical change. A [transitional fossil](../../biology.md#transitional-fossil) can preserve a mosaic of ancestral and derived features without being the direct ancestor of any named species. The surviving record samples relatives of ancestors far more readily than it identifies particular parent-to-descendant chains.

A helpful prediction therefore specifies what is missing. One might predict a therian relative that combines derived molar characters with an ancestral reproductive or pelvic condition, or an early aquatic placental retaining terrestrial weight-bearing limbs. The [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) should occur in an independently defensible time interval and be evaluated in a broad character analysis. Naming an unusual [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) after seeing it is weaker than showing that it tests an earlier prediction. Conversely, incompleteness of the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) record is expected from preservation and sampling; absence alone does not falsify common descent unless suitable rocks, preservation and collecting effort make the absence informative.

[Theria](../../organism.md#theria) includes living [marsupials](../../organism.md#marsupial) and [placental mammals](../../organism.md#placental-mammal) and their common ancestry. [Metatheria](../../organism.md#metatheria) and [Eutheria](../../organism.md#eutheria) are broader lineages on either side of that split, whereas the living marsupial and placental [crown groups](../../biology.md#crown-group) are more restricted. These definitions are essential when a [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) is called a “link” to [placental mammals](../../organism.md#placental-mammal). Possession of some [eutherian](../../organism.md#eutheria) characters does not establish that it belonged inside the [crown group](../../biology.md#crown-group) of living placental orders, much less that it was the ancestor of all of them.

[Eomaia scansoria](../../organism.md#eomaia-scansoria), from the Early [Cretaceous](../../planetary-science.md#cretaceous) of China at approximately 125 million years ago, illustrates the distinction. Its skeleton extended knowledge of early [eutherian](../../organism.md#eutheria) anatomy and combined derived characters used in phylogenetic placement with an ancestral overall construction, including [epipubic bones](../../biology.md#epipubic-bone). Limb and foot anatomy also supported climbing capabilities. [Nature 416, pp. 816–822; Eomaia skeleton and stem-eutherian character combination](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-earliest-known-eutherian-mammal-by-qiang-ji-and-others) It is informative as a commonly reconstructed stem [eutherian](../../organism.md#eutheria), rather than as a complete miniature version of a modern placental. The retained [epipubic bones](../../biology.md#epipubic-bone) are particularly valuable because they show why dental and cranial affinities must not be converted automatically into an inference of fully modern reproductive physiology. Soft-tissue details and exact gestation cannot simply be read from the skeleton.

A second example shows why a “missing-link” label must remain revisable. [Sinodelphys](../../organism.md#sinodelphys) was initially interpreted as an Early [Cretaceous](../../planetary-science.md#cretaceous) metatherian, apparently helping sample the early marsupial side of the split. Description of the more anatomically informative [Ambolestes zhoui](../../organism.md#ambolestes-zhoui) and revised comparisons instead supported a [eutherian](../../organism.md#eutheria) placement for Sinodelphys. [Nature 558, pp. 390–395; Ambolestes anatomy and revised placement of Sinodelphys](../../biology-bibliography.md#an-early-cretaceous-eutherian-and-the-placental-marsupial-dichotomy-by-shundong-bi-and-others) The change did not make the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) worthless or refute evolution. It changed which hypothesis its characters support, illustrating the role of missing anatomy, character homology and taxon sampling. Treating the initial identification as a permanent certificate of ancestry would make the metaphor obstruct rather than assist testing.

Transitions among established placental lineages supply more complete examples. Early whales test the prediction that an aquatic mammal lineage descended from terrestrial relatives. [Pakicetids](../../organism.md#pakicetid) combine diagnostic whale ear-region anatomy with a substantially terrestrial skeleton. Protocetid ankle bones preserve the double-pulley [astragalus](../../biology.md#astragalus) characteristic of artiodactyl ancestry, even while the locomotor system was becoming increasingly aquatic. [Nature 413, pp. 277–281; pakicetid postcranial skeletons](../../biology-bibliography.md#skeletons-of-terrestrial-cetaceans-and-the-relationship-of-whales-to-artiodactyls-by-j-g-m-thewissen-and-others) [Science 293, pp. 2239–2242; protocetid ankle anatomy](../../biology-bibliography.md#origin-of-whales-from-early-artiodactyls-hands-and-feet-of-eocene-protocetidae-from-pakistan-by-philip-d-gingerich-and-others) The significance lies in the combination: a whale need not already have every feature of a living dolphin, and the ankle provides an independent character system against which a relationship based on [teeth](../../biology.md#tooth) can be tested.

Later basilosaurids were fully aquatic but retained very small [hind limbs](../../organism.md#hind-limb) incapable of supporting terrestrial walking. [Science 249, pp. 154–157; reduced pelvis and hind limbs](../../biology-bibliography.md#hind-limbs-of-eocene-basilosaurus-evidence-of-feet-in-whales-by-philip-d-gingerich-and-others) Such remains test gradual modification of structures and changing functions, rather than requiring an organism halfway between two modern endpoints in every trait. A reduced structure is not necessarily functionless, and a named basilosaurid is not thereby demonstrated to be the direct ancestor of all living whales. The sequence is assembled from branching relatives and independently dated occurrences, with uncertainty about particular soft tissues and behaviours.

[Pezosiren portelli](../../organism.md#pezosiren-portelli) provides a valuable independent transition in [Sirenia](../../organism.md#sirenia). The [Eocene](../../planetary-science.md#eocene) Jamaican skeleton retains four substantial limbs, a multivertebral sacrum and a strong sacroiliac connection consistent with supporting the body on land, alongside aquatic specializations. [Nature 413, pp. 625–627; Pezosiren locomotor and aquatic character mosaic](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-earliest-known-fully-quadrupedal-sirenian-by-daryl-p-domning) It therefore contradicts the assumption that all members of the sea-cow lineage always possessed the obligately aquatic, hindlimb-reduced condition of living manatees and dugongs. Similarity to early whales in the broad transition to water does not make sirenians whale ancestors: they represent separate placental branches. Comparing the transitions helps distinguish common functional demands from common descent of the entire specialized body plan.

Marsupial history adds a biogeographic use of transitional character combinations. Early [Eocene](../../planetary-science.md#eocene) Australian [Djarthia](../../organism.md#djarthia) preserves ankle evidence informative about the early history of [Australidelphia](../../organism.md#australidelphia), which includes Australian marsupial groups and the South American [Microbiotheria](../../organism.md#microbiotheria). [PLoS ONE 3, e1858; Djarthia tarsals and australidelphian relationships](../../biology-bibliography.md#australia-s-oldest-marsupial-fossils-and-their-biogeographical-implications-by-robin-m-d-beck-and-others) It helps test relationships and former distributions without identifying one [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) as the unique ancestor that physically crossed a particular route. An early sampled occurrence sets a minimum age and a location, not necessarily an evolutionary birthplace. This is a more precise application of the useful intuition behind a geographical “[missing link](../../biology.md#missing-link)”.

These cases test several parts of evolutionary reconstruction: nested anatomical relationships, the timing of innovations, correlated changes in function and the geography of lineages. Strong assessment combines multiple structures rather than choosing a single convenient similarity, compares alternative placements, and treats geological age separately from anatomical inference. An apparent contradiction should first be checked for dating error, mistaken homology or sampling bias; alternatives must themselves make testable predictions rather than becoming unlimited excuses.

**There is no final [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) that completes one universal chain. Therian [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) are most useful when they reveal predicted mosaics, expose previously hidden diversity or force revision of a particular relationship. Their contribution is to test the branching history and mechanisms of change with increasingly specific evidence, not to supply a ceremonial link between two living forms.**

**Genetic data provide relationships, demographic histories and mechanistic hypotheses that can be tested against [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) anatomy and dates; they do not make the synapsid [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) record redundant.** Most deep synapsid evolution is much older than recoverable [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid). Molecular biology therefore often contributes indirectly through living descendants, calibrated models and developmental experiments, while exceptional younger [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) yield [Ancient DNA](../../polymer.md#ancient-dna) or proteins themselves.

A major contribution is an independent framework for relationships. Superficially similar “ungulates” or insectivore-like [mammals](../../organism.md#mammal) need not form natural groups. Molecular analyses recovered large placental groupings such as [Afrotheria](../../organism.md#afrotheria) and [Laurasiatheria](../../organism.md#laurasiatheria), each containing independently specialized ecological forms. [Nature 409, pp. 610–614; molecular placental groups and parallel ecological forms](../../biology-bibliography.md#parallel-adaptive-radiations-in-two-major-clades-of-placental-mammals-by-ole-madsen-and-others) That result changes interpretation of the [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) record: similarities in feeding or locomotion must be tested for [convergent evolution](../../biology.md#convergent-evolution), and extinct taxa must be placed using broader character combinations. Living [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) does not itself score the missing anatomy of an extinct animal, but it changes which ancestral reconstructions and comparisons are plausible.

Whale origins show a particularly clear exchange between molecules and [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil). Molecular evidence placed [Cetacea](../../organism.md#cetacea) within the artiodactyl radiation and allied it with [hippopotamuses](../../organism.md#hippopotamus), challenging interpretations driven strongly by dental resemblance to mesonychians. Analyses of independent [retrotransposon insertion](../../molecular-genetics.md#retrotransposon-insertion) loci supported the whale–hippopotamus relationship. [PNAS 96, pp. 10261–10266; independent insertion loci and artiodactyl relationships](../../biology-bibliography.md#phylogenetic-relationships-among-cetartiodactyls-based-on-insertions-of-short-and-long-interspersed-elements-hippopotamuses-are-the-closest-extant-relatives-of-whales-by-masato-nikaido-and-others) Subsequently described early whale limb skeletons supplied artiodactyl ankle characters consistent with that relationship. [Nature 413, pp. 277–281; pakicetid postcranial skeletons](../../biology-bibliography.md#skeletons-of-terrestrial-cetaceans-and-the-relationship-of-whales-to-artiodactyls-by-j-g-m-thewissen-and-others) [Science 293, pp. 2239–2242; protocetid ankle anatomy](../../biology-bibliography.md#origin-of-whales-from-early-artiodactyls-hands-and-feet-of-eocene-protocetidae-from-pakistan-by-philip-d-gingerich-and-others) This is stronger than agreement among several reanalyses of the same [tooth](../../biology.md#tooth) characters because the anatomical and molecular character systems have different sources of error. A living hippopotamus remains a relative, not a whale ancestor.

Rare genomic changes can be particularly useful, but no character type is infallible. A [retrotransposon insertion](../../molecular-genetics.md#retrotransposon-insertion) requires orthology and correct reconstruction of insertion presence or absence. Ancestral polymorphism and [incomplete lineage sorting](../../biology.md#incomplete-lineage-sorting) can produce discordance among loci; [hybridization](../../genetics.md#hybridization-biology) can produce a genuinely non-tree-like ancestry. The same concerns apply to nucleotide data: one [gene tree](../../biology.md#gene-tree), especially a mitochondrial genealogy, need not equal a [species tree](../../biology.md#species-tree). Broad genomic sampling and explicit models are therefore preferable to declaring a relationship settled by a single attractive marker.

Molecular clocks add time to the framework. Under the simplest two-lineage model, expected sequence divergence is approximately:

$$
d\simeq2rt,
$$

with substitution rate $r$ per site per unit time and divergence time $t$. The sequences alone constrain a rate–time product, not both independently. [Fossil calibration](../../biology.md#fossil-calibration), other external time information and assumptions about rate variation are needed. [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) age, stem-versus-crown placement and the difference between a minimum occurrence and an origin are consequently central molecular inputs, rather than inconvenient qualifications added afterwards.

The timing of placental diversification illustrates the consequences. Some combined anatomical and molecular reconstructions emphasized a major post-[Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary](../../planetary-science.md#cretaceous-palaeogene-boundary) radiation, whereas calibrated phylogenomic timelines support pre-boundary divergences for important deep placental lineages. [Science 339, pp. 662–667; combined morphology and molecular data and post-boundary interpretation](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-placental-mammal-ancestor-and-the-post-k-pg-radiation-of-placentals-by-maureen-a-o-leary-and-others) [Nature 602, pp. 263–267; calibrated molecular divergence estimates](../../biology-bibliography.md#a-species-level-timeline-of-mammal-evolution-integrating-phylogenomic-data-by-sandra-alvarez-carretero-and-others) These results need not disagree about every aspect of the event: separation of deep lineages, diversification within orders and expansion in ecological disparity can occur at different times. [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) identified only as [Eutheria](../../organism.md#eutheria) do not automatically establish a [Cretaceous](../../planetary-science.md#cretaceous) crown placental order. Conversely, failure to sample an earlier crown member is not automatically a hard maximum on its age.

The resulting [ghost lineages](../../biology.md#ghost-lineage) formulate useful [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) predictions. A molecular hypothesis placing two living branches apart before their oldest known [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) predicts unsampled history, potentially in different regions or ecological settings. Appropriate tests compare collecting intensity and preservation, search suitable deposits and assess whether putative older members possess the required characters. Calibration sensitivity should be reported: changing maximum-age assumptions, rates or [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) placement can move the estimate. A date inferred using a particular [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) as its calibration is not an independent molecular confirmation of that same [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil)'s age.

Genomic and developmental work can also test a proposed transformation of anatomy. The mammalian middle ear incorporates bones homologous to components of the ancestral [jaw](../../biology.md#jaw) apparatus. Living mammalian development retains a connection involving [Meckel's cartilage](../../biology.md#meckel-s-cartilage), and experimental study of its breakdown, including interference with cartilage-resorbing cells, links developmental removal to the transition from a jaw-connected to a detached ear apparatus. [Nature Ecology & Evolution 1, 0093; developmental breakdown of the jaw–ear connection](../../biology-bibliography.md#meckel’s-cartilage-breakdown-offers-clues-to-mammalian-middle-ear-evolution-by-neal-anthwal-and-others) These experiments offer a mechanism consistent with [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) intermediates. They do not show that every [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) lineage used an identical gene-regulatory change or that evolution simply repeated the entire embryonic sequence. To test a specific mechanism, compare matched developmental stages and relevant tissues and distinguish a targeted persistence of the connection from generalized developmental delay.

Younger [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) sometimes allow a more direct molecular test. [collagen](../../protein.md#collagen) sequences from [Toxodon](../../organism.md#toxodon) and [Macrauchenia](../../organism.md#macrauchenia) supported affinities with the perissodactyl side of the placental tree, clarifying the relationships of South American ungulate-like [mammals](../../organism.md#mammal) for which convergent anatomy had been problematic. [Nature 522, pp. 81–84; collagen sequences from Toxodon and Macrauchenia](../../biology-bibliography.md#ancient-proteins-resolve-the-evolutionary-history-of-darwin-s-south-american-ungulates-by-frido-welker-and-others) [Palaeoproteomics](../../biology.md#palaeoproteomics) recovers amino-acid information rather than a complete [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome), so the number and independence of informative positions differ from [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) studies. Authentication, contamination checks and comparison with plausible alternatives remain necessary, and the result should not be generalized automatically to every extinct South American ungulate lineage.

Ancient mammoth [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) extend direct inference into the Early [Pleistocene](../../planetary-science.md#pleistocene). More-than-million-year-old [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) revealed distinct mammoth lineages and supported hybrid ancestry in the history of Columbian mammoths. [Nature 591, pp. 265–269; Early Pleistocene genomes and hybrid ancestry of Columbian mammoths](../../biology-bibliography.md#million-year-old-dna-sheds-light-on-the-genomic-history-of-mammoths-by-tom-van-der-valk-and-others) This helps test whether a morphology-based [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) grouping corresponds to one ancestry lineage, reveals population processes invisible in an isolated bone, and connects some adaptive changes to dated specimens. Preservation in a favourable environment does not imply that [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid) can be recovered from Permian or Mesozoic [synapsids](../../organism.md#synapsid). The deep [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) record must therefore remain accessible chiefly through anatomy, chemistry and comparative models.

Finally, genetic data help separate the origin of an underlying pathway from the origin of a visible trait. A conserved developmental [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) can be older than a particular ear structure or locomotor specialization; a [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) duplication or regulatory change is not, by itself, a dated [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) of a phenotype. Functional experiments establish what a mechanism can do in a living context. [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) distribution and comparative anatomy establish when and where the corresponding construction is actually evidenced. [Total-evidence phylogenetics](../../biology.md#total-evidence-phylogenetics) can combine these sources, but must retain their different uncertainties and avoid counting correlated characters as independent confirmations.

**The strongest accounts are reciprocal: molecular hypotheses guide [fossil](../../biology.md#fossil) searches and interpretations, while [fossils](../../biology.md#fossil) calibrate and challenge molecular histories. Genetic data have exposed convergence, refined timescales and revealed reticulate population histories; developmental experiments make some anatomical transitions mechanistically testable without pretending that living [genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) directly record every ancient synapsid form.**
