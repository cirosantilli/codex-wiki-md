**A node-based name selects a common ancestor from included reference taxa; a branch-based name selects a lineage by including one reference and excluding another.** Both designate a [clade](../../../../clade.md), so both include every descendant of the selected ancestor. The distinction concerns the rule locating that ancestor, not whether descendants are included. Neither requires its members to share a particular modern appearance.[Fig. 1 and Introduction; minimum- and maximum-clade definitions and internal/external specifiers](../../../../a-new-phylogenetic-data-standard-for-computable-clade-definitions-the-phyloreference-exchange-format-phyx-by-gaurav-vaidya-and-others.md)

For a [node-based clade definition](../../../../node-based-clade-definition.md), choose internal [phylogenetic specifiers](../../../../phylogenetic-specifier.md) $A$ and $B$. Let $m(A,B)$ be their [last common ancestor](../../../../last-common-ancestor.md), and let $D(v)$ include ancestor $v$ and all its descendants. Then the named group is

$$
N(A,B)=D\bigl(m(A,B)\bigr).
$$

It is the smallest [clade](../../../../clade.md) containing both references. For a [branch-based clade definition](../../../../branch-based-clade-definition.md), choose an internal reference $A$ and an external reference $Z$. The named group is the largest [clade](../../../../clade.md) containing $A$ but excluding $Z$: organisms sharing a more recent common ancestor with $A$ than with $Z$. On a rooted [phylogenetic tree](../../../../phylogenetic-tree.md), this selects the $A$-side lineage after its separation from the lineage containing $Z$. These are also called [minimum-clade definitions](../../../../node-based-clade-definition.md) and [maximum-clade definitions](../../../../branch-based-clade-definition.md). The names describe inclusion constraints, not distances measured from branch lengths.[Fig. 1 and Introduction; minimum- and maximum-clade definitions and internal/external specifiers](../../../../a-new-phylogenetic-data-standard-for-computable-clade-definitions-the-phyloreference-exchange-format-phyx-by-gaurav-vaidya-and-others.md)

An informative avian node example is **crown [Aves](../../../../aves.md)**, here anchored to living [ostrich](../../../../ostrich.md) and [sparrow](../../../../sparrow.md) lineages. Their [last common ancestor](../../../../last-common-ancestor.md) is the ancestor of living [Palaeognathae](../../../../palaeognathae.md) and [Neognathae](../../../../neognathae.md); the name includes that ancestor and all descendants, including extinct descendants within either branch. An informative branch example is **[Panaves](../../../../panaves.md)**, the avian [total group](../../../../total-group.md): the largest [clade](../../../../clade.md) including living [birds](../../../../bird.md) but excluding living [crocodilians](../../../../crocodilian.md). With standard [archosaur](../../../../archosauria.md) relationships it includes [Aves](../../../../aves.md), extinct avian stem relatives, nonavian [dinosaurs](../../../../dinosaur.md) and [pterosaurs](../../../../pterosaur.md). The external reference is decisive: the definition asks which side of the [bird](../../../../bird.md)–[crocodilian](../../../../crocodilian.md) split an organism occupies, not whether it flies. These crown and total usages were distinguished explicitly in the avian nomenclatural analysis by [Jacques A. Gauthier](../../../../jacques-a-gauthier.md) and [Kevin de Queiroz](../../../../kevin-de-queiroz.md).[pp. 9–11 and 24–28; classes of definitions, crown versus total groups, Aves and Panaves](../../../../feathered-dinosaurs-flying-dinosaurs-crown-dinosaurs-and-the-name-%E2%80%9Caves%E2%80%9D-by-jacques-a-gauthier-and-kevin-de-queiroz.md)

Consider a simplified [cladogram](../../../../cladogram.md) in which the [crocodilian](../../../../crocodilian.md) lineage separates first, followed on the avian side by [pterosaurs](../../../../pterosaur.md), successive nonavian [dinosaur](../../../../dinosaur.md) branches, [Archaeopteryx](../../../../archaeopteryx.md), other extinct avian branches and finally the living-bird crown divergence. [Archaeopteryx](../../../../archaeopteryx.md) belongs to [Panaves](../../../../panaves.md) but lies outside crown [Aves](../../../../aves.md). A [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) on the [palaeognath](../../../../palaeognathae.md) branch after the [palaeognath](../../../../palaeognathae.md)–[neognath](../../../../neognathae.md) divergence belongs to both. A [crocodilian](../../../../crocodilian.md) belongs to neither. Finding a still earlier bird-line [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) would extend the known [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) history of [Panaves](../../../../panaves.md) without automatically changing the age or known membership of crown [Aves](../../../../aves.md). This example is schematic: it illustrates how definitions operate and is not a claim that any sampled [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) is the direct ancestor of another.

Two distinctions prevent mistakes. First, a [node-based clade definition](../../../../node-based-clade-definition.md) need not define a [crown group](../../../../crown-group.md): internal references can be extinct, as in a deliberately broader Archaeopteryx-inclusive node definition. Likewise, a [branch-based clade definition](../../../../branch-based-clade-definition.md) need not define an entire [total group](../../../../total-group.md); using a nonavian [theropod dinosaur](../../../../theropod-dinosaur.md) as the excluded reference selects a much narrower avian-lineage branch than excluding [crocodilians](../../../../crocodilian.md). Second, a [stem group](../../../../stem-group.md) is not synonymous with the branch-defined [total group](../../../../total-group.md). The total includes the crown; the stem alone excludes it and normally forms a succession of extinct branches.

The definition must therefore accompany an ambiguous name. Historical uses of [Aves](../../../../aves.md) have included the living-bird crown and more inclusive [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) assemblages; [Avialae](../../../../avialae.md) has also received differing definitions. Discovery of [feathers](../../../../feather.md) in a nonavian [theropod dinosaur](../../../../theropod-dinosaur.md) changes the inferred distribution of a [phylogenetic character](../../../../phylogenetic-character.md), but does not by itself place the animal inside crown [Aves](../../../../aves.md). Conversely, loss of flight does not remove a crown descendant from its [clade](../../../../clade.md). **A name supplies an ancestry rule; anatomical and molecular evidence supply the tree on which that rule is applied.** Uncertain [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) placement produces uncertain membership even when the definition is precise.
