↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**The [mirror-mark test](../../../../mirror-mark-test.md) can provide evidence that an animal uses reflected information to identify and act on its own body. It is a narrower assay than a general test of [self-awareness](../../../../self-awareness.md), and failure is especially difficult to interpret.** The core inference rests on directing an action to the otherwise unseen marked body part, rather than merely responding to something unusual in a mirror.

In a suitable design, an animal first has opportunities to inspect a mirror without a mark. A visible mark is then placed where it cannot be seen directly, using a procedure that avoids an informative tactile, olfactory or handling cue. After recovery from any necessary handling, investigators compare responses with the mirror available and unavailable, and with a matched invisible [sham treatment](../../../../sham-treatment.md). The predicted response is mark-directed investigation of the animal's own body specifically when the visible change can be seen in reflection. Looking at the mirror, attacking it or searching behind it are interesting observations, but do not alone establish [bodily self-recognition](../../../../bodily-self-recognition.md).

The classic [chimpanzee](../../../../chimpanzee.md) study used prior mirror exposure and otherwise unseen dye marks to demonstrate self-directed investigation. [Science 167, pp. 86–87; mirror exposure, hidden dye marks and self-directed touching](../../../../chimpanzees-self-recognition-by-gordon-g-gallup-jr.md) Its conceptual advance was to turn a claim about self-recognition into a behavioural prediction that could be compared with alternative responses. A mark on the reflected forehead corresponds to a location on the subject's own forehead. Reaching there uses information about the relation between the image and body; trying to touch the glass instead supports a different interpretation. An odourless mark that is imperceptible by touch makes this distinction stronger.

A rigorous modern design makes those alternatives explicit. In a factorial comparison, let $T_{VM}$ and $T_{SM}$ denote rates of appropriately localized body-directed actions for visible and sham marks with a mirror, and let $T_{VC}$ and $T_{SC}$ denote the corresponding rates with the mirror covered. The interaction contrast:

$$
D=(T_{VM}-T_{SM})-(T_{VC}-T_{SC})
$$

asks whether the visible mark's effect specifically depends on reflective information. A positive $D$ is useful only if the actions are correctly localized, opportunities to act are comparable and uncertainty is estimated across animals. It does not acquire a cognitive interpretation just because its statistical test is significant. General increases in [grooming](../../../../grooming.md), attention or agitation need to be coded separately.

Controls should match application pressure, odour, texture and handling, vary mark side or location, and counterbalance condition order. A covered mirror controls its physical presence; a live or recorded other animal helps characterize social responses. Noncontingent or delayed self-images can test the importance of immediate [sensorimotor contingency](../../../../sensorimotor-contingency.md), though they also increase task difficulty. Separate trials with directly visible marks help establish that a subject notices such a change and has an available response, without training the critical hidden-mark action in advance. Coders should be [blinded](../../../../blinding.md) where feasible, and the criteria for a pass specified before viewing the outcome. Repeated touches by one animal are not independent evidence from many animals.

A positive result supports at least a learned mapping between reflected information and the subject's body. How much more it supports is a problem of [construct validity](../../../../construct-validity.md). An animal might combine visual contingencies, [proprioception](../../../../proprioception.md) and a [body schema](../../../../body-schema.md) without representing itself as a named, temporally continuous individual. Conversely, [learning](../../../../learning.md) the mapping does not make the result meaningless: learned information about one's own body can itself be a form of [self-representation](../../../../self-representation.md). The question is which alternative mechanisms the design distinguishes. A pass does not by itself demonstrate [theory of mind](../../../../theory-of-mind.md), [autobiographical memory](../../../../autobiographical-memory.md), moral understanding or a particular [subjective experience](../../../../subjective-experience.md).

Negative results are weaker because successful performance requires several capacities together: noticing the mark, attending to reflection, relating the image to the body, being motivated to investigate, and possessing a suitable action. Fear, social attraction to the image, poor visual access or indifference to marks can prevent the predicted response even when some self-related information is available. [ecological validity](../../../../ecological-validity.md) is therefore central. Mirror dimensions, access, viewing geometry and prior experience must suit the [species](../../../../species.md). Uniformly presenting a small mirror to animals of very different sizes does not create a biologically equivalent test.

The elephant study illustrates this dependence. Three [asian elephants](../../../../asian-elephant.md) were exposed to a large accessible mirror and visible and sham marks. One passed the mark criterion, while the others also showed informative mirror-oriented exploration. [PNAS 103, pp. 17053–17057; large accessible mirror, visible and sham marks, one of three subjects passing](../../../../self-recognition-in-an-asian-elephant-by-joshua-m-plotnik-and-others.md) This is evidence about the observed individuals and assay, not a species-wide estimate based on one success, nor proof that the nonpassing individuals lacked every form of [self-representation](../../../../self-representation.md). The apparatus enabled close physical exploration that smaller or inaccessible mirrors would not permit.

The required action must also reflect anatomy. [Bottlenose dolphins](../../../../bottlenose-dolphin.md) cannot use a hand to touch a facial mark. Investigators therefore examined orientations and movements exposing marked regions to a reflective surface, comparing marked and sham-marked conditions. [PNAS 98, pp. 5937–5942; marked and sham-marked dolphins and mirror-oriented body inspection](../../../../mirror-self-recognition-in-the-bottlenose-dolphin-a-case-of-cognitive-convergence-by-diana-reiss-and-lori-marino.md) Such an [adaptation](../../../../adaptation.md) avoids treating hand use as a cognitive prerequisite, but creates a greater interpretive burden: body inspection must be distinguished from ordinary swimming, attention to handling or generalized interest in the mirror. Spatially specific changes and [blinded](../../../../blinding.md) coding are particularly valuable.

[cleaner wrasse](../../../../cleaner-wrasse.md) challenge assumptions about which animals should count as passing. Following mirror exposure, [fish](../../../../fish.md) with coloured marks on otherwise unseen regions scraped their bodies in the mirror condition; transparent marks and coloured marks without a mirror did not elicit the same pattern. [PLoS Biology 17, e3000021; cleaner wrasse, coloured versus transparent marks and mirror-dependent scraping](../../../../if-a-fish-can-pass-the-mark-test-what-are-the-implications-for-consciousness-and-self-awareness-testing-in-animals-by-masanori-kohda-and-others.md) The ecological relevance of parasite-like marks helps explain motivation and sharpens the test. It also raises mechanistic questions about whether the animal identifies a persistent visual self or uses learned reflected cues to localize a possible [parasite](../../../../parasite.md). Those alternatives require further controls and transfer tests; accepting a behavioural criterion in [mammals](../../../../mammal.md) but rejecting identical criteria solely because the animal is a [fish](../../../../fish.md) would be inconsistent.

Tests in other modalities can help identify capacities missed by a visual assay. [dogs](../../../../dog.md) investigated modified own odours more than unmodified own odours, with comparisons involving other dogs and the added odour alone. [Behavioural Processes 143, pp. 17–24; own, modified-own, other-dog and modifier-only odour comparisons](../../../../smelling-themselves-dogs-investigate-their-own-odours-longer-when-modified-in-an-%E2%80%9Colfactory-mirror%E2%80%9D-test-by-alexandra-horowitz.md) The modifier-only comparison addresses simple interest in a novel smell, but sniffing longer does not require locating a change on one's own body. It consequently supports odour discrimination and a possible self-related comparison more directly than it establishes the same representation tested by hidden-mark localization. Ecologically appropriate tasks should be compared in terms of their information and inferential demands, rather than all being declared interchangeable mirror tests.

A stronger account would combine spontaneous mirror-guided inspection, transfer to new untrained body locations or viewing arrangements, and independent tasks probing self-related information. Training should be reported explicitly: success after reinforcement demonstrates a learned capacity under those conditions, while spontaneous generalization supports a different claim. Samples should include individual and developmental variation, and comparative analyses should retain uncertainty rather than coding entire [species](../../../../species.md) as cognitively present or absent after a handful of observations.

**A well-controlled pass is evidence for mirror-guided bodily identification; it is not a complete measure of a self-concept. A failure is compatible with absent capacity, unsuitable task demands or insufficient motivation.** The [mirror-mark test](../../../../mirror-mark-test.md) is most useful as one component of a comparative research programme that separates those possibilities.
