**Lack of direct access to another animal's subjective experience limits some claims about consciousness, but does not prevent testable explanations of sensory processing, behavioural function or evolutionary history.** The essential distinction is between occupying another individual's standpoint and explaining what information it acquires, how it uses that information, and the consequences for survival and reproduction. [Thomas Nagel](../../../../thomas-nagel.md)'s argument concerns the subjective character of experience, rather than a claim that objective bat biology is inaccessible. [The Philosophical Review 83, pp. 435–450, especially pp. 439–442: subjective standpoint versus objective description](../../../../what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat-by-thomas-nagel.md)

For behavioural biology, [Tinbergen's four questions](../../../../tinbergen-s-four-questions.md) keep the explanatory targets separate. Mechanism asks what sensory and neural processes produce a response; development asks how it changes with maturation and experience; function asks how it affects performance or fitness; evolutionary history asks how it arose and differs among relatives. [Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie 20, pp. 410–433; causation, development, survival value and evolution](../../../../on-aims-and-methods-of-ethology-by-nikolaas-tinbergen.md) Not knowing a bat's exact experience does not make the latter questions equivalent to guessing its feelings. It does mean that a functional explanation, even a very successful one, is not by itself an explanation of the qualitative character of consciousness.

[Echolocation](../../../../echolocation.md) illustrates substantial knowledge without shared phenomenology. Acoustic recordings and experiments on sensory access linked ultrasonic vocalization and hearing to obstacle avoidance in flying [bats](../../../../bat.md). [Journal of Experimental Zoology 86, pp. 481–506; acoustic and sensory tests of obstacle avoidance](../../../../the-sensory-basis-of-obstacle-avoidance-by-flying-bats-by-donald-r-griffin-and-others.md) Modern tests can vary echo delay, level or interference while measuring detection or flight responses. A prediction about ranging is available from propagation physics: for sound speed $c$, a round-trip echo delay $\tau$ from a stationary target at distance $r$ gives approximately:

$$
r=\frac{c\tau}{2}.
$$

An illustrative $2\,\mathrm{ms}$ delay at $343\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ corresponds to $0.343\,\mathrm m$. The factor two comes from outward and return travel. Real ranging also depends on target motion, atmospheric conditions, filtering and the timing of emission. None of these testable relationships requires a scientist to experience an echo as the bat does.

The experimental controls matter because a successful animal can exploit an unintended cue. Changing a visual target might also change its acoustic reflection; changing an echo's delay might accidentally change its intensity. Appropriate comparisons manipulate the putative information while holding other cues constant, include sham manipulation, and measure baseline performance. A [psychometric function](../../../../psychometric-function.md) across stimulus levels is more informative than a single success rate. [Signal detection theory](../../../../signal-detection-theory.md) distinguishes sensitivity from response criterion, helping separate a sensory change from greater willingness to respond. Failure in an unfamiliar apparatus may reflect motivation or the required motor response rather than absence of the sensory capacity.

This approach also prevents treating human [perception](../../../../perception.md) as the definition of available information. A stimulus invisible to us or outside our hearing range can be salient to the animal. The correct physical description must use the animal's receptors, spatial and temporal resolution, and ecological setting. Human photographs, displays and loudspeakers can omit relevant information or introduce artefacts. Conversely, an animal's failure to categorize objects in our preferred way need not show poor understanding: the experimental categories may not correspond to the distinctions its biology makes useful. [Sensory ecology](../../../../sensory-ecology.md) is therefore a substantive constraint on design, even though the animal's qualitative viewpoint remains partly inaccessible.

Causal intervention can connect sensory machinery to behaviour without settling phenomenology. The adult squirrel-monkey opsin-addition experiment demonstrated improved chromatic discrimination following a specified alteration of receptor input. [Nature 461, pp. 784–787; adult squirrel monkeys, added L opsin and colour discrimination](../../../../gene-therapy-for-red-green-colour-blindness-in-adult-primates-by-katherine-mancuso-and-others.md) That supports a functional inference about information use. It cannot report the appearance of the new colours to a monkey. Distinguishing these claims makes the experiment more, rather than less, informative: its conclusion is tied to an observable prediction instead of an assumed human-like experience.

Similarly, we can study affect-like influences on decisions without claiming to have measured a feeling directly. In [bumblebees](../../../../bumblebee.md), unexpected sucrose reward altered responses to ambiguous cues, with evidence for a dopamine-dependent component and tests of behaviour in more than one context. [Science 353, pp. 1529–1531; ambiguous-cue tests, sucrose reward and dopamine manipulation](../../../../unexpected-rewards-induce-dopamine-dependent-positive-emotion-like-state-changes-in-bumblebees-by-clint-j-perry-and-others.md) Such [judgement bias](../../../../judgement-bias.md) is evidence of an internal state influencing decisions; it is not a direct readout of happiness. Changes in activity, hunger, reward valuation or stimulus discrimination can produce superficially similar results and require control. Nor is an unexplained behavioural difference stronger evidence for subjective experience merely because an experimenter finds it familiar.

Evolutionary explanations require an additional bridge. Showing that a bat uses echoes establishes a mechanism; showing improved capture or obstacle avoidance under relevant conditions tests a current function. Demonstrating historical adaptation additionally requires comparisons, inherited variation and an account of the selection pressures or ancestry involved. One can test the prediction that signal structure differs with clutter or prey characteristics while allowing for shared ancestry and body size. A useful hypothesis should predict differences under specified circumstances, rather than explain every outcome retrospectively by invoking what the animal “wanted”.

There are nevertheless real limits. Different internal models can generate the same choice, and an animal cannot necessarily provide the linguistic reports that make some human experiments possible. Success with one discrimination does not reveal the complete content of a representation, and a neural correlate does not uniquely specify an experience. Questions about felt intensity, emotional quality or a reflective awareness of self demand converging behavioural and physiological evidence and may remain underdetermined. This is an epistemic limitation, not evidence that the corresponding experiences are absent.

A productive position avoids both unconstrained [anthropomorphism](../../../../anthropomorphism.md) and an equally unsupported denial of mental processing. Specify which sensory information or internal state the model requires, derive predictions, test alternatives and state the level of inference. **We can explain much of the evolution and function of animal behaviour through objective relationships among information, decisions and fitness, while remaining uncertain about precisely what those processes feel like to the animal.** The residual uncertainty should limit the wording of our conclusions and improve experimental design, rather than stop the research.
