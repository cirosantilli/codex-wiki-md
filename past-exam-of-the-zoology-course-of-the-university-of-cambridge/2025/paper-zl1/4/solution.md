<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**Repeated movements out of Africa are compatible with a predominant shared source for the ancestry of living non-Africans.** The apparent contradiction largely disappears once the unit being counted is stated: all dispersals of [Homo](../../../../homo.md), dispersals of [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md), independently established populations, or expansions that contributed substantial ancestry to people alive today. These are different historical claims.

For the genus [Homo](../../../../homo.md), an account involving only one recent departure is untenable. Archaeological occupation at [Dmanisi](../../../../dmanisi.md), Georgia, is dated to approximately 1.85–1.78 Ma, long before the late [Pleistocene](../../../../pleistocene.md) expansion of [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md). The earliest occupation levels must be distinguished from the somewhat younger hominin-bearing deposits: the site is not a single instantaneous event. Stratigraphy, dating of the underlying volcanic context and [palaeomagnetism](../../../../palaeomagnetism.md) constrain the occupation chronology. [Results, stratigraphy and palaeomagnetic chronology; distinguish earliest occupation from hominin-bearing beds](../../../../earliest-human-occupations-at-dmanisi-georgian-caucasus-dated-to-1-85-1-78-ma-by-reid-ferring-and-others.md) Later Eurasian archaic lineages, including [Neanderthals](../../../../neanderthal.md) and [Denisovans](../../../../denisovan.md), cannot simply be folded into the recent expansion of modern humans as though all were the same population moving at the same time.

Even restricting attention to [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md), the [fossil](../../../../fossil.md) chronology is more extensive than a single 50–70 ka departure. The [Misliya](../../../../misliya.md) maxilla and associated [teeth](../../../../tooth.md) in Israel were assigned to [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md) and dated to approximately 177–194 ka using complementary dating approaches. The same primary report places the better-known [Skhul](../../../../skhul.md) and [Qafzeh](../../../../qafzeh.md) modern-human occurrences at approximately 90–120 ka. [Science 359, pp. 456–459; maxillary morphology and 177–194 ka dating interval](../../../../the-earliest-modern-humans-outside-africa-by-israel-hershkovitz-and-others.md) These occurrences demonstrate presence outside Africa before the dominant later expansion if the taxonomic and chronological assignments hold. They do not by themselves demonstrate uninterrupted occupation, a particular migration route through Arabia, or a large surviving genetic contribution to present-day Eurasians.

[Apidima](../../../../apidima.md) adds an important but less secure claim. Reconstruction of the incomplete Apidima 1 cranium supported attribution to [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md) and an age exceeding 210 ka; Apidima 2 was interpreted as [Neanderthal](../../../../neanderthal.md) and younger. [Nature 571, pp. 500–504; reconstruction and uranium-series dating of Apidima 1 and 2](../../../../apidima-cave-fossils-provide-earliest-evidence-of-homo-sapiens-in-eurasia-by-katerina-harvati-and-others.md) The very fragmentary [anatomy](../../../../anatomy.md), reconstruction choices and complicated depositional context require scrutiny. An assigned minimum age is not an exact date of arrival, and a different reconstruction or taxonomic diagnosis can alter the biogeographic inference. The sensible argument for early departures therefore uses the converging set of dated remains and its uncertainty, rather than treating the oldest proposed specimen as a decisive, uncontested fact.

The main evidence favouring a single principal source is genetic. Non-African [genomes](../../../../genome.md) share much ancestry and show effects of a substantial [population bottleneck](../../../../population-bottleneck.md), consistent with descent predominantly from a subset of African diversity followed by expansion and differentiation. Genome-wide comparisons in the [Simons Genome Diversity Project](../../../../simons-genome-diversity-project.md) found that the ancestry of Aboriginal Australians, New Guineans and other non-African populations could be accommodated by a common principal dispersal, without requiring a deeply separate founding expansion for Australasia. [population history analyses comparing Australo-Melanesian and other non-African ancestry](../../../../the-simons-genome-diversity-project-300-genomes-from-142-diverse-populations-by-swapan-mallick-and-others.md) Such a result favours a parsimonious demographic model for the bulk of surviving ancestry. It does not observe a unique group crossing a border on one date, nor rule out movements whose descendants disappeared.

Uniparentally inherited [mitochondrial genomes](../../../../mitochondrial-genome.md) and the [Y chromosome](../../../../y-chromosome.md) provide complementary genealogies, but each traces only one portion of ancestry. Their dates under [coalescent theory](../../../../coalescent-theory.md) describe common ancestors of sampled sequences, not the calendar date when a population left Africa. [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md), sex-biased demography and lineage loss can remove evidence of earlier occupation. Genome-wide analyses reduce dependence on one genealogy, although the inferred expansion dates, often broadly placed around 50–70 ka, still depend on the [mutation rate](../../../../mutation-rate.md), [generation interval](../../../../generation-interval.md), ancestral population structure and the migration models compared. A prolonged connected expansion can resemble one founder event in a simplified model.

There is also genetic evidence that complicates an absolute single-expansion claim. One analysis inferred a small contribution to Papuan ancestry from an earlier expansion, while still attributing the great majority of non-African ancestry to a shared later expansion. [analyses of a predominant shared expansion and proposed earlier ancestry in Papuans](../../../../genomic-analyses-inform-on-migration-events-during-the-peopling-of-eurasia-by-luca-pagani-and-others.md) The size and necessity of such a component are model-dependent: unsampled ancestral structure or archaic [introgression](../../../../introgression.md) can mimic some signals. The appropriate comparison fits alternative histories to the same data and asks whether extra parameters improve distinctive predictions, rather than treating every deep branch in a [gene tree](../../../../gene-tree.md) as a separate exit.

Archaic [admixture](../../../../genetic-admixture.md) makes the ancestry history explicitly reticulate. Excess sharing of derived [alleles](../../../../allele.md) between [Neanderthals](../../../../neanderthal.md) and non-African modern humans supports interbreeding, beyond a simple tree in which the lineages remained completely isolated. [Science 328, pp. 710–722; excess allele sharing and evidence of admixture](../../../../a-draft-sequence-of-the-neandertal-genome-by-richard-e-green-and-others.md) Thus a model in which most modern-human ancestry comes from one late expansion can still contain genetic contributions from populations whose ancestors left Africa much earlier. [incomplete lineage sorting](../../../../incomplete-lineage-sorting.md) must be distinguished from [introgression](../../../../introgression.md) using patterns across many regions, their lengths and the distribution of [allele](../../../../allele.md) sharing. Back-migration into Africa further prevents “African” and “non-African” from being permanently sealed categories.

Africa itself should not be represented as a homogeneous ancestral village. Models with weakly differentiated African ancestral populations connected by long-term [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) can explain features of present variation without requiring a single sharply isolated birthplace population. [Nature 617, pp. 755–763; model comparison with long-term gene flow among ancestral African populations](../../../../a-weakly-structured-stem-for-human-origins-in-africa-by-aaron-p-ragsdale-and-others.md) That result concerns ancestry within Africa; it neither erases the African origin of [Homo sapiens](../../../../homo-sapiens.md) nor directly counts subsequent exits. Geographically, northern routes through the Levant and southern routes through Arabia remain testable alternatives or complements. [fossils](../../../../fossil.md) and archaeological sites constrain occupation corridors, while environmental evidence constrains opportunities; neither a genetic branching date nor lowered sea level alone proves a particular crossing route.

The reconciliation is a sequence of early, potentially intermittent expansions, considerable regional loss or replacement, a later expansion responsible for most surviving non-African modern-human ancestry, and subsequent [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) with archaic and African populations. Testing it requires directly dated remains, secure taxonomic comparisons, independent genetic samples, explicit demographic alternatives and allowance for unsampled populations. **“One dominant ancestry expansion” is a much narrower and better-supported proposition than “only one movement out of Africa”; multiple movements need not imply several equally large surviving founding ancestries.**
