<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

External chemical signals can guide [cell migration](../../../../cell-migration.md), but the statement is too strong if “determined” means that they uniquely specify whether, how fast and in which direction a cell moves. A [chemoattractant](../../../../chemoattractant.md) [gradient](../../../../gradient.md) biases a cell's internal polarity machinery; it does not directly supply the mechanical work of locomotion. [Chemotaxis](../../../../chemotaxis.md) is directional bias, whereas a change in speed or persistence without directional preference is [chemokinesis](../../../../chemokinesis.md).

A chemical response depends on receptor abundance, signalling adaptation and the cell's existing [cell polarity](../../../../cell-polarity.md). The same external field can therefore yield different trajectories in different cell states. Uniform stimulation can increase motility without providing a direction, and spontaneous fluctuations of the [actin](../../../../actin.md) system can initiate movement without a spatial chemical instruction. Persistent migration combines feedback within the cell with a cue that biases that feedback.

Mechanical and geometrical inputs also matter. [Durotaxis](../../../../durotaxis.md) describes a bias associated with substrate stiffness, while [haptotaxis](../../../../haptotaxis.md) follows spatial variation in adhesive ligands. [Contact guidance](../../../../contact-guidance.md) by fibre alignment can channel motion even when soluble signals are uniform. Narrow spaces constrain the available paths and can force a switch of protrusive mechanism. These effects are not wholly separate from chemistry: cells sense mechanical forces through molecular interactions. Nevertheless, their directional information resides in mechanics or geometry, not necessarily in an imposed soluble [concentration gradient](../../../../concentration-gradient.md).

Contacts between cells supply another source of organization. Experiments on [neural crest](../../../../neural-crest.md) cells showed that collisions can cause protrusion collapse and repolarization, and that perturbing noncanonical [Wnt signalling](../../../../wnt-signalling.md) disrupts this behaviour and group directionality.[Collision assays in vivo and in vitro; perturbation of noncanonical Wnt signalling and local RhoA activation](../../../../contact-inhibition-of-locomotion-in-vivo-controls-neural-crest-directional-migration-by-carlos-carmona-fontaine-and-others.md) [Contact inhibition of locomotion](../../../../contact-inhibition-of-locomotion.md) therefore helps generate collective trajectories through local interactions, rather than each cell independently reading a complete external chemical map. Cohesion and other attractive interactions can prevent such repulsion from simply dispersing the group.

A discriminating experiment independently varies soluble [gradient](../../../../gradient.md), adhesive pattern and mechanical confinement. Measure direction, speed and persistence separately; reverse the [gradient](../../../../gradient.md), remove it, and then block the relevant receptor while checking viability and the ability to move. If a directional bias follows the reversed chemical field, that supports chemical guidance. If speed rises under uniform stimulation, that supports [chemokinesis](../../../../chemokinesis.md). If trajectories remain aligned with fibres after chemical reversal, physical guidance is influential. Manipulating intracellular polarity can test whether a cue is instructive or merely permissive.

The useful conclusion is that motion emerges from an internally powered, state-dependent cell interacting with chemical, mechanical and cellular surroundings. External chemical signals are often decisive inputs, but the trajectory is a property of the coupled system.
