<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

A good in-vitro model should distinguish [cancer invasion](../../../../cancer-invasion.md), movement across a tissue boundary into surrounding material, from simple growth or movement across a flat surface. No single assay reproduces the entire sequence from a primary tumour to a distant metastasis. Choose the assay to answer a specified mechanistic question, then confirm the result in a model that retains additional biological constraints.

A scratch assay measures closure of a gap in a monolayer. It is inexpensive and permits [live-cell imaging](../../../../live-cell-imaging.md), but closure combines [cell migration](../../../../cell-migration.md), spreading and [cell proliferation](../../../../cell-proliferation.md), and the wound releases signals and damages cells. It does not by itself demonstrate invasion through a [basement membrane](../../../../basement-membrane.md). A porous-membrane assay can measure transit toward a stimulus; adding an extracellular barrier tests an additional requirement. Yet pore size, coating thickness, chemical gradients and the counting time can dominate the result, and endpoint cell counts hide whether treatment altered survival or actual migration.

Three-dimensional [tumour spheroids](../../../../tumour-spheroid.md) or [organoids](../../../../organoid.md) embedded in a defined [extracellular matrix](../../../../extracellular-matrix.md) allow individual and collective invasion to be followed over time. Quantify invasion distance, cell escape, strand architecture and leader-follower behaviour, alongside changes in spheroid size and viability. Use collagen-rich and basement-membrane-like environments to test whether a phenotype depends on matrix identity. Independently vary ligand presentation, [matrix stiffness](../../../../matrix-stiffness.md) and pore dimensions where possible: increasing collagen concentration changes several of them simultaneously.

A primary study of breast tumour organoids identified invasive leader cells expressing a basal epithelial programme, including K14 and p63; reducing either component impaired collective invasion in that system. The experiment connected a defined cellular subpopulation to an invasion phenotype rather than assuming that every tumour cell was equivalent.[Three-dimensional organoid invasion assays; K14/p63 leader-cell observations and knockdown tests](../../../../collective-invasion-in-breast-cancer-requires-a-conserved-basal-epithelial-program-by-kevin-j-cheung-and-others.md) Its conclusion remains specific to the tested settings and does not make these markers universal requirements for invasion.

Add stromal cells, immune cells or a vessel-like boundary if the question concerns their interactions. [Microfluidics](../../../../microfluidics.md) can impose controlled gradients and permit [live-cell imaging](../../../../live-cell-imaging.md) of entry into or exit from a channel. Include non-invasive and invasive controls, perturbation controls, rescue where feasible, and matched measurements of [cell proliferation](../../../../cell-proliferation.md) and death. Follow individual cells rather than relying solely on a final stained area. Patient-derived material should include multiple independent tumours: many fields from one culture are not independent patient replicates.

Limitations remain substantial. Culture selects cells that tolerate the medium; long-passaged lines may lose the original tumour's diversity. Matrices and added growth factors can have variable composition. Isolated cultures omit native immune surveillance, vascular perfusion, whole-organ architecture and systemic influences. Even elaborate organoids incompletely reproduce these features. Finally, invasion is only one stage: it does not establish survival in blood, colonization of a distant organ, or clinical response. Agreement across complementary assays strengthens a mechanism; no dish-based result alone proves a metastatic outcome in a person.
