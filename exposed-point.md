# Exposed point

↑ **Parent:** [Extreme point](extreme-point.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposed_point)

An [exposed point](exposed-point.md) is a [point](point-geometry.md) uniquely selected by maximizing a [linear functional](linear-functional.md). In $\mathbb R^d$, a [point](point-geometry.md) $p$ of a [convex set](convex-set-split.md) $C$ is exposed if some [vector](vector.md) $c$ satisfies

$$
c^{\mathsf T}p>c^{\mathsf T}z\qquad(z\in C,\ z\ne p).
$$

Geometrically, a [supporting hyperplane](supporting-hyperplane.md) touches the [convex set](convex-set-split.md) only at $p$. Every [exposed point](exposed-point.md) is an [extreme point](extreme-point.md): a [convex combination](convex-combination.md) that attains the maximum can put positive weight only on maximizers, and there is only one.[§6, Definition 6.2 and Proposition 6.7, printed pp. 36–37: exposed points and their inclusion among extremal points](convex-geometry-bme-course-bmete94am22.md)

The [unit disk](unit-disk.md) calculation in [extreme point](extreme-point.md) exposes every [point](point-geometry.md) of the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md). For a corner $(a,b)$ of the [square](square.md), $c=(a,b)$ exposes it: $ax+by$ attains two only at $(a,b)$. However, maximizing $x$ exposes no single [point](point-geometry.md) on the right edge, since all of them have the same objective value. A [linear functional](linear-functional.md) may have several optimal [points](point-geometry.md), even when some of them are extreme.
