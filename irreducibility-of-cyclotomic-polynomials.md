# Irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials

↑ **Parent:** [Cyclotomic polynomial](cyclotomic-polynomial.md)

Every [cyclotomic polynomial](cyclotomic-polynomial.md) $\Phi_n$ is an [irreducible polynomial](irreducible-polynomial.md) over $\mathbb Q$. If a monic irreducible factor $f\in\mathbb Z[X]$ contains a primitive root $\zeta$, then it also contains $\zeta^p$ for every [prime number](prime-number.md) $p\nmid n$: otherwise, writing $\Phi_n=fg$, reduction modulo $p$ and the [Frobenius endomorphism](frobenius-endomorphism.md) give $\bar f\mid \bar g(X)^p$. Some irreducible factor would then divide both $\bar f$ and $\bar g$, giving a repeated factor of $X^n-1$, contrary to [separability of roots of unity](separability-of-roots-of-unity.md). Iterating over the prime factors of every integer coprime to $n$ shows that $f$ contains all primitive $n$th roots, so $f=\Phi_n$.
