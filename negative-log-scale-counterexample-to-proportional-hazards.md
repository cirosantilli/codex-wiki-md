# Negative log-scale counterexample to proportional hazards

↑ **Parent:** [Accelerated life family](accelerated-life-family.md)

For $\log T_z=bz-X$, with $e^X$ unit [exponential distribution](exponential-distribution.md) and $b=\log2$, the [survivor functions](survival-function.md) are $S_z(t)=1-e^{-2^z/t}$ for $t>0$. These are time-scaled [Fréchet distributions](frechet-distribution.md), so they form an [accelerated life family](accelerated-life-family.md). However their [hazard ratio](hazard-ratio.md) is $2/(e^{1/t}+1)$, tending to zero as $t\downarrow0$ and to one as $t\to\infty$. It is not constant. Thus a positive-scale assumption matters when deriving a [proportional hazards family](proportional-hazards-family.md) from the usual log-location-scale model.
