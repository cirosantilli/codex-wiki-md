# Lebesgue measurable sections

↑ **Parent:** [Fubini's theorem](fubini-s-theorem.md)

For a planar [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) $B$, its vertical section $B_x$ is a one-dimensional [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) for $x$ [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). Write $B\mathbin\triangle C\subseteq Z$ with $C$ a planar [Borel set](borel-set.md) and $Z$ a Borel [Lebesgue null set](lebesgue-null-set.md). Every section $C_x$ and $Z_x$ is Borel; [Tonelli's theorem](tonelli-theorem.md) gives $\int m(Z_x)\,dx=m_2(Z)=0$. Thus $m(Z_x)=0$ for $x$ [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md), and $B_x\mathbin\triangle C_x\subseteq Z_x$ proves the assertion by [completeness of Lebesgue measure](completeness-of-lebesgue-measure.md). No finite-measure condition on $B$ is needed.

Exceptional sections need not be measurable: $\{0\}\times A$ is a planar [Lebesgue null set](lebesgue-null-set.md) for every $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, even when $A$ is a [non-measurable set](non-measurable-set.md). Conversely, if both $A_1$ and $A_2$ are non-measurable, $A_1\times A_2$ cannot be measurable. Otherwise every $x\in A_1$ would have to lie in the null exceptional set, making $A_1$ measurable by [completeness of Lebesgue measure](completeness-of-lebesgue-measure.md).
