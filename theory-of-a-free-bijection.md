# Theory of a free bijection

↑ **Parent:** [Complete theory](complete-theory.md)

The [first-order theory](first-order-theory.md) of a [bijection](bijection.md) $f$ with named inverse $g$ and no finite cycles has [first-order models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) consisting of disjoint [integer](integer-split.md) orbits. Atomic [logical formulas](logical-formula.md) reduce to $x_j=f^k(x_i)$ for [integer](integer-split.md) $k$. An existential [logical variable](logical-variable.md) is either fixed by such a [logical equality](logical-equality.md) or must avoid finitely many points. This proves [quantifier elimination](quantifier-elimination.md) and completeness. There is one [complete one-type](complete-one-type.md); the [complete two-types](complete-two-type.md) are the [integer](integer-split.md) offsets and the nonprincipal different-orbit [partial type](partial-type.md). There are countably many finite-arity [partial types](partial-type.md) but [countable](countable-set.md) [first-order models](model-of-a-first-order-theory.md) with different numbers of orbits are not [isomorphic](isomorphism.md).
