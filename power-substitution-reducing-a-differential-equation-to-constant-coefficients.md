# Power substitution reducing a differential equation to constant coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Linear ordinary differential equation](linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md)

On an [interval](interval-mathematics.md) where the substitution is real and $x\ne0$, a factor formed by [exponentiation](exponentiation.md) can remove inverse powers from the coefficients of a [linear ordinary differential equation](linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md). For

$$
y''+\left(a+\frac b x\right)y'+\left(c+\frac d x+\frac e{x^2}\right)y=0,
$$

the substitution $y=x^nz$ gives

$$
z''+\left(a+\frac{2n+b}{x}\right)z'
+\left(c+\frac{an+d}{x}+\frac{n(n-1)+bn+e}{x^2}\right)z=0.
$$

It becomes a [constant-coefficient differential equation](constant-coefficient-differential-equation.md) exactly when $2n+b=0$, $an+d=0$ and $n(n-1)+bn+e=0$. Derive this by the [product rule](product-rule.md); checking only the coefficient of $z'$ is insufficient.
