# Reflection of a P-wave from a rigid plane

↑ **Parent:** [P wave](p-wave.md)

For a stable isotropic solid in $y<0$, use $u_x=\phi_x+\psi_y$, $u_y=\phi_y-\psi_x$. An incident [P wave](p-wave.md) with [frequency](frequency.md) $\omega$, tangential [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $\kappa$ and upward normal [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $a$ reflects into a P potential $Ae^{i(\kappa x-ay-\omega t)}$ and an SV potential $Be^{i(\kappa x-by-\omega t)}$, where $b^2=\omega^2/c_s^2-\kappa^2$. Vanishing of both displacements at the rigid plane gives $\kappa(1+A)-bB=0$, $a(1-A)-\kappa B=0$. Hence $A=(ab-\kappa^2)/(ab+\kappa^2)$ and $B=2a\kappa/(ab+\kappa^2)$. Because $c_p>c_s$ and $\kappa^2\leq\omega^2/c_p^2$, $b^2>0$ and the converted [S wave](s-wave.md) propagates rather than being evanescent.
