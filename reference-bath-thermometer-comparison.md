# Reference-bath thermometer comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Zeroth-law thermometer comparison](zeroth-law-thermometer-comparison.md)

A motivating real procedure is NIST's ambient [thermometer](thermometer.md) verification. Its apparatus is a deep beaker of [water](water.md), a calibrated reference [thermometer](thermometer.md), a test [thermometer](thermometer.md), and a magnetic stirrer with any heater switched off. Let the [water](water.md) approach room [temperature](temperature.md), stir gently, immerse both sensing tips well below the surface, and wait before reading. NIST specifies about $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ of [water](water.md), $10$–$15\,\mathrm{cm}$ immersion where possible, and an initial five-minute wait. Record test then reference, repeat in reverse order, and apply the reference's certificate correction. Subtract corrected reference from test: the difference estimates the test instrument's error at this bath [temperature](temperature.md). Agreement between the two reading orders checks repeatability. [“Comparison with another calibrated thermometer at ambient temperature”, apparatus and steps 1–10; “Measurement at the steam point”, pressure and elevation correction](mercury-thermometer-alternatives-verification-methods-for-alternative-thermometers-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)

For example, suppose a test reads $24.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, a reference reads $24.4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, and its certificate says to add $0.1\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The corrected reference is $24.5\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, so the test error is $+0.2\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$: it reads high. These are illustrative readings. This verification tests a previously calibrated instrument at one point; it does not establish the two-point response used in [two-point thermometer calibration example](two-point-thermometer-calibration-example.md).

Additional controls follow from the [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md) premise. Keep tips away from walls and bottom, interchange probe positions to test a [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md), and check that longer waits and modest immersion changes leave agreement unchanged within the required tolerance. A flat trace alone can be a [steady state](steady-state.md) maintained by competing [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) paths. Five minutes is a procedural starting point, not a universal equilibration time. Excessive stirring can supply [work](work.md) and warm the bath, while probe stems can exchange [heat](heat.md) with the room. These controls test whether both probes sample the same state; neither one-point agreement nor this experiment proves the [zeroth law of thermodynamics](zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md) for every material and condition.
