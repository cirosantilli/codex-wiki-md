# Optimal normal-fault dip relation

↑ **Parent:** [Andersonian faulting regime](andersonian-faulting-regime.md)

For a cohesionless, optimally oriented [normal fault](normal-fault.md) with vertical maximum compression and horizontal minimum compression,

$$
\delta=45^\circ+\frac\varphi2,\qquad\mu_f=\tan\varphi=\tan(2\delta-90^\circ),
$$

where $\delta$ is [fault dip](fault-dip.md) and $\varphi$ the [internal friction angle](internal-friction-angle.md). This is a geometric inference under the model, not a measurement of friction from dip alone.

For a compression-positive [Mohr circle](mohr-s-circle.md), put $C=(\sigma_1'+\sigma_3')/2$ and $R=(\sigma_1'-\sigma_3')/2$. Tangency to the cohesionless [Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion](mohr-coulomb-failure-criterion.md) $\tau=\mu_f\sigma_n'$ gives $R/C=\sin\varphi$. The contact point is $(\sigma_n',\tau)=(C\cos^2\varphi,C\sin\varphi\cos\varphi)$. The physical plane-normal angle to $\sigma_1'$ is half the corresponding central angle, giving the stated dip. Equivalently the plane itself makes $45^\circ-\varphi/2$ with $\sigma_1'$.

The associated stress ratio is $\sigma_1'/\sigma_3'=(1+\sin\varphi)/(1-\sin\varphi)$. A reactivated nonoptimal plane, nonzero [cohesion](cohesion.md) or unknown [principal stress](principal-stress.md) directions defeats the simple dip-only inversion.
