# Rate law

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction rate](reaction-rate.md)

A [rate law](rate-law.md) specifies how a [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) depends on [concentrations](concentration.md) under stated conditions. A common form is

$$
v=k[\mathrm A]^\alpha[\mathrm B]^\beta,
$$

where $k$ is a [rate constant](rate-constant.md) and the exponents are partial [orders of reaction](order-of-reaction.md). Determine them experimentally, or derive them from a justified [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). Do not infer $\alpha=a$ and $\beta=b$ from the net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md): its coefficients describe consumption ratios, whereas the exponents describe sensitivity to [concentration](concentration.md). See [IUPAC Gold Book: rate law](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/R05141) and [Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.3: rate laws](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-3-rate-laws).

A [rate law](rate-law.md) becomes a prediction of time evolution when combined with [stoichiometry](stoichiometry.md) in a [rate equation](rate-equation.md), for example $d[\mathrm A]/dt=-av$. Agreement with a measured [rate law](rate-law.md) is useful evidence for a [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md), but several routes can give the same dependence.
