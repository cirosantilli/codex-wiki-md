# Electrostatic corner-field exponent

↑ **Parent:** [Conducting-wedge potential expansion](conducting-wedge-potential-expansion.md)

If the lowest allowed angular harmonic has a nonzero coefficient, differentiating its potential gives the displayed field scale. The field vanishes at a right-angle recess, stays finite at a smooth face, and diverges at a protruding conducting corner whose exterior vacuum [angle](angle.md) exceeds $\pi$. A rounded tip cuts off the ideal power law at its [radius](radius.md) scale. If the first coefficient vanishes, the first nonzero higher harmonic determines the exponent instead.
