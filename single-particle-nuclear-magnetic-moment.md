# Single-particle nuclear magnetic moment

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear shell model](nuclear-shell-model.md)

In the simplest [nuclear shell model](nuclear-shell-model.md) of an odd-[mass number](mass-number.md) [nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), paired [nucleons](nucleon.md) contribute zero total [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) and [magnetic dipole moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md). The last unpaired [nucleon](nucleon.md) supplies the nuclear [spin](spin.md) $j$ and the stretched-state moment $\mu=g_jj\mu_N$, where $g_j$ is the [Landé g-factor](lande-g-factor.md) and $\mu_N$ the [nuclear magneton](nuclear-magneton.md).

Using [addition of angular momentum](addition-of-angular-momentum.md) with $s=1/2$ gives

$$
g_j=g_\ell+(g_s-g_\ell)\frac{j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)+3/4}{2j(j+1)}.
$$

For $j=\ell+1/2$, this reduces to $\mu/\mu_N=g_\ell\ell+g_s/2$. For $j=\ell-1/2$, it gives

$$
\frac\mu{\mu_N}=\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell+1}\left[g_\ell(\ell+1)-\frac{g_s}{2}\right].
$$

The free-nucleon orbital factors are $g_\ell=1$ for a [proton](proton.md) and $0$ for a [neutron](neutron-split.md). Residual nuclear interactions and configuration mixing can shift measured moments away from this ideal prediction. A single hole in an otherwise closed $j$ shell has the same stretched-state moment as a particle in that shell: removing the $m_j=-j$ particle leaves total $J_z=j$ and moment $g_jj\mu_N$.
