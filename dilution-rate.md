# Dilution rate

↑ **Parent:** [Chemostat](chemostat.md)

The [dilution rate](dilution-rate.md) is the fraction of a constant-volume [chemostat](chemostat.md) replaced per unit time: volumetric flow $Q$ divided by culture volume $V$. For $Q=1\,\mathrm{L}\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ and $V=2\,\mathrm{L}$, $D=0.5\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$. A well-mixed constituent with no source and absent from the feed has [concentration](concentration.md) $a(t)=a(0)e^{-Dt}$, since $\dot a=-Da$. Thus $1/D=2\,\mathrm{h}$ is the time for its [concentration](concentration.md) to fall by a factor $e$, not the time by which all original material has left.

At a positive [chemostat steady state](chemostat-steady-state.md), the [specific growth rate](specific-growth-rate.md) equals the [dilution rate](dilution-rate.md); the [biomass concentration](biomass-concentration.md) stays constant because production balances outflow. For $D=0.5\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, growth alone would have doubling time $\ln2/D\approx1.39\,\mathrm{h}$, even though the vessel's [biomass concentration](biomass-concentration.md) does not double. Raising the [dilution rate](dilution-rate.md) beyond $\mu(s_{\mathrm{in}})$ causes [chemostat washout](chemostat-washout.md); comparing it only with $\mu_{\max}$ misses the nutrient limitation imposed by the feed.
