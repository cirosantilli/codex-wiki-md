# Oxidation state sum rule

↑ **Parent:** [Oxidation state](oxidation-state.md)

Sum [oxidation states](oxidation-state.md) over all [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) of the specified [molecule](molecule.md), [ion](ion.md) or [formula unit](formula-unit.md), including multiplicities:

$$
\sum_i\mathrm{OS}_i=z,\qquad q=ze.
$$

Here $q$ is the net [electric charge](electric-charge.md) and $e$ the positive [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). For a neutral entity the sum is zero; for an [ion](ion.md) it equals its signed [electric charge](electric-charge.md) number $z$. The reason is simple: the allocation assigns every [valence electron](valence-electron.md) exactly once, so the sum is the neutral [Electron](electron.md) budget minus the actual [Electron](electron.md) count.

For [water](water.md), $2(+1)+(-2)=0$; for [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md), $2(+1)+2(-1)=0$. In [permanganate](permanganate.md), $\mathrm{MnO}_4^-$, assigning each [oxygen](oxygen.md) $-2$ gives $x+4(-2)=-1$, so [manganese](manganese.md) has [oxidation state](oxidation-state.md) $+7$. This is not a free $\mathrm{Mn}^{7+}$ [ion](ion.md): the entire [permanganate](permanganate.md) [anion](anion.md) has net [electric charge](electric-charge.md) $-e$. The sum rule supplies a constraint; without additional assignments it cannot determine every individual [oxidation state](oxidation-state.md).
