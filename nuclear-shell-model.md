# Nuclear shell model

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model)

Why should adding one [neutron](neutron-split.md) be easy for one [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) and much less favorable for its neighbor? In the [nuclear shell model](nuclear-shell-model.md), [nucleons](nucleon.md) occupy discrete [energy levels](energy-level.md) in a [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md). Filling all available states below a sufficiently large gap produces a [nuclear shell closure](nuclear-shell-closure.md). This connects [nuclear separation energies](nuclear-separation-energy.md) with the counting of [quantum states](quantum-state.md), and connects the [spin](spin.md) of an odd [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) with its last unpaired [nucleon](nucleon.md).

The starting point is an approximate [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md)

$$
H=\sum_{i=1}^{A}\left[\frac{\mathbf p_i^2}{2m_i}+U_i(r_i)-a_i(r_i)\frac{\mathbf L_i\cdot\mathbf S_i}{\hbar^2}\right]+V_{\mathrm{res}},
$$

where $U_i$ is a confining [potential energy](potential-energy.md), $a_i$ specifies [nuclear spin-orbit splitting](nuclear-spin-orbit-splitting.md), and $V_{\mathrm{res}}$ contains interactions left out of the [nuclear mean field](nuclear-mean-field.md). This is an effective organization of the nuclear [many-body problem](many-body-problem.md), rather than a derivation of the [nuclear force](nuclear-force-split.md). In a spherical approximation, [addition of angular momentum](addition-of-angular-momentum.md) combines the [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md) $l$ with [spin](spin.md) $s=1/2$ to give $j=l\pm1/2$; for $l=0$, only $j=1/2$ exists.

A level labelled $nlj$ has $2j+1$ magnetic substates $m_j=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$. The [Pauli exclusion principle](pauli-exclusion-principle.md) permits one [neutron](neutron-split.md) in each such state and, separately, one [proton](proton.md). Thus a $j=7/2$ level holds eight [neutrons](neutron-split.md), or eight [protons](proton.md); it does not hold sixteen identical [neutrons](neutron-split.md). Here $n=1,2,\ldots$ counts radial levels of the same $l$, starting with the lowest; it is not the hydrogenic principal [quantum number](quantum-number.md). The letters $s,p,d,f,g,h,i$ mean $l=0,1,2,3,4,5,6$.
