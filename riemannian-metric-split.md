# Riemannian metric

↑ **Parent:** [Differential geometry](differential-geometry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric)

A Riemannian metric is a smoothly varying [positive-definite](positive-definite-bilinear-form.md) [inner product](inner-product-split.md) $g_p$ on each [tangent space](tangent-space.md) $T_pM$ of a [smooth manifold](smooth-manifold-split.md). It measures [tangent vectors](tangent-vector-split.md) before measuring entire journeys:

$$
\|v\|_g=\sqrt{g_p(v,v)},\qquad
\cos\theta=\frac{g_p(u,v)}{\|u\|_g\|v\|_g}\quad(u,v\ne0).
$$

Integrating this speed along a [curve](curve.md) gives [arc length](arc-length.md); taking the [infimum](infimum.md) over routes gives [Riemannian distance](riemannian-distance.md). A [smooth manifold](smooth-manifold-split.md) together with this measuring rule is a [Riemannian manifold](riemannian-manifold.md).

In a [coordinate chart](manifold-chart.md) $(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$, the rule is represented by a smooth [positive-definite symmetric matrix](symmetric-positive-definite-matrix.md) $G=(g_{ij})$:

$$
ds^2=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}(x)\,dx^i\,dx^j,\qquad
g(v,v)=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}(x)v^iv^j.
$$

The notation $ds^2$ describes the squared infinitesimal line element, rather than an ordinary distance between distinct points. Changing coordinates changes the [matrix](matrix.md) entries; it preserves the measured [norm](norm.md) of the same [tangent vector](tangent-vector-split.md).

For the plane with $ds^2=4dx^2+dy^2$, the [vector](vector.md) $(1,0)$ has [norm](norm.md) $2$ and $(0,1)$ has [norm](norm.md) $1$. The coordinate change $X=2x,Y=y$ turns this into $ds^2=dX^2+dY^2$: coefficients that look different can describe the same geometry. On a [circular cylinder](circular-cylinder.md) the [induced metric](induced-metric.md) $R^2d\theta^2+dz^2$ similarly becomes Euclidean when unrolled. On a round [sphere](sphere.md), polar coordinates give $R^2d\phi^2+R^2\sin^2\phi\,d\theta^2$, away from the poles; the apparent degeneracy at a pole belongs to this coordinate choice, not to the [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric-split.md).

The point of the construction is that measurements make sense intrinsically, even without a chosen surrounding [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md). It gives a common starting point for [geodesics](geodesic.md), [curvature](curvature.md) encoded by the [Riemann curvature tensor](riemann-curvature-tensor-split.md), and the [Laplace-Beltrami operator](laplace-beltrami-operator.md). [Peter Petersen, Riemannian Geometry, §1.1: smoothly varying tangent-space inner products](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~petersen/RG.pdf).
