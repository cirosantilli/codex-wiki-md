<h1 id="boltzmann-s-entropy-formula">Boltzmann's entropy formula</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Entropy](entropy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann's_entropy_formula)

Why does a [gas](gas.md) spread through an available container? One part of the answer is counting: a nearly uniform [macrostate](macrostate.md) can be realized by vastly more [microstates](microstate.md) than a [macrostate](macrostate.md) with every particle crowded into one corner. [Boltzmann's entropy formula](boltzmann-s-entropy-formula.md) turns that multiplicity into [entropy](entropy.md). The useful insight is that a large [entropy](entropy.md) measures how many microscopic realizations fit a specified macroscopic description, rather than how untidy a picture looks.

For a specified [macrostate](macrostate.md) $M$ containing $\Omega(M)$ accessible, equally likely [microstates](microstate.md), write

$$
S(M)=k_B\ln\Omega(M),\qquad p_i=\frac1{\Omega(M)}\quad(i\text{ compatible with }M).
$$

Here $k_B$ is the [Boltzmann constant](boltzmann-constant.md), and $\ln$ is the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md), also written $\log$ in this article's title. The count $\Omega(M)$ is dimensionless; [entropy](entropy.md) has units of [energy](energy.md) divided by [temperature](temperature.md). Always specify the [macrostate](macrostate.md), the constraints and the model's microscopic resolution before counting. A [microcanonical ensemble](microcanonical-ensemble.md) provides a familiar equilibrium setting for the equal-[probability](probability.md) assumption. [MIT, §7.4: equiprobable microstates and S = k ln Ω](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node57.html)

The [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) compresses an enormous count into a manageable number and makes products of counts into sums of [entropies](entropy.md); see [additivity of Boltzmann entropy](additivity-of-boltzmann-entropy.md). With this counting convention, one allowed [microstate](microstate.md) has zero [Boltzmann entropy](boltzmann-s-entropy-formula.md). This zero refers to the chosen model and resolution; it does not by itself assign an absolute [entropy](entropy.md) to a real material.
