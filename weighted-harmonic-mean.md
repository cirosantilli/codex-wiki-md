# Weighted harmonic mean

↑ **Parent:** [Harmonic mean](harmonic-mean.md)

For positive [real numbers](real-number.md) $x_i$ and nonnegative weights with $\sum_iw_i=1$, the weighted harmonic mean is

$$
H_w=\left(\sum_i\frac{w_i}{x_i}\right)^{-1}.
$$

Equal weights give the [harmonic mean](harmonic-mean.md), while a zero weight excludes that input. For travel at positive constant [speeds](speed.md) $v_i$ over positive leg [lengths](length.md) $d_i$, the weights are fractions of total distance:

$$
w_i=\frac{d_i}{\sum_jd_j},\qquad v_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{\sum_id_i}{\sum_id_i/v_i}=\left(\sum_i\frac{w_i}{v_i}\right)^{-1}.
$$

For $30\,\mathrm{km}$ at $30\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and $90\,\mathrm{km}$ at $60\,\mathrm{km/h}$, the weights are $1/4,3/4$, elapsed [time](time-in-physics.md) is $5/2$ hours, and **the average [speed](speed.md) is $48\,\mathrm{km/h}$**. Weighting the [speeds](speed.md) by their time fractions instead gives the same result as a [weighted arithmetic mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md): $(2/5)30+(3/5)60=48$. The two weight systems describe the same journey in different coordinates and must not be interchanged. [University of Cambridge, Underground Mathematics: time weights and distance weights](https://undergroundmathematics.org/introducing-calculus/average-speed/averages).
