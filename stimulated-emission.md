# Stimulated emission

↑ **Parent:** [Laser](laser.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulated_emission)

Stimulated emission is emission induced by incident [light](light.md), in contrast with the unprompted decay in [spontaneous emission](spontaneous-emission.md). It supplies the amplification mechanism of a [laser](laser.md). [OpenStax, §8.6: stimulated emission](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/8-6-lasers).

For an emitter with upper and lower [energy](energy.md) values $E_2>E_1$, the resonant [photon](photon.md) has [frequency](frequency.md) $\nu$ satisfying

$$
\Delta E=E_2-E_1=h\nu,
$$

where $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md). In the elementary [photon](photon.md) picture, an incident [photon](photon.md) stimulates an emitter in the upper [quantum state](quantum-state.md) to emit an additional [photon](photon.md) into the same optical [normal mode](normal-mode.md), with the same [frequency](frequency.md), propagation direction and [photon polarization](photon-polarization.md). The emitter supplies the extra [energy](energy.md); the stimulating [photon](photon.md) is not consumed. This builds the existing field and its [optical coherence](optical-coherence.md). An emitter in the lower [quantum state](quantum-state.md) can instead undergo [photon absorption](photon-absorption.md), so [stimulated emission](stimulated-emission.md) alone does not establish net [optical gain](optical-gain.md).

For example, a $2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ gap corresponds to $\nu\approx4.8360\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and a [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) of about $620\,\mathrm{nm}$. One ideal [stimulated emission](stimulated-emission.md) event converts one incoming [photon](photon.md) into two outgoing [photons](photon.md) in the selected optical [normal mode](normal-mode.md), adding $2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ of [light](light.md) [energy](energy.md) while lowering the emitter's [energy](energy.md) by that amount.
