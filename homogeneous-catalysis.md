# Homogeneous catalysis

↑ **Parent:** [Catalysis](catalysis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_catalysis)

In [homogeneous catalysis](homogeneous-catalysis.md), the [catalyst](catalyst.md) and [reactants](reactant.md) occupy the same [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md).

For a liquid example, specify [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) as the [reactant](reactant.md), dissolved [iodide](iodide.md) as the [catalyst](catalyst.md), and [water](water.md) as the [solvent](solvent.md). If both reacting [chemical species](chemical-species.md) remain dissolved in that one liquid [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md), this is [homogeneous catalysis](homogeneous-catalysis.md). The University of Wisconsin–Madison's [The Rate of a Reaction](https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/sstutorial/Text13/Tx133/tx133.html) identifies [iodide](iodide.md)-catalysed [hydrogen peroxide](hydrogen-peroxide.md) decomposition in its final example; the aqueous setting here makes the phase roles explicit.[The Rate of a Reaction, final Example: iodide-catalysed decomposition of hydrogen peroxide](the-rate-of-a-reaction-by-university-of-wisconsin-madison-department-of-chemistry.md) Its net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) is

$$
2\mathrm{H_2O_2}(aq)\longrightarrow2\mathrm{H_2O}(l)+\mathrm{O_2}(g).
$$

The [molecular oxygen](molecular-oxygen.md) is a [reaction product](product-chemistry.md): its bubbles do not make the dissolved catalytic route [heterogeneous catalysis](heterogeneous-catalysis.md). The classification compares the participating [catalyst](catalyst.md) and [reactants](reactant.md), rather than demanding that everything in the vessel share a phase.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §12.7, Homogeneous Catalysts: catalyst in the same phase as the reactants](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/12-7-catalysis.md)

Being in the same [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md) describes where the [chemical species](chemical-species.md) are; it does not specify their [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). A uniform-looking liquid can contain unresolved [solid](solid.md) particles, so appearance alone is insufficient. Conversely, suspended [solid](solid.md) particles do not establish that their surfaces supply the observed [catalysis](catalysis.md): dissolved material released from them could contribute. Phase identification and catalytic participation need separate evidence.

The bookkeeping of a [catalyst](catalyst.md) in [homogeneous catalysis](homogeneous-catalysis.md) is particularly transparent in the schematic [catalytic cycle](catalytic-cycle.md) A plus C forming AC, followed by AC releasing B plus C. This is the generic model in [catalyst](catalyst.md), not an asserted [iodide](iodide.md) [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). Suppose a fixed [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) initially contains $1.00\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of A and $0.20\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of C. At a chosen snapshot, let $0.60\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of B and $0.12\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of AC be present, with no other [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md). Each AC contains one A unit and one C unit, so

$$
n_{\mathrm A}+n_{\mathrm{AC}}+n_{\mathrm B}=1.00\,\mathrm{mmol},\qquad
n_{\mathrm C}+n_{\mathrm{AC}}=0.20\,\mathrm{mmol}.
$$

Thus free A is $0.28\,\mathrm{mmol}$ and free C is $0.08\,\mathrm{mmol}$. B formation already exceeds the initial [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of C by a factor of three, while the total C inventory remains $0.20\,\mathrm{mmol}$. Adding the two steps cancels C and AC, giving the net A-to-B [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md); counting their constituents proves these balances. Conserved total [catalyst](catalyst.md) does not mean constant free [catalyst](catalyst.md) [concentration](concentration.md).

Dissolved contact is useful because [reactants](reactant.md) can encounter the catalytic [chemical species](chemical-species.md) throughout the mixed liquid, without first reaching a macroscopic [solid](solid.md) surface. Recovering a dissolved [catalyst](catalyst.md) from the same [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) can instead require a separation. Neither ease of contact nor phase classification determines an [activation energy](activation-energy.md) or an [equilibrium constant](equilibrium-constant.md); those depend on the relevant [chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics-split.md) and endpoint [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md).
