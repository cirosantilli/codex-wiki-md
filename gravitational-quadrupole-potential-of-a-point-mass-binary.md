# Gravitational quadrupole potential of a point-mass binary

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md)

For a [binary star](binary-star.md) viewed far from its [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md), let $\mathbf r$ be the separation and $\mu$ the [reduced mass](reduced-mass.md), $M=M_1+M_2$ the total [mass](mass.md), $r=|\mathbf r|$ and $x=|\mathbf x|$. The [Taylor expansion](taylor-expansion.md) of the point-mass [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) has zero dipole because $\sum_A M_A\mathbf x_A=0$. Its second moment is $\sum_A M_Ax_{Ai}x_{Aj}=\mu r_i r_j$, so

$$
\Phi=-\frac{GM}{x}-\frac{G\mu}{2x^5}\left[3(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf r)^2-x^2r^2\right]+O(GMr^3/x^4).
$$

This holds for an instantaneous separation in any orbit, not only a [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md). The [mass quadrupole moment](mass-quadrupole-moment.md) convention $Q_{ij}=\mu(r_ir_j-r^2\delta_{ij}/3)$ gives $\Phi_2=-3GQ_{ij}x_ix_j/(2x^5)$.
