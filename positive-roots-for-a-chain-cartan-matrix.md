# Positive roots for a chain Cartan matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Cartan matrix](cartan-matrix.md)

Suppose the [Cartan matrix](cartan-matrix.md) has diagonal two, entries minus one between adjacent indices, and all other entries zero. Normalize the simple-[root](root-of-a-root-system.md) squared lengths to two. Every [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) is a [Weyl group](weyl-group.md) translate of a simple [root](root-of-a-root-system.md), so all [roots](root-of-a-root-system.md) have squared length two. If a positive [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) has coefficients $c_1,\ldots,c_l$ in the simple-[root](root-of-a-root-system.md) [basis](basis.md), let $c_0=c_{l+1}=0$. Then

$$
2=\sum_{i=0}^l(c_{i+1}-c_i)^2.
$$

The nonnegative integer coefficients force exactly one upward jump of one and one downward jump of one. Thus the coefficients are one on a consecutive interval and zero elsewhere. Conversely, an interval [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) can be extended by the next simple [root](root-of-a-root-system.md): its Cartan pairing with that [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) is minus one, so the corresponding simple [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) adds it. Starting from a simple [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) constructs every interval and proves the displayed description. This also identifies the [root](root-of-a-root-system.md) system as type $A_l$ without needing to enumerate [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) words individually.
