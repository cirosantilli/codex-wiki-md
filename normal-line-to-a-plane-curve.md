# Normal line to a plane curve

↑ **Parent:** [Tangent line](tangent-line.md)

At a regular point of a differentiable [plane curve](plane-curve.md), the normal line passes through that point perpendicular to its [tangent line](tangent-line.md). If the tangent slope is a finite nonzero number $m$, the normal slope is $-1/m$, since the direction vectors $(1,m)$ and $(1,-1/m)$ have zero [dot product](dot-product.md). A horizontal tangent has a vertical normal, and a vertical tangent has a horizontal normal. For a regular [level set](level-set.md) $F(x,y)=c$, the nonzero [gradient](gradient.md) $\nabla F$ gives a normal direction because differentiating $F$ along the curve gives $\nabla F\cdot\mathbf r'=0$.
