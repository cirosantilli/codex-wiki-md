<h1 id="dynamical-proof-of-hindman-s-theorem">Dynamical proof of Hindman's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Hindman theorem](hindman-theorem.md)

Extend a [finite coloring](finite-coloring.md) of the [positive integers](positive-integer.md) to a point $x$ of a two-sided [full shift](full-shift.md). A [minimal subsystem](minimal-subsystem.md) of its forward [orbit closure](orbit-closure.md), together with the proximal-minimal existence theorem, supplies a [minimal point](minimal-point.md) $y$ [proximal](proximality.md) to $x$. Put $q=y(0)$. If all sums in $\{0\}\cup\operatorname{FS}(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$, the augmented [finite-sums set](finite-sums-set.md), have color $q$ in $y$, their coordinate constraints define a [cylinder set](cylinder-set.md) containing $y$. The [joint return lemma for a proximal minimal pair](joint-return-lemma-for-a-proximal-minimal-pair.md) chooses a new positive term so that all new sums have color $q$ in both $x$ and $y$. [Mathematical induction](mathematical-induction.md) gives an infinite [monochromatic](monochromatic-set.md) [finite-sums set](finite-sums-set.md). The new term can exceed the sum of all previous terms, giving unique representations. The proximal-minimal existence theorem is a substantive input to this proof.
