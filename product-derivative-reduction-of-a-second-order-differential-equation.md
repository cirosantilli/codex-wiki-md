# Product-derivative reduction of a second-order differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Linear ordinary differential equation](linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md)

Recognizing the displayed product derivative reduces a homogeneous second-order equation to $(py)''=0$. On any interval where $p\ne0$, its general solution is $y=(A+Bx)/p$. For $p=1-x^3$, the equation becomes $(1-x^3)y''-6x^2y'-6xy=0$. Data $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=0$ give $1/(1-x^3)=\sum_{j\geq0}x^{3j}$, with [radius of convergence](radius-of-convergence.md) one. The poles occur at the three cube roots of unity. A solution singularity can disappear for a special numerator that cancels the corresponding factor, so an equation's singular point need not be a singularity of every solution.
