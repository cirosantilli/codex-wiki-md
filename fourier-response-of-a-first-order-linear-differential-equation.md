# Fourier response of a first-order linear differential equation

↑ **Parent:** [Linear ordinary differential equation](linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md)

For $y'+ay=g$ with real $a>0$ and $2\pi$-periodic forcing, comparing [Fourier coefficients](fourier-coefficient.md) gives $(a+in)\widehat y_n=\widehat g_n$. There is no vanishing denominator, including the constant mode. With real cosine and sine coefficients $a_n,b_n$ for $g$ and $A_n,B_n$ for $y$, the equivalent equations give

$$
A_n=\frac{a a_n-nb_n}{a^2+n^2},\qquad B_n=\frac{na_n+a b_n}{a^2+n^2}.
$$

Whenever termwise [differentiation](differentiation.md) is justified, these formulas construct a periodic [particular solution](particular-solution.md). For a forcing series with coefficients $O(n^{-2})$, the differentiated response coefficients are also summable, which supplies such a justification by [uniform convergence](uniform-convergence.md). All other solutions add the [homogeneous solution](homogeneous-solution.md) $Ce^{-ax}$. Since this exponential cannot be periodic unless $C=0$, the periodic solution is unique. This is also a simple example of how a linear equation changes the amplitude and phase of each harmonic independently.
