# Principal ideal ring

↑ **Parent:** [Ring](ring.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_ideal_ring)

A principal ideal ring is a [ring](ring.md) whose left ideals and right ideals each have one generator. For a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md), this says that every [ideal](ideal.md) is a [principal ideal](principal-ideal.md). The examples here use commutative rings. Unlike a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md), it may have [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md): $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ has this property, with [ideals](ideal.md) $(0)$, $(2)$, and $(1)$. A finite [direct product of rings](direct-product-of-rings.md) that are principal ideal rings is again a principal ideal ring. Indeed, the coordinate [idempotent elements](idempotent.md) show that every [ideal](ideal.md) is a product of coordinate [ideals](ideal.md); a tuple of their generators generates the product.
