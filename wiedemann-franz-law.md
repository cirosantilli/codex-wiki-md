# Wiedemann-Franz law

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiedemann–Franz_law)

The [Wiedemann-Franz law](wiedemann-franz-law.md) relates the electronic [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) of a normal degenerate [metal](metal.md) to its [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md):

$$
K_e=L_0\sigma T,\qquad L_0=\frac{\pi^2k_B^2}{3e^2}.
$$

An isotropic [kinetic theory](kinetic-theory-split.md) estimate gives $K_e=c_ev_F^2\tau_Q/3$. With [electronic heat capacity](electronic-heat-capacity.md) density $c_e=\pi^2nk_B^2T/(2E_F)$, $v_F^2=2E_F/m^*$ and $\sigma=ne^2\tau_\sigma/m^*$, the ratio is $L_0\tau_Q/\tau_\sigma$. Approximately elastic scattering with a smooth energy dependence gives equal heat and charge relaxation times. Inelastic [electron-phonon scattering](electron-phonon-scattering.md) can make them different; the law also concerns the electronic contribution, not additional lattice heat conduction.[Abstract: acoustic-phonon violations at finite temperature and restoration when impurity scattering dominates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05646)
