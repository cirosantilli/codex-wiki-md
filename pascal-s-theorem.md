<h1 id="pascal-s-theorem">Pascal's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Projective plane curve](projective-plane-curve.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal's_theorem)

For six distinct points on an irreducible [conic](conic-section.md) in the complex [projective plane](projective-plane.md), the three intersections of opposite sides of the resulting hexagon are collinear. Form the two cubics given by the products of alternate side equations. They meet at the six [conic](conic-section.md) points and the three opposite-side intersections. Applying [residual intersection through a curve pencil](residual-intersection-through-a-curve-pencil.md) with degrees three and two places the last three points on a line. This is the [conic](conic-section.md) counterpart of [Pappus's hexagon theorem](pappus-s-hexagon-theorem.md) for two lines.
