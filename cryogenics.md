# Cryogenics

↑ **Parent:** [Temperature](temperature-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenics)

[Cryogenics](cryogenics.md) studies the production and use of very low [temperatures](temperature-split.md). Its experimental challenge is a balance of rates: a [refrigerator](refrigerator.md) must remove [energy](energy-split.md) from a cold region faster than unwanted [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) supplies it. A low [temperature](temperature-split.md) reading alone does not establish a useful cooling capacity. A maintained cold [liquid](liquid.md) bath is especially valuable because [latent heat](latent-heat.md) allows it to absorb [heat](heat-split.md) while its [phase fraction](phase-fraction.md) changes at nearly fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [pressure](pressure-split.md).

[Helium liquefaction](helium-liquefaction.md) illustrates why staged cooling and recovery of a returning stream's cooling capacity matter. A [counterflow heat exchanger](counterflow-heat-exchanger.md) transfers [heat](heat-split.md) from the incoming stream to the colder returning stream without mixing them. This reduces the incoming [specific enthalpy](specific-enthalpy.md) before expansion. The [heat exchanger](heat-exchanger.md) recovers cooling already produced; the surrounding [refrigerator](refrigerator.md) still requires input [work](work.md) and rejection of [heat](heat-split.md) to a warmer environment.
