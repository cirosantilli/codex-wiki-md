# Subcritical Hopf bifurcation

↑ **Parent:** [Hopf bifurcation](hopf-bifurcation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcritical_Hopf_bifurcation)

In a subcritical [Hopf bifurcation](hopf-bifurcation.md), the small [periodic orbit](periodic-orbit.md) repels within the critical [centre manifold](center-manifold.md) and lies on the side where the [equilibrium point](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) attracts within that manifold. With the critical [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) [real part](real-part.md) increasing through zero, the positive cubic sign gives the exact radial [Hopf normal form](hopf-normal-form.md)

$$
\dot r=\mu r+r^3,\qquad \dot\theta=\omega>0.
$$

For $\mu<0$, the nonzero radius is $r_*=\sqrt{-\mu}$. Radii below $r_*$ shrink to zero, whereas radii above it grow. The radial [derivative](derivative.md) at the cycle is $\mu+3r_*^2=-2\mu>0$, proving that the [periodic orbit](periodic-orbit.md) repels. For example, $\mu=-0.04$ gives an unstable circle of radius $0.2$: a radius of $0.1$ decays while one of $0.3$ grows. The unstable circle shrinks into zero as $\mu\uparrow0$; at $\mu=0$, $\dot r=r^3$ already drives every small positive radius outward.

For $\mu>0$ there is no nonzero constant radius in this cubic model, and zero repels. In a general [dynamical system](dynamical-system.md), this says only that the nearby [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) leave the local neighbourhood. It does not guarantee a distant attracting [limit cycle](limit-cycle.md), a jump to a particular [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), or [hysteresis](hysteresis.md). Additional nonlinear or global information is needed; the [quintic radial Hopf equation](quintic-radial-hopf-equation.md) illustrates one possible stabilizing extension. Reversing the parameter orientation reverses which numerical side contains the branch, so the defining distinction between [supercritical Hopf bifurcation](supercritical-hopf-bifurcation.md) and subcritical [Hopf bifurcation](hopf-bifurcation.md) is their reduced stability and their relation to the [equilibrium point](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), rather than the bare sign of an arbitrarily named parameter.
