# Uniformly continuous integrable dissipation tends to zero

↑ **Parent:** [Uniform continuity](uniform-continuity.md)

Suppose $d$ is nonnegative, integrable and [uniformly continuous](uniform-continuity.md) on the positive half-line. If $d(t_n)\geq\epsilon>0$ at times tending to infinity, [uniform continuity](uniform-continuity.md) supplies intervals of one fixed positive length around those times on which $d\geq\epsilon/2$. An infinite disjoint subsequence of these intervals contradicts integrability. Thus the displayed implication holds. Mere smoothness at every finite time is insufficient: smooth peaks of shrinking widths can have finite integral and heights bounded away from zero.
