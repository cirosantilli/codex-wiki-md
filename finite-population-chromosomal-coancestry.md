# Finite-population chromosomal coancestry

↑ **Parent:** [Kinship coefficient](kinship-coefficient.md)

For $N$ individuals in monogamous families of exactly two offspring, let $\Phi_G$ be [mean](expected-value.md) pairwise [kinship coefficient](kinship-coefficient.md), $F_G$ [mean](expected-value.md) inbreeding and $\Phi_{m,G}$ [mean](expected-value.md) kinship of mates. A random distinct offspring pair are siblings with [probability](probability.md) $1/(N-1)$. Accounting separately for within-family and cross-family pairs gives the displayed recurrence, while $F_{G+1}=\Phi_{m,G}$. Random mating makes $\Phi_{m,G}=\Phi_G$. Sibling avoidance changes the mating-pair [mean](expected-value.md); it does not create unrelated founders or stop eventual drift to ancestral identity in a closed finite population.
