# Genetics

↑ **Parent:** [Biology](biology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics)

[Genetics](genetics.md) studies how [genes](gene.md) are inherited, how their [alleles](allele.md) vary, and how that variation contributes to [phenotypes](phenotype.md). Its central distinction is between an inherited description, the [genotype](genotype.md), and what an [organism](organism.md) actually expresses, the [phenotype](phenotype.md). Following [alleles](allele.md) through a [pedigree](pedigree.md) answers a family-level question; following [allele frequencies](allele-frequency.md) through generations leads to [population genetics](population-genetics.md) and [evolution](evolution.md).

The beauty of [Mendelian segregation](mendelian-segregation.md) is that discrete inherited copies can generate predictable statistical patterns without making every offspring identical. A single [genetic locus](genetic-locus.md) already connects [meiosis](meiosis.md), [probability](probability.md) and observable variation. The [Hardy-Weinberg principle](hardy-weinberg-principle.md) then supplies a population baseline: it separates reshuffling inherited copies from processes such as [natural selection](natural-selection.md) that change their frequencies. These simple models are useful precisely because their assumptions can be stated and checked.
