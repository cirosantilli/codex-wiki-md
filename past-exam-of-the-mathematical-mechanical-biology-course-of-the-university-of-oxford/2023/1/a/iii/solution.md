<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [second moment of area](../../../../../second-moment-of-area.md) about an axis weights each element of [cross-sectional area](../../../../../cross-sectional-area.md) by its squared perpendicular distance from that axis. The [ellipse](../../../../../ellipse.md) extends farther along $\mathbf d_2$ than along $\mathbf d_1$, so its material lies, on balance, farther from the $\mathbf d_1$ axis. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I_1>I_2.}
$$

With zero [intrinsic curvature of an elastic filament](../../../../../intrinsic-curvature-of-an-elastic-filament.md), the respective [bending moments](../../../../../bending-moment.md) required by the [constitutive equation](../../../../../constitutive-equation.md) are $EI_1\kappa\mathbf d_1$ and $EI_2\kappa\mathbf d_2$. Thus their magnitudes satisfy

$$
\boxed{|m_{\mathbf d_1}|=EI_1|\kappa|>EI_2|\kappa|=|m_{\mathbf d_2}|}
$$

for $\kappa\ne0$: bending about the minor axis is harder. Here $E$ is [Young's modulus](../../../../../young-s-modulus.md), and $EI_i$ is the corresponding [bending stiffness](../../../../../bending-stiffness.md).
