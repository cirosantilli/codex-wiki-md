<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because the two [elastic rods](../../../../../elastic-rod.md) each have thickness $a$, their centrelines are separated by $a$ along the common director $\mathbf d_1$. The bonded geometry is

$$
\mathbf r^+(S^+)=\mathbf r(S)+a\mathbf d_1(S),\qquad S^+=S.
$$

Differentiate with respect to reference [arc length](../../../../../arc-length.md). The first rod is an [inextensible rod](../../../../../inextensible-rod.md), and the printed [director kinematics of a rod](../../../../../director-kinematics-of-a-rod.md) give

$$
\frac{d\mathbf r^+}{dS^+}
=\mathbf d_3+a(-u\mathbf d_3)=(1-au)\mathbf d_3.
$$

For the physically admissible orientation-preserving configuration $1-au>0$, the rods have the same unit tangent $\mathbf d_3^+=\mathbf d_3$ and the same planar [material frame of a rod](../../../../../material-frame-of-a-rod.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha^+=1-au.}
$$

In particular, the second rod is shortened on the inside of a bend with $u>0$.

The [bending strain of a rod](../../../../../bending-strain-of-a-rod.md) is defined here by differentiation with respect to reference [arc length](../../../../../arc-length.md). Since the reference labels match,

$$
\frac{d\mathbf d_3^+}{dS^+}=\frac{d\mathbf d_3}{dS}=u\mathbf d_1
=u^+\mathbf d_1^+,
\qquad\boxed{u^+=u,\quad\beta=1.}
$$

The distinction from spatial [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md) matters: the second centreline has

$$
\kappa^+=\left|\frac{d\mathbf d_3^+}{ds^+}\right|
=\frac{|u|}{1-au}.
$$

Thus $u/(1-au)$ is its signed spatial [curvature](../../../../../curvature.md), rather than the $u^+$ appearing in the given [elastic energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md).
