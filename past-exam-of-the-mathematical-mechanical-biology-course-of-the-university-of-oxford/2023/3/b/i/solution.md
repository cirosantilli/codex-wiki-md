<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In [morphoelasticity](../../../../../morphoelasticity.md), the [deformation gradient](../../../../../deformation-gradient.md) factors as $\mathbf F=\mathbf A\mathbf G$, where $\mathbf G$ accounts for local stress-free growth and $\mathbf A$ accounts for the subsequent elastic accommodation. [Elastic incompressibility](../../../../../elastic-incompressibility.md) means that the latter preserves volume:

$$
\det\mathbf A=1.
$$

It does not forbid volume increase by growth: here $\det\mathbf F=\det\mathbf G=\gamma$.

For the homogeneous diagonal deformation, the two unloaded transverse directions are equivalent. Taking the transversely symmetric branch implicit in the paper's hint gives $\alpha_1=\alpha_2>0$. Set $\alpha_3=\alpha>0$. The [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) constraint is then

$$
\alpha_1^2\alpha=1,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf A=\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha\right).}
$$

Equality of the transverse stretches uses that symmetry assumption; [elastic incompressibility](../../../../../elastic-incompressibility.md) alone would only give $\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3=1$. The total [deformation gradient](../../../../../deformation-gradient.md) and the actual axial length are

$$
\mathbf F=\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha\gamma\right),
\qquad \ell=\alpha\gamma,
$$

because the initial [cuboid](../../../../../rectangular-parallelepiped.md) is a unit cube.
