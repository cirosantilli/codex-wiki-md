<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $\lambda=L_1/L_0>1$. The one-dimensional [multiplicative decomposition of the deformation gradient](../../../../../multiplicative-decomposition-of-the-deformation-gradient.md) reads $\ell/L_0=\alpha\gamma$. Before contact, $\alpha=1$; afterwards, the imposed fixed length gives

$$
\alpha(t)=\frac{\lambda}{1+ct},\qquad t\ge t_c.
$$

Substituting in the linear [constitutive equation](../../../../../constitutive-equation.md) yields

$$
\boxed{n(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le t_c,\\
E_s\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{1+ct}-1\right),&t>t_c.
\end{cases}}
$$

Here negative [axial stress](../../../../../axial-stress.md) means compression. The two branches agree at $t_c$ because $1+ct_c=\lambda$. At late times,

$$
\alpha\to0,\qquad n\to-E_s.
$$

Thus the law permits arbitrarily severe elastic shortening at a finite compressive [stress](../../../../../stress.md). It is a small-[strain](../../../../../strain.md) approximation, appropriate near $\alpha=1$, and is not a credible material law as $\alpha\to0$. The one-dimensional constraint rules out [buckling](../../../../../buckling.md) in this particular model; the finite limiting [stress](../../../../../stress.md) is a defect of extrapolating the linear [constitutive equation](../../../../../constitutive-equation.md), even with that constraint retained.
