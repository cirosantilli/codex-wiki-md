<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

The [material frame of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#material-frame-of-a-rod) obeys the [director kinematics of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#director-kinematics-of-a-rod)

$$
\mathbf d_i'=\mathbf u\times\mathbf d_i.
$$

In the stress-free [reference configuration](../continuum-mechanics.md#reference-configuration), the [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) gives $u_1=0$, $u_2=\hat u$, and $u_3=0$, since all three stiffnesses are positive. Thus $\mathbf u=\hat u\mathbf d_2$. Its [cross product](../vector-space.md#cross-product) with $\mathbf d_2$ vanishes, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf d_2'=\hat u\mathbf d_2\times\mathbf d_2=\mathbf0.}
$$

Consequently this director is a fixed vector in space, even though the other two directors rotate along the [elastic rod](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-rod).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

The right-handed [material frame of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#material-frame-of-a-rod) has $\mathbf d_2\times\mathbf d_1=-\mathbf d_3$ and $\mathbf d_2\times\mathbf d_3=\mathbf d_1$. Hence the [director kinematics of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#director-kinematics-of-a-rod) reduce to

$$
\mathbf d_1'=-\hat u\mathbf d_3,\qquad
\mathbf d_3'=\hat u\mathbf d_1,\qquad \mathbf d_2=\mathbf e_2.
$$

Solving these constant-coefficient [ordinary differential equations](../differential-equation.md#ordinary-differential-equation) with the given initial frame gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbf d_1(S)&=\cos(\hat u S)\mathbf e_1-\sin(\hat u S)\mathbf e_3,\\
\mathbf d_3(S)&=\sin(\hat u S)\mathbf e_1+\cos(\hat u S)\mathbf e_3.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The [inextensible rod](../elasticity-physics.md#inextensible-rod) has $\mathbf r'=\mathbf d_3$. An [integration](../integral.md) using $\mathbf r(0)=\mathbf0$ yields, for $\hat u\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r(S)=\frac{1-\cos(\hat u S)}{\hat u}\mathbf e_1
+\frac{\sin(\hat u S)}{\hat u}\mathbf e_3.}
$$

Indeed,

$$
\left|\mathbf r(S)-\frac{\mathbf e_1}{\hat u}\right|^2=\frac1{\hat u^2},
$$

so the centreline is a [circular arc](../topology.md#circular-arc) of radius $1/|\hat u|$ in the plane perpendicular to $\mathbf e_2$. The continuous $\hat u\to0$ limit is $\mathbf d_1=\mathbf e_1$, $\mathbf d_3=\mathbf e_3$, and $\mathbf r=S\mathbf e_3$.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

The [second moment of area](../elasticity-physics.md#second-moment-of-area) about an axis weights each element of [cross-sectional area](../euclidean-geometry.md#cross-sectional-area) by its squared perpendicular distance from that axis. The [ellipse](../euclidean-geometry.md#ellipse) extends farther along $\mathbf d_2$ than along $\mathbf d_1$, so its material lies, on balance, farther from the $\mathbf d_1$ axis. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I_1>I_2.}
$$

With zero [intrinsic curvature of an elastic filament](../elasticity-physics.md#intrinsic-curvature-of-an-elastic-filament), the respective [bending moments](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-moment) required by the [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) are $EI_1\kappa\mathbf d_1$ and $EI_2\kappa\mathbf d_2$. Thus their magnitudes satisfy

$$
\boxed{|m_{\mathbf d_1}|=EI_1|\kappa|>EI_2|\kappa|=|m_{\mathbf d_2}|}
$$

for $\kappa\ne0$: bending about the minor axis is harder. Here $E$ is [Young's modulus](../elasticity-physics.md#young-s-modulus), and $EI_i$ is the corresponding [bending stiffness](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-stiffness).

<h4 id="1/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/a/iv)

Zero applied end [force](../force.md) and the [force balance](../force.md#force-balance) $\mathbf n'=\mathbf0$ imply $\mathbf n(S)=\mathbf0$ everywhere. The [moment balance](../momentum.md#moment-balance) then reduces to $\mathbf m'=\mathbf0$.

Choose an untwisted straight configuration: all three directors are constant, $\mathbf r'=\mathbf d_3$, and $\mathbf u=\mathbf0$. The [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) gives

$$
\mathbf m=-EI_2\hat u\mathbf d_2,
$$

which is constant in space and therefore satisfies the [moment balance](../momentum.md#moment-balance). The required end [bending moment](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-moment) is consequently

$$
\boxed{m_2=-EI_2\hat u.}
$$

Its sign opposes the positive [intrinsic curvature of an elastic filament](../elasticity-physics.md#intrinsic-curvature-of-an-elastic-filament). If the initial position and frame from part (ii) are retained, this solution is simply $\mathbf r(S)=S\mathbf e_3$ with $\mathbf d_i=\mathbf e_i$; the clamp supplies the opposite reaction [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) at the other end.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Because the two [elastic rods](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-rod) each have thickness $a$, their centrelines are separated by $a$ along the common director $\mathbf d_1$. The bonded geometry is

$$
\mathbf r^+(S^+)=\mathbf r(S)+a\mathbf d_1(S),\qquad S^+=S.
$$

Differentiate with respect to reference [arc length](../riemannian-geometry.md#arc-length). The first rod is an [inextensible rod](../elasticity-physics.md#inextensible-rod), and the printed [director kinematics of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#director-kinematics-of-a-rod) give

$$
\frac{d\mathbf r^+}{dS^+}
=\mathbf d_3+a(-u\mathbf d_3)=(1-au)\mathbf d_3.
$$

For the physically admissible orientation-preserving configuration $1-au>0$, the rods have the same unit tangent $\mathbf d_3^+=\mathbf d_3$ and the same planar [material frame of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#material-frame-of-a-rod). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha^+=1-au.}
$$

In particular, the second rod is shortened on the inside of a bend with $u>0$.

The [bending strain of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-strain-of-a-rod) is defined here by differentiation with respect to reference [arc length](../riemannian-geometry.md#arc-length). Since the reference labels match,

$$
\frac{d\mathbf d_3^+}{dS^+}=\frac{d\mathbf d_3}{dS}=u\mathbf d_1
=u^+\mathbf d_1^+,
\qquad\boxed{u^+=u,\quad\beta=1.}
$$

The distinction from spatial [curvature](../differential-geometry.md#curvature) matters: the second centreline has

$$
\kappa^+=\left|\frac{d\mathbf d_3^+}{ds^+}\right|
=\frac{|u|}{1-au}.
$$

Thus $u/(1-au)$ is its signed spatial [curvature](../differential-geometry.md#curvature), rather than the $u^+$ appearing in the given [elastic energy](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

The first term is the [bending energy](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-energy) of rod 1, whose preferred [bending strain of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-strain-of-a-rod) is zero. The second is the [bending energy](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-energy) of rod 2 relative to its preferred [intrinsic curvature of an elastic filament](../elasticity-physics.md#intrinsic-curvature-of-an-elastic-filament) $\hat u$. The third is its [stretching energy](../elasticity-physics.md#stretching-energy), with stretching stiffness $kB_2$ and stress-free [stretch ratio](../continuum-mechanics.md#stretch-ratio) $\alpha^+=1$. Each term is an energy per unit reference [arc length](../riemannian-geometry.md#arc-length) before [integration](../integral.md).

Substitute $u^+=u$ and $\alpha^+-1=-au$. The resulting [quadratic function](../polynomial.md#quadratic-function) of $u$ has density

$$
q(u)=\frac12(B_1+B_2+ka^2B_2)u^2-B_2\hat u\,u+\frac12B_2\hat u^2.
$$

There are no derivatives of $u$ or imposed end constraints in this [minimum potential energy principle in elasticity](../elasticity-physics.md#minimum-potential-energy-principle-in-elasticity). Since

$$
q'(u)=(B_1+B_2+ka^2B_2)u-B_2\hat u,\qquad
q''(u)=B_1+B_2+ka^2B_2>0,
$$

the unique pointwise minimizer is also the constant global minimizer:

$$
\boxed{u_* =\frac{B_2\hat u}{B_1+B_2+ka^2B_2}
=\frac{\hat u}{B_1/B_2+1+ka^2}.}
$$

For example, [completing the square](../polynomial.md#completing-the-square) gives

$$
\mathcal E[u]-\mathcal E[u_*]
=\frac{B_1+B_2+ka^2B_2}{2}\int_0^L(u-u_*)^2\,dS\ge0,
$$

which also proves uniqueness, up to equality [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), without merely assuming a uniform [curvature](../differential-geometry.md#curvature).

With $k$, $a$ and $\hat u$ fixed, the two limits are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
B_1/B_2\to\infty &: \quad u_*\to0,\\
B_1/B_2\to0 &: \quad u_*\to\frac{\hat u}{1+ka^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

In the first limit, rod 1 dominates the [bending energy](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-energy) and suppresses the bend. In the second, rod 1 offers negligible bending resistance, but following rod 2's preferred [bending strain of a rod](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-strain-of-a-rod) still shortens rod 2 through the bonded geometry. The competition between its [bending energy](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-energy) and [stretching energy](../elasticity-physics.md#stretching-energy) leaves $u_*<\hat u$ when $\hat u>0$ and $ka^2>0$. Only when that stretching penalty also vanishes does $u_*\to\hat u$.

These formulae describe an admissible bonded [elastic rod](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-rod) provided $1-au_*>0$. If parameters instead force $au_*\ge1$, the unconstrained quadratic minimizer is outside the kinematic regime of the model; the given energy does not itself enforce positive [stretch ratio](../continuum-mechanics.md#stretch-ratio).

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use the [cylindrical coordinate system](../multivariable-calculus.md#cylindrical-coordinate-system) basis $\mathbf e_r,\mathbf e_\varphi,\mathbf e_z$. The meridional unit [tangent vector](../tangent-vector.md) and outward unit [normal vector](../differential-geometry.md#normal-vector) consistent with the given geometry are

$$
\mathbf e_s=\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_r-\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_z,
\qquad
\mathbf n=\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_r+\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_z.
$$

An [surface of revolution](../differential-geometry.md#surface-of-revolution) has $\theta$ independent of $\varphi$. Consequently

$$
\partial_s\mathbf e_s=-\theta_s\mathbf n,\qquad
\partial_\varphi\mathbf e_\varphi=-\mathbf e_r,
\qquad
\mathbf e_r=\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_s+\sin\theta\,\mathbf n.
$$

Apply the [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) to the vector [force balance](../force.md#force-balance):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_s(rt_s\mathbf e_s)
&=(r_s t_s+r\partial_s t_s)\mathbf e_s-rt_s\theta_s\mathbf n,\\
\partial_\varphi(t_\varphi\mathbf e_\varphi)
&=(\partial_\varphi t_\varphi)\mathbf e_\varphi
-t_\varphi\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_s-t_\varphi\sin\theta\,\mathbf n.
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the three independent components to zero, and using $r_s=\cos\theta$, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf e_s &: \quad r\partial_s t_s+(t_s-t_\varphi)\cos\theta=0,\\
\mathbf e_\varphi &: \quad \partial_\varphi t_\varphi=0,\\
\mathbf n &: \quad rP-rt_s\theta_s-t_\varphi\sin\theta=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, away from the rotation axis where $r>0$, the scalar [mechanical equilibrium](../thermodynamics.md#mechanical-equilibrium) equations are

$$
\boxed{P=t_s\theta_s+t_\varphi\frac{\sin\theta}{r},\qquad
\partial_s t_s=\frac{\cos\theta}{r}(t_\varphi-t_s),\qquad
\partial_\varphi t_\varphi=0.}
$$

The two geometric factors $\theta_s$ and $\sin\theta/r$ are the signed meridional and azimuthal [principal curvatures](../second-fundamental-form.md#principal-curvature) in this convention. The normal equation is the [Young–Laplace equation](../surface-tension.md#young-laplace-equation) generalized to unequal principal [membrane tensions](../mathematical-biology.md#membrane-tension).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Take $s=\sigma$ up to an irrelevant additive constant, $r=a$ constant, $z=z_0-\sigma$, and $\theta=\pi/2$. These satisfy the geometric relations and give the [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch)

$$
\lambda_s=1,\qquad\lambda_\varphi=\frac a{a_0}.
$$

The [constitutive equations](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) therefore produce constant [membrane tensions](../mathematical-biology.md#membrane-tension)

$$
t_s=f_s(1,a/a_0),\qquad t_\varphi=f_\varphi(1,a/a_0).
$$

The tangential [mechanical equilibrium](../thermodynamics.md#mechanical-equilibrium) equations hold automatically, while the normal equation reduces to

$$
\boxed{Pa=f_\varphi\left(1,\frac a{a_0}\right).}
$$

Every positive root of this algebraic equation provides a cylindrical solution with no axial [stretch ratio](../continuum-mechanics.md#stretch-ratio) change. There is no further constraint on $t_s$ in the interior equations when end [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) are discarded. In particular, a closed-end pressure balance would impose additional axial loading data that are absent here. Existence of a positive root for an arbitrary specified $P$ depends on the given [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Here a prime denotes $d/d\sigma$. It is useful to retain terms through $O(\epsilon^2)$ in the normal [mechanical equilibrium](../thermodynamics.md#mechanical-equilibrium) equation, even though only first-order shape corrections are sought. The undeformed [elastic membrane](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-membrane) is tensionless, so a first-order change of [curvature](../differential-geometry.md#curvature) contributes to normal force only at second order.

The geometric equations written in reference [arc length](../riemannian-geometry.md#arc-length) are

$$
r'=s'\cos\theta,\qquad z'=-s'\sin\theta.
$$

Their first-order [Taylor expansions](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion) give

$$
\epsilon r_1'=-\epsilon\theta_1+O(\epsilon^2),\qquad
-1+\epsilon z_1'=-1-\epsilon s_1'+O(\epsilon^2),
$$

and hence

$$
\theta_1=-r_1',\qquad s_1'=-z_1'.
$$

The [principal stretches](../continuum-mechanics.md#principal-stretch) and the first-order [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) are

$$
\lambda_s=1+\epsilon s_1'+O(\epsilon^2),\qquad
\lambda_\varphi=1+\epsilon\frac{r_1}{a_0}+O(\epsilon^2),
\qquad
\boxed{t_1=-Az_1'+\frac B{a_0}r_1.}
$$

Because $t_s=t_\varphi=t$ exactly, the tangential [mechanical equilibrium](../thermodynamics.md#mechanical-equilibrium) equation gives $\partial_s t=0$. Thus both $t_1$ and $t_2$ are spatial constants: $t_1'=t_2'=0$.

Expand the geometric factors in the normal balance:

$$
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial s}=\epsilon\theta_1'+O(\epsilon^2),\qquad
\frac{\sin\theta}{r}=\frac1{a_0}-\epsilon\frac{r_1}{a_0^2}+O(\epsilon^2).
$$

Consequently

$$
\epsilon p=\epsilon\frac{t_1}{a_0}
+\epsilon^2\left(t_1\theta_1'+\frac{t_2}{a_0}-\frac{t_1r_1}{a_0^2}\right)
+O(\epsilon^3).
$$

Matching the first-order coefficient gives the requested relation

$$
\boxed{p=\frac{t_1}{a_0},\qquad t_1=a_0p.}
$$

Since the prescribed [pressure](../pressure.md) has no second-order term, and $\theta_1'=-r_1''$, its second-order coefficient gives

$$
-t_1r_1''+\frac{t_2}{a_0}-\frac{t_1r_1}{a_0^2}=0.
$$

A closed [boundary value problem](../boundary-value-problem.md) for the three requested unknowns is therefore

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
p a_0^2 r_1''+p r_1&=t_2,\\
A z_1'&=\frac B{a_0}r_1-a_0p,\\
t_2'&=0,
\end{aligned}\qquad
r_1(0)=r_1(L)=0,\quad z_1(0)=z_1(L)=0.}
$$

The first equation has order two, the second order one, and the last order one, so the four [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) match the total order. The unknown constant $t_2$ is selected by the axial endpoint constraint, rather than by an additional specified [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction).

For $p\ne0$, an equivalent formulation eliminates $z_1$ and displays that constraint as an [integral](../integral.md):

$$
r_1''+\frac{r_1}{a_0^2}=\frac{t_2}{p a_0^2},\qquad
r_1(0)=r_1(L)=0,\qquad
\int_0^L r_1\,d\sigma=\frac{a_0^2pL}{B}.
$$

After solving this system, recover $z_1$ by [integration](../integral.md) from $z_1(0)=0$. No second derivatives of the unspecified function $f$ are required: they enter the next-order shape corrections through the second-order [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation), whereas the displayed normal balance already determines the leading shape. The case $p=0$ has the undeformed solution $r_1=z_1=t_2=0$.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Before contact with both walls, the [elastic rod](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-rod) can grow freely, so its elastic [stretch ratio](../continuum-mechanics.md#stretch-ratio) is $\alpha=1$ and its length is its stress-free grown length,

$$
\ell(t)=L_0\gamma(t)=L_0(1+ct).
$$

The first contact occurs when $\ell(t_c)=L_1$. For the intended growth case $c>0$,

$$
\boxed{t_c=\frac{L_1/L_0-1}{c}=\frac{L_1-L_0}{cL_0}.}
$$

The condition $L_1>L_0$ ensures $t_c>0$. If $c=0$, contact never occurs; negative $c$ describes shrinkage rather than the stated continuing growth.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Write $\lambda=L_1/L_0>1$. The one-dimensional [multiplicative decomposition of the deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#multiplicative-decomposition-of-the-deformation-gradient) reads $\ell/L_0=\alpha\gamma$. Before contact, $\alpha=1$; afterwards, the imposed fixed length gives

$$
\alpha(t)=\frac{\lambda}{1+ct},\qquad t\ge t_c.
$$

Substituting in the linear [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) yields

$$
\boxed{n(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le t_c,\\
E_s\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{1+ct}-1\right),&t>t_c.
\end{cases}}
$$

Here negative [axial stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#axial-stress) means compression. The two branches agree at $t_c$ because $1+ct_c=\lambda$. At late times,

$$
\alpha\to0,\qquad n\to-E_s.
$$

Thus the law permits arbitrarily severe elastic shortening at a finite compressive [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). It is a small-[strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) approximation, appropriate near $\alpha=1$, and is not a credible material law as $\alpha\to0$. The one-dimensional constraint rules out [buckling](../elasticity-physics.md#buckling) in this particular model; the finite limiting [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) is a defect of extrapolating the linear [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation), even with that constraint retained.

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

The kinematic relation is unchanged: $\alpha=1$ before contact and $\alpha=\lambda/(1+ct)$ afterwards. Therefore the nonlinear [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) gives

$$
\boxed{n(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le t_c,\\
E_s\left[\dfrac{\lambda^2}{(1+ct)^2}-\dfrac{1+ct}{\lambda}\right],&t>t_c.
\end{cases}}
$$

The first term in the post-contact expression is $O(t^{-2})$. Hence its [asymptotic expansion](../asymptotic-expansion.md) is

$$
n(t)=-\frac{E_sc}{\lambda}t-\frac{E_s}{\lambda}+O(t^{-2}),\qquad
\boxed{n(t)\sim-\frac{E_scL_0}{L_1}t\quad(t\to\infty).}
$$

In this model the compressive [axial stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#axial-stress) diverges in magnitude, because the $-\alpha^{-1}$ term penalizes vanishing elastic [stretch ratio](../continuum-mechanics.md#stretch-ratio) without bound.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

In [morphoelasticity](../mathematical-biology.md#morphoelasticity), the [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) factors as $\mathbf F=\mathbf A\mathbf G$, where $\mathbf G$ accounts for local stress-free growth and $\mathbf A$ accounts for the subsequent elastic accommodation. [Elastic incompressibility](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-incompressibility) means that the latter preserves volume:

$$
\det\mathbf A=1.
$$

It does not forbid volume increase by growth: here $\det\mathbf F=\det\mathbf G=\gamma$.

For the homogeneous diagonal deformation, the two unloaded transverse directions are equivalent. Taking the transversely symmetric branch implicit in the paper's hint gives $\alpha_1=\alpha_2>0$. Set $\alpha_3=\alpha>0$. The [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) constraint is then

$$
\alpha_1^2\alpha=1,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf A=\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha\right).}
$$

Equality of the transverse stretches uses that symmetry assumption; [elastic incompressibility](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-incompressibility) alone would only give $\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3=1$. The total [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) and the actual axial length are

$$
\mathbf F=\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha\gamma\right),
\qquad \ell=\alpha\gamma,
$$

because the initial [cuboid](../euclidean-geometry.md#rectangular-parallelepiped) is a unit cube.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

Write $W_i=\partial W/\partial\alpha_i$, evaluated at $(\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha^{-1/2},\alpha)$. Applying the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) to the reduced [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) gives

$$
\hat W'(\alpha)
=-\frac12\alpha^{-3/2}W_1-\frac12\alpha^{-3/2}W_2+W_3.
$$

Multiply by $\alpha$ and use $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha^{-1/2}$:

$$
\alpha\hat W'(\alpha)
=\alpha_3W_3-\frac12\alpha_1W_1-\frac12\alpha_2W_2.
$$

The transverse symmetry specified in the hint has $W_1=W_2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\alpha\hat W'(\alpha)=\alpha_3W_3-\alpha_1W_1.}
$$

The lateral faces have zero [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction), so their diagonal [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) components satisfy $t_1=t_2=0$. The [hydrostatic pressure](../fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) enforcing [elastic incompressibility](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-incompressibility) is therefore $p=\alpha_1W_1=\alpha_2W_2$, and the axial component simplifies to

$$
\boxed{t_3=\alpha_3W_3-p=\alpha\hat W'(\alpha).}
$$

This elimination of the [Lagrange multiplier](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) $p$ is what makes the reduced [strain energy density](../elasticity-physics.md#strain-energy-density) useful for the spring balance.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

The end displacement relative to the initial unit axial length is

$$
\delta=\ell-1=\alpha\gamma-1.
$$

By [Hooke's law](../elasticity-physics.md#hooke-s-law), the total resisting [force](../force.md) supplied by the spring is $F_3=-K\delta$. To convert this resultant into the axial [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor), divide by the current end [cross-sectional area](../euclidean-geometry.md#cross-sectional-area). The transverse total stretches are both $\alpha^{-1/2}$, so that area is

$$
\mathcal A=\alpha^{-1/2}\alpha^{-1/2}=\alpha^{-1}.
$$

Uniform [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) over that face consequently gives

$$
t_3=\frac{F_3}{\mathcal A}=-K\alpha(\alpha\gamma-1).
$$

Equate this to the expression from part (ii):

$$
\boxed{-K\alpha(\alpha\gamma-1)=\alpha\hat W'(\alpha).}
$$

The extra factor $\alpha$ on the left is essential: the spring law specifies a total [force](../force.md), whereas the material [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) specifies [force](../force.md) per current area. The reference end area is one, so the same spring resultant can also be read as a nominal end [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction).

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

For the [neo-Hookean solid](../elasticity-physics.md#neo-hookean-solid), the axial [Cauchy stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) under the unloaded lateral [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) is

$$
t_3=\mu(\alpha^2-\alpha^{-1}).
$$

With $K,\mu>0$, the spring balance is equivalent, after multiplication by $\alpha>0$, to the [cubic equation](../polynomial.md#cubic-equation)

$$
\boxed{(K\gamma+\mu)\alpha^3-K\alpha^2-\mu=0.}
$$

For a fixed $\gamma\ge1$, write its left side as $h(\alpha)$. It obeys $h(0)=-\mu<0$ and tends to $+\infty$ as $\alpha\to\infty$. Moreover

$$
h'(\alpha)=\alpha\big[3(K\gamma+\mu)\alpha-2K\big].
$$

Thus $h$ first decreases from a negative value and then increases strictly, crossing zero exactly once on $\alpha>0$. For $\gamma>1$,

$$
h(1)=K(\gamma-1)>0,\qquad
h(1/\gamma)=\mu(\gamma^{-3}-1)<0,
$$

so the unique equilibrium has $1/\gamma<\alpha<1$. The tissue is elastically compressed, while its actual length $\alpha\gamma$ exceeds one and compresses the spring.

To determine the large-growth limit without presuming a power law, solve for $\gamma$:

$$
\gamma=\frac1\alpha+\frac\mu K(\alpha^{-3}-1).
$$

This is a strictly decreasing function of $\alpha>0$, since its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is

$$
-\alpha^{-2}-\frac{3\mu}{K}\alpha^{-4}<0.
$$

It tends to $+\infty$ only as $\alpha\to0^+$. Consequently unlimited growth forces $\alpha\to0^+$, and

$$
\boxed{t_3=\mu(\alpha^2-\alpha^{-1})\to-\infty.}
$$

More precisely, the [dominant balance](../asymptotic-expansion.md#dominant-balance) is $K\gamma\alpha^3\sim\mu$, giving

$$
\boxed{\alpha\sim\left(\frac\mu{K\gamma}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
t_3\sim-\mu^{2/3}(K\gamma)^{1/3}.}
$$

The surrounding spring softens the divergence relative to rigid confinement but does not bound the compressive [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). Both axial extension and lateral expansion help accommodate growth:

$$
\ell=\alpha\gamma\sim\left(\frac\mu K\right)^{1/3}\gamma^{2/3},\qquad
\mathcal A=\alpha^{-1}\sim\left(\frac K\mu\right)^{1/3}\gamma^{1/3},
$$

whose product is the total volume $\gamma$, as required by [elastic incompressibility](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-incompressibility) and the imposed [growth tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#growth-tensor).
