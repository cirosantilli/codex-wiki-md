<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**A sufficiently self-gravitating disk can fragment directly, bypassing the slow assembly of a massive solid core.** In the simplest thin [gas](../../../../../gas.md)-disk model the competition is between self-[gravity](../../../../../gravity.md), [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) and rotational shear. Axisymmetric perturbations have

$$
\omega^2=\kappa^2+c_s^2k^2-2\pi G\Sigma|k|,\qquad\boxed{Q_T=\frac{c_s\kappa}{\pi G\Sigma}\lesssim1,}
$$

where $\kappa$ is the [radial epicyclic frequency](../../../../../radial-epicyclic-frequency.md), not the [opacity](../../../../../opacity.md). In a nearly Keplerian disk, $\kappa\simeq\Omega$. This is [Toomre's stability criterion](../../../../../toomre-s-stability-criterion.md); finite thickness and global/nonaxisymmetric modes modify the precise boundary. The fastest local wavelength is $\lambda=2c_s^2/(G\Sigma)$, of order the [disk scale height](../../../../../disk-scale-height.md) times $2\pi$ when $Q_T\simeq1$.

Instability alone does not ensure bound planets. Compression and shocks heat the [gas](../../../../../gas.md), raising $Q_T$; a slowly cooling disk can maintain self-regulated spiral turbulence. [Cooling-limited disk fragmentation](../../../../../cooling-limited-disk-fragmentation.md) requires energy loss on a sufficiently short dynamical timescale, conventionally expressed as $\beta=\Omega t_{\rm cool}$ below a model-dependent threshold. The often quoted $\beta\sim3$ belongs to a particular local razor-thin calculation, not every real disk.[2001 local model: cooling, self-regulated turbulence and fragmentation](../../../../../nonlinear-outcome-of-gravitational-instability-in-cooling-gaseous-disks-by-charles-f-gammie.md) Cold massive outer disks are promising sites, but fragment contraction, tidal survival, further accretion and migration determine whether the result remains a planetary-[mass](../../../../../mass.md) body or grows into a [brown dwarf](../../../../../brown-dwarf.md).

For [gravitational collapse of a settled particle layer](../../../../../gravitational-collapse-of-a-settled-particle-layer.md), if “core collapse” instead denotes a **solid-particle layer**, its random-velocity dispersion replaces [gas](../../../../../gas.md) [sound speed](../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) in an appropriate particle-disk stability condition. Settling and concentration can enable collapse into [planetesimals](../../../../../planetesimal.md); [gas](../../../../../gas.md) turbulence and particle stirring oppose it. This route makes solid seeds for later [core accretion](../../../../../core-accretion.md) and is physically distinct from direct [gas](../../../../../gas.md) fragmentation. In both cases one must establish self-[gravity](../../../../../gravity.md) against velocity support and tides, rather than infer collapse from high [surface density](../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) alone.
