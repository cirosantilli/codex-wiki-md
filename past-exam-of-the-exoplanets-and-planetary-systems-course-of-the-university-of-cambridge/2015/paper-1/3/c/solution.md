<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**[Type I migration](../../../../../type-i-migration.md) is orbital angular-momentum exchange between an embedded low-[mass](../../../../../mass.md) [planet](../../../../../planet.md) and its disk without a deep gap.** The [planet](../../../../../planet.md) excites spiral [density](../../../../../density.md) waves near [Lindblad resonances](../../../../../lindblad-resonance.md); the disk's reaction torques act back on the [planet](../../../../../planet.md). The outer wake usually supplies a negative torque and the inner wake a positive one, with their small imbalance often driving inward migration. [Gas](../../../../../gas.md) on [horseshoe orbits in a planetary disk](../../../../../horseshoe-orbit-in-a-planetary-disk.md) near corotation adds a [corotation torque](../../../../../corotation-torque.md) that depends on entropy and [vortensity](../../../../../vortensity.md) gradients.

For a thin disk, define $q=M_p/M_\star$, $h=H/a$ and

$$
\Gamma_0=\left(\frac qh\right)^2\Sigma_g a^4\Omega^2,\qquad\Gamma=C\Gamma_0.
$$

The coefficient $C$ is signed and depends on disk gradients, thermodynamics and diffusion; it is not a universal constant. For a circular orbit with fixed [planet](../../../../../planet.md) [mass](../../../../../mass.md), $L_p=M_pa^2\Omega\propto a^{1/2}$. The [Type I migration timescale from disk torque](../../../../../type-i-migration-timescale-from-disk-torque.md) follows from $\dot L_p=\Gamma$:

$$
\dot a=\frac{2\Gamma}{M_pa\Omega},\qquad\boxed{\frac a{|\dot a|}=\frac1{2|C|}\frac{M_\star}{M_p}\frac{M_\star}{\Sigma_g a^2}h^2\Omega^{-1}.}
$$

Thus a more massive embedded core generally migrates faster within this regime, unlike a picture in which migration starts only after giant-[planet](../../../../../planet.md) formation.

In a simple locally isothermal smooth disk the net torque is commonly negative, potentially removing cores before they acquire [gas](../../../../../gas.md). In a non-isothermal disk an unsaturated [entropy-related corotation torque](../../../../../entropy-related-corotation-torque.md) can balance or exceed the negative wave torque, giving a [planet trap](../../../../../planet-trap.md) or outward migration. Viscosity and thermal diffusion help maintain the background gradients across the horseshoe region; without adequate replenishment [corotation-torque saturation](../../../../../corotation-torque-saturation.md) can suppress that contribution. The linear low-[mass](../../../../../mass.md) scaling requires a small disturbance, roughly $q\ll h^3$; larger planets, gap formation and strong disk feedback require a different treatment. Type I migration is therefore neither universally inward nor literally viscous advection of the [planet](../../../../../planet.md).[Sections on Lindblad and corotation torques, saturation and low-[mass](../../../../../mass.md) migration](../../../../../planet-disc-interactions-and-early-evolution-of-planetary-systems-by-clement-baruteau-and-others.md)
