# Escape velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Potential energy](potential-energy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_velocity)

The [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md) is the least initial [speed](speed.md) that permits a freely coasting body to move arbitrarily far from an isolated attracting body. Despite its conventional name, it is a [speed](speed.md), not a unique [velocity](velocity.md) vector. For a fixed [point mass](point-mass.md) $M$, or outside a body with [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md), set [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md) zero at infinity. An exterior [test particle](test-particle.md) of constant [mass](mass.md) $m\ll M$ then has

$$
U(r)=-\frac{GMm}{r},\qquad E=\frac12mv^2-\frac{GMm}{r},\qquad v_{\mathrm{esc}}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}}.
$$

Here $\mu=GM$ is the [standard gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md), and $r$ is distance from the centre, not height above the surface. Set $r=R$ for surface [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md). Evaluate [velocity](velocity.md) in the body's rest [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). Neglect [drag](drag-physics.md), later [rocket thrust](rocket-thrust.md), other bodies' [gravity](gravity.md), and relativistic effects.[§13.3, equations 13.4–13.6 and “Escape velocity”: infinity reference, small test mass and exterior launch](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/13-3-gravitational-potential-energy-and-total-energy.md)

[Conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) gives the threshold by requiring $E\geq0$: at infinity the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) tends to zero and the [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) cannot be negative. If $E=0$, the outward motion loses [speed](speed.md) continuously toward zero. If $E>0$, the asymptotic [speed](speed.md) is $v_\infty=\sqrt{2E/m}=\sqrt{v_0^2-v_{\mathrm{esc}}(r_0)^2}$. If $E<0$, infinity is inaccessible. For a radial outward launch, its outer [turning point](turning-point.md) is $r_{\max}=-GMm/E$. For nonradial motion use [angular momentum and the escape energy threshold](angular-momentum-and-the-escape-energy-threshold.md) to distinguish zero radial [velocity](velocity.md) from zero total [speed](speed.md).

Direction still determines the [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) and whether the body hits an obstacle. An initially inward [velocity](velocity.md) can cause collision even when $E\geq0$. For an isolated spherical body, an exterior outward launch, or a tangential launch at or above [escape velocity](escape-velocity.md), has an outward escape branch without intersecting the body. A [parabolic trajectory](parabolic-trajectory.md) is the nonradial threshold path; a [hyperbolic Kepler orbit](hyperbolic-kepler-orbit.md) has positive [energy](energy.md). The formula specifies their required local [speed](speed.md), not their orientation.[§13.3, “Energy and gravitationally bound objects”: scalar energy and finite-body collisions](university-physics-volume-1-by-william-moebs-and-others/13-3-gravitational-potential-energy-and-total-energy.md)
