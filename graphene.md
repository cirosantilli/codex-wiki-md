# Graphene

↑ **Parent:** [Carbon](carbon.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene)

[Graphene](graphene.md) is a single layer of [carbon](carbon.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) arranged in a [honeycomb lattice](honeycomb-lattice.md). A nearby gate can select predominantly [electron](electron.md) or [electron hole](electron-hole.md) conduction, as demonstrated by the [graphene exfoliation and field-effect experiments](graphene-exfoliation-and-field-effect-experiments.md).[pp.1–2, mechanically cleaved films, gate and Hall response](electric-field-effect-in-atomically-thin-carbon-films-by-konstantin-novoselov-and-others.md)

A useful question is how geometry changes motion. The [nearest-neighbour model of graphene](nearest-neighbour-model-of-graphene.md) starts with three equivalent bonds from each site and two sites per primitive cell. Interference between those three hopping paths makes two [energy bands](energy-band.md) meet; near their meeting, [energy](energy-split.md) grows linearly with distance in [wave vector](wavevector.md). The resulting [Dirac cones](dirac-cone.md) explain why an atomic sheet can imitate one aspect of a relativistic particle while remaining an ordinary material with contacts, defects and boundaries.
