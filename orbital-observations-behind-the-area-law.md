# Orbital observations behind the area law

↑ **Parent:** [Areal velocity](areal-velocity.md)

Why infer an [area](surface-area.md) law from [astronomy](astronomy-split.md)? In the authored account [The great Martian catastrophe and how Kepler fixed it by Owen Gingerich](the-great-martian-catastrophe-and-how-kepler-fixed-it-by-owen-gingerich.md), a 13 August 1593 [Mars](mars.md) observation has [ecliptic longitude](ecliptic-longitude.md) $346^\circ7.5'$, against predicted $342^\circ0'$ and $351^\circ26'$. These are Earth-based directions with a date, not measured Sun-based [areas](surface-area.md). The observer's [armillary sphere](armillary-sphere.md) used graduated circles and sighting arms to read [declination](declination.md); reversing it allowed a second reading. Comparing [stars](star.md) at different altitudes exposed [refraction](refraction.md), and a planet-based check revealed an erroneous solar correction table. The apparent [Mars](mars.md) distance signal had been contaminated by that correction. [Johannes Kepler](johannes-kepler.md) subsequently corrected the observer's [Earth](earth.md) [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md), then tested [Mars](mars.md) trajectories. The account describes typical angular errors of $2'$ or less ($120$ [arcseconds](arcsecond.md)): small residuals needed care.[pp. 51-53: Fig. 2, refraction checks, 13 August log table, Earth-orbit correction and angular errors](the-great-martian-catastrophe-and-how-kepler-fixed-it-by-owen-gingerich.md)

The Tufts class notes [Kepler's Astronomia Nova and the Orbit of Mars](the-newtonian-revolution-part-one-philosophy-167-science-before-newton-s-principia/kepler-s-astronomia-nova-and-the-orbit-of-mars.md) explain the key limitation: an [area](surface-area.md) requires a trajectory and distances, so [Kepler's second law](kepler-s-second-law.md) cannot be directly tested from angular observations alone. They describe checking the combined [ellipse](ellipse.md) and [area](surface-area.md) law against 28 dated observations around seven oppositions during 1583-1595, using an [Earth](earth.md) model to predict Earth-based directions; agreement improved greatly but some discrepancies remained.[p. 20, items 3-5, and pp. 26-27, section IV.D: predictions, residuals and dependence of the area test on a trajectory](the-newtonian-revolution-part-one-philosophy-167-science-before-newton-s-principia/kepler-s-astronomia-nova-and-the-orbit-of-mars.md)

The inferential chain is useful today too: recover relative [positions](position.md) and [times](time-in-physics.md), compare swept [areas](surface-area.md), and ask what [forces](force-split.md) are compatible with their evolution. Agreement with equal [areas](surface-area.md) supports zero [torque](torque.md) about the chosen origin under the assumptions below; it alone does not identify an [inverse-square law](inverse-square-law.md). Recovering the radial [force](force-split.md) also needs the trajectory and its timing, as in [inferring an inverse-square force from Kepler laws](inferring-an-inverse-square-force-from-kepler-laws.md). The two diagrams here are calculated examples, not the historical measurements.
