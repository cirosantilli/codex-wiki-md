# Minimal-polynomial coefficient proof of Northcott finiteness

↑ **Parent:** [Northcott theorem](northcott-theorem.md)

For the primitive minimal polynomial $a_d\prod_j(T-\alpha_j)$, the [height-Mahler measure formula](height-mahler-measure-formula.md) gives $H(\alpha)^d=a_d\prod_j\max(1,|\alpha_j|)$. Expanding the product bounds every integer coefficient by $2^D B^D$. There are finitely many bounded coefficient lists and each has finitely many roots, proving finiteness of the algebraic numbers. On a projective coordinate chart, every coordinate ratio has height at most the point's [projective height](projective-height.md); applying the scalar bound proves the projective [Northcott theorem](northcott-theorem.md) too. A degree bound is essential, as the infinitely many [roots of unity](root-of-unity.md) all have height one.
