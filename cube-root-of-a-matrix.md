# Cube root of a matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Matrix power](matrix-power.md)

A [cube root of a matrix](cube-root-of-a-matrix.md) $A$ is a [square matrix](square-matrix.md) $B$ of the same size over the specified [field](field.md) satisfying $B^3=A$. Every complex [invertible matrix](invertible-matrix.md) has one: put it in [Jordan normal form](jordan-normal-form.md), choose a scalar [cube root](cube-root.md) of each nonzero [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md), and apply the [truncated binomial cube root of the identity plus a nilpotent matrix](truncated-binomial-cube-root-of-the-identity-plus-a-nilpotent-matrix.md) on each block. The [Nilpotent Jordan block](nilpotent-jordan-block.md) $J_3(0)$ has none, even though its square is nonzero. If $B^3=J_3(0)$, then $B^9=0$, making $B$ a [nilpotent matrix](nilpotent-matrix.md) of size three; the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](cayley-hamilton-theorem-split.md) then gives $B^3=0$, a contradiction.
