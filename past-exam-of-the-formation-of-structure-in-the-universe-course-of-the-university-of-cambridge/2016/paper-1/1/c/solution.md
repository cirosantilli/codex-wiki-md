<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Only the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) energy above the [hydrogen](../../../../../hydrogen.md) [ionization](../../../../../ionization.md) threshold initially becomes [photoelectron](../../../../../photoelectron.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The stated mean therefore supplies

$$
E_{\rm heat}=\langle h\nu\rangle-h\nu_{\rm Lye}=k_BT_*.
$$

Let $\mathcal R$ be the local number of [ionizations](../../../../../ionization.md) per volume per time. [Photoionization equilibrium](../../../../../photoionization-equilibrium.md) makes this equal to the recombination rate. If $\langle E_{\rm rec}(T)\rangle$ is the mean [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) removed per recombination, thermal balance reduces to

$$
\mathcal R k_BT_*=\mathcal R\langle E_{\rm rec}(T)\rangle,
\qquad
\boxed{\langle E_{\rm rec}(T_{\rm eq})\rangle=k_BT_*.}
$$

Neither the [ionization](../../../../../ionization.md) threshold nor the density remains in this event-by-event balance. The threshold energy is stored as atomic [ionization energy](../../../../../ionization-energy.md) and returned in recombination radiation; it is not a net thermal input. This separation between [photoelectron](../../../../../photoelectron.md) heating and [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) lost by recombining [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) is the relevant cooling balance.[Heating by photoionization and Recombination radiation sections](../../../../../heating-and-cooling-of-hii-regions-by-ian-czekala.md)

In the elementary mean-thermal-energy approximation, a captured [electron](../../../../../electron.md) removes $\tfrac32k_BT$. This gives the concise estimate

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm eq}\simeq\frac23T_*
\quad\text{if }\langle E_{\rm rec}\rangle=\tfrac32k_BT.}
$$

That coefficient is a stated approximation, not a universal recombination identity. Real recombination weights the [electron](../../../../../electron.md) distribution by its velocity-dependent capture rate. If the chosen cooling model instead writes $\langle E_{\rm rec}\rangle=\eta_{\rm rec}(T)k_BT$, the precise answer is $\eta_{\rm rec}(T_{\rm eq})T_{\rm eq}=T_*$. The source supplies no recombination cooling coefficient or capture kernel from which a unique more accurate coefficient can be computed. Its emitted-photon average is also being used as the average of the absorbed ionizing [photons](../../../../../photon.md); spectral filtering would require a different weighted average.
