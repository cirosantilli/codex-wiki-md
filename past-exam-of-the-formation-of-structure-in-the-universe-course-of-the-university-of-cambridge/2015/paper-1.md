**A [spiral density wave](../../galaxy.md#spiral-density-wave) is an organized [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) and gravitational-potential perturbation through which stars and [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) can move; it is not generally a permanently labelled set of stars.** [Differential rotation](../../astrophysical-fluid-dynamics.md#differential-rotation) winds a material arm rapidly. In a wave description, a perturbation has phase $m\phi+\int k\,dr-\omega t$, with [pattern speed](../../galaxy.md#pattern-speed) $\Omega_p=\omega/m$ distinct from the local stellar angular speed.

For a thin fluid disk the local tightly wound dispersion relation is

$$
(\omega-m\Omega)^2=\kappa^2+c_s^2k^2-2\pi G\Sigma|k|.
$$

Rotation and [pressure](../../pressure.md) resist self-gravity. A collisionless stellar disk requires its stellar response function instead of simply replacing $c_s$ by a dispersion; the familiar local axisymmetric stability parameter is $Q_\star=\sigma_R\kappa/(3.36G\Sigma)$. This is distinct from the [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) $Q_g=c_s\kappa/(\pi G\Sigma)$.

Waves exchange [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) at [Lindblad resonances](../../planetary-science.md#lindblad-resonance) and near [corotation radius](../../astrophysical-disk.md#corotation-radius). [Swing amplification](../../galaxy.md#swing-amplification) can strengthen a leading disturbance as shear turns it into a trailing one. Bars or companions can force a coherent spiral; recurrent self-excited disturbances also occur. Thus a single indefinitely steady wave is one model, not a description established for every [spiral galaxy](../../galaxy.md#spiral-galaxy). [Gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) compression and shocks along arms can organize [star formation](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#star-formation), while gravitational torques redistribute [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum). Arm brightness, recent [star formation](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#star-formation) and stellar [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) need not trace precisely the same pattern.[Sections4–6: wave dynamics, recurrent versus quasi-steady patterns, and [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) response](../../physics-bibliography.md#spirals-in-galaxies-by-jerry-a-sellwood-and-karen-l-masters)

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

[Supermassive black holes](../../astrophysical-black-hole.md#supermassive-black-hole) are compact galactic nuclei with masses typically of millions to billions of solar masses. **Their gravitational influence is measured from the motion of surrounding matter; accretion luminosity is evidence of feeding, not a necessary property of every black hole.** Keplerian stellar orbits near the Galactic Centre and molecular-maser rotation curves in other nuclei provide particularly direct compact-[mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) constraints. A rough [black-hole sphere of influence](../../astrophysical-black-hole.md#black-hole-sphere-of-influence) is $r_{\rm infl}\sim GM_\bullet/\sigma_\star^2$; resolving it helps dynamical [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) estimates.

Accretion converts binding energy into radiation,

$$
L=\eta\dot M_{\rm in}c^2,\qquad\boxed{L_E=\frac{4\pi GM_\bullet c}{\kappa_{\rm es}}.}
$$

The latter is the [Eddington luminosity](../../stellar-structure.md#eddington-luminosity) for isotropic radiation opposed by electron-scattering [opacity](../../stellar-structure.md#opacity), not an absolute luminosity ceiling for every geometry. The retained black-hole [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) grows at $(1-\eta)\dot M_{\rm in}$. Sustained feeding can produce an [active galactic nucleus](../../astrophysical-black-hole.md#active-galactic-nucleus), whereas [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas)-poor nuclei may be faint. Loss of [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) is a central feeding problem.

Black-hole [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) correlates with classical-bulge stellar [velocity dispersion](../../galaxy.md#velocity-dispersion) through the [M-sigma relation](../../astrophysical-black-hole.md#m-sigma-relation). Accretion-powered radiation, winds and jets can heat or remove [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas), linking nuclear growth to galaxy evolution. Such correlations do not establish one universal feedback history, and disks and pseudobulges do not follow the same tight relations as classical bulges. Seed formation, accretion and mergers all contribute; seed masses and feeding histories remain model dependent.[Dynamical evidence, bulge-dependent correlations and different feedback regimes](../../physics-bibliography.md#coevolution-or-not-of-supermassive-black-holes-and-host-galaxies-by-john-kormendy-and-luis-c-ho)

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Galactic [star formation](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#star-formation) requires [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) to collect into cold dense regions that can become self-gravitating. Disk-scale gravity, turbulent motions, shear, [pressure](../../pressure.md) and magnetic fields affect this collection; formation then proceeds within [molecular clouds](../../galaxy.md#molecular-cloud). A useful local parametrization is

$$
\dot\rho_\star=\epsilon_{\rm ff}\frac{\rho}{t_{\rm ff}},\qquad t_{\rm ff}=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{32G\rho}},
$$

where the [star-formation efficiency per free-fall time](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#star-formation-efficiency-per-free-fall-time) summarizes inefficient conversion rather than requiring every cloud to collapse at once.

Across galactic disks the empirical [Kennicutt–Schmidt law](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#kennicutt-schmidt-law) relates [star formation rate](../../galaxy.md#star-formation-rate) [surface density](../../astrophysical-disk.md#surface-density-of-a-disk) to [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) [surface density](../../astrophysical-disk.md#surface-density-of-a-disk), $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}\propto\Sigma_g^N$. A slope near $1.4$ is a familiar integrated total-[gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) fit, while molecular-[gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) relations in ordinary disks can be closer to linear; the slope and normalization depend on tracer, averaging scale and environment. The [gas-depletion time](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#gas-depletion-time) $t_{\rm dep}=M_g/\Psi$ is the corresponding fuel-consumption scale, not a prediction that inflow and recycling cease.

Massive stars supply ionizing radiation, winds and [supernova](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#supernova) energy and momentum. This [stellar feedback](../../galaxy.md#stellar-feedback) can disperse clouds, sustain turbulence and drive outflows, regulating rather than simply extinguishing all future [star formation](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#star-formation). Fresh accretion and recycled [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) replenish the reservoir. UV and recombination-line emission trace young stars, while infrared emission traces absorbed and re-radiated light; extinction, stellar populations and the assumed [initial mass function](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#initial-mass-function) affect inferred rates. **A galactic rate reflects supply, cloud formation and feedback together, rather than only the free-fall time of its densest [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas).**[Sections3–6: diagnostics, [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) relations and physical interpretation](../../physics-bibliography.md#star-formation-in-the-milky-way-and-nearby-galaxies-by-robert-c-kennicutt-jr-and-neal-j-evans-ii)

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

In a matter-dominated [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#einstein-de-sitter-universe), the growing linear [density contrast](../../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) is proportional to the scale factor. When $|\delta|\sim1$, linear evolution no longer describes collapse. An overdense region initially expands more slowly than the background, reaches [cosmological turnaround](../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#cosmological-turnaround), and then contracts under self-gravity.

The [spherical top-hat collapse model](../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#spherical-top-hat-collapse-model) supplies a useful idealization: pressureless shells obey $\ddot r=-GM/r^2$. At formal collapse, the linearly extrapolated growing mode is

$$
\boxed{\delta_c=\frac35\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{2/3}\simeq1.686.}
$$

This is a linear extrapolation, not the true finite nonlinear [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) at that instant. In the ideal energy-conserving spherical model, [virial equilibrium](../../classical-mechanics.md#virial-equilibrium) gives a virial [radius](../../topology.md#radius) about half the turnaround [radius](../../topology.md#radius); its mean [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) at the collapse time is $18\pi^2\simeq178$ times the cosmic matter [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density).

Real perturbations are nonspherical: tides and anisotropic collapse produce sheets, filaments and haloes. Collisionless [dark matter](../../cosmology.md#dark-matter) develops [shell crossing](../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#shell-crossing) and phase mixing rather than radiatively losing its orbital energy. Baryonic [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) can shock, cool and collect within the resulting potential wells. Hierarchical merging then assembles larger systems where small-scale fluctuations have sufficient initial power. [Press-Schechter formalism](../../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#press-schechter-formalism) connects a collapse threshold with the statistics of the smoothed initial field, but halo abundances and internal structure need more than the single spherical model. These ideas apply after perturbations become nonlinear; one must not extend pressureless subhorizon equations through all radiation-era or horizon-scale conditions.[Section3.3: [gravitational collapse](../../astrophysics.md#gravitational-collapse) and structure formation](../../physics-bibliography.md#cosmology-by-david-tong)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

With constant [isothermal sound speed](../../compressible-flow.md#isothermal-sound-speed) $a_T$, the equation of state is $p=a_T^2\rho$. Spherical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../statistical-physics.md#hydrostatic-equilibrium) and [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) continuity require

$$
a_T^2\frac{d\rho}{dr}=-\rho\frac{GM(r)}{r^2},\qquad\frac{dM}{dr}=4\pi r^2\rho.
$$

Try $\rho=A/r^2$. With no central point [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass), $M(r)=4\pi Ar$, and hydrostatic balance becomes $2a_T^2A=4\pi GA^2$. The positive-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) branch is therefore

$$
\boxed{A=\frac{a_T^2}{2\pi G},\qquad\rho=\frac{a_T^2}{2\pi Gr^2},\qquad M(r)=\frac{2a_T^2r}{G}.}
$$

The constant external [pressure](../../pressure.md) fixes the truncation [radius](../../topology.md#radius) $r_s$ through $p(r_s)=p_0$, giving

$$
\boxed{r_s=\frac{a_T^2}{\sqrt{2\pi Gp_0}},\qquad M_s=\frac{2a_T^2r_s}{G}.}
$$

This is the [pressure-truncated singular isothermal gas sphere](../../galaxy.md#pressure-truncated-singular-isothermal-gas-sphere), one hydrostatic solution, not a claim that every isothermal cloud has a singular centre. Its central [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) diverges, but its enclosed [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) tends to zero as $r\to0$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For [inside-out collapse of a singular isothermal sphere](../../astrophysics.md#inside-out-collapse-of-a-singular-isothermal-sphere), the expansion front is at $r_f=a_Tt$. The original [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) swept by this front is

$$
M_{\rm swept}=\frac{2a_T^2r_f}{G}=\frac{2a_T^3t}{G}.
$$

Thus the only time-independent accretion scale is $a_T^3/G$. The front-sweeping estimate has coefficient two, but [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) takes time to fall from the front to the object; it is not an exact central accretion coefficient. Write

$$
\boxed{\dot M_\star=m_0\frac{a_T^3}{G}\propto\left(\frac{k_BT}{\mu m_H}\right)^{3/2}\propto T^{3/2},}
$$

for fixed mean particle [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $\mu m_H$. Determining the dimensionless coefficient $m_0$ requires the collapse solution; it is not fixed by front propagation alone and is not needed for the requested [temperature](../../temperature.md) scaling.[1977 expansion-wave solution: central accretion and the free-falling envelope](../../physics-bibliography.md#self-similar-collapse-of-isothermal-spheres-and-star-formation-by-frank-h-shu)

Well inside the front, where the point [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) dominates and infall is approximately pressureless and locally steady, $|v_r|\simeq\sqrt{2GM_\star/r}$. Conserving the radial [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) flux gives the [free-fall density profile around a growing point mass](../../astrophysics.md#free-fall-density-profile-around-a-growing-point-mass):

$$
\boxed{\rho(r,t)=\frac{\dot M_\star}{4\pi r^2|v_r|}\simeq\frac{\dot M_\star}{4\pi\sqrt{2GM_\star(t)}}r^{-3/2}.}
$$

The inner slope is $-3/2$, versus $-2$ in the undisturbed outer cloud. For constant $\dot M_\star$ and negligible initial object [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass), $M_\star\propto t$ and the inner normalization varies as $t^{-1/2}$. These are inner asymptotes, not an assertion of an abrupt exact power-law join at the front.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

At distance $r$, one grain intercepts power through its projected area and emits through its full surface:

$$
P_{\rm abs}=\pi a^2\frac{L}{4\pi r^2},\qquad P_{\rm em}=4\pi a^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T_d^4.
$$

The [blackbody dust sublimation radius](../../critical-phenomenon.md#blackbody-dust-sublimation-radius) follows by setting $T_d=T_{\rm sub}$:

$$
\boxed{r_{\rm sub}=\left(\frac{L}{16\pi\sigma_{\rm SB}T_{\rm sub}^4}\right)^{1/2}\simeq1.62\times10^{15}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.0526\,\mathrm{pc}.}
$$

Here the supplied luminosity is $3.8\times10^{37}\,\mathrm W$. The grain [radius](../../topology.md#radius) cancels because both cross-sections scale as $a^2$. The [temperature](../../temperature.md) exterior to this boundary scales as $T_d\propto r^{-1/2}$. This estimate assumes isotropic illumination, no shielding, and unit absorption/emission efficiency as specified; real grain emissivities can change it. The numerical evaluation uses the external conversion values $\sigma_{\rm SB}\simeq5.6704\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}}$ and $1\,\mathrm{pc}\simeq3.0857\times10^{16}\,\mathrm m$.[Stefan-Boltzmann constant, rounded at the precision relevant here](../../physics-bibliography.md#codata-recommended-values-of-the-fundamental-physical-constants-2014-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology)

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

**The dust-cooled equilibrium is thermally stable under the stated constant irradiation, with fixed dust properties and dust-to-[gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) ratio.** At a fixed position, absorbed power per grain is independent of its [temperature](../../temperature.md), while emission is $4\pi a^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4$. A positive [temperature](../../temperature.md) perturbation increases cooling, causing a restoring energy loss; a negative perturbation reduces cooling, allowing reheating.

For heat capacity $C>0$ of a grain together with its thermally coupled [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas),

$$
C\,\delta\dot T=-16\pi a^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T_0^3\delta T,
$$

so the perturbation decays. The [thermal stability of externally heated blackbody dust](../../critical-phenomenon.md#thermal-stability-of-externally-heated-blackbody-dust) relies on remaining below sublimation with the grain population intact; destruction, variable illumination or finite dust-[gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) equilibration can change the response. An isobaric parcel with a fixed grain-to-[gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) ratio has the same restoring sign when heating and cooling are compared per particle.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

First specify the perturbation: take fixed [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density), fixed ionic abundance and the same [temperature](../../temperature.md)-independent heating, so thermal stability requires $d\mathcal C/dT>0$ at an equilibrium. Let $\theta=\Delta E/k_B$, $n_e$ be the collider [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) and $n_A=n_1+n_2$ the atomic [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density). A two-level optically thin balance is

$$
n_1n_eq_{12}=n_2(A_{21}+n_eq_{21}),\qquad\mathcal C=n_2A_{21}\Delta E.
$$

The [critical density of an atomic transition](../../atomic-physics.md#critical-density-of-an-atomic-transition) is approximately $A_{21}/q_{21}$. At **low [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density)**, each rare collisional excitation is followed by an escaping [photon](../../quantum-mechanics.md#photon), so

$$
\mathcal C\simeq n_An_eq_{12}\Delta E\propto T^{-1/2}e^{-\theta/T},\qquad\frac{d\ln\mathcal C}{d\ln T}=-\frac12+\frac\theta T.
$$

The [isochoric thermal stability of a collisionally excited line](../../atomic-physics.md#isochoric-thermal-stability-of-a-collisionally-excited-line) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{T<2\theta:\ \text{stable};\qquad T>2\theta:\ \text{unstable}.}
$$

At $T=2\theta$ the linear restoring coefficient vanishes; the cooling maximum is marginal to first order and is not robust two-sided stability.

At **high [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density)**, collisional de-excitation competes successfully with radiative decay, and collisions establish [local thermodynamic equilibrium](../../astrophysics.md#local-thermodynamic-equilibrium) level populations. Detailed balance gives $n_2/n_1=g e^{-\theta/T}$, where $g=g_2/g_1$. The cooling is then

$$
\mathcal C=n_AA_{21}\Delta E\,f_u(T),\qquad f_u=\frac{g e^{-\theta/T}}{1+g e^{-\theta/T}},\qquad\frac{d\ln f_u}{d\ln T}=\frac\theta T(1-f_u)>0.
$$

Thus **the high-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) fixed-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) branch is stable for finite $T$**, becoming weakly restoring as the upper fraction saturates at high [temperature](../../temperature.md). It is incorrect to retain the low-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) factor $T^{-1/2}$ in the [radiative cooling](../../heat.md#radiative-cooling) law once populations are thermalized.[Section2.2: collisional excitation, critical [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) and the high-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) Boltzmann limit](../../physics-bibliography.md#agn-emission-lines-by-hagai-netzer)

The [isobaric thermal stability of a two-level coolant](../../atomic-physics.md#isobaric-thermal-stability-of-a-two-level-coolant) is a distinct question. If the intended heating is a fixed rate **per particle**, then $n\propto T^{-1}$ and stability tests the cooling per particle. The low-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) boundary becomes $T<2\theta/3$; the high-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) cooling per particle remains proportional to $f_u$, so remains stable. If instead heating is constant **per volume**, the isobaric low-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) boundary is $T<2\theta/5$, and the high-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) stability condition is $\theta(1-f_u)/T>1$. The source gives no [pressure](../../pressure.md) constraint or explicit heating normalization: the boxed $2\theta$ result is the fixed-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) answer, with these conditional alternatives stated to avoid asserting a universal boundary.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For constant inflow $\Phi$ and efficiency $\epsilon>0$, with no recycling or outflow, [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) conservation gives the [constant-inflow gas regulator solution](../../galaxy.md#constant-inflow-gas-regulator-solution):

$$
\dot M_g=\Phi-\epsilon M_g,\qquad\boxed{M_g(t)=\frac\Phi\epsilon+\left(M_0-\frac\Phi\epsilon\right)e^{-\epsilon t}.}
$$

The equilibrium $M_{g,\rm eq}=\Phi/\epsilon$ is stable: a [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas)-[mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) perturbation obeys $\delta\dot M_g=-\epsilon\delta M_g$. At late times the [star formation rate](../../galaxy.md#star-formation-rate) approaches $\Psi=\Phi$, while the [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) reservoir approaches a constant.

Integrating $\dot M_\star=\epsilon M_g$ with $M_\star(0)=0$ gives the [stellar mass in a constant-inflow gas regulator](../../galaxy.md#stellar-mass-in-a-constant-inflow-gas-regulator):

$$
\boxed{M_\star(t)=\Phi t+\left(M_0-\frac\Phi\epsilon\right)(1-e^{-\epsilon t}).}
$$

It also follows immediately from $M_g+M_\star=M_0+\Phi t$. **The [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) equilibrates; the stellar [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) generally keeps growing.** With $\Phi=0$, these reduce to exponential [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) depletion and $M_\star=M_0(1-e^{-\epsilon t})$.

A constant inflow is implicit in the requested steady asymptote, not automatic for an arbitrary prescribed rate. For time-dependent $\Phi(t)$ the exact integrating-factor result is

$$
M_g(t)=M_0e^{-\epsilon t}+\int_0^t e^{-\epsilon(t-s)}\Phi(s)\,ds;
$$

a constant late-time [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) [mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) then requires a corresponding asymptotically steady supply. Both printed mark groups remain in this single unlettered continuation of part(a).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Let $D$ be the separation of galaxy centres and $x$ a small displacement from the satellite centre along the line of centres. Expanding the central point-[mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#mass) field gives the radial differential acceleration

$$
\delta g_{\rm tide}\simeq\frac{2GM}{D^3}x.
$$

Inside a uniform-[density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) satellite, the restoring self-gravity is

$$
g_{\rm self}(x)=\frac{G(4\pi\rho x^3/3)}{x^2}=\frac{4\pi G\rho}{3}x.
$$

Comparing the two at the same displacement yields the [differential-gravity disruption limit of a uniform satellite](../../galaxy.md#differential-gravity-disruption-limit-of-a-uniform-satellite):

$$
\boxed{D_{\min}\simeq\left(\frac{3M}{2\pi\rho}\right)^{1/3}\propto\left(\frac M\rho\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

The satellite [radius](../../topology.md#radius) cancels because both accelerations are linear in $x$. This is the instantaneous tidal-gravity estimate requested by the given criterion, not a full rotating Roche calculation. Including centrifugal terms for a circular corotating orbit replaces the radial coefficient two by three, giving $D_{\min}\simeq[9M/(4\pi\rho)]^{1/3}$ while preserving the required scaling. An eccentric orbit requires checking the stronger tide near periapsis, and extended [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) profiles require a more detailed restoring-force calculation.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Put $H=2/(3t)$ and let $f_B=\rho_B/\bar\rho$, $f_D=1-f_B$ be constant background matter fractions. Define the total matter perturbation $S=f_B\Delta_B+f_D\Delta_D$ and the relative mode $D=\Delta_D-\Delta_B$. Adding with the background weights and subtracting the two given equations respectively gives

$$
\ddot S+\frac4{3t}\dot S-\frac2{3t^2}S=0,\qquad\ddot D+\frac4{3t}\dot D=0.
$$

The [total and relative density modes of two pressureless matter fluids](../../linear-cosmological-perturbation-theory.md#total-and-relative-density-modes-of-two-pressureless-matter-fluids) are consequently

$$
\boxed{S=A t^{2/3}+B t^{-1},\qquad D=C+E t^{-1/3},\qquad\Delta_B=S-f_DD,\quad\Delta_D=S+f_BD.}
$$

These are the general consequences of the supplied equations; a single ratio additionally needs initial-mode assumptions.

For the usual dark-matter-dominated growing-mode approximation, take $f_B\ll1$, neglect the decaying modes, and set the relative-velocity mode $E=0$. Then $\Delta_D\propto t^{2/3}\propto R$. With $\Delta_B(t_r)=0$ at recombination, $C=\Delta_D(t_r)$, so the [baryon catch-up approximation after recombination](../../linear-cosmological-perturbation-theory.md#baryon-catch-up-approximation-after-recombination) is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_B(t)=\Delta_D(t)\left[1-\frac{R(t_r)}{R(t)}\right]=\Delta_D(t)\left[1-\frac{1+z}{1+z_0}\right].}
$$

At redshifts $z,z_0\gg1$, replacing $1+z$ by $z$ gives the printed $\Delta_B\simeq\Delta_D(1-z/z_0)$. **The printed relation is a high-redshift approximation with a specified relative mode, not an exact identity for arbitrary solutions of the two-fluid equations.**

For example, retaining a finite baryon fraction with a growing total mode and constant relative mode gives, with $x=R/R(t_r)$,

$$
\frac{\Delta_B}{\Delta_D}=\frac{f_D(x-1)}{f_Dx+f_B}.
$$

Alternatively, if initially both $\Delta_B=0$ and $\dot\Delta_B=0$ while the dark-matter growing mode is already present, then in the dark-matter-dominated limit the relative transient is not zero and

$$
\frac{\Delta_B}{\Delta_D}=1-3x^{-1}+2x^{-3/2}.
$$

These different solutions show why the initial-velocity convention matters. **The quoted catch-up law is the dark-matter-dominated branch with the relative decaying mode neglected**, together with the high-redshift approximation stated above. The general solution retains the transient and a finite baryon fraction, so the additional assumptions needed for that special case remain explicit.

The physical conclusion is robust: after decoupling, baryonic [gas](../../thermodynamics.md#gas) can fall into pre-existing dark-matter potential wells and its fractional [density](../../fluid-mechanics.md#density) perturbation catches up. The absolute constant offset need not vanish; it becomes small relative to the growing common mode. The pressureless approximation applies on scales above the baryonic Jeans scale and while perturbations remain linear; subsequent collapse requires nonlinear treatment.[Sections3.1–3.3: matter growth, decoupling, Jeans support and dark-matter seeds](../../physics-bibliography.md#cosmology-by-david-tong)
