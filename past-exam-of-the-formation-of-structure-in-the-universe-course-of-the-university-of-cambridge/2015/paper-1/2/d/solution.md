<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**The dust-cooled equilibrium is thermally stable under the stated constant irradiation, with fixed dust properties and dust-to-[gas](../../../../../gas.md) ratio.** At a fixed position, absorbed power per grain is independent of its [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), while emission is $4\pi a^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4$. A positive [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) perturbation increases cooling, causing a restoring energy loss; a negative perturbation reduces cooling, allowing reheating.

For heat capacity $C>0$ of a grain together with its thermally coupled [gas](../../../../../gas.md),

$$
C\,\delta\dot T=-16\pi a^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T_0^3\delta T,
$$

so the perturbation decays. The [thermal stability of externally heated blackbody dust](../../../../../thermal-stability-of-externally-heated-blackbody-dust.md) relies on remaining below sublimation with the grain population intact; destruction, variable illumination or finite dust-[gas](../../../../../gas.md) equilibration can change the response. An isobaric parcel with a fixed grain-to-[gas](../../../../../gas.md) ratio has the same restoring sign when heating and cooling are compared per particle.
