# Heat-engine calorimetry

↑ **Parent:** [Heat engine](heat-engine.md)

Heat-engine calorimetry measures [heat](heat.md) transfers so that observed [work](work.md) can be compared with the correct [energy](energy.md) input. Measuring motion alone cannot supply the denominator of [thermal efficiency](thermal-efficiency.md).

The institutional [Laboratory 16: Stirling Engine](laboratory-16-stirling-engine.md) describes a modern teaching arrangement: an [ethanol](ethanol.md) lamp drives a [Stirling engine](stirling-engine.md) coupled to an electrical output device, which supplies a [resistor](resistor.md) immersed in [water](water.md) in a [calorimeter](calorimeter.md). The prescribed readouts include fuel [mass](mass.md) consumed, the [pressure](pressure.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) loop, electrical readings, and [water](water.md) [temperature](temperature.md). The sequence separates fuel input, gas-cycle [work](work.md), electrical output, and [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) of the [water](water.md). Warming of the [water](water.md) is the final useful output; it is not the engine's rejected $Q_c$. The handout distinguishes warm-up from steady operation and asks students to compare the final warming with direct heating by the lamp. It gives a procedure, not a table of measured efficiencies. Its pp. 1–3 therefore establish an apparatus and measurement design without supplying a new experimental result.

For a proposed calibration, use a known input of electrical [work](work.md) to determine the effective [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) $C_{\rm eff}$ of the [water](water.md), vessel and immersed parts; stir to make the measured [temperature](temperature.md) representative. A matched run with no input estimates exchange with the room. For constant $C_{\rm eff}$ and no [phase transition](phase-transition.md), the resulting model is

$$
E_{\rm in}=C_{\rm eff}(T_f-T_i)+Q_{\rm leak,out},
$$

where $Q_{\rm leak,out}$ is the signed net [heat](heat.md) lost to the room during the measurement. Here $E_{\rm in}$ is supplied electrical [work](work.md) when the [resistor](resistor.md) lies inside the calibration boundary; for a separate external [heat](heat.md) input it is the received [heat](heat.md). These are proposed controls, not controls reported in that handout. Slow cooling, changing material properties and imperfectly matched room exchange limit this inference; the approach to [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md) and [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) matter as much as the thermometer reading.

To measure the actual engine-input $Q_h$, one must instrument its hot-side transfer separately, or close a calibrated [energy](energy.md) balance around a specified heater boundary. A heater's electrical [work](work.md) includes changes in its stored [internal energy](internal-energy.md) and [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) that bypasses the engine. At periodic operation a cold-side flowing-[water](water.md) measurement can instead estimate $\dot Q_c=\dot m c_p(T_{\rm out}-T_{\rm in})$, if [mass flow rate](mass-flow-rate.md) $\dot m$ and [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md) $c_p$ are known, with negligible storage and other [heat](heat.md) exchange in that measurement boundary. It measures only the rejection captured by that stream. Include all remaining transfers before using the residual to infer $\dot Q_h=P_{\rm out}+\dot Q_c$. This measurement discipline makes a comparison with [Carnot's theorem](carnot-s-theorem.md) meaningful.
