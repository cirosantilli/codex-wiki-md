# Rich line covering bound over a finite field

↑ **Parent:** [Polynomial method in combinatorics](polynomial-method-in-combinatorics.md)

Suppose every point of $\mathbb F_q^n$ lies on an [affine line in a vector space](affine-line-in-a-vector-space.md) meeting a subset $N$ in at least $m$ points, with $1\le m\le q$. A smaller $N$ would admit a nonzero [multivariate polynomial](multivariate-polynomial.md) of [total degree](total-degree-of-a-polynomial.md) at most $m-1$ vanishing on it, by the [dimension of a bounded-total-degree polynomial space](dimension-of-a-bounded-total-degree-polynomial-space.md). Each selected line then has more [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) than the degree of its restriction, so the [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) vanishes at every point. The [Schwartz-Zippel lemma](schwartz-zippel-lemma.md) forbids this because $m-1<q$. Thus the displayed bound holds, and is at least $m^n/n!$. This is a point-covering condition; the [finite-field Kakeya set](finite-field-kakeya-set.md) condition instead quantifies over directions.
