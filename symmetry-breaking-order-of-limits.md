# Symmetry-breaking order of limits

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamic limit](thermodynamic-limit.md)

At zero field and with [spin inversion symmetry](spin-inversion-symmetry.md) in its [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md), a finite [Ising model](ising-model.md) pairs every configuration with its spin-reversed configuration. Their equal [Boltzmann weights](boltzmann-factor.md) force the [expected value](expected-value-split.md) of its [spin magnetization](spin-magnetization.md) $m=N^{-1}\sum_i s_i$ to vanish. Below a model's ordering [critical temperature](critical-temperature.md), an arbitrarily small positive field can instead select the positive [spontaneous magnetization](spontaneous-magnetization.md) if size is taken to infinity first:

$$
\lim_{h\downarrow0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle m\rangle_{N,h}=m_+>0,
\qquad
\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{h\to0}\langle m\rangle_{N,h}=0.
$$

The distinction is the order of limits and the stated symmetric [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md); [plus boundary conditions](plus-boundary-condition.md) already break [spin inversion symmetry](spin-inversion-symmetry.md). A zero-field symmetric mixture of the two ordered states can retain zero mean [spin magnetization](spin-magnetization.md) even in the [thermodynamic limit](thermodynamic-limit.md). Also, $\langle|m|\rangle>0$ in a finite system is not by itself proof of [spontaneous symmetry breaking](spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md).[§1.2.1, Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking: displayed nested limit and the immediately following explanation](statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong/1-from-spins-to-fields.md)
