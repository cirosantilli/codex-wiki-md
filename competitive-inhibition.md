# Competitive inhibition

↑ **Parent:** [Reversible enzyme inhibition](reversible-enzyme-inhibition.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_inhibition)

In the model of [reversible enzyme inhibition](reversible-enzyme-inhibition.md), [competitive inhibition](competitive-inhibition.md) makes [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) binding and inhibitor binding mutually exclusive: $EI$ is allowed but $ESI$ is absent. Set $\alpha'=1$, equivalently take $K_i'\to\infty$. Then

$$
v=\frac{V_{\max}s}{\alpha K_M+s},\qquad
V_{\max,\rm app}=V_{\max},\quad K_{M,\rm app}=\alpha K_M.
$$

For the chosen $I=30\,\mathrm{\mu M}$, $\alpha=4$: the half-limiting [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) [concentration](concentration.md) rises from $20$ to $80\,\mathrm{\mu M}$, while the limiting [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) remains $100\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$. At $s=200\,\mathrm{\mu M}$ the inhibited [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) is $500/7\approx71.43\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$, compared with the uninhibited $1000/11\approx90.91\,\mathrm{\mu M\,min^{-1}}$. “Overcome by [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md)” means that their ratio tends to one as $s\to\infty$, not that any finite addition removes the difference.
