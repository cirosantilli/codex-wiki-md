# Divergence of normalized iid partial sums without a first absolute moment

↑ **Parent:** [Strong law of large numbers](strong-law-of-large-numbers.md)

For independent identically distributed [random variables](random-variable-split.md), the [tail-sum formula](tail-sum-formula.md) applied to $|X_1|/\lambda$ shows that $\sum_n\mathbb P(|X_n|\geq\lambda n)=\infty$ for every $\lambda>0$ when the first absolute moment is infinite. The [Borel-Cantelli second lemma](borel-cantelli-second-lemma.md), followed by a countable intersection over positive integer $\lambda$, gives the first limsup assertion.

If $|S_n|/n$ had finite limsup along such a sample path, it would eventually be bounded by some finite constant $C$. Then $X_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$ would imply $|X_n|/n\leq2C$ eventually, a contradiction. This is a necessary-integrability counterpart to the [strong law of large numbers](strong-law-of-large-numbers.md); without a finite first absolute moment, the normalized sums cannot converge to any finite value.
