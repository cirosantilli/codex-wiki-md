<h1 id="braun-s-receiver-coupling-experiment">Braun's receiver coupling experiment</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Tuned spark wireless apparatus](tuned-spark-wireless-apparatus.md)

In his 1909 lecture, [Karl Ferdinand Braun](karl-ferdinand-braun.md) recalled two experimental stations on Strasbourg forts in summer 1902. Known [capacitances](capacitance.md) and calculated [inductances](inductance.md) set the transmitter tuning; the receiver searched for its two coupled [oscillations](oscillation.md). Its receiving coil fed a second coil in a tuned indicator circuit. Separating these coils increased both the sharpness of [resonance](resonance.md) and excitation intensity, until further separation reduced the response. The varied coil spacing was the control; transmitter coupling need not be loose for this receiver effect. For intensity measurements, Braun described an air-thermometer readout in the related resonant measuring apparatus.[1909 lecture, printed p. 234: Strasbourg stations, coil-spacing test and air-thermometer readout](electrical-oscillations-and-wireless-telegraphy-by-karl-ferdinand-braun.md)

The comparison tests receiver coupling, not an unrestricted law that looser coupling always helps. The paragraph does not identify the particular indicator used in the coil-spacing test or give a response curve from which to reconstruct its optimum.
