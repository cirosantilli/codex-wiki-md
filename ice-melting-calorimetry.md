# Ice-melting calorimetry

↑ **Parent:** [Latent heat of fusion](latent-heat-of-fusion.md)

[Ice-melting calorimetry](ice-melting-calorimetry.md) measures the fusion [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) through [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md), rather than inferring it from the small [temperature](temperature.md) change of the [melting](melting.md) [ice](ice.md) itself. In a mixing experiment, warm [water](water.md) and the container cool while added [ice](ice.md) melts and the resulting [water](water.md) warms. Their combined [energy](energy.md) balance separates fusion from [sensible heat](sensible-heat.md).

Weigh the container and stirrer, then weigh the added warm [water](water.md) to obtain $m_w$. Let the container and [water](water.md) reach a common initial [temperature](temperature.md) $T_i$. Prepare [ice](ice.md) in a clean [ice](ice.md)-[water](water.md) mixture near $T_m$, rather than assuming freezer [ice](ice.md) is already at $T_m$; drain and blot its surface immediately before transfer. Add it without splashing, stir gently, and record [temperature](temperature.md) before addition and until after the last [ice](ice.md) disappears. Reweigh to find the added [mass](mass.md) $m_i$. Wisconsin's laboratory guide specifies drying each piece, weighing before and after, and recording the [temperature](temperature.md) history; it places the initial and final [temperatures](temperature.md) on opposite sides of the jacket [temperature](temperature.md) to reduce net environmental exchange. [University of Wisconsin, H-2: Latent heat of fusion of ice, procedure](https://badger.physics.wisc.edu/trash/lab_old/manual/node30.html)

For fully melted, initially dry [ice](ice.md) at $T_m$, a common final [temperature](temperature.md) $T_f>T_m$, constant [water](water.md) [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md) $c_w$, and apparatus [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) $C_{\rm cal}$ initially at $T_i$,

$$
(m_wc_w+C_{\rm cal})(T_i-T_f)+Q_{\rm env}=m_iL+m_ic_w(T_f-T_m).
$$

Here $Q_{\rm env}>0$ denotes net [heat](heat.md) entering the apparatus from its surroundings. The left side supplies the fusion [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) and the [sensible heat](sensible-heat.md) on the right; the [water](water.md) produced by [melting](melting.md) must be warmed too. The balance extends Case Western's mixing demonstration by including the apparatus and environmental terms. If the [specific heat capacities](specific-heat-capacity.md) vary appreciably, replace their products with the corresponding [temperature](temperature.md) integrals. [Case Western, mixing-calorimetry energy balance](https://case.edu/artsci/phys/courses/demos/spec.htm)

Determine $C_{\rm cal}$ independently, for example by a warm/cold [water](water.md) mixing balance with no [phase transition](phase-transition.md), including the wetted stirrer and [thermometer](thermometer.md) in the apparatus. Check the [thermometer calibration](thermometer-calibration.md), keep the lid in place between transfers, and monitor the surroundings. Before/after drift and a comparable run without [ice](ice.md) help bound $Q_{\rm env}$; such corrections assume comparable [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) conditions and are not automatically exact. Choosing a jacket [temperature](temperature.md) between $T_i$ and $T_f$ reduces exchange but does not prove $Q_{\rm env}=0$. Ensure all the [ice](ice.md) has melted and the contents are well mixed before using the measured $T_f$: with residual [ice](ice.md), the weighed addition is not the [mass](mass.md) that has undergone fusion. Repeat the experiment and report [measurement errors](measurement-error.md) in [mass](mass.md), [temperature](temperature.md), $C_{\rm cal}$ and the leak correction.

An independent way to see the plateau is David Roundy's Oregon State experiment: heat an [ice](ice.md)-[water](water.md) mixture slowly with a waterproof resistor in an insulated cup, stir, and record [temperature](temperature.md), heater [electric current](electric-current.md) $I(t)$, [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V(t)$ and elapsed time. Plot [temperature](temperature.md) against $E_{\rm el}=\int I(t)V(t)\,dt$, recording changes in $I$ or $V$. The initial [solid](solid.md) [mass](mass.md) must exclude accompanying [water](water.md). For a boundary enclosing the heater as well as the sample, this input is electrical [work](work.md), so $\Delta H=E_{\rm el}+Q_{\rm env}$ under the constant-[pressure](pressure.md) assumptions above. Across an ideal isothermal plateau the apparatus has no [sensible heat](sensible-heat.md) change, giving $m_sL=E_{\rm el}+Q_{\rm env}$; departures from uniform [temperature](temperature.md) require accounting for the heater and container response. [David Roundy, Oregon State, Ice Calorimetry Lab: apparatus, stirring and electrical input](https://paradigms.physics.oregonstate.edu/act/handout/2108)
