# Oblique coherent reflection from a stationary rigid rough surface

↑ **Parent:** [Small-height Neumann scattering](small-height-neumann-scattering.md)

For a zero-mean stationary rough height, the mean first-order correction vanishes. At second order, the boundary derivative is $-h\partial_z^2\psi_1+h'\partial_x\psi_1$. Averaging this expression using the [power spectrum of surface height](power-spectrum-of-surface-height.md) gives the displayed coherent reflection coefficient. Thus the [root mean square](root-mean-square.md) height alone does not fix the coherent mean: the height correlation spectrum matters. For roughness varying slowly enough that $q\simeq\alpha$ in the integral, $r_N\simeq1-2\beta^2\sigma^2$. A local random-height phase average needs this additional approximation and is not a general consequence of stationarity.
