# Geodesics on a generalized cylinder

↑ **Parent:** [Cylinder (geometry)](cylinder-geometry.md)

For a smooth surface obtained by translating the plane graph $z=F(y)$ along the $x$ direction, the [induced metric](induced-metric.md) is $ds^2=dx^2+[1+F'(y)^2]dy^2$. The transverse [arc length](arc-length.md) coordinate $u(y)=\int^y\sqrt{1+F'(s)^2}ds$ makes it $ds^2=dx^2+du^2$. This is a local [isometry](isometry.md) to the Euclidean plane, extending the [intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder](intrinsic-flatness-of-a-circular-cylinder.md) to arbitrary smooth generating graphs.

Its [geodesics](geodesic.md) therefore lift to straight lines in $(x,u)$ coordinates. Those that are graphs over $x$ satisfy $u=ax+b$, while the additional family $x=\text{constant}$ cannot be written as $y(x)$. The same graph formula follows from the [Beltrami identity](beltrami-identity.md) for the arc-length integrand $\sqrt{1+[1+F'(y)^2]y'^2}$. These straight lines are locally minimizing; on cylinders with a closed generating curve, global identifications can create several different winding paths between two points.
