# Geostationary orbit

↑ **Parent:** [Synchronous circular orbit](synchronous-circular-orbit.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geostationary_orbit)

A [geostationary orbit](geostationary-orbit.md) is a prograde equatorial [circular Kepler orbit](circular-kepler-orbit.md) whose [orbital period](orbital-period.md) equals one [sidereal day](sidereal-day.md), so its satellite remains above a fixed location on [Earth](earth.md)'s equator. It is the terrestrial case of a [planet-stationary orbit](planet-stationary-orbit.md). Its radius follows from [Newton's law of universal gravitation](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) and circular [acceleration](acceleration.md): $r=(GM_ET^2/(4\pi^2))^{1/3}$, approximately $42164\ \mathrm{km}$ from [Earth](earth.md)'s centre. Its altitude above the surface is smaller by [Earth](earth.md)'s radius.
