# Thermal expansion of a weakly anharmonic bond

↑ **Parent:** [Linear thermal expansion coefficient](linear-thermal-expansion-coefficient.md)

For a bond free of applied tension, use a local [potential energy](potential-energy.md) $V(a+x)=V(a)+\kappa x^2/2+\gamma x^3/6+\cdots$ with $\kappa>0$. Classical [equipartition](equipartition-theorem.md) gives $\langle x^2\rangle_0=k_BT/\kappa$; vanishing mean [force](force-split.md) then yields $\langle x\rangle=-\gamma k_BT/(2\kappa^2)$ to first order in [anharmonicity](anharmonicity.md). Thus the [linear thermal expansion coefficient](linear-thermal-expansion-coefficient.md) is $\alpha=-\gamma k_B/(2a\kappa^2)$. For a [Morse potential](morse-potential.md), $\gamma=-3\kappa/\xi$, giving positive expansion $3k_B/(2a\kappa\xi)$. This is a local bound-state approximation; the isolated cubic truncation is not a globally stable equilibrium potential.
