# Physical properties of salts

↑ **Parent:** [Ionic bonding](ionic-bonding.md)

Many [ionic crystals](ionic-crystal.md) have high [melting](melting.md) [temperatures](temperature.md) compared with small molecular [solids](solid.md), because reorganizing their strongly interacting [ions](ion.md) costs [energy](energy.md). But [melting](melting.md) leaves a dense [liquid](liquid.md) with interactions; it does not perform the complete separation defining a [lattice energy](lattice-energy.md). At fixed pressure, the transition occurs when the [Gibbs free energies](gibbs-free-energy.md) of the [solid](solid.md) and [liquid](liquid.md) match. The relevant [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) and [entropy](entropy.md) differences both matter, so [lattice energy](lattice-energy.md) alone is not a universal ordering of [melting](melting.md) [temperatures](temperature.md). Strong [covalent bonds](covalent-bond.md) in extended networks can also give high [melting](melting.md) [temperatures](temperature.md).[§10.5, “Ionic Solids” and “Covalent Network Solid”; §16.4, equations defining Gibbs free energy and its change](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)
