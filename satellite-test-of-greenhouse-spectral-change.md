# Satellite test of greenhouse spectral change

↑ **Parent:** [Climate model](climate-model.md)

A [satellite test of greenhouse spectral change](satellite-test-of-greenhouse-spectral-change.md) compares outgoing [radiance](radiance.md) with [radiative transfer](radiative-transfer.md) predictions. The 2001 study compared IRIS on Nimbus 4 (1970) and IMG on ADEOS (1997). Matching April–June ocean regions, masking land, screening clouds and correcting resolution and [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) controlled comparability. Spectral [radiance](radiance.md) became [brightness temperature](brightness-temperature.md) readouts. Regional comparisons and independent calculations tested interpretation. [Carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) and [methane](methane.md) features supported changed absorption; residual clouds, calibration and interannual variability limited background inference.[Harries et al., 2001, pp. 355–357, Table 1, Fig. 1 and Methods](increases-in-greenhouse-forcing-inferred-from-the-outgoing-longwave-radiation-spectra-of-the-earth-in-1970-and-1997-by-john-e-harries-and-others.md)

This observational comparison tests [radiative transfer](radiative-transfer.md); it does not by itself measure equilibrium [climate sensitivity](climate-sensitivity.md) or validate a complete [climate model](climate-model.md).

To understand the readout, write an instrument's spectral channel schematically as

$$
y_j=\int K_j(\widetilde\nu)I(\widetilde\nu,\widehat{\mathbf n})\,d\widetilde\nu+b_j+e_j,
\qquad \int K_j(\widetilde\nu)\,d\widetilde\nu=1.
$$

Here $I$ is spectral [radiance](radiance.md) in the viewing direction $\widehat{\mathbf n}$, $K_j$ is a normalized channel response, $b_j$ is calibration offset and $e_j$ is random error. Subtracting two channel readings isolates an atmospheric difference only after their response functions and sampling are comparable. Matching kernels reduces a false difference caused merely by observing a narrow [spectral line](spectral-line.md) at different resolutions; it cannot remove every calibration offset.

[Outgoing longwave radiation](outgoing-longwave-radiation.md) in an [energy](energy-split.md) budget also integrates [radiance](radiance.md) over upward directions and over the thermal spectrum, with the appropriate projected-angle factor. A [brightness temperature](brightness-temperature.md) converts one spectral [radiance](radiance.md) to the equivalent [blackbody](blackbody.md) value through [Planck's law](planck-s-law.md); it is not automatically surface [temperature](temperature-split.md). This explains why a spectral difference, a broadband [radiative flux](radiative-flux.md) difference and surface warming are related observables with different units and different inferences.
