# Potential of a charged cylindrical shell with angular Fourier data

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](electric-potential.md)

For an infinite circular cylinder of radius $R$, assume the [electrostatic potential](electric-potential.md) is independent of the axial coordinate. An angular [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) $s_n\cos n\theta$, or $s_n\sin n\theta$, in the prescribed jump $\partial_r\Phi|_{R^+}-\partial_r\Phi|_{R^-}=-\sigma(\theta)$ produces the same angular mode in the potential, multiplied by

$$
\frac{Rs_n}{2n}\begin{cases}(r/R)^n,&r<R,\\(R/r)^n,&r>R.\end{cases}
$$

Here $r_<=\min(r,R)$ and $r_>=\max(r,R)$ in the abbreviated header formula. [Separation of variables](separation-of-variables.md) selects $r^n$ inside by regularity and $r^{-n}$ outside by decay. Continuity equates their boundary values, and the derivative jump is minus $2n/R$ times that value. A nonzero angular mean of the [surface charge density](surface-charge-density.md) introduces a logarithmic term, so zero mean is necessary for a potential decaying at infinity in this two-dimensional reduction.
