<h1 id="fourier-s-law">Fourier's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier's_law)

[Fourier's law](fourier-s-law.md) predicts the local conductive [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) from the local [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md). In a [continuum approximation](continuum-approximation.md) with [isotropic](isotropy.md) [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) $k>0$, its [vector](vector.md) form and one-dimensional form are

$$
\mathbf q=-k\nabla T,\qquad
q=-k\frac{dT}{dx},\qquad
\dot Q=Aq\quad\text{for uniform normal flux over area }A.
$$

The [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) $q$ has units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, the [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md) has units $\mathrm{K\,m^{-1}}$ and the [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md) has units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. The rate of [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md) $\dot Q$ has units $\mathrm W$. These units distinguish a local material response from the total [power](power.md) crossing a surface.

The minus sign makes [heat](heat.md) flow toward lower [temperature](temperature.md): for $k>0$, $\mathbf q\cdot\nabla T=-k|\nabla T|^2\leq0$. In the worked bar in [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md), $dT/dx=-200\,\mathrm{K\,m^{-1}}$ therefore gives $q=+400\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. Reversing the end [temperatures](temperature.md) reverses the [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md). A zero [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md) gives zero conductive [heat flux](heat-flux-density.md) in this model.

[Fourier's law](fourier-s-law.md) is a constitutive relation expressing measured material behavior; it is not a consequence of [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) alone. It applies locally and does not require a [steady state](steady-state.md). The straight-line bar solution additionally assumes constant [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md), constant cross-sectional [area](surface-area.md), no sources or side losses, and fixed end [temperatures](temperature.md). [Anisotropic](anisotropy.md) materials require the directional response described by the [thermal conductivity tensor](thermal-conductivity-tensor.md). The [scalar](scalar.md) $k$ used in the examples expresses the stated [isotropy](isotropy.md) assumption. [E. M. Greitzer, Z. S. Spakovszky and I. A. Waitz, MIT Thermodynamics and Propulsion, section 16.2: Fourier's law and thermal conductivity](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node116.html)

Historically, [Joseph Fourier](joseph-fourier.md)'s _Théorie analytique de la chaleur_ was published in 1822. In Chapter II, article 112, he writes the [heat](heat.md) crossing a spherical surface as the product of [thermal conductivity](thermal-conductivity.md), elapsed time, surface [area](surface-area.md) and the negative radial [temperature gradient](temperature-gradient.md); article 113 balances the incoming and outgoing [heat](heat.md) to obtain the [heat equation](heat-equation.md) for a [sphere](sphere.md). The enduring idea is this connection between a local flow rule and a global prediction of changing [temperature](temperature.md). [Joseph Fourier, Théorie analytique de la chaleur, Firmin Didot, 1822](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Théorie_analytique_de_la_chaleur) [Fourier, 1822, Chapter II, articles 112–113](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Théorie_analytique_de_la_chaleur/Chapitre_2)
