<h1 id="ohm-s-law">Ohm's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm's_law)

[Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) states that an [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md) has [electric current](electric-current.md) proportional to its applied [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), with constant [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) under fixed material conditions:

$$
V=IR,\qquad I=\frac VR.
$$

Here $V=\phi(A)-\phi(B)$ is the terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), and positive $I$ enters at $A$ and exits at $B$. This sign convention makes $VI$ the electrical [power](power.md) absorbed. The straight-line relation through the origin lets one constant predict the response at many applied [electric potential differences](electric-potential-difference.md). Merely defining $R=V/I$ at one nonzero operating point does not establish [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md): a component may have a ratio that varies with applied [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). Material and [temperature](temperature-split.md) must be specified and held fixed; many components have a nonlinear response. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §9.4: Ohmic and non-Ohmic responses](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-4-ohms-law).

For the ideal [resistor](resistor.md) model $R=2\,\Omega$ at [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V=5\,\mathrm V$,

$$
I=\frac{5\,\mathrm V}{2\,\Omega}=2.5\,\mathrm A,\qquad P=VI=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}=12.5\,\mathrm W.
$$

This [power](power.md) is converted to [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) by [Joule heating](joule-heating.md); a [watt](watt.md) is a [joule](joule.md) per second. If the model holds for [time](time-in-physics.md) $10\,\mathrm s$, the transferred [energy](energy-split.md) is $125\,\mathrm J$. Doubling [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) doubles [electric current](electric-current.md) and quadruples [power](power.md) for the same fixed $R$. The model keeps material [temperature](temperature-split.md) fixed even while calculating [Joule heating](joule-heating.md); appreciable changes in [temperature](temperature-split.md) may require a changing [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md). [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §9.5: electrical power and heating](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/9-5-electrical-energy-and-power.md).

Ohm's law gives linear electric current and quadratic Joule heating power at fixed electrical resistance**. [Source](ohmic-wire-voltage-and-current.py). The ideal model holds $R=2\,\Omega$ fixed. At $V=5\,\mathrm V$, the marked values are $I=2.5\,\mathrm A$ and $P=12.5\,\mathrm W$.

In 1827, [Georg Ohm](georg-ohm.md) published his mathematical account of electrical transport in _Die galvanische Kette, mathematisch bearbeitet_. On printed page 36, his relation $S=A/L$ expresses [electric current](electric-current.md) in terms of total driving tension and a reduced [length](length.md) incorporating [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) and geometry. This is historical notation: his $A$ is not the [wire cross-sectional area](wire-cross-sectional-area.md) used here, and his $L$ is not simply the wire's physical [length](length.md). The striking idea is that material response and geometry can be combined into one quantity governing an [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit-split.md). [Ohm, Die galvanische Kette, mathematisch bearbeitet (1827), p. 36, original edition](https://archive.org/details/diegalvanischek00ohmgoog).
