# Rocket thrust

↑ **Parent:** [Rocket equation](rocket-equation.md)

[Rocket thrust](rocket-thrust.md) is the propulsive [force](force-split.md) associated with ejecting [rocket propellant](rocket-propellant.md). In the ideal [rocket equation](rocket-equation.md) with no [pressure](pressure-split.md) correction, $T=u(-\dot M)$, where $\dot M=dM/dt<0$ is the signed rate of change of rocket [mass](mass.md) and $-\dot M$ is the positive [mass loss rate](mass-loss-rate.md). For $u=2000\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $\dot M=-5\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, $T=10000\,\mathrm N$. With no other [force](force-split.md), the [acceleration](acceleration.md) is $T/M$: initially $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $M=1000\,\mathrm{kg}$, rising as [mass](mass.md) falls if this [rocket thrust](rocket-thrust.md) stays constant.

A real exhaust outlet can also contribute a [pressure](pressure-split.md) term: $T=u(-\dot M)+A_e(p_e-p_a)$, with exit [area](surface-area.md) $A_e$, exit [pressure](pressure-split.md) $p_e$ and ambient [pressure](pressure-split.md) $p_a$. The ideal formula here assumes that term vanishes; it must not silently absorb this correction while still interpreting $u$ as the actual relative [rocket exhaust speed](rocket-exhaust-speed.md). [NASA Glenn Research Center: Rocket Thrust and its pressure correction](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/rocket-thrust/)
