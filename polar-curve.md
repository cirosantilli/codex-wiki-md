# Polar curve

↑ **Parent:** [Projective plane curve](projective-plane-curve.md)

For a [homogeneous polynomial](homogeneous-polynomial.md) $F$ of degree $d$, its first polar with respect to $[a:b:c]$ is the equation $aF_x+bF_y+cF_z=0$, of degree $d-1$ when this [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) is nonzero. Every [singular point](singular-point-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) of $V(F)$ lies on every such polar. Polars turn the [Bézout's theorem](bezout-s-theorem.md) intersection count into bounds on singularities and tangencies. The equation is covariant under a linear change of [homogeneous coordinates](homogeneous-coordinate.md), by the [chain rule](chain-rule.md).
