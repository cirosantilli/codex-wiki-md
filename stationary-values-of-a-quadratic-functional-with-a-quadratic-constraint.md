# Stationary values of a quadratic functional with a quadratic constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic functional](quadratic-functional.md)

Impose zero [Dirichlet boundary conditions](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) on an interval. The [isoperimetric constraint](isoperimetric-constraint.md) $I[y]=1$ is regular because $DI_y[y]=2I[y]=2\ne0$. The [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) rule applied to $J-\lambda(I-1)$ gives the [Sturm-Liouville equation](sturm-liouville-equation.md)

$$
(py')'+qy+\lambda wy=0.
$$

Multiplying by $y$ and using [integration by parts](integration-by-parts.md) gives $J[y]=\lambda I[y]=\lambda$. Thus the multiplier is the stationary value of the original functional, or its [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md). This calculation does not require $w$ to have one sign; positive-weight spectral conclusions require additional hypotheses.

If two stationary functions have different multipliers, multiplying their equations crosswise and subtracting gives $(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)\int wy_ny_m=0$. The endpoint terms vanish. This proves the weighted [orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions](orthogonality-of-sturm-liouville-eigenfunctions.md). Higher stationary values need not be local minima of the constrained functional.
