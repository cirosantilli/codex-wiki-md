<h1 id="raoult-s-law">Raoult's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ideal solution](ideal-solution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoult's_law)

In the [ideal solution](ideal-solution.md) and [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) [vapor](vapor.md) approximation, each component's [partial pressure](partial-pressure.md) equals its [liquid](liquid.md) [mole fraction](mole-fraction.md) times its pure-component [vapor pressure](vapor-pressure.md) at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md):

$$
p_i=x_i p_i^*(T),\qquad
P=\sum_i p_i=\sum_i x_i p_i^*(T),\qquad
y_i=\frac{p_i}{P}.
$$

These [pressure](pressure-split.md) formulas neglect the change in the pure-[liquid](liquid.md) reference between its saturation [pressure](pressure-split.md) and $P$; appreciable [real gas](real-gas.md) behavior also requires a more general treatment. The [ideal solution](ideal-solution.md) definition in terms of [chemical potentials](chemical-potential-split.md) does not require either approximation.[§9.4.1, printed pp. 248–249: partial-pressure form and its distinction from the general thermodynamic law; §9.4.2: ideal liquid mixture](thermodynamics-and-chemistry-by-howard-devoe.md)

A short derivation explains the reference choice. At [vapor-liquid equilibrium](vapor-liquid-equilibrium.md), each component has the same [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) in the [liquid](liquid.md) and [vapor](vapor.md). For an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) [mixture](mixture-split.md), [chemical potential of an ideal gas](chemical-potential-of-an-ideal-gas.md) gives

$$
\mu_i^{\rm v}=\mu_i^{\rm v,\circ}(T)+RT\ln\frac{p_i}{p^\circ},
\qquad
\mu_i^*(T,p_i^*)=\mu_i^{\rm v,\circ}(T)+RT\ln\frac{p_i^*}{p^\circ},
$$

where $p^\circ$ is a reference [pressure](pressure-split.md). Approximate $\mu_i^*(T,P)$ by $\mu_i^*(T,p_i^*)$ and subtract the pure-component equation from the [mixture](mixture-split.md) equation:

$$
RT\ln x_i=RT\ln\frac{p_i}{p_i^*}.
$$

Taking the [exponential function](exponential-function.md) gives [Raoult's law](raoult-s-law.md). Summing [partial pressures](partial-pressure.md) and normalizing gives $P$ and $y_i$; this last step uses the [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) [vapor](vapor.md) assumption separately from the [liquid](liquid.md) model.

The law is named after [François-Marie Raoult](francois-marie-raoult.md), who measured [vapor pressures](vapor-pressure.md) and [freezing](freezing.md) behavior of [chemical solutions](solution-chemistry.md).[Printed p. 380, Biographical Sketch: François-Marie Raoult; experimental work on dilute solutions](thermodynamics-and-chemistry-by-howard-devoe.md)
