# Multiplicity of a root

↑ **Parent:** [Multiplicity (mathematics)](multiplicity-mathematics.md)

For a nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) over a [field](field.md), the multiplicity of a root $a$ is the largest [integer](integer-split.md) $m\geq1$ such that $(X-a)^m$ divides it. Write $p(X)=(X-a)^m q(X)$ with $q(a)\ne0$. For real [polynomials](polynomial-split.md), [continuity](continuous-function.md) keeps the sign of $q$ fixed sufficiently near $a$, so an [odd integer](odd-integer.md) $m$ makes the graph cross the axis and an [even integer](even-number.md) $m$ makes it touch without crossing.

The right-hand panel above replaces $(X-2)^2$ by $(X-2)^2-\varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon=0.04$, its two [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $1.8$ and $2.2$; for $\varepsilon=-0.04$, they are $2\pm0.2i$. The unperturbed double root lies between these two cases. Small coefficient changes can therefore split a repeated root into distinct real roots or move it off the real axis, illustrating why repeated-root problems can be numerically sensitive.
