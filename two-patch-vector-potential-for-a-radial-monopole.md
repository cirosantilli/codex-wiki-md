# Two-patch vector potential for a radial monopole

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic monopole](magnetic-monopole.md)

The radial [vector field](vector-field.md) $B=e_r/r^2$ on punctured three-dimensional [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) has flux $4\pi$ through every centered [sphere](sphere.md). It has no [vector potential](vector-potential.md) smooth on an entire enclosing sphere: the [Stokes theorem](stokes-theorem.md) on a closed surface would force zero flux. Two overlapping patches suffice. In [spherical coordinates](spherical-coordinate-system.md) take $A_+=(1-\cos\theta)e_\phi/(r\sin\theta)$, regular away from the negative $z$ axis, and $A_-=-(1+\cos\theta)e_\phi/(r\sin\theta)$, regular away from the positive $z$ axis. Their [curls](curl.md) both equal $B$.

The Cartesian forms $A_+=(-y,x,0)/[r(r+z)]$ and $A_-=(y,-x,0)/[r(r-z)]$ show that the first is smooth at the north pole and the second at the south pole. On the overlap their difference is $A_+-A_-=2\nabla\phi$, where the [azimuth](azimuth.md) $\phi$ is defined locally. Its multivaluedness around the axis explains the circulation difference $4\pi$. Splitting the sphere along a latitude, applying the [Stokes theorem](stokes-theorem.md) with $A_+$ on the north and $A_-$ on the south, and using their opposite boundary orientations recovers the total flux. A [coordinate singularity](coordinate-singularity.md) in the tangential spherical basis does not by itself make a vanishing smooth vector field singular at a pole.
