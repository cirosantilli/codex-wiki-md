# Pericentre-to-crossover flight time

↑ **Parent:** [Barker equation](barker-equation.md)

A [parabolic Kepler orbit](parabolic-trajectory.md) obeys $t=\sqrt{2q^3/(GM)}(D+D^3/3)$ with $D=\tan(f/2)$. At the [high-eccentricity angular-speed crossover](high-eccentricity-angular-speed-crossover.md), $q\ll r_x$, so $n_pt_x\simeq(\sqrt2/3)(r_x/a_p)^{3/2}=2^{7/8}(q/a_p)^{3/8}/3$. The small time fraction explains why an eccentric orbit turns through almost $\pi$ of [true anomaly](true-anomaly.md) while a distant perturber barely moves.
