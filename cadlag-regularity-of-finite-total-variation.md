<h1 id="cadlag-regularity-of-finite-total-variation">Càdlàg regularity of finite total variation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Total-variation process](total-variation-process.md)

If a [càdlàg function](cadlag.md) has [finite variation](total-variation-of-a-function.md) on compact intervals, its [total-variation process](total-variation-process.md) is finite, nondecreasing, and [càdlàg](cadlag.md). Left limits follow from monotonicity. For right continuity, approximate the variation on $[t,s]$ by one finite partition. Changing its first endpoint from $t$ to $u\downarrow t$ changes the sum by at most $|X_u-X_t|$, which tends to zero. Additivity of variation then bounds $V(u)-V(t)$ by an arbitrarily small error. Its jump is the absolute jump of $X$. Without locally finite variation, right continuity can fail even as an extended-valued assertion: $X_t=t\sin(1/t)$ with $X_0=0$ has infinite variation on every interval starting at zero.
