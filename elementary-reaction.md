# Elementary reaction

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_reaction)

An [elementary reaction](elementary-reaction.md) is one microscopic step in a [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md), with no intervening [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) needed within that step. Its [molecularity](molecularity.md) counts the participating reacting entities. In an ideal dilute, uniformly mixed model, the [law of mass action](law-of-mass-action.md) gives the directional [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) from the step's [reactant](reactant.md) [concentrations](concentration.md): $\mathrm A\longrightarrow\mathrm P$ has $r=k[\mathrm A]$, while $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\longrightarrow\mathrm P$ has $r=k[\mathrm A][\mathrm B]$. Applying those powers to a net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) requires separate evidence that it is elementary.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §12.6, Unimolecular Elementary Reactions and Bimolecular Elementary Reactions: step equations and directional rate laws](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/12-6-reaction-mechanisms.md)

For a reversible step $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\rightleftharpoons\mathrm I$, apply that rule separately: $r_f=k_f[\mathrm A][\mathrm B]$ and $r_r=k_r[\mathrm I]$, giving net production of I as $r_f-r_r$. Their difference can vanish at [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) while both directional [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) remain nonzero. For $2\mathrm A\longrightarrow\mathrm P$, the elementary normalized [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md) is $r=k[\mathrm A]^2$, whereas A's [disappearance rate](disappearance-rate.md) is $2r$; see [second-order reaction](second-order-reaction.md) for the complete factor-of-two example.

Calling a step slow does not remove the need for its actual [reactant](reactant.md) [concentrations](concentration.md). If a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) enters its [rate law](rate-law.md), determine that [concentration](concentration.md) from the other [rate equations](rate-equation.md) or a justified approximation. In the [consecutive first-order reaction mechanism](consecutive-first-order-reaction-mechanism.md), an intermediate maximum is only one instant at which its production and consumption match. Replacing its entire evolution by that one balance would lose the initial buildup and delayed [reaction product](product-chemistry.md) formation. A successful overall [first-order reaction](first-order-reaction.md) fit likewise does not establish an elementary event: both worked [reaction mechanisms](reaction-mechanism.md) above provide ways for microscopic detail to remain hidden in one [reactant](reactant.md) trace.
