# Alignment of current and field in an axisymmetric conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Oriented-axis invariant Cartesian tensor](oriented-axis-invariant-cartesian-tensor.md)

For a real [conductivity tensor](conductivity-tensor.md) invariant under every rotation about a unit vector $n$, the [electric current density](current-density.md) is $J=aE_\perp+dE_\parallel+h\,n\times E_\perp$. Its [collinearity](collinearity.md) with a nonzero real [electric field](electric-field-split.md) $E$ has three cases. Axial fields are always mapped to an axial current. Purely transverse fields give a collinear current exactly when $h=0$, equivalently when this [tensor](tensor.md) is symmetric. Fields with nonzero axial and transverse parts require both $h=0$ and $a=d$, making the [tensor](tensor.md) isotropic. The zero [vector](vector.md) is included as a scalar multiple; requiring a nonzero current also requires the relevant scalar conductivity to be nonzero.

To prove necessity, take the [dot product](dot-product.md) of $J=\lambda E$ with $n\times E_\perp$. If $E_\perp\ne0$ this gives $h=0$; the transverse and axial equations then respectively give $\lambda=a$ and, when $E_\parallel\ne0$, $\lambda=d$. Reversing these steps proves sufficiency. Rotations about the axis alone permit $h\ne0$; symmetry under a transverse half-turn would eliminate this antisymmetric term.
