## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Fix an iteration and abbreviate $x=x^k$, $s=s^k$, $d=\nabla f(x)^Ts$. Since $s$ is a [descent direction](../mathematical-optimization.md#descent-direction), $d<0$. The [directional derivative](../multivariable-calculus.md#directional-derivative) gives

$$
f(x+\alpha s)-f(x)-\beta_1\alpha d=(1-\beta_1)\alpha d+o(\alpha).
$$

Choose $\bar\alpha>0$ so that $|o(\alpha)|\leq\tfrac12(1-\beta_1)(-d)\alpha$ for $0<\alpha\leq\bar\alpha$. Then

$$
f(x+\alpha s)-f(x)-\beta_1\alpha d\leq\tfrac12(1-\beta_1)\alpha d<0.
$$

This is the [Armijo condition](../mathematical-optimization.md#armijo-condition) for every such positive [step size](../convex-optimization.md#step-size), and $\alpha=0$ gives equality. In particular, a [backtracking line search](../mathematical-optimization.md#backtracking-line-search) terminates after finitely many reductions.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write $\phi(\alpha)=f(x+\alpha s)$ and $h(\alpha)=\phi(\alpha)-\phi(0)-\beta_1\alpha d$. Part (a) gives $h(\alpha)<0$ just to the right of zero. If $L$ is a lower bound for $f$, then

$$
h(\alpha)\geq L-\phi(0)-\beta_1\alpha d\longrightarrow+\infty,
$$

because $d<0$. By [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function), there is a first positive zero $A$ of $h$, and $h(\alpha)\leq0$ on $[0,A]$. Apply the [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) to $\phi$ on this interval: for some $t\in(0,A)$,

$$
\nabla f(x+ts)^Ts=\phi'(t)=\frac{\phi(A)-\phi(0)}A=\beta_1d>\beta_2d.
$$

At this same $t$, $h(t)\leq0$ supplies the [Armijo condition](../mathematical-optimization.md#armijo-condition). Thus $\alpha=t>0$ satisfies both weak [Wolfe conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#wolfe-conditions). The boundedness assumption prevents the [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) from following its initial negative slope forever; this is the mechanism behind [Existence of a Wolfe step](../mathematical-optimization.md#existence-of-a-wolfe-step).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

If $g=\nabla f(x)\ne0$, the [positive-definite symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix) $B$ is an [invertible matrix](../linear-algebra.md#invertible-matrix) and $s=-B^{-1}g\ne0$. Multiplying $Bs=-g$ by $s^T$ gives

$$
\boxed{g^Ts=-s^TBs<0}.
$$

Hence $s$ is a [descent direction](../mathematical-optimization.md#descent-direction). If $g=0$, then $s=0$ and the algorithm has already reached a [stationary point](../calculus-of-variations.md#stationary-point); the strict descent assertion applies before termination.

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Set $p=x^{k+1}-x^k=\alpha^ks^k$ and $y=\nabla f(x^{k+1})-\nabla f(x^k)$. The [Quasi-Newton secant equation](../mathematical-optimization.md#quasi-newton-secant-equation) is $B^{k+1}p=y$. If $B^{k+1}$ is a [positive-definite symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix) and $p\ne0$, it follows that

$$
p^Ty=p^TB^{k+1}p>0.
$$

The weak curvature part of the [Wolfe conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#wolfe-conditions) ensures precisely this necessary condition:

$$
p^Ty=\alpha^k\bigl(\nabla f(x^{k+1})^Ts^k-\nabla f(x^k)^Ts^k\bigr)\geq\alpha^k(\beta_2-1)\nabla f(x^k)^Ts^k>0.
$$

The last product is positive because $\alpha^k>0$, $\beta_2-1<0$, and $s^k$ is a [descent direction](../mathematical-optimization.md#descent-direction). This explains why a [Quasi-Newton method](../mathematical-optimization.md#quasi-newton-method) often uses the [Wolfe conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#wolfe-conditions) rather than only the [Armijo condition](../mathematical-optimization.md#armijo-condition).

<h4 id="1/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/c/iii)

Interpret boundedness above uniformly: there is an $M<\infty$ such that $B^k\preceq MI$ for every $k$. Since $B^k$ is a [positive-definite symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix), the [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem) implies $(B^k)^{-1}\succeq M^{-1}I$. Therefore

$$
-\nabla f(x^k)^Ts^k=\nabla f(x^k)^T(B^k)^{-1}\nabla f(x^k)\geq\frac1M\|\nabla f(x^k)\|^2.
$$

Combining the [Armijo condition](../mathematical-optimization.md#armijo-condition) with $\alpha^k\geq C>0$ yields

$$
f(x^k)-f(x^{k+1})\geq\frac{\beta_1C}{M}\|\nabla f(x^k)\|^2.
$$

If $f\geq L$, a [telescoping sum](../arithmetic.md#telescoping-sum) gives

$$
\frac{\beta_1C}{M}\sum_{k=0}^N\|\nabla f(x^k)\|^2\leq f(x^0)-f(x^{N+1})\leq f(x^0)-L.
$$

The nonnegative [series](../sequence-and-series.md#series-mathematics) converges, so its terms tend to zero:

$$
\boxed{\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x^k)\|=0}.
$$

This proves first-order global convergence in the sense of [stationarity from uniformly bounded quasi-Newton matrices](../mathematical-optimization.md#stationarity-from-uniformly-bounded-quasi-newton-matrices). It does not imply that every nonconvex [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) reaches a [global minimum](../analysis.md#global-minimum), or that the iterates themselves converge. Every finite [limit point](../topology.md#limit-point) is a [stationary point](../calculus-of-variations.md#stationary-point), by [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient).

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [Cauchy point](../mathematical-optimization.md#cauchy-point) minimizes the model along the negative [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) ray inside the [trust region](../mathematical-optimization.md#trust-region). With $q=g^THg$, write

$$
s_C=-\tau g,\qquad m(-tg)=a-t\|g\|^2+\tfrac12t^2q,\qquad 0\leq t\leq\Delta/\|g\|.
$$

If $q\leq0$, this last [quadratic function](../polynomial.md#quadratic-function) decreases throughout the interval. If $q>0$, its unconstrained [minimum](../set.md#minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order) occurs at $t=\|g\|^2/q$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\tau=\begin{cases}\Delta/\|g\|,&g^THg\leq0,\\\min\{\Delta/\|g\|,\|g\|^2/(g^THg)\},&g^THg>0.\end{cases}}
$$

The additive constant $a$ has no effect on the [Cauchy point](../mathematical-optimization.md#cauchy-point). To obtain the [Cauchy decrease bound](../mathematical-optimization.md#cauchy-decrease-bound), first suppose $q>0$. Since $\tau q\leq\|g\|^2$,

$$
a-m(s_C)=\tau\|g\|^2-\tfrac12\tau^2q\geq\tfrac12\tau\|g\|^2.
$$

The [spectral norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#matrix-2-norm) inequality $q\leq\|H\|\|g\|^2$ gives $\tau\geq\min\{\Delta/\|g\|,1/\|H\|\}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{a-m(s_C)\geq\tfrac12\|g\|\min\{\Delta,\|g\|/\|H\|\}}.
$$

If $q\leq0$, the decrease is at least $\Delta\|g\|$, which also implies the displayed bound. When $H=0$, interpret $\|g\|/\|H\|=+\infty$. Thus the universal choice is $c=1/2$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

At $x^k$, choose $a=f(x^k)$, $g=\nabla f(x^k)$, $H=\nabla^2f(x^k)$, and the current radius $\Delta=\Delta_k>0$. A bounded [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) approximation to the [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) can also be used. Stop if the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is sufficiently small; otherwise approximately solve the [trust-region subproblem](../mathematical-optimization.md#trust-region-subproblem) to obtain $s_k$ with $\|s_k\|\leq\Delta_k$ and at least a fixed [fraction of Cauchy decrease](../mathematical-optimization.md#fraction-of-cauchy-decrease). Its predicted decrease is then positive, so calculate the [trust-region acceptance ratio](../mathematical-optimization.md#trust-region-acceptance-ratio)

$$
\rho_k=\frac{f(x^k)-f(x^k+s_k)}{m_k(0)-m_k(s_k)}.
$$

Fix $0<\eta_1<\eta_2<1$, $0<\gamma_d<1<\gamma_u$, and a maximum radius $\Delta_{\max}$. One valid update is:
- If $\rho_k<\eta_1$, reject the trial: $x^{k+1}=x^k$ and $\Delta_{k+1}=\gamma_d\Delta_k$.
- Otherwise accept $x^{k+1}=x^k+s_k$. If additionally $\rho_k>\eta_2$ and the step reaches the boundary, enlarge the radius to $\min\{\gamma_u\Delta_k,\Delta_{\max}\}$; otherwise keep it unchanged.

The [trust region](../mathematical-optimization.md#trust-region) controls the reliability of the model; the ratio decides whether that reliability was borne out by the actual [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function).

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

The [Cauchy point](../mathematical-optimization.md#cauchy-point) provides both an inexpensive fallback step and a standard against which a better step can be judged. Requiring a fixed [fraction of Cauchy decrease](../mathematical-optimization.md#fraction-of-cauchy-decrease) gives

$$
m_k(0)-m_k(s_k)\geq\frac\kappa2\|\nabla f(x^k)\|\min\{\Delta_k,\|\nabla f(x^k)\|/\|H_k\|\},\qquad\kappa>0.
$$

Consequently an exact [global minimum](../analysis.md#global-minimum) of each [trust-region subproblem](../mathematical-optimization.md#trust-region-subproblem) is unnecessary. A step from any inner solver is adequate if it meets this bound.

The convergence argument combines this decrease with model accuracy. For example, suppose the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is uniformly [Lipschitz continuous](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) on a neighborhood of the visited [level set](../topology.md#level-set), the model [Hessian matrices](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) are uniformly bounded, and the usual acceptance and radius safeguards are used. If $\|\nabla f(x^k)\|\geq\epsilon>0$, the [Taylor expansion](../calculus.md#taylor-expansion) gives a model error bounded by $K\|s_k\|^2$. For sufficiently small $\Delta_k$, division by the [Cauchy decrease bound](../mathematical-optimization.md#cauchy-decrease-bound) makes $|1-\rho_k|$ small. Such trials are successful and cannot keep shrinking the radius. With the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) bounded away from zero, the radius therefore has a positive lower bound, and each accepted step decreases $f$ by a fixed positive amount. There must be infinitely many accepted steps, since repeated rejection would force the radius below the small-radius acceptance threshold. This contradicts boundedness below of $f$ and proves $\liminf_k\|\nabla f(x^k)\|=0$.

Stronger standard conclusions can be obtained with further regularity and safeguards. Merely being twice [continuously differentiable](../multivariable-calculus.md#continuously-differentiable-function) and bounded below does not supply uniform [Hessian](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) bounds on an unbounded [level set](../topology.md#level-set); those uniform assumptions must be stated when using this argument.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Taking the [natural logarithm](../calculus.md#natural-logarithm) of the positive product constraint gives $h(x)=\sum_i a_i\log x_i=0$. Its [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) has entries $a_i/x_i>0$, so the equality [constraint qualification](../mathematical-optimization.md#constraint-qualification) holds everywhere on the [feasible set](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-region). Use the [optimization Lagrangian](../mathematical-optimization.md#optimization-lagrangian)

$$
L(x,\lambda)=\sum_i a_ix_i-\lambda\sum_i a_i\log x_i.
$$

Stationarity requires $a_i-\lambda a_i/x_i=0$, so every $x_i=\lambda$. Substitution into the constraint, using $\sum_i a_i=1$, gives $\lambda=1$. Thus the only candidate for a [local minimum](../analysis.md#local-minimum) is $x=(1,\ldots,1)$.

To prove global minimality without assuming the inequality to be established, set $u_i=\log x_i$. The constraint becomes the [affine hyperplane](../vector-space.md#affine-hyperplane) $\sum_i a_iu_i=0$, and the [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) becomes $F(u)=\sum_i a_ie^{u_i}$. Its [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) $\operatorname{diag}(a_ie^{u_i})$ is [positive-definite](../bilinear-form.md#positive-definite-bilinear-form), so $F$ is [strictly convex](../real-analysis.md#strictly-convex-function). The elementary tangent inequality $e^t\geq1+t$, with equality only at zero, gives

$$
F(u)\geq\sum_i a_i(1+u_i)=1.
$$

Equality forces every $u_i=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{x_*=(1,\ldots,1),\qquad\min\sum_i a_ix_i=1}.
$$

This point is the unique [global minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) and the only [local minimizer](../analysis.md#local-minimizer). The [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) change of variables also explains why a multiplicative constraint leads to a [convex optimization](../convex-optimization.md) argument.

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Let $G=\prod_i y_i^{a_i}>0$ be the weighted [geometric mean](../arithmetic.md#geometric-mean) and set $x_i=y_i/G$. Since the weights sum to one,

$$
\prod_i x_i^{a_i}=\frac{\prod_i y_i^{a_i}}{G^{\sum_i a_i}}=1.
$$

Part (i) implies $\sum_i a_iy_i/G\geq1$, proving the [weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#weighted-arithmetic-geometric-mean-inequality):

$$
\boxed{\prod_i y_i^{a_i}\leq\sum_i a_iy_i}.
$$

Equality holds exactly when $x_i=1$ for every $i$, or equivalently when all the $y_i$ are equal.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

If $x,y$ are [feasible points](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point) and $0\leq t\leq1$, [concavity](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) gives

$$
c_i(tx+(1-t)y)\geq tc_i(x)+(1-t)c_i(y)\geq0
$$

for every $i$. Thus the [feasible set](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-region) is a [convex set](../convex-set.md). Since the [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) is [convex](../real-analysis.md#convex-function), this is a [convex optimization](../convex-optimization.md) problem.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

With inequalities written as $c_i\geq0$, use the [optimization Lagrangian](../mathematical-optimization.md#optimization-lagrangian) $L(x,\lambda)=f(x)-\sum_i\lambda_ic_i(x)$. The [Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions) at $(x_*,\lambda_*)$ are

$$
c_i(x_*)\geq0,\quad\lambda_{*,i}\geq0,\quad\lambda_{*,i}c_i(x_*)=0,\quad\nabla f(x_*)=\sum_i\lambda_{*,i}\nabla c_i(x_*).
$$

For any [feasible point](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point) $z$, the first-order inequalities for a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) and a [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z)-f(x_*)&\geq\nabla f(x_*)^T(z-x_*)\\
&=\sum_i\lambda_{*,i}\nabla c_i(x_*)^T(z-x_*)\\
&\geq\sum_i\lambda_{*,i}\bigl(c_i(z)-c_i(x_*)\bigr)\\
&=\sum_i\lambda_{*,i}c_i(z)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality uses [complementary slackness](../linear-programming.md#complementary-slackness). Thus $x_*$ is a [global minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer). [Slater's condition](../linear-programming.md#slater-s-condition) is useful for necessity and existence of [Lagrange multipliers](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier), but is not required for this sufficiency argument once a [KKT point](../mathematical-optimization.md#kkt-point) is supplied.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

For $\mu>0$, the [logarithmic barrier](../convex-optimization.md#logarithmic-barrier) objective is

$$
\boxed{f_\mu(x)=f(x)-\mu\sum_{i=1}^p\log c_i(x)},\qquad D=\{x:c_i(x)>0\text{ for all }i\}.
$$

The [domain of a function](../function.md#domain-of-a-function) $D$ is a nonempty [convex set](../convex-set.md) by the same [concavity](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) argument as in part (a), with strict inequalities. Differentiating twice on $D$ gives

$$
\nabla^2f_\mu(x)=\nabla^2f(x)-\mu\sum_i\frac{\nabla^2c_i(x)}{c_i(x)}+\mu\sum_i\frac{\nabla c_i(x)\nabla c_i(x)^T}{c_i(x)^2}.
$$

Here $\nabla^2f\succeq0$ by [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function), $-\nabla^2c_i\succeq0$ by [concavity](../real-analysis.md#concave-function), and each [outer product](../vector-space.md#outer-product) is a [positive semidefinite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-semidefinite-matrix). Hence $\nabla^2f_\mu\succeq0$, proving [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) on $D$.

The domain qualification matters: the displayed real-valued [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) expression is not defined on all of $\mathbb R^n$. The precise whole-space version is the extended-real [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) equal to $f_\mu$ on $D$ and $+\infty$ outside $D$.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

<h5 id="3/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/d/i)

Choose a strictly [feasible point](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point), an initial barrier parameter $\mu_0>0$, and $0<\theta<1$. At outer iteration $k$, approximately minimize the [logarithmic barrier](../convex-optimization.md#logarithmic-barrier) objective $f_{\mu_k}$ over its strictly feasible [domain of a function](../function.md#domain-of-a-function). For example, use damped [Newton methods](../mathematical-optimization.md#newton-s-method-in-optimization), choosing an inner [step size](../convex-optimization.md#step-size) by [backtracking line search](../mathematical-optimization.md#backtracking-line-search) so that every $c_i$ stays positive and the barrier [objective function](../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) decreases. Solve to a tolerance $\|\nabla f_{\mu_k}(x^k)\|\leq\varepsilon_k$, where $\varepsilon_k\to0$. Set $\mu_{k+1}=\theta\mu_k$ and use $x^k$ as the next starting point.

The associated [Lagrange multiplier](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) estimates are

$$
\lambda_i^k=\frac{\mu_k}{c_i(x^k)}>0,\qquad\lambda_i^kc_i(x^k)=\mu_k.
$$

Reducing $\mu_k$ makes these perturbed [complementary slackness](../linear-programming.md#complementary-slackness) equations approach the [KKT conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions). This describes the generic [interior-point method](../convex-optimization.md#interior-point-method); existence of a solution to every inner minimization needs appropriate additional attainment assumptions and does not follow from strict feasibility alone.

<h4 id="3/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

<h5 id="3/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/d/ii)

A precise conditional convergence statement is: with the smoothness and [convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) assumptions of the question, a strict [feasible point](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point), $\mu_k\downarrow0$, and barrier stationarity residuals

$$
r_k=\nabla f(x^k)-\sum_i\lambda_i^k\nabla c_i(x^k)\longrightarrow0,
$$

every convergent sequence of strictly [feasible points](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point) $x^k\to x_*$ has a [global minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) as its [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function). The [Lagrange multiplier](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) estimates are bounded and have a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) converging to multipliers satisfying the [KKT conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions). The theorem does not assert convergence of the primal sequence without further assumptions.

First, [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) gives $c_i(x_*)\geq0$. For an inactive [optimization constraint](../numerical-analysis.md#optimization-constraint), $c_i(x_*)>0$, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda_i^k=\mu_k/c_i(x^k)\longrightarrow0}.
$$

For the [active constraints](../linear-programming.md#active-constraint), the hint permits assuming $\lambda_i^k\to\lambda_i^*$. Combining this with the inactive limits gives $\lambda_*\geq0$, and passage to the [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) in the residual equation proves stationarity. Since $\lambda_i^kc_i(x^k)=\mu_k\to0$, [complementary slackness](../linear-programming.md#complementary-slackness) also holds. All [KKT conditions](../mathematical-optimization.md#karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions) follow, and part (b) proves global optimality.

The hint's multiplier assumption can in fact be weakened to selecting a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence). To see why the estimates are bounded, choose a strict [feasible point](../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point) $z$ and put $\delta=\min_i c_i(z)>0$. [Concavity](../real-analysis.md#concave-function) gives

$$
\delta\|\lambda^k\|_1\leq\sum_i\lambda_i^kc_i(z)\leq p\mu_k+(\nabla f(x^k)-r_k)^T(z-x^k).
$$

The right side is bounded because $x^k\to x_*$ and $r_k\to0$. This is the [bounded barrier multipliers under Slater's condition](../convex-optimization.md#bounded-barrier-multipliers-under-slater-s-condition) argument. A convergent multiplier [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) then gives the same [KKT point](../mathematical-optimization.md#kkt-point), which suffices to prove that the assumed primal [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) is globally optimal.

<h4 id="3/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

<h5 id="3/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/d/iii)

For an [active constraint](../linear-programming.md#active-constraint) with $\lambda_i^*>0$, the perturbed [complementary slackness](../linear-programming.md#complementary-slackness) equation gives $c_i(x^k)\sim\mu_k/\lambda_i^*$. The [Hessian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) from part (c) therefore includes

$$
\frac{\mu_k}{c_i(x^k)^2}\nabla c_i(x^k)\nabla c_i(x^k)^T\sim\frac{(\lambda_i^*)^2}{\mu_k}\nabla c_i(x_*)\nabla c_i(x_*)^T.
$$

If $\nabla c_i(x_*)\ne0$, curvature normal to that boundary grows like $1/\mu_k$. By contrast, a direction tangent to every [active constraint](../linear-programming.md#active-constraint) annihilates these large normal [outer products](../vector-space.md#outer-product) at the limiting point, and its curvature may remain of order one. A large spread of [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) then makes the Newton linear system poorly conditioned. Under bounded remaining curvature and a positive bounded tangential curvature, the [condition number](../quadratic-form.md#condition-number) typically grows like $1/\mu_k$; this is the usual [ill-conditioning of a logarithmic barrier](../convex-optimization.md#ill-conditioning-of-a-logarithmic-barrier).

It is not a universal rate: there may be no nonzero common tangent direction, some limiting [Lagrange multipliers](../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) may vanish, or all curvatures may grow together. The [Hessian](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) formula identifies the source of the difficulty without assuming these extra nondegeneracy conditions.
