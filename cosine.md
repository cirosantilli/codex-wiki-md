# Cosine

↑ **Parent:** [Trigonometric function](trigonometric-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine)

For a real [angle](angle.md) $\theta$, $\cos\theta$ is the horizontal coordinate on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) reached by a counterclockwise [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) through $\theta$ [radians](radian.md). For an acute [angle](angle.md) in a [right triangle](right-triangle.md), it is the adjacent side divided by the [hypotenuse](hypotenuse.md). Basic values include

$$
\cos0=1,\qquad \cos(\pi/3)=\frac12,\qquad
\cos(\pi/2)=0,\qquad \cos\pi=-1.
$$

At $\theta=2\pi/3$, the horizontal coordinate is $-1/2$ even though the vertical [sine](sine.md) coordinate remains positive. A positive [angle](angle.md) need not produce a positive [cosine](cosine.md).

Reflection across the horizontal axis leaves this coordinate unchanged, giving $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$: [cosine](cosine.md) is an [even function](even-function.md). A full turn gives $\cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta$. For real arguments, its range is $[-1,1]$ and its [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) occur at $\theta=\pi/2+k\pi$. A quarter-turn shifts between the two coordinate traces, with $\cos\theta=\sin(\theta+\pi/2)$.
