# Reynolds transport theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Material derivative](material-derivative.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_transport_theorem)

For a smoothly moving volume whose boundary has normal velocity $v_b\cdot n$, the derivative of an integral equals the local time-change integral plus the boundary-transport flux. The boundary term comes from the thin layer swept by the moving surface during a short time interval. Taking $f=1$ gives $d|V|/dt=\int v_b\cdot n\,dS$. If the boundary moves with the fluid, this combines with the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) to give the integral of the [material derivative](material-derivative.md) plus $f$ times the velocity divergence.
