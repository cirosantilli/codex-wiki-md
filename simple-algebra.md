# Simple algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md)

A simple unital [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md) is nonzero and has no nonzero proper two-sided [ideals](ideal.md). Finite-dimensional complex examples are exactly the full [matrix algebras](matrix-algebra.md) $M_d(\mathbb C)$, by the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](artin-wedderburn-theorem.md). A simple [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md) need not be a [division algebra](division-algebra.md): for $d>1$ it has noninvertible nonzero matrices. Any nonzero two-sided [ideal](ideal.md) of $M_d(\mathbb C)$ contains a [matrix unit](matrix-unit.md), by multiplying a nonzero entry on the left and right by suitable [matrix units](matrix-unit.md), and therefore contains all of them.
