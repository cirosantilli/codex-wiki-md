# Divisor proof of the degree parallelogram law

↑ **Parent:** [Degree of an isogeny](degree-of-an-isogeny.md)

On the product of an [elliptic curve](elliptic-curve.md) with itself, let $D_+$ and $D_-$ be the diagonal and the graph of negation. The [principal divisor](principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) of $x(P)-x(Q)$ is $D_++D_--2(\{O\}\times E)-2(E\times\{O\})$: equality of the coordinates means $Q=\pm P$, while $x$ has a double pole at $O$. Pulling back along $(\phi,\psi)$ and taking degrees proves the [parallelogram law](parallelogram-law.md) for the [degree of an isogeny](degree-of-an-isogeny.md) when $\phi,\psi$ are nonzero and $\phi\ne\pm\psi$. The exceptional cases follow from $\deg[2]=4$. The same argument uses the quotient by negation in characteristic two. Thus degree, with degree zero assigned to the zero map, is a positive [quadratic form](quadratic-form.md) on $\operatorname{Hom}(E_1,E_2)$.
