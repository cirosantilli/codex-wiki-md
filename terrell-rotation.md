# Terrell rotation

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_rotation)

The apparent rotation of a moving object arises when [length contraction](length-contraction.md) is combined with different light emission times for photons arriving at a camera together. For an edge-on plane lamina moving along x, let X,Y be its rest coordinates and put a distant camera on the negative y axis. Simultaneous reception requires $t+Y/c=\text{constant}$. Since $x=X/\gamma+vt$, its transverse image coordinate is $X/\gamma-\beta Y$. This is the projection of a stationary lamina rotated through $\theta$ with $\cos\theta=1/\gamma$, $\sin\theta=\beta$. It describes a photographic projection; simultaneous laboratory positions retain [length contraction](length-contraction.md). The simple angle formula assumes the transverse distant-camera geometry.
