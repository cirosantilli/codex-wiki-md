# Linearly graded junction capacitance

↑ **Parent:** [Linearly graded p-n junction](linearly-graded-p-n-junction.md)

For a [linearly graded p-n junction](linearly-graded-p-n-junction.md), the signed p-side depletion charge per area is $Q_p=-e\alpha w^2/8$. Differentiating with respect to positive p-side voltage $V$, using the [linearly graded junction depletion width](linearly-graded-junction-depletion-width.md), gives the positive differential [junction capacitance](junction-capacitance.md) per area

$$
C_A=\frac{dQ_p}{dV}=\frac{\varepsilon_s}{w}
=\left[\frac{e\alpha\varepsilon_s^2}{12(V_b-V)}\right]^{1/3}.
$$

Thus $C_A^{-3}$ is linear in $V$. If stored charge denotes the positive n-side magnitude $Q_n=-Q_p$, its derivative with respect to $V$ is negative; the positive capacitance is instead $-dQ_n/dV=dQ_n/dV_R$ for reverse-voltage magnitude $V_R=-V$. Neither definition uses the zero net charge of the whole depletion layer.
