# Approximation property of a supremum

↑ **Parent:** [Supremum](supremum.md)

Let $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ be nonempty and bounded above. An [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $s$ equals $\sup A$ exactly when, for every $\varepsilon>0$, some $a\in A$ satisfies $s-\varepsilon<a\leq s$. If no such member existed, $s-\varepsilon$ would be a smaller [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Conversely, the condition defeats every proposed [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $u<s$ by taking $\varepsilon=s-u$. [Basic Analysis: Introduction to Real Analysis, Volume I by Jiří Lebl; Lebl, Basic Analysis I, §1.2: approximation of a supremum](basic-analysis-introduction-to-real-analysis-volume-i-by-jiri-lebl/1-2-the-set-of-real-numbers.md).

For $(0,1)$ and $\varepsilon=0.2$, $a=0.9$ is one witness: $0.8<0.9<1$. Choosing $a_n\in A$ with $s-1/n<a_n\leq s$ produces a [sequence](sequence.md) converging to $s$ by the [squeeze theorem](squeeze-theorem.md). The choices need not form a [monotone sequence](monotone-sequence.md).
