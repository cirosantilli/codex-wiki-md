# Prokaryote

↑ **Parent:** [Organism](organism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prokaryote)

A [prokaryote](prokaryote.md) is an [organism](organism.md) whose [biological cells](biological-cell.md) lack a membrane-bounded [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). The [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) of its [chromosomes](chromosome.md) occupies a [nucleoid](nucleoid.md) within the [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md). [Bacteria](bacteria.md) and [archaea](archaea.md) have this cellular organization. Both contain [ribosomes](ribosome.md) that perform [biological translation](translation-biology.md) and a selective [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md); neither is a bag of unorganized chemicals. See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.2: prokaryotic organization](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-2-prokaryotic-cells).

The word [prokaryotic](prokaryote.md) describes organization, not a single exclusive [clade](clade.md). Grouping [bacteria](bacteria.md) and [archaea](archaea.md) by the absence of a [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) is useful for comparing [biological cells](biological-cell.md), but does not establish that they share a more recent ancestor with one another than either does with [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md). See [three-domain system](three-domain-system.md) and [two-domain system](two-domain-system.md) for the different ancestry models.

[Prokaryotic](prokaryote.md) does not mean membrane-free. [Cyanobacteria](cyanobacteria.md) contain internal [thylakoid](thylakoid.md) membranes for [oxygenic photosynthesis](oxygenic-photosynthesis.md), despite lacking [chloroplasts](chloroplast.md) and a [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). Many [prokaryotes](prokaryote.md) have circular [chromosomes](chromosome.md), but circular shape and a single [chromosome](chromosome.md) are not defining requirements. See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 22.2: bacterial and archaeal structures](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/22-2-structure-of-prokaryotes-bacteria-and-archaea).
