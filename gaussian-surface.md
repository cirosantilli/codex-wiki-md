# Gaussian surface

↑ **Parent:** [Gauss's law](gauss-s-law.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_surface)

A [Gaussian surface](gaussian-surface.md) is a [closed surface](closed-surface.md) used with the integral form of [Gauss's law](gauss-s-law.md) to relate [electric flux](electric-flux.md) to enclosed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). It need not coincide with the surface of any charged body. Choosing it to match a [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md) can make the normal component of the [electric field](electric-field-split.md) constant on a portion of it, allowing that component to be taken outside a [surface integral](surface-integral.md). A concentric [sphere](sphere.md) works for an isolated [spherically symmetric](spherical-symmetry.md) charge distribution; an arbitrarily placed [sphere](sphere.md) generally does not.

Zero enclosed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) implies zero total [electric flux](electric-flux.md). Inferring zero [electric field](electric-field-split.md) additionally needs information such as the [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md) used in the [electric field of a uniformly charged spherical shell](electric-field-of-a-uniformly-charged-spherical-shell.md). The imagined boundary does not itself block or redirect the [electric field](electric-field-split.md).
