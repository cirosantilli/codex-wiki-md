# X-ray tube

↑ **Parent:** [X-ray](x-ray.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_tube)

An [X-ray tube](x-ray-tube.md) accelerates [electrons](electron.md) from a negative [cathode](cathode.md) to a positive target [anode](anode.md). A heated [cathode](cathode.md) supplies [electrons](electron.md) by [thermionic emission](thermionic-emission-split.md); the evacuated envelope limits collisions before the target. This differs from the [gas-discharge tube](gas-discharge-tube.md) in the [Röntgen X-ray discovery experiment](rontgen-x-ray-discovery-experiment.md).[Diagnostic Radiology Physics: A Handbook for Teachers and Students; IAEA, Chapter 5: X-Ray Production, §§5.2–5.3.2; authored teaching slides, inspected transcription](diagnostic-radiology-physics-a-handbook-for-teachers-and-students/chapter-5-x-ray-production-teaching-slides-by-k-p-maher.md)

**[Figure 5](#image-x-ray-tube-transmission-detection). From accelerated electrons to a measured X-ray transmission signal**. Original schematic of a modern heated-cathode [X-ray tube](x-ray-tube.md) and a narrow-beam measurement, not Röntgen's apparatus. [Electrons](electron.md) stop in the target; emerging [X-rays](x-ray.md) continue toward the detector. Apertures of a [collimator](collimator.md) select a small angular range and reject much scattered radiation. The two reference readings use identical geometry. Arrow widths and component sizes are schematic.

In [bremsstrahlung](bremsstrahlung.md), an [electron](electron.md) changes [velocity](velocity.md) in the target's [electric fields](electric-field-split.md), emitting a [photon](photon.md) while losing [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). Different encounters allow a continuous range of transferred [energies](energy-split.md). This is distinct from the discrete [electronic transitions](electronic-transition.md) producing [Characteristic X-rays](characteristic-x-ray.md).

Changing the accelerating [voltage](voltage.md) changes the available [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) per [electron](electron.md) and the [X-ray tube cutoff](x-ray-tube-cutoff.md). Changing the number of arriving [electrons](electron.md) changes the number of opportunities for [photon emission](photon-emission.md). These are distinct controls: a larger [electric current](electric-current.md) does not by itself give each [electron](electron.md) more [energy](energy-split.md). For an original idealized budget, $V=50\,\mathrm{kV}$ and tube [electric current](electric-current.md) $i=0.20\,\mathrm{mA}$ give electrical [power](power.md) $Vi=10\,\mathrm W$ and arrival rate $i/e\simeq1.25\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, where $e$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). This is input [power](power.md), not emitted [X-ray](x-ray.md) [power](power.md); obtaining the latter requires a conversion efficiency.
