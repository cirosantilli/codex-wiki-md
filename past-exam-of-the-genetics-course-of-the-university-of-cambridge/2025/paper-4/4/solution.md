**The prediction depends on whether effect means a phenotypic change or a [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md) advantage, and whether we sample new [mutations](../../../../mutation.md), successful substitutions at one stage, or a complete adaptive walk.** These distributions differ even in simple models. An [adaptive substitution](../../../../adaptive-substitution.md) is a change that spreads because it increases [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md) in the relevant [genetic background](../../../../genetic-background.md) and environment; its effect on a measured [phenotypic trait](../../../../phenotypic-trait.md) is a separate quantity.

[Fisher's geometric model](../../../../fisher-s-geometric-model.md) represents a [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md) as a point displaced from an optimum. As derived in question 3, a [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) is favorable when $2\mathbf z\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf a+\|\mathbf a\|^2<0$. If its length is $r$ and the current distance is $d$, favorable directions require $\cos\theta<-r/(2d)$. Thus $r>2d$ cannot improve an isotropic distance-based [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md), whereas small steps have some favorable directions. With random directions in a high-dimensional [phenotypic trait](../../../../phenotypic-trait.md) space, large steps are particularly unlikely to improve all the selected components together. This is a probability-of-benefit argument, not yet a prediction for what fixes: extremely small advantageous effects are easily lost through [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md).

During an [adaptive walk](../../../../adaptive-walk.md) toward a fixed optimum, the remaining distance shrinks. Substantial changes can be favorable early, whereas fine adjustments dominate late. In a classical treatment of this process, the distribution of magnitudes of the phenotypic factors fixed over the walk is approximately an [exponential distribution](../../../../exponential-distribution.md), with many small changes and a diminishing tail of large ones. This can reconcile a few prominent changes with numerous minor contributors; it is not the assertion that every step must be tiny.[Evolution 52, pp. 935–949; approximate distribution of factors fixed during approach to a phenotypic optimum](../../../../the-population-genetics-of-adaptation-the-distribution-of-factors-fixed-during-adaptive-evolution-by-h-allen-orr.md) The shape concerns the stated model and effect measure. A moving optimum, structured developmental effects, [standing genetic variation](../../../../standing-genetic-variation.md) or strong [epistasis](../../../../epistasis.md) can change it.

A related but distinct argument uses [extreme value theory](../../../../extreme-value-theory-split.md). If the current [genotype](../../../../genotype.md) lies high in a distribution of possible [genotype fitnesses](../../../../genotype-fitness.md), beneficial neighbors sample its upper tail. For a broad class of rapidly declining tails, excesses above a high threshold approximately follow an [exponential distribution](../../../../exponential-distribution.md). But bounded tails and heavy tails need not give the same answer; they correspond to other cases of a [generalized Pareto distribution](../../../../generalized-pareto-distribution.md). Neither an exponential tail nor the assumption of a high initial threshold is a law of nature. Also, changing the [genetic background](../../../../genetic-background.md) changes the set of available beneficial effects.

Even if the [distribution of beneficial fitness effects](../../../../distribution-of-beneficial-fitness-effects.md) among newly arising [mutations](../../../../mutation.md) is exponential at a particular stage, successful substitutions at that stage are biased toward larger advantages. In an ideal [haploid](../../../../haploid.md) population with independent reproduction and offspring-number [variance](../../../../variance-split.md) near one, let a rare new [allele](../../../../allele.md) have relative [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md) $1+s$, with $1/N_e\ll s\ll1$, and assume that it completes its fate before the next successful beneficial lineage competes with it. The usual branching approximation gives [fixation probability](../../../../fixation-probability.md) $P_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)\approx2s$. More generally, if $f_b(s)$ includes the relative arrival rates of beneficial [mutations](../../../../mutation.md),

$$
g_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)=\frac{f_b(s)P_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)}{\int_0^\infty f_b(u)P_{\mathrm{fix}}(u)\,du}.
$$

For the illustrative small-effect approximation $f_b(s)=\lambda^{-1}e^{-s/\lambda}$, this becomes

$$
\boxed{g_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)\approx\frac{s}{\lambda^2}e^{-s/\lambda},\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{fix}}[s]\approx2\lambda.}
$$

It is a [gamma distribution](../../../../gamma-distribution.md) of shape 2, not an [exponential distribution](../../../../exponential-distribution.md). The mathematical tail extends beyond the small-$s$ approximation, so this is biologically appropriate when $\lambda$ is small enough that that tail carries negligible mass. With $\lambda=0.01$, the fraction exceeding $s=0.02$ is $e^{-2}\approx0.135$ among arrivals but $3e^{-2}\approx0.406$ among substitutions in this approximation. This calculation and the pooled phenotypic-walk result answer different questions and are not contradictory.

Tests can begin with [experimental evolution](../../../../experimental-evolution.md). Start replicate [biological populations](../../../../population-biology.md) from a known ancestor, archive samples, sequence through time, and reconstruct individual [mutations](../../../../mutation.md) in the ancestral and contemporaneous [genetic backgrounds](../../../../genetic-background.md). Competitive assays against a marked reference estimate [selection coefficients](../../../../selection-coefficient.md); swap reference labels and include unmodified reconstruction controls to detect marker and manipulation costs. A short assay that directly samples one-step mutants tests available effects, while time-series substitutions test the filtering caused by [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md) and competing lineages. In particular, experiments on two viruses rejected an exponential model for the beneficial effects they sampled, showing why tail shape must be tested rather than assumed.[Journal of Molecular Evolution 67, pp. 368–376; inferred upper-tail shape for the two viral systems](../../../../beneficial-fitness-effects-are-not-exponential-for-two-viruses-by-darin-r-rokyta-and-others.md)

Natural [adaptation](../../../../adaptation.md) can be studied with [quantitative trait locus mapping](../../../../quantitative-trait-locus-mapping.md), [association mapping](../../../../association-mapping.md) and functional reconstruction. However, a mapped interval can contain several causal changes, and detection favors large effects and common [alleles](../../../../allele.md). Effect estimates from selected discoveries suffer [winner's curse](../../../../winner-s-curse.md); independent samples and [confidence intervals](../../../../confidence-interval.md) are essential. The appropriate comparison fits the predicted distribution after accounting for detection thresholds, [mutation rate](../../../../mutation-rate.md) differences and uncertainty, rather than drawing a histogram of significant hits and comparing its outline with an exponential curve. The biggest known [genetic locus](../../../../genetic-locus.md) need not be the biggest actual causal substitution.

Further obstacles include distinguishing adaptive changes from neutral passengers, measuring lifetime [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md) in the historical environment, and recovering substitutions whose effects changed through [epistasis](../../../../epistasis.md). [clonal interference](../../../../clonal-interference.md) favors large advantages when several beneficial lineages compete; [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) changes that competition. [diminishing-returns epistasis](../../../../diminishing-returns-epistasis.md) can make the same [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) less beneficial in fitter descendants. Sampling only surviving, detectable lineages misses small effects; observing a rise in [allele frequency](../../../../allele-frequency.md) does not prove eventual [allele fixation](../../../../allele-fixation.md). **Theory offers conditional distributional predictions that are testable only when the effect measure, sampling stage, population regime and observation process are matched.**
