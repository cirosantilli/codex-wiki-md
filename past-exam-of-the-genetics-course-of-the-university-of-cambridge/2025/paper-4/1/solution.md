**Visible divergence and reproductive incompatibility measure different outcomes of [evolution](../../../../evolution.md).** A few changes in a [gene regulatory network](../../../../gene-regulatory-network.md) can transform a conspicuous [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md), whereas differences in the machinery of [meiosis](../../../../meiosis.md) or [biological development](../../../../biological-development.md) can prevent reproduction without greatly changing adult appearance. Neither membership of the same genus nor morphological similarity specifies divergence time or the extent of [reproductive isolation](../../../../reproductive-isolation.md).

First separate barriers. [premating isolation](../../../../premating-isolation.md) can arise through differences in courtship, habitat or breeding season even when a forced cross produces fertile offspring. [postzygotic isolation](../../../../postzygotic-isolation.md) concerns the survival or fertility of the offspring themselves. Thus fertile captive [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md) do not refute substantial [reproductive isolation](../../../../reproductive-isolation.md) in nature: they may never encounter suitable mates, may have intermediate ecological requirements, or may be unattractive to both parental [biological populations](../../../../population-biology.md). Under the [biological species concept](../../../../biological-species-concept.md), the relevant issue is effective [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) among natural [biological populations](../../../../population-biology.md), not the success of one laboratory mating. Conversely, a laboratory failure can reflect husbandry rather than an intrinsic incompatibility.

Rapid visible divergence is especially plausible when [sexual selection](../../../../sexual-selection.md) changes plumage or other signals, or [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) changes a feeding structure. Such changes can involve a small part of the [genome](../../../../genome.md), leaving most interactions required for fertility compatible. In Darwin's finches, [genome](../../../../genome.md) comparisons associated an [ALX1](../../../../alx1.md) region with beak shape and also revealed interspecific [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md). This is an example of conspicuous adaptive differentiation coexisting with exchange of [alleles](../../../../allele.md), rather than evidence that all avian [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md) are fertile.[Results on interspecific gene flow and the ALX1 haplotype association with beak shape](../../../../evolution-of-darwin-s-finches-and-their-beaks-revealed-by-genome-sequencing-by-sangeet-lamichhaney-and-others.md) Continued [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md) can itself help maintain compatibility through much of the [genome](../../../../genome.md) while divergent [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) preserves differences at selected [genetic loci](../../../../genetic-locus.md).

For cryptic incompatibility, consider a [Dobzhansky-Muller incompatibility](../../../../dobzhansky-muller-incompatibility.md). Two lineages start with compatible [alleles](../../../../allele.md) $a,b$. One evolves $A$ in the presence of $b$, while the other evolves $B$ in the presence of $a$. Each change is tolerable in its own [genetic background](../../../../genetic-background.md), but the combination $A,B$ disrupts function in [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md). This requires neither lineage to pass through a low-fertility intermediate. Such [epistasis](../../../../epistasis.md) can affect [spermatogenesis](../../../../spermatogenesis.md), [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md) pairing, or interactions between nuclear and [mitochondrial genomes](../../../../mitochondrial-genome.md), while leaving the adult body plan nearly unchanged. The relevant changes can arise through [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md), [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md), or conflict over inheritance; adaptive divergence in external form is not required.

A particularly informative mammalian experiment concerns [PRDM9](../../../../prdm9.md), which helps specify [recombination hotspots](../../../../recombination-hotspot.md). In a particular house-mouse cross, changing its [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md)-binding region to the corresponding human sequence restored [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md) pairing and male fertility. The experiment implicates compatibility between a [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) regulator and its binding sites, rather than visible morphological divergence. Crucially, this was a rescue in a specified cross and [genetic background](../../../../genetic-background.md), not a demonstration that [PRDM9](../../../../prdm9.md) explains all mammalian [speciation](../../../../speciation.md).[Figure 1 and section “Humanizing Prdm9 restores hybrid fertility”; parental-background and engineered-allele comparisons](../../../../re-engineering-the-zinc-fingers-of-prdm9-reverses-hybrid-sterility-in-mice-by-benjamin-davies-and-others.md)

Comparative work on birds lacking [PRDM9](../../../../prdm9.md) found relatively stable [recombination hotspots](../../../../recombination-hotspot.md) associated with functional genomic features.[Science 350, pp. 928–932; maps inferred from two finch species and comparisons of hotspot conservation](../../../../stable-recombination-hotspots-in-birds-by-sonal-singhal-and-others.md) This suggests a possible difference in the routes to incompatibility: turnover of [PRDM9](../../../../prdm9.md) and its targets is unavailable in those lineages. The inference is mechanistic, however; hotspot stability by itself does not demonstrate that avian [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md) will be fertile.

Differences in [karyotypes](../../../../karyotype.md) provide another mechanism. A balanced [chromosomal rearrangement](../../../../chromosomal-rearrangement.md) can have little visible effect in either parent yet cause faulty pairing or unbalanced [gametes](../../../../gamete.md) in a [heterozygous](../../../../heterozygosity.md) [hybrid](../../../../hybrid-biology.md). However, a different [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md) count alone does not prove sterility: the arrangement and segregation behavior matter. Differences in [genomic imprinting](../../../../genomic-imprinting.md) may also disturb [placental development](../../../../placental-development.md) or embryonic growth in mammalian crosses, sometimes producing direction-dependent outcomes. Parent-of-origin growth abnormalities and disrupted [genomic imprinting](../../../../genomic-imprinting.md) in particular rodent [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md) provide evidence that this mechanism can matter.[Nature Genetics 20, pp. 362–365; reciprocal-cross growth abnormalities and allelic expression](../../../../genomic-imprinting-is-disrupted-in-interspecific-peromyscus-hybrids-by-paul-b-vrana-and-others.md) These are hypotheses to test in the relevant lineages, not a universal explanation for the bird–mammal comparison.

The sex of the offspring matters. [Haldane's rule](../../../../haldane-s-rule.md) predicts that, when one sex of first-generation [hybrids](../../../../hybrid-biology.md) is absent, rare or sterile, it tends to be the [heterogametic sex](../../../../heterogametic-sex.md): typically XY males in mammals and ZW females in birds.[Journal of Genetics 12, pp. 101–109; formulation of the heterogametic-sex pattern](../../../../sex-ratio-and-unisexual-sterility-in-hybrid-animals-by-j-b-s-haldane.md) Recessive incompatibilities on a [sex chromosome](../../../../sex-chromosome.md) can be exposed when there is no corresponding second copy; rapid [evolution](../../../../evolution.md) of reproductive functions can contribute too. Therefore a report of fertile avian males is insufficient without testing females. The rule predicts a sex bias in a given cross, not that birds as a class have weaker [reproductive isolation](../../../../reproductive-isolation.md).

To discriminate these mechanisms, compare multiple independently diverged pairs, match their estimated divergence times using [phylogenetics](../../../../phylogenetics.md), and avoid treating taxonomic rank as a clock. Perform reciprocal crosses with parental within-lineage controls; measure [fertilization](../../../../fertilization.md), embryonic survival, both sexes' fertility, and second-generation and backcross success. A fertile first generation can conceal [hybrid breakdown](../../../../hybrid-breakdown.md) after [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) disrupts coadapted combinations. Pair [karyotype](../../../../karyotype.md) analysis and observations of [meiosis](../../../../meiosis.md) with [genome](../../../../genome.md) mapping and carefully controlled rescue experiments. Field measurements of mating and ancestry then determine whether compatibility translates into [gene flow](../../../../gene-flow.md). **The contrast is explained by different rates and genetic targets of visible divergence and reproductive divergence, with sex, cross direction, ancestry and assay conditions determining the observed outcome.**
