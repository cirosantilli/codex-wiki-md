**[evolutionary developmental biology](../../../../evolutionary-developmental-biology.md) explains many novelties as new uses, arrangements or regulation of inherited components.** A new organ need not require an entirely new set of [genes](../../../../gene.md): changes to a [gene regulatory network](../../../../gene-regulatory-network.md) can deploy existing activities at a new place, time or strength during [biological development](../../../../biological-development.md). Novelty in the resulting [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md) is therefore compatible with continuity of the underlying materials.

One route is [gene co-option](../../../../gene-co-option.md). A regulator already controlling a useful cellular program can acquire a new domain of [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md), bringing downstream activities into a different structure. The important unit may be an entire interacting module, rather than a single [gene](../../../../gene.md). In the classic ectopic-eye experiment, targeted [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md) of [eyeless](../../../../eyeless.md) in developing fruit-fly tissues induced eye structures in abnormal locations. This shows that competent tissues can deploy an existing developmental program when a regulatory input changes; it does not show that a regulator alone constructs an eye independently of its cellular context.[Science 267, pp. 1788–1792; targeted expression and differentiated ectopic eye structures](../../../../induction-of-ectopic-eyes-by-targeted-expression-of-the-eyeless-gene-in-drosophila-by-georg-halder-and-others.md) Shared regulators can constitute [deep homology](../../../../deep-homology.md) even where the final structures have different architectures. Sharing one regulator, however, is not sufficient to establish that two complete organs are homologous.

[cis-regulatory evolution](../../../../cis-regulatory-evolution.md) supplies a way to change one deployment while preserving others. A [transcription factor](../../../../transcription-factor.md) may function in several tissues, giving coding changes extensive [pleiotropy](../../../../pleiotropy.md). A tissue-specific [enhancer](../../../../enhancer.md) can instead change the spatial pattern of [gene expression](../../../../gene-expression.md). In sticklebacks, recurrent deletion of a pelvic [enhancer](../../../../enhancer.md) of [Pitx1](../../../../pitx1.md) contributes to pelvic reduction while leaving other deployments of the [gene](../../../../gene.md) available. Mapping, [enhancer](../../../../enhancer.md) assays and transgenic restoration connected the regulatory region to the skeletal outcome.[Science 327, pp. 302–305; pelvic enhancer deletions, reporter activity and transgenic rescue](../../../../adaptive-evolution-of-pelvic-reduction-in-sticklebacks-by-recurrent-deletion-of-a-pitx1-enhancer-by-yingguang-frank-chan-and-others.md) This is primarily a reduction of a structure, but it demonstrates the regulatory modularity that can also enable gains or transformations. Not every [enhancer](../../../../enhancer.md) is tissue-specific, and regulatory changes can themselves have extensive [pleiotropy](../../../../pleiotropy.md).

Developmental transformations can also involve [heterochrony](../../../../heterochrony.md), a change in timing or duration, and [heterotopy](../../../../heterotopy.md), a change in location. Extending a growth phase can alter a structure's proportions; moving a signaling source can alter a pattern. Changes in [Hox genes](../../../../hox-gene.md) or their regulation can alter regional identities along the body axis, while changes in downstream targets can modify one segment without changing every segment. [serial homology](../../../../serial-homology.md) supplies repeated structures that can subsequently specialize. These routes modify inherited developmental processes, but the resulting functional organization can still be new. The size of an adult change is not a reliable measure of the number of underlying [mutations](../../../../mutation.md).

[gene duplication](../../../../gene-duplication.md) makes another form of reuse possible. Initially the copies may share a function. [subfunctionalization](../../../../subfunctionalization.md) can partition ancestral deployments between them; [neofunctionalization](../../../../neofunctionalization.md) can give one copy a new function while the other preserves an old one. Changes in regulatory connections and [protein](../../../../protein-split.md) sequence can then accumulate with fewer immediate costs. Duplication is not guaranteed to generate novelty: most extra copies may be lost or change [gene dosage](../../../../gene-dosage.md), and retaining a copy requires an account of its inheritance and effects on [genotype fitness](../../../../genotype-fitness.md). Similarly, [exaptation](../../../../exaptation.md) describes recruitment of an existing character to a new use; it is a functional historical claim, whereas [gene co-option](../../../../gene-co-option.md) names a developmental mechanism that may underlie that history.

Reuse can also generate a new biochemical function. Comparative sequence analysis traced Antarctic fish [antifreeze glycoproteins](../../../../antifreeze-glycoprotein.md) to an ancestral [trypsinogen](../../../../trypsinogen.md) [gene](../../../../gene.md): retained ancestral segments and amplification of a short sequence helped construct a new repetitive coding region.[PNAS 94, pp. 3811–3816; comparison of gene structures and reconstruction of the amplification process](../../../../evolution-of-antifreeze-glycoprotein-gene-from-a-trypsinogen-gene-in-antarctic-notothenioid-fish-by-liangbiao-chen-and-others.md) An inherited coding framework supplied material for an ice-binding protective product with a different function. This is a molecular novelty rather than a new organ, but it illustrates how reuse can build a new activity rather than merely preserve or remove an old one.

Development also changes which variants are accessible. A [gene regulatory network](../../../../gene-regulatory-network.md) can buffer many perturbations but respond sharply to a particular change, so [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) does not sample all imaginable forms equally. [developmental bias](../../../../developmental-bias.md) can make similar outcomes recur in independent lineages. [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) then filters the variants that actually arise; developmental accessibility and selective advantage are distinct explanations that can operate together. Repeated involvement of the same [gene](../../../../gene.md) might reflect its useful regulatory position, an unusually high local [mutation rate](../../../../mutation-rate.md), or inheritance of the same ancestral [allele](../../../../allele.md). Distinguishing these requires comparative and functional evidence.

The quotation should not be interpreted as denying new sequence or new function. [de novo gene birth](../../../../de-novo-gene-birth.md) can produce a newly translated product from previously [non-coding DNA](../../../../non-coding-dna.md); [mutations](../../../../mutation.md) can create a new regulatory binding site; and a genuinely new interaction can arise between old components. Such cases challenge the narrower assertion that every new [gene](../../../../gene.md) is a modified pre-existing coding [gene](../../../../gene.md). They still involve changes to existing [DNA](../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md), cellular machinery and developmental context, rather than construction without material antecedents. Failure to detect a distant sequence relative is also insufficient evidence for [de novo gene birth](../../../../de-novo-gene-birth.md), because rapid divergence and incomplete sampling can hide ancestry.

A convincing evolutionary developmental account therefore combines a [phylogenetic tree](../../../../phylogenetic-tree.md) establishing ancestral states, comparative measurements at matched developmental stages, and perturbations that connect the proposed change to the new outcome. For a candidate [enhancer](../../../../enhancer.md), compare ancestral and derived sequences in the same integration context, include an inactive construct and the normal sequence as controls, and test replacement or rescue at the endogenous [genetic locus](../../../../genetic-locus.md). A reporter alone tests regulatory potential, not the necessity of the sequence in its native context. **Novelties commonly arise by modifying inherited developmental systems, but their new organization and function are real, and the origin of new genes cannot be reduced to duplication alone.**
