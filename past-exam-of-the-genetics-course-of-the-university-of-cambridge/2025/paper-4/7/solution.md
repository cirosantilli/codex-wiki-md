<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

**[DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) preserves usable inherited information, while [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) can improve the combinations in which that information is transmitted.** Their [evolution](../../../../evolution.md) need not have the production or prevention of diversity as an ultimate goal. [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) favors consequences for survival and reproduction in particular contexts; novelty is neither automatically beneficial nor something an [organism](../../../../organism-split.md) must maximize.

[mutation](../../../../mutation.md) creates new sequence variants; [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) usually rearranges variants already present. A [chromosomal crossover](../../../../chromosomal-crossover.md) can produce a new [haplotype](../../../../haplotype.md) without changing the [allele frequency](../../../../allele-frequency.md) at either of two [genetic loci](../../../../genetic-locus.md). Conversely, [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) can restore sequence integrity after damage without eliminating the inherited differences between individuals. In particular, a normal alternative [allele](../../../../allele.md) is not an error merely because it differs from another individual's sequence. These operations address different features of inheritance.

Unrepaired damage can block [DNA replication](../../../../dna-replication.md) or [transcription](../../../../transcription-biology.md), break a [chromosome](../../../../chromosome.md), or generate a harmful [mutation](../../../../mutation.md). [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) thus has immediate benefits to a [biological cell](../../../../biological-cell-split.md) and can reduce the future burden of deleterious changes in descendants. In multicellular [organisms](../../../../organism-split.md), [somatic mutation](../../../../somatic-mutation.md) can compromise tissue function, while [germline mutation](../../../../germline-mutation.md) changes what offspring inherit. The relevant balance includes the biochemical costs of [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md), the probability and severity of damage, and the possibility of [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) errors. [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) for increased fidelity can become weak relative to [genetic drift](../../../../genetic-drift.md) when an additional improvement is extremely small; perfect fidelity need not evolve. Some error-prone damage responses can also improve immediate survival even if surviving descendants carry more [mutations](../../../../mutation.md).

The biochemical relationship is complementary. [homologous recombination](../../../../homologous-recombination.md) repairs a [DNA double-strand break](../../../../dna-double-strand-break.md) by using a related intact sequence as a template. In [meiosis](../../../../meiosis.md), programmed breaks can be repaired using a [homologous chromosome](../../../../homologous-chromosome.md), sometimes producing [chromosomal crossovers](../../../../chromosomal-crossover.md) and sometimes noncrossover products. Biochemical identification of [Spo11](../../../../spo11.md) linked a component of the meiotic machinery to programmed break formation, demonstrating that meiotic diversification can begin with a controlled DNA-damage-and-repair process.[Cell 88, pp. 375–384; purification and identification of the protein covalently associated with meiotic breaks](../../../../meiosis-specific-dna-double-strand-breaks-are-catalyzed-by-spo11-a-member-of-a-widely-conserved-protein-family-by-scott-keeney-and-others.md) Crossovers also help establish the physical connections needed for reliable segregation in many meiotic systems. This immediate transmission function can favor the machinery independently of any later benefit from a novel [haplotype](../../../../haplotype.md). [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) with [sister chromatids](../../../../sister-chromatids.md) in another context can preserve sequence without the same exchange between parental homologues.

There are also population-level benefits of reshuffling. Suppose two favorable [alleles](../../../../allele.md), $A$ and $B$, occur in different lineages as $Ab$ and $aB$. With no exchange, their lineages compete and either favorable [allele](../../../../allele.md) can be lost when the other lineage prevails. [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) can create $AB$, allowing selection to combine their benefits; it can also separate a favorable [allele](../../../../allele.md) from linked harmful changes. This is the core of the [Fisher-Muller hypothesis](../../../../fisher-muller-hypothesis.md) and relates to [Hill-Robertson interference](../../../../hill-robertson-interference.md), the reduction in the efficacy of [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) caused by associations among selected [genetic loci](../../../../genetic-locus.md). Recreating low-load [genotypes](../../../../genotype.md) can also slow [Muller's ratchet](../../../../muller-s-ratchet.md) in finite [biological populations](../../../../population-biology.md).

The bookkeeping illustrates why this is not simply increasing all diversity. Let $x_{AB},x_{Ab},x_{aB},x_{ab}$ be [haplotype frequencies](../../../../haplotype-frequency.md) and $D=x_{AB}x_{ab}-x_{Ab}x_{aB}$ be [linkage disequilibrium](../../../../linkage-disequilibrium.md). Under the standard random-mating model with [recombination fraction](../../../../recombination-fraction.md) $r$ and no other evolutionary forces,

$$
x'_{AB}=x_{AB}-rD,\qquad D'=(1-r)D.
$$

For $x_{Ab}=x_{aB}=1/2$ and the other two zero, $D=-1/4$. At $r=0.1$, the next frequencies are $x'_{AB}=x'_{ab}=0.025$ and $x'_{Ab}=x'_{aB}=0.475$. Each favorable [allele](../../../../allele.md) still has frequency $1/2$: the process creates both the favorable combination and its unfavorable counterpart. Subsequent [natural selection](../../../../natural-selection.md) determines their fates. If the original associations are already advantageous, breaking them can instead impose a [recombination load](../../../../recombination-load.md).

In controlled yeast [experimental evolution](../../../../experimental-evolution.md), sexual and asexual treatments differed in their rates and genomic routes of [adaptation](../../../../adaptation.md), with the sexual treatment more efficiently separating beneficial from deleterious changes.[Nature 531, pp. 233–236; sexual versus asexual treatments, fitness changes and mutation trajectories](../../../../sex-speeds-adaptation-by-altering-the-dynamics-of-molecular-evolution-by-michael-j-mcdonald-and-others.md) A mechanistic test should control the costs and bottlenecks of the sexual cycle, compare otherwise matched [genetic backgrounds](../../../../genetic-background.md), and use reconstructed changes to distinguish [genetic linkage](../../../../genetic-linkage.md) from intrinsically different effects of a [mutation](../../../../mutation.md). Such results establish a benefit under the tested population regime, not an unconditional advantage of sex in every environment. [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) can disrupt favorable [epistasis](../../../../epistasis.md), and the costs of mating are distinct from those of crossover formation.

For an explanation of the [evolution](../../../../evolution.md) of the machinery itself, the association with its bearer matters. A [recombination modifier](../../../../recombination-modifier.md) must on average increase the representation of its own descendants, perhaps through improved segregation or through association with combinations that selection favors. Merely increasing long-run adaptability of the [species](../../../../species.md) is insufficient. Similarly, a [mutator allele](../../../../mutator-allele.md) can spread in an asexual lineage by hitchhiking with a beneficial [mutation](../../../../mutation.md) it helped generate despite producing many harmful ones; exchange can weaken that association. The advantages of fidelity and of exchange consequently depend on [genetic linkage](../../../../genetic-linkage.md), population size, environmental change and the current supply of beneficial variants.

[DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) can regulate which exchanges are permitted as well. [DNA mismatch repair](../../../../dna-mismatch-repair.md) corrects copying errors and can reject exchange between sufficiently diverged sequences. In bacteria, disabling mismatch-repair functions greatly relaxed a barrier to [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) between divergent lineages.[Nature 342, pp. 396–401; comparison of mismatch-repair-deficient mutants with the normal recombination barrier](../../../../the-barrier-to-recombination-between-escherichia-coli-and-salmonella-typhimurium-is-disrupted-in-mismatch-repair-mutants-by-christiane-rayssiguier-and-others.md) An experiment separating these roles should compare [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) of copying errors with exchange between templates of controlled divergence, using a restored [DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) [gene](../../../../gene.md) as a rescue. **[DNA repair](../../../../dna-repair.md) and [genetic recombination](../../../../genetic-recombination.md) can therefore be parts of the same evolved solution: preserve functional sequence, transmit [chromosomes](../../../../chromosome.md) reliably, and reshuffle existing [alleles](../../../../allele.md) when doing so improves [reproductive success](../../../../reproductive-success.md).**
