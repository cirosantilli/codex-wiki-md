**Visible divergence and reproductive incompatibility measure different outcomes of [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution).** A few changes in a [gene regulatory network](../../mathematical-biology.md#gene-regulatory-network) can transform a conspicuous [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype), whereas differences in the machinery of [meiosis](../../genetics.md#meiosis) or [biological development](../../biology.md#biological-development) can prevent reproduction without greatly changing adult appearance. Neither membership of the same genus nor morphological similarity specifies divergence time or the extent of [reproductive isolation](../../biology.md#reproductive-isolation).

First separate barriers. [premating isolation](../../biology.md#premating-isolation) can arise through differences in courtship, habitat or breeding season even when a forced cross produces fertile offspring. [postzygotic isolation](../../biology.md#postzygotic-isolation) concerns the survival or fertility of the offspring themselves. Thus fertile captive [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology) do not refute substantial [reproductive isolation](../../biology.md#reproductive-isolation) in nature: they may never encounter suitable mates, may have intermediate ecological requirements, or may be unattractive to both parental [biological populations](../../ecology.md#population-biology). Under the [biological species concept](../../biology.md#biological-species-concept), the relevant issue is effective [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow) among natural [biological populations](../../ecology.md#population-biology), not the success of one laboratory mating. Conversely, a laboratory failure can reflect husbandry rather than an intrinsic incompatibility.

Rapid visible divergence is especially plausible when [sexual selection](../../biology.md#sexual-selection) changes plumage or other signals, or [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) changes a feeding structure. Such changes can involve a small part of the [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome), leaving most interactions required for fertility compatible. In Darwin's finches, [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) comparisons associated an [ALX1](../../genetics.md#alx1) region with beak shape and also revealed interspecific [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow). This is an example of conspicuous adaptive differentiation coexisting with exchange of [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele), rather than evidence that all avian [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology) are fertile.[Results on interspecific gene flow and the ALX1 haplotype association with beak shape](../../biology-bibliography.md#evolution-of-darwin-s-finches-and-their-beaks-revealed-by-genome-sequencing-by-sangeet-lamichhaney-and-others) Continued [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow) can itself help maintain compatibility through much of the [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) while divergent [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) preserves differences at selected [genetic loci](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus).

For cryptic incompatibility, consider a [Dobzhansky-Muller incompatibility](../../biology.md#dobzhansky-muller-incompatibility). Two lineages start with compatible [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele) $a,b$. One evolves $A$ in the presence of $b$, while the other evolves $B$ in the presence of $a$. Each change is tolerable in its own [genetic background](../../genetics.md#genetic-background), but the combination $A,B$ disrupts function in [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology). This requires neither lineage to pass through a low-fertility intermediate. Such [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis) can affect [spermatogenesis](../../biology.md#spermatogenesis), [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) pairing, or interactions between nuclear and [mitochondrial genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#mitochondrial-genome), while leaving the adult body plan nearly unchanged. The relevant changes can arise through [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection), [genetic drift](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-drift), or conflict over inheritance; adaptive divergence in external form is not required.

A particularly informative mammalian experiment concerns [PRDM9](../../genetics.md#prdm9), which helps specify [recombination hotspots](../../genetics.md#recombination-hotspot). In a particular house-mouse cross, changing its [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid)-binding region to the corresponding human sequence restored [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) pairing and male fertility. The experiment implicates compatibility between a [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) regulator and its binding sites, rather than visible morphological divergence. Crucially, this was a rescue in a specified cross and [genetic background](../../genetics.md#genetic-background), not a demonstration that [PRDM9](../../genetics.md#prdm9) explains all mammalian [speciation](../../biology.md#speciation).[Figure 1 and section “Humanizing Prdm9 restores hybrid fertility”; parental-background and engineered-allele comparisons](../../biology-bibliography.md#re-engineering-the-zinc-fingers-of-prdm9-reverses-hybrid-sterility-in-mice-by-benjamin-davies-and-others)

Comparative work on birds lacking [PRDM9](../../genetics.md#prdm9) found relatively stable [recombination hotspots](../../genetics.md#recombination-hotspot) associated with functional genomic features.[Science 350, pp. 928–932; maps inferred from two finch species and comparisons of hotspot conservation](../../biology-bibliography.md#stable-recombination-hotspots-in-birds-by-sonal-singhal-and-others) This suggests a possible difference in the routes to incompatibility: turnover of [PRDM9](../../genetics.md#prdm9) and its targets is unavailable in those lineages. The inference is mechanistic, however; hotspot stability by itself does not demonstrate that avian [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology) will be fertile.

Differences in [karyotypes](../../genetics.md#karyotype) provide another mechanism. A balanced [chromosomal rearrangement](../../genetics.md#chromosomal-rearrangement) can have little visible effect in either parent yet cause faulty pairing or unbalanced [gametes](../../biology.md#gamete) in a [heterozygous](../../genetics.md#heterozygosity) [hybrid](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology). However, a different [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome) count alone does not prove sterility: the arrangement and segregation behavior matter. Differences in [genomic imprinting](../../molecular-genetics.md#genomic-imprinting) may also disturb [placental development](../../biology.md#placental-development) or embryonic growth in mammalian crosses, sometimes producing direction-dependent outcomes. Parent-of-origin growth abnormalities and disrupted [genomic imprinting](../../molecular-genetics.md#genomic-imprinting) in particular rodent [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology) provide evidence that this mechanism can matter.[Nature Genetics 20, pp. 362–365; reciprocal-cross growth abnormalities and allelic expression](../../biology-bibliography.md#genomic-imprinting-is-disrupted-in-interspecific-peromyscus-hybrids-by-paul-b-vrana-and-others) These are hypotheses to test in the relevant lineages, not a universal explanation for the bird–mammal comparison.

The sex of the offspring matters. [Haldane's rule](../../biology.md#haldane-s-rule) predicts that, when one sex of first-generation [hybrids](../../genetics.md#hybrid-biology) is absent, rare or sterile, it tends to be the [heterogametic sex](../../genetics.md#heterogametic-sex): typically XY males in mammals and ZW females in birds.[Journal of Genetics 12, pp. 101–109; formulation of the heterogametic-sex pattern](../../biology-bibliography.md#sex-ratio-and-unisexual-sterility-in-hybrid-animals-by-j-b-s-haldane) Recessive incompatibilities on a [sex chromosome](../../genetics.md#sex-chromosome) can be exposed when there is no corresponding second copy; rapid [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) of reproductive functions can contribute too. Therefore a report of fertile avian males is insufficient without testing females. The rule predicts a sex bias in a given cross, not that birds as a class have weaker [reproductive isolation](../../biology.md#reproductive-isolation).

To discriminate these mechanisms, compare multiple independently diverged pairs, match their estimated divergence times using [phylogenetics](../../biology.md#phylogenetics), and avoid treating taxonomic rank as a clock. Perform reciprocal crosses with parental within-lineage controls; measure [fertilization](../../biology.md#fertilization), embryonic survival, both sexes' fertility, and second-generation and backcross success. A fertile first generation can conceal [hybrid breakdown](../../biology.md#hybrid-breakdown) after [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) disrupts coadapted combinations. Pair [karyotype](../../genetics.md#karyotype) analysis and observations of [meiosis](../../genetics.md#meiosis) with [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) mapping and carefully controlled rescue experiments. Field measurements of mating and ancestry then determine whether compatibility translates into [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow). **The contrast is explained by different rates and genetic targets of visible divergence and reproductive divergence, with sex, cross direction, ancestry and assay conditions determining the observed outcome.**

**[evolutionary developmental biology](../../biology.md#evolutionary-developmental-biology) explains many novelties as new uses, arrangements or regulation of inherited components.** A new organ need not require an entirely new set of [genes](../../genetics.md#gene): changes to a [gene regulatory network](../../mathematical-biology.md#gene-regulatory-network) can deploy existing activities at a new place, time or strength during [biological development](../../biology.md#biological-development). Novelty in the resulting [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) is therefore compatible with continuity of the underlying materials.

One route is [gene co-option](../../biology.md#gene-co-option). A regulator already controlling a useful cellular program can acquire a new domain of [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression), bringing downstream activities into a different structure. The important unit may be an entire interacting module, rather than a single [gene](../../genetics.md#gene). In the classic ectopic-eye experiment, targeted [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression) of [eyeless](../../genetics.md#eyeless) in developing fruit-fly tissues induced eye structures in abnormal locations. This shows that competent tissues can deploy an existing developmental program when a regulatory input changes; it does not show that a regulator alone constructs an eye independently of its cellular context.[Science 267, pp. 1788–1792; targeted expression and differentiated ectopic eye structures](../../biology-bibliography.md#induction-of-ectopic-eyes-by-targeted-expression-of-the-eyeless-gene-in-drosophila-by-georg-halder-and-others) Shared regulators can constitute [deep homology](../../biology.md#deep-homology) even where the final structures have different architectures. Sharing one regulator, however, is not sufficient to establish that two complete organs are homologous.

[cis-regulatory evolution](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-evolution) supplies a way to change one deployment while preserving others. A [transcription factor](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-factor) may function in several tissues, giving coding changes extensive [pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy). A tissue-specific [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) can instead change the spatial pattern of [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression). In sticklebacks, recurrent deletion of a pelvic [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) of [Pitx1](../../genetics.md#pitx1) contributes to pelvic reduction while leaving other deployments of the [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) available. Mapping, [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) assays and transgenic restoration connected the regulatory region to the skeletal outcome.[Science 327, pp. 302–305; pelvic enhancer deletions, reporter activity and transgenic rescue](../../biology-bibliography.md#adaptive-evolution-of-pelvic-reduction-in-sticklebacks-by-recurrent-deletion-of-a-pitx1-enhancer-by-yingguang-frank-chan-and-others) This is primarily a reduction of a structure, but it demonstrates the regulatory modularity that can also enable gains or transformations. Not every [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) is tissue-specific, and regulatory changes can themselves have extensive [pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy).

Developmental transformations can also involve [heterochrony](../../biology.md#heterochrony), a change in timing or duration, and [heterotopy](../../biology.md#heterotopy), a change in location. Extending a growth phase can alter a structure's proportions; moving a signaling source can alter a pattern. Changes in [Hox genes](../../genetics.md#hox-gene) or their regulation can alter regional identities along the body axis, while changes in downstream targets can modify one segment without changing every segment. [serial homology](../../biology.md#serial-homology) supplies repeated structures that can subsequently specialize. These routes modify inherited developmental processes, but the resulting functional organization can still be new. The size of an adult change is not a reliable measure of the number of underlying [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation).

[gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication) makes another form of reuse possible. Initially the copies may share a function. [subfunctionalization](../../molecular-genetics.md#subfunctionalization) can partition ancestral deployments between them; [neofunctionalization](../../molecular-genetics.md#neofunctionalization) can give one copy a new function while the other preserves an old one. Changes in regulatory connections and [protein](../../protein.md) sequence can then accumulate with fewer immediate costs. Duplication is not guaranteed to generate novelty: most extra copies may be lost or change [gene dosage](../../genetics.md#gene-dosage), and retaining a copy requires an account of its inheritance and effects on [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness). Similarly, [exaptation](../../biology.md#exaptation) describes recruitment of an existing character to a new use; it is a functional historical claim, whereas [gene co-option](../../biology.md#gene-co-option) names a developmental mechanism that may underlie that history.

Reuse can also generate a new biochemical function. Comparative sequence analysis traced Antarctic fish [antifreeze glycoproteins](../../protein.md#antifreeze-glycoprotein) to an ancestral [trypsinogen](../../protein.md#trypsinogen) [gene](../../genetics.md#gene): retained ancestral segments and amplification of a short sequence helped construct a new repetitive coding region.[PNAS 94, pp. 3811–3816; comparison of gene structures and reconstruction of the amplification process](../../biology-bibliography.md#evolution-of-antifreeze-glycoprotein-gene-from-a-trypsinogen-gene-in-antarctic-notothenioid-fish-by-liangbiao-chen-and-others) An inherited coding framework supplied material for an ice-binding protective product with a different function. This is a molecular novelty rather than a new organ, but it illustrates how reuse can build a new activity rather than merely preserve or remove an old one.

Development also changes which variants are accessible. A [gene regulatory network](../../mathematical-biology.md#gene-regulatory-network) can buffer many perturbations but respond sharply to a particular change, so [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) does not sample all imaginable forms equally. [developmental bias](../../biology.md#developmental-bias) can make similar outcomes recur in independent lineages. [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) then filters the variants that actually arise; developmental accessibility and selective advantage are distinct explanations that can operate together. Repeated involvement of the same [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) might reflect its useful regulatory position, an unusually high local [mutation rate](../../genetics.md#mutation-rate), or inheritance of the same ancestral [allele](../../genetics.md#allele). Distinguishing these requires comparative and functional evidence.

The quotation should not be interpreted as denying new sequence or new function. [de novo gene birth](../../molecular-genetics.md#de-novo-gene-birth) can produce a newly translated product from previously [non-coding DNA](../../molecular-genetics.md#non-coding-dna); [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) can create a new regulatory binding site; and a genuinely new interaction can arise between old components. Such cases challenge the narrower assertion that every new [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) is a modified pre-existing coding [gene](../../genetics.md#gene). They still involve changes to existing [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid), cellular machinery and developmental context, rather than construction without material antecedents. Failure to detect a distant sequence relative is also insufficient evidence for [de novo gene birth](../../molecular-genetics.md#de-novo-gene-birth), because rapid divergence and incomplete sampling can hide ancestry.

A convincing evolutionary developmental account therefore combines a [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree) establishing ancestral states, comparative measurements at matched developmental stages, and perturbations that connect the proposed change to the new outcome. For a candidate [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer), compare ancestral and derived sequences in the same integration context, include an inactive construct and the normal sequence as controls, and test replacement or rescue at the endogenous [genetic locus](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus). A reporter alone tests regulatory potential, not the necessity of the sequence in its native context. **Novelties commonly arise by modifying inherited developmental systems, but their new organization and function are real, and the origin of new genes cannot be reduced to duplication alone.**

[pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy) means that a single [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) or [genetic variant](../../genetics.md#genetic-variant) influences more than one distinguishable [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait). Its evolutionary consequence depends on the directions and magnitudes of those effects and on how they affect [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness). It must be distinguished from [genetic linkage](../../genetics.md#genetic-linkage), in which different causal [genetic loci](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus) travel together, and from [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis), in which the effect of one [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) depends on another. **[pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy) can constrain [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation), facilitate coordinated change, and maintain trade-offs; its sign is not intrinsically negative.**

For a small change in a [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) [vector](../../vector-space.md#vector) $\mathbf z$, write its effect as $\mathbf a$ and define the [phenotypic selection gradient](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-selection-gradient) $\boldsymbol\beta=\nabla_{\mathbf z}\log w$. A first-order [Taylor polynomial](../../calculus.md#taylor-polynomial) gives

$$
\Delta\log w\approx\boldsymbol\beta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf a.
$$

A [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) that improves one component can therefore be harmful overall if its other effects oppose sufficiently strong [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection). For an illustrative two-trait case, $\boldsymbol\beta=(1,1)$ and $\mathbf a=(0.2,-0.3)$ give $\Delta\log w\approx-0.1$. With $\mathbf a=(0.2,0.1)$, the same approximation gives $0.3$. What matters is the full effect [vector](../../vector-space.md#vector), not whether the [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) has been called “large” or “pleiotropic”. Near an optimum the first-order term can vanish, making curvature important.

This geometric constraint is explicit in [Fisher's geometric model](../../population-genetics.md#fisher-s-geometric-model). Put the optimum at the origin and let $\mathbf z$ denote the current displacement. A step $\mathbf a$ improves an isotropic quadratic measure of [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) exactly when

$$
\|\mathbf z+\mathbf a\|^2<\|\mathbf z\|^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2\mathbf z\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf a+\|\mathbf a\|^2<0.
$$

A step toward improvement in one component may damage many other components. In models where a step has random direction across many independent selected dimensions, the set of favorable directions can become restricted: this motivates a [cost of complexity](../../genetics.md#cost-of-complexity). But the prediction depends on how total step size, per-trait effect size and the number of affected [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) scale. Organismal complexity is not automatically equal to the number of dimensions affected by every [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation).

At the population level, the [multivariate breeder's equation](../../genetics.md#multivariate-breeder-s-equation) is

$$
\Delta\overline{\mathbf z}=\mathbf G\boldsymbol\beta,
$$

where $\mathbf G$ is the [additive genetic covariance matrix](../../genetics.md#additive-genetic-covariance-matrix). This is a local approximation under the relevant inheritance and weak-selection assumptions.[Evolution 33, pp. 402–416; multivariate response to selection](../../biology-bibliography.md#quantitative-genetic-analysis-of-multivariate-evolution-applied-to-brain-body-size-allometry-by-russell-lande) The off-diagonal entries cause correlated responses. For example,

$$
\mathbf G=\begin{pmatrix}1&-0.8\\-0.8&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\boldsymbol\beta=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta\overline{\mathbf z}=\begin{pmatrix}0.2\\0.2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

There is abundant [genetic variance](../../genetics.md#genetic-variance) for each [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) separately, yet their negative [genetic covariance](../../genetics.md#genetic-covariance) slows simultaneous increase. For $\boldsymbol\beta=(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$, the response instead becomes $(1.8,-1.8)^{\mathsf T}$: the same covariance facilitates this direction. A singular $\mathbf G$ can make an instantaneous response unavailable, but present covariance does not establish an eternal constraint. Moreover, [genetic linkage](../../genetics.md#genetic-linkage) can also produce [genetic covariance](../../genetics.md#genetic-covariance); estimating $\mathbf G$ alone does not demonstrate [pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy).

[antagonistic pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#antagonistic-pleiotropy) arises when effects favor different outcomes in different components of [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness), environments, sexes or life stages. An [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) that increases early reproduction but reduces late survival can be favored if its early benefit receives greater reproductive weighting. This provides a possible route to [senescence](../../biology.md#senescence), without implying that deterioration is itself selected for. Sex-specific benefits and costs can produce [sexual antagonism](../../biology.md#sexual-antagonism). Such conflicts may maintain variation under some combinations of [dominance](../../genetics.md#dominance-genetics), demography and environmental variation, but opposite effects alone do not guarantee a protected [polymorphism](../../genetics.md#polymorphism). The invasion success of each alternative when rare must be checked.

[pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy) can also promote [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation) by coordinating changes that work well together. A developmental regulator can change a group of interacting structures; a single physiological change can improve several measures of performance. [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) may favor [developmental modularity](../../biology.md#developmental-modularity), tissue-specific regulation or [gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication) when they reduce costly coupling. The pelvic [enhancer](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) example in question 2 illustrates how [cis-regulatory evolution](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-evolution) can localize an effect; it does not establish a universal privilege of regulatory over coding [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation).

Empirical measurement requires care. Screens of yeast, nematode and mouse mutants found strongly modular relationships between [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) and measured [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait), challenging assumptions of universal equal-effect [pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy).[Results on the degree of pleiotropy and modularity; Figures 1–3](../../biology-bibliography.md#genomic-patterns-of-pleiotropy-and-the-evolution-of-complexity-by-zhi-wang-and-others) A deletion can nevertheless have more severe and widespread effects than a naturally segregating regulatory [allele](../../genetics.md#allele); unmeasured environments and [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) hide effects, whereas multiple correlated measurements can inflate apparent dimensionality. To test causal [pleiotropy](../../genetics.md#pleiotropy), introduce the same precise [genetic variant](../../genetics.md#genetic-variant) into matched [genetic backgrounds](../../genetics.md#genetic-background), measure multiple prespecified [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) across environments and life stages, and use recombinant or edited lines to exclude a neighboring causal [genetic locus](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus). Assay [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) as well as convenient laboratory [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait). **The evolutionary constraint is the alignment between available heritable effects and current selection, not the mere count of affected [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait).**

**The prediction depends on whether effect means a phenotypic change or a [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) advantage, and whether we sample new [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation), successful substitutions at one stage, or a complete adaptive walk.** These distributions differ even in simple models. An [adaptive substitution](../../population-genetics.md#adaptive-substitution) is a change that spreads because it increases [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) in the relevant [genetic background](../../genetics.md#genetic-background) and environment; its effect on a measured [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) is a separate quantity.

[Fisher's geometric model](../../population-genetics.md#fisher-s-geometric-model) represents a [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) as a point displaced from an optimum. As derived in question 3, a [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) is favorable when $2\mathbf z\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf a+\|\mathbf a\|^2<0$. If its length is $r$ and the current distance is $d$, favorable directions require $\cos\theta<-r/(2d)$. Thus $r>2d$ cannot improve an isotropic distance-based [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness), whereas small steps have some favorable directions. With random directions in a high-dimensional [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) space, large steps are particularly unlikely to improve all the selected components together. This is a probability-of-benefit argument, not yet a prediction for what fixes: extremely small advantageous effects are easily lost through [genetic drift](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-drift).

During an [adaptive walk](../../population-genetics.md#adaptive-walk) toward a fixed optimum, the remaining distance shrinks. Substantial changes can be favorable early, whereas fine adjustments dominate late. In a classical treatment of this process, the distribution of magnitudes of the phenotypic factors fixed over the walk is approximately an [exponential distribution](../../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution), with many small changes and a diminishing tail of large ones. This can reconcile a few prominent changes with numerous minor contributors; it is not the assertion that every step must be tiny.[Evolution 52, pp. 935–949; approximate distribution of factors fixed during approach to a phenotypic optimum](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-population-genetics-of-adaptation-the-distribution-of-factors-fixed-during-adaptive-evolution-by-h-allen-orr) The shape concerns the stated model and effect measure. A moving optimum, structured developmental effects, [standing genetic variation](../../population-genetics.md#standing-genetic-variation) or strong [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis) can change it.

A related but distinct argument uses [extreme value theory](../../extreme-value-theory.md). If the current [genotype](../../genetics.md#genotype) lies high in a distribution of possible [genotype fitnesses](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness), beneficial neighbors sample its upper tail. For a broad class of rapidly declining tails, excesses above a high threshold approximately follow an [exponential distribution](../../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution). But bounded tails and heavy tails need not give the same answer; they correspond to other cases of a [generalized Pareto distribution](../../extreme-value-theory.md#generalized-pareto-distribution). Neither an exponential tail nor the assumption of a high initial threshold is a law of nature. Also, changing the [genetic background](../../genetics.md#genetic-background) changes the set of available beneficial effects.

Even if the [distribution of beneficial fitness effects](../../population-genetics.md#distribution-of-beneficial-fitness-effects) among newly arising [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) is exponential at a particular stage, successful substitutions at that stage are biased toward larger advantages. In an ideal [haploid](../../genetics.md#haploid) population with independent reproduction and offspring-number [variance](../../variance.md) near one, let a rare new [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) have relative [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) $1+s$, with $1/N_e\ll s\ll1$, and assume that it completes its fate before the next successful beneficial lineage competes with it. The usual branching approximation gives [fixation probability](../../genetics.md#fixation-probability) $P_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)\approx2s$. More generally, if $f_b(s)$ includes the relative arrival rates of beneficial [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation),

$$
g_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)=\frac{f_b(s)P_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)}{\int_0^\infty f_b(u)P_{\mathrm{fix}}(u)\,du}.
$$

For the illustrative small-effect approximation $f_b(s)=\lambda^{-1}e^{-s/\lambda}$, this becomes

$$
\boxed{g_{\mathrm{fix}}(s)\approx\frac{s}{\lambda^2}e^{-s/\lambda},\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{fix}}[s]\approx2\lambda.}
$$

It is a [gamma distribution](../../continuous-probability-distribution.md#gamma-distribution) of shape 2, not an [exponential distribution](../../continuous-probability-distribution.md#exponential-distribution). The mathematical tail extends beyond the small-$s$ approximation, so this is biologically appropriate when $\lambda$ is small enough that that tail carries negligible mass. With $\lambda=0.01$, the fraction exceeding $s=0.02$ is $e^{-2}\approx0.135$ among arrivals but $3e^{-2}\approx0.406$ among substitutions in this approximation. This calculation and the pooled phenotypic-walk result answer different questions and are not contradictory.

Tests can begin with [experimental evolution](../../biology.md#experimental-evolution). Start replicate [biological populations](../../ecology.md#population-biology) from a known ancestor, archive samples, sequence through time, and reconstruct individual [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) in the ancestral and contemporaneous [genetic backgrounds](../../genetics.md#genetic-background). Competitive assays against a marked reference estimate [selection coefficients](../../population-genetics.md#selection-coefficient); swap reference labels and include unmodified reconstruction controls to detect marker and manipulation costs. A short assay that directly samples one-step mutants tests available effects, while time-series substitutions test the filtering caused by [genetic drift](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-drift) and competing lineages. In particular, experiments on two viruses rejected an exponential model for the beneficial effects they sampled, showing why tail shape must be tested rather than assumed.[Journal of Molecular Evolution 67, pp. 368–376; inferred upper-tail shape for the two viral systems](../../biology-bibliography.md#beneficial-fitness-effects-are-not-exponential-for-two-viruses-by-darin-r-rokyta-and-others)

Natural [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation) can be studied with [quantitative trait locus mapping](../../genetics.md#quantitative-trait-locus-mapping), [association mapping](../../genetics.md#association-mapping) and functional reconstruction. However, a mapped interval can contain several causal changes, and detection favors large effects and common [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele). Effect estimates from selected discoveries suffer [winner's curse](../../statistical-inference.md#winner-s-curse); independent samples and [confidence intervals](../../statistical-inference.md#confidence-interval) are essential. The appropriate comparison fits the predicted distribution after accounting for detection thresholds, [mutation rate](../../genetics.md#mutation-rate) differences and uncertainty, rather than drawing a histogram of significant hits and comparing its outline with an exponential curve. The biggest known [genetic locus](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus) need not be the biggest actual causal substitution.

Further obstacles include distinguishing adaptive changes from neutral passengers, measuring lifetime [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) in the historical environment, and recovering substitutions whose effects changed through [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis). [clonal interference](../../population-genetics.md#clonal-interference) favors large advantages when several beneficial lineages compete; [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) changes that competition. [diminishing-returns epistasis](../../genetics.md#diminishing-returns-epistasis) can make the same [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) less beneficial in fitter descendants. Sampling only surviving, detectable lineages misses small effects; observing a rise in [allele frequency](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency) does not prove eventual [allele fixation](../../genetics.md#allele-fixation). **Theory offers conditional distributional predictions that are testable only when the effect measure, sampling stage, population regime and observation process are matched.**

**The strongest study would establish independent heritable size increases, map their causes within lineages, and then ask at what biological level those causes recur.** Similar adult sizes alone do not establish independent [adaptive substitutions](../../population-genetics.md#adaptive-substitution), and a repeated change in the same [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) need not be the same causal [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation).

Begin with dense sampling of island and mainland relatives across the [clade](../../biology.md#clade), including small-bodied island lineages where available. Reconstruct a [phylogenetic tree](../../biology.md#phylogenetic-tree) from many genomic regions, estimate colonization relationships, and compare body-size histories with uncertainty in ancestral states. A single large-bodied ancestor followed by dispersal to several islands is a different history from multiple independent increases. Discordant local trees require attention to [incomplete lineage sorting](../../biology.md#incomplete-lineage-sorting) and [introgression](../../biology.md#introgression). Record island area, resource availability, predators, competitors and colonization history, because these can explain why [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) favored size, or why some apparent replicates share the same historical cause. The number of independently evolved shifts, rather than the number of animals sampled, determines replication for a claim about [convergent evolution](../../biology.md#convergent-evolution).

Define the [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) carefully. Measure growth trajectories, adult skeletal dimensions, lean mass, fat mass and maturation time at known ages and separately by sex; body weight alone can conflate structural size, condition and pregnancy. Measure individuals blind to ancestry. Rear descendants from several independent families in a [common garden experiment](../../genetics.md#common-garden-experiment), randomizing cages and controlling diet, density, temperature and pathogen exposure. Include more than one rearing environment to test [genotype-environment interaction](../../genetics.md#genotype-environment-interaction). Reciprocal crosses and cross-fostering can help distinguish inherited [genetic variants](../../genetics.md#genetic-variant) from maternal provisioning and early rearing effects. Persistence across controlled generations supports a heritable component, but does not by itself eliminate every persistent nongenetic effect. Field pedigrees and [heritability](../../genetics.md#heritability) estimates can complement, rather than substitute for, these controls.

Generate comparable [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) data. High-quality assemblies from representative island and mainland animals help detect [structural variants](../../genetics.md#structural-variant) and [gene duplication](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-duplication); population resequencing estimates [allele frequencies](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency) and [haplotypes](../../genetics.md#haplotype). Mapping every lineage only to a mainland reference can preferentially miss island sequence, so use alternative references or a [pangenome](../../molecular-genetics.md#pangenome) where feasible and compare mapping quality across groups. Sequence both sexes and retain [sex chromosomes](../../genetics.md#sex-chromosome) and [mitochondrial genomes](../../molecular-genetics.md#mitochondrial-genome) when technically possible. Design sampling and coverage using a [statistical power](../../probability-and-statistics.md#statistical-power) analysis, with multiple unrelated individuals per lineage and independent cohorts for replication; one [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome) per island is inadequate for within-population inference.

Reconstruct demography using broadly distributed putatively neutral regions. Island founding can create a [population bottleneck](../../population-genetics.md#population-bottleneck), increased [inbreeding](../../population-genetics.md#inbreeding) and long-range [linkage disequilibrium](../../population-genetics.md#linkage-disequilibrium). These processes can imitate aspects of a [selective sweep](../../population-genetics.md#selective-sweep). Compare candidate regions with null simulations that reproduce plausible colonization sizes, [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow), [recombination rates](../../genetics.md#recombination-rate) and sequencing ascertainment; propagate uncertainty in those quantities. High [FST](../../population-genetics.md#fixation-index) can reflect reduced within-population diversity rather than an exceptional accumulation of adaptive differences, so examine absolute divergence and diversity alongside it. A high ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous changes can also reflect relaxed [purifying selection](../../biology.md#purifying-selection), especially after a decline in [effective population size](../../population-genetics.md#effective-population-size). No single outlier statistic identifies a body-size mechanism.

Where fertile crosses are possible, perform [quantitative trait locus mapping](../../genetics.md#quantitative-trait-locus-mapping) using island–mainland intercrosses, with enough recombinants to resolve several causal regions. Measure longitudinal growth and test sex-dependent effects, [dominance](../../genetics.md#dominance-genetics) and [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis), while controlling family and shared rearing. Natural [association mapping](../../genetics.md#association-mapping) can help when large samples segregate relevant [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele); account for [population stratification](../../genetics.md#population-stratification) and relatedness with an appropriate [Gaussian linear mixed model](../../generalized-linear-model.md#gaussian-linear-mixed-model). Fixed island–mainland differences cannot be mapped within an island where they no longer vary, and a [genome-wide association study](../../genetics.md#genome-wide-association-study) that simply contrasts [species](../../biology.md#species) confounds ancestry with body size. As an empirical precedent, Gough Island mouse intercrosses identified multiple regions affecting body weight and growth rate, illustrating that rapid extreme size [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) can have a distributed genetic basis.[Genetics 201, pp. 213–228; intercross design and separate body-weight and growth-rate QTL analyses](../../biology-bibliography.md#genetics-of-rapid-and-extreme-size-evolution-in-island-mice-by-melissa-m-gray-and-others) These regions are starting points for resolution, not automatically individual causal [genes](../../genetics.md#gene).

Integrate mapping with measurements in relevant tissues and developmental windows. [RNA sequencing](../../molecular-genetics.md#rna-sequencing) of growth plate, muscle or endocrine tissue may identify candidate changes in [gene expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#gene-expression), but match developmental stage, sex, diet and cellular composition so that a larger animal's different tissue mixture is not mistaken for a regulatory cause. [allele-specific expression](../../molecular-genetics.md#allele-specific-expression) in an informative first-generation cross can distinguish some effects of [cis-regulatory evolution](../../molecular-genetics.md#cis-regulatory-evolution) from shared trans-acting influences, provided reference-mapping bias is controlled. Test accessible [enhancers](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) or coding changes suggested jointly by mapping, sequence and tissue evidence. Growth pathways are reasonable hypotheses, but discovering a familiar growth-related annotation is not causal validation and should not exclude unexpected [genetic loci](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus).

Resolve the level of convergence explicitly. Independent lineages might share the same new [nucleotide substitution](../../genetics.md#nucleotide-substitution), different changes in the same [gene](../../genetics.md#gene), different [genes](../../genetics.md#gene) in one pathway, or many changes distributed across a [polygenic trait](../../genetics.md#polygenic-trait). Shared long [haplotypes](../../genetics.md#haplotype) and local ancestry can reveal reuse through [introgression](../../biology.md#introgression) or [standing genetic variation](../../population-genetics.md#standing-genetic-variation). Distinguish these from recurrent [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation) using local genealogies, surrounding sequence and age estimates. For pathway enrichment, control [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) length, annotation density, [mutation rate](../../genetics.md#mutation-rate), [genetic linkage](../../genetics.md#genetic-linkage) and multiple testing; a shared [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) annotation is not an independent replicate for every linked site. Failure to find repeated individual hits does not exclude convergence at a broader biological level.

Finally, replace candidate [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele) or [enhancers](../../molecular-genetics.md#enhancer) in controlled [genetic backgrounds](../../genetics.md#genetic-background), ideally in both directions, and measure their effects on growth and its costs. Use unedited siblings, sham-edited lines, independent edited founders and a rescue or precise reversion to address manipulation and off-target effects. Test combinations if mapping indicates [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis). Cell assays or another model [species](../../biology.md#species) can establish biochemical activity, but need not reproduce the original mammal's whole-body effect. To call the change adaptive, also connect size and the candidate [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele) to survival and [reproductive success](../../biology.md#reproductive-success) under relevant island conditions; mainland and island environments provide a useful reciprocal comparison. **A persuasive result connects independent history, heritable size, specific causal variants and [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) consequences, while allowing convergence to involve different genes or reuse of ancestral variation.**

[phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) is the capacity of a [genotype](../../genetics.md#genotype) to produce different [phenotypes](../../genetics.md#phenotype) in different environments. It can change an individual's performance within a generation, before any change in [allele frequency](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency). **[phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) can buy time for evolutionary [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation) and generate new targets for selection, but an induced response is neither necessarily beneficial nor evidence of inherited evolutionary change.**

A [reaction norm](../../genetics.md#reaction-norm) makes the distinction precise. Write an individual's [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) as $z=a+bC$, where $C$ is an environmental cue and the [genotype](../../genetics.md#genotype) helps determine the intercept $a$ and responsiveness $b$. Differences in $a$ represent different baseline [phenotypic traits](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait); differences in $b$ represent genetic variation in sensitivity, a form of [genotype-environment interaction](../../genetics.md#genotype-environment-interaction). If the [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) maximizing [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) in the later selective environment $E$ is $z^*=kE$, an informative cue can move individuals nearer that optimum immediately. Appropriate changes in developmental timing or physiology can therefore prevent demographic collapse and preserve the variation and numbers needed for subsequent [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection).

The importance of cue reliability follows from a simple model. Suppose [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) loss is proportional to squared mismatch, the cue and environment have finite second moments, and we ignore the costs of responding. Minimizing $\mathbb E[(a+bC-kE)^2]$ over $a,b$, with $\operatorname{Var}(C)>0$, gives

$$
\boxed{b^*=k\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(E,C)}{\operatorname{Var}(C)},\qquad
a^*=k\mathbb E[E]-b^*\mathbb E[C].}
$$

This is a model calculation, not a universal optimal [reaction norm](../../genetics.md#reaction-norm). It shows why reliable prediction of the selective environment can favor responsiveness, whereas an uninformative cue gives $b^*=0$. A response evolved under one cue–environment relation can become harmful when that relation changes. Temporal delays, irreversible [biological development](../../biology.md#biological-development), limited response speed and energetic costs further constrain the advantage. Stress-induced loss of growth, for example, is not evidence of adaptive [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) unless it improves an appropriate measure of [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) relative to a feasible alternative.

[phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) can affect later [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) in several ways. It can expose previously unexpressed [genetic variation](../../genetics.md#genetic-variation) when a novel environment changes developmental pathways. It can let a lineage enter a new habitat where [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) then improves performance. It can also reduce the mismatch so effectively that [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) on the underlying constitutive [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) weakens, slowing genetic change. Even maladaptive initial [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) can intensify [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) for compensatory change, provided the [biological population](../../ecology.md#population-biology) survives. Thus a rapid phenotypic response, rapid genetic [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation) and long-term diversification are three distinct outcomes, not interchangeable measures of success.

[genetic accommodation](../../biology.md#genetic-accommodation) is evolutionary change in an environmentally responsive [phenotypic trait](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-trait) or its regulation. It can change the mean, threshold, magnitude or specificity of the response and can preserve or increase [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity). [genetic assimilation](../../biology.md#genetic-assimilation) is the particular case in which a formerly induced [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) becomes expressed without the original inducing condition. Neither process requires the environment to write a beneficial change directly into the inherited [DNA](../../polymer.md#deoxyribonucleic-acid); [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) or imposed breeding can sort existing and newly arising variation affecting the response. Assimilation need not occur when environments continue to fluctuate, because maintaining a responsive [reaction norm](../../genetics.md#reaction-norm) may remain advantageous.

The classic experimental demonstration selected fruit flies for a wing-vein [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) induced by heat treatment, eventually obtaining expression without that treatment.[Evolution 7, pp. 118–126; heat-induced crossveinless phenotype and selection for its expression](../../biology-bibliography.md#genetic-assimilation-of-an-acquired-character-by-conrad-hal-waddington) The key observation is the changed relation between environment and [phenotype](../../genetics.md#phenotype) across selected generations. It demonstrates the feasibility of [genetic assimilation](../../biology.md#genetic-assimilation), not that losing the vein was an adaptive response in nature, nor that acquired bodily modifications were directly copied into the [genome](../../molecular-genetics.md#genome). A modern replication should include heat-exposed unselected lines, untreated lines, replicated selection lines, controlled rearing and [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) assays, and should distinguish segregating variation from a new major-effect [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation).

For diversification, [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) can permit use of several resources or produce alternative specialist forms. Repeated exposure to a new resource can increase the frequency with which one form is expressed, giving [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) more opportunity to refine it. In spadefoot tadpoles, experiments compared the competitive performance of an induced carnivore form among [biological populations](../../ecology.md#population-biology) differing in how often they produce it; the association between expression frequency and competitive refinement supports one prediction of plasticity-led [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution).[Proceedings of the Royal Society B 286, article 20182754; carnivore-frequency comparisons and competition experiments](../../biology-bibliography.md#plasticity-led-evolution-evaluating-the-key-prediction-of-frequency-dependent-adaptation-by-nicholas-a-levis-and-david-w-pfennig) Such comparisons do not directly observe the ancestor or establish that every difference arose after induction rather than from prior genetic divergence.

Alternative forms can lead to [ecological speciation](../../biology.md#ecological-speciation) if specialization causes habitat-associated mating, divergent [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection), and reduced [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow). A mating preference coupled to the adopted habitat could help turn ecological divergence into [premating isolation](../../biology.md#premating-isolation). But the opposite outcome is also plausible: one flexible [genotype](../../genetics.md#genotype) can succeed in both habitats, reducing divergent [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) and maintaining [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow). A difference in [reaction norms](../../genetics.md#reaction-norm) is consequently not itself evidence of [speciation](../../biology.md#speciation). A plastic radiation can persist without producing new [species](../../biology.md#species), and a radiation can occur through ordinary heritable divergence with little contribution from [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity).

Testing the causal historical claim requires more than comparing present-day induced and constitutive forms. Use related lineages and explicit ancestral-state uncertainty, raise multiple [genotypes](../../genetics.md#genotype) in crossed environments, and measure both [reaction norms](../../genetics.md#reaction-norm) and [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness). Split-family rearing controls ancestry; cross-fostering and controlled generations help identify maternal carry-over; experimental manipulation of the cue separates induction from resource quality. Replicate [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) with and without access to the cue and quantify survival, subsequent genetic response and reproductive barriers. An extant sister lineage is an imperfect proxy for an ancestor, and correlations between [phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) and [species](../../biology.md#species) richness can reflect reverse causation or ecological opportunity. **[phenotypic plasticity](../../genetics.md#phenotypic-plasticity) contributes most convincingly when a demonstrated cue-dependent [genotype fitness](../../population-genetics.md#genotype-fitness) benefit precedes heritable refinement, and any proposed diversification is independently connected to reduced [gene flow](../../biology.md#gene-flow).**

**[DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) preserves usable inherited information, while [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) can improve the combinations in which that information is transmitted.** Their [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) need not have the production or prevention of diversity as an ultimate goal. [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) favors consequences for survival and reproduction in particular contexts; novelty is neither automatically beneficial nor something an [organism](../../organism.md) must maximize.

[mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) creates new sequence variants; [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) usually rearranges variants already present. A [chromosomal crossover](../../genetics.md#chromosomal-crossover) can produce a new [haplotype](../../genetics.md#haplotype) without changing the [allele frequency](../../genetics.md#allele-frequency) at either of two [genetic loci](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus). Conversely, [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) can restore sequence integrity after damage without eliminating the inherited differences between individuals. In particular, a normal alternative [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) is not an error merely because it differs from another individual's sequence. These operations address different features of inheritance.

Unrepaired damage can block [DNA replication](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-replication) or [transcription](../../molecular-genetics.md#transcription-biology), break a [chromosome](../../genetics.md#chromosome), or generate a harmful [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation). [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) thus has immediate benefits to a [biological cell](../../biological-cell.md) and can reduce the future burden of deleterious changes in descendants. In multicellular [organisms](../../organism.md), [somatic mutation](../../genetics.md#somatic-mutation) can compromise tissue function, while [germline mutation](../../genetics.md#germline-mutation) changes what offspring inherit. The relevant balance includes the biochemical costs of [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair), the probability and severity of damage, and the possibility of [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) errors. [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) for increased fidelity can become weak relative to [genetic drift](../../population-genetics.md#genetic-drift) when an additional improvement is extremely small; perfect fidelity need not evolve. Some error-prone damage responses can also improve immediate survival even if surviving descendants carry more [mutations](../../genetics.md#mutation).

The biochemical relationship is complementary. [homologous recombination](../../genetics.md#homologous-recombination) repairs a [DNA double-strand break](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-double-strand-break) by using a related intact sequence as a template. In [meiosis](../../genetics.md#meiosis), programmed breaks can be repaired using a [homologous chromosome](../../genetics.md#homologous-chromosome), sometimes producing [chromosomal crossovers](../../genetics.md#chromosomal-crossover) and sometimes noncrossover products. Biochemical identification of [Spo11](../../genetics.md#spo11) linked a component of the meiotic machinery to programmed break formation, demonstrating that meiotic diversification can begin with a controlled DNA-damage-and-repair process.[Cell 88, pp. 375–384; purification and identification of the protein covalently associated with meiotic breaks](../../biology-bibliography.md#meiosis-specific-dna-double-strand-breaks-are-catalyzed-by-spo11-a-member-of-a-widely-conserved-protein-family-by-scott-keeney-and-others) Crossovers also help establish the physical connections needed for reliable segregation in many meiotic systems. This immediate transmission function can favor the machinery independently of any later benefit from a novel [haplotype](../../genetics.md#haplotype). [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) with [sister chromatids](../../genetics.md#sister-chromatids) in another context can preserve sequence without the same exchange between parental homologues.

There are also population-level benefits of reshuffling. Suppose two favorable [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele), $A$ and $B$, occur in different lineages as $Ab$ and $aB$. With no exchange, their lineages compete and either favorable [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) can be lost when the other lineage prevails. [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) can create $AB$, allowing selection to combine their benefits; it can also separate a favorable [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) from linked harmful changes. This is the core of the [Fisher-Muller hypothesis](../../genetics.md#fisher-muller-hypothesis) and relates to [Hill-Robertson interference](../../population-genetics.md#hill-robertson-interference), the reduction in the efficacy of [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) caused by associations among selected [genetic loci](../../genetics.md#genetic-locus). Recreating low-load [genotypes](../../genetics.md#genotype) can also slow [Muller's ratchet](../../population-genetics.md#muller-s-ratchet) in finite [biological populations](../../ecology.md#population-biology).

The bookkeeping illustrates why this is not simply increasing all diversity. Let $x_{AB},x_{Ab},x_{aB},x_{ab}$ be [haplotype frequencies](../../genetics.md#haplotype-frequency) and $D=x_{AB}x_{ab}-x_{Ab}x_{aB}$ be [linkage disequilibrium](../../population-genetics.md#linkage-disequilibrium). Under the standard random-mating model with [recombination fraction](../../genetics.md#recombination-fraction) $r$ and no other evolutionary forces,

$$
x'_{AB}=x_{AB}-rD,\qquad D'=(1-r)D.
$$

For $x_{Ab}=x_{aB}=1/2$ and the other two zero, $D=-1/4$. At $r=0.1$, the next frequencies are $x'_{AB}=x'_{ab}=0.025$ and $x'_{Ab}=x'_{aB}=0.475$. Each favorable [allele](../../genetics.md#allele) still has frequency $1/2$: the process creates both the favorable combination and its unfavorable counterpart. Subsequent [natural selection](../../biology.md#natural-selection) determines their fates. If the original associations are already advantageous, breaking them can instead impose a [recombination load](../../genetics.md#recombination-load).

In controlled yeast [experimental evolution](../../biology.md#experimental-evolution), sexual and asexual treatments differed in their rates and genomic routes of [adaptation](../../biology.md#adaptation), with the sexual treatment more efficiently separating beneficial from deleterious changes.[Nature 531, pp. 233–236; sexual versus asexual treatments, fitness changes and mutation trajectories](../../biology-bibliography.md#sex-speeds-adaptation-by-altering-the-dynamics-of-molecular-evolution-by-michael-j-mcdonald-and-others) A mechanistic test should control the costs and bottlenecks of the sexual cycle, compare otherwise matched [genetic backgrounds](../../genetics.md#genetic-background), and use reconstructed changes to distinguish [genetic linkage](../../genetics.md#genetic-linkage) from intrinsically different effects of a [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation). Such results establish a benefit under the tested population regime, not an unconditional advantage of sex in every environment. [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) can disrupt favorable [epistasis](../../genetics.md#epistasis), and the costs of mating are distinct from those of crossover formation.

For an explanation of the [evolution](../../biology.md#evolution) of the machinery itself, the association with its bearer matters. A [recombination modifier](../../genetics.md#recombination-modifier) must on average increase the representation of its own descendants, perhaps through improved segregation or through association with combinations that selection favors. Merely increasing long-run adaptability of the [species](../../biology.md#species) is insufficient. Similarly, a [mutator allele](../../genetics.md#mutator-allele) can spread in an asexual lineage by hitchhiking with a beneficial [mutation](../../genetics.md#mutation) it helped generate despite producing many harmful ones; exchange can weaken that association. The advantages of fidelity and of exchange consequently depend on [genetic linkage](../../genetics.md#genetic-linkage), population size, environmental change and the current supply of beneficial variants.

[DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) can regulate which exchanges are permitted as well. [DNA mismatch repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-mismatch-repair) corrects copying errors and can reject exchange between sufficiently diverged sequences. In bacteria, disabling mismatch-repair functions greatly relaxed a barrier to [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) between divergent lineages.[Nature 342, pp. 396–401; comparison of mismatch-repair-deficient mutants with the normal recombination barrier](../../biology-bibliography.md#the-barrier-to-recombination-between-escherichia-coli-and-salmonella-typhimurium-is-disrupted-in-mismatch-repair-mutants-by-christiane-rayssiguier-and-others) An experiment separating these roles should compare [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) of copying errors with exchange between templates of controlled divergence, using a restored [DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) [gene](../../genetics.md#gene) as a rescue. **[DNA repair](../../molecular-genetics.md#dna-repair) and [genetic recombination](../../genetics.md#genetic-recombination) can therefore be parts of the same evolved solution: preserve functional sequence, transmit [chromosomes](../../genetics.md#chromosome) reliably, and reshuffle existing [alleles](../../genetics.md#allele) when doing so improves [reproductive success](../../biology.md#reproductive-success).**
