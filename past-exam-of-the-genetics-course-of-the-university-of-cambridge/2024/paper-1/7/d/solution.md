<h1 id="7/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the regime where salt promotes [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) condensation by screening repulsion between DNA-rich surfaces, reducing salt from 0.15 M to 0.1 M weakens the effective attraction. In the schematic upper-critical model this means a lower critical temperature and a smaller coexistence region; at a fixed suitable temperature the dilute-phase [saturation concentration](../../../../../saturation-concentration.md) rises. The second curve is therefore drawn inside and below the first. The magnitude of the shift is not specified by the question.

This direction has a microscopic basis: reducing ionic strength increases the range of electrostatic repulsion between negatively charged [DNA](../../../../../deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) segments. Modelling of interacting arrays supports a role for screening and [nucleosome](../../../../../nucleosome.md) contacts in stabilizing chromatin-rich phases.[Salt-dependent coexistence and the role of nucleosome breathing](../../../../../nucleosome-plasticity-is-a-critical-element-of-chromatin-liquid-liquid-phase-separation-and-multivalent-nucleosome-interactions-by-stephen-e-farr-and-others.md)

In the idealized limit of unscreened DNA-DNA repulsion, the ordinary condensed phase can disappear, rather than simply acquiring an arbitrarily small positive critical temperature. However, **zero added NaCl is not literally zero counterions**: charged [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) requires charge compensation, and buffer ions and counterions associated with the preparation remain. Salt also screens attractive interactions, and very high salt can alter [nucleosome](../../../../../nucleosome.md) stability. The proposed shift applies to the stated low-salt screening regime, not a universal monotonic law at every salt [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md).
