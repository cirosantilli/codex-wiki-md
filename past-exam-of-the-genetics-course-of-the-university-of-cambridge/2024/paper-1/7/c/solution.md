<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At fixed volume and temperature, equilibrium minimizes the [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md) $F=U-TS$. Mixing increases translational [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md), whereas favourable contacts between [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) segments can lower the interaction contribution to [internal energy](../../../../../internal-energy-split.md). At low enough temperature, favourable contacts can compensate the [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) lost on concentrating the arrays; at higher temperature, the mixing contribution can dominate. This gives the upper-critical dome assumed in (a).

A simple illustrative [Flory–Huggins solution theory](../../../../../flory-huggins-solution-theory.md) writes the dimensionless mixing free energy per site as

$$
g(\phi)=\frac{\phi}{N}\ln\phi+(1-\phi)\ln(1-\phi)+\chi(T)\phi(1-\phi),\qquad \chi(T)=A/T.
$$

Here $N$ is an effective polymer size, not automatically the number 12, and $A>0$ represents an effective energetic incompatibility of polymer and solvent. The [common-tangent construction](../../../../../common-tangent-construction.md) determines coexisting compositions. Solving $g''=g^{(3)}=0$ gives

$$
\boxed{\phi_c=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt N},\qquad \chi_c=\frac12(1+N^{-1/2})^2.}
$$

Since $\chi$ decreases on heating, the coexistence interval narrows and closes at $T_c=A/\chi_c$. For fixed pressure the corresponding potential is [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md), with the enthalpic term in $G=H-TS$; enthalpy and [internal energy](../../../../../internal-energy-split.md) should not be interchanged without stating the mechanical constraint. Hydration, ion release or temperature-dependent effective interactions can change this simple temperature trend.
