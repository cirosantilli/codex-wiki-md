↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A useful schematic is the [upper critical solution temperature](../../../../../upper-critical-solution-temperature.md) form of a [binodal](../../../../../binodal.md): a dome in temperature versus [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md). Below the dome, an intermediate overall [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) separates into a dilute phase and a chromatin-rich phase; above it the liquid is homogeneous. At a fixed temperature, the two intersections of a horizontal tie line give the compositions of the coexisting phases, not the [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md) of two different initial samples.

The diagram is a **model, not measured phase-boundary data**. The stated observation at one salt [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) does not by itself establish whether heating suppresses or promotes demixing. A [lower critical solution temperature](../../../../../lower-critical-solution-temperature.md) system would instead demix on heating, and would need the corresponding inverted geometry. The following answers make the usual enthalpically favourable-demixing assumption explicit.
