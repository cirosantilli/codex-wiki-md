<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [saturation concentration](../../../../../saturation-concentration.md) $c_{\rm sat}(T)$ is the dilute-phase coexistence [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) at the specified temperature and solution conditions. On increasing the overall [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) through this boundary, a macroscopic condensed phase can first coexist with the dilute phase. The other branch gives the dense-phase [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) $c_{\rm dense}(T)$.

At the [thermodynamic critical point](../../../../../thermodynamic-critical-point.md), the two branches meet: their [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) difference vanishes. Its coordinates define the [critical concentration](../../../../../critical-concentration.md) $c_c$ and critical temperature $T_c$. In the upper-critical model, $T>T_c$ admits no two-liquid coexistence. Thus **$c_{\rm sat}$ is a temperature-dependent branch, whereas $c_c$ is the [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) at the endpoint**.

Between coexistence [concentrations](../../../../../concentration.md), the [lever rule](../../../../../lever-rule.md) gives the dense-phase [volume fraction](../../../../../volume-fraction.md) $v=(\bar c-c_{\rm sat})/(c_{\rm dense}-c_{\rm sat})$, assuming consistent [concentration](../../../../../concentration.md) and volume definitions. Increasing total [chromatin](../../../../../chromatin.md) then primarily changes phase amounts rather than allowing the dilute phase to increase without bound.
