↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**Susceptibility of an isolated bacterium and failure to clear an infection measure different things.** The scenario does not establish a particular resistance gene or a real species-specific mechanism. First distinguish [antibiotic resistance](../../../../antibiotic-resistance.md), an increased ability to grow at inhibitory drug [concentrations](../../../../concentration.md), from [antibiotic tolerance](../../../../antibiotic-tolerance.md), slower killing without the corresponding increase in the growth-inhibiting [concentration](../../../../concentration.md), and [bacterial persistence](../../../../bacterial-persistence.md), survival of a subpopulation in an otherwise susceptible population.

One explanation is a reversible physiological state. Slowly growing or metabolically altered [bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) can survive an [antibiotic](../../../../antibiotic.md) whose lethal action depends on the active process it targets. Regrown survivors can then recover the original susceptibility. [Nathalie Q. Balaban](../../../../nathalie-q-balaban.md) and colleagues directly followed individual [bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) and showed switching between growth states associated with persistence. The important inference is phenotypic heterogeneity, not that all survivors must share one dormant state or one toxin-antitoxin pathway.[Single-cell observations and susceptibility after regrowth](../../../../bacterial-persistence-as-a-phenotypic-switch-by-nathalie-q-balaban-and-others.md)

A second explanation is the local infection environment. [Biofilms](../../../../biofilm.md) can generate heterogeneous growth conditions, altered penetration and protected reservoirs; intracellular localization can separate [bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) from otherwise adequate extracellular drug exposure. Nutrient limitation, oxygen conditions, pH and host stress can change physiology or drug activity. Conversely, a laboratory sample may miss a resistant minority, mixed infection or an inducible [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md), so apparent susceptibility should not be treated as proof that every bacterium at every site has the same response.

Triggers can include changes imposed by the host environment, entry into a slow-growth state, or stress associated with prior exposure. These are alternative hypotheses: the question supplies no data selecting one. [Bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) surviving because exposure never reached an effective level are also different from [bacteria](../../../../bacteria.md) surviving an adequately delivered exposure. Persistence may pre-exist exposure rather than being induced by it.

At the level of investigation, compare inhibition of growth with survival over time, examine whether the [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md) persists after regrowth, and assess population heterogeneity and protected infection sites. A high-level response to treatment failure is to identify the reservoir and exposure problem, establish which organism and [phenotype](../../../../phenotype.md) are actually present, and consider approaches addressing that mechanism rather than assuming that simply increasing the same drug will solve it. Removal of a colonized foreign-body reservoir, where relevant, addresses a different cause from a change in antimicrobial choice. The information supplied is insufficient to name an effective drug regimen or infer a single molecular trigger.
