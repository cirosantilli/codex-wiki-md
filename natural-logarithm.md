# Natural logarithm

↑ **Parent:** [Logarithm](logarithm.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_logarithm)

The [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) $\ln x$ is the [logarithm](logarithm.md) with base [Euler's number](e-mathematical-constant.md), and the [inverse function](inverse-function.md) of the real [exponential function](exponential-function.md). A particularly geometric definition is

$$
\ln x=\int_1^x\frac{dt}{t},\qquad x>0.
$$

For $x>1$, it is the [area](surface-area.md) under the [hyperbola](hyperbola.md) $y=1/t$ from $1$ to $x$. For $0<x<1$, the reversed [integration](integral-split.md) limits give a negative value. The [fundamental theorem of calculus](fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) gives $(\ln x)'=1/x$, so the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) is increasing, while $(\ln x)''=-1/x^2$ gives [concavity](concave-function.md).

<a id="image-exponential-function-and-natural-logarithm-graphs-reflect-across-y-x-equal-multiplicative-intervals-have-equal-logarithmic-areas"></a>
![](exponential-logarithm-geometry.png)

**[Figure 8](#image-exponential-function-and-natural-logarithm-graphs-reflect-across-y-x-equal-multiplicative-intervals-have-equal-logarithmic-areas). Exponential function and natural logarithm graphs reflect across $y=x$; equal multiplicative intervals have equal logarithmic areas**.

The [change of variables](change-of-variables-formula.md) $t=au$ shows $\int_a^{ab}dt/t=\int_1^bdu/u=\ln b$ for $a,b>0$. Thus the intervals from $1$ to $2$ and from $2$ to $4$ each contribute [area](surface-area.md) $\ln2$, explaining $\ln4=2\ln2$. Equal ratios give equal increments on a [logarithmic scale](logarithmic-scale.md), even though the ordinary differences grow. This makes the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md) useful for comparing multiplicative changes and solving for time in [exponential growth](exponential-growth.md) or [exponential decay](exponential-decay.md). The [integral](integral-split.md) construction and [inverse function](inverse-function.md) properties are discussed in [OpenStax, the natural logarithm as an integral](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/6-7-integrals-exponential-functions-and-logarithms).
