# Respiratory control ratio

↑ **Parent:** [Respiratory control](respiratory-control.md)

The [respiratory control ratio](respiratory-control-ratio.md) divides the [oxygen](oxygen.md) consumption rate during [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md)-stimulated respiration by that after supplied [ADP](adenosine-diphosphate.md) has been used. It compares [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md), not [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) formed per [oxygen](oxygen.md).[pp. 386–387, Figure 2 and discussion of the ratio of respiration rates with and without ADP](respiratory-enzymes-in-oxidative-phosphorylation-i-kinetics-of-oxygen-utilization-by-britton-chance-and-g-r-williams.md)

A large ratio means strong stimulation under the comparison's conditions; it is not a universal measure of all mitochondrial functions.

For a constructed example, choose constant suspension [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) $V=2\,\mathrm{mL}$ and [oxygen](oxygen.md) [concentration](concentration.md) declines of $0.030$ and $0.006\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}\,min^{-1}}$ during the two comparison windows. Assume no gas exchange or dilution and a correctly calibrated readout. A [derivative](derivative.md) converts each [concentration](concentration.md) slope into an [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) consumption rate:

$$
v=-V\frac{dc_{\mathrm O_2}}{dt},\qquad
v_{\rm ADP}=60\,\mathrm{nmol\,min^{-1}},\qquad
v_{\rm after}=12\,\mathrm{nmol\,min^{-1}},\qquad
\mathrm{RCR}=5.
$$

The common [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) cancels in the ratio, so dividing the positive slope magnitudes gives the same answer. These are chosen inputs, not the 1955 observations. If an uncorrected influx of $3\,\mathrm{nmol\,min^{-1}}$ of [oxygen](oxygen.md) occurred in both windows, the apparent rates would instead be $57$ and $9$, giving $19/3\approx6.33$. The same absolute error can inflate a ratio because it removes a larger fraction of its smaller denominator.
