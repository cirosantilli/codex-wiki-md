# Axial vector of an antisymmetric second-rank tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Antisymmetric second-rank tensor](antisymmetric-second-rank-tensor.md)

In three-dimensional Euclidean space, contracting an [antisymmetric second-rank tensor](antisymmetric-second-rank-tensor.md) with the [Levi-Civita symbol](levi-civita-symbol.md) gives an [axial vector](pseudovector.md). The epsilon contraction identity proves the inverse formula: $\frac12\epsilon_{kij}\epsilon_{klm}A_{lm}=\frac12(A_{ij}-A_{ji})=A_{ij}$. With this index convention,

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_3&-a_2\\-a_3&0&a_1\\a_2&-a_1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Ax=x\times a.
$$

Thus $A$ is minus the [cross-product matrix](cross-product-matrix.md) for $a\times x$. Proper rotations transform $a$ as a vector; an orientation-reversing orthogonal change adds a determinant sign. Stating the index convention prevents a sign ambiguity when converting an antisymmetric tensor into a cross-product operator.
