# Lateral stability of a spring-supported charge in a wedge

↑ **Parent:** [Image charges in a right-angle conducting wedge](image-charges-in-a-right-angle-conducting-wedge.md)

The electrostatic bisector [force](force-split.md) is laterally anti-restoring, with the positive stiffness coefficient on the right. A [spring](spring.md) of tension $T$ and [length](length.md) $\ell$ adds transverse restoring stiffness $T/\ell$ when its fixed support is directly above the charge. The displayed inequality therefore distinguishes actual transverse stability from the saddle of a model with only a vertical [spring](spring.md) [force](force-split.md). Axial [spring](spring.md) stiffness alone does not specify the transverse verdict. At fixed finite support geometry and positive gravitational load, sufficiently small [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) preserves lateral stability: the destabilizing coefficient tends to zero as $q^2$, while the tension term approaches $mg/\ell>0$. The vertical [equilibrium](equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) must separately satisfy its own positive-stiffness condition.
