# Sudden hydrogenic charge change

↑ **Parent:** [Sudden approximation](sudden-approximation.md)

In the [sudden approximation](sudden-approximation.md), changing the nuclear charge of a one-electron [hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md) from $Z_i e$ to $Z_f e$ leaves its electronic state unchanged during the change. Its subsequent probability to occupy a final state is the squared overlap with that state. Using normalized $1s$ [wavefunctions](wave-function-split.md) $\psi_Z=(\beta_Z^3/\pi)^{1/2}e^{-\beta_Zr}$, $\beta_Z=Z/a_0$, gives

$$
\langle1s,Z_f|1s,Z_i\rangle=\frac{8(\beta_i\beta_f)^{3/2}}{(\beta_i+\beta_f)^3},\qquad \boxed{P_{1s}=\frac{64(Z_iZ_f)^3}{(Z_i+Z_f)^6}.}
$$

This follows by integrating $4(\beta_i\beta_f)^{3/2}r^2e^{-(\beta_i+\beta_f)r}$ over positive $r$. A spherically symmetric initial state has zero overlap with every final state of nonzero [orbital angular momentum](orbital-angular-momentum.md), by the [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md) of the [spherical harmonics](spherical-harmonic.md). Nuclear recoil and interactions with emitted particles can invalidate the perfectly spherical sudden-change model.
