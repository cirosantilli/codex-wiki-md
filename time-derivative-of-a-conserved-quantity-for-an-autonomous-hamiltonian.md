# Time derivative of a conserved quantity for an autonomous Hamiltonian

↑ **Parent:** [Hamiltonian conservation law](hamiltonian-conservation-law.md)

For a time-independent [Hamiltonian](hamiltonian.md) $H$, the explicit time [partial derivative](partial-derivative.md) of a smooth time-dependent [conserved quantity](conserved-quantity.md) is again a [conserved quantity](conserved-quantity.md). Here conservation means the identity $D_tf=f_t+\{f,H\}=0$ on [phase space](phase-space.md), with the standard [Poisson bracket](poisson-bracket.md). Differentiating this identity at fixed canonical coordinates gives

$$
f_{tt}+\{f_t,H\}+\{f,H_t\}=0.
$$

Because $H_t=0$, this is exactly $D_t(f_t)=0$, proving the assertion. Iteration gives the same result for all higher explicit time [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) for which the differentiations are defined. By [Hamiltonian energy balance](hamiltonian-energy-balance.md), requiring $H$ itself to be a [conserved quantity](conserved-quantity.md) on all trajectories is equivalent to $H_t=0$.
