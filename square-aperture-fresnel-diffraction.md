# Square-aperture Fresnel diffraction

↑ **Parent:** [Fresnel diffraction](fresnel-diffraction.md)

For an axial point source a distance $p$ before a square aperture and an observer a distance $q$ beyond it, source and observation [wave phase](phase-waves.md) curvatures add: $R_{\rm eff}^{-1}=p^{-1}+q^{-1}$. With aperture side $d$, put $w=(d/2)\sqrt{2/(\lambda R_{\rm eff})}$. Separation into two one-dimensional [Fresnel integrals](fresnel-integral.md) gives the field normalized to its unobscured value,

$$
\frac{\psi}{\psi_0}=-2i[C(w)+iS(w)]^2,\qquad
\frac{I}{I_0}=4[C(w)^2+S(w)^2]^2.
$$

At an off-axis observer $(X,Y)$, centre the dimensionless coordinates on $x_0=pX/(p+q)$ and $y_0=pY/(p+q)$. If $D_x,D_y$ are the directed [Cornu spiral](euler-spiral.md) chords between the corresponding aperture-edge parameters, $\psi/\psi_0=D_xD_y/(2i)$. This is a product of complex interval factors, rather than a sum of two intensities. The unobscured field is evaluated at the same observation point and includes its common propagation [wave phase](phase-waves.md).
