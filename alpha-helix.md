# Alpha helix

↑ **Parent:** [Protein secondary structure](protein-secondary-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_helix)

An [alpha helix](alpha-helix.md) is a coiled [protein secondary structure](protein-secondary-structure.md) with a repeated pattern of [backbone hydrogen bonds](backbone-hydrogen-bond.md). In the standard pattern, the backbone group of [amino acid residue](amino-acid-residue.md) $i$ bonds to that of residue $i+4$: for example, positions 2 and 6. This is an additional spatial interaction, not a replacement [peptide bond](peptide-bond.md) between those positions. Pauling, Corey and Branson proposed the helical arrangement in their 1951 paper by reasoning from the geometry of small [molecules](molecule.md). See [Pauling, Corey and Branson, The Structure of Proteins: Two Hydrogen-Bonded Helical Configurations of the Polypeptide Chain, PNAS 37, 205–211 (1951)](https://williams.chemistry.gatech.edu/course_Information/6572/papers/pauling_1951_helix.pdf).
