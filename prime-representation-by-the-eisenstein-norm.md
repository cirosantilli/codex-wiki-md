# Prime representation by the Eisenstein norm

↑ **Parent:** [Eisenstein norm](eisenstein-norm.md)

Let $p\geq3$ be a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) dividing $n^2+3$, and set $\omega=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2$. The case $p=3$ is $N(1-\omega)=3$. For $p>3$, use

$$
n^2+3=(n+1+2\omega)(n-1-2\omega).
$$

The [Eisenstein integer](eisenstein-integer.md) $p$ divides this product, but divides neither factor: divisibility by the rational integer $p$ in the [basis](basis.md) $1,\omega$ requires both integer coefficients to be divisible by $p$, whereas the second coefficients are $2$ and $-2$. Thus $p$ is not a [prime element](prime-element.md) of $\mathbb Z[\omega]$.

The [Euclidean norm on the Eisenstein integers](euclidean-norm-on-the-eisenstein-integers.md) makes this ring a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md). Since [irreducible elements of a principal ideal domain are prime](irreducible-elements-of-a-principal-ideal-domain-are-prime.md), $p$ admits a factorization $p=uv$ into two nonunits. The positive integral [Eisenstein norm](eisenstein-norm.md) is multiplicative, and its value is one exactly on [units](unit-in-a-ring.md): when $N(u)=1$, its [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) is an inverse. Thus $N(u),N(v)>1$ and $N(u)N(v)=p^2$, forcing both norms to be $p$. Writing $u=a+b\omega$ proves the displayed representation.
