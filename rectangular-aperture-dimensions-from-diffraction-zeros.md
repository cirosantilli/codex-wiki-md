# Rectangular-aperture dimensions from diffraction zeros

↑ **Parent:** [Fraunhofer diffraction](fraunhofer-diffraction.md)

A uniformly illuminated rectangle of width $w$ and height $h$ has field proportional to $\operatorname{sinc}(q_xw/2)\operatorname{sinc}(q_yh/2)$, with angular spatial [wavenumbers](wavenumber.md) $q_x,q_y$ measured in inverse length. The first envelope zeros give $w=2\pi/|q_{x,0}|$ and $h=2\pi/|q_{y,0}|$. Two identical rectangles with horizontal centre separation $s$ introduce an additional $\cos(q_xs/2)$ factor, so the interference period is $\Delta q_x=2\pi/s$.

The factors $2\pi$ depend on using angular spatial [frequency](frequency.md); axes in cycles per length would use the reciprocal widths directly. Fourier [light intensity](light-intensity.md) alone cannot determine an overall aperture translation or distinguish a mask from its inverted copy. Extra assumptions about shape and coherent illumination are necessary for a unique geometrical reconstruction.
