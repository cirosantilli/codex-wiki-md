<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Put the plate behind the slit at transverse coordinate $+d/2$, and let positive screen coordinate $x$ point toward that slit. The extra [optical path length](../../../optical-path-length.md) is

$$
\Delta=(n_1-1)t=0.52(200\,\mu\mathrm m)=104\,\mu\mathrm m.
$$

In the [paraxial approximation](../../../paraxial-approximation.md), the covered path minus the uncovered path has total [optical path difference](../../../optical-path-difference.md) $\Delta-dx/L$. Its zero determines the shifted central [interference fringe](../../../interference-fringe.md):

$$
\boxed{x_0=\frac{L(n_1-1)t}{d}=1.04\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm m=10.4\,\mathrm{mm},}
$$

**toward the slit covered by the plate**. Moving toward that slit shortens its geometric path and compensates the extra delay in the plate.

More generally, the [phase difference](../../../phase-difference.md) is $(2\pi/\lambda)(\Delta-dx/L)$, so every [interference fringe](../../../interference-fringe.md) translates by the same $x_0$ in this approximation. The spacing $\lambda L/d$ is unchanged, and the shift corresponds to $\Delta/\lambda$ fringe periods. For an ideal uniform transparent plate that changes only the [wave phase](../../../phase-waves.md), the envelope and [interferometric visibility](../../../interferometric-visibility.md) are unchanged. Reflection losses or unequal transmission through a real plate could additionally reduce the [interferometric visibility](../../../interferometric-visibility.md); those are separate amplitude effects. No numerical [wavelength](../../../wavelength.md) is needed for the physical displacement of the zero-order fringe.
