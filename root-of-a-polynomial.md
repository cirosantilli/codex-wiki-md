# Root of a polynomial

↑ **Parent:** [Zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md)

A root of a polynomial $f$ is a scalar $a$ satisfying $f(a)=0$ in the chosen [field](field.md) or [ring](ring.md). For $p(X)=(X+1)(X-2)^2$ over the [real numbers](real-number.md), the distinct [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $-1$ and $2$. The second has [multiplicity of a root](multiplicity-of-a-root.md) two, so there are three roots when counted with multiplicity.

The domain matters. $X^2+1$ has no real [polynomial root](root-of-a-polynomial.md), but has the two [complex number](complex-number.md) roots $i,-i$. The [fundamental theorem of algebra](fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) guarantees a [polynomial root](root-of-a-polynomial.md) in the [complex numbers](complex-number.md) for every nonconstant complex [polynomial](polynomial-split.md), and complete factorization counts its [degree of a polynomial](degree-of-a-polynomial.md) many roots with [multiplicity of a root](multiplicity-of-a-root.md). It does not promise that those roots are real or that they have a simple expression.
