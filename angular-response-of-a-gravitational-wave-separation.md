# Angular response of a gravitational-wave separation

↑ **Parent:** [TT coordinate separation and measured separation](tt-coordinate-separation-and-measured-separation.md)

For a short pair of stationary test particles in [transverse-traceless gauge](transverse-traceless-gauge.md), spatial coordinates stay fixed while the measured [proper length](proper-length.md) changes. In background signature $+---$, let $h_{xx}=-h_{yy}=H_+$ and $h_{xy}=H_\times$. A coordinate separation of length $\Delta$ at angle $\theta$ has

$$
\ell^2=\Delta^2[1-H_+\cos(2\theta)-H_\times\sin(2\theta)],\qquad
\frac{\delta\ell}{\Delta}=-\frac12[H_+\cos(2\theta)+H_\times\sin(2\theta)]+O(H^2).
$$

The [plus polarization](plus-polarization.md) and [cross polarization](cross-polarization.md) differ by a $45$-degree rotation of their principal axes. With background signature $-+++$ and the same numerical spatial perturbation, the sign reverses because the positive spatial metric is then $\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}$ rather than $\delta_{ij}-h_{ij}$. Consistently redefining the signed strain leaves the physical effect unchanged.
