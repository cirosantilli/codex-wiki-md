<h1 id="e/2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) of belonging to the same fibre:

$$
\boxed{x\mathcal R x'\iff f(x)=f(x').}
$$

The map $\bar f:X/\mathcal R\to Y$, $[x]\mapsto f(x)$, is well defined, injective by the definition of the relation, and surjective because $f$ is surjective. It is continuous by the [quotient topology](../../../../../../quotient-topology.md): for open $V\subseteq Y$, $q^{-1}(\bar f^{-1}(V))=f^{-1}(V)$ is open.

The [quotient space](../../../../../../quotient-topological-space.md) is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) as the continuous image of [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) $X$, and $Y$ is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). The [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../../../../compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md) makes $\bar f$ a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Indeed, images of closed subsets of a [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) space are [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) and hence closed in a [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) space, so the inverse is continuous.
