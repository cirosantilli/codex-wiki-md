# Attainment of the limit of a monotone sequence

↑ **Parent:** [Bounded monotone sequence theorem](bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md)

The [supremum](supremum.md) of an increasing [bounded sequence](bounded-sequence.md) need not be a [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) of its [set](set-split.md) of terms. For $a_n=1-1/n$, every term is strictly less than one; the [supremum](supremum.md) and [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) are one, but there is no [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). Every candidate term is beaten by the next. The decreasing [sequence](sequence.md) $1+1/n$ similarly has [infimum](infimum.md) one without a [minimum](minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md).

If an increasing [sequence](sequence.md) with [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) $L$ attains $a_k=L$, it must remain there: $L=a_k\leq a_n\leq L$ for $n\geq k$. The decreasing case is identical with inequalities reversed. Thus attainment of the [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) is equivalent to an eventually constant [tail of a sequence](tail-of-a-sequence.md) for a convergent [monotone sequence](monotone-sequence.md). The increasing [sequence](sequence.md) $0,1/2,1,1,\ldots$ has both [supremum](supremum.md) and [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) one.
