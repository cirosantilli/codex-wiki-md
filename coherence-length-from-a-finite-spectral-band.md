# Coherence length from a finite spectral band

↑ **Parent:** [Optical coherence length](optical-coherence-length.md)

For [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) endpoints $\lambda_{\min}<\lambda_{\max}$, the corresponding [frequency](frequency.md) span is $\Delta\nu=c(1/\lambda_{\min}-1/\lambda_{\max})$. The reciprocal-bandwidth estimate of [optical coherence time](optical-coherence-time.md) is $\tau_c\sim1/\Delta\nu$, giving $\ell_c\sim c/\Delta\nu$. This endpoint conversion is valid even for a broad band, whereas $\ell_c\sim\lambda_0^2/\Delta\lambda$ requires a relatively narrow [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md).

For constant spectral density per unit [frequency](frequency.md) in the band, the normalized [first-order optical coherence](first-order-optical-coherence.md) is $g^{(1)}(\tau)=e^{-i2\pi\nu_0\tau}\sin(\pi\Delta\nu\tau)/(\pi\Delta\nu\tau)$. Its first zero occurs at $\tau=1/\Delta\nu$. A different spectral shape or a different contrast criterion changes the numerical [optical coherence length](optical-coherence-length.md); endpoints alone do not define an exact universal value.
