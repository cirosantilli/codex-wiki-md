<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A close [binary star](../../../../binary-star.md) can produce two [compact stellar objects](../../../../compact-stellar-object.md) if both initial stellar [masses](../../../../mass.md) are sufficiently large. The initially more massive star evolves first, transfers material or loses its envelope, and leaves a [neutron star](../../../../neutron-star.md) or [black hole](../../../../black-hole-split.md) after core collapse. The system must remain bound despite [mass loss](../../../../mass-loss-astrophysics.md) and any natal kick. Subsequent evolution of the companion can produce a [common envelope](../../../../common-envelope.md): orbital [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) transferred to the envelope can expel it and greatly reduce the separation. A second collapse must again leave a bound pair. A sufficiently close surviving [binary star](../../../../binary-star.md) then loses orbital [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) through [gravitational waves](../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) and may merge. Stable transfer, winds and kicks make this a possible channel rather than an inevitable outcome; dynamical encounters in dense stellar systems provide another channel.
