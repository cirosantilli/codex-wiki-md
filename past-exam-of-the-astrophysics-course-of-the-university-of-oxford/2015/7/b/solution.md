<h1 id="7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For spherical gravity, $d\Phi/dr=GM(\le r)/r^2$. Inserting $\beta=1-\sigma_t^2/\sigma_r^2$ into the [Spherical Jeans equation](../../../../spherical-jeans-equation.md) and solving for the total enclosed [mass](../../../../mass.md) gives

$$
\boxed{M(\le r)=-\frac{r^2}{G\nu}\left[\frac{d(\nu\sigma_r^2)}{dr}+\frac{2\beta\nu\sigma_r^2}{r}\right]
=-\frac{r\sigma_r^2}{G}\left(\frac{d\ln\nu}{d\ln r}+\frac{d\ln\sigma_r^2}{d\ln r}+2\beta\right)}.
$$

The [mass](../../../../mass.md) includes all sources of the potential, including [dark matter](../../../../dark-matter.md) or a central compact object; the stellar tracer density need not follow total [mass](../../../../mass.md). The bracket must yield nonnegative enclosed [mass](../../../../mass.md) in a physically admissible model. Density, radial stress and anisotropy are independent observational/model inputs before this equation becomes a [mass](../../../../mass.md) estimate.
