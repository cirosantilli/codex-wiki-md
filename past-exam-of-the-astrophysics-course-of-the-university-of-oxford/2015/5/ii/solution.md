<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Stellar radiation and nebular ionization.** The [planetary-nebula spectral energy distribution](../../../../planetary-nebula-spectral-energy-distribution.md) is constrained by a shared radiation and thermal balance. The central star's [effective temperature](../../../../effective-temperature.md), [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) and evolutionary state set its continuum and ionizing-photon supply. A hotter star shifts the ionizing spectrum and allows higher ionization stages; the total recombination output also depends on how many ionizing [photons](../../../../photon.md) the gas actually absorbs. In an ionization-bounded nebula, approximate [photoionization](../../../../photoionization.md) balance is

$$
Q_H\simeq\int n_en_p\alpha_B(T_e)\,dV,
$$

whereas a density-bounded nebula can leak [photons](../../../../photon.md). Dust absorption of ionizing [photons](../../../../photon.md) changes this relation further. Stellar-atmosphere and wind effects make a blackbody an approximation, especially in the extreme ultraviolet. A changing core [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and expanding envelope explain why nebular spectra evolve.

**Nebular continua and [recombination lines](../../../../recombination-line.md).** Their strength depends on the [emission measure](../../../../emission-measure.md), roughly $\int n_en_i\,dV$, [electron temperature](../../../../electron-temperature.md), ionic abundances and radiative branching probabilities. For a [recombination line](../../../../recombination-line.md),

$$
L_{ul}\simeq h\nu_{ul}\alpha^{\rm eff}_{ul}(T_e)\int n_en_{\rm ion}\,dV.
$$

Case-B line ratios assume appropriate trapping of Lyman-series [photons](../../../../photon.md) and escape of the lower-series radiation. Density, optical-depth effects and extinction can alter observed ratios. The free-free emissivity has the schematic dependence $j_\nu^{\rm ff}\propto n_en_iT_e^{-1/2}\exp[-h\nu/(k_BT_e)]g_{\rm ff}$; the [nebular free-bound continuum](../../../../nebular-free-bound-continuum.md) also carries ionization thresholds and recombination cross-sections. Two-photon emission depends on the population of the hydrogen $2s$ state and can be reduced by collisional mixing into $2p$. Density clumping matters because these rates are weighted approximately by density squared, not simply by total gas [mass](../../../../mass.md).

**Collisionally excited lines.** Below a line's [critical density of an atomic transition](../../../../critical-density-of-an-atomic-transition.md),

$$
j_{ul}\simeq n_en(X^i)q_{lu}(T_e)h\nu_{ul},\qquad
q_{lu}\propto T_e^{-1/2}\exp[-\Delta E/(k_BT_e)].
$$

Elemental abundance, ionic fraction and [electron temperature](../../../../electron-temperature.md) therefore all enter. The ionizing spectrum and local [ionization parameter](../../../../ionization-parameter.md) help determine which ion is present. Above the [critical density of an atomic transition](../../../../critical-density-of-an-atomic-transition.md), collisional de-excitation competes with radiative decay and can suppress a forbidden line. Low-energy infrared fine-structure lines are generally less exponentially [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) sensitive than high-excitation optical/ultraviolet lines. Metal-line cooling also helps set the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), so changing an abundance changes both the number of emitters and the thermal balance; line [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) need not be proportional to abundance at fixed everything else.

**Dust and molecules.** [Nebular dust spectral features](../../../../nebular-dust-spectral-features.md) identify the carriers as well as contributing to the integrated [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md). In an optically thin dust component,

$$
F_\nu\propto\int \kappa_\nu B_\nu(T_d)\,dM_d.
$$

The grain [mass](../../../../mass.md) sets its normalization, while the radiation field, stellar distance, grain size and absorption efficiency determine its [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) distribution. For approximately grey heating, radiative equilibrium suggests $T_d\propto L_*^{1/4}r^{-1/2}$; non-grey grains change this scaling. Long-wavelength [opacity](../../../../opacity.md) is often approximated as $\kappa_\nu\propto\nu^{\beta_d}$. Small aromatic grains undergo transient heating, so one equilibrium [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) is not adequate for every infrared feature. Carbon-to-oxygen chemistry, grain processing and shielding affect aromatic versus silicate bands. Molecular line emission further depends on molecule abundance, collisional density, [kinetic temperature](../../../../kinetic-temperature.md), ultraviolet pumping and shocks, while shielding and dissociation set whether molecules survive at all.

Finally, [interstellar extinction](../../../../interstellar-extinction.md) preferentially suppresses ultraviolet and optical light, and dust reradiates some absorbed [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in the infrared. Aperture, angular resolution and distance must be treated consistently before comparing stellar, nebular and dust components. These dependencies make the combined spectrum an [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and abundance diagnostic, but also prevent a unique reconstruction from a single broad-band colour.
