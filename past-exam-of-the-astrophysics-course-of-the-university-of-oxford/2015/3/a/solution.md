<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use $c=1$ and a spatially flat background dominated by the stated perfect fluid, so $4\pi Ga^2\rho_0=3\mathcal H^2/2$. In [Newtonian gauge](../../../../newtonian-gauge.md), the scalar [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) and momentum constraints from the linearized [Einstein field equations](../../../../einstein-field-equations-split.md) combine to eliminate the potential's time derivative and give the stated relativistic Poisson constraint. Its source contains the velocity correction to the coordinate density perturbation. On sub-horizon scales that correction is small for the ordinary growing modes, recovering the [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../../../../poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity.md); near or outside the horizon the gravitational field cannot be sourced by $\delta$ alone. Equality of the two metric potentials also assumes negligible [scalar anisotropic stress](../../../../scalar-anisotropic-stress.md).

The fluid equations are the linear energy-continuity and momentum-Euler parts of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$. Here $c_s^2=\delta p/\delta\rho$ is the relevant dimensionless sound-speed squared in the adopted fluid closure; for a constant-$w$ adiabatic barotropic fluid it equals $w$. A fluid with entropy perturbations needs additional [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) information. The equations actually use $\theta=\nabla\cdot\mathbf v$ in Fourier space, the [peculiar-velocity divergence](../../../../peculiar-velocity-divergence.md). Calling it simply momentum density is imprecise: it is the divergence of the velocity, with momentum weighted by $\rho_0+p_0$ before normalization. In particular $\theta$ must have inverse-conformal-time units for the displayed continuity equation to be dimensionally consistent.
