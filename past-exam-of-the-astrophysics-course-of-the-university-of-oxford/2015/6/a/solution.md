<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Cold [molecular hydrogen](../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) is difficult to observe directly: its homonuclear symmetry forbids electric-dipole rotational transitions, and its lowest useful quadrupole rotational excitation is high compared with a cold cloud's [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). [Carbon monoxide](../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) is relatively abundant, has a permanent dipole moment and has low-lying rotational transitions that can be collisionally excited in cold gas. The low-$J$ lines lie in accessible millimetre bands and suffer much less dust extinction than visible light. They therefore reveal molecular-gas velocities and spatial structure efficiently.

The advantages do not turn CO into a direct [mass](../../../../mass.md) counter without a model. Common-isotope lines can be optically thick; excitation can be subthermal or have multiple [temperatures](../../../../temperature-split.md). Abundance changes with metallicity, chemistry, shielding and photodissociation, and CO can freeze onto grains. [Molecular hydrogen](../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) can also survive where CO is weak, giving CO-dark gas. A calibrated [carbon-monoxide tracer of molecular hydrogen](../../../../carbon-monoxide-tracer-of-molecular-hydrogen.md) conversion may be useful for appropriate cloud populations but should not be transplanted uncritically to newly formed supernova ejecta. The line calculations here infer CO [mass](../../../../mass.md), not total molecular [mass](../../../../mass.md) unless a CO-to-hydrogen abundance is separately known.
