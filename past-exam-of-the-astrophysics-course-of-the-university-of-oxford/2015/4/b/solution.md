<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the local shock rest frame. Let $u$ and $d$ label upstream and downstream gas, with positive normal speeds $v_u,v_d$ through the shock. The unshocked ISM is almost stationary in the stellar frame, so $v_u\simeq V_s$, whereas the shocked gas moves outward at $V_s-v_d$ in that frame. For an ideal monatomic gas, $\gamma=5/3$; neglect upstream [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) and enthalpy in the strong-shock limit. Conservation of [mass](../../../../mass.md), momentum and [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) per unit [mass](../../../../mass.md) gives

$$
\rho_uv_u=\rho_dv_d,\qquad p_d+\rho_dv_d^2=\rho_uv_u^2,\qquad
\frac{v_u^2}{2}=\frac{v_d^2}{2}+\frac\gamma{\gamma-1}\frac{p_d}{\rho_d}.
$$

Put $q=\rho_d/\rho_u=v_u/v_d$. The momentum equation gives $p_d=\rho_uv_u^2(1-1/q)$. Substitution into the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) equation gives

$$
1=\frac1{q^2}+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{q-1}{q^2}.
$$

Discarding the unshocked root $q=1$ leaves

$$
\boxed{q=\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}=4,\quad v_d=\frac{V_s}{4},\quad
p_d=\frac34\rho_uV_s^2}.
$$

The downstream speed in the stellar frame is therefore $3V_s/4$, not $V_s/4$. These conditions describe immediate shock heating; subsequent cooling and evolution behind the front require the downstream flow profile. Cosmic-ray [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md), radiative [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) loss inside the shock layer or a different equation of state would alter the compression ratio.
