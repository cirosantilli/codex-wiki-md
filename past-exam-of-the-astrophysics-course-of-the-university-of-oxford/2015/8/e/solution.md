<h1 id="8/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a small peculiar line-of-sight velocity $v_\parallel$ within the group, cosmological and Doppler factors multiply:

$$
1+z_{\rm obs}=(1+z_g)(1+z_{\rm pec}),\qquad z_{\rm pec}\simeq v_\parallel/c.
$$

At fixed group [redshift](../../../../redshift.md) this gives $\delta z=(1+z_g)v_\parallel/c$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\sigma_v=\frac{c\sigma_z}{1+z_g}=99.9\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}}.
$$

Using $c\sigma_z$ directly would give a detector-frame [redshift](../../../../redshift.md) interval without removing the cosmological stretch. This is the one-dimensional line-of-sight [velocity dispersion](../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) requested, not the three-dimensional RMS speed. [Redshift](../../../../redshift.md) measurement errors or a large unbound line-of-sight extent would require additional corrections; neither is supplied here.
