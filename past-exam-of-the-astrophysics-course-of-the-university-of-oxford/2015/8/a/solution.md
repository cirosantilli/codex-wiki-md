<h1 id="8/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the supplied [comoving distance](../../../../comoving-radial-distance.md) $D_C=5421$ Mpc. In a flat background, $D_A=D_C/(1+z)=1807$ Mpc and $D_L=(1+z)D_C=16263$ Mpc. The observed wavelength is stretched by $1+z$, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\rm em}=600/3=200\ \mathrm{nm}}.
$$

Care is needed with a [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) _per [frequency](../../../../frequency.md)_. For corresponding emitted and observed frequencies, $d\nu_{\rm em}=(1+z)d\nu_{\rm obs}$. Combining that bandwidth relation with the bolometric definition of $D_L$ gives

$$
F_{\nu,\rm obs}=\frac{(1+z)L_{\nu,\rm em}}{4\pi D_L^2},\qquad
\boxed{L_{\nu,\rm em}=\frac{4\pi D_L^2F_{\nu,\rm obs}}{1+z}=1.06\times10^{24}\ \mathrm{W\,Hz^{-1}}}.
$$

This is the spectral [luminosity](../../../../luminosity.md) at the rest-frame [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) corresponding to 200 nm. A value at some different rest wavelength would require the [quasar](../../../../quasar.md)'s spectral shape. The factor is different for a density per unit wavelength; interchanging these two conventions would give an incorrect [redshift](../../../../redshift.md) factor.
