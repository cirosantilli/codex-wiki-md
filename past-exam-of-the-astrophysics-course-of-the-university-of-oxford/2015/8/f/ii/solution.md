<h1 id="8/f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a small radial comoving interval, $dD_C/dz=c/H(z)$. At $z=2$,

$$
H(2)=70\sqrt{0.75+0.25(3)^3}=191.7\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\Delta z\simeq\frac{H(2)\ell_c}{c}=0.0639}.
$$

This uses the unbound cosmological radial separation between separate [quasars](../../../../../quasar.md), unlike the peculiar-velocity [redshift](../../../../../redshift.md) dispersion of galaxies inside the bound group. Integrating the distance relation instead of linearizing gives a small correction for a specified direction and endpoint. The quoted characteristic spacing alone does not assert that the same nearest object lies both purely transverse and purely radial.
