<h1 id="8/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The projected quasar-to-cloud separation is

$$
R_\perp=10\left(\frac{\pi}{180\times3600}\right)D_A=87.6\ \mathrm{kpc}.
$$

For the usual transverse-separation estimate, take the cloud to lie at the same line-of-sight proper distance as the [quasar](../../../../quasar.md). Since the cloud and galaxy are gravitationally bound, the local source-to-cloud path does not acquire the [redshift](../../../../redshift.md) factor $1+z=3$ that separates that system from us. Neglect internal relative Doppler shifts and attenuation. The observer in the cloud sees

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\rm cloud}\simeq200\ \mathrm{nm},\qquad
F_{\nu,\rm cloud}\simeq\frac{L_{\nu,\rm em}}{4\pi R_\perp^2}=1.15\times10^{-20}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}}}.
$$

This uses the [quasar](../../../../quasar.md)'s isotropic intrinsic emission at the same rest [frequency](../../../../frequency.md); anisotropic radiation or obscuration would modify it.

An angular separation only fixes $R_\perp$. If $s$ is a source-to-cloud displacement toward us, the true local distance is $r=\sqrt{R_\perp^2+s^2}$ and the general flux is $L_\nu/(4\pi r^2)$. The quoted transverse value is therefore an upper estimate at fixed projected separation. Equality of cosmological [redshift](../../../../redshift.md) and gravitational binding do not by themselves measure the small internal line-of-sight displacement; a [peculiar velocity](../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) can also mask it.
