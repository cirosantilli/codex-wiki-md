<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [specific intensity](../radiometry.md#spectral-radiance) $I_\nu$ is [energy](../energy.md) transported per unit time, projected area, [solid angle](../euclidean-geometry.md#solid-angle) and [frequency](../wave.md#frequency). The path coordinate $s$ increases along the ray. The [emission coefficient](../astrophysics.md#emission-coefficient) $j_\nu$ is emitted [energy](../energy.md) per unit time, volume, [solid angle](../euclidean-geometry.md#solid-angle) and [frequency](../wave.md#frequency), whereas the [absorption coefficient](../astrophysics.md#absorption-coefficient) $\alpha_\nu$ has units of inverse length. Thus $j_\nu ds$ adds [specific intensity](../radiometry.md#spectral-radiance) and $\alpha_\nu I_\nu ds$ removes it. [Stimulated emission](../laser.md#stimulated-emission) can be included as negative [absorption](../radiometry.md#absorption-electromagnetic-radiation).

Define the [optical depth](../astrophysics.md#optical-depth) between two points by $\tau_\nu(s_1,s_2)=\int_{s_1}^{s_2}\alpha_\nu(u)\,du$. Multiplying the [radiative transfer equation](../astrophysics.md#radiative-transfer-equation) by the [integrating factor](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#integrating-factor) $\exp[\tau_\nu(s_0,s)]$ and integrating gives

$$
\boxed{I_\nu(s)=I_\nu(s_0)e^{-\tau_\nu(s_0,s)}+\int_{s_0}^{s}j_\nu(u)e^{-\tau_\nu(u,s)}\,du.}
$$

Each local contribution is attenuated only by the material between its emission point and the observer. Where $\alpha_\nu\ne0$, the [source function](../astrophysics.md#radiative-transfer-source-function) is $S_\nu=j_\nu/\alpha_\nu$ and the same [formal solution of the radiative transfer equation](../astrophysics.md#formal-solution-of-the-radiative-transfer-equation) is $I_\nu(\tau)=I_\nu(0)e^{-\tau}+\int_0^\tau S_\nu(t)e^{-(\tau-t)}dt$. A constant [source function](../astrophysics.md#radiative-transfer-source-function) gives $I_\nu=I_\nu(0)e^{-\tau}+S_\nu(1-e^{-\tau})$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Use the [Einstein coefficients](../atomic-physics.md#einstein-coefficients) convention in which $B_{ij}$ multiplies spectral [radiation energy density](../statistical-physics.md#radiation-energy-density) $u_\nu$, with $u_\nu=c^{-1}\int I_\nu\,d\Omega$. A normalized [spectral line profile](../atomic-physics.md#spectral-line-profile) obeys $\int\varphi(\nu)d\nu=1$. The [spontaneous emission](../atomic-physics.md#spontaneous-emission) probability per upper-state atom per unit time is $A_{21}$; the [stimulated emission](../laser.md#stimulated-emission) and upward [absorption](../radiometry.md#absorption-electromagnetic-radiation) rates per atom are respectively $B_{21}\int u_\nu\varphi(\nu)d\nu$ and $B_{12}\int u_\nu\varphi(\nu)d\nu$.

For [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) [spontaneous emission](../atomic-physics.md#spontaneous-emission), $n_2A_{21}$ transitions occur per unit volume and time, each supplying [photon energy](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon-energy) $h\nu_{12}$. Dividing among $4\pi$ steradians gives

$$
\boxed{j_\nu=\frac{h\nu_{12}}{4\pi}n_2A_{21}\varphi(\nu).}
$$

For a small incident beam, its contribution to $u_\nu$ is $I_\nu d\Omega/c$. [Absorption](../radiometry.md#absorption-electromagnetic-radiation) removes and [stimulated emission](../laser.md#stimulated-emission) adds [photons](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) in the incident direction. Matching the net loss to $\alpha_\nu I_\nu d\Omega$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha_\nu=\frac{h\nu_{12}}{c}(n_1B_{12}-n_2B_{21})\varphi(\nu).}
$$

Replacing $\nu_{12}$ by $\nu$ across the narrow [spectral line profile](../atomic-physics.md#spectral-line-profile) gives the printed expressions. The negative sign is essential: an inverted population can amplify the incident [specific intensity](../radiometry.md#spectral-radiance). [Thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md) and the [Planck function](../astrophysics.md#planck-function) imply $g_1B_{12}=g_2B_{21}$ and $A_{21}/B_{21}=8\pi h\nu_{12}^3/c^3$, where $g_i$ is the [state degeneracy](../statistical-physics.md#state-degeneracy). A convention using [mean intensity](../astrophysics.md#mean-intensity) instead changes both $B$ coefficients by $4\pi/c$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

With the [spontaneous emission](../atomic-physics.md#spontaneous-emission) term suppressed, $j_\nu=0$. A uniform [absorption coefficient](../astrophysics.md#absorption-coefficient) over length $\ell$ gives directly

$$
\boxed{I_\nu=I_\nu^0e^{-\tau_\nu},\qquad \tau_\nu=\ell\alpha_\nu.}
$$

The normalized [specific intensity](../radiometry.md#spectral-radiance) deficit is unchanged by conversion between [wavelength](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) and [frequency](../wave.md#frequency), since both numerator and denominator acquire the same [Jacobian determinant](../multivariable-calculus.md#jacobian-determinant). The [Boltzmann distribution](../thermodynamics.md#boltzmann-distribution) and the [Einstein coefficients](../atomic-physics.md#einstein-coefficients) give $n_2B_{21}/(n_1B_{12})=e^{-h\nu_{12}/(k_BT_e)}\ll1$. Consequently, in the [optically thin](../astrophysics.md#optically-thin-medium) limit,

$$
W=\int(1-e^{-\tau_\nu})\frac{c}{\nu^2}\,d\nu
\simeq\frac{hN_1B_{12}}{\nu_{12}}\int\varphi(\nu)d\nu
=\boxed{\frac{hB_{12}N_1}{\nu_{12}}}.
$$

Here $N_1=n_1\ell$ is the [column density](../statistical-physics.md#column-density), and $\nu\simeq\nu_{12}$ has been used in slowly varying factors. This linear relation between [equivalent width](../astrophysics.md#equivalent-width) and [column density](../statistical-physics.md#column-density) fails once the central [optical depth](../astrophysics.md#optical-depth) becomes appreciable.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

For the unresolved [Lyman-alpha line](../mathematical-physics.md#lyman-alpha-line), the lower $1s$ and combined upper $2p$ [state degeneracies](../statistical-physics.md#state-degeneracy) are $g_1=2$ and $g_2=6$, so the [Einstein coefficients](../atomic-physics.md#einstein-coefficients) give

$$
B_{12}=3\frac{A_{21}c^3}{8\pi h\nu_{12}^3}=2.03\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^3\,J^{-1}\,s^{-2}}.
$$

The measured [equivalent width](../astrophysics.md#equivalent-width) $W=7.0\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm m$ then implies

$$
N_{\rm HI}=\frac{8\pi W\nu_{12}^4}{3A_{21}c^3}
=1.29\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}=1.29\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}.
$$

Assume a central transverse [line of sight](../astrophysics.md#line-of-sight) through a uniform filament, so $\ell=d=0.5\ \mathrm{Mpc}=1.54\times10^{22}\ \mathrm m$. Nearly all neutral [hydrogen](../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen) is in its ground state at this [temperature](../temperature.md), hence

$$
\boxed{n_{\rm HI}=N_{\rm HI}/d=8.3\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}=8.3\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}.}
$$

The cylinder's length $L$ determines its total volume but does not enter a transverse [column density](../statistical-physics.md#column-density). Geometry must be specified: an axial [line of sight](../astrophysics.md#line-of-sight) has $\ell=L$ and gives $n_{\rm HI}=4.2\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ for the same measured [equivalent width](../astrophysics.md#equivalent-width). Intermediate chords give intermediate path-dependent estimates. The numbers above treat $W$ as a rest-frame [equivalent width](../astrophysics.md#equivalent-width); an observed value at [redshift](../optics.md#redshift) $z$ must first be divided by $1+z$.

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

True [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md) requires a thermal ionizing [radiation](../physics.md#radiation) field as well as chemical balance; equality of [photoionization](../atomic-physics.md#photoionization) and recombination rates alone would require a specified external [radiation](../physics.md#radiation) field. Under the stronger equilibrium assumption, apply the [Saha equation](../cosmology.md#saha-ionization-equation) to pure [hydrogen](../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen). The electron spin factor cancels the ground-state electronic degeneracy, giving

$$
\frac{n_en_p}{n_{\rm HI}}=S(T),\qquad
S(T)=\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_BT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}e^{-\chi/(k_BT)}.
$$

At $T=10^4\ \mathrm K$, $k_BT=0.862\ \mathrm{eV}$ and $S=3.38\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$. Charge neutrality gives $n_e=n_p$. Importantly, the known quantity from [d](#1/d) is the _neutral_ [number density](../statistical-physics.md#number-density), not the total [number density](../statistical-physics.md#number-density); use [Saha neutral fraction from a measured neutral density](../cosmology.md#saha-neutral-fraction-from-a-measured-neutral-density) to obtain $n_p=\sqrt{Sn_{\rm HI}}=5.31\times10^7\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{1-x_e=\frac{n_{\rm HI}}{n_p+n_{\rm HI}}=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{S/n_{\rm HI}}}\simeq1.57\times10^{-13}.}
$$

This extraordinarily small neutral fraction is the result of applying [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md) to dilute intergalactic gas; it is not a realistic inference from [photoionization equilibrium](../galaxy.md#photoionization-equilibrium) with a nonthermal cosmic background. For the axial geometry of [d](#1/d), the equilibrium neutral fraction is smaller by $\sqrt{20}$.

<h3 id="1/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#1/f)

Adopt the stipulated neutral fraction $f_{\rm HI}=10^{-6}$, independently of the equilibrium estimate in [e](#1/e). For pure [hydrogen](../chemical-substance.md#hydrogen), $n_e=(1-f_{\rm HI})n_{\rm HI}/f_{\rm HI}\simeq8.3\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$. The [thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect](../cosmic-microwave-background.md#thermal-sunyaev-zeldovich-effect) measures integrated electron [pressure](../pressure.md):

$$
y=\frac{\sigma_T}{m_ec^2}\int n_ek_BT_e\,ds
\simeq\frac{\sigma_Tk_BT_e}{m_ec^2}\frac{N_{\rm HI}}{f_{\rm HI}}.
$$

Using the same absorbing [line of sight](../astrophysics.md#line-of-sight) as in [d](#1/d),

$$
\boxed{y=1.44\times10^{-11},\qquad y/(10^{-9})=0.0144.}
$$

Thus one such filament is below the stated measurement sensitivity. If the [number density](../statistical-physics.md#number-density) inferred transversely is instead used to view the _same cylinder_ end-on, its path is twenty times longer and $y=2.9\times10^{-10}$, still below the stated sensitivity. Changing the assumed path of the original absorption measurement while keeping its [column density](../statistical-physics.md#column-density) fixed does not change its predicted $y$. The quoted neutral-fraction floor is a premise of this estimate, not a universal bound derivable from [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md).

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

There are two different physical interpretations. The supplied logarithmic integral signals [free fall in a fixed singular isothermal sphere](../astrophysics.md#free-fall-in-a-fixed-singular-isothermal-sphere), with a test particle moving through a frozen background. Write $C=\rho_0r_0^2$. By the [shell theorem](../mathematical-physics.md#spherical-shell-theorem), the enclosed [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) is $M(r)=4\pi Cr$ and the radial [equation of motion](../classical-mechanics.md#equation-of-motion) is

$$
\ddot r=-\frac{4\pi GC}{r},\qquad
\frac{\dot r^2}{2}=4\pi GC\ln\frac{r_0}{r},\qquad \dot r<0.
$$

The second equality follows by multiplying by $\dot r$ and using the initial rest condition. The [free fall](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#free-fall) trajectory is given implicitly by

$$
t(r)=\frac{r_0}{\sqrt{8\pi GC}}\int_{r/r_0}^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\ln(1/x)}}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8G\rho_0}}\operatorname{erf}\!\sqrt{\ln(r_0/r)}.
$$

Thus the intended fixed-profile answer is

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm ff,fixed}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8G\rho_0}}.}
$$

**A collapsing material shell gives a different answer.** If the entire cloud undergoes pressureless [gravitational collapse](../astrophysics.md#gravitational-collapse) from rest, the edge follows the fixed enclosed [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) $M_0=4\pi\rho_0r_0^3$, not the instantaneous fixed-profile value $4\pi Cr$. With no [shell crossing](../large-scale-structure-of-the-universe.md#shell-crossing), $\ddot r=-GM_0/r^2$, so

$$
t_{\rm ff,shell}=\int_0^{r_0}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{2GM_0(1/r-1/r_0)}}
=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{r_0^3}{2GM_0}}
=\boxed{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{32G\rho_0}}}.
$$

This is a concrete counterexample to treating the fixed-profile result as the exact collapse time of the whole nebula. The initial mean [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) inside $r_0$ is $3\rho_0$, consistent with the usual [free-fall time of a uniform sphere](../astrophysics.md#free-fall-time-of-a-uniform-sphere) expressed using that mean [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density). The logarithmic integral is valid for the explicitly frozen background approximation.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Assume an isolated, bound, nonrotating Newtonian gas cloud, negligible surface [pressure](../pressure.md), negligible magnetic and radiation stresses, no persistent bulk flow, and a stationary or time-averaged virialized state. The [stellar virial theorem](../stellar-structure.md#stellar-virial-theorem) then reduces to

$$
3\int P(\mathbf x)\,dV+\Omega=0,
$$

where $\Omega<0$ is the total [gravitational potential energy](../energy.md#gravitational-energy). Define the volume-averaged [pressure](../pressure.md) $\overline P=V^{-1}\int P\,dV$ and average gravitational [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) $u_g=\Omega/V$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\overline P=-u_g/3.}
$$

Equivalently, for a nonrelativistic [monatomic gas](../ideal-gas.md#monatomic-gas), the thermal [internal energy](../internal-energy.md) $U=(3/2)\int P\,dV$ obeys $2U+\Omega=0$. This second derivation must not count the thermal motions again as a separate bulk [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy). If the external [pressure](../pressure.md) is $P_s$, the correct result is $\overline P-P_s=-u_g/3$; non-negligible bulk motion adds $2T_{\rm bulk}$ to the [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem). The initial sharp-edged $r^{-2}$ profile need not survive virialization; maintaining that profile hydrostatically to its edge would generally require a surface term.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For a family of spherical profiles with a fixed dimensionless shape, write the [gravitational potential energy](../energy.md#gravitational-energy) as $\Omega=-\alpha GM^2/R$, where $\alpha>0$ depends on that shape. The [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem) in [b](#2/b) and the sphere volume $V=4\pi R^3/3$ give

$$
\boxed{\overline P=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\frac{GM^2}{R^4}\propto\frac{GM^2}{R^4}.}
$$

For a uniform [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), $\alpha=3/5$; formally integrating $-\int_0^R GM(r)\,dM(r)/r$ for $M(r)=Mr/R$ gives $\alpha=1$. Neither the [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem) nor the initial profile guarantees a specific final shape, so the proportionality requires the stated homologous-profile assumption. Surface [pressure](../pressure.md) must be handled consistently with [b](#2/b) before using either coefficient as an equilibrium model.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

For a regular spherical star, let $\rho_c=\rho(0)$ and $P_c=P(0)$. The [stellar mass conservation equation](../stellar-structure.md#stellar-mass-conservation-equation) and [stellar hydrostatic equation](../stellar-structure.md#hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation) are

$$
M'(r)=4\pi r^2\rho(r),\qquad P'(r)=-\frac{GM(r)\rho(r)}{r^2}.
$$

Assuming the smoothness needed for the requested [Taylor series](../calculus.md#taylor-series), write $M=a_0+a_1r+a_2r^2+a_3r^3+\cdots$. There is no point [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) at the centre, so $a_0=0$, and matching the [stellar mass conservation equation](../stellar-structure.md#stellar-mass-conservation-equation) gives $a_1=a_2=0$, $a_3=4\pi\rho_c/3$. Substitution in the [stellar hydrostatic equation](../stellar-structure.md#hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation) gives $P'(r)=-(4\pi G/3)\rho_c^2r+O(r^3)$, hence

$$
\boxed{M(r)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_cr^3+O(r^5),\qquad
P(r)=P_c-\frac{2\pi G}{3}\rho_c^2r^2+O(r^4).}
$$

The remainder orders use a smooth spherically symmetric scalar [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), for which $\rho(r)=\rho_c+O(r^2)$. Merely finite continuous [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) gives the same leading coefficients with $o(r^3)$ and $o(r^2)$ remainders. In particular $P'(0)=0$, despite the apparent $r^{-2}$ in the [stellar hydrostatic equation](../stellar-structure.md#hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation).

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

For a gas-pressure-supported [stellar core](../stellar-structure.md#stellar-core) of fixed [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) and composition, neglect boundary terms for the illustrative whole-core estimate. The [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem) gives $E=U+\Omega=-U$, and the [monatomic gas](../ideal-gas.md#monatomic-gas) gives $U=3M_ck_B\overline T/(2\mu m_u)$. Thus the effective [heat capacity](../heat.md#heat-capacity) along the equilibrium sequence is

$$
\boxed{\frac{dE}{d\overline T}=-\frac{3M_ck_B}{2\mu m_u}<0.}
$$

This [negative heat capacity of a virialized gravitational system](../classical-mechanics.md#negative-heat-capacity-of-a-virialized-gravitational-system) means that net [energy](../energy.md) loss makes a self-gravitating core contract and heat. It is different from the positive local material [specific heat capacity](../heat.md#specific-heat-capacity). On the [main sequence](../stellar-astrophysics.md#main-sequence), [hydrogen burning](../stellar-astrophysics.md#hydrogen-burning) supplies most of the radiated [energy](../energy.md), so rapid contraction is arrested. Hydrogen depletion increases the [mean molecular weight](../ideal-gas.md#mean-molecular-weight); slow structural readjustment usually raises the central [temperature](../temperature.md) rather than making it fall.

After core hydrogen exhaustion, a massive [stellar core](../stellar-structure.md#stellar-core) contracts and heats more rapidly until a later nuclear fuel ignites. There is no general reversal to cooling simply upon leaving the [main sequence](../stellar-astrophysics.md#main-sequence). If [radiation pressure](../pressure.md#radiation-pressure) becomes dominant, the [internal energy](../internal-energy.md) becomes $U=U_{\rm gas}+U_{\rm rad}$ with $3\int P\,dV=2U_{\rm gas}+U_{\rm rad}$. The [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem) therefore gives $E=-U_{\rm gas}$; the radiation-dominated limit is marginally bound and its effective [adiabatic index](../ideal-gas.md#heat-capacity-ratio) approaches $4/3$.

Quasistatic contraction can still maintain [virial equilibrium](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-equilibrium). It fails dynamically only when sufficient support is lost, for example if pair creation reduces the effective [adiabatic index](../ideal-gas.md#heat-capacity-ratio) below $4/3$, or when an iron core undergoes [electron capture](../physics.md#electron-capture) and photodisintegration. A fixed-composition gas-dominated contracting core with $T\propto1/R$ is already a counterexample to any assertion that leaving the [main sequence](../stellar-astrophysics.md#main-sequence) alone reverses the heating or destroys [virial equilibrium](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-equilibrium).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Primordial [density contrasts](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) perturb the intrinsic photon [temperature](../temperature.md), the [gravitational potential](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass), and the [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) of the last-scattering material. Intrinsic photon-density fluctuations obey $\delta T/T=\delta_\gamma/4$ because $\rho_\gamma\propto T^4$; adiabatic initial conditions relate these to matter [density contrasts](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast). Subsequent acoustic evolution, [Doppler effect](../wave.md#doppler-effect) and gravitational frequency shifts determine the observed [Cosmic microwave background anisotropy](../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy.md). There is no scale-independent identification of the final sky map with matter [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) alone.

The [spherical harmonic](../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) coefficients are random variables inherited from these perturbations. After subtracting the mean sky [temperature](../temperature.md), $\langle a_{lm}\rangle=0$ for the anisotropy modes. Gaussian primordial perturbations give approximately [Gaussian random variables](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable); this extra Gaussian assumption is not needed for diagonal covariance. Reality of the sky map implies $a_{l,-m}=(-1)^ma_{lm}^*$.

For a statistically isotropic sky, its two-point function depends only on $\hat n\cdot\hat m$. Rotations mix the $m$ components at fixed $l$ and do not mix different irreducible $l$ spaces. A rotation-invariant [covariance matrix](../variance.md#covariance-matrix) is therefore a multiple of the identity in each $l$ block:

$$
\boxed{\langle a_{lm}a_{l'm'}^*\rangle=C_l\delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}.}
$$

On our one sky, average the $2l+1$ angular components at fixed $l$, or equivalently suitable rotated sky pairs, rather than treating different universes as observed samples. The [CMB angular power spectrum](../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy.md#cosmic-microwave-background-power-spectrum) is estimated by

$$
\boxed{\widehat C_l=\frac{1}{2l+1}\sum_{m=-l}^l|a_{lm}|^2.}
$$

Under the Gaussian, full-sky, noiseless assumptions, there are $2l+1$ independent real degrees of freedom and $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat C_l)=2C_l^2/(2l+1)$. This [cosmic variance](../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy.md#cosmic-variance) remains even with perfect detectors; a finite sky mask and detector noise add further uncertainty. The estimator is unbiased, $\langle\widehat C_l\rangle=C_l$, but does not turn one realization into the exact ensemble mean.

The ordinary [Sachs-Wolfe effect](../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy.md#sachs-wolfe-effect) combines intrinsic last-scattering [temperature](../temperature.md) with the gravitational redshift of [photons](../quantum-mechanics.md#photon) escaping the last-scattering [gravitational potential](../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass). For adiabatic large-scale perturbations in matter domination, with the potential convention in the question, the combination is $\Theta=\phi/(3c^2)$, up to the observer monopole. It is distinct from the [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy.md#integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect) caused by a time-varying potential along the later [line of sight](../astrophysics.md#line-of-sight).

Use the [Fourier transform](../fourier-transform.md) convention in which $P_\delta(k)=Ak$ is the [matter power spectrum](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#matter-power-spectrum) and $\langle\delta_{\mathbf k}\delta_{\mathbf k'}^*\rangle=(2\pi)^3\delta_D(\mathbf k-\mathbf k')P_\delta(k)$. The [Poisson equation](../partial-differential-equation.md#poisson-s-equation) becomes $-k^2\phi_{\mathbf k}=4\pi G\bar\rho_0\delta_{\mathbf k}$, giving

$$
P_\phi(k)=\frac{(4\pi G\bar\rho_0)^2A}{k^3},\qquad
\Delta_\phi^2=\frac{(4\pi G\bar\rho_0)^2A}{2\pi^2c^4}.
$$

With the matter-dominated critical-density approximation $\bar\rho_0=3H_0^2/(8\pi G)$,

$$
\boxed{\Delta_\phi^2=\frac{9AH_0^4}{8\pi^2c^4},}
$$

independent of $k$. Thus the [Harrison-Zeldovich spectrum](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#harrison-peebles-zeldovich-spectrum) gives scale-invariant potential fluctuations and the requested $H_0^4$ dependence. Keeping a general present matter fraction would insert $\Omega_m^2$; the answer here follows the stipulated critical matter-dominated normalization. In this [Fourier transform](../fourier-transform.md) convention, $A$ has dimensions of length to the fourth power.

An ultrarelativistic [electron](../physics.md#electron) has [kinetic energy](../energy.md#kinetic-energy) much larger than its rest [energy](../energy.md): $(\gamma-1)m_ec^2\gg m_ec^2$, equivalently $\gamma\gg1$. <h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

[Diffusive shock acceleration](../galaxy.md#diffusive-shock-acceleration) is viable in principle at $511\ \mathrm{GeV}$: repeated scattering off magnetic irregularities on opposite sides of a strong nonrelativistic [shock wave](../transport-equation.md#shock-wave) can reach such [energies](../energy.md). Actual viability requires a sufficiently large and long-lived accelerator with an acceleration time shorter than its escape and loss times; $\gamma$ alone does not establish those conditions.

Take positive [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) toward the upstream material, and let the front move at $+v_s$ in the upstream rest frame. For a strong adiabatic [shock wave](../transport-equation.md#shock-wave) in a gas with [adiabatic index](../ideal-gas.md#heat-capacity-ratio) $5/3$, the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../transport-equation.md#rankine-hugoniot-conditions) give $\rho_d/\rho_u=4$ and the shock-frame inflow [speeds](../classical-mechanics.md#speed) satisfy $u_1=v_s$, $u_2=v_s/4$. The signed [velocities](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) in the three frames are

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{rest frame}&v_u&v_d&v_{\rm front}\\ \hline
\text{upstream}&0&3v_s/4&v_s\\
\text{downstream}&-3v_s/4&0&v_s/4\\
\text{front}&-v_s&-v_s/4&0
\end{array}
$$

Thus the two scattering media converge at $V=u_1-u_2=3v_s/4$. For a crossing particle with incident [energy](../energy.md) $E$ in its _local plasma rest frame_, a [Lorentz transformation](../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) to the approaching other plasma gives

$$
E'=\Gamma_V(E+Vp\mu)=E\left[1+\frac{V}{c}\mu+O(V^2/c^2)\right],
$$

where $\mu>0$ is the incident direction cosine measured toward the approaching medium and $pc\simeq E$. For an almost [isotropic](../continuum-mechanics.md#isotropy) crossing population, flux weighting gives $\langle\mu\rangle=\int_0^1\mu^2d\mu/\int_0^1\mu d\mu=2/3$. Scattering randomizes directions without changing [energy](../energy.md) in that plasma frame, and the reverse crossing has the same mean gain. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\left\langle\frac{\Delta E}{E}\right\rangle_{\rm crossing}=\frac{2V}{3c},\qquad
\left\langle\frac{\Delta E}{E}\right\rangle_{\rm cycle}=\frac{4V}{3c}=\frac{v_s}{c}.}
$$

These are first-order mean gains. They are not an increase of [energy](../energy.md) in one fixed frame caused by an instantaneous boundary crossing. Literally choosing the [electron](../physics.md#electron)'s instantaneous rest frame gives incident $E=m_ec^2$, and the other gas approaches at nearly $c$, so the expansion in small $V/c$ is invalid. This concrete frame check explains why the printed description of $V$ must instead refer to the upstream/downstream scattering frames.

<h3 id="4/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

[Relativistic beaming](../special-relativity.md#relativistic-beaming) concentrates the emission into a forward cone about the instantaneous [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity). The [aberration of light](../mathematical-physics.md#relativistic-aberration) maps a rest-frame ray at $\theta'=\pi/2$ to $\cos\theta=\beta$, so the characteristic cone half-angle is $\theta\simeq\gamma^{-1}$. Thus its conventional full width is $2/\gamma=2\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{rad}$.

**The isotropic-emission assumption has no beam nulls.** For example, its received bolometric angular [power](../energy.md#power) is proportional to $D^4$, where the [Doppler factor](../special-relativity.md#doppler-factor) is $D=[\gamma(1-\beta\cos\theta)]^{-1}>0$. In particular it is nonzero at $\theta=1/\gamma$, disproving a literal null there. A true dipole pattern for an accelerated [electron](../physics.md#electron) is not isotropic in its rest frame and needs a specified polarization and viewing plane. The width just quoted is a characteristic beaming angle, not a null-to-null width under the stated premise.

For a circular orbit seen in its orbital plane, let the [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) direction sweep past the observer through $-1/\gamma<\psi<1/\gamma$. The emission interval is $\Delta t_{\rm em}\simeq2/(\gamma\omega_g)=1.14\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm s$. The observed interval must also include light-travel compression:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm obs}=\frac{1}{\omega_g}\int_{-1/\gamma}^{1/\gamma}(1-\beta\cos\psi)d\psi
=\frac{2}{\omega_g}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma}-\beta\sin\frac{1}{\gamma}\right]
\simeq\boxed{\frac{4}{3\omega_g\gamma^3}=7.6\times10^{-15}\ \mathrm s}.
$$

Its ratio to the [gyromotion](../relativistic-electromagnetism.md#gyromotion) period is $\Delta t_{\rm obs}/T_g\simeq2/(3\pi\gamma^3)=2.1\times10^{-19}$. Since the boundary of a smooth beam is conventional, only the scale $\omega_g^{-1}\gamma^{-3}$ is invariant under reasonable pulse-width definitions. For helical motion the viewing geometry and pitch angle are additional inputs; the numerical estimate assumes transverse motion and a sightline intercepted by the cone.[Chapter 5, section 5.1.3: relativistic beaming and the difference between emission duration and pulse arrival duration](../physics-bibliography.md#essential-radio-astronomy-by-james-j-condon-and-scott-m-ransom)

<h3 id="4/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#4/e)

One explanation is an upper [electron](../physics.md#electron) [energy](../energy.md) imposed by the accelerator: finite age, finite confinement size, escape, or a balance of acceleration and losses limits $\gamma$ to $\gamma_{\max}$. Since the [synchrotron critical frequency](../electromagnetic-radiation.md#synchrotron-critical-frequency) scales as $\nu_c\propto B\gamma^2\sin\alpha$, the absence of higher-energy [electrons](../physics.md#electron) produces a corresponding high-[frequency](../wave.md#frequency) cutoff, broadened by the single-particle spectrum.

The other is [synchrotron cooling](../electromagnetic-radiation.md#synchrotron-cooling), with [inverse Compton scattering](../mathematical-physics.md#inverse-compton-scattering) often contributing: $\dot\gamma=-b\gamma^2$ gives $t_{\rm cool}=1/(b\gamma)$. High-energy [electrons](../physics.md#electron) disappear fastest, so a population of age $t$ develops a cooling scale $\gamma_b\sim1/(bt)$ and $\nu_b\propto B/(b^2t^2)$. A burst population can develop a strong cutoff. With continuous power-law injection, the simplest steady cooling model instead steepens the [electron](../physics.md#electron) [energy](../energy.md) index by one and the [synchrotron radiation](../electromagnetic-radiation.md#synchrotron-radiation) spectral index by $1/2$, giving a break rather than necessarily an exponential cutoff. The detailed spectral shape therefore distinguishes finite acceleration from aging and injection history.

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

Let $n(\mathbf x)=\int f\,d^3v$ and $\overline A=n^{-1}\int Af\,d^3v$. Assume the [one-particle distribution function](../boltzmann-equation.md#one-particle-distribution-function) and its weighted tails decay enough that all required [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) integrals converge and boundary terms at infinite [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) vanish. Multiply the steady [Collisionless Boltzmann equation](../galaxy.md#collisionless-boltzmann-equation) by $v_j$ and integrate over [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity). In [Cartesian coordinates](../linear-algebra.md#cartesian-coordinate-system), spatial differentiation commutes with this integration, while [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives

$$
\int v_j\frac{\partial f}{\partial v_i}d^3v=-\delta_{ij}n.
$$

Consequently the three force-balance equations are

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^3\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\left(n\overline{v_iv_j}\right)=-n\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_j},\qquad j=1,2,3.}
$$

Explicitly the $x$ component is $\partial_x(n\overline{v_x^2})+\partial_y(n\overline{v_xv_y})+\partial_z(n\overline{v_xv_z})=-n\Phi_x$, with cyclic counterparts for $y,z$. For equal stellar [masses](../classical-mechanics.md#mass), multiplying by the common [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) replaces $n$ by the [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) $\rho$; for multiple stellar [masses](../classical-mechanics.md#mass), use a mass-weighted [one-particle distribution function](../boltzmann-equation.md#one-particle-distribution-function) or derive a separate equation for each tracer population.

<h3 id="5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/b)

Define mean streaming [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $u_i=\overline{v_i}$ and the [velocity dispersion](../galaxy.md#velocity-dispersion) tensor $\sigma_{ij}^2=\overline{(v_i-u_i)(v_j-u_j)}=\overline{v_iv_j}-u_iu_j$. The zeroth [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) moment of the steady [Collisionless Boltzmann equation](../galaxy.md#collisionless-boltzmann-equation) gives the [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation) $\sum_i\partial_i(nu_i)=0$. Therefore

$$
\sum_i\partial_i(nu_iu_j)=n\sum_i u_i\partial_i u_j.
$$

Substituting in [a](#5/a) yields the standard steady [Jeans equations](../galaxy.md#jeans-equation):

$$
\boxed{\sum_i u_i\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i}=-\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_j}-\frac1n\sum_i\frac{\partial(n\sigma_{ij}^2)}{\partial x_i},\qquad j=1,2,3.}
$$

The unsteady version adds $\partial_tu_j$ on the left. Defining the stellar [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor) $P_{ij}=\rho\sigma_{ij}^2$ makes the analogy with fluid momentum balance explicit. The off-diagonal terms are correlations between random [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) components; they need not vanish or equal the diagonal terms.

<h3 id="5/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/c)

The [Jeans equations](../galaxy.md#jeans-equation) express local momentum conservation for a [collisionless stellar system](../galaxy.md#collisionless-stellar-system), just as the [Euler equations](../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md) do for a gas. The mean streaming [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) plays the role of fluid flow, the gravitational force is a body force, and random stellar motions supply a [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor) through $\rho\sigma_{ij}^2$. Taking moments smooths over individual stellar orbits and produces fields of [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), mean [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) and random-motion stress; this is the sense of “stellar hydrodynamics”.

Ordinary gas [pressure](../pressure.md) is usually nearly isotropic because collisions relax its local [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) distribution. A [collisionless stellar system](../galaxy.md#collisionless-stellar-system) can retain an anisotropic [velocity ellipsoid](../galaxy.md#velocity-ellipsoid), so its [pressure tensor](../pressure.md#pressure-tensor) is not generally a scalar times the identity. There are more unknown second moments than momentum equations, and their evolution introduces higher moments. The [Jeans equations](../galaxy.md#jeans-equation) therefore require further dynamical assumptions or a specified [one-particle distribution function](../boltzmann-equation.md#one-particle-distribution-function) for closure; they do not by themselves establish local [thermal equilibrium](../thermal-equilibrium.md) or a gas [equation of state](../pressure.md#equation-of-state).

<h3 id="5/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#5/d)

Use fixed [masses](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) in an isolated Newtonian $N$-body system, with origin at its [centre of mass](../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass). Define the $xx$ moment $I_{xx}=\sum_i m_ix_i^2$ and $K_{xx}=\tfrac12\sum_i m_iv_{x,i}^2$. Twice differentiating gives

$$
\frac12\ddot I_{xx}=\sum_i m_iv_{x,i}^2+\sum_i m_ix_i\dot v_{x,i}=2K_{xx}+W_{xx}.
$$

Insert the gravitational [equation of motion](../classical-mechanics.md#equation-of-motion) and pair each ordered term $(i,k)$ with $(k,i)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{xx}&=-G\sum_i\sum_{k\ne i}\frac{m_im_kx_i(x_i-x_k)}{r_{ik}^3}\\
&=-\frac G2\sum_i\sum_{k\ne i}\frac{m_im_k(x_i-x_k)^2}{r_{ik}^3}
=-G\sum_{i<k}\frac{m_im_k(x_i-x_k)^2}{r_{ik}^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus one component of the [tensor virial theorem](../galaxy.md#tensor-virial-theorem) is

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ddot I_{xx}=2K_{xx}-G\sum_{i<k}\frac{m_im_k(x_i-x_k)^2}{r_{ik}^3}.}
$$

A stationary or time-averaged bounded system has $\langle\ddot I_{xx}\rangle=0$, yielding $2\langle K_{xx}\rangle+\langle W_{xx}\rangle=0$. If ordered and random motions are separated, $2K_{xx}=2T_{xx}+\Pi_{xx}$, where $T_{xx}=\tfrac12\int\rho u_x^2dV$ and $\Pi_{xx}=\int\rho\sigma_{xx}^2dV$. Summing the three diagonal equations recovers the scalar [virial theorem](../classical-mechanics.md#virial-theorem), with the pair [gravitational potential energy](../energy.md#gravitational-energy) counted once.

## 6

↑ **Parent:** [2022](2022.md)

<h3 id="6/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/a)

Treat the long-wavelength nearly uniform spherical perturbation as a separate locally homogeneous expanding region, neglecting pressure gradients and tidal corrections. Its local [Friedmann equation](../cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) is

$$
\boxed{H_s^2+\frac{K}{a_s^2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho_s.}
$$

The curvature term represents the [spatial curvature of an FLRW universe](../cosmology.md#spatial-curvature-of-an-flrw-universe) in the local patch; for the convention $a_s$ dimensionless, $K=kc^2$ has units of inverse time squared. Positive $K$ corresponds to a closed, more tightly bound patch. Here and below the printed $\rho$ is interpreted as [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) or [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) divided by $c^2$; literal SI [energy density](../statistical-physics.md#energy-density) requires $G\rho/c^2$ on the right.

The region generally has its own [scale factor](../cosmology.md#scale-factor-cosmology) and [Hubble parameter](../cosmology.md#hubble-parameter). The two printed equations with an identical $H(t)$ can be compared as a leading-order _equal-expansion_ construction: on a slicing where $H_s=H$, use $a_s=a$ in the already first-order curvature term to obtain $K/a^2=(8\pi G/3)\delta\rho$. This [equal-expansion curvature overdensity](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#equal-expansion-curvature-overdensity) construction does not make them both exact evolution equations with a permanently identical expansion history at common proper time. For example, a growing dust [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) obeys the [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation) $H_s-H=-\dot\delta/3$, which is nonzero when $\delta\propto a$. This explicit example prevents an inconsistent identification of the two local histories.

<h3 id="6/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

Within the equal-expansion construction of [a](#6/a), subtract the flat-background [Friedmann equation](../cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) to obtain

$$
\delta\rho=\frac{3K}{8\pi Ga^2},\qquad
\delta=\frac{3K}{8\pi Ga^2\rho_b}.
$$

The background [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) scales as $\rho_b\propto a^{-4}$ during [radiation domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#radiation-domination) and $\rho_b\propto a^{-3}$ during [matter domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#matter-domination). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\delta\propto a^2\quad(a\ll a_{\rm eq}),\qquad
\delta\propto a\quad(a\gg a_{\rm eq}).}
$$

These are the regular adiabatic growing-mode scalings on scales outside the [Hubble radius](../cosmology.md#hubble-radius), with a specified time slicing. In a consistent comoving description the numerical relation between curvature and [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) differs from the naive subtraction, but the powers agree. The result does not imply that every gauge's [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast), every decaying mode, or subhorizon dark matter during [radiation domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#radiation-domination) has these powers.

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/c)

For $r_s\gg d_H$, a regular adiabatic perturbation follows the large-scale [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) growth in [b](#6/b); this does not require causal communication across the perturbation. Since $d_H=c/H$, the [Friedmann equation](../cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) gives $d_H\propto a^2$ during [radiation domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#radiation-domination) and $d_H\propto a^{3/2}$ during [matter domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#matter-domination). A fixed comoving perturbation has physical size $r_s=aR$, so its ratio to $d_H$ decreases as $a^{-1}$ or $a^{-1/2}$ until [cosmological horizon entry](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#cosmological-horizon-entry).

**The numerical “exactly at equality” assertion is inconsistent with the supplied parameters and $d_H=c/H$.** Normalize $a_0=1$ and use the supplied $\Omega_\gamma$ as the whole radiation component. Then

$$
a_{\rm eq}=\frac{\Omega_\gamma}{\Omega_m}=\frac1{3000},\qquad
H^2=H_0^2\left(\Omega_\gamma a^{-4}+\Omega_ma^{-3}\right).
$$

Both terms must be retained at [matter-radiation equality](../cosmology.md#matter-radiation-equality). The present comoving size corresponding to its [Hubble radius](../cosmology.md#hubble-radius) is

$$
\boxed{R_{H,\rm eq}=\frac{c}{a_{\rm eq}H_{\rm eq}}
=\frac{c}{H_0}\frac{\sqrt{\Omega_\gamma}}{\sqrt2\,\Omega_m}=106.4\ \mathrm{Mpc}.}
$$

Its physical size then is $0.0355\ \mathrm{Mpc}$. A mode with $R=79\ \mathrm{Mpc}$ has physical size only $0.0263\ \mathrm{Mpc}$ at equality, already inside the [Hubble radius](../cosmology.md#hubble-radius). More explicitly, putting $q=RH_0/c$ in $aR=c/H(a)$ gives

$$
a_{\rm ent}=\frac{q^2\Omega_m+\sqrt{q^4\Omega_m^2+4q^2\Omega_\gamma}}2
=2.27\times10^{-4}=0.681a_{\rm eq}.
$$

This is a direct numerical counterexample using the stated data. Neglecting one component exactly at equality instead gives $150.5\ \mathrm{Mpc}$, which also does not produce $79\ \mathrm{Mpc}$. Late-time dark energy is negligible at these early times.

<h3 id="6/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#6/d)

For conserved nonrelativistic [dark matter](../cosmology.md#dark-matter) and [radiation](../physics.md#radiation), $\rho_{\rm DM}\propto a^{-3}$ and $\rho_\gamma\propto a^{-4}$, so $y=\rho_{\rm DM}/\rho_\gamma=a/a_{\rm eq}$ obeys $\dot y=yH$. Denote differentiation with respect to $y$ by a prime. Then

$$
\dot\delta=yH\delta',\qquad
\ddot\delta=y^2H^2\delta''+y(\dot H+H^2)\delta'.
$$

The supplied [Friedmann equations](../cosmology.md#friedmann-equations) imply

$$
\frac{\dot H}{H^2}=-\frac{4+3y}{2(1+y)},\qquad
\frac{4\pi G\rho_{\rm DM}}{H^2}=\frac{3y}{2(1+y)}.
$$

Substitution in the linear growth equation and division by $y^2H^2$ give the [Mészáros equation](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#meszaros-equation)

$$
\boxed{\delta''+\frac{2+3y}{2y(1+y)}\delta'-\frac{3}{2y(1+y)}\delta=0.}
$$

For $D_1=1+3y/2$, $D_1'=3/2$ and $D_1''=0$, making the two remaining terms cancel exactly. For [reduction of order](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#reduction-of-order), put $\delta=fD_1$ and $P=(2+3y)/[2y(1+y)]=1/y+1/[2(1+y)]$. The equation reduces to $f''+(2D_1'/D_1+P)f'=0$, whence

$$
f'=\frac{C}{y\sqrt{1+y}(1+3y/2)^2}.
$$

Using the supplied antiderivative and reversing an irrelevant overall sign gives the independent solution

$$
\boxed{D_2(y)=\left(1+\frac32y\right)\ln\frac{\sqrt{1+y}+1}{\sqrt{1+y}-1}-3\sqrt{1+y},\qquad
\delta=A_1D_1+A_2D_2.}
$$

Its [Wronskian](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#wronskian) is $D_1D_2'-D_1'D_2=-1/[y\sqrt{1+y}]\ne0$. The asymptotic expansions are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&D_1&D_2\\ \hline
y\ll1&1+3y/2&\ln(4/y)-3+O(y\ln(1/y))\\
y\gg1&3y/2+1&\frac4{15}y^{-3/2}+O(y^{-5/2})
\end{array}
$$

Thus subhorizon [dark matter](../cosmology.md#dark-matter) during [radiation domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#radiation-domination) has constant and logarithmic modes, rather than the superhorizon $a^2$ growth of [b](#6/b). A combination with negative $A_2$ supplies positive logarithmic growth. During [matter domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#matter-domination), the growing mode is proportional to $a$, agreeing with [b](#6/b), while the second mode decays as $a^{-3/2}$. These results neglect subhorizon radiation clustering; the [Mészáros equation](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#meszaros-equation) is the approximation stated in the question, not a full photon–matter perturbation system through horizon entry.

<h3 id="6/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#6/e)

For a $1\ \mathrm{Mpc}$ mode, evolve the regular superhorizon [density contrast](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#density-contrast) as $\delta\propto a^2$ until [cosmological horizon entry](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#cosmological-horizon-entry). In the [radiation domination](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#radiation-domination) approximation its entry scale is $a_{\rm ent}\simeq(H_0R/c)\sqrt{\Omega_\gamma}=2.2\times10^{-6}$, well before [matter-radiation equality](../cosmology.md#matter-radiation-equality). At that point use the [Mészáros equation](../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation.md#meszaros-equation) basis of [d](#6/d), fixing both constants by continuity of $\delta$ and $\dot\delta$, equivalently $\delta$ and $d\delta/d\ln a$.

Explicitly, for entry data $\delta_e$ and $\delta'_e=2\delta_e/y_e$, solve

$$
\begin{pmatrix}D_1(y_e)&D_2(y_e)\\D_1'(y_e)&D_2'(y_e)\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_1\\A_2\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_e\\2\delta_e/y_e\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The nonzero [Wronskian](../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md#wronskian) guarantees a unique match. This produces slow logarithmic growth before equality, then $\delta\propto a$ with a decaying transient after equality. For example, at $y_e\ll1$, $A_2\simeq-2\delta_e$ and $A_1\simeq\delta_e+2\delta_e[\ln(4/y_e)-3]$. An abrupt match estimates the growth history; a precise amplitude requires a common gauge and the coupled relativistic perturbation equations through entry. Linear evolution also ceases to apply once $|\delta|$ approaches unity, and late [dark energy](../cosmology.md#dark-energy) further suppresses growth.
