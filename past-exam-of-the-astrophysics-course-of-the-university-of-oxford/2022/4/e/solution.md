<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

One explanation is an upper [electron](../../../../electron.md) [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) imposed by the accelerator: finite age, finite confinement size, escape, or a balance of acceleration and losses limits $\gamma$ to $\gamma_{\max}$. Since the [synchrotron critical frequency](../../../../synchrotron-critical-frequency.md) scales as $\nu_c\propto B\gamma^2\sin\alpha$, the absence of higher-energy [electrons](../../../../electron.md) produces a corresponding high-[frequency](../../../../frequency.md) cutoff, broadened by the single-particle spectrum.

The other is [synchrotron cooling](../../../../synchrotron-cooling.md), with [inverse Compton scattering](../../../../inverse-compton-scattering.md) often contributing: $\dot\gamma=-b\gamma^2$ gives $t_{\rm cool}=1/(b\gamma)$. High-energy [electrons](../../../../electron.md) disappear fastest, so a population of age $t$ develops a cooling scale $\gamma_b\sim1/(bt)$ and $\nu_b\propto B/(b^2t^2)$. A burst population can develop a strong cutoff. With continuous power-law injection, the simplest steady cooling model instead steepens the [electron](../../../../electron.md) [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) index by one and the [synchrotron radiation](../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) spectral index by $1/2$, giving a break rather than necessarily an exponential cutoff. The detailed spectral shape therefore distinguishes finite acceleration from aging and injection history.
