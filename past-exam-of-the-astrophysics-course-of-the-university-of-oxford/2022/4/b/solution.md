<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Diffusive shock acceleration](../../../../diffusive-shock-acceleration.md) is viable in principle at $511\ \mathrm{GeV}$: repeated scattering off magnetic irregularities on opposite sides of a strong nonrelativistic [shock wave](../../../../shock-wave.md) can reach such [energies](../../../../energy-split.md). Actual viability requires a sufficiently large and long-lived accelerator with an acceleration time shorter than its escape and loss times; $\gamma$ alone does not establish those conditions.

Take positive [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) toward the upstream material, and let the front move at $+v_s$ in the upstream rest frame. For a strong adiabatic [shock wave](../../../../shock-wave.md) in a gas with [adiabatic index](../../../../heat-capacity-ratio.md) $5/3$, the [Rankine-Hugoniot conditions](../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) give $\rho_d/\rho_u=4$ and the shock-frame inflow [speeds](../../../../speed.md) satisfy $u_1=v_s$, $u_2=v_s/4$. The signed [velocities](../../../../velocity.md) in the three frames are

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{rest frame}&v_u&v_d&v_{\rm front}\\ \hline
\text{upstream}&0&3v_s/4&v_s\\
\text{downstream}&-3v_s/4&0&v_s/4\\
\text{front}&-v_s&-v_s/4&0
\end{array}
$$

Thus the two scattering media converge at $V=u_1-u_2=3v_s/4$. For a crossing particle with incident [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) $E$ in its _local plasma rest frame_, a [Lorentz transformation](../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) to the approaching other plasma gives

$$
E'=\Gamma_V(E+Vp\mu)=E\left[1+\frac{V}{c}\mu+O(V^2/c^2)\right],
$$

where $\mu>0$ is the incident direction cosine measured toward the approaching medium and $pc\simeq E$. For an almost [isotropic](../../../../isotropy.md) crossing population, flux weighting gives $\langle\mu\rangle=\int_0^1\mu^2d\mu/\int_0^1\mu d\mu=2/3$. Scattering randomizes directions without changing [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in that plasma frame, and the reverse crossing has the same mean gain. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\left\langle\frac{\Delta E}{E}\right\rangle_{\rm crossing}=\frac{2V}{3c},\qquad
\left\langle\frac{\Delta E}{E}\right\rangle_{\rm cycle}=\frac{4V}{3c}=\frac{v_s}{c}.}
$$

These are first-order mean gains. They are not an increase of [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) in one fixed frame caused by an instantaneous boundary crossing. Literally choosing the [electron](../../../../electron.md)'s instantaneous rest frame gives incident $E=m_ec^2$, and the other gas approaches at nearly $c$, so the expansion in small $V/c$ is invalid. This concrete frame check explains why the printed description of $V$ must instead refer to the upstream/downstream scattering frames.
