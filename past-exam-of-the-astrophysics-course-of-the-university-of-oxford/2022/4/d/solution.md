<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Relativistic beaming](../../../../relativistic-beaming.md) concentrates the emission into a forward cone about the instantaneous [velocity](../../../../velocity.md). The [aberration of light](../../../../relativistic-aberration.md) maps a rest-frame ray at $\theta'=\pi/2$ to $\cos\theta=\beta$, so the characteristic cone half-angle is $\theta\simeq\gamma^{-1}$. Thus its conventional full width is $2/\gamma=2\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{rad}$.

**The isotropic-emission assumption has no beam nulls.** For example, its received bolometric angular [power](../../../../power.md) is proportional to $D^4$, where the [Doppler factor](../../../../doppler-factor.md) is $D=[\gamma(1-\beta\cos\theta)]^{-1}>0$. In particular it is nonzero at $\theta=1/\gamma$, disproving a literal null there. A true dipole pattern for an accelerated [electron](../../../../electron.md) is not isotropic in its rest frame and needs a specified polarization and viewing plane. The width just quoted is a characteristic beaming angle, not a null-to-null width under the stated premise.

For a circular orbit seen in its orbital plane, let the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) direction sweep past the observer through $-1/\gamma<\psi<1/\gamma$. The emission interval is $\Delta t_{\rm em}\simeq2/(\gamma\omega_g)=1.14\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm s$. The observed interval must also include light-travel compression:

$$
\Delta t_{\rm obs}=\frac{1}{\omega_g}\int_{-1/\gamma}^{1/\gamma}(1-\beta\cos\psi)d\psi
=\frac{2}{\omega_g}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma}-\beta\sin\frac{1}{\gamma}\right]
\simeq\boxed{\frac{4}{3\omega_g\gamma^3}=7.6\times10^{-15}\ \mathrm s}.
$$

Its ratio to the [gyromotion](../../../../gyromotion.md) period is $\Delta t_{\rm obs}/T_g\simeq2/(3\pi\gamma^3)=2.1\times10^{-19}$. Since the boundary of a smooth beam is conventional, only the scale $\omega_g^{-1}\gamma^{-3}$ is invariant under reasonable pulse-width definitions. For helical motion the viewing geometry and pitch angle are additional inputs; the numerical estimate assumes transverse motion and a sightline intercepted by the cone.[Chapter 5, section 5.1.3: relativistic beaming and the difference between emission duration and pulse arrival duration](../../../../essential-radio-astronomy-by-james-j-condon-and-scott-m-ransom.md)
