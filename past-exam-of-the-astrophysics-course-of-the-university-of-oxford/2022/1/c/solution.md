<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With the [spontaneous emission](../../../../spontaneous-emission.md) term suppressed, $j_\nu=0$. A uniform [absorption coefficient](../../../../absorption-coefficient.md) over length $\ell$ gives directly

$$
\boxed{I_\nu=I_\nu^0e^{-\tau_\nu},\qquad \tau_\nu=\ell\alpha_\nu.}
$$

The normalized [specific intensity](../../../../spectral-radiance.md) deficit is unchanged by conversion between [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md) and [frequency](../../../../frequency.md), since both numerator and denominator acquire the same [Jacobian determinant](../../../../jacobian-determinant.md). The [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) and the [Einstein coefficients](../../../../einstein-coefficients.md) give $n_2B_{21}/(n_1B_{12})=e^{-h\nu_{12}/(k_BT_e)}\ll1$. Consequently, in the [optically thin](../../../../optically-thin-medium.md) limit,

$$
W=\int(1-e^{-\tau_\nu})\frac{c}{\nu^2}\,d\nu
\simeq\frac{hN_1B_{12}}{\nu_{12}}\int\varphi(\nu)d\nu
=\boxed{\frac{hB_{12}N_1}{\nu_{12}}}.
$$

Here $N_1=n_1\ell$ is the [column density](../../../../column-density.md), and $\nu\simeq\nu_{12}$ has been used in slowly varying factors. This linear relation between [equivalent width](../../../../equivalent-width.md) and [column density](../../../../column-density.md) fails once the central [optical depth](../../../../optical-depth.md) becomes appreciable.
