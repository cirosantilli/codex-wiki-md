<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [Einstein coefficients](../../../../einstein-coefficients.md) convention in which $B_{ij}$ multiplies spectral [radiation energy density](../../../../radiation-energy-density.md) $u_\nu$, with $u_\nu=c^{-1}\int I_\nu\,d\Omega$. A normalized [spectral line profile](../../../../spectral-line-profile.md) obeys $\int\varphi(\nu)d\nu=1$. The [spontaneous emission](../../../../spontaneous-emission.md) probability per upper-state atom per unit time is $A_{21}$; the [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) and upward [absorption](../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) rates per atom are respectively $B_{21}\int u_\nu\varphi(\nu)d\nu$ and $B_{12}\int u_\nu\varphi(\nu)d\nu$.

For [isotropic](../../../../isotropy.md) [spontaneous emission](../../../../spontaneous-emission.md), $n_2A_{21}$ transitions occur per unit volume and time, each supplying [photon energy](../../../../photon-energy.md) $h\nu_{12}$. Dividing among $4\pi$ steradians gives

$$
\boxed{j_\nu=\frac{h\nu_{12}}{4\pi}n_2A_{21}\varphi(\nu).}
$$

For a small incident beam, its contribution to $u_\nu$ is $I_\nu d\Omega/c$. [Absorption](../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) removes and [stimulated emission](../../../../stimulated-emission.md) adds [photons](../../../../photon.md) in the incident direction. Matching the net loss to $\alpha_\nu I_\nu d\Omega$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha_\nu=\frac{h\nu_{12}}{c}(n_1B_{12}-n_2B_{21})\varphi(\nu).}
$$

Replacing $\nu_{12}$ by $\nu$ across the narrow [spectral line profile](../../../../spectral-line-profile.md) gives the printed expressions. The negative sign is essential: an inverted population can amplify the incident [specific intensity](../../../../spectral-radiance.md). [Thermal equilibrium](../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md) and the [Planck function](../../../../planck-function.md) imply $g_1B_{12}=g_2B_{21}$ and $A_{21}/B_{21}=8\pi h\nu_{12}^3/c^3$, where $g_i$ is the [state degeneracy](../../../../state-degeneracy.md). A convention using [mean intensity](../../../../mean-intensity.md) instead changes both $B$ coefficients by $4\pi/c$.
