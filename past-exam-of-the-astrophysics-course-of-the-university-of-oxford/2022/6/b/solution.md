<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Within the equal-expansion construction of [a](../a.md), subtract the flat-background [Friedmann equation](../../../../friedmann-equations.md) to obtain

$$
\delta\rho=\frac{3K}{8\pi Ga^2},\qquad
\delta=\frac{3K}{8\pi Ga^2\rho_b}.
$$

The background [density](../../../../density.md) scales as $\rho_b\propto a^{-4}$ during [radiation domination](../../../../radiation-domination.md) and $\rho_b\propto a^{-3}$ during [matter domination](../../../../matter-domination.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\delta\propto a^2\quad(a\ll a_{\rm eq}),\qquad
\delta\propto a\quad(a\gg a_{\rm eq}).}
$$

These are the regular adiabatic growing-mode scalings on scales outside the [Hubble radius](../../../../hubble-radius.md), with a specified time slicing. In a consistent comoving description the numerical relation between curvature and [density contrast](../../../../density-contrast.md) differs from the naive subtraction, but the powers agree. The result does not imply that every gauge's [density contrast](../../../../density-contrast.md), every decaying mode, or subhorizon dark matter during [radiation domination](../../../../radiation-domination.md) has these powers.
