<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Jeans equations](../../../../jeans-equation.md) express local momentum conservation for a [collisionless stellar system](../../../../collisionless-stellar-system.md), just as the [Euler equations](../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid-split.md) do for a gas. The mean streaming [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) plays the role of fluid flow, the gravitational force is a body force, and random stellar motions supply a [pressure tensor](../../../../pressure-tensor.md) through $\rho\sigma_{ij}^2$. Taking moments smooths over individual stellar orbits and produces fields of [density](../../../../density.md), mean [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) and random-motion stress; this is the sense of “stellar hydrodynamics”.

Ordinary gas [pressure](../../../../pressure-split.md) is usually nearly isotropic because collisions relax its local [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) distribution. A [collisionless stellar system](../../../../collisionless-stellar-system.md) can retain an anisotropic [velocity ellipsoid](../../../../velocity-ellipsoid.md), so its [pressure tensor](../../../../pressure-tensor.md) is not generally a scalar times the identity. There are more unknown second moments than momentum equations, and their evolution introduces higher moments. The [Jeans equations](../../../../jeans-equation.md) therefore require further dynamical assumptions or a specified [one-particle distribution function](../../../../one-particle-distribution-function.md) for closure; they do not by themselves establish local [thermal equilibrium](../../../../thermal-equilibrium-split.md) or a gas [equation of state](../../../../equation-of-state.md).
