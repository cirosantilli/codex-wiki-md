<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Primordial [density contrasts](../../../../density-contrast.md) perturb the intrinsic photon [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), the [gravitational potential](../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md), and the [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) of the last-scattering material. Intrinsic photon-density fluctuations obey $\delta T/T=\delta_\gamma/4$ because $\rho_\gamma\propto T^4$; adiabatic initial conditions relate these to matter [density contrasts](../../../../density-contrast.md). Subsequent acoustic evolution, [Doppler effect](../../../../doppler-effect.md) and gravitational frequency shifts determine the observed [Cosmic microwave background anisotropy](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy-split.md). There is no scale-independent identification of the final sky map with matter [density contrast](../../../../density-contrast.md) alone.

The [spherical harmonic](../../../../spherical-harmonic.md) coefficients are random variables inherited from these perturbations. After subtracting the mean sky [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), $\langle a_{lm}\rangle=0$ for the anisotropy modes. Gaussian primordial perturbations give approximately [Gaussian random variables](../../../../gaussian-random-variable.md); this extra Gaussian assumption is not needed for diagonal covariance. Reality of the sky map implies $a_{l,-m}=(-1)^ma_{lm}^*$.

For a statistically isotropic sky, its two-point function depends only on $\hat n\cdot\hat m$. Rotations mix the $m$ components at fixed $l$ and do not mix different irreducible $l$ spaces. A rotation-invariant [covariance matrix](../../../../covariance-matrix.md) is therefore a multiple of the identity in each $l$ block:

$$
\boxed{\langle a_{lm}a_{l'm'}^*\rangle=C_l\delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}.}
$$

On our one sky, average the $2l+1$ angular components at fixed $l$, or equivalently suitable rotated sky pairs, rather than treating different universes as observed samples. The [CMB angular power spectrum](../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-power-spectrum.md) is estimated by

$$
\boxed{\widehat C_l=\frac{1}{2l+1}\sum_{m=-l}^l|a_{lm}|^2.}
$$

Under the Gaussian, full-sky, noiseless assumptions, there are $2l+1$ independent real degrees of freedom and $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat C_l)=2C_l^2/(2l+1)$. This [cosmic variance](../../../../cosmic-variance.md) remains even with perfect detectors; a finite sky mask and detector noise add further uncertainty. The estimator is unbiased, $\langle\widehat C_l\rangle=C_l$, but does not turn one realization into the exact ensemble mean.
