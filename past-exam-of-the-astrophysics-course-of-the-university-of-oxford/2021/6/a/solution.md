<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a spherical [mass](../../../../mass.md) distribution, the [shell theorem](../../../../spherical-shell-theorem.md) says the circular gravitational acceleration is $GM(<R)/R^2$. Balancing it against the centripetal acceleration $V_c^2/R$ gives

$$
M(<R)=\frac{RV_c^2(R)}G.
$$

At $R=16\ \mathrm{kpc}=4r_0$, the [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) gives $V_c=200(1-e^{-4})=196.337\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. With $R=4.93708\times10^{20}\ \mathrm m$,

$$
\boxed{M(<16\ \mathrm{kpc})=2.8515\times10^{41}\ \mathrm{kg}=1.4340\times10^{11}M_\odot.}
$$

This is the total dynamically inferred enclosed [mass](../../../../mass.md), including any [dark matter](../../../../dark-matter.md); the [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) alone does not separate its components.
