<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md) has $a\propto t^{2/3}$ and $H=\dot a/a\propto a^{-3/2}$. Normalizing $a_0=1$ therefore gives $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3/2}$. Differentiating $1+z=1/a$ gives

$$
\frac{dt}{dz}=-\frac1{(1+z)H(z)}=-\frac1{H_0(1+z)^{5/2}}.
$$

In the local comoving rest frame, a [photon](../../../../photon.md) crosses proper path length $d\ell=c|dt|$. Galaxies with proper [number density](../../../../number-density.md) $n(z)$ and geometric [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) $\sigma_G=\pi r_G^2$ contribute the mean number of intersections $d\tau=n(z)\sigma_Gd\ell$. The infinitesimal probability of an intersection is the same to first order, giving

$$
\boxed{dP=d\tau=\frac{c\pi r_G^2n(z)}{H_0(1+z)^{5/2}}\,dz.}
$$

The radius is a physical radius, and $n(z)$ is a proper [number density](../../../../number-density.md) evaluated at epoch $z$, rather than a density already multiplied by $dz$. For a finite path with overlaps, the integrated mean count $\tau$ need not itself be the probability of at least one intersection.
