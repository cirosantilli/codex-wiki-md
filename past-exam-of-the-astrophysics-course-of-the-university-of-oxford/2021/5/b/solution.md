For corresponding populations whose intrinsic [absolute magnitudes](../../../../absolute-magnitude.md) are calibrated to match, the [distance modulus](../../../../distance-modulus.md) in the same filter is

$$
m-M=5\log_{10}\frac D{10\ \mathrm{pc}}+A,
$$

where $A$ is [interstellar extinction](../../../../interstellar-extinction.md) in that filter. Subtracting the two equations yields

$$
\boxed{\frac{D_2}{D_1}=10^{[\Delta m-\Delta M-\Delta A]/5}.}
$$

Here $\Delta m=m_2-m_1$, $\Delta M=M_2-M_1$, and $\Delta A=A_2-A_1$. For a matched [horizontal branch](../../../../horizontal-branch.md) after a [metallicity](../../../../metallicity.md) correction, or matched unevolved [main sequences](../../../../main-sequence.md) after correcting colour and composition, $\Delta M\simeq0$. Neglecting differential [interstellar extinction](../../../../interstellar-extinction.md) then gives $D_2/D_1\simeq10^{\Delta m/5}$. A one-[astronomical magnitude](../../../../astronomical-magnitude.md) shift would mean a factor $1.585$ in distance, illustrating the calibration rather than measuring these panels.

**A defensible numerical ratio cannot be extracted from the surviving figure.** The corresponding sequences and most of the vertical scales are hidden by the corrupted image. Different bottom-axis labels or limiting [astronomical magnitudes](../../../../astronomical-magnitude.md) do not establish a common-population vertical offset: exposure depth and intrinsic population differences can also change them. An intact figure or numerical [photometry](../../../../photometry-optics.md) is needed to measure $\Delta m$, then correct $\Delta M$ and $\Delta A$. The equation above is the quantitative inference supported by the available source; inventing an offset would not solve the missing-data problem.
