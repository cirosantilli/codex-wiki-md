<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Doppler factors](../../../../doppler-factor.md) for the two discrete blobs are $\mathcal D_\pm=[\gamma(1\mp\beta\cos\theta)]^{-1}$. Their ratio is $(1+x)/(1-x)=77/33=7/3$. [Lorentz-invariant specific intensity](../../../../lorentz-invariant-specific-intensity.md) means $I_\nu/\nu^3$ is unchanged; with $\nu=\mathcal D\nu'$ and the same rest-frame [power-law spectrum](../../../../power-law-spectrum.md),

$$
I_\nu(\nu)=\mathcal D^3I'_{\nu'}(\nu/\mathcal D)
\propto\mathcal D^{3+\alpha}\nu^{-\alpha}.
$$

The brightness ratio at the common observed [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) therefore gives

$$
24=\left(\frac73\right)^{3+\alpha},\qquad
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\ln24}{\ln(7/3)}-3=0.75081.}
$$

The stated identical intrinsic [specific intensities](../../../../spectral-radiance.md) must refer to comparable intrinsic states. If the blobs fade with their proper ages, equal observer times sample different ages and need that correction. A steady continuous [jet](../../../../jet-particle-physics.md) has a different boosting exponent, $2+\alpha$; the discrete ejecta and the specified [specific intensity](../../../../spectral-radiance.md) transformation justify $3+\alpha$ here.

For [optically thin](../../../../optically-thin-medium.md) [synchrotron radiation](../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) from an isotropic [electron](../../../../electron.md) distribution $dN/dE\propto E^{-p}$, the characteristic emitted [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) scales as $BE^2$ and the total single-particle [power](../../../../power.md) as $B^2E^2$. Thus $j_\nu\propto E^{-p}E^2/(d\nu/dE)\propto\nu^{-(p-1)/2}$; integration over the full single-particle spectrum preserves this exponent. Hence

$$
\boxed{p=2\alpha+1\simeq2.50.}
$$

The test-particle strong nonrelativistic [diffusive shock acceleration](../../../../diffusive-shock-acceleration.md) prediction is $p=(r+2)/(r-1)=2$ for compression ratio $r=4$, giving $\alpha=0.5$. The inferred spectrum is steeper. If interpreted literally within that uncooled formula, $p=2.50$ would correspond to $r\simeq3$, but cooling, escape, and other acceleration conditions can also steepen it.

The observed Galactic [cosmic ray](../../../../cosmic-ray.md) differential [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) spectrum below the knee is approximately $E^{-2.7}$, steeper still; its formal [synchrotron radiation](../../../../synchrotron-radiation.md) equivalent would be $\alpha\simeq0.85$. Thus $p\simeq2.50$ lies between the simplest source prediction and the propagated [cosmic ray](../../../../cosmic-ray.md) slope. The comparison is qualitative: most measured [cosmic rays](../../../../cosmic-ray.md) are nuclei, whereas the radio slope here probes [electrons](../../../../electron.md), and propagation and radiative losses alter the spectra differently.
