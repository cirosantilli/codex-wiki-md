<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Apply the same symmetric-ejection and [causality](../../../../causality.md) argument to the launch [proper motions](../../../../proper-motion.md), when the proposed ejecta are fastest. One obtains

$$
x_0=\frac{101-59}{101+59}=0.2625,
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\max}=\arccos(0.2625)=74.78^\circ,\qquad
D_{\max}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{101\times59}\ \mathrm{mas\,day^{-1}}}=2.243\ \mathrm{kpc}.}
$$

The fitted angular decelerations describe subsequent evolution; they do not relax the bound already imposed at launch. They are observer-time phenomenological coefficients, not directly identical physical decelerations in the source frame.

The [stellar parallax](../../../../stellar-parallax.md) distance $2.96\pm0.33\ \mathrm{kpc}$ is compatible with the constant-[velocity](../../../../velocity.md) bound $3.435\ \mathrm{kpc}$, but its central value exceeds the decelerating-launch bound by $0.717\ \mathrm{kpc}$, or about $2.17$ quoted standard deviations if the uncertainty is treated as symmetric Gaussian. Even its one-standard-deviation lower endpoint, $2.63\ \mathrm{kpc}$, exceeds $2.243\ \mathrm{kpc}$. At $D=2.96\ \mathrm{kpc}$ the respective inferred intrinsic [speeds](../../../../speed.md) are $\beta=0.885$ and $\beta=1.300$. The latter is a concrete violation of [causality](../../../../causality.md) under the symmetric model assumptions.

Thus the supplied distance favours the constant-[velocity](../../../../velocity.md) fit. This is a consistency comparison, not a full statistical model selection: uncertainties in the fitted [proper motions](../../../../proper-motion.md), their correlations, intrinsic asymmetry and the distance likelihood would be required for that.
