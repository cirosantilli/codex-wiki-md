<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Work in the source rest frame and neglect the source's small [cosmological redshift](../../../../cosmological-redshift.md). Over an emission interval $\Delta t$, a ballistic [relativistic jet](../../../../relativistic-jet.md) feature travels $\beta c\Delta t$, advancing $\beta c\Delta t\cos\theta$ toward the observer and $\beta c\Delta t\sin\theta$ across the sky. The second [photon](../../../../photon.md) has a shorter journey, so its arrival is delayed by only

$$
\Delta t_{\rm obs}=\Delta t(1-\beta\cos\theta).
$$

Dividing the projected displacement by this arrival interval gives

$$
\boxed{\beta_{\rm app}=\frac{\beta\sin\theta}{1-\beta\cos\theta}.}
$$

[Apparent superluminal motion](../../../../apparent-superluminal-motion.md) is a combination of projection and light-travel delay. The feature's local [speed](../../../../speed.md) remains $\beta c<c$; its inferred transverse displacement divided by the observer's arrival interval is not a local signal [velocity](../../../../velocity.md).
