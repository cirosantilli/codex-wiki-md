<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the [Double-degenerate Type Ia supernova scenario](../../../../double-degenerate-type-ia-supernova-scenario.md), measure the [Type Ia supernova](../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) rate per formed stellar mass in old galaxies, grouped by the time since their main star-formation episode. Correct the event counts for observing time and detection efficiency. In an ideal burst population, the normalized rate measures the [delay-time distribution](../../../../delay-time-distribution.md) directly. If [gravitational waves](../../../../gravitational-wave-split.md) inspiral dominates the delay and the initial white-dwarf separations are uniformly distributed in separation, [c](../c.md) predicts a slope $-3/4$.

More generally, if $dN/dA\propto A^\beta$ and $t_m\propto A^4$, the predicted rate scales as $t_m^{(\beta-3)/4}$. A distribution uniform in $\log A$ has $\beta=-1$ and gives $t_m^{-1}$. Thus a slope differing from $-3/4$ does not alone rule out mergers: the separation distribution must also be tested.

An extended [star formation history](../../../../star-formation-history.md) gives a convolution, $R(t)=\int d\tau\,\mathrm{SFR}(t-\tau)\Psi(\tau)$, rather than the single-burst expression. The time since [star formation](../../../../star-formation.md) stopped need not equal each progenitor's age. Stellar lifetimes before formation of the second [white dwarf](../../../../white-dwarf.md), common-envelope evolution, masses and mass ratios, metallicity, delay-dependent explosion efficiencies, galaxy-age estimates and survey selection all affect the comparison. Some CO-white-dwarf mergers need not make [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../type-ia-supernova.md), and other channels can contribute to the observed rate. Agreement in slope and normalization therefore supports the hypothesis under stated assumptions but is not a unique identification of the channel.
