<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the stated $120\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ as the radius of a complete, volume-limited search, with uniform galaxy [density](../../../../density.md) $n_g$. Its surveyed volume and number of galaxies are

$$
V=\frac{4\pi}{3}(120\,\mathrm{Mpc})^3=7.24\times10^6\,\mathrm{Mpc^3},\qquad
N_g=n_gV=7.24\times10^4.
$$

The observing time is $\Delta t=117/365.25=0.3203\,\mathrm{yr}$. One detection estimates

$$
\boxed{\nu_{\mathrm{BNS}}\simeq\frac1{N_g\Delta t}
=4.31\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}\,galaxy^{-1}},}
$$

or one merger per approximately $2.3\times10^4$ years per Milky-Way-type galaxy. The equivalent volume rate is about $431\,\mathrm{Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}$. The event's actual $40\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ distance is not the survey radius and must not replace $120\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ in the exposure.

A representative Galactic [core-collapse supernova](../../../../core-collapse-supernova.md) rate is about two per century, roughly $4.6\times10^2$ times larger than this merger-rate estimate. The historical $^{26}\mathrm{Al}$ estimate was $1.9\pm1.1$ per century.[Abstract: Galactic core-collapse rate inferred from radioactive aluminium](../../../../radioactive-26al-and-massive-stars-in-the-galaxy-by-roland-diehl-and-others.md) The one-event [Poisson](../../../../poisson-distribution.md) uncertainty is large. Detector orientation, duty cycle, completeness and whether the quoted radius is an optimal horizon or an effective range also change the exposure; the boxed value is the specified simple-volume estimate.
