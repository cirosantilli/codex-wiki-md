<h1 id="5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\alpha$ be the reduced, observed angular displacement between source and image. In the small-angle lens geometry, the physical bending angle is weighted by the lens-to-source and observer-to-source [angular diameter distances](../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md):

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{D_{LS}}{D_S}\,\xi.}
$$

In a Euclidean diagram this follows because the transverse displacement at the source plane is $D_{LS}\xi$, whose observed angle is that displacement divided by $D_S$. In cosmology the same thin-lens expression uses the corresponding [angular diameter distances](../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md). For a signed one-dimensional coordinate, the direction is supplied by the image's side of the lens.
