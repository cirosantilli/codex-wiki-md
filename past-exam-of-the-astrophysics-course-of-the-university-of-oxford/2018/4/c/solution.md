<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The infrared and optical transitions share the same upper level 3. Using the supplied infrared photon [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) gives

$$
n_3=\frac{1.5\times10^{-21}}{(3.3\times10^{-7})hc/(314\times10^{-6})}
=7.185\times10^6\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.
$$

With the same $T_e,n_e$ and three-level fraction $f_3$ used in the abundance calculation of [b](../b.md),

$$
\boxed{n(\mathrm S^+)=5.31\times10^7\,\mathrm{m^{-3}},\qquad
\frac{n(\mathrm S^+)}{n_e}=5.31\times10^{-5}.}
$$

This exceeds the optical abundance by a factor $5.53$. Because the upper level is common, its population cancels in the intrinsic emissivity ratio:

$$
\frac{j_{32}}{j_{31}}=\frac{A_{32}\nu_{32}}{A_{31}\nu_{31}}
=2.715\times10^{-6}.
$$

The observed ratio is $1.5\times10^{-5}$. Thus changing only the [electron temperature](../../../../electron-temperature.md) or a common level-population correction cannot reconcile these two lines from the same gas. Dust extinction suppressing the optical line is a natural explanation. If the far-infrared attenuation is negligible, the required optical attenuation is

$$
\boxed{A_{671.6}=2.5\log_{10}(5.53)\simeq1.86\ \mathrm{mag}.}
$$

Different apertures, filling factors, optical-depth assumptions or emissivity calibrations could also cause a discrepancy. A total sulfur abundance would still need an [ionization](../../../../ionization.md) correction beyond this ionic comparison.
