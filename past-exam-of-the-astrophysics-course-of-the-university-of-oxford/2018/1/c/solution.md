<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A massive main-sequence donor loses material in a [stellar wind](../../../../stellar-wind.md). Gravitational capture by the [neutron star](../../../../neutron-star.md) can produce a nearly radial, supersonic infall when the captured material has little specific [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md). A [magnetic field](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) may channel the final infall toward polar [accretion columns](../../../../accretion-column.md), where a standing [shock](../../../../shock-wave.md) decelerates the flow. Large [angular momentum](../../../../angular-momentum.md) instead favours an [accretion disk](../../../../accretion-disk.md); strictly radial accretion is an idealization.

For [mean particle mass](../../../../mean-particle-mass.md) $\mu$, the [ideal gas law](../../../../ideal-gas-law.md) is $p_{\mathrm d}=\rho_{\mathrm d}k_{\mathrm B}T_{\mathrm d}/\mu$. Combining it with [b](../b.md) gives

$$
\boxed{k_{\mathrm B}T_{\mathrm d}=\frac{\mu p_{\mathrm d}}{\rho_{\mathrm d}}
=\frac3{16}\mu v_{\mathrm u}^2.}
$$

For Newtonian [free fall](../../../../free-fall.md) from a distance large compared with the stellar radius $R_*$, $v_{\mathrm u}^2\simeq2GM_*/R_*$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_{\mathrm d}\simeq\frac{3\mu GM_*}{8k_{\mathrm B}R_*},\qquad
k_{\mathrm B}T_{\mathrm d}\simeq\frac{3\mu GM_*}{8R_*}.}
$$

**TODO: a unique numerical [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and characteristic photon band require source data not supplied here.** The held question gives $M_*=1.4M_\odot$ but gives neither $R_*$ nor a composition fixing $\mu$. The mass alone does not fix the free-fall speed or the [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). For example, doubling $R_*$ at the same mass and composition halves both quantities in the last box.

A normalization, rather than an additional claimed datum, makes the scale explicit:

$$
T_{\mathrm d}=8.44\times10^{11}\,\mathrm K
\left(\frac{\mu}{m_p}\right)
\left(\frac{M_*}{1.4M_\odot}\right)
\left(\frac{10\,\mathrm{km}}{R_*}\right),
$$



$$
\boxed{k_{\mathrm B}T_{\mathrm d}=7.27\times10^7\,\mathrm{eV}
\left(\frac{\mu}{m_p}\right)
\left(\frac{M_*}{1.4M_\odot}\right)
\left(\frac{10\,\mathrm{km}}{R_*}\right).}
$$

Here $k_{\mathrm B}T$ is the characteristic particle [energy](../../../../energy-split.md); the mean translational [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) of a nonrelativistic thermal particle is $3k_{\mathrm B}T/2$. If one additionally chose $R_*=10\,\mathrm{km}$ and $\mu=m_p$, this ideal one-temperature estimate would correspond to gamma-ray-scale thermal photon energies. Those are conditional choices, not information recovered from the source. Real accreting [neutron stars](../../../../neutron-star.md) commonly emit [X-rays](../../../../x-ray.md): electron-ion disequilibrium, [radiative cooling](../../../../radiative-cooling.md), [radiation pressure](../../../../radiation-pressure.md), reprocessing and relativistic infall invalidate a direct identification of this ideal ion-heating estimate with the escaping photon spectrum.
