<h1 id="8/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8](../8.md)

A [core-collapse supernova](../../../core-collapse-supernova.md) occurs when the core of a massive evolved star loses stable [pressure](../../../pressure-split.md) support and collapses. Iron-group nuclei no longer provide an exothermic fusion route, while [electron](../../../electron.md) capture and photodisintegration reduce [electron](../../../electron.md) [pressure](../../../pressure-split.md) and consume [energy](../../../energy-split.md). Collapse accelerates until nuclear-density matter stiffens the inner core and launches a shock. That shock loses [energy](../../../energy-split.md) to dissociation and neutrinos and can stall. Neutrino heating, aided by multidimensional flow, can revive it; sufficiently rapid rotation and magnetic stresses provide other possible channels. Most of the liberated compact-object binding [energy](../../../energy-split.md) escapes as neutrinos rather than optical light or ejecta kinetic [energy](../../../energy-split.md).

Successful explosions can leave [neutron stars](../../../neutron-star.md), with additional fallback sometimes producing [black holes](../../../black-hole-split.md). Failed explosions or very substantial fallback can form [black holes](../../../black-hole-split.md) more directly. Important theoretical uncertainties include progenitor structure and [mass](../../../mass.md) loss, nuclear equation of state, neutrino transport and interaction rates, convection and shock instabilities, rotation, [magnetic fields](../../../magnetic-field-split.md) and the relation between these effects and explosion asymmetry. A successful shock launch alone does not guarantee unbinding all overlying layers.

For an initially uniform-density iron core, $r(m)=[3m/(4\pi\rho)]^{1/3}$. Assemble spherical shells from infinity:

$$
E_{\rm grav}=-\int_0^{M_{\rm Fe}}\frac{Gm}{r(m)}\,dm
=-\frac35G\left(\frac{4\pi\rho}{3}\right)^{1/3}M_{\rm Fe}^{5/3}
=-\frac{3GM_{\rm Fe}^2}{5R_{\rm Fe}}.
$$

At the supplied density $3\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, the numerical coefficient is about $6.3\times10^{43}\,\mathrm J$, consistent with the printed rounded estimate:

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm grav}\simeq-6\times10^{43}\left(\frac{M_{\rm Fe}}{M_\odot}\right)^{5/3}\,\mathrm J.}
$$

The negative sign represents the work recovered in assembling the bound core.

The [ejecta binding energy of an initially uniform core](../../../ejecta-binding-energy-of-an-initially-uniform-core.md) is the negative gravitational [energy](../../../energy-split.md) of the outer shells in the original core, including their interaction with the material inside them. It is not the final [neutron star](../../../neutron-star.md)'s entire binding [energy](../../../energy-split.md). With $m_c=M_c/M_\odot=1.4$ and $d=\Delta M/M_\odot$, the shell integral gives

$$
E_{\rm bind,ej}\simeq6\times10^{43}\left[(1.4+d)^{5/3}-1.4^{5/3}\right]\,\mathrm J.
$$

Adding the supplied dissociation cost, $E_{\rm diss}=10^{45}d\,\mathrm J$, gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
d&E_{\rm bind,ej}\ (\mathrm J)&E_{\rm diss}\ (\mathrm J)&E_{\rm required}\ (\mathrm J)\\\hline
10^{-3}&1.25\times10^{41}&1.00\times10^{42}&1.13\times10^{42}\\
0.1&1.28\times10^{43}&1.00\times10^{44}&1.13\times10^{44}\\
1&1.53\times10^{44}&1.00\times10^{45}&1.15\times10^{45}
\end{array}
$$

The calculation assumes the initial common density for the shell binding, all the indicated outer iron encounters the dissociating shock, and dissociation is charged as the stated [energy](../../../energy-split.md) cost. It does not subtract the much larger final compact-object binding [energy](../../../energy-split.md) as though all that [energy](../../../energy-split.md) were directly available to the ejecta. Later recombination can return some nuclear binding [energy](../../../energy-split.md), and overlying noniron layers add their own [energy](../../../energy-split.md) requirements, so the table is a simplified shock-[energy](../../../energy-split.md) budget.

Compared with a characteristic $10^{44}\,\mathrm J$ explosion, ejecting a very thin remaining core layer is relatively easy in this estimate; $0.1M_\odot$ is already demanding, and $1M_\odot$ requires substantially more [energy](../../../energy-split.md). More massive tightly bound cores therefore tend to make shock revival and complete ejection harder, increasing the possibility of fallback or failed explosions and black-hole remnants. A thin steep-gradient outer layer may allow a rapid, relatively weak explosion and a surviving [neutron star](../../../neutron-star.md).

A natal kick requires asymmetry of ejecta, neutrino emission or subsequent gravitational interaction, not merely a large scalar [energy](../../../energy-split.md) budget. Rapid relatively symmetric low-ejecta explosions can produce small kicks; a shock that develops strong asymmetries can give larger neutron-star kicks, while direct collapse need not do so. A schematic momentum estimate is $v_{\rm kick}\sim\epsilon_{\rm asym}\sqrt{2M_{\rm ej}E_{\rm kin}}/M_c$, but neither the asymmetry nor its direction is fixed by the three core [masses](../../../mass.md). The source data support tendencies, not a unique mapping from iron-core [mass](../../../mass.md) to remnant kick.
