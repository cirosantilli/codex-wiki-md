# Heat bath

↑ **Parent:** [Thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_bath)

A heat bath, or thermal reservoir, is an idealized system so large that exchanging a finite amount of [heat](heat-split.md) does not appreciably change its [temperature](temperature-split.md).

The ideal [heat bath](heat-bath.md) supplies a fixed absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T_b$ while its [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) changes through [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md). A real body's suitability as a [heat bath](heat-bath.md) depends on its [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) relative to the exchange being studied, and on how quickly its internal [temperature gradients](temperature-gradient.md) disappear. Large physical size alone supplies neither condition. A [finite heat bath](finite-heat-bath.md) makes the approximation quantitative; a [regulated calibration bath](regulated-calibration-bath.md) instead uses [feedback control](closed-loop-control.md) to maintain its [temperature](temperature-split.md) by exchanging [energy](energy-split.md) with external equipment.

For a weakly coupled system that exchanges [energy](energy-split.md) but no matter with an ideal [heat bath](heat-bath.md), the [canonical ensemble](canonical-ensemble-split.md) assigns the [Boltzmann distribution](boltzmann-distribution.md)

$$
p_n=\frac{e^{-E_n/(k_BT_b)}}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_m e^{-E_m/(k_BT_b)}.
$$

Here $E_n$ is a [microstate](microstate.md)'s [energy](energy-split.md), $k_B$ the [Boltzmann constant](boltzmann-constant.md) and $Z$ the [partition function](canonical-partition-function.md). The fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) removes the need to track every exchange with the environment. A substantially changing bath [temperature](temperature-split.md) no longer gives this one fixed [Boltzmann distribution](boltzmann-distribution.md).[Statistical Physics, §1.3, printed pp. 17–18, equation (1.19): reservoir assumption and canonical probabilities](statistical-physics-by-david-tong/1-the-fundamentals-of-statistical-mechanics.md)
