# Kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow

↑ **Parent:** [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md)

For a fixed geometry, the steady [Stokes equation](stokes-equation.md) maps $(\mathbf u,p',\mathbf f)$ to $(-\mathbf u,-p',-\mathbf f)$ when all imposed boundary [velocities](velocity.md) and disturbance [forces](force.md) are reversed; $p'$ is [pressure](pressure.md) relative to any unchanged [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md). [Linearity of Stokes flow](linearity-of-stokes-flow.md) and [Uniqueness of Stokes flow](uniqueness-of-stokes-flow.md) therefore reverse the entire [velocity field](velocity-field.md). If the driving schedule and evolving geometry are retraced, fluid elements retrace their paths too. Reversing just one drive while leaving another applied [force](force.md) unchanged does not meet these conditions.

This reversal concerns the paths, while [viscous dissipation](viscous-dissipation.md) remains positive: for a [sphere](sphere.md), the required [power](power.md) $6\pi\mu aU^2$ is unchanged by $U\mapsto-U$. A reversed experiment must supply fresh [energy](energy.md). [Inertia](inertia.md), [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md) and irreversible contact can prevent perfect retracing. Edward M. Purcell illustrated the mechanism with a [viscous fluid flow](viscous-fluid-flow-split.md) demonstration in [Life at low Reynolds number, American Journal of Physics 45, 3–11 (1977)](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mdunphy/purcell.pdf).
