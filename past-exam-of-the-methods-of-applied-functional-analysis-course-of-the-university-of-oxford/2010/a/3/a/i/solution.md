The real [Lax-Milgram theorem](../../../../../../lax-milgram-theorem.md) states: let $H$ be a [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) and let $a:H\times H\to\mathbb R$ be a [bounded bilinear form](../../../../../../bounded-bilinear-form.md) such that $a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_H^2$ for some $\alpha>0$ and every $v\in H$. For every bounded [linear functional](../../../../../../linear-functional.md) $\ell\in H^*$ there exists a unique $u\in H$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad(v\in H),\qquad\|u\|_H\leq\alpha^{-1}\|\ell\|_{H^*}.}
$$

Symmetry of $a$ is not required. The complex version replaces bilinearity by sesquilinearity and the lower bound by $\operatorname{Re}a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_H^2$.
