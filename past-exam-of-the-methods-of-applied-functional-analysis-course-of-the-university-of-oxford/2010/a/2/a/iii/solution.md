↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations](../../../../../../fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations.md) says that if $h\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ and $\int h\varphi=0$ for every $\varphi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$, then $h=0$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md).

If $v_1,v_2$ both represent $D^\alpha u$, subtracting their defining identities gives $\int(v_1-v_2)\varphi=0$ for every [test function](../../../../../../test-function.md). The lemma gives $v_1=v_2$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), proving uniqueness. If $u\in C^1$, part (i) gives exactly the defining identity with $v=\partial_ju$. Since this classical derivative is locally integrable, it is the unique [weak derivative](../../../../../../weak-derivative.md) $D_ju$, up to equality [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md).
