<h1 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Using the [dual pairing](../../../../../dual-pairing.md) between $X^*$ and $X$, a [monotone operator](../../../../../monotone-operator.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle\geq0\quad(u,v\in M).}
$$

A [nonlinear coercive operator](../../../../../nonlinear-coercive-operator.md) with respect to $u_0$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\frac{\langle A(u),u-u_0\rangle}{\|u-u_0\|}\longrightarrow+\infty\quad\text{as }u\in M,\ \|u-u_0\|\to\infty.}
$$

The [directional derivative](../../../../../directional-derivative.md) is $\partial_vF(u)=\lim_{t\to0}[F(u+tv)-F(u)]/t$, when this finite limit exists through admissible points. At a boundary point of $M$ only an inward direction may be admissible; then one uses the corresponding one-sided limit.

If $A:X\to X^*$ is monotone and $s<t$, apply its defining inequality at $u+tv$ and $u+sv$. It becomes $(t-s)[\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)]\geq0$, so $\varphi$ is nondecreasing. Conversely choose $u=y$, $v=x-y$. Nondecrease from zero to one gives $\langle A(x)-A(y),x-y\rangle\geq0$. This proves the [line criterion for a monotone operator](../../../../../line-criterion-for-a-monotone-operator.md).
