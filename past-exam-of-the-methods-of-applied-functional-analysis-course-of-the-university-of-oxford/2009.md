The [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) condition is

$$
\boxed{\int_\Omega u\,\partial_i\phi\,dx=-\int_\Omega v\phi\,dx\qquad(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)).}
$$

Both [integrals](../integral.md) exist since $u,v$ are [locally integrable](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) and the [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) has [compact support](../function.md#compact-support). [Derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) are defined up to almost-everywhere equality. The space $W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ consists of $u\in L^p(\Omega)$ whose first [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) $D_i u$ all lie in $L^p(\Omega)$. One standard [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is

$$
\|u\|_{1,p}=\left(\|u\|_p^p+\sum_{i=1}^n\|D_i u\|_p^p\right)^{1/p}\quad(p<\infty),\qquad
\|u\|_{1,\infty}=\max(\|u\|_\infty,\|D_1u\|_\infty,\ldots,\|D_nu\|_\infty).
$$

A sum in the last formula gives an equivalent [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). For restriction to $E$, extend any $\phi\in C_c^\infty(E)$ by zero to $\Omega$. Its support is compactly contained in $E$, so the extension is smooth. The identity over $\Omega$ then reduces exactly to the identity over $E$, proving locality of the [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative).

For the [jump contribution to a piecewise weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#jump-contribution-to-a-piecewise-weak-derivative), [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) on the two sides of the joining point gives the [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative)

$$
Du=2x\,\mathbf1_{(0,1)}+\mathbf1_{(1,2)}+(a-1)\delta_1.
$$

The coefficient is the right [trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) $1+a$ minus the left [trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) $2$. If a [locally integrable](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) existed, locality on each open side would identify it [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) with the displayed regular part. Substituting that into the global test identity would force $(a-1)\phi(1)=0$ for every [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function), hence $a=1$. Equivalently a nonzero Dirac [point mass](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) cannot be represented by a [locally integrable](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative).

For $a=1$ the [point-mass](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) term vanishes, and both the [function](../function.md) and its piecewise [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) are [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) on the finite [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics). Thus $u\in W^{1,\infty}$ and hence in every listed $W^{1,p}$. Therefore **membership holds exactly for $a=1$**. Changing a single point value is irrelevant to Sobolev membership; it cannot remove mismatched [one-sided traces](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) when $a\ne1$.

[Weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) $u_j\rightharpoonup u$ means $F(u_j)\to F(u)$ for every $F\in X^*$. [Weak-star convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) $T_j\overset*\rightharpoonup T$ means $T_j(x)\to T(x)$ for every $x\in X$, viewing $X$ as the predual. The [Sequential Banach-Alaoglu theorem for a separable predual](../functional-analysis.md#sequential-banach-alaoglu-theorem-for-a-separable-predual) says that every norm-bounded [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) in $X^*$ has a [weak-star convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence). Separability is what turns the [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) [weak-star](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) statement into this sequential form.

For the [weak-star limit of periodic rescalings](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-limit-of-periodic-rescalings), apply it with $X=L^1(\mathbb R)$, which is separable, and $X^*=L^\infty(\mathbb R)$. The rescaled [functions](../function.md) have uniform [essential-supremum](../measure-theory.md#essential-supremum) bound $\|\theta\|_\infty$. Let $q=\theta-\bar\theta$ and $F(t)=\int_0^tq(s)\,ds$. Since $q$ is one-periodic with zero mean, $F$ is a [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) one-periodic absolutely [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) primitive. Thus for every finite [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics),

$$
\int_a^b[\theta(jx)-\bar\theta],dx=\frac{F(jb)-F(ja)}j\longrightarrow0.
$$

Every [weak-star subsequential limit](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) $h$ therefore satisfies $\int_a^b(h-\bar\theta)=0$ for all $a<b$. To apply the supplied uniqueness fact without assuming global integrability, fix $R>0$ and [set](../set.md) $v=(h-\bar\theta)\mathbf1_{[-R,R]}$. This is in $L^1(\mathbb R)$, and its [integral](../integral.md) over any [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics) is zero, since intersecting with $[-R,R]$ again gives an [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics). Hence $v=0$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). Taking integer $R\to\infty$ yields $h=\bar\theta$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) on the whole line.

Finally, if the full [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) did not converge [weak-star](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) to this constant, some $g\in L^1$ and some [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) would have its pairing with $\theta(jx)-\bar\theta$ [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) away from zero. Sequential [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) gives a further [weak-star convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence), whose [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) must be the constant just identified, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\theta(j\cdot)\overset*\rightharpoonup\bar\theta\quad\text{in }L^\infty(\mathbb R).}
$$

This is averaging against integrable [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function), not a claim of [pointwise](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence) or strong [norm convergence](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-convergence).

For a [coercive Neumann energy with a positive reaction term](../differential-equation.md#coercive-neumann-energy-with-a-positive-reaction-term), let $\nu>0$ be the ellipticity constant and $A_0=\max_{i,j}\|a_{ij}\|_\infty$. [Cauchy-Schwarz](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives

$$
|B[u,v]|\le nA_0\|\nabla u\|_2\|\nabla v\|_2+\|c\|_\infty\|u\|_2\|v\|_2
\le C\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}.
$$

Bilinearity follows from the [integral](../integral.md) and weak-derivative [linearity](../vector-space.md#linearity). Ellipticity and the strictly positive zeroth-order coefficient give

$$
\boxed{B[u,u]\ge\nu\|\nabla u\|_2^2+\mu\|u\|_2^2\ge\kappa\|u\|_{H^1}^2,\quad\kappa=\min(\nu,\mu)>0.}
$$

This controls constants as well as [gradients](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient), so no zero-mean constraint or [Neumann](../differential-equation.md#neumann-boundary-condition) compatibility condition is required. Symmetry of the coefficients makes $B$ symmetric.

Write $F(v)=\int_\Omega fv$. This is a [bounded linear functional](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-functional) on $H^1$, with $|F(v)|\le\|f\|_2\|v\|_{H^1}$. Consequently

$$
I(v)\ge\frac\kappa2\|v\|_{H^1}^2-\|f\|_2\|v\|_{H^1}\longrightarrow+\infty
$$

as the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) tends to [infinity](../mathematics.md#infinity). The infimum is finite, and every [minimizing sequence](../calculus-of-variations.md#minimizing-sequence) is [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness). The [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) $H^1$ is reflexive, so a minimizing [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) converges [weakly](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) to some $u\in H^1$.

To verify lower semicontinuity directly, put $w_j=v_j-u$. Boundedness of $B$ makes $B[u,w_j]\to0$ under [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence), and symmetry gives

$$
B[v_j,v_j]=B[u,u]+2B[u,w_j]+B[w_j,w_j]\ge B[u,u]+o(1).
$$

Also $F(v_j)\to F(u)$. Therefore $I(u)\le\liminf I(v_j)$, so **the absolute minimum is attained**.

For every $h\in H^1$ the real [function](../function.md) $t\mapsto I(u+th)$ has a minimum at zero. Its [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) there is $B[u,h]-F(h)$, yielding the required weak equation for every $h$. If two [weak solutions](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) exist, their difference $z$ satisfies $B[z,h]=0$ for all $h$; choose $h=z$ and use coercivity to obtain $z=0$. In fact the weak equation also gives the exact energy identity

$$
\boxed{I(v)-I(u)=\tfrac12B[v-u,v-u]\ge\tfrac\kappa2\|v-u\|_{H^1}^2.}
$$

Thus the [minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) and [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) are both unique. The natural [boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) is encoded by testing in the whole $H^1$ space, without needing [pointwise](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence) [conormal derivatives](../partial-differential-equation.md#conormal-flux-of-a-divergence-form-operator) for rough coefficients.

The [Lax-Milgram theorem](../functional-analysis.md#lax-milgram-theorem) states that a [bounded bilinear form](../bilinear-form.md#bounded-bilinear-form) $a$ on a real [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) $V$, satisfying $a(v,v)\ge\alpha\|v\|_V^2$ for some $\alpha>0$, has for each $F\in V^*$ a unique $u\in V$ with $a(u,v)=F(v)$ for all $v$. Moreover $\|u\|_V\le\|F\|/\alpha$.

For a [compact operator](../compact-operator.md) $K$ on a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md), the [Fredholm alternative](../compact-operator.md#fredholm-alternative) says that $I-\lambda K$ has closed range, [finite-dimensional](../vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-vector-space) [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and cokernel of equal dimension, and

$$
\operatorname{Ran}(I-\lambda K)=\ker(I-\lambda K^*)^\perp\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
$$

Thus a zero [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) gives unique solvability for every right-hand side; a nonzero [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) gives solvability exactly under the adjoint [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) conditions, and then all solutions differ by a [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) [vector](../vector-space.md#vector).

Take $V=H_0^1(\Omega)$. [Gårding's inequality](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#garding-s-inequality) supplies constants $a>0$, $b\ge0$ with $B[v,v]\ge a\|v\|_{H^1}^2-b\|v\|_2^2$. Therefore for $\lambda\le-b$ the shifted form $B_\lambda[u,v]=B[u,v]-\lambda(u,v)_{L^2}$ is coercive. It is [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) for every fixed $\lambda$, and $v\mapsto(f,v)$ is [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) on $V$. Lax-Milgram gives **unique weak solvability for $\lambda\le-\gamma$, with $\gamma=b$**. The inequality itself follows, for example, by absorbing the drift cross-term $\|b\|_\infty\|\nabla v\|_2\|v\|_2$ using Young's inequality and allowing a lower $L^2$ correction for $c$.

For the [compact inverse and adjoint of a coercive elliptic operator](../functional-analysis.md#compact-inverse-and-adjoint-of-a-coercive-elliptic-operator), under the additional zero-shift coercivity assumption, define $Gg\in V$ by $B[Gg,v]=(g,v)$ for all $v\in V$, and let $K$ be this solution viewed in $L^2$. The solution operator is linear and [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) into $V$, and the [Rellich-Kondrachov](../sobolev-space.md#rellich-kondrachov-theorem) [compact embedding](../functional-analysis.md#compact-embedding) $V\hookrightarrow L^2$ makes $K$ compact. Rewriting the weak equation as $B[u,v]=(f+\lambda u,v)$ gives

$$
\boxed{(I-\lambda K)u=Kf.}
$$

Conversely an $L^2$ solution of this operator equation lies in $V$, because it equals $K(f+\lambda u)$, and satisfies the weak [differential equation](../partial-differential-equation.md).

For constant drift coefficients and zero [Dirichlet](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) [traces](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator), [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives $\int b_iD_i v\,u=-\int b_i vD_i u$. Symmetry of the principal coefficients therefore makes $B^*[u,v]=B[v,u]$ the unshifted weak form of the displayed formal adjoint. **The shifted adjoint equation requires**

$$
\boxed{B^*[u,v]-\lambda(u,v)=(f,v)\qquad(v\in V).}
$$

The [spectral shift in an adjoint weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#spectral-shift-in-an-adjoint-weak-formulation) is essential: the printed sentence omitting it is false for nonzero $\lambda$. For instance take a smooth ball, $L=-\Delta+1$, any nonzero $u\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and $f=-\Delta u+u$. The printed unshifted identity holds, but $(L^*-\lambda)u=f-\lambda u\ne f$ whenever $\lambda\ne0$. This counterexample also meets zero-shift coercivity and constant-drift assumptions. The corrected identity above is used for the remainder of the argument.

Let $G_*g\in V$ solve $B^*[G_*g,v]=(g,v)$, which exists by Lax-Milgram because the diagonal coercivity is unchanged. For $p,q\in L^2$,

$$
(Kp,q)=B[Gp,G_*q]=(p,G_*q).
$$

Thus $G_*$, viewed as an $L^2$ operator, is exactly $K^*$. Applying this inverse to the corrected shifted adjoint equation gives

$$
\boxed{(I-\lambda K^*)u=K^*f,}
$$

and the converse follows from its range lying in $V$, just as before.

Finally let the original homogeneous equation have a nonzero solution. Its parameter is nonzero, since the unshifted problem is coercive. Fredholm gives solvability precisely when $(Kf,w)=0$ for every $w\in\ker(I-\lambda K^*)$. Such a $w$ satisfies $K^*w=w/\lambda$, so $(Kf,w)=(f,w)/\lambda$. Hence the necessary and sufficient condition is

$$
\boxed{\int_\Omega fw\,dx=0\quad\text{for every }w\in H_0^1(\Omega)\text{ satisfying }(L^*-\lambda)w=0\text{ weakly}.}
$$

When this holds, the solutions are an [affine translate](../vector-space.md#affine-subspace) of the original [finite-dimensional](../vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-vector-space) [homogeneous solution space](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md). The compatibility space is the adjoint [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md); a drift term does not generally permit replacing it by the original [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md).

[Banach's fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) states that a map $T:Y\to Y$ on a nonempty [complete metric space](../topological-analysis.md#complete-metric-space) with $d(Tx,Ty)\le qd(x,y)$ for a constant $0\le q<1$ has a unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), approached from every starting point by iteration.

Choose $x_0$ and let $x_{n+1}=Tx_n$. Induction gives $d(x_{n+1},x_n)\le q^nd(x_1,x_0)$. For $m>n$, the triangle inequality and [geometric series](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series) give

$$
d(x_m,x_n)\le\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0).
$$

Thus the [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) is Cauchy and converges by completeness to some $x_*$. The contraction estimate implies [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function), so $Tx_*=\lim Tx_n=\lim x_{n+1}=x_*$. If $y_*$ were another [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point), $d(x_*,y_*)\le qd(x_*,y_*)$ would force equality of the points. Passing $m\to\infty$ also yields the quantitative error estimate

$$
\boxed{d(x_n,x_*)\le\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0).}
$$

A [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) belongs to $V=H_0^1(\Omega)$ and satisfies

$$
\boxed{\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla h\,dx=\int_\Omega f(u)h\,dx\qquad(h\in V),}
$$

with the nonlinear right-hand side defining a [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) [functional](../calculus-of-variations.md#functional) on $V$. Under the stated global [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) hypothesis this requirement is automatic: $|f(s)|\le|f(0)|+\epsilon|s|$ and boundedness of $\Omega$ give $f(u)\in L^2$ whenever $u\in V$. [Continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $f$ alone, without growth control, would not guarantee this for an arbitrary [Sobolev function](../sobolev-space.md).

For the [semilinear Poisson contraction threshold](../analysis.md#semilinear-poisson-contraction-threshold), use the [gradient norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm) on $V$, and let $C_P$ satisfy the [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) $\|v\|_2\le C_P\|\nabla v\|_2$. For $g\in L^2$, Lax-Milgram gives the [zero-Dirichlet](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) Poisson solution $Pg\in V$. Testing with $Pg$ yields $\|\nabla Pg\|_2\le C_P\|g\|_2$. Define $T(v)=P(f(v))$, a self-map of the complete space $V$. For $v,w\in V$,

$$
\|T(v)-T(w)\|_V\le C_P\|f(v)-f(w)\|_2
\le\epsilon C_P^2\|v-w\|_V.
$$

Therefore **$\epsilon C_P^2<1$ is a sufficient condition** for [Banach's theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) to give a unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) and hence a unique [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution). There is no need to assume $f(0)=0$ or to restrict to a small ball: the [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) bound makes the map defined on all of $V$. The same estimates give

$$
\|\nabla u\|_2\le\frac{C_P|\Omega|^{1/2}|f(0)|}{1-\epsilon C_P^2}.
$$

With the optimal [Poincaré](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) constant, the threshold is $\epsilon<\lambda_1$ for the [First Dirichlet eigenvalue](../sturm-liouville-theory.md#first-dirichlet-eigenvalue); the stated sufficient condition does not depend on knowing that optimal constant.

For a [uniform contraction from a comparison function on a bounded set](../analysis.md#uniform-contraction-from-a-comparison-function-on-a-bounded-set), the [closed set](../topology.md#closed-set) $M$ is complete and has diameter at most $2R$. Suppose first $R>0$. The [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) condition supplies $r_0>0$ and $q_0<1$ such that $\psi(r)/r\le q_0$ for $0<r\le r_0$. On the remaining compact [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics) $[r_0,2R]$, if nonempty, the [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) ratio $\psi(r)/r$ attains a maximum $q_1<1$, since it is strictly below one at every point. Thus there is one $q<1$ with $\psi(r)\le qr$ for every distance $0\le r\le2R$ arising in $M$.

Consequently $d(Tx,Ty)\le qd(x,y)$ throughout $M$. [Banach's theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) applies and gives **a unique [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) in $M$**. No linear structure or [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) of the ball is used; the compact object in the argument is only the [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar) distance [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics). If the [closed ball](../topological-analysis.md#closed-ball) is the singleton at radius zero, its self-map fixes that point. The estimate on the ratio near zero is what prevents the compact-interval supremum argument from approaching one at its missing endpoint.

<h5 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h5>

[Brouwer](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem)'s fixed-point theorem says that every [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) self-map of a nonempty closed [Euclidean ball](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-ball) has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point). It does not promise convergence of repeated application. For example **$f(x)=1-x$ with starting point $x_1=0$** gives the nonconvergent alternating [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) $0,1,0,1,\ldots$, although $1/2$ is a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point).

For an [invariant ball from sublinear growth](../topological-analysis.md#invariant-ball-from-sublinear-growth), choose

$$
R\ge\max\{(2A)^{1/(1-\alpha)},\,2B\}.
$$

Then $AR^\alpha\le R/2$ and $B\le R/2$. For every $|x|\le R$ the given estimate yields $|f(x)|\le A R^\alpha+B\le R$. Thus $f$ restricts to a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) self-map of the [closed ball](../topological-analysis.md#closed-ball) of this radius, and [Brouwer](../topological-analysis.md#brouwer-fixed-point-theorem) gives a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point). The exponent $\alpha<1$ ensures that the linear radius eventually dominates its sublinear growth; no [differentiability](../analysis.md#differentiability) or contraction estimate is needed.

<h4 id="b/5/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/5/b)

One form of [Schauder](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem)'s theorem says that if $M$ is a nonempty closed [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) convex [subset](../set.md#subset) of a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) and $T:M\to M$ is [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) with relatively compact image, then $T$ has a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point).

For a [Schauder solution of a small Hammerstein integral equation](../analysis.md#schauder-solution-of-a-small-hammerstein-integral-equation), take $X=C([0,1])$ with the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) and $M=\{u:\|u\|_\infty\le R\}$. This [set](../set.md) is nonempty, closed, [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) and convex. Put

$$
F_R=\max_{0\le y\le1,\ |z|\le R}|f(y,z)|,\qquad G_0=\max_{x,y\in[0,1]}|G(x,y)|,
\qquad\mu_0=\frac{R}{1+G_0F_R}>0.
$$

Both maxima are finite by [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) on [compact sets](../topology.md#compact-space). Define $(T_\mu u)(x)=\mu\int_0^1G(x,y)f(y,u(y))\,dy$. [Continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) and [uniform continuity](../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity) of the [kernel](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) give $T_\mu u\in X$, and for $0<\mu\le\mu_0$,

$$
\|T_\mu u\|_\infty\le\mu G_0F_R\le R,
$$

so it is a self-map. This choice of $\mu_0$ also handles the case when either maximum is zero without division by zero.

If $u_n\to u$ uniformly in $M$, [uniform continuity](../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity) of $f$ on $[0,1]\times[-R,R]$ makes $f(\cdot,u_n(\cdot))\to f(\cdot,u(\cdot))$ uniformly. The [integral](../integral.md) estimate then proves [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $T_\mu$ in the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm). Moreover

$$
|(T_\mu u)(x)-(T_\mu u)(x')|\le\mu F_R\sup_y|G(x,y)-G(x',y)|,
$$

which tends to zero with $|x-x'|$ uniformly in $u\in M$. Thus the image is uniformly [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness) and [equicontinuous](../topological-analysis.md#equicontinuity). [Arzelà-Ascoli](../topological-analysis.md#arzela-ascoli-theorem) states that such a family on a compact [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics) has compact closure in the [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm). Hence $T_\mu(M)$ is relatively compact, and [Schauder](../analysis.md#schauder-fixed-point-theorem) gives a [fixed point](../function.md#fixed-point) for each $\mu$ in the specified [interval](../real-analysis.md#interval-mathematics). Therefore **a [continuous](../calculus.md#continuous-function) solution with [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) at most $R$ exists**. The argument imposes no [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) hypothesis on $f$ and makes no uniqueness assertion.

<h4 id="b/6/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

Using the [dual pairing](../dual-space.md#dual-pairing), [monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#monotone-operator) means

$$
\boxed{\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle\ge0\qquad(u,v\in X).}
$$

[Strict monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#strictly-monotone-operator) requires strict positivity whenever $u\ne v$. Coercivity with respect to zero, in the growth sense used for monotone-operator existence theory, means

$$
\boxed{\frac{\langle A(v),v\rangle}{\|v\|_X}\longrightarrow+\infty\qquad\text{as }\|v\|_X\to\infty.}
$$

This is a condition on all [sequences](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) escaping to [infinity](../mathematics.md#infinity), not just each fixed direction with a bound depending on that direction. [Strict monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#strictly-monotone-operator) concerns pairs of distinct arguments; it does not by itself imply this uniform large-norm growth.

<h4 id="b/6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

For the [log-cosh energy on a Sobolev space](../calculus-of-variations.md#log-cosh-energy-on-a-sobolev-space), the elementary inequalities $0\le\log\cosh t\le t^2/2$ follow by integrating $|\tanh t|\le|t|$ from zero. Thus

$$
0\le I(v)\le\tfrac12(\|\nabla v\|_2^2+\|v\|_2^2)<\infty,
$$

so the energy is well-defined on $H^1$. For a fixed $v$, its proposed [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is linear in the direction $h$ and [bounded](../topological-analysis.md#boundedness), because $|\tanh v|\le|v|$ gives

$$
|\partial_h I(v)|\le\|\nabla v\|_2\|\nabla h\|_2+\|v\|_2\|h\|_2\le\|v\|_{H^1}\|h\|_{H^1}.
$$

To justify differentiation, use $(\log\cosh t)''=\operatorname{sech}^2t\in(0,1]$. Taylor's [integral](../integral.md) remainder [pointwise](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence), combined with the exact quadratic [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) expansion, gives

$$
0\le I(v+h)-I(v)-\int_\Omega(\nabla v\cdot\nabla h+\tanh(v)h)\,dx
\le\tfrac12\|h\|_{H^1}^2.
$$

Every term is integrable by the displayed bounds. The remainder is quadratic in the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), so the [functional](../calculus-of-variations.md#functional) is even [Fréchet differentiable](../multivariable-calculus.md#frechet-derivative), and in particular [Gâteaux differentiable](../multivariable-calculus.md#gateaux-derivative) with the required [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative). This also proves that $A(v)$ belongs to the stated [dual space](../dual-space.md).

For $v,w\in H^1$,

$$
\langle A(v)-A(w),v-w\rangle=\|\nabla(v-w)\|_2^2+
\int_\Omega[\tanh v-\tanh w](v-w)\,dx.
$$

Both terms are nonnegative, and the integrand in the second is strictly positive wherever $v\ne w$, since $\tanh$ is strictly increasing. Distinct [Sobolev elements](../sobolev-space.md) differ on a [set](../set.md) of positive measure, so the expression is positive. Hence **$A$ is strictly monotone**.

This gives [strict monotonicity without coercivity on H1](../convex-optimization.md#strict-monotonicity-without-coercivity-on-h1): it is **not coercive on $H^1$**. For [constant functions](../function.md#constant-function) $v_t=t$ with $t\to+\infty$, the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is $t|\Omega|^{1/2}$ and

$$
\frac{\langle A(v_t),v_t\rangle}{\|v_t\|_{H^1}}=|\Omega|^{1/2}\tanh t\longrightarrow|\Omega|^{1/2},
$$

which is finite. In contrast it **is coercive on $H_0^1$**: if $\|v\|_2\le C_P\|\nabla v\|_2$, then

$$
\langle A(v),v\rangle=\|\nabla v\|_2^2+\int_\Omega v\tanh v\,dx
\ge\frac{\|v\|_{H^1}^2}{1+C_P^2}.
$$

Dividing by the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) proves growth to [infinity](../mathematics.md#infinity). On this smaller space the operator is understood by restricting its [dual action](../dual-space.md#dual-pairing) to [zero-trace](../sobolev-space.md#zero-boundary-sobolev-space) [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function). Removing the constant directions by the [boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition) is what restores coercivity.

<h4 id="b/6/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#b/6/c)

For the [log-cosh obstacle variational inequality](../partial-differential-equation.md#log-cosh-obstacle-variational-inequality), the admissible [set](../set.md) is convex: for any $v\in M$, $u+t(v-u)$ remains above the obstacle and in $H_0^1$ for $0\le t\le1$. Minimality and the right [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) at zero therefore give

$$
\boxed{\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla(v-u)+\tanh(u)(v-u)\,dx\ge0\qquad(v\in M).}
$$

The one-sided sign comes from admissibility, which permits motion toward every $v\in M$ but does not permit an arbitrary negative variation at a contact point.

Now fix $\phi\in C_c^\infty(D)$. Smoothness on $D$ and [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of the obstacle give a strictly positive minimum of $u-g$ on the [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) of $\phi$. Hence $u+t\phi$ is admissible for both signs of sufficiently small $t$. Applying the inequality to both variations yields

$$
\int_D\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi+\tanh(u)\phi\,dx=0.
$$

[Integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives $-\Delta u+\tanh u=0$ distributionally on $D$, and smoothness makes it [pointwise](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-convergence). Thus

$$
\boxed{-\Delta u+\tanh u=0\quad\text{where }u>g.}
$$

On the contact [set](../set.md) the [variational inequality](../mathematical-optimization.md#variational-inequality), rather than unrestricted stationarity, remains the governing statement. [Convexity](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) of the energy also makes the inequality sufficient for minimization; [strict monotonicity](../convex-optimization.md#strictly-monotone-operator) implies at most one [minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer), although existence was already stipulated in this part.
