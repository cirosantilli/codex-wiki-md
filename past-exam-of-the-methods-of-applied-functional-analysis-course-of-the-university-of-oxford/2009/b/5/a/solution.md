<h1 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Brouwer](../../../../../brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md)'s fixed-point theorem says that every [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) self-map of a nonempty closed [Euclidean ball](../../../../../euclidean-ball.md) has a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md). It does not promise convergence of repeated application. For example **$f(x)=1-x$ with starting point $x_1=0$** gives the nonconvergent alternating [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $0,1,0,1,\ldots$, although $1/2$ is a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md).

For an [invariant ball from sublinear growth](../../../../../invariant-ball-from-sublinear-growth.md), choose

$$
R\ge\max\{(2A)^{1/(1-\alpha)},\,2B\}.
$$

Then $AR^\alpha\le R/2$ and $B\le R/2$. For every $|x|\le R$ the given estimate yields $|f(x)|\le A R^\alpha+B\le R$. Thus $f$ restricts to a [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) self-map of the [closed ball](../../../../../closed-ball.md) of this radius, and [Brouwer](../../../../../brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md) gives a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md). The exponent $\alpha<1$ ensures that the linear radius eventually dominates its sublinear growth; no [differentiability](../../../../../differentiability.md) or contraction estimate is needed.
