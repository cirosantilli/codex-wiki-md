<h1 id="b/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a [uniform contraction from a comparison function on a bounded set](../../../../../uniform-contraction-from-a-comparison-function-on-a-bounded-set.md), the [closed set](../../../../../closed-set.md) $M$ is complete and has diameter at most $2R$. Suppose first $R>0$. The [limit](../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) condition supplies $r_0>0$ and $q_0<1$ such that $\psi(r)/r\le q_0$ for $0<r\le r_0$. On the remaining compact [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $[r_0,2R]$, if nonempty, the [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) ratio $\psi(r)/r$ attains a maximum $q_1<1$, since it is strictly below one at every point. Thus there is one $q<1$ with $\psi(r)\le qr$ for every distance $0\le r\le2R$ arising in $M$.

Consequently $d(Tx,Ty)\le qd(x,y)$ throughout $M$. [Banach's theorem](../../../../../contraction-mapping-theorem.md) applies and gives **a unique [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) in $M$**. No linear structure or [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) of the ball is used; the compact object in the argument is only the [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) distance [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). If the [closed ball](../../../../../closed-ball.md) is the singleton at radius zero, its self-map fixes that point. The estimate on the ratio near zero is what prevents the compact-interval supremum argument from approaching one at its missing endpoint.
