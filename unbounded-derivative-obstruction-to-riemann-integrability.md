# Unbounded derivative obstruction to Riemann integrability

↑ **Parent:** [Riemann integral](riemann-integral.md)

A proper [Riemann integral](riemann-integral.md) on a compact interval requires a bounded integrand, but a [derivative](derivative.md) can be unbounded. For $f(x)=x^2\sin(1/x^2)$ away from zero and $f(0)=0$, the [difference quotient](difference-quotient.md) gives $f'(0)=0$, whereas $f'(x)=2x\sin(1/x^2)-2\cos(1/x^2)/x$ for $x\ne0$. At $x_n=(2\pi n)^{-1/2}$, this derivative is $-2/x_n$ and is unbounded. The [fundamental theorem of calculus](fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md) version that integrates a derivative therefore needs an explicit integrability hypothesis.
