# Thermal transpiration

↑ **Parent:** [Kinetic theory](kinetic-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_transpiration)

[Thermal transpiration](thermal-transpiration.md) is gas transport driven by an imposed [temperature](temperature-split.md) difference in a rarefied connection. Gas can move from cold to hot even when the two end [pressures](pressure-split.md) start equal. If the reservoirs are closed, redistribution builds a counteracting [pressure](pressure-split.md) difference until the net transfer stops. This final stationary state is not global [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md): the two [temperatures](temperature-split.md) remain different. It explains why a heated pressure sensor can read a different [pressure](pressure-split.md) from the vessel to which it is connected. The correction depends on gas, connection geometry and molecular interaction with its surfaces. [Daudé, Elandaloussi and Janssen (2014), thermal-transpiration corrections for diaphragm gauges, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.6794)

The simple molecular limit is beautiful: equal opposing particle rates need not mean equal [pressures](pressure-split.md). Consider [effusion](effusion.md) through a thin hole connecting two locally equilibrated reservoirs of the same gas, at $T_h>T_c$. Equating their one-way rates gives

$$
\frac{p_c}{\sqrt{T_c}}=\frac{p_h}{\sqrt{T_h}},\qquad \frac{p_h}{p_c}=\sqrt{\frac{T_h}{T_c}}.
$$

At equal [pressure](pressure-split.md), the hotter reservoir has lower [number density](number-density.md); its faster molecules do not fully compensate for that reduction in outgoing particle rate. With $T_c=300\,\mathrm K$ and $T_h=351\,\mathrm K$, the ideal zero-transfer ratio is $\sqrt{351/300}\simeq1.0817$. The hydrodynamic, zero-flow limit instead has equal [pressures](pressure-split.md) when other forces are absent. These are limiting models, not an interpolation formula for a glass capillary. [Fitzpatrick, Molecular Effusion, equations 5.286–5.287](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/355/Surveyhtml/node224.html) In a real tube, incomplete exchange of molecular [energy](energy-split.md) with the wall can alter the limiting ratio; a [Knudsen number](knudsen-number.md) does not specify that exchange. [Daudé, Elandaloussi and Janssen, §I, limitations of the ideal Knudsen pressure ratio](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.6794)
