# Meissner effect discovery

↑ **Parent:** [Meissner effect](meissner-effect.md)

The 1933 report [Ein neuer Effekt bei Eintritt der Supraleitfähigkeit by W. Meissner and R. Ochsenfeld](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01504252) describes cooling tin and [lead](lead.md) cylinders in a fixed transverse [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md). Two parallel cylinders were about $140\,\mathrm{mm}$ long and $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ thick, separated by $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. A roughly $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ rotating search coil between them drove a ballistic [galvanometer](galvanometer.md). At about $5\,\mathrm{G}$, the reported below/above-transition induction ratios were $1.70$ for tin single crystals and $1.77$ for polycrystalline [lead](lead.md). The increased gap [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) indicates redistribution outside the cylinders, rather than an unchanged frozen field. Coil-winding geometry and noncircular tin specimens limited comparison with the ideal prediction. [1933 report, first arrangement; English translation PDF pp. 1–2, printed p. 119, alongside the German text](ein-neuer-effekt-bei-eintritt-der-supraleitfahigkeit-by-walther-meissner-and-others.md)

Their second arrangement used a [lead](lead.md) tube, about $130\,\mathrm{mm}$ long with $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ outer and $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ inner diameters. A rotating search coil could sit inside or alongside it. The bore retained [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md) after removing the applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md); the coil nearly filled the bore, preventing a test of interior field uniformity. A hole containing [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) is distinct from [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) penetrating the surrounding material exhibiting [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md). [1933 report, second arrangement; translation PDF p. 2, printed p. 119, bore retention and explicitly stated spatial-resolution limitation](ein-neuer-effekt-bei-eintritt-der-supraleitfahigkeit-by-walther-meissner-and-others.md)

The modern [Field-cooled Meissner experiment](field-cooled-meissner-experiment.md) provides a complementary readout. MIT's probe I places a sample in a brass holder, with an $810$-turn test coil of $7.1\,\mathrm{mm}$ inner diameter, $10.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ outer diameter and $12.3\,\mathrm{mm}$ length, inside a longer [solenoid](solenoid.md). A nearby silicon-diode [thermometer](thermometer.md) tracks cooling by helium gas. With [lead](lead.md) still normal, the guide applies about $200\,\mathrm{mA}$ to the [solenoid](solenoid.md) and connects the test coil to a current-integrator box and [oscilloscope](oscilloscope.md). Cooling produces a transient; warming gives the opposite sign. Switching off the [solenoid](solenoid.md) while the sample is normal checks the sign. [Probe I, §4.2 and Fig. 6, printed pp. 5–6; §4.3 temperature control; §5.2.2, printed p. 10, DC field cooling and sign control](superconductivity-the-meissner-effect-persistent-currents-and-the-josephson-effects-by-mit-department-of-physics.md)

The inferred change is in linked [magnetic flux](magnetic-flux.md), not a direct scan of $B(z)$. For a stationary test coil the pulse obeys $\int V\,dt=-\Delta\Psi$, where $\Psi$ is its [magnetic flux linkage](magnetic-flux-linkage.md). An integrator or AC-coupled [oscilloscope](oscilloscope.md) needs its transfer response accounted for before extracting this pulse area. Keep the source [electric current](electric-current.md) fixed during cooling, monitor [temperature](temperature-split.md), and compare warming and cooling slowly enough to reduce sample–[thermometer](thermometer.md) lag. An AC [inductance](inductance.md) change alone can reflect changed normal-state [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md); the fixed-field expulsion test distinguishes that alternative. [§5.1, printed pp. 8–9, thermometer lag; §5.2.2, printed p. 10, limitation of AC mutual-inductance evidence](superconductivity-the-meissner-effect-persistent-currents-and-the-josephson-effects-by-mit-department-of-physics.md) Neither this coil readout nor the historical gap-field measurement resolves the microscopic [London penetration depth](london-penetration-depth.md) by itself.
