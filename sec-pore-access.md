# SEC pore access

↑ **Parent:** [Size-exclusion chromatography](size-exclusion-chromatography.md)

In ideal [SEC pore access](sec-pore-access.md), geometric exclusion changes the fraction of pore liquid a [solute](solute.md) can occupy. [Diffusion](diffusion.md) permits entry into and exit from accessible pores; the through-flow chiefly transports the sample between packing particles. A [hydrodynamic radius](hydrodynamic-radius.md) is a useful size descriptor, but actual pore entry also depends on shape and pore geometry. A flexible [polymer](polymer-split.md) coil is not a rigid ball with a perfectly sharp entry diameter.

**[Figure 3](#image-size-exclusion-chromatography-pore-paths). SEC pore access: excluded species leave before species that explore pore liquid**. Original schematic of a porous packing and representative excursions. Colored routes distinguish exclusion, partial access, and extensive access; they are not measured trajectories or a calculation of travel times. The circles represent effective [hydrodynamic sizes](hydrodynamic-size.md), not actual [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md). There is no [adsorption](adsorption.md) in this illustration.

The extra delay is best understood as extra accessible storage, rather than a prescribed longer streamline. Pore visits are reversible and random. An entirely excluded species samples the [SEC void volume](sec-void-volume.md); a sufficiently small nonbinding species samples the [SEC total accessible liquid volume](sec-total-accessible-liquid-volume.md). Intermediate species lie between these limits. Larger [hydrodynamic size](hydrodynamic-size.md) generally means earlier elution within the column's [SEC fractionation range](sec-fractionation-range.md).
