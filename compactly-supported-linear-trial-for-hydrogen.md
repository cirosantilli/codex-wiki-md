# Compactly supported linear trial for hydrogen

↑ **Parent:** [Hydrogen ground-state energy](hydrogen-ground-state-energy.md)

In [atomic units](atomic-units.md), use the radial trial [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) $\psi(r)=\max(1-r/\beta,0)$ with $\beta>0$. Its radial norm integral is $\int r^2\psi^2dr=\beta^3/30$, its gradient contribution is $\int r^2\psi'^2dr=\beta/3$, and its Coulomb contribution is $-2\int r\psi^2dr=-\beta^2/6$. In the hydrogen radial [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md), this gives the displayed energy, minimized at $\beta=4$. It is above the exact [hydrogen ground-state energy](hydrogen-ground-state-energy.md) $-1/2$, as required by the [quantum variational principle](quantum-variational-principle.md). The derivative jumps at $r=\beta$, but the function has a square-integrable weak first derivative and is an admissible trial for the energy quadratic form. One must use that form rather than assume that a classical second derivative exists at the join.
