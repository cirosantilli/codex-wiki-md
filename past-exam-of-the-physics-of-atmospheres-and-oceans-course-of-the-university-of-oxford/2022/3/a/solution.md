<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [dilute Köhler curve](../../../../dilute-kohler-curve.md) follows because the [Kelvin equation](../../../../kelvin-equation.md) multiplies the planar-solution [saturation pressure](../../../../vapor-pressure.md) by the curvature correction. Combining it with [Raoult's law](../../../../raoult-s-law.md) therefore gives

$$
S_{\rm eq}(r)=\frac{e_S^{\rm sol}(r)}{e_S(\infty)}
=\left(1-\frac B{r^3}\right)\exp\left(\frac Ar\right).
$$

For a dilute [chemical solution](../../../../solution-chemistry.md) and a small curvature correction, $B/r^3\ll1$ and $A/r\ll1$. A first-order [Taylor expansion](../../../../taylor-expansion.md) gives

$$
S_{\rm eq}=1+\frac Ar-\frac B{r^3}
+O\left((A/r)^2,(A/r)(B/r^3)\right).
$$

Thus the simplified [Köhler theory](../../../../kohler-theory.md) relation is

$$
\boxed{S_{\rm eq}(r)\simeq1+\frac Ar-\frac B{r^3}.}
$$

The curvature term raises the required ambient [vapor pressure](../../../../vapor-pressure.md); the solute term lowers it. These laws combine multiplicatively before linearization, so the displayed simplified relation is an approximation, not an exact identity.
