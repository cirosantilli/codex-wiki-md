<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In the quasisteady [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md) approximation, the vapour-[density](../../../../density.md) disturbance outside a small stationary particle satisfies [Laplace's equation](../../../../laplace-equation.md), with a specified equilibrium value on its surface and a uniform far-field value. This has the same mathematical boundary-value problem as the electrostatic potential outside a conductor held at fixed potential. The integrated normal gradient is proportional to the conductor's geometrical [capacitance](../../../../diffusion-capacitance-analogy.md).

Here $C$ is a _length_: a sphere of [radius](../../../../radius.md) $r$ has $C=r$. The corresponding electrical [capacitance](../../../../capacitance.md) in SI units would be $4\pi\epsilon_0C$, not $C$ farads. Replacing the spherical diffusive conductance $4\pi r$ by $4\pi C$ accounts for the crystal's non-spherical geometry. Coupling the analogous heat-conduction problem to vapour [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md) supplies the ice-specific thermal and diffusional resistances.

The approximation assumes continuum [diffusion](../../../../diffusion.md), equilibration of the surface vapour concentration, an approximately isothermal particle, a slowly changing shape, weak perturbations and a far field sufficiently distant relative to the crystal size. It neglects ventilation by falling motion, interactions with neighbouring particles and facet-dependent attachment kinetics. Shape affects diffusive growth through [capacitance](../../../../diffusion-capacitance-analogy.md), not merely through projected collecting area as in a collision calculation.
