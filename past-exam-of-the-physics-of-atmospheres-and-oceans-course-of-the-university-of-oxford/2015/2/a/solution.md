[Meridional overturning circulation](../../../../meridional-overturning-circulation.md) links relatively warm upper-ocean transport to a colder return flow at depth, with exchanges among basins and vertical branches. Surface heat loss and [salinity](../../../../salinity.md) changes alter water [density](../../../../density.md); sufficiently dense water can sink and spread along deep pathways. Freshwater input lowers [salinity](../../../../salinity.md) and can inhibit dense-water formation. Winds, particularly in the Southern Ocean, and [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) supplied to mixing are also important to the global return and upwelling pathways. The circulation is not powered by surface [density](../../../../density.md) forcing alone in an otherwise closed, unmixed ocean.

The [Atlantic meridional overturning circulation](../../../../atlantic-meridional-overturning-circulation.md) carries heat northward, helping regulate ocean-atmosphere heat exchange and regional climate. Overturning also ventilates the deep ocean and redistributes carbon, oxygen and nutrients. Changes in its strength can alter regional sea-surface [temperatures](../../../../temperature-split.md), air-sea heat release and atmospheric circulation. They need not eliminate every western-boundary current or every source of European warmth: wind-driven [ocean circulation](../../../../ocean-circulation.md) and atmospheric [heat transport](../../../../heat-transport.md) are distinct, interacting contributions. The two-box model below isolates a [density](../../../../density.md)/[salinity](../../../../salinity.md) feedback rather than representing all these mechanisms.
