<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Interpret the stated detector width $d=20\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ as one square spatial sample. At the point directly below the spacecraft, the [small-angle approximation](../../../../small-angle-approximation.md) gives pixel angular width $d/f\simeq\Delta x/h$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f=\frac{dh}{\Delta x}=\frac{(20\times10^{-6})(1.70087\times10^7)}{500}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.680\,\mathrm m.}
$$

There is a definition error in the source: the usual [focal ratio](../../../../focal-ratio.md), or f-number, divides [focal length](../../../../focal-length.md) by the entrance-aperture diameter $D$, whereas the printed parenthesis divides by detector width $d$. Reporting both avoids silently changing the question:

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac f d\right)_{\rm printed}\simeq3.40\times10^4,\qquad
\left(\frac f D\right)_{\rm conventional}\simeq\frac{0.680}{0.50}=1.36.}
$$

The actual optical design is therefore approximately f/1.36, a fast [optical telescope](../../../../optical-telescope.md), not an optically slow f/34000 instrument. Such a focal ratio is demanding but possible in principle: [optical aberrations](../../../../optical-aberration.md), obscuration, field coverage, [stray light](../../../../stray-light.md) and alignment need careful design. The single-sample angular width is $2.94\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{rad}$. Spatial sampling alone does not guarantee true resolving power; [diffraction](../../../../diffraction.md), pointing and [signal-to-noise ratio](../../../../signal-to-noise-ratio.md) also matter. At large viewing angles, the same angular pixel covers a larger, distorted surface footprint. If the detector is literally one element, scanning is additionally needed to form a map.
