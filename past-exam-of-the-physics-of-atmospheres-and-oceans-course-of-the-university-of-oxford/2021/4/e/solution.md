<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A calibrated multispectral filter [radiometer](../../../../radiometer.md) is a natural choice for rapid thermal mapping: a few selected bands provide higher photon throughput and faster imaging than spreading the light over many [spectrometer](../../../../spectrometer.md) channels. Several bands can help separate [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), dust [emissivity](../../../../emissivity.md) and atmospheric effects. A [spectrometer](../../../../spectrometer.md) would be justified if identifying composition or resolving spectral structure were a central additional objective; one broadband [radiance](../../../../radiance.md) alone cannot generally determine both [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and unknown [emissivity](../../../../emissivity.md).

Thermal [infrared](../../../../infrared.md) radiation is preferable to reflected visible light for measuring [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). The [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) sets

$$
1.22\frac\lambda D\lesssim\frac{500}{h},\qquad
\boxed{\lambda\lesssim12.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}.}
$$

A band near $10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ is therefore a reasonable compromise between useful planetary thermal emission and the required [diffraction-limited resolution](../../../../diffraction-limited-resolution.md). At that [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md), the nominal surface [Rayleigh criterion](../../../../rayleigh-criterion.md) separation is about $415\,\mathrm m$; at $15\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ it is about $623\,\mathrm m$ and fails the target. The exact bands must avoid or model strong atmospheric absorption and account for the dust spectrum. The $500\,\mathrm m$ sampling pitch is still not a guarantee of faithful reconstruction of arbitrary $500\,\mathrm m$ features: detector sampling and the [point spread function](../../../../point-spread-function-split.md) must be considered together.
