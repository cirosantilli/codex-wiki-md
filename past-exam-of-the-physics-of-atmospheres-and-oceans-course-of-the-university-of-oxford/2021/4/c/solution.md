<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a prograde equatorial [synchronous circular orbit](../../../../synchronous-circular-orbit.md), equate [gravitational acceleration](../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) and centripetal [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md). With planetary radius $R_M$, $GM=g_sR_M^2$, and rotation period $P$,

$$
\frac{GM}{r_o^2}=\left(\frac{2\pi}P\right)^2r_o,\qquad
r_o=\left[\frac{g_sR_M^2P^2}{4\pi^2}\right]^{1/3}.
$$

The tabulated $g_s$ is a [gravitational acceleration](../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md); its printed unit must be read as $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, rather than $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Using $P=88642\,\mathrm s$ gives

$$
r_o\simeq20398.7\,\mathrm{km},\qquad
\boxed{h=r_o-R_M\simeq17008.7\,\mathrm{km}.}
$$

The [orbital altitude](../../../../orbital-altitude.md) is measured from the surface, rather than the centre.

At the visible horizon, the line of sight is tangent to the spherical surface. The limiting central angle $\theta_h$ satisfies $\cos\theta_h=R_M/r_o$. The visible [spherical cap](../../../../spherical-cap.md) has area $2\pi R_M^2(1-\cos\theta_h)$, giving

$$
\boxed{\frac{A_{\rm vis}}{4\pi R_M^2}=\frac12\left(1-\frac{R_M}{r_o}\right)\simeq0.4169.}
$$

About $41.7\%$ of the whole surface is geometrically visible, with $\theta_h\simeq80.4^\circ$. This is a surface-area fraction, not an apparent-disc area ratio. Useful imaging near the horizon is further limited by foreshortening and the long atmospheric path.
