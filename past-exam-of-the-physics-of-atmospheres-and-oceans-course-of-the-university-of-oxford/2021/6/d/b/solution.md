<h1 id="6/d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

To return the broad interior transport, the [western boundary current](../../../../../western-boundary-current.md) has opposite sign and characteristic peak

$$
v_W\sim-\frac{F_0L}r,\qquad
\boxed{|v_W|\sim57.3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\quad\text{southward}.}
$$

The leading exponential profile retains the smaller interior offset, $v(0)\simeq(F_0/\beta)(1-L/\delta_W)\simeq-52.1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Because $\gamma L\simeq0.593$ is not very small, the exact two-wall solution gives a more accurate linear-model peak:

$$
\boxed{v(0,0)=A[\lambda C_W-\gamma C_Ee^{-\gamma L}]\simeq-40.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

All three evaluations identify an intense southward [western boundary current](../../../../../western-boundary-current.md), with peak at $x=0,y=0$, of order tens of metres per second. They are predictions of the stated steady linear barotropic model. Nonlinear [advection](../../../../../advection.md), stratification and the actual basin geometry could alter them substantially; the calculation does not establish that real [Pluto](../../../../../pluto.md) winds attain these values.
