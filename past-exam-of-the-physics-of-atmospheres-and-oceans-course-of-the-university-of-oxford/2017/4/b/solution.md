<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume a plane-parallel atmosphere in [local thermodynamic equilibrium](../../../../local-thermodynamic-equilibrium.md), negligible infrared [scattering](../../../../scattering.md), a [black surface](../../../../blackbody.md), and surface [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) equal to the adjacent air [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md). For a specified ray, let $\mathfrak T$ be its [transmittance](../../../../transmittance.md) from a level to space, increasing from $\mathfrak T_0$ at the surface to one at the top. The formal [radiative transfer](../../../../radiative-transfer.md) solution is

$$
L=B_0\mathfrak T_0+\int_{\mathfrak T_0}^{1}B\,d\mathfrak T.
$$

Using $\mathfrak T_0+\int_{\mathfrak T_0}^1d\mathfrak T=1$ yields

$$
\boxed{L=B_0+\int_{\mathfrak T_0}^1(B-B_0)\,d\mathfrak T.}
$$

This expresses [absorption](../../../../absorption-electromagnetic-radiation.md) and re-emission together: [opacity](../../../../opacity.md) does not simply remove surface radiation without adding atmospheric radiation. An absorber close to a thermally coupled surface has nearly the same [Planck function](../../../../planck-function.md) as that surface, so $B-B_0$ is small. Its emission nearly compensates the surface radiation it absorbs, giving weak [radiance](../../../../radiance.md) sensitivity to near-surface concentration. In an exactly [isothermal atmosphere](../../../../atmosphere.md) at surface [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md), $L=B_0$ for every [optical depth](../../../../optical-depth.md), a concrete example of complete concentration ambiguity in this thermal measurement. Finite surface emissivity, reflection, [scattering](../../../../scattering.md) or a surface-air [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) discontinuity would require additional terms.
