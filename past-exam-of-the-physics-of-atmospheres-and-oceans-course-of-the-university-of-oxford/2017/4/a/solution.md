<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a unit mass of [ideal gas](../../../../ideal-gas-split.md), the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) in enthalpy form is $\delta Q=c_p\,dT-v\,dp$. A reversible adiabatic displacement has $\delta Q=0$, and the [ideal gas law](../../../../ideal-gas-law.md) gives $v=RT/p$. Thus

$$
\frac{dT}{T}=\frac R{c_p}\frac{dp}{p},\qquad
\boxed{T(p)=T_0(p/p_0)^\gamma,\quad \gamma=R/c_p.}
$$

The atmospheric profile approaches this [dry adiabat](../../../../dry-adiabat.md) when [convection](../../../../convection-split.md) efficiently mixes potential [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and there is no [condensation](../../../../condensation.md) or other strong diabatic source. The first law by itself determines a parcel path, not every possible environmental [temperature profile](../../../../temperature-split.md); the [convective mixing](../../../../convection-split.md) assumption is needed. [Hydrostatic balance](../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) gives the corresponding height lapse rate $dT/dz=-g/c_p$.

For rigid linear molecules with translation and rotation active but vibration frozen, the molecular [heat capacities](../../../../heat-capacity.md) are $C_V=(5/2)R_u$ and $C_P=(7/2)R_u$, hence $R/c_p=2/7$. [Earth](../../../../earth.md) is dominated by the diatomic gases nitrogen and oxygen. [Mars](../../../../mars.md) is dominated by carbon dioxide, which is triatomic but linear and has the same two [rotational degrees of freedom](../../../../equipartition-theorem.md). In the rigid-molecule approximation it therefore has the same ratio. The value is not exact for carbon dioxide when its bending vibrations contribute appreciably to [heat capacity](../../../../heat-capacity.md), and variable [heat capacities](../../../../heat-capacity.md) give a nonconstant dry-adiabatic exponent. Here $\gamma$ denotes $R/c_p$, not the distinct adiabatic index $c_p/c_v$.
