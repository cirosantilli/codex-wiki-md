<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Both descriptions are depth-averaged [hydrostatic](../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) shallow-layer models. In a one-layer free-surface [shallow water equations](../../../../shallow-water-equations.md), a homogeneous layer overlies a rigid bottom and its upper surface moves. Surface elevation changes give a restoring acceleration based on full gravity $g$, with external-wave speed $\sqrt{gH_0}$. [Barotropic](../../../../barotropic-mode.md) coastal storm surges, long surface [gravity waves](../../../../gravity-wave-split.md) or a basin seiche are examples.

In the rigid-lid [reduced gravity](../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md) model, the movable boundary is a [density](../../../../density.md) interface beneath the active upper layer, while the much deeper lower layer remains at rest. The internal interface mode instead has speed $\sqrt{g'h_0}$ and represents, for example, thermocline displacements and an upper-ocean [baroclinic eddy](../../../../baroclinic-mode.md). The mathematical shallow-water form is retained with $g$ replaced by $g'$ and with the appropriate interface-depth convention. A freely moving two-layer surface would support both external and internal modes; the rigid lid filters out the fast external mode, and the resting abyss removes its independent dynamics.
