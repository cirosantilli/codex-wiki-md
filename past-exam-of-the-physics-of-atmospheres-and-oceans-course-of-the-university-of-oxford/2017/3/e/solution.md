<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A numerical scale estimate requires a reference ocean [density](../../../../density.md) and [latitude](../../../../geographical-latitude.md) not specified exactly. Taking $\rho_0\simeq1025\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $g\simeq9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and a representative midlatitude $|f|\simeq10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ gives

$$
g'\simeq9.6\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},\qquad
c=\sqrt{g'h_0}\simeq2.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
\boxed{L_R\simeq22\,\mathrm{km}.}
$$

For a small-amplitude interface displacement of scale $L=1\,\mathrm{km}\ll L_R$, gravity drives rapid horizontal acceleration and radiates most of the displacement in [inertia-gravity waves](../../../../inertia-gravity-wave.md). The gravity adjustment time is $L/c\sim8$ minutes, much shorter than $1/|f|\sim2.8$ hours. A weak balanced remnant remains in an unbounded inviscid adjustment problem; [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is carried away by waves, not dissipated merely by [geostrophic adjustment](../../../../geostrophic-adjustment.md).

For $L=100\,\mathrm{km}\gg L_R$, rotational adjustment produces a largely retained interface displacement with a [geostrophic current](../../../../geostrophic-flow.md). Wave emission and [inertial oscillations](../../../../inertial-oscillation.md) accompany the adjustment, but the balanced structure is much more important. To quantify that distinction under an initially resting, linear single-Fourier-component assumption, potential-vorticity conservation gives

$$
\frac{\eta_{\rm balanced}}{\eta_{\rm initial}}
=\frac{1}{1+(KL_R)^2},\qquad K\sim1/L.
$$

This is about $0.002$ for the kilometre scale and $0.95$ for the hundred-kilometre scale. These fractions use $L$ as an inverse [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md) scale, not a full [wavelength](../../../../wavelength.md), and are illustrative rather than unique predictions for an unspecified disturbance or bounded basin.
