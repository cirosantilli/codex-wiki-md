<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $K=(k^2+l^2)^{1/2}$ and $c=\sqrt{g'h_0}$. The three branches are

$$
\boxed{\omega=0,\qquad \omega_\pm=\pm\sqrt{f^2+c^2K^2}.}
$$

For nonzero $K$ and $f$, the zero-frequency mode is stationary [geostrophic flow](../../../../geostrophic-flow.md), with $f\mathbf k\times\mathbf u=-g'\nabla\eta$ and zero horizontal divergence. It carries the linear potential-vorticity disturbance. There is no Rossby-wave propagation on a constant-$f$ plane; a varying [Coriolis parameter](../../../../coriolis-parameter.md) would be needed for that.

The other two branches are oppositely phased [inertia-gravity waves](../../../../inertia-gravity-wave.md). Their phase-speed magnitude and radial [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) are $|\omega|/K=\sqrt{c^2+f^2/K^2}$ and $|\partial\omega/\partial K|=c^2K/\sqrt{f^2+c^2K^2}$. When $KL_R\gg1$, gravity dominates: $|\omega|\simeq cK$ and the waves are approximately nondispersive, with both speeds approaching $c$. When $KL_R\ll1$, rotation dominates: $|\omega|\simeq|f|+c^2K^2/(2|f|)$, the [frequency](../../../../frequency.md) approaches the [inertial frequency](../../../../coriolis-parameter.md) and the [group velocity](../../../../group-velocity.md) tends to zero. The large long-wave [phase speed](../../../../phase-velocity.md) is not a large signal speed.

Without rotation the propagating branches become nondispersive [gravity waves](../../../../gravity-wave-split.md), $\omega=\pm cK$, and the stationary branch is a nondivergent vortical velocity disturbance rather than a finite-pressure [geostrophic balance](../../../../geostrophic-balance.md). At $K=0$, the modes reduce to constant interface height and spatially uniform [inertial oscillations](../../../../inertial-oscillation.md). The wave branches have zero linear potential-vorticity perturbation; it is the stationary branch that retains the initial [potential vorticity](../../../../potential-vorticity.md).
