<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume an [ideal gas](../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) dominated by carbon dioxide, constant mean molar [heat capacity](../../../../heat-capacity.md) $C_p$, and a dry convective [adiabat](../../../../adiabat.md) connecting the stated emitting layer to the ground. Conservation of [entropy](../../../../entropy-split.md) gives $C_p\,d\ln T=R\,d\ln p$, where $R$ is the universal [molar gas constant](../../../../molar-gas-constant.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{T_s=260\left(\frac{90}{0.2}\right)^{8.31446/47}\,\mathrm K\simeq766\,\mathrm K.}
$$

The estimate extrapolates across a pressure ratio of $450$. It neglects radiatively stable layers, variable [heat capacity](../../../../heat-capacity.md), departures from an [ideal gas](../../../../ideal-gas-split.md) and changes in composition; these approximations limit its accuracy. A brightness temperature observed from space cannot generally be equated to a unique material level, but this calculation uses the layer interpretation explicitly supplied by the question.
