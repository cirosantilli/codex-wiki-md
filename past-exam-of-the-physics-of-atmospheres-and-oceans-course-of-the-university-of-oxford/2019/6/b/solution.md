<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity](../../../../three-dimensional-quasi-geostrophic-potential-vorticity.md) perturbation combines relative [vorticity](../../../../vorticity-split.md), $\nabla_h^2\psi'$, with the stretching contribution $f_0^2\partial_z(N^{-2}\psi'_z)$. The latter measures how buoyancy surfaces and rotating fluid columns are displaced in a stratified fluid. Even a flow with no vertical dependence can have nonzero relative vorticity, while its explicit stretching term vanishes.

For a vertically uniform plane wave with horizontal wavenumbers $(k,l)$, $q'=-(k^2+l^2)\psi'$. Substitution gives

$$
\omega-Uk=-\frac{\beta k}{k^2+l^2},\qquad
\boxed{c_{px}-U=-\frac{\beta}{k^2+l^2}<0\quad(\beta>0).}
$$

Thus phase propagates westward relative to the current. Physically, northward parcel displacement carries lower background planetary vorticity into a region with larger $f$, requiring negative relative vorticity to conserve total [potential vorticity](../../../../potential-vorticity.md); southward displacement requires positive relative vorticity. The velocity induced by that vorticity pattern shifts the displacement wave westward. This is a statement about phase motion, not a claim that every Rossby-wave packet has westward group velocity.
