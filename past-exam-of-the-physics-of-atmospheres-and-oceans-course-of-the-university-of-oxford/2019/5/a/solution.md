<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the horizontal rotating-fluid [equation of motion](../../../../equation-of-motion.md), scale advective acceleration as $U^2/L$ and [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) as $|f|U$, where $f=2\Omega\sin\phi$. Their ratio is the [Rossby number](../../../../rossby-number.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Ro}=\frac{U}{|f|L}.}
$$

A small [Rossby number](../../../../rossby-number.md) means that rotation strongly constrains the flow; with slow evolution, weak friction and a suitable pressure field it permits [geostrophic balance](../../../../geostrophic-balance.md). Local time dependence introduces the separate condition $1/(|f|t_{\rm evol})\ll1$. A synoptic midlatitude cyclone with $U\sim10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $L\sim1000\,\mathrm{km}$ and $|f|\sim10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ has $\mathrm{Ro}\sim0.1$. A tornado with $U\sim50\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $L\sim1\,\mathrm{km}$ has $\mathrm{Ro}\sim500$; its rotational motion does not mean that the Earth's rotation dominates its local dynamics.

The vertical [relative vorticity](../../../../relative-vorticity.md) is $\zeta=\partial_xv-\partial_yu\sim U/L$, while planetary [vorticity](../../../../vorticity-split.md) is $f$. Thus $|\zeta/f|$ is of the order of the [Rossby number](../../../../rossby-number.md), although it is not an identity for arbitrary velocity fields: an irrotational strain can have nonzero $U/L$ but zero $\zeta$. Absolute [vorticity](../../../../vorticity-split.md) is $f+\zeta$.

On large, slowly evolving midlatitude scales, the horizontal [pressure gradient force](../../../../pressure-gradient-force.md) is mainly balanced by the [Coriolis force](../../../../coriolis-force.md), which is perpendicular to velocity. Consequently the wind is nearly parallel to [atmospheric isobars](../../../../atmospheric-isobar.md), rather than accelerating directly into a low or out of a high. Curvature adds a [gradient wind](../../../../gradient-wind.md) correction, and surface friction permits the cross-isobar component considered in part (d).
