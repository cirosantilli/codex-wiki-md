# Minkowski metric

↑ **Parent:** [Minkowski spacetime](minkowski-spacetime.md)

With the mostly-plus [metric signature](metric-signature.md) $(-,+,+,+)$ and [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) $X^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$, the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) is the [nondegenerate bilinear form](nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md)

$$
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1),\qquad
U\cdot V=-U^0V^0+U^1V^1+U^2V^2+U^3V^3.
$$

It is a [symmetric bilinear form](symmetric-bilinear-form.md), but not a [positive-definite bilinear form](positive-definite-bilinear-form.md). For example, $U=(1,1,0,0)$ is nonzero yet $U\cdot U=0$, making it a [null vector](null-vector.md). This does not make the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) degenerate: some other [vectors](vector.md) have nonzero pairing with $U$.

Applied to infinitesimal displacements, the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) gives

$$
ds^2=-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2.
$$

For a [timelike curve](timelike-curve.md), a clock records $d\tau=\sqrt{-ds^2}/c$. For a [null curve](null-curve.md), $ds^2=0$. For [spacelike separation](spacelike-separation.md), the positive [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md) measures a different kind of separation. A [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md) preserves this form, expressed in [matrix](matrix.md) notation by $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta$, although it need not preserve ordinary [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md) in the four-component coordinate list.
