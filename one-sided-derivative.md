# One-sided derivative

↑ **Parent:** [Derivative](derivative.md)

The right and left [one-sided derivatives](one-sided-derivative.md) are the limits of the [difference quotient](difference-quotient.md) restricted respectively to positive and negative increments:

$$
f'_+(a)=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}h,
\qquad
f'_-(a)=\lim_{h\uparrow0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}h.
$$

At an interior point, [differentiability](differentiability.md) is equivalent to existence and equality of these two finite limits. For the [absolute value function](absolute-value.md), $f'_+(0)=1$ and $f'_-(0)=-1$, so it is continuous at zero but not differentiable there.
