# Planetary quadrupole coefficient

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md)

The dimensionless coefficient $J_2$ describes the leading axisymmetric departure of a planet's external [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) from a monopole: $\Phi=-GM_p/r+[GM_pJ_2R_p^2/r^3]P_2(\cos\vartheta)$, where $\vartheta$ is colatitude from the spin axis. An oblate mass distribution has positive $J_2$. The [disturbing function](disturbing-function.md) is minus the nonspherical potential term. On a circular inclined [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md), $\cos\vartheta=\sin I\sin M$, so [orbit averaging](orbit-averaging.md) gives $\overline{\mathcal R}_{J_2}=GM_pJ_2R_p^2a^{-3}(1/2-3\sin^2I/4)$. The exact circular nodal rate is $-3nJ_2(R_p/a)^2\cos I/2$; its small-tilt limit is $-3nJ_2(R_p/a)^2/2$.
