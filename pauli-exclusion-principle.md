# Pauli exclusion principle

↑ **Parent:** [Fermion](fermion.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle)

The [Pauli exclusion principle](pauli-exclusion-principle.md) says that each complete [one-particle state](one-particle-state.md) can have [fermionic occupation number](fermionic-occupation-number.md) zero or one. “Complete” includes [spin](spin.md) and any other internal labels, as well as the spatial [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md). Thus two [electrons](electron.md) can share a [spatial orbital](spatial-orbital.md) with opposite [spin](spin.md) projections: these are two different [spin orbitals](spin-orbital.md). Equal energy alone does not mean equal [quantum state](quantum-state.md), because [degenerate energy levels](degenerate-energy-levels.md) contain several independent [one-particle states](one-particle-state.md).

This distinction explains how an [electron shell](electron-shell.md) can accommodate several [electrons](electron.md) while exclusion still allows only one per complete [one-particle state](one-particle-state.md). It also makes a finite model of [fermionic state counting](fermionic-state-counting.md) surprisingly simple: two [spatial orbitals](spatial-orbital.md) with two [spin](spin.md) choices each give four complete [one-particle states](one-particle-state.md), and exactly six two-[electron](electron.md) basis states. The following diagram enumerates them; its boxes represent [occupation numbers](occupation-number.md), not locations in space.

**[Figure 1](#image-six-two-electron-occupation-states-in-two-spatial-orbitals-with-two-spin-projections-each). Six two-electron occupation states in two spatial orbitals with two spin projections each**.

Pauli's [Exclusion principle and quantum mechanics by Wolfgang Pauli](exclusion-principle-and-quantum-mechanics-by-wolfgang-pauli.md) is his retrospective account, delivered on 13 December 1946 for the 1945 Nobel Prize. On printed pp. 29–30, he describes proposing an extra two-valued [electron](electron.md) property in autumn 1924 and formulating exclusion in 1925. The crucial clue linked counts of resolved [energy eigenstates](energy-eigenstate.md) in alkali spectra under a [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) with the populations of closed [electron shells](electron-shell.md). Resolving every label turned a complicated shell capacity into one [electron](electron.md) per complete [one-particle state](one-particle-state.md). This historical route preceded the modern [spin](spin.md) interpretation and the antisymmetric [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) formulation; it was not originally a deduction from a [Slater determinant](slater-determinant.md).
