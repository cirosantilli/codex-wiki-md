# Associative Lie product

↑ **Parent:** [Associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md)

Over a [field](field.md) of [characteristic zero](characteristic-zero.md), an associative Lie product is a [bilinear map](bilinear-map.md) that is simultaneously an [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md) product and a [Lie bracket](lie-bracket.md). Such a product has every triple product zero. To prove this, write multiplication by juxtaposition. By [associativity](associative-property.md) and [antisymmetry of a Lie bracket](antisymmetry-of-a-lie-bracket.md),

$$
xyz=-xzy=zxy,
$$

since moving $z$ left through $y$ and then through $x$ introduces two minus signs. Applying [antisymmetry of a Lie bracket](antisymmetry-of-a-lie-bracket.md) instead to the two elements $xy$ and $z$ gives $(xy)z=-z(xy)$. Thus $xyz=-xyz$, and $A^3=0$ in [characteristic zero](characteristic-zero.md).

Conversely, an [alternating multilinear map](alternating-multilinear-map.md) defining a product whose triple products all vanish is associative, because both associations give zero, and satisfies the [Jacobi identity](jacobi-identity.md), because each nested product gives zero. A nonzero example has [basis](basis.md) $p,q,z$, products $pq=z=-qp$, and all other products of [basis vectors](basis-vector.md) zero. It is the [Heisenberg Lie algebra](heisenberg-lie-algebra.md) with its [Lie bracket](lie-bracket.md) used as the [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md) product. Its image $A^2=kz$ annihilates $A$ on both sides, and it has no [identity element](identity-element.md).
