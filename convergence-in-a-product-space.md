# Convergence in a product space

↑ **Parent:** [Product topology](product-topology.md)

For arbitrary [topological spaces](topological-space.md) $X_i$, an [index set](index-set.md) $I$, a [sequence](sequence.md) $x^{(n)}\in\prod_iX_i$, and a point $x$ in that [Cartesian product](cartesian-product.md), [convergence in a product space](convergence-in-a-product-space.md) to $x$ in the [product topology](product-topology.md) is equivalent to [coordinatewise convergence](convergence-in-a-product-space.md) in every factor. No countability or [Hausdorff](hausdorff-space.md) assumption on the factors is needed. For a [product space](product-space.md) of copies of the [real line](real-line.md), regarding each point as a [function](function-split.md) $I\to\mathbb R$ identifies this with [pointwise convergence](pointwise-convergence.md).

Proof outline: the forward implication follows because each [projection map](projection-map.md) is [continuous](continuous-function.md). Conversely, given a basic [neighborhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) $B(F,U)$ of $x$, each of its finitely many coordinate tests holds for all $n\geq N_i$. Taking $N=\max_{i\in F}N_i$ makes all tests hold simultaneously; if $F$ is empty, any threshold works. This proves eventual membership in every [neighborhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md), rather than only in a sampled collection of cylinders. The same equivalence holds for [nets](net-mathematics.md): a [directed set](directed-set.md) supplies a common upper bound for finitely many thresholds. [Sequences](sequence.md) alone need not characterize the whole [topology](topology-split.md) of an arbitrary [product space](product-space.md).
