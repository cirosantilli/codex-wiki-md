# Arc length

↑ **Parent:** [Riemannian geometry](riemannian-geometry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_length)

The arc length of a piecewise smooth [curve](curve.md) $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ on a [Riemannian manifold](riemannian-manifold.md) is the [integral](integral-split.md) of its speed:

$$
L_g(\gamma)=\int_a^b\|\dot\gamma(t)\|_g\,dt
=\int_a^b\sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))}\,dt.
$$

An odometer measures this accumulated travel rather than displacement between the endpoints. In [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md), the [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric-split.md) is the ordinary [inner product](inner-product-split.md), so the formula becomes $\int_a^b\sqrt{\sum_i\dot\gamma_i(t)^2}\,dt$.

For a [circle](circle.md) of radius $R$ traversed through an [angle](angle.md) $\alpha$, use $\gamma(t)=(R\cos(\alpha t),R\sin(\alpha t))$, $0\leq t\leq1$. Its speed is $R|\alpha|$, hence its [arc length](arc-length.md) is $R|\alpha|$. Traversing a full circle twice gives $4\pi R$, even though the starting and ending points coincide. The same formula measures the two [great circle](great-circle.md) arcs pictured in [geodesic](geodesic.md).

The [curve parameter](curve-parameter.md) controls how fast the journey happens, not its [arc length](arc-length.md). A regular increasing or decreasing [reparametrization](reparametrization.md) preserves the [integral](integral-split.md) by [change of variables](change-of-variables-formula.md); reversing direction leaves the speed [norm](norm.md) unchanged. Retracing part of a route does add [length](length.md), because it changes the actual journey.
