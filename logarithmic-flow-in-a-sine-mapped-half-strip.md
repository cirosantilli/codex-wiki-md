# Logarithmic flow in a sine-mapped half-strip

↑ **Parent:** [Complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md)

The [conformal map of a vertical half-strip by sine](conformal-map-of-a-vertical-half-strip-by-sine.md) sends $-\pi/2<\operatorname{Re}z<\pi/2$, $\operatorname{Im}z>0$ to the [upper half-plane](upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). The [complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow](complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md) $W(z)=\log(a+\sin z)$ therefore pulls back the radial logarithmic flow originating at $\zeta=-a$. Its complex velocity is $W'=\cos z/(a+\sin z)$, with a boundary singularity at $z=-\pi/2+i\operatorname{arcosh}a$. For $z=x+iy$, define $D=(a+\sin x\cosh y)^2+\cos^2x\sinh^2y$. Then

$$
u=\frac{\cos x(a\cosh y+\sin x)}D,\qquad v=\frac{\sinh y(\cosh y+a\sin x)}D.
$$

The bottom boundary carries rightward flow, the right boundary upward flow, and the left boundary flow away from the singular point. The velocity tends to $(0,1)$ at large height. The finite bottom corners are stagnation points, not singularities of $W$.
