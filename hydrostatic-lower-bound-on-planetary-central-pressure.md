# Hydrostatic lower bound on planetary central pressure

↑ **Parent:** [Exoplanet interior](exoplanet-interior.md)

The [hydrostatic pressure support equation](hydrostatic-pressure-support-equation.md) in enclosed-mass coordinate is $dP/dm=-Gm/(4\pi r^4)$. Since $r(m)\le R$, integration from surface pressure zero to the centre gives

$$
P_c=\int_0^M\frac{Gm}{4\pi r(m)^4}\,dm\ge\frac{GM^2}{8\pi R^4}.
$$

If [mass density](density.md) decreases outward, the mean density within $r$ is at least the global mean, so $r(m)\le R(m/M)^{1/3}$. This improves the bound to

$$
P_c\ge\frac{3GM^2}{8\pi R^4},
$$

with equality for uniform [mass density](density.md). Centrally concentrated planets need larger central [pressure](pressure.md) to support their inner mass.
