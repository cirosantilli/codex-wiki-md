# Aristarchus of Samos

↑ **Parent:** [Lunar phase](lunar-phase.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristarchus_of_Samos)

[Aristarchus of Samos](aristarchus-of-samos.md) used the half-lit [Moon](moon.md) to estimate the relative distances of the [Sun](sun.md) and [Moon](moon.md). In the spherical illumination model a half-lit disk means [phase angle](phase-angle-astronomy.md) $\alpha=\pi/2$, so the [Sun](sun.md)–[Moon](moon.md)–[Earth](earth.md) triangle is right-angled at the [Moon](moon.md). Keeping the [Sun](sun.md) at a finite distance, let $d_{EM}$ and $d_{ES}$ be the distances from [Earth](earth.md) to the [Moon](moon.md) and [Sun](sun.md). Then [elongation](elongation-astronomy.md) $\epsilon$ at [Earth](earth.md) gives

$$
\cos\epsilon=\frac{d_{EM}}{d_{ES}},\qquad \frac{d_{ES}}{d_{EM}}=\frac{1}{\cos\epsilon}.
$$

The $87^\circ$ [elongation](elongation-astronomy.md) attributed to [Aristarchus of Samos](aristarchus-of-samos.md) gives a ratio about $19.1$, consistent with his conclusion that the [Sun](sun.md) was between $18$ and $20$ times farther away. The example shows how [lunar phase](lunar-phase.md) geometry becomes a distance measurement, but the ratio is extremely sensitive to an [angle](angle.md) close to $90^\circ$. Unlike the distant-[Sun](sun.md) approximation, this finite triangle has [elongation](elongation-astronomy.md) slightly less than $90^\circ$ at half illumination. [Siebren van der Werf and Tjeerd van Albada, An explanation for Aristarchus' measurement of the sun's distance, 2025](https://doi.org/10.5802/crphys.246).
