<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [trine state ensemble](../../../../../../trine-state-ensemble.md)

$$
\boxed{|t_j\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+\omega^j|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
p_j=\frac13,\qquad j=0,1,2,\quad\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.}
$$

All three states are normalized. Since $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$, their average [density matrix](../../../../../../density-matrix-split.md) is

$$
\frac13\sum_{j=0}^2|t_j\rangle\langle t_j|
=\frac16\sum_{j=0}^2\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega^{-j}\\\omega^j&1\end{pmatrix}
=\frac I2.
$$

Geometrically, their unit [Bloch vectors](../../../../../../bloch-vector.md) form an [equilateral triangle](../../../../../../equilateral-triangle.md) on the equator and sum to zero. [Orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) is unnecessary for an ensemble decomposition of a [mixed state](../../../../../../mixed-state.md): three distinct, nonorthogonal [pure states](../../../../../../pure-state.md) can give the same maximally mixed average as two orthogonal states.
