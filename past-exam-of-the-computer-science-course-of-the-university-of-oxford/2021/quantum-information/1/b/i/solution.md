**No.** [Perfect discrimination of pure states requires orthogonality](../../../../../../perfect-discrimination-of-pure-states-requires-orthogonality.md), whereas

$$
\langle\phi_0|\phi_1\rangle=\frac14-\frac14+\frac12=\frac12\ne0.
$$

To see the obstruction directly for a [basic measurement](../../../../../../basic-measurement.md), let $\{|b_j\rangle\}$ be its [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md). Certain identification would require disjoint sets of possible outcome labels for two different preparations. For every $j$, at least one of $\langle b_j|\phi_0\rangle$ and $\langle b_j|\phi_1\rangle$ would therefore vanish. Completeness would then give

$$
\langle\phi_0|\phi_1\rangle=\sum_j\langle\phi_0|b_j\rangle\langle b_j|\phi_1\rangle=0,
$$

contradicting the computed overlap. Failure to distinguish just this pair already rules out certain identification of the three-state family.
