<h1 id="2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

**There is exactly one solution: $X_i=i$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$.** Strict increase makes all $n$ values distinct. Since all lie in the $n$-element domain $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, they must use every value, and their increasing order fixes their positions.

Equivalently, $X_i\ge i$ by propagation from the lower endpoint, and $X_i\le i$ by propagating backwards from $X_n\le n$. For general $m\ge n$, choosing any $n$-element subset determines one increasing solution, giving the [binomial coefficient](../../../../../binomial-coefficient-split.md) $\binom mn$; the case $m=n$ gives $1$.
