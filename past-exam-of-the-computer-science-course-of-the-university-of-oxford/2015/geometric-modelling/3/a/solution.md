For solid modelling, a [polyhedron](../../../../../polyhedron.md) is a solid whose boundary consists of finitely many planar [polygons](../../../../../polygon.md), called faces, meeting along straight edges and at [vertices of a polyhedron](../../../../../vertex-of-a-polyhedron.md). A face is not merely a list of arbitrary spatial [points](../../../../../point-geometry.md): its boundary must lie in one [plane](../../../../../plane.md) and bound a valid planar region. The boundary is closed and consistently oriented so that inside and outside are defined.

For example, a [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) has four triangular faces, six edges and four [vertices of a polyhedron](../../../../../vertex-of-a-polyhedron.md). A more general [polyhedron](../../../../../polyhedron.md) need not be [convex](../../../../../convex-function.md) and can have handles or cavities. The concise definition is

$$
\boxed{\text{a solid enclosed by finitely many planar polygonal faces}.}
$$
