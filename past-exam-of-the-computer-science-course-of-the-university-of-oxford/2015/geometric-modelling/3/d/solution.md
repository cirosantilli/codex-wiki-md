<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a finite nonempty collection $X=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}\subset\mathbb R^3$, its [convex hull](../../../../../convex-hull.md) is the smallest [convex set](../../../../../convex-set-split.md) containing all the [points](../../../../../point-geometry.md). Equivalently, it is the [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of all [convex combinations](../../../../../convex-combination.md):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{conv}X=
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_ip_i:\lambda_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i=1\right\}.}
$$

Every [convex set](../../../../../convex-set-split.md) containing the $p_i$ must contain these combinations; the displayed [set](../../../../../set-split.md) is itself [convex](../../../../../convex-function.md) and contains each $p_i$, proving the equivalence. For a full-dimensional finite collection this is a [convex polyhedron](../../../../../convex-polyhedron.md). A collinear or coplanar collection instead has a lower-dimensional [convex hull](../../../../../convex-hull.md), so the solid case needs a separate dimensionality assumption.
