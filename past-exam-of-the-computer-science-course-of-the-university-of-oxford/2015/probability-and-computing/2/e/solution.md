<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Consider any [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $\{u,v\}$ of the [simple graph](../../../../../simple-graph.md). One endpoint comes first in the [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) $\sigma$; suppose it is $u$. Then $v$ has a [graph neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) preceding it, namely $u$, so $v\notin S(\sigma)$. Therefore no [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) has both endpoints in $S(\sigma)$:

$$
\boxed{S(\sigma)\text{ is an independent set for every }\sigma.}
$$

This argument uses only the order of the endpoints, with no random choice required.
