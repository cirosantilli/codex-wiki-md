<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

With [existential rules](../../../../../../tuple-generating-dependency.md) understood as function-free [tuple-generating dependencies](../../../../../../tuple-generating-dependency.md), $\boxed{\text{only (ii)}}$ belongs to the fragment. Split its antecedent to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{Undergrad}(x)&\to\exists y\,\mathit{SupervisedBy}(x,y),\\
\mathit{Masters}(x)&\to\exists y\,\mathit{SupervisedBy}(x,y).
\end{aligned}
$$

These two [existential rules](../../../../../../tuple-generating-dependency.md) have exactly the same meaning as (ii): either category requires a supervisor, and when both hold the same supervisor may witness both requirements.

Sentence (i) requires a genuine choice between two positive alternatives and also an exclusion condition. Positive [existential rules](../../../../../../tuple-generating-dependency.md) cannot enforce that exclusion: interpreting all their relations as full relations on a one-element domain always satisfies them, whereas that interpretation violates (i). Sentence (iii), in the supplied vocabulary, uses the nonconstant function $\mathit{CountSupervise}$ and is therefore outside the function-free fragment. Introducing a new relation for the counting comparison would change the representation and require additional axioms; it is not an equivalent sentence in the stated vocabulary.
