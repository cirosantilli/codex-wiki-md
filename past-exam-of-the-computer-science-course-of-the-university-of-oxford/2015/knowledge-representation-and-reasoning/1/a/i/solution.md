↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The condition about taking the course restricts which students the [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) talks about. Within that condition we need both an inclusive [logical disjunction](../../../../../../logical-disjunction.md) and a prohibition on satisfying both alternatives:

$$
\boxed{\forall x\Bigl((\mathit{Student}(x)\land\mathit{Takes}(x,\mathit{KRR},2015))\to\bigl((\mathit{Undergrad}(x)\lor\mathit{Masters}(x))\land\neg(\mathit{Undergrad}(x)\land\mathit{Masters}(x))\bigr)\Bigr).}
$$

The [logical conjunction](../../../../../../logical-conjunction.md) on the right expresses exactly one of the two categories. It does not prohibit unrelated individuals from belonging to both categories: the restriction applies to the students in the antecedent.
