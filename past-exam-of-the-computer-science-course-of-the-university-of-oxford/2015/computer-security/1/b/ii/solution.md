<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The essential exponent condition is

$$
\gcd\bigl(3,(p-1)(q-1)\bigr)=1.
$$

Otherwise the [modular multiplicative inverse](../../../../../../modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) defining $d$ does not exist. For large [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) greater than three, this is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{p\equiv q\equiv2\pmod3.}
$$

If, for example, $p\equiv1\pmod3$, the multiplicative group modulo $p$ has order divisible by three, and cubing is not a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md); distinct [plaintexts](../../../../../../plaintext.md) can have the same [ciphertext](../../../../../../ciphertext.md). Unique [decryption](../../../../../../decryption.md) of all messages would then be impossible.

The [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) must also be distinct for the displayed totient formula, and selected sufficiently large and independently that [integer factorization](../../../../../../integer-factorization.md) of $n=pq$ is infeasible. A small factor, equal factors, or an exceptionally small difference between $p$ and $q$ gives an avoidable factorisation weakness. These size and generation conditions address security; the congruence condition addresses invertibility of cubing.
