↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**The [formal language](../../../../../../formal-language.md) is a [regular language](../../../../../../regular-language.md).** A [regular expression](../../../../../../regular-expression.md) for it is

$$
\boxed{(ab)^*(bc)^*.}
$$

The first [Kleene star](../../../../../../kleene-star.md) independently chooses $m$ copies of the [word](../../../../../../string.md) $ab$, and the second independently chooses $n$ copies of $bc$. Their [concatenation](../../../../../../concatenation.md) produces exactly the required [words](../../../../../../string.md). Either count may be zero, including both counts zero, which gives the [empty word](../../../../../../empty-word.md). By [Kleene theorem](../../../../../../kleene-theorem.md), the [regular expression](../../../../../../regular-expression.md) establishes regularity.
