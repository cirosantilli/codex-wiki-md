<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [feasible point](../../../../../feasible-point.md) $x$ is an [extreme point](../../../../../extreme-point.md) solution of a [linear program](../../../../../linear-programming-split.md) if it cannot be written as a nontrivial [convex combination](../../../../../convex-combination.md) of two distinct [feasible points](../../../../../feasible-point.md). Explicitly, if

$$
x=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)z,\qquad y,z\text{ feasible},\quad0<\lambda<1,
$$

then $y=z=x$. Equivalently, **$x$ lies in no open [line segment](../../../../../line-segment.md) joining two distinct [feasible points](../../../../../feasible-point.md)**. Being an [extreme point](../../../../../extreme-point.md) concerns the [feasible region](../../../../../feasible-region.md); it does not by itself mean that $x$ optimizes the [objective function](../../../../../objective-function.md).
