A [game tree](../../../../../game-tree.md) is a rooted [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) representing successive histories. A nonterminal [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) specifies the player who moves, its outgoing [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) are the available actions, and each leaf specifies a terminal [payoff](../../../../../payoff.md) for every player. A [pure strategy](../../../../../pure-strategy.md) specifies an action at every decision point belonging to that player, including decision points not reached by the eventual play.

For a [perfect-information game](../../../../../perfect-information-game.md), each player observes the exact preceding history. An [extensive-form game](../../../../../extensive-form-game.md) with imperfect information additionally groups indistinguishable decision points into [information sets](../../../../../information-set.md), with compatible available actions. Chance moves, if present, carry specified [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md); the displayed game has none. A [subgame perfect equilibrium](../../../../../subgame-perfect-equilibrium.md) must prescribe a [Nash equilibrium](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md) in every proper subgame as well as in the whole [game](../../../../../game.md).
