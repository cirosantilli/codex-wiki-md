↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The corresponding conditional [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are $|0\rangle/\sqrt2$ and $|+\rangle/\sqrt2$. The second has a nonzero $|1\rangle$ component while the first does not, so they are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md). Therefore **the third state is also [entangled](../../../../../../entangled-state.md)**. [Orthogonality](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) of these two conditional [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) is not required for this conclusion.

For an independent [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) check, expanding the second factor gives

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt2&0\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det C=\frac1{2\sqrt2}\ne0.
$$

The original expression is normalized because its two first-wire factors are [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md): its squared [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) is $(1+1)/2=1$, despite the overlap between $|0\rangle$ and $|+\rangle$ on the second wire.
