<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Taking [partial inner products](../../../../../../partial-inner-product.md) with $\langle0|$ and $\langle1|$ on the first wire gives $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/2$ and $(-|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/2$. They are nonzero and [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), hence [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md). This contradicts the necessary product-state property proved in part (a), so **the second state is [entangled](../../../../../../entangled-state.md)**.

Equivalently, its [tensor-product coefficient matrix](../../../../../../tensor-product-coefficient-matrix.md) is $C=\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, with $\det C=1/2\ne0$. The [two-qubit product-state determinant criterion](../../../../../../two-qubit-product-state-determinant-criterion.md) gives the same conclusion. In fact $CC^\dagger=I/2$, so its [Schmidt coefficients](../../../../../../schmidt-coefficient.md) are both $1/\sqrt2$: it is maximally [entangled](../../../../../../entangled-state.md).
