<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the three given [qutrit](../../../../../qutrit.md) [vectors](../../../../../vector.md), $\langle\phi_0|=(\langle0|-i\langle2|)/\sqrt2$ and $\langle\phi_2|=(\langle0|+i\langle2|)/\sqrt2$. Taking the [complex conjugate](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) in the bra is essential. Their squared [norms](../../../../../norm.md) are $1,1,1$, and

$$
\langle\phi_0|\phi_2\rangle=\tfrac12\{1+(-i)(-i)\}=0,\qquad \langle\phi_0|\phi_1\rangle=\langle\phi_2|\phi_1\rangle=0.
$$

Thus they are an [orthonormal set](../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) of three [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) in the three-dimensional [inner product space](../../../../../inner-product-space.md) $\mathbb C^3$. Such a set is [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) and spans the space, so **it is an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md)**.
