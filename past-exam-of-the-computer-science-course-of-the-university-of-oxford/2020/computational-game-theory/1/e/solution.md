<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**Neither asymmetric purchase outcome is a [Nash equilibrium](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md) for any permitted $P>0,n>0$.** At $(b,nb)$, X's chosen purchase requires $P\le2t/3$, but Y's chosen nonpurchase, against X buying, requires $P\ge t$. These requirements are incompatible because $2t/3<t$.

At $(nb,b)$, Y's chosen purchase requires $P\le t/3$, whereas X's nonpurchase against a buying Y requires $P\ge t$. Again the inequalities are incompatible. Thus every [Nash equilibrium](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md) in [pure strategies](../../../../../pure-strategy.md) has both players taking the same action; the conclusion is stronger than merely observing symmetry in their available actions, since their original market shares differ.
