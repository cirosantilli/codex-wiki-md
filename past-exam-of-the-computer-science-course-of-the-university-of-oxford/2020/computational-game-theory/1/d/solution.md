<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

When neither buys, X's [unilateral deviation](../../../../../unilateral-deviation.md) gains $2t/3-P$, while Y's gains $t/3-P$. For both to be nonpositive, $P$ must exceed the larger threshold. Hence

$$
\boxed{(nb,nb)\text{ is a Nash equilibrium iff }P\ge\frac{2n}{3(n+3)}.}
$$

Equality is included because a zero-gain deviation does not violate a [Nash equilibrium](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md). Consequently both joint-purchase and joint-nonpurchase [Nash equilibria](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md) exist throughout $2t/3\le P\le t$, a coordination region.
