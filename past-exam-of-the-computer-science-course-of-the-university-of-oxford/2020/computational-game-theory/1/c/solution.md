<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At joint purchase, each player's gain from continuing to buy rather than deviating to $nb$ is $(t-P)/2$. Both actions are [best responses](../../../../../best-response.md) exactly when this is nonnegative. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(b,b)\text{ is a Nash equilibrium iff }0<P\le\frac n{n+3}.}
$$

At equality both players are indifferent to their individual [unilateral deviations](../../../../../unilateral-deviation.md), which is permitted in a [Nash equilibrium](../../../../../nash-equilibrium.md). The condition is less restrictive than requiring either player's action to be a [weakly dominant strategy](../../../../../weakly-dominant-strategy.md): only the opponent's actual action needs to be considered.
