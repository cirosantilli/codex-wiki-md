<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The assignment is [Pareto optimal](../../../../../pareto-efficiency.md).** Write $a_i$ for the room selected by the $i$th student. Suppose another assignment $a'$ makes every student at least as well off. The first student already selected their uniquely most valuable room, so $a'_1=a_1$. Once the rooms of students $1,\ldots,i-1$ are fixed, the rooms available to student $i$ in the original [serial dictatorship](../../../../../serial-dictatorship.md) are exactly the rooms that remain after those fixed assignments. Student $i$ chose the unique maximum among them, so weak improvement requires $a'_i=a_i$.

[Mathematical induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) fixes every student's room. Thus $a'=a$ and nobody can be strictly better off, contradicting the definition of a [Pareto improvement](../../../../../pareto-improvement.md). Strict room rankings and the absence of [allocation externalities](../../../../../allocation-externality.md) are the essential assumptions: a student's [utility](../../../../../utility.md) is determined solely by their own room.[§13.2, Definition 13.1 and Theorem 13.2: Pareto efficiency for strict preferences](../../../../../one-sided-matchings-house-allocation-problems-in-the-cornell-networks-and-markets-text.md)
