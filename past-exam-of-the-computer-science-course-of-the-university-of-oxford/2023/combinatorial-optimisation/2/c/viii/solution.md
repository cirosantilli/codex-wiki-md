<h1 id="2/c/viii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Viii](../viii.md)

Write $r(Y)=r$. Since $y\notin\sigma(Y)$, the one-element increment bound gives $r(Y+y)=r+1$. Since $y\in\sigma(Y+x)$,

$$
r(Y+x+y)=r(Y+x)\leq r+1.
$$

But $Y+y\subseteq Y+x+y$ gives the reverse lower bound $r(Y+x+y)\geq r(Y+y)=r+1$. Hence $r(Y+x+y)=r(Y+y)$, which is precisely **$x\in\sigma(Y+y)$**. This is the [matroid closure exchange property](../../../../../../matroid-closure-exchange-property.md); it follows from one-element [rank of a matroid](../../../../../../rank-of-a-matroid.md) increments and does not require exchanging an entire [matroid basis](../../../../../../basis-of-a-matroid.md).
