<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Two [unitary transformations](../../../../../../unitary-operator.md) $U,V$ on the same [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) are $\varepsilon$-close in [operator-norm distance between unitary gates](../../../../../../operator-norm-distance-between-unitary-gates.md) when

$$
\boxed{\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\sup_{\|\psi\|_2=1}\bigl\|U|\psi\rangle-V|\psi\rangle\bigr\|_2\leq\varepsilon.}
$$

Thus their outputs are $\varepsilon$-close in [pure-state norm distance](../../../../../../pure-state-norm-distance.md) for every normalized input. This is an [operator norm](../../../../../../operator-norm.md) condition, stronger than checking only one chosen input. If [global phase](../../../../../../global-phase.md) is disregarded for the whole operation, use $\min_\gamma\|U-e^{i\gamma}V\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ instead, with one phase chosen for the operator rather than separately for each input.
