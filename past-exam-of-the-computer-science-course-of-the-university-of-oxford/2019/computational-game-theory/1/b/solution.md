<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Neither player has a [weakly dominant strategy](../../../../../weakly-dominant-strategy.md) among its [pure strategies](../../../../../pure-strategy.md).** Against an opponent at site one, the unique [best response](../../../../../best-response.md) is site two, yielding $2k-2$. Co-location yields only $k$, and moving further right reduces one's [payoff](../../../../../payoff.md). Against an opponent at site $k$, the unique [best response](../../../../../best-response.md) is $k-1$, by reflection. For $k\geq4$, these two sites differ. No single [pure strategy](../../../../../pure-strategy.md) can therefore be a [best response](../../../../../best-response.md) to both opposing actions, as a [weakly dominant strategy](../../../../../weakly-dominant-strategy.md) would require.
