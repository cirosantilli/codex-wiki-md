<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A pure action is weakly dominated by a mixture when the mixture's [expected payoff](../../../../../expected-payoff.md) is at least as high against every opposing pure [strategy profile](../../../../../strategy-profile.md), and strictly higher against at least one. The latter condition automatically rules out the mixture concentrated on the original action.

**Yes: the pure-domination conclusion remains true for this weak version.** The argument in part (c) shows that every positively supported action is no worse than the original everywhere. At a profile with strict mixed improvement, the original binary [payoff](../../../../../payoff.md) must be zero and the mixed [expected payoff](../../../../../expected-payoff.md) positive. Some positively supported action therefore pays one there. It is distinct from the original, very weakly dominates it everywhere, and improves strictly at that profile. Thus it **weakly dominates the original pure action**. Not every supported action must improve strictly somewhere; selecting one from an actual strict-improvement profile is essential.
