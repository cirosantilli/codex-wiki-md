<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For normalized [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) representatives in the same [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), the vector-norm convention is **$\||\psi\rangle-|\phi\rangle\|_2\le\varepsilon$**. Since physical [pure states](../../../../../../pure-state.md) identify [global phases](../../../../../../global-phase.md), a phase-insensitive version is

$$
\boxed{\min_{\theta\in\mathbb R}\||\psi\rangle-e^{i\theta}|\phi\rangle\|_2=\sqrt{2-2|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|}\le\varepsilon.}
$$

The equality follows by choosing the phase to make the overlap real and nonnegative. This states an explicit [pure-state norm distance](../../../../../../pure-state-norm-distance.md) convention; fidelity-based or trace-distance conventions use different numerical error parameters. We use the phase-insensitive convention for the universality claim below.
