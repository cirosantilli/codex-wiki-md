<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $p=|\alpha|^2$ and $1-p=|\beta|^2$. The first [basic measurement](../../../../../basic-measurement.md) has outcome [probabilities](../../../../../probability.md) $p$ and $1-p$, obtained by conjugating the amplitudes in the bras. Conditional on its first outcome, the next [computational-basis measurement](../../../../../quantum-measurement-in-the-computational-basis.md) returns 0 with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $p$; conditional on its second outcome, that [probability](../../../../../probability.md) is $1-p$. The [law of total probability](../../../../../law-of-total-probability.md) therefore gives

$$
\Pr(\text{second outcome }0)=p^2+(1-p)^2=\frac12+2\left(p-\frac12\right)^2.
$$

Consequently the **minimum is $1/2$, attained exactly when $|\alpha|=|\beta|=1/\sqrt2$**. The relative complex phases do not affect this result. The alternatives after the first measurement form a classical mixture; their amplitudes must not be added coherently as if no measurement occurred.
