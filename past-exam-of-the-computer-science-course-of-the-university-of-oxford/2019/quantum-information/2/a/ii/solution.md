A bipartite [unitary operator](../../../../../../unitary-operator.md) is an **[entangling gate](../../../../../../entangling-gate.md) if it maps at least one [product state](../../../../../../product-state.md) to an [entangled state](../../../../../../entangled-state.md)**. It need not entangle every product input. For example, the [controlled-NOT gate](../../../../../../controlled-not-gate.md) entangles $|+\rangle|0\rangle$ but leaves $|00\rangle$ as a product. A gate that preserves every product input is not entangling, even if it exchanges the two subsystems.
