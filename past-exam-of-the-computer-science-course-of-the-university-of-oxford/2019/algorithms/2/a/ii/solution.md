<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An [extreme point](../../../../../../extreme-point.md) $p\in P$ cannot be written as a nontrivial [convex combination](../../../../../../convex-combination.md) of two distinct [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) of $P$: if $p=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v$ with $u,v\in P$ and $0<\lambda<1$, then $u=v=p$.

A [vertex of a linear polyhedron](../../../../../../vertex-of-a-linear-polyhedron.md) is a [feasible point](../../../../../../feasible-point.md) at which the [active constraints](../../../../../../active-constraint.md) include $n$ [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) row normals. Equivalently, some $n$ independent bounding [hyperplanes](../../../../../../hyperplane.md) intersect uniquely at that [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md). This definition does not require $P$ to be bounded or full-dimensional. For a [linear polyhedron](../../../../../../linear-polyhedron.md), it is also equivalent to the [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) uniquely maximizing a suitable [linear function](../../../../../../linear-function.md): sum the selected active inequalities to construct one such [linear function](../../../../../../linear-function.md).
