**No.** For the [linear polyhedron](../../../../../../linear-polyhedron.md) $P=[0,1]^2$, take $c=(1,0)^T$ and $x^*=(1,1/2)^T$. The maximum of $c^Tx$ is one, attained at $x^*$. Yet

$$
x^*=\tfrac12(1,0)^T+\tfrac12(1,1)^T,
$$

so $x^*$ is not an [extreme point](../../../../../../extreme-point.md), hence not a [vertex of a linear polyhedron](../../../../../../vertex-of-a-linear-polyhedron.md). An [optimal face](../../../../../../optimal-face-of-a-linear-program.md) can contain many [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md); an arbitrary maximizer need not be one of its [vertices of a linear polyhedron](../../../../../../vertex-of-a-linear-polyhedron.md).
