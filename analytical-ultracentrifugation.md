# Analytical ultracentrifugation

↑ **Parent:** [Centrifugation](centrifugation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_ultracentrifugation)

Analytical ultracentrifugation observes concentration profiles during rotation, rather than only recovering a separated pellet. Sector-shaped sample cells rotate past optical detectors. Absorbance scans measure attenuation at selected radii. A [Rayleigh interferometer](rayleigh-interferometer.md) compares a sample sector with a solvent reference, converting concentration-dependent refractive-index changes into fringe shifts. Repeating scans follows a sedimentation boundary or the approach to a stationary distribution.[Radial concentration scans, synchronized absorbance detection and reference-sector interference](optical-systems-used-for-auc-by-beckman-coulter-life-sciences.md)

For a dilute solute of buoyant mass $m_b$ and translational friction $f_h$, radial force balance gives $s=m_b/f_h$, where $s$ is the [sedimentation coefficient](sedimentation-coefficient.md). [Diffusion](diffusion.md) supplies $D=k_BT/f_h$. A velocity experiment resolves boundary motion and broadening; a [sedimentation equilibrium](sedimentation-equilibrium.md) experiment instead measures $d\ln c/d(r^2)=m_b\omega^2/(2k_BT)$. This follows from the [Boltzmann distribution](boltzmann-distribution.md) in centrifugal potential $-m_b\omega^2r^2/2$. The two approaches separate frictional size information from buoyant mass information; shape, hydration and solvent properties must be specified to infer a unique geometric dimension.
