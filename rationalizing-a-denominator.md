# Rationalizing a denominator

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)

Multiplying numerator and denominator of a [fraction](fraction.md) by the same nonzero expression can remove a [square root](square-root.md) from the denominator. For instance,

$$
\frac1{\sqrt a+b}=\frac{\sqrt a-b}{a-b^2}
$$

when $a\geq0$ and both denominators are nonzero. This uses the difference-of-squares identity $(\sqrt a+b)(\sqrt a-b)=a-b^2$. If $a=b^2$, the original expression may still be defined even though the rationalized quotient is not, so that case must be handled separately. The related [rationalization of a square-root difference](rationalization-of-a-square-root-difference.md) applies the same multiplication to a numerator.
