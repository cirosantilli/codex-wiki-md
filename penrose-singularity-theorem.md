# Penrose singularity theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Null geodesic completeness](null-geodesic-completeness.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrose_singularity_theorem)

The Penrose singularity theorem states that a globally hyperbolic spacetime with a noncompact Cauchy hypersurface, a closed trapped surface, and the null convergence condition is future null-geodesically incomplete. Time reversal replaces the trapped surface by an anti-trapped surface and gives past incompleteness.

In his 1965 paper [Gravitational Collapse and Space-Time Singularities](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.14.57), [Roger Penrose](roger-penrose.md) used [trapped surfaces](trapped-surface.md) and global [causal structure](causal-structure.md) to address whether nonspherical perturbations could avert a breakdown during [gravitational collapse](gravitational-collapse.md). [Quasars](quasar.md) supplied the opening astrophysical motivation. The paper used [metric signature](metric-signature.md) $(+,-,-,-)$ and a stronger timelike local-energy assumption; the statement above uses the modern [null convergence condition](null-convergence-condition.md).[pp. 57–58, motivation and trapping](gravitational-collapse-and-space-time-singularities-by-roger-penrose.md)

For this version, take a smooth connected four-dimensional [spacetime](spacetime.md) with a [time orientation](time-orientation.md). A [Cauchy hypersurface](cauchy-surface.md) meets every inextendible [causal curve](causal-curve.md) exactly once; its existence expresses [global hyperbolicity](globally-hyperbolic-spacetime.md). The hypersurface must be noncompact, meaning not [compact](compact-space.md); this is a global spatial condition, not an assumption about the size of the collapsing body. The [null convergence condition](null-convergence-condition.md) is $R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0$ for every [null vector](null-vector.md) $k$. Through the [Einstein field equations](einstein-field-equations-split.md) it follows from the [null energy condition](null-energy-condition.md); a [cosmological constant](cosmological-constant.md) drops out of the null contraction.[Theorem 2, printed p. 10; convergence condition (6)](the-1965-penrose-singularity-theorem-by-jose-m-martin-senovilla-and-david-garfinkle.md)

The conclusion is that at least one future-inextendible [null geodesic](null-geodesic.md) has a finite upper endpoint of its [affine parameter](affine-parameter.md). It neither asserts infinite [mass density](density.md) or divergent [curvature](curvature.md) at every endpoint, nor says that such an endpoint is visible. Locating an [event horizon](event-horizon.md) requires additional global information; the theorem is not a horizon-finding rule for arbitrary [spacetimes](spacetime.md). [Weak cosmic censorship conjecture](weak-cosmic-censorship-conjecture.md) is an additional issue.[section 4.1, printed pp. 11–12; section 6.1, cosmic censorship](the-1965-penrose-singularity-theorem-by-jose-m-martin-senovilla-and-david-garfinkle.md)

The [supermassive black hole](supermassive-black-hole.md) account describes the motivating observational evidence from [stellar orbits](stellar-orbit.md) at the [Galactic centre](galactic-center.md). Such measurements infer a compact gravitating [mass](mass.md). They do not observe a [trapped surface](trapped-surface.md), measure a geodesic's complete future, or experimentally prove the [Penrose singularity theorem](penrose-singularity-theorem.md).
