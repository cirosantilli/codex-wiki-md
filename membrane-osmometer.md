# Membrane osmometer

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](osmosis.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_osmometer)

A [membrane osmometer](membrane-osmometer.md) connects [osmosis](osmosis.md) to a visible liquid level or a measured balancing [pressure](pressure-split.md). In a teaching version, a [solute](solute.md)-containing pouch is joined to a narrow vertical tube and submerged in external [water](water.md). Entry increases the enclosed [volume](volume.md) and raises the meniscus. Both the column and any stretched pouch can contribute an opposing [pressure](pressure-split.md).

The University of Toronto's [Osmosis (Brine) by University of Toronto Department of Physics](osmosis-brine-by-university-of-toronto-department-of-physics.md) demonstration (activity Me-012b, Description, steps 1–14) specifies soaking a dialysis membrane tube, tying one end closed and fastening the other around a thistle tube's narrow end. A stand holds the tube. Colored brine is cooled to room [temperature](temperature-split.md) and injected using a syringe to reduce bubbles; the pouch is completely immersed in a $1$ or $2\,\mathrm L$ beaker of distilled [water](water.md). A taped marker records the initial level; subsequent meniscus height is the readout. The protocol proposes another pouch containing distilled [water](water.md) as a comparison, and its older instructions explicitly check seals for leaks.[Osmosis (Brine), Description steps 1–14, water-filled comparison and Old Description A.2: apparatus, readout and leak check](osmosis-brine-by-university-of-toronto-department-of-physics.md)

A rise is consistent with net [water](water.md) entry. To infer [osmotic pressure](osmotic-pressure.md) quantitatively, use a retained [solute](solute.md) and establish a stable equilibrium after accounting for [dilution](dilution.md). If both free surfaces are at the same atmospheric [pressure](pressure-split.md), the liquids have approximately equal [mass densities](density.md) $\rho$, and the pouch adds negligible mechanical load, the [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) difference at membrane height is approximately $\Delta P=\rho gh$, where $h$ is the meniscus height above the external surface and $g$ is [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md). This is the liquid-column inference, not a pressure calibration supplied by the Toronto demonstration. For unequal [mass densities](density.md), compare the two column pressures separately.

Useful additional controls are matched initial levels and geometry, constant [temperature](temperature-split.md), replicate runs, and monitoring external [solute](solute.md) to test retention. These are proposed checks, not reported Toronto results. Leaks, trapped bubbles, [surface tension](surface-tension-split.md) in the narrow tube, [evaporation](evaporation.md) and membrane stretching can alter the level. In particular, a dialysis membrane may pass dissolved salt: the brine setup illustrates transient movement but cannot be assumed to retain every dissolved [ion](ion.md). Color improves visibility without proving salt retention. A stationary level can reflect weakening [concentration](concentration.md) differences or leakage rather than osmotic [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md); a rising level alone supplies neither $n$ nor a verified value of $nRT/V$.
