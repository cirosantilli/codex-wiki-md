# Intrinsic phase averages in a two-fluid pipe model

↑ **Parent:** [Multiphase flow](multiphase-flow.md)

Here $\langle\cdot\rangle$ means integration over a fixed pipe cross-section divided by its area, and $\chi_1$ is the indicator of one immiscible fluid. The [volume fraction](volume-fraction.md) is $\alpha$; intrinsic [density](density.md) averages over the phase-occupied area rather than over the whole section. The [velocity](velocity.md) must be [density](density.md)-weighted for its product with mean phase [density](density.md) to represent [mass flux](mass-flux.md) exactly.

If there is no interphase mass transfer, integrating $\partial_t(\rho\chi_1)+\nabla\cdot(\rho v\chi_1)=0$ over a section with impermeable outer wall gives $\partial_t(\rho_1\alpha)+\partial_x(\rho_1\alpha u_1)=0$. The second phase uses $1-\alpha$ and its own intrinsic averages. For constant phase [density](density.md) the [velocity](velocity.md) reduces to the ordinary phase-area average. These definitions apply where the corresponding phase fraction is positive; zero-fraction intrinsic quantities need a separate convention.
