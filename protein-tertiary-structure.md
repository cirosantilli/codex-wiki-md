# Protein tertiary structure

↑ **Parent:** [Protein structure](protein-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_tertiary_structure)

The [protein tertiary structure](protein-tertiary-structure.md) is the overall spatial arrangement of one [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md). It includes how its [protein secondary structures](protein-secondary-structure.md) and intervening regions fit together. For example, the schematic under [protein](protein.md) places positions 4 and 9 near each other despite their separation in [protein primary structure](protein-primary-structure.md). Such cooperation can help construct an [active site](active-site.md); sequence distance and spatial separation answer different questions. [Hydrophobicity](hydrophobicity.md), [backbone hydrogen bonds](backbone-hydrogen-bond.md) and other interactions help stabilize folds, and some [proteins](protein.md) additionally contain [protein disulfide bonds](protein-disulfide-bond.md). See [Cooper: tertiary structure and interactions between chain regions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9879/).
