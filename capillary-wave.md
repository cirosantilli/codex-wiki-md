# Capillary wave

↑ **Parent:** [Surface tension](surface-tension.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave)

A [capillary wave](capillary-wave.md) is a [wave](wave.md) along a [fluid](fluid.md) interface whose restoring stress is predominantly supplied by [surface tension](surface-tension.md). A curved [free surface](free-surface.md) requires a [pressure](pressure.md) jump by the [Young–Laplace equation](young-laplace-equation.md); increasing its corrugation also costs [surface energy](surface-energy.md). In the ideal limit, inertia carries the interface through its flat position and the resulting [oscillation](oscillation.md) propagates. When [gravity](gravity.md) also contributes appreciably, use the combined [gravity-capillary wave](gravity-capillary-wave.md) model. Within the [capillary wave](capillary-wave.md) regime, shortening a ripple makes it travel faster. [David Tong, Fluid Mechanics, §4.1.3: the capillary restoring pressure](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/fluid-mechanics/fluids3.pdf)

The [laser-diffraction measurement of capillary waves](laser-diffraction-measurement-of-capillary-waves.md) below turns this mechanism into a test: set the [frequency](frequency.md), measure the [wavelength](wavelength.md) independently with [light](light.md), and ask whether the [dispersion relation](dispersion-relation.md) follows. The theory is useful both for predicting how ripple [wave packets](wave-packet.md) separate and for inferring [surface tension](surface-tension.md) from their motion.
