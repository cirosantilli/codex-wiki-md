# Edge count of a complete graph

↑ **Parent:** [Complete graph](complete-graph.md)

For a [finite set](finite-set.md) of $n$ [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md), a [simple graph](simple-graph.md) selects some of their unordered pairs as [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md). There are $n(n-1)$ ordered pairs of distinct [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md); each unordered pair is counted twice, giving $\binom n2$ possibilities. Hence every [simple graph](simple-graph.md) $G$ on those [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) satisfies

$$
|E(G)|\leq\binom n2,
$$

with equality exactly when $G$ is a [complete graph](complete-graph.md). The [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md) also shows why the count grows quadratically with the number of [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md).
