<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The generic instrument uses a magnetically responsive bead, a controllable external field and optical tracking of bead position or orientation. A field gradient supplies translational [force](../../../../../force-split.md); rotating an appropriate anisotropic field or bead moment can supply [torque](../../../../../torque.md). For a permanent dipole,

$$
U=-\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B,\qquad\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B),\qquad\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf m\times\mathbf B.
$$

For an isotropic unsaturated induced moment with effective susceptibility contrast $\Delta\chi$ and bead volume $V$, the [induced magnetic bead force](../../../../../induced-magnetic-bead-force.md) instead follows from

$$
U=-\frac{V\Delta\chi}{2\mu_0}B^2,\qquad\boxed{\mathbf F=\frac{V\Delta\chi}{2\mu_0}\nabla B^2.}
$$

The factor one-half accounts for inducing the moment. A spatially uniform field alone supplies no translational [force](../../../../../force-split.md); an isotropic induced moment parallel to the field supplies no orienting [torque](../../../../../torque.md) either. Saturation or magnetic anisotropy requires the corresponding [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) model.

Position tracking needs a length calibration, and [force](../../../../../force-split.md) calibration needs measured field gradients, a known reference load, or calibrated [thermal fluctuations](../../../../../thermal-fluctuation.md). For a generic tensioned tether of extension $\ell$, small transverse displacement $x$ costs energy $Fx^2/(2\ell)$, so $F=k_BT\ell/\langle x^2\rangle$ under equilibrium, small-angle and single-mode assumptions. Attachment geometry, bead rotation, wall drag and finite acquisition bandwidth can affect this calibration. These equations describe generic [magnetic tweezers](../../../../../magnetic-tweezers.md) mechanics, without assigning a biological response.
