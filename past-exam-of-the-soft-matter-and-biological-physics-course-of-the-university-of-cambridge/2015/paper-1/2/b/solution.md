**[Fluctuation-induced forces](../../../../../fluctuation-induced-force.md) arise because separation changes the fluctuations available to a system and therefore changes its [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md).** The [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is $F=-\partial\mathcal F/\partial d$ at fixed relevant control variables, not necessarily a [force](../../../../../force-split.md) from a permanent charge or dipole.

For two neutral polarizable particles, correlated electromagnetic fluctuations give a short-distance dispersion attraction of the form $-C_6/r^6$. Retardation changes the asymptotic distance dependence. Boundaries produce a related [Casimir force](../../../../../casimir-force.md); for parallel ideal conducting plates at zero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md),

$$
\frac{\mathcal E}{A}=-\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{720d^3},\qquad\boxed{P=-\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240d^4}.}
$$

The negative [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) denotes attraction. Material response, nonplanar geometry and finite [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) alter the ideal result. The effect can be described through the separation-dependent electromagnetic mode spectrum rather than by treating a divergent absolute vacuum energy as observable.[Section2.3.2: Casimir effect and boundary-dependent fluctuation energy](../../../../../quantum-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)

In a classical [depletion force](../../../../../depletion-force.md), smaller particles or [polymer](../../../../../polymer-split.md) coils are excluded from a region near larger surfaces. Overlap of excluded regions increases their available volume. For ideal dilute depletants of osmotic [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) $\Pi$, $U(d)=-\Pi V_{\rm overlap}(d)$; the resulting attraction is entropic and scales with [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Confined interface fluctuations can instead generate repulsion when close approach removes [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md). Near a continuous phase transition, a large [correlation length](../../../../../correlation-length.md) makes boundary-conditioned order-parameter fluctuations produce a [critical Casimir force](../../../../../critical-casimir-force.md). Its sign depends on the boundary preferences, so fluctuation forces are not universally attractive.

The useful common idea is a free-energy difference with explicit boundary conditions; quantum vacuum, thermal field and excluded-volume mechanisms have different physical fluctuations and different ranges.
