# Surface tidal time scale of a spherical gravitating body

↑ **Parent:** [Tidal tensor](tidal-tensor.md)

At [radius](radius.md) $R$ outside a spherical [mass](mass.md) $M$, the Newtonian relative-acceleration [tensor](tensor.md) has [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $-g/R,-g/R,2g/R$ with $g=GM/R^2$. It compresses [free fall](free-fall.md) separations tangentially and stretches them radially. The displayed time scale is the inverse square root of the [curvature](curvature.md) scale controlling this tidal evolution. An ideal [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md) at that [radius](radius.md) has period $2\pi t_{\rm tide}$, providing a second physical interpretation. For Earth's surface it is about $806$ seconds, while the corresponding ideal low-orbit period is about $84$ minutes. A uniform gravitational acceleration does not determine this relative motion; spatial variation of the field does.
