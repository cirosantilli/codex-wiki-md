# Metric

↑ **Parent:** [Metric space](metric-space.md)

A [metric](metric.md) on a [set](set-split.md) $X$ is a [function](function-split.md) $d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)$ satisfying, for every $x,y,z\in X$:

- $d(x,y)\geq0$: distances are nonnegative and finite.
- $d(x,y)=0$ if and only if $x=y$: distinct elements have positive distance.
- $d(x,y)=d(y,x)$: exchanging the endpoints leaves the distance unchanged.
- $d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)$: the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md).

A [metric](metric.md) equips its underlying [set](set-split.md) with a [metric space](metric-space.md) structure; the distance [function](function-split.md) and the space carrying it are distinct concepts. The second requirement distinguishes a [metric](metric.md) from a [pseudometric](pseudometric.md), which may identify distinct elements at zero distance. The symmetry requirement also explains why directed travel times, such as uphill versus downhill times, need not define a [metric](metric.md).

A tempting alternative on the [real numbers](real-number.md) is $d(x,y)=(x-y)^2$. It is nonnegative, symmetric, and zero exactly on equal endpoints, but fails the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md): $d(0,2)=4>1+1=d(0,1)+d(1,2)$. Checking only the first three requirements would miss the failure.
