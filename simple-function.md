# Simple function

↑ **Parent:** [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_function)

A simple function is a [measurable function](measurable-function.md) whose [image](image-of-a-function.md) is a [finite set](finite-set.md). Its value sets $A_j=\{x:s(x)=a_j\}$ form a finite [measurable partition](measurable-partition.md). For nonnegative $a_j$, its [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md) is $\sum_ja_j\mu(A_j)$, with the convention $0\cdot\infty=0$. The pieces need not be intervals, or even [connected](connected-space.md) [sets](set-split.md). See [MIT lecture 10, Definitions 100 and 106](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-102-introduction-to-functional-analysis-spring-2021/ebbbc59aa40252dff3d4ec766c057451_MIT18_102s21_lec10.pdf).

For example, on $[0,1]$ let $s$ be $2$ on $[0,1/4)$, $1$ on $[1/4,3/4)$, and $0$ on $[3/4,1]$. Its [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md) is $2(1/4)+1(1/2)=1$. Its [superlevel sets](superlevel-set.md) have [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) $3/4$ for $0\leq t<1$, $1/4$ for $1\leq t<2$, and $0$ thereafter. The [layer cake representation](layer-cake-representation.md) gives the same answer: $(3/4)(1)+(1/4)(1)=1$. These two calculations are the building blocks of the general [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md).
