# Sussman anomaly

↑ **Parent:** [Goal interaction in planning](goal-interaction-in-planning.md)

The [Sussman anomaly](sussman-anomaly.md) is a [blocks world](blocks-world.md) example in which independently constructing the obvious subplans for two stacking goals gives destructive [goal interaction in planning](goal-interaction-in-planning.md). Clearing a block in advance allows a short joint [plan](plan.md), while prematurely achieving either isolated goal can block the other. It demonstrates the inadequacy of restricted [goal-by-goal planning](goal-by-goal-planning.md), rather than a prohibition on every possible ordering of final goal achievements. Preparatory actions can make such an ordering succeed. For a concrete instance, start with $C$ on $A$ and $A,B$ on the table, and require $A$ on $B$ and $B$ on $C$. Moving $C$ to the table, then $B$ onto $C$, then $A$ onto $B$ succeeds. The first move is preparation that an isolated shortest subplan for the second stacking goal would omit.
