# Scale-restricted Hausdorff content

↑ **Parent:** [Hausdorff measure](hausdorff-measure.md)

For $\delta>0$, [scale-restricted Hausdorff content](scale-restricted-hausdorff-content.md) is the [infimum](infimum.md) of $\sum_j(\operatorname{diam}U_j)^s$ over [countable](countable-set.md) [covers](cover-topology.md) of $F$ by [sets](set-split.md) of [diameter](diameter.md) at most $\delta$, using the convention in [Hausdorff measure](hausdorff-measure.md). It increases as $\delta$ decreases; its [limit](limit-of-a-function.md) is [Hausdorff measure](hausdorff-measure.md). Unrestricted Hausdorff content imposes no [diameter](diameter.md) bound and can behave differently.

Any explicit [cover](cover-topology.md) supplies an upper bound on [scale-restricted Hausdorff content](scale-restricted-hausdorff-content.md), rather than its exact value. The level-$n$ intervals of the [Cantor set](cantor-set.md), for example, give

$$
\mathcal H_{3^{-n}}^s(C)\leq2^n3^{-ns}.
$$

If this right-hand side tends to zero, then $\mathcal H^s(C)=0$. If it grows, that alone proves no positive lower bound: a different [cover](cover-topology.md) might be cheaper. The [mass distribution principle](mass-distribution-principle.md) addresses precisely this gap by bounding the cost of every sufficiently small [cover](cover-topology.md).
