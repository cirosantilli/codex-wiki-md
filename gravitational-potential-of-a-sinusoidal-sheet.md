# Gravitational potential of a sinusoidal sheet

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md)

A sinusoidal surface-density component $\Sigma_k\cos(kx)$ on $y=0$, with $k>0$ and translational invariance in the remaining direction, produces the displayed [gravitational potential](newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md). [Laplace's equation](laplace-equation.md) away from the sheet separates into a cosine in $x$ and a decaying exponential in $|y|$. Continuity across the sheet excludes a potential jump, while integrating [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity.md) across it gives $[\Phi_y]_{0^-}^{0^+}=4\pi G\Sigma_k\cos(kx)$. For a potential $C\cos(kx)e^{-k|y|}$ that derivative jump is $-2kC\cos(kx)$, giving $C=-2\pi G\Sigma_k/k$.

More generally a specified jump $J\cos(kx)$ gives potential $-J\cos(kx)e^{-k|y|}/(2k)$. Each spatial [Fourier mode](fourier-mode.md) decays on the length scale $1/k$, so fine density structure has a shorter-range field. The sinusoid changes sign and is naturally interpreted as a density perturbation component. General sheet distributions are built by superposing their [Fourier modes](fourier-mode.md); decay alone does not restrict a harmonic potential to a single mode.
