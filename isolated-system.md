# Isolated system

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md)

An [isolated system](isolated-system.md) exchanges neither matter nor [energy](energy-split.md) with its surroundings. Its total [energy](energy-split.md) is fixed, while the [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) requires its total [entropy](entropy-split.md) to be nondecreasing. Its [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md) alone is fixed when bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and external [potential energy](potential-energy.md) also remain unchanged. For example, an initially spinning stirrer inside an insulated rigid vessel can lose [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) while the contents gain [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md), with no external input.

An [isolated system](isolated-system.md) need not yet be in [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md). Two enclosed bodies at different [temperatures](temperature-split.md) can exchange [heat](heat-split.md) internally until they reach [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md); their combined [energy](energy-split.md) stays constant. Their internal transfer is absent from the outer boundary ledger. A vacuum-insulated container is only an approximation to isolation: residual [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md) and [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md) can still carry [energy](energy-split.md) across its boundary.
