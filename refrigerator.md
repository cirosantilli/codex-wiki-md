# Refrigerator

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)

A refrigerator removes [heat](heat-split.md) from the region we want to keep cold and releases [heat](heat-split.md) to warmer surroundings. Here we consider a work-driven cyclic device: its useful output is the cold-side extraction $Q_c$, and its input is net [work](work.md) $W$. A food compartment is a familiar example; the same cooling objective applies when removing [heat](heat-split.md) from a room. The [coefficient of performance](coefficient-of-performance.md) measures extraction per unit [work](work.md), rather than the fraction of input [energy](energy-split.md) converted into output. [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, University Physics Volume 2, §4.3: refrigerators and useful output](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/4-3-refrigerators-and-heat-pumps)

Choose the complete machine as the [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md). Let $Q_c>0$ be [heat](heat-split.md) entering from the colder side, $Q_h>0$ be [heat](heat-split.md) delivered to the hotter side, and $W>0$ be net [work](work.md) supplied, all over the same complete cycle. Assume these are its only [energy](energy-split.md) transfers, no matter crosses the outer boundary, and there is no net change in stored [energy](energy-split.md). Returning to the initial state gives $\Delta U=0$, so the [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) reads

$$
0=Q_c-Q_h+W,\qquad Q_h=Q_c+W.
$$

The [heat](heat-split.md) leaving the warmer side includes both the extracted [heat](heat-split.md) and the supplied [work](work.md). For instance, stipulated transfers $Q_c=3\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and $W=1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ require $Q_h=4\,\mathrm{kJ}$; the cooling [coefficient of performance](coefficient-of-performance.md) is $3$. This is an illustrative budget, not measured performance. At steady operation the same balance holds for average transfer rates, $\dot Q_h=\dot Q_c+P_{\rm in}$, where $P_{\rm in}$ is input [power](power.md).

This budget explains a useful counterexample: operating a [refrigerator](refrigerator.md) with its door open inside an otherwise isolated room cannot cool the room as a whole. If both [heat](heat-split.md) transfers occur within the room and the electrical input comes from outside, the room gains $Q_h-Q_c=W$ each cycle. Locally cold air does not imply net cooling. The [Second law of thermodynamics](second-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) permits cold-to-hot transfer because [work](work.md) is supplied; it forbids a cyclic device whose sole effect is that transfer. See the [Clausius statement of the second law](clausius-statement-of-the-second-law.md).
