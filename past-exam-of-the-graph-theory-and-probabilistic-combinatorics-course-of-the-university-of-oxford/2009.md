<h4 id="a/1/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/1/a)

[Hall's marriage theorem](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-s-marriage-theorem) says that a finite [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md) with parts $L,R$ has a [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) covering $L$ if and only if $|N(S)|\geq|S|$ for every $S\subseteq L$. Necessity follows because [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) edges send the vertices of $S$ to distinct neighbours.

For sufficiency, choose a maximum [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) $M$. If it misses $a\in L$, explore all alternating paths from $a$, taking unmatched edges from left to right and matched edges from right to left. Let $S\subseteq L$ and $T\subseteq R$ be the reached vertices. No vertex of $T$ can be unmatched, since flipping the edges along the resulting [augmenting path](../flow-network.md#augmenting-path) would enlarge $M$.

Every neighbour of a reached left vertex is reached on the right: unmatched edges are exploration steps, and a matched edge leads back to an already reached [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) partner. Thus $T=N(S)$. Each right vertex in $T$ is matched to a reached left vertex other than $a$, and every reached left vertex other than $a$ arose this way. These [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) partners give a bijection $T\leftrightarrow S\setminus\{a\}$. Hence $|N(S)|=|S|-1$, contradicting the assumed inequality. Therefore the maximum [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) covers $L$.

<h4 id="a/1/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#a/1)

<h5 id="a/1/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/1/b)

Write the bipartition as $L,R$. Counting edges by their left and right ends gives $2009|L|=2009|R|$, so the parts have equal size. Put $H=G-F$. If some $S\subseteq L$ violated [Hall's marriage theorem](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-s-marriage-theorem) in $H$, let $T=N_H(S)$, so $|T|\leq|S|-1$.

There are $2009|S|$ edges of $G$ incident with $S$, of which at most $2009|T|$ can end in $T$. Thus at least $2009(|S|-|T|)\geq2009$ end in $R\setminus T$. All those edges must have been deleted, by the definition of $T$. This contradicts $|F|=2008$. Hence $H$ has a [matching](../matching-graph-theory.md) covering $L$, and equal part sizes make it a **[perfect matching](../matching-graph-theory.md#perfect-matching) avoiding every edge of $F$**.

<h3 id="a/2">2</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/2/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/2/a)

For a finite simple loopless [graph](../graph.md), $p_G(q)$ counts [proper vertex colorings](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) with $q$ labelled available colors, initially for nonnegative integer $q$. For an edge $e=uv$, proper colorings of $G-e$ split into those with different endpoint colors, counted by $p_G(q)$, and those with equal endpoint colors, counted by the contracted [graph](../graph.md). Hence the [deletion-contraction recurrence for the chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#deletion-contraction-recurrence-for-the-chromatic-polynomial) is

$$
p_G(q)=p_{G-e}(q)-p_{G/e}(q).
$$

Parallel edges created by contraction can be suppressed since they impose the same coloring constraint. A [graph](../graph.md) with no edges has $p_G(q)=q^{|V(G)|}$. [Induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) by this recurrence therefore gives a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) agreeing with the counting function on all nonnegative integers. That [polynomial](../polynomial.md) is unique because two distinct polynomials cannot agree at infinitely many points.

<h4 id="a/2/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/2/b)

Induct using the same [deletion-contraction recurrence for the chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#deletion-contraction-recurrence-for-the-chromatic-polynomial). The edgeless [graph](../graph.md) has [polynomial](../polynomial.md) $x^n$, so its leading coefficient is one and the others are zero. All nonempty-vertex chromatic polynomials have zero constant term, since no coloring uses zero colors.

For an existing edge, write the alternating coefficient lists of the deletion and simplified contraction as $a_i'$ and $a_i''$. The contraction has $n-1$ vertices, so subtracting its [polynomial](../polynomial.md) changes its leading sign by one place. Thus

$$
a_i(G)=a_i'+a_{i-1}'',
$$

with nonexistent indices understood as zero. Both summands are nonnegative by [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction). The leading coefficient is $a_0=1$, and the next is

$$
a_1=(m-1)+1=m.
$$

This proves the alternating expansion and **nonnegativity of every $a_i$**. The coefficient assertion for $a_1$ is relevant when $n\geq2$; the one-vertex [graph](../graph.md) simply has [polynomial](../polynomial.md) $x$.

<h4 id="a/2/c">c</h4>

$$
(-1)^np_G(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_it^{n-i}.
$$

Every summand is nonnegative and the leading term is $t^n>0$, since $a_0=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(-1)^np_G(x)>0\quad(x<0)}.
$$

<h4 id="a/2/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#a/2)

<h5 id="a/2/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/2/d)

For the [complete graph](../graph-theory.md#complete-graph), $p_{K_n}(x)=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)>0$ whenever $x>n-1$.

Use [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on the number of vertices, and for fixed $n$ on the number of missing edges. If $G$ is not complete, choose a missing edge $e$ and put $H=G+e$. The recurrence rearranges as

$$
p_G(x)=p_H(x)+p_{H/e}(x).
$$

The first term is positive by the missing-edge [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction). The second [graph](../graph.md) has $n-1$ vertices, so the vertex [induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) makes its [polynomial](../polynomial.md) positive for $x>n-2$, in particular for the present range. The one-vertex base case is $x>0$. Thus **$p_G(x)>0$ for all $x>n-1$**, including noninteger $x$; counting colorings alone would not establish that real-variable claim.

<h3 id="a/3">3</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a)

<h4 id="a/3/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a/3/a)

For disjoint nonempty vertex sets, their [edge density](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#edge-density-of-a-bipartite-graph) is $d(X,Y)=e(X,Y)/(|X||Y|)$. The pair is [epsilon-regular](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#regular-pair-of-vertex-sets) or epsilon-uniform if for all $X'\subseteq X$, $Y'\subseteq Y$ with $|X'|\geq\epsilon|X|$ and $|Y'|\geq\epsilon|Y|$,

$$
|d(X',Y')-d(X,Y)|<\epsilon.
$$

It controls density on every sufficiently large subpair, rather than merely on average over chosen subpairs.

<h4 id="a/3/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a/3/b)

An epsilon-uniform partition has disjoint classes $V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_k$ covering the vertex set, with $|V_0|\leq\epsilon|V(G)|$, equal nonexceptional class sizes $|V_1|=\cdots=|V_k|$, and at most $\epsilon k^2$ unordered pairs of distinct nonexceptional classes failing epsilon-uniformity. The exceptional set need not have the common size.

[Szemerédi regularity lemma](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#szemeredi-regularity-lemma) states that for every $\epsilon>0$ and positive integer $m_0$, there is $M=M(\epsilon,m_0)$ such that every sufficiently large finite [graph](../graph.md), for example every [graph](../graph.md) of order at least $M$ after enlarging $M$ if needed, has such a partition with $m_0\leq k\leq M$. The bound depends only on the accuracy and minimum class count, not on the [graph](../graph.md)'s order.

<h4 id="a/3/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a/3/c)

Let $S\subseteq X$ be the vertices with fewer than $dn/2$ neighbours in $Y$. If $|S|>\epsilon n$, its density to all of $Y$ is below $d/2$. But epsilon-uniformity applies to $(S,Y)$ and requires

$$
d(S,Y)>d(X,Y)-\epsilon\geq d-\epsilon>d/2,
$$

a contradiction. Therefore **$|S|\leq\epsilon n$**.

<h4 id="a/3/d">d</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#a/3)

<h5 id="a/3/d/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#a/3/d)

Apply part (c) to the pairs $(X_1,X_2)$ and $(X_1,X_3)$. Outside a union of at most $2\epsilon n$ exceptional vertices, every $v\in X_1$ has sets $Y=N(v)\cap X_2$ and $Z=N(v)\cap X_3$ of size at least $dn/2$.

These sets are large enough for epsilon-uniformity of $(X_2,X_3)$, because $d/2>\epsilon$. Hence

$$
e(Y,Z)\geq(d-\epsilon)|Y||Z|\geq\frac d2\left(\frac{dn}2\right)^2=\frac{d^3n^2}8.
$$

Every such edge completes a distinct triangle through $v$. Thus at most $2\epsilon n$ vertices of $X_1$ fail the claimed triangle count. Summing these triangles over the good vertices counts each triangle with one vertex in each class exactly once. Since $d\leq1$ and $\epsilon<d/4\leq1/4$,

$$
\#\{\text{triangles in }G\}\geq(1-2\epsilon)n\frac{d^3n^2}8
\geq\boxed{\frac{d^3n^3}{16}}.
$$

<h5 id="b/4/a/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/4/a)

<h6 id="b/4/a/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/4/a/i)

[Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) states that if $X$ has finite mean $\mu$ and [variance](../variance.md) $\sigma^2$, then for $t>0$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(|X-\mu|\geq t)\leq\frac{\sigma^2}{t^2}}.
$$

Indeed $(X-\mu)^2\geq t^2\mathbf1_{\{|X-\mu|\geq t\}}$ pointwise. Taking expectations proves the result, including the zero-[variance](../variance.md) case.

<h5 id="b/4/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/4/a)

<h6 id="b/4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/a/ii)

The assumptions imply

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X_n)=\mathbb EX_n^2-\mu_n^2=o(\mu_n^2).
$$

Eventually $\mu_n>0$, and $X_n=0$ implies $|X_n-\mu_n|\geq\mu_n$. Thus [Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X_n=0)\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X_n)}{\mu_n^2}\longrightarrow0}.
$$

No [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) between the different $X_n$ is needed; this is a [second moment method](../probability-inequality.md#second-moment-method).

<h4 id="b/4/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#b/4)

<h5 id="b/4/b/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/4/b/i)

For each cyclic starting position let $I_i$ indicate that its length-$k$ block is increasing. All $k!$ relative orders of its distinct entries are equally likely, so $\mathbb EI_i=1/k!$. By [linearity of expectation](../expected-value.md#linearity-of-expectation),

$$
\boxed{\mathbb EX_k=\frac n{k!}}.
$$

The indicators need not be independent for this calculation.

<h5 id="b/4/b/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/4/b/ii)

Write $X_k=\sum_iI_i$. For two disjoint blocks, their relative orders are independent and each is increasing with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/k!$, giving joint [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/(k!)^2$. If their cyclic starting positions differ by $s$, with $1\leq s<k$, and $k\leq n/2$, they overlap in just one interval. Both blocks increasing is then equivalent to their entire length-$k+s$ union increasing, so the joint [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is $1/(k+s)!$.

For each $s$ there are $2n$ ordered overlapping pairs, while there are $n(n-2k+1)$ ordered disjoint pairs and $n$ diagonal pairs. Thus for $k\geq2$,

$$
\mathbb EX_k^2=\frac n{k!}+2n\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\frac1{(k+s)!}+\frac{n(n-2k+1)}{(k!)^2}.
$$

The tail is bounded using $(k+s)!\geq k!(k+1)^s$:

$$
\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\frac1{(k+s)!}\leq\frac1{k!}\sum_{s\geq1}(k+1)^{-s}=\frac1{k\,k!}\leq\frac1{2k!}.
$$

Dropping the negative correction in the disjoint term proves

$$
\boxed{\mathbb EX_k^2\leq\frac{n^2}{(k!)^2}+\frac{2n}{k!}}.
$$

For $k=1$, $X_1=n$ deterministically and the same bound is immediate.

<h5 id="b/4/b/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/4/b)

<h6 id="b/4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/4/b/iii)

Take $k=k_0=\min\{j:j!\geq n\}$. Then $k\to\infty$ and $(k-1)!<n\leq k!$. Also $k=O(\log n)$, because $j!\geq2^{j-1}$, so the needed block lengths are below $n/2$ for large $n$.

If $L\geq k+1$, then $X_{k+1}\geq1$. [Markov's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#markov-s-inequality) gives

$$
\mathbb P(L\geq k+1)\leq\frac n{(k+1)!}\leq\frac1{k+1}\to0.
$$

For $j=k-2$, the expected count satisfies $\mu=n/j!>k-1\to\infty$. Part (ii) bounds its [variance](../variance.md) by $2\mu$, so [Chebyshev's inequality](../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-s-inequality) gives

$$
\mathbb P(L<k-2)=\mathbb P(X_{k-2}=0)\leq\frac2\mu<\frac2{k-1}\to0.
$$

Combining the tails proves

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(k_0-2\leq L\leq k_0)\longrightarrow1}.
$$

The blocks are consecutive in the cyclic order throughout; this is not the longest increasing subsequence with arbitrary omitted positions.

This establishes the [longest increasing cyclic run of a random permutation](../combinatorics.md#longest-increasing-cyclic-run-of-a-random-permutation) concentration bound.

<h3 id="b/5">5</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/5/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/a/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/5/a)

<h6 id="b/5/a/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/5/a/i)

Let $I_i=\mathbf1_{A_i}$ and $X=\sum_iI_i$. Define

$$
\mu=\sum_i\mathbb P(A_i),\qquad
\Delta=\sum_{\substack{i\ne j\\E_i\cap E_j\ne\varnothing}}\mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j).
$$

The dependency sum is over **ordered** pairs, so each unordered overlapping pair is counted twice. Disjoint prescribed edge sets give [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables).

Order the events. For each $i$, let $B_i$ be the event that all earlier events fail. Split it as $B_i=C_i\cap D_i$, where $C_i$ excludes earlier events with edge sets disjoint from $E_i$, and $D_i$ excludes the overlapping earlier events. These are [down-sets](../extremal-set-theory.md#down-set). The event $A_i$ is a principal [up-set](../extremal-set-theory.md#up-set), and it is independent of $C_i$ since their determining edge sets are disjoint.

If some $B_i$ has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero the desired zero-event bound is immediate. Otherwise the allowed conditional correlation inequality gives

$$
\mathbb P(C_i\cap D_i\mid A_i)\geq\mathbb P(C_i\mid A_i)\mathbb P(D_i\mid A_i)
=\mathbb P(C_i)\mathbb P(D_i\mid A_i).
$$

Since $\mathbb P(B_i)\leq\mathbb P(C_i)$, it follows that

$$
q_i:=\mathbb P(A_i\mid B_i)
\geq\mathbb P(A_i)\mathbb P(D_i\mid A_i)
\geq\mathbb P(A_i)-\sum_{\substack{j<i\\E_j\cap E_i\ne\varnothing}}\mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j).
$$

The last step is the [union bound](../probability-inequality.md#boole-s-inequality) under conditioning on $A_i$. Zero-[probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) events may simply be omitted, and a certain event makes the target [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero. Finally the sequential product and $1-u\leq e^{-u}$ give

$$
\mathbb P(X=0)=\prod_i(1-q_i)
\leq\exp\left(-\sum_iq_i\right)
\leq\boxed{e^{-\mu+\Delta/2}}.
$$

This proves the first [Janson inequality](../probability-inequality.md#janson-inequality) with the dependency-sum convention explicit.

<h5 id="b/5/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/5/a)

<h6 id="b/5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/5/a/ii)

Independently retain each event with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $t$, using a fresh Bernoulli mark for every index. Its retained occurrence is the original principal [up-set](../extremal-set-theory.md#up-set) together with its mark being one, so the first proof applies in the enlarged independent product space. The new parameters are $\mu_t=t\mu$ and $\Delta_t=t^2\Delta$: distinct retained events have independent marks, and pairs of disjoint original supports remain independent.

If no original event occurs, no retained event occurs. Thus

$$
\mathbb P(X=0)\leq\exp(-t\mu+t^2\Delta/2),\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
$$

When $\Delta\geq\mu>0$, choose $t=\mu/\Delta$ to minimize this quadratic exponent. It gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X=0)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\mu^2}{2\Delta}\right)}.
$$

If $\mu=0$, the appropriate trivial bound is one, rather than an undefined zero-over-zero exponent. The argument is the [Janson lower-tail bound by independent thinning](../probability-inequality.md#janson-lower-tail-bound-by-independent-thinning).

<h4 id="b/5/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#b/5)

<h5 id="b/5/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/5/b)

Count one copy for each four-element vertex set. It requires six independent edges, so

$$
\boxed{\mu=\mathbb EX=\binom n4\,2^{-6}}.
$$

Distinct copies share an edge exactly when their vertex intersection has size two or three. For each first copy, their numbers are $6\binom{n-4}2$ and $4(n-4)$, respectively. The required union then has eleven or nine edges. Hence the ordered [Janson dependency sum](../probability-inequality.md#janson-dependency-sum) is

$$
\Delta=\binom n4\left(6\binom{n-4}2\,2^{-11}+4(n-4)\,2^{-9}\right).
$$

For large $n$, $\mu=\Theta(n^4)$ and $\Delta=\Theta(n^6)$, so $\Delta\geq\mu$ and $\mu^2/(2\Delta)\geq an^2$ for some constant $a>0$. The second [Janson inequality](../probability-inequality.md#janson-inequality) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X=0)\leq e^{-an^2}}.
$$

This is the asserted asymptotic bound. It can also be made valid for every $n\geq4$ by decreasing $a$ to cover the finitely many smaller values, since each then has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) strictly below one of containing no copy. For $n<4$ the event has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) one and no positive exponential bound of this form can hold.

<h3 id="b/6">6</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b)

<h4 id="b/6/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/a/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

<h6 id="b/6/a/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/a/i)

A [dependency graph of events](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#dependency-graph-of-events), written here as a loopless directed [graph](../graph.md) $D$ on the event indices, must satisfy a joint [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) condition: for each $i$, $A_i$ is independent of the [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) generated by all $A_j$ with $j\ne i$ and no arc $i\to j$. In particular this means [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) from every Boolean combination of those non-neighbour events, not merely pairwise [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables). The outgoing neighbours list the events for which that [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) is not required.

<h5 id="b/6/a/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

<h6 id="b/6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/a/ii)

The [asymmetric Lovász local lemma](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#asymmetric-lovasz-local-lemma) states that if there are numbers $0\leq x_i<1$ with

$$
\mathbb P(A_i)\leq x_i\prod_{j\in\Gamma^+(i)}(1-x_j)
$$

for every index, then

$$
\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_i A_i^c\right)\geq\prod_i(1-x_i)>0.
$$

For maximum out-degree $d\geq1$, take every $x_i=1/(d+1)$. Since

$$
\left(1-\frac1{d+1}\right)^d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}\geq e^{-1},
$$

one has $x_i(1-x_i)^d\geq1/[e(d+1)]\geq p$. Fewer than $d$ outgoing neighbours only increases the product, so the hypothesis is satisfied whenever $ep(d+1)\leq1$.

If $d=0$, take $x_i=p<1$; the neighbour products are empty and equal one. This separately covers the case in which $1/(d+1)$ would equal the forbidden value one. Thus the **symmetric local lemma** follows in every case.

<h5 id="b/6/a/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#b/6/a)

<h6 id="b/6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/a/iii)

For distinct indices put arcs in both directions whenever $S_i\cap S_j\ne\varnothing$. If $j$ is not an outgoing neighbour of $i$, every variable determining $A_j$ lies outside $S_i$. The entire family of such events is measurable with respect to variables outside $S_i$, whose [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) is independent of the variables inside $S_i$. Thus $A_i$ is independent of their whole generated [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra), establishing the required [dependency graph of events](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#dependency-graph-of-events). This justifies joint [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) rather than just checking each pair.

<h4 id="b/6/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#b/6)

<h5 id="b/6/b/i">i</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

<h6 id="b/6/b/i/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#b/6/b/i)

Color every edge independently and uniformly with one of $k$ colors. For each unoriented simple four-edge path $P$, let $A_P$ be its repetition event. Its four distinct edges must satisfy two prescribed color equalities, so $\mathbb P(A_P)=k^{-2}$.

Events with edge-disjoint paths depend on disjoint random variables. A fixed edge lies on at most $4\Delta^3$ unoriented four-edge paths: count its four possible positions and two orientations, bound the remaining choices by $\Delta^3$, then divide the oriented path count by two. Thus the union of all paths sharing any of $P$'s four edges has size at most $16\Delta^3$. This union includes $P$ itself, so a dependency [graph](../graph.md) has $d+1\leq16\Delta^3$.

For $k\geq8\Delta^{3/2}$,

$$
e\,k^{-2}(d+1)\leq\frac{16e\Delta^3}{64\Delta^3}=\frac e4<1.
$$

The symmetric [Lovász local lemma](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#lovasz-local-lemma) yields positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) of avoiding every event. Therefore **such an [edge coloring](../graph-coloring.md#edge-coloring) exists**. If there are no four-edge paths the conclusion is immediate.

<h5 id="b/6/b/ii">ii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

<h6 id="b/6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#b/6/b/ii)

Use the same independent coloring, now with events indexed by unoriented six-edge paths. A repetition fixes the second three-edge color block to equal the first, so each event has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $k^{-3}$. A fixed edge belongs to at most $6\Delta^5$ such paths, and a six-edge path has at most $36\Delta^5$ events in its closed dependency neighbourhood. Thus $d+1\leq36\Delta^5$.

For $k\geq5\Delta^{5/3}$,

$$
e\,k^{-3}(d+1)\leq\frac{36e\Delta^5}{125\Delta^5}<1.
$$

The symmetric [Lovász local lemma](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#lovasz-local-lemma) again guarantees a coloring avoiding all six-edge repetition events. Thus **the required coloring exists**, with the empty event family handled trivially.

<h5 id="b/6/b/iii">iii</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#b/6/b)

<h6 id="b/6/b/iii/solution">Solution</h6>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#b/6/b/iii)

Use both families of bad events in one [asymmetric Lovász local lemma](../probabilistic-combinatorics.md#asymmetric-lovasz-local-lemma) application. Give every four-edge event parameter $x_4$ and every six-edge event parameter $x_6$. A path of length $\ell$ shares edges with at most $4\ell\Delta^3$ four-edge paths and $6\ell\Delta^5$ six-edge paths. It therefore suffices to verify

$$
k^{-2}\leq x_4(1-x_4)^{16\Delta^3}(1-x_6)^{24\Delta^5},
$$



$$
k^{-3}\leq x_6(1-x_4)^{24\Delta^3}(1-x_6)^{36\Delta^5},\qquad0<x_4,x_6<1.
$$

These slightly excessive exponents are safe even though they may count the event itself. Choose $x_4=2/k^2$ and $x_6=2/k^3$. For $k\geq C\Delta^{5/3}$, the sums of exponents times their parameters are bounded by constants times $C^{-2}\Delta^{-1/3}+C^{-3}$, uniformly in $\Delta\geq1$. Taking $C$ sufficiently large makes each product of factors at least $1/2$, for example using $(1-x)^r\geq1-rx$ and multiplying the two factors. Both displayed inequalities then hold. Positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) of no bad event gives **simultaneous avoidance of both lengths**. Keeping two event parameters avoids replacing the rarer six-edge events by the larger four-edge [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability).

These are the [avoiding short repetitions in an edge coloring](../graph-theory.md#avoiding-short-repetitions-in-an-edge-coloring) bounds, for [repetitively colored paths](../graph-theory.md#repetitively-colored-path).
