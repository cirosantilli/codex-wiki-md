<h1 id="b/8/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For $p=1/4$, use $k=\log_4n+\omega(n)$. Then $\mathbb EX=4^{-\omega(n)}$, so its limits are exactly those needed for the same two arguments.

For $p=1/\log_e n$, a convenient definition that also accounts for the varying probability is

$$
\boxed{k=\frac{\log_e n+\omega(n)}{\log_e\log_e n}}.
$$

It makes $\mathbb EX=e^{-\omega(n)}$. Thus $\omega\to+\infty$ gives no runs with high probability, and $\omega\to-\infty$ gives runs and relative concentration. For sufficiently large $n$, $p\le1/2$, so the same uniform [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) bound applies. Definitions are made from the actual [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md) $k$; no [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) rounding assumption is required. These conclusions retain the stipulated range $1\le k\le n/2$.
