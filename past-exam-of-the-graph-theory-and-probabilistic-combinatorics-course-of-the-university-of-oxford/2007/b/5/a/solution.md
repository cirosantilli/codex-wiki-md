<h1 id="b/5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For events $A_i$ with a dependency [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md), assume each event is independent of the [sigma-algebra](../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) generated by its nonneighbours. If numbers $x_i\in[0,1)$ satisfy $P(A_i)\le x_i\prod_{j\in\Gamma(i)}(1-x_j)$, the general [Lovász local lemma](../../../../../lovasz-local-lemma.md) gives

$$
P\left(\bigcap_iA_i^c\right)\ge\prod_i(1-x_i)>0.
$$

The symmetric version says that if every event has probability at most $q$, the dependency [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) has [maximum degree](../../../../../maximum-degree.md) at most $d$, and $eq(d+1)\le1$, then none occurs with positive probability. For $d\ge1$, set $x_i=1/(d+1)$. Since $(1-1/(d+1))^d\ge e^{-1}$, the general condition follows from the symmetric numerical hypothesis. The logarithmic inequality $\log(1+1/d)\le1/d$ proves that estimate. When $d=0$, the events are mutually independent and each has probability below one, so the conclusion is immediate. This is the intended implication from the general to the symmetric form; the source repeats the word symmetric in its final clause.
