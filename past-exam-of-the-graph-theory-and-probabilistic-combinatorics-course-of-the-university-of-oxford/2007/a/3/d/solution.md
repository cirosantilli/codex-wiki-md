<h1 id="a/3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A vertex with at least $R(t,t)$ neighbours has either a $t$-[clique](../../../../../clique-graph-theory.md) among them, producing $K_{t+1}$ with the vertex, or an independent $t$-set among them, producing an induced star with that centre.

For the second assertion put $D=R(t,t)-1$. If either of those two outcomes is absent, the preceding result forces [maximum degree](../../../../../maximum-degree.md) at most $D$. In a [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) of this [maximum degree](../../../../../maximum-degree.md), a ball of radius $t-1$ has at most $1+D+\cdots+D^{t-1}$ vertices, using the rough bound on walks at each distance. If the [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) has more vertices, some vertex lies at distance at least $t$ from a chosen root. A shortest path to it is induced, since a chord would shorten it; its first $t$ edges give an induced path of the required length. Thus a sufficiently large [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) has **one of the three listed configurations**.
