<h1 id="a/2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Part (ii) shows that $\chi(G)\leq\lambda(D)$ for every [acyclic orientation](../../../../../../acyclic-orientation.md) $D$, so $\chi(G)$ is at most their [minimum](../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). Part (i), applied to an optimal [colouring](../../../../../../colouring-of-a-set.md), supplies an [acyclic orientation](../../../../../../acyclic-orientation.md) with $\lambda(D)\leq\chi(G)$. Combining the [inequalities](../../../../../../inequality-mathematics.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\chi(G)=\min_{D\text{ acyclic orientation of }G}\lambda(D).}
$$

The parameter counts [vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) on a [graph path](../../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md); if [graph path](../../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) length were instead counted in [edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), the corresponding [minimum](../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) would be $\chi(G)-1$.

These constructions prove [chromatic number from acyclic longest paths](../../../../../../chromatic-number-from-acyclic-longest-paths.md).
