<h1 id="a/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $B\subseteq X$ be the [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) with fewer than $(d/2)|Y|$ [neighbours](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) in $Y$. If $|B|\geq\varepsilon|X|$, regularity applies to the pair $(B,Y)$ and gives

$$
d(B,Y)\geq d(X,Y)-\varepsilon\geq d-\varepsilon\geq d/2.
$$

But averaging the defining strict degree [inequality](../../../../../inequality-mathematics.md) over $B$ gives $d(B,Y)<d/2$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|B|<\varepsilon|X|.}
$$

Regularity controls the [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of almost every [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) because a large [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of exceptional low-degree [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) would itself witness an irregular density.
