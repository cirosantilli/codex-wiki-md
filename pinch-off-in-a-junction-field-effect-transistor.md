# Pinch-off in a junction field-effect transistor

↑ **Parent:** [Junction field-effect transistor](junction-field-effect-transistor.md)

In an n-channel [junction field-effect transistor](junction-field-effect-transistor.md), increasing drain voltage increases the local gate-channel reverse bias near the drain. The [depletion region](depletion-region.md) can meet there, ending the quasi-neutral channel before the drain. Carriers already in the channel are swept across the remaining high-field region, so drain-end pinch-off produces current saturation rather than zero current. A sufficiently negative gate voltage can instead deplete the whole channel and cut the device off. In the one-sided [depletion approximation](depletion-approximation.md), a channel half-width $a$ and donor density $N_D$ give a depletion threshold voltage $U_P=eN_Da^2/(2\varepsilon_s)$; with built-in junction voltage $V_{\rm bi}$, drain-end pinch-off occurs approximately at $V_{DS}=U_P-V_{\rm bi}+V_{GS}$.
