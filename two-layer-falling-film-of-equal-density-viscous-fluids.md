# Two-layer falling film of equal-density viscous fluids

↑ **Parent:** [Falling film flow](falling-film-flow.md)

Let the lower fluid have thickness $h$ and [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu$, and the upper fluid thickness $\alpha h$ and [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\beta\mu$, with common density $\rho$. For steady flow down a plane inclined at angle $\theta$, set $H=(1+\alpha)h$, $A=\rho g\sin\theta/\mu$. The common [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md) is $p_{\rm atm}+\rho g\cos\theta(H-y)$. No slip at the wall, continuous [velocity](velocity.md) and [shear stress](shear-stress.md) at $y=h$, and zero upper surface shear give $u_1=A(Hy-y^2/2)$ and $u_2(h+z)=Ah^2(\alpha+1/2)+(A/\beta)(\alpha hz-z^2/2)$. These follow by integrating $\mu_i u_i''=-\rho g\sin\theta$ in each layer. The stress applied to the lower fluid equals the downslope weight of the upper layer, independent of its [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md); the upper [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) affects its shear rate but not that transmitted stress.
