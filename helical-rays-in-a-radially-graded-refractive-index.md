# Helical rays in a radially graded refractive index

↑ **Parent:** [Geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md)

For a positive [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $\mu(r)$ in [cylindrical coordinates](cylindrical-coordinate-system.md), a [light ray](light-ray.md) with constant radius $R>0$ and varying axial coordinate has the form $\theta(z)=\theta_0+\omega z$ when it exists. [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md) makes $L=\mu(r)\sqrt{1+r'^2+r^2\theta'^2}$ stationary. Conservation of the momentum conjugate to the cyclic angle makes $\theta'$ constant along a constant-radius path. The radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) is then

$$
\mu'(R)(1+R^2\omega^2)+\mu(R)R\omega^2=0,
$$

giving the displayed formula. A nonzero real [helix](helix.md) requires $0<-R\mu'(R)/\mu(R)<1$. If $\mu'(R)=0$, $\omega=0$ gives a straight axial [light ray](light-ray.md); a generic radial profile need not support a constant-radius [helix](helix.md). Its [helical pitch](pitch-of-a-helix.md) is $2\pi/|\omega|$. This ray model is useful for a [graded-index optical fiber](graded-index-optical-fiber.md) when the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) varies smoothly and [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) applies; it does not by itself impose wave-mode quantization or boundary confinement.
