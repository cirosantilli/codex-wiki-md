# Cubic polytrope fails isolated gravitational matching

↑ **Parent:** [Cubic polytropic interior](cubic-polytropic-interior.md)

For the [cubic polytropic interior](cubic-polytropic-interior.md), $\nabla\rho$ vanishes at each vertex, so the locally defined potential $\Phi=C-2K\rho$ predicts zero gravitational acceleration there. But at the vertex $(0,0,0)$, the actual self-gravitational acceleration of the positive mass distribution is

$$
\mathbf g(0)=G\int_{[0,L]^3}\rho(\mathbf r')\frac{\mathbf r'}{|\mathbf r'|^3}\,d^3r',
$$

whose three components are strictly positive. The fields cannot match continuously. Thus the interior [Helmholtz equation](helmholtz-equation.md) and zero density on the faces do not construct an isolated static cubic star. External stresses or an external gravitational field would be needed to realize such a boundary-value construction.
