# Specular reflection

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specular_reflection)

At a smooth mirror with unit [normal vector](normal-vector.md) $n$, an incoming unit direction $i$ has outgoing direction $i-2(i\cdot n)n$. Thus the incident and reflected rays make equal angles with the normal.

More precisely, [specular reflection](specular-reflection.md) redirects each incident [light ray](light-ray.md) into the direction prescribed by the [law of reflection](law-of-reflection.md), rather than spreading it over many outgoing directions. The incident [light ray](light-ray.md), reflected [light ray](light-ray.md), and local [normal vector](normal-vector.md) lie in one plane. The surface must be sufficiently smooth on the scale of the [wavelength](wavelength.md) for this model to describe its reflected beam; a visibly flat surface can still scatter [light](light.md) if its microscopic roughness is appreciable. [OpenStax: The law of reflection](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-2-the-law-of-reflection).

The displayed formula uses [unit vectors](unit-vector.md) $\mathbf i,\mathbf n,\mathbf r$ and the [dot product](dot-product.md). It preserves the component parallel to the mirror and reverses the [normal component](normal-component.md). For a mirror along $y=0$, take $\mathbf n=(0,1)$ and $\mathbf i=(3/5,-4/5)$. Then $\mathbf i\cdot\mathbf n=-4/5$ and $\mathbf r=(3/5,4/5)$: both directions have [vector norm](norm.md) one, and both make an [angle](angle.md) $\arccos(4/5)\approx36.87^\circ$ with the relevant normal direction. Replacing $\mathbf n$ by $-\mathbf n$ leaves the result unchanged.

This same local rule explains a flat mirror's [virtual image](virtual-image.md). For a source at $(0,a)$ above $y=0$, backward extensions of reflected [light rays](light-ray.md) intersect at $(0,-a)$. The [light](light.md) remains above the mirror; its apparent origin lies below it. A curved mirror changes its [normal vector](normal-vector.md) from point to point and can instead make reflected [light rays](light-ray.md) converge. The geometry of image formation therefore starts with one simple direction rule. [OpenStax: Images formed by plane mirrors](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-1-images-formed-by-plane-mirrors).
