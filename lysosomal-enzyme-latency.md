# Lysosomal enzyme latency

↑ **Parent:** [Lysosome](lysosome.md)

[Lysosomal enzyme latency](lysosomal-enzyme-latency.md) is the difference between the [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) activity accessible to an added [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) before disruption of the surrounding membrane and the activity revealed after disruption. Low accessible activity can reflect separation of [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) from [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md), rather than absence of [enzyme](enzyme-split.md). This is an assay-accessibility statement, not a claim that the intact [lysosome](lysosome.md) is inactive in the living [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md).[Christian de Duve, Exploring Cells with a Centrifuge (Nobel Lecture, 12 December 1974), printed pp. 157–159, enzyme-latency discussion and Figure 6](exploring-cells-with-a-centrifuge-by-christian-de-duve.md)

The soluble-[enzyme](enzyme-split.md) comparison is an additional control proposed for the schematic, not an asserted measurement from the [rat-liver acid-phosphatase latency experiment](rat-liver-acid-phosphatase-latency-experiment.md).

Let $F$ be the blank-corrected initial rate measured with a membrane-preserving preparation and $T>0$ the rate after a treatment that exposes all assayable [enzyme](enzyme-split.md). Use matched amounts of starting material, [pH](ph.md), [substrate](substrate-biochemistry.md) and measurement time. Define the apparent latent fraction by

$$
\ell=1-\frac{F}{T}.
$$

This definition requires more than a large treated signal. Disruption must preserve [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) activity, and the treatment must not independently alter the readout. A soluble-[enzyme](enzyme-split.md) comparison with and without treatment helps test direct activation or inhibition; an appropriate blank tests signal unrelated to [enzyme](enzyme-split.md)-catalysed [hydrolysis](hydrolysis.md). These are controls for interpreting the constructed assay, rather than additional historical observations.

For a deliberately chosen example, take $F=10$ and $T=100$ arbitrary rate units. Then $\ell=0.90$: ninety percent of the assayable activity was initially inaccessible. After hypothetical partial damage, $F=25$ with unchanged $T=100$ gives $\ell=0.75$. The preparation has lost latency without gaining total assayable activity. These percentages describe activity accessibility; they do not count intact [lysosomes](lysosome.md). Unequal [enzyme](enzyme-split.md) contents, partial leakage and mixed particle populations prevent that identification. If $F>T$, the formula is negative, flagging failure of this simple accessibility interpretation rather than a negative population of hidden [enzymes](enzyme-split.md).

To check the logic, divide the same uniformly mixed preparation into two equal portions. If dilution halves both measured rates, $(F/2)/(T/2)=F/T$, so $\ell$ is unchanged. In contrast, comparing an undiluted untreated portion with a diluted treated portion changes the ratio artificially. Normalization to the same amount of original material is therefore part of the definition, not an optional numerical correction.
