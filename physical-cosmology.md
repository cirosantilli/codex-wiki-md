# Physical cosmology

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmology](cosmology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_cosmology)

[Physical cosmology](physical-cosmology.md) connects the universe's history to the measurable behaviour of its contents. The [Friedmann equations](friedmann-equations.md) describe cosmic expansion; [atomic physics](atomic-physics-split.md), [radiation pressure](radiation-pressure.md) and [gravity](gravity.md) determine what that expanding universe looks like. A particularly revealing example is the route from [cosmological recombination](recombination-cosmology.md) through the [photon-baryon fluid](photon-baryon-fluid.md) to the [Cosmic microwave background acoustic peaks](cosmic-microwave-background-acoustic-peak.md). The universe supplies the [initial conditions](initial-condition.md) and the laboratory; the prediction must still pass through a model of the telescope before it can be compared with observations.

The practical reward is that one sky contains several different tests. The [Cosmic microwave background power spectrum](cosmic-microwave-background-power-spectrum.md) carries information about the [sound horizon](sound-horizon.md), the [cosmological baryon density](cosmological-baryon-density.md) and cosmic geometry. These are related quantities with different observational signatures. [Angular sound-horizon degeneracy](angular-sound-horizon-degeneracy.md) explains why measuring a characteristic angle alone cannot count the universe's [baryons](baryon.md).
