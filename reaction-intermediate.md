# Reaction intermediate

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_intermediate)

A [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) is formed during a [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) and consumed in a subsequent step, cancelling from the net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md). It can accumulate, as I does in the [consecutive first-order reaction mechanism](consecutive-first-order-reaction-mechanism.md). A [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) can also connect transformations in [energy coupling](energy-coupling.md). Unlike a [catalyst](catalyst.md), it need not be present before conversion begins.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §12.6, opening ozone example: intermediate formation, consumption, and cancellation](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/12-6-reaction-mechanisms.md)

A [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) is distinct from a [transition state](transition-state.md): the former can persist between steps, whereas the latter describes passage through a barrier. The existing [potential energy surface](potential-energy-surface.md) and [energy profile](energy-profile-chemistry.md) provide the configuration-space picture; the time-dependent [concentration](concentration.md) of an intermediate instead measures an ensemble of reacting entities. Low [concentration](concentration.md) or short lifetime can make detection difficult, so failure to detect a proposed [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md) is not by itself proof of absence. Conversely, detecting a [chemical species](chemical-species.md) does not establish that it carries the proposed route to the final [reaction products](product-chemistry.md); it could belong to a competing route.
