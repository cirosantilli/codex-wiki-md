# Manganese monoxide neutron diffraction experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic neutron diffraction](magnetic-neutron-diffraction.md)

In 1949, [Clifford Shull](clifford-glenwood-shull.md) and [J. Samuel Smart](j-samuel-smart.md) compared powdered [manganese(II) oxide](manganese-ii-oxide.md) at room [temperature](temperature-split.md) and $80\,\mathrm K$. Powder preparation supplies many [crystal](crystal.md) orientations. Their Fig. 1 records scattered-neutron [intensity](intensity-physics.md) versus counter angle. Nuclear reflections remained, while the cold pattern gained strong reflections forbidden by the chemical [unit cell](unit-cell.md). They indexed these with a magnetic [unit cell](unit-cell.md) having twice the chemical cell edge. The warm pattern already contained diffuse magnetic scattering: loss of sharp magnetic peaks does not mean loss of individual [magnetic moments](magnetic-dipole-moment.md).[Shull and Smart, Detection of Antiferromagnetism by Neutron Diffraction (1949), Fig. 1 and accompanying paragraphs; original reprint inspected at Carnegie Mellon University](detection-of-antiferromagnetism-by-neutron-diffraction-by-clifford-glenwood-shull-and-j-samuel-smart.md)

The source was Oak Ridge's graphite-moderated reactor. In Early Development of Neutron Scattering (1994), [Clifford Shull](clifford-glenwood-shull.md) describes how the early two-axis instruments used a monochromating [crystal](crystal.md) to select the incident-neutron [wavelength](wavelength.md); a shielded neutron counter rotated around the specimen to record angular [intensity](intensity-physics.md). This specifies the source–[monochromator](monochromator.md)–sample–counter path, without identifying which instrument revision produced the MnO trace.[Early Development of Neutron Scattering (1994), pp. 99–103, Figs. 2–4, and p. 106, Fig. 8; inspected CMU reprint pp. 99–106](early-development-of-neutron-scattering-by-clifford-glenwood-shull.md)

The control was the same compound above and below magnetic ordering, with retained nuclear peaks identifying the underlying chemical periodicity. The original paper labels an aluminium impurity peak and defers a complete magnetic-structure account. It supplies neither a chemical synthesis recipe nor detailed cold-stage construction. The inference is periodic magnetic order, rather than a claim that every possible structural change was excluded at arbitrary resolution.[Fig. 1: aluminium impurity label, nuclear/magnetic indexing and room-temperature/80 K comparison; final paragraph before conclusion](detection-of-antiferromagnetism-by-neutron-diffraction-by-clifford-glenwood-shull-and-j-samuel-smart.md)

The connection to the measured angle is the [elastic Bragg scattering condition](elastic-bragg-scattering-condition.md). If the incident and outgoing [wave vectors](wavevector.md) have equal magnitude $2\pi/\lambda$ and make an angle $2\theta$, their difference has magnitude

$$
|\mathbf Q|=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\sin\theta.
$$

A longer real-space period supplies additional, smaller [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md) spacings, which can put magnetic reflections at new counter angles. In the one-dimensional toy model above, doubling the period changes the allowed spacing from $2\pi/a$ to $\pi/a$. The actual MnO indexing is three-dimensional; the toy row does not assert its reflection positions or relative peak strengths.

[Louis Néel](louis-neel.md) supplied the ordering theory; the 1949 diffraction work was a later experimental test. His 1970 [Nobel Prize in Physics](nobel-prize-in-physics-split.md) recognized [antiferromagnetism](antiferromagnetism.md) and [ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md). [Clifford Shull](clifford-glenwood-shull.md)'s neutron-diffraction award followed in 1994.[sections on neutron diffraction and magnetic ordering: Néel 1970 and Shull 1994](the-nobel-prizes-in-physics-1901-2000-by-nobelprize-org.md)
