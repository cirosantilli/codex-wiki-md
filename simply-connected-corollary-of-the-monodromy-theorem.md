# Simply connected corollary of the monodromy theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Monodromy theorem](monodromy-theorem.md)

On a [simply connected domain](simply-connected-domain.md) $D$, a [germ of a holomorphic function](germ-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) admitting [analytic continuation](analytic-continuation.md) along every [path](continuous-path.md) from its base point determines a unique [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) $F:D\to\mathbb C$ with that initial [germ of a holomorphic function](germ-of-a-holomorphic-function.md). [Section 9.3.6, printed p. 159: globally defined holomorphic function](a-guide-to-complex-variables-by-steven-g-krantz.md)

Indeed, any two [paths](continuous-path.md) with the same endpoints are [homotopic](homotopy-split.md) with endpoints fixed. Define $F(b)$ using the terminal [germ of a holomorphic function](germ-of-a-holomorphic-function.md). The [monodromy theorem](monodromy-theorem.md) makes this independent of the [path](continuous-path.md). In a small [open disc](open-disc.md) about $b$, extend the chosen route by a short segment; the same local representative supplies $F$ throughout the [open disc](open-disc.md), proving [holomorphy](holomorphic-function-split.md). The [identity theorem](identity-theorem.md) proves uniqueness. The [existence hypothesis of the monodromy theorem](existence-hypothesis-of-the-monodromy-theorem.md) must still be checked.
