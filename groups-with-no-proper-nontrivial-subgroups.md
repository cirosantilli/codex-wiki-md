# Groups with no proper nontrivial subgroups

↑ **Parent:** [Cyclic group](cyclic-group.md)

A nontrivial [group](group-split.md) has no proper nontrivial [subgroups](subgroup.md) exactly when it is a [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) of prime [group order](order-of-a-finite-group.md). To prove necessity without presupposing finiteness, take $g\ne e$. Its cyclic subgroup is nontrivial and hence equals $G$. An infinite-order generator would give the proper nontrivial subgroup $\langle g^2\rangle$, so its order $N$ is finite. If $N$ were composite, a proper divisor $d$ with $1<d<N$ would make $\langle g^d\rangle$ another proper nontrivial subgroup. Thus $N$ is prime. Conversely [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md) excludes intermediate subgroup orders in a prime-order group. The qualification “nontrivial” is necessary because every group with more than one element has the proper [trivial subgroup](trivial-subgroup.md) $\{e\}$.
