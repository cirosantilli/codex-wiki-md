# First correction to a regular singular solution by reduction of order

↑ **Parent:** [Reduction of order](reduction-of-order.md)

Suppose $y''-P(x)y'/x-Q(x)y/x=0$ has analytic $P,Q$ near zero, $P(x)=P_0+P_1x+\cdots$, and a solution $y_1=1+\beta x+\cdots$, with $P_0$ a positive integer. The [indicial roots](indicial-root.md) are $0,P_0+1$. The [Abel identity](abel-s-identity.md) gives $W=Cx^{P_0}(1+P_1x+\cdots)$. Dividing by $y_1^2$ gives $Cx^{P_0}[1+(P_1-2\beta)x+\cdots]$. Integrating this [reduction of order](reduction-of-order.md) expression from zero, multiplying by $y_1$, and choosing $C=P_0+1$ proves the displayed normalized expansion. The integral's zero constant eliminates the added multiple of $y_1$. Its nonzero [Wronskian](wronskian.md) proves independence. In this setting the assumed exponent-zero solution prevents a logarithm in the constructed exponent-$P_0+1$ solution.
