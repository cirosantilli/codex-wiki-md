# Information theory

↑ **Parent:** [Probability and statistics](probability-and-statistics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory)

Information theory quantifies how much uncertainty messages contain, how efficiently they can be represented, and how reliably they can pass through a noisy [channel](communication-channel.md). Its central quantities connect three questions: [information entropy](information-entropy.md) measures uncertainty in a source, [conditional entropy](conditional-entropy.md) measures what remains after an observation, and [mutual information](mutual-information.md) measures what that observation reveals. These are properties of a specified [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md), rather than measures of a message's importance or truth.

For a concrete source, suppose symbols $A,B,C,D$ have [probabilities](probability.md) $1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8$. A fixed-length binary representation needs two [bits](bit.md) per symbol. Instead assign the [prefix code](prefix-code.md) $A\mapsto0$, $B\mapsto10$, $C\mapsto110$, $D\mapsto111$. Reading from left to right gives an unambiguous decoding because no codeword begins another. The [expected value](expected-value.md) of the length is

$$
\frac12\cdot1+\frac14\cdot2+\frac18\cdot3+\frac18\cdot3=\frac74.
$$

This saves an average of $1/4$ of a [bit](bit.md) per symbol, or $12.5\%$ of the fixed-length representation, with no loss of symbols. The lengths are exactly $-\log_2 p$, so the average equals the source's [Shannon entropy](information-entropy.md). For [independent and identically distributed](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) repetitions, this code works without storing the whole message first. This example shows the appeal of [lossless source coding](lossless-source-coding.md): unequal [probabilities](probability.md) become shorter descriptions for more frequent outcomes.

[Channel coding](channel-coding.md) addresses a different problem: errors during transmission. A shorter representation removes predictable structure; an error-correcting representation adds carefully chosen structure that helps recover corrupted symbols. [Shannon's source coding theorem](shannon-s-source-coding-theorem.md) and the [Shannon second coding theorem](noisy-channel-coding-theorem.md) identify limits for these respective tasks under their hypotheses. The beauty is that source details and hardware details can meet through a small number of mathematical quantities, notably [information entropy](information-entropy.md) and [channel capacity](channel-capacity.md).

Claude E. Shannon's _A Mathematical Theory of Communication_ appeared in the _Bell System Technical Journal_ in July and October 1948. Its introduction separates the engineering problem of reproducing messages from their semantic meaning; subsequent sections develop probabilistic source models, noiseless coding, and transmission with noise. It also acknowledges earlier work by Harry Nyquist and Ralph Hartley.[A Mathematical Theory of Communication by Claude Shannon](a-mathematical-theory-of-communication-by-claude-shannon.md)
