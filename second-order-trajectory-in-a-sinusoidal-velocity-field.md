# Second-order trajectory in a sinusoidal velocity field

↑ **Parent:** [Stokes drift](stokes-drift.md)

For prescribed one-dimensional [velocity](velocity.md) $u=u_0\sin(kx-\omega t)$, let $\alpha=kX_0$, $\theta=\alpha-\omega t$ and expand the [Lagrangian trajectory](lagrangian-trajectory.md) as $x=X_0+u_0a+u_0^2b$. The [Taylor expansion](taylor-expansion.md) of $\dot x=u(x,t)$ gives $\dot a=\sin\theta$ and $\dot b=ka\cos\theta$. Initial position $X_0$ requires $a=(\cos\theta-\cos\alpha)/\omega$ and

$$
b=\frac k\omega\left[\frac t2+\frac{\sin2\alpha-\sin2\theta}{4\omega}-\frac{\cos\alpha(\sin\alpha-\sin\theta)}\omega\right].
$$

The secular [velocity](velocity.md) $ku_0^2/(2\omega)$ is [Stokes drift](stokes-drift.md), even though the [Eulerian time average](eulerian-time-average.md) vanishes. This fixed-time expansion requires $|ku_0/\omega|\ll1$ and is not uniform once the secular displacement changes the phase by order unity.
