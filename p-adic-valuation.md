# P-adic valuation

↑ **Parent:** [Number theory](number-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_valuation)

For a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) $p$ and a nonzero [integer](integer-split.md) $N$, the [P-adic valuation](p-adic-valuation.md) counts the exponent of $p$ in its [prime factorization](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md):

$$
v_p(N)=\max\{a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:p^a\mid N\}.
$$

For $72=2^3\cdot3^2$, it gives $v_2(72)=3$, $v_3(72)=2$, and $v_5(72)=0$. The sign of a nonzero [integer](integer-split.md) does not change these [prime exponents](p-adic-valuation.md). Relative to an ordered [factor base](factor-base.md), the valuations supply the coordinates of its [exponent vector](exponent-vector.md).

The [P-adic valuation](p-adic-valuation.md) extends to nonzero [rational numbers](rational-number.md) by $v_p(a/b)=v_p(a)-v_p(b)$. For example, $v_5(72/25)=-2$ records a factor in the denominator instead of the numerator. [Multiplication](multiplication.md) adds valuations, $v_p(xy)=v_p(x)+v_p(y)$; this is the arithmetic reason why adding exponent vectors can make a product a square. In particular, a positive [integer](integer-split.md) is a [square of a number](square-of-a-number.md) precisely when all its [prime exponents](p-adic-valuation.md) are [even integers](even-number.md), the criterion used by a [factor base](factor-base.md).
