# Cryogenic radiometer

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical substitution radiometer](electrical-substitution-radiometer.md)

A [cryogenic radiometer](cryogenic-radiometer.md) uses low-[temperature](temperature-split.md) operation to improve [electrical substitution radiometry](electrical-substitution-radiometer.md) through particular material and thermal properties. Lower [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) of a copper receiver permits a substantial cavity without proportionately slow relaxation; cold shields reduce unwanted [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md), and heater leads with [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md) can suppress resistive lead heating. These features do not make [radiometric absorptance](radiometric-absorptance.md) exactly one or abolish [optical-electrical heating non-equivalence](optical-electrical-heating-non-equivalence.md).[§5.2, printed pp. 30–32: cryogenic advantages and remaining window/non-equivalence corrections](spectroradiometric-detector-measurements-ultraviolet-visible-and-near-infrared-detectors-for-spectral-power-by-thomas-c-larason-and-others.md)

Cold surroundings matter as much as a cold receiver. In a simplified gray-surface model, net outward [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md) is $\epsilon\sigma A(T^4-T_s^4)$, using [emissivity](emissivity.md) $\epsilon$, [area](surface-area.md) $A$, shield [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T_s$ and the [Stefan-Boltzmann constant](stefan-boltzmann-constant.md) $\sigma$. Cold shields reduce the incoming term as well as the receiver's emission; cooling the receiver alone while leaving it exposed to warm surroundings does not remove incoming radiation. An entrance window still provides an optical and thermal path. [Vacuum](vacuum.md) reduces gas-mediated [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md), while an intentional [radiometer thermal link](radiometer-thermal-link.md) remains necessary. This model explains specific losses, rather than promising a universal improvement factor or perfect accuracy.
