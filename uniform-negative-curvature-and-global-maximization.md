# Uniform negative curvature and global maximization

↑ **Parent:** [Strictly concave function](strictly-concave-function.md)

If a twice differentiable real [function](function-split.md) on $\mathbb R$ satisfies $f''(x)\le-c$ for some fixed $c>0$, then it has a unique [global maximum](global-maximum.md). The [mean value theorem](mean-value-theorem.md) shows that $f'$ is strictly decreasing, and that $f'(x)+cx$ is nonincreasing. Hence $f'(x)\le f'(0)-cx$ for $x>0$ and $f'(x)\ge f'(0)-cx$ for $x<0$. These inequalities force opposite derivative signs at the two ends. The [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md) supplies a unique zero of $f'$, at which $f$ changes from increasing to decreasing. Merely having $f''<0$ does not guarantee existence: $f(x)=-e^{-x}$ is increasing and approaches its unattained supremum zero.
