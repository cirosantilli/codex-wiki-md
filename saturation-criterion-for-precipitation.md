# Saturation criterion for precipitation

↑ **Parent:** [Solubility product](solubility-product.md)

Relative to the specified [solid](solid.md), $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}<K_{\mathrm{sp}}$ favors [dissolution](dissolution.md) if that [solid](solid.md) is available; $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}>K_{\mathrm{sp}}$ favors [precipitation](precipitation-chemistry.md); equality marks saturation. These comparisons identify a driving direction favored by [thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md), not a [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md).[Chemistry 2e, §15.1, Predicting Precipitation: unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated cases](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/15-1-precipitation-and-dissolution.md)

A proof outline comes from the reaction [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) for dissolution:

$$
\Delta_rG=RT\ln\!\left(\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sp}}}{K_{\mathrm{sp}}}\right).
$$

Here $R$ is the [molar gas constant](molar-gas-constant.md) and $T>0$ the absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md). If $Q_{\mathrm{sp}}>K_{\mathrm{sp}}$, the [logarithm](logarithm.md) is positive, making the reverse transfer into [solid](solid.md) favorable. At equality the bulk driving difference vanishes. Equality can also occur at the boundary where the last [solid](solid.md) disappears, but it is not a universal constraint on every solid-free [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md).
