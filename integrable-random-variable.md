# Integrable random variable

↑ **Parent:** [Expected value](expected-value-split.md)

A real or complex [random variable](random-variable-split.md) $X$ is integrable when $\mathbb E|X|<\infty$. This is [integrability](integrability.md) with respect to its [probability measure](probability-measure.md), and ensures that its [expected value](expected-value-split.md) is finite. A bounded [random variable](random-variable-split.md) is always an [integrable random variable](integrable-random-variable.md); so is the example taking $-1,2,8$, for which $\mathbb E|X|=2.7$.

For a real [random variable](random-variable-split.md), put $X_+=\max(X,0)$ and $X_-=\max(-X,0)$, its [positive part](positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md) and [negative part](negative-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md). Each has a nonnegative [Lebesgue integral](lebesgue-integral.md), possibly infinite. The expression $\mathbb E[X]=\mathbb E[X_+]-\mathbb E[X_-]$ is defined when at least one of these two [expected values](expected-value-split.md) is finite; if both are infinite, the [expected value](expected-value-split.md) is undefined. [Integrability](integrability.md) makes both finite. A nonnegative [random variable](random-variable-split.md) can have [expected value](expected-value-split.md) $+\infty$, even though it is not an [integrable random variable](integrable-random-variable.md).

The standard [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) is a useful warning. With its [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) $f(x)=1/[\pi(1+x^2)]$, the positive contribution is $\int_0^Rxf(x)\,dx=\log(1+R^2)/(2\pi)$, which tends to infinity; the negative contribution has the same magnitude. Integrating symmetrically over $[-R,R]$ gives zero for each $R$, but this cancellation does not define an [expected value](expected-value-split.md). The [Cauchy sample mean has an unchanged distribution](cauchy-sample-mean-has-an-unchanged-distribution.md), showing why the [integrability](integrability.md) hypothesis in the [strong law of large numbers](strong-law-of-large-numbers.md) matters.

For an [integrable random variable](integrable-random-variable.md), [conditional expectation](conditional-expectation.md) preserves [integrability](integrability.md), and [linearity of expectation](linearity-of-expectation.md) can safely combine finite averages without forming $\infty-\infty$.
