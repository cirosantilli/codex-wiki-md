# Friction

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian mechanics](newtonian-mechanics.md)

[Friction](friction.md) is a contact [force](force.md) tangent to the surfaces. [Kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) opposes [contact slip](contact-slip.md); [static friction](static-friction.md) maintains contact without [contact slip](contact-slip.md), including [rolling without slipping](rolling-without-slipping.md). The perpendicular contact component is the [normal force](normal-force.md). These two components answer different questions: how strongly the surfaces press together, and how strongly they resist sliding past one another.

The ideal Coulomb dry-contact model combines a bound on [static friction](static-friction.md) with a sliding law for [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md). Its parameters are the [coefficient of static friction](coefficient-of-static-friction.md) and the [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md); constant coefficients are an empirical approximation, rather than a consequence of [Newton's laws of motion](newton-s-laws-of-motion.md). Surface condition matters, and the static coefficient need not universally exceed the kinetic coefficient. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics I, §12-2: friction and the limits of the empirical law](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_12.html#Ch12-S2)

The model's historical setting was practical machinery: Coulomb's _Théorie des machines simples_ investigated [friction](friction.md) and the stiffness of ropes. The Académie's bibliography records its 1781 prize and its appearance in volume 10, pages 161–332, of the _Recueil des savants étrangers_. [Académie des sciences: Coulomb's published works](https://www.academie-sciences.fr/pdf/dossiers/Coulomb/Coulomb_pubd1.htm)
