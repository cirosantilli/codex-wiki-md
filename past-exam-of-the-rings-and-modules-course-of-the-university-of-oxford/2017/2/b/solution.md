<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The three answers, in the given order, are **no, yes, yes**.

For $\mathbb Z[x]$, consider the [ideal](../../../../ideal.md) $I=(2,x)$. Its members have even constant coefficient, so $1\notin I$ and the ideal is proper. If $I=(g)$, then $g$ divides the constant $2$, forcing $g$ to be constant by additivity of [polynomial degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) in this [integral domain](../../../../integral-domain.md). Since $g$ also divides $x$, it must divide the coefficient $1$, giving $g=\pm1$. That would make $I$ the whole ring, a contradiction. Thus $\mathbb Z[x]$ is not a [principal ideal domain](../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md), and part (a) shows it cannot be a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md). This is the [nonprincipal ideal generated by a prime and an indeterminate](../../../../nonprincipal-ideal-generated-by-a-prime-and-an-indeterminate.md) obstruction.

Evaluation at $i$ gives the [ring isomorphism](../../../../ring-isomorphism.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb Z[i].}
$$

Indeed, division by the [monic polynomial](../../../../monic-polynomial.md) $x^2+1$ leaves a unique remainder $a+bx$, and its value $a+bi$ is zero only when both integer coefficients vanish. Thus the quotient maps bijectively, with operations preserved, onto the [Gaussian integers](../../../../gaussian-integer.md). These form a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) for the [Gaussian integer norm](../../../../gaussian-integer-norm.md) $N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2$, a standard Euclidean example allowed in this part. Geometrically, rounding the real and imaginary coordinates gives a quotient error with squared modulus at most $1/2<1$.

Finally,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z[x,y]/(2,x)\cong\mathbb F_2[y],}
$$

via $f(x,y)\mapsto f(0,y)\bmod2$. Every polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$ occurs. The kernel consists exactly of polynomials whose $x$-independent part has even coefficients: write $f(x,y)=xg(x,y)+h(y)$ to see that this is precisely $(2,x)$. Hence the induced quotient map is a bijective [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md). Its target is a [Euclidean domain](../../../../euclidean-domain.md) with Euclidean function $\deg$: a [polynomial ring over a field is Euclidean](../../../../polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-euclidean.md) by the division algorithm in part (a).
