<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**No such ring exists.** Characteristic two gives $r+r=0$ for every $r$. Choose a nonzero element $a$, which exists in a five-element set. Translation $r\mapsto r+a$ is an [involution](../../../../../involution.md) without fixed points: applying it twice gives $r$, while $r+a=r$ would force $a=0$ by additive cancellation. Its disjoint pairs would partition all five elements, which is impossible because five is [odd](../../../../../odd-function.md).

This argument applies even to a [nonunital ring](../../../../../rng-algebra.md). With the unital convention, it is also the case $n=2$ of [finite ring characteristic divides cardinality](../../../../../finite-ring-characteristic-divides-cardinality.md).
