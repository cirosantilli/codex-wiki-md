<h1 id="1/b/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Under the unital [ring](../../../../../ring.md) convention used elsewhere in this wiki, **no such pair exists**. In a two-element ring the elements are $0$ and $1$. Additive cancellation forces $1+1=0$, and the identity axiom forces $1\cdot1=1$. The [distributive property](../../../../../distributive-property.md) forces products involving zero to vanish. Every operation is therefore determined, giving the [prime field](../../../../../prime-field.md) $\mathbb F_2$ as the unique possibility up to [ring isomorphism](../../../../../ring-isomorphism.md).

If the word ring allows a [nonunital ring](../../../../../rng-algebra.md), there are instead **two non-isomorphic examples: $\mathbb F_2$ and the [zero-multiplication rng](../../../../../zero-multiplication-rng.md) on the additive group $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$**. In the second example the unique nonzero element $u$ satisfies $u+u=0$ and $u^2=0$; every product is zero, so associativity and both distributive laws hold. An isomorphism would have to send $1$ to $u$ because it preserves zero and is bijective, but then it would send $1^2=1$ to both $u^2=0$ and $u$, which is impossible. These exhaust the [rngs with two elements](../../../../../rng-with-two-elements.md), since $u^2$ can only be $0$ or $u$. Specifying the identity convention is essential for this part.
