<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A suitable [field with four elements](../../../../../field-with-four-elements.md) is

$$
\boxed{R=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1).}
$$

Write $\alpha$ for the class of $t$. [Monic polynomial division over a ring](../../../../../monic-polynomial-division-over-a-ring.md) gives a unique representative $a+b\alpha$, with $a,b\in\mathbb F_2$: existence follows by division, and uniqueness follows because a nonzero multiple of the degree-two modulus cannot have degree less than two. The four elements are therefore $0,1,\alpha,\alpha+1$.

The defining relation gives $\alpha^2=\alpha+1$, and hence $\alpha(\alpha+1)=1$. Thus the nonzero elements have inverses

$$
1^{-1}=1,\qquad\alpha^{-1}=\alpha+1,\qquad(\alpha+1)^{-1}=\alpha.
$$

The [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) is commutative and every nonzero element is a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), so it is a [field](../../../../../field.md). This verifies the field property directly, without quoting a theorem about irreducible-polynomial quotients.
