<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**No such property exists.** The [ring isomorphism](../../../../../ring-isomorphism.md) in part (b)(ii) identifies [quotient rings](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) by elements having different [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) behaviour.

The element $X^3$ is an [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) of $T$. It is nonzero and a [nonunit](../../../../../nonunit.md). Every nonzero [nonunit](../../../../../nonunit.md) of $T$ has [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) at least $2$: the only possible [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of smaller [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) are [constant polynomials](../../../../../constant-polynomial.md), and every nonzero [constant polynomial](../../../../../constant-polynomial.md) is a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). If $X^3=uv$ with $u,v\in T$ both [nonunits](../../../../../nonunit.md), additivity of [polynomial degree](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) in the [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) $\mathbb R[X]$ would give

$$
3=\deg u+\deg v\geq2+2=4,
$$

which is impossible. Thus one factor must be a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md).

In $\mathbb R[Y]$, however, $Y^3=Y\cdot Y^2$ is a product of two [nonunits](../../../../../nonunit.md), so $Y^3$ is not an [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md). If a [ring isomorphism invariant](../../../../../ring-isomorphism-invariant.md) $P$ characterized [irreducible elements](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) by the indicated [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md), the [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) $X^3\in T$ would imply that $T/(X^3)_T$ has $P$. Its isomorphic [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) $\mathbb R[Y]/(Y^3)$ would then have $P$, incorrectly making $Y^3\in\mathbb R[Y]$ an [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md).

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\text{irreducibility cannot be determined from the isomorphism type of }R/(x)}.
$$
