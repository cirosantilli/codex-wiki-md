<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The required property is

$$
\boxed{P:\quad\text{being an integral domain}}.
$$

For a nonzero element $x$ of an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) $R$, this follows from

$$
\begin{aligned}
x\text{ is a prime element}
&\iff (x)\text{ is a prime ideal}\\
&\iff R/(x)\text{ is an integral domain}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Indeed, $\overline a\,\overline b=0$ in the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) exactly when $x\mid ab$. The [prime element](../../../../../prime-element.md) condition says this forces $x\mid a$ or $x\mid b$, exactly the absence of [zero divisors](../../../../../zero-divisor.md) in the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md). The [nonunit](../../../../../nonunit.md) condition makes the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) nonzero. Being an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) is preserved by every [ring isomorphism](../../../../../ring-isomorphism.md).

With the usual definition of a [prime element](../../../../../prime-element.md), $x\ne0$ is understood. The [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) formulation works for every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of any [commutative ring](../../../../../commutative-ring.md), including the zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md); it avoids this convention about elements.
