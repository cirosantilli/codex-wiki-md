<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Define the [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md)

$$
\theta:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_d\oplus\mathbb Z_h,\qquad
t\longmapsto(t+d\mathbb Z,t+h\mathbb Z).
$$

By part (b)(i), its [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is

$$
\ker\theta=d\mathbb Z\cap h\mathbb Z=dh\mathbb Z.
$$

It is [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md). Indeed, choose $r,s\in\mathbb Z$ with $rd+sh=1$ by [Bézout's identity](../../../../../bezout-s-identity.md). For any prescribed classes $u+d\mathbb Z$ and $v+h\mathbb Z$, the [integer](../../../../../integer-split.md)

$$
t=ush+vrd
$$

satisfies $t\equiv u\pmod d$ and $t\equiv v\pmod h$. The [first isomorphism theorem for groups](../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z_{dh}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathbb Z_d\oplus\mathbb Z_h,\qquad
t+dh\mathbb Z\longmapsto(t+d\mathbb Z,t+h\mathbb Z)}.
$$

This proves the required isomorphism and is the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) for [coprime integers](../../../../../coprime-integers.md). It also shows that $(1+d\mathbb Z,1+h\mathbb Z)$ generates the [direct sum of modules](../../../../../direct-sum-of-modules.md) when these additive [groups](../../../../../group-split.md) are viewed as $\mathbb Z$-[modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md).
