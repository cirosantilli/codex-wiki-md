<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Every multiple of $dh$ is a multiple of both $d$ and $h$, so $dh\mathbb Z\subseteq d\mathbb Z\cap h\mathbb Z$.

For the reverse inclusion, let $z=du=hv$ lie in this [intersection of ideals](../../../../../intersection-of-ideals.md). Since $d,h$ are [coprime integers](../../../../../coprime-integers.md), [Bézout's identity](../../../../../bezout-s-identity.md) supplies $r,s\in\mathbb Z$ with $rd+sh=1$. Multiplying by $u$ gives

$$
u=rdu+shu=rhv+shu=h(rv+su).
$$

Thus $h\mid u$, and $dh\mid du=z$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{d\mathbb Z\cap h\mathbb Z=dh\mathbb Z}.
$$

Here [Bézout's identity](../../../../../bezout-s-identity.md) follows from the allowed [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) property of $\mathbb Z$: the [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $d\mathbb Z+h\mathbb Z$ is generated by their [greatest common divisor](../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md), which is $1$.
