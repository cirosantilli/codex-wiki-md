<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $N=|G|$ be a [square-free integer](../../../../../square-free-integer.md). If $N=1$, then $G$ is the trivial [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md). Otherwise, finiteness means that a [direct sum of modules](../../../../../direct-sum-of-modules.md) decomposition into [cyclic groups](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) has only finitely many nontrivial factors, each of finite [group order](../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md). Omit trivial factors and write

$$
G\cong\mathbb Z_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Z_{n_r},\qquad
n_j>1,\qquad N=\prod_{j=1}^r n_j.
$$

The [integers](../../../../../integer-split.md) $n_i,n_j$ are [coprime integers](../../../../../coprime-integers.md) whenever $i\ne j$. If a [prime number](../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$ divided both, then $p^2\mid N$, contradicting the [square-free integer](../../../../../square-free-integer.md) hypothesis.

Moreover, $n_1\cdots n_{j-1}$ and $n_j$ are [coprime integers](../../../../../coprime-integers.md): any common [prime divisor](../../../../../prime-factor.md) would divide one of the earlier factors as well as $n_j$. Thus part (b)(ii) can be applied repeatedly to combine factors, giving

$$
\boxed{G\cong\mathbb Z_{n_1\cdots n_r}=\mathbb Z_N}.
$$

In particular $G$ is a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md). Under the displayed identifications, the element whose coordinate in every factor is the class of $1$ is a [generator of a group](../../../../../generator-of-a-group.md).
