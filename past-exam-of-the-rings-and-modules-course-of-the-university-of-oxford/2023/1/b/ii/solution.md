<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Every multiple of $x_1\cdots x_r$ is a multiple of each $x_j$, giving one inclusion. For the reverse inclusion, a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) is a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md), so each [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) $x_j$ is a [prime element](../../../../../prime-element.md).

Let $a\in\bigcap_{j=1}^r(x_j)$. The case $a=0$ is immediate. Otherwise, write $a=x_1b$. Since $x_2\mid a$ and the [prime element](../../../../../prime-element.md) $x_2$ cannot divide the non-[associate](../../../../../associate-element.md) [irreducible element](../../../../../irreducible-element.md) $x_1$, it divides $b$. Write $a=x_1x_2b_2$, and repeat. At stage $j$, the [prime element](../../../../../prime-element.md) $x_j$ divides none of $x_1,\ldots,x_{j-1}$, so it divides the remaining factor. Hence $x_1\cdots x_r\mid a$, proving

$$
\boxed{(x_1\cdots x_r)=(x_1)\cap\cdots\cap(x_r)}.
$$
