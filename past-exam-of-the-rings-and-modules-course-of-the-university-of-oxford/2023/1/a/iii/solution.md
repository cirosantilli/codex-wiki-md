<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The element $-1_R$ lies in the [unit group](../../../../../unit-group.md) and satisfies $(-1_R)^2=1_R$. Its [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) divides two and, by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), divides the odd number $|U(R)|$. Its order is therefore one, so $-1_R=1_R$ and $2\cdot1_R=0$.

It follows that

$$
\boxed{\mathbb F_2\longrightarrow R,\qquad\overline a\longmapsto a\cdot1_R}
$$

is a well-defined unital [ring homomorphism](../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md). For a nonzero [ring](../../../../../ring.md), this says its [characteristic of a ring](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-ring.md) is two. If the [zero ring](../../../../../zero-ring.md) is permitted, the same map still exists. This is the [odd unit group forces characteristic two](../../../../../odd-unit-group-forces-characteristic-two.md) argument, with that harmless exception to the characteristic wording.
