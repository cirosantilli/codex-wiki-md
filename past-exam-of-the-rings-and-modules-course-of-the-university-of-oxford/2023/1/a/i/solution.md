<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Multiplication in a [direct product of rings](../../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md) is coordinatewise. Thus $(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$ has a two-sided inverse exactly when each $r_j$ has a two-sided inverse in $R_j$, and then

$$
(r_1,\ldots,r_n)^{-1}=(r_1^{-1},\ldots,r_n^{-1}).
$$

Consequently its [unit group](../../../../../unit-group.md) is the [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md)

$$
\boxed{U(R_1\times\cdots\times R_n)=U(R_1)\times\cdots\times U(R_n)}.
$$

The identification preserves multiplication because both sides use coordinatewise multiplication. This is the [unit group of a direct product of rings](../../../../../unit-group-of-a-direct-product-of-rings.md) formula.
