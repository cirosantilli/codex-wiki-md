<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The nonconstant [invariant factors of a linear operator](../../../../../invariant-factors-of-a-linear-operator.md) satisfy $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_k=m_A$. By part (a)(i), $m_A=X^2+1$ is an [irreducible polynomial](../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md), so every $d_j$ must equal $X^2+1$. Their [polynomial degrees](../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) sum to $n$, giving $n=2k$.

The [companion matrix](../../../../../companion-matrix.md) of $X^2+1$, in the [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $(1,X)$ of $\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2+1)$, is

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Therefore, for each fixed even $n$, every such $A$ has [rational canonical form](../../../../../rational-canonical-form.md)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{C,\ldots,C}_{n/2\text{ blocks}})}.
$$

There are no such [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) when $n$ is odd. This is an instance of the [rational canonical form with irreducible minimal polynomial](../../../../../rational-canonical-form-with-irreducible-minimal-polynomial.md) principle; equality of [minimal polynomials](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) alone does not determine [rational canonical form](../../../../../rational-canonical-form.md) in general.
