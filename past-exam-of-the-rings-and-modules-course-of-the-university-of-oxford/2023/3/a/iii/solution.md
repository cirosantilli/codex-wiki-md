<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $v\in\mathbb Q^2$ be nonzero. If the columns $v,Av$ were [linearly dependent](../../../../../linear-dependence.md) over the [rational numbers](../../../../../rational-number.md), then $Av=\lambda v$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb Q$. Applying $A$ once more would give

$$
-v=A^2v=\lambda Av=\lambda^2v,
$$

so $(\lambda^2+1)v=0$. Since $v\ne0$, this forces $\lambda^2=-1$, impossible in $\mathbb Q$. Thus the two columns are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md), and the square [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md)

$$
\boxed{E=\begin{pmatrix}|&|\\v&Av\\|&|\end{pmatrix}}
$$

is an [invertible matrix](../../../../../invertible-matrix.md).
