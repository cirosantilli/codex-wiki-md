<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Consider

$$
W=\{w\in B:w(j)\in\{-2^j,2^j\}\text{ for every }j\in\mathbb N_0\}.
$$

The map sending a sequence of signs $(\varepsilon_j)$ to $(\varepsilon_j2^j)$ is a bijection from $\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb N_0}$ onto $W$. This is an [uncountable set](../../../../../uncountable-set.md) by the [Cantor diagonal argument](../../../../../cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md): given a proposed list $w_0,w_1,\ldots$ of its elements, define $w(j)=-w_j(j)$. Then $w\in W$ differs from $w_j$ at coordinate $j$ for every $j$.

Since $\ker\pi$ is [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md), $W$ cannot be contained in it. Consequently there exists

$$
\boxed{w\in W\setminus\ker\pi,\qquad\pi(w)\ne0}.
$$
