<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the [direct sum of modules](../../../../../direct-sum-of-modules.md) $M=N\oplus P$, every $m\in M$ has a unique expression $m=n+p$, with $n\in N$ and $p\in P$. Define

$$
\boxed{\pi:M\to M,\qquad\pi(n+p)=p}.
$$

The uniqueness makes this well-defined. For $m=n+p$, $m'=n'+p'$ and $r\in R$,

$$
\pi(m+m')=p+p'=\pi(m)+\pi(m'),\qquad\pi(rm)=rp=r\pi(m),
$$

so $\pi$ is a [module homomorphism](../../../../../module-homomorphism.md). Finally, $\pi(n+p)=0$ exactly when $p=0$, equivalently $m\in N$. Therefore $\boxed{\ker\pi=N}$. Its image is $P$, and $\pi^2=\pi$, so it is the projection onto that [direct summand](../../../../../direct-summand.md).
