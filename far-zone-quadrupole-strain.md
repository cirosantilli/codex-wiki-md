# Far-zone quadrupole strain

↑ **Parent:** [Quadrupole formula](quadrupole-formula.md)

In units $c=1$, a slowly moving isolated source has trace-free [mass quadrupole moment](mass-quadrupole-moment.md) $Q^{ij}=\int T^{00}(x^ix^j-\delta^{ij}|\mathbf x|^2/3)\,d^3x$. The retarded [linearized gravity](linearized-gravity.md) solution and the [stress-energy quadrupole identity](stress-energy-quadrupole-identity.md) give the displayed physical spatial strain, with $\mathbf n=\mathbf x/r$ and the [transverse-traceless projector](transverse-traceless-projector.md) $\Lambda$. If the spatial metric is written $g_{ij}=-\delta_{ij}-2E_{ij}$ for [metric signature](metric-signature.md) $(+---)$, its trace-free Lorenz-gauge coefficient before the transverse projection is $E_{ij}=G\ddot Q_{ij}(t-r)/r$. Thus $H^{\mathrm{TT}}=2E^{\mathrm{TT}}$. Far distance alone does not permit dropping source-dependent retardation: the quadrupole approximation also requires the source size to be small compared with the radiation wavelength.
