# Dissolution

↑ **Parent:** [Solvation](solvation.md)

[Dissolution](dissolution.md) transfers material into a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md). It can include separation from an [ionic crystal](ionic-crystal.md) and [solvation](solvation.md), or dispersal of neutral [molecules](molecule-split.md). For [sucrose](sucrose.md) and [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), respectively,

$$
\mathrm{C}_{12}\mathrm{H}_{22}\mathrm{O}_{11}(s)\longrightarrow\mathrm{C}_{12}\mathrm{H}_{22}\mathrm{O}_{11}(aq),\qquad
\mathrm{NaCl}(s)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Na}^{+}(aq)+\mathrm{Cl}^{-}(aq).
$$

Here $(aq)$ specifies [water](water.md) as the [solvent](solvent.md); it does not specify a fixed number of attached [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md). The neutral [sucrose](sucrose.md) remains molecular. [Dissociation of an ionic compound](dissociation-of-an-ionic-compound.md) separates pre-existing [ions](ion.md) in the second example; [solvation](solvation.md) stabilizes their new surroundings.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; Opening molecular and ionic dissolution examples](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-1-the-dissolution-process.md)[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; “Ionic Electrolytes”](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-2-electrolytes.md)

[Ionization](ionization.md) instead creates [ions](ion.md) from neutral species. [Hydrogen chloride](hydrogen-chloride.md) reacting with [water](water.md) illustrates [protonation](protonation.md) of [water](water.md) rather than mere rearrangement of a [hydration shell](hydration-shell.md):

$$
\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm O\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm O^{+}+\mathrm{Cl}^{-}.
$$

The [hydronium](hydronium.md) and negative [ion](ion.md) together preserve total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). [Solvation](solvation.md) can influence this [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md), but an [ion](ion.md)'s [solvation](solvation.md) does not itself change its net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md).[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; “Covalent Electrolytes”, HCl proton-transfer example](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-2-electrolytes.md)
