# Optical fiber coupling and bend experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Optical fiber](optical-fiber.md)

MIT's [Optical Waveguides and Fibers](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-161-modern-optics-project-laboratory-fall-2005/862b690eb7d638cd6dd5a063942902af_lab7.pdf), Laboratory Exercise No. 7 (Fall 2005), describes a teaching apparatus. A cleaned, flat-ended [multimode optical fiber](multimode-optical-fiber.md) receives [helium](helium.md)–[neon](neon.md) [laser](laser-split.md) [light](light.md) through a microscope objective [optical lens](lens-optics.md); a micropositioner aligns the input. The launch deliberately overfills the fibre's cone. A [beam splitter](beam-splitter.md) sends a sample to a reference [photodetector](photodetector.md) to track [laser](laser-split.md) fluctuations. The fixed output faces a screen or a [photodetector](photodetector.md) on a rotation stage with angular [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md). Readings every two degrees, divided by simultaneous reference readings, give an angular profile. Repeating with an underfilled launch is a control: too few excited [normal modes](normal-mode.md) underestimate [numerical aperture](numerical-aperture.md). Optional index-matching fluid removes cladding-carried [light](light.md). Bending the fibre reveals leakage and reduced output on an [oscilloscope](oscilloscope.md). The handout supplies no measured trace or absolute detector [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md), and this angular scan does not determine loss per kilometre.[Optical Waveguides and Fibers, MIT 6.161 Modern Optics Project Laboratory, Fall 2005, Laboratory Exercise No. 7, pp. 3 and 6–7, end-face preparation, §7.3 and Fig. 1: reference-normalized angular scan, underfill control and bend test](optical-waveguides-and-fibers-mit-6-161-laboratory-exercise.md)
