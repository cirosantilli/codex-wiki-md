# Dimension (vector space)

↑ **Parent:** [Vector space](vector-space-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_(vector_space))

The dimension of a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) is the [cardinality](cardinality.md) of any of its [bases](basis.md). In finite dimension this counts the [scalars](scalar.md) needed to specify an arbitrary [vector](vector.md) once an [ordered basis](ordered-basis.md) is chosen. The exchange argument behind the [basis extension](basis-extension.md) theorem shows that any two finite [bases](basis.md) have the same size: an independent family cannot be larger than a spanning family.

Thus $\mathbb R^2$ has [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) two even with an oblique [basis](basis.md), $\mathbb R[x]_{\leq2}$ has [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) three, and the homogeneous plane in [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) has [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) two. This count concerns independent freedom, so an embedded plane remains two-dimensional while sitting in a three-dimensional ambient [vector space](vector-space-split.md). [Dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) also depends on the [field](field.md), as the two interpretations of $\mathbb C$ in [vector space](vector-space-split.md) illustrate.
