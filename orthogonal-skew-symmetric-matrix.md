# Orthogonal skew-symmetric matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Skew-symmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md)

A real [skew-symmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) is an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md) exactly when its square is minus the [identity matrix](identity-matrix.md). Indeed, $B^{\mathsf T}B=-B^2$. Such a [matrix](matrix.md) can exist only in even [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md): taking [determinants](determinant.md) of $B^2=-I$ would otherwise give $(\det B)^2=-1$. In two dimensions every example is

$$
B=\pm\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

These are [rotation matrices](rotation-matrix.md) through opposite right angles. Their [orthogonal similarity](orthogonal-similarity.md) transforms preserve [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md), but do not in general produce a [diagonal matrix](diagonal-matrix.md).
