<h1 id="gas-syringe-measurement-of-boyle-s-law">Gas-syringe measurement of Boyle's law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Boyle's law](boyle-s-law.md)

Vernier's Boyle's Law: Pressure-Volume Relationship in Gases, Experiment 6 of Chemistry with Vernier, specifies a graduated $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ [gas syringe](gas-syringe.md), a [pressure sensor](pressure-sensor.md) and LabQuest recording. Preparation sets the disconnected syringe to $10.0\,\mathrm{mL}$, then attaches it to the sensor valve, trapping air. At several plunger positions, the displayed [pressure](pressure-split.md) is allowed to stabilize before the paired [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) is entered. The analysis tests whether $pV$ remains approximately constant. It adds $0.8\,\mathrm{mL}$ to each syringe reading for the sensor's internal [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md); this correction belongs to that apparatus. The worksheet supplies a blank results table, not measured values.[Experiment 6, printed pp. 6–1 to 6–3: Figure 1, preparation 1b–c, procedure 3–6, blank data table, and Processing the Data 7–9](chemistry-with-vernier/6-boyle-s-law-pressure-volume-relationship-in-gases.md)

The manufacturer's Gas Pressure Sensor User Manual identifies a membrane sensor referenced to a [vacuum](vacuum.md), hence an [absolute pressure](absolute-pressure.md) readout. Its calibration section uses the actual local [atmospheric pressure](atmospheric-pressure.md) for an ambient reference check, rather than a weather report corrected to sea level. Checking this baseline before sealing separates an offset error from compression physics.[Calibration: station pressure and one-point reference; Specifications: internal volume; How the Sensor Works: vacuum-referenced membrane and absolute-pressure output](gas-pressure-sensor-user-manual-vernier.md)

Additional controls follow from the model. Keep the same sealed [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md), connections and ambient [temperature](temperature-split.md); persistent [pressure](pressure-split.md) drift at held [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) after [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) suggests a leak or another uncontrolled change. Small steps and waiting allow compression [heat](heat-split.md) to leave the [gas](gas.md); avoiding hand warming reduces another [heat](heat-split.md) input. Compare settled readings when approaching a position from either direction. Plunger [friction](friction.md) affects the applied [force](force-split.md) and position reproducibility, so record actual [pressure](pressure-split.md) and actual plunger position rather than predicting [pressure](pressure-split.md) from the load. These are proposed controls, not reported experimental outcomes.

Nearly constant $pV$ across equilibrated states supports the fixed-[temperature](temperature-split.md) [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) approximation over the tested range. Stabilized [pressure](pressure-split.md) alone does not demonstrate uniform or constant [temperature](temperature-split.md); the [isothermal gas syringe experiment](isothermal-gas-syringe-experiment.md) adds a [temperature](temperature-split.md) readout to distinguish a transient from its cooled endpoint. Finite sensor accuracy and uncertain total [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) limit the test, and agreement does not establish ideal behavior at every [pressure](pressure-split.md). Repeated return readings help distinguish reproducibility from a one-direction trend.
