# Criterion for membership in a diagonal basis

↑ **Parent:** [Sylvester's law of inertia](sylvester-s-law-of-inertia.md)

A vector $v$ can be included in a diagonal [basis](basis.md) for a real [quadratic form](quadratic-form.md) precisely when $v\ne0$ and either its quadratic value is nonzero or it lies in the [radical of a bilinear form](radical-of-a-bilinear-form.md). A nonzero quadratic value gives the direct splitting $\mathbb Rv\oplus v^\perp$ and induction. A nonzero radical vector can be included in a radical basis. Conversely, an [isotropic vector](isotropic-vector.md) in a diagonal basis is orthogonal to every basis vector, and must lie in the radical.
