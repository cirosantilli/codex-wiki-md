# Force-free transport of a nearly occluding sphere

↑ **Parent:** [Nearly occluding sphere in a cylindrical tube](nearly-occluding-sphere-in-a-cylindrical-tube.md)

For a nonrotating [force-free](force-free.md) [sphere](sphere.md) moving with speed $U$ in a stationary tube, use its moving frame: the tube wall has velocity $-U$. In the [parabolic lubrication gap](parabolic-lubrication-gap.md) $h=h_0+x^2/(2a)$, $h_0=\epsilon a(1+\lambda\cos\theta)$, [moving-boundary lubrication flux](moving-boundary-lubrication-flux.md) gives $q=-h^3p_x/(12\mu)-Uh/2$. The [wall-shear and pressure-drop balance in a tube](wall-shear-and-pressure-drop-balance-in-a-tube.md) rules out an $O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ net pressure jump. Applying this common pressure recovery to each angular strip gives $q=-2Uh_0/3$. The next balance yields the displayed positive localized pressure drop. Meanwhile the integrated leading [shear stress](shear-stress.md) on the sphere vanishes in each strip; this is consistent with negligible leading torque, and does not mean the local shear or pressure force vanishes.
