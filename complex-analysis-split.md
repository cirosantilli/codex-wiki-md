# Complex analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Analysis](analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analysis)

Complex analysis studies [functions](function-split.md) of a [complex number](complex-number.md), especially [holomorphic functions](holomorphic-function-split.md). Its distinctive feature is that a single complex [derivative](derivative.md) controls variation in every direction of the [complex plane](complex-plane.md). The map $f(z)=z^2$ gives a first picture: straight coordinate lines become curved, yet small perpendicular arrows remain perpendicular wherever $f'(z)\ne0$.

The complex square bends a grid globally and acts near 1 + i as a rotation through 45 degrees and a scaling by twice the square root of two**.

The bottom panels show displacements rather than absolute positions. Multiplication by $f'(1+i)=2+2i$ predicts the image of each small arrow; the exact image differs by $h^2$. The origin is exceptional because $f'(0)=0$. This distinction between local [conformal maps](conformal-map.md) and a [critical point](critical-point.md) is central: [complex differentiability](complex-differentiability.md) imposes strong geometric structure, without making every [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) globally one-to-one.

The remarkable next step is that [holomorphic functions](holomorphic-function-split.md) are determined inside a circle by their values on its boundary. Precisely, let $R>0$, let $D=\{z:|z-a|<R\}$, and suppose $f$ is a [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) on an [open set](open-set.md) containing the closed disc $\overline D$. Orient its boundary counterclockwise. The [Cauchy integral formula](cauchy-integral-formula.md) states that, for every $w\in D$,

$$
f(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-a|=R}\frac{f(z)}{z-w}\,dz.
$$

The [contour integral](contour-integral.md) combines boundary values with a kernel depending on the requested interior point. It is a reconstruction formula, not merely a trick for evaluating an [integral](integral-split.md). The hypothesis about the closed disc ensures that all boundary values and the [contour integral](contour-integral.md) are well defined. [Orloff, MIT Topic 4, Theorem 4.1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-04-complex-variables-with-applications-spring-2018/b1a90df19f643b974555bbbb93138a48_MIT18_04S18_topic4.pdf)

At the centre, substituting $z=a+Re^{i\theta}$ cancels the denominator against $dz=iRe^{i\theta}d\theta$:

$$
f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+Re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.
$$

Thus the centre value is the angular average of the boundary values. For the [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $f(z)=z^2$, $a=1+i$ and $R=1/2$, expand the boundary data as

$$
f(a+Re^{i\theta})=2i+(1+i)e^{i\theta}+\tfrac14e^{2i\theta}.
$$

Both oscillating terms have zero average, leaving $f(a)=2i$. Away from the centre the weights change: on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md),

$$
f(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{f(e^{i\theta})}{1-we^{-i\theta}}\,d\theta,\qquad |w|<1.
$$

With $f(z)=z^2$ and $w=1/2$, expanding the denominator as a [geometric series](geometric-series.md) leaves only its second-power term after integration, giving $f(1/2)=1/4$. This also explains how the [Cauchy integral formula](cauchy-integral-formula.md) recovers an entire [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) from boundary data.

When a [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md) has [isolated singularities](isolated-singularity-split.md), a different boundary calculation detects their [residues](residue.md). Suppose $\Gamma$ is a counterclockwise simple closed [contour](complex-integration-contour.md) and $f$ is [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) on a neighbourhood of it and its interior except at finitely many interior [isolated singularities](isolated-singularity-split.md) $a_j$. The [residue theorem](residue-theorem.md) gives

$$
\oint_\Gamma f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_j\operatorname{Res}(f,a_j).
$$

A [residue](residue.md) is the coefficient of $(z-a_j)^{-1}$ in the [Laurent series](laurent-series.md). A short proof idea is to deform the [contour](complex-integration-contour.md) to small circles around the [isolated singularities](isolated-singularity-split.md) using the [Cauchy integral theorem](cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md). Integrating the [Laurent series](laurent-series.md) on each circle kills every power except $-1$. [Orloff, MIT Topic 8, residue theorem](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-04-complex-variables-with-applications-spring-2018/3c58ce71ec6f7573804221d76158a0bd_MIT18_04S18_topic8.pdf)

Here is how a complex [pole](pole.md) determines a real [improper integral](improper-integral.md). Set $g(z)=1/(1+z^2)$ and use the [contour](complex-integration-contour.md) consisting of the segment from $-R$ to $R$, followed by the upper semicircle $A_R$, with $R>1$. Only the [simple pole](simple-pole.md) $i$ lies inside, and

$$
\operatorname{Res}(g,i)=\lim_{z\to i}\frac{z-i}{(z-i)(z+i)}=\frac{1}{2i},\qquad \int_{-R}^R\frac{dx}{1+x^2}+\int_{A_R}g(z)\,dz=\pi.
$$

The arc must actually disappear. On $A_R$, the [reverse triangle inequality](reverse-triangle-inequality.md) gives $|1+z^2|\ge R^2-1$, and its length is $\pi R$, so

$$
\left|\int_{A_R}g(z)\,dz\right|\le\frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The real integrand is bounded by $x^{-2}$ for $|x|\ge1$, so both tails converge separately. Taking the limit therefore proves the ordinary [improper integral](improper-integral.md)

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\pi.
$$

For example, at $R=10$ the real segment contributes $2\arctan(10)\approx2.942255$, and the arc contributes $0.199337$, below the bound $10\pi/99\approx0.317333$. The usefulness of the [residue theorem](residue-theorem.md) is that the same strategy works for many [rational functions](rational-function.md) whose real [antiderivatives](antiderivative.md) are cumbersome: find the enclosed [poles](pole.md), compute their [residues](residue.md), and control the connecting arc. [Orloff, MIT Topic 9, contour decay estimates](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-04-complex-variables-with-applications-spring-2018/f69bc227ae476839819fb18e5b283072_MIT18_04S18_topic9.pdf)

Historically, Cauchy's 1825 memoir on [integrals](integral-split.md) with imaginary limits develops complex [contour integration](contour-integration.md) and its use in transforming and evaluating definite [integrals](integral-split.md). The opening sections define complex path [integrals](integral-split.md), discuss changing the path, and examine what happens near singular points. These are recognizable ancestors of the modern link between [holomorphic functions](holomorphic-function-split.md), [contours](complex-integration-contour.md) and [residues](residue.md). [Cauchy, Mémoire sur les intégrales définies, prises entre des limites imaginaires, 1825; reprinted 1874, sections 1–6](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMA_1874__7__265_0.pdf)
