# Radiant intensity

↑ **Parent:** [Radiometry](radiometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_intensity)

[Radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md) describes how a source distributes its [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) over directions. A small cone of [solid angle](solid-angle.md) $\Delta\Omega$ carries approximately $I\Delta\Omega$ when $I$ varies little across it; integrating $I$ over all emitting directions recovers the total [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md). For a source with [isotropy](isotropy.md) emitting $4\pi\,\mathrm W$ over a full [sphere](sphere.md), $I=1\,\mathrm{W\,sr^{-1}}$ and a $0.01\,\mathrm{sr}$ cone carries $0.01\,\mathrm W$. A planar source satisfying [Lambert's cosine law](lambert-s-cosine-law.md) instead has direction-dependent [radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md), even though its [radiance](radiance.md) is direction-independent.
