# Bipolar coordinates

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonal coordinates](orthogonal-coordinates.md)

For a length scale $c>0$, planar [bipolar coordinates](bipolar-coordinates.md) may be defined by

$$
x=\frac{c\sinh\tau}{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma},\qquad y=\frac{c\sin\sigma}{\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma},\qquad0\leq\sigma<2\pi.
$$

The common [scale factor](scale-factor-cosmology.md) is $h=c/(\cosh\tau-\cos\sigma)$, so [Laplace's equation](laplace-equation.md) becomes $\Phi_{\sigma\sigma}+\Phi_{\tau\tau}=0$ away from coordinate singularities. For $\tau>0$, constant-$\tau$ curves are circles

$$
(x-c\coth\tau)^2+y^2=c^2\operatorname{csch}^2\tau.
$$

Their radii decrease as $\tau$ increases; for $0<a<b$ the $\tau=b$ circle lies inside the $\tau=a$ circle. The region $a<\tau<b$ is therefore an eccentric circular annulus. This ordering is useful when translating coordinate boundary data into geometric language.
