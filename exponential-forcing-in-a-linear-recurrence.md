# Exponential forcing in a linear recurrence

↑ **Parent:** [Inhomogeneous linear recurrence](inhomogeneous-linear-recurrence.md)

Let $E$ be the forward shift on sequences and $P$ the polynomial defining a constant-coefficient [linear recurrence relation](linear-recurrence-relation.md). For a nonzero base $r$, $P(E)r^n=P(r)r^n$, so $r^n/P(r)$ is a [particular solution](particular-solution.md) when $P(r)\ne0$. At a simple root of the [characteristic equation of a linear recurrence](characteristic-equation-of-a-linear-recurrence.md), the correct trial acquires a factor $n$:

$$
P(E)(nr^n)=r^n\bigl(nP(r)+rP'(r)\bigr),\qquad
y_n=\frac{nr^n}{rP'(r)}.
$$

The identity follows by substituting $(n+j)r^{n+j}$ into each shift term. A root of multiplicity $m$ analogously requires a degree-$m$ polynomial factor; the lower-degree homogeneous terms are supplied by the [repeated characteristic root of a linear recurrence](repeated-characteristic-root-of-a-linear-recurrence.md).
