# Energy ratio between equal-phase damped crossings

↑ **Parent:** [Logarithmic decrement](logarithmic-decrement.md)

A linearly damped oscillator $m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=0$ has $x=Ae^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_dt+\phi)$, with $\gamma=b/(2m)$ and $\omega_d^2=k/m-\gamma^2>0$. After one damped period $T=2\pi/\omega_d$, both displacement and [velocity](velocity.md) are multiplied by $e^{-\gamma T}$. Their quadratic [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) is therefore multiplied exactly by $e^{-2\gamma T}$, regardless of the starting [phase](phase-waves.md). In particular successive same-direction equilibrium crossings have this ratio even though [potential energy](potential-energy.md) is zero at each. A cosine [phase](phase-waves.md) maximum is not exactly a displacement turning point when [damping](damping.md) is nonzero.
