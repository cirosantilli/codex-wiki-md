# Citric acid cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Metabolic pathway](metabolic-pathway.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Energy metabolism](energy-metabolism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citric_acid_cycle)

The [citric acid cycle](citric-acid-cycle.md) is a [metabolic pathway](metabolic-pathway.md) that accepts a two-[carbon](carbon.md) [acetyl group](acetyl-group.md) from [acetyl-CoA](acetyl-coa.md), releases two [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md) per turn, and regenerates its four-[carbon](carbon.md) acceptor, [oxaloacetate](oxaloacetate.md). Its [oxidation](oxidation.md) steps load [electron carriers](electron-carrier.md); [oxidative phosphorylation](oxidative-phosphorylation.md) subsequently uses their [electrons](electron.md) to support [ATP](adenosine-triphosphate.md) production. In [mitochondria](mitochondria.md), most steps occur in the [mitochondrial matrix](mitochondrial-matrix.md), while [succinate dehydrogenase](succinate-dehydrogenase.md) lies in the [inner mitochondrial membrane](inner-mitochondrial-membrane.md).[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Chapter 2, How Cells Obtain Energy from Food, The Citric Acid Cycle Generates NADH by Oxidizing Acetyl Groups to CO2; Figure 2-79 and Panel 2-9](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/how-cells-obtain-energy-from-food.md)

The economy is striking: the acceptor is rebuilt rather than supplied anew for every incoming [acetyl group](acetyl-group.md). Regeneration does not create extra [oxaloacetate](oxaloacetate.md). Withdrawal of intermediates for [amino acid](amino-acid.md) synthesis must be balanced by replenishment if the available pool is to remain steady. Thus the [citric acid cycle](citric-acid-cycle.md) connects [energy metabolism](energy-metabolism.md) to synthesis as well as to breakdown.[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Chapter 2, How Cells Obtain Energy from Food, final paragraph of The Citric Acid Cycle Generates NADH by Oxidizing Acetyl Groups to CO2: oxaloacetate and alpha-ketoglutarate as biosynthetic precursors](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/how-cells-obtain-energy-from-food.md)
