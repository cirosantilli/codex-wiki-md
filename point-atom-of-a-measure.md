# Point atom of a measure

↑ **Parent:** [Atom (measure theory)](atom-measure-theory.md)

A point atom is a point $x$ whose singleton is a [measurable set](measurable-set.md) with strictly positive [measure](measure-split.md). That singleton is an [atom of a measure](atom-measure-theory.md). This is more specific than a general [atom of a measure](atom-measure-theory.md), which is defined as a positive-measure [set](set-split.md) that cannot be divided into two [measurable sets](measurable-set.md) of positive [measure](measure-split.md).

For a [probability measure](probability-measure.md) on the [Borel sigma-algebra](borel-sigma-algebra.md) of $\mathbb R$, a point atom at $x$ appears as a jump of its [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md):

$$
\mu(\{x\})=F_\mu(x)-F_\mu(x-).
$$

[Continuity of probability](continuity-of-probability.md) applied to intervals approaching $x$ proves this identity. Keeping such jumps is essential when deciding whether an endpoint belongs to an [integration](integral-split.md) region.
