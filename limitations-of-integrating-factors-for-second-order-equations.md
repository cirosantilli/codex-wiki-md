# Limitations of integrating factors for second-order equations

↑ **Parent:** [Integrating factor](integrating-factor.md)

On an interval where $A\ne0$, the displayed factor gives

$$
w(Ay''+By'+Cy)=(wAy')'+wCy.
$$

This follows from $(wA)'=wB$. It gives a useful self-adjoint form but does not solve a general second-order [ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md), since the $wCy$ term remains. If $C=0$, it does reduce to one [integration](integral-split.md) for $y'$. Another reducible class has $C=B'-A''$, because the original left side is then $[Ay'+(B-A')y]'$. These are sufficient special cases, not a classification of all solvable equations.
