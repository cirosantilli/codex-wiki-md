# Impulse on an end mass of a free symmetric three-mass chain

↑ **Parent:** [Normal modes of three masses connected by two equal springs](normal-modes-of-three-masses-connected-by-two-equal-springs.md)

For end masses $m$, central mass $M$ and two equal springs of stiffness $k$, use the [normal modes](normal-mode.md) $v_0=(1,1,1)^T$, $v_s=(1,0,-1)^T$ and $v_a=(1,-2m/M,1)^T$. Their squared frequencies are $0$, $\omega_s^2=k/m$ and $\omega_a^2=k/m+2k/M$. The prescribed velocity decomposes as

$$
(u,0,0)^T=\frac{mu}{M+2m}v_0+\frac u2v_s+\frac{Mu}{2(M+2m)}v_a.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{x(t)=\frac{mu}{M+2m}t\,v_0+\frac{u}{2\omega_s}\sin(\omega_st)v_s+\frac{Mu}{2(M+2m)\omega_a}\sin(\omega_at)v_a.}
$$

The zero mode is a uniform [center-of-mass motion](center-of-mass-motion.md), with conserved total momentum $mu$ and velocity $mu/(M+2m)$. The other two modes have fixed centre of mass. Dividing their velocity coefficients by their nonzero frequencies gives the sinusoidal displacement amplitudes; for the zero mode the correct time dependence is linear rather than a bounded sine. This distinguishes translation of the complete system from its internal vibration.
