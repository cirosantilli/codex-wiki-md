# Modulus

↑ **Parent:** [Complex number](complex-number.md)

The [modulus](modulus.md) of a [complex number](complex-number.md) $z=x+iy$ is

$$
|z|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=\sqrt{z\overline z}.
$$

It extends [absolute value](absolute-value.md) to the [complex plane](complex-plane.md): the [modulus](modulus.md) is the [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md) from $z$ to zero, or the [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) of its coordinate [vector](vector.md). The [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) gives $|3+4i|=5$, even though its two coordinates have sizes $3$ and $4$. The [modulus](modulus.md) is always nonnegative, and is zero exactly at zero.

Two essential rules are

$$
|zw|=|z||w|,\qquad |z+w|\leq |z|+|w|.
$$

The second is the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md). For $z=3+4i$ and $w=1+i$, the product has [modulus](modulus.md) $\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt2\approx7.071068$, while the sum has [modulus](modulus.md) $\sqrt{41}\approx6.403124<5+\sqrt2\approx6.414214$. The strict inequality records that the two displacement [vectors](vector.md) point in different directions.

The [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md) between two [complex numbers](complex-number.md) $z,w$ is $|z-w|$. Consequently $|z-w|<\varepsilon$ expresses geometric closeness without treating the two coordinates separately. This makes the [modulus](modulus.md) a natural measure of error in [complex analysis](complex-analysis-split.md); the [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) bounds accumulated error, while the product rule tracks how [multiplication](multiplication.md) changes its size. Equal [moduli](modulus.md) do not imply equal [complex numbers](complex-number.md): $5$, $-5$, and $3+4i$ all lie on the same [circle](circle.md) around zero. The [modulus](modulus.md) records size and deliberately forgets direction; the [argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) supplies the missing information.
