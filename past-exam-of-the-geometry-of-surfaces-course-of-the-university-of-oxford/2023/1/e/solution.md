<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Take a hexagonal [polygonal schema](../../../../polygonal-schema.md) with [boundary](../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) word

$$
\boxed{a\,a\,b\,b\,c\,c.}
$$

Identify equally labelled [polygon edges](../../../../side-of-a-polygon.md) in the indicated directions. The two consecutive occurrences of $a$ identify the first three vertices; the two $b$ edges extend this class to the next two vertices, and the $c$ edges include the remaining vertex. Thus all six [polygon vertices](../../../../vertex-of-a-polygon.md) become one, while the six edges become three and the interior is one face. Its [Euler characteristic](../../../../euler-characteristic.md) is

$$
\boxed{\chi(Y)=1-3+1=-1.}
$$

This is the usual [polygonal schema](../../../../polygonal-schema.md) for the [connected sum of surfaces](../../../../connected-sum-of-surfaces.md) of three [real projective planes](../../../../real-projective-plane.md). The same-sign edge pairs force a [nonorientable surface](../../../../non-orientable-surface.md), consistently with $\chi(N_3)=2-3=-1$. A closed [orientable surface](../../../../orientable-surface.md) cannot have odd [Euler characteristic](../../../../euler-characteristic.md).
