<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $\theta_i$ for the internal [angle](../../../../angle.md) at $v_i$, using the numbering in part (b), and use the [hyperbolic plane](../../../../hyperbolic-plane.md) with [Gaussian curvature](../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) $-1$. The necessary and sufficient conditions are **equal [lengths](../../../../length.md) for each pair of equally labelled [polygon sides](../../../../side-of-a-polygon.md), and total [angle](../../../../angle.md) $2\pi$ at each vertex [equivalence class](../../../../equivalence-class.md)**. Explicitly, the two [angle](../../../../angle.md) conditions are

$$
\boxed{\theta_0+\theta_1+\theta_3+\theta_4+\theta_6+\theta_8=2\pi,\qquad
\theta_2+\theta_5+\theta_7+\theta_9=2\pi.}
$$

Necessity follows because the identification must preserve [arc length](../../../../arc-length.md) along each seam, and a smooth interior point of a [Riemannian surface](../../../../riemannian-surface.md) has total [angle](../../../../angle.md) $2\pi$. For sufficiency, equally long [hyperbolic geodesic](../../../../geodesic-in-the-poincare-half-plane-model.md) segments can be identified by [isometries](../../../../isometry.md). Across their interiors, two half-disc [neighbourhoods](../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) join to a hyperbolic disc. At an identified vertex, the incident sectors form a cyclic disc with total [angle](../../../../angle.md) $2\pi$, so no [cone singularity of a surface metric](../../../../cone-singularity-of-a-surface-metric.md) remains. These [local isometries](../../../../local-isometry.md) therefore extend the [Riemannian metric](../../../../riemannian-metric-split.md) smoothly everywhere. This is the [smooth hyperbolic polygon gluing criterion](../../../../smooth-hyperbolic-polygon-gluing-criterion.md).

The ten internal [angles](../../../../angle.md) sum to $4\pi$. The [area of a hyperbolic geodesic polygon](../../../../area-of-a-hyperbolic-geodesic-polygon.md) consequently gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Area}(X)=(10-2)\pi-4\pi=4\pi.}
$$

Equivalently, the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](../../../../gauss-bonnet-theorem.md) gives $-\operatorname{Area}(X)=2\pi\chi(X)=-4\pi$.
