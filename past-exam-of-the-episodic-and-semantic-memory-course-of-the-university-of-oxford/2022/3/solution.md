<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**Specific memory operations can depend on communication between regions even when the required information is measurable within each region separately.** Binding an event, selecting its relevant context and using it to guide a response impose different demands. Regional involvement alone does not tell us whether two regions cooperate, act in parallel or merely share an input. Two experimentally characterized interactions illustrate what stronger evidence can establish.

For [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md)–prefrontal coordination, [Matthew W. Jones](../../../matthew-w-jones.md) and [Matthew A. Wilson](../../../matthew-a-wilson.md) in 2005 recorded simultaneously with tetrodes in rat [hippocampal CA1](../../../hippocampal-ca1.md) and [medial prefrontal cortex](../../../medial-prefrontal-cortex.md) during a spatial memory task. Correlated firing and coupling in the theta range increased during behavior recruiting spatial [working memory](../../../working-memory.md). This links interaction to a particular task demand rather than merely showing that both areas are active. It offers a mechanism by which spatial information can become coordinated with task-dependent behavior.[2005, simultaneous CA1/PFC recordings, correlated firing and task-selective theta coupling](../../../theta-rhythms-coordinate-hippocampal-prefrontal-interactions-in-a-spatial-memory-task-by-matthew-w-jones-and-others.md)

The proposed benefit of timing is precise: if downstream [neurons](../../../neuron-split.md) are sensitive to when inputs arrive, consistent relative phases can make selected inputs effective together. This does not mean that synchrony itself supplies the remembered content. Nor does a common theta rhythm prove transmission from one named region to the other. Shared rhythmic input, changes in movement or another task-related state could increase coupling. [Correlation](../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) provides evidence for [functional connectivity](../../../functional-connectivity.md); demonstrating the necessary route or direction requires a stronger intervention or a constrained causal model.

The [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md)–retrosplenial example adds such an intervention. [Brenton G. Cooper](../../../brenton-g-cooper.md) and colleagues in 2001 combined temporary [retrosplenial cortex](../../../retrosplenial-cortex.md) inactivation with [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md) recordings during radial-maze behavior. Place-field locations changed during inactivation. In well-trained animals, behavioral impairment was particularly evident in darkness, when visual landmarks could not supply the same information. Crucially, changes in [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md) spatial coding also occurred without a behavioral deficit in other conditions. Remote neural reorganization and overt impairment therefore need not have a one-to-one relation.[2001, Experiments 1–2, light/dark conditions and hippocampal coding changes](../../../temporary-inactivation-of-the-retrosplenial-cortex-causes-a-transient-reorganization-of-spatial-coding-in-the-hippocampus-by-brenton-g-cooper-and-others.md)

This finding supports a causal dependence of [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md) coding on the state of retrosplenial circuitry. A plausible interpretation is that the network combines landmark and movement-related information to maintain spatial context. It remains an interpretation of the computation, not proof of a direct monosynaptic message or a complete model of human [recollection](../../../recollection.md). Inactivation can influence indirect pathways, and a spatial navigation task tests ingredients useful for episodes without itself demonstrating autonoetic recall.

These interactions can be related to specific mnemonic demands. A [hippocampal](../../../hippocampus.md) representation may distinguish the relationships within an event, while a prefrontal interaction makes the appropriate relationship relevant to the current rule or query. A retrosplenial interaction may help maintain or update the spatial frame in which that relationship is interpreted. The hypotheses predict different vulnerabilities: changing retrieval goals should particularly challenge selection, whereas removing reliable external spatial cues should increase reliance on internally updated context. They do not require every region, including all posterior parietal subdivisions, to be equally necessary on every trial.

A particularly informative next experiment would combine a controlled perturbation with simultaneous recording and a task that dissociates item recognition, spatial binding and source or sequence judgments. Preserved recognition alongside disrupted binding would narrow a general arousal or sensory explanation. Comparing matched movement and reward conditions would test behavioral confounds. A [crossed disconnection experiment](../../../crossed-disconnection-experiment.md) could examine cooperation between a specified pair if the relevant pathways and their laterality were established: the comparison must include unilateral and same-side controls rather than assuming that any two lesions diagnose an interaction.

The critical question is not simply whether performance declines. The perturbation should disrupt the predicted representation or its task-dependent use at the relevant time, and the remote change should be measured. Even this requires caution about indirect effects and compensation. Conversely, normal performance after perturbation may reflect redundant routes or the availability of external cues, rather than complete irrelevance of the region.

**The evidence supports task-dependent interactions for coordinating, contextualizing and using event information. It does not justify equating a regional activation map, a synchrony measure or one navigation impairment with a universal four-region circuit for every aspect of episodic remembering.**
