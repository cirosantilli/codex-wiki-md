# Arithmetic mean

↑ **Parent:** [Weighted arithmetic mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_mean)

For a nonempty finite list of [real numbers](real-number.md) $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) is

$$
\bar x=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i.
$$

It is the [weighted mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md) with equal weights, giving each observation equal influence. For $(2,4,9)$, the [sum](sum.md) is $15$ and the [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) is $5$. Replacing every value by $5$ retains the same total. The residuals $-3,-1,4$ sum to zero, so the mean is also the balancing point of these equally weighted observations.

The [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) minimizes the [sum](sum.md) of squared deviations by the [weighted mean minimizes squared deviations](weighted-mean-minimizes-squared-deviations.md) identity with all weights one. A large input can consequently have a substantial effect: replacing $9$ by $90$ changes the mean to $32$, while the [median](median.md) remains $4$. The choice of summary depends on what aspect of the data matters.
