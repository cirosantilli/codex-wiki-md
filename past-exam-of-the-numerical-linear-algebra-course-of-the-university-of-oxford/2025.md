## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

The [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns give $A^TA=I_n$. Thus $P=AA^T$ is the [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) onto the [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space) of $A$, and

$$
Ax-b=A(x-A^Tb)-(I_m-P)b.
$$

The two terms are [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors), so the [Pythagorean identity](../inner-product.md#pythagorean-theorem-in-an-inner-product-space) gives

$$
\|Ax-b\|_2^2=\|x-A^Tb\|_2^2+\|(I_m-AA^T)b\|_2^2.
$$

Only the first term depends on $x$. Consequently the [linear least-squares problem](../inner-product.md#linear-least-squares-problem) has the unique answer

$$
\boxed{x_*=A^Tb},\qquad \min_x\|Ax-b\|_2=\|(I_m-AA^T)b\|_2.
$$

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

For every [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) $z\in\mathbb R^n$, splitting the rows gives

$$
\|A_1z\|_2^2+\|A_2z\|_2^2=\|Az\|_2^2=z^TA^TAz=\|z\|_2^2.
$$

Both summands are nonnegative. Taking the supremum over [unit vectors](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) in the definition of the [spectral norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#matrix-2-norm) proves

$$
\boxed{\|A_1\|_2\leq1,\qquad \|A_2\|_2\leq1}.
$$

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Put $B=A_1^TA_1$. It is a [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) and a [positive semidefinite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-semidefinite-matrix), while $A_2^TA_2=I_n-B$ is also a [positive semidefinite matrix](../quadratic-form.md#positive-semidefinite-matrix). By the [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem), choose an [orthogonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) $V=[v_1,\ldots,v_n]$ such that

$$
V^TBV=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n),\qquad 0\leq\lambda_j\leq1.
$$

The bounds follow directly from $z^TBz=\|A_1z\|_2^2\in[0,\|z\|_2^2]$. Define the [diagonal matrices](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix)

$$
\Sigma_1=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\ldots,\sqrt{\lambda_n}),\qquad
\Sigma_2=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{1-\lambda_1},\ldots,\sqrt{1-\lambda_n}).
$$

The columns of $A_1V$ are mutually [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors), since $(A_1v_i)^T(A_1v_j)=\lambda_j\delta_{ij}$. For each $\lambda_j>0$, set $u_{1j}=A_1v_j/\sqrt{\lambda_j}$. These columns are [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set). At a zero $\lambda_j$, one has $A_1v_j=0$; choose $u_{1j}$ by completing the already prescribed columns to an [orthonormal set](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) of $n$ columns in $\mathbb R^k$. Such a completion exists because $k\geq n$. This gives $A_1V=U_1\Sigma_1$, including the zero columns.

Likewise, the [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix) of $A_2V$ is $\operatorname{diag}(1-\lambda_j)$. Normalize its nonzero columns and complete at its zero columns in $\mathbb R^{m-k}$, using $m-k\geq n$. The resulting $U_2$ has [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns and $A_2V=U_2\Sigma_2$. Combining the identities gives

$$
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}U_1&0\\0&U_2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_1\\\Sigma_2\end{pmatrix}V^T}.
$$

This is a form of the [cosine-sine decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#cosine-sine-decomposition). Completing the zero columns is essential: neither block was assumed to have [full column rank](../vector-space.md#full-column-rank).

<h4 id="1/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/a/iv)

Using the [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns of $U_1,U_2$ in the factorization gives

$$
I_n=A^TA=V(\Sigma_1^2+\Sigma_2^2)V^T.
$$

Multiplying by the [orthogonal matrices](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) $V^T$ and $V$ gives

$$
\boxed{\Sigma_1^2+\Sigma_2^2=I_n}.
$$

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Choose the nonnegative square roots of the [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $A^TA$ for the diagonal entries of $\Sigma$, in nonincreasing order. Its assumed [matrix inverse](../matrix-inverse.md) exists, so these entries are positive. The [eigendecomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectral-decomposition) gives

$$
U^TU=\Sigma^{-1}V^TA^TAV\Sigma^{-1}
=\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma^2\Sigma^{-1}=I_n.
$$

Hence $U$ has [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns. Also

$$
\boxed{U\Sigma V^T=AV\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma V^T=A}.
$$

Together with the [orthogonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) $V$ and the nonnegative [diagonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#diagonal-matrix) $\Sigma$, these are precisely the properties of a thin [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Let $\widetilde A=\widehat U\widehat\Sigma\widehat V^T$. Interpret the diagonal of $\widehat\Sigma$ as nonnegative computed [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) and sort them consistently. If signed diagonal entries are used instead, take their absolute values and absorb signs into the [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns. Because the two outer factors have the stated [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set), these sorted numbers are exactly $\sigma_j(\widetilde A)$.

For completeness, the [singular value perturbation bound](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-perturbation-bound) follows from the [Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem](../linear-algebra.md#eckart-young-mirsky-theorem). If $B_{j-1}$ is the truncated [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) of a [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $B$, the [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) gives

$$
\sigma_j(A)=\min_{\operatorname{rank}D\leq j-1}\|A-D\|_2
\leq\|A-B_{j-1}\|_2
\leq\|A-B\|_2+\sigma_j(B).
$$

Exchange $A$ and $B$ to get $|\sigma_j(A)-\sigma_j(B)|\leq\|A-B\|_2$. Applying this to $B=\widetilde A$ and the given [backward error](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-error) proves

$$
\boxed{|\sigma_j(A)-\widehat\sigma_j|\leq\epsilon\quad(1\leq j\leq n)}.
$$

Thus [backward stability](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-stability) of the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) gives an absolute [forward error](../numerical-analysis.md#forward-error) bound for every [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value), even a very small one.

<h4 id="1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/b/iii)

An error of order $\epsilon$ in a squared [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) can become an error of order $\sqrt\epsilon$ after taking its square root. To keep $A$ nonsingular as required, take $0<\epsilon<1/2$ and

$$
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,\epsilon),\qquad
\widehat V=I_2,\qquad
\widehat\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon}).
$$

Then $\|A\|_2=1$, and the computed [eigendecomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectral-decomposition) has exactly the permitted [backward error](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-error):

$$
\|A^TA-\widehat V\widehat\Sigma^2\widehat V^T\|_2
=\|\operatorname{diag}(0,-\epsilon)\|_2=\epsilon.
$$

Nevertheless the second computed [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) has error

$$
\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon}-\epsilon
=\sqrt\epsilon\bigl(\sqrt{1+\epsilon}-\sqrt\epsilon\bigr)
\sim\sqrt\epsilon.
$$

By the [singular value perturbation bound](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-perturbation-bound), every product with these computed [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) and exactly [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) outer factors has [spectral norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#matrix-2-norm) error at least this large. In particular, even the favorable choice $\widehat U=I_2$ gives

$$
\boxed{\|A-\widehat U\widehat\Sigma\widehat V^T\|_2
=\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon}-\epsilon\sim\sqrt\epsilon},
$$

which has no uniform $O(\epsilon)$ bound in [big O notation](../real-analysis.md#big-o-notation). Therefore [backward stability](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-stability) for the [eigendecomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectral-decomposition) of the [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix) does not imply [backward stability](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-stability) for the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition).

There is a related loss of [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) in the raw formula: evaluating $A\widehat V\widehat\Sigma^{-1}$ exactly for these perturbed factors gives $\operatorname{diag}(1,\epsilon/\sqrt{\epsilon^2+\epsilon})$, whose second column has length tending to zero. Its product with $\widehat\Sigma\widehat V^T$ equals $A$, but its diagonal factors are consequently not a [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition). The paper's idealized [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) assumption lets us isolate the unavoidable [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) error even if this additional problem is repaired.

<h4 id="1/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/b/iv)

**No uniform absolute error bound of order $\epsilon$ follows.** Take the [Jordan block](../linear-operator-theory.md#jordan-block)

$$
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
E=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\\epsilon&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Here $\|B\|_2=1$ and $\|E\|_2=\epsilon$. The exact [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $B$ are both zero, whereas

$$
\det(\lambda I_2-B-E)=\lambda^2-\epsilon,
\qquad \boxed{\lambda_\pm(B+E)=\pm\sqrt\epsilon}.
$$

These are admissible outputs under a [backward error](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-error) guarantee of size $\epsilon$, yet their [forward errors](../numerical-analysis.md#forward-error) are $\sqrt\epsilon$. This is the [square-root splitting of a defective double eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#square-root-splitting-of-a-defective-double-eigenvalue). More generally, [eigenvalue sensitivity](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue-sensitivity) depends on the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix); [backward stability](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-stability) alone does not control it. The [singular value perturbation bound](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-perturbation-bound) used in part (ii) has no equally uniform counterpart for the [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of arbitrary nonsymmetric [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2025](2025.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

By the [Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem](../linear-algebra.md#eckart-young-mirsky-theorem), truncate the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition):

$$
\boxed{A_r=\sum_{j=1}^r\sigma_j u_jv_j^T},\qquad
\boxed{\|A-A_r\|_2=\sigma_{r+1}},\qquad
\boxed{\|A-A_r\|_F=\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^n\sigma_j^2\right)^{1/2}}.
$$

The same $A_r$ minimizes the [spectral norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#matrix-2-norm) and [Frobenius norm](../compact-operator.md#frobenius-norm) errors over [matrices](../vector-space.md#matrix) of [matrix rank](../vector-space.md#matrix-rank) at most $r$. This conventional meaning of rank-$r$ [low-rank approximation](../linear-algebra.md#low-rank-approximation) includes the case $\operatorname{rank}A<r$, when $A_r=A$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Choose the first $r$ [left singular vectors](../linear-algebra.md#left-singular-vector):

$$
\boxed{Q_*=[u_1,\ldots,u_r]}.
$$

Their [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) gives $Q_*^TQ_*=I_r$. The [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) $Q_*Q_*^T$ fixes $u_j$ for $j\leq r$ and annihilates $u_j$ for $j>r$. Applying it to the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) therefore gives

$$
Q_*Q_*^TA=\sum_{j=1}^r\sigma_j u_jv_j^T=A_r.
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Put $P=QQ^T$. Since $Q$ has $r$ columns, the [rank bound for a matrix product](../vector-space.md#rank-bound-for-a-matrix-product) gives $\operatorname{rank}(PA)\leq r$. The [Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem](../linear-algebra.md#eckart-young-mirsky-theorem) and part (a) then give

$$
\boxed{\|PA-A\|_2\geq\sigma_{r+1}=\|Q_*Q_*^TA-A\|_2}.
$$

One can also see the lower bound directly. The restriction of $PA$ to the $(r+1)$-dimensional [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) of $v_1,\ldots,v_{r+1}$ has a nonzero [null space](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), because its [matrix rank](../vector-space.md#matrix-rank) is at most $r$. For a [unit vector](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) $z$ in that [null space](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), $\|(A-PA)z\|_2=\|Az\|_2\geq\sigma_{r+1}$ by the [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

The [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) $P=QQ^T$ gives

$$
QM-A=Q(M-Q^TA)-(I_m-P)A.
$$

The first summand has every column in the [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space) of $Q$, and the second has every column in its [orthogonal complement](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-complement). They therefore have zero [Frobenius inner product](../inner-product.md#frobenius-inner-product). The [Pythagorean identity](../inner-product.md#pythagorean-theorem-in-an-inner-product-space), together with $Q^TQ=I_r$, gives the stronger exact formula

$$
\|QM-A\|_F^2=\|M-Q^TA\|_F^2+\|(I_m-P)A\|_F^2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\|QQ^TA-A\|_F\leq\|QM-A\|_F},
$$

with equality exactly when $M=Q^TA$. Thus solving the [linear least-squares problem](../inner-product.md#linear-least-squares-problem) for each column chooses the best coefficients in the prescribed [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space).

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

The thin [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition) gives $AX=QR$. Its columns already belong to the [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space) of $Q$, so the [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) $QQ^T$ leaves them fixed:

$$
\boxed{\widehat AX=QQ^TAX=QQ^TQR=QR=AX}.
$$

This identity uses only $AX=QR$ and $Q^TQ=I_r$; it remains valid if $R$ is singular, provided such a factorization with [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) $Q$ is used.

<h4 id="2/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/b/iv)

Because $r<n$, choose a [unit vector](../vector-space.md#unit-vector) $v$ in the [null space](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of $X^T$, so that $v^TX=0$. Let $J\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ have [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns, for example the first $n$ columns of $I_m$, and choose a real number $\alpha>1$. Set

$$
\boxed{A=J\bigl(I_n+(\alpha-1)vv^T\bigr)}.
$$

Its [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) along $v$ is $\alpha$, and its other [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) are $1$. Thus $\sigma_1(A)=\alpha>1=\sigma_2(A)$. Meanwhile $AX=JX$. If $X$ has [full column rank](../vector-space.md#full-column-rank), the [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space) of the thin [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition) is exactly $\operatorname{range}(JX)$, which is [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) to $Jv$. Hence $PJv=0$ for $P=QQ^T$, and

$$
\|(I_m-P)A\|_2\geq\|(I_m-P)Av\|_2=\|\alpha Jv\|_2=\alpha.
$$

Conversely, an [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) is a contraction in the [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm), so $\|(I_m-P)A\|_2\leq\|A\|_2=\alpha$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\|A-\widehat A\|_2=\sigma_1(A)=\alpha>\sigma_2(A)=1}.
$$

If $X$ is rank deficient, complete the [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition) within $J(v^\perp)$: this space has dimension $n-1\geq r$ and contains all columns of $JX$, so the required [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) completion exists there. The same proof applies. For rank-deficient data the choice of unused columns of $Q$ matters; the construction specifies a valid choice. If $Q$ instead contains only a [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) of $\operatorname{range}(AX)$, the conclusion holds without any completion. A fixed sampling [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) can thus miss the unique largest [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) completely.

<h4 id="2/b/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#2/b/v)

Write $\ell=r/2$; the requested expression requires the positive even integer $r\geq2$. Split the thin [singular value decomposition](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value-decomposition) after $\ell$ columns:

$$
A=U_aD_aV_a^T+U_bD_bV_b^T,\qquad
\Omega_a=V_a^TX\in\mathbb R^{\ell\times r},\qquad
\Omega_b=V_b^TX\in\mathbb R^{(n-\ell)\times r}.
$$

Here $\|D_b\|_2=\sigma_{\ell+1}=\|A-A_\ell\|_2$. Each column of a [Gaussian random matrix](../probability-theory.md#gaussian-random-matrix) is an [orthogonally invariant random vector](../characteristic-function.md#orthogonally-invariant-random-vector): its [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) is proportional to $\exp(-\|z\|_2^2/2)$, and an [orthogonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) preserves both $\|z\|_2$ and volume. Thus $V^TX$, where $V=[V_a,V_b]$, again has [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) [standard normal](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution) entries. In particular both displayed blocks have the Gaussian distributions needed below.

First prove a [deterministic error bound for a randomized range finder](../linear-algebra.md#deterministic-error-bound-for-a-randomized-range-finder). Put $P=QQ^T$ and suppose $\Omega_a$ has [full row rank](../vector-space.md#full-row-rank). Its [Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse](../matrix-inverse.md#moore-penrose-inverse) is $\Omega_a^\dagger=\Omega_a^T(\Omega_a\Omega_a^T)^{-1}$, so $\Omega_a\Omega_a^\dagger=I_\ell$. The [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition) implies $(I_m-P)AX=0$, hence

$$
(I_m-P)U_aD_a\Omega_a=-(I_m-P)U_bD_b\Omega_b.
$$

Multiplication by $\Omega_a^\dagger$ expresses the missed leading columns in terms of the trailing columns. Substitution into $(I_m-P)A$ gives the exact identity

$$
(I_m-P)A=(I_m-P)U_bD_b
\begin{pmatrix}-\Omega_b\Omega_a^\dagger&I_{n-\ell}\end{pmatrix}V^T.
$$

For any [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $F$, the [spectral norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#matrix-2-norm) of $[-F\ I]$ is $\sqrt{1+\|F\|_2^2}$, because its product with its [transpose](../vector-space.md#transpose) is $FF^T+I$. Using [submultiplicativity of the operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#submultiplicativity-of-the-operator-norm), the [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) of $U_b$, and the contraction property of the [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection), we obtain

$$
\boxed{\|A-PA\|_2\leq\sigma_{\ell+1}
\sqrt{1+\|\Omega_b\Omega_a^\dagger\|_2^2}}.
$$

This estimate is deterministic and also covers rank-deficient $A$; if $\sigma_{\ell+1}=0$, its right-hand side vanishes.

Now use the paper's permitted [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) estimates. Apply the lower estimate to the tall [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $\Omega_a^T$ of size $r\times\ell$, rather than to its wide [transpose](../vector-space.md#transpose). It gives

$$
\sigma_{\min}(\Omega_a)\geq\sqrt r-\sqrt\ell
=\sqrt r(1-1/\sqrt2)>0,\qquad
\|\Omega_a^\dagger\|_2\leq\frac1{\sqrt r-\sqrt\ell}.
$$

The upper estimate for $\Omega_b$, transposing if necessary, gives

$$
\|\Omega_b\|_2\leq\sqrt{n-\ell}+\sqrt r\leq2\sqrt n.
$$

Thus, with $K=2/(1-1/\sqrt2)$,

$$
\|\Omega_b\Omega_a^\dagger\|_2\leq K\sqrt{n/r}.
$$

Since $n/r>1$, the deterministic estimate yields

$$
\boxed{\|A-\widehat A\|_2
\leq C\sqrt{\frac nr}\,\|A-A_{r/2}\|_2},\qquad C=\sqrt{1+K^2}.
$$

This proves the requested conclusion under the explicitly authorized estimates.

**The probabilistic qualification is necessary.** A [Gaussian random matrix](../probability-theory.md#gaussian-random-matrix) does not always have all its [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) between the two endpoints printed in the hint. Already a $1\times1$ example would require $|Z|\leq2$ for every [standard normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-random-variable) $Z$, which fails with positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability). The proof above holds on the event where the two block estimates hold. In a probabilistic formulation one supplies estimates for that event, usually allowing a positive margin in both endpoints; no failure probability is specified by the paper.

Nor can the requested inequality with one universal $C$ hold almost surely for every $A$. For example, take $n=m=3$, $r=2$, $A=\operatorname{diag}(L,1,1)$ with $L>1$, and a sampling [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix)

$$
X_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

At $X_0$, the sampled [column space](../vector-space.md#column-space) misses the first coordinate, so $\|A-\widehat A\|_2=L$, whereas $\|A-A_1\|_2=1$. Choose $L>C\sqrt{3/2}$. Because $AX_0$ has [full column rank](../vector-space.md#full-column-rank), its [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) depends continuously on $X$ near $X_0$ through $AX(X^TA^TAX)^{-1}X^TA^T$. The strict violation persists in an open neighborhood of $X_0$. The joint Gaussian [probability density function](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#probability-density-function) is positive there, so that neighborhood has positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability). This identifies the missing qualification without weakening the conditional calculation.

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Write $x=Q_ky$ and $\beta=\|b\|_2$. The [Arnoldi decomposition](../gradient-descent.md#arnoldi-decomposition) and $b=\beta Q_{k+1}e_1$ give

$$
Ax-b=Q_{k+1}(\widetilde H_ky-\beta e_1).
$$

Because $Q_{k+1}$ has [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns, it preserves the [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm). The [generalized minimal residual method](../gradient-descent.md#generalized-minimal-residual-method) therefore reduces to the small [linear least-squares problem](../inner-product.md#linear-least-squares-problem)

$$
\boxed{y_k=\underset{y\in\mathbb R^k}{\operatorname{argmin}}
\|\widetilde H_ky-\beta e_1\|_2,\qquad x_k=Q_ky_k}.
$$

Use [Givens rotations](../numerical-analysis.md#givens-rotation) on the [upper Hessenberg matrix](../vector-space.md#upper-hessenberg-matrix) to obtain a [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition)

$$
Z^T\widetilde H_k=\begin{pmatrix}R_k\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
g=Z^T(\beta e_1),
$$

where $Z$ is an [orthogonal matrix](../linear-algebra.md#orthogonal-matrix) and $R_k$ is an [upper triangular matrix](../linear-algebra.md#upper-triangular-matrix). Then solve $R_ky_k=g_{1:k}$ by [backward substitution in a triangular system](../linear-algebra.md#backward-substitution-in-a-triangular-system) and form $Q_ky_k$. The resulting minimum [linear-system residual](../gradient-descent.md#linear-system-residual) has [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm) $|g_{k+1}|$.

The small problem has a unique answer: if $\widetilde H_ky=0$, the [Arnoldi decomposition](../gradient-descent.md#arnoldi-decomposition) gives $AQ_ky=0$. The [invertible matrix](../linear-algebra.md#invertible-matrix) $A$ implies $Q_ky=0$, and the [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) of $Q_k$ then gives $y=0$. Thus $\widetilde H_k$ has [full column rank](../vector-space.md#full-column-rank) and $R_k$ is nonsingular.

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

For the small [QR decomposition](../inner-product.md#qr-decomposition), each of the $k$ [Givens rotations](../numerical-analysis.md#givens-rotation) eliminates one subdiagonal entry of the [upper Hessenberg matrix](../vector-space.md#upper-hessenberg-matrix). At column $j$, apply the rotation to two rows in columns $j,\ldots,k$, costing $O(k-j+1)$ arithmetic operations. Summing gives $O(k^2)$, and applying the rotations to the right-hand side costs $O(k)$. [Backward substitution in a triangular system](../linear-algebra.md#backward-substitution-in-a-triangular-system) costs $O(k^2)$.

Computing $\|b\|_2$ costs $O(n)$ and the final dense [matrix multiplication](../vector-space.md#matrix-multiplication) $Q_ky_k$ costs $O(nk)$. Thus the total, with the [Arnoldi decomposition](../gradient-descent.md#arnoldi-decomposition) already supplied, is

$$
O(k^2+nk+n)=\boxed{O(nk)},\qquad \boxed{s=1,\quad t=1}.
$$

Here $1\leq k<n$, since $Q_{k+1}$ has [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) columns in $\mathbb R^n$. In the standard dense arithmetic model, forming the general [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix)–[vector](../vector-space.md#vector) product gives the sharp $\Theta(nk)$ cost. The pair above records that two-parameter bound; looser bounds such as $O(n^2)$ are also possible because $k<n$. Treating $\widetilde H_k$ as an unstructured dense [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) would unnecessarily replace its $O(k^2)$ factorization by an $O(k^3)$ computation.

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

**The claim is true**, with

$$
\boxed{M=A^TA}.
$$

It is a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix): for every nonzero [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) $z$, the fact that $A$ is an [invertible matrix](../linear-algebra.md#invertible-matrix) gives $z^TMz=\|Az\|_2^2>0$. Since $Ax_*=b$, every candidate $x$ satisfies

$$
\|Ax-b\|_2^2=\|A(x-x_*)\|_2^2
=(x-x_*)^TA^TA(x-x_*)=\|x-x_*\|_M^2.
$$

The [generalized minimal residual method](../gradient-descent.md#generalized-minimal-residual-method) therefore minimizes precisely this [weighted norm](../inner-product.md#weighted-norm) of the error over the [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) of $Q_k$. The same [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $M$ works for every $k$ and does not depend on $b$.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Multiplying the [Arnoldi decomposition](../gradient-descent.md#arnoldi-decomposition) by $Q_k^T$ shows that the leading square block is

$$
H_k=Q_k^TAQ_k.
$$

It is an [upper Hessenberg matrix](../vector-space.md#upper-hessenberg-matrix) by the [Arnoldi iteration](../gradient-descent.md#arnoldi-iteration). Because $A=A^T$, it is also a [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix). Entries below the first subdiagonal vanish by the Hessenberg structure, and their reflected entries above the first superdiagonal vanish by symmetry. Consequently

$$
\boxed{T_k=Q_k^TAQ_k\text{ is symmetric tridiagonal}},\qquad
\boxed{AQ_k=Q_{k+1}\widetilde T_k}.
$$

The extra last row has only its final entry possibly nonzero. With the usual choice of positive normalization factors, the individual column relations are the three-term [Lanczos algorithm](../gradient-descent.md#lanczos-algorithm) recurrence

$$
Aq_j=\beta_{j-1}q_{j-1}+\alpha_jq_j+\beta_jq_{j+1},
\qquad \alpha_j=q_j^TAq_j,\quad \beta_j>0,
$$

where the first term is omitted for $j=1$. This reduction is an exact-arithmetic statement; [round-off error](../numerical-analysis.md#round-off-error) can affect the [orthogonality](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) in an implementation.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

For any nonzero [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) $y\in\mathbb R^k$, [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) gives $\|Q_ky\|_2=\|y\|_2>0$. Since $A$ is a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix),

$$
\boxed{y^TT_ky=(Q_ky)^TA(Q_ky)>0}.
$$

The preceding part already gives symmetry, so $T_k$ is a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix). In fact the [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem) gives the useful bounds

$$
\lambda_{\min}(A)\|y\|_2^2
\leq y^TT_ky\leq\lambda_{\max}(A)\|y\|_2^2,
$$

which place its [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) between the smallest and largest [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $A$.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

The [least-squares normal equations](../linear-regression.md#normal-equations-for-linear-least-squares) are

$$
\boxed{Hx=c,\qquad H=G^TG,\quad c=G^Tb}.
$$

The [full column rank](../vector-space.md#full-column-rank) of $G$ gives $z^THz=\|Gz\|_2^2>0$ for nonzero $z$, so $H$ is a [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix). Apply the [Conjugate gradient method](../gradient-descent.md#conjugate-gradient-method) to this [linear system](../linear-algebra.md#system-of-linear-equations). To verify that its solution is the desired one, let $x_*$ solve the displayed equations. Then $G^T(Gx_*-b)=0$, and for any $x$ the [Pythagorean identity](../inner-product.md#pythagorean-theorem-in-an-inner-product-space) gives

$$
\|Gx-b\|_2^2=\|Gx_*-b\|_2^2+\|G(x-x_*)\|_2^2.
$$

Thus $x_*$ is the unique minimizer of the [linear least-squares problem](../inner-product.md#linear-least-squares-problem).

One can apply $H$ without forming the [Gram matrix](../inner-product.md#gram-matrix): compute $Hp=G^T(Gp)$. More explicitly, initialize $x_0$, $r_0=c-Hx_0$, $p_0=r_0$; until the [linear-system residual](../gradient-descent.md#linear-system-residual) vanishes, use the [Conjugate gradient method](../gradient-descent.md#conjugate-gradient-method) recurrence

$$
\alpha_j=\frac{r_j^Tr_j}{p_j^THp_j},\quad
x_{j+1}=x_j+\alpha_jp_j,\quad
r_{j+1}=r_j-\alpha_jHp_j,
$$



$$
\beta_j=\frac{r_{j+1}^Tr_{j+1}}{r_j^Tr_j},\qquad
p_{j+1}=r_{j+1}+\beta_jp_j.
$$

Each dense application of $H$ this way costs $O(mn)$ operations. The [condition number](../quadratic-form.md#condition-number) satisfies $\kappa_2(H)=\kappa_2(G)^2$, where $\kappa_2(G)=\sigma_{\max}(G)/\sigma_{\min}(G)$; this is the conditioning cost of the [least-squares normal equations](../linear-regression.md#normal-equations-for-linear-least-squares).

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

Under the permitted [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) bounds, $m=4n$ gives

$$
\sqrt n\leq\sigma_{\min}(G)\leq\sigma_{\max}(G)\leq3\sqrt n.
$$

For the [symmetric positive-definite matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-positive-definite-matrix) $H=G^TG$ in part (iii), its [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are the squared [singular values](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) of $G$, so

$$
n\leq\lambda_{\min}(H)\leq\lambda_{\max}(H)\leq9n,
\qquad \boxed{\kappa_2(H)\leq9}.
$$

Apply the supplied [conjugate gradient convergence bound](../gradient-descent.md#conjugate-gradient-convergence-bound) to $H$. For $e_k=x_k-x_*$ and nonzero $e_0$, the function $(\sqrt\kappa-1)/(\sqrt\kappa+1)$ increases with $\kappa\geq1$, giving

$$
\boxed{\frac{\|e_k\|_H}{\|e_0\|_H}\leq2\left(\frac{3-1}{3+1}\right)^k
=2^{1-k}}.
$$

Thus the error in this [weighted norm](../inner-product.md#weighted-norm) contracts at a guaranteed exponential factor of at most $1/2$ per iteration, up to the prefactor $2$. Here $\|e\|_H=\|Ge\|_2$. The [Pythagorean identity](../inner-product.md#pythagorean-theorem-in-an-inner-product-space) in part (iii) also identifies $\|e_k\|_H^2$ with the excess squared [linear least-squares problem](../inner-product.md#linear-least-squares-problem) objective, which consequently decreases by a bound proportional to $4^{-k}$. For the [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm), the same [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) bounds give $\|e_k\|_2/\|e_0\|_2\leq6\,2^{-k}$.

For relative accuracy $0<\epsilon<1$ in the $H$-[weighted norm](../inner-product.md#weighted-norm), it is sufficient to take

$$
k\geq\left\lceil\log_2\frac2\epsilon\right\rceil.
$$

Compute $c=G^Tb$ once in $O(mn)$ operations. Each iteration applies $p\mapsto G^T(Gp)$ with two dense [matrix multiplications](../vector-space.md#matrix-multiplication) and uses $O(n)$ further arithmetic operations; when $m=4n$ this costs $O(n^2)$. Hence the total cost is

$$
\boxed{O\!\left(n^2\log\frac2\epsilon\right)}.
$$

Using [finite termination of the conjugate gradient method](../gradient-descent.md#finite-termination-of-the-conjugate-gradient-method) in exact arithmetic refines this to $O(n^2\min\{n,\lceil\log_2(2/\epsilon)\rceil\})$. Explicitly constructing $G^TG$ first would add $O(mn^2)=O(n^3)$ setup work and is unnecessary. These accuracy estimates concern relative error; an absolute tolerance replaces $2/\epsilon$ by $2\|e_0\|_H/\epsilon$ when $\|e_0\|_H>\epsilon$. If $e_0=0$, the initial guess already solves the problem.

As in part 2(b)(v), the Gaussian assumption alone does not make the printed [singular value](../linear-algebra.md#singular-value) interval a certainty. For example, the event that every entry of $G$ has absolute value below a sufficiently small positive number has positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) and forces $\|G\|_F<\sqrt n$, contradicting the asserted lower endpoint. The displayed rate is therefore **conditional on the singular-value bounds authorized by the paper**. The cost and convergence derivation are exact under that condition; an unconditional probabilistic statement would need an additional failure-probability estimate.
