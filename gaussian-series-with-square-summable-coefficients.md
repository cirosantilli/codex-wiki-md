# Gaussian series with square-summable coefficients

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian random variable](gaussian-random-variable.md)

For independent standard Gaussian $X_j$ and $\sum\alpha_j^2<\infty$, the partial sums are Cauchy in [L2 space](l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md), since a tail's squared norm is the corresponding sum of coefficient squares. Completeness gives an $L^2$ limit. The finite-sum characteristic functions converge to $\exp[-t^2\sum\alpha_j^2/2]$, identifying the limit as Gaussian, including the zero-variance case. The identification needs convergence of distributions, not just agreement of the first two moments.
