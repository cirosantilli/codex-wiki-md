# Big omega notation

↑ **Parent:** [Big O notation](big-o-notation.md)

For real-valued [functions](function-split.md) on the natural numbers, $f(n)=\Omega(g(n))$ means that constants $c>0$ and $N$ satisfy $|f(n)|\geq c|g(n)|$ for every $n\geq N$. This is an eventual [lower bound](lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) on magnitude. In algorithm analysis the [functions](function-split.md) are usually nonnegative, so the [absolute values](absolute-value.md) can be omitted. Together with the [upper bound](upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of [big O notation](big-o-notation.md), it gives [big theta notation](big-theta-notation.md). This is the algorithmic lower-bound convention for $\Omega$, rather than a convention expressing only a positive [limit superior](limit-superior.md) along a [subsequence](subsequence.md).
