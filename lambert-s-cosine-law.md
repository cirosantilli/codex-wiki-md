<h1 id="lambert-s-cosine-law">Lambert's cosine law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert's_cosine_law)

For an ideal diffuse emitting or reflecting planar surface with uniform direction-independent [radiance](radiance.md), the [radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md) in a direction at [angle](angle.md) $\theta$ to its [unit normal](unit-normal.md) is $I(\theta)=I(0)\cos\theta$. This follows immediately from $I=LA\cos\theta$ and the [projected area](projected-area.md) formula. The worked [radiance](radiance.md) example and diagram show the $0^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ cases. [Lambertian reflectance](lambertian-reflectance.md) supplies one physical idealization realizing the law; an ideal [blackbody](blackbody.md) provides an emitting example.

The historical setting is Johann Heinrich Lambert's _Photometria sive de mensura et gradibus luminis, colorum et umbrae_, published in Augsburg in 1760. Its Part I, §139, explicitly compares normal and oblique illumination using the sine of the source's elevation, equivalently the [cosine](cosine.md) of its [angle](angle.md) from the receiving [normal vector](normal-vector.md). This is an early receiving-surface projection calculation; the modern outgoing-patch statement above keeps [radiance](radiance.md), [radiant intensity](radiant-intensity.md) and [irradiance](irradiance.md) distinct.
