# Cantor distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Continuous probability distribution](continuous-probability-distribution-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_distribution)

The [Cantor distribution](cantor-distribution.md) is the [law of a random variable](probability-distribution-split.md)

$$
X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2B_n}{3^n},
$$

where the $B_n$ are [independent](independent-random-variables.md) [Bernoulli random variables](bernoulli-distribution.md) taking values zero and one with equal probability. Its values lie in the [Cantor set](cantor-set.md), and its [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) is the [Cantor function](cantor-function.md). Every individual value has probability zero, but the [Cantor set](cantor-set.md) has probability one despite having zero [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md). A [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) on that set would integrate to zero, so it cannot describe this law. The example motivates the distinction between a [continuous probability distribution](continuous-probability-distribution-split.md) and an [absolutely continuous probability distribution](absolutely-continuous-probability-distribution.md).
