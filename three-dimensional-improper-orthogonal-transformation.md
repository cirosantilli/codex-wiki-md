# Three-dimensional improper orthogonal transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Improper orthogonal transformation](improper-orthogonal-transformation.md)

A real [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md) $T$ of size three with $\det T=-1$ has an [eigenvector](eigenvector.md) $n$ of [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $-1$. Its [orthogonal complement](orthogonal-complement.md) is invariant, and the restriction there is an orientation-preserving plane rotation. Thus $T$ is a rotation about the axis $\mathbb Rn$ composed with a [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) across the plane perpendicular to $n$. Writing the plane angle as $\phi$, $\operatorname{tr}T=-1+2\cos\phi$. A pure plane [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) is the case $\phi=0$; if $\det(T-I)\ne0$, it cannot be a pure plane [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md).
