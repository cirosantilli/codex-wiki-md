<h1 id="a10/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md) $D(E)$ counts available one-electron states: $D(E)\,dE$ is the number with energies in $[E,E+dE]$. It describes the spectrum, irrespective of whether those states contain electrons.

The [density of occupied states](../../../../../../density-of-occupied-states.md) additionally weights each state by the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md):

$$
\boxed{Z(E)=D(E)f(E)}.
$$

Thus $Z(E)\,dE$ is the mean number of electrons in that energy interval, and $N_e=\int Z(E)\,dE$. State and electron densities must use the same normalization, including whether they are extensive or per volume and whether $D$ already includes [spin degeneracy](../../../../../../spin-degeneracy.md). The symbol $Z(E)$ here denotes occupied density, not a canonical [partition function](../../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md).
