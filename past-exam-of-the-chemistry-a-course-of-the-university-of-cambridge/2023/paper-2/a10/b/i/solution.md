<h1 id="a10/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[Arsenic](../../../../../../arsenic.md) has five valence electrons, whereas [silicon](../../../../../../silicon.md) has four. A substitutional arsenic atom can therefore supply an extra electron after participating in the silicon bonding network. It acts as a shallow [donor impurity](../../../../../../donor-impurity.md), introducing a localized level $E_D$ just below the [conduction band](../../../../../../conduction-band.md). Ionizing that donor costs much less energy than excitation across the full [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md).

At the lowest temperatures, donor electrons remain bound and the [valence band](../../../../../../valence-band.md) is filled. Mobile-carrier populations are negligible: this is [dopant freeze-out](../../../../../../dopant-freeze-out.md). Initial heating releases donor electrons into the [conduction band](../../../../../../conduction-band.md), producing a rapid rise in conductivity governed by the shallow-donor energy scale. The positive ionized donor charge compensates the released electronic charge; the bulk need not acquire net charge.

At intermediate temperatures, nearly all donors are ionized but intrinsic electron–hole generation is still small. In an uncompensated sample,

$$
n\simeq N_D,\qquad p\ll n,\qquad \sigma\simeq eN_Du_e,
$$

where $N_D$ is donor number density. The supply of donor electrons is finite, so further heating cannot keep creating them. This is the **extrinsic plateau** under the approximation of weakly varying mobility, and its [carrier density](../../../../../../carrier-density.md) is much larger than that of the undoped sample at the same temperature. In real material, scattering changes mobility, so donor exhaustion alone predicts a carrier-density plateau rather than guaranteeing perfectly flat conductivity.

At higher temperatures, intrinsic generation becomes comparable to and then exceeds the donor contribution. Once $n_i\gg N_D$, the populations approach $n\simeq p\simeq n_i$, and activation across the main gap causes the steeper rise. The relevant regimes are therefore **freeze-out, donor exhaustion, and intrinsic excitation**, not three successive types of dopant.
