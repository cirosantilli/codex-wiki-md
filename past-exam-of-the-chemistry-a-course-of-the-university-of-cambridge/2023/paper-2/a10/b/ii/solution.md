<h1 id="a10/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The ambient-temperature [Fermi level](../../../../../../fermi-level.md) is raised into the upper part of the gap, toward the conduction-band edge, relative to intrinsic silicon.** The sample is an [n-type semiconductor](../../../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md): ionized donors supply many more [conduction electrons](../../../../../../conduction-electron.md) than the intrinsic population.

For a nondegenerate, substantially ionized sample with negligible acceptors, $n\simeq N_D$. With [effective density of states](../../../../../../effective-density-of-states.md) $N_c$, the conduction-band relation gives

$$
n=N_c e^{-(E_c-\mu)/(k_BT)},
\qquad
\boxed{\mu\simeq E_c-k_BT\ln\frac{N_c}{N_D}}.
$$

Because a few ppm normally gives $N_D\ll N_c$ at ambient temperature, the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) remains below the conduction-band edge rather than entering it as in a strongly [degenerate semiconductor](../../../../../../degenerate-semiconductor.md). Its precise position requires the dopant density, [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md) and degree of ionization; the broad qualitative shift is fixed by donor doping.

It is **not necessary for $\mu$ to lie between the donor level and the conduction edge**. In a substantially ionized nondegenerate sample, the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) can lie below the shallow donor level, as in the illustrative diagram. Donor ionization is determined by the level's occupation relative to $\mu$; a finite-temperature Fermi level is not the highest filled one-electron level of every individual configuration.
