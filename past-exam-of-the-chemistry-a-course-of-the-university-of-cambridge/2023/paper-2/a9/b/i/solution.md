<h1 id="a9/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For direct-lattice points $x_n=na$, a [lattice-periodic plane wave](../../../../../../lattice-periodic-plane-wave.md) must have the same phase at every translated lattice point:

$$
e^{iG(x+a)}=e^{iGx},\qquad e^{iGa}=1.
$$

Thus $Ga=2\pi m$, with integer $m$, and the [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) is

$$
\boxed{G_m=\frac{2\pi m}{a},\qquad m\in\mathbb Z}.
$$

Its spacing is $2\pi/a$. These plane waves execute an integer number of complete cycles within a direct-lattice period, so their wavelength magnitudes are $a/|m|$ for $m\ne0$.

A general electronic Bloch wave need not itself be lattice-periodic: wavevectors differing by $G_m$ have the same translation phase. The [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) labels that equivalence, while a large finite specimen with [periodic boundary conditions](../../../../../../periodic-boundary-conditions.md) has a much finer grid of allowed electronic wavevectors.
