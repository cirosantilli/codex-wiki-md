<h1 id="a9/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With the chosen basis, let $\mathbf b_1=(u,v)$. The [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) conditions give

$$
a(u+v)=2\pi,\qquad a(-u+v)=0,
$$

so $u=v=\pi/a$. Similarly, $\mathbf a_1\cdot\mathbf b_2=0$ and $\mathbf a_2\cdot\mathbf b_2=2\pi$ give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf b_1=\frac{\pi}{a}(1,1),\qquad
\mathbf b_2=\frac{\pi}{a}(-1,1)}.
$$

All reciprocal points are

$$
\mathbf G=m\mathbf b_1+n\mathbf b_2
=\frac{\pi}{a}(m-n,m+n),\qquad m,n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Thus, in coordinates measured in $\pi/a$, the two integer coordinates have the same parity. The [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) is another rotated [square Bravais lattice](../../../../../../square-bravais-lattice.md), with nearest-neighbor spacing $\sqrt2\pi/a$.
