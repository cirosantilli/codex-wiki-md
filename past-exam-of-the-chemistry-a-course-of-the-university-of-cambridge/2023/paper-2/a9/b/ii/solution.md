<h1 id="a9/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Construct [Bragg planes](../../../../../../bragg-plane.md) by taking the midpoints between the reciprocal origin and the other [reciprocal lattice](../../../../../../reciprocal-lattice.md) points. In one dimension these are points at $k=m\pi/a$, for nonzero integer $m$.

The **[first Brillouin zone](../../../../../../first-brillouin-zone.md)** is the interval bounded by the nearest two such points:

$$
\boxed{-\frac{\pi}{a}\leq k\leq\frac{\pi}{a}}.
$$

The **[second Brillouin zone](../../../../../../second-brillouin-zone.md)** consists of the next intervals on both sides:

$$
\boxed{-\frac{2\pi}{a}\leq k\leq-\frac{\pi}{a}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{\pi}{a}\leq k\leq\frac{2\pi}{a}}.
$$

Boundary points may be assigned to one adjacent zone to avoid double counting. The first zone is reached from the origin without crossing a Bragg point; the second is reached after crossing one. Each zone has total length $2\pi/a$. In particular, the two separated pieces of the second zone are not the entire translated first-zone interval centered at $2\pi/a$.
