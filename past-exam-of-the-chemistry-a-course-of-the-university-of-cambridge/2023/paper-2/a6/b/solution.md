<h1 id="a6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md), [adsorption](../../../../../adsorption.md) and [desorption](../../../../../desorption.md) balance:

$$
(1-\theta_1)Mp k_a=\theta_1Mk_d.
$$

Canceling $M$ and rearranging gives

$$
\frac{\theta_1}{1-\theta_1}=\frac{k_a}{k_d}p=K_{\rm ads}p,
\qquad
\boxed{\theta_1=\frac{K_{\rm ads}p}{1+K_{\rm ads}p}}.
$$

Thus **$K_{\rm ads}=b$** when the kinetic and thermodynamic descriptions refer to the same ideal surface and energy convention. In particular,

$$
\frac{k_a}{k_d}=\frac{\Lambda^3}{k_BT}
 e^{(\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_s)/(k_BT)}.
$$

The ratio fixes the [adsorption isotherm](../../../../../adsorption-isotherm.md), while the individual [rate constants](../../../../../rate-constant.md) also determine relaxation toward equilibrium. Here $k_a$ has units of inverse pressure per time and $k_d$ of inverse time, so $K_{\rm ads}$ is dimensional. This equivalent-site arrival/departure argument is the simple case of the [Langmuir adsorption model](../../../../../langmuir-adsorption-model.md).[theoretical part, Case I, pp. 1368–1370: equivalent elementary surface spaces and equilibrium adsorption](../../../../../the-adsorption-of-gases-on-plane-surfaces-of-glass-mica-and-platinum-by-irving-langmuir.md)
