<h1 id="a8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md), $\mu_{\rm AB}=\mu_{\rm A}+\mu_{\rm B}$. Using the number-concentration form gives

$$
0=\Delta\varepsilon_0-k_BT\ln\left[
\frac{f_{\rm A}f_{\rm B}}{f_{\rm AB}}
\frac{c_{\rm AB}}{c_{\rm A}c_{\rm B}}\right],
\qquad
\Delta\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon^{0,\rm A}+\varepsilon^{0,\rm B}-\varepsilon^{0,\rm AB}.
$$

Here $\Delta\varepsilon_0$ is the reaction energy between the chosen molecular reference states. Rearranging,

$$
\frac{c_{\rm A}c_{\rm B}}{c_{\rm AB}}
=\frac{f_{\rm A}f_{\rm B}}{f_{\rm AB}}e^{-\Delta\varepsilon_0/(k_BT)}.
$$

Define the [dimensionless concentration equilibrium constant](../../../../../dimensionless-concentration-equilibrium-constant.md) by

$$
K_c=\frac{(c_{\rm A}/c^\circ)(c_{\rm B}/c^\circ)}{c_{\rm AB}/c^\circ}
=\frac{c_{\rm A}c_{\rm B}}{c_{\rm AB}c^\circ}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{K_c=\frac{f_{\rm A}f_{\rm B}}{f_{\rm AB}}
\frac1{c^\circ}e^{-\Delta\varepsilon_0/(k_BT)}}.
$$

The [standard concentration](../../../../../standard-concentration.md) $c^\circ$ has the same units as $c_i$. It converts concentration ratios to ideal [thermodynamic activities](../../../../../thermodynamic-activity.md); changing it changes the numerical dimensionless $K_c$ for this dissociation, which creates an extra particle. The unnormalized quotient $c_{\rm A}c_{\rm B}/c_{\rm AB}$ instead has concentration units.[entries equilibrium constant and standard equilibrium constant: distinguish concentration quotients from the thermodynamic dimensionless standard constant](../../../../../iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md)
