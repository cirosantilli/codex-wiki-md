<h1 id="a8/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

First construct the energy cost for [heterolysis](../../../../../../heterolysis.md) from the molecular potential minimum. Dissociation to neutral atoms costs $D_e$, ionizing [caesium](../../../../../../caesium.md) costs $I$, and attachment of the electron to [iodine](../../../../../../iodine.md) releases EA. Therefore

$$
\Delta\varepsilon_{\rm min}=D_e+I-\mathrm{EA}=4.36\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

For the **ground-state reference convention** used above, the initial molecule already has vibrational [zero-point energy](../../../../../../zero-point-energy.md). Thus its [ground-state dissociation energy](../../../../../../ground-state-dissociation-energy.md) is $D_0=D_e-\tfrac12k_B\theta_{\rm vib}$ and

$$
E_{\rm ZP}=\tfrac12(8.61733\times10^{-5})(172)\,\mathrm{eV}
=0.0074109\,\mathrm{eV},
$$



$$
\boxed{\Delta\varepsilon_0=D_0+I-\mathrm{EA}
=4.35259\,\mathrm{eV}\simeq4.353\,\mathrm{eV}}.
$$

At $3000\,\mathrm K$, $k_BT=0.258520\,\mathrm{eV}$, giving

$$
\boxed{e^{-\Delta\varepsilon_0/(k_BT)}=4.8750\times10^{-8}}.
$$

Alternatively, retaining the molecular minimum as reference gives **$\Delta\varepsilon_{\rm min}=4.36\,\mathrm{eV}$** and a Boltzmann factor **$4.7372\times10^{-8}$**. That convention must use the full [vibrational partition function](../../../../../../vibrational-partition-function.md), including $e^{-\theta_{\rm vib}/(2T)}$, in the next part. The ground-state convention instead uses $q_{\rm vib}^0$. Either consistent convention gives the same final [equilibrium constant](../../../../../../equilibrium-constant.md); using the ground-state reaction energy with the full zero-point factor would count the correction twice. The distinction between $D_e$ and $D_0$ is a distinction between initial states, not a change in the dissociation products.[dissociation energy and bond-dissociation energy in theoretical chemistry: well-minimum and ground-state energy conventions](../../../../../../iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md)
