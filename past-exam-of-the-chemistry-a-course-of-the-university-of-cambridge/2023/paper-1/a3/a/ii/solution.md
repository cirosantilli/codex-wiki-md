<h1 id="a3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a transition starting at $J$ and ending at $J+2$, the molecule gains

$$
\Delta\widetilde\varepsilon
=\widetilde B\big[(J+2)(J+3)-J(J+1)\big]
=\widetilde B(4J+6).
$$

[Energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) conservation therefore gives the scattered-light [wavenumbers](../../../../../../wavenumber.md)

$$
\boxed{\widetilde\nu_{\rm S}(J)=\widetilde\nu_L-(4J+6)\widetilde B,\qquad J=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$

The first shifts below the laser are **$6\widetilde B,10\widetilde B,14\widetilde B,18\widetilde B$**. Adjacent Stokes lines are separated by **$4\widetilde B$**, while the first line is $6\widetilde B$ from the Rayleigh line. The figure uses positive [Raman shift](../../../../../../raman-shift.md) $\widetilde\nu_L-\widetilde\nu_{\rm S}$; increasing shift means decreasing scattered-light wavenumber.
