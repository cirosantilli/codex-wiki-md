<h1 id="a1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Linearity and the shared $l=1$ angular [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) give

$$
\boxed{\hat l^2\psi_x=2\psi_x,\qquad\hat l^2\psi_y=2\psi_y.}
$$

Using the relative phases in part (c),

$$
\boxed{\hat l_z\psi_x=i\psi_y,\qquad\hat l_z\psi_y=-i\psi_x.}
$$

Thus each real orbital remains an [energy eigenstate](../../../../../energy-eigenstate.md) with **$E=-1/8$ hartree** and has definite [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) magnitude **$\sqrt2\hbar$**, but neither is an [eigenfunction](../../../../../eigenfunction.md) of $\hat l_z$. A measurement of $l_z$ gives **$+\hbar$ or $-\hbar$, each with [probability](../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$**. Its mean is zero and its variance is $\hbar^2$; a zero mean does not mean that a measurement gives zero.
