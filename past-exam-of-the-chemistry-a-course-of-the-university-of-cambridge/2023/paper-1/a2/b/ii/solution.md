↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**A gives the same optimum:** replacing $\alpha$ by $2\alpha$ merely reparameterizes the same set of positive-width [Gaussian functions](../../../../../../gaussian-function.md), so its best bound is unchanged.

**B is worse as a ground-energy estimate:** it has odd parity and a node at the origin, whereas the [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) of this even confining one-dimensional potential is even and nodeless. It is therefore [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to the exact [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) and belongs to the odd sector, whose lowest [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) is already above the ground [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md); the extra factor $x$ cannot be removed by varying its width. A separate numerical minimization gives a best value about $1.92372$ for B, well above the original [Gaussian function](../../../../../../gaussian-function.md) bound.

**C can improve the bound:** setting $\beta=0$ recovers every original [Gaussian function](../../../../../../gaussian-function.md), while nonzero $\beta$ adds shape freedom. Minimizing over this larger family can never give a worse optimum, and the additional freedom does lower the quartic-oscillator estimate. Inclusion of a family alone proves “no worse”; improvement is not guaranteed for every conceivable potential, for example one already solved exactly by a [Gaussian function](../../../../../../gaussian-function.md).
