↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [D4h molecular point group](../../../../../../d4h-molecular-point-group.md) contains an [inversion centre](../../../../../../inversion-centre.md), so [infrared and Raman mutual exclusion](../../../../../../infrared-and-raman-mutual-exclusion.md) applies. A [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) of $u$ parity can have [vibrational infrared activity](../../../../../../vibrational-infrared-activity.md), whereas the quadratic [polarizability tensor](../../../../../../polarizability-tensor.md) components responsible for [Raman vibrational activity](../../../../../../raman-vibrational-activity.md) have $g$ parity. No individual fundamental can be active in both spectra.

The [Td molecular point group](../../../../../../td-molecular-point-group.md) lacks an [inversion centre](../../../../../../inversion-centre.md). In particular, $T_2$ [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) transform both as electric-dipole components and as off-diagonal [polarizability tensor](../../../../../../polarizability-tensor.md) components, so they occur in both spectra. **Coincident IR and Raman fundamentals therefore favour the tetrahedral structure; symmetry requires no such coincidences for the square.** Weak or unresolved features can conceal coincidences experimentally, while accidental equality of different frequencies is not a violation of mutual exclusion.
