<h1 id="a4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The angular factors of the nickel $3d$ [atomic orbitals](../../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) transform like quadratic Cartesian functions. In the [D4h molecular point group](../../../../../../d4h-molecular-point-group.md), the assignments are

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{orbital}&D_{4h}\\\hline
d_{z^2}&A_{1g}\\
d_{x^2-y^2}&B_{1g}\\
d_{xy}&B_{2g}\\
(d_{xz},d_{yz})&E_g
\end{array}}
$$

All five are even under [inversion symmetry](../../../../../../inversion-symmetry.md), hence the $g$ suffixes. The pair $(d_{xz},d_{yz})$ is mixed by a quarter-turn and spans a two-dimensional [irreducible representation](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md).

In the [Td molecular point group](../../../../../../td-molecular-point-group.md), the corresponding result is

$$
\boxed{(d_{z^2},d_{x^2-y^2})\sim E,\qquad
(d_{xy},d_{xz},d_{yz})\sim T_2.}
$$

There is no inversion label because $T_d$ has no [inversion centre](../../../../../../inversion-centre.md). In both cases the dimensions add to five.
