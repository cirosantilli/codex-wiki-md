In [transition state theory](../../../../../../transition-state-theory.md), $K^\ddagger$ is the dimensionless equilibrium-like constant for forming the activated complex from the reactants under the chosen standard-state convention. It is not the equilibrium constant for forming the stable product.

$\Delta_{\mathrm r}G^{\circ,\ddagger}$ is the standard [activation free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-energy-of-activation.md), $\Delta_{\mathrm r}H^{\circ,\ddagger}$ the standard [activation enthalpy](../../../../../../activation-enthalpy.md), and $\Delta_{\mathrm r}S^{\circ,\ddagger}$ the standard [activation entropy](../../../../../../activation-entropy.md). Each is the activated-complex quantity minus the corresponding sum for the reactants, with the unstable reaction-coordinate motion treated by the transition-state flux construction.

$[\ ]^\circ=c^\circ$ is the [standard concentration](../../../../../../standard-concentration.md), conventionally $1\ \mathrm{mol\,dm^{-3}}$ for the rate units used here. Its inverse converts the attempt frequency $k_{\mathrm B}T/h$ into the units of a bimolecular [rate constant](../../../../../../rate-constant.md). The stated [Eyring equation](../../../../../../eyring-equation.md) assumes unit transmission coefficient.
