<h1 id="a7/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At fixed temperature, differentiating the [Eyring equation](../../../../../../eyring-equation.md) with a pressure-independent prefactor and fixed concentration convention gives

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\ln k_2}{\partial p}\right)_T
=-\frac1{RT}\left(\frac{\partial\Delta G^{\ddagger\circ}}{\partial p}\right)_T.
$$

Since $\mathrm dG=V\,\mathrm dp-S\,\mathrm dT$, the derivative is the difference between the activated complex's [partial molar volume](../../../../../../partial-molar-volume.md) and the sum for the reactants:

$$
\boxed{\Delta V^{\ddagger\circ}
=-RT\left(\frac{\partial\ln k_2}{\partial p}\right)_T.}
$$

If [activation volume](../../../../../../activation-volume.md) is approximately constant over the pressure range, integration gives

$$
\boxed{\ln k_2(p)=\ln k_2(p_0)-\frac{\Delta V^{\ddagger\circ}}{RT}(p-p_0).}
$$

Thus plot $\ln k_2$ against pressure: its slope is $-\Delta V^{\ddagger\circ}/RT$. Use pressure in pascals for a volume in $\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$. The model neglects pressure dependence of the transmission coefficient, substantial changes in mechanism, and strong curvature from a pressure-dependent activation volume. Fixed concentration-unit conventions and compression corrections matter for non-first-order rates.[Definition and concentration-unit qualification](../../../../../../volume-of-activation-in-the-iupac-gold-book.md)
