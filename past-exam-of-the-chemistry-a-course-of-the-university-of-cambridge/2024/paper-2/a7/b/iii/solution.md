↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [activation enthalpy](../../../../../../activation-enthalpy.md) is obtained from the slope across the measured $1/T$ interval. The [activation entropy](../../../../../../activation-entropy.md) comes from the intercept at $1/T=0$, far outside that interval, so a small slope uncertainty can produce a much larger uncertainty in the extrapolated intercept. Slope and intercept errors are consequently strongly correlated.

The entropy is also sensitive to the assumed prefactor and [standard concentration](../../../../../../standard-concentration.md), whereas a constant multiplicative calibration error shifts the intercept without changing the slope. This explains why its inferred precision is often poorer. Since enthalpy and entropy have different units, a claim about which absolute numerical uncertainty is larger requires a stated comparison convention.
