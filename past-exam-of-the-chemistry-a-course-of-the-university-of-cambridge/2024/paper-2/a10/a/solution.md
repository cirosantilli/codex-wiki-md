A [direct band gap](../../../../../direct-band-gap.md) has its [conduction band](../../../../../conduction-band.md) minimum and [valence band](../../../../../valence-band.md) maximum at the same [crystal momentum](../../../../../crystal-momentum.md). Near-edge absorption or emission can therefore conserve momentum with the electron and photon alone, since optical photon momentum is small on the Brillouin-zone scale. An [indirect band gap](../../../../../indirect-band-gap.md) has its extrema at different wavevectors and normally needs a [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) or another momentum-transfer process in addition to the photon.

**Direct-gap materials are usually preferred for efficient light emission**, because the radiative transition avoids the extra phonon-assisted process. Their stronger near-edge absorption also permits thinner photovoltaic absorbers. Indirect-gap materials can nevertheless make excellent solar cells: sufficiently thick optical paths, light trapping, long carrier lifetimes and suitable material quality can compensate for weaker absorption. The direct/indirect distinction alone does not rank every device.

This momentum distinction and the difference between bound exciton lines and the free-pair continuum are treated together in the primary effective-mass absorption theory.[Abstract, direct versus phonon-assisted indirect transitions](../../../../../intensity-of-optical-absorption-by-excitons-by-roger-james-elliott.md)
