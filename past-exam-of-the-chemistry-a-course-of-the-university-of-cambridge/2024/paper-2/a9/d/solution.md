<h1 id="a9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At zero temperature, states are occupied up to $E_{\mathrm F}$. Write the [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) as $g(E)=C\sqrt E$, including any fixed volume and spin factors in $C$. Then

$$
N=\int_0^{E_{\mathrm F}}C E^{1/2}\,\mathrm dE
=\frac{2C}{3}E_{\mathrm F}^{3/2},
$$

while

$$
U=\int_0^{E_{\mathrm F}}E\,g(E)\,\mathrm dE
=\frac{2C}{5}E_{\mathrm F}^{5/2}.
$$

Dividing eliminates $C$:

$$
\boxed{\langle E\rangle=\frac UN=\frac35E_{\mathrm F}.}
$$

The mean is positive even at absolute zero because the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) fills a range of momenta; electrons cannot all occupy the zero-momentum state.
