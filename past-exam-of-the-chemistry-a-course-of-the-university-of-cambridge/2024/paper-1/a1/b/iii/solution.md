<h1 id="a1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Solving $-i\hbar u'(\phi)=\lambda u(\phi)$ gives an exponential. The [periodic boundary condition](../../../../../../periodic-boundary-conditions.md) quantizes its phase winding:

$$
\boxed{u_k(\phi)=\frac{e^{ik\phi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\quad
\hat l_z u_k=\hbar k u_k,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

These are normalized with respect to $\mathrm d\phi$. The corresponding complex [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) is

$$
\boxed{f(\phi)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_k u_k(\phi),\qquad
c_k=\int_0^{2\pi}u_k(\phi)^*f(\phi)\,\mathrm d\phi.}
$$

Unlike the wrapped sine subset, this [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) includes the constant $k=0$ state and both sine and cosine combinations. If one retains arc-length normalization instead, replace $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ by $1/\sqrt a$ and integrate with $r\,\mathrm d\phi$.
