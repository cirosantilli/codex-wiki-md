<h1 id="a1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the allowed $n=2k$ states, the normalized standing wave is a [quantum superposition](../../../../../../quantum-superposition.md) of two opposite [angular momentum eigenstates](../../../../../../angular-momentum-eigenstate.md):

$$
\Phi_{2k}(\phi)=\frac{\sin(k\phi)}{\sqrt\pi}
=\frac{u_k(\phi)-u_{-k}(\phi)}{i\sqrt2}.
$$

The [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md) therefore predicts

$$
\boxed{l_z=+k\hbar\ \text{or}\ -k\hbar,\qquad P(+k\hbar)=P(-k\hbar)=\tfrac12.}
$$

The [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md) is zero, but a single measurement never gives zero for these $k\ge1$ states. Their uncertainty is $\Delta l_z=k\hbar$. The two components have the same rotor energy $\hbar^2k^2/(2I)$, so a definite energy does not imply definite angular momentum.
