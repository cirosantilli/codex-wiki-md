<h1 id="a5/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Eight chloride ions contribute charge $-8$. For an overall $4-$ charge, the two metals together contribute $+4$, so each is formally Mo(II). [Molybdenum](../../../../../../molybdenum.md) is in group six, giving four d electrons per centre and **eight metal-based electrons in total**.

In the ligand-perturbed configuration developed below, they occupy $\sigma^2\pi^4\delta^2$, with no Mo–Mo antibonding electron. Therefore the formal [bond order](../../../../../../bond-order.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{N_b-N_a}{2}=\frac{8-0}{2}=4.}
$$

This is one [sigma bond](../../../../../../sigma-bond.md), two [pi bonds](../../../../../../pi-bond.md) and one [delta bond](../../../../../../delta-bond.md). Before including ligands, the two ideal delta channels are degenerate and the allocation of their last two electrons is not determined by the bare energy sketch alone; the ligands remove that ambiguity and select the surviving low delta channel. The eight-electron bonding count still gives formal order four.

The occupied bonding groups are experimentally meaningful: molybdenum X-ray emission resolves three metal–metal bonding energy groups in this ion, alongside a deeper metal–chloride bonding feature.[Primary X-ray emission report, abstract and pages 262–263](../../../../../../direct-evidence-for-the-quadruple-metal-metal-bond-in-the-octachlorodimolybdenum-ii-ion-using-x-ray-emission-spectroscopy-by-derek-e-haycock-and-others.md)
