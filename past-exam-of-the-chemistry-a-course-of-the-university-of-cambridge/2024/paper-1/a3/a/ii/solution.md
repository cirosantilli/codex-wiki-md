<h1 id="a3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The two extra progressions are **rotational transitions within thermally populated $v=1$ and $v=2$ vibrational levels**. Isotopic purity rules out attributing them to different isotope masses. Their [rotational constants](../../../../../../rotational-constant.md) are

$$
\boxed{B_1=10.9115\ \mathrm{GHz},\qquad B_2=10.8235\ \mathrm{GHz}.}
$$

The [Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../boltzmann-distribution.md) makes each higher vibrational manifold less populated, so its rotational spectrum is weaker. A [Morse oscillator](../../../../../../morse-oscillator.md) explores larger separations as it is excited: the bond's vibrationally averaged moment of inertia increases and the rotational spacing decreases. More precisely, $B_v$ involves an average of the inverse square separation; it is not simply the inverse square of the mean separation.

The observed decrement is constant to the given precision:

$$
B_0-B_1=B_1-B_2=\boxed{\alpha_e=0.0880\ \mathrm{GHz}}.
$$

This fits the leading [vibration-rotation interaction](../../../../../../vibration-rotation-interaction.md) law $B_v=B_e-\alpha_e(v+\tfrac12)$.
