<h1 id="a3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A polar diatomic molecule can absorb microwaves through its permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md). In the [linear rigid rotor](../../../../../../linear-rigid-rotor.md) approximation,

$$
E_J=hB_0J(J+1),\quad J=0,1,2,\ldots,
\qquad B_0=\frac{h}{8\pi^2I_0}.
$$

The electric-dipole [selection rule](../../../../../../selection-rule.md) is $\Delta J=+1$ in absorption. It gives

$$
\nu_{J\to J+1}=\frac{E_{J+1}-E_J}{h}=2B_0(J+1),
$$

so adjacent lines are separated by $2B_0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{B_0=\frac{21.999}{2}=10.9995\ \mathrm{GHz}.}
$$

Assign a line near $m\times21.999\ \mathrm{GHz}$ to $J=m-1\to m$ for positive integer $m$. The first member is $0\to1$, the next $1\to2$, and so on; if the recorded window misses the lowest lines, their absolute frequencies fix the integer offset. At high temperature many rotational states are populated, so the strongest line need not be $0\to1$.

This is the leading rotational pattern, not a claim that real ZnF lacks spin or hyperfine structure. Fine splittings and [centrifugal distortion](../../../../../../centrifugal-distortion.md) can perturb the ideal lines; they do not explain the three systematically displaced progressions as economically as vibrational excitation.
