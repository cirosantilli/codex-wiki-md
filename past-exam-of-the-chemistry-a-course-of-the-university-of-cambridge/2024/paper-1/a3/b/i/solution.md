↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A **[vibrational fundamental](../../../../../../vibrational-fundamental.md)** is the transition $v=0\to1$. A **first [vibrational overtone](../../../../../../vibrational-overtone.md)** is $v=0\to2$. A **[hot band](../../../../../../hot-band.md)** starts from a thermally populated vibrationally excited level, such as $v=1\to2$.

For a strictly harmonic potential and a dipole linear in displacement, the allowed transitions have $\Delta v=\pm1$. A fundamental and a $1\to2$ hot band then share the same frequency, while the overtone has zero intensity. [Anharmonicity](../../../../../../anharmonicity.md) separates the adjacent gaps and can give overtone intensity; nonlinear displacement dependence of the dipole can also contribute. A hot band is defined by its lower state, not merely by being weak.
