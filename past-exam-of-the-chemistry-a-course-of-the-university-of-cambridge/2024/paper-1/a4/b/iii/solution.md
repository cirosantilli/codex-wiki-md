Taking the [matrix trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of those matrices gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
\text{class}&E&2S_4&C_2&2C_2^\prime&2\sigma_d\\\hline
\chi_p&3&-1&-1&-1&1
\end{array}
$$

The one-dimensional $p_z$ subspace has characters $(1,-1,1,-1,1)$, identifying $B_2$. The pair $(p_x,p_y)$ has characters $(2,0,-2,0,0)$, identifying $E$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_{p_x,p_y,p_z}=B_2\oplus E.}
$$

The dimensions add to three, as required. The $p_x,p_y$ pair must be retained together: a ninety-degree improper rotation mixes them, so either one alone is not a complete symmetry basis.[Printed page 12, $D_{2d}$ vector functions](../../../../../../tables-for-group-theory-by-p-w-atkins-and-others.md)
