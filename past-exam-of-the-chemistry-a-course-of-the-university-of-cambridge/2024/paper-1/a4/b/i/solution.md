A **[symmetry-closed orbital basis](../../../../../../symmetry-closed-orbital-basis.md)**, often called a complete basis in this context, is a linearly independent set whose span is carried into itself by every group operation. No transformed basis function requires a function outside that span. This is completeness for the chosen [group representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md), not a claim that a small atomic-orbital set spans the full electronic Hilbert space.

A **representation matrix** $D(g)$ specifies the linear transformation produced by a [molecular symmetry operation](../../../../../../molecular-symmetry-operation.md) $g$. With the column convention,

$$
\hat U(g)\phi_j=\sum_i\phi_iD_{ij}(g),\qquad
D(gh)=D(g)D(h).
$$

Its **[character of a representation](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md)** is the [matrix trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md),

$$
\boxed{\chi(g)=\operatorname{tr}D(g)=\sum_iD_{ii}(g).}
$$

The character is unchanged by a simultaneous change of basis and is constant on a [conjugacy class](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), which is why character tables have one column per class.
