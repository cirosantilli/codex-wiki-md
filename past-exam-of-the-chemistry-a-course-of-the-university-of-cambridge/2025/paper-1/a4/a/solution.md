<h1 id="a4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $Q_k$ be a vibrational [normal mode coordinate](../../../../../normal-coordinate.md) with symmetry $\Gamma_k$. The harmonic vibrational ground state is totally symmetric; the one-quantum state transforms as $Q_k$. Integration over a symmetry-invariant domain annihilates any integrand lacking the [totally symmetric representation](../../../../../totally-symmetric-representation.md). Thus a fundamental requires

$$
\Gamma(v'=1_k)\otimes\Gamma(\mu_\alpha)\otimes\Gamma(v''=0)
\supset A_1
$$

for some Cartesian component $\alpha$. For the one-dimensional irreducible representations used here, this means $\Gamma_k=\Gamma(x)$, $\Gamma(y)$ or $\Gamma(z)$. Equivalently, expanding the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) gives

$$
\mu_\alpha=\mu_{\alpha,0}+\left(\frac{\partial\mu_\alpha}{\partial Q_k}\right)_0Q_k+\cdots;
$$

the derivative can be nonzero only if $Q_k$ and $\mu_\alpha$ have compatible symmetry. This is the symmetry criterion for [vibrational infrared activity](../../../../../vibrational-infrared-activity.md); it permits a transition without guaranteeing a large intensity.
