<h1 id="a2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Keeping the same two spin states but incorrectly imposing [boson](../../../../../boson.md) exchange symmetry would require a symmetric total [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md). The spin-antisymmetric [singlet state](../../../../../singlet-state.md) would then accompany $\Phi_-$, while the spin-symmetric [triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md) would accompany $\Phi_+$. Thus

$$
E_S^{\rm hypothetical}=E_{\rm one}+J-K,\qquad
E_T^{\rm hypothetical}=E_{\rm one}+J+K,
$$

and $\boxed{E_T^{\rm hypothetical}-E_S^{\rm hypothetical}=2K}$. The ordering reverses: the triplet would be destabilized by $2K$ relative to the singlet. A genuinely spinless [boson](../../../../../boson.md) would have no singlet/triplet spin distinction; this comparison changes exchange symmetry while retaining the question's spin degrees of freedom.
