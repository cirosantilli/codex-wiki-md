<h1 id="a2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $a=\phi_{1s}$ and $b=\phi_{2s}$ be orthonormal [atomic orbitals](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md). The normalized spatial factors are

$$
\Phi_\pm(1,2)=\frac{a(1)b(2)\pm b(1)a(2)}{\sqrt2}.
$$

For the [singlet state](../../../../../singlet-state.md), use the antisymmetric spin factor

$$
\chi_{00}=\frac{\alpha(1)\beta(2)-\beta(1)\alpha(2)}{\sqrt2},\qquad \Psi_S=\Phi_+\chi_{00}.
$$

For the three components of the [triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md), use

$$
\chi_{11}=\alpha(1)\alpha(2),\quad
\chi_{10}=\frac{\alpha(1)\beta(2)+\beta(1)\alpha(2)}{\sqrt2},\quad
\chi_{1,-1}=\beta(1)\beta(2),\qquad \Psi_{T,M}=\Phi_-\chi_{1M}.
$$

Each total [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) obeys the [Pauli principle](../../../../../pauli-principle.md). At coincident spatial coordinates,

$$
\Phi_-(\mathbf r,\mathbf r)=\frac{a(\mathbf r)b(\mathbf r)-b(\mathbf r)a(\mathbf r)}{\sqrt2}=0.
$$

Consequently the triplet pair [probability density](../../../../../probability-density.md) vanishes at coincidence, even for its $M=0$ component. This zero is stronger than the trivial statement that any exact point has zero integrated probability in a continuum.
