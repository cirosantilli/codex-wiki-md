<h1 id="a2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The $3d$ and $4s$ [atomic orbitals](../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) of [chromium](../../../../../chromium.md) are close enough in energy that electron-electron terms can overturn the simple filling order. The configuration $[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^54s^1$ permits five singly occupied $3d$ orbitals and a parallel-spin $4s$ electron. Relative to $3d^44s^2$, it removes the $4s$ pair and permits more parallel-spin exchange interactions, lowering the Coulomb-and-exchange contribution enough to offset the orbital promotion cost.

As qualitative bookkeeping, six parallel [electron spins](../../../../../electron-spin.md) give $\binom62=15$ same-spin pairs, compared with $\binom52=10$ in high-spin $3d^44s^2$. The individual [exchange integrals](../../../../../exchange-integral.md) are unequal, so this is an explanation of the tendency, rather than a quantitative atomic energy calculation. The observed [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) configuration is $\boxed{[\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^54s^1}$.[Cr I ground level, 3d5(6S)4s, a7S, J=3, energy 0 cm−1](../../../../../energy-levels-of-neutral-chromium-in-the-nist-handbook.md)
