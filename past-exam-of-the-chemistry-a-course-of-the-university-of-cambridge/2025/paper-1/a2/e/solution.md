<h1 id="a2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

If two [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md) occupied the same complete [spin orbital](../../../../../spin-orbital.md) $u$, their antisymmetrized product would be

$$
\frac{u(1)u(2)-u(2)u(1)}{\sqrt2}=0.
$$

The zero vector cannot represent a normalized [quantum state](../../../../../quantum-state.md). This is the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md): at most one identical [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) occupies any complete one-particle state. Two [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) may occupy the same [spatial orbital](../../../../../spatial-orbital.md) with opposite spins, because $a\alpha$ and $a\beta$ are different [spin orbitals](../../../../../spin-orbital.md).
