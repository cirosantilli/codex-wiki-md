<h1 id="a1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The decisive observation is that $x$ is itself an [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md):

$$
\widehat Qx=\frac d{dx}\left[(x^2-1)\frac{dx}{dx}\right]=2x.
$$

On the regular [Legendre polynomial](../../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md) domain, integration by parts has endpoint factor $x^2-1$, so the boundary form vanishes for regular functions at $x=\pm1$. The interval alone is not an endpoint condition; regularity is the usual condition selecting the [Legendre polynomials](../../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md). Their [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $n(n+1)$, and the $n=1$ normalized function is

$$
\psi_1(x)=\sqrt{\frac32}\,x,\qquad \int_{-1}^{1}|\psi_1|^2dx=1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{x=\sqrt{\frac23}\,\psi_1(x),\qquad c_1=\sqrt{\frac23},\quad c_n=0\ (n\ne1).}
$$

Equivalently, the [Hermitian operator](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) identity gives $(q_n-2)c_n=0$, and nondegeneracy leaves only one possible nonzero coefficient. The unnormalized function $x$ has squared [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) $2/3$, consistent with this coefficient.
