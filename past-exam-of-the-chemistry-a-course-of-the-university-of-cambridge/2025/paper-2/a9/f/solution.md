<h1 id="a9/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Lattice vibrations contribute a [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md). At temperatures well below the [Debye temperature](../../../../../debye-temperature.md), its leading contribution is proportional to $T^3$, whereas the electronic part is proportional to $T$:

$$
\boxed{C_{V,m}\simeq\gamma T+\beta T^3}.
$$

The lattice term can therefore become important rapidly as [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) rises, even if the electronic term dominates sufficiently close to zero. At higher [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) the lattice contribution approaches the [Dulong-Petit law](../../../../../dulong-petit-law.md) value $3R$ per mole of atoms within the harmonic approximation. This is not a claim that the low-temperature $T^3$ law holds at all temperatures.
