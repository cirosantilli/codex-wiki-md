<h1 id="a9/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [Fermi temperature](../../../../../fermi-temperature.md) is

$$
\boxed{T_F=E_F/k=2.21414\times10^5\ \mathrm K}.
$$

Inserting it into the question's quoted estimate gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{2R(4\ \mathrm K)}{T_F}=3.0041\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}}.
$$

As explained in (d), that count is per mole of conduction [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). Using the stipulated six [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) per [tungsten](../../../../../tungsten.md) atom gives $1.8025\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ per mole of [tungsten](../../../../../tungsten.md) in the same rough model. The accurate free-electron [Sommerfeld expansion](../../../../../sommerfeld-expansion.md) coefficient would instead give $4.4474\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ per mole of [tungsten](../../../../../tungsten.md). These are different approximations and counting conventions, not three interchangeable exact answers.
