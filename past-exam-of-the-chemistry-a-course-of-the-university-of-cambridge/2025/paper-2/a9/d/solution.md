<h1 id="a9/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For $T\ll T_F$, only [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) within an energy window of order $kT$ around the [Fermi energy](../../../../../fermi-energy.md) can change occupation appreciably, because of the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md). The active fraction is of order $kT/E_F=T/T_F$. Giving each of these [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) energy of order $kT$ estimates an excitation energy $U-U_0\sim N_ekT^2/T_F$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{C_V\sim\frac{2N_ekT}{T_F}}.
$$

Per mole of conduction [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), $N_ek=R$, which is the quoted $2RT/T_F$. Per mole of metal atoms with $z$ model conduction [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) each, the [conduction-electron count in the molar electronic heat capacity](../../../../../conduction-electron-count-in-the-molar-electronic-heat-capacity.md) instead gives $2zRT/T_F$. The numerical factor is only approximate: the [Sommerfeld expansion](../../../../../sommerfeld-expansion.md) gives $C_V=(\pi^2/2)N_ekT/T_F$ for a three-dimensional free-electron gas.
