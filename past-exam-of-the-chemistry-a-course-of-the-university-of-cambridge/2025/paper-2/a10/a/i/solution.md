<h1 id="a10/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For an intrinsic, nondegenerate [semiconductor](../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md), charge neutrality and the [Boltzmann approximation](../../../../../../boltzmann-approximation.md) to the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) give $n_i=\sqrt{N_CN_V}\exp[-E_g/(2kT)]$. Assume the two polymorphs have equal effective-density-of-states prefactors and retain the quoted [band gaps](../../../../../../band-gap.md) at the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) being estimated. Their carrier-density ratio is then

$$
\frac{n_{i,\beta}}{n_{i,\alpha}}\simeq
\exp\left(\frac{3.4-3.2}{2k_BT}\right),
$$

with $k_B$ expressed in eV per kelvin. At $77\ \mathrm K$,

$$
\boxed{n_{i,\beta}/n_{i,\alpha}\simeq3.51\times10^6}.
$$

Both absolute intrinsic densities are extremely small at this [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md); a large ratio does not imply that either material is a good intrinsic conductor. Defects or unintended dopants can dominate real “undoped” specimens.
