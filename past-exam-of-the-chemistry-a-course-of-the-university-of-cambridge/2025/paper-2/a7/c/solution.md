<h1 id="a7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

As a first estimate, neglect the small [mass](../../../../../mass.md) change and changes in geometry and vibrational populations. Removing an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) creates an unpaired spin, giving at least the spin-only electronic [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) increase

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{m,\rm spin}\simeq R\log2=5.76\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}}.
$$

For a more specific estimate, the actual cation ground term is $X\,{}^2\Pi_g$, rather than an orbitally nondegenerate doublet. Ignoring [spin-orbit coupling](../../../../../spin-orbit-interaction.md), its two orbital components and two spin components give electronic degeneracy four and

$$
\boxed{\Delta S_{m,\rm el}\simeq R\log4=11.53\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}}.
$$

The ground-state assignment is confirmed by state-resolved photoionization measurements.[Measurements restricted to channels leaving the cation in its X 2Πg ground electronic state](../../../../../photoionization-of-co2-experimental-procedure-by-the-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md) Spin-orbit splitting and changed vibrational/rotational constants refine this estimate; the question's supplied [partition functions](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) alone do not determine those corrections. The $R\log2$ answer is a spin-only estimate, while $R\log4$ additionally retains the orbital degeneracy.
