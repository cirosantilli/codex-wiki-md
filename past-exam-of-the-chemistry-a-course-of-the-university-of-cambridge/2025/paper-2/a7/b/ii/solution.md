<h1 id="a7/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the same standard pressure and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), the same bond length, and the same electronic and vibrational-ground-state approximations. The [translational partition function](../../../../../../translational-partition-function.md) scales as [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) to the power $3/2$, giving the factor $(47/44)^{3/2}$. For the original isotopologue, $I=32m_uR^2$ and the [rotational symmetry number](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry-number.md) is two. For the isotope-substituted molecule, the two oxygen ends are distinguishable, so the symmetry number is one. Hence

$$
\frac{q_{\rm rot,new}}{q_{\rm rot,old}}=2\frac{33.91}{32}.
$$

The change in standard molar [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md) is

$$
\Delta S_m=R\left[\frac32\log\frac{47}{44}+\log\left(2\frac{33.91}{32}\right)\right]
=7.0678\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}},
$$

so

$$
\boxed{S_{m,\rm new}^{\circ}\simeq218.91\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}}.
$$

This uses a convention omitting a separate nuclear-spin [entropy](../../../../../../entropy-split.md). If nuclear-spin degeneracies are instead included explicitly, the spin-$1/2$ carbon-13 nucleus adds a further $R\log2$ relative to the spin-zero original isotopologue; that convention must be stated rather than silently mixed with the quoted [partition functions](../../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md).
