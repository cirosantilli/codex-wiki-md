<h1 id="a6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

“Low” and “high” compare $kT$ with the vibrational spacing $hc\widetilde\omega$, not [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) with an arbitrary number of kelvin. For $T\ll\theta_v$, occupation of excited levels is exponentially suppressed and

$$
\boxed{U\sim Nk\theta_v e^{-\theta_v/T}\longrightarrow0}.
$$

This vanishing result uses the ground-relative energy zero. For $T\gg\theta_v$, expand $e^{\theta_v/T}-1\sim\theta_v/T$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{U\sim NkT}.
$$

This agrees with [equipartition](../../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md): the oscillator has one quadratic kinetic and one quadratic potential term, contributing $kT/2$ each. The corresponding limiting vibrational [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) is zero at low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and $Nk$ at high [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).
