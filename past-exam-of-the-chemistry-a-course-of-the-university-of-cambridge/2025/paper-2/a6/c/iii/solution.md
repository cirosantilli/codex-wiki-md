↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For one mole, $Nk=R$, so the three-level [Morse oscillator](../../../../../../morse-oscillator.md) result is

$$
\boxed{U_m=RT^2\frac{q_3'}{q_3}=124.21\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

For the full [quantum harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) with the given wavenumber,

$$
\boxed{U_{m,\mathrm{harm}}=\frac{R(215/0.695)}{e^{215/(0.695\cdot100)}-1}
=122.16\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

The specified anharmonic calculation is about $1.7\%$ higher. Anharmonicity lowers the first excitation spacings and increases excited-state occupation. Both results remain small because $100\ \mathrm K$ is well below the vibrational [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). The comparison also mixes a three-level anharmonic truncation with an infinite harmonic sum; omitted higher Morse levels would make a further small positive correction at this [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).
