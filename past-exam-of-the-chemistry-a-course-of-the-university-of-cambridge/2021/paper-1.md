## A1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="a1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](#a1)

<h4 id="a1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a1/a)

<h5 id="a1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a1/a/i)

Write $D_x=y\partial_z-z\partial_y$, $D_y=z\partial_x-x\partial_z$ and $D_z=x\partial_y-y\partial_x$. The [product rule](../../calculus.md#product-rule) applied to a smooth test [function](../../function.md) $f$ gives

$$
[D_x,D_y]f=y\partial_xf-x\partial_yf=-D_zf.
$$

The mixed second [partial derivatives](../../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) cancel because they commute. Since the [orbital angular momentum](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum) components are $\hat j_i=-i\hbar D_i$, their [commutator](../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) is

$$
\boxed{[\hat j_x,\hat j_y]=(-i\hbar)^2(-D_z)=i\hbar\hat j_z.}
$$

This is one of the [orbital angular momentum commutation relations](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum-commutation-relations); the [Planck constant](../../quantum-mechanics.md#planck-constant) enters through the definition of each [linear operator](../../vector-space.md#linear-operator).

<h4 id="a1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a1/a)

<h5 id="a1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a1/a/ii)

[Cyclic permutation](../../combinatorics.md#cyclic-permutation) of the coordinates in the preceding [commutator](../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) calculation gives the remaining [orbital angular momentum commutation relations](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum-commutation-relations):

$$
\boxed{[\hat j_y,\hat j_z]=i\hbar\hat j_x,\qquad [\hat j_z,\hat j_x]=i\hbar\hat j_y.}
$$

Reversing the order of a [commutator](../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) reverses its sign.

<h4 id="a1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a1/a)

<h5 id="a1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a1/a/iii)

Use the [commutator derivation identity](../../lie-algebra.md#commutator-derivation-identity) $[A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C]$ and the [orbital angular momentum commutation relations](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum-commutation-relations). They give

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\hat j_z,\hat j_x^2]&=i\hbar(\hat j_y\hat j_x+\hat j_x\hat j_y),\\
[\hat j_z,\hat j_y^2]&=-i\hbar(\hat j_x\hat j_y+\hat j_y\hat j_x),\\
[\hat j_z,\hat j_z^2]&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first two expressions cancel on addition, so

$$
\boxed{[\hat j_z,\hat{\mathbf j}^{\,2}]=0.}
$$

The same argument works for each component of the [angular momentum operator](../../angular-momentum-operator.md).

<h4 id="a1/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a1/a)

<h5 id="a1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a1/a/iv)

The squared [angular momentum operator](../../angular-momentum-operator.md) and one selected component are [commuting operators](../../vector-space.md#commuting-operators), so a complete set of common [eigenfunctions](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) can be chosen: for the $z$ component these are the usual $|j,m\rangle$ [angular momentum eigenstates](../../angular-momentum-operator.md#angular-momentum-eigenstate). Distinct components do not have a common complete [eigenbasis](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis), because their [commutator](../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) is another nonzero component. A $j=0$ state is an exception in which all components vanish; noncommutation does not forbid every individual common [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction).

<h4 id="a1/a/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a1/a)

<h5 id="a1/a/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a1/a/v)

**The squared [angular momentum](../../momentum.md#angular-momentum) and one chosen component can have simultaneously sharp values. All three components cannot have simultaneously sharp values in a nonzero-$j$ state.** The possible measured component values are $m\hbar$, while the squared magnitude is $j(j+1)\hbar^2$. This concerns the [quantum state](../../quantum-system.md#quantum-state), rather than imperfect experimental resolution. The [Robertson uncertainty relation](../../quantum-theory.md#robertson-uncertainty-principle), for example,

$$
\Delta j_x\,\Delta j_y\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}|\langle\hat j_z\rangle|,
$$

quantifies the restriction; its right-hand side may vanish in some states, without making all three components simultaneously sharp.

<h3 id="a1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A1](#a1)

<h4 id="a1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a1/b)

<h5 id="a1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a1/b/i)

It represents the **squared [orbital angular momentum](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum)**. In [atomic units](../../physics.md#atomic-units), $\hbar=1$, so its [eigenvalues](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $l(l+1)$. In ordinary units the corresponding observable is $\hbar^2l(l+1)$, with [orbital angular momentum quantum number](../../quantum-system.md#orbital-angular-momentum-quantum-number) $l=0,1,2,\ldots$.

<h4 id="a1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a1/b)

<h5 id="a1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a1/b/ii)

The [spherical harmonic](../../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic) is also an [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) of the $z$ component of [orbital angular momentum](../../quantum-mechanics.md#orbital-angular-momentum):

$$
\boxed{\hat l_zY_{lm}=mY_{lm},\qquad m=-l,-l+1,\ldots,l.}
$$

These are [atomic units](../../physics.md#atomic-units); restoring units gives $m\hbar$. The integer $m$ is the [magnetic quantum number](../../quantum-mechanics.md#magnetic-quantum-number).

<h4 id="a1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a1/b)

<h5 id="a1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a1/b/iii)

[Separation of variables](../../partial-differential-equation.md#separation-of-variables) makes the angular factor a [spherical harmonic](../../analysis.md#spherical-harmonic). Applying the [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) gives

$$
\hat H(RY_{lm})=
Y_{lm}\left[-\frac{1}{2r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2R'\right)
+\frac{l(l+1)}{2r^2}R-\frac{R}{r}\right].
$$

Consequently $RY_{lm}$ is an [energy eigenfunction](../../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenfunction) **provided the radial factor satisfies**

$$
\boxed{-\frac{1}{2r^2}\frac{d}{dr}(r^2R')+
\left[\frac{l(l+1)}{2r^2}-\frac1r\right]R=ER.}
$$

This is the [radial Schrödinger equation for a central potential](../../mathematical-physics.md#radial-schrodinger-equation-for-a-central-potential), here with the attractive [Coulomb potential](../../electrostatics.md#coulomb-potential-energy). An arbitrary $R$ does not give an [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction); regularity, decay and [wavefunction normalization](../../wave-function.md#wavefunction-normalization) select admissible bound [quantum states](../../quantum-system.md#quantum-state).

<h4 id="a1/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a1/b)

<h5 id="a1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a1/b/iv)

For a $2p$ [atomic orbital](../../chemistry.md#atomic-orbital), $l=1$. The radial factor obeys

$$
R'=Ne^{-r/2}(1-r/2),\qquad R''=Ne^{-r/2}(r/4-1).
$$

Substitution into the [radial Schrödinger equation for a central potential](../../mathematical-physics.md#radial-schrodinger-equation-for-a-central-potential) gives

$$
-\frac12R''-\frac{R'}r+\frac{R}{r^2}-\frac Rr=-\frac18R.
$$

Thus the [energy eigenvalue](../../energy-eigenvalue.md) is

$$
\boxed{E=-\frac18\,E_{\rm h}\simeq-3.40\ \mathrm{eV}.}
$$

The [normalization constant](../../continuous-probability-distribution.md#normalizing-constant) cancels; $E_{\rm h}$ is the [Hartree energy](../../physics.md#hartree-energy).

## A2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="a2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](#a2)

<h4 id="a2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a2/a)

<h5 id="a2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a2/a/i)

The [quantum variational principle](../../quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-variational-principle) says that every admissible nonzero trial [wavefunction](../../wave-function.md) obeys

$$
\boxed{\widetilde E=
\frac{\langle\widetilde\Psi|\hat H|\widetilde\Psi\rangle}
{\langle\widetilde\Psi|\widetilde\Psi\rangle}\geq E_0,}
$$

where $E_0$ is the [ground-state energy](../../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state-energy). Thus the [Rayleigh quotient](../../linear-operator-theory.md#rayleigh-quotient) is an upper bound. Equality holds when the trial lies entirely in the [ground-state subspace](../../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state-subspace).

<h4 id="a2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a2/a)

<h5 id="a2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a2/a/ii)

Expand the trial in a complete [orthonormal eigenbasis](../../linear-operator-theory.md#orthonormal-eigenbasis) of the [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics), $\widetilde\Psi=\sum_nc_n\phi_n$, with $\hat H\phi_n=E_n\phi_n$. [Orthonormality](../../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) gives

$$
\widetilde E=
\frac{\sum_n|c_n|^2E_n}{\sum_n|c_n|^2},\qquad
\widetilde E-E_0=
\frac{\sum_n|c_n|^2(E_n-E_0)}{\sum_n|c_n|^2}\geq0.
$$

Every summand in the numerator is nonnegative because $E_n\geq E_0$. This proves the [quantum variational principle](../../quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-variational-principle) for the stated discrete expansion; the trial [energy](../../energy.md) is a [weighted mean](../../arithmetic.md#weighted-arithmetic-mean) of exact [energy eigenvalues](../../energy-eigenvalue.md).

<h3 id="a2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](#a2)

<h4 id="a2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a2/b)

<h5 id="a2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a2/b/i)

Inside the [particle in a box](../../quantum-mechanics.md#infinite-square-well), the [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics) is

$$
\boxed{\hat H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2},\qquad 0<x<a.}
$$

The infinite potential outside imposes the [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) $\psi(0)=\psi(a)=0$ and a zero [wavefunction](../../wave-function.md) outside the interval. These boundary conditions are part of the [Hamiltonian operator](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics), rather than an optional addition.

<h4 id="a2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a2/b)

<h5 id="a2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a2/b/ii)

Twice [differentiation](../../calculus.md#differentiation) the sine gives

$$
\hat H\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}
=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}.
$$

Both [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) hold when $n$ is an integer. The zero function at $n=0$ is not an [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction), and negative $n$ merely changes an overall sign. Therefore

$$
\boxed{n=1,2,\ldots,\qquad E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}.}
$$

The corresponding normalized [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) is $\sqrt{2/a}\sin(n\pi x/a)$.

<h3 id="a2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A2](#a2)

<h4 id="a2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a2/c/i)

The trial already vanishes at $x=0$. The second [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) requires $a(a-c)=0$, hence **$c=a$** for a box of positive length. This makes the trial an admissible [particle in a box](../../quantum-mechanics.md#infinite-square-well) [wavefunction](../../wave-function.md).

<h4 id="a2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a2/c/ii)

Up to a [global phase](../../quantum-system.md#global-phase), $x(a-x)$ is real, positive throughout the interior, has no interior [nodes of a wavefunction](../../wave-function.md#node-of-a-wavefunction), and is symmetric about $x=a/2$. It is smooth inside the box and has one central maximum. These are the shape and [reflection symmetry](../../physics.md#reflection-symmetry) properties of the lowest [particle in a box](../../quantum-mechanics.md#infinite-square-well) [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction), $\sin(\pi x/a)$; they make it a plausible [variational method](../../quantum-theory.md#variational-method) trial without making it exact.

<h4 id="a2/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a2/c/iii)

Since

$$
\int_0^a x^2(a-x)^2\,dx=\frac{a^5}{30},
$$

an explicitly normalized trial is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde\psi(x)=\sqrt{\frac{30}{a^5}}\,x(a-x),\qquad 0<x<a.}
$$

It is zero outside the box. The original expression $x(x-a)$ differs by a minus sign, which changes only the [global phase](../../quantum-system.md#global-phase) and leaves [probabilities](../../probability-theory.md#probability) and [expectation values](../../quantum-system.md#expectation-value) unchanged.

<h4 id="a2/c/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a2/c/iv)

[Integration by parts](../../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) and the zero endpoint values turn the [Rayleigh quotient](../../linear-operator-theory.md#rayleigh-quotient) into a positive [kinetic energy](../../energy.md#kinetic-energy) integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde E&=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int_0^a|\widetilde\psi'(x)|^2\,dx\\
&=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{30}{a^5}\int_0^a(a-2x)^2\,dx
=\boxed{\frac{5\hbar^2}{ma^2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the [parabolic variational trial for a particle in a box](../../quantum-mechanics.md#parabolic-variational-trial-for-a-particle-in-a-box). The [potential energy](../../energy.md#potential-energy) vanishes inside the box.

<h4 id="a2/c/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a2/c/v)

Comparing the [variational method](../../quantum-theory.md#variational-method) estimate with the exact [ground-state energy](../../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state-energy) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\widetilde E}{E_1}=\frac{10}{\pi^2}=1.01321\ldots>1.}
$$

The estimate is about **$1.32\%$ too high**, consistently with the [quantum variational principle](../../quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-variational-principle). Its closeness does not mean that the trial [wavefunction](../../wave-function.md) is an exact [energy eigenfunction](../../energy-eigenvalue.md#energy-eigenfunction).

<h4 id="a2/c/vi">vi</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#a2/c)

<h5 id="a2/c/vi/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#a2/c/vi)

The trial is even under reflection about the midpoint. The normalized box [eigenfunctions](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) satisfy $\phi_n(a-x)=(-1)^{n+1}\phi_n(x)$, so [orthogonality](../../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) between even and odd functions gives $c_n=0$ for even $n$. If the higher odd contributions $n\geq5$ are neglected, the [weighted mean](../../arithmetic.md#weighted-arithmetic-mean) from the [quantum variational principle](../../quantum-mechanics.md#quantum-variational-principle) becomes

$$
\widetilde E\simeq
\frac{|c_1|^2E_1+|c_3|^2E_3}{|c_1|^2+|c_3|^2}.
$$

Rearranging yields the [two-level variational contamination estimate](../../quantum-theory.md#two-level-variational-contamination-estimate)

$$
\boxed{\frac{|c_3|^2}{|c_1|^2}\simeq
\frac{\widetilde E-E_1}{E_3-\widetilde E}.}
$$

The required assumption concerns the neglected contributions to the [energy](../../energy.md) as well as their small probabilities. Numerically the two-level estimate is $0.0016542$. It is not an exact identity: direct expansion of this parabolic trial gives $c_n\propto n^{-3}$ for odd $n$, hence the exact ratio is $1/729=0.0013717$. The omitted higher odd states account for the difference.

<h4 id="a3/a/i">i</h4>

<h5 id="a3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

Place the [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) and [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) nuclei on a line separated by $r$, with masses $m_{\rm C}$ and $m_{\rm S}$. The [center of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) lies between them and closer to the heavier [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) nucleus. In the [linear rigid rotor](../../molecule.md#linear-rigid-rotor) approximation, $r$ remains fixed while the [molecule](../../molecule.md) rotates.

<h4 id="a3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a3/a)

<h5 id="a3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a3/a/ii)

Measure $x$ from the [carbon](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon) nucleus toward the [sulfur](../../chemical-substance.md#sulfur) nucleus. The definition of the [center of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) gives

$$
x_{\rm cm}=\frac{m_{\rm C}\,0+m_{\rm S}r}{m_{\rm C}+m_{\rm S}}.
$$

Therefore the requested distance is

$$
\boxed{r_1=\frac{m_{\rm S}}{m_{\rm C}+m_{\rm S}}r.}
$$

The other distance is $r_2=r-r_1=m_{\rm C}r/(m_{\rm C}+m_{\rm S})$, consistent with $m_{\rm C}r_1=m_{\rm S}r_2$.

<h4 id="a3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a3/a)

<h5 id="a3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a3/a/iii)

For an axis through the [center of mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#center-of-mass) perpendicular to the bond, the point-mass [moment of inertia](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#moment-of-inertia) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=m_{\rm C}r_1^2+m_{\rm S}r_2^2\\
&=\frac{m_{\rm C}m_{\rm S}^2+m_{\rm S}m_{\rm C}^2}
{(m_{\rm C}+m_{\rm S})^2}r^2
=\boxed{\frac{m_{\rm C}m_{\rm S}}{m_{\rm C}+m_{\rm S}}r^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The prefactor is the [reduced mass](../../classical-mechanics.md#reduced-mass) $\mu$; this is the standard reduction of a two-body [linear rigid rotor](../../molecule.md#linear-rigid-rotor) to $I=\mu r^2$.

<h4 id="a3/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a3/a)

<h5 id="a3/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a3/a/iv)

Using the stipulated integer [isotope](../../chemical-substance.md#isotope) masses gives

$$
\mu=\frac{12\times32}{44}u=8.72727u,\qquad
I=\mu(1.53\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m)^2
=3.39243\times10^{-46}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}.
$$

The [rotational constant](../../molecule.md#rotational-constant) in [wavenumber](../../wave-equation.md#wavenumber) units is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde B=\frac{h}{8\pi^2cI}=0.8252\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}.}
$$

Here $c$ must be expressed in $\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$ when $I$ is in SI units. Equivalently calculate $B=h/(8\pi^2I)$ in $\mathrm{Hz}$ first, then divide by $c$ in $\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$.

<h3 id="a3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A3](#a3)

<h4 id="a3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a3/b)

<h5 id="a3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a3/b/i)

The [rotational state populations](../../molecule.md#rotational-state-populations) start at $N_0/N_0=1$. Their $2J+1$ [statistical weight](../../quantum-mechanics.md#statistical-weight) factor initially favors higher $J$, while the [Boltzmann factor](../../canonical-ensemble.md#boltzmann-factor) eventually suppresses them, producing one maximum followed by a tail tending to zero. The physically allowed values occur at integer $J$; a continuous curve only guides the eye.

![](../../past-exam-of-the-chemistry-a-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2021/paper-1-carbon-monosulfide-populations.png)

**[Figure 2](#a3/b/i/image-original-linear-rigid-rotor-populations-for-carbon-monosulfide-at-300-mathrm-k-and-8-7-mathrm-k). Original linear rigid rotor populations for carbon monosulfide at $300\ \mathrm K$ and $8.7\ \mathrm K$**.

<h4 id="a3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a3/b)

<h5 id="a3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a3/b/ii)

Set $\theta_{\rm rot}=hc\widetilde B/k_{\rm B}$, the [rotational temperature](../../molecule.md#rotational-temperature). For a continuous nonnegative variable $J$,

$$
\frac{d}{dJ}\log\frac{N_J}{N_0}
=\frac{2}{2J+1}-\frac{\theta_{\rm rot}}T(2J+1).
$$

Equating this [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) to zero gives the [most populated rotational level](../../molecule.md#most-populated-rotational-level)

$$
\boxed{J_*=\frac12\left(\sqrt{\frac{2T}{\theta_{\rm rot}}}-1\right)
=\sqrt{\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{2hc\widetilde B}}-\frac12.}
$$

The second [derivative](../../calculus.md#derivative) is $-4/(2J+1)^2-2\theta_{\rm rot}/T<0$, so this is a maximum when $J_*\geq0$. At lower temperatures the constrained continuous maximum is $J=0$. For the physical integer maximum compare the nearby allowed levels explicitly.

<h4 id="a3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a3/b)

<h5 id="a3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a3/b/iii)

The calculated [rotational constant](../../molecule.md#rotational-constant) gives $\theta_{\rm rot}=1.18721\ \mathrm K$. At room [temperature](../../temperature.md), taken as $300\ \mathrm K$, the continuous maximum is $J_*=10.7404$; comparing the adjacent integer populations gives **$J_{\max}=11$**. Taking $298.15\ \mathrm K$ instead gives $J_*=10.7057$ and the same integer answer.

At $8.7\ \mathrm K$, $J_*=1.41417$. In particular,

$$
\frac{N_2}{N_1}=\frac53e^{-4\theta_{\rm rot}/T}=0.966\ldots<1,
$$

and $N_1>N_0$, so **$J_{\max}=1$**. The integer comparison avoids treating a noninteger [angular momentum quantum number](../../quantum-system.md#angular-momentum-quantum-number) as a physical state.

<h4 id="a3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a3/b)

<h5 id="a3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a3/b/iv)

Low [rotational energy](../../molecule.md#rotational-energy) spacings can be excited in cold interstellar gas when electronic and most vibrational excitations are frozen out. A polar [molecule](../../molecule.md) such as [carbon monosulfide](../../chemical-substance.md#carbon-monosulfide) has allowed [electric dipole transition](../../quantum-mechanics.md#electric-dipole-transition) lines whose frequencies depend on its [moment of inertia](../../rigid-body-dynamics.md#moment-of-inertia), providing a distinctive pattern for [rotational spectral line assignment](../../molecule.md#rotational-spectral-line-assignment). Several matching [spectral lines](../../stellar-astrophysics.md#spectral-line) and their [isotope shifts](../../physics.md#isotope-shift) give a much stronger identification than one coincidence. The method is especially effective for polar species; a molecule without a permanent [electric dipole moment](../../electrostatics.md#electric-dipole-moment) lacks an ordinary pure electric-dipole [rotational spectrum](../../molecule.md#rotational-spectrum).

## A4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="a4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](#a4)

<h4 id="a4/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a4/a)

<h5 id="a4/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a4/a/i)

The [trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry](../../molecule.md#trigonal-pyramidal-molecular-geometry) has **[C3v point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#c3v-molecular-point-group) symmetry**: identity, two rotations about the threefold axis, and three vertical mirror reflections. Choose $z$ along the threefold axis through [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) and the center of the hydrogen triangle.

<h4 id="a4/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a4/a)

<h5 id="a4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a4/a/ii)

In the [C3v point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#c3v-molecular-point-group), the [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) valence [atomic orbitals](../../chemistry.md#atomic-orbital) transform as

$$
\boxed{2s:A_1,\qquad 2p_z:A_1,\qquad (2p_x,2p_y):E.}
$$

The $s$ orbital is invariant under every [symmetry operation](../../euclidean-geometry.md#symmetry-operation); $p_z$ is also invariant because each operation preserves the $z$ coordinate. The other two components mix as a two-dimensional [irreducible representation](../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation).

<h4 id="a4/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a4/a)

<h5 id="a4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a4/a/iii)

Use the classes $(E,2C_3,3\sigma_v)$ of the [C3v point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#c3v-molecular-point-group). For the three hydrogen $1s$ [atomic orbitals](../../chemistry.md#atomic-orbital), the [character of a representation](../../representation-theory.md#character-of-a-representation) counts orbitals fixed by each [symmetry operation](../../euclidean-geometry.md#symmetry-operation). Identity fixes three, a nontrivial threefold rotation fixes none, and a vertical mirror fixes one. Therefore

$$
\chi_{\rm H}=(3,0,1).
$$

The [character inner product](../../representation-theory.md#character-inner-product) gives multiplicities

$$
n_{A_1}=\frac{3+3}{6}=1,\quad
n_{A_2}=\frac{3-3}{6}=0,\quad
n_E=\frac{2\times3}{6}=1.
$$

Thus **$\Gamma_{\rm H}=A_1\oplus E$**, with the $E$ contribution accounting for two independent [symmetry orbitals](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination).

<h4 id="a4/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a4/a)

<h5 id="a4/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a4/a/iv)

Neglecting intersite [orbital overlap](../../chemistry.md#orbital-overlap), one normalized real set of [symmetry-adapted linear combinations](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\chi_{A_1}&=(s_A+s_B+s_C)/\sqrt3,\\
\chi_{E,1}&=(2s_A-s_B-s_C)/\sqrt6,\\
\chi_{E,2}&=(s_B-s_C)/\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The coefficient vectors are [orthonormal](../../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set). The first has equal phase at all three sites; the second opposes site $A$ to $B,C$; the third has zero amplitude at $A$ and opposite phases at $B,C$. Any orthonormal rotation of the two $E$ functions is equally valid. With nonzero intersite [orbital overlap](../../chemistry.md#orbital-overlap), normalization must instead use the overlap [matrix](../../vector-space.md#matrix).

<h4 id="a4/a/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a4/a)

<h5 id="a4/a/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#a4/a/v)

The hydrogen $A_1$ [symmetry orbital](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) mixes with both [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) $2s$ and $2p_z$, giving three $a_1$ [molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital). The two hydrogen $E$ functions mix with $(2p_x,2p_y)$, giving a bonding $e$ pair and an antibonding $e$ pair. Eight valence [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) fill the lower levels as

$$
\boxed{(2a_1)^2(1e)^4(3a_1)^2.}
$$

This uses conventional numbering that reserves $1a_1$ for the omitted [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) core $1s$ orbital. The low $2a_1$ and $1e$ levels provide the principal N–H [bonding molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#bonding-orbital); the highest occupied $3a_1$ level is the mainly nitrogen [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair), with some bonding admixture permitted by the pyramidal geometry. Empty $4a_1$ and $2e$ levels are antibonding. Symmetry fixes which orbitals can mix, rather than their quantitative energy separations.

<h3 id="a4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A4](#a4)

<h4 id="a4/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a4/b)

<h5 id="a4/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a4/b/i)

The planar equilateral structure has **[D3h point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#d3h-molecular-point-group) symmetry**. In addition to the threefold axis, it has a horizontal mirror plane containing all four nuclei and three twofold axes lying in that plane.

<h4 id="a4/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a4/b)

<h5 id="a4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a4/b/ii)

For planar [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia), the [nitrogen](../../chemical-substance.md#nitrogen) valence [atomic orbitals](../../chemistry.md#atomic-orbital) have symmetries $2s:A_1'$, $(2p_x,2p_y):E'$ and $2p_z:A_2''$. The hydrogen [symmetry orbitals](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) span $A_1'\oplus E'$. Consequently the in-plane orbitals form a bonding $a_1'$ orbital, a bonding $e'$ pair and their antibonding partners. The out-of-plane $2p_z$ has no matching hydrogen combination and remains a [nonbonding orbital](../../chemistry.md#nonbonding-orbital). With the core omitted, the conventional valence occupation is

$$
\boxed{(2a_1')^2(1e')^4(1a_2'')^2.}
$$

The preceding [ammonia molecular orbital diagram](../../chemistry.md#ammonia-molecular-orbital-diagram) shows the correlation. On pyramidalization, $A_1'$ and $A_2''$ both correlate with $A_1$ of the [C3v point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#c3v-molecular-point-group), permitting [s–p orbital mixing](../../chemistry.md#s-p-orbital-mixing). The pure planar lone pair can acquire bonding character, lowering its energy and helping stabilize the pyramid. Symmetry alone does not provide a numerical inversion barrier.[The ammonia molecule, Tables 5–6 and Figures 7–9: planar nonbonding $p_z$ and the pyramidal comparison](../../chemistry-bibliography.md#explaining-the-geometry-of-simple-molecules-using-molecular-orbital-energy-level-diagrams-built-by-using-symmetry-principles-by-sergio-p-machado-and-roberto-b-faria)

<h4 id="a4/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a4/b)

<h5 id="a4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a4/b/iii)

The nitrogen-centered [highest occupied molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#highest-occupied-molecular-orbital) of [ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) supplies a [lone pair](../../covalent-bond.md#lone-pair); planar [borane](../../chemical-substance.md#borane) has an empty out-of-plane $p$-derived [lowest unoccupied molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#lowest-unoccupied-molecular-orbital). Approach along this acceptor axis gives constructive [orbital overlap](../../chemistry.md#orbital-overlap) and donation of the nitrogen pair into the boron-centered acceptor. The resulting **N-to-B [coordinate covalent bond](../../covalent-bond.md#coordinate-covalent-bond) forms the [ammonia borane](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia-borane) adduct**.

The occupied [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) formed by this interaction is bonding, while the corresponding antibonding combination remains empty. [Borane](../../chemical-substance.md#borane) pyramidalizes as coordination increases from three to four. [Ammonia](../../chemical-substance.md#ammonia) can use its existing outward-pointing lone pair; its inversion to the planar transition geometry is not a prerequisite. The [Lewis base](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#lewis-base) and [Lewis acid](../../acid-base-chemistry.md#lewis-acid) descriptions summarize the donor and acceptor roles, while the frontier [molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) explain their directional interaction.

## A5

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="a5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](#a5)

<h4 id="a5/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a5/a)

<h5 id="a5/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a5/a/i)

A [symmetry orbital](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) is a [linear combination of atomic orbitals](../../chemistry.md#linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals) transforming as a component of an [irreducible representation](../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) of the molecular [point group](../../euclidean-geometry.md#point-group). A [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) is a one-electron [wavefunction](../../wave-function.md) for the molecule, obtained as an [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) of the chosen effective one-electron [Hamiltonian operator](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics). Symmetry organizes the eigenproblem, but a symmetry label alone does not determine its coefficients.

<h4 id="a5/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#a5/a)

<h5 id="a5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a5/a/ii)

**A [symmetry orbital](../../chemistry.md#symmetry-adapted-linear-combination) is a [molecular orbital](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital) when it is also an [eigenfunction](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) of the effective one-electron [Hamiltonian operator](../../quantum-system.md#hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics).** If an [irreducible representation](../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) occurs once in a complete invariant model space, a symmetry-invariant [Hamiltonian](../../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian) acts as a scalar on that copy by [Schur's lemma](../../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma). Its basis functions are then degenerate [eigenfunctions](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction). When the same symmetry occurs in several copies, those copies can mix; one must diagonalize the corresponding block. Merely having a one-dimensional symmetry label is insufficient if other orbitals have that same label.

<h3 id="a5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [A5](#a5)

<h4 id="a5/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a5/b)

<h5 id="a5/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#a5/b/i)

The **$z$ axis is a $C_2$ axis** through the centers of opposite cube faces: a half turn permutes atoms within each species. A **body diagonal through sites 1 and 8 is a $C_3$ axis**: a third turn fixes those two opposite vertices and cycles the other three vertices of each tetrahedral sublattice. These are [symmetry operations](../../euclidean-geometry.md#symmetry-operation) of the [tetrahedral symmetry](../../finite-group-theory.md#tetrahedral-symmetry) group $T_d$; a quarter turn about $z$ would interchange the two chemical species and is not a symmetry of the specified cluster.

<h4 id="a5/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a5/b)

<h5 id="a5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#a5/b/ii)

For the [tetrahedral symmetry](../../finite-group-theory.md#tetrahedral-symmetry) classes $(E,8C_3,3C_2,6S_4,6\sigma_d)$, each set of four $s$ [atomic orbitals](../../chemistry.md#atomic-orbital) has

$$
\boxed{\chi_{\rm Li}=\chi_{\rm Na}=(4,1,0,0,2).}
$$

The counts are the fixed sites of each [symmetry operation](../../euclidean-geometry.md#symmetry-operation): a threefold rotation fixes one vertex of each tetrahedron, a twofold or improper fourfold operation fixes none, and a diagonal mirror fixes two. [Character orthogonality](../../representation-theory.md#character-orthogonality) reduces each set to

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_{\rm Li}=\Gamma_{\rm Na}=A_1\oplus T_2.}
$$

The dimensions $1+3=4$ provide a check. Thus the complete eight-orbital model contains two copies of each of $A_1$ and $T_2$, which can mix across the two species.

<h4 id="a5/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a5/b)

<h5 id="a5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#a5/b/iii)

Write $L_i$ for the [lithium](../../chemical-substance.md#lithium) $2s$ orbital at site $i$ and $N_i$ for the [sodium](../../chemical-substance.md#sodium) $3s$ orbital. Reading the printed axes, the lithium coordinates have signs $1=(-,+,+)$, $2=(+,-,+)$, $3=(+,+,-)$ and $4=(-,-,-)$; the sodium sites are $5=(+,+,+)$, $6=(-,-,+)$, $7=(-,+,-)$ and $8=(+,-,-)$. The normalized [tetrahedral s-orbital symmetry combinations](../../chemistry.md#tetrahedral-s-orbital-symmetry-combinations) are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&\mathrm{Li}&\mathrm{Na}\\ \hline
A_1&(L_1+L_2+L_3+L_4)/2&(N_5+N_6+N_7+N_8)/2\\
T_{2x}&(-L_1+L_2+L_3-L_4)/2&(N_5-N_6-N_7+N_8)/2\\
T_{2y}&(L_1-L_2+L_3-L_4)/2&(N_5-N_6+N_7-N_8)/2\\
T_{2z}&(L_1+L_2-L_3-L_4)/2&(N_5+N_6-N_7-N_8)/2
\end{array}
$$

Each $T_2$ row takes its signs from the corresponding coordinate, so the three functions transform together like $(x,y,z)$. These coefficient vectors are [orthonormal](../../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) when intersite [orbital overlap](../../chemistry.md#orbital-overlap) is neglected. A common sign reversal of any row is physically immaterial.

<h4 id="a5/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#a5/b)

<h5 id="a5/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#a5/b/iv)

Use the stipulated common [Hückel Coulomb parameter](../../chemistry.md#huckel-coulomb-parameter) $\alpha$, an equal [Hückel resonance integral](../../chemistry.md#huckel-resonance-integral) $\beta$ along each cube edge, and zero coupling elsewhere; take an orthonormal site basis. Each lithium vertex has three sodium neighbors. The totally symmetric combinations therefore have the block

$$
H_{A_1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&3\beta\\3\beta&\alpha\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For a coordinate-like $T_2$ combination, the sum of the three neighboring sodium coordinate signs equals the lithium coordinate sign. Thus each of the three equivalent blocks is

$$
H_{T_2}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta&\alpha\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Their [eigenvalues](../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) give the [cube nearest-neighbour Hückel model](../../chemistry.md#cube-nearest-neighbour-huckel-model):

$$
\boxed{E=\alpha+3\beta\ (a_1),\quad\alpha+\beta\ (t_2),\quad
\alpha-\beta\ (t_2),\quad\alpha-3\beta\ (a_1).}
$$

For the bonding convention $\beta<0$ these are in increasing energy order, with [statistical weights](../../quantum-mechanics.md#statistical-weight) $1,3,3,1$. The eight valence [electrons](../../physics.md#electron) occupy **$(a_1)^2(t_2)^6$**, filling the four bonding spatial [molecular orbitals](../../chemistry.md#molecular-orbital). Their total one-electron energy is $8\alpha+12\beta$. This is a deliberately simplified model: unequal atomic energies or unequal overlaps require a generalized calculation.
