For planar [ammonia](../../../../../../ammonia.md), the [nitrogen](../../../../../../nitrogen.md) valence [atomic orbitals](../../../../../../atomic-orbital.md) have symmetries $2s:A_1'$, $(2p_x,2p_y):E'$ and $2p_z:A_2''$. The hydrogen [symmetry orbitals](../../../../../../symmetry-adapted-linear-combination.md) span $A_1'\oplus E'$. Consequently the in-plane orbitals form a bonding $a_1'$ orbital, a bonding $e'$ pair and their antibonding partners. The out-of-plane $2p_z$ has no matching hydrogen combination and remains a [nonbonding orbital](../../../../../../nonbonding-orbital.md). With the core omitted, the conventional valence occupation is

$$
\boxed{(2a_1')^2(1e')^4(1a_2'')^2.}
$$

The preceding [ammonia molecular orbital diagram](../../../../../../ammonia-molecular-orbital-diagram.md) shows the correlation. On pyramidalization, $A_1'$ and $A_2''$ both correlate with $A_1$ of the [C3v point group](../../../../../../c3v-molecular-point-group.md), permitting [s–p orbital mixing](../../../../../../s-p-orbital-mixing.md). The pure planar lone pair can acquire bonding character, lowering its energy and helping stabilize the pyramid. Symmetry alone does not provide a numerical inversion barrier.[The ammonia molecule, Tables 5–6 and Figures 7–9: planar nonbonding $p_z$ and the pyramidal comparison](../../../../../../explaining-the-geometry-of-simple-molecules-using-molecular-orbital-energy-level-diagrams-built-by-using-symmetry-principles-by-sergio-p-machado-and-roberto-b-faria.md)
