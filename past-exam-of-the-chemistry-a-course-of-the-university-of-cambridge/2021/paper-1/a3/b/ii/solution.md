<h1 id="a3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $\theta_{\rm rot}=hc\widetilde B/k_{\rm B}$, the [rotational temperature](../../../../../../rotational-temperature.md). For a continuous nonnegative variable $J$,

$$
\frac{d}{dJ}\log\frac{N_J}{N_0}
=\frac{2}{2J+1}-\frac{\theta_{\rm rot}}T(2J+1).
$$

Equating this [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) to zero gives the [most populated rotational level](../../../../../../most-populated-rotational-level.md)

$$
\boxed{J_*=\frac12\left(\sqrt{\frac{2T}{\theta_{\rm rot}}}-1\right)
=\sqrt{\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{2hc\widetilde B}}-\frac12.}
$$

The second [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $-4/(2J+1)^2-2\theta_{\rm rot}/T<0$, so this is a maximum when $J_*\geq0$. At lower temperatures the constrained continuous maximum is $J=0$. For the physical integer maximum compare the nearby allowed levels explicitly.
