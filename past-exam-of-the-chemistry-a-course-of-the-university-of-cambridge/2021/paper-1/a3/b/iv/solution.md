<h1 id="a3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Low [rotational energy](../../../../../../rotational-energy.md) spacings can be excited in cold interstellar gas when electronic and most vibrational excitations are frozen out. A polar [molecule](../../../../../../molecule-split.md) such as [carbon monosulfide](../../../../../../carbon-monosulfide.md) has allowed [electric dipole transition](../../../../../../electric-dipole-transition.md) lines whose frequencies depend on its [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), providing a distinctive pattern for [rotational spectral line assignment](../../../../../../rotational-spectral-line-assignment.md). Several matching [spectral lines](../../../../../../spectral-line.md) and their [isotope shifts](../../../../../../isotope-shift.md) give a much stronger identification than one coincidence. The method is especially effective for polar species; a molecule without a permanent [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) lacks an ordinary pure electric-dipole [rotational spectrum](../../../../../../rotational-spectrum.md).
