<h1 id="a7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the same atoms in a three-dimensional volume $V$, the one-atom [translational partition function](../../../../../translational-partition-function.md) is $q_{\rm trans}^{(3)}=V/\Lambda^3$. Comparing the same $N$ atoms at the same [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) cancels the common $N!$ in their [canonical partition functions](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md). Therefore the molar [adsorption confinement free energy](../../../../../adsorption-confinement-free-energy.md), with the sign defined as adsorbed minus free, is

$$
\boxed{\Delta F_m^{\rm trans}=-RT\ln\frac{q_{\rm trans}^{(2)}}{q_{\rm trans}^{(3)}}
=RT\ln\frac{V}{\sigma\Lambda}.}
$$

The logarithm is dimensionless: $V/\sigma$ and $\Lambda$ are both lengths. For a macroscopic gas depth $V/\sigma\gg\Lambda$, confinement **raises the translational [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md)**. Equivalently, the translational state count is reduced by a factor $\sigma\Lambda/V$ per atom. The question supplies neither a volume nor an adsorption binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md), so this answer is symbolic and describes the confinement cost, not a numerical net adsorption free [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md).

For example, a binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) $D>0$ per atom would supply a separate $-N_{\rm A}D$ to $\Delta F_m$, together with any normal-motion internal contribution retained by a more detailed model. Adsorption can then be favourable despite the loss of free translational phase space. Merely calling the change a loss must not reverse the sign of the explicitly defined $F_{\rm ads}-F_{\rm gas}$.
