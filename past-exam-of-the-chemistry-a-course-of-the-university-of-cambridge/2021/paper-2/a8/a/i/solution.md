<h1 id="a8/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The two [protons](../../../../../../proton.md) are identical spin-one-half [fermions](../../../../../../fermion.md), so the total state is antisymmetric under exchange. In the electronic and vibrational [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md), an even rotational $J$ has a symmetric spatial wavefunction and therefore uses the antisymmetric nuclear-spin [singlet state](../../../../../../singlet-state.md); odd $J$ uses the symmetric [triplet state](../../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). Thus [parahydrogen](../../../../../../parahydrogen.md) has one nuclear-spin state, and [orthohydrogen](../../../../../../orthohydrogen.md) has three. Each rotational level also has spatial [state degeneracy](../../../../../../state-degeneracy.md) $2J+1$.

The [translational partition functions](../../../../../../translational-partition-function.md) and all common electronic/vibrational factors cancel in the ratio of equilibrium populations. Using the same zero of [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) for the two [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) species gives

$$
\boxed{K=\frac{n_o}{n_p}=\frac{q_o}{q_p}
=\frac{3\sum_{J\ {\rm odd}}(2J+1)e^{-J(J+1)\theta_{\rm rot}/T}}
{\sum_{J\ {\rm even}}(2J+1)e^{-J(J+1)\theta_{\rm rot}/T}}.}
$$

Subtracting the $J=1$ [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) separately from all odd levels without restoring that offset in the equilibrium ratio would incorrectly remove the low-temperature preference for [parahydrogen](../../../../../../parahydrogen.md). The [nuclear spin statistics](../../../../../../nuclear-spin-statistics.md) account for the factor three, independently of the spatial factor $2J+1$.
