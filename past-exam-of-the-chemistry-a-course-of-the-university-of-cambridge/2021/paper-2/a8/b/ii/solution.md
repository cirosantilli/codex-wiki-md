<h1 id="a8/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a high-temperature [linear rigid rotor](../../../../../../linear-rigid-rotor.md), $q_{\rm rot}=T/\theta_{\rm rot}$ up to temperature-independent symmetry/[spin](../../../../../../spin.md) factors. Therefore $q'/q=1/T$ and $q''=0$, giving

$$
\boxed{\frac{C_{V,m}^{\rm rot}}R=0-1+2=1.}
$$

This agrees with the [equipartition theorem](../../../../../../equipartition-theorem.md) for two quadratic rotational coordinates. It is the rotational contribution; adding free three-dimensional translation gives $C_{V,m}=5R/2$ when vibrations remain unexcited. A constant [rotational symmetry number](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry-number.md) does not affect this derivative.
