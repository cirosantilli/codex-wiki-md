<h1 id="a10/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Substitutional [arsenic](../../../../../../arsenic.md) has one more valence [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) than [germanium](../../../../../../germanium.md), so it acts as a [donor impurity](../../../../../../donor-impurity.md). Thermal ionization of its shallow bound state supplies mobile conduction-band [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and leaves fixed positive donor charge. The resulting [n-type semiconductor](../../../../../../n-type-semiconductor.md) can have $n_e\gg n_i$ even when only a small fraction of host atoms are replaced. Its [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) then rises because the mobile carrier count increases; the opposite donor charge preserves macroscopic [charge neutrality](../../../../../../charge-neutrality.md).

This argument assumes that the donors are substantially ionized at the measurement [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) and that the added impurity scattering does not overwhelm the carrier increase. The screened bound-donor picture and its departures from a simple hydrogenic model are described in institutional [semiconductor](../../../../../../semiconductor-split.md) spectroscopy notes.[Chapter 7, sections 7.2–7.3, printed pp. 76–79: hydrogenic impurities and limitations](../../../../../../optical-properties-of-solids-by-mildred-s-dresselhaus.md)
