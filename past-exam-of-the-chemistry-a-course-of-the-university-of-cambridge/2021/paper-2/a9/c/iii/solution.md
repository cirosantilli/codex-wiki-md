<h1 id="a9/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

An isolated spin-degenerate lowest [energy band](../../../../../../energy-band.md) accommodates two [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) per atom when there is one atom per primitive cell. Starting from one per atom, the estimate for filling the band is therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{one additional electron per Au atom}.}
$$

This corresponds to an additional areal carrier count of about $4.77\times10^{19}\ {\rm m^{-2}}$. If a genuine [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md) separates the filled first band from the next band, there are no partially occupied band states at low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), so ordinary metallic conduction is lost.

Simply touching an edge is insufficient. In the circular approximation first contact would occur at $k_F=\pi/a$, corresponding to $n_ea^2=\pi/2\simeq1.57$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) per cell; that is a partly filled band and need not be insulating. Near zone boundaries, the periodic potential reshapes the free-electron contours. The two-electron filling estimate assumes a global gap without overlapping bands, rather than inferring an insulator merely from one local avoided crossing.
