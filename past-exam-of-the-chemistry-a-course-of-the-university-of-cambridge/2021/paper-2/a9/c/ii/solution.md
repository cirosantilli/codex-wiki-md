<h1 id="a9/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For one [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per square cell, $n_e=1/a^2$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{k_F}{\pi/a}=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\simeq0.7979<1.}
$$

Numerically, $\pi/a\simeq2.17\times10^{10}\ {\rm m^{-1}}$ exceeds $k_F\simeq1.73\times10^{10}\ {\rm m^{-1}}$, so the [fermi circle](../../../../../../fermi-circle.md) lies entirely inside the [first Brillouin zone](../../../../../../first-brillouin-zone.md).

The state-counting explanation is that a spin-degenerate band has two [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) states per primitive cell. One [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per cell therefore occupies half of that band. In the circular free-electron approximation the occupied reciprocal-space area is

$$
\frac{\pi k_F^2}{(2\pi/a)^2}=\frac12.
$$

A disk of that area fits inside this square: its radius is less than the distance to an edge. Half filling alone is not a shape-independent theorem that every possible [Fermi surface](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) must avoid every zone boundary; the square geometry and circular free-electron dispersion make the statement true here.
