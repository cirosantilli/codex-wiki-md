<h1 id="a9/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Metallic atomic densities place very many [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) in a small volume. The [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) prevents all these [fermions](../../../../../../fermion.md) occupying the lowest spatial state, so filling the available states produces a [Fermi energy](../../../../../../fermi-energy.md) of order electronvolts. At room [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), $k_{\rm B}T\simeq0.026\ {\rm eV}$, much smaller than that scale. Equivalently, **ordinary metals are at $T\ll T_F=E_F/k_{\rm B}$**, where $T_F$ is the [Fermi temperature](../../../../../../fermi-temperature.md). Their [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) are quantum degenerate even at ordinary laboratory temperatures.
