<h1 id="a7/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For monatomic species, there are no molecular rotational or vibrational factors. Assume excited electronic states are negligible, so the internal partition sums are the ground-state degeneracies. A free [electron](../../../../../electron.md) has two [spin](../../../../../spin.md) states, giving

$$
f_e=2\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_{\rm B}T}{h^2}\right)^{3/2},\qquad
f_{\rm M}=g_{\rm M}\left(\frac{2\pi m_{\rm M}k_{\rm B}T}{h^2}\right)^{3/2},
$$

with the analogous expression for the ion. Since losing one [electron](../../../../../electron.md) changes the atomic mass negligibly, $(m_{\rm M^+}/m_{\rm M})^{3/2}\simeq1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{K_c=\left(\frac{2\pi m_ek_{\rm B}T}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}
\frac1{c^\circ}\frac{2g_{\rm M^+}}{g_{\rm M}}e^{-E_I/k_{\rm B}T}.}
$$

This is the ground-state form of the [Saha ionization equation](../../../../../saha-ionization-equation.md). Its assumptions include a dilute, nondegenerate ideal mixture, negligible interparticle interactions, thermal and [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md), no additional ionization stages, and consistent treatment of internal degeneracies. Nuclear-spin factors may be omitted when they cancel between the atom and ion; they must not be counted on only one side.
