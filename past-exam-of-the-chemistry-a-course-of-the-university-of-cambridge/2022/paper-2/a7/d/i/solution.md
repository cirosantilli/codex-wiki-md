<h1 id="a7/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\alpha$ be the [degree of ionization](../../../../../../degree-of-ionization.md). Conservation of nuclei and [charge neutrality](../../../../../../charge-neutrality.md), with no pre-existing [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) or other ions, give

$$
c_{\rm M}=(1-\alpha)c_{\rm tot},\qquad
c_{\rm M^+}=c_e=\alpha c_{\rm tot}.
$$

Substitution into the definition of $K_c$ gives

$$
\frac{\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}=y,\qquad
 y=\frac{K_cc^\circ}{c_{\rm tot}}.
$$

The physical root lies between zero and one:

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{y^2+4y}-y}{2}
=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+4/y}}.}
$$

The second form avoids subtracting nearly equal terms at large $y$. The limits are $\alpha\simeq\sqrt y$ when $y\ll1$ and $\alpha\simeq1-1/y$ when $y\gg1$. The dependence on initial [number density](../../../../../../number-density.md) matters: $K_c$ alone, without $c_{\rm tot}$ and the standard-state convention, does not specify the ionized fraction.
