<h1 id="a10/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

There are three useful [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) regimes.

At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), [dopant freeze-out](../../../../../../dopant-freeze-out.md) keeps most donor [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) bound. Warming ionizes donors and increases conductivity through the rising mobile-electron concentration. For an uncompensated dilute donor system, negligible holes and [spin degeneracy](../../../../../../spin-degeneracy.md) two, [charge neutrality with partial donor ionization](../../../../../../charge-neutrality-with-partial-donor-ionization.md) gives

$$
\frac{n^2}{N_D-n}=\frac{N_c}{2}e^{-E_b/k_{\rm B}T}.
$$

Deep in freeze-out, $n\ll N_D$, so

$$
n\simeq\sqrt{\frac{N_DN_c}{2}}e^{-E_b/(2k_{\rm B}T)}.
$$

The activation scale is set by the shallow donor binding, rather than the whole [band gap](../../../../../../band-gap.md).

In the extrinsic regime, donors are largely ionized and $n\simeq N_D$ is nearly constant. The [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence of conductivity then mainly follows the [electron mobility](../../../../../../electron-mobility.md). Increasing [phonon](../../../../../../phonon.md) scattering can make conductivity fall as [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) rises, even though donors remain ionized; impurity scattering can give a different mobility trend at lower [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md).

At sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), thermally generated [electron](../../../../../../electron.md)–hole pairs overwhelm the dopant population. **The intrinsic, gap-activated increase in conductivity returns.** The boundaries depend on donor concentration, compensation and scattering, so no one exponential describes the entire doped-sample curve.
