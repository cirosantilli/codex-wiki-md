<h1 id="a10/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The extra [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) is bound to an approximately singly charged donor through a dielectric-screened Coulomb attraction. In the isotropic [hydrogenic donor state](../../../../../../hydrogenic-donor-state.md) model, replace the vacuum [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) mass by $m^*$ and the vacuum permittivity by $\epsilon_r\epsilon_0$:

$$
E_n=-\frac{R^*}{n^2},\qquad
\boxed{R^*=R_H\frac{m^*/m_e}{\epsilon_r^2}.}
$$

At low [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), the donor is mainly in its $1s$ state. Allowed electric-dipole transitions to $np$ states have

$$
h\nu_n=R^*\left(1-\frac1{n^2}\right).
$$

Assign the three printed lines to $n=2,3,4$. Their independently inferred binding energies are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
h\nu/\mathrm{meV}&n&R^*/\mathrm{meV}\\\hline
7.97&2&7.97/(3/4)=10.6267\\
9.44&3&9.44/(8/9)=10.6200\\
9.97&4&9.97/(15/16)=10.6347
\end{array}
$$

They are mutually close, giving **$R^*\simeq10.63\ \mathrm{meV}$**. The [hydrogenic impurity absorption](../../../../../../hydrogenic-impurity-absorption.md) series accumulates at the donor-ionization limit. **Above about $10.6\ \mathrm{meV}$, absorption can place the [electron](../../../../../../electron.md) in the conduction-band continuum**, explaining the continuous spectrum.

Using the supplied $m^*=0.2m_e$ and $R_H=13.61\ \mathrm{eV}$,

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_r\simeq\sqrt{\frac{(0.2)(13.61)}{0.01063}}\simeq16.0.}
$$

The corresponding effective radius is

$$
a^*=a_0\frac{\epsilon_r}{m^*/m_e}\simeq80a_0\simeq\boxed{4.23\ \mathrm{nm}}.
$$

These are the parameters inferred from the printed lines within the stipulated scalar model, rather than substituted tabulated material constants. Real impurity spectra can depart from a single hydrogenic ladder through anisotropy and short-distance impurity effects.[Chapter 7, sections 7.2–7.3, printed pp. 76–79: screened hydrogenic levels and departures from the model](../../../../../../optical-properties-of-solids-by-mildred-s-dresselhaus.md)
