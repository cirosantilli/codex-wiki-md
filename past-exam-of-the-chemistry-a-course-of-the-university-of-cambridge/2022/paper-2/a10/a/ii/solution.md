<h1 id="a10/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In a [nondegenerate semiconductor](../../../../../../nondegenerate-semiconductor.md), the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) reduces near the conduction edge to $f(E)\simeq e^{-(E-E_F)/k_{\rm B}T}$. The corresponding dilute hole probability near the valence edge gives

$$
n=N_c e^{-(E_c-E_F)/k_{\rm B}T},\qquad
p=N_v e^{-(E_F-E_v)/k_{\rm B}T}.
$$

An intrinsic, neutral sample has $n=p=n_i$. Multiplying the two expressions therefore yields

$$
\boxed{n_i=\sqrt{N_cN_v}\,e^{-E_g/(2k_{\rm B}T)}.}
$$

Each excitation creates an [electron-hole pair](../../../../../../electron-hole-pair.md); [charge neutrality](../../../../../../charge-neutrality.md) is why the carrier concentration has half the gap in its activation exponent. The [electrical conductivity](../../../../../../electrical-conductivity.md) is

$$
\sigma=e(n\mu_e+p\mu_h)
=e(\mu_e+\mu_h)\sqrt{N_cN_v}\,e^{-E_g/(2k_{\rm B}T)}.
$$

Thus **$\sigma\propto e^{-E_g/(2k_{\rm B}T)}$ to leading exponential accuracy**. The prefactor contains the mobilities and the density-of-states [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence; for fixed parabolic masses, $\sqrt{N_cN_v}\propto T^{3/2}$.

Measure conductivity in the intrinsic regime and plot $\ln\sigma$ against $1/T$. If the prefactor is constant or its [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence has been corrected, a slope $m$ gives

$$
\boxed{E_g=-2k_{\rm B}m.}
$$

Use the intrinsic branch rather than a donor freeze-out or extrinsic branch. A temperature-dependent gap or mobility changes the apparent activation [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), so a precision extraction must account for those prefactors.
