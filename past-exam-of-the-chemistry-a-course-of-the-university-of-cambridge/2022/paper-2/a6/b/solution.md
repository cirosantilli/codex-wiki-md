↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $x=\mu_{\rm B}B/(k_{\rm B}T)$. Summing the two [Boltzmann factors](../../../../../boltzmann-factor.md) gives the occupied-site internal [partition function](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md)

$$
\boxed{z=e^{-\varepsilon_+/k_{\rm B}T}+e^{-\varepsilon_-/k_{\rm B}T}
=2e^{E_b/k_{\rm B}T}\cosh x.}
$$

If the single atom can occupy any of the $M$ identical sites, its complete surface [partition function](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{q_{\rm ads}=Mz=2M e^{E_b/k_{\rm B}T}\cosh x.}
$$

One also commonly calls the one-site factor $z$ itself $q_{\rm ads}$. Both conventions are valid, but the combinatorial factor in part (c) must use the same convention. We use the complete one-atom surface function $q_{\rm ads}=Mz$ below and give the equivalent one-site expression.
