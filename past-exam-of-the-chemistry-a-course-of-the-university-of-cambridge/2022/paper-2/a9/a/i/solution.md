<h1 id="a9/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the orthogonal nearest-neighbour [tight-binding model](../../../../../../tight-binding.md),

$$
\alpha_s=\langle s_j|H|s_j\rangle,\qquad
\beta_s=\langle s_j|H|s_{j+1}\rangle.
$$

The on-site integral $\alpha_s$ sets the band centre, while the [hopping integral](../../../../../../hopping-integral.md) $\beta_s$ describes coupling between neighbouring orbitals. With a consistent phase convention, the bonding combination at the zone centre has $\beta_s<0$.

For [p orbitals](../../../../../../p-orbital.md) directed along the chain, use

$$
\boxed{E_p(k)=\alpha_p+2\beta_p\cos(ka),\qquad \beta_p>0.}
$$

Here every positive $p$ lobe points in the same spatial direction. Neighbouring equal-sign coefficients then bring opposite-sign facing lobes together and make the zone-centre combination antibonding; the alternating zone-boundary combination is bonding. Thus the usual axial $p$ hopping has the opposite sign to $s$ hopping. [Orbital phase and the sign of one-dimensional hopping](../../../../../../orbital-phase-and-the-sign-of-one-dimensional-hopping.md) must be treated consistently; replacing only $\alpha_s$ by $\alpha_p$ while silently keeping the same physical hopping sign would give the wrong band ordering.
