<h1 id="a3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The expanded feature resolves the [Q branch](../../../../../../q-branch.md): each line has $\Delta J=0$ but starts from a different lower rotational level. In the simple [rigid rotor](../../../../../../rigid-rotor.md) band model,

$$
\boxed{\widetilde\nu_Q(J)=\widetilde\nu_*+(B'_Q-B'')J(J+1).}
$$

Because the upper [rotational constant](../../../../../../rotational-constant.md) is larger, the lines extend towards higher wavenumber. Their adjacent spacings are

$$
\widetilde\nu_Q(J+1)-\widetilde\nu_Q(J)=2(B'_Q-B'')(J+1),
$$

so they crowd together at the low-frequency edge and become increasingly separated. A strictly resolved bending-band analysis may distinguish the upper parity component sampled by Q lines from that sampled by P/R lines; it is not necessary to impose identical high-resolution constants on every branch in this sketch.

The intensity envelope reflects rotational state populations and the rotational transition strength. For this perpendicular [Q branch](../../../../../../q-branch.md), the usual rotational line-strength factor gives the approximate dependence

$$
\boxed{I_Q(J)\propto(2J+1)
\exp\!\left[-\frac{hcB''J(J+1)}{k_{\rm B}T}\right].}
$$

At small $J$, the increasing magnetic degeneracy makes the line intensity rise; at large $J$, [Boltzmann suppression](../../../../../../boltzmann-suppression.md) dominates. The maximum is therefore at an intermediate $J$. This is a population envelope across distinct transitions, rather than one intrinsically broad vibrational line.
