<h1 id="a3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Count the successive [Q branch](../../../../../../q-branch.md) lines from the low-frequency edge, taking $J=1$ as the first allowed bend transition; the first lines are crowded and partly unresolved. The maximum near $712.55\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ is approximately **$J=12$**. Treating $J$ as continuous in the intensity envelope from part (i),

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dJ}\log I_Q(J)
=\frac2{2J+1}-\frac{hcB''}{k_{\rm B}T}(2J+1)=0.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{T\simeq\frac{hcB''}{2k_{\rm B}}(2J_{\max}+1)^2.}
$$

Using $hc/k_{\rm B}=1.43878\ \mathrm{K\,cm}$, $B''=1.47786\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and $J_{\max}\simeq12$ gives

$$
T\simeq\frac{(1.43878)(1.47786)}2(25)^2
\simeq664\ \mathrm K.
$$

A useful check avoids locating the exact maximum. The plotted $J\simeq20$ line has roughly $0.70$ of the $J\simeq12$ peak height. Thus

$$
\frac{I_{20}}{I_{12}}\simeq\frac{41}{25}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{hcB''(420-156)}{k_{\rm B}T}\right]
$$

gives about $659\ \mathrm K$. **An appropriate graphical estimate is $T\simeq6.6\times10^2\ \mathrm K$**, with precision limited by line counting, line overlap and reading the plotted intensities. There is no basis for reporting a temperature to the nearest kelvin from this figure.
