↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The resolved lines arise from simultaneous changes of vibration and rotation in [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md). A vibrational fundamental connects many populated lower rotational levels to different upper rotational levels, giving the fine structure of a [rovibrational spectrum](../../../../../../rovibrational-spectrum.md).

The prominent central [Q branch](../../../../../../q-branch.md), together with flanking [P branch](../../../../../../p-branch.md) and [R branch](../../../../../../r-branch.md) lines, identifies a perpendicular band. For linear HCN, this is the **doubly degenerate bending fundamental**, not either stretching fundamental. Bending creates a transition-dipole component perpendicular to the molecular axis; its [vibrational angular momentum](../../../../../../vibrational-angular-momentum.md) permits $\Delta J=0$ as well as $\Delta J=\pm1$.

The two perpendicular bending coordinates share one vibrational frequency. Their degeneracy is different from the series of resolved rotational transitions superimposed on that fundamental. The stretches have their [electric dipole transition moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-transition-moment.md) along the molecular axis and would give the usual parallel-band P/R pattern without this strong central Q feature.
