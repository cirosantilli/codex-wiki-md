<h1 id="a4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The uniform planar eight-electron ring has a partially filled frontier degeneracy. [Bond-length alternation](../../../../../../bond-length-alternation.md) splits those levels, permitting the two frontier [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) to occupy the lowered orbital and opening a gap to the empty partner. This is the electronic mechanism for destabilizing the equal-bond planar geometry.

A controlled comparison keeps the mean resonance integral fixed. Put

$$
\beta_S=\beta(1+\delta),\qquad
\beta_L=\beta(1-\delta),\qquad \beta<0,\quad0<\delta<1.
$$

Using part (i), the change from the uniform ring is

$$
\Delta E_\pi=4\beta\delta
+4\sqrt2\,\beta\big(\sqrt{1+\delta^2}-1\big)
=4\beta\delta+2\sqrt2\,\beta\delta^2+O(\delta^4)<0.
$$

Thus **a small alternation lowers the electronic [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md)**, with a leading gain linear in $\delta$. An ordinary elastic cost starts quadratically, so the equal-bond planar state is unstable in this model. The equilibrium distortion requires those geometric and sigma-bond costs as well.

The eight $\pi$ [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) constitute a $4n$ cyclic count rather than the closed-shell $4n+2$ case associated with [aromaticity](../../../../../../aromaticity.md). The result concerns the imposed planar geometry; it does not establish the unconstrained three-dimensional equilibrium shape of [cyclooctatetraene](../../../../../../cyclooctatetraene.md).
