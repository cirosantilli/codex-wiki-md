<h1 id="a2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

- The [atomic nuclei](../../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) are much heavier than [electrons](../../../../../../electron.md), so nuclear motion is slower. In the [Born-Oppenheimer approximation](../../../../../../born-oppenheimer-approximation.md), first hold the nuclear positions $\mathbf R$ fixed and solve$$
  \hat H_e(\mathbf r;\mathbf R)\psi_n(\mathbf r;\mathbf R)
  =U_n(\mathbf R)\psi_n(\mathbf r;\mathbf R).
  $$
- Approximate the complete [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function-split.md) by $\Psi(\mathbf r,\mathbf R)=\psi_n(\mathbf r;\mathbf R)\chi_n(\mathbf R)$ and neglect the nuclear-derivative couplings involving $\nabla_{\mathbf R}\psi_n$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf R}^2\psi_n$.
- Nuclear motion then occurs on one electronic [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) surface:$$
  \boxed{\left[\hat T_N+U_n(\mathbf R)+V_{NN}(\mathbf R)\right]\chi_n=E\chi_n.}
  $$

  Here $U_n$ excludes the nuclear repulsion $V_{NN}$, which must be included once.
- The mass separation makes the discarded coupling terms small when electronic [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) gaps are sufficiently large; near a degeneracy or avoided crossing, a single-surface description can fail.
