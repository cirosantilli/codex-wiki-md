<h1 id="a1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

With the physics convention $\langle\Phi|\Psi\rangle=\int\Phi^*\Psi\,\mathrm d\tau$, the [adjoint operator](../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) is defined by

$$
\boxed{\langle\Phi|\hat A\Psi\rangle
=\langle\hat A^\dagger\Phi|\Psi\rangle.}
$$

This definition includes the operator domains and their [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md). If $\hat A$ is a [Hermitian operator](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) on the common domain, then $\hat A^\dagger\Phi=\hat A\Phi$. Conjugate symmetry of the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) consequently gives

$$
\langle\Phi|\hat A\Psi\rangle
=\langle\hat A\Phi|\Psi\rangle
=\langle\Psi|\hat A\Phi\rangle^*,
$$

which is the required integral identity.

The [boundary conditions](../../../../../boundary-condition.md) must make the boundary form from [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) vanish for every pair of allowed functions. For a Schrödinger kinetic operator in one dimension, this means

$$
\left[\Phi^*\Psi'-(\Phi')^*\Psi\right]_{\rm boundary}=0.
$$

Sufficient examples are decay of the functions and derivatives at spatial infinity, homogeneous hard-wall conditions $\Phi=\Psi=0$ at finite endpoints, or matching periodic conditions on both functions and their derivatives. The exact boundary form depends on $\hat A$; square integrability alone does not replace the domain requirement.
