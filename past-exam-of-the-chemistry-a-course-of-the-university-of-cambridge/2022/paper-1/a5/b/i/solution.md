<h1 id="a5/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The transition strength depends on $|R_{ji}|^2$, so a necessary condition is that at least one Cartesian [electric dipole transition moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-transition-moment.md) is nonzero. An integral can be nonzero only if its integrand contains the [totally symmetric representation](../../../../../../totally-symmetric-representation.md):

$$
\Gamma_f^*\otimes\Gamma(\mu_a)\otimes\Gamma_i\supset A_{1g}.
$$

The harmonic vibrational [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) is totally symmetric, and a one-quantum state of [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) $k$ transforms as $\Gamma^{(k)}$. For the real [irreducible representations](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) here, the fundamental is therefore symmetry-allowed precisely when

$$
\boxed{\Gamma^{(k)}\text{ occurs among the Cartesian dipole components.}}
$$

Equivalently, expand the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) near equilibrium:

$$
\mu_a(\mathbf Q)=\mu_a(0)+\sum_k
\left(\frac{\partial\mu_a}{\partial Q_k}\right)_0Q_k+\cdots.
$$

The fundamental has $\langle1_k|Q_k|0_k\rangle\ne0$, so [vibrational infrared activity](../../../../../../vibrational-infrared-activity.md) requires a nonzero dipole derivative of the matching symmetry. In $D_{6h}$, $z\sim A_{2u}$ and $(x,y)\sim E_{1u}$, giving **$A_{2u}$ and $E_{1u}$ as the IR-active species**. Symmetry permission does not guarantee a large band intensity.
