<h1 id="a5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [electronic transition](../../../../../../electronic-transition.md) starts from $A_{1g}$, so the electric-dipole test reduces to whether the final electronic [irreducible representation](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) occurs among $A_{2u}+E_{1u}$. The excited $E_{1u}$ state matches the $(x,y)$ dipole components and is therefore **electric-dipole allowed**.

The $B_{2u}$ state matches neither $z$ nor $(x,y)$ and is **electric-dipole forbidden** at the equilibrium geometry. Both excited states are singlets, so the difference is spatial symmetry rather than the [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) selection rule. Inversion parity alone does not decide the result: both final states are ungerade, but only the matching vector representation is allowed.
