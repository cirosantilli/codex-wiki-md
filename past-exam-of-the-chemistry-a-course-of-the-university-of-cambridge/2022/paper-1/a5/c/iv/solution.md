<h1 id="a5/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The initial [vibronic state](../../../../../../vibronic-state.md) is $A_{1g}$ and the final one is $E_{1u}$, so an in-plane dipole component satisfies

$$
E_{1u}\otimes E_{1u}\otimes A_{1g}
=A_{1g}+A_{2g}+E_{2g}.
$$

The product contains the [totally symmetric representation](../../../../../../totally-symmetric-representation.md). **The combined electronic–vibrational transition is therefore symmetry-allowed**, with an in-plane [electric dipole transition moment](../../../../../../electric-dipole-transition-moment.md).

A nonzero intensity still needs [vibronic coupling](../../../../../../vibronic-coupling.md). In a coordinate-independent separation, the forbidden electronic [matrix element](../../../../../../matrix-element.md) would remain zero. Its first derivative with respect to the $E_{2g}$ coordinate can instead be nonzero, and the vibrational factor $\langle1|Q|0\rangle$ supplies the required one-quantum transition. This is [vibrational activation of an electronic transition](../../../../../../vibrational-activation-of-an-electronic-transition.md) and explains why the band can be weak.
