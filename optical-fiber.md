# Optical fiber

↑ **Parent:** [Optical instrument](optical-instrument-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fiber)

An [optical fiber](optical-fiber.md) is a thin, flexible [electromagnetic waveguide](electromagnetic-waveguide.md) that carries [light](light.md) along its length. In a conventional [step-index optical fiber](step-index-optical-fiber.md), an [optical fiber core](optical-fiber-core.md) with [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n_1$ is surrounded by an [optical fiber cladding](optical-fiber-cladding.md) with lower [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n_2$. Suitable [light rays](light-ray.md) remain confined by [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md) at this interface. The cladding is an optical part of the guide, distinct from its protective coating.[Introduction to Optical Fibers, dB, Attenuation and Measurements, Optical Fiber Structure, Fig. 1](introduction-to-optical-fibers-db-attenuation-and-measurements-by-cisco.md)

The useful surprise is that a transparent strand can deliver [light](light.md) around a route that a free beam would leave. This makes [optical fibers](optical-fiber.md) useful for communications and for carrying collected [light](light.md) to a [fiber-fed spectrograph](fiber-fed-spectrograph.md). Guidance, successful launch, and surviving [optical power](optical-power.md) are separate questions: the [optical fiber acceptance angle](optical-fiber-acceptance-angle.md) constrains launch geometry, while [optical fiber attenuation](optical-fiber-attenuation.md) describes loss along the route.
