# Rational torsion on y squared equals x cubed plus positive k x

↑ **Parent:** [Torsion point of an elliptic curve](torsion-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md)

For positive integral $k$, the rational [torsion subgroup](torsion-subgroup.md) of $y^2=x^3+kx$ has order dividing four. Indeed, [torsion-freeness of the formal group over Qp for odd p](torsion-freeness-of-the-formal-group-over-qp-for-odd-p.md), [reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve](reduction-of-torsion-points-on-an-elliptic-curve.md), and the [point-count criterion for y squared equals x cubed plus k x](point-count-criterion-for-y-squared-equals-x-cubed-plus-k-x.md) bound its order by $p+1$ at all good [primes](prime-number.md) $p\equiv3\pmod4$. The [Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](dirichlet-s-theorem-on-arithmetic-progressions.md) excludes every odd torsion [prime](prime-number.md) and bounds the two-primary part by four, by choosing $p\equiv3\pmod8$. There is exactly one nonzero rational [2-torsion](2-torsion.md) point, namely $(0,0)$. A rational half of it must have $x^2=k$ by the [elliptic-curve addition formula](elliptic-curve-addition-formula.md). Writing $k=s^2$ with $s>0$, its equation is $y^2=2s^3$, so $s=2t^2$ and $k=4t^4$. Conversely $(2t^2,4t^3)$ has order four. Thus the cyclic group of order four occurs exactly for $k=4t^4$, $t\geq1$. The order bound itself holds for every nonzero integral $k$ of either sign: positivity was used only to classify the rational points of orders two and four.
