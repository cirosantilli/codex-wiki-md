<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

In [pulse-echo ultrasound](../../../../../pulse-echo-ultrasound.md), a transducer emits a pulse and records returning echoes. For a straight round-trip path in a medium of assumed [sound speed](../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) $c$, an echo delay $t$ gives depth $z=ct/2$. [Reflections](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) depend on acoustic-impedance changes, while attenuation, scattering and propagation geometry affect the received amplitude.

An [A-mode ultrasound scan](../../../../../a-mode-ultrasound-scan.md) displays echo amplitude against inferred depth along one beam. A [B-mode ultrasound scan](../../../../../b-mode-ultrasound-scan.md) maps received amplitude to brightness and assembles many beam directions or translated beam lines into a spatial image. An [M-mode ultrasound scan](../../../../../m-mode-ultrasound-scan.md) repeats one beam line and displays its depth profile versus time, making motion along that line visible. Thus the display axes and acquisition pattern differ even when the pulse-echo principle is shared.

For a pulse lasting $\tau_p$, a simple axial-separation estimate is $c\tau_p/2$. Lateral resolution depends on aperture, wavelength, focusing and depth. Shorter pulses and broader bandwidth can improve axial resolution; an assumed [sound speed](../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) or a multiple-reflection path can instead misplace an echo. Brightness is not a direct universal map of composition.

[Doppler ultrasound](../../../../../doppler-ultrasound.md) uses [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) or pulse-to-pulse [phase](../../../../../phase-waves.md) changes from moving scatterers. In a simple backscatter geometry, $\Delta f\simeq2f_0v\cos\theta/c$. Inferring [speed](../../../../../speed.md) requires the beam-to-motion angle; pulsed sampling can alias frequencies, and an unknown angle leaves a velocity-projection ambiguity. This describes generic acoustic acquisition rather than a clinical diagnosis.
