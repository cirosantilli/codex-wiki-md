The generic physics of [positron emission tomography](../../../../../positron-emission-tomography.md) uses [electron-positron annihilation](../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation.md). A slowly moving electron-positron pair predominantly produces two nearly opposite [photons](../../../../../photon.md) with [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) close to $m_ec^2=511\,\mathrm{keV}$ each. A ring of detectors records approximately simultaneous interactions; a coincidence identifies a [coincidence line of response](../../../../../coincidence-line-of-response.md) along which an annihilation could have occurred. Finite [Positron](../../../../../positron.md) travel before annihilation and nonzero pair [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) limit how directly this line identifies the original emission location.

Many such measurements constrain an emission distribution through a [tomography](../../../../../tomography.md) model. Attenuation, scattered coincidences, unrelated random coincidences, finite detector resolution and dead time alter the observed counts and require [calibration](../../../../../calibration-differential-geometry.md) or modeling. With useful time-of-flight information, the arrival-time difference further localizes [position](../../../../../position.md) along the line: a timing uncertainty $\delta t$ corresponds to a [position](../../../../../position.md) scale $c\delta t/2$. The coincidence data therefore provide spatial constraints, rather than each event directly supplying a complete three-dimensional image point.
