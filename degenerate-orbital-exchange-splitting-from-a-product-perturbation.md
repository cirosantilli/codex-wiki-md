# Degenerate orbital exchange splitting from a product perturbation

↑ **Parent:** [Degenerate perturbation theory](degenerate-perturbation-theory.md)

Two distinguishable particles with equal one-particle spectra have degenerate ordered orbital products $|r,s\rangle$ and $|s,r\rangle$ for $r\ne s$. Suppose both individual position means vanish, as in a centred parity-symmetric well. The perturbation $\alpha x_1x_2$ then has zero diagonal [matrix elements](matrix-element.md), but its restriction to this two-dimensional subspace is

$$
W=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}0&|x_{rs}|^2\\|x_{rs}|^2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad x_{rs}=\langle r|x|s\rangle.
$$

[Degenerate perturbation theory](degenerate-perturbation-theory.md) gives normalized symmetric and antisymmetric [eigenfunctions](eigenfunction.md) $(|r,s\rangle\pm|s,r\rangle)/\sqrt2$ with shifts $\pm\alpha|x_{rs}|^2$. Thus vanishing diagonal expectations in a chosen degenerate basis do not imply vanishing first-order energy shifts. Distinguishability permits both exchange symmetries; it does not forbid their energy splitting.

For an [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) of width $a$, the first two orbitals have $|x_{12}|^2=256a^2/(81\pi^4)$ by the [position matrix elements in a symmetric infinite square well](position-matrix-elements-in-a-symmetric-infinite-square-well.md). If the particles are identical [spin one-half](spin-one-half.md) [fermions](fermion.md), the spatially symmetric and antisymmetric combinations must instead be paired with the [singlet state](singlet-state.md) and [triplet states](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md), respectively. A spin-independent perturbation leaves each allowed spin multiplicity intact.
