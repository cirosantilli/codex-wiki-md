# Time dilation

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_dilation)

If one clock measures a [proper time](proper-time.md) interval $\Delta\tau$ while moving at constant [speed](speed.md) $v$ in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), that frame measures $\Delta t=\gamma\Delta\tau$, where $\gamma$ is the [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md). The two [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) must lie on that clock's [world line](world-line.md).

Suppose a clock moving at $0.6c$ advances by $4\,\mu\mathrm s$ between two ticks. Set $t=x=t'=x'=0$ at the first tick. The laboratory's synchronized clocks assign $\Delta t=1.25\times4=5\,\mu\mathrm s$. During that interval the clock travels $\Delta x=0.6c\times5\,\mu\mathrm s=3c\,\mu\mathrm s$, approximately $899.38\,\mathrm m$. The [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md) checks the result:

$$
s^2=-c^2(5\,\mu\mathrm s)^2+(3c\,\mu\mathrm s)^2=-c^2(4\,\mu\mathrm s)^2.
$$

Both frames therefore recover the same $4\,\mu\mathrm s$ of [proper time](proper-time.md). This calculation is useful whenever a process is timed on a moving apparatus but described using laboratory clocks.

A [light clock](light-clock.md) makes the mechanism visible. Two mirrors separated by $h$ perpendicular to the motion exchange a pulse; define one tick as its complete return to the lower mirror. In the clock's [rest frame](rest-frame.md) the tick takes $\Delta\tau=2h/c$. In the laboratory each half-tick lasts $T$ and the light traverses a diagonal, so the [Pythagorean theorem](pythagorean-theorem.md) gives

$$
(cT)^2=h^2+(vT)^2,\qquad T=\gamma h/c,\qquad \Delta t=2T=\gamma\Delta\tau.
$$

For $h=1\,\mathrm m$ and $v=0.6c$, each leg covers $1.25\,\mathrm m$ while the mirrors move $0.75\,\mathrm m$. The return tick lasts $2.5\,\mathrm m/c$ instead of $2\,\mathrm m/c$: the light has the same [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) in both accounts. [Joe Wolfe, UNSW: time dilation and the light clock](https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/einsteinlight/jw/module4_time_dilation.htm).

Reciprocal [time dilation](time-dilation.md) is consistent: each [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) times the other frame's single moving clock using its own synchronized clock network. These comparisons use different simultaneous [spacetime events](event-relativity.md). Comparing reunited clocks instead requires their complete [world lines](world-line.md), as in the [twin paradox](twin-paradox.md).
