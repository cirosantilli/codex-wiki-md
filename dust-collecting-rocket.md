# Dust-collecting rocket

↑ **Parent:** [Rocket equation](rocket-equation.md)

A classical rocket ejecting fuel at rate $\alpha>0$ and relative exhaust [speed](speed.md) $u>0$, while collecting stationary dust of [mass](mass.md) $\beta\,dx$ per traveled distance, obeys

$$
\dot m=\beta v-\alpha,\qquad m\dot v=\alpha u-\beta v^2.
$$

These are [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md) and [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md) for the rocket, incoming dust and outgoing gas. The dust-loading term supplies the [momentum](momentum.md) needed to accelerate incoming stationary material. Put $\lambda=\sqrt{\alpha/(\beta u)}$ and $V=\lambda u$. While the engines operate and $0\leq v<V$, an initially stationary rocket satisfies

$$
\frac m{m_0}=\left(\frac{V-v}{V}\right)^{(\lambda-1)/2}\left(\frac{V+v}{V}\right)^{-(\lambda+1)/2}.
$$

For $\lambda>1$, continuing to $V$ would drive total [mass](mass.md) to zero. For $0<\lambda<1$, total [mass](mass.md) instead grows without bound and $V$ is approached only after infinite time under indefinitely maintained thrust. In both cases a finite fuel supply runs out before that limiting [speed](speed.md). Total stored [mass](mass.md) must not be confused with remaining fuel.
