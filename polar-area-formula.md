# Polar area formula

↑ **Parent:** [Polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md)

Suppose $0\le r_1(\theta)\le r_2(\theta)$ and the region is described without overlap by $\alpha\le\theta\le\beta$ and $r_1(\theta)\le r\le r_2(\theta)$. Integrating the [polar area element](polar-area-element.md) gives its [area](surface-area.md):

$$
A=\int_\alpha^\beta\int_{r_1(\theta)}^{r_2(\theta)}r\,dr\,d\theta
=\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta\left(r_2(\theta)^2-r_1(\theta)^2\right)\,d\theta.
$$

For a region bounded by a single radial graph and two rays, take $r_1=0$. If the two bounding graphs cross, split the angular interval at their intersections so that the larger radius is used consistently.
