# SIR first integral

↑ **Parent:** [SIR model](sir-model.md)

For $s>0$, the [SIR model equations](sir-model-equations.md) have the [first integral of an ordinary differential equation](first-integral-of-an-ordinary-differential-equation.md)

$$
i(t)+s(t)-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\ln s(t)
=i_0+s_0-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\ln s_0.
$$

Here $\ln$ is the [natural logarithm](natural-logarithm.md). A short proof outline is to use the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) and substitute the [SIR model equations](sir-model-equations.md):

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(i+s-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\ln s\right)
=-\gamma i-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\frac{-\beta si}{s}=0.
$$

The [SIR first integral](sir-first-integral.md) gives $i$ directly from $s$, so it describes the curve in a [phase portrait](phase-portrait.md) even before its time parametrization is known.
