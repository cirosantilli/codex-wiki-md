# Formula unit

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical formula](chemical-formula.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_unit)

A [formula unit](formula-unit.md) is a specified compositional accounting entity for a [chemical substance](chemical-substance-split.md) that need not consist of discrete [molecules](molecule-split.md). For ordinary crystalline $\mathrm{NaCl}$, one [formula unit](formula-unit.md) counts one [sodium](sodium.md) [ion](ion.md) $\mathrm{Na}^+$ and one [chlorine](chlorine.md) [ion](ion.md) $\mathrm{Cl}^-$; it does not identify an isolated pair within the crystal. [Cohen et al., IUPAC Green Book, 3rd ed., §2.10.1(iii)(b), p. 51](https://iupac.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/IUPAC-GB3-2012-2ndPrinting-PDFsearchable.pdf#page=65) defines a [formula unit](formula-unit.md) as a specified group of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), rather than a unit of measurement. An [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) of one [mole](mole-unit.md) of $\mathrm{NaCl}$ [formula units](formula-unit.md) therefore corresponds to one [mole](mole-unit.md) of each kind of [ion](ion.md).
