**[Mirror neurons](../../../mirror-neuron.md) provide an observation–execution matching property that can contribute to action processing; their existence does not establish one exclusive function such as reading [intentions](../../../intention.md).** Plausible roles include linking seen actions to motor representations, predicting familiar action sequences and supporting [imitation](../../../imitation.md). The evidence is strongest when a specified task, representation and causal contribution are identified, and weaker when every social capacity is attributed to a broadly defined mirror system.

The original evidence was unusually direct. Single-neuron recordings in macaque premotor area F5 identified cells active both when the animal performed an action and when it observed a related action. Some observation–execution relations were relatively specific, whereas others generalized across aspects of the act. This is evidence about individual recorded cells, rather than merely overlapping brain regions. Yet the experimenter’s action label does not tell us exactly what the cell encodes: movement, object interaction, learned outcome and context can covary.[Summary and pp. 593–609: macaque F5 recordings and observation–execution congruence](../../../action-recognition-in-the-premotor-cortex-by-vittorio-gallese-and-others.md)

An important extension tested incomplete visual information. Some cells responded when the final object interaction was hidden but could be inferred from the preceding situation. Thus a full visual display of the critical movement was not always necessary for activation. This is compatible with an internally generated action representation or prediction. It is not proof that a [neuron](../../../neuron-split.md) understands another person’s [beliefs](../../../belief.md), nor that the representation is generated without input from other visual or contextual systems. Familiar-context prediction and mental-state attribution are distinguishable hypotheses.[Abstract and pp. 155–165: responses during visible and occluded action completion](../../../i-know-what-you-are-doing-a-neurophysiological-study-by-maria-alessandra-umilta-and-others.md)

Human evidence requires care about measurement scale. Functional imaging showing activation during both action observation and execution can involve different cell populations within one voxel. A [motor-evoked potential](../../../motor-evoked-potential.md) during observation measures a downstream excitability change, not one cell’s selectivity. Intracranial recordings in a clinical epilepsy sample did identify individual observation–execution matching responses, including in supplementary motor and medial-temporal regions. That establishes relevant human cellular responses in the sampled tissue. Electrode placement was clinically determined, however, so it does not supply a representative census of the putative inferior-frontal/parietal system or of the general population.[Abstract and Experimental Procedures: individual-unit responses, clinical sample and electrode-placement constraints](../../../single-neuron-responses-in-humans-during-execution-and-observation-of-actions-by-roy-mukamel-and-others.md)

A functional claim then needs a task contrast. Recognizing that a hand grasps an object, anticipating how a lift will unfold, judging the object’s weight and understanding a deceptive [intention](../../../intention.md) are not interchangeable forms of “[action understanding](../../../action-understanding.md).” Matching circuitry could improve one without being necessary for all. It may supply motor predictions that combine with visual object knowledge and contextual inference. Calling every successful judgment simulation would make the hypothesis difficult to falsify; the account must predict a deficit or response pattern that alternatives do not predict.

A stimulation experiment addressed a narrower causal question. Disrupting left inferior frontal cortex impaired judgments of a box’s weight from a person lifting it, with comparison tasks involving a bouncing ball and stimulus duration and comparison stimulation sites. These controls strengthen the inference that this region contributes to interpreting the action under those conditions. The manipulation still affected a cortical region, not selectively its [mirror neurons](../../../mirror-neuron.md). It therefore cannot establish that mirror cells are the sole mechanism, or that the region is necessary for all conceptual understanding of actions.[Summary and Results, pp. 524–529: weight judgments, nonhuman-motion/duration controls and stimulation sites](../../../action-understanding-requires-the-left-inferior-frontal-cortex-by-gorana-pobric-and-others.md)

Developmental origin is a separate question from present function. In counter-mirror training, participants repeatedly performed one finger action while observing another. Subsequent muscle-specific responses to motor-cortex stimulation changed from the usual matching pattern toward the trained mismatching relation. This supports experience-dependent calibration of the action-observation/motor relationship. It challenges an account in which every relevant mapping is fixed and independent of experience, while leaving open contributions from developmental constraints. The measure was a motor-evoked response, so the experiment did not directly reprogramme an individually recorded mirror cell.[Abstract and pp. 1527–1531: pre/post training muscle-specific responses and reversed sensorimotor pairing](../../../sensorimotor-learning-configures-the-human-mirror-system-by-caroline-catmur-and-others.md)

This evidence also separates function from adaptation. A learned matching circuit may currently assist prediction or action selection even if it was not selected by evolution specifically to understand minds. Conversely, showing a plausible contribution to [imitation](../../../imitation.md) does not show that every matching response exists for [imitation](../../../imitation.md). The same representation can be used in several tasks. Evolutionary stories require comparative or developmental evidence beyond the presence of useful response properties in an adult laboratory preparation.

Broad clinical explanations supply further tests. In one child study, several goal-imitation and action-representation measures were preserved in [autistic](../../../autism.md) participants despite poorer theory-of-mind performance. These behavioural results argue against a universal loss of action matching explaining every social difficulty. They do not demonstrate normality of every mirror population, because cells were not directly recorded. The important inference is that different social tasks cannot be assumed to stand in for one another.[Abstract: preserved action-representation measures alongside theory-of-mind differences](../../../imitation-and-action-understanding-in-autistic-spectrum-disorders-how-valid-is-the-hypothesis-of-a-deficit-in-the-mirror-neuron-system-by-antonia-hamilton-and-others.md)

The most defensible functional account is therefore contributory and conditional. Matching responses provide a route by which action observation can access action-related representations; [learning](../../../learning.md), context and task goals determine how those representations are used. Single-cell recordings establish the property, controlled training tests plasticity, and well-designed perturbations test task-specific necessity. A comprehensive theory must connect all three while allowing other systems to supply object knowledge, [language](../../../language.md), [attention](../../../attention.md) and mental-state inference. Existence, selectivity, developmental origin and behavioural usefulness are four questions, not four names for the same finding.
