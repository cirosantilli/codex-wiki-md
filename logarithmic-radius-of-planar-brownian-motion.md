# Logarithmic radius of planar Brownian motion

↑ **Parent:** [Planar Brownian motion](planar-brownian-motion.md)

Started at radius one, [planar Brownian motion](planar-brownian-motion.md) avoids the origin, and its logarithmic radius is a [continuous local martingale](continuous-local-martingale.md) because the logarithmic potential is harmonic away from zero. It is a [strict local martingale](strict-local-martingale.md): at deterministic $t>0$, the [angular average of a logarithmic potential](angular-average-of-a-logarithmic-potential.md) gives $\mathbb E\log|B_t|=\int_1^\infty e^{-r^2/(2t)}\,dr/r>0$, whereas the initial value is zero. Absolute integrability follows from integrability of $r|\log r|$ near zero and the Gaussian tail. Its positive [expectation](expected-value.md) does not contradict nonnegative-local-martingale bounds, since this process takes both signs.
