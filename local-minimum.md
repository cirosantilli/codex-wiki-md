# Local minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Critical point](critical-point.md)

A [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$, with $D\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, has a local minimum at $x_*$ if some $\delta>0$ satisfies

$$
f(x)\geq f(x_*)\qquad\text{for all }x\in D\text{ with }\|x-x_*\|<\delta.
$$

Equality is allowed; a [strict local minimum](strict-local-minimum.md) excludes equality at every other sufficiently nearby point. The comparison uses a [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) relative to the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md). On an [open set](open-set.md), this is an ordinary small ball around $x_*$. At an endpoint, only points belonging to the [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) are tested. Some elementary texts reserve "local" for [interior](interior-topology.md) points; here endpoints are allowed under the relative convention, but the zero-[gradient](gradient.md) condition still requires an [interior](interior-topology.md) point.

For $f(x)=x^4-2x^2$, each of $-1$ and $1$ is a [strict local minimum](strict-local-minimum.md), although both attain the same [global minimum](global-minimum.md). For $h(x)=|x|$, zero is a [strict local minimum](strict-local-minimum.md) despite the missing [derivative](derivative.md). Thus the definition compares values and does not require [differentiability](differentiability.md).

A [local minimum](local-minimum.md) describes a valley that cannot be improved by a sufficiently small move; [mathematical optimization](mathematical-optimization-split.md) also asks whether a better value lies farther away. At a smooth [interior](interior-topology.md) [local minimum](local-minimum.md), the [gradient](gradient.md) is zero. If the [function](function-split.md) is twice continuously [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) nearby, its [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) is [positive semidefinite](positive-semidefinite-matrix.md): restrict to a line through the point and its second [derivative](derivative.md) is nonnegative. The converse fails for a degenerate [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md), as $-x^4$ at zero shows.
