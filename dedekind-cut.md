# Dedekind cut

↑ **Parent:** [Completeness of the real numbers](completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_cut)

A [Dedekind cut](dedekind-cut.md) can be represented by a nonempty proper [subset](subset.md) $A$ of the [rational numbers](rational-number.md) satisfying two conditions: if $q\in A$ and $r<q$ is rational, then $r\in A$; and $A$ has no [maximum](maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). The complementary [set](set-split.md) contains all the rational values on the other side of the boundary. Using the convention that the lower [set](set-split.md) never includes its boundary avoids two representations of a rational boundary.

The [set](set-split.md) $\{q\in\mathbb Q:q<0\}$ represents zero. The [set](set-split.md) $\{q\in\mathbb Q:q<0\text{ or }q^2<2\}$ represents $\sqrt2$, even though no [rational number](rational-number.md) occupies that boundary by the [irrationality of the square root of two](irrationality-of-the-square-root-of-two.md). Thus a [Dedekind cut](dedekind-cut.md) describes an [irrational number](irrational-number.md) entirely using rational comparisons. Ordering these cuts by the [subset](subset.md) relation and defining their arithmetic gives a construction of the [real numbers](real-number.md). [Dedekind, Continuity and Irrational Numbers, §§IV–VI: cuts, continuity, and arithmetic](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/21016/21016-pdf.pdf).
