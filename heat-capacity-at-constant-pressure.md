# Heat capacity at constant pressure

↑ **Parent:** [Enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md)

For a fixed-composition [closed system](closed-system.md), the [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) at fixed [pressure](pressure-split.md) is $C_p=(\partial H/\partial T)_p$, with [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) $H$. Under the [quasistatic process](quasistatic-process.md) assumptions in [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md), only [pressure](pressure-split.md)-[volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) [work](work.md) is permitted, so $dp=0$ gives $\delta Q=dH=C_p\,dT$. Thus supplied [heat](heat-split.md) is $Q=\Delta H$, or $Q=C_p\Delta T$ within a single [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md) when $C_p$ is constant. For an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md), the expansion [work](work.md) explains why this exceeds the required [heat](heat-split.md) at fixed [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) for the same [temperature](temperature-split.md) increase. Its per-unit-[mass](mass.md) counterpart is [specific heat capacity at constant pressure](specific-heat-capacity-at-constant-pressure.md).
