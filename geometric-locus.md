# Geometric locus

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean geometry](euclidean-geometry.md)

A [geometric locus](geometric-locus.md) is the [set](set-split.md) of points satisfying a specified geometric or algebraic condition in an ambient space. For example, points at a fixed positive distance $r$ from $p$ form a [circle](circle.md) in the plane and a [sphere](sphere.md) in three dimensions. In the [complex plane](complex-plane.md), $|z-z_0|=r$ has Cartesian equation $(x-\operatorname{Re}z_0)^2+(y-\operatorname{Im}z_0)^2=r^2$, proving the [circle](circle.md) description. A locus can also be a union, such as $x(y-1)=0$, which consists of two lines; domain restrictions in the original equation must be retained when simplifying it.
