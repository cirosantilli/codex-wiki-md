# Finite-time Hubble-velocity spherical-collapse offset

↑ **Parent:** [Spherical-collapse model](spherical-collapse-model.md)

For a pressureless overdensity initialized with the background [Hubble flow](hubble-flow.md) in an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](einstein-de-sitter-universe.md), the cycloid $r=A(1-\cos\theta)$, $t+T=B(\theta-\sin\theta)$ has $A=r_i(1+\delta_i)/(2\delta_i)$, $B=(1+\delta_i)/(2H_i\delta_i^{3/2})$, and $\theta_i=2\arctan\sqrt{\delta_i}$. These follow from [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) and matching the initial radius and [velocity](velocity.md). With $t_i=2/(3H_i)$, $T=B(\theta_i-\sin\theta_i)-t_i=-t_i\delta_i/5+O(t_i\delta_i^2)$. Thus an exactly common background and perturbation bang time is incompatible with these finite-time data. Linear mass conservation instead gives a growing amplitude $3\delta_i/5$ and a decaying amplitude $2\delta_i/5$, whose initial time derivatives cancel.
