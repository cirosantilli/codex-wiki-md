# Zero-energy escape takes infinite time

↑ **Parent:** [Escape velocity](escape-velocity.md)

“Arrives at infinity with zero [speed](speed.md)” is an asymptotic statement. For outward radial motion at zero [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md),

$$
\dot r=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}},\qquad t(r)=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{2\mu}}\left(r^{3/2}-r_0^{3/2}\right),\qquad r(t)=\left(r_0^{3/2}+\frac32\sqrt{2\mu}\,t\right)^{2/3}.
$$

Every finite distance takes finite [time](time-in-physics.md), but $t(r)\to\infty$ as $r\to\infty$. There is no instant of rest at an infinitely distant destination. Differentiating the displayed solution gives $\ddot r=-\mu/r^2$, independently checking [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md). In the synthetic example, reaching $r=4\,\mathrm m$ takes exactly $(4-\sqrt2)/3\,\mathrm s\approx0.861929\,\mathrm s$. For nonzero [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md), the [Barker equation](barker-equation.md) gives the corresponding infinite-[time](time-in-physics.md) limit along a [parabolic trajectory](parabolic-trajectory.md). Positive-[energy](energy.md) escape also takes infinite [time](time-in-physics.md) to reach infinity; it retains nonzero asymptotic [speed](speed.md).
