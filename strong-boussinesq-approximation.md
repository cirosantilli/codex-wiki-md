# Strong Boussinesq approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Boussinesq approximation](boussinesq-approximation.md)

In the constant-property convection convention, the strong [Boussinesq approximation](boussinesq-approximation.md) supplements small relative [mass density](density.md) changes with constant [dynamic viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md), [thermal diffusivity](thermal-diffusivity.md), [specific heat capacity](specific-heat-capacity.md) and [thermal expansion coefficient](thermal-expansion-coefficient.md) throughout the modeled temperature interval. A uniform reference density replaces density in inertia and [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md), while a linear density anomaly is retained in gravitational [buoyancy](buoyancy.md). The temperature obeys an [advection-diffusion equation](advection-diffusion-equation.md) with constant coefficients. The term “strong” is not uniform across all fluid-mechanics literature: some stratified-flow treatments instead emphasize the use of a globally constant reference density. The constant-property convection usage is explained in [https://courses.physics.ucsd.edu/2021/Spring/physics218c/Spiegel%20Convection.pdf](https://courses.physics.ucsd.edu/2021/Spring/physics218c/Spiegel%20Convection.pdf) .
