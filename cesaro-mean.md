<h1 id="cesaro-mean">Cesàro mean</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Convergent sequence](convergent-sequence.md)

The [Cesàro mean](cesaro-mean.md) of the first $n\geq1$ terms of a [sequence](sequence.md) is their [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md). It is a cumulative average: increasing $n$ includes the whole initial segment, rather than a fixed-length sliding window. For $a_k=(-1)^k$, the even-length averages are zero and the odd-length averages are $-1/n$, so they tend to zero even though the [sequence](sequence.md) does not converge.

For a [periodic sequence](periodic-sequence.md) with period $p\geq1$ and one-cycle [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) $M$, split an initial segment into full cycles and a remainder of fewer than $p$ terms. The full cycles contribute exactly $M$ per term; the remainder contributes a fixed bounded error divided by $n$. Thus the [Cesàro means](cesaro-mean.md) tend to $M$. The cycle $0,2,1$ in the [bounded partial sums need not be Cesàro summable](bounded-partial-sums-need-not-be-cesaro-summable.md) illustration has [arithmetic mean](arithmetic-mean.md) one. Treating it as a sequence of [partial sums](partial-sum.md) gives a [series](series-mathematics.md) with first-order [Cesàro sum](cesaro-sum.md) one; averaging a sequence and summing its terms remain different operations.
