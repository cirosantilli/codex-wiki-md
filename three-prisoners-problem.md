# Three prisoners problem

↑ **Parent:** [Conditional probability](conditional-probability.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_prisoners_problem)

A messenger names an excluded candidate while concealing the uniquely selected one. The [conditional probability](conditional-probability.md) depends on the naming rule. With three equally likely selected candidates and a messenger forbidden to name the observing candidate A, let $s$ be the probability of naming B when A is selected. When C is selected the messenger must name B, and when B is selected cannot name B. Thus the probability that A is not selected, conditional on B being named, is $1/(1+s)$. Unbiased naming has $s=1/2$ and leaves this probability $2/3$; bias toward B gives $1/2$, and bias toward C gives one. Conditioning on the name is different from conditioning only on B's exclusion.
