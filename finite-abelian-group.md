# Finite abelian group

↑ **Parent:** [Abelian group](abelian-group.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_abelian_group)

A [finite abelian group](finite-abelian-group.md) combines a finite [set](set-split.md) of reversible operations with [commutativity](commutativity.md). All [finite cyclic groups](finite-cyclic-group.md) qualify, but [direct products of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) supply examples that need more than one [generator of a group](generator-of-a-group.md). The [Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups.md) says that every [finite abelian group](finite-abelian-group.md) is a [direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) of finite [cyclic groups](cyclic-group.md); the finite case has no infinite cyclic factors.[Theorem 1.54](group-theory-v4-01-by-james-s-milne.md)

These factors distinguish different ways to have the same total number of states. A single four-position dial gives $C_4$, while two independent binary switches give $C_2\times C_2$. Both have four states and [commutative](commutativity.md) composition, yet only the dial permits a single operation whose repetition visits all four states. The following [Cayley tables](cayley-table.md) make that distinction visible without relying on the labels alone.
