# Quartic potential with fourfold symmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Critical point](critical-point.md)

For $b>0$, the origin is a strict [local minimum](local-minimum.md). The four axis points at distance one have [Hessian](hessian-matrix.md) eigenvalues $-4$ and $2(1-b)$. The four diagonal points with $x^2=y^2=1/(1+b)$ have [Hessian](hessian-matrix.md) eigenvalues $-4$ and $-4(1-b)/(1+b)$. Thus for $b<1$ the diagonal points are the global maxima and the axis points are [saddle points of a scalar function](saddle-point-of-a-scalar-function.md); for $b>1$ these roles reverse. In [polar coordinates](polar-coordinates.md), $f=r^2-\tfrac12q(\theta)r^4$, where $q=1+\tfrac12(b-1)\sin^2(2\theta)>0$. Maximizing over $r$ gives $1/(2q)$ and proves the global statements. At $b=1$, $f=\tfrac12-\tfrac12(r^2-1)^2$, so the entire unit circle is the maximum set.
