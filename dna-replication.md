# DNA replication

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular genetics](molecular-genetics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication)

[DNA replication](dna-replication.md) copies a double-stranded [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md) into two daughter double-stranded [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md). In [semiconservative replication](semiconservative-replication.md), each daughter retains one parental strand and receives one newly synthesized complementary strand. [DNA polymerases](dna-polymerase.md) build the new strands from incoming [nucleotides](nucleotide.md); they do not make [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) or [proteins](protein-split.md) in this copying step. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition (2002): DNA replication mechanisms](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26850/).

For a five-[base pair](base-pair.md) example, separate $5'\text{-}\mathrm{ATGCC}\text{-}3'$ from its partner $3'\text{-}\mathrm{TACGG}\text{-}5'$. [Base pairing](base-pairing.md) specifies a new TACGG partner for the old ATGCC strand and a new ATGCC partner for the old TACGG strand. With error-free copying, both daughter [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md) have the original [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md), but different physical strand histories. One initial duplex contains ten [nucleotides](nucleotide.md); one complete round produces two duplexes containing twenty [nucleotides](nucleotide.md), ten retained and ten newly incorporated.

![](dna-replication-semi-conservative.png)

**[Figure 11](#image-semiconservative-replication-follow-two-original-dna-strands-through-two-complete-copying-rounds). Semiconservative replication: follow two original DNA strands through two complete copying rounds**. Blue O1 and purple O2 are the original strands; green strands were made in round 1 and orange strands in round 2. Each arrow connects a parent duplex to its daughters. After round 2, two of four duplexes retain an original strand, and the other two retain a round-1 strand. All eight strands have the appropriate complementary [nucleotide sequences](nucleic-acid-sequence.md); colors identify strand age, not different [nucleobases](nucleotide-base.md). The flattened duplexes show ancestry and orientation, not a replication fork or molecular shape.

This is useful for inheritance: both daughter [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md) can receive a copy of the [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) record. The elegant feature is that [base pairing](base-pairing.md) lets each strand specify its missing partner. Direction matters too: a replicating [DNA polymerase](dna-polymerase.md) reads its template $3'\to5'$ and extends the new strand at its $3'$ end, so synthesis proceeds $5'\to3'$. These are opposite directions on [antiparallel nucleic acid strands](antiparallel-nucleic-acid-strands.md), not two alternative directions of synthesis. The enzyme needs a pre-existing paired strand end to extend; the diagram omits the machinery that starts and coordinates copying. See [Alberts et al.: template-directed synthesis and the requirement for a primer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26850/).

The strand accounting predicts observable differences between copying mechanisms. In 1958, Meselson and Stahl grew [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) with the heavier [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) [isotope](isotope.md) $^{15}\mathrm{N}$, then supplied $^{14}\mathrm{N}$ and measured extracted [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) by density-gradient centrifugation. After one generation their samples contained intermediate-density [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md); after two, intermediate-density and light [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) occurred in equal amounts. This supported [semiconservative replication](semiconservative-replication.md) in the tested [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) culture. It was an [isotope](isotope.md) assay of sampled [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md), not simultaneous testing of every kind of [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) or direct colored-strand photography. See [The replication of DNA in Escherichia coli by Matthew Meselson and Franklin W. Stahl](the-replication-of-dna-in-escherichia-coli-by-matthew-meselson-and-franklin-w-stahl.md).
