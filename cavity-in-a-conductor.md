# Cavity in a conductor

↑ **Parent:** [Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md)

A [cavity in a conductor](cavity-in-a-conductor.md) is a void surrounded by an [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md); **completely enclosed** is the essential qualification in exact [electrostatic shielding](electrostatic-shielding.md) arguments. At [electrostatic equilibrium](electrostatic-equilibrium.md), the surrounding connected metal is an [equipotential](equipotential.md). An empty closed [cavity in a conductor](cavity-in-a-conductor.md) has constant [electric potential](electric-potential.md) and zero [electric field](electric-field.md), even if the metal is isolated and charged. An occupied closed [cavity in a conductor](cavity-in-a-conductor.md) generally has a nonzero [electric field](electric-field.md); its inner-wall total [electric charge](electric-charge.md) cancels the total [electric charge](electric-charge.md) of the internal sources. These are distinct statements, illustrated by [Faraday's ice pail experiment](faraday-s-ice-pail-experiment.md).[Richard Fitzpatrick, Ideal Conductors, equations 2.127 and the following uniqueness argument: a charge-free enclosed cavity](intermediate-college-physics-by-richard-fitzpatrick/ideal-conductors.md)
