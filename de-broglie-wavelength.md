# de Broglie wavelength

↑ **Parent:** [Matter wave](matter-wave.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/de_Broglie_wavelength)

The [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) is the distance over which a [matter wave](matter-wave.md) accumulates one full spatial [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) cycle in its propagation direction:

$$
\lambda=\frac{h}{|\mathbf p|}=\frac{2\pi}{|\mathbf k|},\qquad \mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k,\qquad \hbar=\frac{h}{2\pi}.
$$

Here $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md), $\mathbf p$ is the [momentum](momentum.md), and $\mathbf k$ is the angular [wavevector](wavevector.md). The scalar $p$ in $\lambda=h/p$ means the positive [momentum](momentum.md) magnitude, not a signed component. Reversing a [momentum](momentum.md) reverses the [wavevector](wavevector.md) without making the [wavelength](wavelength.md) negative. At zero [momentum](momentum.md) there is no finite spatial phase period: the limiting [wavelength](wavelength.md) diverges.

This relation makes microscopic [diffraction](diffraction.md) accessible with particles: increasing the [momentum](momentum.md) shortens the [wavelength](wavelength.md) until it is comparable with the spacings in a [crystal scattering](crystal-scattering.md) problem. The [electron accelerating-voltage wavelength](electron-accelerating-voltage-wavelength.md) gives a practical way to tune this scale, while the [Davisson–Germer experiment](davisson-germer-experiment.md) tests it by comparing [electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md) directions with independently known crystal geometry. The same relation applies to neutral particles in a [neutron interferometer](neutron-interferometer.md); [electric charge](electric-charge.md) is not a requirement for a [matter wave](matter-wave.md).

The [wavelength](wavelength.md) belongs to the [wavefunction](wave-function.md), not to a sinusoidal path of a particle. A [free-particle momentum convention](free-particle-momentum-convention.md) makes the distinction especially clear: a [plane wave](plane-wave.md) has a rotating complex [quantum phase](quantum-phase.md) but a constant [probability density](probability-density.md). A localized [wave packet](wave-packet.md) contains a range of [momenta](momentum.md) and therefore generally has a range of [wavelengths](wavelength.md), rather than one exact spatial period.
