# Logarithmic derivative

↑ **Parent:** [Riemann zeta function](riemann-zeta-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_derivative)

The [logarithmic derivative](logarithmic-derivative.md) of a [differentiable function](differentiable-function.md) $f$ is $f'/f$ wherever $f\ne0$. For a nonvanishing [holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md), a local [holomorphic logarithm](holomorphic-logarithm.md) differentiates to this expression even when no global logarithm exists. If a [meromorphic function](meromorphic-function.md) has local factorization $f(z)=(z-a)^mh(z)$ with $h(a)\ne0$, differentiation gives $f'/f=m/(z-a)+h'/h$. Its [residue](residue.md) is therefore $m$, positive for a zero and negative for a [pole](pole.md); this explains the multiplicity count in the [argument principle](argument-principle.md). For $\Re s>1$, the [Euler product](euler-product.md) for the [Riemann zeta function](riemann-zeta-function.md) gives

$$
-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.
$$
