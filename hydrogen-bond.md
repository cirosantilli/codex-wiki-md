# Hydrogen bond

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bond)

A [hydrogen bond](hydrogen-bond.md) is an attractive association involving a [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) attached to another [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), and an accepting atom or group. In the IUPAC 2011 definition, the atom $X$ in $X-\mathrm H$ is more [electronegative](electronegativity.md) than [hydrogen](hydrogen.md), and evidence of bond formation is required. The familiar strong examples use O–H or N–H groups, but restricting the concept to only nitrogen, oxygen and fluorine misses weaker cases. A short distance alone is not the entire definition.[Arunan et al., Definition of the hydrogen bond, IUPAC Recommendations 2011](https://publications.iupac.org/pac/pdf/2011/pdf/8308x1637.pdf)

**[Figure 22](#image-two-water-molecules-play-different-hydrogen-bond-roles-while-retaining-their-own-covalent-o-h-bonds). Two water molecules play different hydrogen-bond roles while retaining their own covalent O–H bonds**.

In the depicted [water](water.md) pair, one O–H group is the [hydrogen bond donor](hydrogen-bond-donor.md); the other [oxygen](oxygen.md) supplies the [hydrogen bond acceptor](hydrogen-bond-acceptor.md). The solid O–H line denotes a [covalent bond](covalent-bond.md) within a [water](water.md) [molecule](molecule.md); the dotted H···O line denotes the [hydrogen bond](hydrogen-bond.md) between them. One [water](water.md) [molecule](molecule.md) can play both roles in a larger arrangement because it has O–H groups and [lone pairs](lone-pair.md).

This makes [hydrogen bonds](hydrogen-bond.md) useful for understanding organization beyond a single [molecular formula](molecular-formula.md). They connect [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule.md) and contribute to [protein](protein.md) folding and recognition between [molecules](molecule.md). The same types of [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) can support different associations depending on which donor and acceptor sites are exposed and how they are oriented. Alberts and colleagues discuss these noncovalent interactions in [Molecular Biology of the Cell, the chemical components of a cell](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26883/).

[Hydrogen bonds](hydrogen-bond.md) are often directional, with an approximately straight donor–H–acceptor arrangement favourable in familiar cases; strength and preferred geometry depend on the system. The IUPAC definition uses converging experimental or theoretical evidence rather than one universal distance threshold. The author-hosted [2009 task-group draft, §§3.1–3.3](https://ipc.iisc.ac.in/~ea/iupac/iupac/IUPAC_finalreport_Nov_2009TG.pdf) explains the definition and typical criteria leading to the recommendation.
