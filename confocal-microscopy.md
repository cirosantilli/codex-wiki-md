# Confocal microscopy

↑ **Parent:** [Fluorescence microscope](fluorescence-microscope.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confocal_microscopy)

[Confocal microscopy](confocal-microscopy.md) forms an image by scanning a localized illumination spot and preferentially detecting [light](light.md) from the same small region. In a thick specimen emitting [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md), a conventional [fluorescence microscope](fluorescence-microscope.md) also collects a haze from material away from the selected [optical focus](focus-optics.md). [Confocal optical sectioning](confocal-optical-sectioning.md) suppresses that haze, revealing structure at a chosen depth without cutting the specimen. The useful idea is a paired selection: where excitation occurs, and which emitted [photons](photon.md) reach the [photodetector](photodetector.md). [Introductory Confocal Concepts, opening paragraphs and Fig. 2: out-of-focus fluorescence and optical sections](introductory-confocal-concepts-by-nikon-instruments-inc.md)

The scope here is single-point, linear [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) imaging. [Confocal microscopy](confocal-microscopy.md) can also use reflected [light](light.md); its broader implementations need not all have the same scanning mechanism. The patent [Microscopy apparatus, US3013467A, by Marvin Minsky](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3013467A/en) describes a point source imaged onto a specimen, a second pinhole receiving its image, specimen scanning, and a synchronized display. It was filed on 7 November 1957 and published on 19 December 1961; the filing date should not be described as the grant date. This is evidence for that apparatus and those dates, rather than a claim that all later [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md) instruments were already present in it. [Front-page filing/publication metadata; description, Optical System, Fig. 1; Stage Scanning System](microscopy-apparatus-by-marvin-minsky.md)
