<h1 id="1/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because $(r)$ is a proper [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md), each $(r^k)$ is proper and its [radical of an ideal](../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) is

$$
\sqrt{(r^k)}=(r)=\mathfrak p.
$$

Indeed, $(r^k)\subseteq(r)$ gives one inclusion of [radicals of ideals](../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md), while $(rc)^k\in(r^k)$ gives the other.

We prove the saturation condition for a [primary ideal](../../../../../primary-ideal.md): if $ab\in(r^k)$ and $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $b\in(r^k)$. For $k=1$ this is the defining property of a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md). For $k>1$, write $ab=r^kc$. Since $a\notin(r)$, the [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) condition gives $b=rb_1$. The element $r$ is a [non-zero-divisor](../../../../../non-zero-divisor.md), so cancellation gives

$$
ab_1=r^{k-1}c.
$$

Induction gives $b_1\in(r^{k-1})$, hence $b\in(r^k)$. Together with the computed [radical of an ideal](../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md), this proves

$$
\boxed{(r^k)\text{ is }\mathfrak p\text{-primary for every }k\geq1.}
$$
