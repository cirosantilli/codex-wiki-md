<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the [direct product of rings](../../../../direct-product-of-rings.md)

$$
T=\prod_{n\geq0}\mathbb R,
$$

with coordinatewise addition and multiplication and unit $(1,1,\ldots)$. Define the shift

$$
g(a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots)=(a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots).
$$

Coordinatewise operations show that $g$ preserves addition, multiplication and the unit, so it is a [ring endomorphism](../../../../ring-endomorphism.md). It is [surjective](../../../../surjective-function.md) because $(0,b_0,b_1,\ldots)$ maps to any prescribed $(b_0,b_1,\ldots)$. Its [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) is

$$
\boxed{\ker g=\{(a,0,0,\ldots):a\in\mathbb R\}\ne0,}
$$

so it is not [injective](../../../../injective-function.md). This is the [noninjective surjective shift on a product of fields](../../../../noninjective-surjective-shift-on-a-product-of-fields.md).

This [ring](../../../../ring.md) fails the [Noetherian](../../../../noetherian-ring.md) hypothesis in [c](../c.md). Indeed, let $e_n$ have a single $1$ in coordinate $n$. The [ideals](../../../../ideal.md) $I_n=(e_0,\ldots,e_n)$ form the strictly ascending chain

$$
I_0\subsetneq I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq\cdots,
$$

since $e_{n+1}\notin I_n$.
