<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The image $f/1$ is a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) in the [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) $R_{\mathfrak p}$, because $f\notin\mathfrak p$. The [universal property of localization](../../../../universal-property-of-localization.md), applied to the [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) $R\to R_{\mathfrak p}$ and the [multiplicative subset](../../../../multiplicatively-closed-set.md) $\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}$, gives a unique [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) fixing the image of $R$:

$$
\boxed{\theta_f:R_f\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak p},\qquad \theta_f(a/f^n)=a/f^n.}
$$

Fixing the image of $R$ is exactly the required compatibility with the [algebra over a ring](../../../../algebra-over-a-commutative-ring.md) structures. Uniqueness also follows directly: such a [ring homomorphism](../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) must send $1/f$ to the inverse of $f/1$, and these elements together with $R$ generate $R_f$.
