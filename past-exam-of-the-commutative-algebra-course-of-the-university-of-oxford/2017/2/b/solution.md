<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $\sqrt I+\sqrt J=R$, write $1=a+b$ with $a\in\sqrt I$ and $b\in\sqrt J$. Choose $m,n\geq1$ such that $a^m\in I$ and $b^n\in J$. In the [binomial theorem](../../../../binomial-theorem.md) expansion

$$
1=(a+b)^{m+n-1},
$$

each term has either at least $m$ factors $a$, or at least $n$ factors $b$. Hence every term belongs to the [sum of ideals](../../../../sum-of-ideals.md) $I+J$, and

$$
\boxed{\sqrt I+\sqrt J=R\ \Longrightarrow\ I+J=R.}
$$

Thus [comaximal ideals](../../../../comaximal-ideals.md) can be recognized from their [radicals of ideals](../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md).

For the general identity, $I+J\subseteq\sqrt I+\sqrt J$ gives

$$
\sqrt{I+J}\subseteq\sqrt{\sqrt I+\sqrt J}.
$$

On the other hand, $I,J\subseteq I+J$ implies $\sqrt I,\sqrt J\subseteq\sqrt{I+J}$. Since the latter is an [ideal](../../../../ideal.md), $\sqrt I+\sqrt J\subseteq\sqrt{I+J}$. Taking [radicals of ideals](../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) and using the idempotence proved in [a](../a.md) yields

$$
\sqrt{\sqrt I+\sqrt J}\subseteq\sqrt{\sqrt{I+J}}=\sqrt{I+J}.
$$

Therefore the [radical of a sum of ideals](../../../../radical-of-a-sum-of-ideals.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rad}(\operatorname{rad}(I)+\operatorname{rad}(J))=\operatorname{rad}(I+J).}
$$
