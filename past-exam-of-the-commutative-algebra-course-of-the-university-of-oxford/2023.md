## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

An [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $\mathfrak q$ is $\mathfrak p$-[primary](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) if it is a proper [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) whose [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) is $\mathfrak p$. Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{\mathfrak q}=\mathfrak p,\qquad ab\in\mathfrak q,\ a\notin\mathfrak q\ \Longrightarrow\ b^n\in\mathfrak q\text{ for some }n\geq1.}
$$

Equivalently, $ab\in\mathfrak q$ and $b\notin\mathfrak p$ imply $a\in\mathfrak q$: multiplication by every element outside $\mathfrak p$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function) on the [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $R/\mathfrak q$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

A [minimal primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-primary-decomposition) is a finite expression

$$
\boxed{I=\bigcap_{j=1}^t\mathfrak q_j}
$$

in proper [primary ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) such that the [prime ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak p_j=\sqrt{\mathfrak q_j}$ are pairwise distinct and no [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) can be omitted. More explicitly, for every $j$,

$$
\bigcap_{i\ne j}\mathfrak q_i\not\subseteq\mathfrak q_j.
$$

Thus minimality means both irredundancy and distinct [radicals of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal); it does not assert that the [primary ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) themselves are uniquely determined. For $I=R$, one may use the empty intersection convention.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

In the terminology of a [minimal primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-primary-decomposition), an [associated prime of a primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#associated-prime-of-a-primary-decomposition) of $I$ is a [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) occurring as $\sqrt{\mathfrak q_j}$ in such a decomposition. The [first uniqueness theorem for primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#first-uniqueness-theorem-for-primary-decomposition) says that this collection is independent of the chosen [minimal primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-primary-decomposition). Among these [prime ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal), an [isolated prime of a primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#isolated-prime-of-a-primary-decomposition) is minimal under inclusion, equivalently a [minimal prime over an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-prime-over-an-ideal) $I$; an embedded [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) strictly contains another member of the collection. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{isolated}=\text{minimal over }I,\qquad\text{embedded}=\text{not minimal over }I.}
$$

When $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), these are precisely the [associated primes of a module](../module-theory.md#associated-prime-of-a-module) $R/I$. In that case the equivalent definition is

$$
\mathfrak p=\operatorname{Ann}_R(x+I)=(I:x)
\quad\text{for some }x\notin I,
$$

where $(I:x)$ is a [colon ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#colon-ideal). The decomposition terminology applies when a [minimal primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-primary-decomposition) exists; a general [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) need not provide one for every [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Suppose first that $\mathfrak a$ is $\mathfrak m$-[primary](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal). Since $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), choose a finite [generating set of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#generating-set-of-an-ideal) $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$. The equality of [radicals of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}=\mathfrak m$ supplies positive integers $e_i$ with $x_i^{e_i}\in\mathfrak a$. Set

$$
n=1+\sum_{i=1}^d(e_i-1).
$$

Every product of $n$ generators has some $x_i$ appearing at least $e_i$ times. Such products generate the [product of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#product-of-ideals) $\mathfrak m^n$, so $\mathfrak m^n\subseteq\mathfrak a$. Also $\mathfrak a\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak a}=\mathfrak m$. If $\mathfrak m=0$, take $n=1$ directly.

Conversely, suppose $\mathfrak m^n\subseteq\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak m$. Taking [radicals of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) gives

$$
\mathfrak m=\sqrt{\mathfrak m^n}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak a}\subseteq\mathfrak m.
$$

Hence $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}=\mathfrak m$. The only [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) containing $\mathfrak a$ is $\mathfrak m$: any such [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) contains $\sqrt{\mathfrak a}$ and therefore equals $\mathfrak m$. By the [correspondence theorem for ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#correspondence-theorem-for-ideals), the [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $R/\mathfrak a$ is a [local ring](../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring) with [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak a$. Every element outside this [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring).

If $ab\in\mathfrak a$ and $a\notin\mathfrak a$, then $b$ cannot be outside $\mathfrak m$, since multiplying by its inverse in the [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) would give $a\in\mathfrak a$. Thus $b\in\mathfrak m$ and $b^n\in\mathfrak a$, proving the required [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) condition. We have proved the [maximal-primary ideal criterion](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-primary-ideal-criterion)

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak a\text{ is }\mathfrak m\text{-primary}\iff\mathfrak m^n\subseteq\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak m\text{ for some }n\geq1.}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Put $A=\mathbb C[X,Y]$, $P=(X)$ and $Q=(X^2,Y)$. The [quotient ring](../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $A/P\cong\mathbb C[Y]$ is an [integral domain](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), so $P$ is a [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) and hence a [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal). Put $\mathfrak m=(X,Y)$. Then

$$
\mathfrak m^2=(X^2,XY,Y^2)\subseteq Q\subseteq\mathfrak m.
$$

The [polynomial ring](../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring) $A$ is a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) by the [Hilbert basis theorem](../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem), and $\mathfrak m$ is a [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) since $A/\mathfrak m\cong\mathbb C$. Therefore the [maximal-primary ideal criterion](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-primary-ideal-criterion) from [c](#1/c) makes $Q$ $\mathfrak m$-[primary](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal). In particular its [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) is $\mathfrak m$, distinct from $P$.

Neither [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) can be omitted: $X\in P\setminus Q$, while $Y\in Q\setminus P$. Thus the given intersection is already a [minimal primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#minimal-primary-decomposition). To express the [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) by generators, write an element of $P\cap Q$ as $Xh=X^2u+Yv$. Reducing modulo the [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $(X)$ gives $Y\bar v=0$ in the [integral domain](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $\mathbb C[Y]$, so $v=Xw$. Cancelling $X$ in the [integral domain](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $A$ gives $h=Xu+Yw$. Hence

$$
\boxed{J=(X^2,XY)=(X)\cap(X^2,Y).}
$$

By the [first uniqueness theorem for primary decomposition](../commutative-algebra.md#first-uniqueness-theorem-for-primary-decomposition), the [associated primes of a module](../module-theory.md#associated-prime-of-a-module) $A/J$ are exactly the two [radicals of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal). Their strict inclusion $P\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Ass}_A(A/J)=\{(X),(X,Y)\},\quad (X)\text{ isolated},\quad (X,Y)\text{ embedded}.}
$$

One can also see both [associated primes of a module](../module-theory.md#associated-prime-of-a-module) directly through [annihilators](../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory):

$$
\operatorname{Ann}_A(Y+J)=(J:Y)=(X),\qquad
\operatorname{Ann}_A(X+J)=(J:X)=(X,Y).
$$

For the first [colon ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#colon-ideal), reducing $hY\in J$ modulo $(X)$ forces $h\in(X)$, and the reverse inclusion follows from $XY\in J$. For the second [colon ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#colon-ideal), cancel $X$ in $hX\in X(X,Y)$. Both represented elements of the [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $A/J$ are nonzero.

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

<h5 id="1/e/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/e/i)

Because $(r)$ is a proper [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal), each $(r^k)$ is proper and its [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) is

$$
\sqrt{(r^k)}=(r)=\mathfrak p.
$$

Indeed, $(r^k)\subseteq(r)$ gives one inclusion of [radicals of ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal), while $(rc)^k\in(r^k)$ gives the other.

We prove the saturation condition for a [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal): if $ab\in(r^k)$ and $a\notin\mathfrak p$, then $b\in(r^k)$. For $k=1$ this is the defining property of a [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal). For $k>1$, write $ab=r^kc$. Since $a\notin(r)$, the [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) condition gives $b=rb_1$. The element $r$ is a [non-zero-divisor](../mathematics.md#non-zero-divisor), so cancellation gives

$$
ab_1=r^{k-1}c.
$$

Induction gives $b_1\in(r^{k-1})$, hence $b\in(r^k)$. Together with the computed [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal), this proves

$$
\boxed{(r^k)\text{ is }\mathfrak p\text{-primary for every }k\geq1.}
$$

<h4 id="1/e/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

<h5 id="1/e/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/e/ii)

Since the [radical of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) $\sqrt{\mathfrak q}$ is $(r)$, some positive power of $r$ belongs to $\mathfrak q$. Choose the least such exponent $k$. Then $(r^k)\subseteq\mathfrak q$ and $r^j\notin\mathfrak q$ for $0\leq j<k$, with $r^0=1$ excluded because the [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) $\mathfrak q$ is proper.

Take $x\in\mathfrak q$. If $x\notin(r^k)$, choose the largest $j\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$ for which $x\in(r^j)$, and write $x=r^ju$. Maximality of $j$ implies $u\notin(r)=\sqrt{\mathfrak q}$: otherwise $x\in(r^{j+1})$, a contradiction, including when $j=k-1$. The [primary ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal) condition applied to $r^ju\in\mathfrak q$ therefore forces $r^j\in\mathfrak q$, contrary to the choice of $k$.

Consequently every $x\in\mathfrak q$ lies in $(r^k)$, and

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak q=(r^k),\qquad k=\min\{n\geq1:r^n\in\mathfrak q\}.}
$$

Together with [i](#1/e/i), this classifies the [primary ideals for a regular principal prime](../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideals-for-a-regular-principal-prime). The exponent is unique: if $(r^k)=(r^{k+1})$, then $r^k=r^{k+1}c$, and cancellation of the [non-zero-divisor](../mathematics.md#non-zero-divisor) $r^k$ would give $1=rc$, contradicting that $(r)$ is proper. No [Noetherian](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis is needed.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The image $f/1$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) in the [localization at a prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-at-a-prime-ideal) $R_{\mathfrak p}$, because $f\notin\mathfrak p$. The [universal property of localization](../commutative-algebra.md#universal-property-of-localization), applied to the [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $R\to R_{\mathfrak p}$ and the [multiplicative subset](../commutative-algebra.md#multiplicatively-closed-set) $\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}$, gives a unique [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) fixing the image of $R$:

$$
\boxed{\theta_f:R_f\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak p},\qquad \theta_f(a/f^n)=a/f^n.}
$$

Fixing the image of $R$ is exactly the required compatibility with the [algebra over a ring](../commutative-algebra.md#algebra-over-a-commutative-ring) structures. Uniqueness also follows directly: such a [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) must send $1/f$ to the inverse of $f/1$, and these elements together with $R$ generate $R_f$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Choose $f=1$, so that $R_f=R$ and $f\notin\mathfrak p$. For an [integral domain](../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), the canonical [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $R\to R_{\mathfrak p}$ is [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function): if $a/1=0$, the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) gives $sa=0$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak p$. Such an $s$ is nonzero, so the absence of [zero divisors](../mathematics.md#zero-divisor) gives $a=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f=1\text{ works whenever }R\text{ is an integral domain}.}
$$

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Consider the [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism)

$$
K=\ker(R\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak p})
=\{a\in R:sa=0\text{ for some }s\notin\mathfrak p\}.
$$

Since $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), this [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) has a finite [generating set of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#generating-set-of-an-ideal), say $K=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$. For each $i$, choose $s_i\notin\mathfrak p$ with $s_i a_i=0$, and put

$$
f=\prod_{i=1}^t s_i.
$$

Then $f\notin\mathfrak p$ by the [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) condition, and $fK=0$. If $K=0$, take the empty product $f=1$.

If $a/f^n$ maps to zero under the [ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\theta_f$ from [a](#2/a), then $a/1=0$ in $R_{\mathfrak p}$ because $f/1$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) there. Hence $a\in K$, so $fa=0$. The [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) now gives $a/f^n=0$ already in $R_f$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\ker\theta_f=0,\qquad\theta_f:R_f\hookrightarrow R_{\mathfrak p}.}
$$

This is the [finite generation clears localization kernels](../commutative-algebra.md#finite-generation-clears-localization-kernels) argument: the finite [generating set of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#generating-set-of-an-ideal) permits a single element outside $\mathfrak p$ to annihilate the entire [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For $n\geq1$, let $K_n=\ker(\phi^n)$, where the power denotes repeated composition of the [ring endomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-endomorphism). These [ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) form an ascending chain:

$$
K_1\subseteq K_2\subseteq K_3\subseteq\cdots.
$$

The [ascending chain condition](../algebra.md#ascending-chain-condition) for a [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) gives $K_N=K_{N+1}$ for some $N\geq1$. Take $a\in\ker\phi$. Since $\phi$ is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function), its iterate $\phi^N$ is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function), so choose $b\in R$ with $\phi^N(b)=a$. Then

$$
\phi^{N+1}(b)=\phi(a)=0,
$$

so $b\in K_{N+1}=K_N$. Therefore $a=\phi^N(b)=0$.

Thus the [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism) $\phi$ is zero. This proves that a [surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring is an automorphism](../algebra.md#surjective-endomorphism-of-a-noetherian-ring-is-an-automorphism), and in particular

$$
\boxed{\phi\text{ is injective}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Use the [direct product of rings](../commutative-algebra.md#direct-product-of-rings)

$$
T=\prod_{n\geq0}\mathbb R,
$$

with coordinatewise addition and multiplication and unit $(1,1,\ldots)$. Define the shift

$$
g(a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots)=(a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots).
$$

Coordinatewise operations show that $g$ preserves addition, multiplication and the unit, so it is a [ring endomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#ring-endomorphism). It is [surjective](../algebra.md#surjective-function) because $(0,b_0,b_1,\ldots)$ maps to any prescribed $(b_0,b_1,\ldots)$. Its [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism) is

$$
\boxed{\ker g=\{(a,0,0,\ldots):a\in\mathbb R\}\ne0,}
$$

so it is not [injective](../algebra.md#injective-function). This is the [noninjective surjective shift on a product of fields](../commutative-algebra.md#noninjective-surjective-shift-on-a-product-of-fields).

This [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) fails the [Noetherian](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis in [c](#2/c). Indeed, let $e_n$ have a single $1$ in coordinate $n$. The [ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $I_n=(e_0,\ldots,e_n)$ form the strictly ascending chain

$$
I_0\subsetneq I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq\cdots,
$$

since $e_{n+1}\notin I_n$.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A [ring](../commutative-algebra.md#ring) is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring) if its only [nilpotent element](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) is zero. Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{x^n=0\text{ for some }n\geq1\ \Longrightarrow\ x=0,\qquad\text{or }\sqrt{(0)}=0.}
$$

The second expression says that its [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical) vanishes. A [reduced ring](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring) may have [zero divisors](../mathematics.md#zero-divisor); for example, the [direct product of rings](../commutative-algebra.md#direct-product-of-rings) $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring) but $(1,0)(0,1)=0$.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Write $N=\sqrt{(0)}$. Every element of the [extension of an ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#extension-of-an-ideal) $NR_{\mathfrak p}$ is a fraction $a/s$ with $a\in N$ and $s\notin\mathfrak p$: a finite sum of such fractions has a common denominator and numerator still in the [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $N$. If $a^n=0$, then $(a/s)^n=0$, so

$$
NR_{\mathfrak p}\subseteq\sqrt{(0)_{R_{\mathfrak p}}}.
$$

Conversely, let $a/s$ be a [nilpotent element](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) of the [localization at a prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-at-a-prime-ideal) $R_{\mathfrak p}$. For some $n\geq1$, $a^n/s^n=0$. The [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) supplies $t\notin\mathfrak p$ with $ta^n=0$. It follows that

$$
(ta)^n=t^{n-1}(ta^n)=0,
$$

so $ta\in N$. Since $ts\notin\mathfrak p$,

$$
\frac as=\frac{ta}{ts}\in NR_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

This proves that the [nilradical commutes with localization](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical-commutes-with-localization):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Nil}(R_{\mathfrak p})=NR_{\mathfrak p}.}
$$

The multiplication by $t$ is necessary: the numerator $a$ of a [nilpotent element](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) in a [localization](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) need not itself be [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) in $R$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

If $R$ is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring), its [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical) $N$ is zero. By [b](#3/b), every [localization at a prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-at-a-prime-ideal) has zero [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical), so in particular $R_{\mathfrak m}$ is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring) for every [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$.

Conversely, suppose every such [localization](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring). Take a [nilpotent element](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) $x\in R$. Then $x/1$ is [nilpotent](../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent) in every $R_{\mathfrak m}$, hence zero there. To see directly that $x=0$, suppose otherwise and consider the proper [annihilator](../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory)

$$
\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)=\{a\in R:ax=0\}.
$$

Choose a [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$ containing this [annihilator](../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory). The equality $x/1=0$ in $R_{\mathfrak m}$ would imply $sx=0$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak m$, by the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization). But then $s\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)\subseteq\mathfrak m$, a contradiction. This is the principle that [localization detects zero elements](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-detects-zero-elements). Therefore

$$
\boxed{R\text{ is reduced}\iff R_{\mathfrak m}\text{ is reduced for every maximal ideal }\mathfrak m.}
$$

The argument also includes the [zero ring](../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring), which is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring) and has no [maximal ideals](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Let $N$ be the [nilradical](../commutative-algebra.md#nilradical) of $R$. By [b](#3/b), the [reduced locus of a ring](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-locus-of-a-ring) is

$$
\mathcal R=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R:NR_{\mathfrak p}=0\}.
$$

The [Noetherian ring](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis makes the [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $N$ finitely generated; write $N=(n_1,\ldots,n_t)$. Fix $\mathfrak p\in\mathcal R$. For every $i$, the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) gives $s_i\notin\mathfrak p$ with $s_in_i=0$. The product $f=s_1\cdots s_t$ lies outside the [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak p$ and satisfies $fN=0$. When $N=0$, take $f=1$.

For every [prime ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak q$ in the [distinguished open set](../algebraic-geometry.md#distinguished-open-set)

$$
D(f)=\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}R:f\notin\mathfrak q\},
$$

the element $f/1$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) in $R_{\mathfrak q}$. The equality $fN=0$ therefore implies $NR_{\mathfrak q}=0$, so $R_{\mathfrak q}$ is [reduced](../commutative-algebra.md#reduced-ring). Thus $\mathfrak p\in D(f)\subseteq\mathcal R$, proving that $\mathcal R$ is a [Zariski-open subset](../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-open-set) of the [spectrum of a ring](../locally-ringed-space.md#spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring).

More precisely, let $A=\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)$, the [annihilator of a module](../module-theory.md#annihilator-of-a-module) $N$. The argument shows that $NR_{\mathfrak p}=0$ if and only if some $f\in A$ lies outside $\mathfrak p$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R=\bigcup_{f\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)}D(f)
=\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus V(\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)).}
$$

Equivalently, the complement is the [support of a module](../module-theory.md#support-of-a-module) $N$. This is [vanishing of a finitely generated module near a prime](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-of-a-finitely-generated-module-near-a-prime) applied to $N$: its finite generation lets the separately chosen denominators be combined into one element annihilating all of $N$.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

Set $L=M/\mathfrak aM$, a [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module). The [exactness of localization](../commutative-algebra.md#exactness-of-localization) gives, for every [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$,

$$
L_{\mathfrak m}\cong M_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak aR_{\mathfrak m}M_{\mathfrak m}.
$$

If $\mathfrak m\supseteq\mathfrak a$, the hypothesis $M_{\mathfrak m}=0$ makes this [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) zero. If $\mathfrak m\not\supseteq\mathfrak a$, choose $s\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak m$. Its image in $R_{\mathfrak m}$ is a [unit](../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), so $\mathfrak aR_{\mathfrak m}=R_{\mathfrak m}$ and the displayed [quotient module](../module-theory.md#quotient-module) is again zero. Thus

$$
L_{\mathfrak m}=0\qquad\text{for every maximal ideal }\mathfrak m.
$$

For completeness, if $0\ne\ell\in L$, its [annihilator](../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) is a proper [ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#ideal), and hence lies in a [maximal ideal](../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$. The [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) shows $\ell/1\ne0$ in $L_{\mathfrak m}$: any element annihilating $\ell$ lies inside $\mathfrak m$. This contradicts the vanishing just proved. Hence $L=0$, and

$$
\boxed{M/\mathfrak aM=0,\qquad\mathfrak aM=M.}
$$

The use of [localization detects zero elements](../commutative-algebra.md#localization-detects-zero-elements) is valid for every [module](../module-theory.md#module-mathematics); neither finite generation nor a [Noetherian](../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis is needed.
