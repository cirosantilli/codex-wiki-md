# Isolating integrals and orbital closure dimension

↑ **Parent:** [Isolating integral](isolating-integral.md)

In three-dimensional autonomous mechanics, a regular common level set of $N$ independent [isolating integrals](isolating-integral.md) has dimension $6-N$ in [phase space](phase-space.md). If an [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) is dense in a $d$-dimensional [invariant torus](invariant-torus.md) and the integrals characterize its closure, $N=6-d$. A generic three-frequency regular [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) has three integrals; a spherical nonclosed rosette has energy and three components of [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md), with two-dimensional closure and four integrals. A periodic nonstationary [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) has one-dimensional closure and can be isolated by five independent [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) labels on the invariant family. The maximum is five wherever the [Hamiltonian vector field](hamiltonian-vector-field.md) is nonzero, since all integral differentials annihilate that field. A single resonant [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) need not yield an additional smooth single-valued integral on an open neighborhood; global superintegrability is stronger than orbital closure.
