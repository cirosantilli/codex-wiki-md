### Linguistics

↑ **Parent:** [Science](#science)

#### Morpheme

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

A minimal unit contributing lexical or grammatical meaning. A [morpheme](#morpheme) is not generally identical to a written character, syllable or [phoneme](#phoneme) across all languages.

#### Language comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

Understanding linguistic input, whether spoken or written. It combines access to words and meanings with sentence-level integration, context and control.

##### Auditory comprehension

↑ **Parent:** [Language comprehension](#language-comprehension)

Understanding language presented through [hearing](neuroscience.md#hearing). Acoustic and [phonological analysis](#phonological-analysis) add demands beyond those shared with written or nonverbal semantic tasks.

#### Lexical access

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

Access to stored information about words during comprehension or production. A failure to retrieve a name need not imply loss of the underlying concept.

#### Prosody

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

#### Pragmatics

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

Interpretation of utterances in their conversational context, including what a speaker is asking the listener to do. Making “initial search” explicit can change the interpretation of a [false belief](psychology.md#false-belief) question without changing the represented event.[Cognition 38, 1–12; initial-search wording manipulation](psychology-bibliography.md#where-to-look-first-for-children-s-knowledge-of-false-beliefs-by-michael-siegal-and-karen-beattie)

A contrastive sound unit in a [language](#language). A phonetic difference can be perceptible without functioning as a phonemic contrast in every [language](#language).

#### Phonetics

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

##### Phonetic category

↑ **Parent:** [Phonetics](#phonetics)

A grouping of speech sounds defined by a relevant perceptual or articulatory organisation. Experimental discrimination of two endpoints does not, by itself, establish a complete inventory of linguistic categories.

#### Phonology

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

The organization of sound-based distinctions and patterns in a [language](#language). Phonological representations connect acoustic inputs, lexical information and articulatory behavior without being identical to any one of them.

##### Phonological awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Phonology](#phonology)

Explicit access to and manipulation of sound structure, including units such as syllables and [phonemes](#phoneme). It is distinct from ordinary ability to understand speech.

###### Phonemic awareness

↑ **Parent:** [Phonological awareness](#phonological-awareness)

Awareness and manipulation of phoneme-sized units. Its development can support alphabetic [reading](psychology.md#reading) and be altered by [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) to read.[Cognition 7, 323–331; illiterate and adult-literate comparisons on phone addition/deletion](psychology-bibliography.md#does-awareness-of-speech-as-a-sequence-of-phones-arise-spontaneously-by-jose-morais-and-others)

##### Phonological representation

↑ **Parent:** [Phonology](#phonology)

A representation of the sound structure relevant to a language. Recognition, temporary retention and [speech production](neuroscience.md#speech-production) can use such information while imposing different additional demands.

##### Phonological analysis

↑ **Parent:** [Phonology](#phonology)

Analysis of a [speech](#speech) signal in terms of the sound structure relevant to language. Auditory discrimination, mapping sounds to words and access to meaning impose distinguishable demands.

#### Semantics

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

The study and representation of meaning in [language](#language). Lexical interpretation and sentence interpretation require interacting information; a contrast with a syntactic task need not isolate every semantic process.

##### Semantic association

↑ **Parent:** [Semantics](#semantics)

A relation based on meaning among concepts or expressions. Semantic-association judgments must be distinguished from perceptual similarity and response-selection demands.

##### Thematic role

↑ **Parent:** [Semantics](#semantics)

A relation specifying how an entity participates in a described event, such as an agent or recipient. Grammatical constructions can assign different roles to the same familiar words, making reversible sentences useful in comprehension tests.

#### Syntax

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

Principles and representations governing structured combinations of linguistic elements. Experiments must distinguish building or interpreting a structure from holding its elements in memory, detecting errors and selecting responses.

##### Syntactic complexity

↑ **Parent:** [Syntax](#syntax)

A description of structural demands in a sentence, such as a noncanonical dependency. Its operational definition should be explicit; longer strings, unfamiliar words and greater working-memory load do not define [syntax](#syntax) by themselves.

##### Agrammatism

↑ **Parent:** [Syntax](#syntax)

An impairment in grammatical structure in production or comprehension. It is not equivalent to reduced [speech](#speech) rate, dysarthria or all forms of [aphasia](neuroscience.md#aphasia); task design must separate grammatical dependencies from lexical and memory demands.

##### Sentential complement

↑ **Parent:** [Syntax](#syntax)

An embedded clause serving as a complement, such as “that the toy is in the box” in “she says that the toy is in the box”. The full report can be true even when the embedded proposition is false, making such constructions relevant to distinguishing a representation from reality.[Developmental Science 6, 346–359; random allocation to complement, false-belief or relative-clause training](psychology-bibliography.md#the-influence-of-language-on-theory-of-mind-by-courtney-melinda-hale-and-helen-tager-flusberg)

#### Language

↑ **Parent:** [Linguistics](#linguistics)

A system of structured representations and conventions supporting expression and interpretation. Its study distinguishes sound structure, grammatical organization and meaning without assuming that each maps one-to-one onto a single [brain](neuroscience.md#brain) region.

##### Vocabulary

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

Knowledge of word forms, meanings and their uses. Number of recognised words and depth or flexibility of knowledge are different dimensions.

###### Incidental vocabulary learning from reading

↑ **Parent:** [Vocabulary](#vocabulary)

[Learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) about unfamiliar words during engagement with text without direct [vocabulary](#vocabulary) instruction. Small encounter-level gains can be measurable, but long-term extrapolations depend on [reading](psychology.md#reading) amount, prior knowledge and test sensitivity.[Reading Research Quarterly 20, 233–253; incidental word knowledge after reading natural text and limited extrapolation](psychology-bibliography.md#learning-words-from-context-by-william-e-nagy-and-others)

###### Vocabulary depth

↑ **Parent:** [Vocabulary](#vocabulary)

Richness and precision of knowledge about a word, including relations, context and multiple meanings.

###### Vocabulary breadth

↑ **Parent:** [Vocabulary](#vocabulary)

The range of words known under a specified criterion. A recognition test and an expressive definition test need not identify the same boundary.

##### Written language

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

[Language](#language) represented through a writing system, including [reading](psychology.md#reading) and written production. [Spelling](#spelling), text generation and comprehension make partly different demands.

###### Written composition

↑ **Parent:** [Written language](#written-language)

Generating and organising written text. [Vocabulary](#vocabulary), sentence formulation and discourse interact with [spelling](#spelling), transcription and planning demands.[Exceptional Children 73, 147–164; 64 children with language-impairment history, measures at eight and written text at ten](psychology-bibliography.md#constraints-in-the-production-of-written-text-in-children-with-specific-language-impairments-by-julie-e-dockrell-and-others)

###### Transcription in writing

↑ **Parent:** [Written composition](#written-composition)

Processes producing the written form, including [spelling](#spelling) and the mechanics of recording text. Dictated and independent composition differ in these demands as well as in available support.

###### Spelling

↑ **Parent:** [Written language](#written-language)

Producing conventional written word forms. Immediate phonological plausibility, knowledge of a particular [spelling](#spelling) and fluent written production are distinguishable achievements.

###### Orthography

↑ **Parent:** [Written language](#written-language)

The conventional organisation of a writing system, including recurring patterns and relations to [language](#language).

###### Orthographic depth

↑ **Parent:** [Orthography](#orthography)

The degree of complexity or inconsistency in mappings between written and spoken forms. Its consequences depend on the units and outcomes assessed; early pronunciation accuracy, fluency and comprehension need not show identical patterns.

###### Artificial orthography

↑ **Parent:** [Orthography](#orthography)

An experimentally defined writing system with unfamiliar symbols and controlled mappings. It permits manipulation of [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) history while requiring separate tests of generalisation to natural [reading](psychology.md#reading).[Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition 37, 60–76; sixteen/32 adults, novel script, frequency/consistency and pretraining](psychology-bibliography.md#the-influence-of-consistency-frequency-and-semantics-on-learning-to-read-an-artificial-orthography-paradigm-by-joanne-s-h-taylor-and-others)

###### Orthographic consistency

↑ **Parent:** [Orthography](#orthography)

The regularity with which a written pattern predicts a pronunciation or other linguistic outcome under a specified context. Frequency and consistency are different properties.

###### Grapheme

↑ **Parent:** [Written language](#written-language)

A unit in a writing system, distinguished from a particular font shape and from its spoken interpretation.

##### Oral language

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

[Language](#language) expressed or understood through speech. [Phonology](#phonology), [vocabulary](#vocabulary), [grammar](#grammar) and discourse are distinct dimensions, and listening and speaking impose different task demands.

##### Sign language

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

A natural [language](#language) using structured visual-manual and other bodily signals. [Sign languages](#sign-language) have phonological organisation, meaning and [syntax](#syntax); they are not universal pantomime or merely a manual transcription of [speech](#speech).

###### Handshape

↑ **Parent:** [Sign language](#sign-language)

A configuration of the hand contributing to the form of a sign. Contrastive sublexical form need not be acoustic to participate in a phonological system.

###### Japanese Sign Language

↑ **Parent:** [Sign language](#sign-language)

A natural [sign language](#sign-language) studied in comparisons of [sentence comprehension](neuroscience.md#sentence-comprehension) across sign and Japanese [speech](#speech). Modality and [language](#language) differences must be considered separately.

###### British Sign Language

↑ **Parent:** [Sign language](#sign-language)

A natural [sign language](#sign-language) distinct from [American Sign Language](#american-sign-language). Acquisition history and [language](#language) proficiency matter to comparisons with spoken-language tasks.

###### American Sign Language

↑ **Parent:** [Sign language](#sign-language)

A natural [sign language](#sign-language) central to many studies of deaf and [hearing](neuroscience.md#hearing) native signers. It is not structurally identical to English despite frequent bilingual use.

##### Linguistic relativity

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

Accounts in which [language](#language) influences aspects of cognition or [perception](neuroscience.md#perception). A task-specific effect of verbal resources is weaker than a claim that [language](#language) is necessary for the existence of a sensory experience.

##### Speech

↑ **Parent:** [Language](#language)

Spoken-[language](#language) production and perception, involving acoustic, phonological, articulatory and higher-level information. [Speech](#speech) repetition and comprehension place different demands on these interacting processes.

### Open science

↑ **Parent:** [Science](#science)

Practices making research plans, materials, analyses and outputs inspectable and reusable where ethically appropriate. [Preregistration](#preregistration), transparent reporting and executable analysis support distinct aspects of openness. Protecting confidentiality can require restricted data access rather than unrestricted publication.

#### Research transparency

↑ **Parent:** [Open science](#open-science)

Making the provenance, design, measurements and analytical choices underlying a research claim sufficiently inspectable for informed evaluation. It can support [computational reproducibility](#computational-reproducibility) and [scientific replication](#scientific-replication), but does not by itself establish that the measured variable represents the intended construct.

#### Center for Open Science

↑ **Parent:** [Open science](#open-science)

##### Work by Center for Open Science

↑ **Parent:** [Center for Open Science](#center-for-open-science)

#### HARKing

↑ **Parent:** [Open science](#open-science)

Presenting a hypothesis suggested by inspected results as if it had been specified beforehand. The misrepresentation, rather than discovery itself, is the problem. Transparent [exploratory analysis](statistical-inference.md#exploratory-analysis), [preregistration](#preregistration) and independent testing preserve the distinction between generating and evaluating a hypothesis.[Details: HARKing; Scientific Creativity and Exploration](psychology-bibliography.md#registered-reports-by-center-for-open-science)

#### Registered report

↑ **Parent:** [Open science](#open-science)

A publication format involving review of the question and methods before outcomes are known, with in-principle publication acceptance conditional on adequate adherence and reporting. It differs from [preregistration](#preregistration) alone, which need not involve peer review or publication commitment.[Eligibility features and two-stage workflow](psychology-bibliography.md#registered-reports-by-center-for-open-science)

#### Preregistration

↑ **Parent:** [Open science](#open-science)

A time-stamped record of a research plan made before relevant results are examined. It distinguishes planned decisions from result-informed changes; it need not prohibit [exploratory analysis](statistical-inference.md#exploratory-analysis). For existing data, disclose previous access and knowledge.[Definition and FAQ on existing data](psychology-bibliography.md#preregistration-by-center-for-open-science)

#### Education

↑ **Parent:** [Social science](#social-science)

##### Science communication

↑ **Parent:** [Education](#education)

Communication that supports understanding and evaluation of scientific claims. Its effects should be assessed through relevant understanding or decisions, rather than the prestige of scientific terminology alone.

###### Debunking

↑ **Parent:** [Science communication](#science-communication)

Correcting a misconception with relevant evidence and an interpretable alternative. Immediate endorsement change, durable reasoning and practical decisions are distinct outcomes for evaluating [debunking](#debunking).

###### Refutation text

↑ **Parent:** [Debunking](#debunking)

A text that identifies a mistaken claim and explains an alternative account or why the claim fails. A short-term effect on a belief questionnaire does not establish a lasting change in teaching practice.

##### Primary education

↑ **Parent:** [Education](#education)

Early formal schooling. Psychological research in [primary education](#primary-education) must specify age, prior instruction, language and school context rather than assuming that one age or curriculum defines a universal developmental stage.

###### Attainment grouping

↑ **Parent:** [Primary education](#primary-education)

Organizing pupils partly by assessed achievement. The commonly used label [ability grouping](#attainment-grouping) should not imply that a placement measures a fixed innate capacity; grouping format, movement and instruction must be specified.

###### Within-class grouping

↑ **Parent:** [Attainment grouping](#attainment-grouping)

Organizing smaller groups inside a shared class for some instruction. Flexible grouping for a particular [learning](animal-behaviour.md#learning) goal differs from a permanent whole-curriculum stream.

###### Subject setting

↑ **Parent:** [Attainment grouping](#attainment-grouping)

Regrouping pupils by current attainment for a particular subject. The educational effect depends on assessment, movement, teaching and the comparison arrangement rather than the name alone.

###### Streaming by attainment

↑ **Parent:** [Attainment grouping](#attainment-grouping)

Assigning pupils to a group across much of the curriculum. It differs from [subject setting](#subject-setting) and from task-specific [within-class grouping](#within-class-grouping), and can concentrate differences in curriculum or opportunity across subjects.

##### Academic achievement

↑ **Parent:** [Education](#education)

Knowledge and skills acquired in an educational domain, assessed through specified tasks or records. [Mathematics](mathematics.md) procedures, mathematical concepts, [word decoding](psychology.md#word-decoding) and [reading comprehension](psychology.md#reading-comprehension) are distinct outcomes; a single aggregate score can conceal different cognitive demands.

##### Social stigma

↑ **Parent:** [Sociology](#sociology)

Social devaluation associated with an attributed characteristic in a particular context. [Social stigma](#social-stigma) concerns relationships and opportunities as well as individual attitudes; a diagnostic label does not have the same consequences in every setting.

###### Structural stigma

↑ **Parent:** [Social stigma](#social-stigma)

Institutional arrangements and practices that disadvantage a socially marked group. Changing personal attitudes does not alone establish that access to housing, work or appropriate care has become equitable.

###### Social distance

↑ **Parent:** [Social stigma](#social-stigma)

Desired or intended separation from a person or group in specified relationships. A social-distance [questionnaire](psychology.md#questionnaire) measures a stated orientation, not necessarily actual behavior or explicit denial of humanity.

###### Dehumanization

↑ **Parent:** [Social stigma](#social-stigma)

Denial or reduction of qualities or standing ordinarily accorded to persons, such as agency, emotional depth or equal moral consideration. [Dehumanization](#dehumanization) is related to [prejudice](psychology.md#prejudice) and [discrimination](psychology.md#discrimination) but is not established by every measure of [social distance](#social-distance).

###### Mechanistic dehumanization

↑ **Parent:** [Dehumanization](#dehumanization)

Representing a person primarily as an impersonal mechanism lacking a recognized subjective perspective or agency. A scientific biological explanation does not logically require [mechanistic dehumanization](#mechanistic-dehumanization); how an explanation is framed and used is an empirical question.

###### Mental illness stigma

↑ **Parent:** [Social stigma](#social-stigma)

Devaluation or exclusion associated with a perceived [mental disorder](psychiatry.md#mental-disorder). Public attitudes, [experienced discrimination](#experienced-discrimination), [anticipated discrimination](#anticipated-discrimination), [self-stigma](#self-stigma) and [structural stigma](#structural-stigma) are distinguishable and require different measures.

###### Genetic essentialism

↑ **Parent:** [Mental illness stigma](#mental-illness-stigma)

Treating genetic influence as a fixed, defining and categorically separating essence. Genetic contribution does not logically imply immutability, uniformity or inability to participate in ordinary social life.

###### Diagnostic overshadowing

↑ **Parent:** [Mental illness stigma](#mental-illness-stigma)

Allowing an existing diagnosis to substitute for adequate consideration of a separate difficulty. In mental-health settings, a psychiatric label can be inappropriately used to explain a physical complaint without examining relevant alternatives.

###### Experienced discrimination

↑ **Parent:** [Mental illness stigma](#mental-illness-stigma)

Reported or observed unfair treatment associated with an attributed characteristic. [Self-report](psychology.md#self-report), direct observation and institutional records provide different evidence and have distinct sampling limitations.

###### Anticipated discrimination

↑ **Parent:** [Mental illness stigma](#mental-illness-stigma)

An expectation of unfair treatment linked to an attributed characteristic. It can influence participation or concealment before a particular rejection occurs; absence of reported prior [discrimination](psychology.md#discrimination) does not prove that the expectation is unfounded.

###### Self-stigma

↑ **Parent:** [Mental illness stigma](#mental-illness-stigma)

Applying a socially devaluing [stereotype](psychology.md#stereotype) to oneself, with consequences for self-understanding or expectations. Anticipating realistic [discrimination](psychology.md#discrimination) is different from accepting the [stereotype](psychology.md#stereotype) as true.

#### Empirical equivalence

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

Two scientific accounts are empirically equivalent relative to a stated domain when they give the same observable consequences there. Agreement on the observations so far is weaker than agreement on every possible observation.

##### Empirical equivalence under uniform boosts

↑ **Parent:** [Empirical equivalence](#empirical-equivalence)

For [Newtonian mechanics](newtonian-mechanics.md) with forces depending only on relative positions, a common [Galilean transformation](special-relativity.md#galilean-transformation) leaves relative positions and accelerations unchanged. Internal mechanical observations therefore cannot discriminate rival choices of a wholly uncoupled privileged uniform rest state. The qualification concerns uniform motion; it does not imply that rotation or acceleration is unobservable, or that [special relativity](special-relativity.md) makes identical predictions.

#### Scientific confirmation

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

Evidence supports a scientific hypothesis without necessarily proving it. Distinguish increasing its credibility from showing it highly credible in absolute terms; a low [prior probability](bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability) can rise substantially while remaining low.

##### Inductive reasoning

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_reasoning)

Inference beyond what the premises deductively entail, often from observed cases to unobserved cases. This is distinct from [mathematical induction](foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction).

###### Problem of induction

↑ **Parent:** [Inductive reasoning](#inductive-reasoning)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_induction)

The challenge of justifying [inductive reasoning](#inductive-reasoning) without either assuming the reliability being justified or demanding a [deduction](mathematical-logic.md#deductive-reasoning) from premises that do not entail the conclusion.[section IV, part II](#an-enquiry-concerning-human-understanding-by-david-hume)

###### New riddle of induction

↑ **Parent:** [Problem of induction](#problem-of-induction)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_riddle_of_induction)

It concerns [projectibility](#projectibility), not a demonstration that every conceivable continuation is equally credible.[Chapter III, section 4](#fact-fiction-and-forecast-by-nelson-goodman)

###### Projectibility

↑ **Parent:** [New riddle of induction](#new-riddle-of-induction)

The eligibility of a predicate or generalization for extending an inference beyond examined cases. Agreement with positive instances alone does not establish projectibility: [grue](#grue) and green can fit the same past examples while supporting incompatible predictions.

###### Predicate entrenchment

↑ **Parent:** [Projectibility](#projectibility)

In [Nelson Goodman](#nelson-goodman)'s account, the standing a predicate acquires through a history of actual projections. It concerns the use of the predicate in inference, rather than merely the number of past objects satisfying it. Entrenchment is revisable and is not a proof of future reliability.[Chapter IV, sections 2–4](#fact-fiction-and-forecast-by-nelson-goodman)

###### Grue

↑ **Parent:** [New riddle of induction](#new-riddle-of-induction)

For a fixed cutoff $t$, let $E_t(x)$ mean examined before $t$. [Nelson Goodman](#nelson-goodman)'s predicate is $G_t(x)\iff(E_t(x)\land\operatorname{green}(x))\lor(\neg E_t(x)\land\operatorname{blue}(x))$. An already examined green object remains grue after $t$ without a colour change. The conflicting projection concerns objects not examined before $t$.

##### Duhem problem

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation)

From $T\land A\Rightarrow E$ and $\neg E$, infer only $\neg T\lor\neg A$. A failed prediction therefore does not logically isolate failure of the target $T$ from failure of [auxiliary hypotheses](#auxiliary-hypothesis) $A$. Independent calibration and varied experiments can discriminate packages; the logical ambiguity does not imply that every package is equally supported.[part II, chapter VI, section 2](#the-aim-and-structure-of-physical-theory-by-pierre-duhem)

##### Auxiliary hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation)

An assumption used with a target scientific hypothesis to obtain a prediction, for example an apparatus calibration or an initial condition. Independent evidence for an [auxiliary hypothesis](#auxiliary-hypothesis) helps distinguish a failure of the target from a failure of the test setup.

###### Ad hoc hypothesis

↑ **Parent:** [Auxiliary hypothesis](#auxiliary-hypothesis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc_hypothesis)

##### Bayesian confirmation

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation)

Evidence $E$ confirms $H$ relative to background $K$ when it increases its [conditional probability](probability-theory.md#conditional-probability). With $0<P(H\mid K)<1$ and positive evidence [probability](probability-theory.md#probability), [Bayes' theorem](probability-theory.md#bayes-theorem) gives

$$
P(H\mid E,K)>P(H\mid K)\iff P(E\mid H,K)>P(E\mid\neg H,K).
$$

Indeed, writing $p=P(H\mid K)$, $a=P(E\mid H,K)$ and $b=P(E\mid\neg H,K)$ gives the [posterior probability](bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability) $pa/(pa+(1-p)b)$; comparison with $p$ reduces to $a>b$. Thus [scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation) depends on the alternatives and background, not just on a statement's logical form.

###### Finite-population model of raven confirmation

↑ **Parent:** [Bayesian confirmation](#bayesian-confirmation)

Fix $r>1$ [ravens](organism.md#raven) and $M>1$ nonblack objects in a finite population. Under $H_0$, all [ravens](organism.md#raven) are black; under $H_1$, exactly one is nonblack. The total numbers $r,M$ are held fixed under both [hypotheses](#hypothesis), so the classification of one nonraven also changes between hypotheses. This is a deliberately specified model for [Bayesian confirmation](#bayesian-confirmation), not a universal rule about how bird observations supply evidence.

A uniform draw among nonblack objects returns a nonraven with [probabilities](probability-theory.md#probability) $1$ under $H_0$ and $1-1/M$ under $H_1$. A uniform draw among [ravens](organism.md#raven) returns a black object with [probabilities](probability-theory.md#probability) $1$ and $1-1/r$. The respective [likelihood ratios](statistical-hypothesis-test.md#likelihood-ratio) for $H_0$ over $H_1$ are therefore $(1-1/M)^{-1}$ and $(1-1/r)^{-1}$. When $M>r$, the former is closer to one, so supplies less support under these assumptions.

With equal [prior probabilities](bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability), [Bayes' theorem](probability-theory.md#bayes-theorem) gives [posterior probabilities](bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability) $1/(2-1/M)$ and $1/(2-1/r)$. More generally, with [prior probability](bayesian-statistics.md#prior-probability) $p$ for $H_0$, replace the two expressions by $p/[p+(1-p)(1-1/M)]$ and $p/[p+(1-p)(1-1/r)]$. Previously known information or a different sampling protocol requires different [likelihoods](maximum-likelihood-estimation.md#likelihood-function); selecting an object already known to be a nonraven need not supply new evidence.

###### Problem of old evidence

↑ **Parent:** [Bayesian confirmation](#bayesian-confirmation)

Already accepted evidence can support a newly considered theory even though conditioning again on a fact of [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) one produces no increase in its [posterior probability](bayesian-statistics.md#posterior-probability). Distinguish new learning, retrospective support and discovering a previously unrecognized theory–evidence connection.[chapter 3 of Theory and Evidence](#why-i-am-not-a-bayesian-by-clark-glymour)

##### Raven paradox

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raven_paradox)

“All ravens are black” is [logically equivalent](mathematical-logic.md#logical-equivalence) to “all nonblack things are nonravens”. An unrestricted rule that positive instances confirm universal generalizations would make a white shoe confirm the first statement. A [Bayesian confirmation](#bayesian-confirmation) account instead conditions on background knowledge and the sampling procedure.[section 5](#studies-in-the-logic-of-confirmation-i-by-carl-hempel)

#### Scientific explanation

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

##### Explanatory asymmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation)

Some physical relations permit [deduction](mathematical-logic.md#deductive-reasoning) in both directions while supporting different explanatory roles. Pole height and solar angle can explain a shadow length even though the height can also be inferred from that length. An evidential inference need not have the same direction as the [causal explanation](#causal-explanation).

##### Explanatory unification

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation)

##### Causal explanation

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation)

###### Causal relevance

↑ **Parent:** [Causal explanation](#causal-explanation)

##### Covering-law model

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation)

An approach to [scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation) that subsumes a phenomenon under general laws and specified circumstances. Its deductive form is the [deductive-nomological model](#deductive-nomological-model); statistical forms permit probabilistic support rather than entailment.

###### Inductive-statistical model

↑ **Parent:** [Covering-law model](#covering-law-model)

A form of [scientific explanation](#scientific-explanation) using statistical laws and high [conditional probability](probability-theory.md#conditional-probability), relative to appropriate available information. A low-probability event can nevertheless have a [mechanistic explanation](#mechanistic-explanation), so high [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) is not an uncontested necessary condition for explanation.

###### Deductive-nomological model

↑ **Parent:** [Covering-law model](#covering-law-model)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive-nomological_model)

An explanation derives an event description from laws and circumstances, with valid [deduction](mathematical-logic.md#deductive-reasoning), essential use of a law, empirical content and true premises. These requirements do not by themselves settle explanatory direction or eliminate irrelevant additions.[section 3](#studies-in-the-logic-of-explanation-by-carl-hempel-and-paul-oppenheim)

#### Demarcation problem

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demarcation_problem)

##### Verificationism

↑ **Parent:** [Demarcation problem](#demarcation-problem)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verificationism)

Distinguish a criterion of empirical meaningfulness from a methodological demand for verification, and conclusive verification from fallible [scientific confirmation](#scientific-confirmation).

##### Falsifiability

↑ **Parent:** [Demarcation problem](#demarcation-problem)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsifiability)

A hypothesis is falsifiable if some possible empirical outcomes conflict with it under specified testing assumptions. [Falsifiability](#falsifiability) does not mean that an anomalous reading identifies which member of a theoretical package is false, or that a falsifiable statement is well supported.

###### Falsificationism

↑ **Parent:** [Falsifiability](#falsifiability)

A methodology emphasizing conjectures exposed to demanding attempts at refutation. [Falsifiability](#falsifiability) is a property of claims under testing assumptions; falsificationism is a view about scientific practice.

###### Basic statement

↑ **Parent:** [Falsificationism](#falsificationism)

An intersubjectively testable report of an observable occurrence in specified circumstances, capable of participating in a test or attempted falsification. In [Karl Popper](#karl-popper)'s account, accepting a basic statement is provisional and may be reopened by further testing.[Sections 28–30](#the-logic-of-scientific-discovery-by-karl-popper)

###### Scientific corroboration

↑ **Parent:** [Falsificationism](#falsificationism)

[Karl Popper](#karl-popper)'s appraisal of a conjecture's survival of demanding tests relative to accepted [basic statements](#basic-statement). It is a retrospective methodological assessment, distinct from a proof of truth or an inductive [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) of truth.[Sections 82–84](#the-logic-of-scientific-discovery-by-karl-popper)

##### Pseudoscience

↑ **Parent:** [Demarcation problem](#demarcation-problem)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience)

#### Theory-ladenness

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory-ladenness)

The dependence of scientific observation, description or interpretation on background concepts and assumptions. Dependence on some theory is weaker than dependence on the truth of the particular theory being tested.

#### Constructive empiricism

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_empiricism)

Acceptance of a scientific theory involves belief in its [empirical adequacy](#empirical-adequacy) and practical commitment to its use, without requiring belief in its full truth about unobservables. This concerns epistemic attitudes, whereas the distinction between a [constructive theory](physics.md#constructive-theory) and a [principle theory](physics.md#principle-theory) concerns explanatory organization.[section III](#constructive-empiricism-now-by-bas-van-fraassen)

##### Empirical adequacy

↑ **Parent:** [Constructive empiricism](#constructive-empiricism)

A theory is empirically adequate when it correctly represents the observable phenomena, including those not yet observed. Agreement with the data already collected is therefore insufficient to establish [empirical adequacy](#empirical-adequacy).

#### Natural kind

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_kind)

A classification whose usefulness in prediction and explanation is grounded in relevant natural structure, rather than solely in a convenient label. Exact membership conditions can help identify a [natural kind](#natural-kind) but do not alone demonstrate its explanatory significance.

##### Homeostatic property cluster theory

↑ **Parent:** [Natural kind](#natural-kind)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostatic_property_cluster_theory)

An account of some [natural kinds](#natural-kind) in terms of traits whose association is sustained by causal processes, without requiring every member to possess every trait. For [species](biology.md#species), shared descent, [gene flow](evolution.md#gene-flow) and developmental processes can sustain relevant similarities. Whether a cluster supports reliable inference is an empirical issue.[sections 0.0–0.1](#homeostasis-species-and-higher-taxa-by-richard-boyd)

#### Scientific realism

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_realism)

A family of positions taking scientific theories to make literal claims about a mind-independent world, with warranted belief extending to at least some unobservable aspects. The extent of that commitment and the meaning of approximate truth require specification.

#### Primary-secondary quality distinction

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary–secondary_quality_distinction)

In an account of perception, distinguish an object's physical constitution from its powers to produce experiences in perceivers. The distinction between [primary qualities](#primary-quality) and [secondary qualities](#secondary-quality) does not by itself prove an external world or imply that dispositions are unreal.[book II, chapter VIII, sections 8–15 and 23–26](#an-essay-concerning-human-understanding-by-john-locke)

##### Secondary quality

↑ **Parent:** [Primary-secondary quality distinction](#primary-secondary-quality-distinction)

In [John Locke](#john-locke)'s account, a body's power to produce an experience such as a colour or taste through its physical constitution. Distinguish that power from the experience itself.

##### Primary quality

↑ **Parent:** [Primary-secondary quality distinction](#primary-secondary-quality-distinction)

In [John Locke](#john-locke)'s account, a quality such as extension, solidity, shape, number or [motion](classical-mechanics.md#motion), attributed to bodies independently of whether they are perceived. This historical list should not be treated as an unrevisable inventory of fundamental physical properties.

###### Lockean solidity

↑ **Parent:** [Primary quality](#primary-quality)

In [John Locke](#john-locke)'s terminology, a body's exclusion of another body from the space it occupies. This is not the material-science notion of [hardness](engineering.md#hardness): [John Locke](#john-locke) treats water as solid in this sense while distinguishing its yielding response from a diamond's resistance.[Book II, chapter IV, especially section 4: [https://barryfvaughan.org/text/philtext/locke/bk2/4.html](https://barryfvaughan.org/text/philtext/locke/bk2/4.html)](#an-essay-concerning-human-understanding-by-john-locke)

#### Scientific reproducibility

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

##### Scientific replication

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific reproducibility](#scientific-reproducibility)

Testing a scientific finding with newly collected data or an independently conducted study. Terminology varies, so the operational meaning should be stated.

##### Computational reproducibility

↑ **Parent:** [Scientific reproducibility](#scientific-reproducibility)

Obtaining consistent computational results from the same input data, code and stated computational conditions. Exact bitwise agreement is stronger than agreement to a justified numerical or statistical tolerance.

###### Data provenance

↑ **Parent:** [Computational reproducibility](#computational-reproducibility)

The recorded origins and transformations of data, including references, versions, preprocessing and identifiers.

#### Mechanistic explanation

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

An explanation in terms of entities, activities and their organization that produce a phenomenon.

#### Reductionism

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

##### Explanatory reductionism

↑ **Parent:** [Reductionism](#reductionism)

The stronger claim that higher-level explanations can or should be replaced by explanations in terms of more basic components and their interactions.

##### Ontological reductionism

↑ **Parent:** [Reductionism](#reductionism)

A claim about what entities and processes are made of, distinguished from a claim that every useful explanation must be expressed at the microscopic level.

#### Philosophy of biology

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of science](#philosophy-of-science)

##### Biological function

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of biology](#philosophy-of-biology)

A role of a biological feature, interpreted either through its contribution to a current system or through its evolutionary history. A claim about function does not imply foresight in evolution.

##### Multiple realizability

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of biology](#philosophy-of-biology)

A higher-level relationship or function can be implemented by different underlying physical mechanisms.

##### Explanatory autonomy

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of biology](#philosophy-of-biology)

The capacity of a field to frame and test useful explanations at its own level of description, consistently with the physical realization of its objects.

##### Physicalism

↑ **Parent:** [Philosophy of biology](#philosophy-of-biology)

The view that living systems are physically realized and do not require a separate nonphysical substance. This does not by itself settle which level of description provides the best scientific explanation.
