# Irradiance

↑ **Parent:** [Radiometry](radiometry-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irradiance)

[Irradiance](irradiance.md) measures incident [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) per actual receiving [area](surface-area.md). Its units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ match [radiant exitance](radiant-exitance.md), but it counts the incoming side of the radiation exchange. Measure incoming [angles](angle.md) from the [unit normal](unit-normal.md) pointing toward the incident hemisphere. Then

$$
E=\int_{\Omega_{\rm incident}}L_{\rm incoming}(\theta,\varphi)\cos\theta\,d\Omega.
$$

For $2\,\mathrm W$ distributed uniformly over $0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $E=200\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. If the receiving plane is tilted $60^\circ$ in the same broad uniform parallel beam, the incident [power](power.md) per actual [area](surface-area.md) is halved to $100\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. This receiving-surface [cosine](cosine.md) factor is geometric and does not assume [Lambertian reflectance](lambertian-reflectance.md). Together, [irradiance](irradiance.md) and [radiant exitance](radiant-exitance.md) describe how radiation supplies and removes [energy](energy-split.md) at a surface, while [radiance](radiance.md) preserves the directional information.
