# Complete positively curved paraboloid

↑ **Parent:** [Myers's theorem](myers-s-theorem.md)

The graph $z=x^2+y^2$ in Euclidean three-space has the displayed induced [Riemannian metric](riemannian-metric.md) and positive [Gaussian curvature](gaussian-curvature.md). It is smooth at its vertex; the polar-coordinate singularity is only a coordinate artifact. The metric dominates the Euclidean plane metric, so [upward stability of Riemannian completeness](upward-stability-of-riemannian-completeness.md) proves completeness. It has infinite diameter. In dimension two [Ricci curvature](ricci-curvature.md) equals Gaussian curvature times the metric, so this is a counterexample to compactness under merely pointwise positive Ricci curvature. Its curvature tends to zero at infinity.
