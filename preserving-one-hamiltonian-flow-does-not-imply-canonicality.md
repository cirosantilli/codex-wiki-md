# Preserving one Hamiltonian flow does not imply canonicality

↑ **Parent:** [Canonical transformation](canonical-transformation.md)

A [canonical transformation](canonical-transformation.md) preserves the [symplectic form](symplectic-form.md), equivalently the canonical [Poisson brackets](poisson-bracket.md), and therefore the Hamiltonian form of every Hamiltonian system. Preservation of just one selected flow is weaker. For $H=(q_1^2+p_1^2)/2$ on two coordinate pairs, $Q_1=q_1$, $P_1=p_1$, $Q_2=2q_2$, $P_2=p_2$ preserves both that physical Hamiltonian and its equations, but $\{Q_2,P_2\}=2$. Thus it is not canonical. Universally preserving Hamiltonian form instead forces $DF\,J(DF)^T=J$ by applying the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) to arbitrary Hamiltonian gradients.
