# Volume magnetic susceptibility

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetic susceptibility](magnetic-susceptibility.md)

[Volume magnetic susceptibility](volume-magnetic-susceptibility.md) is the dimensionless ratio of longitudinal [magnetization](magnetization.md) to internal [magnetic field intensity](magnetic-field-intensity.md) in a linear regime through the origin. Positive response gives [paramagnetism](paramagnetism.md). Always distinguish [magnetization](magnetization.md) $M=\mathcal M/V$ from total [magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md) $\mathcal M$: dividing by [volume](volume.md) changes the normalization. Mass-normalized and mole-normalized values likewise require the [mass density](density.md) or the [volume](volume.md) per [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) before comparison with $\chi_v$.

In the existing [Pauli paramagnetism](pauli-paramagnetism.md) formula, $M$ denotes total [magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md), and $g(E_F)$ is the total [spin-summed density of states](spin-summed-density-of-states.md). Relabel that response as

$$
\chi_B^{\rm tot}=\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal M}{\partial B}\right)_T
=\mu_B^2g(E_F),\qquad
\chi_B^{\rm vol}=\frac{\chi_B^{\rm tot}}{V}.
$$

Here $\mu_B$ is the [Bohr magneton](bohr-magneton.md). If its $B$ is a prescribed vacuum/applied [magnetic flux density](magnetic-field-split.md) $B_0=\mu_0H_{\rm app}$, the dimensionless apparent slope is $\mu_0\chi_B^{\rm vol}$. It agrees approximately with intrinsic $\chi_v$ only when the [demagnetizing field](demagnetizing-field.md) correction and magnetic feedback are negligible. The existing [Curie's law](curie-s-law.md) formula also uses a total-[magnetic moment](magnetic-dipole-moment.md), $B$-derivative convention; its $N$ counts moments rather than giving a [number density](number-density.md).
