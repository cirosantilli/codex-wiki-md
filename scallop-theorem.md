# Scallop theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow](kinematic-reversibility-of-stokes-flow.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scallop_theorem)

A [force-free](force-free.md), [torque-free](torque-free.md) [microswimmer](microswimmer.md) in a stationary [Newtonian fluid](newtonian-fluid.md), with negligible fluid and body [inertia](inertia.md) and steady [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md) at each instant, gains no net displacement from a reciprocal stroke: one whose sequence of shapes is exactly retraced in reverse. The environment and boundary conditions must also be unchanged during that retracing. Opening a two-shell swimmer and then closing it follows the same sequence backwards; doing the closure faster does not help, because [Linearity of Stokes flow](linearity-of-stokes-flow.md) changes the instantaneous [velocity](velocity.md) by the same factor as the stroke rate.

A single real shape coordinate that travels out and back along an interval gives this cancellation, as computed in the [force-free two-sphere stroke](force-free-two-sphere-stroke.md). Two independently controlled shape coordinates can instead execute a loop that is not retraced, avoiding this obstruction. Continuous rotation also need not retrace an interval: the [helical microswimmer with a spherical head](helical-microswimmer-with-a-spherical-head.md) provides another route to propulsion. The [scallop theorem](scallop-theorem.md) is a restriction on a stroke under stated conditions, not a claim that small organisms cannot swim. Purcell presented the opening-and-closing scallop and a swimmer with two hinges in [Life at low Reynolds number (1977), figures 6–7](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mdunphy/purcell.pdf).
