# Frozen scalar fields and the Friedmann constraint

↑ **Parent:** [Inflaton equation of motion](inflaton-equation-of-motion.md)

For a homogeneous canonical [scalar field](scalar-field.md), the [cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation](cosmological-perfect-fluid-continuity-equation.md) reduces to $\dot\phi(\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V')=0$. It implies the [inflaton equation of motion](inflaton-equation-of-motion.md) wherever $\dot\phi\ne0$, and by continuity at isolated turning points. It cannot by itself exclude a frozen field on a time interval. Indeed, choose $\phi=\phi_0$ with $V(\phi_0)>0$ but $V'(\phi_0)\ne0$; a [de Sitter scale factor in flat slicing](de-sitter-scale-factor-in-flat-slicing.md) with $H^2=V(\phi_0)/(3M_{\rm Pl}^2)$ satisfies the homogeneous [Friedmann equation](friedmann-equations.md) and [Friedmann acceleration equation](friedmann-acceleration-equation.md), but not the scalar equation. Varying the [inflaton action in an expanding universe](inflaton-action-in-an-expanding-universe.md) supplies the missing equation also for a frozen field, requiring $V'(\phi_0)=0$.
