# Surface area of revolution of a centrally symmetric curve

↑ **Parent:** [Surface of revolution](surface-of-revolution.md)

Let a simple closed regular planar [curve](curve.md) be invariant under [point reflection](point-reflection.md) through a center and remain strictly on one side of a coplanar [axis of rotation](axis-of-rotation.md), at [distance](distance.md) $d$ from its center. If $L$ is its [arc length](arc-length.md), its [surface of revolution](surface-of-revolution.md) has [surface area](surface-area.md) $2\pi dL$. To prove this, choose coordinates with the center at the origin and the [axis of rotation](axis-of-rotation.md) at $y=-d$. The [surface area](surface-area.md) formula is $2\pi\oint(d+y)\,ds$. Pair each point with its reflected point: the [arc length](arc-length.md) element $ds$ is unchanged and $y$ changes sign. Thus $\oint y\,ds=0$, proving the result. The one-side condition prevents the [surface](topological-surface-split.md) from passing through the [axis of rotation](axis-of-rotation.md) or requiring an absolute value with changing sign.
