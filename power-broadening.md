# Power broadening

↑ **Parent:** [Two-level saturation population model](two-level-saturation-population-model.md)

In the [two-level saturation population model](two-level-saturation-population-model.md), $p_e$ and the population-difference reduction $q$ reach half their resonant values at $|\Delta|=(\Gamma/2)\sqrt{1+s}$. Their half width at half maximum is therefore $(\Gamma/2)\sqrt{1+s}$ and their [full width at half maximum](full-width-at-half-maximum.md) is $W_\omega=\Gamma\sqrt{1+s}$ in [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md), or $W_\nu=W_\omega/(2\pi)$ in [hertz](hertz.md). This is [power broadening](power-broadening.md): stronger excitation addresses a wider range of detunings even though the lifetime has not changed.

Increasing $s$ improves the local population-difference reduction $q(0)=s/(1+s)$ but broadens the response. From $s=1$ to $s=9$, this reduction increases from $0.5$ to $0.9$, while the width increases from $\sqrt2\,\Gamma$ to $\sqrt{10}\,\Gamma$. The contrast approaches a limit and the width keeps growing. This single-atom width is not automatically the width of a velocity-averaged pump–probe feature.

For example, if the distribution of [velocities](velocity.md) is approximately constant across the narrow resonant classes, the population-only overlap integral contains two [Lorentzian line shapes](lorentzian-line-shape.md): a probe of half width $\gamma=\Gamma/2$ and a pump hole of half width $a=\gamma\sqrt{1+s}$. Their centres in $kv$ are separated by $2\delta$. The [convolution](convolution.md) of these profiles has half width $a+\gamma$ in that separation, so its [full width at half maximum](full-width-at-half-maximum.md) as a function of $\delta$ is $a+\gamma=(\Gamma/2)(1+\sqrt{1+s})$. Even this simplified pump–probe width differs from the single-atom [power broadening](power-broadening.md) formula. Finite [Doppler broadening](doppler-broadening.md) and coherent or multilevel effects require further analysis.
