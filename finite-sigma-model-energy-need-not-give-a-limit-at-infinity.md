# Finite sigma-model energy need not give a limit at infinity

↑ **Parent:** [Finite-energy field configuration](finite-energy-field-configuration.md)

For arbitrary maps $\phi:\mathbb R^2\to S^2$, integrability of the Dirichlet [energy](energy.md) does not imply continuous extension to the [one-point compactification](alexandroff-extension.md). For large $r$, take

$$
\phi(r,\vartheta)=(\sin a(r),0,\cos a(r)),\qquad a(r)=\log\log r,
$$

and interpolate smoothly to a constant on a bounded disk. Its exterior [energy](energy.md) is

$$
\frac12\int_{|x|\geq R}|\nabla\phi|^2\,d^2x
=\pi\int_R^\infty\frac{dr}{r\log^2r}=\frac{\pi}{\log R}<\infty.
$$

The field has no limit because $a(r)$ keeps winding. A continuous extension at the point at infinity instead requires $\phi(x)\to\phi_\infty$ uniformly outside sufficiently large disks. This fixed-limit boundary condition is standard for a [sigma-model lump](sigma-model-lump.md), but is extra information beyond finite [energy](energy.md) for arbitrary fields.
