# Intermediate value theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Continuous function](continuous-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_value_theorem)

The [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md) says that a real-valued [continuous function](continuous-function.md) cannot skip a value between two of its outputs. Let $a<b$ and let $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ be a [continuous function](continuous-function.md) on the entire [closed interval](closed-real-interval.md) $[a,b]$. Then

$$
\min\{f(a),f(b)\}\leq y\leq\max\{f(a),f(b)\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }c\in[a,b]\text{ with }f(c)=y.
$$

The word "between" includes both endpoint values: choose $c=a$ when $y=f(a)$, or $c=b$ when $y=f(b)$. If $y$ is strictly between them, every such $c$ lies in the [open interval](open-interval.md) $(a,b)$. [Continuity](continuous-function.md) on $[a,b]$ means [continuity](continuous-function.md) at every interior point, together with the endpoint [one-sided limits](one-sided-limit.md) $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=f(a)$ and $\lim_{x\to b^-}f(x)=f(b)$. No [derivative](derivative.md) is required.[§2.4, Continuity over an Interval and Theorem 2.11, The Intermediate Value Theorem](calculus-volume-1-by-gilbert-strang-and-others.md)

For finding a [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md), the useful special case is

$$
f(a)f(b)<0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }c\in(a,b)\text{ with }f(c)=0.
$$

Strictly opposite endpoint signs certify a [root bracket](root-bracket.md), because zero is strictly between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$. If an endpoint value is already zero, that endpoint supplies a [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) directly; an interior [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) need not exist. For example, $f(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$ has its only [zero of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) at the left endpoint. The [intermediate value theorem](intermediate-value-theorem.md) guarantees existence, but neither uniqueness nor a necessary sign-change test. Its numerical usefulness is that the [bisection method](bisection-method.md) can turn existence into successively tighter [root brackets](root-bracket.md).
