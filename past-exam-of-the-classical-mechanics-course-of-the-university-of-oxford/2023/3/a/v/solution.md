<h1 id="3/a/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Since $H$ is a [conserved quantity](../../../../../conserved-quantity.md), the [Hamiltonian energy balance](../../../../../hamiltonian-energy-balance.md) gives $H_t=0$. Since $f$ is a [conserved quantity](../../../../../conserved-quantity.md), $f_t+\{f,H\}=0$. Take an explicit [partial derivative](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) with respect to time, holding the [phase space](../../../../../phase-space.md) coordinates fixed:

$$
f_{tt}+\{f_t,H\}+\{f,H_t\}=0.
$$

The last [Poisson bracket](../../../../../poisson-bracket.md) vanishes because $H_t=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right)
=f_{tt}+\{f_t,H\}=0.}
$$

This is the [time derivative of a conserved quantity for an autonomous Hamiltonian](../../../../../time-derivative-of-a-conserved-quantity-for-an-autonomous-hamiltonian.md). The conservation hypotheses are understood to hold for every trajectory, as identities on [phase space](../../../../../phase-space.md).
