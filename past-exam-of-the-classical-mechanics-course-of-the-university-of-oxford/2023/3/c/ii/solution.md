<h1 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On the interior of the principal inverse-cosine branch, $0<q<\pi$, the given [canonical transformation](../../../../../canonical-transformation.md) has

$$
Q=\cos q\cos P,\qquad
\sqrt{1-Q^2\sec^2P}=\sin q,\qquad
p=-\sin q\sin P.
$$

Integrating $Q=\partial_PF_2$ suggests $F_2=\cos q\sin P+C(q)$. Its other derivative is $\partial_qF_2=-\sin q\sin P+C'(q)$; comparison with $p$ requires $C'(q)=0$. Hence a suitable [type-two generating function for a canonical transformation](../../../../../type-two-generating-function-for-a-canonical-transformation.md) is

$$
\boxed{F_2(q,P)=\cos q\sin P.}
$$

Both derivative equations reproduce the specified transformation exactly. Its mixed derivative is $F_{2,qP}=-\sin q\cos P$, so it defines a local [canonical transformation](../../../../../canonical-transformation.md) where $0<q<\pi$ and $\cos P\ne0$, equivalently where $|Q/\cos P|<1$. At $q=0,\pi$ or $\cos P=0$, the stated formulas fail to give a regular coordinate chart. These restrictions explain the square-root branch and the local meaning of canonicity. This is a [trigonometric type-two canonical transformation](../../../../../trigonometric-type-two-canonical-transformation.md).
