<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $\mathbf r=(x,y,z)$ with $\mathbf e_3$ along $z$, and interpret the symmetry as [axisymmetry](../../../../../axisymmetric-vector-field.md) of the whole [mass density](../../../../../density.md). A half-turn about $\mathbf e_3$ sends $(x,y,z)$ to $(-x,-y,z)$ and leaves the integration domain and [mass density](../../../../../density.md) invariant. Therefore $\int\rho xz\,d^3r=\int\rho yz\,d^3r=0$. A quarter-turn sends $(x,y,z)$ to $(-y,x,z)$; it gives

$$
\int\rho xy\,d^3r=-\int\rho xy\,d^3r=0,\qquad
\int\rho x^2\,d^3r=\int\rho y^2\,d^3r.
$$

All off-diagonal entries of the [inertia tensor](../../../../../inertia-tensor.md) consequently vanish. Writing $s^2=x^2+y^2$, the [principal moments of inertia](../../../../../principal-moment-of-inertia.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{I_1=I_2=\int_B\rho\left(\frac{s^2}{2}+z^2\right)\,d^3r,\qquad
I_3=\int_B\rho s^2\,d^3r.}
$$

Thus every [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) with third vector along the symmetry axis is a [principal body frame](../../../../../principal-body-frame.md). This is [axisymmetry of the inertia tensor](../../../../../axisymmetry-of-the-inertia-tensor.md). In general there is no requirement that $I_3=I_1$.
