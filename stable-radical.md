# Stable radical

↑ **Parent:** [Radical (chemistry)](radical-chemistry.md)

An unpaired [Electron](electron.md) does not set a universal [reaction rate](reaction-rate.md). A [stable radical](stable-radical.md) can retain radical character during isolation and storage under specified conditions; a persistent [radical](radical-chemistry.md) survives long enough under specified conditions to be observed. These descriptions concern lifetime and handling, rather than immunity to every possible [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). [DPPH](2-2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl.md) is a practical counterexample to the claim that all [free radicals](radical-chemistry.md) react immediately.

Two mechanisms help explain persistence. Spreading electronic character over several [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), represented by [chemical resonance](resonance-chemistry.md), can lower the [energy](energy.md) relative to a localized description. Bulky surrounding groups can impede approach to a reactive site and slow recombination. Lower [energy](energy.md) and slower [chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics.md) are different advantages: neither alone guarantees a long lifetime. A primary computational study explicitly separates these effects, using electronic and geometric descriptors; it does not directly measure every predicted lifetime.[A quantitative metric for organic radical stability and persistence using thermodynamic and kinetic features, Shree Sowndarya S. V., Peter C. St. John and Robert S. Paton, 2021: Abstract and Introduction, thermodynamic stabilization versus kinetic persistence](a-quantitative-metric-for-organic-radical-stability-and-persistence-using-thermodynamic-and-kinetic-features-by-shree-sowndarya-s-v-and-others.md)
