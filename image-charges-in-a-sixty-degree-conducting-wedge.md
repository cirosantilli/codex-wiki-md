# Image charges in a sixty-degree conducting wedge

↑ **Parent:** [Method of images](method-of-images.md)

For two grounded conducting half-planes meeting at angle $\pi/3$, place a [point charge](point-charge.md) $q$ at radius $d$ on the bisector $\theta=\pi/6$. The [method of images](method-of-images.md) closes after six copies: positive copies at angles $\pi/6+2k\pi/3$ and negative copies at $-\pi/6+2k\pi/3$, for $k=0,1,2$. Only the first positive copy is real. Opposite-sign reflected pairs make the [electric potential](electric-potential.md) vanish on both faces; the [uniqueness theorem for the electrostatic potential](uniqueness-theorem-for-the-electrostatic-potential.md) proves the construction. Summing the five [image charge](image-charge.md) [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) gives the [electrostatic force](electrostatic-force.md) $\mathbf F=-q^2(5/4-1/\sqrt3)\widehat{\mathbf e}_r/(4\pi\epsilon_0d^2)$, towards the intersection. The coefficient follows from distances $d$, $\sqrt3d$, and $2d$ to the adjacent negative, nonadjacent positive, and opposite negative copies.
