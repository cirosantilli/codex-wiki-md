# Discovery of 51 Pegasi b

↑ **Parent:** [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md)

[Michel Mayor](michel-mayor.md) and [Didier Queloz](didier-queloz.md) began observing 51 Pegasi in September 1994 and revisited its repeating [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md) signal in 1995. Their circular fit used 35 measurements: [orbital period](orbital-period.md) $4.2293\pm0.0011$ days, [stellar radial-velocity semi-amplitude](stellar-radial-velocity-semi-amplitude.md) $59\pm3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and residual scatter $13\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ after epoch-dependent [velocity](velocity.md) offsets. Apparently stable comparison [stars](star.md) checked the instrumental zero point. Low chromospheric [stellar activity](stellar-activity.md) and an inferred roughly 30-day [stellar rotation](stellar-rotation.md) period argued against [starspots](starspot.md); [light curves](light-curve.md) and the [spectral line bisector](spectral-line-bisector.md) tested [stellar pulsation](stellar-pulsation.md). These checks favoured orbital motion but had finite sensitivity. The reported $M_p\sin i=0.47\pm0.02$ [Jupiter masses](jupiter-mass.md) was not a true [mass](mass.md). Stronger upper bounds used stellar-spin information and assumed spin–orbit alignment, limiting a nearly face-on [binary star](binary-star.md) interpretation conditionally.[Mayor and Queloz (1995), pp. 356–358, figures 2–4, table 1, and alternative interpretations; scan: [https://wwelsh.sdsu.edu/CLASSES/ASTR510/PAPERS/Mayor-Queloz_51Peg.pdf](https://wwelsh.sdsu.edu/CLASSES/ASTR510/PAPERS/Mayor-Queloz_51Peg.pdf)](a-jupiter-mass-companion-to-a-solar-type-star-by-michel-mayor-and-didier-queloz.md)

The [hot Jupiter](hot-jupiter.md) matters both for [planetary migration](planetary-migration.md) and for how [exoplanets](exoplanet-split.md) are found. At fixed small companion [mass](mass.md), [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md) and [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md), the [stellar radial-velocity semi-amplitude](stellar-radial-velocity-semi-amplitude.md) scales as $K\propto P^{-1/3}$. Comparing chosen [orbital periods](orbital-period.md) of $4$ days and $12$ years gives an amplitude ratio of about $10.3$: a close companion can be much easier to detect and complete many [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md) during one observing season. Finding a giant [planet](planet.md) close to its [star](star.md) also asks how its present [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md) relates to where it formed. [Doppler spectroscopy](doppler-spectroscopy.md) supplies a dynamical constraint; it does not by itself establish a formation pathway or measure the [exoplanet](exoplanet-split.md)'s [radius](radius.md).
