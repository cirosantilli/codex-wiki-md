# Simultaneous diagonalization of two transverse conics

↑ **Parent:** [Conic section](conic-section.md)

Two [complex plane](complex-plane.md) [conics](conic-section.md) meeting in four distinct points can be diagonalized by the same invertible change of homogeneous coordinates. Their intersection points form a [projective frame](projective-frame.md): three on a line would force that line to be a common component by [Bézout theorem](bezout-s-theorem.md), contradicting a finite intersection. Move the four points to the coordinate points and $[1:1:1]$. Each equation becomes $ayz+bzx+cxy=0$ with $a+b+c=0$. Substituting $x=v+w,y=w+u,z=u+v$ gives $au^2+bv^2+cw^2$; the substitution matrix has determinant two. Composing this with the frame change yields the displayed simultaneous congruence of the symmetric coefficient matrices. This is congruence of quadratic forms, not a claim of simultaneous similarity of arbitrary matrices.
