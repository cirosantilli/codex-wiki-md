# Solvation

↑ **Parent:** [Solubility](solubility.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvation)

[Solvation](solvation.md) is stabilization through interactions between a [solute](solute.md) and its [solvent](solvent.md). It involves both the [solute](solute.md)'s surroundings and reorganization of the [solvent](solvent.md): neighbors move, orientations change, and existing interactions compete with new ones. [Electric forces](electric-force.md), [Van der Waals forces](van-der-waals-force.md), and specific interactions such as [hydrogen bonds](hydrogen-bond.md) can contribute. [Solvation](solvation.md) can also occur at groups on an insoluble material, so it does not require complete [dissolution](dissolution.md).[solvation](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/solvation.md)

[Solvation](solvation.md) by [water](water.md) is called hydration. [Hydration of ions](hydration-of-ions.md) is the charged special case, but neutral [molecules](molecule-split.md) such as [sucrose](sucrose.md) can also interact with [water](water.md) without becoming [ions](ion.md). A hydrated [cation](cation.md) retains its positive net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md); orienting neutral [water](water.md) [molecules](molecule-split.md) around it does not supply an extra [electron](electron.md). [Dissolution](dissolution.md), [desolvation](desolvation.md), and [ionization](ionization.md) describe different changes, as the examples below show.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; Opening sucrose example](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-1-the-dissolution-process.md)[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; “Ionic Electrolytes” and “Covalent Electrolytes”](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/11-2-electrolytes.md)

The useful question is therefore not just whether a [solute](solute.md) attracts a [solvent](solvent.md), but how much the whole system gains after paying for the rearrangements. [Solvation free energy](solvation-free-energy.md) describes a specified transfer into a [solvent](solvent.md); [solvation thermodynamic balance](solvation-thermodynamic-balance.md) explains why even cooling during [dissolution](dissolution.md) can accompany favorable mixing. The existing [solubility](solubility.md) discussion concerns how much remains dissolved at [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md).
