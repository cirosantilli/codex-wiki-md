# Reaction mechanism

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical kinetics](chemical-kinetics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_mechanism)

A [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md) describes how [reactants](reactant.md) become [reaction products](product-chemistry.md) through specified [elementary reactions](elementary-reaction.md). Adding the steps must recover the overall [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md); intermediate [chemical species](chemical-species.md) cancel. That accounting is necessary, but the proposed steps must also predict a [rate law](rate-law.md) compatible with experiment. Several [reaction mechanisms](reaction-mechanism.md) can pass the same test.[Chemistry 2e by Paul Flowers and others; §12.6, opening discussion and Relating Reaction Mechanisms to Rate Laws: net equations, elementary steps, and experimental constraints](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/12-6-reaction-mechanisms.md)

For example, the stipulated steps $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\longrightarrow\mathrm I$ and $\mathrm I\longrightarrow\mathrm P$ sum to $\mathrm A+\mathrm B\longrightarrow\mathrm P$. I is a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md): it is formed and then consumed. The [catalyst](catalyst.md) C in the existing [catalytic cycle](catalytic-cycle.md) instead starts available, participates, and is regenerated; its bound form can be a [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md). Both can disappear from the summed [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md), so cancellation alone does not distinguish their roles. Cancellation is algebraic bookkeeping, not an assertion that every step finishes instantaneously.

The net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) supplies consumption ratios, not a clock. For the stipulated two-step example, directional [reaction rates](reaction-rate.md) $r_1=k_1[\mathrm A][\mathrm B]$ and $r_2=k_2[\mathrm I]$ give $d[\mathrm I]/dt=r_1-r_2$ and $d[\mathrm P]/dt=r_2$. Initially $[\mathrm I]=0$ can mean zero [reaction product](product-chemistry.md) formation while A already disappears. In particular, $-d[\mathrm A]/dt=d[\mathrm P]/dt$ requires negligible intermediate accumulation here. A single [elementary reaction](elementary-reaction.md) with the same net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) would instead predict immediate formation at $k[\mathrm A][\mathrm B]$. Measuring both sides can reveal a distinction hidden by the net equation.

This makes [reaction mechanisms](reaction-mechanism.md) useful for designing observations: follow a candidate [reaction intermediate](reaction-intermediate.md), measure individual [reaction products](product-chemistry.md), or vary one starting [concentration](concentration.md) at a time. The [consecutive first-order reaction mechanism](consecutive-first-order-reaction-mechanism.md) quantifies an intermediate's rise and fall; the [parallel first-order reaction mechanism](parallel-first-order-reaction-mechanism.md) shows how identical [reactant](reactant.md) traces can conceal different [reaction products](product-chemistry.md). The [acetone iodination rate-law experiment](acetone-iodination-rate-law-experiment.md) connects such reasoning to a real laboratory protocol. The existing [reaction coordinate](reaction-coordinate.md), [potential energy surface](potential-energy-surface.md), and [energy profile](energy-profile-chemistry.md) describe configurations and barriers along a route; a [concentration](concentration.md) plotted against time describes a different observable.
