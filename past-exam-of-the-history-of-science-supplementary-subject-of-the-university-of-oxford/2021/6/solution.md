**Darwin's evolutionary argument.** [Thomas Robert Malthus](../../../thomas-robert-malthus.md)'s population argument supplied [Charles Darwin](../../../charles-darwin.md) with a way of making the excess production of offspring explanatory. In the first edition of [An Essay on the Principle of Population by Thomas Robert Malthus](../../../an-essay-on-the-principle-of-population-by-thomas-robert-malthus.md), potential population increase is contrasted with the growth of subsistence. Darwin's autobiographical account identifies his reading of Malthus in 1838 as important to his account of selection. That is evidence for a particular intellectual connection, not a claim that Malthus had already formulated Darwinian evolution.[1798 edition, chapters I–II: population and subsistence](../../../an-essay-on-the-principle-of-population-by-thomas-robert-malthus.md)[account of reading Malthus in 1838 and developing the selection explanation](../../../autobiography-of-charles-darwin.md)

The biological inference needs additional premises. Organisms vary, some differences are heritable, and those differences can affect persistence and [reproduction](../../../reproduction-split.md) under particular conditions. Producing more offspring than can all leave equivalent descendants creates an opportunity for differential reproductive success. [Natural selection](../../../natural-selection.md) then changes the representation of heritable variants across generations. Malthusian pressure without heritable variation does not by itself give this evolutionary mechanism.

In [On the Origin of Species by Charles Darwin](../../../on-the-origin-of-species-by-charles-darwin.md), the [struggle for existence](../../../struggle-for-existence.md) includes dependencies and physical conditions as well as direct competition. A plant failing to survive drought is relevant without a literal fight between organisms. Nor does the argument establish that a population always sits exactly at a fixed [carrying capacity](../../../carrying-capacity.md): environments and population sizes fluctuate. The key claim is a difference between potential reproductive increase and realized descendant contributions.[1859 edition, chapters III–IV: broad use of struggle and selection of heritable variation](../../../on-the-origin-of-species-by-charles-darwin.md)

**The wider environmental movement.** Population pressure could instead organize arguments about land, resources and environmental damage. [Road to Survival by William Vogt](../../../road-to-survival-by-william-vogt.md) connects human numbers with ecological productive capacity and its degradation. Later debates connected population with consumption and technology rather than treating head count as the only variable.[population, land degradation and ecological productive capacity](../../../road-to-survival-by-william-vogt.md)[opening decomposition of total impact into population and impact per person](../../../impact-of-population-growth-by-paul-r-ehrlich-and-john-p-holdren.md)

The elementary identity $I=Pq$ makes the distinction explicit: total impact $I$ is population $P$ times mean impact per person $q$. It does not establish that $q$ is constant or that every impact scales identically. If both are differentiable and positive, $\dot I/I=\dot P/P+\dot q/q$. A fall in one component can offset growth in the other. Likewise, agricultural output can change through technology, institutions and resource use; a food limit is not automatically an immutable natural constant.

Malthus therefore influenced different explanatory and political projects through a shared concern with limits and [reproduction](../../../reproduction-split.md). Darwin transformed the argument into a mechanism requiring heritable differences; environmental writers applied it to interacting resource and population systems. Neither the biological mechanism nor a population–impact identity alone entails a moral or policy prescription. Their historical influence should be analyzed together with the extra scientific assumptions that gave each application its content.
