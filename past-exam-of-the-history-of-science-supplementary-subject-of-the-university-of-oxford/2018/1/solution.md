**Acceptance was conditional, selective and historically constructed.** Christian university authorities could employ [Aristotelian natural philosophy](../../../aristotelian-natural-philosophy.md) because it offered a powerful account of an intelligible natural order and a teachable framework for reasoning about it. They did not thereby endorse every conclusion of [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md). The distinction between adopting an intellectual framework and granting its author unlimited authority is essential across this long period.

The attraction was partly explanatory. [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md) treated natural objects as things with characteristic capacities for change, rather than as arbitrary collections of appearances. His distinctions between matter and form, potentiality and actuality, and the [four causes](../../../four-causes.md) supplied a connected vocabulary for discussing living bodies, physical change and explanation. [Final cause](../../../final-cause.md) could make the regular organization of nature intelligible in terms of ends. Such an ordered world was compatible with a creator whose works could be investigated through reason. Compatibility did not require identifying [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md)'s own conception of divinity with Christian doctrine.[book II, chapters 1–3: nature, mathematical abstraction and the kinds of cause](../../../physics-by-aristotle.md)

There were also institutional advantages. A curriculum organized around authoritative texts provided a common sequence of study, established questions for commentary, and standards for evaluating an argument. [Aristotelian logic](../../../aristotelian-logic.md) was useful in theology and other learned disciplines as well as in [natural philosophy](../../../natural-philosophy.md). Training students to distinguish a valid inference from an unsupported assertion could serve religious education. The university did not have to choose between intellectual discipline and religious authority: authorities could present disciplined reasoning as part of the preparation required for higher study.

The Paris statutes of 1215 nevertheless provide a decisive counterexample to any claim of uninterrupted acceptance. They prescribed the teaching of [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md)'s logical works while prohibiting his books of [natural philosophy](../../../natural-philosophy.md) and metaphysics. The same document therefore exhibits both appropriation and restriction. A ban on particular books at one institution is not evidence that every Christian institution rejected all reasoning associated with [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md); conversely, later prominence does not erase the earlier prohibition.[1215: prescribed logical texts and prohibited books of natural philosophy and metaphysics](../../../statutes-for-the-university-of-paris-by-robert-de-courcon.md)

[Thomas Aquinas](../../../thomas-aquinas.md) illustrates a different strategy from wholesale prohibition. An argument could be philosophically valuable while failing to establish a revealed article of faith. Discussing the world's beginning, he rejected a necessary eternal world but also denied that reason could demonstrate its temporal beginning. Creation's dependence on God and a demonstrable beginning in time were distinct claims. This allowed extensive use of philosophical reasoning without letting an allegedly Aristotelian demonstration override revelation. It was not a doctrine that two contradictory propositions become true in separate compartments: the point was to distinguish the powers and limits of different kinds of justification.[part I, question 46, articles 1–2: necessity, creation and the limits of demonstration](../../../summa-theologiae-by-thomas-aquinas.md)

The resulting [scholastic natural philosophy](../../../scholastic-natural-philosophy.md) was consequently an interpreted tradition. Commentators could alter an explanation, distinguish its senses or deny that a particular conclusion followed, while retaining the questions and much of the vocabulary. This made the tradition more adaptable than a picture of students merely repeating an ancient book suggests. It also made departures possible from within university learning rather than only from outside it.

Its persistence into the seventeenth century should not be mistaken for scientific immobility or universal agreement. [Galileo Galilei](../../../galileo-galilei.md)'s telescopic account of lunar irregularities challenged a familiar contrast between terrestrial change and perfect celestial bodies. Yet the very argument addressed inherited problems about the constitution of the heavens. New observations could undermine particular Aristotelian claims without instantly replacing every part of a university curriculum.[1610: observations and interpretation of lunar relief](../../../sidereus-nuncius-by-galileo-galilei.md) The best explanation of acceptance is therefore a combination of explanatory usefulness, institutional entrenchment and selective theological accommodation. None establishes that acceptance was complete throughout 1200–1650.
