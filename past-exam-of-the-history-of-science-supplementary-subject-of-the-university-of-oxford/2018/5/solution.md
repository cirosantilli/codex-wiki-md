**Geological inquiry weakened the Bible's standing as a sufficient technical chronology of the Earth, while producing several different religious responses.** The authority of a text to date rocks, its authority as a record of human events and its religious authority were distinguishable. Between 1780 and 1850 they did not necessarily rise or fall together, and Christian readers did not all defend the same interpretation.

The difficulty was not simply that someone proposed an exceptionally large number for the Earth's age. [Stratigraphy](../../../stratigraphy.md) could establish a succession of events for which a short, single catastrophic history was inadequate. At Jedburgh, [James Hutton](../../../james-hutton.md) described steeply inclined older beds truncated beneath much more nearly horizontal younger deposits. Rounded fragments of the older material occurred in an intervening conglomerate. A natural reconstruction required deposition of the older beds, their consolidation and disturbance, exposure and removal of material, and later deposition. The fragments supplied evidence that the older material had already become rock before it was worn down and incorporated into the younger formation. The [unconformity](../../../unconformity.md) was therefore an argument about an ordered history, not merely an impressive landscape.[volume I, chapter VI, section II: the vertical and horizontal strata of the Jed and intervening pudding-stone](../../../theory-of-the-earth-by-james-hutton-1795.md)

Such sequences supported [deep time](../../../deep-time.md) without supplying a radiometric age. No [radiometric dating](../../../radiometric-dating.md) was available in this period, and a modern numerical age of the Earth must not be put into these authors' mouths. The accumulation of many independently inferred episodes instead changed what counted as a plausible timescale. [James Hutton](../../../james-hutton.md)'s cycles of destruction and renewal also belonged to a vision of an inhabitable, ordered Earth. His opening appeal to wisdom and purpose shows why the investigation cannot simply be equated with an intention to abolish religious explanation.[volume I, chapter I, sections I and IV: habitability and the system of decay and renovation](../../../theory-of-the-earth-by-james-hutton-1795.md)

[Fossils](../../../fossil.md) created a related difficulty. Comparisons of fossil and living animals supported [extinction](../../../extinction.md), and differing fossil assemblages could mark successive conditions. [Georges Cuvier](../../../georges-cuvier.md)'s reconstruction of lost animals thus made the geological past different from the present. Yet catastrophic change did not necessarily mean a recent creation of the whole Earth. In the 1827 English [Essay on the Theory of the Earth by Georges Cuvier (1827)](../../../essay-on-the-theory-of-the-earth-by-georges-cuvier-1827.md), a recent major revolution was distinguished from earlier revolutions and earlier populations of animals. A chronology assigned to the latest event was not automatically the age of every underlying formation.[main essay, concluding account at printed pp. 239–240: latest revolution and preceding animal populations](../../../essay-on-the-theory-of-the-earth-by-georges-cuvier-1827.md)

This distinction also complicates a simple opposition between biblical [catastrophism](../../../catastrophism.md) and irreligious [uniformitarianism](../../../uniformitarianism.md). [Robert Jameson](../../../robert-jameson.md)'s additions to that English edition explicitly related geological revolutions to the Mosaic deluge. Those additions must be identified as editorial material rather than silently attributed to [Georges Cuvier](../../../georges-cuvier.md). The volume demonstrates an attempt to make geological evidence corroborate sacred history even while recognizing a more extended sequence of changes. Disagreement therefore concerned which event a biblical account described and how its historical testimony should be compared with physical evidence.[separately headed Geological Illustrations by Jameson, On the Universal Deluge, printed pp. 417–418](../../../essay-on-the-theory-of-the-earth-by-georges-cuvier-1827.md)

[Charles Lyell](../../../charles-lyell.md)'s [Principles of Geology by Charles Lyell (1830)](../../../principles-of-geology-by-charles-lyell-1830.md) made explanation by causes now in operation a programme stated in its very title. This shifted authority toward comparison with observable processes rather than using a scriptural narrative as a ready-made geological mechanism. But [uniformitarianism](../../../uniformitarianism.md) contains separable commitments: continuity of natural laws does not logically imply an unchanging rate for every process, and neither claim by itself determines the interpretation of a biblical text. Conversely, acceptance of major catastrophes does not logically require accepting a universal Noachian flood as the explanation of every deposit.[volume I, title: former changes explained by reference to causes now in operation](../../../principles-of-geology-by-charles-lyell-1830.md)

Religious accommodation remained a live response. The title and purpose of [William Buckland](../../../william-buckland.md)'s 1836 [Geology and Mineralogy Considered with Reference to Natural Theology by William Buckland](../../../geology-and-mineralogy-considered-with-reference-to-natural-theology-by-william-buckland.md) are themselves evidence that a leading geological work could present the new knowledge as material for [natural theology](../../../natural-theology.md), rather than treat the subject as intrinsically hostile to religion.[1836 title page and stated subject](../../../geology-and-mineralogy-considered-with-reference-to-natural-theology-by-william-buckland.md) What became harder to sustain was an interpretation that required all geological formations and their organic succession to fit one short chronology and one flood. The result by 1850 was a redistribution and contestation of interpretive authority, not the uniform disappearance of the Bible's cultural or religious authority.
