**[Steven Shapin](../../../steven-shapin.md)'s challenge is persuasive as a criticism of a single, uniform founding event; it is much less persuasive if read as denying substantial changes in knowledge.** His opening provocation introduces an argument about the diversity of early modern practices, purposes and standards. It should be assessed as a historical interpretation, rather than treated as a literal assertion that nothing happened. The useful question is whether the [Scientific Revolution](../../../scientific-revolution.md) names an explanatory pattern or imposes an artificial unity on unlike developments.[Introduction: the opening challenge to a single Scientific Revolution and discussion of the diversity of practices conventionally grouped under that name](../../../the-scientific-revolution-by-steven-shapin.md)

There is a strong case for retaining the category in a qualified form. Compare [Aristotle](../../../aristotle.md)'s programme of explaining natural [motion](../../../motion.md) through the nature of bodies and different kinds of [cause](../../../cause.md) with [Isaac Newton](../../../isaac-newton.md)'s organization of [classical mechanics](../../../classical-mechanics-split.md) around definitions, [Newton's laws of motion](../../../newton-s-laws-of-motion.md) and mathematical consequences of specified [forces](../../../force-split.md). [Newton's law of universal gravitation](../../../newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation-split.md) brought terrestrial falling bodies and [planetary motion](../../../planetary-motion.md) into one quantitative framework. That changed both the content of explanations and the problems they could solve: the same relation could constrain [orbits](../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md), lunar [motion](../../../motion.md) and terrestrial [gravitational acceleration](../../../gravitational-acceleration.md). This is a substantial transformation even though [Newtonian mechanics](../../../newtonian-mechanics-split.md) was later revised.[Book II, chapters 1–3: natural change and causal explanation](../../../physics-by-aristotle.md)[Definitions and Laws of Motion; Book III, propositions I–VIII, connecting celestial motion and terrestrial gravity](../../../the-mathematical-principles-of-natural-philosophy-by-isaac-newton-1846-edition.md)

Instrumentation likewise altered what could count as evidence. [Robert Boyle](../../../robert-boyle.md)'s [air pump](../../../air-pump.md) attached a transparent receiver to a mechanical means of removing air. Its value was not merely that an observer saw an unusual event. Successive operations could vary the condition of the enclosed [gas](../../../gas.md), while observers compared such effects as the expansion of a partly inflated bladder and changes in [combustion](../../../combustion.md) or animal breathing. The apparatus made contrasts that ordinary unaided observation could not easily produce. Yet its results depended on the quality of seals, the operator's skill and decisions about whether a trial had worked. [Experiment](../../../experiment.md) was an organized achievement rather than nature speaking without mediation.[1660: apparatus description and experiments on the spring of air, combustion and respiration, including experiments 40–41](../../../new-experiments-physico-mechanicall-by-robert-boyle.md)

The difficulty for an unqualified revolution narrative is that these changes did not have one method. [Francis Bacon](../../../francis-bacon.md) emphasized the disciplined ordering of instances and cautioned against premature generalization. [Galileo Galilei](../../../galileo-galilei.md) deliberately reasoned about idealized [motion](../../../motion.md), then connected its mathematical relations to particular trials. [Isaac Newton](../../../isaac-newton.md) built geometrical arguments concerning [forces](../../../force-split.md) and used astronomical phenomena to select their application. These are related challenges to inherited authority, but not interchangeable versions of a universal method introduced on one date. Nor does a successful mathematical treatment of [planetary motion](../../../planetary-motion.md) establish an equally sudden transformation of all [biology](../../../biology-split.md) or medical practice.[Book I, aphorisms 19 and 95–105: routes from experience to axioms and the proposed reform of induction](../../../novum-organum-by-francis-bacon.md)[Third and Fourth Days: mathematical treatment of acceleration and projectile motion](../../../two-new-sciences-by-galileo-galilei.md)[Book I, opening sections; Book III, phenomena and propositions I–VIII](../../../the-mathematical-principles-of-natural-philosophy-by-isaac-newton-1846-edition.md)

Continuities also matter. [Euclid's Elements](../../../euclid-s-elements.md) furnished a demonstrative tradition that early modern authors could adapt rather than invent. Existing [astronomy](../../../astronomy-split.md) supplied observations and problems without which a new [celestial mechanics](../../../celestial-mechanics-split.md) would have had little to explain. The [Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica by Isaac Newton](../../../philosophiae-naturalis-principia-mathematica-by-isaac-newton.md) itself is predominantly geometrical in presentation: identifying modernity solely with replacement of [geometry](../../../geometry.md) by symbolic [calculus](../../../calculus-split.md) would misdescribe a central work of the supposed revolution. Equally, an investigator's religious commitments need not have disappeared for mathematical or experimental work to develop. The [Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica by Isaac Newton](../../../philosophiae-naturalis-principia-mathematica-by-isaac-newton.md)'s General Scholium combines claims about the limits of physical explanation with natural theology.[Book I: the demonstrative organization later mathematical natural philosophy inherited](../../../euclid-s-elements.md)[Book I: geometrical propositions; General Scholium, added in the 1713 edition](../../../the-mathematical-principles-of-natural-philosophy-by-isaac-newton-1846-edition.md)

These qualifications expose a geographical and social selection problem. If the historian defines the event by a few successful European texts in [astronomy](../../../astronomy-split.md) and [classical mechanics](../../../classical-mechanics-split.md), then concludes that Europe suddenly acquired the scientific mentality, the selection has already determined much of the result. [Robert Boyle](../../../robert-boyle.md)'s apparatus depended on skilled construction and operation; printed demonstrations depended on readers able to understand and assess them. A history of disembodied ideas misses the work that made claims credible. Conversely, locating that work in a social setting does not invalidate the measured relationships or make every contemporary theory equally successful.

**The [Scientific Revolution](../../../scientific-revolution.md) is defensible as a retrospective name for interconnected, uneven transformations, especially in [astronomy](../../../astronomy-split.md), [classical mechanics](../../../classical-mechanics-split.md) and experimental practice. It is misleading as a single moment when a universal scientific essence replaced ignorance.** [Steven Shapin](../../../steven-shapin.md)'s provocation improves the category by forcing its boundaries and assumptions into view. Abandoning the capitalized label is optional; abandoning the expectation of one origin, one method and one uniformly affected society is the more important historical correction.
