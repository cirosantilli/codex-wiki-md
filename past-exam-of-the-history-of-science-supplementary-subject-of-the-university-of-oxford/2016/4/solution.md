**The seventeenth-century transformation extended well beyond [astronomy](../../../astronomy-split.md) and mathematical [physics](../../../physics-split.md), but it was neither simultaneous nor uniform across every field.** An answer limited to new planetary arrangements and laws of [motion](../../../motion.md) would miss changes in the study of living bodies, small structures, materials and the organization of inquiry. Calling these changes a [Scientific Revolution](../../../scientific-revolution.md) should introduce a problem of explanation rather than decide in advance that they formed one event.

[William Harvey](../../../william-harvey.md)'s [On the Motion of the Heart and Blood in Animals by William Harvey](../../../on-the-motion-of-the-heart-and-blood-in-animals-by-william-harvey.md) provides a clear counterexample to a narrow account. His argument for [blood circulation](../../../blood-circulation.md) combined [observation](../../../observation.md) of cardiac action, the direction of flow indicated by [valves](../../../valve.md), and interventions using ligatures. It also employed a quantitative constraint: if the amount propelled at each beat is multiplied by the number of beats, the throughput soon exceeds the amount that could plausibly be freshly produced and consumed as nourishment. In modern symbols, throughput is $V_{\mathrm{through}}=qN$. This is an argument about a repeatedly circulating stock, not a requirement that all that [volume](../../../volume.md) be present at once. The book's chapter IX explicitly develops the comparison.[1628 work, chapters VIII–IX and XIII: the circulation hypothesis, quantitative throughput and venous valves](../../../on-the-motion-of-the-heart-and-blood-in-animals-by-william-harvey.md)

The intervention was not simply an application of a completed mechanical worldview to [physiology](../../../physiology-split.md). It drew on medical and anatomical problems and retained explanatory commitments which do not coincide with later accounts of the body. A historian can recognize the force of the circulation argument without making its author an advocate of every subsequent development in [medicine](../../../medicine.md). Nor did a circulation argument by itself supply microscopic [observation](../../../observation.md) of the entire connecting pathway.

[Microscopy](../../../microscopy.md) offers a second case. [Robert Hooke](../../../robert-hooke.md)'s [Micrographia by Robert Hooke](../../../micrographia-by-robert-hooke.md) made minute structures available to a new audience through selected [observations](../../../observation.md) and elaborate images. His examination of cork described the small compartments for which he used the word cells; this was not yet a general modern [cell theory](../../../cell-theory.md). The work ranged across biological specimens and manufactured objects, including the point of a needle. Its preface made the improvement of the senses through [scientific instruments](../../../scientific-instrument.md) a programme of inquiry. Preparation, illumination and representation were consequently part of making an [observation](../../../observation.md), rather than optional matters outside it.[Preface; Observation I on a needle; Observation XVIII on cork and its compartments](../../../micrographia-by-robert-hooke.md)

The investigation of air supplied another transformation. [Robert Boyle](../../../robert-boyle.md)'s pneumatic programme varied the conditions surrounding a specimen by using an [air pump](../../../air-pump.md). A claim about [pressure](../../../pressure-split.md), burning or sound could be pursued by operating an apparatus and reporting what happened as air was removed or admitted. This joined work on materials to questions about experimental evidence and the acceptability of explanations. It does not follow that every old chemical practice disappeared or that experimental facts selected one complete account of matter without further reasoning.[The apparatus and the experiments originally published in 1660; linked collected edition dated 1682](../../../new-experiments-physico-mechanical-by-robert-boyle-1682-edition.md)

These cases also direct attention to institutions and labour. The [Royal Society](../../../royal-society.md) and the printing of experimental reports created ways of advertising findings, soliciting responses and establishing reputations. Skilled construction, preparation and repair supported claims associated in print with learned authors. A new society did not automatically make everyone a witness on equal terms, and access to a costly [scientific instrument](../../../scientific-instrument.md) was not universal access to its results.

The breadth of change therefore strengthens the case for studying more than a sequence of theories. It does not justify treating every innovation from 1600 to 1700 as one inevitable step toward the present. Different investigations retained different parts of inherited [natural philosophy](../../../natural-philosophy.md); some techniques were older than their new settings, and changes in public credibility differed from changes in explanation. The useful conclusion is a plural one: significant transformations in [physiology](../../../physiology-split.md), [microscopy](../../../microscopy.md), experimental work on matter, and knowledge-making institutions accompanied developments in [astronomy](../../../astronomy-split.md) and [mechanics](../../../mechanics.md). Their connections have to be demonstrated through particular practices, not inferred solely from sharing a century.
