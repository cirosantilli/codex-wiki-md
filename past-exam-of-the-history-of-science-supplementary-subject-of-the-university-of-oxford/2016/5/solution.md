↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

The strongest immediate explanation of [Antoine Lavoisier](../../../antoine-lavoisier.md)'s execution is his position within the former system of royal revenue collection and the revolutionary prosecution of its personnel. He was executed on 8 May 1794 as one of the condemned former [farmers-general](../../../farmers-general.md). The proceedings were not a judicial assessment of whether his account of [combustion](../../../combustion.md) or [oxygen](../../../oxygen.md) was correct. Nevertheless, a simple opposition between a private scientist and a separate public official would misdescribe how his work had been organized.

The [Ferme générale](../../../ferme-generale.md) was a contracted system of revenue collection under the monarchy. Its members advanced resources and collected specified taxes under financial arrangements with the Crown. This made them prominent beneficiaries and agents of an unpopular structure. Its official character should not be confused with a modern salaried civil service: private financial interests and public fiscal power were intertwined. Such a position made a person vulnerable when revolutionary authorities treated the old financial establishment as a political danger.

A contemporary official report provides a better starting point than the later image of a scientist killed for revealing a natural truth. The Convention's report of 5 May 1794 concerned the administration and accounts of the former [farmers-general](../../../farmers-general.md) and led to their referral to the Revolutionary Tribunal. Its subject and procedural setting locate the accusation in public finance. The subsequent conviction treated the defendants as a group implicated in counter-revolutionary wrongdoing. The existence of accusations is evidence for what authorities alleged, not by itself [proof](../../../mathematical-proof.md) that every disputed calculation or allegation of fraud was true.[5 May 1794, Archives parlementaires, volume XC, pp. 74–80; referral to the Revolutionary Tribunal](../../../report-on-the-former-farmers-general-to-the-national-convention.md)[Contemporary or near-contemporary print, dated between 1794 and 1810, presenting the conviction through his membership of the tax-collecting company](../../../lavoisier-arrest-and-conviction-print-held-by-library-of-congress.md)

The wording of the central causal claim matters. A person might suffer because his scientific work made enemies, because a scientific institution was attacked, because an unrelated political category included him, or because the same resources supported both his research and the office under attack. These are distinct mechanisms. In this case membership of the fiscal establishment explains why he stood with the other defendants. Merely observing that he was an eminent [chemist](../../../chemist.md) does not establish persecution for a chemical doctrine.

Yet his scientific activities were not socially detached from State affairs. Wealth, official responsibilities, access to equipment and relations with other skilled workers helped make large experimental projects possible. His [Elements of Chemistry by Antoine Lavoisier](../../../elements-of-chemistry-by-antoine-lavoisier.md) presents a programme in which the handling of materials and exact accounting are central. The practice of weighing substances before and after a transformation depended on specifying what entered and left the experimental system. Such disciplined material accounting had a public and administrative setting as well as an intellectual one. That connection helps explain why the two sides of his career could coexist; it does not turn an accusation about financial accounts into a refutation of [conservation of mass](../../../mass-conservation.md).[1789 work, Part I on the composition and decomposition of substances and Part III on experimental apparatus](../../../elements-of-chemistry-by-antoine-lavoisier.md)

There is also a danger in reading the Revolution as a single actor uniformly opposed to knowledge. Demands for practical expertise and attacks on established privileges could coexist. Scientific achievement might be invoked in a request for protection without succeeding in removing a political classification. Failure to protect one practitioner does not establish that all practitioners, all disciplines or all institutions were being treated alike.

Later stories often condense the event into a striking declaration that the Republic had no use for learned investigators. An unverified dramatic sentence cannot carry the explanation. The evidence needed would be a traceable contemporary record of the words, their speaker and their procedural role; even that would not replace analysis of why the prosecution occurred. A careful answer can explain the execution without repeating the anecdote as an established quotation. [Antoine Lavoisier](../../../antoine-lavoisier.md) was a scientist whose official and financial connections placed him in a targeted political group. His case demonstrates the vulnerability of knowledge-making to struggles over State power, rather than a tribunal executing a chemical theory.
