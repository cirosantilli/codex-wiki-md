# Newtonian gravitational potential

↑ **Parent:** [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md)

The [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) $\Phi$ is [potential energy](potential-energy.md) per unit [mass](mass.md) of a [test particle](test-particle.md). Its [gradient](gradient.md) determines the [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md):

$$
U=m\Phi,\qquad \mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi,\qquad \mathbf F=-\nabla U=-m\nabla\Phi.
$$

In [SI](international-system-of-units.md), $U$ has units $\mathrm J$, while $\Phi$ has units $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}=\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$; its [gradient](gradient.md) has units of [acceleration](acceleration.md), not [force](force-split.md). Adding a constant to $\Phi$ leaves the [Newtonian gravitational field](newtonian-gravitational-field.md) unchanged. For an isolated [point mass](point-mass.md), the convenient reference is $\Phi(\infty)=0$. [Peraire and Widnall, MIT Dynamics, Lecture 16: potential per unit mass](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-07-dynamics-fall-2009/0bb19205a6316ba88f88284bf7257ec6_MIT16_07F09_Lec16.pdf)

Increasing the radius from $r_0$ to $r_1>r_0$ raises the [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md):

$$
\Delta U=GMm\left(\frac1{r_0}-\frac1{r_1}\right)>0.
$$

An external agent supplies this positive [work](work.md) if the initial and final [kinetic energies](kinetic-energy.md) agree; gravity does [work](work.md) $-\Delta U$. For $r_1=2r_0$, the required [work](work.md) is $GMm/(2r_0)$.
