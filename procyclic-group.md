# Procyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Profinite group](profinite-group.md)

A [profinite group](profinite-group.md) $G$ is procyclic if every quotient by an [open normal subgroup](open-normal-subgroup.md) is cyclic. Equivalently $G=\overline{\langle g\rangle}$ for one element $g$. For the forward implication, the sets of lifts of generators of $G/N$ are nonempty [clopen](clopen-set.md) sets. Any finite intersection contains a lift of a generator of the cyclic quotient by the intersection of those [open normal subgroups](open-normal-subgroup.md). [Compactness](compact-space.md) supplies one element generating every finite quotient, and hence generating densely. The reverse implication follows by projecting that generator. For a [pro-p group](pro-p-group.md), two distinct maximal [open subgroups](open-subgroup.md) would give a quotient $C_p^2$, so a nontrivial [procyclic group](procyclic-group.md) has a unique maximal [open subgroup](open-subgroup.md) $M$. Its [profinite Frattini subgroup](profinite-frattini-subgroup.md) is $M$, and any $g\notin M$ generates topologically: otherwise the proper [closed subgroup](closed-subgroup.md) it generates lies in a maximal [open subgroup](open-subgroup.md). The additive [group](group-split.md) of [p-adic integers](p-adic-integer.md) is an infinite example, generated topologically by $1$.
