# Reversing one slit at an array minimum

↑ **Parent:** [Fraunhofer diffraction](fraunhofer-diffraction.md)

Let a coherent array of identical narrow slits have total far-field [complex amplitude](complex-amplitude.md) zero at a chosen direction. Reversing one slit's [phase](phase-waves.md) by $\pi$ changes its [amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) from $a_j$ to $-a_j$, so the new total is $-2a_j$. Its [intensity](intensity-physics.md) is four times the single-slit [intensity](intensity-physics.md), independent of which slit was changed. For $N$ equally spaced slits, adjacent [phase](phase-waves.md) step $2\pi m/N$ gives such a zero when $m$ is not a multiple of $N$. A finite slit envelope multiplies the common [intensity](intensity-physics.md) factor.
