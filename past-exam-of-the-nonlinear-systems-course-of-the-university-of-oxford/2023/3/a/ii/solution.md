<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The space $\Sigma$ of all infinite [binary sequences](../../../../../bitstream.md) is [uncountable](../../../../../uncountable-set.md) by the [Cantor diagonal argument](../../../../../cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md): given any list $s^{(1)},s^{(2)},\ldots$, the [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $t_i=1-s_i^{(i)}$ differs from its $i$th entry at position $i$, so it is absent from the list. Part (i) shows that only countably many [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) are periodic. Their complement is therefore [uncountable](../../../../../uncountable-set.md).

Each [forward orbit](../../../../../forward-orbit.md) $\{\sigma^n(s):n\geq0\}$ is countable. If there were only countably many nonperiodic [forward orbits](../../../../../forward-orbit.md), their union together with the periodic points would be countable, contradicting the preceding argument. Hence **there are uncountably many nonperiodic [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md)**, rather than merely uncountably many nonperiodic starting points.

One can also exclude eventual periodicity. An eventually periodic [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) is specified by a finite initial word and a finite repeating word, so only countably many exist. Removing all of them still leaves an [uncountable set](../../../../../uncountable-set.md). This proves the assertion even if “nonperiodic” is understood to exclude [orbits](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) that eventually enter a [periodic orbit](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md).
