<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [left shift](../../../../../left-shift.md), $\sigma^p(s)=s$ precisely when the [binary sequence](../../../../../bitstream.md) is obtained by repeating its first $p$ symbols. There are exactly $2^p$ such [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md), although some have smaller least period. Hence the set of all periodic [sequences](../../../../../sequence.md) is a [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of finite sets and is at most countable. Passing from periodic points to [periodic orbits](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) cannot increase this cardinality.

To prove infinitude, for every integer $p\geq1$ take the repeated word $(0^{p-1}1)^\infty$. Its least period is $p$, because its ones are separated by exactly $p$ positions; a smaller positive period would carry a one to a zero. Distinct least periods give distinct [periodic orbits](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md). Thus **there are countably infinitely many [periodic orbits](../../../../../periodic-orbit.md)**.
