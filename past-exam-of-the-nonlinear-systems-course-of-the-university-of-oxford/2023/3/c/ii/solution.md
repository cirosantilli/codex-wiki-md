<h1 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $G=g_{s_1}\circ\cdots\circ g_{s_{n-1}}$. Because $g_{s_n}([0,1])\subseteq[0,1]$, taking the image under the same [function composition](../../../../../function-composition.md) gives

$$
\boxed{I_{s_1\cdots s_n}=G(g_{s_n}([0,1]))\subseteq G([0,1])=I_{s_1\cdots s_{n-1}}.}
$$

This uses preservation of set inclusion by every [function](../../../../../function-split.md), so decreasing [inverse branches](../../../../../inverse-branch.md) cause no difficulty. Since $f\circ g_L=f\circ g_R=\operatorname{id}_{[0,1]}$, cancellation of the first [inverse branch](../../../../../inverse-branch.md) gives the equality of images

$$
\boxed{f(I_{s_1\cdots s_n})=I_{s_2\cdots s_n}.}
$$

These compact [intervals](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) are [itinerary cylinders](../../../../../itinerary-cylinder.md): their symbols record successive positions under [iteration of a map](../../../../../iterated-function.md). In particular,

$$
\boxed{x\in I_{LLR}\ \Longrightarrow\ x\in L,\quad f(x)\in L,\quad f^2(x)\in R.}
$$

Conversely these three memberships allow successive substitution of the appropriate [inverse branches](../../../../../inverse-branch.md), and therefore characterize $I_{LLR}$ exactly.
