# Local cosmological tidal acceleration

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmology](cosmology-split.md)

Neighboring freely falling comoving particles in a homogeneous [Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric](friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) have separation $\mathbf r=a(t)\boldsymbol\chi$ and relative [acceleration](acceleration.md) $\ddot{\mathbf r}=(\ddot a/a)\mathbf r$, where $t$ is [cosmic time](cosmic-time.md) and $\boldsymbol\chi$ is constant. This is a local [tidal force](tidal-force.md) effect, of order $H^2r$ when the dimensionless background parameters are of order one. In a weak-field central-mass model retaining the homogeneous background tide, one writes $\ddot r=-GM/r^2+\ell^2/r^3+(\ddot a/a)r$. The detailed residual tide depends on the cosmological embedding; a bound orbit is not required to satisfy $\dot r=Hr$. Comparing $H^2$ with $GM/r^3$ estimates whether that background tide can affect its dynamics. A locally vacuum region with a [cosmological constant](cosmological-constant.md) has the corresponding outward term $\Lambda c^2r/3$.
