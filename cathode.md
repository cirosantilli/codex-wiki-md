# Cathode

↑ **Parent:** [Electrode](electrode.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode)

The [cathode](cathode.md) is the [electrode](electrode.md) at which the net [electrode](electrode.md) [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) is [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md): the reacting [chemical species](chemical-species.md) gains [Electrons](electron.md). This definition is independent of terminal sign. During [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) discharge, $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ [cations](cation.md) accept [Electrons](electron.md) arriving at the [copper](copper.md) [cathode](cathode.md) and become neutral [copper](copper.md). The [cathode](cathode.md) is then the positive terminal. In an externally driven [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md), the source's negative terminal supplies [Electrons](electron.md) to the negative [cathode](cathode.md). See [Pingarrón et al., §3.4: cathode](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2018-0109) and [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md).

An [ion](ion.md) carrying [electric current](electric-current.md) need not undergo [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) at the [cathode](cathode.md). For example, in the ideal [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) charge-balance picture, [potassium](potassium.md) [cations](cation.md) arriving from a [salt bridge](salt-bridge.md) remain dissolved while $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ [cations](cation.md) are consumed. Follow the [half-reaction](half-reaction.md) to identify the reacting species; follow [electric charge conservation](charge-conservation.md) to account for the other moving [ions](ion.md).
