# Full-film journal-bearing whirl

↑ **Parent:** [Journal bearing](journal-bearing.md)

A freely translating inner axle with prescribed positive [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) $\Omega$ and downward load $F>0$ has [full-film journal bearing](full-film-journal-bearing.md) [force balance](force-balance.md) equations

$$
R_r\dot r=-F\sin\phi,\qquad R_t r(\dot\phi-\Omega/2)=-F\cos\phi,
$$

where $R_r,R_t$ are the [squeeze resistance of an eccentric journal bearing](squeeze-resistance-of-an-eccentric-journal-bearing.md). Rotation supplies tangential fluid [force](force.md) $R_t\Omega r/2$. The equilibrium lies horizontally to the right and satisfies $F=R_t\Omega r/2$. [Linearization](linearization.md) there has imaginary [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md): the prescribed rotating axle continuously supplies energy, so viscous dissipation does not imply settling. For an initially concentric axle, putting $e=r/\Delta$ and $K=6\pi\mu\Omega a^3/\Delta^2$ gives the closed orbit

$$
\frac x\Delta=\frac{2K}{5F}\left[(1-e^2)^{-1/2}-(1-e^2)^{3/4}\right],\qquad z^2=\Delta^2e^2-x^2.
$$

This follows by eliminating time and integrating $x_e+3ex/[2(1-e^2)]=\Omega\Delta^2R_0e/[2F(1-e^2)^{3/2}]$. It applies to the leading inertia-free full-film model; adding [cavitation](cavitation.md), constraints or finite-length leakage changes the prediction.
