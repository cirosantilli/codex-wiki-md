# Discrete Fourier conjugate symmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md)

For real samples, taking the [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) of the forward [discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) changes the exponent's sign and proves the displayed symmetry. Hence $X_0$ is real; for even $N$, $X_{N/2}$ is also real. In the eight-sample example, $X_7=\overline{X_1}=4$ and $X_6=\overline{X_2}=2i$. This is the finite counterpart of [conjugate symmetry of trigonometric polynomial coefficients](conjugate-symmetry-of-trigonometric-polynomial-coefficients.md). It reduces storage for real [signals](signal.md) and provides a quick consistency check on a computed [discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md).
