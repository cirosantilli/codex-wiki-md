# Residual wall films in a receding gravity current

↑ **Parent:** [Falling film flow](falling-film-flow.md)

A falling [free surface](free-surface.md) does not instantaneously dry previously wetted rigid walls. Thin films remain above the bulk liquid and drain under the component of [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) parallel to the walls. For a film of thickness $b$ over a wetted distance $\ell$, [falling film flow](falling-film-flow.md) gives a speed of order $gb^2/\nu$, hence $b\sim(\nu\ell/(gt))^{1/2}$ at late times for an order-one wall angle. These films can be visible in a cross-section while holding asymptotically negligible volume. In a [V-shaped channel gravity current](v-shaped-channel-gravity-current.md), local bulk depth falls only as $t^{-1/7}$; at a fixed nonzero station with a finite previous maximum wetted height, film volume decays as $t^{-1/2}$ while bulk-sectional volume decays as $t^{-2/7}$. Thus the film does not change the leading spreading law.
