# Unbounded continuous witnesses to noncompactness of a real subspace

↑ **Parent:** [Heine-Borel theorem](heine-borel-theorem.md)

If $X\subseteq\mathbb R$ is not [compact](compact-space.md), there is an unbounded [continuous function](continuous-function.md) $X\to\mathbb R$. By the [Heine-Borel theorem](heine-borel-theorem.md), either $X$ is [unbounded](unbounded.md), when $f(x)=x$ works, or it is not [closed](closed-set.md). In the latter case choose $a\in\overline X\setminus X$; the [continuous function](continuous-function.md) $f(x)=|x-a|^{-1}$ is unbounded because points of $X$ approach the missing [limit point](limit-point.md) $a$. This proof uses the specific compactness characterization of real subspaces; boundedness of a general [metric space](metric-space.md) does not imply compactness.
