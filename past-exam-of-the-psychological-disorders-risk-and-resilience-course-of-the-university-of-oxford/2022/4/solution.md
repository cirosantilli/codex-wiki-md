↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**Individual characteristics contribute to adaptation, but their effects depend on [adversity](../../../adversity.md), relationships, opportunities and the outcome being measured.** [Psychological resilience](../../../psychological-resilience.md) is therefore not well explained by dividing people into inherently strong and weak types. A useful account asks which characteristic predicts which aspect of functioning under which conditions, and whether changing that characteristic would improve the outcome.

Cognitive capacities can help a person understand a problem, plan a response and use available support. [Temperament](../../../temperament.md) and [emotion regulation](../../../emotion-regulation.md) may influence how strongly threat is experienced and how readily attention can be redirected. [Coping](../../../coping.md) confidence may encourage engagement, while flexible strategies can help match responses to a controllable or uncontrollable situation. These are plausible mechanisms, not proof that a single score measures [psychological resilience](../../../psychological-resilience.md). Some apparently personal resources, such as social competence, also operate by eliciting or maintaining helpful relationships.

Longitudinal evidence supports contributions from both person and context. [Emmy E. Werner](../../../emmy-e-werner.md)'s Kauai study followed a birth cohort from early life into adulthood, identifying protective patterns among people exposed to substantial developmental risks. Individual capacities, supportive caregivers and opportunities outside the immediate family all featured in the account. Because these resources were not randomly allocated, their predictive value does not isolate a pure individual effect from the environments that helped develop or express it.[1993, prospective developmental course and protective-resource clusters](../../../risk-resilience-and-recovery-by-emmy-e-werner.md)

More specific evidence comes from [Ann S. Masten](../../../ann-s-masten.md) and colleagues' ten-year follow-up of 205 children recruited during elementary school. Using multiple methods and informants, they examined academic, conduct and peer competence in relation to [adversity](../../../adversity.md). Intellectual functioning and [parenting](../../../parenting.md) resources predicted favorable outcomes across domains; findings also suggested protective roles for IQ and [parenting](../../../parenting.md) in relation to antisocial behavior. Resilient adolescents resembled competent low-[adversity](../../../adversity.md) peers in several ordinary resources rather than exhibiting an entirely separate human type.[1999, longitudinal sample, competence domains and intellectual/parenting resources](../../../competence-in-the-context-of-adversity-by-ann-s-masten-and-others.md)

The causal interpretation remains qualified. Better intellectual performance can reflect previous schooling, health and caregiving as well as inherited variation. [Parenting](../../../parenting.md) can respond to child behavior, and both may share unmeasured antecedents. A variable measured before a later outcome is a stronger predictor than a simultaneous self-description, but prediction alone does not establish that raising its score will reproduce the observed benefit. An intervention needs to test the proposed process rather than assume the correlational ordering is causal.

Promotive and buffering effects should also be separated. For an adverse outcome $D$, [adversity](../../../adversity.md) $S$ and resource $P$, consider an illustrative model,

$$
\mathbb E[D\mid S,P]=\beta_0+\beta_1S+\beta_2P+\beta_3SP.
$$

When $\beta_3=0$ and $\beta_2<0$, the resource has a constant favorable association across the modeled [adversity](../../../adversity.md) range. A negative $\beta_3$ instead means that increasing the resource reduces the [adversity](../../../adversity.md) slope, $\beta_1+\beta_3P$. This is a [psychological stress](../../../psychological-stress.md)-buffering pattern on this scale. Neither coefficient alone proves causation; interaction can change with the outcome scale, and a statistically nonsignificant interaction may reflect poor measurement or insufficient power rather than equal effects everywhere.[2000, precision in protective terminology and interactive patterns](../../../the-construct-of-resilience-by-suniya-s-luthar-and-others.md)

Context can enable or suppress a capacity. Planning is less useful when there are no safe options, and willingness to seek support cannot ensure that responsive support exists. A [coping](../../../coping.md) response that helps during a brief challenge can be costly during prolonged insecurity. High observable achievement can coexist with internal distress, so defining success entirely by school performance can exaggerate the apparent protection conferred by conscientious behavior. Outcome selection changes which characteristics seem important.

Experimental evidence argues against treating context as a passive background. The supported foster-care offer in the [Bucharest Early Intervention Project](../../../bucharest-early-intervention-project.md) improved some later mental-health outcomes, with sex-specific uncertainty. Such a change cannot be explained solely by an immutable difference between supposedly resilient and nonresilient children. It demonstrates that modifying a developmental environment can change observed adaptation, while not establishing that every child benefits equally or through one unique mechanism.[2012, randomized environmental intervention and outcome heterogeneity](../../../attachment-security-as-a-mechanism-linking-foster-care-placement-to-improved-mental-health-outcomes-in-previously-institutionalized-children-by-katie-a-mclaughlin-and-others.md)

The relative importance of characteristics can change over time. A relationship or opportunity encountered later can alter a developmental course; stable adaptation after one event does not guarantee the same response to another. Prospective bereavement findings also show why baseline functioning matters: a favorable post-event score can represent stable functioning or improvement from earlier difficulty. A [personality](../../../personality.md) correlate measured only after the event may partly be a consequence of the adjustment being explained.[2002, prospective baseline and heterogeneous courses](../../../resilience-to-loss-and-chronic-grief-by-george-a-bonanno-and-others.md)

Genetic findings do not restore a simple fixed-trait account. A heritable [regression residual](../../../regression-residual.md)-based adaptation score still depends on [exposure](../../../exposure.md) measurement, the surrounding environment and inherited influences on the component symptoms. [Heritability](../../../heritability.md) partitions variation in a studied population; it does not say that an individual's [coping](../../../coping.md) capacity is fixed or that a social intervention cannot help. Equally, environmental influence does not make every individual difference transient or unimportant.

**Individual characteristics are consequential resources within a developmental system, not sufficient explanations of who will adapt successfully.** Good research compares personal and contextual predictors prospectively, tests interactions and mechanisms, and evaluates functioning across domains. Prevention can strengthen relevant skills while also protecting relationships and material conditions. This is a more useful consequence of individual-difference evidence than attributing continuing difficulty to an alleged lack of personal [psychological resilience](../../../psychological-resilience.md).
