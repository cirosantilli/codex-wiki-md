# Constant far-field phase excludes net vortex winding

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum vortex](quantum-vortex.md)

If a continuous nonzero complex field tends uniformly to one on large circles, its values on a sufficiently large circle lie in a disk about one that excludes zero. That disk is contractible, so the field's [winding number](winding-number.md) on that circle is zero. A lone unit-charge [quantum vortex](quantum-vortex.md) therefore cannot satisfy the literal boundary $\psi\to1$. For a vortex it is the [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md), rather than the entire complex field, that can approach one; $f(r)e^{i\theta}$ gives a simple illustration of direction-dependent far-field [complex argument](argument-complex-analysis.md).
