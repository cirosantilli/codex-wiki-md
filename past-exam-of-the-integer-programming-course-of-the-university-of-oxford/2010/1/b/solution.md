<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Delete vertex one and its two incident edges from a [Hamiltonian cycle](../../../../hamilton-cycle.md). The remaining edges form a Hamiltonian path through all other vertices, hence a [spanning tree](../../../../spanning-tree.md). Thus every tour is a [one-tree](../../../../1-tree.md), and both problems use the same edge-cost objective. The relaxation enlarges the feasible set, giving

$$
\boxed{z^{(1)}\leq z^{(2)}.}
$$

Conversely, a [one-tree](../../../../1-tree.md) in which every vertex has degree two is connected and two-regular, so it is a [Hamiltonian cycle](../../../../hamilton-cycle.md). Such a minimum [one-tree](../../../../1-tree.md) therefore also solves the travelling-salesman problem. This is the [one-tree travelling-salesman bound](../../../../one-tree-travelling-salesman-bound.md).
