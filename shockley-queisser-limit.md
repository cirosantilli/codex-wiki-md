# Shockley-Queisser limit

↑ **Parent:** [Solar cell](solar-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shockley–Queisser_limit)

The [Shockley-Queisser limit](shockley-queisser-limit.md) is the radiative conversion-efficiency bound for an ideal single-junction [solar cell](solar-cell.md), with one collected [electron](electron.md)-[electron hole](electron-hole.md) pair per absorbed above-gap [photon](photon.md), carrier thermalization, and [radiative recombination](radiative-recombination.md) required by the reverse optical process. Photons below the [band gap](band-gap.md) are not absorbed, while excess photon [energy](energy-split.md) above it is thermalized. Maximizing the electrical [power](power.md) over the operating [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) and then the [band gap](band-gap.md) gives a ceiling of roughly thirty percent for ordinary unconcentrated sunlight; its precise value depends on the incident spectrum, cell [temperature](temperature-split.md) and emission geometry. The original calculation by [William Shockley](william-shockley.md) and [Hans-Joachim Queisser](hans-joachim-queisser.md) obtained approximately $30\%$ at $1.1\,\mathrm{eV}$ for a $6000\,\mathrm K$ [blackbody](blackbody.md) source and a cell at $300\,\mathrm K$.[p.510, abstract: "maximum efficiency is found to be 30%"; band gap 1.1 eV, blackbody source 6000 K, cell 300 K, radiative fraction equal to one](detailed-balance-limit-of-efficiency-of-p-n-junction-solar-cells-by-william-shockley-and-hans-joachim-queisser.md)
