# Runge-Kutta method

↑ **Parent:** [Numerical analysis](numerical-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runge-Kutta_method)

A Runge-Kutta method advances an [initial value problem](initial-value-problem.md) $y'=f(t,y)$, $y(t_0)=y_0$, by sampling its [vector field](vector-field.md) at several trial states within one time step. These samples are [Runge-Kutta stages](runge-kutta-stage.md); a weighted combination of their slopes produces the next state. The construction improves on the single [tangent line](tangent-line.md) used by the [Euler method](euler-method.md) while requiring evaluations of $f$, rather than explicit higher [derivatives](derivative.md) of the solution. It applies to scalar equations and systems of [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md) alike. The coefficient choices distinguish methods such as [Heun's method](heun-s-method.md), the [classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method](classical-fourth-order-runge-kutta-method.md) and [implicit Runge-Kutta methods](implicit-runge-kutta-method.md).

For $s$ stages, write $A=(a_{ij})$ for the stage [matrix](matrix.md), $c_i$ for relative time nodes and $b_i$ for output weights. A time step of length $h$ computes

$$
Y_i=y_n+h\sum_{j=1}^s a_{ij}k_j,\qquad k_i=f(t_n+c_i h,Y_i),\qquad y_{n+1}=y_n+h\sum_{i=1}^s b_i k_i.
$$

Here $k_i$ is a slope, with units of state per time; $hk_i$ is a state increment. Another common convention writes $K_i=hk_i$, so its stage states are $Y_i=y_n+\sum_j a_{ij}K_j$ and its output is $y_n+\sum_i b_iK_i$. These are equivalent conventions: including $h$ both inside $K_i$ and again in the output would count the time step twice. The textbook formulation uses the increment convention.[Fundamentals of Numerical Computation, §6.4.3, equations 6.4.10–6.4.11: multistage recipe and tableau](fundamentals-of-numerical-computation-by-tobin-a-driscoll-and-others/6-4-runge-kutta-methods.md)

Usually the [Butcher tableau](butcher-tableau.md) obeys $c_i=\sum_ja_{ij}$, called internal consistency. Stage $i$ then combines slopes over an effective time $c_i h$. The [second-order conditions with independent Runge-Kutta abscissae](second-order-conditions-with-independent-runge-kutta-abscissae.md) explain what changes when time nodes are specified independently. A [Runge-Kutta stage](runge-kutta-stage.md) is generally an auxiliary approximation, not a separate accepted solution value at its node. Several stages can even have the same time node but different state arguments, as in the [classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method](classical-fourth-order-runge-kutta-method.md).

The useful idea is to approximate the [integral](integral-split.md) $y(t_n+h)-y(t_n)=\int_{t_n}^{t_n+h}f(t,y(t))\,dt$ without knowing the intermediate exact trajectory. Trial states provide places to sample $f$, and [Butcher order conditions](butcher-order-condition.md) make the resulting combination agree with the exact [Taylor expansion](taylor-expansion.md). More stages alone do not guarantee a higher [order of a numerical method](order-of-a-numerical-method.md): their coefficients must satisfy the appropriate conditions. [Embedded Runge-Kutta pairs](embedded-runge-kutta-pair.md) reuse these same samples for a second output and a practical [local error estimator](local-error-estimator.md).
