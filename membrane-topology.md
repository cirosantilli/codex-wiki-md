# Membrane topology

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](membrane-protein.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_topology)

[Membrane topology](membrane-topology.md) specifies the sidedness of a membrane-associated [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md): where its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md), [C-terminus](c-terminus.md) and intervening [protein domains](protein-domain.md) lie relative to the [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md). Side-specific placement is essential to interpreting a [cell-surface receptor](cell-surface-receptor.md).[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Membrane Proteins Can Be Associated with the Lipid Bilayer in Various Ways: aqueous domains on either side and receptor orientation](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/membrane-proteins.md)

For an original worked example, stipulate a continuous [polypeptide chain](polypeptide.md) with its [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) outside a [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md), exactly three complete crossings of the [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md), and no additional excursions into the [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md). Its segments lie, in order, outside, inside, outside, inside. A [protein domain](protein-domain.md) between the first and second crossings faces the [cytosol](cytosol.md); one between the second and third faces the exterior; the [C-terminus](c-terminus.md) faces the [cytosol](cytosol.md). Counting crossings determines these placements without specifying an atomic [protein structure](protein-structure.md).

The proof is a side-switching argument. Assign exterior the sign $+1$ and interior $-1$. Each complete crossing multiplies the sign by $-1$, so after $m$ crossings,

$$
s_m=(-1)^m s_0.
$$

An odd number puts the termini on opposite sides, an even number on the same side. This assumes complete crossings of one boundary; a chain that enters and returns to the same side without crossing must not be counted as a complete crossing. Nor does the rule determine which side the [N-terminus](n-terminus.md) starts on.

As a constructed accessibility test, put an [epitope](epitope.md) on the outside segment between crossings two and three, and another on the inside segment between crossings one and two. Stipulate an intact [cell membrane](cell-membrane-split.md) that excludes the test [antibody](antibody.md) and accessible [epitopes](epitope.md) that bind it. Applying the [antibody](antibody.md) outside can label only the first [epitope](epitope.md). If both become accessible after opening the boundary, the difference tests sidedness under those assumptions. Failure to label an [epitope](epitope.md) alone is insufficient: it could also be inaccessible because of [protein folding](protein-folding.md) or poor [antibody affinity](antibody-affinity.md). This is a proposed reasoning exercise, separate from the historical mixing experiment below.
