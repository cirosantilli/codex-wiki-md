# Asymptotic power series

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic expansion](asymptotic-expansion-split.md)

An [asymptotic power series](asymptotic-power-series.md) uses powers as its [asymptotic sequence](asymptotic-sequence.md). Near a finite point $a$, the condition in the title applies along the specified domain for every fixed $N$; at [infinity](infinity.md), inverse powers are convenient. It need not be a convergent [power series](power-series.md). Its [coefficients](coefficient.md) record the successive limiting responses, as the integral example below demonstrates.

The finite-order [Taylor theorem](taylor-s-theorem.md) supplies an [asymptotic power series](asymptotic-power-series.md) for a [smooth function](smooth-function.md) near a point, but [smoothness](smoothness.md) does not imply that its [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) converges to the [function](function-split.md). Define $g(x)=e^{-1/x}$ for $x>0$ and $g(x)=0$ for $x\leq0$. Every right-hand [derivative](derivative.md) is an [exponential function](exponential-function.md) times a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) in $1/x$, tending to zero as $x\downarrow0$. Consequently $g$ is a [smooth function](smooth-function.md) with every [derivative](derivative.md) zero at the origin. Its all-zero [Taylor series](taylor-series.md) misses $g(x)>0$ to the right. Zero and $g$ also have the same all-zero [asymptotic power series](asymptotic-power-series.md) on that side, despite being different [functions](function-split.md). An [asymptotic expansion](asymptotic-expansion-split.md) can determine all its own [coefficients](coefficient.md) without determining the represented [function](function-split.md) uniquely.
