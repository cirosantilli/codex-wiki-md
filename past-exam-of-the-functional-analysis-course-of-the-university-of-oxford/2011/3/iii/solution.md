<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Define the [finite-rank operator](../../../../finite-rank-operator.md)

$$
\boxed{P_nx=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^*(x)x_i.}
$$

It is linear and bounded, with $\|P_n\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^n\|x_i^*\|\|x_i\|$. Biorthogonality gives exactly the two required values on $x_j$. It also gives

$$
P_n^2x=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j^*(x)x_i^*(x_j)x_i=P_nx.
$$

Thus **$P_n$ is a bounded projection** onto $\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$, with kernel $\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker x_i^*$. Its action on the dense span determines it uniquely among [bounded linear operators](../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md). These are the partial-sum projections; no uniform bound in $n$ has yet been assumed.
