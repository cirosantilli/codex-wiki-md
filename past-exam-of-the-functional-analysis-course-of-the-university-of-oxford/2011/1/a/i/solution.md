<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [linear operator](../../../../../linear-operator.md) $T:X\to Y$ is a **[compact operator](../../../../../compact-operator-split.md)** if the image of the closed [unit ball](../../../../../unit-ball.md) has [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md) in $Y$. Equivalently, every bounded [sequence](../../../../../sequence.md) $(x_n)$ in $X$ has a [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) whose images converge in the [norm](../../../../../norm.md) of $Y$. This implies boundedness of $T$.

The **[Schauder theorem for compact operators](../../../../../schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators.md)** says that for a [bounded linear operator](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) between [Banach spaces](../../../../../banach-space-split.md),

$$
\boxed{T:X\to Y\text{ compact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T^*:Y^*\to X^*\text{ compact},}
$$

where the [dual map of bounded linear operator](../../../../../transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator.md) is $T^*f=f\circ T$. The forward implication also holds for normed spaces; completeness of the target gives the usual converse. The [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) definition requires the image [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md)'s limit to belong to $Y$, so that completeness qualification matters when stating the theorem beyond [Banach spaces](../../../../../banach-space-split.md).
