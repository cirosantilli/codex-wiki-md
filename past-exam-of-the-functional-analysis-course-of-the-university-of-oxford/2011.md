## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

A [linear operator](../vector-space.md#linear-operator) $T:X\to Y$ is a **[compact operator](../compact-operator.md)** if the image of the closed [unit ball](../normed-vector-space.md#unit-ball) has [compact](../topology.md#compact-space) [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) in $Y$. Equivalently, every bounded [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) $(x_n)$ in $X$ has a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) whose images converge in the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) of $Y$. This implies boundedness of $T$.

The **[Schauder theorem for compact operators](../compact-operator.md#schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators)** says that for a [bounded linear operator](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-operator) between [Banach spaces](../banach-space.md),

$$
\boxed{T:X\to Y\text{ compact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T^*:Y^*\to X^*\text{ compact},}
$$

where the [dual map of bounded linear operator](../continuous-dual-space.md#transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator) is $T^*f=f\circ T$. The forward implication also holds for normed spaces; completeness of the target gives the usual converse. The [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) definition requires the image [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence)'s limit to belong to $Y$, so that completeness qualification matters when stating the theorem beyond [Banach spaces](../banach-space.md).

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Let $A_n$ be finite-rank bounded operators with $\|T-A_n\|\to0$ in [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). Fix $\epsilon>0$ and choose $n$ with $\|T-A_n\|<\epsilon/3$. The set $A_n(B_X)$ is bounded in a finite-dimensional [subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace), hence has a finite $\epsilon/3$-net $y_1,\ldots,y_m$.

For each $x\in B_X$, some $j$ satisfies

$$
\|Tx-y_j\|\leq\|(T-A_n)x\|+\|A_nx-y_j\|<2\epsilon/3.
$$

Thus $T(B_X)$ is [totally bounded](../topological-analysis.md#totally-bounded-space). Its [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) is [totally bounded](../topological-analysis.md#totally-bounded-space) and, as a closed subset of the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $Y$, [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness). A [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) [totally bounded](../topological-analysis.md#totally-bounded-space) metric space is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space). Consequently **$T$ is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space)**.

The operator-norm limit is bounded, since $\|T\|\leq\|A_n\|+\|T-A_n\|$ for any sufficiently good approximation. This proves directly that an [approximable operator](../topological-vector-space.md#approximable-operator) into a [Banach space](../banach-space.md) is [compact](../topology.md#compact-space); no assertion that every [compact operator](../compact-operator.md) on an arbitrary [Banach space](../banach-space.md) is approximable is needed.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Let $Q_N$ be truncation to the first $N$ coordinates in $\ell^1$. For $x\in\ell^1$, boundedness of $T$ and absolute convergence give

$$
(T-TQ_N)x=\sum_{n>N}x_nTe_n,\qquad
\|(T-TQ_N)x\|_1\leq\sup_{n>N}\|Te_n\|_1\,\|x\|_1.
$$

Applying the difference to each $e_n$ with $n>N$ gives the reverse bound on the [operator norm](../continuous-dual-space.md#operator-norm). Hence

$$
\boxed{\|T-TQ_N\|=\sup_{n>N}\|Te_n\|_1.}
$$

The stated condition makes this tend to zero. Each $TQ_N$ has finite rank, so part (a)(ii) proves [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space). Thus the condition is **sufficient** on $\ell^1$.

For failure of necessity, set $Tx=(\sum_{n\geq1}x_n)e_1$. The sum defines a bounded [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) on $\ell^1$, so $T$ is a bounded rank-one operator and therefore [compact](../topology.md#compact-space). But $Te_n=e_1$ for every $n$, so $\|Te_n\|_1=1$. Thus **[compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) need not make the basis images tend to zero on $\ell^1$**.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Let $1<p<\infty$ and $q=p/(p-1)$. The dual of $\ell^p$ is $\ell^q$. For every $a\in\ell^q$, $\langle a,e_n\rangle=a_n\to0$, so $e_n\rightharpoonup0$ weakly. A [compact operator](../compact-operator.md) sends a weakly convergent [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) to a norm-convergent [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) with the corresponding limit. Indeed, any failure of $\|Te_n\|\to0$ would give a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) bounded away from zero, with a further norm-convergent [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) by [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space); weak [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $T$ forces that [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) limit to be zero, a contradiction. The condition is therefore **necessary**.

For failure of sufficiency, partition the positive integers into consecutive blocks

$$
B_k=\{k(k-1)/2+1,\ldots,k(k+1)/2\},\qquad |B_k|=k,
$$

and define the [block averaging compactness counterexample](../compact-operator.md#block-averaging-compactness-counterexample)

$$
\boxed{(Tx)_k=k^{-1/q}\sum_{j\in B_k}x_j.}
$$

By [Hölder's inequality](../real-analysis.md#holder-s-inequality), $|(Tx)_k|^p\leq\sum_{j\in B_k}|x_j|^p$. Summing over disjoint blocks shows $\|Tx\|_p\leq\|x\|_p$, so $T$ is bounded. If $j\in B_k$, then $Te_j=k^{-1/q}e_k$; as $j\to\infty$, also $k\to\infty$, so $\|Te_j\|_p\to0$.

However, the [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) $x^{(k)}=k^{-1/p}\mathbf1_{B_k}$ have [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) one and satisfy $Tx^{(k)}=e_k$. Distinct output [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) have distance $2^{1/p}$, so this bounded [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence) of inputs has no norm-convergent image [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence). Thus **$T$ is not [compact](../topology.md#compact-space)**.

To connect with the hint, define $(Uy)_j=k^{-1/q}y_k$ for $j\in B_k$. Then $U:\ell^q\to\ell^q$ is an isometry, and the dual pairing calculation gives $T=U^*$. The [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) $Ue_k$ have disjoint supports and [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) one. The direct estimates above establish the counterexample without relying only on the hint.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

The [weak topology](../weak-topology.md) $\sigma(X,X^*)$ is the coarsest topology on $X$ making every [continuous linear functional](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-functional) $f\in X^*$ continuous. A basic neighborhood of $x_0$ is specified by finitely many inequalities $|f_j(x-x_0)|<\epsilon$.

The [weak-star topology](../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) $\sigma(X^*,X)$ is the coarsest topology on $X^*$ making every evaluation $f\mapsto f(x)$, for $x\in X$, continuous. Its basic neighborhoods use finitely many such evaluations. Nets converge in these topologies exactly when all the corresponding [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar) evaluations converge.

Let $J_X:X\to X^{**}$ be the canonical isometric embedding, $J_Xx(f)=f(x)$. **[Goldstine theorem](../functional-analysis.md#goldstine-theorem)** states

$$
\boxed{\overline{J_X(B_X)}^{\sigma(X^{**},X^*)}=B_{X^{**}}.}
$$

Here the balls are closed [unit balls](../normed-vector-space.md#unit-ball). Thus any bidual unit [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) can be approximated in finitely many dual evaluations by unit [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) from $X$; this does not claim [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) density or sequential density in [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) generality.

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The [weak topology](../weak-topology.md) is coarser than the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) topology, so [norm convergence](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-convergence) implies [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence), and

$$
\overline C^{\|\cdot\|}\subseteq\overline C^{\mathrm{weak}}.
$$

For the reverse inclusion, let $x\notin K=\overline C^{\|\cdot\|}$. The set $K$ is closed and convex. The [Hahn-Banach separation theorem](../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-separation-theorem) supplies $f\in X^*$ and $a\in\mathbb R$ such that $f(x)>a\geq\sup_{y\in K}f(y)$. The set $\{z:f(z)>a\}$ is weakly open, contains $x$ and misses $C$. Hence $x$ is outside the weak [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) as well. If $C$ is empty the equality is immediate. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\overline C^{\mathrm{weak}}=\overline C^{\|\cdot\|}\quad\text{for every convex }C.}
$$

The separating functional explains the role of convexity: an arbitrary norm-closed subset need not be weakly closed.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

A **[reflexive Banach space](../functional-analysis.md#reflexive-banach-space)** is one for which $J_X:X\to X^{**}$ is onto. If $X$ is reflexive, every $\theta\in X^{**}$ has the form $\theta(f)=f(x)$ for some $x\in X$. Thus the functionals defining $\sigma(X^*,X^{**})$ and $\sigma(X^*,X)$ are precisely the same, proving equality of the topologies.

Conversely, assume those topologies coincide. For any $\theta\in X^{**}$, the [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) $f\mapsto\theta(f)$ on $X^*$ is then weak-star continuous. Its [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) at zero gives finitely many $x_1,\ldots,x_m\in X$ and $\epsilon>0$ such that

$$
|f(x_j)|<\epsilon\ \text{for all }j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\theta(f)|<1.
$$

If all these evaluations are zero, arbitrary [scalar](../vector-space.md#scalar) multiples of $f$ still satisfy the inequalities, forcing $\theta(f)=0$. Hence $\theta$ factors through the finite-dimensional evaluation map $f\mapsto(f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_m))$. A [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) on its image extends to $\mathbb R^m$, so for suitable [scalars](../vector-space.md#scalar) $a_j$,

$$
\theta(f)=\sum_{j=1}^ma_jf(x_j)=f\left(\sum_{j=1}^ma_jx_j\right).
$$

Thus $\theta$ lies in $J_X(X)$. Since it was arbitrary, $X$ is reflexive. We have proved

$$
\boxed{X\text{ reflexive}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma(X^*,X)=\sigma(X^*,X^{**}).}
$$

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

The domain carries $\sigma(Z^{**},Z^*)$, and the target carries $\sigma(X^*,X)$. For each $x\in X\subseteq Z^*$,

$$
(R\theta)(x)=\theta(x),
$$

which is one of the defining weak-star continuous evaluations on $Z^{**}$. Thus **$R$ is weak-star continuous**.

Restriction cannot increase a functional's [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), so $R(B_{Z^{**}})\subseteq B_{X^*}$. Conversely, any $f\in B_{X^*}$ is a bounded [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) on the [subspace](../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) $X$ of $Z^*$. The [Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem) extends it to a functional $\theta\in(Z^*)^*=Z^{**}$ with the same [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). Then $R\theta=f$ and $\|\theta\|\leq1$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{R(B_{Z^{**}})=B_{X^*}.}
$$

This proves exact surjectivity on closed balls, not just density of the image.

<h3 id="2/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#2/v)

Put $T=R\circ J_Z:Z\to X^*$. Then $(Tz)(x)=x(z)$ and $\|Tz\|\leq\|z\|$. We must prove $T$ onto.

Take $f\in B_{X^*}$. Part (iv) supplies $\theta\in B_{Z^{**}}$ with $R\theta=f$. [Goldstine's theorem](../functional-analysis.md#goldstine-theorem) gives a net $z_\alpha\in B_Z$ with $J_Zz_\alpha\to\theta$ weak-star. By [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $R$, $Tz_\alpha\to f$ in $\sigma(X^*,X)$. Reflexivity of $X$ makes this the [weak topology](../weak-topology.md) on $X^*$ by part (iii). Thus $B_{X^*}$ is contained in the weak [closure](../topology.md#closure-topology) of $T(B_Z)$.

The reverse containment holds because $T(B_Z)\subseteq B_{X^*}$, and the latter ball is weakly closed: it is the intersection of the closed evaluation constraints $|g(x)|\leq1$ for $x\in B_X$. The set $T(B_Z)$ is convex, so part (ii), now on the [Banach space](../banach-space.md) $X^*$, gives

$$
\boxed{\overline{T(B_Z)}^{\|\cdot\|}=B_{X^*}.}
$$

Density of the ball gives exact surjectivity by a convergent correction argument. For nonzero $f\in X^*$, choose $z_1$ with $\|z_1\|\leq\|f\|$ and $\|f-Tz_1\|\leq\|f\|/2$. Inductively approximate the current residual $r_{n-1}$ by $Tz_n$ with $\|z_n\|\leq\|r_{n-1}\|$ and $\|r_{n-1}-Tz_n\|\leq\|r_{n-1}\|/2$. If a residual is zero, stop.

The residual [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) are bounded by $2^{-n}\|f\|$, while $\sum_n\|z_n\|\leq2\|f\|$. Completeness of $Z$ gives $z=\sum_nz_n\in Z$, and [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $T$ gives $Tz=f$. For $f=0$ use $z=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\forall f\in X^*\ \exists z\in Z:\quad x(z)=f(x)\text{ for every }x\in X.}
$$

This is [evaluation onto the dual of a reflexive dual subspace](../functional-analysis.md#evaluation-onto-the-dual-of-a-reflexive-dual-subspace). The correction argument is needed: a dense image alone would not imply surjectivity.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2011](2011.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

A **[biorthogonal system](../dual-space.md#biorthogonal-system)** is a family $(x_i,f_i)_{i\in I}$ with $x_i\in X$, $f_i\in X^*$, satisfying

$$
\boxed{f_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}\quad(i,j\in I).}
$$

Thus each continuous coordinate functional takes value one on its own [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) and zero on all others. The [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) need not be orthogonal in any inner product, and totality of their [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) is an additional condition.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

Fix $i$ and let $M_i=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_j:j\ne i\}$. The hypothesis gives $M_i\ne X$. Moreover $x_i\notin M_i$, since otherwise the closed span of all the [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) would be contained in $M_i$. Hence $d_i=\operatorname{dist}(x_i,M_i)>0$.

On $M_i+\mathbb Rx_i$, define $\phi_i(m+tx_i)=t$. It is well-defined because $x_i\notin M_i$. For $t\ne0$,

$$
\|m+tx_i\|=|t|\|x_i+m/t\|\geq|t|d_i,
$$

so $|\phi_i(m+tx_i)|\leq\|m+tx_i\|/d_i$; the inequality also holds for $t=0$. The [Hahn-Banach theorem](../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem) extends $\phi_i$ without increasing its [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) to $x_i^*\in X^*$. This gives $x_i^*(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$.

If two continuous functionals have these values, their difference vanishes on the [linear span](../vector-space.md#linear-span) of every $x_j$, which is dense in $X$. [Continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) then makes the difference zero everywhere. Thus each functional is unique, proving the required **unique biorthogonal system**. In fact its [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is $\|x_i^*\|=1/d_i$, by taking [vectors](../vector-space.md#vector) in $M_i$ approaching the distance infimum. This is the coordinate-functional construction for a [minimal total family in a normed space](../normed-vector-space.md#minimal-total-family-in-a-normed-space).

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Define the [finite-rank operator](../compact-operator.md#finite-rank-operator)

$$
\boxed{P_nx=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^*(x)x_i.}
$$

It is linear and bounded, with $\|P_n\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^n\|x_i^*\|\|x_i\|$. Biorthogonality gives exactly the two required values on $x_j$. It also gives

$$
P_n^2x=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n x_j^*(x)x_i^*(x_j)x_i=P_nx.
$$

Thus **$P_n$ is a bounded projection** onto $\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$, with kernel $\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker x_i^*$. Its action on the dense span determines it uniquely among [bounded linear operators](../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-operator). These are the partial-sum projections; no uniform bound in $n$ has yet been assumed.

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

Assume first that $\|P_n\|\leq M$ for all $n$. Given $x\in X$ and $\epsilon>0$, choose a finite linear combination $y$ of the $x_i$ with $\|x-y\|<\epsilon/(M+1)$. For all sufficiently large $n$, $P_ny=y$. Therefore

$$
\|P_nx-x\|\leq\|P_n(x-y)\|+\|y-x\|\leq(M+1)\|x-y\|<\epsilon.
$$

Thus $P_nx\to x$, and the formula in part (iii) yields the norm-convergent expansion

$$
\boxed{x=\sum_{i=1}^\infty x_i^*(x)x_i.}
$$

This proves the stated existence of coefficients.

Conversely, suppose every $x$ has some norm-convergent expansion $x=\sum_i\alpha_ix_i$. Applying the continuous functional $x_j^*$ to its partial sums and passing to the limit gives $\alpha_j=x_j^*(x)$. Hence those partial sums are precisely $P_nx$, so $P_nx\to x$ for every $x$. In particular $\sup_n\|P_nx\|<\infty$ for each fixed $x$. The [Uniform boundedness principle](../banach-space.md#uniform-boundedness-principle), using completeness of $X$, now gives

$$
\boxed{\sup_n\|P_n\|<\infty.}
$$

This proves the reverse implication. The coefficients are automatically unique, so the equivalence is the [uniform partial-sum criterion for a Schauder basis](../functional-analysis.md#uniform-partial-sum-criterion-for-a-schauder-basis) for the given minimal total [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence). Its projection bound is the [basis constant](../functional-analysis.md#basis-constant).
