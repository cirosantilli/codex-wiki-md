# Lattice energy

↑ **Parent:** [Ionic bonding](ionic-bonding.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_energy)

A [lattice energy](lattice-energy.md) needs a reaction, a sign convention, and a stated amount. Using the lattice-dissociation [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) convention for [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md),

$$
\mathrm{NaCl}(s)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Na}^+(g)+\mathrm{Cl}^-(g),\qquad
\Delta H_{\rm latt,diss}>0.
$$

The reverse lattice-formation [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) is negative with the same magnitude when initial and final states match. [Chemistry 2e](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md) gives $769\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ for this separation, per [mole](mole-unit.md) of [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md) [formula units](formula-unit.md). This is about $7.97$ [electronvolts](electronvolt.md) per [formula unit](formula-unit.md), not per individual [ion](ion.md). It concerns separated gaseous [ions](ion.md), rather than neutral [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md), molten [salt](salt-chemistry.md), or hydrated [ions](ion.md) in [water](water.md).[§7.5, “Ionic Bond Strength and Lattice Energy”, positive convention, reverse convention and NaCl value](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)

Also distinguish an [internal energy](internal-energy.md) change from an [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) change: $H=U+pV$. A static point-charge calculation omits motion and thermal contributions. Reversing a reaction reverses its [energy](energy.md) change; replacing [internal energy](internal-energy.md) with [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) additionally changes the defined quantity. The [Born-Haber cycle](born-haber-cycle.md) infers a lattice-formation [enthalpy](enthalpy.md) by combining other [enthalpies](enthalpy.md), [ionization energies](ionization-energy.md), and [electron affinities](electron-affinity.md). These imagined stages are a thermodynamic accounting path, not a claim about the actual sequence of a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md).[§7.5, “The Born-Haber Cycle”, Figure 7.13 and Table 7.4](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others.md)
