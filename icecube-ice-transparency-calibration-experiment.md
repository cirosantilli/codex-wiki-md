# IceCube ice-transparency calibration experiment

↑ **Parent:** [IceCube Neutrino Observatory](icecube-neutrino-observatory.md)

In the 2013 calibration study, six horizontal [light-emitting diodes](light-emitting-diode.md) per module on string 63 flashed sequentially; other strings recorded charges and [photon](photon.md) arrival times. A global fit inferred depth-dependent [scattering of light](scattering-of-light.md) and [absorption coefficients](absorption-coefficient.md). Dust-logger measurements checked layer structure; independent [muon](muon.md) events tested simulated time patterns. [Photomultiplier](photomultiplier.md) response and refrozen ice near modules remained limitations. Thus a late hit need not imply a late interaction: [scattering of light](scattering-of-light.md) changes the path.[Sections 3, 8–11; Figs. 14, 20–23: flashing, cross-checks and limitations](measurement-of-south-pole-ice-transparency-with-the-icecube-led-calibration-system-by-icecube-collaboration.md)

An original limiting calculation isolates this issue. Suppose a compact source emits at time $t_0$ into homogeneous, nondispersive [ice](ice.md) with stipulated [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $n=1.32$. At distance $r$, the unscattered light arrives at

$$
t_{\mathrm{direct}}=t_0+\frac{nr}{c},
$$

where $c$ is the vacuum [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). For chosen $r=100\,\mathrm m$, the delay is $440.3\,\mathrm{ns}$. A scattered path $30\,\mathrm m$ longer adds $132.1\,\mathrm{ns}$ even with perfect clocks. These chosen values are not calibration measurements. Real [ice](ice.md) requires wavelength-dependent propagation, module response and a spatially varying light-transport model; replacing that response by one arrival time discards information.
