# Electron shelving

↑ **Parent:** [Trapped-ion quantum control](trapped-ion-quantum-control.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_shelving)

Electron shelving distinguishes an internal [quantum state](quantum-state.md) outside a cycling optical transition from one that scatters many [photons](photon.md). In the mercury-ion example in [David J. Wineland](david-j-wineland.md)'s lecture, $S\leftrightarrow P$ excitation produces $194\ \mathrm{nm}$ [fluorescence](fluorescence-split.md); transfer to a [metastable atomic level](metastable-atomic-level.md) $D$ suppresses it. A [photomultiplier](photomultiplier.md) records bright/dark intervals. Coherent $282\ \mathrm{nm}$ interrogation precedes detection to avoid perturbation by the detection beam.[§III, pp. 1105–1106, Figs. 1–2: shelving, photomultiplier counts and alternating interrogation/detection](nobel-lecture-superposition-entanglement-and-raising-schrodinger%E2%80%99s-cat-by-david-j-wineland.md)

“Dark” means weak scattering under a particular detection setting. The motional [ground state](ground-state.md) is dark to an ideal red [motional sideband](motional-sideband.md) for a different reason: there is no lower [number state](number-state.md). It can still fluoresce strongly on a cycling detection transition. Shelving's population readout also need not preserve a [quantum superposition](quantum-superposition.md)'s [quantum coherence](quantum-coherence-in-a-specified-basis.md); scattered [photons](photon.md) carry information about the internal state, and their recoil can disturb motion.
