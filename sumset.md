# Sumset

↑ **Parent:** [Additive combinatorics](additive-combinatorics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset)

For [finite sets](finite-set.md) $A,B\subseteq G$ in an [abelian group](abelian-group.md) $G$, their [sumset](sumset.md) is

$$
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}\subseteq G.
$$

Each distinct value occurs only once in this [set](set-split.md), even if many [ordered pairs](ordered-pair.md) produce it. When $B=A$, repetitions are allowed: $a+a$ belongs to $A+A$. For the two [integer](integer.md) [sets](set-split.md) introduced above,

$$
\begin{aligned}
A+A&=\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\},& |A+A|&=7,\\
B+B&=\{0,1,2,4,5,8,10,11,14,20\},& |B+B|&=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $A$, the value $3$ comes from $(0,3),(1,2),(2,1),(3,0)$. For $B$, two [ordered pairs](ordered-pair.md) give the same sum only when one reverses the other, apart from a pair being identical to itself. Thus its four pairs $(b,b)$ and six pairs with distinct members, counted without regard to order, produce ten distinct sums.

Every nonempty [finite set](finite-set.md) of [integers](integer.md) $S\subseteq\mathbb Z$ satisfies $|S+S|\geq2|S|-1$. To see this, list its members as $s_1<\cdots<s_n$. The $2n-1$ distinct sums

$$
s_1+s_1<s_1+s_2<\cdots<s_1+s_n<s_2+s_n<\cdots<s_n+s_n
$$

all belong to $S+S$. A nonconstant [arithmetic progression](arithmetic-progression.md) attains this bound. The ordered list is essential: in the [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, the [subgroup](subgroup.md) $H=\{0,2,4\}$ instead satisfies $H+H=H$, of [cardinality](cardinality.md) three. Addition wraps around, for example $4+4=2$ in this [group](group-split.md).
