# Compact self-similar disk with square-root density viscosity

↑ **Parent:** [Viscous evolution of an accretion disk](viscous-evolution-of-an-accretion-disk.md)

For $\nu=\nu_0(\Sigma/\Sigma_0)^{1/2}(R/R_0)^{9/4}$, the variables $x=\sqrt{R/R_0}$, $S=(\Sigma/\Sigma_0)x^3$ and $\tau=3\nu_0t/(4R_0^2)$ reduce the source-free [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation.md) to $S_\tau=(xS^{3/2})_{xx}$. The displayed [self-similar solution](similarity-solution.md) has an outer edge $R_e=36R_0\tau$ and a surface-density cusp proportional to $R^{-3/2}$ at the origin. The positive-part cutoff is essential: beyond the edge, $S^{3/2}$ is the cube of the absolute value, so continuing the squared polynomial without truncation does not solve the same equation.

The disk mass is $8\pi\Sigma_0R_0^2\tau^{-1/2}$ and its [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) is $12\pi\Sigma_0R_0^2\sqrt{GMR_0}$, independent of time. It therefore describes accretion with outward spreading and conserved disk angular momentum, under the formal zero-torque condition at the origin. The inward mass flux changes sign at $R=9R_0\tau/4$, so the outer portion moves outward while the inner portion accretes.
