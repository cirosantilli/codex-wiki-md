# Isotope

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical element](chemical-element.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope)

[Isotopes](isotope.md) of a [chemical element](chemical-element.md) have the same [atomic number](atomic-number.md) $Z$, hence the same number of [protons](proton.md), but different [neutron numbers](neutron-number.md) $N$ and therefore different [mass numbers](mass-number.md) $A=Z+N$. For example, every [neon](neon.md) [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) contains ten [protons](proton.md): [neon-20](neon-20.md), [neon-21](neon-21.md), and [neon-22](neon-22.md) contain ten, eleven, and twelve [neutrons](neutron-split.md), respectively. They occupy one [chemical element](chemical-element.md) box in the [periodic table](periodic-table.md), rather than three boxes.

<a id="image-neon-isotopes-the-same-ten-protons-with-different-neutron-numbers"></a>
![](isotope-nucleus-neon-neutron-counts.png)

**[Figure 14](#image-neon-isotopes-the-same-ten-protons-with-different-neutron-numbers). Neon isotopes: the same ten protons with different neutron numbers**. Every dot counts one [nucleon](nucleon.md). The arrays inventory [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md); they do not model the positions or sizes of [protons](proton.md) and [neutrons](neutron-split.md). The [mass numbers](mass-number.md) 20, 21, and 22 are exact counts.

Three labels answer different questions. The [chemical element](chemical-element.md) fixes $Z$; the [isotope](isotope.md) specifies which [neutron number](neutron-number.md) accompanies that $Z$; the [ion](ion.md) label specifies the net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). Neutral ${}^{22}_{10}\mathrm{Ne}$ and the [ion](ion.md) ${}^{22}_{10}\mathrm{Ne}^{+}$ both contain ten [protons](proton.md) and twelve [neutrons](neutron-split.md); they contain ten and nine [electrons](electron.md), respectively. Ordinary [ionization](ionization.md) changes the [electron](electron.md) count, leaving the [isotope](isotope.md) unchanged. Changing the [neutron number](neutron-number.md) can change [nuclear binding energy](nuclear-binding-energy.md) and the possibility of [radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md). [Isotopic abundances](isotopic-abundance.md) can help distinguish material sources, while [radioactive decay](radioactive-decay.md) can additionally supply chronological information.

A [mass spectrum](mass-spectrum.md) makes the distinction experimentally visible: a single [chemical element](chemical-element.md) can give separate [ion](ion.md) peaks at different [mass-to-charge ratios](mass-to-charge-ratio.md). Its bulk [atomic weight](relative-atomic-mass.md) is a [weighted mean](weighted-arithmetic-mean.md) governed by [isotopic abundances](isotopic-abundance.md), rather than the location of one compulsory peak. The historical [mass spectrograph](mass-spectrograph.md) experiment and the synthetic [neon](neon.md) [mass spectrum](mass-spectrum.md) below illustrate these complementary observations.
