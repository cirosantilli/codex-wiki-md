# Projected pulsar semimajor axis

↑ **Parent:** [Binary pulsar](binary-pulsar.md)

The [semimajor axis](semi-major-axis.md) $a_1$ of the [pulsar](pulsar.md)'s orbit about the binary [barycenter](barycenter.md), projected along the line of sight and divided by the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). The [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md) $i$ is zero for a face-on [orbit](orbit-dynamical-system.md). Thus $x$ is a time, often quoted as the numerical number of [light-seconds](light-second.md) in $a_1\sin i$, not the true binary separation. The relative-orbit [semimajor axis](semi-major-axis.md) is instead $a=a_1+a_2$; [conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md) gives $a_1=a m_2/(m_1+m_2)$.

Combining [Kepler's third law](kepler-s-third-law.md) with that [barycenter](barycenter.md) relation gives the timing form of the [binary mass function](binary-mass-function.md), also valid for eccentric [Kepler orbits](kepler-orbit.md):

$$
f=\frac{4\pi^2(cx)^3}{GP_b^2}=\frac{(m_2\sin i)^3}{(m_1+m_2)^2}.
$$

Choose $x=2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $P_b=8\,\mathrm{h}$ for an original example: $a_1\sin i=599{,}585\,\mathrm{km}$ and $f\simeq0.0773M_\odot$, where $M_\odot$ is a [solar mass](solar-mass.md). For a specified pulsar [mass](mass.md) $m_1=1.4M_\odot$, the smallest allowed companion [mass](mass.md) occurs at $\sin i=1$ and is approximately $0.698M_\odot$. A different assumed $m_1$ changes this bound. One measured $f$ does not determine both [masses](mass.md) and [orbital inclination](orbital-inclination.md).
