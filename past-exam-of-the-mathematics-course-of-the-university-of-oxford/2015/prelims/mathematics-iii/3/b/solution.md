<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At a regular point of the [optimization constraint](../../../../../../optimization-constraint.md), meaning $\nabla g\ne0$, the necessary [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) condition is

$$
\boxed{\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\quad\text{for some }\lambda\in\mathbb R.}
$$

Indeed, every allowed [tangent vector](../../../../../../tangent-vector-split.md) $t$ to the [level set](../../../../../../level-set.md) $g=0$ satisfies $\nabla g\cdot t=0$. At a constrained [extremum](../../../../../../maximum-and-minimum.md), the [directional derivative](../../../../../../directional-derivative.md) $\nabla f\cdot t$ must vanish too. Thus both [gradients](../../../../../../gradient.md) are normal to the same [tangent line](../../../../../../tangent-line.md) and are parallel. This includes $\nabla f=0$. At a singular point with $\nabla g=0$, the displayed condition need not be necessary; those points require a separate examination.
