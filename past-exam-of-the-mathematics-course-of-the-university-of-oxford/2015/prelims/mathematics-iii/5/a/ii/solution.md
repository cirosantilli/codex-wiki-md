<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Under the extra hypothesis that $G(s)$ exists for every real $s$, its [power series](../../../../../../../power-series.md) has infinite [radius of convergence](../../../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md). [Termwise differentiation of a power series](../../../../../../../termwise-differentiation-of-a-power-series.md) is therefore valid to every finite order. For $k\geq1$,

$$
G^{(k)}(s)=\sum_{j=k}^\infty j(j-1)\cdots(j-k+1)p(j)s^{j-k}.
$$

At $s=1$ this is the [expected value](../../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) of the [falling factorial](../../../../../../../falling-factorial.md) of $X$:

$$
\boxed{G^{(k)}(1)=\mathbb E[(X)_k]=\mathbb E[X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)].}
$$

In particular $\boxed{G'(1)=\mathbb E[X]}$. The terms with $j<k$ vanish because their [falling factorials](../../../../../../../falling-factorial.md) contain a zero factor. These [factorial moments](../../../../../../../factorial-moment.md) are finite under the stated hypothesis.
