<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an [integrable](../../../../../../../integrability.md) [random variable](../../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) $X$, the [law of total expectation](../../../../../../../law-of-total-expectation.md) for the [partition of a sample space](../../../../../../../partition-of-a-sample-space.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[X]=\sum_{i:\,\mathbb P(B_i)>0}\mathbb E[X\mid B_i]\,\mathbb P(B_i).}
$$

Parts of zero [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md) contribute zero and need no definition of $\mathbb E[X\mid B_i]$. To see the identity, write $X=\sum_i X\mathbf1_{B_i}$ and use [linearity of expectation](../../../../../../../linearity-of-expectation.md); integrability justifies the countable sum since $\sum_i\mathbb E[|X|\mathbf1_{B_i}]=\mathbb E[|X|]<\infty$.
