The [central limit theorem](../../../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) states that if $W_1,W_2,\ldots$ are [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with finite [mean](../../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) $\mu$ and finite positive [variance](../../../../../../../variance-split.md) $\sigma^2$, then

$$
\boxed{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nW_i-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).}
$$

Here the arrow means [convergence in distribution](../../../../../../../convergence-in-distribution.md): for every real $z$, the [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md) that the expression on the left is at most $z$ tends to $\Phi(z)$, the [standard normal distribution function](../../../../../../../standard-normal-distribution-function.md). If $\sigma^2=0$, the variables equal $\mu$ [almost surely](../../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) and their [sample mean](../../../../../../../sample-mean.md) is exactly $\mu$.
