<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Writing the unknown [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) as $(x,y,z)^T$, the first two [linear equations](../../../../../../linear-equation.md) give $x=z-1$ and $y=z-\beta$. Substitution into the third gives

$$
-(z-1)-(z-\beta)+\alpha z=1,\qquad(\alpha-2)z=-\beta.
$$

Thus, when $\alpha\ne2$, put $t=-\beta/(\alpha-2)$. There is exactly one solution:

$$
\boxed{(x,y,z)=(t-1,t-\beta,t),\qquad t=-\frac{\beta}{\alpha-2}.}
$$

When $\alpha=2$, the remaining equation is $0=-\beta$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\alpha=2,\ \beta\ne0&:\quad\varnothing,\\
\alpha=2,\ \beta=0&:\quad\{(-1,0,0)+t(1,1,1):t\in\mathbb R\}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

The second case is an [affine line in a vector space](../../../../../../affine-line-in-a-vector-space.md), so it contains infinitely many solutions. These cases exhaust all [real numbers](../../../../../../real-number.md) $\alpha,\beta$.
