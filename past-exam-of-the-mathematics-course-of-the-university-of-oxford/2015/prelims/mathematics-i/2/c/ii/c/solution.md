↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $\alpha\ne2$, both reduced forms are $I_3$, so $A''=(A')^T$. At $\alpha=2$, the [reduced column echelon form](../../../../../../../../reduced-column-echelon-form.md) is the [transpose](../../../../../../../../transpose.md) of the [reduced row echelon form](../../../../../../../../reduced-row-echelon-form.md) of $A^T$, giving

$$
A''=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\-1&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
(A')^T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\-1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For example, $C_2\mapsto-C_2$ followed by $C_3\mapsto C_3+C_1-C_2$, using the updated second column, produces the displayed $A''$ directly. Its third column is zero. The two [matrices](../../../../../../../../matrix.md) differ, so **(C) is true exactly when $\alpha\ne2$**. Taking a [transpose](../../../../../../../../transpose.md) exchanges the kind of reduction, but also replaces the original $A$ by $A^T$; it does not generally identify the two reduced forms of $A$ itself.
