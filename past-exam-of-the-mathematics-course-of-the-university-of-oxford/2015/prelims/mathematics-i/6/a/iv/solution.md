<h1 id="6/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Choose distinct $a,b\ne e$, possible because $|G|>2$. By [iii](../iii.md), $a$ and $b$ commute, and $a^2=b^2=e$. The four elements

$$
H=\{e,a,b,ab\}
$$

are distinct: $ab=e$ would give $a=b$, while $ab=a$ or $ab=b$ would make $b=e$ or $a=e$. Commutativity and $a^2=b^2=e$ show that products stay in $H$, and every element is its own inverse. More explicitly,

$$
\boxed{C_2\times C_2\longrightarrow H,\qquad(i,j)\longmapsto a^ib^j\quad(i,j\in\{0,1\})}
$$

is a [group isomorphism](../../../../../../../group-isomorphism.md), with addition of exponents modulo two. Hence $G$ contains a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) isomorphic to the [Klein four-group](../../../../../../../klein-four-group.md).

The converse is false. The [direct product of groups](../../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) $C_2\times C_2\times C_3$ contains $C_2\times C_2\times\{e\}$, but it has elements of [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) three. Thus the existence of the specified [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) does not force every nonidentity element to have [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) two.
