<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Label the vertices of a [regular tetrahedron](../../../../../../regular-tetrahedron.md) $1,2,3,4$, and include all its [Euclidean isometries](../../../../../../euclidean-isometry.md), both [rotations](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) and [reflections](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md). The following list describes every [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) at most six, allowing arbitrary relabelling of the vertices. The numbers count actual [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md), rather than just their [group isomorphism](../../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) types.

- **One trivial [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md)** contains only the [identity element](../../../../../../identity-element.md).
- **Six reflection [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $C_2$** are generated by a [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), for example $(12)$. Its mirror plane contains vertices $3,4$ and the midpoint of the opposite edge $12$.
- **Three half-turn [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $C_2$** are generated by a product of two disjoint [transpositions](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), for example $(12)(34)$. The [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) axis joins the midpoints of the opposite edges $12$ and $34$.
- **Four [cyclic subgroups](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $C_3$** consist of the three [rotations](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) about an axis joining a vertex to the centre of its opposite face. For example, $\langle(123)\rangle$ fixes vertex $4$.
- **Three [cyclic subgroups](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $C_4$** are generated by an [improper orthogonal transformation](../../../../../../improper-orthogonal-transformation.md): a quarter-turn about an axis through opposite edge midpoints, composed with a [reflection](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in the perpendicular plane through the centre. For $\langle(1234)\rangle$, the axis joins the midpoints of edges $13$ and $24$. Its square is the half-turn $(13)(24)$.
- **One rotational [Klein four-group](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md)** consists of the [identity element](../../../../../../identity-element.md) and the three half-turns about opposite-edge midpoint axes:$$
  \{e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}.
  $$
- **Three further [Klein four-groups](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md)** arise from a pair of opposite edges. For edges $12,34$, the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) is$$
  \{e,(12),(34),(12)(34)\}.
  $$

  It contains two [reflections](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in perpendicular planes and their product, the half-turn about the line where those planes meet. It preserves each of the two opposite edges as a [set](../../../../../../set-split.md).
- **Four [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $S_3$** are the [stabilizer subgroups](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of individual vertices. Each contains three [rotations](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) about the vertex-to-opposite-face axis and three [reflections](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in planes containing that axis. For the fixed vertex $4$, this is every [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md) of $1,2,3$.

Here is why this is exhaustive. Part [b](../b.md) leaves only the [trivial group](../../../../../../trivial-group.md), $C_2,C_3,C_4,C_2\times C_2,S_3$. The [disjoint cycle decomposition](../../../../../../disjoint-cycle-decomposition.md) gives exactly the two listed kinds of [involutions](../../../../../../involution.md), the three-cycles, and the four-cycles, accounting for all the [cyclic subgroups](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md). In a [Klein four-group](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md), the nonidentity elements commute. If it contains $(12)$, any other nonidentity element must be a commuting [involution](../../../../../../involution.md), hence $(34)$ or $(12)(34)$; either choice yields the displayed opposite-edge [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). If it has no [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), its three nonidentity elements must be all three double [transpositions](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), yielding the rotational [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md).

Finally, an $S_3$ [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) has a unique [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) of [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) three. Its two three-cycles fix the same unique vertex. Every element of the $S_3$ [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) preserves this vertex, since [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) preserves that unique [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md). Thus it is the full [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of that vertex. The counts in the list are consequently exhaustive, giving

$$
\boxed{1+6+3+4+3+1+3+4=25\text{ subgroups of order at most }6.}
$$

The two kinds of $C_2$ and the two kinds of [Klein four-groups](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md) have different geometric behavior despite having the same abstract [group isomorphism](../../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) type within each pair. This is the distinction recorded by the [small subgroups of tetrahedral symmetry](../../../../../../small-subgroups-of-tetrahedral-symmetry.md).
