↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g$ is the smallest positive integer $d$ with $g^d=e$. Such an integer exists in a [finite group](../../../../../../../finite-group.md): two powers in $e,g,g^2,\ldots$ must agree, and cancellation gives a positive power equal to $e$.

By minimality, $e,g,\ldots,g^{d-1}$ are distinct. Every integer power of $g$ is one of them, by division of its exponent by $d$. They form the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md)

$$
\langle g\rangle=\{e,g,\ldots,g^{d-1}\},\qquad|\langle g\rangle|=d.
$$

Applying [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) to this [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) gives

$$
\boxed{o(g)=|\langle g\rangle|\mid|G|.}
$$
