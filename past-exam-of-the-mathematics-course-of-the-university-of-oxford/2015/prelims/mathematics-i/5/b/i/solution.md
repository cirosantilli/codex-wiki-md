<h1 id="5/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $a\in A$, the element $a=ae$ belongs to both $A$ and $aA$. The assumed equal-or-disjoint property therefore gives $aA=A$.

If $a,b\in A$, then $ab\in aA=A$, so $A$ is closed under multiplication. Also $e\in A=aA$, so some $c\in A$ satisfies $ac=e$. Therefore $c=a^{-1}\in A$. Along with the given [identity element](../../../../../../../identity-element.md), closure and existence of [inverse elements](../../../../../../../inverse-element.md) establish that **$A$ is a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of $G$**. This is the [subgroup test](../../../../../../../subgroup-test.md). The proof even works without assuming that $G$ is finite.
