<h1 id="7/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A left [group action](../../../../../../../group-action-split.md) of $G$ on a [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) $S$ is a [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) $G\times S\to S$, written $(g,s)\mapsto g\cdot s$, satisfying

$$
\boxed{e\cdot s=s,\qquad(gh)\cdot s=g\cdot(h\cdot s)}
$$

for all $g,h\in G$, $s\in S$. The [orbit of a group action](../../../../../../../orbit-group-theory.md) through $s$ and the [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of $s$ are, respectively,

$$
\boxed{G\cdot s=\{g\cdot s:g\in G\},\qquad G_s=\{g\in G:g\cdot s=s\}.}
$$

The [identity element](../../../../../../../identity-element.md) belongs to $G_s$. If $g,h\in G_s$, then $(gh)\cdot s=g\cdot(h\cdot s)=g\cdot s=s$, so $gh\in G_s$. If $g\in G_s$, applying $g^{-1}$ to $g\cdot s=s$ gives $s=g^{-1}\cdot s$, so $g^{-1}\in G_s$. These facts prove that the [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of $G$.
