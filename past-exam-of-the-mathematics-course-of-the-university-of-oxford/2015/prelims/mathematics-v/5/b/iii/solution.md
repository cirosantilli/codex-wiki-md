<h1 id="5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

When $\alpha>n$, set $\mu=\sqrt{\alpha^2-n^2}>0$. The reduced [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) is $F''-\mu^2F=0$. Enforcing the same [initial conditions](../../../../../../../initial-condition.md) gives

$$
\boxed{F(t)=\frac{\sinh(\mu t)}\mu,\qquad\mu=\sqrt{\alpha^2-n^2}.}
$$

The corresponding [overdamped](../../../../../../../overdamping.md) [normal mode](../../../../../../../normal-mode.md) can also be written

$$
y(x,t)=\frac{e^{(-\alpha+\mu)t}-e^{(-\alpha-\mu)t}}{2\mu}\sin(nx).
$$

Both rates in the [exponential functions](../../../../../../../exponential-function.md) are negative because $0<\mu<\alpha$, so the [hyperbolic sine](../../../../../../../hyperbolic-sine.md) factor does not prevent decay.
