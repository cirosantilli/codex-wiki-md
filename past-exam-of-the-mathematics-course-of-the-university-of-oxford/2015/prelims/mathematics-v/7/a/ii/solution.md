↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The space $\mathbb R^3$ is [simply connected](../../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md), and the [electric field](../../../../../../../electric-field-split.md) has zero [curl](../../../../../../../curl.md). Hence it is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md). More explicitly, [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) gives zero [circulation](../../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) around any closed loop, since such a loop bounds a spanning [surface](../../../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) in $\mathbb R^3$. Therefore the [line integral](../../../../../../../line-integral.md) between two fixed endpoints is independent of the chosen path.

Fix a reference point $\mathbf r_0$ and define the [electric potential](../../../../../../../electric-potential.md)

$$
\varphi(\mathbf r)=-\int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r}\mathbf E\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell+C.
$$

Taking a short straight terminal segment in each coordinate direction shows $\partial_j\varphi=-E_j$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=-\nabla\varphi.}
$$

The [electric potential](../../../../../../../electric-potential.md) is determined up to an additive constant; the later condition at infinity fixes that constant for the finite [charge distribution](../../../../../../../charge-distribution.md) in part (b).
