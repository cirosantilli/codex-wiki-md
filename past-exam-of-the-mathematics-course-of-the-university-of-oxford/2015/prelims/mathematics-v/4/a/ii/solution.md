<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Under the stated piecewise smooth hypotheses, the [Dirichlet convergence theorem for Fourier series](../../../../../../../dirichlet-s-theorem-on-fourier-series.md) gives the average of the two one-sided limits of the corresponding [periodic extension](../../../../../../../periodic-extension.md). The [even extension](../../../../../../../even-extension.md) is continuous at both endpoints, so the [Fourier cosine series](../../../../../../../fourier-cosine-series.md) converges to

$$
\boxed{f(x)\quad\text{for every }x\in[0,L].}
$$

The [odd extension](../../../../../../../odd-extension.md) agrees with $f$ on $(0,L)$, but its left and right limits are $-f(0),f(0)$ at zero and $f(L),-f(L)$ at the join $L$. Therefore the [Fourier sine series](../../../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md) converges to

$$
\boxed{\begin{cases}f(x),&0<x<L,\\0,&x=0\text{ or }x=L.\end{cases}}
$$

Indeed every [partial sum](../../../../../../../partial-sum.md) of the [Fourier sine series](../../../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md) is already zero at both endpoints. The endpoint values agree with $f$ only when the corresponding [boundary values](../../../../../../../boundary-value.md) vanish.
