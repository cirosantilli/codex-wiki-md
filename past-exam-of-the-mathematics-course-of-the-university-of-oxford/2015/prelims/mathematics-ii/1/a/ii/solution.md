<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem](../../../../../../../bolzano-weierstrass-theorem.md) states: **every [bounded sequence](../../../../../../../bounded-sequence.md) of [real numbers](../../../../../../../real-number.md) has a [convergent subsequence](../../../../../../../convergent-subsequence.md).**

By the [monotone subsequence theorem](../../../../../../../monotone-subsequence-theorem.md) proved in the preceding part, a [bounded sequence](../../../../../../../bounded-sequence.md) $(a_n)$ has a [monotone subsequence](../../../../../../../monotone-subsequence.md) $(b_r)$. It inherits the bounds of $(a_n)$. For completeness, the [bounded monotone sequence theorem](../../../../../../../bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md) follows directly from [completeness of the real numbers](../../../../../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md). If $(b_r)$ is increasing, let $L=\sup\{b_r:r\geq1\}$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, the definition of [supremum](../../../../../../../supremum.md) supplies $r_0$ with $L-\varepsilon<b_{r_0}\leq L$. Then

$$
r\geq r_0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L-\varepsilon<b_{r_0}\leq b_r\leq L,
$$

so $b_r\to L$. If $(b_r)$ is decreasing, apply the increasing case to $(-b_r)$, or use its [infimum](../../../../../../../infimum.md). Thus in either case

$$
\boxed{\text{some }n_1<n_2<\cdots\text{ and }L\in\mathbb R\text{ satisfy }a_{n_r}\longrightarrow L.}
$$
