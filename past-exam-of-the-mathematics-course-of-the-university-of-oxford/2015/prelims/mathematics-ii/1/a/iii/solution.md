↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

It is useful to prove the [contrapositive](../../../../../../../contrapositive.md). Suppose $k$ is a [peak of a sequence](../../../../../../../peak-of-a-sequence.md) $(a_n)$ and $m$ is a [peak of a sequence](../../../../../../../peak-of-a-sequence.md) $(-a_n)$. The second condition says $a_j\geq a_m$ for every $j>m$. Hence

$$
j>\max\{k,m\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_m\leq a_j\leq a_k.
$$

Only finitely many earlier terms remain, and their [absolute values](../../../../../../../absolute-value.md) have a finite [maximum](../../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md). Together these give a bound for every term, so $(a_n)$ is a [bounded sequence](../../../../../../../bounded-sequence.md). Consequently,

$$
\boxed{(a_n)\text{ unbounded}\ \Longrightarrow\ (a_n)\text{ or }(-a_n)\text{ has no peaks}.}
$$
