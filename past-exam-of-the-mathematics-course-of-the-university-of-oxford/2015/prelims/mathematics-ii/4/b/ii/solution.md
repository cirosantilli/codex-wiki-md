<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take

$$
\boxed{g(x)=1+|x|,\qquad f(x)=(1+|x|)^2.}
$$

Both [functions](../../../../../../../function-split.md) have values in $[1,\infty)$. The [reverse triangle inequality](../../../../../../../reverse-triangle-inequality.md) gives $|g(x)-g(y)|\leq|x-y|$, so $g$ is [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) and hence has [uniform continuity](../../../../../../../uniform-continuity.md).

For $x_n=n$ and $y_n=n+1/n$, the distances satisfy $|x_n-y_n|=1/n\to0$, whereas

$$
|f(y_n)-f(x_n)|=2\frac{n+1}{n}+\frac1{n^2}>2.
$$

In particular tolerance $\varepsilon=1$ defeats every proposed $\delta$: choose $n$ with $1/n<\delta$. Thus **$f$ does not have [uniform continuity](../../../../../../../uniform-continuity.md) on $\mathbb R$.**
