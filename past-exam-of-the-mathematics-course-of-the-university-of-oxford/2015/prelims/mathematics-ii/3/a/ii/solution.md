<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $T_N=\sum_{n=1}^N|a_n|$ and $S_N=\sum_{n=1}^Na_n$. If $(T_N)$ converges, it is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md). Thus, for every $\varepsilon>0$, some $N$ makes

$$
\left|S_q-S_p\right|=\left|\sum_{n=p+1}^qa_n\right|\leq\sum_{n=p+1}^q|a_n|=T_q-T_p<\varepsilon
\qquad(q>p\geq N),
$$

where the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives the bound. Therefore $(S_N)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the [complex numbers](../../../../../../../complex-number.md). These form a [complete metric space](../../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md), so $(S_N)$ has a finite [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md). Equivalently, apply the [Cauchy criterion for series](../../../../../../../cauchy-criterion-for-series.md). Hence **[absolute convergence](../../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) implies convergence.**
