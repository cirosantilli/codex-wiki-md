<h1 id="question-5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $y(x,t)=Y(\xi,\eta)$ with $\xi=x-ct$, $\eta=x+ct$. The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives the [differential operators](../../../../../../differential-operator.md)

$$
\partial_x=\partial_\xi+\partial_\eta,
\qquad
\partial_t=-c\partial_\xi+c\partial_\eta.
$$

Their squared difference is

$$
y_{tt}-c^2y_{xx}=-4c^2Y_{\xi\eta}.
$$

Consequently the [wave equation](../../../../../../wave-equation-split.md) becomes $Y_{\xi\eta}=0$. Integrating first in $\eta$ gives $Y_\xi=F'(\xi)$; integrating in $\xi$ gives the general solution

$$
\boxed{y(x,t)=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct).}
$$

The arbitrary twice differentiable [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) describe right-moving and left-moving [waves](../../../../../../wave-split.md), respectively. Conversely the [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) shows that each such sum satisfies the [wave equation](../../../../../../wave-equation-split.md).
