<h1 id="question-3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [annulus](../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) is $1\leq r\leq2$ in [polar coordinates](../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md). Their [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) supplies $dx\,dy=r\,dr\,d\theta$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\iint_D e^{-x^2-y^2}\,dx\,dy
&=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^2 e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta\\
&=\pi\left[-e^{-r^2}\right]_1^2
=\boxed{\pi(e^{-1}-e^{-4})}.
\end{aligned}
$$
