<h1 id="question-7/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the convention that the [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) tends to zero at infinity and the [gravitational field](../../../../../../../gravitational-field.md) is $-\nabla\phi$. A source element of [mass](../../../../../../../mass.md) $dm$ at [position vector](../../../../../../../position-vector.md) $\mathbf r'$ contributes $-G\,dm/|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|$, where $G$ is the [gravitational constant](../../../../../../../gravitational-constant.md). For the curve, $dm=\gamma\,ds'$, so

$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbf r)=-G\gamma\int_C\frac{ds'}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}.}
$$

Here $ds'$ is [arc length](../../../../../../../arc-length.md) along the source [curve](../../../../../../../curve.md); the expression is finite away from it. This normalization fixes the otherwise arbitrary additive constant.
