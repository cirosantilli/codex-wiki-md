<h1 id="question-7/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use source [polar coordinates](../../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) $x'=s\cos\theta$, $y'=s\sin\theta$ on the [semicircular disk](../../../../../../../semicircular-disk.md), with $0\leq s\leq a$ and $0\leq\theta\leq\pi$. Its [surface area element](../../../../../../../surface-area-element.md) is $s\,ds\,d\theta$, and the separation from $(0,0,h)$ is $\sqrt{s^2+h^2}$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi(h)
&=-G\sigma\int_0^\pi\int_0^a\frac{s\,ds\,d\theta}{\sqrt{s^2+h^2}}\\
&=-\pi G\sigma\left[\sqrt{s^2+h^2}\right]_0^a
=\boxed{-\pi G\sigma\bigl(\sqrt{a^2+h^2}-h\bigr)},\qquad h>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

This [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) is half that of the full [disk](../../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) because the separation is independent of the polar angle. At large $h$ it approaches $-G(\pi a^2\sigma/2)/h$, with the correct total [mass](../../../../../../../mass.md).
