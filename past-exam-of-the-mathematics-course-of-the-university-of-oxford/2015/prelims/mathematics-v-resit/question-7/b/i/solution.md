<h1 id="question-7/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write the observation point as $(0,0,h)$ with $h>0$, reserving source coordinates for the [integral](../../../../../../../integral-split.md). For the straight segment, $ds'=dx'$ and the separation is $\sqrt{x'^2+h^2}$. Therefore its [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi(h)
&=-G\gamma\int_{-a}^a\frac{dx'}{\sqrt{x'^2+h^2}}\\
&=-2G\gamma\left[\log\bigl(x'+\sqrt{x'^2+h^2}\bigr)\right]_0^a\\
&=\boxed{-2G\gamma\log\left(\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2+h^2}}{h}\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Equivalently $\phi(h)=-2G\gamma\operatorname{arsinh}(a/h)$. At large $h$ it approaches $-G(2a\gamma)/h$, the [Newtonian potential of a point mass](../../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) with the segment's total [mass](../../../../../../../mass.md).
