<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define the classical axial spin by the [axial angular velocity of a symmetric top](../../../../../../axial-angular-velocity-of-a-symmetric-top.md),

$$
\boxed{n=\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf e.}
$$

Since $\mathbf e$ is a [unit vector](../../../../../../unit-vector.md), $\mathbf e\cdot\dot{\mathbf e}=0$. The [vector triple product](../../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) identity applied to $\dot{\mathbf e}=\boldsymbol\omega\wedge\mathbf e$ gives

$$
\mathbf e\wedge\dot{\mathbf e}
=\boldsymbol\omega-(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf e,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf e\wedge\dot{\mathbf e}+n\mathbf e.
$$

The [inertia tensor](../../../../../../inertia-tensor.md) multiplies the component perpendicular to $\mathbf e$ by $A$ and the component along $\mathbf e$ by $C$. Therefore the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) about $Q$ is

$$
\mathbf L_Q=A\mathbf e\wedge\dot{\mathbf e}+Cn\mathbf e.
$$

Its time [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
\dot{\mathbf L}_Q=A\mathbf e\wedge\ddot{\mathbf e}+C\dot n\mathbf e+Cn\dot{\mathbf e},
$$

because $\dot{\mathbf e}\wedge\dot{\mathbf e}=\mathbf0$.

The gravitational [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) about $Q$ is $h\mathbf e\wedge(-mg\mathbf k)$, and the applied [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) is $\ell\mathbf e\wedge(\kappa\mathbf s)$. The support [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) acts at $Q$, so has zero [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) about $Q$. The [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) equation is consequently

$$
\boxed{A\mathbf e\wedge\ddot{\mathbf e}+C\dot n\mathbf e+Cn\dot{\mathbf e}
=-mgh\mathbf e\wedge\mathbf k+\kappa\ell\mathbf e\wedge\mathbf s.}
$$
