<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The three [lines](../../../../../../../straight-line.md) have the convenient [parametric equations](../../../../../../../parametric-equation.md)

$$
L_1:\ (s,0,1),\qquad L_2:\ (1,s,0),\qquad L_3:\ (0,1,s),\qquad s\in\mathbb R.
$$

For $L_1$, substitute $\mathbf a=\mathbf i$, $\mathbf b=\mathbf j$ in the [plane through a point and a line given by a cross product](../../../../../../../plane-through-a-point-and-a-line-given-by-a-cross-product.md) formula. Its [normal vector](../../../../../../../normal-vector.md) is $\mathbf j+\mathbf i\wedge\mathbf y=(0,1-y_3,y_2)$. Cyclically permuting the [coordinates](../../../../../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md) gives all three [planes](../../../../../../../plane.md):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Pi_1:&\quad(1-y_3)r_2+y_2r_3=y_2,\\
\Pi_2:&\quad y_3r_1+(1-y_1)r_3=y_3,\\
\Pi_3:&\quad(1-y_2)r_1+y_1r_2=y_1.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Each [normal vector](../../../../../../../normal-vector.md) is nonzero because $\mathbf y$ is not on the corresponding [line](../../../../../../../straight-line.md). Thus these are genuine [planes](../../../../../../../plane.md), and each is unique.
