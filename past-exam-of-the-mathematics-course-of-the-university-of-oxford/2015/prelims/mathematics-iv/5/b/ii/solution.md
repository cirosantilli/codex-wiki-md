<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The inequality $3y_1+y_2\ge6$, together with $y_2\ge0$, implies

$$
y_1+y_2=\frac13(3y_1+y_2)+\frac23y_2\ge2>1.
$$

Also $-y_1+y_2\ge-1$ implies

$$
-y_1+3y_2=(-y_1+y_2)+2y_2\ge-1>-5.
$$

Thus **both inequalities are strictly slack throughout the feasible region**. They are redundant: the remaining inequalities imply them, even with strict inequality. By [complementary slackness](../../../../../../../complementary-slackness.md), any primal optimum must therefore have $x_1=x_3=0$; this is the principle that [strictly slack dual constraints force zero primal variables](../../../../../../../strictly-slack-dual-constraints-force-zero-primal-variables.md).
