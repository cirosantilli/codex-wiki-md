<h1 id="5/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

At $\mathbf y^*=(4/3,2)$, the first three dual left sides are $10/3$, $2/3$ and $14/3$, strictly above their bounds $1$, $-1$ and $-5$. Thus [complementary slackness](../../../../../../../complementary-slackness.md) forces $x_1=x_2=x_3=0$. Both [dual variables](../../../../../../../dual-variable.md) are positive, so both primal inequalities are equalities:

$$
3x_4+3x_5=2,\qquad 4x_4+x_5=1.
$$

Solving this [linear system](../../../../../../../system-of-linear-equations.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf x^*=\left(0,0,0,\frac19,\frac59\right),\qquad
\max\left(x_1-x_2-5x_3+12x_4+6x_5\right)=\frac{14}{3}.}
$$

All components are nonnegative, the two original upper bounds are met exactly, and the [objective function](../../../../../../../objective-function.md) equals the dual value. The [weak duality](../../../../../../../weak-duality.md) bound therefore certifies optimality. The forced zero components and unique solution of the two remaining equalities also prove that the primal optimum is unique.
