<h1 id="9/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Let $n=2m\ge2$. Between the two middle [order statistics](../../../../../../../order-statistic.md), $k=m$ and the slope $A'(\mu)=2m-n$ is zero. To the left $A$ decreases and to the right it increases. Consequently all joint [maximum-likelihood estimates](../../../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimator.md) are

$$
\boxed{\widehat\mu\in[x_{(m)},x_{(m+1)}],\qquad
\widehat\lambda=\frac{n}{A_{\min}},}
$$

where the constant minimum is

$$
\boxed{A_{\min}=\sum_{i=m+1}^{2m}x_{(i)}-\sum_{i=1}^mx_{(i)}>0.}
$$

The strict positivity follows from distinct observations. Thus the location [maximum-likelihood estimate](../../../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimator.md) is not unique, while the rate [maximum-likelihood estimate](../../../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimator.md) is unique and the same for every location in the displayed [interval](../../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). Choosing the arithmetic midpoint of the two middle values is the usual [sample median](../../../../../../../sample-median.md) convention and gives one member of this entire maximizing family.
