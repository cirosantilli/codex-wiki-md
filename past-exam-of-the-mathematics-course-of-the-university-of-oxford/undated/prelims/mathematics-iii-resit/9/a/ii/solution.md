<h1 id="9/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At [sample size](../../../../../../../sample-size.md) 100, keep essentially the same numerical [confidence interval](../../../../../../../confidence-interval.md), but treat its calibration as approximate. For independent heights with finite, nonzero [variance](../../../../../../../variance-split.md), the [central limit theorem](../../../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) makes the [sample mean](../../../../../../../sample-mean.md) approximately [normally distributed](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md), and the [sample variance](../../../../../../../sample-variance.md) consistently estimates the population [variance](../../../../../../../variance-split.md). Hence the [studentized sample mean](../../../../../../../studentized-sample-mean.md) is approximately standard normal even without a [normal distribution](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md) for individual heights.

Using the permitted cutoff 2 gives

$$
\boxed{\mu\approx\overline x\pm2\frac{s}{\sqrt n}
=[170.0,173.4]\ \mathrm{cm}\quad\text{at approximately 95\% confidence}.}
$$

A [Student t-distribution](../../../../../../../student-s-t-distribution.md) cutoff gives a similar approximation for large [sample size](../../../../../../../sample-size.md), but its justification at fixed [sample size](../../../../../../../sample-size.md) is no longer exact. Extreme skewness or influential [outliers](../../../../../../../outlier.md) could make 100 observations insufficient for accurate [coverage probability](../../../../../../../coverage-probability.md); abandoning [normality](../../../../../../../normality-statistics.md) alone supplies no specific numerical correction. The [sampling with replacement](../../../../../../../sampling-with-replacement.md) assumption still precludes a [finite population correction](../../../../../../../finite-population-correction.md).
