<h1 id="5/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $A=\{|Z|>2\}$ and $B=\{Z>0\}$. The [standard normal distribution](../../../../../../../standard-normal-distribution.md) is symmetric and assigns no [probability mass](../../../../../../../probability-mass.md) to zero. Thus $\mathbb P(B)=1/2$, and its equal two-sided tails give

$$
\mathbb P(A)=2\mathbb P(Z>2),\qquad
\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(Z>2).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B),}
$$

so **the two [events](../../../../../../../event.md) are [independent](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md)**. The permitted approximation gives $\mathbb P(A)\approx0.05$ and $\mathbb P(A\cap B)\approx0.025$, but [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) is exact.
