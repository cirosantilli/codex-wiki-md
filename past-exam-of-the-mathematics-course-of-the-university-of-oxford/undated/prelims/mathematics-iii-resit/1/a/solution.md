<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Divide by $x>1$. The [product rule](../../../../../../product-rule.md) turns the [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation-split.md) into

$$
(\ln x\,y)'=\frac{\ln x}{x}.
$$

Equivalently, $\ln x$ is an [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) after division by $x\ln x$. An [antiderivative](../../../../../../antiderivative.md) gives $\ln x\,y=\tfrac12(\ln x)^2+C$. The [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md) at $e$ gives $C=-1/2$, hence

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\frac12\left(\ln x-\frac1{\ln x}\right),\qquad x>1.}
$$

Its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $y'=[1+(\ln x)^{-2}]/(2x)$, so substitution gives $x\ln x\,y'+y=\ln x$, and $y(e)=0$. Every [solution of a differential equation](../../../../../../solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) has the integrated form above, and the [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md) fixes its only constant. This agrees with the [uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations](../../../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-ordinary-differential-equations.md) on the stated [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md).
