<h1 id="8/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**It is [single-linkage clustering](../../../../../../../single-linkage-clustering.md).** Its intercluster distance is the smallest cross-pair [Euclidean distance](../../../../../../../euclidean-distance.md). Successive nearby points can join around the ring and within the dense central cloud before any edge crosses the gap between them. Equivalently, at a threshold below the between-group gap but above the lengths needed to connect each group, the [single-linkage clustering](../../../../../../../single-linkage-clustering.md) components are precisely the central group and the ring.

[Complete-linkage clustering](../../../../../../../complete-linkage-clustering.md) uses the largest cross-pair [Euclidean distance](../../../../../../../euclidean-distance.md). Opposite points on the ring are far apart, so an intact ring has a large [diameter](../../../../../../../diameter.md). That rule favors compact pieces and can attach a ring segment to the center before all ring segments join. The visible gap and short links within each group explain why [single-linkage clustering](../../../../../../../single-linkage-clustering.md) recovers the two intended [clusters in cluster analysis](../../../../../../../cluster-in-cluster-analysis.md) in this example; it is not a universal guarantee for arbitrary noisy rings.
