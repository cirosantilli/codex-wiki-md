<h1 id="7/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**They need not be [independent](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).** For a counterexample take $n=2$, $x_1=-1$, $x_2=1$, and independent errors taking $-1,1$ with equal [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md). Then

$$
\widehat\alpha-\alpha=\frac{\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2}{2},\qquad
\widehat\beta-\beta=\frac{\epsilon_2-\epsilon_1}{2}.
$$

Each centered [estimator](../../../../../../../estimator.md) is zero with [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$, but they are never both zero. Their zero [events](../../../../../../../event.md) therefore violate [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).

A sufficient additional assumption is that the errors have a common [normal distribution](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md). The two [estimators](../../../../../../../estimator.md) are then [Jointly Gaussian](../../../../../../../multivariate-normal-distribution.md), being linear combinations of the independent [Gaussian random variables](../../../../../../../gaussian-random-variable.md). Their zero [covariance](../../../../../../../covariance.md), established in [iii](../iii.md), implies [independence of uncorrelated jointly normal variables](../../../../../../../independence-of-uncorrelated-jointly-normal-variables.md). Normal errors are a sufficient condition here, not a claim that no other error [probability distribution](../../../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md) could ever work.
