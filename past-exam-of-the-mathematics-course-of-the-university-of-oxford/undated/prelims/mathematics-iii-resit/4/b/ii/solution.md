<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an outcome $\omega=(i,j)$, the two [random variables](../../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) are the coordinate maps

$$
\boxed{X(i,j)=i,\qquad Y(i,j)=j.}
$$

Their [marginal probability mass functions](../../../../../../../marginal-probability-mass-function.md) are $p_X(i)=p_Y(j)=1/6$ on $\{1,\ldots,6\}$, and their [joint probability mass function](../../../../../../../joint-probability-mass-function.md) satisfies

$$
p_{X,Y}(i,j)=\frac1{36}=p_X(i)p_Y(j).
$$

Off that support, the same factorization holds with zeros. Hence **$X$ and $Y$ are [independent random variables](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md)**, by the [joint probability mass function](../../../../../../../joint-probability-mass-function.md) criterion for [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).
