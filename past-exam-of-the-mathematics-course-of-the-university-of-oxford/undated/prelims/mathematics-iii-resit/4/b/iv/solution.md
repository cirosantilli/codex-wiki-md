**No: [pairwise independence](../../../../../../../pairwise-independent-events.md) does not imply that the three are [independent events](../../../../../../../independent-events.md).** Take $C=\{Y\text{ is even}\}$. Then $\mathbb P(C)=1/2$, and $\mathbb P(A\cap C)=9/36=1/4$ by [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the two rolls. Also $B\cap C$ is precisely the event that both faces are even, so $\mathbb P(B\cap C)=1/4$. Thus all three pairs of [events](../../../../../../../event.md) are [independent](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md).

But $A\cap B\cap C$ also consists of the nine even-even outcomes, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A\cap B\cap C)=\frac14\ne\frac18
=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\mathbb P(C).}
$$

This violates the triple-intersection requirement for [independent events](../../../../../../../independent-events.md).
