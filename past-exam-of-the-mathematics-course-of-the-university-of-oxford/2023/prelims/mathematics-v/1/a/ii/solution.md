<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In [polar coordinates](../../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md), put $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$. Differentiating along the curve gives

$$
x\,dy-y\,dx=r^2\,d\theta.
$$

The [cardioid](../../../../../../../cardioid.md) has $r\geq0$ and is traversed once counterclockwise as $\theta$ increases from $0$ to $2\pi$. Its cusp at $\theta=\pi$ causes no difficulty: split the [line integral](../../../../../../../line-integral.md) there, or exclude a small arc and take its limit. Thus [signed area as a boundary integral](../../../../../../../signed-area-as-a-boundary-integral.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
|D|&=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^2\,d\theta\\
&=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}\left(\frac32+2\cos\theta+\frac12\cos2\theta\right)d\theta
=\boxed{\frac{3\pi}{2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
