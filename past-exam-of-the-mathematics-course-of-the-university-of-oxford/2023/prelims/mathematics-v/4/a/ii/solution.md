<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Using the usual piecewise smooth interpretation of the derivative hypothesis, [Dirichlet's theorem on Fourier series](../../../../../../../dirichlet-s-theorem-on-fourier-series.md) gives the average of the two one-sided limits of the appropriate [periodic extension](../../../../../../../periodic-extension.md). The [even extension](../../../../../../../even-extension.md) is continuous at $0$ and at the identified endpoints $\pm\pi$, so the [Fourier cosine series](../../../../../../../fourier-cosine-series.md) converges to

$$
\boxed{f(x)\quad\text{for every }0\leq x\leq\pi.}
$$

For the [odd extension](../../../../../../../odd-extension.md), the one-sided limits at zero are $-f(0)$ and $f(0)$, while at $\pi$ the periodic one-sided limits are $f(\pi)$ and $-f(\pi)$. Hence the [Fourier sine series](../../../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md) converges to

$$
\boxed{\begin{cases}f(x),&0<x<\pi,\\0,&x=0\text{ or }x=\pi.\end{cases}}
$$

At an endpoint this equals $f$ precisely when $f$ is zero there. One can also see the zero endpoint values directly: every [partial sum](../../../../../../../partial-sum.md) consists of [sines](../../../../../../../sine.md) that vanish at both endpoints.
