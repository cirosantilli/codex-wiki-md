<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On $E=\mathbb R$, take $f_n(x)=x$ and $g_n(x)=1/n$. Then $f_n\to f(x)=x$ with [uniform convergence](../../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md), because there is no error, and $g_n\to0$ with [uniform convergence](../../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md), with error $1/n$. Their products $f_ng_n=x/n$ converge [pointwise](../../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md) to zero, but for every $n$ the choice $x=n$ gives $|f_n(x)g_n(x)|=1$. Thus **the products do not converge uniformly**. Here the first family consists of unbounded [functions](../../../../../../../function-split.md), exactly the missing hypothesis in the preceding estimate.
