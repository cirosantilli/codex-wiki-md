<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Uniform Cauchy criterion](../../../../../../../uniform-cauchy-criterion.md) says that $(f_n:E\to\mathbb R)$ [converges uniformly](../../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) on $E$ if and only if

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\ \forall m,n\geq N\ \forall x\in E:\quad |f_n(x)-f_m(x)|<\varepsilon.
$$

For necessity, use the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) through the [uniform limit](../../../../../../../uniform-limit.md). For sufficiency, the condition makes $(f_n(x))_n$ a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) for each $x$, so [completeness of the real numbers](../../../../../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) defines a [pointwise limit](../../../../../../../pointwise-limit.md) $f(x)$. Apply the condition with $\varepsilon/2$ and let $m\to\infty$ to obtain $|f_n(x)-f(x)|\leq\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon$ uniformly in $x$.
