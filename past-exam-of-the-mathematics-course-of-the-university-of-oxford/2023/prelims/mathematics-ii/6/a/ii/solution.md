↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [constancy theorem](../../../../../../../constancy-theorem.md) states that a [differentiable function](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) on an [interval](../../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) whose [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes everywhere is [constant](../../../../../../../constant-mathematics.md). At any included endpoints, [continuity](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) there is understood.

Indeed, take arbitrary $x<y$ in the [interval](../../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md). The [mean value theorem](../../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) applies on $[x,y]$ and gives $f(y)-f(x)=f'(c)(y-x)=0$. Every pair of values is equal, proving the assertion. The [interval](../../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) hypothesis ensures that the whole segment $[x,y]$ lies in the [domain](../../../../../../../domain-mathematical-analysis.md).
