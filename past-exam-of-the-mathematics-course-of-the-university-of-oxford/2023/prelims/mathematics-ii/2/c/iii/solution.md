<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $u(x)=(\log x)x^{-2/3}$. Its [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
u'(x)=x^{-5/3}\left(1-\frac23\log x\right),
$$

which is negative for $x>e^{3/2}$. Also $u(x)\to0$, since a positive power eventually dominates the [natural logarithm](../../../../../../../natural-logarithm.md). Thus the [alternating series test](../../../../../../../alternating-series-test.md) proves convergence of $\sum(-1)^n u(n)$.

For $n\geq3$, $u(n)\geq n^{-2/3}$, whose [p-series](../../../../../../../p-series.md) diverges. The [comparison test](../../../../../../../direct-comparison-test.md) therefore rules out [absolute convergence](../../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md). The answer is **conditional convergence**.
