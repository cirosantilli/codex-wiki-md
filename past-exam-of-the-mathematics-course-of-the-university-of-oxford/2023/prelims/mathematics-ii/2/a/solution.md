<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges when its [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) $s_N=\sum_{n=1}^N a_n$ have a finite [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md).

The [comparison test](../../../../../../direct-comparison-test.md) says that if $0\leq a_n\leq b_n$ eventually and $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges. Indeed, the [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) of the nonnegative terms $a_n$ are increasing, and are bounded above by a fixed initial contribution plus the sum of the comparison [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md). The [bounded monotone sequence theorem](../../../../../../bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md) proves their convergence. Finite initial terms do not affect convergence. Applying this result to $|a_n|$ also proves [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) whenever $|a_n|\leq b_n$ eventually with $\sum b_n<\infty$.
