<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [limit of a function](../../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) $f:E\to\mathbb R$ at a [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) $p$ of $E$ is $\ell$ when

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x\in E:\quad
0<|x-p|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ |f(x)-\ell|<\varepsilon.
$$

The [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) converges as $x\to p$ if such a finite $\ell$ exists. The puncture excludes any value assigned at $p$.

For $p\in E$, [continuity](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $p$ means

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x\in E:\quad
|x-p|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ |f(x)-f(p)|<\varepsilon.
$$

When $p$ is also a [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md), this says that the [limit of a function](../../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) is $f(p)$. At an [isolated point](../../../../../../../isolated-point.md) of $E$, every [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) relative to $E$, even though the punctured limit does not have a unique forced value.
