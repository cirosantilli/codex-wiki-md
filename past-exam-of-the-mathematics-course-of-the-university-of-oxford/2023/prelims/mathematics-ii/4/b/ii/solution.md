<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take $y=f(p)$ with $p\in\overline E$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, [continuity](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) gives $\delta>0$ such that $|x-p|<\delta$ implies $|f(x)-f(p)|<\varepsilon$. Since $p\in\overline E$, choose $x\in E$ with $|x-p|<\delta$. Thus every [neighbourhood](../../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $y$ meets $f(E)$, proving

$$
\boxed{f(\overline E)\subseteq\overline{f(E)}.}
$$

Equality need not hold. Let $f(x)=e^x$ and $E=\mathbb R$. The [exponential function](../../../../../../../exponential-function.md) is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md), but

$$
f(\overline E)=(0,\infty)\ \varsubsetneq\ [0,\infty)=\overline{f(E)}.
$$

The additional point zero comes from arguments escaping to $-\infty$, not from a finite point of $\overline E$.
