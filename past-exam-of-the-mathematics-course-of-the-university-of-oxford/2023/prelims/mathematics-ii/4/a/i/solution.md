↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) $p$ of $E\subseteq\mathbb R$ is a point such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, the punctured [neighbourhood](../../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) $(p-\varepsilon,p+\varepsilon)\setminus\{p\}$ meets $E$. A [closed set](../../../../../../../closed-set.md) contains all of its [limit points](../../../../../../../limit-point.md).

Every nonempty [open interval](../../../../../../../open-interval.md) contains a [rational number](../../../../../../../rational-number.md); applying this to $(p,p+\varepsilon)$ shows that every $p\in\mathbb R$ is a [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) of $\mathbb Q$. Thus **the [limit points](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) of $\mathbb Q$ form $\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb Q$ is not closed**.

If $p\in\mathbb Z$, radius $1/2$ excludes all other [integers](../../../../../../../integer-split.md). If $p\notin\mathbb Z$, its distances to the two neighbouring [integers](../../../../../../../integer-split.md) have a positive minimum, and a smaller radius excludes every [integer](../../../../../../../integer-split.md). Thus **$\mathbb Z$ has no [limit points](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) in $\mathbb R$ and is closed**.
