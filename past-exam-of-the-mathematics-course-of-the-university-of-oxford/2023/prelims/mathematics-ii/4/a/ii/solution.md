<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $E$ is a [closed set](../../../../../../../closed-set.md) and $p\notin E$. Then $p$ is not a [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) of $E$, so some punctured [neighbourhood](../../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $p$ misses $E$. As $p\notin E$, the entire [neighbourhood](../../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) misses $E$. Thus $\mathbb R\setminus E$ is an [open set](../../../../../../../open-set.md).

Conversely, suppose every point $p\notin E$ has a [neighbourhood](../../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) disjoint from $E$. No such point can be a [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) of $E$, so every [limit point](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) lies in $E$. This proves the equivalence between containing all [limit points](../../../../../../../limit-point.md) and having an complement that is an [open set](../../../../../../../open-set.md).
