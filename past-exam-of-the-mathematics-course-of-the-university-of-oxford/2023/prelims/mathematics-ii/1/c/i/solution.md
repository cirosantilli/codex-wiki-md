<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At [even integers](../../../../../../../even-number.md) $n=2k$, the terms are $1+1/(2k)\to1$. At [odd integers](../../../../../../../odd-integer.md) they are $-1-1/(2k-1)\to-1$. The largest eventual values therefore come from the subsequence at [even integers](../../../../../../../even-number.md), and

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}(-1)^n(1+1/n)=1.}
$$

Explicitly, every sufficiently late term is below $1+\varepsilon$, while infinitely many [even integers](../../../../../../../even-number.md) give terms greater than $1-\varepsilon$.
