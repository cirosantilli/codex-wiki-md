<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Cauchy-Hadamard theorem](../../../../../../../cauchy-hadamard-theorem.md) states $R^{-1}=\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}$. At the [prime numbers](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $n=p$, this root equals $p^{2/p}\to1$. There are infinitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), so these indices form an unbounded [subsequence](../../../../../../../subsequence.md). For every other positive index the root is zero. The [limit superior](../../../../../../../limit-superior.md) is therefore one, and $\boxed{R=1}$.
