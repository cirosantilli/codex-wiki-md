<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At the nonzero indices $n=m!$ with $m\geq2$,

$$
|a_{m!}|^{1/(m!)}=10^{m/(m!)}\longrightarrow1,
$$

since $m/(m!)=1/(m-1)!\to0$. At all other sufficiently large indices the coefficient is zero. The initial coefficients do not affect the [limit superior](../../../../../../../limit-superior.md), which equals one. The [Cauchy-Hadamard theorem](../../../../../../../cauchy-hadamard-theorem.md) therefore gives $\boxed{R=1}$.
