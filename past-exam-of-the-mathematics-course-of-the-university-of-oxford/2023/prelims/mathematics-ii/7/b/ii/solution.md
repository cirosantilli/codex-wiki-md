<h1 id="7/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take $f_n(x)=x^n$ on $[0,1]$. Each [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md), and the [pointwise limit](../../../../../../../pointwise-limit.md) is $f(x)=0$ for $0\leq x<1$, while $f(1)=1$. Nevertheless

$$
\boxed{\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx=\frac1{n+1}\longrightarrow0=\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx.}
$$

The limiting [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is [Riemann integrable](../../../../../../../riemann-integrable-function.md), since changing a value at one point does not change its [Riemann integral](../../../../../../../riemann-integral.md). There is no [uniform convergence](../../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md): for every $n$, $\sup_{[0,1]}|f_n-f|=1$, with the [supremum](../../../../../../../supremum.md) approached as $x\uparrow1$ from below. Equivalently, the [uniform limit theorem](../../../../../../../uniform-limit-theorem.md) would require the limit to be [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md).
