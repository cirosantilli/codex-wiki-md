<h1 id="9/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If the two regressors are strongly associated, the data may provide little support for varying one while holding the other fixed. [Multicollinearity](../../../../../../../multicollinearity.md) makes the separate slope estimates unstable, and exact [linear dependence](../../../../../../../linear-dependence.md) makes them unidentifiable. A conditional slope may also be an extrapolation beyond combinations actually observed.

Furthermore, a slope in a [linear regression model](../../../../../../../linear-regression-model.md) is not automatically a [causal effect](../../../../../../../causal-effect.md). A causal interpretation needs appropriate design or assumptions excluding [confounding](../../../../../../../confounding.md); independence of the errors across observations and zero mean alone do not establish those assumptions. The coefficients describe the specified linear mean relation, but changing a regressor by intervention can have a different interpretation.
