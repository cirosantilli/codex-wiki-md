↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [partition of a sample space](../../../../../../../partition-of-a-sample-space.md) is a finite or countable family $(A_j)$ of pairwise disjoint [events](../../../../../../../event.md) whose union is the whole [sample space](../../../../../../../sample-space.md). If $X$ is [integrable](../../../../../../../integrability.md), the [law of total expectation](../../../../../../../law-of-total-expectation.md) states

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[X]=\sum_{j:\mathbb P(A_j)>0}\mathbb P(A_j)\,\mathbb E[X\mid A_j].}
$$

This follows by summing $X\mathbf1_{A_j}$ over the [partition of a sample space](../../../../../../../partition-of-a-sample-space.md); absolute integrability justifies the countable sum. For nonnegative $X$, the identity also holds with infinite expectations. Zero-probability parts contribute zero without requiring a conditional value on them.
