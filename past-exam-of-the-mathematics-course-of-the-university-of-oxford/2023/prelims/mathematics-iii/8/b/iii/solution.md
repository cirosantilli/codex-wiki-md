<h1 id="8/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Since $\sum_{i=1}^nD_i=0$, we have $D_1=-\sum_{i=2}^nD_i$. Squaring and adding the other residual squares gives

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^nD_i^2=\left(\sum_{i=2}^nD_i\right)^2+\sum_{i=2}^nD_i^2.}
$$

Therefore $S^2$ is a measurable [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) of $(D_2,\ldots,D_n)$. By the block [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) proved in part [i](../i.md), it is independent of $\overline X$. This is the [independence of the normal sample mean and sample variance](../../../../../../../independence-of-the-normal-sample-mean-and-sample-variance.md); pairwise independence alone would not justify passing to an arbitrary function of several residuals.
