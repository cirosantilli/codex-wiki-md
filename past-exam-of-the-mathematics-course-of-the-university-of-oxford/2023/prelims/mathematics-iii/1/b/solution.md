<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Separation of variables](../../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) gives

$$
\frac{y\,dy}{3y^2+1}=\frac{x\,dx}{x^2-1},\qquad
\frac16\log(3y^2+1)=\frac12\log(x^2-1)+C.
$$

Here $x>1$ makes the argument of the second [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) positive. Substituting the [initial condition](../../../../../../initial-condition.md) yields $3y^2+1=4(x^2-1)^3$. The positive branch is therefore

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\sqrt{\frac{4(x^2-1)^3-1}{3}},\qquad x>\sqrt{1+4^{-1/3}}.}
$$

This is the maximal connected [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) containing $\sqrt2$ on which this branch solves the original [differential equation](../../../../../../differential-equation-split.md). At its left endpoint $y=0$, the original right-hand side has a zero denominator, so one cannot extend the solution through that point merely by continuing the squared equation.
