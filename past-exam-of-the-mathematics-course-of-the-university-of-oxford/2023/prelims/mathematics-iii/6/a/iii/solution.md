<h1 id="6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [integers](../../../../../../../integer-split.md) form a [countable set](../../../../../../../countable-set.md), and the [events](../../../../../../../event.md) $\{X=k\}$ are disjoint. By [countable additivity](../../../../../../../countable-additivity.md) and part [ii](../ii.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X\in\mathbb Z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb P(X=k)=0.}
$$

Countability is essential: one cannot use the same sum argument over an arbitrary uncountable collection of zero-probability events.
