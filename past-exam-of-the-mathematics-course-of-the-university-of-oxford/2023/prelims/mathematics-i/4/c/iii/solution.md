Yes. Consider the following $n^2$ [matrices](../../../../../../../matrix.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathcal B=\{E_{ii}:1\le i\le n\}
\ \cup\ \{E_{ii}+E_{ij}:1\le i,j\le n,\ i\ne j\}.}
$$

Each has only row $i$ nonzero and has $i$th diagonal entry $1$, so part (c)(ii) proves that it is a [diagonalisable matrix](../../../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md). The collection is a [spanning set](../../../../../../../spanning-set.md), because it contains every $E_{ii}$ and

$$
E_{ij}=(E_{ii}+E_{ij})-E_{ii}\qquad(i\ne j).
$$

Thus it spans the [matrix unit](../../../../../../../matrix-unit.md) [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md). A [spanning set](../../../../../../../spanning-set.md) of $n^2$ [vectors](../../../../../../../vector.md) in an $n^2$-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) is a [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md). Alternatively, in a vanishing [linear combination](../../../../../../../linear-combination.md) of $\mathcal B$, each off-diagonal entry first forces its own coefficient to be zero, and then each diagonal entry forces the remaining coefficient to be zero.
