Yes: use the [non-abelian group](../../../../../../../non-abelian-group.md) $H$ from part (a). Every element is $I_3+N$ for a [strictly upper triangular matrix](../../../../../../../strictly-upper-triangular-matrix.md) $N$ with $N^3=0$. In the [finite field](../../../../../../../finite-field.md) $\mathbb Z_3$, the [binomial theorem for commuting matrices](../../../../../../../binomial-theorem-for-commuting-matrices.md) gives

$$
(I_3+N)^3=I_3+3N+3N^2+N^3=I_3.
$$

Thus $\boxed{T(h)=I_3\text{ for all }h\in H}$. This constant identity-valued map is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md), since $T(xy)=I_3=T(x)T(y)$. Part (a)(iv) already proves that $H$ is not an [abelian group](../../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Here $T$ is neither injective nor surjective, consistently with the preceding part.
