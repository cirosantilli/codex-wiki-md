<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Put $L=\operatorname{span}\{v\}$. Every two-dimensional [vector subspace](../../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) containing $v$ is $W=\operatorname{span}\{v,w\}$ for some $w\notin L$. There are $p^n-p$ choices for $w$ in $V$. Within any one such $W$, exactly $p^2-p$ choices of $w\in W\setminus L$ yield that same $W$: each forms a [linearly independent](../../../../../../../linear-independence.md) pair with $v$, and that pair is a [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) of $W$.

These sets of choices partition $V\setminus L$, because $w$ determines $\operatorname{span}\{v,w\}$ uniquely. Thus the number is

$$
\boxed{\frac{p^n-p}{p^2-p}=\frac{p^{n-1}-1}{p-1}.}
$$

Equivalently these [vector subspaces](../../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) correspond to the one-dimensional [vector subspaces](../../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of the [quotient vector space](../../../../../../../quotient-vector-space.md) $V/L$. For $n=1$ there are none and the formula gives zero.
