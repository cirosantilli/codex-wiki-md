<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

If $A$ is an [invertible matrix](../../../../../../../invertible-matrix.md), then

$$
BA=A^{-1}(AB)A.
$$

If instead $B$ is an [invertible matrix](../../../../../../../invertible-matrix.md), then

$$
AB=B^{-1}(BA)B.
$$

Thus in either case $AB$ and $BA$ are [similar matrices](../../../../../../../matrix-similarity.md). Explicitly, whenever $Y=P^{-1}XP$ and $Xu=\lambda u$ with $u\ne0$, one has $Y(P^{-1}u)=\lambda P^{-1}u$ and $P^{-1}u\ne0$. Applying the same argument with $P^{-1}$ in place of $P$ proves equality of the sets of [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), including zero.
