↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Yes. The sums of the columns of $A$ are the sums of the rows of its [transpose](../../../../../../../transpose.md) $A^T$. By the previous argument, $A^T\mathbf1=\lambda\mathbf1$, so $\det(\lambda I_n-A^T)=0$. The [determinant](../../../../../../../determinant.md) is unchanged by [transpose](../../../../../../../transpose.md), giving

$$
\det(\lambda I_n-A)
=\det\bigl((\lambda I_n-A)^T\bigr)
=\det(\lambda I_n-A^T)=0.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of }A}$. The [vector](../../../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf1$ need not itself be an [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $A$ in the column-sum case.
