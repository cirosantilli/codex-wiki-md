<h1 id="6/b/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [v](../v.md)

Put $L=\bigcap_{i=1}^kH_i$. Repeated application of part (iv), or [mathematical induction](../../../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on $k$, gives

$$
|G/L|\le\prod_{i=1}^k n_i<\infty.
$$

Part (ii) therefore implies that $L$ is an [infinite set](../../../../../../../infinite-set.md). If $G\setminus U$ is nonempty, choose $g\notin U$. We claim its [left coset](../../../../../../../left-coset.md) $gL$ is wholly outside $U$. If $g\ell\in H_i$ for some $\ell\in L$, then $\ell\in H_i$ and closure under products and inverses would give $g=(g\ell)\ell^{-1}\in H_i$, a contradiction.

Thus $gL\subseteq G\setminus U$. Part (i) makes $gL$ infinite, so the complement is infinite. If there is no such $g$, the complement is empty. This proves the [complement of a finite union of finite-index subgroups](../../../../../../../complement-of-a-finite-union-of-finite-index-subgroups.md) property:

$$
\boxed{G\setminus(H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_k)\text{ is empty or infinite}.}
$$
