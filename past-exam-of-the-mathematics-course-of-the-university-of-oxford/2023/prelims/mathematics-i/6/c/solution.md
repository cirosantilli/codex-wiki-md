<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

Suppose there were only finitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), say $p_1,\ldots,p_k$. Work in the [infinite group](../../../../../../infinite-group.md) $(\mathbb Z,+)$ and set $H_i=p_i\mathbb Z$. The [left cosets](../../../../../../left-coset.md) are the $p_i$ residue classes modulo $p_i$, so each $H_i$ is a [finite-index subgroup](../../../../../../finite-index-subgroup.md), with $|\mathbb Z/H_i|=p_i$.

By part (b)(v), $\mathbb Z\setminus\bigcup_i p_i\mathbb Z$ is empty or infinite. But part (a)(ii) identifies it as the nonempty two-element set $\{-1,1\}$. This contradiction proves [Euclid's theorem](../../../../../../euclid-s-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{\text{There are infinitely many prime numbers}.}
$$
