<h1 id="6/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Neither $1$ nor $-1$ is divisible by a [prime number](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), so both lie outside $U$. On the other hand, $0\in2\mathbb Z\subseteq U$. If an [integer](../../../../../../../integer-split.md) $z$ has $|z|>1$, part (a)(i) supplies a [prime number](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) $p$ dividing the positive [integer](../../../../../../../integer-split.md) $|z|$; the same $p$ divides $z$, so $z\in p\mathbb Z\subseteq U$. These cases exhaust $\mathbb Z$, proving

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z\setminus U=\{-1,1\}.}
$$
