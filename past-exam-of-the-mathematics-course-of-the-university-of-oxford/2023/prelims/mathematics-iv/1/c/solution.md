<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $V=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)$. The [vector triple product identity](../../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) gives

$$
(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\times(\mathbf c\times\mathbf a)
=\mathbf c[(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf a]-\mathbf a[(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf c]
=V\mathbf c.
$$

Taking its [dot product](../../../../../../dot-product.md) with $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$ and using cyclic invariance of the [scalar triple product](../../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md) yields

$$
\boxed{(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)\cdot[(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)\times(\mathbf c\times\mathbf a)]=V^2.}
$$

There was no division by $V$, so this also proves the identity for coplanar [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md).
