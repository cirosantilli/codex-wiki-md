<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With $L=1/\sqrt{n^2-1}$, one has $\nu=n$. Since $n\geq3>\sqrt3$, [ii](../ii.md) proves boundedness. Write the positive [rational number](../../../../../../../rational-number.md) $n=p/q$ in lowest terms. The [radius](../../../../../../../radius.md) and its angular [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) repeat after $\Delta\theta=2\pi/n$. After $p$ such radial oscillations,

$$
p\Delta\theta=2\pi q,
$$

so both the [position](../../../../../../../position.md) and the radial and transverse [velocities](../../../../../../../velocity.md) return to their initial values. The elapsed time is

$$
T=\frac1L\int_0^{2\pi q}r(\theta)^2\,d\theta,
$$

which is finite and positive because $r$ is bounded above and below by positive constants. Thus **the motion is periodic**. The [orbit](../../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is a closed, generally self-intersecting [rosette orbit](../../../../../../../rosette-orbit.md), with $p$ radial oscillations during $q$ complete revolutions; for integral $n$ it has $n$ evenly spaced lobes around the origin. It never reaches the origin and is not a [circular orbit](../../../../../../../circular-orbit.md).
