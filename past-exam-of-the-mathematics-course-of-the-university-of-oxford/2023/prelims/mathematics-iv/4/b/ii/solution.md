<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The oscillatory numerator $\cos(\nu\theta)+\nu\sin(\nu\theta)$ has [amplitude](../../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) $\sqrt{1+\nu^2}$. Therefore the reciprocal [radius](../../../../../../../radius.md) stays bounded away from zero exactly when

$$
\nu^2-1>\sqrt{1+\nu^2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\nu^2>3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L^2<\frac12.
$$

In this case the radial bounds are

$$
\frac{a\nu^2}{\nu^2-1+\sqrt{1+\nu^2}}
\leq r\leq
\frac{a\nu^2}{\nu^2-1-\sqrt{1+\nu^2}}.
$$

For completeness, the [potential energy](../../../../../../../potential-energy.md) per unit [mass](../../../../../../../mass.md) is $\Phi=-1/(ar)+1/(2r^2)$, and [conservation of mechanical energy](../../../../../../../conservation-of-mechanical-energy.md) gives

$$
\frac12\dot r^2+U(r)=\mathcal E,\qquad
U(r)=\frac{1+L^2}{2r^2}-\frac1{ar},\qquad
\mathcal E=\frac{L^2-1/2}{a^2}.
$$

This [effective potential](../../../../../../../effective-potential.md) diverges positively at zero, has one minimum at $r=a(1+L^2)$, and tends to zero from below at infinity. Since the initial radial [velocity](../../../../../../../velocity.md) is negative, the particle first reaches an inner [turning point](../../../../../../../turning-point.md). If $L^2\geq1/2$, the subsequent outward motion has no finite outer [turning point](../../../../../../../turning-point.md) and escapes. At equality the escape [speed](../../../../../../../speed.md) tends to zero, but the [radius](../../../../../../../radius.md) is still unbounded; infinity is approached only as time tends to infinity. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{The path is bounded exactly for }0<L<1/\sqrt2.}
$$

The minimum of $U$ permits a [circular orbit](../../../../../../../circular-orbit.md) for other initial data, but the nonzero initial radial [velocity](../../../../../../../velocity.md) here excludes that exceptional solution.
