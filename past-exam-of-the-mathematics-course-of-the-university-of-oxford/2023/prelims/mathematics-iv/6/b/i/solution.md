<h1 id="6/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The disc is rigidly attached, so its rotation axis is perpendicular to its face. Its [moment of inertia](../../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about its center is $Ma^2/2$: with [surface mass density](../../../../../../../projected-surface-mass-density.md) $M/(\pi a^2)$, an annulus of [radius](../../../../../../../radius.md) $s$ contributes $s^2[M/(\pi a^2)]2\pi s\,ds$, whose integral is $Ma^2/2$. The [parallel axis theorem](../../../../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) adds $Md^2$ at the pivot. Adding the rod's [moment of inertia](../../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) gives

$$
\boxed{I_O=\frac13m\ell^2+M\left(d^2+\frac{a^2}{2}\right)
=m\ell^2\left[\frac13+\mu\left(\delta^2+\frac{\alpha^2}{2}\right)\right].}
$$
