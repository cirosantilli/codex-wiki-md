<h1 id="6/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a fixed attachment ratio $\delta>0$, making the disc much heavier than the rod gives

$$
\omega^2\longrightarrow\frac g\ell\frac{\delta}{\delta^2+\alpha^2/2}
=\frac{gd}{d^2+a^2/2}.
$$

If the disc is also small relative to the attachment distance, $\alpha/\delta=a/d\to0$, its [moment of inertia](../../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about its own center is negligible compared with $Md^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\omega^2\longrightarrow\frac gd,\qquad T\longrightarrow2\pi\sqrt{\frac dg}.}
$$

This is a [simple pendulum](../../../../../../../simple-pendulum-split.md) with a [point mass](../../../../../../../point-mass.md) at the disc's center and effective length $d$; it is length $\ell$ only if $d=\ell$. Even before the [small-angle approximation](../../../../../../../small-angle-approximation.md), its equation tends to $\ddot\theta=-(g/d)\sin\theta$.

If $\delta$ is allowed to tend to zero together with the other parameters, the bare conditions $\alpha\ll1$ and $\mu\gg1$ do not alone justify that reduction. The needed dominance conditions are $\mu\delta\gg1$, $\mu\delta^2\gg1$, and $\alpha/\delta\ll1$, so that the rod contributes negligible gravitational [torque](../../../../../../../torque.md) and [moment of inertia](../../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md), and the disc behaves as a [point mass](../../../../../../../point-mass.md). They follow automatically in the combined limit with fixed $\delta>0$.
