<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The uniform rod has [linear mass density](../../../../../../linear-mass-density.md) $m/\ell$. Integrating squared distance from the pivot gives its [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) about the axis perpendicular to the plane of motion:

$$
\boxed{I_O=\int_0^\ell s^2\frac m\ell\,ds=\frac13m\ell^2.}
$$

Measure $\theta$ from the downward vertical. The [center of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) is at distance $\ell/2$ from the pivot, so [gravity](../../../../../../gravity.md) produces the restoring [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) $-mg(\ell/2)\sin\theta$. The fixed-axis [rotational equation of motion](../../../../../../rotational-equation-of-motion.md) is $I_O\ddot\theta=\tau_O$. Therefore the [physical pendulum](../../../../../../physical-pendulum.md) obeys

$$
\boxed{\ddot\theta=-\frac{3g}{2\ell}\sin\theta.}
$$

The pivot [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) has zero [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) about the pivot.
