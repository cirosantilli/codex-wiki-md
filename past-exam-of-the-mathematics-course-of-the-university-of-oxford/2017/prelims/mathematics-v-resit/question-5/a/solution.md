<h1 id="question-5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [a](../a.md)

Let the uniform string have constant [tension](../../../../../../tension.md) $\tau>0$ and [linear mass density](../../../../../../linear-mass-density.md) $\mu>0$. Assume small transverse motion with $|u_x|\ll1$, negligible longitudinal motion, and no external transverse force. If the string makes angle $\beta$ with the horizontal, then $\tan\beta=u_x$, and the [small-angle approximation](../../../../../../small-angle-approximation.md) gives $\sin\beta\simeq u_x$ and $ds\simeq dx$ to first order.

The net upward force on the segment from $x$ to $x+\Delta x$ is therefore

$$
\tau\bigl(u_x(x+\Delta x,t)-u_x(x,t)\bigr).
$$

By [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md), its mass $\mu\Delta x$ times its transverse [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) equals this force, to the order retained in the small displacement model. Divide by $\Delta x$ and let $\Delta x\to0$ to obtain

$$
\mu u_{tt}=\tau u_{xx}.
$$

Thus the [wave equation on a string](../../../../../../wave-equation-on-a-string.md) is

$$
\boxed{u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx},\qquad c=\sqrt{\frac\tau\mu}},
$$

where $c$ is the [wave speed](../../../../../../wave-speed.md). The horizontal force balance makes the horizontal component of [tension](../../../../../../tension.md) constant; to first order it equals $\tau$.
