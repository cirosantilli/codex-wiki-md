↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

Here $f=0$ and $ct=1$, so [D'Alembert's formula](../../../../../../d-alembert-s-formula.md) gives

$$
u\left(\frac32,\frac1c\right)
=\frac1{2c}\int_{1/2}^{5/2}g(s)\,ds.
$$

Only the overlap $[1/2,1]$ with the [support](../../../../../../support.md) of the initial velocity contributes. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
u\left(\frac32,\frac1c\right)
&=\frac1{2c}\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-s^2)\,ds\\
&=\frac1{2c}\left[s-\frac{s^3}{3}\right]_{1/2}^{1}
=\frac1{2c}\left(\frac23-\frac{11}{24}\right)
=\boxed{\frac5{48c}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The relevant interval is determined by the two [characteristic lines for the one-dimensional wave equation](../../../../../../characteristic-line-for-the-one-dimensional-wave-equation.md) through the observation point.
