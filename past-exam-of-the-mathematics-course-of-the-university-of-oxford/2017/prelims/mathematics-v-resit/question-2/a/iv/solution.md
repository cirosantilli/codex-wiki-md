<h1 id="question-2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iv](../iv.md)

For $\alpha=3$, the [divergence](../../../../../../../divergence.md) vanishes at every point with $r>0$, so its ordinary [volume integral](../../../../../../../volume-integral.md), ignoring the undefined single point, is zero. Nevertheless, the outward [flux integral](../../../../../../../flux-integral.md) through the unit [sphere](../../../../../../../sphere.md) is still $4\pi$:

$$
\boxed{\iiint_{0<r\leq1}\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\,dV=0,
\qquad\iint_{r=1}\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=4\pi}.
$$

**The hypotheses of the divergence theorem fail at the origin.** Indeed $|\mathbf u|=r^{-2}$ there, so no continuous extension is possible. On the punctured region $\varepsilon<r<1$, the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) remains valid: the outer [flux](../../../../../../../flux.md) is $4\pi$ and the inner [flux](../../../../../../../flux.md) is $-4\pi$. Unlike [iii](../iii.md), this inner contribution does not tend to zero. The discrepancy therefore comes from the persistent [flux](../../../../../../../flux.md) at the singular point, rather than a failure of the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) under its hypotheses.
