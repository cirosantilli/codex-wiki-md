<h1 id="question-3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

Let $S$ be a piecewise smooth [oriented surface](../../../../../../oriented-surface.md) with piecewise smooth boundary $\partial S$, and let $\mathbf F$ be a [continuously differentiable vector field](../../../../../../continuously-differentiable-vector-field.md) on a neighbourhood of $S$. The [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r
=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS}.
$$

The [boundary orientation](../../../../../../boundary-orientation.md) is induced by the chosen [unit normal vector](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) $\mathbf n$ through the [right-hand rule](../../../../../../right-hand-rule.md): with the right thumb along $\mathbf n$, the curled fingers indicate positive boundary traversal. Equivalently, when walking along the boundary with one's head pointing along $\mathbf n$, the [surface](../../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) lies to the left. An upward oriented [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) therefore has counterclockwise boundary traversal when viewed from above. Reversing the [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) reverses both the [line integral](../../../../../../line-integral.md) and the [surface integral](../../../../../../surface-integral.md).
