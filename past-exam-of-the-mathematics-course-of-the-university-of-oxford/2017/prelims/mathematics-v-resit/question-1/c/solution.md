<h1 id="question-1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

The inequalities become the [rectangle](../../../../../../rectangle.md) $1/2\leq u\leq1$, $1/2\leq v\leq1$ in [parabolic polar coordinates](../../../../../../parabolic-polar-coordinates.md). The [change of variables formula](../../../../../../change-of-variables-formula.md) and the [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) from [a](../a.md) give the [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md)

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\int_{1/2}^1\int_{1/2}^1(u^2+v^2)\,dv\,du\\
&=\frac12\int_{1/2}^1u^2\,du+\frac12\int_{1/2}^1v^2\,dv
=\left[\frac{s^3}{3}\right]_{1/2}^1
=\boxed{\frac7{24}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The middle panel in [b](../b.md) shows the curved region in [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md); its straight edges in [parabolic polar coordinates](../../../../../../parabolic-polar-coordinates.md) explain the simple [integration limits](../../../../../../integration-limit.md).
