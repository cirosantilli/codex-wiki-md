<h1 id="6/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), every nonidentity [group element](../../../../../../../group-element.md) has order $5$, $7$, or $35$. If some element has order $35$, its seventh and fifth powers have orders $5$ and $7$, respectively, and we are done.

Otherwise every nonidentity element has order $5$ or $7$. Distinct [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of a given [prime number](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) order intersect only in the identity: a shared nonidentity element generates both. If there were no element of order $5$, the $34$ nonidentity elements would therefore partition into sets of six, the nonidentity elements of the [cyclic subgroups](../../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) of order seven. This would force $6\mid34$, a contradiction. If there were no element of order $7$, the analogous partition into sets of four would force $4\mid34$, again a contradiction. Thus **elements of both orders exist**.
