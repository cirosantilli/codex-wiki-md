<h1 id="6/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $P$ and $Q$ be [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of orders five and seven, which exist by [i](../i.md). Their [normalizers](../../../../../../../normalizer.md) contain $P$ and $Q$, respectively. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), $N_G(P)$ has order $5$ or $35$, and $N_G(Q)$ has order $7$ or $35$. If either has order $35$, its [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) is normal by [iv](../../b/iv.md).

Suppose neither is normal. Then [iv](../../b/iv.md) gives at least seven distinct [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order five and at least five distinct [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order seven. Distinct [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of the same prime order intersect only in the identity, and a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order five and one of order seven also intersect only in the identity by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md). Thus $G$ would have at least

$$
7(5-1)+5(7-1)=58
$$

nonidentity elements, contradicting $|G|-1=34$. Hence **at least one of $P,Q$ is a normal subgroup**.
