<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that for a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $K$ of a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md) $G$,

$$
|G|=[G:K]|K|;
$$

in particular, $|K|$ divides $|G|$. Here $[G:K]$ is the [subgroup index](../../../../../../index-of-a-subgroup.md), the number of left [cosets](../../../../../../coset.md) of $K$.

The [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g\in G$ is the least positive integer $k$ such that $g^k=e$. It exists because $G$ is finite. The [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$ consists of exactly $e,g,\ldots,g^{k-1}$, so has order $k$. Applying [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) to this [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) gives

$$
\boxed{o(g)\mid|G|.}
$$
