<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The elimination in [i](../i.md) determines $x_3$ uniquely exactly when $\lambda\ne5$. The other two coordinates are then also fixed. Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{\lambda\ne5:\quad
\mathbf x=\begin{pmatrix}
1-2(\mu-2)/(\lambda-5)\\
2-5(\mu-2)/(\lambda-5)\\
(\mu-2)/(\lambda-5)
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

For $\lambda=5$, the reduced equation is either inconsistent or leaves $x_3$ free, so a unique solution is impossible.
