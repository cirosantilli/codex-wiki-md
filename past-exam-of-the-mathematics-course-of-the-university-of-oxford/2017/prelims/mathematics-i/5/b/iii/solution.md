<h1 id="5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $\sigma$ has [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) two. On a cycle of length $k$, $\sigma^2$ moves each entry two places. Since $\sigma^2=e$, every such $k$ divides two, so $k=1$ or $k=2$. After omitting the fixed points, the [disjoint cycle decomposition](../../../../../../../disjoint-cycle-decomposition.md) is therefore a product of [disjoint permutation cycles](../../../../../../../disjoint-permutation-cycles.md) of length two, which are [transpositions](../../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md). There is at least one, because $\sigma\ne e$.
