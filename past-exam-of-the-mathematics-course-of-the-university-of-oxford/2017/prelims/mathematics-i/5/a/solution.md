<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) in the [symmetric group](../../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_n$ exchanges two distinct symbols and fixes all others. A [permutation cycle](../../../../../../permutation-cycle.md) $(a_1\ a_2\ \cdots\ a_k)$ sends $a_j$ to $a_{j+1}$, sends $a_k$ to $a_1$, and fixes the other symbols. Its entries are distinct. For $k=1$ this is the identity [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md).

A [group element](../../../../../../group-element.md) has [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $k$ if $k$ is the least positive integer for which its $k$th power is the [identity element](../../../../../../identity-element.md). For the displayed [permutation cycle](../../../../../../permutation-cycle.md), its $j$th power sends $a_i$ forward $j$ places, with subscripts read modulo $k$. Hence the $k$th power fixes every symbol, whereas for $0<j<k$ the image of $a_1$ is different from $a_1$. Thus its [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) is exactly $k$, including $k=1$.
