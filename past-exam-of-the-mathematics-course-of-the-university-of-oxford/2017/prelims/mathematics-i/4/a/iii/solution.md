<h1 id="4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The definition of an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) and the [kernel](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) criterion for a singular [square matrix](../../../../../../../square-matrix.md) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue}
&\iff\ker(T-\lambda I)\ne\{0\}\\
&\iff\lambda I-A\text{ is not invertible}\\
&\iff\det(\lambda I-A)=0\\
&\iff\chi_T(\lambda)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

For the second equivalence, a [linear map](../../../../../../../linear-map.md) from a finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) to itself has zero [kernel](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) exactly when its [rank of a linear map](../../../../../../../rank-of-a-linear-map.md) is the full [dimension](../../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md), by the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md), and therefore exactly when it is invertible. This proves both directions.
