<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A real number $\lambda$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $T$ if $Tv=\lambda v$ for some nonzero [vector](../../../../../../../vector.md) $v\in V$. Its [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is

$$
E_\lambda=\{v\in V:Tv=\lambda v\}=\ker(T-\lambda I).
$$

It is a [vector subspace](../../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) because it contains $0$ and $T(av+bw)=aTv+bTw=\lambda(av+bw)$ whenever $v,w\in E_\lambda$ and $a,b\in\mathbb R$.

If $v\in E_\lambda\cap E_\mu$, then $\lambda v=Tv=\mu v$, so $(\lambda-\mu)v=0$. Since $\lambda\ne\mu$, this forces $v=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E_\lambda\cap E_\mu=\{0\}.}
$$
