<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Subtracting the first [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) from each of the other two preserves the [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) property by [iii](../a/iii.md). The resulting family is

$$
e_1,\quad(0,\alpha,\alpha^2)^T,\quad(0,\beta,\beta^2)^T.
$$

Its last two [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) belong to the plane with first coordinate zero. By [b](../b.md), they are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) there precisely when

$$
\alpha\beta^2-\beta\alpha^2=\alpha\beta(\beta-\alpha)\ne0.
$$

In that case their two-dimensional [span](../../../../../../linear-span.md) is the whole plane, and adjoining $e_1$ gives a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of $\mathbb R^3$. If the displayed expression vanishes, a nontrivial [linear relation](../../../../../../linear-relation.md) among those two [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) prevents a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\alpha\ne0,\quad\beta\ne0,\quad\alpha\ne\beta.}
$$
