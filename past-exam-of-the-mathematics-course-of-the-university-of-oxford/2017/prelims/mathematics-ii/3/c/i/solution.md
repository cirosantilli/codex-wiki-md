<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For fixed $z\ne0$, the ratio of successive [absolute values](../../../../../../../absolute-value.md) is

$$
\frac{|z|^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{|z|^n/n!}=\frac{|z|}{n+1}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The [ratio test](../../../../../../../ratio-test.md) says that a [series](../../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges absolutely when this ratio has a [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) strictly below one. Thus this [power series](../../../../../../../power-series.md) converges absolutely for every complex $z$; at zero the conclusion is immediate. Its [radius of convergence](../../../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is $\boxed{R=\infty}$.
