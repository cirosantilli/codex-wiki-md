<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**True.** For every $m,n$,

$$
\inf C_n\leq x_{m,n}\leq\sup R_m.
$$

Fix $m$. The number $\sup R_m$ is an [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) for every column [infimum](../../../../../../../infimum.md), so $\sup_n\inf C_n\leq\sup R_m$. This holds for every $m$, making $\sup_n\inf C_n$ a [lower bound](../../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of the row [suprema](../../../../../../../supremum.md). Taking their [infimum](../../../../../../../infimum.md) gives the [weak minimax inequality](../../../../../../../weak-minimax-inequality.md)

$$
\boxed{\sup_{n\geq1}\inf_{m\geq1}x_{m,n}\leq\inf_{m\geq1}\sup_{n\geq1}x_{m,n}.}
$$
