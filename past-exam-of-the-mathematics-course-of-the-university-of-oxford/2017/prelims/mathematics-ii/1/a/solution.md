↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [infimum](../../../../../../infimum.md) of $A$ is its greatest [lower bound](../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md): $\alpha=\inf A$ means that $\alpha\leq x$ for every $x\in A$, and that every [lower bound](../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $l$ of $A$ satisfies $l\leq\alpha$. The [supremum](../../../../../../supremum.md) is its least [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md): $\beta=\sup A$ means that $x\leq\beta$ for every $x\in A$, and every [upper bound](../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $u$ satisfies $\beta\leq u$. The [completeness of the real numbers](../../../../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) ensures that both exist for a nonempty bounded [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) of [real numbers](../../../../../../real-number.md).
