<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $s=\sup(-A)$. For every $x\in A$, $-x\leq s$, hence $-s\leq x$, so $-s$ is a [lower bound](../../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $A$. If $l$ is any [lower bound](../../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $A$, then $-x\leq-l$ for every $x\in A$, making $-l$ an [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $-A$. The definition of [supremum](../../../../../../../supremum.md) gives $s\leq-l$, or $l\leq-s$. Thus $-s$ is the greatest [lower bound](../../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), and

$$
\boxed{\inf A=-\sup(-A).}
$$
