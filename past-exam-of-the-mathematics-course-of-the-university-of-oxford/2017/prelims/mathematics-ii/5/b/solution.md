**No.** Let $E=\mathbb R$, $x_0=0$ and

$$
f_n(x)=\frac1n\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(x),\qquad f(x)=0.
$$

The [supremum norm](../../../../../../supremum-norm.md) satisfies $\|f_n-f\|_\infty=1/n\to0$, so there is [uniform convergence](../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) to the [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f$. But every $f_n$ is [discontinuous](../../../../../../discontinuity-of-a-function.md) at zero: $f_n(0)=1/n$ whereas $f_n(x)=0$ for every $x\ne0$. No tail consists of [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) continuous at $x_0$.
