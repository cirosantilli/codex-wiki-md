<h1 id="4/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $x\ne y$, one of $x<y$ and $y<x$ holds. Since $f$ is [strictly increasing](../../../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md), the corresponding values are strictly ordered, so $f(x)\ne f(y)$. Thus $f$ is an [injection](../../../../../../../injective-function.md). Every element of the [image of a function](../../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) $f([a,b])$ equals $f(x)$ for some $x\in[a,b]$, which is precisely [surjectivity](../../../../../../../surjective-function.md) onto that [image of a function](../../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md). Hence $f:[a,b]\to f([a,b])$ is a **[bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md)**.
