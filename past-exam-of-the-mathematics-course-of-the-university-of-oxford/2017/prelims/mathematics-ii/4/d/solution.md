<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

With the strict [monotonicity](../../../../../../monotonic-function.md) assumed at the start of the question, parts (a) and (c) give a [strictly increasing](../../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md) [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md)

$$
g:[f(a),f(b)]\longrightarrow[a,b],\qquad g(f(a))=a,\quad g(f(b))=b.
$$

Its [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) is exactly the [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) between its endpoint values. Apply part (c) to $g$, with domain $[f(a),f(b)]$, to conclude that **$g$ is a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md)**, including at both endpoints.

If the word “increasing” in this subpart were interpreted as merely [nondecreasing](../../../../../../nondecreasing-function.md), an [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) need not exist: a constant [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) is a counterexample. The preceding strict hypothesis supplies the required [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md).
