↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The real [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) of $f$ is written

$$
\boxed{f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx\bigr),}
$$

where the [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) are

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a_0&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx,\\
a_n&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos nx\,dx,\qquad n\geq1,\\
b_n&=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin nx\,dx,\qquad n\geq1.
\end{aligned}}
$$

By part (a)(i), any other interval of length $2\pi$ gives the same [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md), because each integrand is a [periodic function](../../../../../../periodic-function.md) with period $2\pi$. The notation $\sim$ associates $f$ with its [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md); existence of the [integrals](../../../../../../integral-split.md) alone does not assert [pointwise convergence](../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md).
