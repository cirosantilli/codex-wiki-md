<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [mass density](../../../../../../../density.md) depends only on $r$, so it has [spherical symmetry](../../../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md). For this isolated planet, choose the [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) to vanish at infinity, or equivalently choose $\varphi=-\Phi$ to do so. There is no imposed external [gravitational field](../../../../../../../gravitational-field.md). Rotating the [mass distribution](../../../../../../../mass-distribution.md) therefore leaves $\varphi$ unchanged: $\varphi=\varphi(r)$.

The [gradient](../../../../../../../gradient.md) in [spherical coordinates](../../../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) consequently has no angular components, and the paper's [gravitational potential sign conventions](../../../../../../../gravitational-potential-sign-conventions.md) give

$$
\boxed{\mathbf g=\nabla\varphi=\frac{d\varphi}{dr}\mathbf e_r,\qquad r>0.}
$$

At the centre there is no distinguished radial direction, and [spherical symmetry](../../../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) makes $\mathbf g(0)=\mathbf0$.
