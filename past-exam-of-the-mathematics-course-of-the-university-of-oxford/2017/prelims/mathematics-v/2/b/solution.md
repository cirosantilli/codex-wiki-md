<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [specific heat capacity](../../../../../../specific-heat-capacity.md) $c$ is the [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) needed per unit [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) per unit increase in [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). With $c$ constant, a small element of [volume](../../../../../../volume.md) $dV$ has [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $\rho\,dV$ and thermal [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md) $\rho cT\,dV$, measured relative to the reference at which $T=0$. Summing over $\Omega$ gives

$$
\boxed{E_\Omega(t)=\iiint_\Omega\rho cT(\mathbf x,t)\,dV.}
$$

Choosing a different reference [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) only adds a time-independent constant to this [energy](../../../../../../energy-split.md), so it does not affect the [heat balance with volumetric heat generation](../../../../../../heat-balance-with-volumetric-heat-generation.md).
