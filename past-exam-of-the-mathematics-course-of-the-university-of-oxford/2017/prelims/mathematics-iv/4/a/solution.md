<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [a](../a.md)

Put $C=q^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0)>0$. The [spring](../../../../../../spring.md) exerts the [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) $k(\ell-x)$ by [Hooke's law](../../../../../../hooke-s-law.md), while [Coulomb's law](../../../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) gives the attractive [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) $-C/x^2$. [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) therefore gives the [equation of motion](../../../../../../equation-of-motion.md)

$$
\boxed{m\ddot x=f(x)=k(\ell-x)-\frac C{x^2}.}
$$

For $x>0$, the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $f'(x)=-k+2C/x^3$. Thus $f$ increases up to $x_*=(2C/k)^{1/3}$ and decreases thereafter. Also $f(x)\to-\infty$ at both ends of the positive half-axis. Its unique [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is

$$
f(x_*)=k\left(\ell-\frac32x_*\right).
$$

There are therefore two [equilibrium points](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) precisely when

$$
\boxed{\ell>\ell_1=\frac32\left(\frac{q^2}{2\pi\epsilon_0k}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

Writing them $x_-<x_*<x_+$, the [linearization](../../../../../../linearization.md) $m\ddot\eta=f'(x_\pm)\eta$ has exponentially growing solutions at $x_-$ and oscillatory solutions at $x_+$. Equivalently, the [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) has a strict [local maximum](../../../../../../local-maximum.md) at $x_-$ and a strict [local minimum](../../../../../../local-minimum.md) at $x_+$. Hence **the smaller [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) is an [unstable equilibrium](../../../../../../unstable-equilibrium.md); the larger is a [stable equilibrium](../../../../../../stable-equilibrium.md)**, with no [damping](../../../../../../damping.md) to make it attracting.

At equality $\ell=\ell_1$ there is one degenerate [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) $x_*$; it is unstable, since a displacement to its left gives a leftward [force](../../../../../../force-split.md). For $\ell<\ell_1$ there are no [equilibrium points](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md).
