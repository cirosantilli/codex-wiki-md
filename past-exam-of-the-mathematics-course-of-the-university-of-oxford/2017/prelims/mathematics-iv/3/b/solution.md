<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [b](../b.md)

Using the [normal vector](../../../../../../normal-vector.md) from [i](../a/i.md), the [normal line](../../../../../../normal-line.md) is

$$
\boxed{L:\ \mathbf x=\mathbf r(\theta,\phi)+\lambda(\sqrt2\cos\theta,\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb R.}
$$

For $0<\theta<\pi$, the $y$- and $z$-[coordinates](../../../../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md) vanish together exactly when $\lambda=-1$. At that [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md), the $x$-[coordinate](../../../../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md) is

$$
\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt2}-\sqrt2\cos\theta=-\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{a=-\frac1{\sqrt2}.}
$$

At $\theta=0$ or $\theta=\pi$, both the [position vector](../../../../../../position-vector.md) and [normal vector](../../../../../../normal-vector.md) lie along the $x$-axis, so **$L$ is the whole $x$-axis in either case**.
