<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

**The required [lines](../../../../../../straight-line.md) are the $x$-axis and every [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) through the origin in the [plane](../../../../../../plane.md) $x=0$.** Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{L=\{(t,0,0):t\in\mathbb R\}\quad\text{or}\quad L=\{(0,t\cos\phi,t\sin\phi):t\in\mathbb R\},\quad0\le\phi<\pi.}
$$

The $x$-axis meets the [ellipsoid](../../../../../../ellipsoid.md) at its two poles and is a [normal line](../../../../../../normal-line.md) at each. A [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) of the second kind meets it at the antipodal [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) $(0,\pm\cos\phi,\pm\sin\phi)$, whose [normal vectors](../../../../../../normal-vector.md) are parallel to that [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md).

To prove completeness, suppose a common [normal line](../../../../../../normal-line.md) passes through distinct [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) $P=\mathbf r(\theta,\phi)$ and $Q=\mathbf r(\theta',\phi')$. If either is a pole, [b](../b.md) forces the [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) to be the $x$-axis. Otherwise [b](../b.md) says that the [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) meets the $x$-axis at $(-\cos\theta/\sqrt2,0,0)$ and at $(-\cos\theta'/\sqrt2,0,0)$. A [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) other than the $x$-axis has at most one such intersection, so $\cos\theta=\cos\theta'$.

The [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) $P,Q$ consequently have equal $x$-[coordinates](../../../../../../coordinate-linear-algebra.md). Since they are distinct, their joining [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) has zero $x$-component in its direction. Its [normal vector](../../../../../../normal-vector.md) at $P$ therefore has $\sqrt2\cos\theta=0$. Hence $P,Q$ lie in $x=0$, and the [normal line](../../../../../../normal-line.md) there passes through the origin. This gives exactly the listed family.
