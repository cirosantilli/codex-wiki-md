<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [b](../b.md)

Subtracting the first row from the other two in the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) gives

$$
f(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c)
=\det\begin{pmatrix}b_1-a_1&b_2-a_2\\c_1-a_1&c_2-a_2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This vanishes exactly when the two [displacements](../../../../../../displacement.md) $\mathbf b-\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf c-\mathbf a$ are [linearly dependent](../../../../../../linear-dependence.md). If $\mathbf a\ne\mathbf b$, that means $\mathbf c-\mathbf a=t(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)$ for some [real number](../../../../../../real-number.md) $t$. Thus all three [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) lie on the [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md)

$$
\mathbf x=\mathbf a+s(\mathbf b-\mathbf a),\qquad s\in\mathbb R.
$$

Conversely, putting three [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) on any one [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) makes their [displacements](../../../../../../displacement.md) [linearly dependent](../../../../../../linear-dependence.md), so the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) vanishes.

**The equivalence remains true without distinctness.** If exactly two [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) coincide, the two distinct locations determine a [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) containing all three, and two rows of the original [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) coincide. If all three [points](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) coincide, any [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) through that [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) works and the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is again zero. Existence persists, although uniqueness of the [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) can fail.
