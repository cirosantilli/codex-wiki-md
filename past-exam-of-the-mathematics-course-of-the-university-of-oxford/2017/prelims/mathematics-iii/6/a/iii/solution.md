<h1 id="6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The products $TU$ and $UV$ share $U$, so they are not [independent](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md). Using the definition of [covariance](../../../../../../../covariance.md) and the [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of $T,U,V$,

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(TU,UV)=\mathbb E(TU^2V)-\mathbb E(TU)\mathbb E(UV)
=(\mathbb E T)(\mathbb E U^2)(\mathbb E V)-1=2-1=1.
$$

By contrast, $TU$ and $VW$ depend on disjoint sets of [independent random variables](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), so they are [independent](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) and have zero [covariance](../../../../../../../covariance.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(TU,UV)=1,\qquad\operatorname{Cov}(TU,VW)=0.}
$$
