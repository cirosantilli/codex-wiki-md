<h1 id="8/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The algebraic identity $\widehat\beta_0-\beta_0=n^{-1}\sum_i\epsilon_i$ does not require a [normal distribution](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md) for the errors. Applying the [central limit theorem](../../../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) to their [sample mean](../../../../../../../sample-mean.md) gives

$$
\frac{\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_0-\beta_0)}{\sigma}\xrightarrow d N(0,1).
$$

Thus, for large $n$,

$$
\boxed{\widehat\beta_0\ \text{is approximately }N(\beta_0,\sigma^2/n).}
$$

Its [mean](../../../../../../../expected-value-split.md) $\beta_0$ and [variance](../../../../../../../variance-split.md) $\sigma^2/n$ remain exact, even though its [normal distribution](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md) is now only an approximation. With the error [probability distribution](../../../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md) unspecified, the formula is retained as an estimator; it is not being asserted to remain a [maximum likelihood estimation](../../../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimation-split.md) formula.
