↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [center of a group](../../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) consists of its elements commuting with every element. First check it is a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md). The [identity element](../../../../../../../identity-element.md) is central. If $a,b\in Z(G)$ and $x\in G$, then $b^{-1}$ commutes with $x$ and

$$
(ab^{-1})x=a(xb^{-1})=x(ab^{-1}).
$$

Thus $ab^{-1}\in Z(G)$, proving closure under the subgroup criterion. Moreover, if $z\in Z(G)$, then $xzx^{-1}=z$ for every $x\in G$. Hence conjugation fixes the center elementwise, so

$$
\boxed{xZ(G)x^{-1}=Z(G)\quad(x\in G).}
$$

Therefore the [center of a group](../../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).
