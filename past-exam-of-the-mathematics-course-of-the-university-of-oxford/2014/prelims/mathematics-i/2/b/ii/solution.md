<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [image of a linear map](../../../../../../../image-of-a-linear-map.md) $\operatorname{im}(ST)=S(\operatorname{im}T)$ lies inside $\operatorname{im}S$. Therefore $r(ST)\leq r(S)$. Also $S$ restricted to $\operatorname{im}T$ cannot have [rank of a linear map](../../../../../../../rank-of-a-linear-map.md) greater than its domain's [dimension](../../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md), so $r(ST)\leq\dim\operatorname{im}T=r(T)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{r(ST)\leq\min\{r(S),r(T)\}.}
$$

Equivalently, apply the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md) to that restriction: $r(ST)=r(T)-\dim(\operatorname{im}T\cap\ker S)\leq r(T)$. This proof keeps track of the intermediate [vector subspace](../../../../../../../vector-subspace.md), rather than assuming an invertible factor.
