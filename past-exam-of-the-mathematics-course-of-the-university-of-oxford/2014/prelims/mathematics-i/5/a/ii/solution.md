↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $o(g)$ is the least positive integer $n$ for which $g^n=e$, whose existence follows from part (i). If $g^k=e$, use the [Euclidean division](../../../../../../../euclidean-division.md) to write $k=qn+r$, where $0\leq r<n$. Then

$$
e=g^k=(g^n)^qg^r=g^r.
$$

Minimality of $n$ forces $r=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{o(g)\mid k.}
$$

The argument also works for negative $k$, using integer powers and the same remainder convention.
