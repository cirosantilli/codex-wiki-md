↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that for a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$ of a finite [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) $G$,

$$
|G|=[G:H]|H|,
$$

where $[G:H]$ is the [index of a subgroup](../../../../../../../index-of-a-subgroup.md), the number of its [left cosets](../../../../../../../left-coset.md). In particular, $|H|$ divides $|G|$.

The [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$ consists of the distinct elements $e,g,\ldots,g^{n-1}$, where $n=o(g)$. Every power reduces to one of these by the [Euclidean division](../../../../../../../euclidean-division.md), and a repetition among them would contradict minimality of $n$. Thus $|\langle g\rangle|=o(g)$. Applying [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{o(g)\mid|G|.}
$$
