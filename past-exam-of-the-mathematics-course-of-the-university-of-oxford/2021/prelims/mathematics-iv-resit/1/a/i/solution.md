<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [vector triple product identity](../../../../../../../vector-triple-product.md)

$$
\mathbf a\times(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)=\mathbf b(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)-\mathbf c(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b).
$$

Since $\mathbf n$ is a [unit vector](../../../../../../../unit-vector.md), put

$$
\mathbf w=\mathbf n\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf n)=\mathbf v-(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n,\qquad \mathbf q=\mathbf n\times\mathbf v.
$$

Thus $\mathbf w$ is the component of $\mathbf v$ in the [orthogonal complement](../../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) of $\mathbf n$, and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf w=0$. A [cross product](../../../../../../../cross-product.md) is [orthogonal](../../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both its factors, so $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf q=0$ and $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf q=0$. Consequently

$$
\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf w=\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf v-(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf n)(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n)=0.
$$

**All three pairs are [orthogonal](../../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md).** This remains true when $\mathbf v$ is parallel to $\mathbf n$: then $\mathbf q$ and $\mathbf w$ are [zero vectors](../../../../../../../zero-vector.md). Pairwise [orthogonality](../../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) does not assert [linear independence](../../../../../../../linear-independence.md) in this case.
