<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the convention $[\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c]=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)$ for the [scalar triple product](../../../../../../../scalar-triple-product.md). With $\mathbf q,\mathbf w$ as above, $\mathbf q=\mathbf n\times\mathbf w$. The [vector triple product identity](../../../../../../../vector-triple-product.md) gives

$$
\mathbf q\times\mathbf w=-\mathbf w\times(\mathbf n\times\mathbf w)=-\mathbf n|\mathbf w|^2+\mathbf w(\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf n)=-\mathbf n|\mathbf w|^2.
$$

The [Lagrange identity for the cross product](../../../../../../../lagrange-identity-for-the-cross-product.md), $|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|^2=|\mathbf a|^2|\mathbf b|^2-(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)^2$, also gives

$$
|\mathbf q|^2=|\mathbf v|^2-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v)^2=|\mathbf w|^2.
$$

Taking the [dot product](../../../../../../../dot-product.md) with the [unit vector](../../../../../../../unit-vector.md) $\mathbf n$ proves

$$
\boxed{[\mathbf n,\mathbf n\times\mathbf v,\mathbf n\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf n)]=-|\mathbf n\times\mathbf v|^2.}
$$
