<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $d$ be any positive [integer](../../../../../../integer-split.md). If $d$ divides $a$ and $b$, it divides the [integer linear combination](../../../../../../integer-linear-combination.md) $a-qb=r$. Conversely, if $d$ divides $b$ and $r$, it divides $qb+r=a$. Thus $(a,b)$ and $(b,r)$ have exactly the same positive common [divisors](../../../../../../divisor.md). Their greatest elements coincide:

$$
\boxed{\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,r).}
$$

This [greatest common divisor](../../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md) invariance is the step behind the [Euclidean algorithm](../../../../../../euclidean-algorithm.md); it does not require $r<b$, although that additional bound ensures the algorithm terminates. If the convention $0\in\mathbb N$ permits $r=0$, the same argument works and $\gcd(b,0)=b$ for $b>0$.
