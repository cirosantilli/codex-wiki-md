<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The solid [cylinder](../../../../../../cylinder-geometry.md) has constant [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) $\rho=m/(\pi a^2l)$. A thin cylindrical shell of radius $s$ and thickness $ds$ has [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $dm=2\pi\rho ls\,ds$. Its contribution to the axial [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) is $s^2dm$. Therefore

$$
\mathcal I_{33}=\int_0^a s^2(2\pi\rho ls)\,ds=2\pi\rho l\frac{a^4}{4}=\boxed{\frac12ma^2.}
$$

The attached wheels are massless, so they add no [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md). This is the [moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia-of-a-uniform-solid-cylinder.md) about its central longitudinal [axis of rotation](../../../../../../axis-of-rotation.md).
