↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

One sufficient version of the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) is the following. Let $R$ be a bounded region in $\mathbb R^3$ with a closed, piecewise [smooth surface](../../../../../../../smooth-surface.md) as its boundary, and let $\mathbf F$ be a [continuously differentiable function](../../../../../../../continuously-differentiable-function.md) with values in $\mathbb R^3$ on an open neighbourhood of the [closure](../../../../../../../closure-topology.md) of $R$. With $\mathbf n$ the outward [unit normal vector](../../../../../../../unit-normal.md),

$$
\boxed{\iiint_R\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\,dV
=\iint_{\partial R}\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS}.
$$

The boundary [surface integral](../../../../../../../surface-integral.md) is the total outward [flux integral](../../../../../../../flux-integral.md). If the boundary has several components, each has the outward [orientation](../../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) relative to the material of $R$; inner components therefore point into the holes. Edges and isolated vertices may be omitted when assigning the [unit normal vector](../../../../../../../unit-normal.md), since they have zero [surface area](../../../../../../../surface-area.md).
