<h1 id="question-2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

The [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) is

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf F
=\bigl(\partial_yz^2-\partial_z0,\partial_z0-\partial_xz^2,\partial_x0-\partial_y0\bigr)
=\boxed{\mathbf0}.
$$

To establish that $\mathbf F$ is a [conservative vector field](../../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md), construct a [potential of a conservative vector field](../../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) directly:

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y,z)=\frac{z^3}3+C},\qquad
\nabla\phi=(0,0,z^2)=\mathbf F.
$$

This [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) calculation is valid on all of $\mathbb R^3$, and hence also on the [cone](../../../../../../cone-geometry.md). The explicit [potential of a conservative vector field](../../../../../../potential-of-a-conservative-vector-field.md) supplies the required global justification.
