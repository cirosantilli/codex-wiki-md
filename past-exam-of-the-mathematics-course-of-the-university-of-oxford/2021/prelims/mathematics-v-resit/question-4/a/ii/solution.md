<h1 id="question-4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

The theorem on [pointwise convergence of a piecewise smooth Fourier series](../../../../../../../pointwise-convergence-of-a-piecewise-smooth-fourier-series.md) gives the average of the two one-sided values of the corresponding [periodic extension](../../../../../../../periodic-extension.md). Under the usual piecewise smooth interpretation of the paper's derivative assumption, the [even extension](../../../../../../../even-extension.md) is continuous at $0$ and at the joined endpoints $\pm\pi$. Thus

$$
\boxed{C_f(x)=f(x)\quad(0\leq x\leq\pi)}.
$$

For the [odd extension](../../../../../../../odd-extension.md), the values on either side of zero are $-f(0)$ and $f(0)$; those on either side of $\pi$ are $f(\pi)$ and $-f(\pi)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{S_f(x)=
\begin{cases}
f(x),&0<x<\pi,\\
0,&x=0\text{ or }x=\pi.
\end{cases}}
$$

The endpoint values are also immediate because each [sine](../../../../../../../sine.md) term vanishes there. If $f(0)$ or $f(\pi)$ is nonzero, the [Fourier sine series](../../../../../../../fourier-sine-series.md) does not reproduce that endpoint value. Merely assuming that the derivative is locally piecewise continuous in the open interval would be weaker than the usual piecewise smooth hypothesis; the convergence assertion uses the standard endpoint regularity implicit in that hypothesis.
