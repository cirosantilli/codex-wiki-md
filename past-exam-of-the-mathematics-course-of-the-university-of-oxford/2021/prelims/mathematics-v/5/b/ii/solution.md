<h1 id="5/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Eliminate the two pure second derivatives by taking $\alpha,\beta$ to be the [roots](../../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) of $z^2+2Bz+C=0$. They are distinct [real numbers](../../../../../../../real-number.md) exactly when

$$
\boxed{B^2>C,\qquad\alpha=-B+\sqrt{B^2-C},\quad\beta=-B-\sqrt{B^2-C}.}
$$

Thus the required [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is $H(B)=B^2$. The mixed coefficient becomes $4(C-B^2)\ne0$, so the transformed equation is $y_{\xi\eta}=0$. Integrating once in each coordinate gives

$$
\boxed{y(x,t)=F(x+\alpha t)+G(x+\beta t),}
$$

with arbitrary sufficiently smooth [functions](../../../../../../../function-split.md) $F,G$. This is the characteristic form of a strictly [hyperbolic partial differential equation](../../../../../../../hyperbolic-partial-differential-equation.md).
