<h1 id="6/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

Suppose there were only finitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md). There is at least one, namely $2$, so this finite [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) would have a largest element $q$. The [integer](../../../../../../../integer-split.md) $2^q-1$ is greater than $1$ and therefore has a [prime factor](../../../../../../../prime-factor.md) $p$: for example, its least divisor greater than $1$ must be a [prime number](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), since any proper factor of that divisor would contradict minimality.

By [ii](../ii.md), $p>q$, contradicting the choice of $q$. Therefore **there are infinitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md)**, proving [Euclid's theorem](../../../../../../../euclid-s-theorem.md) by [orders of group elements](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) in finite multiplicative [groups](../../../../../../../group-split.md).
