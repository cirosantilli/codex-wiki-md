↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

A smallest example is the [Klein four-group](../../../../../../../klein-four-group.md)

$$
\boxed{C_2\times C_2=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\},}
$$

with coordinatewise [addition](../../../../../../../addition.md) modulo $2$. Every nonidentity element has [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $2$, so none generates all four elements; hence this [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) is not a [cyclic group](../../../../../../../cyclic-group.md).

A [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) of [group order](../../../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) $1$ is a [cyclic group](../../../../../../../cyclic-group.md). A [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) of [group order](../../../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) $2$ or $3$ is also a [cyclic group](../../../../../../../cyclic-group.md): choose a nonidentity element; by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), its [order of a group element](../../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) is a nontrivial divisor of that [prime number](../../../../../../../prime-number-split.md), so equals the whole [group order](../../../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md). Thus no smaller example exists, and **the smallest possible [group order](../../../../../../../order-of-a-finite-group.md) is $4$**.
