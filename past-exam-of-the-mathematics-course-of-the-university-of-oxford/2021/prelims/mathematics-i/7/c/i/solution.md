<h1 id="7/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

A [conjugacy class](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) $C(x)$ is a [singleton](../../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) exactly when $gxg^{-1}=x$ for every $g\in G$, equivalently when $gx=xg$ for every $g$. Thus the [singleton](../../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) correspond exactly to elements of the [center of a group](../../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) $A$, and there are $|A|$ of them.

Every other [conjugacy class](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) has at least two elements. Since the [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) form a [partition of a set](../../../../../../../partition-of-a-set.md) $G$, if $r$ is the number of these remaining classes then $2r\leq|G|-|A|$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{m(G)=|A|+r\leq|A|+\frac{|G|-|A|}{2}
=\frac{|G|+|A|}{2}.}
$$

This [conjugacy class count bound from the center](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class-count-bound-from-the-center.md) needs no classification of the [finite group](../../../../../../../finite-group.md).
