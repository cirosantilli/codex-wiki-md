↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

The [orbit](../../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) of $x$ under [conjugation](../../../../../../../conjugation.md) is its [conjugacy class](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md)

$$
C(x)=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}.
$$

It contains $x$ by using $g=e$, so the [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) cover $G$. If $z=gxg^{-1}$, then $x=g^{-1}zg$, and the [group action](../../../../../../../group-action-split.md) identity shows that conjugating either element further gives the same [orbit](../../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md); hence $C(z)=C(x)$. If $C(x)$ and $C(y)$ intersect at $z$, this gives $C(x)=C(z)=C(y)$. Thus distinct [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) are disjoint and **the [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) form a [partition of a set](../../../../../../../partition-of-a-set.md) $G$**.
