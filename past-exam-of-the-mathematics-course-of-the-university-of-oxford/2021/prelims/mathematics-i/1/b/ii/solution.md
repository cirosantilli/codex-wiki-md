↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Write $C=(c_{ij})$ and $(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(0,2,4)$. Because $D$ is a [diagonal matrix](../../../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md),

$$
(CD)_{ij}=c_{ij}d_j,\qquad (DC)_{ij}=d_ic_{ij}.
$$

Thus $CD=DC$ exactly when $(d_j-d_i)c_{ij}=0$ for every $i,j$. The three [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are distinct, so every off-diagonal entry of $C$ must be zero; diagonal entries are unrestricted. Consequently the [centralizer of a diagonal matrix](../../../../../../../centralizer-of-a-diagonal-matrix.md) here is

$$
\boxed{\{C:CD=DC\}=\{\operatorname{diag}(a,b,c):a,b,c\in\mathbb R\}.}
$$

Every displayed [matrix](../../../../../../../matrix.md) indeed [commutes](../../../../../../../commuting-elements.md) with $D$, since [diagonal matrices](../../../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md) multiply entry by entry on the diagonal.
