<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [b](../b.md)

For $\phi(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i u_i$, its [image of a linear map](../../../../../../image-of-a-linear-map.md) is exactly $\langle u_1,\ldots,u_k\rangle$. Consequently $\phi$ is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) if and only if $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ are a [spanning set](../../../../../../spanning-set.md) for $V$.

If the [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) and $\phi(\alpha)=\phi(\beta)$, then $\sum_i(\alpha_i-\beta_i)u_i=0$, so $\alpha_i=\beta_i$ for all $i$. Hence $\phi$ is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Conversely, if $\phi$ is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md), any relation $\sum_i\alpha_i u_i=0$ says $\phi(\alpha)=\phi(0)$ and thus $\alpha=0$, proving [linear independence](../../../../../../linear-independence.md).

Combining both equivalences gives

$$
\boxed{(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\text{ is a basis of }V\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\phi\text{ is a bijection}.}
$$

A [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) is therefore exactly a choice of [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) giving existence and uniqueness of coordinates, expressed by a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md).
