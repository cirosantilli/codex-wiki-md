<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

For a [singleton](../../../../../../../singleton-mathematics.md) $X$, the [symmetric group](../../../../../../../symmetric-group.md) contains only the [identity function](../../../../../../../identity-function.md). For a two-element [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md), it contains the [identity function](../../../../../../../identity-function.md) and the [transposition](../../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) exchanging the two elements; the latter squares to the [identity function](../../../../../../../identity-function.md). In both cases all elements [commute](../../../../../../../commuting-elements.md), so the [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) is [Abelian](../../../../../../../abelian-group.md).

If $X$ contains three distinct elements $a,b,c$, define the [transpositions](../../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) $\sigma=(a\ b)$ and $\tau=(b\ c)$, fixing every other element of $X$. They are [bijections](../../../../../../../bijection.md), even when $X$ is infinite. With [function composition](../../../../../../../function-composition.md) read from right to left,

$$
(\sigma\circ\tau)(a)=b,\qquad (\tau\circ\sigma)(a)=c.
$$

Thus they do not [commute](../../../../../../../commuting-elements.md), so the [symmetric group](../../../../../../../symmetric-group.md) is not [Abelian](../../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Since $X$ is nonempty, this proves

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sym}(X)\text{ is abelian}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\text{ is finite and }|X|\leq2.}
$$
