<h1 id="5/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

For each $g\in G$, left [translation in a group](../../../../../../../translation-in-a-group.md) $L_g:x\mapsto gx$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md), with [inverse function](../../../../../../../inverse-function.md) $L_{g^{-1}}$. Thus $\theta(g)=L_g$ belongs to $\operatorname{Sym}(G)$. For $g,h,x\in G$, [associativity](../../../../../../../associative-property.md) of the [group operation](../../../../../../../group-operation.md) gives

$$
\theta(gh)(x)=(gh)x=g(hx)=(\theta(g)\circ\theta(h))(x).
$$

Hence $\theta$ is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md). If $\theta(g)=\theta(h)$, evaluation at the [identity element](../../../../../../../identity-element.md) $e$ gives $g=ge=he=h$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\theta:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(G)\text{ is an injective group homomorphism}.}
$$

This is the left regular representation used in [Cayley's theorem](../../../../../../../cayley-s-theorem.md).
