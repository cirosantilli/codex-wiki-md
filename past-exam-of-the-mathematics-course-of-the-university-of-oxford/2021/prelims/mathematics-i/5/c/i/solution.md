<h1 id="5/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

An [injective](../../../../../../../injective-function.md) [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) from a [finite set](../../../../../../../finite-set.md) to itself is [surjective](../../../../../../../surjective-function.md): its [image of a function](../../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) contains as many distinct elements as the entire [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md), so it cannot omit one. Thus every element of $I(X)$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md), and conversely every [bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md) is [injective](../../../../../../../injective-function.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{I(X)=\operatorname{Sym}(X)\quad\text{for finite }X.}
$$

It is a [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) under [function composition](../../../../../../../function-composition.md) by [i](../../a/i.md).
