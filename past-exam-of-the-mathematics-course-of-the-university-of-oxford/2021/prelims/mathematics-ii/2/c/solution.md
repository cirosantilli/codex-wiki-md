<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) does not converge.** For $n=2^k$, the [largest prime factor](../../../../../../largest-prime-factor.md) is $p(n)=2$, while the [sum of prime factors with multiplicity](../../../../../../sum-of-prime-factors-with-multiplicity.md) is $s(n)=2k$. This [subsequence](../../../../../../subsequence.md) therefore has ratio $1/k\to0$.

There are infinitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md): if there were only $q_1,\ldots,q_r$, a [prime factor](../../../../../../prime-factor.md) of $q_1\cdots q_r+1$ would be absent from the list. Listing the [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number-split.md) in increasing order gives a [subsequence](../../../../../../subsequence.md) of indices $n=q_j$ for which $p(n)=s(n)=q_j$, so the ratio is constantly one. Every [subsequence](../../../../../../subsequence.md) of a [convergent sequence](../../../../../../convergent-sequence.md) has the same [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md). The two distinct [subsequential limits](../../../../../../subsequential-limit.md) $\boxed{0\text{ and }1}$ rule out [convergence](../../../../../../convergence.md).
