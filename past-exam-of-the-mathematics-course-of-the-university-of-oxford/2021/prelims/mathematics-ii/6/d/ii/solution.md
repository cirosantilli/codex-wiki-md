<h1 id="6/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**A [maximum](../../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) or [minimum](../../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) can occur at an endpoint.** The rule that a [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes at an extremum requires an interior extremum and [differentiability](../../../../../../../differentiability.md) there. The hypotheses give no two-sided [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) at the endpoints, and even an existing one-sided [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) need not vanish there.

For example, $f(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$ has $m=0$ at $x_m=0$ and $M=1$ at $x_M=1$, but $f'(x)=1$ at every interior point; its endpoint one-sided [derivatives](../../../../../../../derivative.md) are also one. Its graph meets both horizontal bounding lines without being tangent to either. Thus the claimed conclusion about two zeros of the [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) fails.

There is a second logical issue: the argument never establishes two distinct extremizing points. For a [constant function](../../../../../../../constant-function.md), the [maximum](../../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) and [minimum](../../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) coincide and may be represented by the same point. The [constant function](../../../../../../../constant-function.md) does have many [stationary points](../../../../../../../stationary-point.md), but that needs its own argument; it does not follow from counting the same selected point twice. In [Rolle's theorem](../../../../../../../rolle-theorem.md), equal endpoint values ensure an interior extremum when the [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is nonconstant, which is the missing mechanism here.
