<h1 id="6/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [extreme value theorem](../../../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md) gives finite [real numbers](../../../../../../../real-number.md)

$$
\alpha=\min_{x\in[a,b]}f(x),\qquad\beta=\max_{x\in[a,b]}f(x),\qquad\alpha\leq\beta.
$$

Since these values are attained, the [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) of strict [upper bounds](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) in the definition of $M$ is precisely $(\beta,\infty)$, and the [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) of strict [lower bounds](../../../../../../../lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) in the definition of $m$ is precisely $(-\infty,\alpha)$. They are nonempty and bounded on the required sides. By the definitions of [infimum](../../../../../../../infimum.md) and [supremum](../../../../../../../supremum.md),

$$
\boxed{M=\inf(\beta,\infty)=\beta,\qquad m=\sup(-\infty,\alpha)=\alpha,\qquad m\leq M.}
$$
