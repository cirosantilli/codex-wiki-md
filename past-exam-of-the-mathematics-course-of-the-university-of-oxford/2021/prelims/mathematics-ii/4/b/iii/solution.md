<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If there were no interior $x_2$ with $f^s(x_2)\leq0$, the [symmetric derivative](../../../../../../../symmetric-derivative.md) would be positive everywhere in $(a,b)$. Part [ii](../ii.md) would make $f$ [strictly increasing](../../../../../../../strictly-increasing-function.md) there. Choose $a<u<v<b$. [Continuity](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at the endpoints and [monotonicity](../../../../../../../monotonic-function.md) on the interior imply

$$
f(a)\leq f(u)<f(v)\leq f(b),
$$

contradicting $f(a)=f(b)$. Hence such an $x_2$ exists.

If there were no interior $x_1$ with $f^s(x_1)\geq0$, then $(-f)^s=-f^s>0$ everywhere. Apply the same argument to the [continuous function](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $-f$, whose endpoint values are equal. This is again impossible. Thus

$$
\boxed{\exists x_1,x_2\in(a,b):\quad f^s(x_1)\geq0,\quad f^s(x_2)\leq0.}
$$

The two points need not be distinct.
