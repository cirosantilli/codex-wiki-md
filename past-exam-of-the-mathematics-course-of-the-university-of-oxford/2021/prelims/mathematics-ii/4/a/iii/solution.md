<h1 id="4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take any $a<u<v<b$. By [differentiability implies continuity](../../../../../../../differentiability-implies-continuity.md), $f$ is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) on $[u,v]$, and it is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) on $(u,v)$. The [mean value theorem](../../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) gives $c\in(u,v)$ for which

$$
f(v)-f(u)=f'(c)(v-u)\geq0.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{f(u)\leq f(v)\text{ whenever }u<v}$: $f$ is a [nondecreasing function](../../../../../../../nondecreasing-function.md), which is the non-strict meaning of increasing here.
