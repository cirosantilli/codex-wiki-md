↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**True.** Put $\alpha=\sup A$, $\beta=\sup B$ and $u=\max\{\alpha,\beta\}$. These [suprema](../../../../../../../supremum.md) exist by [completeness of the real numbers](../../../../../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md), since the [sets](../../../../../../../set-split.md) are nonempty and bounded. Every [set element](../../../../../../../element-of-a-set.md) of the [union](../../../../../../../set-union.md) $A\cup B$ is at most $u$, so $u$ is an [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Conversely, every [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of $A\cup B$ bounds both $A$ and $B$, and is therefore at least both [suprema](../../../../../../../supremum.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\sup(A\cup B)=\max\{\sup A,\sup B\}.}
$$
