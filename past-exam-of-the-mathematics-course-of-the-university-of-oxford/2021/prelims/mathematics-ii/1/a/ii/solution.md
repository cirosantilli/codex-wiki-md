<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** Write $\alpha=\sup A$ and $\beta=\inf B$. Every $a\in A$, $b\in B$ satisfies $a-b\leq\alpha-\beta$, giving an [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) for the [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) $A-B$ of pairwise differences. For every $\varepsilon>0$, the definitions of [supremum](../../../../../../../supremum.md) and [infimum](../../../../../../../infimum.md) give $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ with

$$
a>\alpha-\varepsilon/2,\qquad b<\beta+\varepsilon/2.
$$

Then $a-b>\alpha-\beta-\varepsilon$. Hence no smaller [upper bound](../../../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) works, and

$$
\boxed{\sup(A-B)=\sup A-\inf B.}
$$

Here $A-B$ means pairwise subtraction, rather than [set difference](../../../../../../../set-difference.md).
