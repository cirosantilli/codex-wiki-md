<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [c](../c.md)

Let $D$ be the filled projected [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md). The part of the [plane](../../../../../../plane.md) bounded by $C$ is the [parametrized surface](../../../../../../parametrized-surface.md)

$$
\mathbf R(x,y)=(x,y,1-x-y),\qquad (x,y)\in D.
$$

Its [tangent vectors](../../../../../../tangent-vector-split.md) satisfy

$$
\mathbf R_x=(1,0,-1),\qquad \mathbf R_y=(0,1,-1),\qquad
\|\mathbf R_x\times\mathbf R_y\|=\|(1,1,1)\|=\sqrt3.
$$

The [surface area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A(C)=\iint_D\sqrt3\,dx\,dy=\sqrt3\frac{8\pi}{3\sqrt3}=\frac{8\pi}{3}.}
$$

This also illustrates the [orthogonal projection area formula](../../../../../../orthogonal-projection-area-formula.md): the [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) to the [plane](../../../../../../plane.md) has vertical component $1/\sqrt3$, so projection multiplies [area](../../../../../../surface-area.md) by $1/\sqrt3$.
