<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [b](../b.md)

Let $A=(Y+Z)/2$, $B=(Z+X)/2$, and $C=(X+Y)/2$, interpreting these expressions as [position vectors](../../../../../../position-vector.md). The resulting [medial triangle](../../../../../../medial-triangle.md) is nondegenerate: for example,

$$
\mathbf b-\mathbf c=\tfrac12(\mathbf z-\mathbf y).
$$

Thus $BC$ is parallel to $YZ$, and the same calculation holds cyclically. The [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) through $A$ is consequently the [line](../../../../../../straight-line.md) through the [midpoint](../../../../../../midpoint.md) of $YZ$ that is [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to $YZ$, namely its [perpendicular bisector](../../../../../../perpendicular-bisector.md). Each remaining [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) similarly gives one of the other two [perpendicular bisectors](../../../../../../perpendicular-bisector.md).

By [iii](../a/iii.md), the [medial triangle](../../../../../../medial-triangle.md) has a unique [orthocenter](../../../../../../orthocenter.md), so **the three [perpendicular bisectors](../../../../../../perpendicular-bisector.md) meet in one [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md)**. Every [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) on the [perpendicular bisector](../../../../../../perpendicular-bisector.md) of $YZ$ is equally far from $Y$ and $Z$: expanding the squares of the two [Euclidean distances](../../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) gives $2\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y)=\|\mathbf z\|^2-\|\mathbf y\|^2$. Applying this to the three sides shows that the common [point](../../../../../../point-geometry.md) is equally far from $X,Y,Z$. It is the [circumcenter](../../../../../../circumcenter.md).
