<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [iii](../iii.md)

The [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) through $A$ has [equation](../../../../../../../equation.md) $(\mathbf a-\mathbf d)\cdot(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)=0$, and the [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) through $B$ has [equation](../../../../../../../equation.md) $(\mathbf b-\mathbf d)\cdot(\mathbf a-\mathbf c)=0$. Their [normal vectors](../../../../../../../normal-vector.md) $\mathbf b-\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf a-\mathbf c$ are [linearly independent](../../../../../../../linear-independence.md) because the [triangle](../../../../../../../triangle.md) is nondegenerate. Consequently these two [lines](../../../../../../../straight-line.md) meet in exactly one [point](../../../../../../../point-geometry.md) $D$.

Substitute its [position vector](../../../../../../../position-vector.md) $\mathbf d$ in the supplied identity. Its first term vanishes by the first [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) [equation](../../../../../../../equation.md), and its second term vanishes by the second [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) [equation](../../../../../../../equation.md). Hence

$$
(\mathbf a-\mathbf b)\cdot(\mathbf c-\mathbf d)=0.
$$

This is exactly the condition that $D$ lies on the [altitude of a triangle](../../../../../../../altitude-of-a-triangle.md) through $C$. **The three [lines](../../../../../../../straight-line.md) therefore meet in a unique [point](../../../../../../../point-geometry.md), the [orthocenter](../../../../../../../orthocenter.md).** This argument includes [right triangles](../../../../../../../right-triangle.md) and [triangles](../../../../../../../triangle.md) whose [orthocenter](../../../../../../../orthocenter.md) lies outside them.
