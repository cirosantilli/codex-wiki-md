<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [ii](../ii.md)

Assume $h\ne0$. The [velocity in polar coordinates](../../../../../../../velocity-in-polar-coordinates.md) is $\dot{\mathbf r}=\dot r\mathbf e_r+r\dot\theta\mathbf e_\theta$. Hence

$$
(\dot{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf e_z)\cdot\mathbf r=r^2\dot\theta=h.
$$

Taking the [dot product](../../../../../../../dot-product.md) of the conserved [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md) with $\mathbf r$ gives

$$
Ar\cos\gamma=m^2h^2-mkr.
$$

Consequently the [polar equation of a conic](../../../../../../../polar-equation-of-a-conic.md) is

$$
\boxed{r(\gamma)=\frac{m^2h^2}{mk+A\cos\gamma}=\frac{mh^2/k}{1+(A/(mk))\cos\gamma}.}
$$

Only [angles](../../../../../../../angle.md) for which the [denominator](../../../../../../../denominator.md) is positive describe the physical trajectory. Its [eccentricity](../../../../../../../eccentricity-of-a-conic.md) is $A/(mk)$, giving an [ellipse](../../../../../../../ellipse.md), [parabola](../../../../../../../parabola.md), or [hyperbola](../../../../../../../hyperbola.md) when this ratio is respectively less than, equal to, or greater than $1$.

If $A=0$, the [angle](../../../../../../../angle.md) $\gamma$ is undefined, but the same [dot product](../../../../../../../dot-product.md) identity gives the [circular orbit](../../../../../../../circular-orbit.md) $\boxed{r=mh^2/k}$. If $h=0$, the trajectory is radial and the displayed division degenerates; for $k>0$, $\mathbf A=-mk\mathbf e_r$ and $\gamma=\pi$ on a fixed ray, so the identity does not determine $r$ as a function of a varying [angle](../../../../../../../angle.md).
