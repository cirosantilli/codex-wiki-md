<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [i](../i.md)

With $\alpha=0$, [a](../../a.md) gives constant $h$, and the [polar coordinates](../../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) satisfies $\dot{\mathbf e}_r=\dot\theta\mathbf e_\theta$, $\mathbf e_r\times\mathbf e_z=-\mathbf e_\theta$. Also $\ddot{\mathbf r}=(F_c/m)\mathbf e_r$. Differentiating the proposed [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md) therefore gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\mathbf A}
&=m^2h\ddot{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf e_z-mk\dot{\mathbf e}_r\\
&=-mhF_c(r)\mathbf e_\theta-mk\dot\theta\mathbf e_\theta\\
&=-mh\left(F_c(r)+\frac{k}{r^2}\right)\mathbf e_\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$

For this to vanish for arbitrary $h$, including nonzero $h$, the [central force](../../../../../../../central-force.md) must satisfy

$$
\boxed{F_c(r)=-\frac{k}{r^2},\qquad V(r)=-\frac{k}{r}+\text{constant}.}
$$

Attraction requires $k>0$. The condition “for any value of $h$” matters: with $h=0$ alone, the [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md) is constant on each radial ray for any [central force](../../../../../../../central-force.md), so that special case would not determine the force law.
