# Point vortex in a wedge

↑ **Parent:** [Line vortex](line-vortex.md)

For a [point vortex](line-vortex.md) of circulation $\Gamma$ in the wedge $0<\theta<\pi/n$, with positive integer $n$, the [conformal map](conformal-map.md) $z\mapsto z^n$ sends the domain to the upper half-plane. An opposite [image vortex](image-vortex-at-a-plane-wall.md) gives the [complex potential](complex-potential-of-a-two-dimensional-potential-flow.md)

$$
w(z)=-\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\log\frac{z^n-c^n}{z^n-\overline c^{,n}}.
$$

Remove the singular self-induced velocity before setting $z=c$. The resulting [complex velocity](complex-velocity.md) is

$$
\frac{d\overline c}{dt}=\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\left(-\frac{n-1}{2c}+\frac{nc^{n-1}}{c^n-\overline c^{,n}}\right).
$$

The finite first term follows from the expansion of $(z^n-c^n)/(z-c)$ at $c$. In polar coordinates this becomes $\dot r=n\Gamma\cot(n\theta)/(4\pi r)$ and $\dot\theta=-\Gamma/(4\pi r^2)$. Therefore $r\sin(n\theta)$ is constant along a [vortex](phase-vortex.md) trajectory. The case $n=1$ is motion parallel to a plane wall, while $n=3$ describes a sixty-degree wedge.
