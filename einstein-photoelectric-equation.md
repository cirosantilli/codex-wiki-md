# Einstein photoelectric equation

↑ **Parent:** [Photoelectric effect](photoelectric-effect.md)

For the ideal single-[photon](photon.md) metal model, [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) gives

$$
h\nu=\Phi+K_{\max},\qquad
K_{\max}=h\nu-\Phi,\qquad
\nu\geq\nu_0=\frac{\Phi}{h}.
$$

Here $h$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md), $\nu$ the incident [frequency](frequency.md), $\Phi$ the [work function](work-function.md), and $K_{\max}$ the largest emitted [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) relative to the nearby [vacuum](vacuum.md) level. An [electron](electron.md) at the highest occupied [energy](energy-split.md) can reach this edge if it escapes without an additional loss. An [electron](electron.md) starting lower or losing [energy](energy-split.md) within the metal emerges below it. The material takes recoil [momentum](momentum-split.md); its recoil [energy](energy-split.md) is neglected in this model.

For an original chosen example, take $\Phi=2.30\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\nu=8.00\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Using $h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$ and the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md) $e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ gives $h\nu=3.308534\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $K_{\max}=1.008534\,\mathrm{eV}$. The ideal [stopping potential](stopping-potential.md) is therefore $1.008534\,\mathrm V$. At $4.00\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ the same model supplies only $1.654267\,\mathrm{eV}$ per [photon](photon.md), so this single-[photon](photon.md) channel is closed. A negative result from $h\nu-\Phi$ means no emission through that channel, not an emitted [electron](electron.md) with negative [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md).
