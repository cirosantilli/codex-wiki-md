<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use **[factor analysis](../../../../factor-analysis.md)** when several observed measurements may reflect fewer unobserved dimensions, such as related [questionnaire](../../../../questionnaire.md) items about planning, prioritization and record keeping. A common-factor model separates shared variation represented by [latent factors](../../../../latent-factor.md) from item-specific variation. [Factor loadings](../../../../factor-loading.md) describe how measurements relate to those [latent factors](../../../../latent-factor.md); [communalities](../../../../communality.md) summarize their shared-variance contributions.

[Exploratory factor analysis](../../../../exploratory-factor-analysis.md) investigates a structure not yet fixed; [confirmatory factor analysis](../../../../confirmatory-factor-analysis.md) evaluates prespecified measurement restrictions, preferably in independent data. Extraction, factor count and [factor rotation](../../../../factor-rotation.md) affect interpretation, so report them and justify the sample and measurement assumptions. A statistically convenient factor solution does not alone establish [construct validity](../../../../construct-validity.md) or demonstrate the existence of a literal psychological mechanism. The distinction from [principal component analysis](../../../../principal-component-analysis.md) is the common-versus-total-variation objective, and from [cluster analysis](../../../../cluster-analysis.md) the modeling of measurement relationships rather than the grouping of cases.
