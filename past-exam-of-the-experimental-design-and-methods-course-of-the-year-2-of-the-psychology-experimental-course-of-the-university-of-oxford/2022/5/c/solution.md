**TODO: the coefficients and their numerical $t$ and $p$ entries cannot be recovered.** The coefficient estimate gives a modeled difference relative to the stated reference coding; its [standard error](../../../../standard-error.md) quantifies uncertainty, and the usual coefficient test uses $t=\widehat\beta/\operatorname{SE}(\widehat\beta)$ for a zero-coefficient [null hypothesis](../../../../null-hypothesis.md). The [p-value](../../../../p-value.md) requires the appropriate reference distribution and [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md). In a conventional R coefficient table an `Estimate` is usually an unstandardized [regression coefficient](../../../../regression-coefficient.md), not automatically a [standardized regression coefficient](../../../../standardized-regression-coefficient.md); the table and model documentation must establish which meaning of $\beta$ is intended. These definitions supply no missing numerical values.
