# Geometric measure theory

↑ **Parent:** [Measure theory](measure-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_measure_theory)

Geometric measure theory uses [measures](measure.md) and [analysis](analysis-split.md) to study geometric objects that need not be smooth. Its measuring rules can distinguish a [curve](curve.md) from its surrounding plane or capture the fine structure of a [fractal](fractal.md). [Hausdorff measure](hausdorff-measure.md) supplies size adapted to an exponent, while [Hausdorff dimension](hausdorff-dimension.md) identifies the exponent at which that size changes character.

The [Cantor set](cantor-set.md) is an elementary starting point: it has zero [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) but positive [Hausdorff dimension](hausdorff-dimension.md). In [Hausdorff dimension of the Cantor set](hausdorff-dimension-of-the-cantor-set.md), explicit small [covers](cover-topology.md) give an upper bound and a distributed [probability measure](probability-measure.md) supplies the matching lower bound. This combination of geometric coverings and analytic control is useful even before more advanced tools for irregular surfaces are introduced.
