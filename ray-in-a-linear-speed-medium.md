# Ray in a linear-speed medium

↑ **Parent:** [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md)

For light speed $c(y)=y>0$, the travel-time integrand is $\sqrt{1+y'^2}/y$. The [Beltrami identity](beltrami-identity.md) gives $y\sqrt{1+y'^2}=R$, so nonvertical rays are semicircles centred on $y=0$. Boundary endpoints at $y=0$ have infinite travel time: along any rectifiable path entering positive height, $ds/y\geq |dy|/y$ and the integral diverges. These semicircles are therefore ideal-endpoint [hyperbolic geodesics](geodesic-in-the-poincare-half-plane-model.md), not finite-time optical minimizers joining zero-speed endpoints.
