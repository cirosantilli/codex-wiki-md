<h1 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Keep the nonzero $z$ of minimal [two-factor tensor rank](../../../../../two-factor-tensor-rank.md) constructed in part (i). For every $a\in A$, its [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) with $a\otimes1$ lies in $J$ and equals

$$
(a\otimes1)z-z(a\otimes1)
=\sum_{i=2}^n(ac_i-c_ia)\otimes d_i.
$$

If this [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) were nonzero, its [two-factor tensor rank](../../../../../two-factor-tensor-rank.md) would be at most $n-1$, contradicting minimality. Thus it is zero. The [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of $d_2,\ldots,d_n$ forces $ac_i=c_ia$ for every $a$ and $i\geq2$. Since $Z(A)=k$, each $c_i$ is a [scalar multiple](../../../../../scalar-multiple.md) of $1$.

But $1,c_2,\ldots,c_n$ are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md). Hence $n=1$, so $z=1\otimes1\in J$. A [two-sided ideal](../../../../../two-sided-ideal.md) containing the multiplicative identity is the entire [ring](../../../../../ring.md); therefore $J=A\otimes_kB$.

We have proved the stronger result that $A\otimes_kB$ is a [simple ring](../../../../../simple-ring.md). It has finite [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $(\dim_kA)(\dim_kB)$, so part (b) proves

$$
\boxed{A\otimes_kB\text{ is a semisimple ring}.}
$$

Part (c)(ii) also gives $Z(A\otimes_kB)=k$, so this proves that a [tensor product of central simple algebras](../../../../../tensor-product-of-central-simple-algebras.md) is again a [central simple algebra](../../../../../central-simple-algebra.md).
