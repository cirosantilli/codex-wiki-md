# Bouwmeester three-photon GHZ experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Photonic GHZ preparation by fourfold coincidence](photonic-ghz-preparation-by-fourfold-coincidence.md)

The 1999 experiment used an approximately $200\,\mathrm{fs}$, $394\,\mathrm{nm}$ pump pulse and a [beta barium borate](beta-barium-borate.md) crystal. Two [polarization-entangled photon pairs](polarization-entangled-photon-pair.md) supplied four photons. A trigger and three other [photodetectors](photodetector.md) selected a [GHZ state](greenberger-horne-zeilinger-state.md) through [fourfold coincidence counting](fourfold-coincidence-counting.md). Narrow [interference filters](interference-filter.md) lengthened the photon coherence time to approximately $500\,\mathrm{fs}$, exceeding the pump duration and suppressing timing information about which pair supplied which photon. The [optical mode matching](optical-mode-matching.md) was essential to coherence, not merely to routing photons.[Printed pp. 1346–1347, figure 1 and equations (1)–(10): pump, filters, indistinguishable alternatives and coincidence conditioning](observation-of-three-photon-greenberger-horne-zeilinger-entanglement-by-dik-bouwmeester-and-others.md)

The observed state was selected by all four detections, which also measured the photons. A trigger alone did not demonstrate a guaranteed surviving three-photon output. This distinction connects [postselection](postselection.md) to what an apparatus actually supplies, rather than identifying a fourfold coincidence with an unconditional source.[Printed p. 1345, paragraph beginning “The main idea”: explicit requirement that both trigger and three output photons be detected](observation-of-three-photon-greenberger-horne-zeilinger-entanglement-by-dik-bouwmeester-and-others.md)
