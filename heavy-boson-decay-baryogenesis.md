# Heavy-boson decay baryogenesis

↑ **Parent:** [Baryogenesis](baryogenesis.md)

Consider equal initial abundances of a heavy [boson](boson.md) $X$ and its [antiparticle](antiparticle.md). Two decay channels with different [baryon numbers](baryon-number.md), for example $X\to uu$ and $X\to\bar d e^+$, have [branching fractions](branching-fraction.md) $r$ and $1-r$. The conjugate channels have [branching fractions](branching-fraction.md) $\bar r$ and $1-\bar r$. Their mean [baryon numbers](baryon-number.md) are $r-1/3$ and $-\bar r+1/3$, so each particle-antiparticle pair produces $r-\bar r$, or $\epsilon$ per decaying particle. Unequal [branching fractions](branching-fraction.md) require [CP violation](cp-violation.md); equal total [decay widths](decay-width.md) are consistent with [CPT symmetry](cpt-symmetry.md). [Baryon number](baryon-number.md) cannot be assigned so that both channels conserve it. An initial abundance decaying outside [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md), after inverse production becomes ineffective, can therefore generate a nonzero [baryon asymmetry](baryon-asymmetry.md). At the microscopic level a decay-rate difference caused by [CP violation](cp-violation.md) requires interference of [decay amplitudes](decay-amplitude.md) with both a relative [weak phase](weak-phase-cp-violation.md) and an [absorptive phase](absorptive-phase-of-a-decay-amplitude.md); complex couplings alone do not guarantee it. Subsequent [baryon-number washout](baryon-number-washout.md) must be ineffective for the produced asymmetry to survive.
