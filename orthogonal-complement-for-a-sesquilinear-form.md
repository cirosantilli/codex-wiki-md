# Orthogonal complement for a sesquilinear form

↑ **Parent:** [Sesquilinear form](sesquilinear-form.md)

For a [Hermitian form](hermitian-form.md) or a [Hermitian form over a quadratic finite field](hermitian-form-over-a-quadratic-finite-field.md), the displayed subspace is the orthogonal complement of $W$. If the form restricts nondegenerately to a finite-dimensional $W$, its invertible Gram matrix supplies, for each $v$, a unique $w_0\in W$ with $B(v-w_0,W)=0$. Consequently $V=W\oplus W^\perp$. When the form on $V$ is nondegenerate, its restriction to $W^\perp$ is too: a radical vector there is orthogonal to both summands and hence is zero.
