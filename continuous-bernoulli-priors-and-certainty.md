# Continuous Bernoulli priors and certainty

↑ **Parent:** [Beta-binomial conjugacy](beta-binomial-conjugacy.md)

With a uniform [prior distribution](prior-probability.md) on a Bernoulli chance $\theta$, $n$ successes yield [posterior density](posterior-density.md) $(n+1)\theta^n$. Integration gives next-trial success [probability](probability.md) $(n+1)/(n+2)$, but the singleton $\theta=1$ retains [probability](probability.md) zero. More generally, ordinary conditioning with a finite integrable [likelihood](likelihood-function.md) and positive finite [normalization constant](normalizing-constant.md) preserves absolute continuity, so a continuous prior does not acquire a new atom at certainty from a finite sample.
