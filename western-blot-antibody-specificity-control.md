# Western blot antibody specificity control

↑ **Parent:** [Western blot controls](western-blot-controls.md)

A known target-containing sample is a positive comparison for [Western blot antibody detection](western-blot-antibody-detection.md). A matched sample with [gene deletion](gene-deletion.md) supplies a negative comparison: disappearance of the proposed band supports the assignment, while a persisting band indicates binding unrelated to the deleted target. Deletion must actually remove the recognized [protein](protein-split.md); changes in other [proteins](protein-split.md) can complicate the comparison. Omitting the [primary antibody](primary-antibody.md) checks binding or background from the [secondary antibody](secondary-antibody.md) and detection reagents, but cannot exclude binding of the [primary antibody](primary-antibody.md) to an unintended [protein](protein-split.md). Independent [antibodies](antibody.md) recognizing different [epitopes](epitope.md) add evidence. [McDonough and others, “Consider the Specificity of the Antibody for the Target”, Figure 2: positive tissue, knockout tissue and antibodies to different termini](considerations-when-quantitating-protein-abundance-by-immunoblot-by-alicia-a-mcdonough-and-others.md)
