# Strictly decreasing function

↑ **Parent:** [Monotonic function](monotonic-function.md)

A [real-valued function](real-valued-function.md) $f:D\to\mathbb R$ is a [strictly decreasing function](strictly-decreasing-function.md) if every pair $x<y$ in its [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) satisfies $f(x)>f(y)$. It is a [nonincreasing function](nonincreasing-function.md) and is [injective](injective-function.md), with a [strictly decreasing](strictly-decreasing-function.md) [inverse function](inverse-function.md) on its [image of a function](image-of-a-function.md). Negating a [strictly increasing function](strictly-increasing-function.md) gives a [strictly decreasing function](strictly-decreasing-function.md): $f(x)=-x^3$ is an example on the [real line](real-line.md), even though its [derivative](derivative.md) vanishes at zero. Equal outputs are forbidden, unlike for a general [nonincreasing function](nonincreasing-function.md).
