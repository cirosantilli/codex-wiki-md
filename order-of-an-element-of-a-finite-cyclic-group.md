# Order of an element of a finite cyclic group

↑ **Parent:** [Finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md)

For a positive [integer](integer-split.md) $n$ and a [congruence class](congruence-class.md) $k$ in the additive [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, the [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md) is

$$
\operatorname{ord}(k)=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,k)}.
$$

Indeed, write $d=\gcd(n,k)$, $n=dn'$ and $k=dk'$. Returning to zero after $m$ steps means $n\mid mk$, equivalently $n'\mid mk'$. Since $n'$ and $k'$ are [coprime](coprime-integers.md), this holds exactly when $n'\mid m$; the least positive such $m$ is $n'$. This argument also covers $k=0$, since $\gcd(n,0)=n$.

For $n=6$, the orders of $0,1,2,3,4,5$ are respectively $1,6,3,2,3,6$. The [translation in a group](translation-in-a-group.md) $x\mapsto x+k$ has the same period from every starting element. Consequently its cycles each have length $n/d$ and there are $d$ of them. They are exactly the [cosets](coset.md) of the [cyclic subgroup](cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by $k$, which explains both the lengths and the number of components in the picture.
