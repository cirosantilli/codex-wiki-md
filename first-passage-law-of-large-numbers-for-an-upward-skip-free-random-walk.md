# First-passage law of large numbers for an upward skip-free random walk

↑ **Parent:** [Upward skip-free random walk](upward-skip-free-random-walk.md)

Suppose an [integer](integer-split.md)-valued [random walk](random-walk-split.md) starts at $S_0=0$, has upward [increments](increment.md) at most one, and satisfies $S_n/n\to\mu>0$ [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md). Write $T_j$ for its [first-passage time](first-passage-time.md) to $j\geq1$. On the event of [almost sure convergence](almost-sure-convergence.md), the walk tends to $+\infty$ and cannot jump over any positive [integer](integer-split.md), so every $T_j$ is finite. The bound $S_n\leq n$ gives $T_j\geq j\to\infty$. Along this subsequence, $j/T_j=S_{T_j}/T_j\to\mu$ on each such [sample path](sample-path.md), proving the displayed reciprocal limit. No [independence](independent-random-variables.md) between the [hitting times](first-passage-time.md) and the path is required. The [strong law of large numbers](strong-law-of-large-numbers.md) supplies the hypothesis for integrable [independent and identically distributed random variables](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) as steps with positive [mean](expected-value-split.md).
