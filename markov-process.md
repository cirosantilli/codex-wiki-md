# Markov process

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process)

A Markov process is a [stochastic process](stochastic-process.md) whose present state contains all the information in its observed past needed to predict its future. Precisely, if $\mathcal F_s$ is the [natural filtration](stochastic-process.md#natural-filtration) generated by observations up to time $s$, then, for $t>s$ and any [measurable set](measure-theory.md#measurable-set) $A$ of states,

$$
\mathbb P(X_t\in A\mid\mathcal F_s)=\mathbb P(X_t\in A\mid X_s).
$$

The equality is between [conditional probabilities](probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) and holds [almost surely](convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). It expresses the [Markov property](markov-chain.md#markov-property): the past and future have [conditional independence](random-variable.md#conditional-independence) given the present. It does not assert [independence](random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of consecutive states. In the sunny/rainy example below, tomorrow's sunny [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) is $0.8$ after sunshine but $0.4$ after rain, so knowing today's state matters.

The memory assumption concerns the chosen [state space](dynamical-systems.md#state-space). If an equipment model's failure [probability](probability-theory.md#probability) depends on its age, recording only “working” or “failed” may lose relevant information; recording age as part of the state can retain it. Similarly, a dependence on two previous observations can sometimes be represented by using their ordered pair as the current state. Whether such a description fits observations is a modeling question.

Transition [Markov kernels](markov-chain.md#markov-kernel) describe the [conditional distribution](probability-theory.md#conditional-distribution) of a state at a later time. Successive kernels and the initial [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) determine every [finite-dimensional distribution](stochastic-process.md#finite-dimensional-distribution), through repeated [conditional probability](probability-theory.md#conditional-probability). The [time-homogeneous Markov property](markov-chain.md#time-homogeneous-markov-property) is an additional assumption: the kernel depends on elapsed time rather than calendar time. The attraction is compression: prediction can proceed from a current state instead of an entire recorded history. [Brownian motion](brownian-motion.md) and [continuous-time Markov chains](continuous-time-markov-chain.md) illustrate that a [Markov process](markov-process.md) need not evolve in discrete time.

## Markov operator

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_operator)

A positive [linear operator](vector-space.md#linear-operator) on bounded [measurable functions](measure-theory.md#measurable-function) that preserves the constant function one. A [Markov semigroup](#markov-semigroup) is a time-indexed family of such operators with the semigroup law.

### Markov semigroup

↑ **Parent:** [Markov operator](#markov-operator)

The transition operators of a time-homogeneous [Markov process](markov-process.md) satisfy $P_{t+s}=P_tP_s$ by the [Markov property](markov-chain.md#markov-property). They preserve constants and positivity. When the generator and function domain permit differentiation, $\partial_tP_tg=L(P_tg)$ is the [Kolmogorov backward equation](stochastic-calculus.md#kolmogorov-backward-equation). [Transition semigroup of a continuous-time Markov chain](continuous-time-markov-chain.md#transition-semigroup-of-a-continuous-time-markov-chain) is the discrete-state special case.

## Invariant probability law of a Markov process

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)

A [probability measure](probability-space.md#probability-measure) is invariant for a [Markov process](markov-process.md) with transition semigroup $P_t$ when starting with that law gives the same law at every later time. Equivalently, $\int P_tf\,d\nu=\int f\,d\nu$ for every bounded measurable function. Invariant laws may be constructed by [weak convergence of probability measures](convergence-of-random-variables.md#weak-convergence-of-probability-measures) of evolved laws when the [Feller semigroup](functional-analysis.md#feller-semigroup) property permits passing the semigroup through the limit, as for the [extremal invariant measures of the contact process](stochastic-process.md#extremal-invariant-measures-of-the-contact-process).

## Affine process

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)

An affine process is a time-homogeneous [Markov process](markov-process.md) whose conditional [moment-generating function](probability-distribution.md#moment-generating-function) is exponential-affine in its starting state. In discrete time one writes $\log\mathbb E_x e^{\theta X_1}=A(\theta)x+B(\theta)$ on the finite [exponential moment](probability-distribution.md#exponential-moment) domain. Continuous-time definitions allow the functions to depend also on elapsed time.

### Affine autoregressive process

↑ **Parent:** [Affine process](#affine-process)

An [autoregressive process of order one](time-series.md#autoregressive-process-of-order-one) with [independent and identically distributed random variables](random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) as innovations is an [affine process](#affine-process) when the innovation [exponential moments](probability-distribution.md#exponential-moment) are finite. Its one-step coefficients are $A(\theta)=a\theta$ and $B(\theta)=b\theta+\psi(\theta)$, where $\psi$ is the innovation [cumulant-generating function](probability-distribution.md#cumulant-generating-function).

### Joint affine transform

↑ **Parent:** [Affine process](#affine-process)

For a scalar discrete-time [affine process](#affine-process), the conditional [cumulant-generating function](probability-distribution.md#cumulant-generating-function) of $\sum_{j=1}^t\theta_jX_j$ is affine in $X_0$. Backward effective parameters $q_t=\theta_t$, $q_j=\theta_j+A(q_{j+1})$ give coefficients $A(q_1)$ and $\sum_jB(q_j)$.

## Doob h-transform

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)

For a killed [Markov process](markov-process.md) and a positive harmonic function $h$, its Doob h-transform weights the path law through a stopping time $\tau$ by $h(X_\tau)/h(X_0)$, with localization when necessary. It describes conditioning on a boundary behaviour of probability zero. In the [complex upper half-plane](complex-analysis.md#upper-half-plane-complex-analysis), $h(z)=\operatorname{Im}z$ conditions killed [planar Brownian motion](brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-motion) to remain in that half-plane.

### Brownian change of measure by a positive space-time harmonic function

↑ **Parent:** [Doob h-transform](#doob-h-transform)

If $h>0$ is smooth and $Z_t=h(X_t,t)$ is the unit-mean density martingale of a locally absolutely continuous probability measure relative to Wiener measure, then $h_t+\frac12h_{xx}=0$. The [Itô formula](stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives $Z=\mathcal E(\int(h_x/h)(X_s,s)dX_s)$ when $Z_0=1$. The true-martingale hypothesis is already supplied by the density process. On each finite horizon the [Girsanov theorem](stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) makes $X-\int(h_x/h)(X_s,s)ds$ Brownian under the new measure. Positivity provides local equivalence; global infinite-horizon equivalence and the Novikov condition are not additional requirements.

### Brownian motion conditioned to exit at a boundary point

↑ **Parent:** [Doob h-transform](#doob-h-transform)

Killed [planar Brownian motion](brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-motion) conditioned to exit at a smooth boundary point $a$ is the [Doob h-transform](#doob-h-transform) by its positive [Poisson kernel](partial-differential-equation.md#poisson-kernel), normalized at the starting point. Its generator is $\tfrac12\Delta+\nabla\log h\cdot\nabla$. Conditioning on shrinking boundary arcs yields the same localized laws and specifies a canonical version of an otherwise probability-zero conditioning event.

#### Radial restriction for a conditioned Brownian path

↑ **Parent:** [Brownian motion conditioned to exit at a boundary point](#brownian-motion-conditioned-to-exit-at-a-boundary-point)

For a Brownian path in the unit disc from $0$ conditioned to exit at $1$, let $\Phi_U:U\to D$ fix $0$ and $1$, and suppose $U$ agrees with the disc near $1$. The probability of avoiding $D\setminus U$ is the ratio of the boundary Poisson-kernel densities, hence $|\Phi_U'(1)|$. Given avoidance, the conformally mapped path has the original law up to the [conformal Brownian clock](brownian-motion.md#conformal-brownian-clock). There is no conformal-radius factor at the interior starting point.

## Hidden Markov model

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Markov_model)

A hidden Markov model is a [Markov process](markov-process.md) whose state is latent and observed only through random emissions whose conditional distribution depends on the current hidden state.

### Emission probability

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

An [emission probability](#emission-probability) in a [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model) is the probability of an observation conditional on its hidden state. For a finite observation alphabet, each state's emission probabilities sum to one. A [match state](#match-state) can therefore emit several different characters, each with its own probability.

### Backward algorithm

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

The [backward algorithm](#backward-algorithm) computes suffix likelihoods in a [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model). With $\beta_t(i)=P(o_{t+1},\ldots,o_T\mid Z_t=i)$, its recurrence is $\beta_t(i)=\sum_j a_{ij}b_j(o_{t+1})\beta_{t+1}(j)$. Together with the [forward algorithm](#forward-algorithm), it gives posterior state probabilities proportional to $\alpha_t(i)\beta_t(i)$. The terminal condition must include any explicit End-state transition.

### Forward algorithm

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

The [forward algorithm](#forward-algorithm) computes sequence likelihoods by summing over all hidden paths through a [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model). For emitting states, $\alpha_t(i)=P(o_1,\ldots,o_t,Z_t=i)$ obeys $\alpha_{t+1}(j)=b_j(o_{t+1})\sum_i\alpha_t(i)a_{ij}$. It reuses partial sums rather than enumerating exponentially many state sequences. Silent states require additional transitions without consuming an observation. Scaling or [log-sum-exp](convex-optimization.md#log-sum-exp-function) prevents [floating-point underflow](floating-point-arithmetic.md#floating-point-underflow).

### Insertion state

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

An [insertion state](#insertion-state) in a [profile hidden Markov model](#profile-hidden-markov-model) emits characters between successive [match states](#match-state). A bypass allows zero inserted characters and a self-transition allows several. Under a fixed loop probability $r<1$, the length of an insertion run conditional on entering has a [geometric distribution](discrete-probability-distribution.md#geometric-distribution) on positive integers with exit probability $1-r$.

### Match state

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

A [match state](#match-state) in a [profile hidden Markov model](#profile-hidden-markov-model) emits one aligned character at a particular profile position whenever it is visited. “Match” names its alignment role, not a requirement to emit one deterministic character or to match another sequence exactly.

### Profile hidden Markov model

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

A [profile hidden Markov model](#profile-hidden-markov-model) represents the position-dependent structure of a [sequence alignment](molecular-genetics.md#sequence-alignment) using an ordered series of [match states](#match-state) and optional [insertion states](#insertion-state), commonly with silent deletion states as well. An emitting state chooses one character from its [probability mass function](probability-distribution.md#probability-mass-function); transitions describe which profile position comes next. If deletion states are forbidden, an alignment column containing gaps cannot be a compulsory [match state](#match-state).

### Screening misclassification model

↑ **Parent:** [Hidden Markov model](#hidden-markov-model)

In a [screening misclassification model](#screening-misclassification-model), true disease states are latent and screen results are emissions. A cancer-free state may emit a positive result with a [false positive rate](statistical-learning.md#false-positive-rate), while a perfectly sensitive pre-clinical state always emits a positive result. Conditional independence of emissions given latent states is an additional model assumption, not a consequence of a marginal false-positive rate.

## Chapman-Kolmogorov equation

↑ **Parent:** [Markov process](markov-process.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman-Kolmogorov_equation)

For a time-homogeneous Markov process with transition kernel $P_t(x,dy)$,

$$
P_{s+t}(x,A)=\int P_s(x,dy)P_t(y,A).
$$

When transition densities exist, this becomes

$$
p(s+t,x,z)=\int p(s,x,y)p(t,y,z)dy.
$$
