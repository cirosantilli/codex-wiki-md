# Bernoulli process

↑ **Parent:** [Stochastic process](stochastic-process-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_process)

A Bernoulli process is a sequence of [independent and identically distributed random variables](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with a [Bernoulli distribution](bernoulli-distribution.md): $\mathbb P(B_n=1)=p$ and $\mathbb P(B_n=0)=1-p$. It models repeated [Bernoulli trials](bernoulli-trial.md) with a fixed success [probability](probability.md) and no dependence between trials. The count $K_n=\sum_{j=1}^nB_j$ has a [binomial distribution](binomial-distribution.md), [expected value](expected-value-split.md) $np$, and [variance](variance-split.md) $np(1-p)$; the first success time has a [geometric distribution](geometric-distribution.md) on $\{1,2,\ldots\}$. The observations $B_n$ and the accumulated count $K_n$ are different [stochastic processes](stochastic-process-split.md): the observations are independent, while consecutive counts share all earlier observations. [MIT, The basics of stochastic processes, section 2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/b9342e718ec4c58d98ec97dcfcb0d56e_MIT6_436JF18_lec20.pdf)
