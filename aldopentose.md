# Aldopentose

↑ **Parent:** [Monosaccharide](monosaccharide.md)

An [aldopentose](aldopentose.md) is a five-[carbon](carbon.md) [aldose](aldose.md): its open-chain reference form has an [aldehyde](aldehyde.md) group at C1. [Ribose](ribose.md) is an example; [deoxyribose](deoxyribose.md) is its 2-deoxy derivative.[McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §25.1: aldose and pentose classification](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/25-1-classification-of-carbohydrates.md)

For the unmodified open-chain [aldopentose](aldopentose.md) skeleton, count one $\mathrm{CHO}$, three $\mathrm{CHOH}$ and one $\mathrm{CH_2OH}$ group: $\mathrm C_5\mathrm H_{10}\mathrm O_5$. The three interior [carbon](carbon.md) positions, C2–C4, are [stereocenters](stereocenter.md). Each allows two configurations, giving $2^3=8$ [stereoisomers](stereoisomer.md), grouped into four [enantiomer](enantiomer.md) pairs. The unlike chain ends prevent a [meso compound](meso-compound.md) identification. D-[ribose](ribose.md) selects the right/right/right [hydroxy group](hydroxy-group.md) pattern in the [Fischer projection](fischer-projection.md); the word [aldopentose](aldopentose.md) alone does not select that pattern. Compare the existing [ribose](ribose.md) and [deoxyribose](deoxyribose.md) diagrams for the separate question of which [oxygen](oxygen.md) is present.
