<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In mixed-valence manganites, mobile $e_g$ electrons hop between sites whose localized $t_{2g}$ core spins are coupled to them by strong [Hund's coupling](../../../../../hund-s-coupling.md). Projecting onto local spin-aligned states gives the [double-exchange mechanism](../../../../../double-exchange-mechanism.md)

$$
\boxed{|t_{ij}^{\rm eff}|=|t|\cos(\theta_{ij}/2).}
$$

Alignment maximizes the hopping bandwidth and lowers carrier kinetic energy, favouring [ferromagnetism](../../../../../ferromagnetism.md). This involves real charge transfer, unlike insulating [superexchange](../../../../../superexchange.md). Heating disorders core spins and reduces coherent transport; a [magnetic field](../../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) aligns spins, increasing mobility and reducing resistivity. The large negative response near the magnetic transition is [colossal magnetoresistance](../../../../../colossal-magnetoresistance.md).

Double exchange supplies the conduction–magnetism link, but its simplest spin-only model is insufficient for the full resistivity change. Lattice distortions, [Jahn-Teller effect](../../../../../jahn-teller-effect.md) and carrier localization can amplify the effect; materials need not obey a universal resistivity-versus-magnetization law.[Double exchange and magnetic conduction](../../../../../interaction-between-the-d-shells-in-the-transition-metals-ii-ferromagnetic-compounds-of-manganese-with-perovskite-structure-by-clarence-zener.md)[1995 abstract: limitations of the spin-only model and electron–phonon coupling](../../../../../double-exchange-alone-does-not-explain-the-resistivity-of-la1-xsrxmno3-by-a-j-millis-and-others.md)
