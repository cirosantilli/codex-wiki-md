The [Hartree-Fock method](../../../../hartree-fock-method.md) minimizes the expectation of an interacting-electron [Hamiltonian](../../../../hamiltonian.md) over normalized [Slater determinants](../../../../slater-determinant.md) of orthonormal one-particle [spin](../../../../spin.md) orbitals. It is a variational, self-consistent mean-field approximation. The determinant enforces fermionic antisymmetry exactly within that class, while each orbital responds to the mean charge [density](../../../../density.md) and a nonlocal exchange operator generated by the occupied orbitals.

For an [electron gas](../../../../electron-gas.md), this accounts for kinetic pressure, the average electrostatic field, same-spin exchange and the associated exchange [hole](../../../../electron-hole.md), and possible [spin polarization](../../../../spin-polarization.md). It does not account for general dynamical [electron correlation](../../../../electron-correlation.md), such as the correlated avoidance of opposite-spin [electrons](../../../../electron.md), and cannot capture all screening or collective fluctuations merely by optimizing one determinant. Uniform plane waves are an additional restriction, not the definition of Hartree-Fock itself.

In the [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) functional, the one-body term is the sum of [electron](../../../../electron.md) [kinetic energy](../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and ionic-potential [energy](../../../../energy-split.md). The direct two-body term is the [Hartree energy](../../../../hartree-energy.md): the classical interaction of the mean [electron](../../../../electron.md) [density](../../../../density.md) with itself. Its factor $1/2$ avoids counting every pair twice. The negative Fock term comes from exchanging the two indistinguishable [electron](../../../../electron.md) labels in the antisymmetric state. For a spin-independent interaction, [spin](../../../../spin.md) [orthogonality](../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) makes this exchange act only between equal [spin](../../../../spin.md) components. For an orbital interacting with itself, direct and exchange contributions cancel exactly; the exchange term removes the spurious one-electron self-interaction. Ionic background terms and their prescribed cancellation must be included consistently with the direct term.
