<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Landau quasiparticles](../../../../../landau-quasiparticle.md) describe the long-lived low-energy excitations of an interacting normal [Fermi liquid](../../../../../fermi-liquid-split.md). Starting from a free [Fermi gas](../../../../../fermi-gas.md), imagine switching on interactions without changing the phase or destroying the one-to-one labelling of low-energy states. The resulting excitation carries the quantum numbers of a dressed particle but has a renormalized [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and interactions with other excitations. Its microscopic [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md) need not resemble a bare [electron](../../../../../electron.md) state. The occupied momenta define a [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md), and the quasiparticle [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) measured from the [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential-split.md) vanishes on that surface.

The [Landau quasiparticle energy functional](../../../../../landau-quasiparticle-energy-functional.md) expresses the change near equilibrium in terms of occupation changes:

$$
\delta(E-\mu N)=\sum_{\mathbf p\sigma}\xi^*_{\mathbf p}\delta n_{\mathbf p\sigma}
+\frac1{2V}\sum_{\mathbf p\sigma,\mathbf p'\sigma'}
f_{\sigma\sigma'}(\mathbf p,\mathbf p')\delta n_{\mathbf p\sigma}\delta n_{\mathbf p'\sigma'}.
$$

Differentiation gives the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) of one quasiparticle in the distorted distribution. This is a phenomenological expansion around the true interacting ground state, not the assertion that its total [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) equals that of independent particles. For an isotropic system, spin-symmetric and spin-antisymmetric components of $f$ are expanded in Legendre polynomials. Multiplication by the appropriate Fermi-surface [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) defines dimensionless [Landau parameters](../../../../../landau-parameter.md) $F_l^s,F_l^a$. The original formulation relates a change in the distribution to the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) shift.[Page 70, quasiparticle interaction and distribution variation](../../../../../on-the-theory-of-the-fermi-liquid-by-lev-davidovich-landau.md)

[Pauli blocking](../../../../../pauli-blocking.md) makes the low-energy excitations sharp. The phase space for a quasiparticle to scatter while creating another particle–[hole](../../../../../electron-hole.md) pair shrinks as the [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md) is approached, giving the regular three-dimensional [low-energy Fermi-liquid quasiparticle lifetime](../../../../../low-energy-fermi-liquid-quasiparticle-lifetime.md)

$$
\tau^{-1}\propto(\epsilon-\mu)^2+(\pi k_BT)^2.
$$

Thus at zero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) its width is small compared with its excitation [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The single-particle spectral function has a pole with [quasiparticle residue](../../../../../quasiparticle-residue.md) $Z$, generally less than one, and an incoherent remainder. In a conventional [Fermi liquid](../../../../../fermi-liquid-split.md) the occupation distribution has a discontinuity of height $Z$ at the [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md), rather than a unit free-particle step.

For an isotropic three-dimensional system, define $N^*(0)$ to include both spins. The quasiparticle [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) determines the low-temperature electronic [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md), $C_V/V=(\pi^2/3)k_B^2N^*(0)T$. In the usual Landau-parameter normalization, the [spin](../../../../../spin.md) response is modified by $1/(1+F_0^a)$ and the [compressibility](../../../../../compressibility.md) by $1/(1+F_0^s)$. In a Galilean-invariant continuum, $m^*/m=1+F_1^s/3$; that particular relation must not be imposed unchanged on a crystal band. A denominator approaching zero signals a possible instability of the assumed normal state.

The same interaction functional and a kinetic equation describe collective [zero sound](../../../../../zero-sound.md) when the excitation frequency exceeds the collision rate. Ordinary [first sound](../../../../../first-sound.md) is the collision-dominated hydrodynamic limit. **The theory organizes thermodynamics, response and transport through a small set of measured effective parameters while explaining why interactions coexist with long-lived fermionic excitations.** It is not applicable without qualification in phases where the Fermi-liquid quasiparticle pole disappears or a gap opens over the relevant [Fermi surface](../../../../../fermi-surface.md).
