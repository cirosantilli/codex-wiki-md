<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

An overlap estimate must use nearest magnetic-ion separations. For the [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md) of Mn, $d_{\rm Mn}=a_{\rm Mn}/\sqrt2=3.147\ \mathrm{\mathring A}$; for the [body-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../body-centered-cubic-lattice.md) of Fe, $d_{\rm Fe}=\sqrt3a_{\rm Fe}/2=2.477\ \mathrm{\mathring A}$. The supplied radial functions have inverse decay lengths $\kappa_Z=Z/(3a_B)$. Their intersite overlap therefore contains approximately $e^{-\kappa_Zd}$, up to polynomial and angular factors. The decisive exponent difference is

$$
\kappa_{\rm Mn}d_{\rm Mn}-\kappa_{\rm Fe}d_{\rm Fe}
=\frac{25(4.45/\sqrt2)-26(\sqrt3\,2.86/2)}{3(0.529)}
\simeq8.99.
$$

The [exponential orbital-tail estimate of exchange](../../../../../exponential-orbital-tail-estimate-of-exchange.md) thus gives a hopping suppression approximately $e^{-8.99}\simeq1.25\times10^{-4}$ before polynomial corrections. Keeping the supplied $Z^{7/2}r^2$ radial prefactor in a midpoint overlap proxy gives

$$
\frac{\phi_{\rm Mn}(d_{\rm Mn}/2)^2}{\phi_{\rm Fe}(d_{\rm Fe}/2)^2}
\sim\left(\frac{25}{26}\right)^7
\left(\frac{d_{\rm Mn}}{d_{\rm Fe}}\right)^4e^{-8.99}
\simeq2.5\times10^{-4}.
$$

This proxy is not an evaluated [hopping integral](../../../../../hopping-integral.md), but confirms that the polynomial factors do not undo the exponential loss. If comparable Coulomb denominators and nonexponential prefactors are assumed, a kinetic-exchange scale proportional to $t^2/U$ is suppressed by roughly $10^{-8}$–$10^{-7}$. Direct intersite Coulomb exchange likewise contains products of the small tails. Coordination-number differences cannot compensate for many orders of magnitude.

By contrast, the measured ordering-temperature ratio supplied in the question is **$122/1043\simeq0.117$**, not an exponentially tiny number. **Within this overlap model, direct Mn–Mn exchange cannot explain MnO's substantial magnetic ordering scale by extrapolating the Fe mechanism.** Another exchange path is required.

This is an order-of-magnitude argument, not an exact relation between an itinerant [Curie temperature](../../../../../curie-temperature.md) and a localized-spin [Néel temperature](../../../../../neel-temperature.md). The hydrogenic functions use the printed bare $Z$ values; actual solid-state orbitals are screened and hybridized. Their literal absolute exchange scales should not be treated as quantitative predictions for Fe or MnO.
