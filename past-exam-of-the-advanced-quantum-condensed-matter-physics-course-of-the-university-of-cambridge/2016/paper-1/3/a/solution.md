A [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is the translation set $\Lambda=\{n_1\mathbf a_1+n_2\mathbf a_2+n_3\mathbf a_3:n_i\in\mathbb Z\}$ generated by [primitive lattice translation vectors](../../../../../primitive-lattice-translation-vector.md). Translating by any member of $\Lambda$ leaves its surroundings equivalent. A [crystal structure](../../../../../crystal-structure.md) requires both this lattice and an atomic basis attached to every lattice point. The basis can lower the symmetry of the bare point lattice; a familiar-looking geometrical arrangement of atoms is not necessarily itself a [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md). For example, the [diamond cubic structure](../../../../../diamond-cubic-structure.md) is a face-centred cubic lattice with an identical-atom basis at fractional positions $(0,0,0)$ and $(1/4,1/4,1/4)$ in conventional cubic coordinates. The displacement between those two basis atoms is not itself a [lattice translation](../../../../../lattice-translation.md).

The seven three-dimensional [crystal systems](../../../../../crystal-system.md) are triclinic, monoclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, trigonal, hexagonal and cubic; the distinct translation types give 14 [Bravais lattices](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md). A conventional cell can contain several lattice points, while a [primitive unit cell](../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) contains one. Simple cubic [primitive lattice translation vectors](../../../../../primitive-lattice-translation-vector.md) can be chosen along the cube edges; for face-centred cubic they can be $(a/2)(0,1,1)$, $(a/2)(1,0,1)$ and $(a/2)(1,1,0)$. Their primitive volume is $a^3/4$, explaining the four lattice points per conventional face-centred cell.

A [crystallographic point group](../../../../../crystallographic-point-group.md) comprises the orthogonal parts of the [crystal](../../../../../crystal.md)'s spatial symmetry operations. It constrains possible rotations, reflections, inversion and their combinations, while forgetting translations. Lattice preservation restricts proper rotational orders in three dimensions to $1,2,3,4,6$; a fivefold axis cannot be a symmetry of a periodic three-dimensional lattice. One quick argument restricts a rotation's integral lattice representation through $1+2\cos\theta$, an integer trace, producing these orders. There are 32 crystallographic point-group types. Examples are the cubic full octahedral group $O_h$ with Hermann–Mauguin symbol $m\bar3m$, the tetrahedral class $T_d$ with symbol $\bar43m$, and the polar tetragonal class $C_{4v}$ with symbol $4mm$.

The [space group](../../../../../space-group.md) retains the full [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md). In [Seitz notation](../../../../../seitz-notation.md) $\{R\mid\mathbf t\}$ acts by $\mathbf x\mapsto R\mathbf x+\mathbf t$, and multiplication is

$$
\{R\mid\mathbf t\}\{R'\mid\mathbf t'\}=\{RR'\mid R\mathbf t'+\mathbf t\}.
$$

Its [translation subgroup](../../../../../translation-subgroup.md) is normal and the quotient is the [crystallographic point group](../../../../../crystallographic-point-group.md). A [screw axis](../../../../../screw-axis.md) combines rotation with translation along the axis; for example, applying a $2_1$ screw twice gives one whole [lattice translation](../../../../../lattice-translation.md). A [glide plane](../../../../../glide-plane.md) combines reflection with a translation parallel to the plane; applying the glide twice likewise gives a translation. These operations can preserve the complete [crystal](../../../../../crystal.md) without their rotation or reflection alone preserving it.

A [symmorphic space group](../../../../../symmorphic-space-group.md) admits an origin and representatives for which the point-group operations have no translation component. A [nonsymmorphic space group](../../../../../nonsymmorphic-space-group.md) has essential fractional translations that cannot all be removed by moving the origin. There are 230 three-dimensional space-group types, distinguished more finely than their [point groups](../../../../../point-group.md). These counts refer to ordinary, nonmagnetic periodic [space groups](../../../../../space-group.md); magnetic and aperiodic generalizations are different classifications.[2016 edition, overview and Part 1 introduction to crystallographic and space-group symmetry](../../../../../international-tables-for-crystallography-volume-a-space-group-symmetry.md)

For concrete comparisons, a monatomic simple-cubic [crystal](../../../../../crystal.md) has [space group](../../../../../space-group.md) $Pm\bar3m$ and [point group](../../../../../point-group.md) $m\bar3m$. Rock salt has a face-centred cubic lattice, a two-species basis and [symmorphic space group](../../../../../symmorphic-space-group.md) $Fm\bar3m$. Diamond has the same cubic [point group](../../../../../point-group.md) and translation-lattice type, but its basis leads to nonsymmorphic $Fd\bar3m$. Replacing the two diamond basis sites by distinct species gives the [zinc blende structure](../../../../../zinc-blende-structure.md), exemplified by [GaAs](../../../../../gallium-arsenide.md), with $F\bar43m$ and [point group](../../../../../point-group.md) $\bar43m$; inversion that would interchange the species is no longer a symmetry. **The lattice specifies translations, the [point group](../../../../../point-group.md) specifies the linear symmetry classes, and the [space group](../../../../../space-group.md) specifies their complete spatial implementation.** These examples show why none alone uniquely describes an atomic structure.
