<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [Mott two-current model](../../../../mott-two-current-model.md) treats the two [spin](../../../../spin.md) populations as parallel conduction channels when [spin-flip scattering](../../../../spin-flip-scattering.md) is weak. Thus $\sigma=\sigma_\uparrow+\sigma_\downarrow$ and $\rho^{-1}=\rho_\uparrow^{-1}+\rho_\downarrow^{-1}$. Light, broad-band carriers conduct efficiently; scattering into narrow $d$-like states relaxes their current. In a simple spin-conserving $s$–$d$ picture,

$$
\tau_\sigma^{-1}\propto|V_{sd,\sigma}|^2D_{d,\sigma}(E_F),\qquad
\sigma_\sigma\simeq\frac{e^2}{3}D_\sigma(E_F)\langle v_\sigma^2\tau_\sigma\rangle.
$$

In [copper](../../../../copper.md), the filled $d$ bands supply little final-state phase space at $E_F$, so both spin channels have low resistivity. In [cobalt](../../../../cobalt.md), the nearly filled majority $d$ band suppresses majority-channel scattering, while minority $d$ states remain available. The stated [iron](../../../../iron.md) reversal corresponds to stronger scattering-weighted majority-channel phase space: its majority $d$ band is not closed as in cobalt. Band filling, exchange splitting, velocities and scattering matrix elements set this [scattering-weighted spin-channel asymmetry](../../../../scattering-weighted-spin-channel-asymmetry.md); majority population alone does not select the better conductor. **The given iron ordering is a material/scattering input, not a universal consequence of the two-current postulate.** Different impurities and interfaces can change the asymmetry.[§2.1, printed p.947: intrinsic transport weights and impurity-dependent asymmetry](../../../../spin-dependent-transport-gmr-and-tmr-by-alain-schuhl-and-daniel-lacour.md)
