<h1 id="2/solution-magnetoresistance">Solution (magnetoresistance)</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Combining the two results gives the [GMR ratio for identical thickness-averaged layers](../../../../gmr-ratio-for-identical-thickness-averaged-layers.md):

$$
R_P=\frac{ab}{2D^2(a+b)},\qquad R_{AP}=\frac{a+b}{8D^2},\qquad
R_{AP}-R_P=\frac{(a-b)^2}{8D^2(a+b)}\ge0.
$$

Thus a field that aligns initially antiparallel moments produces **a resistance decrease**. One spin channel conducts well throughout the aligned stack; in the antiparallel stack both channels sample strong scattering. For the low-resistance-state normalization,

$$
\boxed{\frac{R_{AP}-R_P}{R_P}=\frac{(a-b)^2}{4ab}.}
$$

Normalizing the field-induced drop by $R_{AP}$ instead gives $(a-b)^2/(a+b)^2$. Large channel contrast can produce a large [giant magnetoresistance](../../../../giant-magnetoresistance.md); equal channels give none, and common nonmagnetic scattering dilutes the contrast. This is the spin-dependent alignment effect, distinct from [anisotropic magnetoresistance](../../../../anisotropic-magnetoresistance.md) of an individual magnetic layer.
