<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $D=t_1+t_2$, $a=t_1\rho_{\rm FM}^L+t_2\rho_{\rm NM}$ and $b=t_1\rho_{\rm FM}^H+t_2\rho_{\rm NM}$. In the [CIP thickness-averaged two-current model](../../../../../cip-thickness-averaged-two-current-model.md), assume spin is conserved and electron trajectories sample the layers, so each channel's effective resistivity is the thickness average: $\bar\rho_L=a/D$, $\bar\rho_H=b/D$. This uses averaged relaxation rates with comparable transport weights, neglecting additional interface resistance. Here $\rho_{\rm NM}$ is the per-spin-channel resistivity, twice the measured nonmagnetic bulk resistivity. The stated long [mean free path](../../../../../mean-free-path.md) motivates sampling more than one layer.

A four-layer period has thickness $2D$. For an in-plane square, $R_\sigma=\bar\rho_\sigma L/(2DL)=\bar\rho_\sigma/(2D)$, hence the two parallel spin conductances add:

$$
\boxed{G_P=\frac1{R_{\uparrow,\uparrow}}=2D^2\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right).}
$$

The square's side $L$ cancels. The layers are not being treated as four macroscopic resistors in series along the in-plane current; the averaging is over the traversing carriers' scattering histories.
