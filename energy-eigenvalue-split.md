# Energy eigenvalue

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md)

An energy eigenvalue is an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) of the [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) $H$: there is a nonzero [vector](vector.md) $\psi$ in its [operator domain](operator-domain.md) $D(H)$ with

$$
H\psi=E\psi.
$$

For a [self-adjoint operator](self-adjoint-operator.md), $E$ is real. Normalizing $\psi$ produces an [energy eigenstate](energy-eigenstate.md); an ideal [energy measurement](energy-measurement.md) then returns $E$ with probability one. The associated [energy eigenspace](energy-eigenspace.md) includes all vectors with this same [energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue-split.md), including the zero vector, which represents no [quantum state](quantum-state.md). Different [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md) have orthogonal [energy eigenspaces](energy-eigenspace.md). [Teschl, Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics, Lemma 2.20](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-schroe/schroe.pdf).

The [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md) form the [point spectrum](point-spectrum.md), which can be only part of the [spectrum](spectrum-functional-analysis.md). The term [discrete spectrum](discrete-spectrum.md) more specifically means isolated [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) of finite multiplicity. The usual fixed-nucleus [hydrogen atom](hydrogen-atom.md) model has discrete negative [bound state](bound-state.md) [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md) together with a continuum at nonnegative [energy](energy-split.md). [Teschl, Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics, sections 6.4 and 10.2](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-schroe/schroe.pdf). A [quantum system](quantum-system-split.md) need not have a ladder of discrete [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md): a whole-line [free particle](free-particle.md) has [continuous spectrum](continuous-spectrum.md). A [plane wave](plane-wave.md) used for a [free particle](free-particle.md) is a generalized [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) of the [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md), rather than a normalized vector in the physical [Hilbert space](hilbert-space-split.md). [Barton Zwiebach, MIT 8.05 Wave Mechanics, sections 2 and 4](https://live.ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/61bc31b8d8bf0680c322733910a71aa0_MIT8_05F13_Chap_01.pdf).

For the [infinite square well](infinite-square-well.md) $0<x<L$, the [Dirichlet boundary conditions](dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) give

$$
\phi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad E_n=\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2 n^2}{2mL^2},\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.
$$

Substitution into $H\phi_n=E_n\phi_n$ and [wavefunction normalization](wavefunction-normalization.md) verify these statements directly. Here $E_1$ is the [ground-state energy](ground-state-energy.md), even though its index is one; the labeling convention does not determine which [quantum state](quantum-state.md) is lowest. Equal populations of $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ have [energy](energy-split.md) [expectation value](expectation-value.md) $(E_1+E_2)/2=5E_1/2$, but an [energy measurement](energy-measurement.md) returns $E_1$ or $4E_1$, each with probability $1/2$. An average of allowed [energy eigenvalues](energy-eigenvalue-split.md) need not itself be an [energy eigenvalue](energy-eigenvalue-split.md).
