<h1 id="kelvin-s-circulation-theorem">Kelvin's circulation theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Circulation (physics)](circulation-physics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin's_circulation_theorem)

In an inviscid barotropic fluid subject to conservative body force, the [circulation](circulation-physics.md) of a material loop is constant. For constant density, parameterize that loop by $\mathbf r(s,t)$ with $\mathbf r_t=\mathbf u$. Differentiating $\Gamma=\int\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf r_s\,ds$ gives an integral of $(D\mathbf u/Dt)\cdot d\mathbf r$ plus $\int\partial_s(|\mathbf u|^2/2)ds$. The latter vanishes on a closed loop, and the [Euler equations](euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md) make the former the integral of a gradient, also zero. For a barotropic density, $\nabla p/\rho$ is still a gradient of specific enthalpy. Constant circulation is consistent with changing local [vorticity](vorticity.md) when a vortex tube changes its cross-sectional area.
