# Small nutation about a conical pendulum

↑ **Parent:** [Spherical pendulum](spherical-pendulum.md)

For a [spherical pendulum](spherical-pendulum.md) with nonzero conserved vertical [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) $h$, eliminate the azimuthal [angular velocity](angular-velocity.md) using $h=ml^2\sin^2\theta\dot\phi$. The polar [effective potential](effective-potential.md) is $V_{\mathrm{eff}}=h^2/(2ml^2\sin^2\theta)-mgl\cos\theta$. A [conical pendulum](conical-pendulum.md) at $0<\theta_0<\pi/2$ is a minimum satisfying $h^2=m^2gl^3\sin^4\theta_0/\cos\theta_0$. [Linearization](linearization.md) at fixed $h$ yields polar [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\omega_\theta^2=V_{\mathrm{eff}}''(\theta_0)/(ml^2)=\Omega^2(1+3\cos^2\theta_0)$, where $\Omega^2=g/(l\cos\theta_0)$ is the conical revolution frequency squared. Holding $\dot\phi$ fixed during the perturbation would describe a different, externally driven constraint. The polar perturbation is a [nutation](nutation.md); the exactly planar $h=0$ limit requires separate treatment.
