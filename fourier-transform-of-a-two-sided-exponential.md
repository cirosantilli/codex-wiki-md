# Fourier transform of a two-sided exponential

↑ **Parent:** [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md)

For $a>0$, use the [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) convention $\widehat f(s)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-isx}\,dx$. Splitting the integral at zero gives $1/(a+is)+1/(a-is)=2a/(a^2+s^2)$. By the [Fourier inversion theorem](fourier-inversion-theorem.md), the dual identity is

$$
\mathcal F\!\left(\frac1{a^2+x^2}\right)(s)=\frac\pi a e^{-a|s|}.
$$

This pair connects an [exponential function](exponential-function.md) with the kernel of a [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) and produces the [Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane](poisson-kernel-for-the-upper-half-plane.md) by inversion of $e^{-y|s|}$.
