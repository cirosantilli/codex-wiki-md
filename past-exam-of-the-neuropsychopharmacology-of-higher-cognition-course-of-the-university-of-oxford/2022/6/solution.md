**Correlative and causal approaches answer different questions, and neither is dispensable for understanding [neuromodulation](../../../neuromodulation.md).** An [intervention](../../../treatment.md) is generally stronger evidence about the consequence of changing a specified target, while observation is essential for discovering the naturally occurring signal, its heterogeneity and the contexts in which it appears. Usefulness depends on the claim, measurement and assumptions, not simply on whether an experiment uses an optogenetic actuator.

A [correlation](../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) between a neuromodulatory signal and behaviour can arise because the signal influences the behaviour, behaviour changes the signal, or a third variable influences both. Reward expectation, movement and [arousal](../../../arousal.md) often covary. Temporal precedence helps reject some accounts but does not eliminate an unmeasured common cause. Detailed task manipulations can nevertheless make correlative evidence much more discriminating: varying expectation while holding delivered outcome constant tests a different hypothesis from merely comparing rewarded and unrewarded trials.

The [dopamine](../../../dopamine.md) prediction-error account illustrates this value. Patterns across unexpected rewards, predictive cues and omitted expected rewards constrained an explicit computational hypothesis. A generic rise in activity at a reward would not distinguish reward receipt, expectation and error. Recording before intervening helped identify the timing and task conditions in which a putative teaching signal should be manipulated. Without that information, a stimulation experiment might show that a pathway can change behaviour while leaving the proposed natural computation largely unspecified.

Observations also reveal what population averages hide. Cell-resolved [dopamine](../../../dopamine.md) imaging showed structured sensory, motor and cognitive heterogeneity during a navigation task. Comparing identified [dopamine](../../../dopamine.md) spiking with accumbens release revealed that evolving release dynamics need not mirror recorded somatic activity. These findings constrain the target and measurement level of subsequent [interventions](../../../treatment.md). They are not failures of observation: a perturbation of an entire heterogeneous population could obscure precisely the functional distinctions that recording discovers.[Abstract and cell-resolved encoding analyses: heterogeneous task-variable relationships](../../../specialized-coding-of-sensory-motor-and-cognitive-variables-in-vta-dopamine-neurons-by-ben-engelhard-and-others.md)[Abstract and Figures 2–5: cell-body spiking versus local release](../../../dissociable-dopamine-dynamics-for-learning-and-motivation-by-ali-mohebi-and-others.md)

[Interventions](../../../treatment.md) can then test a well-specified causal prediction. Reward-timed activation of [dopamine](../../../dopamine.md) [neurons](../../../neuron-split.md) overcame associative blocking, changing subsequent cue [learning](../../../learning.md); brief inhibition supported a negative-error-like [learning](../../../learning.md) effect. These manipulations are stronger evidence for a teaching contribution than a [correlation](../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) alone. Yet the manipulated population and timing must match the proposed endogenous signal, and later unstimulated testing is needed to separate acquired [learning](../../../learning.md) from immediate performance effects. Sufficiency to impose an effect is not proof that the artificial activity pattern occurs naturally or is the only possible teaching route.[Abstract and Figures 1–4: temporally targeted manipulation and subsequent associative learning](../../../a-causal-link-between-prediction-errors-dopamine-neurons-and-learning-by-elizabeth-e-steinberg-and-others.md)[Abstract and Results: brief inhibition and later learning effects](../../../brief-optogenetic-inhibition-of-dopamine-neurons-mimics-endogenous-negative-reward-prediction-errors-by-chun-yun-chang-and-others.md)

Claims of [functional necessity and sufficiency](../../../functional-necessity-and-sufficiency.md) are conditional. Selective inhibition that impairs performance can support necessity under the tested conditions, but may act through sensation, movement or [motivation](../../../motivation.md) rather than the hypothesized computation. A null effect can reflect redundant circuits, incomplete [target engagement](../../../target-engagement.md) or compensation. Conversely, stimulation can recruit an unnatural synchrony, reverse-propagating activity or multiple collateral targets. Changing [neurons](../../../neuron-split.md) identified by one transmitter can also change [co-transmission](../../../co-transmission.md), so the [intervention](../../../treatment.md) is not automatically selective for that chemical signal at one [synapse](../../../synapse.md).

Pharmacological perturbations make similar assumptions visible. A systemic drug affects [receptors](../../../receptor-biochemistry.md) according to exposure and selectivity, not according to the experimenter’s functional label. Time of testing, peripheral actions, active metabolites and [off-target drug effects](../../../off-target-drug-effect.md) matter. The chemogenetic ligand clozapine-N-oxide provides a concrete example: receptor-binding and distribution work showed that conversion to clozapine can account for central [DREADD](../../../dreadd.md) activation. Thus an apparent designer-receptor [intervention](../../../treatment.md) cannot be assumed to have the intended pharmacological isolation merely because the [receptor](../../../receptor-biochemistry.md) was genetically targeted. Ligand-only controls and exposure measurements improve the causal interpretation.[Abstract and Figures 1–4: ligand conversion, brain entry and receptor occupancy](../../../chemogenetics-revealed-dreadd-occupancy-and-activation-via-converted-clozapine-by-juan-l-gomez-and-others.md)

Measurement is therefore required inside causal experiments as well as before them. Histology can confirm targeting but not the actual moment-to-moment physiological effect; [electrophysiology](../../../electrophysiology.md) or transmitter measurements can test [target engagement](../../../target-engagement.md). Calcium signals, [fibre photometry](../../../fibre-photometry.md), voltammetry and spikes have different spatial, temporal and chemical limitations. Converging methods are more persuasive when they agree at their common resolution, while disagreements can identify local regulation or a mistaken measurement model rather than simply experimental failure.

[Causal mediation](../../../causal-mediation.md) is a further step. Randomizing a drug can establish an effect of the assigned treatment, but correlating a post-treatment neural signal with symptom change does not prove that signal mediated the effect. Both may reflect another drug consequence. Likewise, statistically controlling for movement can remove a confound in one causal model but remove a genuine intermediate mechanism in another. A causal diagram and an explicit target estimand should guide adjustment rather than a rule to regress out every variable associated with behaviour.

Human and animal approaches have complementary scope. Fine cellular [interventions](../../../treatment.md) can provide internal validity in a restricted animal task, while human longitudinal observations can test whether candidate abnormalities precede illness and whether the same behavioural dimensions matter clinically. Ethical and practical constraints preclude arbitrary human lesions or perfectly selective transmitter manipulation. It would be unhelpful to discard clinically relevant observation for failing to perform an impossible experiment; equally, a prospective association should not be relabelled as a proven biological cause.

The most productive programme iterates among formal models, naturalistic measurements and selective perturbations. Specify the computation, identify its predicted signal, vary task factors that distinguish alternatives, and intervene with appropriate timing, targeting and performance controls. Re-test the resulting behavioural and neural predictions across contexts. Causal experiments generally have an advantage for a precise [intervention](../../../treatment.md) question, but correlative approaches remain essential for deciding what should be changed and what the causal result actually explains.
