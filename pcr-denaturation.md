# PCR denaturation

↑ **Parent:** [PCR thermal cycle](pcr-thermal-cycle.md)

[PCR denaturation](pcr-denaturation.md) is the high-[temperature](temperature-split.md) stage that separates a [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) duplex into single-stranded templates for the next copying step. It disrupts the pairing and stacking arrangement of [nucleobases](nucleotide-base.md); it does not require cutting the [phosphodiester bonds](phosphodiester-bond.md) along either strand. The distinction matters: breaking the backbone would damage the ordered [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md) that is meant to serve as a template.

For an original eight-[base pair](base-pair.md) duplex, complete [PCR denaturation](pcr-denaturation.md) exposes two eight-[nucleotide](nucleotide.md) strands. It leaves sixteen [nucleotides](nucleotide.md) altogether: separation has made the templates accessible without yet making more [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md). [PCR extension](pcr-extension.md), rather than heating alone, adds the new material. Already copied duplexes also need to separate at the beginning of subsequent cycles.

Separation is a condition-dependent process. Template composition and the [buffer solution](buffer-solution.md) affect the required [temperature](temperature-split.md) and duration; heating longer is not automatically better because prolonged heating can reduce [DNA polymerase](dna-polymerase.md) activity. Incomplete separation leaves fewer accessible templates even when the [PCR primers](pcr-primer.md) themselves are well chosen.[Template DNA denaturation assessment, paragraphs 1 and 3–6; DNA denaturation considerations, paragraph 1](pcr-cycling-parameters-six-key-considerations-for-success-by-thermo-fisher-scientific.md)
