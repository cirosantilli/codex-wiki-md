# Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre](stellar-orbits-at-the-galactic-centre.md)

Consider a negligible-[mass](mass.md) [star](star.md) moving in the stationary [Newtonian gravitational potential](newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) of a point mass. Let its [semi-major axis](semi-major-axis.md) be $a$, [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md) $0\le e<1$, and [orbital period](orbital-period.md) $P$. Write $\mu=GM$ for the [gravitational parameter](standard-gravitational-parameter.md). The [eccentric anomaly](eccentric-anomaly.md) $E$ is determined by [Kepler's equation](kepler-s-equation.md),

$$
E-e\sin E=\frac{2\pi(t-\tau)}P,
$$

where $\tau$ is the time of [periapsis](periapsis.md). Coordinates in the orbital plane, with the point mass at the origin, are

$$
\mathbf r'(E)=a\begin{pmatrix}\cos E-e\\\sqrt{1-e^2}\sin E\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\dot{\mathbf r}'(E)=\frac{2\pi a}{P(1-e\cos E)}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin E\\\sqrt{1-e^2}\cos E\\0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The focus is the central mass; the ellipse's geometric centre is displaced by $-ae$ along the first coordinate. The varying denominator makes the [star](star.md) move faster near [periapsis](periapsis.md), even though the [mean anomaly](mean-anomaly.md) increases uniformly.
