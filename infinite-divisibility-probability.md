# Infinite divisibility (probability)

↑ **Parent:** [Probability distribution](probability-distribution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_divisibility_(probability))

A [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) is infinitely divisible if, for every positive integer $n$, it is the distribution of a sum of $n$ [independent and identically distributed](independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) [random variables](random-variable-split.md). Their common distribution may depend on $n$. A [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) has this property by dividing its [mean](expected-value.md) and [variance](variance-split.md) by $n$; a [Poisson distribution](poisson-distribution.md) has it by dividing its rate. The unit-time law of a [Lévy process](levy-process.md) is infinitely divisible because it is the sum of its increments on $n$ equal time intervals.
