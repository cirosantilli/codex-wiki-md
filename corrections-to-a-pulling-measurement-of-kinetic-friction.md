# Corrections to a pulling measurement of kinetic friction

↑ **Parent:** [Coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md)

For a block sliding right on a stationary horizontal plane, take rightward [acceleration](acceleration.md) $a$ as positive. A gauge reading $F$ for a pull at [angle](angle.md) $\alpha$ above horizontal has horizontal component $F\cos\alpha$ and upward component $F\sin\alpha$. With no vertical [acceleration](acceleration.md) or other [forces](force-split.md), [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) gives

$$
N=mg-F\sin\alpha,\qquad F\cos\alpha-|f_k|=ma,
$$

and therefore

$$
\mu_k=\frac{F\cos\alpha-ma}{mg-F\sin\alpha}.
$$

This inference requires continued contact with $N>0$ and rightward [contact slip](contact-slip.md), so its numerator must be nonnegative. An upward pull reduces [normal force](normal-force.md); a downward pull, represented by $\alpha<0$, increases it. The horizontal constant-[velocity](velocity.md) formula is the special case $\alpha=0$, $a=0$.

For the original $2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\mu_k=0.30$ model, an upward pull at $\alpha=30^\circ$ maintaining constant [velocity](velocity.md) needs

$$
F=\frac{\mu_kmg}{\cos\alpha+\mu_k\sin\alpha}\simeq5.7873\,\mathrm N,\qquad N\simeq16.7064\,\mathrm N.
$$

Dividing this gauge reading by $mg$ would give about $0.2953$, rather than $0.30$: the gauge reads the full inclined [force](force-split.md), and the pull has changed the [normal force](normal-force.md).

Even with a horizontal pull, an accelerating measurement needs the $ma$ correction: $\mu_k=(F-ma)/(mg)$. In the same model, a horizontal $7.88\,\mathrm N$ pull produces $a=1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ while the block slides right; subtracting $ma=2\,\mathrm N$ recovers the $5.88\,\mathrm N$ [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md). Dividing the uncorrected reading by $19.6\,\mathrm N$ instead gives about $0.4020$. Measure [acceleration](acceleration.md) in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), along the sliding direction, over the same interval as the gauge reading. These corrections follow from the [force](force-split.md) balance, independently of whether the resulting [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) is constant across runs.
