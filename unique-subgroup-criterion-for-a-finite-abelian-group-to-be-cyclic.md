# Unique-subgroup criterion for a finite abelian group to be cyclic

↑ **Parent:** [Finite abelian group](finite-abelian-group.md)

A [finite abelian group](finite-abelian-group.md) is a [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) if and only if it has at most one [subgroup](subgroup.md) of each order dividing its order. A [cyclic group](cyclic-group.md) $C_n$ has exactly one subgroup of order $d$ for each $d\mid n$, generated by the $n/d$th power of a generator.

Conversely, the [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) expresses $A$ as $C_{d_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{d_s}$ with $2\leq d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_s$. If $s\geq2$, choose a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) $p\mid d_1$. The first and second summands then provide distinct subgroups of order $p$, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence $s\leq1$, so $A$ is cyclic, including the trivial group when $s=0$. This criterion combines with the [root bound for a polynomial](lagrange-root-bound-over-a-field.md) to prove that a [finite subgroup of a field multiplicative group is cyclic](finite-subgroup-of-a-field-multiplicative-group-is-cyclic.md).
