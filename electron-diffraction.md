# Electron diffraction

↑ **Parent:** [Diffraction](diffraction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_diffraction)

[Electron diffraction](electron-diffraction.md) is [interference](interference-wave-propagation.md) between scattered [electron](electron.md) [probability amplitudes](probability-amplitude.md). It connects the [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) of the incident beam to the arrangement of the scattering material. In [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) from a fixed crystal, $|\mathbf k_{\rm out}|=|\mathbf k_{\rm in}|=2\pi/\lambda$, and constructive [crystal scattering](crystal-scattering.md) selects [wavevector](wavevector.md) transfers $\mathbf Q=\mathbf k_{\rm out}-\mathbf k_{\rm in}$ in the [reciprocal lattice](reciprocal-lattice.md). The physical [momentum transfer](momentum-transfer.md) to the [electron](electron.md) is $\hbar\mathbf Q$ in this convention.

[Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md) expresses the same condition as $2d\sin\theta_B=n\lambda$, where $d$ is the spacing of the chosen lattice planes, $n$ the [diffraction order](diffraction-order.md), and $\theta_B$ the angle to those planes. The angle between incoming and outgoing propagation directions is $\chi=2\theta_B$ for that reflection geometry. In normal-incidence backscattering, a detector's angle $\phi$ from the outward surface normal instead gives $\chi=\pi-\phi$; neither detector colatitude nor angle to the surface normal should silently replace $\theta_B$. A [finite-row electron diffraction model](finite-row-electron-diffraction-model.md) uses a different spacing, the spacing between sites along a surface row, and makes that distinction explicit.
