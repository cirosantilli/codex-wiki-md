# Derivation of an algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Derivation into a bimodule](derivation-into-a-bimodule.md)

For an [associative algebra](associative-algebra-split.md) $A$ over a [field](field.md) $k$, a derivation is a $k$-[linear map](linear-map.md) $D:A\to A$ satisfying $D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)$. It is the case $M=A$ of a [derivation into a bimodule](derivation-into-a-bimodule.md); the order of factors matters when $A$ is noncommutative. Substituting $a=b=1$ gives $D(1)=0$. Such maps form a [Lie algebra](lie-algebra-split.md) under the [commutator](commutator.md) $[D,E]=D\circ E-E\circ D$.

More generally, for a [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md) $R$, a derivation from a commutative $R$-[algebra](algebra-split.md) $B$ into a $B$-[module](module-mathematics.md) $M$ is an $R$-[linear map](linear-map.md) $D:B\to M$ with $D(r)=0$ for $r\in R$ and $D(bb')=bD(b')+b'D(b)$. The second term has this order because the [algebra](algebra-split.md) is commutative. [Derivations of smooth functions are vector fields](derivations-of-smooth-functions-are-vector-fields.md) provide a geometric example.
