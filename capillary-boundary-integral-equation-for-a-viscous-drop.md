# Capillary boundary integral equation for a viscous drop

↑ **Parent:** [Boundary integral representation of Stokes flow](boundary-integral-representation-of-stokes-flow.md)

For a drop with internal [viscosity](dynamic-viscosity.md) $\lambda\mu$, exterior viscosity $\mu$, continuous velocity and a decaying exterior [Stokes flow](stokes-flow-split.md), use the drop-outward normal $n$ and curvature $\kappa=\nabla_s\cdot n$, twice the [mean curvature](mean-curvature.md). Constant [surface tension](surface-tension.md) gives $(\sigma^+-\sigma^-)n=\gamma\kappa n$. Multiply the interior boundary representation by $\lambda$ and add it to the exterior one: the traction difference yields the single-layer term shown, while the viscosity-independent stress kernel yields $(\lambda-1)D[u]$. Here $S,D$ denote the kernels in [boundary integral representation of Stokes flow](boundary-integral-representation-of-stokes-flow.md). For equal viscosities the double layer vanishes.
