# Finite iterated integrals without joint integrability

↑ **Parent:** [Fubini's theorem](fubini-s-theorem.md)

Define the displayed [function](function-split.md) away from the origin and set $f(0,0)=0$. Every section in either coordinate is integrable on $[-1,1]$ and is an [odd function](odd-function.md), so both orders of [iterated integrals](iterated-integral.md) on $[-1,1]^2$ are zero. However, on the annulus $\varepsilon<\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<1$, [polar integration](polar-coordinates.md) gives

$$
\int|f|=\int_\varepsilon^1r^{-3}\,dr\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos\theta\sin\theta|\,d\theta=\varepsilon^{-2}-1.
$$

Thus the [function](function-split.md) is not [Lebesgue integrable](lebesgue-integrable-function.md) jointly. Reflection interchanges its [positive part](positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md) and [negative part](negative-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md), whose [integrals](integral-split.md) are both infinite. Existence and even agreement of finite [iterated integrals](iterated-integral.md) do not establish the [absolute integrability](integrability.md) hypothesis of [Fubini's theorem](fubini-s-theorem.md).
