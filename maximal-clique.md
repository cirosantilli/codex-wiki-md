# Maximal clique

↑ **Parent:** [Clique (graph theory)](clique-graph-theory.md)

A [maximal clique](maximal-clique.md) is a [clique](clique-graph-theory.md) not strictly contained in another [clique](clique-graph-theory.md). This is distinct from a [maximum clique](maximum-clique.md), whose size equals the [clique number](clique-number.md). In a triangulated [probabilistic graphical model](probabilistic-graphical-model.md), [maximal cliques](maximal-clique.md) are the natural nodes of a [junction tree](junction-tree.md).

Maximality is a condition on [set inclusion](set-inclusion.md): no outside [vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) is adjacent to every member of the current [clique](clique-graph-theory.md). In the five-[vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) example, $\{c,d\}$ is maximal because $a,b$ are not adjacent to $d$, while $e$ is not adjacent to $c$. Yet $\{a,b,c\}$ is larger. Checking every possible one-[vertex](vertex-graph-theory.md) extension is enough: if a larger containing [clique](clique-graph-theory.md) existed, any one of its new [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) could already be added.[§1.1, printed p. 4 of the third electronic edition (2005): maximal sets refer to inclusion](graph-theory-by-reinhard-diestel.md)
