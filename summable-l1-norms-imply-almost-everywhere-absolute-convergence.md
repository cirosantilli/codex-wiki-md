# Summable L1 norms imply almost-everywhere absolute convergence

↑ **Parent:** [L1 space](l1-space.md)

Suppose measurable $a_n$ on a [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) $E$ satisfy $\sum_n\int_E|a_n|<\infty$. The nonnegative partial sums $S_N=\sum_{n\leq N}|a_n|$ increase to a measurable extended-valued $S$. The [monotone convergence theorem](monotone-convergence-theorem.md) gives $\int_ES=\sum_n\int_E|a_n|<\infty$, forcing $S<\infty$ [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). Each $a_n$ is finite almost everywhere too; discard the union of these countably many [null sets](null-set.md) when defining signed partial sums. The [series](series-mathematics.md) $\sum_na_n$ then converges absolutely outside a null set.
