# Hydraulic head

↑ **Parent:** [Darcy law](darcy-law.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_head)

[Hydraulic head](hydraulic-head.md) is the sum of elevation and pressure head, measured in metres relative to a chosen height datum. For constant [mass density](density.md) $\rho$ and [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) magnitude $g$, the pressure head $p/(\rho g)$ is the height of a column of the same [fluid](fluid.md) supported by [fluid pressure](fluid-pressure.md) $p$. A water-filled open measuring tube records [hydraulic head](hydraulic-head.md) as the elevation of its water level when $p$ is gauge [fluid pressure](fluid-pressure.md). Changing the common pressure reference or height datum adds a constant to [hydraulic head](hydraulic-head.md) and leaves its driving gradient unchanged.

For [water](water.md) with $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, a point at $z=2\,\mathrm m$ with gauge [fluid pressure](fluid-pressure.md) $p=9810\,\mathrm{Pa}$ has $h=3\,\mathrm m$. A second point at $z=0$ with gauge [fluid pressure](fluid-pressure.md) $29430\,\mathrm{Pa}$ also has $h=3\,\mathrm m$: these values are compatible with a stationary column under [hydrostatic pressure](hydrostatic-pressure.md). It is a difference in [hydraulic head](hydraulic-head.md), rather than a difference in [fluid pressure](fluid-pressure.md) alone, that drives saturated [Darcy flow](darcy-flow.md). This form uses constant [mass density](density.md); one cannot generally pull spatially varying $\rho$ through the gradient.[https://books.gw-project.org/hydrogeologic-properties-of-earth-materials-and-principles-of-groundwater-flow/chapter/hydraulic-head/](https://books.gw-project.org/hydrogeologic-properties-of-earth-materials-and-principles-of-groundwater-flow/chapter/hydraulic-head/)
