# Free-particle momentum convention

↑ **Parent:** [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md)

For a [free particle](free-particle.md) of [mass](mass.md) $m>0$ in [nonrelativistic quantum mechanics](nonrelativistic-quantum-mechanics.md), choose the [potential energy](potential-energy.md) zero and write

$$
\psi(\mathbf r,t)=A\exp\!\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r-Et)\right],\qquad E=\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m},\qquad \omega=\frac{E}{\hbar}.
$$

Applying the [momentum operator](momentum-operator.md) $-i\hbar\nabla$ returns $\mathbf p\psi$; this fixes the sign convention. The [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) is measured relative to this chosen [energy](energy.md) zero. Adding a constant [energy](energy.md) changes only a time-dependent [global phase](global-phase.md), leaving the spatial [de Broglie wavelength](de-broglie-wavelength.md) unchanged. No rest-energy term is included in this [nonrelativistic quantum mechanics](nonrelativistic-quantum-mechanics.md) convention.

The [plane wave](plane-wave.md) has $|\psi|^2=|A|^2$ and [probability current](probability-current.md) $\mathbf j=(\mathbf p/m)|A|^2$. Its real part can be drawn as a [sine](sine.md) or [cosine](cosine.md), but that graph is a component of a [probability amplitude](probability-amplitude.md), not a transverse displacement. On infinite space this is a generalized [momentum eigenstate](momentum-eigenstate.md), not a state satisfying [wavefunction normalization](wavefunction-normalization.md). A narrow [wave packet](wave-packet.md) instead moves with [group velocity](group-velocity.md) $\nabla_{\mathbf k}\omega=\mathbf p/m$; the [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) $\omega/|\mathbf k|=|\mathbf p|/(2m)$ is not its transport speed.

The equality of [canonical momentum](canonical-momentum.md) and [kinetic momentum](kinetic-momentum.md) here assumes a field-free region with the [magnetic vector potential](magnetic-vector-potential.md) chosen zero. For a charged particle in a [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md), the [kinetic momentum](kinetic-momentum.md) is $\boldsymbol\Pi=\mathbf p-q\mathbf A$. An arbitrary canonical phase gradient must not then be substituted into $m\mathbf v$; the [free particle](free-particle.md) formula needs its stated assumptions.
