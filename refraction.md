# Refraction

↑ **Parent:** [Optics](optics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refraction)

Refraction changes the direction of a transmitted [wave](wave-split.md) when it crosses between media with different [phase velocities](phase-velocity.md). For [light](light.md) at a planar interface, [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md) relates the angles measured from the [normal vector](normal-vector.md) through the [refractive indices](refractive-index.md). At normal incidence the direction remains unchanged even though the [wavelength](wavelength.md) and [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) can change. The worked example under [geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md) distinguishes transmission from [total internal reflection](total-internal-reflection.md).

For [light](light.md), [refraction](refraction.md) explains why a submerged object looks nearer the surface and why an [optical lens](lens-optics.md) can form an image. A [wavefront](wavefront.md) crossing a boundary must retain a consistent [wave phase](phase-waves.md) along that boundary: its normal spacing changes with [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md), and oblique [light rays](light-ray.md) change direction. [Normal incidence](normal-incidence.md) separates these two effects especially clearly.

Take stationary, transparent, homogeneous media with [isotropy](isotropy.md), [linearity](linearity.md) of their optical response, and real positive [refractive indices](refractive-index.md), at one chosen [frequency](frequency.md) $f$. With $c$ the [vacuum](vacuum.md) [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) and $\lambda_0=c/f$ the [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md), medium $j$ has

$$
n_j=\frac{c}{v_{\mathrm{phase},j}},\qquad
v_{\mathrm{phase},j}=f\lambda_j,\qquad
\lambda_j=\frac{\lambda_0}{n_j},\qquad
k_j=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_j}=\frac{n_j\omega}{c},\qquad \omega=2\pi f.
$$

The [frequency](frequency.md) is unchanged at a stationary boundary; the [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md), [wavelength](wavelength.md) and [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) can change. Here $n_j$ is a phase [refractive index](refractive-index.md), not the [group refractive index](group-refractive-index.md). If there is [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md), evaluate $n_j$ at this same [frequency](frequency.md) on both sides.[§27.1, paragraph preceding equation 27.2 and equation 27.2: unchanged frequency and wavelength in a medium](college-physics-by-paul-peter-urone-and-others/27-1-the-wave-aspect-of-light-interference.md)

For an original idealized air–[water](water.md) example, stipulate $n_{\mathrm a}=1$ and $n_{\mathrm w}=4/3$, with [vacuum](vacuum.md) [wavelength](wavelength.md) $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. Then $f=c/(600\,\mathrm{nm})\approx4.99654\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$, the [water](water.md) [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md) is $3c/4$, and its [wavelength](wavelength.md) is $450\,\mathrm{nm}$. At an air [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) $30^\circ$, [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md) gives a [water](water.md) [angle of refraction](angle-of-refraction.md) $\arcsin(3/8)\approx22.0243^\circ$. These stipulated values illustrate the relations; actual [refractive indices](refractive-index.md) depend on material conditions and [frequency](frequency.md). By [reversibility of an optical ray](reversibility-of-an-optical-ray.md), the same path emerging from [water](water.md) has outgoing air [angle](angle.md) $30^\circ$.
