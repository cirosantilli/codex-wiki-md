# Force on a small linearly magnetizable body

↑ **Parent:** [Induced magnetic dipole](induced-magnetic-dipole.md)

For constant scalar response $\mathbf m=\alpha_B\mathbf B_0$ and a body small compared with the applied field's spatial variation scale, the gradient of the [induced magnetic dipole](induced-magnetic-dipole.md) energy gives $\mathbf F=\alpha_B\nabla B_0^2/2$. Along an axis this is $F_z=m\,dB_0/dz$. For an ellipsoidal body, put $\chi_{\rm eff}=\chi/(1+N_d\chi)$ to obtain $F_z=\mu_0V\chi_{\rm eff}H_0H'_0$. Positive effective [magnetic susceptibility](magnetic-susceptibility.md) attracts the body towards stronger field; a negative response repels it. Reversing the source current leaves the force unchanged because both the induced moment and the field gradient reverse. Shape and orientation enter through the [demagnetizing factor](demagnetizing-factor.md), not merely through volume.
