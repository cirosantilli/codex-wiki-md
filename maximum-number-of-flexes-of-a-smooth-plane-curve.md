# Maximum number of flexes of a smooth plane curve

↑ **Parent:** [Inflection point of an algebraic plane curve](inflection-point-of-an-algebraic-plane-curve.md)

A nonsingular complex [projective plane curve](projective-plane-curve.md) of degree $d\geq2$, with no common [irreducible component](irreducible-component.md) with its [Hessian curve](hessian-curve-of-a-plane-curve.md), has at most $3d(d-2)$ distinct [flexes](inflection-point-of-an-algebraic-plane-curve.md). The [Hessian criterion for a flex](hessian-criterion-for-a-flex.md) identifies the flexes with the intersection, and [Bézout's theorem](bezout-s-theorem.md) counts its total [intersection multiplicity](intersection-multiplicity.md). Higher contact can make this bound on distinct points strict.

For a general nonsingular curve of degree $d\geq3$, all flexes have tangent contact exactly three, making the bound an equality. For a fixed point and line through it, contact at least four imposes four independent linear conditions on the coefficients; the space of such point-line pairs has dimension three. Thus degree-$d$ equations with contact at least four form a proper closed exceptional locus. In local graph coordinates $y=h(x)$, the Hessian determinant along the curve is a nonvanishing factor times $h''(x)$, so contact exactly three makes the intersection simple. A nonsingular [plane conic](plane-conic.md) has no flexes; a line is excluded because its tangent is a component and its Hessian determinant is identically zero.
