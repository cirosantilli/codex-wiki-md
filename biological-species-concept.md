# Biological species concept

↑ **Parent:** [Species concept](species-concept.md)

The [biological species concept](biological-species-concept.md) groups naturally interbreeding [biological populations](population-biology.md) with viable, fertile offspring, distinguished from other such groups by [reproductive isolation](reproductive-isolation.md). An operational test examines mating, [fertilization](fertilization.md), offspring survival and offspring [reproduction](reproduction.md), rather than only whether two adults can be paired. [Sexual reproduction](sexual-reproduction.md) is its scope: [fossils](fossil.md) and [organisms](organism.md) studied only through [asexual reproduction](asexual-reproduction.md) cannot supply the same mating evidence. [§18.2, “Species and the Ability to Reproduce”, “Speciation” and “Reproductive Isolation”](biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others.md)

For an original hypothetical example, imagine two separated [biological populations](population-biology.md) that reproduce successfully after contact. The separation alone did not demonstrate [reproductive isolation](reproductive-isolation.md). Conversely, failure to mate in one unfamiliar chamber could reflect the assay conditions. Field observations of [gene flow](gene-flow.md) and tests under suitable conditions address the biological question more directly. A successful laboratory pairing does not establish frequent interbreeding in nature.
