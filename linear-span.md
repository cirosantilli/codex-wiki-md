# Linear span

↑ **Parent:** [Vector space](vector-space-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_span)

The linear span of a subset $S$ of a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) is the [set](set-split.md) of all finite [linear combinations](linear-combination.md) of elements of $S$. It is the smallest [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) containing $S$: every [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) containing the original [vectors](vector.md) must contain their sums and [scalar multiples](scalar-multiple.md).

In $\mathbb R^3$, the [linear span](linear-span.md) of $(1,0,0)$ is a line, the [linear span](linear-span.md) of $(1,0,0),(0,1,0)$ is the plane $z=0$, and adding $(0,0,1)$ gives all of $\mathbb R^3$. Adding $(2,0,0)$ to the first set changes none of its attainable [vectors](vector.md), because it is already a [scalar multiple](scalar-multiple.md) of $(1,0,0)$. Thus a [spanning set](spanning-set.md) can contain redundant generators; [linear independence](linear-independence.md) identifies when this redundancy is absent. In an infinite-dimensional space, an algebraic [linear span](linear-span.md) still uses finite sums; a [closed linear span](closed-linear-span.md) can also include [limits](limit-of-a-function.md) in a specified [norm](norm.md).
