# Finite Ramsey theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Ramsey's theorem](ramsey-s-theorem.md)

Fix [positive integers](positive-integer.md) $r,k,m$ with $m\geq r$. There is an [integer](integer-split.md) $n\geq m$, depending only on $r,k,m$, such that every [finite coloring](finite-coloring.md)

$$
c:\binom{[n]}{r}\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,k\}
$$

has a [subset](subset.md) $H\subseteq[n]$ of size $m$ for which the [restriction of a function](restriction-mathematics.md) $c$ to $\binom{H}{r}$ is a [constant function](constant-function.md). Here $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and $\binom{S}{r}$ denotes the collection of all $r$-element [subsets](subset.md) of $S$, rather than its [binomial coefficient](binomial-coefficient-split.md) [cardinality](cardinality.md). The notation $[n]\longrightarrow(m)^r_k$ abbreviates this property. Thus $H$ is a [homogeneous set for a colouring](homogeneous-set-for-a-colouring.md); when $r>1$, the [monochromatic set](monochromatic-set.md) consists of its $r$-element [subsets](subset.md), not its individual elements. This is the finite assertion in [Ramsey's original Theorem B, p. 267](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/ramseyorig.pdf).

For $r=1$, this is the [pigeonhole principle](pigeonhole-principle.md), with sharp threshold $n=k(m-1)+1$. For instance, every red-blue coloring of five objects has three of one color; four objects can split two and two. For $r=2$, $k=2$ and $m=3$, the sharp threshold is six, as shown by the [Ramsey number for triangles](ramsey-number-for-triangles.md). Coloring pairs makes this a problem about [edges](edge-of-a-graph.md) of a [complete graph](complete-graph.md). For larger $r$, the analogous viewpoint uses a [hypergraph](hypergraph-split.md).

The [finite Ramsey theorem](finite-ramsey-theorem.md) lets an existence argument discard most of a large [finite set](finite-set.md) while retaining a prescribed amount of uniform structure. Its threshold applies to every coloring; one does not need an assumption that colors were assigned randomly. A diagonal [compactness](compact-space.md) argument deduces finite existence from [Ramsey's theorem](ramsey-s-theorem.md), but does not by itself identify the smallest threshold. For pairs, a [Ramsey number](ramsey-number.md) makes that optimization explicit.
