# Waiting-time solutions of a sublinear Bernoulli equation

↑ **Parent:** [Bernoulli differential equation](bernoulli-differential-equation.md)

Let q be continuous and strictly positive, with a constant a. A nonnegative [solution of a differential equation](solution-of-a-differential-equation.md) may stay zero until any prescribed point $x_0$ and become positive afterwards. For $x>x_0$, define

$$
y(x)=\left[(1-p)e^{(1-p)ax}\int_{x_0}^xq(s)e^{-(1-p)as}\,ds\right]^{1/(1-p)},
$$

and set $y=0$ before $x_0$. The [Bernoulli power substitution](bernoulli-power-substitution.md) verifies the equation after $x_0$. Continuity of q gives $y=O((x-x_0)^{1/(1-p)})$, so the joining derivative is zero and the equation also holds at the join. These different waiting times give nonunique solutions from zero. The right side lacks [Lipschitz continuity](lipschitz-continuity.md) as a function of y near zero, so the uniqueness conclusion of the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](picard-lindelof-theorem.md) does not apply there.
