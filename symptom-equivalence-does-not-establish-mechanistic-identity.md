# Symptom equivalence does not establish mechanistic identity

↑ **Parent:** [Psychopathology](psychopathology.md)

Let $Z$ index either of two candidate pathways and suppose $P(Y=1\mid Z=1)=P(Y=1\mid Z=2)=p$ for a measured [symptom](symptom.md) $Y$. For any mixing [probability](probability.md) $\pi=P(Z=1)$, marginalization gives $P(Y=1)=\pi p+(1-\pi)p=p$. An independent sample with $k$ positive observations out of $n$ therefore has [likelihood](likelihood-function.md) $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$, independent of $\pi$. The [symptom](symptom.md) alone does not identify the mixture proportion or pathway. This is an [identifiability](identifiability.md) counterexample, not an empirical claim that real clinical pathways necessarily have identical [symptom](symptom.md) distributions. Additional indicators or [interventions](treatment.md) may distinguish them.
