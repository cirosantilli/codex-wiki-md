# Energy profile (chemistry)

↑ **Parent:** [Reaction coordinate](reaction-coordinate.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_profile_(chemistry))

An [energy profile](energy-profile-chemistry.md) plots an explicitly specified [energy](energy.md) against a [reaction coordinate](reaction-coordinate.md). Identify whether the vertical axis is [potential energy](potential-energy.md), [enthalpy](enthalpy.md), or [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md); these descriptions are related but not interchangeable. A [potential-energy profile](potential-energy-profile.md) describes a path through configurations, while a free-energy description also accounts for the relevant thermodynamic conditions.

![](reaction-barriers-and-endpoints.png)

**[Figure 56](#image-two-schematic-potential-energy-profiles-share-endpoints-but-have-different-barriers). Two schematic potential-energy profiles share endpoints but have different barriers**. The reactant level is $0$, the product level is $-20\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, and the two maxima are $80$ and $50\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. These chosen curves illustrate endpoint differences and barrier heights; they are not measured surfaces, trajectories in time, or a complete catalytic cycle.

For the two chosen profiles, the [reaction energy barriers](reaction-energy-barrier.md) from the [reactants](reactant.md) are $80$ and $50\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. From the [reaction products](product-chemistry.md), the same maxima are $100$ and $70\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ higher. Both paths have endpoint difference $-20\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. The barrier is measured from the relevant starting endpoint, not from an arbitrary zero of [potential energy](potential-energy.md).

The appeal of the diagram is that it separates two useful questions: which endpoint is lower, and which path is easier to traverse? [Catalysis](catalysis.md) changes the route through [transition states](transition-state.md) while retaining the endpoint thermodynamics of the same overall [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md). A real catalytic route may have several steps and [reaction intermediates](reaction-intermediate.md); the two simple curves do not specify such a [reaction mechanism](reaction-mechanism.md). The plotted potential-energy difference alone is not an [equilibrium constant](equilibrium-constant.md) or a calculated [rate constant](rate-constant.md). See [Chemistry 2e, 12.7: alternative reaction routes and catalysis](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/12-7-catalysis).
