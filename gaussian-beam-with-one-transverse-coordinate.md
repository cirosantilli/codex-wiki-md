# Gaussian beam with one transverse coordinate

↑ **Parent:** [Gaussian beam](gaussian-beam.md)

For the free [parabolic wave equation](parabolic-wave-equation.md) $E_x=iE_{zz}/(2k)$, input $E(0,z)=e^{-Az^2}$ with $\operatorname{Re}A>0$ propagates as

$$
 E(x,z)=q(x)^{-1/2}e^{-Az^2/q(x)},\qquad q(x)=1+2iAx/k.
$$

The branch is continuous from $q(0)^{-1/2}=1$. [One-dimensional transverse Fresnel propagation](one-dimensional-transverse-fresnel-propagation.md) or two [Gaussian integrals](gaussian-integral.md) prove the formula. With $A=D^{-2}+ik/(2F)$, the squared envelope is $|q|^{-1}\exp[-2z^2/(D^2|q|^2)]$. The amplitude power is $-1/2$ because there is only one transverse coordinate. A two-transverse-coordinate [Gaussian beam](gaussian-beam.md) instead has power $-1$ when both coordinates share the same $q$.
