# Bertrand paradox (probability)

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_paradox_(probability))

Different precise measures on random chords give different probabilities; the phrase “random chord” does not select a unique [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md). In a circle of radius $r$, a chord exceeds the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle exactly when its midpoint distance from the center is below $r/2$. Uniform midpoint area gives probability $1/4$, independent uniform endpoints give $1/3$, and uniform midpoint distance gives $1/2$. The difference is in the sampling laws, not a contradiction in [probability theory](probability-theory-split.md).
