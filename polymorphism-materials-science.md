# Polymorphism (materials science)

↑ **Parent:** [State of matter](state-of-matter.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymorphism_(materials_science))

[Crystal polymorphism](polymorphism-materials-science.md) is the existence of different [crystal structures](crystal-structure.md) for the same chemical substance. Each [crystal polymorph](crystal-polymorph.md) can have its own [mass density](density.md), [melting](melting.md) behavior and range of stability on a [phase diagram](phase-diagram.md). A different external shape of the same [crystal structure](crystal-structure.md) alone does not establish a different [crystal polymorph](crystal-polymorph.md).[IUPAC Compendium of Chemical Terminology, fifth edition, entry “polymorph” (15225), definition and Note 2](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/polymorph.md)

At fixed [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [pressure](pressure-split.md), compare the [chemical potentials](chemical-potential-split.md) of the possible [crystal polymorphs](crystal-polymorph.md). The lower value determines the stable bulk [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md); equality permits [phase coexistence](phase-coexistence.md) when neither form is beaten by another [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md). A higher-[chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) form can persist through [metastability](metastability.md), so finding it in a sample does not by itself locate an equilibrium boundary.
