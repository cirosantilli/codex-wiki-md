# Cayley transform of a real skew-symmetric matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Cayley transform (Lie theory)](cayley-transform-lie-theory.md)

For a real [skew-symmetric matrix](skew-symmetric-matrix.md) $A$, $I-A$ is invertible: $(I-A)x=0$ would imply $\|x\|^2=x^TAx=0$. Its [Cayley transform](cayley-transform-lie-theory.md) is an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md), since transposition gives $Q^T=(I+A)^{-1}(I-A)$ and the commuting factors cancel in $Q^TQ$. Also $\det(I+A)=\det((I+A)^T)=\det(I-A)$, so $\det Q=1$ in every dimension. If $Av=\lambda v$, then $Qv=(1+\lambda)(1-\lambda)^{-1}v$. For $\lambda=it$, the transformed [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) is $e^{2i\arctan t}$. In odd dimension $\det A=\det(-A)=-\det A$, so $A$ has a nonzero [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) and $Q$ fixes that direction.
