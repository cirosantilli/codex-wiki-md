<h1 id="analysis/6/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

With the same factors, if $n\leq m$, then

$$
\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}=(z-a)^{m-n}\frac{F(z)}{G(z)}
$$

has no negative power and extends holomorphically across $a$. Thus its apparent singularity is a [removable singularity](../../../../../../removable-singularity.md), with extension value

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac fg\right)(a)=\begin{cases}F(a)/G(a),&m=n,\\0,&m>n.\end{cases}}
$$

For $m>n$ the extended quotient has a [zero](../../../../../../order-of-a-zero-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) of order $m-n$; for $m=n$ the extension is nonzero.
