<h1 id="analysis/6/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [isolated singularity](../../../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) at $a$ means that $f$ is holomorphic in some punctured disk $0<|z-a|<r$, while $a$ itself is an excluded point or a point where the given function is not holomorphic. There are no other singularities arbitrarily close to $a$. By [Laurent's theorem](../../../../../../laurent-theorem.md), the function has a unique [Laurent series](../../../../../../laurent-series.md) on that punctured disk:

$$
f(z)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}c_j(z-a)^j.
$$

The negative-power terms form its [principal part of a Laurent series](../../../../../../principal-part-of-a-laurent-series.md).
