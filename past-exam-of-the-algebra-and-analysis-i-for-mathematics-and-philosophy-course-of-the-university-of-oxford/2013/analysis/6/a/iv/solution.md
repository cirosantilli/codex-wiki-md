<h1 id="analysis/6/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A [zero](../../../../../../order-of-a-zero-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) of order $n\geq1$ at $a$ means that $f$ is holomorphic near $a$ and

$$
f(a)=f'(a)=\cdots=f^{(n-1)}(a)=0,\qquad f^{(n)}(a)\neq0.
$$

Equivalently its [Taylor series](../../../../../../taylor-series.md) starts with the nonzero coefficient of $(z-a)^n$, so

$$
\boxed{f(z)=(z-a)^n h(z),\qquad h\text{ holomorphic near }a,\ h(a)\neq0.}
$$

Unlike a [pole](../../../../../../pole.md), a zero is a regular point of the function itself; its reciprocal has a pole of the corresponding order.
