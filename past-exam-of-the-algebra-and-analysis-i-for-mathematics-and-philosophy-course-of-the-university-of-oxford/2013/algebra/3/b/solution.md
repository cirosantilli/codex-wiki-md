<h1 id="algebra/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [zero ideal](../../../../../zero-ideal.md), choose $d=0$. Otherwise choose a nonzero $d\in I$ for which the [Euclidean function](../../../../../euclidean-function.md) $\delta(d)$ is minimal; the [well-ordering principle](../../../../../well-ordering-principle-for-the-natural-numbers.md) guarantees such a choice. For any $a\in I$, perform Euclidean division:

$$
a=qd+r,\qquad r=0\ \text{or}\ \delta(r)<\delta(d).
$$

Because $I$ is an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md), $r=a-qd\in I$. A nonzero remainder would contradict the minimal choice of $d$, so $r=0$ and $a\in Rd$. Thus $I\subseteq Rd$. Conversely, $d\in I$ implies $Rd\subseteq I$, proving

$$
\boxed{I=Rd.}
$$

Every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is therefore a [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md); in particular, a [Euclidean domain](../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md).
