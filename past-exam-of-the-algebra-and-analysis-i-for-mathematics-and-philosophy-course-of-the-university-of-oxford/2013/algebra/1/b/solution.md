<h1 id="algebra/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_m$ of $U$. Because $v\notin U$, the list $u_1,\ldots,u_m,v$ is [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md). Extend it to a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of $V$. A [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) is specified uniquely by its values on a [basis](../../../../../basis.md), so define $f$ to be zero on the $u_j$ and on every added basis vector other than $v$, and set $f(v)=1$.

Then $f$ vanishes on every [linear combination](../../../../../linear-combination.md) of the $u_j$, so $f\in U^0$, while

$$
\boxed{f(v)=1\neq0.}
$$

This proves that the [annihilator of a vector subspace](../../../../../annihilator-of-a-vector-subspace.md) separates every vector outside the subspace from that subspace; no [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) or preferred coordinates are needed.
