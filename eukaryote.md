# Eukaryote

↑ **Parent:** [Organism](organism.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryote)

A [eukaryote](eukaryote.md) is an [organism](organism.md) belonging to the lineage characterized by a membrane-bounded [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md) and extensive cellular compartmentalization. A [nuclear envelope](nuclear-envelope.md) surrounds the nuclear [genome](genome.md); nuclear [chromosomes](chromosome.md) are typically linear. [Ribosomes](ribosome.md) perform [biological translation](translation-biology.md) outside the [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md). See [OpenStax Microbiology, 3.4: nuclear organization and protein synthesis](https://openstax.org/books/microbiology/pages/3-4-unique-characteristics-of-eukaryotic-cells). Specialized [biological cells](biological-cell.md) can lose their [cell nucleus](cell-nucleus.md), as mature mammalian [red blood cells](red-blood-cell.md) do, while remaining parts of [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) [organisms](organism.md); see [OpenStax Biology 2e, 40.2: mature red blood cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/40-2-components-of-the-blood).

[Yeasts](yeast.md), [plants](plant.md) and [animals](animal.md) are all [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md). Typical cells in these groups have [mitochondria](mitochondria.md), but [chloroplasts](chloroplast.md) belong to photosynthetic [plant](plant.md) and [algae](algae.md) lineages, not to every [eukaryote](eukaryote.md). A [cell wall](cell-wall.md) occurs in typical [plant](plant.md) and [fungus](fungus.md) cells but is absent from [animal](animal.md) cells. These are comparisons of representative cells, not a checklist every specialized cell must satisfy. See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 4.3: nucleus, organelles and representative cells](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells).

The interesting feature is controlled separation: [cell membranes](cell-membrane.md) and [organelle](organelle.md) membranes let different cellular processes occupy distinct compartments while [membrane transport](membrane-transport.md) connects them. The [evolution](evolution.md) of [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) also involved partnership: [mitochondria](mitochondria.md) descend from a bacterial [endosymbiosis](endosymbiosis.md). Modern [phylogenetics](phylogenetics.md) supports an archaeal ancestry for the nuclear lineage, alongside this bacterial contribution; see [two-domain system](two-domain-system.md). Thus the history of a whole [eukaryote](eukaryote.md) cannot be read as the history of just one of its [genes](gene.md).
