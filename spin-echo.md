# Spin echo

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear magnetic resonance](nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_echo)

A [spin echo](spin-echo.md) is a reappearance of transverse [magnetization](magnetization.md) after a pulse sequence reverses an earlier spread of [phases](phase-waves.md). An ensemble can have almost no net transverse [magnetization](magnetization.md) while its components retain enough individual phase memory to reassemble. In the ideal [Hahn echo](spin-echo.md) below, a short 90° pulse prepares transverse [magnetization](magnetization.md), a 180° pulse follows after time $\tau$, and the [signal](signal.md) refocuses at $t=2\tau$. The receiver observes freely precessing [magnetization](magnetization.md), as in [free induction decay](free-induction-decay.md); no third driving pulse is needed at the echo time.

The useful distinction is between cancellation in an ensemble average and loss of recoverable phase memory. A static spread of [Larmor precession](larmor-precession.md) [frequencies](frequency.md) can make [free induction decay](free-induction-decay.md) much faster than [transverse nuclear magnetization decay](transverse-nuclear-magnetization-decay.md). A [spin echo](spin-echo.md) can remove that static spread from the echo amplitude, allowing a more selective measurement of [transverse relaxation](transverse-relaxation.md). It does not in general reverse [noise](noise-signal-processing.md), [population relaxation](population-relaxation.md), or every interaction between [spins](spin.md).
