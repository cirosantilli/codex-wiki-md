## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

Choose $x$ vertically upward. Let $G(x,t)$ be the gas-occupied part of a pipe cross-section, of area $\alpha A$. Define the phase mean [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density), [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass)-weighted [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity), second moment of [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity), and phase mean [pressure](../pressure.md) by

$$
\bar\rho_g=\frac{1}{\alpha A}\int_G\rho_g\,dA,\quad
\bar v=\frac{\int_G\rho_g v_x\,dA}{\alpha A\bar\rho_g},\quad
\overline{v^2}=\frac{\int_G\rho_g v_x^2\,dA}{\alpha A\bar\rho_g},\quad
\bar p_g=\frac{1}{\alpha A}\int_Gp_g\,dA.
$$

The [void fraction](../fluid-mechanics.md#void-fraction) $\alpha$ is a volume fraction, not a [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) fraction. The gas [momentum](../momentum.md) in a slice of length $dx$ is $A\alpha\bar\rho_g\bar v\,dx$; its advected [momentum flux](../mathematical-physics.md#momentum-flux) is $A\alpha\bar\rho_g\overline{v^2}$. The two end [pressures](../pressure.md) give the net force $-A\partial_x(\alpha\bar p_g)\,dx$. Include every other vertical force on the gas, including interfacial forces, in $M_g\,dx$. [Newton's second law](../force.md#newton-s-second-law) for this fixed slice, followed by division by $A\,dx$, therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\partial_t(\alpha\bar\rho_g\bar v)+\partial_x(\alpha\bar\rho_g\overline{v^2})=M_g/A-\partial_x(\alpha\bar p_g).}
$$

This is a [cross-section averaged phase momentum balance](../fluid-mechanics.md#cross-section-averaged-phase-momentum-balance). In particular, [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) weighting makes the [momentum](../momentum.md) and its flux exact averages; one must not replace $\overline{v^2}$ by $\bar v^2$ without an additional closure. With liquid mean [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity) $\bar u$, constant liquid [mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) $\rho_\ell$, and liquid second moment $\overline{u^2}$, the same calculation gives

$$
\boxed{\partial_t[(1-\alpha)\rho_\ell\bar u]+\partial_x[(1-\alpha)\rho_\ell\overline{u^2}]=M_\ell/A-\partial_x[(1-\alpha)\bar p_\ell].}
$$

With the common [pressure](../pressure.md) $p=\bar p_g=\bar p_\ell$, the specified [pressure](../pressure.md) contributions to $M_g,M_\ell$ cancel the phase-fraction [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) from the [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule). The remaining [pressure](../pressure.md) forces per unit mixture volume are $-\alpha p_x$ and $-(1-\alpha)p_x$.

Assume that the bubbles at a given height have the stated radius $a$. Their number per unit mixture volume is $N=3\alpha/(4\pi a^3)$. The [drag force](../fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics) on a rising bubble opposes its relative [velocity](../classical-mechanics.md#velocity), so multiplication of the single-bubble law by $N$ gives the signed magnitude

$$
D_v=\frac{3C_D\rho_\ell\alpha}{4a}\bar v|\bar v|.
$$

[Gravity](../physics.md#gravity) acts downward; the [drag force](../fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics) on the liquid is the opposite of that on the gas. With no wall force,

$$
\boxed{M'_g/A=-\alpha\bar\rho_g g-D_v,\qquad M'_\ell/A=-(1-\alpha)\rho_\ell g+D_v.}
$$

A bubble retains its [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) in the absence of transfer between phases. Consequently $a^3\bar\rho_g=a_0^3\rho_0$ and

$$
\boxed{a=a_0(\rho_0/\bar\rho_g)^{1/3}.}
$$

For clarity suppress bars on $p,\rho_g,v$ in the steady calculation. On a macroscopic variation length $\ell$, gas [inertia](../classical-mechanics.md#inertia) per unit volume scales like $\alpha\rho_gv^2/\ell$, whereas the quadratic [drag force](../fluid-mechanics.md#drag-physics) scales like $\alpha\rho_\ell v^2/a$. Their ratio is $O[(\rho_g/\rho_\ell)(a/\ell)]$. Thus $\rho_g=O(\rho_0)\ll\rho_\ell$, together with the scale separation $a\ll\ell$ inherent in this averaging model, justifies the leading-order neglect of gas [inertia](../classical-mechanics.md#inertia). Small reference density alone does not justify it in an arbitrarily short or strongly compressed region. Neglect gas weight at the same order, with the [liquid fraction](../geophysics.md#liquid-fraction) not vanishing. For a stationary liquid, summing the two [momentum](../momentum.md) equations gives

$$
p_x=-g[\alpha\rho_g+(1-\alpha)\rho_\ell]\sim-\rho_\ell g(1-\alpha).
$$

The leading gas balance is $0=-\alpha p_x-D_v$. Substitution of the bubble-radius law yields

$$
\boxed{p_x=-\rho_\ell g(1-\alpha)=-\frac{3C_D\rho_\ell}{4a_0}(\rho_g/\rho_0)^{1/3}v|v|.}
$$

The gas [continuity equation](../mathematical-physics.md#continuity-equation), with constant pipe area and no [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) exchange, is $\partial_t(\alpha\rho_g)+\partial_x(\alpha\rho_gv)=0$. Hence the steady gas [mass flux](../mathematical-physics.md#mass-flux) $J=\alpha\rho_gv$ is constant. For $0<\alpha<1$ the [pressure](../pressure.md) equation selects $v>0$, and

$$
v^2=\frac{4a_0g}{3C_D}(1-\alpha)(\rho_0/\rho_g)^{1/3},\qquad
J^2=\frac{4a_0g}{3C_D}\rho_0^{1/3}\alpha^2(1-\alpha)\rho_g^{5/3}.
$$

Evaluate the latter constant at a reference point $\rho_g=\rho_0$, $\alpha=\alpha_0$ and divide. The [terminal-drift density law for compressible bubbles](../fluid-mechanics.md#terminal-drift-density-law-for-compressible-bubbles) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\rho_g}{\rho_0}=\left[\frac{\alpha_0^2(1-\alpha_0)}{\alpha^2(1-\alpha)}\right]^{3/5}.}
$$

The exponent $3/5$ comes from combining [mass](../classical-mechanics.md#mass) conservation with the density dependence of bubble radius; no gas equation of state was needed.

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

Write $\Delta T=T_0-T_B$, $\Delta C=C_0-C_B$ and assume these scales are nonzero. The two reference [liquidus](../thermodynamic-potential.md#liquidus) relations imply $\Delta T=-m\Delta C$. For the [similarity variable](../partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-variable) $\eta=z/(2\sqrt{\kappa t})$ and front $h=2\lambda\sqrt{\kappa t}$,

$$
\eta_t=-\frac{\eta}{2t},\quad\eta_z=\frac1{2\sqrt{\kappa t}},\quad h'=\lambda\sqrt{\kappa/t}.
$$

Thus a [temperature](../temperature.md) profile $T=T_*+\Delta T\theta(\eta)$ has $T_t=-\Delta T\eta\theta'/(2t)$ and $\kappa T_{zz}=\Delta T\theta''/(4t)$. Each [heat equation](../diffusion-equation.md#heat-equation) becomes $\theta''+2\eta\theta'=0$. The solute [diffusion equation](../diffusion-equation.md) becomes $\mathcal C^{l\prime\prime}+2Le\eta\mathcal C^{l\prime}=0$, with

$$
\boxed{Le=\kappa/D.}
$$

This [Lewis number](../thermodynamics.md#lewis-number) compares thermal and solutal [diffusivities](../brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient). Inside the [mushy layer](../geophysics.md#mushy-layer), $T^s-T_B=-m(C^s-C_B)$ gives $\boxed{\mathcal C^s=\theta^s}$. The wall and far-field conditions become $\theta^s(0)=0$ and $\theta^l,\mathcal C^l\to0$ as $\eta\to\infty$.

At the moving [phase boundary](../geophysics.md#phase-boundary), equality of [temperatures](../temperature.md) gives $\theta^s=\theta^l+(T_\infty-T_B)/\Delta T$. Equality of [concentrations](../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) gives $\mathcal C^s=\mathcal C^l+1$. Therefore the dimensionless parameters with the convention used here are

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R_\theta=\frac{T_0-T_\infty}{T_0-T_B},\qquad \mathcal R_C=\frac{C_0}{C_0-C_B},\qquad S=\frac{\rho L\kappa}{k(T_0-T_B)}.}
$$

Neither [concentration](../mathematical-physics.md#concentration) nor [temperature](../temperature.md) offsets should be dropped when enforcing continuity. In particular $\mathcal R_\theta$ can be negative. If $k=\rho c_p\kappa$, then $S=L/[c_p\Delta T]$: this is the [inverse Stefan number](../thermodynamic-potential.md#latent-to-sensible-heat-ratio), although the paper calls $S$ its Stefan number.

To check every factor of two in the flux conditions, divide the thermal [Stefan condition](../geophysics.md#stefan-condition) by $k\Delta T/(2\sqrt{\kappa t})$, and the solute balance by $D\Delta C/(2\sqrt{\kappa t})$. This gives

$$
\boxed{2\phi\lambda S=\theta^{s\prime}-\theta^{l\prime},\qquad 2\phi Le\lambda(\mathcal C^l+\mathcal R_C)=-\mathcal C^{l\prime}.}
$$

Finally $T_z^l=-mC_z^l$ and $-m\Delta C=\Delta T$ imply $\boxed{\theta^{l\prime}=\mathcal C^{l\prime}}$. This is [marginal equilibrium at a mush-liquid boundary](../geophysics.md#marginal-equilibrium-at-a-mush-liquid-boundary). Together these calculations produce all the rescaled differential equations and all three interface balances.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

Integrating $Y''+2a\eta Y'=0$ once gives $Y'=K e^{-a\eta^2}$. A second integration expresses the solution using the [error function](../integral.md#error-function) when the value at zero is prescribed, and the [complementary error function](../integral.md#complementary-error-function) when decay at infinity is prescribed. Write $\theta_\lambda=\theta^s(\lambda)$ and take $\lambda>0$, $Le>0$. The wall condition and the interface value give

$$
\boxed{\theta^s(\eta)=\mathcal C^s(\eta)=\theta_\lambda\frac{\operatorname{erf}\eta}{\operatorname{erf}\lambda},\qquad0\leq\eta\leq\lambda.}
$$

For the exterior liquid, continuity at the interface fixes $\theta^l(\lambda)=\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_\theta$ and $\mathcal C^l(\lambda)=\theta_\lambda-1$. Thus the two decaying [similarity solutions](../partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-solution) are

$$
\boxed{\theta^l(\eta)=(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_\theta)\frac{\operatorname{erfc}\eta}{\operatorname{erfc}\lambda},\qquad
\mathcal C^l(\eta)=(\theta_\lambda-1)\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{Le}\eta)}{\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{Le}\lambda)},\quad\eta\geq\lambda.}
$$

The factor $\sqrt{Le}$ is required because solute and heat diffuse on different length scales. Both denominators are positive for a finite positive $\lambda$. These formulas use the differential equations, wall/far-field data and continuity; the remaining three flux conditions determine their unknown constants.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Introduce three positive functions of the unknown front parameter:

$$
P(\lambda)=\frac{e^{-\lambda^2}}{\sqrt\pi\operatorname{erf}\lambda},\quad
Q(\lambda)=\frac{e^{-\lambda^2}}{\sqrt\pi\operatorname{erfc}\lambda},\quad
R(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{Le}\,e^{-Le\lambda^2}}{\sqrt\pi\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{Le}\lambda)}.
$$

Differentiating the [error function](../integral.md#error-function) profiles gives $\theta^{s\prime}=2P\theta_\lambda$, $\theta^{l\prime}=-2Q(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_\theta)$ and $\mathcal C^{l\prime}=-2R(\theta_\lambda-1)$ at the interface. The three flux conditions are therefore exactly

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\phi\lambda S&=P\theta_\lambda+Q(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_\theta),\\
\phi Le\lambda(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_C)&=R(\theta_\lambda-1),\\
Q(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_\theta)&=R(\theta_\lambda-1).
\end{aligned}}
$$

These undivided equations are the safest description of the [constant-fraction mushy-layer similarity solution](../geophysics.md#constant-fraction-mushy-layer-similarity-solution), including degenerate parameter choices. In the nondegenerate case the third equation first gives

$$
\boxed{\theta_\lambda=1+\frac{Q(\lambda)\mathcal R_\theta}{R(\lambda)-Q(\lambda)}.}
$$

The second then gives the [solid fraction](../geophysics.md#solid-fraction)

$$
\boxed{\phi=\frac{R(\lambda)(\theta_\lambda-1)}{Le\lambda(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_C)}.}
$$

Use the third equation to replace the $Q$ term in the first, and eliminate $\phi$. The front parameter is a positive root of the single [transcendental equation](../mathematics.md#transcendental-equation)

$$
\boxed{\frac{S R(\lambda)(\theta_\lambda-1)}{Le(\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_C)}-P(\lambda)\theta_\lambda-R(\lambda)(\theta_\lambda-1)=0,\qquad
\theta_\lambda=1+\frac{Q(\lambda)\mathcal R_\theta}{R(\lambda)-Q(\lambda)}.}
$$

Then substitute back for $\theta_\lambda,\phi$ and $h(t)=2\lambda\sqrt{\kappa t}$. Since the physical parameters have not been assigned numbers, an implicit scalar equation is the appropriate determination of $\lambda$.

The divided formulas require $R\ne Q$ and $\theta_\lambda-1+\mathcal R_C\ne0$. If either vanishes, return to the boxed three-equation system. For example $Le=1$ gives $R=Q$, so the last equation demands $\mathcal R_\theta=0$; otherwise this proposed [similarity solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#similarity-solution) cannot exist. A physical branch must also have $\lambda>0$ and $0\leq\phi\leq1$. The algebra alone does not establish existence or uniqueness of such a branch for arbitrary parameters.
