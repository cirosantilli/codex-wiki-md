<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) gives $p_x=\rho gh_x$, independent of depth. Thus [Darcy's law](../../../../../darcy-law.md) gives $u=-Kh_x$ and the horizontal [volume flux per unit width](../../../../../volume-flux-per-unit-width.md) is

$$
\boxed{q(x,t)=\int_0^hu\,dz=-Khh_x,\qquad
K=\frac{k\rho g}{\mu}.}
$$

The coefficient $K$ is the [hydraulic conductivity](../../../../../hydraulic-conductivity.md), with units of [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md). For a column of unit horizontal area, the stored water volume is $\phi h$, and rainfall supplies [volume flux](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) $R$. The local [mass conservation](../../../../../mass-conservation.md) equation is $\phi h_t+q_x=R$. Therefore the [Boussinesq equation for an unconfined aquifer](../../../../../boussinesq-equation-for-an-unconfined-aquifer.md) is

$$
\boxed{\phi h_t=K(hh_x)_x+R.}
$$

This assumes rainfall is entirely converted into recharge and neglects unsaturated storage and losses before the water reaches the [water table](../../../../../water-table.md).
