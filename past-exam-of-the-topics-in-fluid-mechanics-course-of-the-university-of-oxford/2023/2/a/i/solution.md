<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $z$ vertically upward and the porous skeleton stationary. [Darcy's law](../../../../../darcy-law.md) and [incompressibility](../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) are

$$
\boxed{\mathbf u=-\frac{k}{\mu}(\nabla p+\rho g\mathbf e_z),\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0.}
$$

Here $\mathbf u$ is the [Darcy velocity](../../../../../darcy-velocity.md), a [volume flux](../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) per unit total cross-sectional area, including solid and void. If $\mathbf v$ is the liquid [pore velocity](../../../../../pore-velocity.md) and all the pore space is saturated and connected, then

$$
\boxed{\mathbf u=\phi\mathbf v.}
$$

The [porosity](../../../../../porosity.md) $\phi$ therefore enters storage and the conversion to [pore velocity](../../../../../pore-velocity.md); it does not multiply the [Darcy velocity](../../../../../darcy-velocity.md) again when calculating discharge.
