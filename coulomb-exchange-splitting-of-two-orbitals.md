# Coulomb exchange splitting of two orbitals

↑ **Parent:** [Exchange interaction](exchange-interaction.md)

Let $a,b$ be distinct orthonormal spatial orbitals occupied by two identical [spin one-half](spin-one-half.md) particles, with a weak repulsive [Coulomb interaction](coulomb-interaction.md). The symmetric and antisymmetric spatial combinations have first-order [energy](energy-split.md) shifts $J+K$ and $J-K$, where $J$ is the direct Coulomb integral and

$$
K=g\int d^3r\,d^3r'\,\frac{a^*(\mathbf r)b(\mathbf r)b^*(\mathbf r')a(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|},\qquad g>0.
$$

The Coulomb kernel has positive [Fourier transform](fourier-transform-split.md) $4\pi/|\mathbf k|^2$, so $K\ge0$. [Fermionic exchange symmetry](fermi-dirac-statistics.md) pairs the symmetric spatial function with the [spin-one-half singlet state](spin-one-half-singlet-state.md) and the antisymmetric one with the [spin-one-half triplet state](spin-one-half-triplet-state.md). For these fixed orbitals the triplet lies lower by $2K$ when $K>0$. This does not compare arbitrary singlets and triplets with different orbital occupations; for negligible orbital overlap the splitting can vanish.
