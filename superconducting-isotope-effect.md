# Superconducting isotope effect

↑ **Parent:** [BCS theory](bcs-theory.md)

The [superconducting isotope effect](superconducting-isotope-effect.md) is the dependence of the [superconducting transition temperature](superconducting-transition-temperature.md) on isotopic [mass](mass.md) $M$ at fixed chemical composition. Its discovery in 1950 was a crucial clue that [phonons](phonon.md) participate in conventional [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md).[p.55, isotope-effect discovery and its significance for the electron-phonon mechanism](electron-phonon-interactions-and-superconductivity-by-john-bardeen.md)

The model prediction can be derived from two assumptions. For harmonic motion with a fixed restoring-force constant $K$, a [phonon](phonon.md) frequency scales as $\omega\propto\sqrt{K/M}$. If the [BCS dimensionless coupling](bcs-dimensionless-coupling.md) is independent of $M$, the [weak coupling](weak-coupling.md) result $k_BT_c\simeq1.134\hbar\omega_De^{-1/\lambda}$ then gives $T_c\propto M^{-1/2}$ and $\alpha=1/2$. Changes in the effective interaction can change this exponent; it is not a universal law for every material exhibiting [superconductivity](superconductivity-split.md). For a stipulated mass ratio $M_2/M_1=1.04$, the ideal model predicts $T_{c,2}/T_{c,1}=1/\sqrt{1.04}\simeq0.98058$. This is an original scaling example, not a pair of measured transition temperatures.
