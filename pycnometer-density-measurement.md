# Pycnometer density measurement

↑ **Parent:** [Normalizing extensive quantities](normalizing-extensive-quantities.md)

A [pycnometer](pycnometer.md) makes the distinction between [mass](mass.md) and [mass density](density.md) experimentally tangible. NIST's 1994 report describes a glass vessel with filling capillaries, wire plugs and a covered overflow bowl in a controlled water bath. The operators soaked a pure-water filling to establish the bath [temperature](temperature-split.md), dried the outside, then left it overnight in the balance enclosure to reach [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md) before weighing. Empty and full [force-restoration balance](force-restoration-balance.md) readings, together with known water [mass density](density.md), calibrated its capacity at the bath [temperature](temperature-split.md). A tin-oxide coating suppressed stray [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md); an [electrometer](electrometer.md) detected none during use. The authors distinguish bath and weighing [temperatures](temperature-split.md) and correct for [buoyancy](buoyancy.md) and changes of displacement [volume](volume.md). Capacity and water-reference uncertainties carry into later liquid [mass density](density.md) determinations. Their pycnometer data were not yet analyzed, so this account provides no measured density or achieved precision.[The Electronic Balance and Some Gravimetric Applications (The Density of Solids and Liquids, Pycnometry and Mass), NISTIR 5375, printed pp. 313–316, “Pycnometer Calibration”, Figure 5 and “Pycnometer Application”; p. 315 states that pycnometer data had not yet been analyzed](the-electronic-balance-and-some-gravimetric-applications-the-density-of-solids-and-liquids-pycnometry-and-mass-by-randall-m-schoonover-and-others.md)

The ideal accounting behind this [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md) can be derived without numerical observations. Let $m_0,m_w,m_x$ denote corrected [masses](mass.md) of the empty vessel, water-filled vessel and sample-filled vessel. At the same filling [temperature](temperature-split.md), with known reference water [mass density](density.md) $\rho_w$ and reproducibly filled [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) $V_c$,

$$
V_c=\frac{m_w-m_0}{\rho_w},\qquad
\rho_x=\frac{m_x-m_0}{V_c}
=\rho_w\frac{m_x-m_0}{m_w-m_0}.
$$

The empty-vessel subtraction removes vessel [mass](mass.md); the water filling sets [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md). Doubling $V_c$ for the same uniform sample doubles the sample [mass](mass.md) increment but preserves $\rho_x$. Uncorrected balance indications, trapped bubbles, incomplete filling or unequal filling [temperatures](temperature-split.md) break the simple identification with corrected [mass](mass.md) and a common $V_c$. This explains why replication is a statement about comparable states rather than arbitrary instrument readings.
