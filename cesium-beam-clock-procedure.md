# Cesium-beam clock procedure

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic clock](atomic-clock.md)

An oven supplies a collimated [caesium](caesium.md) beam. State-selecting magnets precede and follow two coherent [microwave](microwave.md) interaction regions; a weak static [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) separates sublevels. The clock transition connects $F=3,m_F=0$ and $F=4,m_F=0$. Transferred atoms reach a heated ionizing collector. A detector scan identifies the resonance before locking.[April 1971 edition, §5.3, pp. 36–38, Fig. 7](the-physical-basis-of-atomic-frequency-standards-by-a-s-risley.md)

Original functional diagram of the [cesium-beam clock procedure](cesium-beam-clock-procedure.md); magnetic selection, schematic paths.

Here $F$ labels the total [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) and $m_F$ its projection. The selected pair is a [two-level system](two-level-system.md) inside a larger set of atomic [energy levels](energy-level.md); treating every oven atom as a prepared [qubit](qubit.md) would overstate the preparation. A measured detector signal may be modeled as $S(\nu)=S_{\mathrm{bg}}+A\overline P(2\pi(\nu_0-\nu))$, with background $S_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and gain $A>0$. Background subtraction and stable transmission are necessary before interpreting the signal as a transition [probability](probability.md). The [velocity-averaged Ramsey line](velocity-averaged-ramsey-line.md) provides one explicit ideal example; its chosen dimensions are not historical specifications.
