# Sine

↑ **Parent:** [Trigonometric function](trigonometric-function.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine)

For a real [angle](angle.md) $\theta$, $\sin\theta$ is the vertical coordinate on the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) reached by a counterclockwise [rotation](rotation-mathematics.md) through $\theta$ [radians](radian.md). For an acute [angle](angle.md) in a [right triangle](right-triangle.md), it is the opposite side divided by the [hypotenuse](hypotenuse.md). In particular,

$$
\sin0=0,\qquad \sin(\pi/6)=\frac12,\qquad
\sin(\pi/2)=1,\qquad \sin\pi=0.
$$

The first-quarter rise and second-quarter fall are visible in the circle projection; the lower semicircle gives negative values.

Reflecting the [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) across the horizontal axis gives $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$, so [sine](sine.md) is an [odd function](odd-function.md). A full turn gives $\sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta$, making it a [periodic function](periodic-function.md). For real arguments, its range is $[-1,1]$ and its [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) occur at $\theta=k\pi$ for [integers](integer-split.md) $k$. The [cosine](cosine.md) describes the perpendicular coordinate of the same point, and the [Pythagorean trigonometric identity](pythagorean-trigonometric-identity.md) keeps their two values tied together.
