<h1 id="strict-godel-to-rosser-implication">Strict Gödel-to-Rosser implication</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Rosser sentence](rosser-sentence.md)

For the standard [Gödel sentence](godel-sentence.md) and [Rosser sentence](rosser-sentence.md), the formalized Rosser unprovability theorem and $G\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(T)$ yield $T\vdash G\to R$. If $T+\operatorname{Con}(T)$ is consistent, the converse is unprovable under the usual formalized incompleteness hypotheses. A proof of $R\to G$ would give $\Box\neg G\to\Box\neg R$. Formalized negative unprovability identifies $\neg\Box\neg R$ with $\operatorname{Con}(T)$ and $\neg\Box\neg G$ with $\operatorname{Con}(T+\operatorname{Con}(T))$. The strengthened theory would then prove its own consistency, contradicting the [Gödel second incompleteness theorem](godel-second-incompleteness-theorem.md). The consistency assumption belongs to this strictness conclusion.
