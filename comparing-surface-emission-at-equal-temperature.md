# Comparing surface emission at equal temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Emissivity](emissivity.md)

Oberlin College's documented demonstration heats a cube internally with a bulb and compares polished [aluminium](aluminium.md), dull [aluminium](aluminium.md), black-painted and white-painted faces at the same [temperature](temperature-split.md). The apparatus includes a [thermopile](thermopile.md) and a digital meter. Its preparation specifies 20 minutes of preheating and heater setting 6; these are instructions, not measured outcomes. The [thermopile](thermopile.md) supplies the electrical readout for the relative [thermal radiation](thermal-radiation.md) comparison. Cover it between readings: otherwise detector warming changes its reference [temperature](temperature-split.md). The account reports little difference between painted black and white faces and a larger contrast between shiny and painted faces. The comparison motivates a surface-dependent [emissivity](emissivity.md) and shows why visible color alone is insufficient.[Oberlin College, Leslie Cube, apparatus list, Description and Notes: four finishes, common temperature, preheat and detector-cover control](leslie-cube-by-oberlin-college.md)

Equal source [temperature](temperature-split.md) is the principal control: it holds the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](stefan-boltzmann-law.md) factor $\sigma T^4$ fixed. For a sharper comparison, also hold detector distance, viewing [angle](angle.md), viewed [area](surface-area.md) and surrounding [temperatures](temperature-split.md) fixed, and record a covered-detector baseline. These are additional diagnostic controls. Under those assumptions, a changed readout can reveal changed [directional emissivity](directional-emissivity.md) averaged over the detector's wavelength response. The demonstration supplies neither an absolute calibration nor a scan over all directions and [wavelengths](wavelength.md); it therefore does not directly measure [total hemispherical emissivity](total-hemispherical-emissivity.md) or establish the fourth-power law. Comparing cooling rates would additionally require separating [convection](convection-split.md), [thermal conduction](thermal-conduction.md) and [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) effects, as in [net radiative heat exchange](net-radiative-heat-exchange.md).
