# Continuous-wave nuclear magnetic resonance

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear magnetic resonance](nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md)

For constant drive, set the three [rotating frame](rotating-reference-frame.md) derivatives to zero. Substitution first gives $u=\Delta T_2v$, then $w=M_0-\omega_1T_1v$, hence

$$
v=\frac{\omega_1T_2M_0}{1+\Delta^2T_2^2+s},\qquad
s=\omega_1^2T_1T_2.
$$

The absorption is proportional to $\omega_1v$. With $s\ll1$, its frequency dependence is a [Cauchy distribution](cauchy-distribution.md) profile, often called a Lorentzian. With appreciable $s$, the response broadens and $w=M_0/(1+s)$ on resonance: [nuclear magnetic resonance saturation](nuclear-magnetic-resonance-saturation.md) limits the response. The short-pulse amplitude in the previous example is not a weak continuous drive; these are different operating regimes of the same equations.
