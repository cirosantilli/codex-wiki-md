# Compatibility of independent lifetime measurements

↑ **Parent:** [Chi-squared test](chi-squared-test.md)

If two independent approximately Gaussian [mean lifetime](mean-lifetime-physics.md) estimates have known [standard deviations](standard-deviation.md) $s_1,s_2$, equality of their true lifetimes implies that their difference has mean zero and [variance](variance-split.md) $s_1^2+s_2^2$. Its squared standardized difference therefore has a one-degree-of-freedom [chi-squared distribution](chi-squared-distribution.md), giving the statistic in the title. A chosen significance threshold converts its upper-tail [P-value](p-value.md) into a compatibility decision.

Testing a fitted decay curve against a fixed numerical lifetime is a different question: that test does not include the external measurement uncertainty. Comparing the two experiments requires both uncertainties or a joint likelihood with a common lifetime. Correlated estimates replace the denominator by $s_1^2+s_2^2-2\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat\tau_1,\widehat\tau_2)$.
