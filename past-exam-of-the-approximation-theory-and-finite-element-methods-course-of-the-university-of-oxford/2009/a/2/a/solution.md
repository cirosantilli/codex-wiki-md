<h1 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $t_1,\ldots,t_m$ be the distinct roots in $(0,1)$ at which $\phi_r$ changes sign, and put $q(x)=\prod_{j=1}^m(x-t_j)$. Both $\phi_r$ and $q$ change sign at each such point, while roots of even multiplicity do not change sign. Consequently $\phi_rq$ has one constant sign on $(0,1)$ except at its zeros, and is not identically zero. Its integral cannot vanish.

If $m<r$, however, orthogonality of the [monic orthogonal polynomial](../../../../../monic-orthogonal-polynomial.md) $\phi_r$ to every polynomial of lower degree gives $(\phi_r,q)=0$, a contradiction. Hence $m\geq r$. Since $\deg\phi_r=r$, there must be exactly $r$ such roots, each of multiplicity one. They exhaust the degree, leaving no complex, exterior, or endpoint roots. Thus **all roots are simple and lie strictly in $(0,1)$**, which proves the requested weaker inclusion in $[0,1]$. This is the sign-change proof of the [zeros of orthogonal polynomials](../../../../../zeros-of-orthogonal-polynomials.md) theorem.
