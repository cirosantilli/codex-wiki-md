<h1 id="a/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the standard convention that the [orthogonal polynomials](../../../../../orthogonal-polynomial-split.md) are nonzero. Their exact degrees then follow from the stated assumptions by induction: $\phi_0$ is a nonzero constant, and if $\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{r-1}$ span $P_{r-1}$, a nonzero $\phi_r\in P_r$ orthogonal to all of them cannot lie in $P_{r-1}$.

Let $t_1,\ldots,t_m$ be the distinct sign-changing roots of $\phi_r$ in $(0,1)$, and put $q(x)=\prod_{j=1}^m(x-t_j)$. Then $\phi_rq$ has constant sign between and across all its roots, and is not identically zero. Because $w>0$ in the interior, $\int_0^1w\phi_rq$ is nonzero. If $m<r$, orthogonality to $P_{r-1}$ would make this integral zero. Thus $m\geq r$.

A degree-$r$ [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) cannot have more than $r$ roots counted with multiplicity, so **all $r$ roots are real, simple, and strictly inside $(0,1)$**. This is the standard [zeros of orthogonal polynomials](../../../../../zeros-of-orthogonal-polynomials.md) argument, with the positive weight ensuring strict noncancellation.
