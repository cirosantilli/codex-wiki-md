<h1 id="a/3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a real-valued linear error functional annihilating $P_n$, the [Peano kernel](../../../../../peano-kernel.md) is

$$
K(t)=\frac1{n!}X[(x-t)_+^n],
$$

where $x$ is the argument on which the functional acts. Under the standard boundedness and extension hypotheses, the [Peano kernel theorem](../../../../../peano-kernel-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{X[u]=\int_0^1K(t)u^{(n+1)}(t)\,dt.}
$$

For example, a bounded functional on continuous [functions](../../../../../function-split.md), represented by a finite signed measure and extended to the truncated powers, has this representation. [Quadrature error](../../../../../quadrature-error.md) functionals have precisely this structure.

The source's bare linearity assumption on $C^{n+1}$ is not by itself a complete theorem hypothesis. Truncated powers need not belong to that domain, and interchange of the functional with an integral needs justification. For instance $X[u]=u^{(n+1)}(t_0)$ annihilates $P_n$ but would require a Dirac point mass rather than an ordinary integrable kernel. The statement above supplies the usual functional class needed for the requested theorem and applies directly to part (c).
