Here the [orthogonal group](../../../../../../orthogonal-group.md) is the real [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $O_n(\mathbb R)$. Differentiating $g(t)^Tg(t)=I$ at zero gives $X^T+X=0$. Conversely a real [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md) satisfies $(e^{tX})^Te^{tX}=e^{-tX}e^{tX}=I$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{L(O_n)=\{X\in M_n(\mathbb R):X^T=-X\},\qquad\dim_{\mathbb R}L(O_n)=n(n-1)/2}.
$$

For $n\ge2$ this is not a complex Lie subalgebra of $M_n(\mathbb C)$: multiplying a nonzero real member by $i$ leaves this real subspace. For $n=1$ the zero algebra is the trivial complex [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) as well. The complex [orthogonal group](../../../../../../orthogonal-group.md) would be a different [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) from the real [orthogonal group](../../../../../../orthogonal-group.md) used here.
