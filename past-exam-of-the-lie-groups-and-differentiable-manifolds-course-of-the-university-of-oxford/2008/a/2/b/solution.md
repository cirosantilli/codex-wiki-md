<h1 id="a/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For fixed $X\in L(G)$, set $b(t)=\phi(e^{tX})$. The [homomorphism](../../../../../homomorphism.md) property gives $b(s+t)=b(s)b(t)$ and $b(0)=I$. Its initial derivative is $L(\phi)(X)=D\phi_I(X)$. The [one-parameter subgroup](../../../../../one-parameter-subgroup.md) uniqueness theorem gives

$$
b(t)=e^{tL(\phi)(X)},\qquad\boxed{\phi(e^X)=e^{L(\phi)(X)}}.
$$

For the [Adjoint representation](../../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra.md), $\operatorname{Ad}_gY=gYg^{-1}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ad}_XY=\left.\frac d{dt}(e^{tX}Ye^{-tX})\right|_{t=0}=XY-YX=[X,Y]}.
$$

The differential $L(\phi)$ is a real linear map of Lie algebras; the bracket identity follows by differentiating the compatibility of conjugation with $\phi$.
