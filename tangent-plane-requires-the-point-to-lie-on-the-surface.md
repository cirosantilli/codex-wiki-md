# Tangent plane requires the point to lie on the surface

↑ **Parent:** [Tangent plane](tangent-plane.md)

For a [level set](level-set.md) $G=0$, the [tangent plane](tangent-plane.md) formula $\nabla G(p)\cdot(x-p)=0$ requires both that $p$ belongs to the [surface](topological-surface-split.md) and that its [gradient](gradient.md) is nonzero. Evaluating the gradient at an off-surface point instead gives a plane tangent to the level set through that different point. For example $G=x^2y-4ze^{x+y}+11$ has $G(2,-2,9)=-33$, so the gradient plane there cannot be a tangent plane to $G=0$. Holding the first two coordinates fixed gives the on-surface point $(2,-2,3/4)$ and its plane $11x-y+4z=27$.
