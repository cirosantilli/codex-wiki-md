<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $A=\int_0^1 f$. Then

$$
Rf(x)=Ax-\int_0^x f(s)\,ds.
$$

The theorem on [differentiation of an indefinite Lebesgue integral](../../../../../differentiation-of-an-indefinite-lebesgue-integral.md) makes this an [absolutely continuous function](../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) on $[0,1]$, with

$$
\boxed{(Rf)'=A-f\text{ almost everywhere},\qquad Rf(0)=Rf(1)=0.}
$$

Both $A-f$ and $Rf$ belong to the [L1 space](../../../../../l1-space.md): for example, $|Rf(x)|\leq|A|+\|f\|_{L^1}\leq2\|f\|_{L^1}$. The ordinary [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) of an [absolutely continuous function](../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) is its [weak derivative](../../../../../weak-derivative.md), as [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) against a [test function](../../../../../test-function.md) shows. Therefore $\boxed{Rf\in W^{1,1}(0,1)}$, the indicated [Sobolev space](../../../../../sobolev-space-split.md).
