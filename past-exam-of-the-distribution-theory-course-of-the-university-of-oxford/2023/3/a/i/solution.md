<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be an [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) and $(U_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}$ an [open cover](../../../../../open-cover.md) of $\Omega$. There exist nonnegative [smooth functions](../../../../../smooth-function.md) $(\chi_j)_{j\in J}$ and choices $\alpha(j)$ such that

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}_\Omega\chi_j\subset U_{\alpha(j)},\qquad
\sum_{j\in J}\chi_j(x)=1\quad(x\in\Omega),}
$$

and the family of [supports](../../../../../support.md) is locally finite: every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many of them. This is a smooth [partition of unity](../../../../../partition-of-unity.md) subordinate to the cover, allowing a refinement of its indexing. In Euclidean [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md), one can choose $J$ countable and the individual [supports](../../../../../support.md) compact in their assigned cover members. Local finiteness ensures that the sum is smooth and that only finitely many terms meet any given [compact set](../../../../../compact-space.md).
