<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\phi\in\mathcal D(\omega)$, let $\widetilde\phi$ be its extension by zero to $\Omega$. Its [compact support](../../../../../compact-support.md) is contained in $\omega$, so this extension remains a [test function](../../../../../test-function.md). The [restriction of a distribution](../../../../../restriction-of-a-distribution.md) is

$$
\boxed{\langle u|_\omega,\phi\rangle=\langle u,\widetilde\phi\rangle.}
$$

Extension by zero preserves convergence in the [space of test functions](../../../../../space-of-test-functions.md), so this defines a [distribution](../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) on $\omega$.

Choose a [partition of unity](../../../../../partition-of-unity.md) $(\chi_j)$ subordinate to the neighborhoods $\omega_x$ from the hypothesis. For an arbitrary $\phi\in\mathcal D(\Omega)$, local finiteness and [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) of $\operatorname{supp}\phi$ give a finite decomposition $\phi=\sum_j\chi_j\phi$. Each term is a [test function](../../../../../test-function.md) supported in one $\omega_x$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle v,\phi\rangle=\sum_j\langle v|_{\omega_{x(j)}},\chi_j\phi\rangle=0.}
$$

Since this holds for every [test function](../../../../../test-function.md), $v=0$. This is the [locality of distributions](../../../../../locality-of-distributions.md).
