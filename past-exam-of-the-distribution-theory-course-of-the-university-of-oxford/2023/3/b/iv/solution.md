<h1 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Interchange the two [Wirtinger derivatives](../../../../../wirtinger-derivatives.md) in part (i). The same real [fundamental solution of the Laplace equation](../../../../../fundamental-solution-of-the-laplace-equation.md) $L$ gives

$$
4\partial_{\bar z}L=\frac{x+iy}{\pi(x^2+y^2)}=\frac1{\pi\bar z},\qquad
\partial_z(4\partial_{\bar z}L)=\Delta L=\delta_0.
$$

The calculation is valid for [distributions](../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) by the same vanishing [boundary term](../../../../../boundary-term.md) estimate. Any other [fundamental solution](../../../../../fundamental-solution-of-a-linear-differential-operator.md) differs by a [distribution](../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) $H$ with $\partial_zH=0$. Then $\Delta H=0$, so the regularity argument of part (ii) makes $H$ smooth, and its [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) make it an [antiholomorphic function](../../../../../antiholomorphic-function.md). On the whole plane it can be written $H(z)=h(\bar z)$ with $h$ an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md). Conversely every such $H$ has $\partial_zH=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F(z)=\frac1{\pi\bar z}+h(\bar z),\qquad h\text{ any entire function}.}
$$
