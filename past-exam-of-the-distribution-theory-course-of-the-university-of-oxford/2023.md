↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

A [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) on $(a,b)$ is a continuous [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) on the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions) $\mathcal D(a,b)=C_c^\infty(a,b)$. It has [order of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#order-of-a-distribution) at most $m$ if, for every [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space) $K\subset(a,b)$, there is $C_K$ such that

$$
|\langle u,\phi\rangle|\leq C_K\max_{0\leq j\leq m}\|\phi^{(j)}\|_\infty
\qquad(\operatorname{supp}\phi\subseteq K).
$$

The [order of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#order-of-a-distribution) is the least such $m$. When saying “of order $m$” means merely that this estimate holds, the wording allows a smaller least order.

For each such $K$, the [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) theorem supplies a nonnegative $\chi\in\mathcal D(a,b)$ equal to one near $K$. If $M=\|\phi\|_\infty$ and $\operatorname{supp}\phi\subseteq K$, then $M\chi\pm\phi\geq0$ everywhere. Positivity of the [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) $V$ therefore gives

$$
0\leq M V(\chi)\pm V(\phi),\qquad
\boxed{|V(\phi)|\leq V(\chi)\|\phi\|_\infty.}
$$

Here $V(\chi)\geq0$ is finite and independent of $\phi$. This is precisely the local bound for an [order-zero distribution](../distribution-theory.md#order-zero-distribution), so the given [positive distribution](../fourier-analysis.md#positive-distribution) is continuous on the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions). The zero [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) also satisfies the estimate.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

The [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) of $u$ is defined by

$$
\boxed{\langle u',\phi\rangle=-\langle u,\phi'\rangle\quad(\phi\in\mathcal D(a,b)).}
$$

It is a [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) because $\phi\mapsto\phi'$ is a continuous map of the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions) into itself. If $f\in C^1(a,b)$ represents a [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution), [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives

$$
\langle f',\phi\rangle=-\int_a^b f\phi'=\int_a^b f'\phi.
$$

The [boundary term](../calculus.md#boundary-term) vanishes because $\phi$ has [compact support](../function.md#compact-support). Thus the [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) agrees with the ordinary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative).

For $\operatorname{supp}\phi\subseteq K$, the assumed [order of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#order-of-a-distribution) estimate gives

$$
|\langle u',\phi\rangle|\leq C_K\max_{0\leq j\leq m}\|\phi^{(j+1)}\|_\infty
\leq C_K\max_{0\leq j\leq m+1}\|\phi^{(j)}\|_\infty.
$$

Consequently $\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(u')\leq m+1}$.

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

On each interval $[k,k+1]$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, the [function](../function.md) is $d(x)=x-k$ on $[k,k+\tfrac12]$ and $d(x)=k+1-x$ on $[k+\tfrac12,k+1]$. Its ordinary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) exists off $\tfrac12\mathbb Z$ and is the [locally integrable function](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function)

$$
s(x)=\begin{cases}
1,&k<x<k+\tfrac12,\\
-1,&k+\tfrac12<x<k+1,
\end{cases}\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Assign arbitrary values at the endpoints. For a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi$, split $-\int d\phi'$ at these corners and apply [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) on each piece. The adjacent [boundary terms](../calculus.md#boundary-term) cancel because $d$ is continuous, and only finitely many pieces meet the [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) of $\phi$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\langle d',\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)\phi(x)\,dx,\qquad d'=s.}
$$

The [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) is exactly the [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) of the ordinary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) defined [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). There are no [Dirac delta distributions](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) in this first [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative): corners change slope, while a jump of the [function](../function.md) itself would create a point mass.

<h4 id="1/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/a/iv)

The bound $0\leq d_n\leq2^{-n-1}$ proves [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence) of the series by the [Weierstrass M-test](../probability-and-statistics.md#weierstrass-m-test). Each summand is continuous and satisfies $d_n(x+1)=d_n(x)$, so its sum $t$, the [Takagi function](../calculus.md#blancmange-curve), is continuous and has period one.

For $n\geq m$, both $2^nu_m$ and $2^nv_m$ are integers, giving $d_n(u_m)=d_n(v_m)=0$. Therefore

$$
S_m:=\frac{t(v_m)-t(u_m)}{v_m-u_m}
=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\frac{d_n(v_m)-d_n(u_m)}{v_m-u_m}.
$$

The corners of $d_n$ occur on the grid $2^{-n-1}\mathbb Z$. When $n<m$, that grid is contained in $2^{-m}\mathbb Z$, so the interior of $[u_m,v_m]$ contains no corner of $d_n$. Its slope on this interval is consequently $+1$ or $-1$.

The interval $[u_{m+1},v_{m+1}]$ is a half of $[u_m,v_m]$. All the old slopes persist on this half, and the new summand contributes one more slope in $\{\pm1\}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{S_{m+1}-S_m\in\{\pm1\}.}
$$

If $t$ had a finite [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) $L$ at $x_0$, write $t(y)=t(x_0)+L(y-x_0)+r(y)$ with $r(y)=o(|y-x_0|)$. Since $u_m\leq x_0<v_m$ and both endpoints tend to $x_0$,

$$
|S_m-L|\leq\frac{|r(v_m)|+|r(u_m)|}{v_m-u_m}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Indeed $|v_m-x_0|+|u_m-x_0|=v_m-u_m$; when $u_m=x_0$, its remainder is zero. This is the [secants straddling a differentiability point](../analysis.md#secants-straddling-a-differentiability-point) argument, including dyadic $x_0$. But the displayed increments prevent $S_m$ from converging. Hence $\boxed{t\text{ is nowhere differentiable}}$, using periodicity to cover all real $x_0$.

For the [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative), set $r_n(x)=s(2^nx)$, using $s$ from part (iii). The change of variable in the [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) pairing gives $d_n'=r_n$; these are the [Rademacher functions](../probability-distribution.md#rademacher-function). [Uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence) implies [weak convergence of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#weak-convergence-of-distributions), and the [continuity of distributional differentiation](../distribution-theory.md#continuity-of-distributional-differentiation) allows termwise differentiation in that topology. Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{t'=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_n\quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb R),\qquad
\langle t',\phi\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb R}t(x)\phi'(x)\,dx.}
$$

This series assertion is a [distributional convergence](../distribution-theory.md#weak-convergence-of-distributions) statement. Its remainder satisfies the concrete estimate

$$
\left|\left\langle t'-\sum_{n=0}^{N}r_n,\phi\right\rangle\right|
\leq2^{-N-1}\|\phi'\|_{L^1}\longrightarrow0.
$$

It supplies a well-defined [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative) even though the ordinary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) exists nowhere.

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Choose $R>0$ with $\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset[-R,R]$. Evenness of $f$ changes the symmetrically truncated integral into

$$
\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(x)}x\phi(x)\,dx
=\int_\varepsilon^R f(x)\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(-x)}x\,dx.
$$

The [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) gives $|\phi(x)-\phi(-x)|\leq2x\|\phi'\|_\infty$. Since $f$ is continuous, it is bounded on $[0,R]$. Thus the [Cauchy principal value](../complex-analysis.md#cauchy-principal-value) exists and

$$
\boxed{\left\langle\operatorname{pv}\frac{f(x)}x,\phi\right\rangle
=\int_0^R f(x)\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(-x)}x\,dx.}
$$

This expression is linear in the [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) and satisfies

$$
\left|\left\langle\operatorname{pv}\frac{f(x)}x,\phi\right\rangle\right|
\leq2R\max_{[0,R]}|f|\,\|\phi'\|_\infty.
$$

For each fixed [compact support](../function.md#compact-support), choose one $R$ once and for all. The estimate proves that the [even-weight principal-value distribution](../distribution-theory.md#even-weight-principal-value-distribution) has [order of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#order-of-a-distribution) at most one.

If $\phi(0)=0$, the [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) also gives $|\phi(x)|\leq|x|\|\phi'\|_\infty$. Hence $f(x)\phi(x)/x$ is absolutely integrable near zero and has [compact support](../function.md#compact-support). The truncated integrals tend to its ordinary [Lebesgue integral](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integral), so

$$
\boxed{\left\langle\operatorname{pv}\frac{f(x)}x,\phi\right\rangle
=\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{f(x)}x\phi(x)\,dx\quad\text{if }\phi(0)=0.}
$$

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

**No.** Consider the continuous even [function](../function.md)

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}
0,&x=0,\\
1/\log(1/|x|),&0<|x|\leq e^{-1},\\
1,&|x|\geq e^{-1}.
\end{cases}
$$

The formulas agree at $|x|=e^{-1}$, and $f(x)\to0$ at zero. Part (i) defines its [even-weight principal-value distribution](../distribution-theory.md#even-weight-principal-value-distribution). However,

$$
\boxed{\int_0^{e^{-1}}\frac{|f(x)|}{x}\,dx
=\int_1^\infty\frac{ds}{s}=\infty,\qquad s=\log(1/x).}
$$

If this were a [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) represented by $g\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R)$, testing on either side of zero would force $g=f/x$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) there, by uniqueness of the [locally integrable function](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) representing a [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution). Changing the value at zero cannot remove the displayed divergence, contradicting [local integrability](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) of $g$.

More generally, the exact condition is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{pv}(f/x)\text{ is regular}\iff
\int_0^r\frac{|f(x)|}{x}\,dx<\infty\text{ for some }r>0.}
$$

Necessity is the preceding argument; sufficiency follows because $f/x$ is then a [locally integrable function](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) and its truncated integrals converge absolutely. The value $f(0)=0$ alone provides no such rate of decay.

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

Convergence $\phi_j\to\phi$ in the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions) means that all [supports](../function.md#support) lie in one [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space) $K\subset\mathbb R$ and $\|\phi_j^{(r)}-\phi^{(r)}\|_\infty\to0$ for every integer $r\geq0$. An eventual common [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) is equivalent, since finitely many initial [supports](../function.md#support) can be added to $K$.

Write the standard [mollifier](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) as $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$, where $\rho\in\mathcal D(\mathbb R)$ is nonnegative, supported in $[-1,1]$, and has integral one. For $0<\varepsilon\leq1$, the [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) $\rho_\varepsilon*\phi$ has [support](../function.md#support) in the fixed [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space) $\operatorname{supp}\phi+[-1,1]$. The given [convolution derivative identity](../fourier-analysis.md#differentiation-commutes-with-convolution) yields

$$
(\rho_\varepsilon*\phi)^{(r)}(x)-\phi^{(r)}(x)
=\int_{-1}^{1}\rho(y)\bigl[\phi^{(r)}(x-\varepsilon y)-\phi^{(r)}(x)\bigr],dy.
$$

Each $\phi^{(r)}$ is uniformly continuous; more explicitly, the [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) bounds the absolute value by $\varepsilon\|\phi^{(r+1)}\|_\infty\int|y|\rho(y)\,dy$, uniformly in $x$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\rho_\varepsilon*\phi\longrightarrow\phi\text{ in }\mathcal D(\mathbb R).}
$$

If $\phi_j\to0$ in the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions) and $K$ is a common [compact support](../function.md#compact-support), then $\|\phi_j\|_{L^1}\leq|K|\|\phi_j\|_\infty\to0$, where $|K|$ is its [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure).

The [mollifiers](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) themselves have no limit in the [L1 space](../measure-theory.md#l1-space). If $\rho_\varepsilon\to g$ in that [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm), then for each $\delta>0$ the integral of $|g|$ on $\{|x|>\delta\}$ is zero, because $\rho_\varepsilon$ vanishes there for $\varepsilon<\delta$. Hence $g=0$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). This contradicts $\|\rho_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}=1$. Therefore $\boxed{\rho_\varepsilon\text{ does not converge in }L^1(\mathbb R)}$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

The [weak convergence of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#weak-convergence-of-distributions) $u_j\to u$ means

$$
\langle u_j,\phi\rangle\longrightarrow\langle u,\phi\rangle
\quad\text{for every }\phi\in\mathcal D(\mathbb R).
$$

For $\theta\in\mathcal D(\mathbb R)$, its [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) with a [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) is the [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function)

$$
(\theta*u)(x)=\langle u_y,\theta(x-y)\rangle.
$$

The subscript records the variable on which the [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) acts. With $\check\theta(x)=\theta(-x)$, its [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) satisfies the useful pairing identity

$$
\langle\theta*u,\phi\rangle=\langle u,\check\theta*\phi\rangle.
$$

Indeed integrating $\theta(x-y)\phi(x)$ in $x$ gives $(\check\theta*\phi)(y)$; continuity of the [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) justifies moving the pairing through that integral, by approximation with sums of [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) in one common [compact support](../function.md#compact-support).

Part (i), applied to the reflected [mollifier](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier), gives $\check\rho_\varepsilon*\phi\to\phi$ in the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions). Continuity of $u$ therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\langle\rho_\varepsilon*u,\phi\rangle
=\langle u,\check\rho_\varepsilon*\phi\rangle\longrightarrow\langle u,\phi\rangle,
\qquad\rho_\varepsilon*u\to u\text{ in }\mathcal D'.}
$$

Reflection is harmless even if the chosen standard [mollifier](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) is not even.

For the [mollifiers](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) themselves, a change of variable gives

$$
\langle\rho_\varepsilon,\phi\rangle=\int\rho(y)\phi(\varepsilon y)\,dy\longrightarrow\phi(0)\int\rho(y)\,dy=\phi(0).
$$

Thus $\boxed{\rho_\varepsilon\to\delta_0\text{ in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb R)}$, with limit the [Dirac delta distribution](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function), despite the failure of convergence in the [L1 space](../measure-theory.md#l1-space).

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

The [L1-normalized dilation of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#l1-normalized-dilation-of-a-distribution) is

$$
\boxed{\langle u_\varepsilon,\phi\rangle=\langle u,\phi(\varepsilon\,\cdot)\rangle.}
$$

For a [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) this is the [function](../function.md) $u_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}u(x/\varepsilon)$. In terms of the wiki's [dilation of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#dilation-of-a-distribution) convention, $u_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_{1/\varepsilon}u$; the normalization preserves the [L1 space](../measure-theory.md#l1-space) norm.

If $u\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, the [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem), with dominating [integrable function](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integrable-function) $|u(y)|\|\phi\|_\infty$, gives

$$
\langle u_\varepsilon,\phi\rangle=\int u(y)\phi(\varepsilon y)\,dy
\longrightarrow\phi(0)\int u(y)\,dy.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{u_\varepsilon\longrightarrow\left(\int_{\mathbb R}u\right)\delta_0
\quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb R).}
$$

An arbitrary [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) need not have this behavior. Take the constant [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) $u=1$. Then $u_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}$, so for a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) with integral one,

$$
\langle u_\varepsilon,\phi\rangle=\varepsilon^{-1}\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$

Hence $\boxed{u=1\text{ gives no distributional limit}}$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Put $A=\int_0^1 f$. Then

$$
Rf(x)=Ax-\int_0^x f(s)\,ds.
$$

The theorem on [differentiation of an indefinite Lebesgue integral](../measure-theory.md#differentiation-of-an-indefinite-lebesgue-integral) makes this an [absolutely continuous function](../sobolev-space.md#absolutely-continuous-function) on $[0,1]$, with

$$
\boxed{(Rf)'=A-f\text{ almost everywhere},\qquad Rf(0)=Rf(1)=0.}
$$

Both $A-f$ and $Rf$ belong to the [L1 space](../measure-theory.md#l1-space): for example, $|Rf(x)|\leq|A|+\|f\|_{L^1}\leq2\|f\|_{L^1}$. The ordinary [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) of an [absolutely continuous function](../sobolev-space.md#absolutely-continuous-function) is its [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative), as [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) against a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) shows. Therefore $\boxed{Rf\in W^{1,1}(0,1)}$, the indicated [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md).

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Using $(Rf)'=\int_0^1f-f$ and [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts), with $\phi$ a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function), gives

$$
\int_0^1Rf\,\phi'=\int_0^1f\phi-\left(\int_0^1f\right)\left(\int_0^1\phi\right).
$$

For each $k\geq1$, apply the same identity to $R^kf$ and the [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi^{(k)}$. Since $\int_0^1\phi^{(k)}=0$, it gives

$$
\int_0^1R^{k+1}f\,\phi^{(k+1)}=\int_0^1R^kf\,\phi^{(k)}.
$$

Starting from $k=1$ and iterating proves

$$
\boxed{\int_0^1f\phi-\left(\int_0^1f\right)\left(\int_0^1\phi\right)
=\int_0^1R^kf\,\phi^{(k)}\quad(k\geq1).}
$$

The intermediate means disappear because derivatives of a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) have zero integral; no assumption that $R^kf$ has zero mean is needed.

For the bound, write

$$
Rf(x)=\int_0^1\bigl(x-\mathbf1_{\{t<x\}}\bigr)f(t)\,dt.
$$

The [triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) and [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem) imply

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|Rf\|_{L^1}
&\leq\int_0^1|f(t)|\left[\int_0^t x\,dx+\int_t^1(1-x)\,dx\right]dt\\
&=\frac12\int_0^1\bigl[t^2+(1-t)^2\bigr]|f(t)|\,dt
\leq\frac12\|f\|_{L^1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\boxed{c=\tfrac12\text{ is valid}}$, and every iterate $R^kf$ is in the [L1 space](../measure-theory.md#l1-space).

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

To repeat [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) across whole periods, choose [periodic primitives with zero mean](../function.md#periodic-primitive-with-zero-mean). Set $h_0=f$, and recursively define

$$
h_{r+1}=Rh_r-\int_0^1Rh_r.
$$

Every $h_r$ has zero mean. Part (i) gives $h_{r+1}'=-h_r$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), and $h_{r+1}(0)=h_{r+1}(1)$ because $Rh_r$ vanishes at both endpoints. Its [periodic extension](../function.md#periodic-extension) is consequently locally an [absolutely continuous function](../sobolev-space.md#absolutely-continuous-function), with no jump at the integers. Part (ii) gives the uniform estimate

$$
\|h_{r+1}\|_{L^1}\leq2\|Rh_r\|_{L^1}\leq\|h_r\|_{L^1}\leq\|f\|_{L^1}.
$$

The [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) identity on the whole line is $D\overline h_{r+1}=-\overline h_r$. After scaling by the integer $j$ and repeating [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) $N$ times,

$$
\int_0^1\overline f(jx)\phi(x)\,dx
=j^{-N}\int_0^1\overline h_N(jx)\phi^{(N)}(x)\,dx.
$$

The [boundary terms](../calculus.md#boundary-term) vanish because $\phi$ has [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) in $(0,1)$. The interval contains exactly $j$ periods, so

$$
\int_0^1|\overline h_N(jx)|\,dx=\int_0^1|h_N(t)|\,dt.
$$

Taking $N=m+1$ therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\left|j^m\int_0^1f_j\phi\right|
\leq j^{-1}\|f\|_{L^1}\|\phi^{(m+1)}\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.}
$$

This [rapid distributional decay of periodic oscillations](../distribution-theory.md#rapid-distributional-decay-of-periodic-oscillations) uses only [integrability](../measure-theory.md#integrability) of $f$ and smoothness of the [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function).

<h4 id="2/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/b/iv)

Let $M=\sup_j j^{-m}\|g_j\|_{L^1}<\infty$. For each $j$, apply the [periodic primitives with zero mean](../function.md#periodic-primitive-with-zero-mean) construction of part (iii) to $g_j$, writing $h_{j,0}=g_j$ and

$$
h_{j,r+1}=Rh_{j,r}-\int_0^1Rh_{j,r}.
$$

The estimates are independent of $j$: $\|h_{j,r}\|_{L^1}\leq\|g_j\|_{L^1}$. With $N=m+1$, the same repeated [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) gives, for every [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi\in\mathcal D(0,1)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\langle u_j,\phi\rangle|
&=j^{-N}\left|\int_0^1\overline h_{j,N}(jx)\phi^{(N)}(x)\,dx\right|\\
&\leq j^{-m-1}\|g_j\|_{L^1}\|\phi^{(m+1)}\|_\infty
\leq\frac{M}{j}\|\phi^{(m+1)}\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is precisely [weak convergence of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#weak-convergence-of-distributions), so $\boxed{u_j\to0\text{ in }\mathcal D'(0,1)}$. The extra [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) beyond the growth exponent $m$ is what ensures decay.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) and $(U_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}$ an [open cover](../topology.md#open-cover) of $\Omega$. There exist nonnegative [smooth functions](../analysis.md#smooth-function) $(\chi_j)_{j\in J}$ and choices $\alpha(j)$ such that

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}_\Omega\chi_j\subset U_{\alpha(j)},\qquad
\sum_{j\in J}\chi_j(x)=1\quad(x\in\Omega),}
$$

and the family of [supports](../function.md#support) is locally finite: every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many of them. This is a smooth [partition of unity](../smooth-manifold.md#partition-of-unity) subordinate to the cover, allowing a refinement of its indexing. In Euclidean [open sets](../topology.md#open-set), one can choose $J$ countable and the individual [supports](../function.md#support) compact in their assigned cover members. Local finiteness ensures that the sum is smooth and that only finitely many terms meet any given [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

If $\phi\in\mathcal D(\omega)$, let $\widetilde\phi$ be its extension by zero to $\Omega$. Its [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) is contained in $\omega$, so this extension remains a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function). The [restriction of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#restriction-of-a-distribution) is

$$
\boxed{\langle u|_\omega,\phi\rangle=\langle u,\widetilde\phi\rangle.}
$$

Extension by zero preserves convergence in the [space of test functions](../distribution-theory.md#space-of-test-functions), so this defines a [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) on $\omega$.

Choose a [partition of unity](../smooth-manifold.md#partition-of-unity) $(\chi_j)$ subordinate to the neighborhoods $\omega_x$ from the hypothesis. For an arbitrary $\phi\in\mathcal D(\Omega)$, local finiteness and [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) of $\operatorname{supp}\phi$ give a finite decomposition $\phi=\sum_j\chi_j\phi$. Each term is a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) supported in one $\omega_x$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle v,\phi\rangle=\sum_j\langle v|_{\omega_{x(j)}},\chi_j\phi\rangle=0.}
$$

Since this holds for every [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function), $v=0$. This is the [locality of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#locality-of-distributions).

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

Define two [open sets](../topology.md#open-set) by

$$
Z(u)=\bigcup\{U\subseteq\Omega:U\text{ open},\ u|_U=0\},\qquad
G(u)=\bigcup\{U\subseteq\Omega:U\text{ open},\ u|_U\in C^\infty(U)\}.
$$

The [support of a distribution](../distribution-theory.md#support-of-a-distribution) and its [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) are

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}u=\Omega\setminus Z(u),\qquad
\operatorname{sing\,supp}u=\Omega\setminus G(u).}
$$

Both are relatively closed because the removed sets are open. The [locality of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#locality-of-distributions) gives $u|_{Z(u)}=0$. On an overlap of two open smoothness regions, the two [smooth functions](../analysis.md#smooth-function) represent the same [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis), so their difference is zero [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) and hence everywhere by [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function). They therefore join to one [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function) on $G(u)$. The same [partition of unity](../smooth-manifold.md#partition-of-unity) argument shows that it represents $u$ there.

For $u=\mathbf1_\omega$, openness of $\omega$ implies

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}\mathbf1_\omega=\overline\omega^{\,\Omega}.}
$$

Indeed every neighborhood of a point in this relative closure meets $\omega$ in an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) containing a nonnegative [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) with positive integral; outside the closure the [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) is identically zero near the point.

The [singular support of an indicator function](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support-of-an-indicator-function) needs a measure-sensitive boundary. For a [Lebesgue measurable set](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measurable-set) $A\subseteq\Omega$, define its [essential closure](../measure-theory.md#essential-closure) by

$$
\operatorname{esscl}_\Omega A
=\{x\in\Omega:|A\cap B_r(x)|>0\text{ whenever }r>0\text{ and }B_r(x)\subseteq\Omega\}.
$$

Then the general answer is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{sing\,supp}\mathbf1_\omega
=\overline\omega^{\,\Omega}\cap\operatorname{esscl}_\Omega(\Omega\setminus\omega).}
$$

Outside this intersection, some ball has either no points of $\omega$ or a complement of [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) zero. The [regular distribution](../distribution-theory.md#regular-distribution) is respectively the constant zero or one on that ball and is smooth. Conversely, at a point of the intersection, every sufficiently small ball has positive [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) in both sets. If a [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function) represented the [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) there, its values would be in $\{0,1\}$ everywhere: any other value, by [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function), would give a ball of positive [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) on which equality [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) fails. On a connected ball such a continuous two-valued [function](../function.md) is constant, contradicting the two positive measures.

In particular, the answer need not be the full topological [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). For $\Omega=\mathbb R^n$ and $\omega=\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$, the [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) is the constant one [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), so its [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) is empty although the [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is $\{0\}$. The full relative [boundary](../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is the answer when every neighborhood of each boundary point contains a positive [Lebesgue measure](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) portion of the complement, as for an [open set](../topology.md#open-set) with smooth boundary.

<h4 id="3/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/a/iv)

Let $u,v\in\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^n)$, with at least one a [compactly supported distribution](../distribution-theory.md#compactly-supported-distribution), so their [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) is defined. The [support and singular support rules for convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#support-and-singular-support-rules-for-convolution) are

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}(u*v)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}u+\operatorname{supp}v,\qquad
\operatorname{sing\,supp}(u*v)\subseteq
\operatorname{sing\,supp}u+\operatorname{sing\,supp}v.}
$$

Here $A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}$ is the [Minkowski sum](../geometry-and-topology.md#minkowski-addition). The compact-factor hypothesis also ensures that these sums of closed sets are closed. More generally, the same rules hold when addition is a [proper map](../cohomology.md#proper-map) on $\operatorname{supp}u\times\operatorname{supp}v$, which is another sufficient condition for defining the [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution). The second inclusion explains why a compactly supported [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function) smooths every [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) under [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Write $z=x+iy$, $r=|z|$, and $L=(4\pi)^{-1}\log(x^2+y^2)$. The [Wirtinger derivatives](../analysis.md#wirtinger-derivatives) satisfy $4\partial_{\bar z}\partial_z=\Delta$. Away from zero, ordinary differentiation gives

$$
4\partial_z L=2(\partial_x-i\partial_y)L
=\frac{x-iy}{\pi(x^2+y^2)}=\frac1{\pi z}.
$$

This equality also holds for [distributional derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative). To check that no [Dirac delta distribution](../distribution-theory.md#dirac-delta-function) was lost at zero, integrate by parts outside the disk $r\leq\varepsilon$ against a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function). The new [boundary term](../calculus.md#boundary-term) has absolute value at most $C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|\|\phi\|_\infty\to0$. Both $L$ and its displayed first [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative), of size $O(r^{-1})$, are [locally integrable functions](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) in two dimensions, so the remaining integrals converge to their full-plane values.

Using the supplied [fundamental solution of the Laplace equation](../partial-differential-equation.md#fundamental-solution-of-the-laplace-equation) identity $\Delta L=\delta_0$, and commutation of [distributional derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative), now gives

$$
\boxed{E=4\partial_zL=\frac1{\pi z},\qquad
\partial_{\bar z}E=4\partial_{\bar z}\partial_zL=\Delta L=\delta_0.}
$$

Thus the [Cauchy kernel fundamental solution](../distribution-theory.md#cauchy-kernel-fundamental-solution) has exactly the required normalization.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

If $F$ is any other [fundamental solution](../distribution-theory.md#fundamental-solution-of-a-linear-differential-operator), then $H=F-E$ satisfies $\partial_{\bar z}H=0$ as a [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis). Applying $4\partial_z$ gives $\Delta H=0$. [Elliptic regularity](../distribution-theory.md#elliptic-regularity) for the [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian) implies that the initially arbitrary [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) $H$ is a [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function). Its equation is then the ordinary [Cauchy-Riemann equation](../analysis.md#cauchy-riemann-equations), so $H$ is an [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function).

For a direct justification of the needed regularity, choose a radial [mollifier](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) $\eta_r$. Locally, $H*\eta_\varepsilon$ is a smooth [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function). On a ball with sufficient room for both radii, the [mean value property](../partial-differential-equation.md#mean-value-property-for-harmonic-functions) gives $(H*\eta_\varepsilon)*\eta_r=H*\eta_\varepsilon$. Passing to the [weak convergence of distributions](../distribution-theory.md#weak-convergence-of-distributions) limit $\varepsilon\to0$ yields $H*\eta_r=H$ on a smaller ball. The left side is smooth, proving the claim locally. This establishes the [distributional Cauchy-Riemann lemma](../analysis.md#distributional-cauchy-riemann-lemma) without assuming in advance that $H$ is a [locally integrable function](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function).

Conversely every [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function) $h$ satisfies $\partial_{\bar z}h=0$ and can be added to $E$. Hence all [fundamental solutions](../distribution-theory.md#fundamental-solution-of-a-linear-differential-operator) are

$$
\boxed{F(z)=\frac1{\pi z}+h(z),\qquad h\text{ any entire function}.}
$$

No growth condition at infinity was imposed, so $h$ need not be a [polynomial](../polynomial.md).

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

Because $f$ is a [compactly supported distribution](../distribution-theory.md#compactly-supported-distribution), the [convolution of distributions with a compactly supported factor](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution-of-distributions-with-a-compactly-supported-factor) defines $E*f$. The [convolution derivative identity](../fourier-analysis.md#differentiation-commutes-with-convolution) and part (i) give

$$
\partial_{\bar z}(E*f)=(\partial_{\bar z}E)*f=\delta_0*f=f.
$$

The difference between any two solutions has zero $\partial_{\bar z}$ [distributional derivative](../distribution-theory.md#distributional-derivative). Part (ii) therefore proves that the general solution of the [partial differential equation](../partial-differential-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{u=\left(\frac1{\pi z}\right)*f+h,\qquad h\text{ any entire function}.}
$$

This formula is an identity of [distributions](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis); a pointwise integral is not required for arbitrary $f$.

If $u$ were a [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function), its [Wirtinger derivative](../analysis.md#wirtinger-derivatives) $\partial_{\bar z}u=f$ would also be smooth, contradicting $\operatorname{sing\,supp}f\ne\varnothing$. In fact the [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) is exactly preserved here:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{sing\,supp}u=\operatorname{sing\,supp}f.}
$$

One inclusion follows because a [differential operator](../analysis.md#differential-operator) cannot create a singularity where $u$ is smooth. For the reverse inclusion, $E$ is smooth off zero, and its [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) is exactly $\{0\}$: if it were smooth near zero, $\partial_{\bar z}E=\delta_0$ would be smooth there. The [support and singular support rules for convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#support-and-singular-support-rules-for-convolution) give $\operatorname{sing\,supp}(E*f)\subseteq\{0\}+\operatorname{sing\,supp}f$, and adding the smooth [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function) $h$ leaves this [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) unchanged. Thus there is no globally smooth solution when the given [singular support](../distribution-theory.md#singular-support) is nonempty.

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

Interchange the two [Wirtinger derivatives](../analysis.md#wirtinger-derivatives) in part (i). The same real [fundamental solution of the Laplace equation](../partial-differential-equation.md#fundamental-solution-of-the-laplace-equation) $L$ gives

$$
4\partial_{\bar z}L=\frac{x+iy}{\pi(x^2+y^2)}=\frac1{\pi\bar z},\qquad
\partial_z(4\partial_{\bar z}L)=\Delta L=\delta_0.
$$

The calculation is valid for [distributions](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) by the same vanishing [boundary term](../calculus.md#boundary-term) estimate. Any other [fundamental solution](../distribution-theory.md#fundamental-solution-of-a-linear-differential-operator) differs by a [distribution](../distribution-theory.md#distribution-mathematical-analysis) $H$ with $\partial_zH=0$. Then $\Delta H=0$, so the regularity argument of part (ii) makes $H$ smooth, and its [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../analysis.md#cauchy-riemann-equations) make it an [antiholomorphic function](../holomorphic-function.md#antiholomorphic-function). On the whole plane it can be written $H(z)=h(\bar z)$ with $h$ an [entire function](../holomorphic-function.md#entire-function). Conversely every such $H$ has $\partial_zH=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F(z)=\frac1{\pi\bar z}+h(\bar z),\qquad h\text{ any entire function}.}
$$
