# Quadratic perturbation proof of the minimum principle

↑ **Parent:** [Superharmonic function](superharmonic-function.md)

For $u\in C^2(D)\cap C(\overline D)$ on a bounded domain in $\mathbb R^n$ with $\Delta u\leq0$, the minimum lies on the boundary. Define $u_\varepsilon=u-\varepsilon|x|^2$, so $\Delta u_\varepsilon\leq-2n\varepsilon<0$. An interior minimum would have positive semidefinite [Hessian matrix](hessian-matrix.md) and nonnegative [trace](matrix-trace.md), a contradiction. Thus $u_\varepsilon$ attains its minimum on the boundary. Boundedness controls $|u-u_\varepsilon|$ uniformly by a constant times $\varepsilon$; letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the weak minimum principle for the original [superharmonic function](superharmonic-function.md). The perturbation handles the non-strict inequality, including constant [functions](function-split.md).
