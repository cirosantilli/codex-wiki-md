# Orthogonality forces many interior zeros

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonal polynomial](orthogonal-polynomial.md)

If a nonzero continuous real function on an interval is orthogonal to every [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) of degree below $n$, it has at least $n$ distinct interior zeros. If it had only finitely many zeros and fewer than $n$ sign changes, multiply it by the product of linear factors at its sign-changing zeros. The product has constant sign and is nonzero on an [open interval](open-interval.md), so its integral cannot vanish. Orthogonality to this degree-below-$n$ [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) contradicts that conclusion. Infinitely many interior zeros already satisfy the assertion. Orthogonal [polynomials](polynomial-split.md) of respective degrees $0,\ldots,n-1$ form a [basis](basis.md) of the needed [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) space.
