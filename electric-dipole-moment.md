# Electric dipole moment

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatics](electrostatics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_dipole_moment)

The [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) measures the first spatial moment of signed [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). For localized [point charges](point-charge.md) $q_i$ at positions $\mathbf r_i$, or an integrable [charge density](charge-density.md) $\rho(\mathbf r)$ with a finite first moment, define

$$
\mathbf p=\sum_i q_i\mathbf r_i,
\qquad
\mathbf p=\int\mathbf r\rho(\mathbf r)\,d^3r,
\qquad
Q=\sum_iq_i=\int\rho(\mathbf r)\,d^3r.
$$

[Richard Fitzpatrick, Electric Dipoles: continuous definition](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/jk1/lectures/node20.html).

The [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) is a [vector](vector.md) with [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units $\mathrm{C\,m}$: [coulombs](coulomb.md) times [length](length.md) in metres. Opposite [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) can cancel in $Q$ while contributing in the same direction to $\mathbf p$. For a neutral pair with positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $q>0$ at $\mathbf r_+$ and negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $-q$ at $\mathbf r_-$,

$$
\mathbf d=\mathbf r_+-\mathbf r_-,\qquad \mathbf p=q\mathbf d.
$$

Thus the [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) points from negative to positive [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), not along the attraction between the two [point charges](point-charge.md). [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, University Physics 2, 5.7: charge-pair geometry and dipole moment](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/5-7-electric-dipoles).

Changing the coordinate origin changes the position vectors, not the physical [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) distribution. If the new origin is at $\mathbf a$ relative to the old origin, $\mathbf r_i'=\mathbf r_i-\mathbf a$, so direct substitution gives

$$
\mathbf p'=\sum_iq_i(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf a)=\mathbf p-Q\mathbf a.
$$

Consequently [charge neutrality](charge-neutrality.md) makes the [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) independent of the origin. For a system with nonzero total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), always specify the origin: a single [point charge](point-charge.md) at the old origin has $\mathbf p=0$ but $\mathbf p'=-Q\mathbf a$. This is coordinate bookkeeping, not a newly created [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md). The full [electric potential](electric-potential.md) stays the same at the same physical point; the terms of its [electric multipole expansion](electric-multipole-expansion.md) and the observation coordinates change together.

As a stipulated numerical example, put $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$ at $(1,0,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{nC}$ at $(-1,0,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$. Their total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) is zero and $\mathbf p=(4\times10^{-12},0,0)\,\mathrm{C\,m}$. Translating the origin by $(3,-2,0)\,\mathrm{mm}$ leaves this [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) unchanged. Adding $+1\,\mathrm{nC}$ at the old origin instead gives $Q=1\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $\mathbf p'=(1,2,0)\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C\,m}$. Distinguishing these two cases prevents the mistaken claim that every [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) is independent of the origin.

The usefulness of the [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) is compression: many internal [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) determine one [vector](vector.md) governing the leading distant [electric potential](electric-potential.md) of a neutral localized object when $\mathbf p\ne0$. [Feynman, Leighton and Sands, II.6.2: distant dipole fields](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_06.html#Ch6-S2). A [polar molecule](polar-molecule.md) need not literally contain two [point charges](point-charge.md); its [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) and [electron density](electron-density.md) collectively give an [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md). For example, the [chemical polarity](chemical-polarity.md) of [water](water.md) reflects its [bent molecular geometry](bent-molecular-geometry.md), whereas the symmetric linear [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) of [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) makes its permanent [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) vanish. [Flowers, Theopold, Langley and Robinson, Chemistry 2e, 7.6: molecular geometry and polarity](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-6-molecular-structure-and-polarity). A collection of such [electric dipole moments](electric-dipole-moment.md) per unit volume gives [electric polarization](polarization-density.md). [Richard Fitzpatrick, Polarization: dipole moment per unit volume](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/jk1/Electromagnetism/node40.html).
