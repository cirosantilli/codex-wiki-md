# Non-Archimedean valued field

↑ **Parent:** [Non-Archimedean absolute value](non-archimedean-absolute-value.md)

A [field](field.md) equipped with a [Non-Archimedean absolute value](non-archimedean-absolute-value.md) is a non-Archimedean valued field. Its metric is $d(x,y)=|x-y|$ and satisfies the [ultrametric inequality](ultrametric-inequality.md). The [P-adic numbers](p-adic-number.md) give the basic mixed-characteristic examples. Completeness means that every [Cauchy sequence](cauchy-sequence.md) for this metric has a limit in the field.

The sets $R=\{x:|x|\le1\}$ and $\mathfrak m=\{x:|x|<1\}$ are its valuation ring and maximal ideal. Multiplicativity and the strong triangle inequality prove that $R$ is a ring and $\mathfrak m$ is an ideal. A nonzero element of $R$ is invertible in $R$ exactly when its absolute value is one, since $|x^{-1}|=|x|^{-1}$. Thus all nonunits are precisely $\mathfrak m$, proving that it is the unique maximal ideal and that $R/\mathfrak m$ is a field. These definitions concern a valuation, independently of whether the field admits an ordering.
