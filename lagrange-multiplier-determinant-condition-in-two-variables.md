# Lagrange multiplier determinant condition in two variables

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md)

At a regular constrained [stationary point](stationary-point.md) of $f$ on $g=0$, the [Lagrange multiplier](lagrange-multiplier.md) equations $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$ make the two [gradients](gradient.md) linearly dependent. Eliminating $\lambda$ gives the displayed zero [determinant](determinant.md). This condition is necessary, not sufficient for an [extremum](maximum-and-minimum.md). At a singular constraint point where $\nabla g=0$, the determinant is automatically zero even when no multiplier exists; for example $g=x^2+y^2$ and $f=x$ at the origin.
