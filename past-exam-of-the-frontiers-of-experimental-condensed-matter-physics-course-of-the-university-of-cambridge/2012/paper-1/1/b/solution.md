In [photoemission electron microscopy](../../../../../photoemission-electron-microscopy.md), illuminate a surface with ultraviolet light or X-rays and form an electron-optical image of the emitted [electrons](../../../../../electron.md). An extraction field accelerates them away from the sample; an immersion objective and projection lenses magnify their spatial distribution onto an [electron](../../../../../electron.md) detector. Unlike a scanned probe, this is a full-field image. Surface preparation and [vacuum](../../../../../vacuum.md) matter because the emitted low-energy [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) are strongly affected by contamination and local [electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md).

Contrast can come from work-function differences, composition, occupied electronic states, illumination and topography. Near threshold, a small work-function change can produce a large emission-yield change. Therefore brightness is not simply geometrical height. An [energy filter](../../../../../energy-filter.md) can select an emitted-electron [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) interval; scanning the selection yields spatially resolved spectra. Sweeping the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) across an [absorption edge](../../../../../absorption-edge.md) can add element and chemical-state sensitivity. Appropriate polarization-dependent measurements can probe magnetic domains.[instrument definition and optional energy-filter imaging](../../../../../photo-emission-electron-microscope-by-jeol.md)

Resolution depends on electron-lens aberrations, the accepted angular and [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) spread, extraction geometry, sample flatness, space charge, mechanical stability and detector sampling. Rejecting [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) improves some aberrations at a cost in signal. An unfiltered [photoelectron](../../../../../photoelectron.md) image and a chemically selected image therefore answer different questions even when they use the same microscope.
