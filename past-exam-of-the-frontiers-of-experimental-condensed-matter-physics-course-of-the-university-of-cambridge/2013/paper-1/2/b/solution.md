<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy](../../../../../ultraviolet-photoelectron-spectroscopy.md) probes occupied valence states through photoemitted [electron](../../../../../electron.md) energies. [Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy](../../../../../angle-resolved-photoemission-spectroscopy.md) adds emission direction and therefore parallel crystal [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md), revealing occupied band dispersions and Fermi contours. For a clean periodic surface, $k_\parallel=\sqrt{2m_eE_{\rm kin}}\sin\theta/\hbar$ modulo a surface reciprocal-lattice vector. A two-dimensional surface band has no intrinsic perpendicular dispersion; varying [photon](../../../../../photon.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) helps distinguish it from bulk bands, while allowing for matrix-element changes. A simple surface-state example is a parabolic band $E(\mathbf k_\parallel)=E_0+\hbar^2k_\parallel^2/(2m_*)$.

[Scanning tunneling spectroscopy](../../../../../scanning-tunneling-spectroscopy.md) measures local differential conductance. With a suitable featureless tip and weak tunneling, $dI/dV$ samples the local [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md), giving occupied and unoccupied features on opposite bias sides. It provides spatial localization, defects and standing-wave patterns, while ordinary spectroscopy at one point does not directly give the complete momentum-resolved band structure. For the parabolic two-dimensional band, the ideal [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) has a step at its onset, while spatial [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) can supply information about allowed wavevectors. [Inverse photoemission spectroscopy](../../../../../inverse-photoemission-spectroscopy.md) accesses unoccupied states by injecting [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) and measuring emitted [photons](../../../../../photon.md). Optical absorption and [electron energy loss spectroscopy](../../../../../electron-energy-loss-spectroscopy.md) instead probe allowed excitation energies and collective modes, with their own selection rules and [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) ranges. These methods supply complementary information; none automatically returns the full interacting band structure without matrix elements, resolution and surface effects.

For the Pt core-level spectra, [electrons](../../../../../electron.md) suffering inelastic losses emerge with reduced [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and appear at larger inferred binding energies. Loss tails and secondary-electron contributions therefore generate the background, principally on the high-binding-energy side of a core peak. The two clean-surface components correspond to different environments of the same Pt core-level branch. The stated values imply a [surface core-level shift](../../../../../surface-core-level-shift.md)

$$
\boxed{E_B^{\rm surface}-E_B^{\rm bulk}=70.5-70.9=-0.4\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$

Reduced coordination at a surface narrows the $d$ band. In a simple almost-filled-band picture, maintaining its occupation moves the band center upward relative to the Fermi level; associated local-potential changes can move core levels upward too, reducing their binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). This is a plausible initial-state explanation of the negative shift. Final-state screening of the core hole also matters, so the measured shift alone does not uniquely determine a local charge or separate these mechanisms.

After adsorption, Pt atoms directly bonded to CO have a different local potential, hybridization and core-hole screening. In [carbon monoxide adsorption on platinum](../../../../../carbon-monoxide-adsorption-on-platinum.md), carbon-end donation and metal-to-molecule back-donation change the Pt–CO bond and hence the Pt core binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md). The additional weak high-binding-energy component is naturally assigned to these chemically modified surface Pt atoms. Its small weight is compatible with only a minority of surface sites being occupied; a quantitative coverage estimate requires fitted areas and appropriate attenuation assumptions.

The new component is centered at approximately $71.9$–$72.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ in the supplied plot. Relative to bare surface Pt at $70.5\,\mathrm{eV}$, the [core-level chemical shift](../../../../../core-level-chemical-shift.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta E_B^{\rm CO}\simeq+1.4\text{–}1.5\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$

Using the bulk peak as the reference would instead give about $+1.0$–$1.1\,\mathrm{eV}$, which answers a different comparison. The plotted axis runs toward decreasing binding [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) to the right, so the additional peak on its left is a positive binding-energy shift. The estimate is a graphical reading, with no claim to sub-tenth-eV precision or a unique charge-transfer value.
