↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [scanning tunneling microscope](../../../../../scanning-tunneling-microscope.md) can modify an adsorbate's potential landscape as well as image it. First locate the atom with a weakly perturbing tunnel-current setpoint. Bringing the tip closer strengthens tip–atom coupling. In [lateral atomic manipulation](../../../../../lateral-atomic-manipulation.md), moving the tip parallel to the surface can pull or push the atom between adsorption sites; reducing the coupling afterwards leaves it at the chosen location. A change in tunneling current during the motion can signal a jump, and a subsequent low-current image checks the final arrangement.

In [vertical atomic manipulation](../../../../../vertical-atomic-manipulation.md), a suitable tip interaction or voltage pulse transfers an atom between substrate and tip. The tip is repositioned and the atom is deposited elsewhere by changing that interaction again. [Electric fields](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) and inelastic tunneling excitation can also change adsorption, induce hopping or modify bonds. The useful mechanism depends on the atom, substrate, tip and bias: there is no universal pulse amplitude that works for every system. Low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) suppresses thermal diffusion and ultrahigh vacuum helps preserve a clean, stable surface.

A motivating demonstration is [IBM in atoms](../../../../../ibm-atoms.md). [Don Eigler](../../../../../don-eigler.md) and [Erhard Schweizer](../../../../../erhard-schweizer.md) used a low-temperature STM to position individual [xenon](../../../../../xenon.md) atoms on a [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) surface, constructing a chosen atomic pattern. The 1990 paper reported operation at about $4\,\mathrm K$ with atomic positioning precision. This demonstrates controlled rearrangement of surface adsorbates, rather than making the image alone prove that every STM feature is an isolated atom.[1990 abstract: low-temperature positioning of individual xenon atoms on single-crystal nickel](../../../../../positioning-single-atoms-with-a-scanning-tunnelling-microscope-by-don-eigler-and-erhard-schweizer.md)
