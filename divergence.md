# Divergence

↑ **Parent:** [Vector calculus](vector-calculus.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence)

For $F=(F_1,F_2,F_3)$ in Cartesian coordinates,

$$
\nabla\cdot F=\partial_iF_i
=\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x}
+\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y}
+\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}.
$$

For a continuously differentiable [vector field](vector-field.md), the divergence is the limiting outward [flux](flux.md) per unit volume through a small closed surface. Opposite faces of a small rectangular box almost cancel; the remaining differences in the three components give the sum of [partial derivatives](partial-derivative.md) above. The [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) extends this local balance to a whole region.

For $F=(x,y,0)$, the divergence is $1+1+0=2$. In a planar unit square, the right edge contributes outward [flux](flux.md) $1$ and the top contributes $1$, while the left and bottom contribute zero. The total $2$ equals the integral of the planar divergence over area $1$.

If $F$ is a [velocity field](velocity-field.md), positive divergence means local volume expansion, not automatically creation of mass. The [continuity equation](continuity-equation.md) gives $D\rho/Dt=-\rho\,\nabla\cdot F$, where $D/Dt$ is the [material derivative](material-derivative.md). For this field, a uniform initial [density](density.md) evolves as $\rho(t)=\rho(0)e^{-2t}$ while material volumes grow as $e^{2t}$. Their product stays constant.
