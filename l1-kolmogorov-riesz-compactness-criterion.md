# L1 Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Lp space](lp-space.md)

A subset of $L^1(\mathbb R)$ is relatively [norm](norm.md) compact exactly when it is bounded, its absolute-integral tails tend to zero uniformly, and $\sup_{f\in S}\|f(\cdot+h)-f\|_1\to0$ as $h\to0$. Necessity follows by taking a finite [norm](norm.md) net and using the tail decay and [translation continuity in Lp](translation-continuity-in-lp.md) of its finitely many centers; the error contributes at most twice the net radius. For sufficiency, convolve with a triangular unit-integral kernel of width $\delta$. Translation continuity gives uniform approximation in $L^1$, while boundedness gives a common supremum bound and a common Lipschitz constant for the smoothed family. The original tail condition controls its tails outside a slightly enlarged interval. On that interval [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](arzela-ascoli-theorem.md) gives a finite uniform net. Extend its centers by zero outside the interval to obtain a finite $L^1$ net, and combine the smoothing and tail errors. Completeness of $L^1$ turns [total boundedness](totally-bounded-space.md) into [relative compactness](relatively-compact-subset.md).
