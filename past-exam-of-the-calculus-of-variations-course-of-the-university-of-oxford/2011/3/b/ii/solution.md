<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The continuous representative of $u$ makes $x\mapsto2xu(x)^3$ continuous on the closed interval. The integrated formula therefore gives a continuously differentiable representative of $u'$, and hence $u\in C^2([0,1])$ with

$$
u''(x)=2xu(x)^3.
$$

Differentiate once more. The right side is continuously differentiable because $u\in C^2$, and

$$
\boxed{u^{(3)}(x)=2u(x)^3+6xu(x)^2u'(x).}
$$

Thus $\boxed{u\in C^3([0,1])}$. These formulas give continuous one-sided derivatives at the endpoints as well. Repeated bootstrapping would actually give smoothness of every order.
