<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A scalar [Tonelli existence theorem](../../../../tonelli-existence-theorem.md) states: if $f(x,y,p)$ is continuous, convex in $p$, and has a uniform superlinear lower bound $f(x,y,p)\geq\Theta(|p|)-C$ with $\Theta(r)/r\to\infty$, and at least one fixed-endpoint admissible [absolutely continuous function](../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) has finite energy, then the energy attains its [infimum](../../../../infimum.md) in the fixed-endpoint $W^{1,1}$ class. One may take $\Theta$ nonnegative and convex. The lower bound makes derivatives uniformly integrable; endpoint control then gives uniform compactness of a minimizing sequence, and [convexity](../../../../convex-function.md) supplies the required [lower semicontinuity](../../../../lower-semicontinuity.md).

For the specified functional, the integrand is continuous, nonnegative, and convex in the derivative, with

$$
f(x,y,p)\geq\frac{|p|^{52}}{52},\qquad
\frac{|p|^{52}/52}{|p|}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The affine function $u_0(x)=2x-1$ has the required endpoints and finite energy. Thus **a global minimizer exists**. In fact any finite-energy minimizer belongs to $W^{1,52}$, since its derivative's 52nd power is integrable.

It is **unique**. The admissible class is convex, and the integrand is strictly convex in $(y,p)$: $|p|^{52}$ and $|y|^{2011}$ are strictly convex, with the latter multiplied by $e^x>0$. If two distinct admissible finite-energy functions minimized the energy, their midpoint would have strictly smaller energy. Distinct continuous representatives differ on a set of positive measure, so the strict [integral](../../../../integral-split.md) inequality really applies. This contradiction proves [uniqueness](../../../../uniqueness.md).
