# Frozen-temperature solutal interface dispersion

↑ **Parent:** [Finite-inventory directional salt rejection](finite-inventory-directional-salt-rejection.md)

For an interface perturbation $\eta e^{ikx+st}$ with [instantaneous interface kinetics](instantaneous-interface-kinetics.md), take the liquid concentration perturbation proportional to $e^{-qz}$. Here $\Gamma>0$ is the coefficient of [curvature-induced melting-temperature depression](curvature-induced-melting-temperature-depression.md). The [advection-diffusion equation](advection-diffusion-equation.md) and interfacial [mass conservation](mass-conservation.md) give

$$
s=Dq^2-Vq-Dk^2,\qquad sC_I=(Dq-V)\left[\frac{VC_I}{D}-\frac{G+\Gamma k^2}{m}\right].
$$

For a growing [normal mode](normal-mode.md) the unique decaying liquid branch has $q=(V+\sqrt{V^2+4D(s+Dk^2)})/(2D)$. Writing $b=(G+\Gamma k^2)/(mC_I)$, its rate is $s=(D/2)(V/D-b)(\sqrt{b^2+4k^2}-b)$. Instability requires $G+\Gamma k^2<mVC_I/D$. The [Gibbs-Thomson effect](gibbs-thomson-relation.md) suppresses sufficiently short waves; a [quasistatic solute diffusion](quasistatic-solute-diffusion.md) to solute diffusion changes the rate and must be stated separately.
