# Completeness of Lebesgue measure

↑ **Parent:** [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md)

Every [subset](subset.md) $B$ of a [Lebesgue null set](lebesgue-null-set.md) is a [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) and is itself a [null set](null-set.md). In fact the same conclusion follows directly from $m^*(B)=0$, without assuming measurability in advance. For any $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, [monotonicity of Lebesgue outer measure](monotonicity-of-lebesgue-outer-measure.md) gives $m^*(A\cap B)=0$ and $m^*(A\setminus B)\leq m^*(A)$. Therefore

$$
m^*(A\cap B)+m^*(A\setminus B)\leq m^*(A).
$$

The reverse inequality follows from [countable subadditivity of Lebesgue outer measure](countable-subadditivity-of-lebesgue-outer-measure.md), applied to the union $A=(A\cap B)\cup(A\setminus B)$. This is [Carathéodory's criterion](caratheodory-s-criterion.md), proving measurability. If $B$ is a subset of an outer-null $N$, its outer measure is zero by the same monotonicity. Thus the restriction of outer measure to measurable sets is a [complete measure](complete-measure.md).
