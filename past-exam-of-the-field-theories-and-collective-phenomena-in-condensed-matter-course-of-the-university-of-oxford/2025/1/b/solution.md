<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [free particle](../../../../free-particle.md), vary the [Euclidean action](../../../../euclidean-action.md) with a variation $h$ obeying $h(0)=h(T_E)=0$. [Integration by parts](../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\delta S_{\mathrm{Euc}}=m\int_0^{T_E}\dot x\dot h\,d\tau
=-m\int_0^{T_E}\ddot xh\,d\tau.
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is therefore $\ddot x_0=0$. The fixed [boundary conditions](../../../../boundary-condition.md) determine its unique solution:

$$
\boxed{x_0(\tau)=x_i+\frac{x_f-x_i}{\beta\hbar}\tau,
\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq\beta\hbar.}
$$

This [classical path](../../../../classical-path.md) is also the unique minimum among paths with finite [Euclidean action](../../../../euclidean-action.md): the second variation is $m\int_0^{T_E}\dot h^2d\tau>0$ for every nonzero admissible variation. Its [Euclidean action](../../../../euclidean-action.md) is

$$
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{Euc}}[x_0]=\frac{m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\beta\hbar}.}
$$
