<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [canonical anticommutation relations](../../../../canonical-anticommutation-relations.md) imply $a^2=(a^\dagger)^2=0$. Thus the [number operator](../../../../number-operator.md) satisfies

$$
N_a^2=a^\dagger aa^\dagger a
=a^\dagger(1-a^\dagger a)a=N_a.
$$

It is a [orthogonal projection](../../../../orthogonal-projection.md), so its [eigenvalues](../../../../eigenvalue.md) obey $n_a^2=n_a$ and are $\boxed{n_a\in\{0,1\}}$. The same argument gives $\boxed{n_b\in\{0,1\}}$. This is the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) for each orbital. The two [number operators](../../../../number-operator.md) commute, so they have simultaneous [eigenstates](../../../../eigenstate.md).

Take the [vacuum state](../../../../vacuum-state.md) to have unit norm and fix the double-occupation convention $|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle$. An [orthonormal basis](../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of the four-dimensional [fermionic Fock space](../../../../fermionic-fock-space.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{state}&n_a&n_b\\\hline
|00\rangle=|0\rangle&0&0\\
|10\rangle=a^\dagger|0\rangle&1&0\\
|01\rangle=b^\dagger|0\rangle&0&1\\
|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle&1&1
\end{array}}
$$

Each individual [number operator](../../../../number-operator.md) has two-dimensional [eigenspaces](../../../../eigenspace.md): for $N_a$ these are spanned by $\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle\}$ and $\{|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}$; for $N_b$ they are spanned by $\{|00\rangle,|10\rangle\}$ and $\{|01\rangle,|11\rangle\}$. Higher [occupation numbers](../../../../occupation-number.md) vanish because the square of a [fermionic creation operator](../../../../fermionic-creation-operator.md) is zero. Reversing the order of the two [fermionic creation operators](../../../../fermionic-creation-operator.md) changes only the sign convention for $|11\rangle$.
