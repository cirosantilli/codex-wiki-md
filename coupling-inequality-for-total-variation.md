# Coupling inequality for total variation

↑ **Parent:** [Total variation distance](total-variation-distance.md)

For any [coupling](coupling.md) of [random variables](random-variable-split.md), the [coupling characterization of total variation distance](coupling-characterization-of-total-variation-distance.md) gives $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathcal L(X),\mathcal L(Y))\leq\mathbb P(X\ne Y)$, where $\mathcal L$ denotes the [law of a random variable](probability-distribution-split.md). If two coupled [Markov chains](markov-chain-split.md) stay identical after their coalescence time $T$, then $\{X_t\ne Y_t\}\subseteq\{T>t\}$ and

$$
\lVert\mathcal L(X_t)-\mathcal L(Y_t)\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\mathbb P(T>t).
$$

The persistent agreement is essential: a first meeting time does not give this bound if the chains can separate again. A [coalescing coupling](coalescing-coupling.md) turns a difficult comparison of [probability distributions](probability-distribution-split.md) into a concrete question about how long a joint simulation takes to merge.
