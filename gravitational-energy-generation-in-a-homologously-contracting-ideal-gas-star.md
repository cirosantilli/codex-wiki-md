# Gravitational energy generation in a homologously contracting ideal-gas star

↑ **Parent:** [Kelvin-Helmholtz mechanism](kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism.md)

For a [monatomic gas](monatomic-gas.md) obeying the [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) law, the [specific internal energy](specific-internal-energy.md) is $e=3P/(2\rho)$. With no [stellar nuclear fusion](stellar-nuclear-fusion.md) heating, the [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) makes the effective [stellar energy-generation rate](stellar-energy-generation-rate.md) $\epsilon=-\partial_te-P\partial_t(1/\rho)$, with the [derivatives](derivative.md) taken at fixed enclosed [mass](mass.md). Differentiating gives $\epsilon=-3\partial_tP/(2\rho)+5P\partial_t\rho/(2\rho^2)$. In a fixed-mass [stellar homology](stellar-homology.md) [Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction](kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism.md), $\rho\propto R^{-3}$ and $P\propto R^{-4}$ at each [mass](mass.md) label. Hence $\epsilon=-3P\dot R/(2\rho R)>0$ when $\dot R<0$. This includes [internal energy](internal-energy.md) storage as well as [pressure](pressure.md) work; using the gravitational potential-energy decrease alone would overestimate the outgoing [luminosity](luminosity.md).
