# Fixed-point bound for automorphisms of a compact Riemann surface

↑ **Parent:** [Riemann-Roch theorem](riemann-roch-theorem.md)

A nonidentity holomorphic [automorphism](automorphism.md) of a compact connected [Riemann surface](riemann-surfaces-split.md) of [genus](genus-of-a-surface.md) $g$ has at most $2g+2$ fixed points. If there were more, choose $g+1$ distinct fixed points and let $D$ be their sum. The [Riemann-Roch theorem](riemann-roch-theorem.md) gives $\ell(D)\geq2$, so there is a nonconstant [meromorphic function](meromorphic-function.md) with at most simple poles at these points and no other poles. The function $h=f-f\circ\varphi$ has at most $g+1$ poles and at least $n-g-1$ zeros at the remaining fixed points. Since a nonzero [principal divisor](principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) has degree zero, $n>2g+2$ forces $h=0$. At an actual pole fixed by $\varphi$, the reciprocal $1/f$ is a local coordinate because its zero is simple. The identity $f=f\circ\varphi$ therefore forces $\varphi$ to be the identity near that pole, and the [identity theorem on a Riemann surface](identity-theorem-on-a-riemann-surface.md) makes it the identity everywhere.
