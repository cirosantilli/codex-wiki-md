## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2020](2020.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Write the increment function of the [one-step method](../numerical-analysis.md#one-step-method) as $\Phi(x,v,h)=f(x+\beta h,v+\beta h f(x,v))$. Its [consistency error](../numerical-analysis.md#normalized-local-truncation-error) is

$$
T_n=\frac{y(x_n+h)-y(x_n)}h-\Phi(x_n,y(x_n),h).
$$

As $h\to0$, the first term tends to $y'(x_n)$ and the second tends to $f(x_n,y(x_n))=y'(x_n)$ by [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function). Both limits are uniform along the exact-solution curve, a [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space): its small stage perturbations lie in a fixed compact neighborhood, so [uniform continuity](../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity) applies there. Thus $\max_{n<N}|T_n|\to0$, proving **consistency for every $\beta\in[0,1]$**.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

By the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule), $y''=f_x+f_yf$ along the exact solution. Applying [Taylor's theorem](../calculus.md#taylor-s-theorem) to the exact increment and the two arguments of $f$ gives

$$
\frac{y(x+h)-y(x)}h=f(x,y(x))+\frac h2y''(x)+O(h^2),\qquad
\Phi(x,y(x),h)=f(x,y(x))+\beta h(f_x+f_yf)(x,y(x))+O(h^2).
$$

The assumed differentiability makes the remainders uniform on $0\leq x\leq1-h$. Subtracting gives the [consistency error](../numerical-analysis.md#normalized-local-truncation-error)

$$
\boxed{T_n=h\left(\frac12-\beta\right)y''(x_n)+O(h^2).}
$$

Here $O$ denotes [Big O notation](../real-analysis.md#big-o-notation). The general [order of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#order-of-a-numerical-method) is therefore one for $\beta\ne1/2$ and at least two for $\beta=1/2$. For example, on $y'=y$ with unit initial value, $y_N=(1+h+\beta h^2)^{1/h}$. Expanding its [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm) gives $y_N=e[1+(\beta-1/2)h+O(h^2)]$, so the first-order term is actually nonzero when $\beta\ne1/2$.

To control accumulation, the [mean value theorem](../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) and the bound on $f_y$ give

$$
|\Phi(x,u,h)-\Phi(x,v,h)|\leq L(1+\beta hL)|u-v|.
$$

Put $K_h=L(1+\beta hL)$, $q_h=1+hK_h$ and $e_n=y(x_n)-y_n$. The exact and numerical updates yield $|e_{n+1}|\leq q_h|e_n|+h|T_n|$, with $e_0=0$. Iterating this recurrence, as in the [Lipschitz one-step error bound](../numerical-analysis.md#lipschitz-one-step-error-bound), gives

$$
|e_n|\leq\frac{q_h^n-1}{K_h}\max_{j<n}|T_j|
\leq\frac{e^{K_hx_n}-1}{K_h}\max_{j<n}|T_j|.
$$

Because $K_h\leq L(1+L)$ for $h\leq1$, the prefactor is bounded independently of the mesh. When $\beta=1/2$, the [consistency error](../numerical-analysis.md#normalized-local-truncation-error) is $O(h^2)$, hence the maximum [global error](../numerical-analysis.md#global-discretization-error) is $O(h^2)$. This proves at least second-order [numerical convergence](../numerical-analysis.md#convergence-of-a-numerical-method) of the [explicit midpoint method](../numerical-analysis.md#explicit-midpoint-method).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For $f(x,y)=y$ and $\beta=1/2$, the [explicit midpoint method](../numerical-analysis.md#explicit-midpoint-method) multiplies the current value by $R=1+h+h^2/2$. Starting from one, induction gives

$$
\boxed{y_n=\left(1+h+\frac{h^2}{2}\right)^n.}
$$

Let $A=e^h$ and $B=R$. The [Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder](../calculus.md#taylor-theorem-with-lagrange-remainder) for the [exponential function](../calculus.md#exponential-function) gives

$$
\frac{h^3}{6}\leq A-B\leq\frac{h^3e^h}{6},\qquad 1\leq B\leq A.
$$

The [difference of powers](../polynomial.md#difference-of-powers) identity gives $A^n-B^n=(A-B)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A^{n-1-k}B^k$. Every summand lies between one and $A^{n-1}$. Since $nh=x_n$, the [global error](../numerical-analysis.md#global-discretization-error) therefore satisfies

$$
\boxed{\frac{h^2x_n}{6}\leq y(x_n)-y_n\leq\frac{h^2x_ne^{x_n}}6.}
$$

The same inequalities hold at $n=0$, where the error vanishes. At the fixed endpoint $x_N=1$, the lower bound $h^2/6$ prevents any higher [order of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#order-of-a-numerical-method), while the upper bound is $eh^2/6$. Thus **the convergence order is exactly two**.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2020](2020.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

A constant-coefficient [linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md), normalized so that $\alpha_k=1$, has the form

$$
\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_jY_{n+j}=h\sum_{j=0}^k\beta_jf(x_{n+j},Y_{n+j}),\qquad x_n=x_0+nh.
$$

Here $Y_n$ approximates the solution $y(x_n)$ of the [initial value problem](../differential-equation.md#initial-value-problem), and $Y_0,\ldots,Y_{k-1}$ are starting approximations. The coefficients are independent of $n$ and $h$. If $\beta_k=0$, the update is explicit; otherwise it is an [implicit time-stepping method](../numerical-analysis.md#implicit-time-stepping-method), whose new value must satisfy the displayed equation.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Fix the following normalization of the [local truncation error](../numerical-analysis.md#local-truncation-error), also called the [consistency error](../numerical-analysis.md#normalized-local-truncation-error): insert a smooth exact solution into the [linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md) and divide the residual by $h$,

$$
T_n(h)=\frac1h\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_jy(x_n+jh)-\sum_{j=0}^k\beta_jy'(x_n+jh).
$$

Thus the unscaled defect is $hT_n(h)$. [Consistency of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#consistency-of-a-numerical-method) means that $T_n(h)\to0$ uniformly over mesh points in each fixed interval for every sufficiently smooth exact solution. Write the [characteristic polynomials of a linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md#characteristic-polynomials-of-a-linear-multistep-method) as

$$
\rho(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_jz^j,\qquad \sigma(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k\beta_jz^j.
$$

By [Taylor's theorem](../calculus.md#taylor-s-theorem), [consistency of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#consistency-of-a-numerical-method) is equivalent to

$$
\sum_j\alpha_j=0,\qquad \sum_jj\alpha_j=\sum_j\beta_j,
$$

or $\rho(1)=0$ and $\rho'(1)=\sigma(1)$.

Second-order accuracy means $T_n(h)=O(h^2)$ for smooth solutions, equivalently an unscaled [local truncation error](../numerical-analysis.md#local-truncation-error) $O(h^3)$. In addition to the two consistency conditions, its order conditions require

$$
\sum_jj^2\alpha_j=2\sum_jj\beta_j.
$$

An [order of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#order-of-a-numerical-method) of exactly two additionally requires that the next order condition fails. These statements concern the local defect; a second-order [global error](../numerical-analysis.md#global-discretization-error) requires stability and suitably accurate starting values as well.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For a constant-coefficient [linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md), [zero-stability](../linear-multistep-method.md#zero-stability) means uniform boundedness of the propagation of starting perturbations as $h\to0$. Equivalently, every solution of the homogeneous [linear recurrence](../algebra.md#linear-recurrence-relation)

$$
\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_jv_{n+j}=0
$$

satisfies $|v_n|\leq K\max_{0\leq j<k}|v_j|$ for all $n\geq0$, with $K$ independent of $n$ and the starting values. The equivalent [root condition for a multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md#root-condition-for-a-multistep-method) is that every [root of a polynomial](../polynomial.md#root-of-a-polynomial) $\zeta$ of the degree-$k$ [polynomial](../polynomial.md) $\rho$ satisfies $|\zeta|\leq1$, and every root on $|\zeta|=1$ is simple. Indeed, a root of multiplicity $m$ produces recurrence modes $n^q\zeta^n$, $0\leq q<m$: repeated roots strictly inside the disk are harmless, whereas roots outside it or repeated roots on its boundary produce unbounded modes.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

The coefficients of this [linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md) are $\alpha_0=b$, $\alpha_1=-a$, $\alpha_2=1$ and $\beta_0=\beta_1=0$, $\beta_2=c$. Substitution into the [order of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#order-of-a-numerical-method) conditions gives

$$
1-a+b=0,\qquad 2-a-c=0,\qquad 2-\frac a2-2c=0.
$$

The second equation gives $c=2-a$; substituting this into the third gives $-2+3a/2=0$. Consequently there is exactly one second-order choice,

$$
\boxed{a=\frac43,\qquad b=\frac13,\qquad c=\frac23.}
$$

The resulting scheme is the [BDF2 method](../numerical-analysis.md#second-order-backward-differentiation-formula),

$$
3Y_{n+2}-4Y_{n+1}+Y_n=2h f(x_{n+2},Y_{n+2}).
$$

For a $C^4$ exact solution, [Taylor's theorem](../calculus.md#taylor-s-theorem) in the original normalization gives the exact leading order defect

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_n(h)
&=\frac{y(x_n+2h)-\frac43y(x_n+h)+\frac13y(x_n)}h-\frac23y'(x_n+2h)\\
&=-\frac29h^2y'''(x_n)+O(h^3).
\end{aligned}
$$

In particular the coefficient $-2/9$ is nonzero: the method has formal order exactly two, rather than three. Bounded third derivatives already suffice for the uniform estimate $T_n(h)=O(h^2)$; the stronger smoothness above only makes the displayed remainder immediate. Multiplying the recurrence by $3/2$ instead gives the common derivative normalization with leading defect $-h^2y'''(x_n)/3$.

The first of the [characteristic polynomials of a linear multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md#characteristic-polynomials-of-a-linear-multistep-method) is

$$
\rho(z)=z^2-\frac43z+\frac13=(z-1)\left(z-\frac13\right).
$$

Both roots are simple and lie in the closed unit disk, so the [root condition for a multistep method](../linear-multistep-method.md#root-condition-for-a-multistep-method) proves [zero-stability](../linear-multistep-method.md#zero-stability).

The [Dahlquist equivalence theorem](../linear-multistep-method.md#dahlquist-equivalence-theorem) states that, for fixed-coefficient [linear multistep methods](../linear-multistep-method.md) on the usual class of [initial value problems](../differential-equation.md#initial-value-problem) with a right-hand side continuous in $x$ and uniformly [Lipschitz continuous](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) in $y$, [convergence of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#convergence-of-a-numerical-method) for every such problem and every convergent set of starting values is equivalent to [consistency of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#consistency-of-a-numerical-method) and [zero-stability](../linear-multistep-method.md#zero-stability). The statement is on fixed finite intervals where the exact solution exists, with implicit updates well defined. Its order estimate says that a [zero-stable](../linear-multistep-method.md#zero-stability) method with $T_n(h)=O(h^p)$ and starting errors $O(h^p)$ has [global error](../numerical-analysis.md#global-discretization-error) $O(h^p)$ under the corresponding solution smoothness and [Lipschitz continuity](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) assumptions; merely convergent starting values do not ensure the order-$p$ rate.

For this application, sufficient assumptions are a unique solution $y\in C^3([x_0,x_0+T])$, a uniform [Lipschitz continuity](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) bound $L$ in $y$ on a neighborhood of its graph, and the nearby solution branch of each implicit update. For sufficiently small $h$, the map

$$
z\longmapsto\frac43Y_{n+1}-\frac13Y_n+\frac23h f(x_{n+2},z)
$$

is a [contraction mapping](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping) with factor at most $2hL/3<1$ on a suitable small closed neighborhood. The [Banach fixed-point theorem](../analysis.md#contraction-mapping-theorem) supplies local solvability of the nearby update. With starting errors

$$
|Y_0-y(x_0)|+|Y_1-y(x_0+h)|=O(h^2),
$$

the order estimate gives

$$
\max_{0\leq nh\leq T}|Y_n-y(x_n)|\leq C_T\left(|Y_0-y(x_0)|+|Y_1-y(x_0+h)|+\max_n|T_n(h)|\right)=O(h^2).
$$

Thus the [BDF2 method](../numerical-analysis.md#second-order-backward-differentiation-formula) is second-order convergent under these standard hypotheses. Exact $Y_0=y(x_0)$ together with one [Euler method](../numerical-analysis.md#euler-method) step for $Y_1$ already gives the required starting error when $y''$ is bounded, since only one starting step is taken.

The question's assumption of [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function) of $f$ alone does not establish uniqueness, the needed solution smoothness, or this uniform convergence estimate. The added [Lipschitz continuity](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) condition is a sufficient hypothesis for the theorem used here; its failure does not imply that every particular [initial value problem](../differential-equation.md#initial-value-problem) is nonunique or that its numerical approximations cannot converge.
