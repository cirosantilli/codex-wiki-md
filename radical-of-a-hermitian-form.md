# Radical of a Hermitian form

↑ **Parent:** [Hermitian form](hermitian-form.md)

The radical of a [Hermitian form](hermitian-form.md) is the [vector subspace](vector-subspace.md) orthogonal to the entire space. The form descends to the [quotient vector space](quotient-vector-space.md) by its radical: adding a radical [vector](vector.md) to either argument does not change the value. For a [positive semidefinite Hermitian form](positive-semidefinite-hermitian-form.md), the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) identifies this radical with its zero-norm [vectors](vector.md), so the quotient form is positive definite. This conclusion does not hold for a general [indefinite Hermitian form](indefinite-hermitian-form.md): a zero-norm [vector](vector.md) need not belong to its radical.
