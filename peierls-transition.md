# Peierls transition

↑ **Parent:** [Electron-phonon interaction](electron-phonon-interaction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peierls_transition)

A [Peierls transition](peierls-transition.md), named for [Rudolf Peierls](rudolf-peierls.md), is a [metal-insulator transition](metal-insulator-transition.md) caused by a [Peierls distortion](peierls-distortion.md) of an initially metallic chain.[Entry P04468: electronic transition caused by a structural distortion and opening a gap at the Fermi energy](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/peierls-transition.md) A modulation at $2k_F$ mixes states near opposite [Fermi points](fermi-point.md) and can open a [band gap](band-gap.md) at the [Fermi energy](fermi-energy.md). At half filling with one [atomic orbital](atomic-orbital.md) per [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), this modulation doubles the original lattice period and alternates strong and weak bonds. In a weak-coupling zero-temperature description, the [Peierls energy gain](logarithmic-energy-gain-from-a-peierls-distortion.md) is proportional to $-(\delta^2/t)\ln(t/|\delta|)$ whereas the elastic cost is proportional to $\delta^2$, where $t>0$ is the undistorted hopping strength and $\delta$ its small alternating change. The logarithm permits a distortion at arbitrarily weak coupling in the ideal model. Finite [temperature](temperature-split.md), finite size, interchain coupling and [thermal fluctuations](thermal-fluctuation.md) matter for a real transition; strictly one-dimensional reasoning alone does not establish finite-temperature long-range order.
