# Rational torsion of a diagonal cubic

↑ **Parent:** [Diagonal cubic elliptic curve](diagonal-cubic-elliptic-curve.md)

For $d\in\mathbb Q^\times$ the rational [torsion subgroup](torsion-subgroup.md) of $X^3+Y^3+dZ^3=0$ is cyclic of order $3$ if $d$ is a cube, cyclic of order $2$ if $d/2$ is a cube, and trivial otherwise.

To bound torsion, use the [Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve](weierstrass-equation-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) $y^2=x^3-432d^2$. At every good [prime](prime-number.md) $\ell\equiv2\pmod3$, cubing is a bijection on $\mathbb F_\ell$, so the number of points is $\ell+1$. Reduction injects the prime-to-$\ell$ torsion, since multiplication by an integer prime to $\ell$ is invertible in the [formal group of an elliptic curve](formal-group-of-an-elliptic-curve.md). For any prime $q>3$, the [Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](dirichlet-s-theorem-on-arithmetic-progressions.md) supplies a good $\ell\equiv2\pmod3$, $\ell\equiv1\pmod q$, excluding $q$-torsion. Good primes $\ell\equiv5\pmod{12}$ exclude order $4$, and good primes $\ell\equiv11\pmod{18}$ exclude order $9$. Thus every rational torsion point is killed by $6$.

The [flexes of a diagonal cubic](flexes-of-a-diagonal-cubic.md) give rational three-torsion exactly when $d$ is a cube, and then it has order $3$. Rational two-torsion requires $x^3=432d^2$, whose sole possible rational root exists exactly when $d/2$ is a cube. This equivalence follows by comparing prime valuations modulo $3$, including the valuation at $2$. The two conditions cannot hold together because $2$ is not a rational cube. The asserted classification follows.
