<h1 id="b/5/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the unlabelled Euler-Lagrange assertion, use its integrated form $f'(u')=c$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md). This follows from the [first variation](../../../../../../first-variation.md) $\int f'(u')\phi'=0$ for all compactly supported tests. If $u\in C^1$, the left side is continuous and so equals $c$ everywhere. Its derivative values lie in the finite fiber $(f')^{-1}(c)$, while the continuous image of the interval under $u'$ is connected. Thus $u'$ is constant. Endpoint values fix it to $m=(\beta-\alpha)/(b-a)$, giving exactly the affine $\bar u$. Conversely that affine function has constant $f'(\bar u')$, so is a solution. This proves **uniqueness among $C^1$ solutions**; the scalar integrand is the one-dimensional case of the notation in the source.

If $f$ is strictly convex, [Jensen's inequality](../../../../../../jensen-s-inequality.md) applied to $u'$ gives

$$
I(u)\geq(b-a)f\left(\frac1{b-a}\int_a^bu'\right)=(b-a)f(m)=I(\bar u).
$$

Equality in the strict inequality can occur only when $u'$ is [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) constant. [Absolute continuity](../../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) and the endpoints then give $u=\bar u$. Hence **the affine function is the unique [global minimizer](../../../../../../global-minimizer.md)**.
