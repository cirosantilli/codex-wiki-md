<h1 id="b/6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [global minimizer](../../../../../../global-minimizer.md) has $I(\bar u)\leq I(v)$ for every admissible $v$. Global minimality implies strong local minimality, which implies weak local minimality. The reverse implications are not valid in general.

Where $f_v$ exists, the [strengthened Weierstrass condition](../../../../../../strengthened-weierstrass-condition.md) says that for some $\delta>0$, the excess

$$
E(x,y,p,q)=f(x,y,q)-f(x,y,p)-f_v(x,y,p)(q-p)
$$

is nonnegative for every $x\in[a,b]$, every trial slope $q\in\mathbb R$, and all $y,p$ with $|y-\bar u(x)|<\delta$, $|p-\bar u'(x)|<\delta$. This is a neighborhood condition in height and reference slope, not just an inequality on the reference curve.

The classical [Weierstrass sufficient condition](../../../../../../weierstrass-sufficient-condition.md), with the usual smooth integrand regularity in addition to continuity, states that an admissible $C^1$ curve satisfying the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md), a coercively positive [second variation](../../../../../../second-variation.md), and this strengthened condition is a strong local minimizer. A strict excess away from the reference slope gives the corresponding strict conclusion. The differentiability assumptions are part of that classical sufficient theorem; continuity alone does not define its first and second variations.
