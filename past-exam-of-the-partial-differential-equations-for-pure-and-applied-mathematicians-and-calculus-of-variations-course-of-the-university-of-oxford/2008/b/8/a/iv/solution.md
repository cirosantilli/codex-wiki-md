<h1 id="b/8/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Provided the limit belongs to the original admissible class, the definition of the [infimum](../../../../../../infimum.md) and the assumed inequality give

$$
\boxed{l\leq I(u)\leq\liminf_k I(u_{j_k})=l,\qquad I(u)=l.}
$$

The needed fundamental property is sequential lower semicontinuity for the convergence in the [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) argument: uniform, or strong $L^1$, convergence of functions together with weak $L^1$ convergence of derivatives. The extra integrand hypothesis in the classical [Tonelli existence theorem](../../../../../../tonelli-existence-theorem.md) is [convexity](../../../../../../convex-function.md) in the derivative variable $v$ for every fixed $(x,u)$, together with the stated coercivity and continuity. In one dimension the resulting integral is lower semicontinuous under the uniform/weak-derivative convergence above.

This lower-semicontinuity assertion does not itself supply the missing Lipschitz membership in (iii). With the natural fixed-trace $W^{1,1}$ admissible class and its own [infimum](../../../../../../infimum.md), it completes Tonelli's direct existence argument. Retaining the printed Lipschitz class requires the additional membership estimate; changing admissible classes can otherwise encounter the [Lavrentiev phenomenon](../../../../../../lavrentiev-phenomenon.md).
