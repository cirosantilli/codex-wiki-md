<h1 id="b/8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The affine competitor $1-x$ has finite energy and the energy is nonnegative. A [minimizing sequence](../../../../../minimizing-sequence.md) therefore has bounded $\int u_j'^2$. Its fixed value at zero gives $|u_j(x)|\leq1+\|u_j'\|_2$, so it is also bounded in $L^2$ and hence in $W^{1,2}$. The estimate

$$
|u_j(x)-u_j(y)|\leq\|u_j'\|_2|x-y|^{1/2}
$$

gives uniform [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md). Extract a weakly convergent subsequence in $W^{1,2}$ and, by the [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](../../../../../arzela-ascoli-theorem.md), a uniformly convergent subsequence to the same limit $u$. The endpoint values are retained. [Uniform convergence](../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) on a common bounded range gives $\int u_j^{2008}\to\int u^{2008}$, while the derivative [norm](../../../../../norm.md) is weakly lower semicontinuous. Consequently

$$
I(u)\leq\liminf_jI(u_j)=\inf_{\mathcal A}I,
$$

so **the global minimum is attained in the stated $W^{1,2}$ class**. In fact the gradient term is strictly convex on this fixed-endpoint affine class and $t^{2008}$ is convex, so the minimizer is unique as well.
