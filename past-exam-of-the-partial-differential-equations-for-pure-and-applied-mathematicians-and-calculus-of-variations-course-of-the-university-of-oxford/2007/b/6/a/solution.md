<h1 id="b/6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Along the reference curve put $p=f_{vv}$, $r=f_{uv}$, $s=f_{uu}$. The [second variation](../../../../../second-variation.md) is

$$
q(\phi)=\delta^2I(\bar u)[\phi,\phi]=\int_a^b(p\phi'^2+2r\phi\phi'+s\phi^2)dx.
$$

Strict positivity means $q(\phi)>0$ for every nonzero zero-trace variation. Under the given regular Legendre condition $p>0$, the no-conjugate-point criterion also supplies a uniform coercive lower bound. The [Jacobi equation](../../../../../jacobi-equation.md) is $-(pz'+rz)'+rz'+sz=0$. A point $c>a$ is conjugate to $a$ when a nonzero solution vanishes at both $a$ and $c$. In this regular setting, absence of [conjugate points](../../../../../conjugate-points.md) in $(a,b]$ is equivalent to existence of a Jacobi solution positive throughout $[a,b]$.

Choose such a positive solution $z$. The given square identity extends from smooth compactly supported variations to $H_0^1$ by density and boundedness of its coefficients. With $v=\phi/z$ it reads $q(\phi)=\int pz^2v'^2$. The positive minima of $p,z$ bound this below by $c\|v'\|_2^2$. A zero-trace [function](../../../../../function-split.md) obeys $\|v\|_2\leq C\|v'\|_2$, while $\phi'=zv'+z'v$ and $\phi=zv$ give $\|\phi\|_{H^1}\leq C_1\|v'\|_2$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{q(\phi)\geq c_1\|\phi\|_{H^1}^2>0\quad(\phi\ne0).}
$$

In particular the required [second variation](../../../../../second-variation.md) is positive for all nonzero Lipschitz zero-trace variations.
