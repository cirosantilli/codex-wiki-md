<h1 id="b/5/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use $\|w\|_{W^{1,\infty}}=\|w\|_\infty+\|w'\|_\infty$. Fix $u$. For $w$ small, all triples $(x,u+tw,u'+tw')$, $0\leq t\leq1$, lie in a fixed [compact set](../../../../../../compact-space.md) up to null sets. The continuous derivatives $f_u,f_v$ are uniformly continuous there, with a modulus $\omega(s)\to0$ as $s\to0$. The integral form of the mean-value expansion gives a remainder bounded by

$$
(b-a)\omega(\|w\|_{W^{1,\infty}})(\|w\|_\infty+\|w'\|_\infty).
$$

The proposed linear derivative is bounded because its coefficient [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) are bounded on that [compact set](../../../../../../compact-space.md). The remainder is little-oh of the [norm](../../../../../../norm.md), proving [Fréchet differentiability](../../../../../../frechet-differentiability.md) and

$$
\boxed{DI(u)w=\int_a^b[f_v(x,u,u')w'+f_u(x,u,u')w]dx.}
$$

The derivative $u'$ only needs to exist almost everywhere; the uniform compact-range bound handles its measurable representative.
