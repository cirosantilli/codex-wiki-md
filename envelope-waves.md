# Envelope (waves)

↑ **Parent:** [Wave packet](wave-packet.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envelope_(waves))

A [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) is a slowly varying [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) multiplying a rapidly oscillating [carrier wave](carrier-wave.md). For a complex wavefield $\psi=A(x,t)e^{i(k_0x-\omega_ct)}$, its nonnegative amplitude envelope is $|A|=|\psi|$; the complex $A$ can also encode a slowly varying [wave phase](phase-waves.md). This choice distinguishes the amplitude envelope from a signed modulation factor, such as the cosine factor in a two-tone [superposition](superposition-principle.md). A slowly varying [wave envelope](envelope-waves.md) is useful when the carrier's [wavelength](wavelength.md) and [period](period-of-a-function.md) are short compared with its variation scales. Strong overlap between distinct packets or a broad [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) can spoil a unique carrier-envelope interpretation.
