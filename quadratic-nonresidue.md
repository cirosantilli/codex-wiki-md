# Quadratic nonresidue

↑ **Parent:** [Quadratic residue](quadratic-residue-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_nonresidue)

A nonzero [congruence class](congruence-class.md) modulo an [odd prime](odd-prime.md) is a quadratic nonresidue when it is not a square. In the convention of [quadratic residue](quadratic-residue-split.md), zero belongs to neither class. Thus $3,5,6$ are the [quadratic nonresidues](quadratic-nonresidue.md) modulo seven, while $2,6,7,8,10$ are the [quadratic nonresidues](quadratic-nonresidue.md) modulo eleven. No [integer](integer-split.md) $x$ satisfies $x^2\equiv3\pmod7$, as the complete square map under [quadratic residue](quadratic-residue-split.md) shows.

A [quadratic nonresidue](quadratic-nonresidue.md) is not an arithmetic dead end: multiplying by any fixed [quadratic nonresidue](quadratic-nonresidue.md) $n$ exchanges the [quadratic residues](quadratic-residue-split.md) and the [quadratic nonresidues](quadratic-nonresidue.md). If $nq$ were a square for a square $q$, division by $q$ would make $n$ a square. Hence $nQ_p$ consists entirely of [quadratic nonresidues](quadratic-nonresidue.md); it has $(p-1)/2$ elements, because multiplication by nonzero $n$ is a [bijection](bijection.md). It therefore equals the entire other [coset](coset.md) of the [quadratic residue](quadratic-residue-split.md) [subgroup](subgroup.md). Modulo seven, $3\{1,2,4\}=\{3,6,5\}$.

The product of two [quadratic nonresidues](quadratic-nonresidue.md) is consequently a [quadratic residue](quadratic-residue-split.md): write them as $nq_1,nq_2$ in that same [coset](coset.md), and their product is $n^2q_1q_2$, a square. For example, $3\cdot5\equiv1\pmod7$, whereas $2\cdot3\equiv6\pmod7$ remains a [quadratic nonresidue](quadratic-nonresidue.md). The [multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol](multiplicativity-of-the-legendre-symbol.md) packages these rules as ordinary sign multiplication.
