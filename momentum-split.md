# Momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum)

For a nonrelativistic [point mass](point-mass.md) of fixed [mass](mass.md) $m>0$, measured in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), its [momentum](momentum-split.md) is

$$
\mathbf p=m\mathbf v.
$$

[Momentum](momentum-split.md) is a [vector](vector.md) pointing along the [velocity](velocity.md), with [International System of Units](international-system-of-units.md) unit $\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$. Doubling either [mass](mass.md) or [velocity](velocity.md) doubles [momentum](momentum-split.md). In one dimension, a chosen positive direction makes $p=mv$ a signed quantity: a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ body moving right at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has $p=+6\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$; moving left at the same [speed](speed.md) gives $p=-6\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, section 9.1: Linear Momentum](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-1-linear-momentum)

For a collection, total [momentum](momentum-split.md) is the [vector](vector.md) sum $\mathbf P=\sum_i m_i\mathbf v_i$. Two equal [masses](mass.md) moving with opposite [velocities](velocity.md) can have zero total [momentum](momentum-split.md) while both have positive [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md). Direction is therefore essential when using [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md); adding [speeds](speed.md) instead of signed [velocities](velocity.md) gives the wrong balance.

[Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) connects the net [force](force-split.md) on one fixed-[mass](mass.md) [point mass](point-mass.md) to its [momentum](momentum-split.md) through $\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=d\mathbf p/dt$. Integrating that change gives [impulse](impulse.md). Here $\mathbf p$ denotes [mechanical momentum](mechanical-momentum.md); when [canonical momentum](canonical-momentum.md) differs from [mass](mass.md) times [velocity](velocity.md), the distinction in [mechanical momentum](mechanical-momentum.md) below applies. The nonrelativistic formula also has a limited regime: [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md) is needed at [speeds](speed.md) comparable with the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md).
