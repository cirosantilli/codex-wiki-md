# Neutral current

↑ **Parent:** [Weak interaction](weak-interaction.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_current)

The weak neutral current couples the [Z boson](z-boson.md) to a fermion without changing its electric charge. Its vector and axial-vector coefficients depend on weak isospin, electric charge, and the weak mixing angle.

The [neutral current](neutral-current.md) transfers no [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) at a [fermion](fermion.md) vertex; “neutral” does not mean that every participating [particle](particle-physics.md) is uncharged. For example, $\nu_\mu e^-\to\nu_\mu e^-$ transfers [energy](energy-split.md) and [momentum](momentum-split.md) to an [electron](electron.md) through [Z boson](z-boson.md) exchange. By contrast, a [charged current](charged-current.md) can convert a [muon neutrino](muon-neutrino.md) into a [muon](muon.md), as in $\nu_\mu n\to\mu^-p$. Both processes conserve total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md). A [photon](photon.md) also carries no [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), but an active [neutrino](neutrino.md) has no electromagnetic charge coupling: its [neutral current](neutral-current.md) is a distinct [weak interaction](weak-interaction.md).

The experimentally useful contrast on a [nucleon](nucleon.md) target is

$$
\nu_\mu+N\longrightarrow\mu^-+X\quad\text{(charged current)},\qquad
\nu_\mu+N\longrightarrow\nu_\mu+X\quad\text{(neutral current)},
$$

where $N$ is a [nucleon](nucleon.md) and $X$ is an allowed final state of [hadrons](hadron.md). The [charged current](charged-current.md) makes a charged [lepton](lepton.md) available for identification; the outgoing [neutrino](neutrino.md) in a [neutral current](neutral-current.md) leaves no [bubble-chamber track](bubble-chamber-track.md). A sample of hadronic events without an identified [muon](muon.md) therefore poses an experimental question: how many could instead be [neutron](neutron-split.md) interactions, or [charged current](charged-current.md) events whose [muon](muon.md) was missed? The answer requires a detector-response and background analysis, not merely an inspection for an absent line.
