# Repeated-timing uncertainty in a pendulum gravity measurement

↑ **Parent:** [Propagation of uncertainty](propagation-of-uncertainty.md)

For [simple pendulum](simple-pendulum-split.md) [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) $T$ in the [small-angle approximation](small-angle-approximation.md), $g=4\pi^2L/T^2$. If the length measurement and $N$ identically precise timing measurements have [independent](independent-random-variables.md) errors, the timing [sample mean](sample-mean.md) has [standard error](standard-error.md) $\sigma_T/\sqrt N$. [Propagation of uncertainty](propagation-of-uncertainty.md) gives the title formula. A target fractional [standard uncertainty](standard-uncertainty.md) $\epsilon$ requires $N\ge4(\sigma_T/T)^2/[\epsilon^2-(\sigma_L/L)^2]$, rounded upward to an integer. No amount of repeated timing can beat the floor $\sigma_L/L$ without improving the length measurement; correlated errors or a timing [systematic error](systematic-error.md) require a different model.
