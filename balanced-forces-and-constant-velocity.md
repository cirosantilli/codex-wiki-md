# Balanced forces and constant velocity

↑ **Parent:** [Newton's first law](newton-s-first-law.md)

Consider a stipulated ideal cart modeled as a [point mass](point-mass.md) of [mass](mass.md) $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ on a horizontal track in a laboratory [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). Its initial [position](position.md) is $x(0)=0$ and its initial [velocity](velocity.md) is $v_x(0)=1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the right. Independent devices exert horizontal [forces](force-split.md) $+6\,\mathrm N$ and $-6\,\mathrm N$ on this same cart. Its upward support [force](force-split.md) balances its downward [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md); no other [forces](force-split.md) contribute. Rotation and deformation are excluded from this model.

$$
F_{\mathrm{net},x}=6-6=0\,\mathrm N,\qquad
a_x=\frac{0\,\mathrm N}{2\,\mathrm{kg}}=0\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad
v_x(t)=1.5\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
x(t)=(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})t.
$$

After $4\,\mathrm s$, the cart is at $6\,\mathrm m$, still moving right at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Starting the same model at rest instead would leave it at rest. [Force balance](force-balance.md) determines the zero [acceleration](acceleration.md), while the initial [velocity](velocity.md) determines whether the cart moves.

Original schematic of the stipulated $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart. The upper panel shows actual [forces](force-split.md) and a separate [velocity](velocity.md) arrow. The lower panels compare an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) translating at constant [velocity](velocity.md) with a nonrotating [translating accelerated reference frame](translating-accelerated-reference-frame.md).

For the plotted model, both horizontal devices are switched off simultaneously at $t=2\,\mathrm s$, with no [impulse](impulse.md). The support and [gravitational weight](weight-physics.md) remain balanced. The individual horizontal [forces](force-split.md) change, but their sum stays zero on both sides of the switch, so the [velocity](velocity.md) stays continuous and unchanged. Switching off just one device would be a different model with a nonzero net [external force](external-force.md).
