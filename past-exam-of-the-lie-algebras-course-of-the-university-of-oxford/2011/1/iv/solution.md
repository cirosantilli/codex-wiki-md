<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Suppose $\operatorname{ad}(x)$ has a nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ and corresponding vector $y$. Then $[x,y]=\lambda y$, so

$$
\operatorname{ad}(y)x=-\lambda y\ne0,\qquad
(\operatorname{ad}y)^2x=0.
$$

For a [diagonalizable](../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) [endomorphism](../../../../endomorphism.md) $T$, its [eigenspace](../../../../eigenspace.md) decomposition shows $\ker T^2=\ker T$. The displayed equations therefore contradict diagonalizability of $\operatorname{ad}(y)$.

Thus no $\operatorname{ad}(x)$ has a nonzero [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md). Each is [diagonalizable](../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) over the [algebraically closed field](../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md), so each is the zero [endomorphism](../../../../endomorphism.md). Hence $[x,z]=0$ for every $x,z\in\mathfrak g$, proving

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak g\text{ is abelian}.}
$$
