<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

If $\mathfrak g$ is nilpotent, every [Lie subalgebra](../../../../lie-subalgebra.md) $\mathfrak a$ is nilpotent, since $\mathfrak a^j\subseteq\mathfrak g^j$ in the respective [lower central series of a Lie algebra](../../../../lower-central-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md). A two-dimensional nilpotent algebra has nonzero centre by part (i). If $0\ne z$ is central, complete it to a [basis](../../../../basis.md) $(z,u)$; every bracket of these [basis](../../../../basis.md) elements is zero. Thus $\mathfrak a$ is abelian.

Conversely, suppose $\mathfrak g$ is not nilpotent. By [Engel's theorem](../../../../engel-s-theorem.md), some $\operatorname{ad}(x)$ is not nilpotent. Since the [field](../../../../field.md) is algebraically closed, it has an [eigenvalue](../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda\ne0$ with [eigenvector](../../../../eigenvector.md) $y\ne0$. Thus

$$
[x,y]=\lambda y.
$$

The vectors $x,y$ are independent: otherwise their bracket would be zero. Their span is therefore a two-dimensional [Lie subalgebra](../../../../lie-subalgebra.md) and it is nonabelian. This contradicts the hypothesis. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak g\text{ is nilpotent}\iff
\text{every two-dimensional subalgebra is abelian}.}
$$
