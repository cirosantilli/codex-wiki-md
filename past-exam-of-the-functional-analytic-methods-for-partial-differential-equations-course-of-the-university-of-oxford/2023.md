<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

Choose a nonnegative [mollifier](../distribution-theory.md#mollifier) $\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ with $\int\rho=1$, and put $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-3}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$. [Sobolev approximation by mollifiers](../fourier-analysis.md#sobolev-approximation-by-mollifiers) in the [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md) under consideration states

$$
\boxed{u_\varepsilon:=\rho_\varepsilon*u\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\cap W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3),\qquad
\|u_\varepsilon-u\|_{W^{1,6}}\longrightarrow0\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).}
$$

In particular, it gives [density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#density-of-smooth-functions-in-a-sobolev-space). We prove convergence of both the function and all its first [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative).

The usual smoothing properties of [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) give $u_\varepsilon\in C^\infty$. For a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ and $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, [Fubini's theorem](../measure-theory.md#fubini-s-theorem) and the definition of a [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int u_\varepsilon(x)\partial_i\phi(x)\,dx
&=\int\rho_\varepsilon(z)\int u(y)\partial_i\phi(y+z)\,dy\,dz\\
&=-\int\rho_\varepsilon(z)\int\partial_i u(y)\phi(y+z)\,dy\,dz\\
&=-\int(\rho_\varepsilon*\partial_i u)(x)\phi(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [integrals](../integral.md) are absolutely convergent: the [compact supports](../function.md#compact-support) of $\rho_\varepsilon$ and $\phi$ restrict $y$ to a [compact set](../topology.md#compact-space), on which $u$ and $\partial_i u$ are integrable by [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality). Consequently

$$
\partial_i u_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*\partial_i u.
$$

The [approximate identity](../fourier-analysis.md#approximate-identity) theorem in the [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space) $L^6$ applies separately to $u$ and $\partial_i u$. Thus

$$
\|u_\varepsilon-u\|_6\to0,\qquad
\|\partial_i u_\varepsilon-\partial_i u\|_6\to0\quad(i=1,2,3).
$$

Each [convolution](../fourier-analysis.md#convolution) belongs to $L^6$ by [Young's convolution inequality](../fourier-analysis.md#young-s-convolution-inequality). With the componentwise [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm), the sixth power of the required difference is the sum of these four sixth powers, proving the boxed claim. Any equivalent [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm) gives the same convergence.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

Here [Morrey's inequality](../sobolev-space.md#morrey-s-inequality) has [Hölder exponent](../sobolev-space.md#holder-exponent) $1-3/6=1/2$. Every $u\in W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)$ has a unique continuous representative $u^*$ such that

$$
\boxed{\|u^*\|_{C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)},\qquad
[u^*]_{C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_6.}
$$

We use the [Hölder norm](../sobolev-space.md#holder-norm) $\|f\|_{C^{0,1/2}}=\|f\|_\infty+[f]_{C^{0,1/2}}$, where the [Hölder seminorm](../sobolev-space.md#holder-seminorm) is $\sup_{x\ne y}|f(x)-f(y)|/|x-y|^{1/2}$. The second bound is the homogeneous form of [Morrey's inequality](../sobolev-space.md#morrey-s-inequality); the first includes control of the values themselves.

Let $u_j=\rho_{1/j}*u$ be the [mollifications](../distribution-theory.md#mollification) from (i). They converge to $u$ in the [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm). Apply the assumed smooth version of [Morrey's inequality](../sobolev-space.md#morrey-s-inequality) to the differences $u_j-u_\ell$:

$$
\|u_j-u_\ell\|_{C^{0,1/2}}\leq C\|u_j-u_\ell\|_{W^{1,6}}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus $(u_j)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) in the complete [Hölder space](../sobolev-space.md#holder-space) $C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)$ and converges there to a function $w$. In particular the convergence is uniform. On every [bounded set](../topological-analysis.md#bounded-set) $K$ of finite volume,

$$
\|w-u\|_{L^6(K)}
\leq |K|^{1/6}\|w-u_j\|_\infty+\|u_j-u\|_{L^6(\mathbb R^3)}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Exhausting $\mathbb R^3$ by [balls](../topological-analysis.md#ball-mathematics) proves $w=u$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere). Set $u^*=w$. Taking limits in the smooth [Hölder norm](../sobolev-space.md#holder-norm) bound gives the first boxed estimate. For any fixed $x\ne y$, taking limits in

$$
|u_j(x)-u_j(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{1/2}\|\nabla u_j\|_6
$$

gives the second boxed estimate after taking the supremum. Finally, two continuous representatives equal [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) must coincide everywhere, since a nonzero continuous difference would remain nonzero on an [open ball](../topology.md#open-ball) of positive volume.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

On the unbounded domain $\mathbb R^3$, translations already prevent [compactness](../topology.md#compact-space) in every indicated [Hölder space](../sobolev-space.md#holder-space). Choose a [bump function](../analysis.md#bump-function) $\eta\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$ with $\eta(0)=1$, put $e_1=(1,0,0)$, and define

$$
\boxed{u_m(x)=\eta(x-3m e_1),\qquad m=1,2,\ldots.}
$$

A [change of variables](../multivariable-calculus.md#change-of-variables-formula) in the [Lebesgue integrals](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integral) defining the [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm) gives $\|u_m\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)}=\|\eta\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)}$, so the sequence is bounded. Each $u_m$ is a [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function) of [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) and belongs to every [Hölder space](../sobolev-space.md#holder-space) $C^{0,\gamma}(\mathbb R^3)$, $0<\gamma\leq1$: its bounded [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) gives the [Lipschitz](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) estimate for distances at most one, and its bounded values control larger distances.

For $m\ne\ell$, the [supports](../function.md#support) are disjoint, and at $x=3m e_1$ we have $u_m(x)=1$ and $u_\ell(x)=0$. Therefore

$$
\|u_m-u_\ell\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\geq\|u_m-u_\ell\|_\infty\geq1
\qquad(0<\gamma\leq1).
$$

No [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) can be a [Cauchy sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#cauchy-sequence) in any of these [Hölder norms](../sobolev-space.md#holder-norm), hence none converges. This uses the translation mechanism for [noncompactness of a Sobolev embedding by translation](../sobolev-space.md#noncompactness-of-a-sobolev-embedding-by-translation); concentration at the critical [Hölder exponent](../sobolev-space.md#holder-exponent) alone would not rule out convergence at every smaller [Hölder exponent](../sobolev-space.md#holder-exponent).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Write $r=|x|$, $u(x)=\bar u(r)$, and let $g(r)$ be the [radial profile](../partial-differential-equation.md#radial-profile) of the [radial derivative](../multivariable-calculus.md#radial-derivative) $\nabla u(x)\cdot x/|x|$, which may be assumed to be a [radial function](../partial-differential-equation.md#radial-function) here. The [spherical coordinate system](../multivariable-calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system) gives

$$
\int_A|u|^6\,dx=4\pi\int_1^2|\bar u(r)|^6r^2\,dr,\qquad
4\pi\int_1^2|g(r)|^6r^2\,dr
\leq\int_A|\nabla u|^6\,dx.
$$

Since $1\leq r^2\leq4$, both $\bar u$ and $g$ belong to the [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space) $L^6(1,2)$. It remains to identify the [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) of the [radial profile](../partial-differential-equation.md#radial-profile) directly.

For $\phi\in C_c^\infty(1,2)$, each component of $\phi(r)x/r$ is a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) on $A$. Sum the three defining [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) identities, using the stated [divergence](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence) formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4\pi\int_1^2g(r)\phi(r)r^2\,dr
&=\int_A\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{r}\phi(r)\,dx\\
&=-\int_Au\left(\phi'(r)+\frac2r\phi(r)\right)dx\\
&=-4\pi\int_1^2\bar u(r)\bigl(r^2\phi(r)\bigr)'\,dr.
\end{aligned}
$$

Given an arbitrary [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\zeta\in C_c^\infty(1,2)$, choose $\phi(r)=\zeta(r)/r^2$. Dividing by $4\pi$ gives $\int g\zeta=-\int\bar u\zeta'$, exactly the definition of the [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative). We have proved

$$
\boxed{\bar u\in W^{1,6}(1,2),\qquad\bar u'=g\ \text{almost everywhere},\qquad
\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(A)}.}
$$

This [radial Sobolev profile on an annulus](../sobolev-space.md#radial-sobolev-profile-on-an-annulus) estimate follows from the two weighted [integrals](../integral.md) above and equivalence of the usual [Sobolev norms](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm).

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Use the continuous representative of $\bar u$ furnished by the one-dimensional [Sobolev embedding theorem](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-embedding-theorem), and define $u^*(x)=\bar u(|x|)$ on $A$. The [one-dimensional Sobolev representative](../sobolev-space.md#one-dimensional-sobolev-representative) is absolutely continuous; [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality) applied to its [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus) gives

$$
|\bar u(r)-\bar u(s)|
=\left|\int_s^r\bar u'(t)\,dt\right|
\leq |r-s|^{5/6}\|\bar u'\|_{L^6(1,2)}.
$$

Also $\|\bar u\|_\infty\leq\|\bar u\|_6+\|\bar u'\|_6$: select a point where $|\bar u|$ is no larger than its $L^6$ [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) on the unit-length interval and apply the same [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus). Thus the one-dimensional [Hölder norm](../sobolev-space.md#holder-norm) is controlled by $\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}$.

The two [Hölder seminorms](../sobolev-space.md#holder-seminorm) must be compared using the correct distances. By the [reverse triangle inequality](../topological-analysis.md#reverse-triangle-inequality),

$$
\frac{|u^*(x)-u^*(y)|}{|x-y|^{5/6}}
\leq[\bar u]_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}
\frac{\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|^{5/6}}{|x-y|^{5/6}}
\leq[\bar u]_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}.
$$

Conversely, take $x=re$ and $y=se$ for any fixed unit vector $e$. Then $|x-y|=|r-s|$, so taking suprema yields equality of the two [Hölder seminorms](../sobolev-space.md#holder-seminorm). Their [supremum norms](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) are equal as well. Part (i) now gives

$$
\boxed{\|u^*\|_{C^{0,5/6}(A)}
=\|\bar u\|_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}
\leq C\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}
\leq C'\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(A)}.}
$$

The representative agrees with $u$ [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), and the assignment is linear and injective on the radial [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md). This proves the requested [continuous embedding](../functional-analysis.md#continuous-embedding). The inner radius being positive makes the [radial profile](../partial-differential-equation.md#radial-profile) [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm) comparable to its weighted radial version.

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

A [locally integrable function](../distribution-theory.md#locally-integrable-function) $u$ has [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) $g_i$ in direction $i$ when $g_i$ is locally integrable and

$$
\boxed{\int_{B_1}u\,\partial_i\phi\,dx=-\int_{B_1}g_i\phi\,dx
\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_1).}
$$

The [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) have [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) inside the domain, so no boundary values enter this definition. Such $g_i$ is unique [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere) and is denoted $\partial_i u$.

For $1\leq p<\infty$, the first-order [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md) consists of the [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space) classes whose first [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) are also in that [Lp space](../measure-theory.md#lp-space):

$$
\boxed{W^{1,p}(B_1)=\{u\in L^p(B_1):\partial_i u\in L^p(B_1),\ i=1,\ldots,n\},\qquad
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}=\left(\|u\|_p^p+\sum_{i=1}^n\|\partial_i u\|_p^p\right)^{1/p}.}
$$

This [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm) is equivalent to $\|u\|_p+\|\nabla u\|_p$; either convention is acceptable. When $p=2$, this is the [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) $H^1(B_1)$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

For $\psi\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$, the functions $\psi$ and $\partial_i\psi$ are bounded. Hence $\psi u$ and $\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi$ belong to $L^p(B_1)$. For a [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi$, the classical [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) and the [weak derivative](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) identity for $u$, tested with $\psi\phi$, give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{B_1}\psi u\,\partial_i\phi
&=\int_{B_1}u\,\partial_i(\psi\phi)-\int_{B_1}u(\partial_i\psi)\phi\\
&=-\int_{B_1}\bigl(\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi\bigr)\phi.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\psi u\in W^{1,p}(B_1),\qquad
\partial_i(\psi u)=\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi.}
$$

In fact $\psi u$ has [compact support](../function.md#compact-support) in $B_1$ and belongs to the corresponding zero-boundary [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md), by [mollification](../distribution-theory.md#mollification) within that support.

For arbitrary $\psi\in C^\infty(B_1)$, the same identity still proves the local [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) for [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative), but membership in the global [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md) can fail. To give a counterexample for every dimension and every $p$ in the question, take

$$
u\equiv1,\qquad\psi(x)=\frac1{1-|x|^2}.
$$

Here $u\in W^{1,p}(B_1)$ and $\psi$ is a [smooth function](../analysis.md#smooth-function) on the open ball, while [polar coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#polar-coordinates) show

$$
\int_{B_1}|\psi|^p\,dx
\geq c_n\int_{1/2}^1(1-r^2)^{-p}\,dr=\infty\qquad(p\geq1).
$$

Thus $\psi u\notin L^p(B_1)$, and the global assertion is false. Boundedness of $\psi$ and its first [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) would suffice without [compact support](../function.md#compact-support).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

Interpret the paper's unspecified $\Omega$ as containing $B_1$, the domain on which the coefficients are used. Write $A(x)=(a_{ij}(x))$, a real [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix). The [strict ellipticity](../distribution-theory.md#strict-ellipticity) condition is pointwise positive definiteness:

$$
\boxed{\xi^TA(x)\xi>0\qquad(x\in B_1,\ \xi\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).}
$$

The lower bound may depend on $x$. By continuity, [strict ellipticity](../distribution-theory.md#strict-ellipticity) gives a positive lower bound on each [compact subset](../topology.md#compact-space) of $B_1$, but does not require one bound throughout the whole open ball.

With the two-constant convention used in the next part, [uniform ellipticity](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) means

$$
\boxed{\lambda|\xi|^2\leq\xi^TA(x)\xi\leq\Lambda|\xi|^2
\qquad(x\in B_1,\ \xi\in\mathbb R^n),\quad0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty.}
$$

Both constants are independent of $x$. Some terminology uses [uniform ellipticity](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) for just the lower bound and states coefficient boundedness separately; here the paper supplies both constants. The [spectral theorem](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem) for the [symmetric matrix](../linear-algebra.md#symmetric-matrix) gives $|A(x)\xi|\leq\Lambda|\xi|$. For example, $A(x)=(1-|x|^2)I$ satisfies [strict ellipticity](../distribution-theory.md#strict-ellipticity) in $B_1$ but has no uniform positive lower bound as $|x|\uparrow1$.

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

There is a small incompatibility in the printed [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) assumptions. No $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$ can equal one on $B_{1/2}$ and satisfy $|\nabla\varphi|\leq2$: for a unit vector $e$, choose $t<1$ beyond its [support](../function.md#support). Continuity gives $\varphi(e/2)=1$, while the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-of-calculus) would give $1\leq2(t-1/2)<1$. Replace the number $2$ by a fixed sufficiently large absolute constant $C_0$; such a [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) exists, and the requested [Caccioppoli inequality](../partial-differential-equation.md#caccioppoli-inequality) is unchanged apart from its constant. We keep $M=\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty$ in the calculation, which also yields the formal bound under the printed assumptions.

The [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) is $\int_{B_1}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta=0$ for [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\zeta$. Since $A$ is bounded, this extends by [density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#density-of-smooth-functions-in-a-sobolev-space) to every $\zeta\in H_0^1(B_1)$. Part (a)(ii), followed by [mollification](../distribution-theory.md#mollification) inside the [compact support](../function.md#compact-support), makes $\varphi^2(u-k)$ an admissible choice. The [product rule](../calculus.md#product-rule) gives

$$
0=\int\varphi^2 A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u
+2\int\varphi(u-k)A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi.
$$

Put $X=\int\varphi^2|\nabla u|^2$ and $Y=\int(u-k)^2|\nabla\varphi|^2$. The lower and upper [uniform ellipticity](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) bounds, together with [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality), imply

$$
\lambda X\leq2\Lambda X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}.
$$

If $X=0$ the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise divide by $X^{1/2}$ and square to obtain $X\leq4\Lambda^2\lambda^{-2}Y$. Since $\varphi=1$ on $B_{1/2}$,

$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}}|\nabla u|^2\,dx
\leq\frac{4\Lambda^2 M^2}{\lambda^2}\int_{B_1}(u-k)^2\,dx.}
$$

For a fixed valid [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function), $M\leq C_0$, this has the required constant independent of $k$. Formally inserting the printed value $M=2$ gives $16\Lambda^2/\lambda^2$. No derivative of $A$ was taken, so differentiability of the coefficients is unnecessary.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

First repeat the [Caccioppoli inequality](../partial-differential-equation.md#caccioppoli-inequality) calculation on two concentric [balls](../topological-analysis.md#ball-mathematics). If $h$ is a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) on $B_R$, choose a [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) equal to one on $B_r$, supported in $B_R$, and with [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) bounded by $C/(R-r)$. The argument of (ii), with $A=I$ and $k=0$, gives

$$
\int_{B_r}|\nabla h|^2\,dx\leq\frac{C_*}{(R-r)^2}\int_{B_R}|h|^2\,dx
\qquad(0<r<R\leq1).
$$

Here $C_*$ depends at most on the dimension. When $h$ is only known to be smooth inside $B_R$ and in $L^2(B_R)$, the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) and all [integrals](../integral.md) involving the [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) are still legitimate, since its [support](../function.md#support) is strictly inside $B_R$.

Every classical [partial derivative](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) of the given smooth [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) $u$ is again a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function), since constant-coefficient [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) commute with the [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian). Choose indices $i_1,\ldots,i_m$ representing the [multi-index](../distribution-theory.md#multi-index-notation) $\alpha$, and define

$$
h_0=u,\qquad h_j=\partial_{i_j}\cdots\partial_{i_1}u,\qquad
R_j=1-\frac{j}{2m}\quad(0\leq j\leq m).
$$

For each $j\geq1$, the preceding [Caccioppoli inequality](../partial-differential-equation.md#caccioppoli-inequality) yields

$$
\int_{B_{R_j}}|h_j|^2
\leq\int_{B_{R_j}}|\nabla h_{j-1}|^2
\leq C_*(2m)^2\int_{B_{R_{j-1}}}|h_{j-1}|^2.
$$

Starting with $u\in L^2(B_1)$, these estimates also establish the integrability needed at each subsequent step. Iterating $m$ times gives

$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}}|\partial^\alpha u|^2\,dx
\leq\bigl(4C_*m^2\bigr)^m\int_{B_1}|u|^2\,dx.}
$$

This is the desired [interior derivative estimate for a harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#interior-derivative-estimate-for-a-harmonic-function), with a constant depending only on $n$ and $m$, uniformly over every [multi-index](../distribution-theory.md#multi-index-notation) of size $m$.

<h4 id="2/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/b/iv)

Set $w=(u-k)^+$, the [positive part](../function.md#positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function) of $u-k$. The allowed [Sobolev chain rule](../distribution-theory.md#sobolev-chain-rule) gives

$$
\nabla w=\mathbf1_{\{u>k\}}\nabla u\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
$$

Use the admissible zero-boundary [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md) function $\varphi^2w$ in the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation), with a valid [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) as explained in (ii). Its [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is $\varphi^2\nabla w+2\varphi w\nabla\varphi$, so

$$
0=\int\varphi^2 A\nabla w\cdot\nabla w
+2\int\varphi w A\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi.
$$

Indeed the [integrands](../integral.md#integrand) vanish off $\{u>k\}$, and on that set $\nabla w=\nabla u$. The [uniform ellipticity](../elliptic-boundary-value-problem.md#uniformly-elliptic-operator) and [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) estimates from (ii) now apply to $w$ and give

$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}\cap\{u>k\}}|\nabla u|^2\,dx
\leq\frac{4\Lambda^2 M^2}{\lambda^2}
\int_{B_1\cap\{u>k\}}(u-k)^2\,dx,\qquad M=\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty.}
$$

Fixing the [cutoff function](../distribution-theory.md#cutoff-function) gives the required constant independent of $k$. This is a [superlevel Caccioppoli inequality](../partial-differential-equation.md#superlevel-caccioppoli-inequality); it uses the [positive part](../function.md#positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function) as a test and does not require the truncated function itself to solve the original equation.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

The zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) must be part of the definition. Thus $u\in H^1(B)$ is a [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) precisely when $u\in H_0^1(B)$, equivalently its [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) vanishes on $\partial B$, and

$$
\boxed{\int_B\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx
=\int_B\chi_E\phi\,dx=\int_E\phi\,dx
\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in H_0^1(B).}
$$

Here $\chi_E$ is the [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) of the [measurable set](../measure-theory.md#measurable-set) $E$. One may initially require the identity only for [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\phi\in C_c^\infty(B)$: both sides are continuous in the [Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-norm), and the definition of the [zero-trace Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#zero-trace-sobolev-space) then extends it to all $H_0^1(B)$. This formulation uses first [weak derivatives](../distribution-theory.md#weak-derivative) and encodes $-\Delta u=\chi_E$ without assuming classical second [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

As printed, the answer is

$$
\boxed{\text{No }q\in[1,\infty]\text{ satisfies the stated estimate for all }u\in H^1(B).}
$$

The constant function $u\equiv1$ has zero [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) and positive [Lp norm](../real-analysis.md#lp-norm) for every such $q$, including its [supremum norm](../normed-vector-space.md#supremum-norm) when $q=\infty$.

The missing assumption is a zero [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator), which is present for the [weak solutions](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) in the surrounding parts. With $H_0^1(B)$ in place of $H^1(B)$, the intended answer is

$$
\boxed{1\leq q\leq6,\qquad
\|u\|_{L^q(B)}\leq C_q\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}\quad(u\in H_0^1(B)).}
$$

This is the three-dimensional [Sobolev inequality](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-inequality) at $q=6$, followed by [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality) on the bounded ball for smaller $q$; it includes the zero-boundary [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) at $q=2$. The subsequent parts use this zero-boundary version.

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

By the definition in (i), $u$ itself is an admissible test in the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation). Hence

$$
\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}^2=\int_Eu\,dx\leq\int_E|u|\,dx=\|u\|_{L^1(E)}.
$$

The absolute value makes a proof of positivity unnecessary. [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality) with exponents $6$ and $6/5$, followed by the zero-boundary [Sobolev inequality](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-inequality), gives

$$
\|u\|_{L^1(E)}\leq |E|^{5/6}\|u\|_{L^6(B)}
\leq C_S|E|^{5/6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}.
$$

Taking square roots of both estimates proves exactly the requested chain:

$$
\boxed{\|\nabla u\|_2\leq\|u\|_{L^1(E)}^{1/2}
\leq C_S^{1/2}|E|^{5/12}\|\nabla u\|_2^{1/2}.}
$$

If $\|\nabla u\|_2=0$, the zero-boundary [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) gives $u=0$ and the conclusion follows. Otherwise divide the first-to-last estimate by $\|\nabla u\|_2^{1/2}$ and square, obtaining $\|\nabla u\|_2\leq C_S|E|^{5/6}$. Finally the same [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) gives

$$
\boxed{\|u\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C|E|^{5/6}.}
$$

All constants depend only on the fixed unit ball and the dimension, and are independent of $E$ and $u$. If $|E|=0$, testing the homogeneous equation with $u$ gives the same zero solution directly.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Let $w_m=|E_m|^{5/6}u_m$. By linearity of the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation), $w_m$ is the zero-boundary [weak solution](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) with forcing $\chi_{E_m}$. The estimate in (a)(iii) therefore gives

$$
\|w_m\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C|E_m|^{5/6},\qquad
\boxed{\|u_m\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C.}
$$

The [change of variables](../multivariable-calculus.md#change-of-variables-formula) $x=r_my$ gives $|E_m|=r_m^3|E|>0$, so the division is legitimate. Since $H^1(B)$ is a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md), [weak sequential compactness in a Hilbert space](../functional-analysis.md#weak-sequential-compactness-in-a-hilbert-space) now gives a [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) and some $u\in H^1(B)$ such that

$$
\boxed{u_{m_j}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }H^1(B).}
$$

In fact $u\in H_0^1(B)$: the [Sobolev trace operator](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) is continuous and linear, so its zero kernel is preserved by [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence).

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

The [Lp norm](../real-analysis.md#lp-norm) of the forcing at the critical exponent is constant:

$$
\|f_m\|_{L^{6/5}(B)}^{6/5}
=|E_m|^{-1}\int_B\chi_{E_m}\,dx=1.
$$

To prove [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) in $L^{6/5}(B)$, the [duality of Lp spaces](../continuous-dual-space.md#duality-of-lp-spaces) says that we must test against an arbitrary $g\in L^6(B)$. [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality), restricted to the shrinking [measurable set](../measure-theory.md#measurable-set) $E_m$, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_B f_mg\,dx\right|
&\leq |E_m|^{-5/6}\int_{E_m}|g|\,dx\\
&\leq |E_m|^{-5/6}|E_m|^{5/6}
\left(\int_{E_m}|g|^6\,dx\right)^{1/6}
=\left(\int_{E_m}|g|^6\,dx\right)^{1/6}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last step is [absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral](../integral.md#absolute-continuity-of-the-lebesgue-integral), since $|E_m|=r_m^3|E|\to0$ and $|g|^6$ is integrable. Thus

$$
\boxed{f_m\rightharpoonup0\text{ in }L^{6/5}(B),\qquad\|f_m\|_{6/5}=1.}
$$

Testing only bounded or smooth functions would not by itself establish this [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence); the restricted [Holder inequality](../functional-analysis.md#holder-inequality) handles every member of the full dual space.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

Take the [weakly convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) in (i), with limit $u\in H_0^1(B)$. For each $\phi\in H_0^1(B)$, the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) gives

$$
\int_B\nabla u_{m_j}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_Bf_{m_j}\phi\,dx.
$$

The left side tends to $\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi$ by [weak convergence](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) in $H^1$. The zero-boundary [Sobolev inequality](../sobolev-space.md#sobolev-inequality) gives $\phi\in L^6(B)$, so (ii) makes the right side tend to zero. Taking $\phi=u$ in the limiting identity yields $\int_B|\nabla u|^2=0$. The zero-boundary [Poincaré inequality](../sobolev-space.md#poincare-inequality) then gives $u=0$.

The same argument applies to a [weakly convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) of any [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) of $(u_m)$, because all remain bounded as in (i). To see why this proves convergence of the full sequence, suppose a continuous [linear functional](../dual-space.md#linear-functional) $F$ on $H^1(B)$ did not satisfy $F(u_m)\to0$. Some [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence) would have $|F(u_{m_j})|\geq\varepsilon>0$. Its boundedness gives a further [weakly convergent](../weak-topology.md#weak-convergence) [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence), whose limit has just been proved to be zero; then $F$ tends to zero along it, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{u_m\rightharpoonup0\quad\text{in }H^1(B)\text{ for the entire sequence}.}
$$

Uniqueness of a possible limit alone is not enough without the [weak sequential compactness in a Hilbert space](../functional-analysis.md#weak-sequential-compactness-in-a-hilbert-space) used to extract a further [subsequence](../sequence-and-series.md#subsequence).

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

The [zero extension of H01](../sobolev-space.md#zero-extension-of-h01) makes $v_m$ an element of $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$. The [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) and [change of variables](../multivariable-calculus.md#change-of-variables-formula) give the exact scaling identities

$$
\boxed{\|\nabla v_m\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}^2=\|\nabla u_m\|_{L^2(B)}^2,\qquad
\|v_m\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}^2=r_m^{-2}\|u_m\|_{L^2(B)}^2.}
$$

Indeed $\nabla v_m(x)=r_m^{3/2}\nabla u_m(r_mx)$ inside the expanded ball, and there is no boundary jump because the [Sobolev trace](../sobolev-space.md#trace-operator) is zero.

First take the printed convergence assertion as an assumption, as the question requests. It would imply $\|\nabla v_m\|_2\to\|\nabla v\|_2>0$. The strict inequality follows because an $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$ function with zero [weak gradient](../distribution-theory.md#weak-gradient) is constant [almost everywhere](../measure-theory.md#almost-everywhere), and the only constant in $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$ is zero; this contradicts the asserted positivity of $v$. If $u_m$ converged strongly in $H^1(B)$, its limit would have to be its [weak limit](../weak-topology.md#weak-limit) zero from (iii), forcing $\|\nabla u_m\|_2\to0$. The first scaling identity contradicts this. Thus the intended answer is

$$
\boxed{u_m\text{ does not converge strongly in }H^1(B).}
$$

There is nevertheless a substantive printing error: the asserted strong convergence of $v_m$ in the full $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$ is false for these positive sources. Here is a check that does not rely on any assertion about the limiting profile. Put $R_m=r_m^{-1}$ and $c=|E|^{-5/6}$. Rescaling the [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) gives

$$
-\Delta v_m=c\chi_E\quad\text{in }B_{R_m},\qquad v_m=0\quad\text{on }\partial B_{R_m}.
$$

The [spherical mean of a Poisson solution outside its source](../integral-transform.md#spherical-mean-of-a-poisson-solution-outside-its-source) provides an exact obstruction. The total source is $M=c|E|=|E|^{1/6}>0$. Let $a_m(s)=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{S^2}v_m(s\omega)\,d\omega$ be its [spherical mean](../integral-transform.md#spherical-mean). On $1<s<R_m$ the function is a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function). The radial flux identity, obtained by testing against radial [test functions](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) or by the [divergence theorem](../multivariable-calculus.md#divergence-theorem), gives $-4\pi s^2a_m'(s)=M$, while the zero boundary value gives $a_m(R_m)=0$. Hence

$$
a_m(s)=\frac{M}{4\pi}\left(\frac1s-\frac1{R_m}\right)\qquad(1<s<R_m).
$$

[Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) on each sphere, followed by [spherical coordinates](../multivariable-calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system), now yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|v_m\|_2^2
&\geq4\pi\int_1^{R_m}s^2a_m(s)^2\,ds\\
&=\frac{M^2}{4\pi}\left(\frac{R_m}{3}-1+\frac1{R_m}-\frac1{3R_m^2}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the printed sequence is not even bounded in the full $H^1$ [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). The result on [Poisson convergence on expanding balls in a homogeneous Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#poisson-convergence-on-expanding-balls-in-a-homogeneous-sobolev-space) gives the appropriate corrected convergence in the [homogeneous Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md#homogeneous-sobolev-space) $\dot H^1(\mathbb R^3)$, which controls the [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) in $L^2$ and the function in $L^6$, without requiring it to belong to $L^2$.

For completeness, the desired nonconvergence of $u_m$ has a direct proof that needs neither the incorrect claim nor its correction. Choose a nonnegative nonzero [test function](../distribution-theory.md#test-function) $\eta\in C_c^\infty(E)$, which exists since $E$ is a domain containing the origin. The rescaled [weak formulation](../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-formulation) and [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) give

$$
0<c\int_E\eta\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\nabla v_m\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx
\leq\|\nabla v_m\|_2\|\nabla\eta\|_2.
$$

Consequently, for every $m$,

$$
\boxed{\|\nabla u_m\|_2=\|\nabla v_m\|_2
\geq\frac{|E|^{-5/6}\int_E\eta\,dx}{\|\nabla\eta\|_2}>0.}
$$

Together with (iii), this rigorously proves failure of [strong convergence](../normed-vector-space.md#norm-convergence) for the actual sequence defined in the paper.
