<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

As printed, the answer is

$$
\boxed{\text{No }q\in[1,\infty]\text{ satisfies the stated estimate for all }u\in H^1(B).}
$$

The constant function $u\equiv1$ has zero [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) and positive [Lp norm](../../../../../lp-norm.md) for every such $q$, including its [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) when $q=\infty$.

The missing assumption is a zero [Sobolev trace](../../../../../trace-operator.md), which is present for the [weak solutions](../../../../../weak-solution.md) in the surrounding parts. With $H_0^1(B)$ in place of $H^1(B)$, the intended answer is

$$
\boxed{1\leq q\leq6,\qquad
\|u\|_{L^q(B)}\leq C_q\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}\quad(u\in H_0^1(B)).}
$$

This is the three-dimensional [Sobolev inequality](../../../../../sobolev-inequality.md) at $q=6$, followed by [Holder inequality](../../../../../holder-inequality.md) on the bounded ball for smaller $q$; it includes the zero-boundary [Poincaré inequality](../../../../../poincare-inequality.md) at $q=2$. The subsequent parts use this zero-boundary version.
