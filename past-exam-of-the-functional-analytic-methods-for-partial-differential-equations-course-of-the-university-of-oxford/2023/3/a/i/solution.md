<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) must be part of the definition. Thus $u\in H^1(B)$ is a [weak solution](../../../../../weak-solution.md) precisely when $u\in H_0^1(B)$, equivalently its [Sobolev trace](../../../../../trace-operator.md) vanishes on $\partial B$, and

$$
\boxed{\int_B\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx
=\int_B\chi_E\phi\,dx=\int_E\phi\,dx
\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in H_0^1(B).}
$$

Here $\chi_E$ is the [indicator function](../../../../../indicator-function.md) of the [measurable set](../../../../../measurable-set.md) $E$. One may initially require the identity only for [test functions](../../../../../test-function.md) $\phi\in C_c^\infty(B)$: both sides are continuous in the [Sobolev norm](../../../../../sobolev-norm.md), and the definition of the [zero-trace Sobolev space](../../../../../zero-trace-sobolev-space.md) then extends it to all $H_0^1(B)$. This formulation uses first [weak derivatives](../../../../../weak-derivative.md) and encodes $-\Delta u=\chi_E$ without assuming classical second [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md).
