# Single-beam gradient trapping experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Optical tweezers](optical-tweezers.md)

In 1986, [Arthur Ashkin](arthur-ashkin.md), [Joseph Martin Dziedzic](joseph-martin-dziedzic.md), [John Ernst Bjorkholm](john-ernst-bjorkholm.md) and [Steven Chu](steven-chu.md) used spatially filtered $514.5\,\mathrm{nm}$ argon [laser](laser-split.md) light, a water-immersion objective of [numerical aperture](numerical-aperture.md) $1.25$, and a water-filled glass cell. An auxiliary beam loaded large glass beads; dispersed small particles entered by [Brownian motion](brownian-motion-split.md). A side [microscope](microscope.md) and $90^\circ$ [scattering of light](scattering-of-light.md) detector provided readout. Their Fig. 1 shows a $10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ bead below the focus with about $100\,\mathrm{mW}$. On p. 290, silica size was inferred by scattering against a latex standard; translating the cell imposed fluid drag. These observations support single-beam confinement, while inferred size depends on the scattering model.[A. Ashkin, J. M. Dziedzic, J. E. Bjorkholm and Steven Chu, Observation of a single-beam gradient force optical trap for dielectric particles, 1986, pp. 288–290, Figs. 1–2 and final experimental paragraphs](observation-of-a-single-beam-gradient-force-optical-trap-for-dielectric-particles-by-arthur-ashkin-and-others.md)

A loading beam can help bring a bead into the capture region without supplying its subsequent confinement. Distinguish loading, holding and sensing when interpreting an apparatus drawing. The following [optical trap position readout](optical-trap-position-readout.md) is a proposed calibration arrangement; its detector layout is not a reconstruction of this historical experiment.
