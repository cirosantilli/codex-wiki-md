<h1 id="5d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g$ is the least positive integer $m$ for which $g^m=e$, if one exists. In a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md), two terms of $e,g,g^2,\ldots,g^{|G|}$ must agree. If $g^i=g^j$ with $i<j$, cancellation gives $g^{j-i}=e$. Thus such positive integers exist and have a least member: **every element of a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md) has finite order**.

If $h=aga^{-1}$, repeated cancellation shows $h^m=ag^ma^{-1}$ for every positive integer $m$. Consequently $h^m=e$ exactly when $g^m=e$. The least positive exponents are therefore equal:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(aga^{-1})=\operatorname{ord}(g).}
$$

This proof also shows that [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) preserves infinite order when the ambient [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is not finite.
