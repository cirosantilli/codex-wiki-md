<h1 id="5a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Combine the real variables into $z=x+iy$. The [linear system of ordinary differential equations](../../../../../../linear-system-of-differential-equations.md) becomes $z'=(1-i\mu)z$, hence $z=(A+iB)e^{(1-i\mu)t}$ for arbitrary real $A,B$. Taking real and imaginary parts gives

$$
\boxed{x=e^t(A\cos\mu t+B\sin\mu t),\qquad y=e^t(-A\sin\mu t+B\cos\mu t).}
$$

The two independent constants cover every real initial condition. In the [phase plane](../../../../../../phase-plane.md), the distance from the origin grows as $e^t$ and the polar angle changes at rate $-\mu$. For $\mu\ne0$ the trajectories are outward [logarithmic spirals](../../../../../../logarithmic-spiral.md); for $\mu=0$ they are outward straight rays.
