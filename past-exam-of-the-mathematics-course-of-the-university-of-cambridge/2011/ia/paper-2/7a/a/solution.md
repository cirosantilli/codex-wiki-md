<h1 id="7a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Wronskian](../../../../../../wronskian.md) is

$$
W=y_1y_2'-y_1'y_2.
$$

On an interval where $p,q$ are continuous, **the solutions are linearly independent exactly when $W(x_0)\ne0$ at one point**, equivalently when the [Wronskian](../../../../../../wronskian.md) is nonzero throughout the interval. Differentiation and the [homogeneous linear differential equation](../../../../../../homogeneous-linear-differential-equation.md) give

$$
W'=y_1y_2''-y_1''y_2
=y_1(-py_2'-qy_2)-(-py_1'-qy_1)y_2=-pW.
$$

Hence [Abel identity](../../../../../../abel-s-identity.md) reads $W(x)=W(x_0)\exp(-\int_{x_0}^x p(s)\,ds)$. If $W(x_0)=0$, the two initial-data vectors are dependent; the [uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations](../../../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-ordinary-differential-equations.md) then makes the corresponding linear combination identically zero. This proves the converse as well as the necessity.
