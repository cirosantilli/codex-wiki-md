<h1 id="6e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Suppose $x^2\equiv-1\pmod p$. Then $p\nmid x$, so [Fermat's little theorem](../../../../../../fermat-little-theorem.md) gives $x^{p-1}\equiv1$. But $p\equiv3\pmod4$ makes $(p-1)/2$ odd, and hence

$$
x^{p-1}=(x^2)^{(p-1)/2}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}=-1\pmod p.
$$

Since this $p$ is odd, $1\not\equiv-1\pmod p$, a contradiction. **There is no square root of minus one in this case.**
