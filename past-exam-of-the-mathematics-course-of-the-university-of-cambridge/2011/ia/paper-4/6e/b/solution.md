<h1 id="6e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set $m=(p-1)/2$. Pairing each of $1,\ldots,m$ with its negative residue gives

$$
(p-1)!\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{m}j(p-j)
\equiv(-1)^m(m!)^2\pmod p.
$$

Here $p\equiv1\pmod4$ makes $m$ even. By [Wilson theorem](../../../../../../wilson-s-theorem.md),

$$
\boxed{x=m!=\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)!\pmod p}
$$

satisfies $x^2\equiv-1\pmod p$. Its negative is the other root. This proves existence by an explicit [factorial](../../../../../../factorial.md) construction.
