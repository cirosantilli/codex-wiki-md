<h1 id="12b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The incident [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) has energy $E$ and spatial momentum $p=\gamma mv$, while the stationary target has energy $mc^2$ and zero spatial momentum. By the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md), $c^2p^2=E^2-m^2c^4$. For the [fixed-target threshold for production of equal-mass particles](../../../../../../fixed-target-threshold-for-production-of-equal-mass-particles.md), the squared total [centre-of-momentum energy](../../../../../../centre-of-momentum-energy.md) is

$$
W^2=(E+mc^2)^2-c^2p^2=2mc^2(E+mc^2).
$$

In the [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md), each of the final $N$ particles has energy at least its [rest energy](../../../../../../rest-energy.md) $mc^2$. [Four-momentum conservation](../../../../../../four-momentum-conservation.md) therefore requires $W\geq Nmc^2$, and thus

$$
\boxed{E\geq\frac{N^2-2}{2}mc^2.}
$$

For the physically relevant $N\geq2$, this is equivalent to $\gamma\geq(N^2-2)/2$. The speed is an increasing function of $\gamma$, so

$$
\frac{v}{c}=\sqrt{1-\gamma^{-2}}
\geq\sqrt{1-\frac4{(N^2-2)^2}}
=\frac{N\sqrt{N^2-4}}{N^2-2}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{v\geq\frac{N\sqrt{N^2-4}}{N^2-2}\,c.}
$$

Equality is the kinematic threshold at which all final particles are at rest in their common [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). It shows why a collision with a stationary target needs more incident energy than merely the additional rest energy: some laboratory energy must remain as collective motion. A one-particle final state of the specified mass, with no other outgoing energy, is impossible because its invariant energy would be $mc^2$, whereas the two-particle initial state has invariant energy at least $2mc^2$.
