<h1 id="1e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**Always true.** If $(f\circ g)(x)=(f\circ g)(x')$, [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md) of $f$ gives $g(x)=g(x')$, and [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md) of $g$ gives $x=x'$. Thus the [function composition](../../../../../../function-composition.md) is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). For any $y\in X$, since $f$ is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md), choose $z$ with $f(z)=y$. Since $g$ is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md), choose $x$ with $g(x)=z$. Then $(f\circ g)(x)=y$, proving that the composition is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md). Consequently $f\circ g$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md); its [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) is $g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}$.
