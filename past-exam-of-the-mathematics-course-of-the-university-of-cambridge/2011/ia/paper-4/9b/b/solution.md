<h1 id="9b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $\psi=\mu/r$, the radial [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) gives

$$
\mathbf B=-\nabla\psi=\frac{\mu}{r^3}\mathbf r.
$$

The force $-\nabla\phi$ is radial and has no [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) about the origin. For the remaining force, use the [vector triple product](../../../../../../vector-triple-product.md):

$$
\frac d{dt}(m\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r})
=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F
=\frac\mu{r^3}\mathbf r\times(\dot{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf r)
=\frac\mu r(\dot{\mathbf r}-\dot r\,\widehat{\mathbf r}).
$$

Meanwhile the radial unit vector obeys

$$
\frac d{dt}\widehat{\mathbf r}
=\frac{\dot{\mathbf r}-\dot r\,\widehat{\mathbf r}}r.
$$

Since $\psi\mathbf r=\mu\widehat{\mathbf r}$, these derivatives cancel. Thus the [generalized angular momentum in a radial monopole field](../../../../../../generalized-angular-momentum-in-a-radial-monopole-field.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf L=m\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}-\mu\widehat{\mathbf r},\qquad\dot{\mathbf L}=0.}
$$

The argument applies away from $r=0$, where the field is defined, for any radial $\phi$. In contrast to ordinary [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md), this conserved vector has $\mathbf L\cdot\widehat{\mathbf r}=-\mu$; it fixes a cone rather than generally fixing a plane.
