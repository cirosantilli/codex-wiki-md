<h1 id="11b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11B](../11b.md)

Let $dm$ be the gas mass ejected in a short interval, and let $dD$ be the stationary dust mass acquired. The rocket mass change is $dM=dD-dm$. Before the interval the [momentum](../../../../../momentum.md) of the rocket and this dust is $Mv$, because the incoming dust is at rest. Afterwards it is

$$
(M+dM)(v+dv)+(v-u)dm
$$

to first order; changing the gas ejection speed by $dv$ affects only second-order terms. [Momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md), with no external force, therefore gives

$$
M\,dv+v\,dM+(v-u)\,dm=0.
$$

Dividing by the time interval produces the [rocket equation with stationary mass accretion](../../../../../rocket-equation-with-stationary-mass-accretion.md):

$$
\boxed{M\dot v+v\dot M+(v-u)\dot m=0.}
$$

The incoming material's zero initial [momentum](../../../../../momentum.md) is essential: accelerating it to the rocket's speed consumes part of the thrust.
