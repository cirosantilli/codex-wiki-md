<h1 id="8b/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write a complex [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) as $v=u+iw$, where $u,w\in\mathbb R^n$. Since $M$ and its [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ are real,

$$
Mu=\lambda u,\qquad Mw=\lambda w.
$$

At least one of $u,w$ is nonzero, and it is a real [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) for the same [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). More generally every complex [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) is a complex linear combination of real ones: its real and imaginary parts lie in the real [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md). Thus **each [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) has a [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) chosen over the reals**. The conclusion does not assert that every arbitrary complex [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) is itself real.
