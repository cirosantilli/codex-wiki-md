<h1 id="8b/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $Mv=\lambda v$ with $v\in\mathbb C^n$ nonzero. For a real [symmetric matrix](../../../../../../../symmetric-matrix.md), $M^*=M$, where the star denotes the [conjugate transpose](../../../../../../../conjugate-transpose.md). Consequently $v^*Mv$ equals its [complex conjugate](../../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) and is real. The [eigenvalue equation](../../../../../../../eigenvalue-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{v^*Mv}{v^*v}\in\mathbb R,}
$$

since $v^*v>0$. Allowing a complex [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) in this argument is essential: reality of an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) must be proved, rather than assumed in advance.
