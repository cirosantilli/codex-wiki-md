<h1 id="1c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Away from the negative real axis, the [complex conjugate](../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) has principal [complex argument](../../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) $-\theta$. Consequently

$$
z^i+\overline z^{\,i}=(e^{-\theta}+e^\theta)e^{i\log r}=2\cosh\theta\,e^{i\log r}.
$$

Taking the [complex modulus](../../../../../../complex-modulus.md) gives $\cosh\theta=1$, hence $\theta=0$. The remaining equation is $e^{i\log r}=i$, so

$$
\boxed{z=e^{\pi/2+2\pi k},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

On the negative real axis, both $z$ and $\overline z$ have principal [complex argument](../../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) $\pi$, so the left-hand side has modulus $2e^{-\pi}$, not two. There are no additional solutions there. The printed second term is the principal power of $\overline z$, not the [complex conjugate](../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) of $z^i$.
