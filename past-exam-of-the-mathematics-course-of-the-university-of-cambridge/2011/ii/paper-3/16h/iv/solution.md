<h1 id="16h/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

[Well-orders](../../../../../../well-order.md) are not first-order [axiomatisable](../../../../../../first-order-axiomatizable-class.md). Suppose a theory $T$ axiomatized them. Enlarge the language by constants $c_0,c_1,\ldots$ and add

$$
c_{i+1}<c_i\qquad(i\geq0).
$$

Every finite subset of the resulting theory has a model in the well-order $(\mathbb N,<)$: assign its finitely many constrained constants to a sufficiently long finite descending chain. Thus the [compactness theorem](../../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) supplies a model of $T$ with an infinite descending chain. The set of the $c_i$ has no least element, contradicting well-ordering. This shows why the second-order assertion that every nonempty subset has a least member cannot be replaced by a first-order theory in the poset language.

$$
\boxed{\text{Well-orders are not first-order axiomatizable.}}
$$
