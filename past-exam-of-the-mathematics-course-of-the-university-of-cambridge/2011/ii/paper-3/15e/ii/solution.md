<h1 id="15e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Both background components are pressure-free, so their densities scale as $a^{-3}$ and $\Omega_H$ is constant. Since $\dot a/a=2/(3t)$ and $\bar\rho_C=(1-\Omega_H)/(6\pi Gt^2)$, a [power law](../../../../../../power-law.md) $\delta=t^\alpha$ satisfies

$$
\alpha(\alpha-1)+\frac43\alpha-\frac23(1-\Omega_H)=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\alpha_\pm=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{25-24\Omega_H}}6.}
$$

For $0\leq\Omega_H<1$ the plus sign gives a growing mode and the minus sign a decaying mode. At $\Omega_H=1$ the growing exponent becomes zero, since no gravitating clumping component remains.

When $\Omega_H=2/3$, $\alpha_+=1/3$, so $\delta\propto t^{1/3}\propto a^{1/2}$. The [redshift](../../../../../../redshift.md) relation gives $a_{\rm today}/a_{\rm eq}=1+z_{\rm eq}\simeq10^5$. Even an entirely growing initial mode therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\delta_{\rm today}\simeq10^{-5}(10^5)^{1/2}=10^{-5/2}\simeq3.2\times10^{-3}.}
$$

The perturbation remains far below order unity, so it cannot produce the observed nonlinear gravitational clumping within the stated model. A decaying admixture does not provide the missing sustained growth.
