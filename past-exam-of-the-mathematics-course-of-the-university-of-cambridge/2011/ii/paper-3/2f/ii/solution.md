↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Yes. Take exterior poles $\zeta_n=1+1/n$. By part (i), choose a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $p_n$ with

$$
\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left|p_n(z)-\frac1{z-\zeta_n}\right|<\frac1n.
$$

For any fixed $z\in\Gamma$, $z\ne1$, and therefore $(z-\zeta_n)^{-1}\to(z-1)^{-1}$. The [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) proves

$$
\boxed{p_n(z)\longrightarrow\frac1{z-1}\quad\text{for every }z\in\Gamma.}
$$

This is [pointwise convergence](../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md). It is not [uniform convergence](../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) on $\Gamma$: every polynomial is bounded on the closed diamond, whereas $(z-1)^{-1}$ is unbounded as $z\to1$ along $\Gamma$.
