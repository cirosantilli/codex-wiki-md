<h1 id="10e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Electrical neutrality gives $n_e\simeq n_p$ at the accuracy of the proposed estimate. With $V\sim R^3$ and $n_p\sim M/(m_pR^3)$, the [degenerate electron gas](../../../../../../degenerate-electron-gas.md) result gives

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm kin}\sim hc\left(\frac M{m_p}\right)^{4/3}\frac1R.}
$$

The [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is $E_{\rm grav}\sim-GM^2/R$. Both scale as $R^{-1}$, so in the strictly ultrarelativistic approximation

$$
E_{\rm total}\sim\frac1R\left[hc\left(\frac M{m_p}\right)^{4/3}-GM^2\right].
$$

For sufficiently large mass the bracket is negative, and contraction lowers the energy without bound in this model: [electron degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../electron-degeneracy-pressure.md) cannot halt collapse. Equating the scales gives the [Chandrasekhar mass](../../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md)

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm crit}\sim\frac{(hc/G)^{3/2}}{m_p^2}.}
$$

Below this scale, the strictly ultrarelativistic expression alone has no finite-radius energy minimum; including the transition to nonrelativistic electrons produces the stable [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) branch. Composition and the stellar density profile supply the numerical factors omitted by this scaling estimate.
