<h1 id="25k/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Now $f\ge0$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md). The functions $\min(f_n,f)$ converge [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) to $f$ and are dominated by the integrable $f$. The [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) gives $\int\min(f_n,f)\to\int f$. Using $|f_n-f|=f_n+f-2\min(f_n,f)$ and the assumed convergence of the nonnegative integrals yields

$$
\boxed{\int|f_n-f|\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.}
$$

This is the nonnegative form of [Scheffé's lemma](../../../../../../scheffe-lemma.md): almost-everywhere convergence together with convergence of total mass implies convergence in the [L1 norm](../../../../../../l1-norm.md).
