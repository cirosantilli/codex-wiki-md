<h1 id="18h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The three [resolvent cubic](../../../../../../cubic-resolvent-of-a-quartic.md) roots correspond to the partitions $12|34$, $13|24$, $14|23$. Permuting the four roots of the quartic permutes these three quantities and defines a homomorphism $G\to S_3$. They are distinct: because the quartic has zero cubic coefficient, their differences include

$$
(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)^2-(\alpha_1+\alpha_3)^2=(\alpha_2-\alpha_3)(\alpha_1-\alpha_4)\ne0,
$$

since [characteristic zero](../../../../../../characteristic-zero.md) and irreducibility imply separability. Thus the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of the action is exactly $V_4$. The [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) of the resolvent is the subfield generated by its roots; restriction from the quartic [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) is surjective onto its [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md), since automorphisms of a normal separable intermediate extension extend to the [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(Q)\cong G/V_4.}
$$

In the four cases these groups are respectively the trivial group, $C_2$, $C_3$ and $S_3$. Computing the symmetric functions also gives $Q(X)=X^3+2bX^2+(b^2-4d)X-c^2$ with the source's root convention.
