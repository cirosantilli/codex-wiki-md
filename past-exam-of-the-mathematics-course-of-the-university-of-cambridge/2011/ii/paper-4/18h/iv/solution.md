<h1 id="18h/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Over $K=\mathbb Q$, take

$$
\boxed{P(X)=X^4-10X^2+1.}
$$

Its roots are $\pm\sqrt2\pm\sqrt3$. For $\alpha=\sqrt2+\sqrt3$, its inverse is $\sqrt3-\sqrt2$, so both square roots belong to $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. Since $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ has degree four, $P$ is irreducible and this field is its [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md). Independently changing the signs of the two square roots gives its [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) $C_2\times C_2=V_4$, which is abelian and satisfies the hypothesis.
