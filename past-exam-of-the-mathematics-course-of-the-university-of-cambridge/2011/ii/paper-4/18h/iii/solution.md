<h1 id="18h/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Factorization over $K$ gives the orbits of the [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) on the three resolvent roots. If $Q$ splits completely, the quotient is trivial and $G=V_4$. If $Q$ is a linear factor times an irreducible quadratic, the quotient is $C_2$ and $G=D_8$.

If $Q$ is irreducible, the quotient acts transitively on three roots, so it is $C_3$ or $S_3$. The product of their pairwise differences is a square root of the nonzero [discriminant](../../../../../../discriminant.md) $\Delta$ and changes by the sign of the [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md). Thus $\Delta$ is a square in $K$ exactly when the quotient contains only even [permutations](../../../../../../permutation.md). The criterion is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{factorization and discriminant of }Q&G\\\hline
\text{three linear factors}&V_4\\
\text{linear times irreducible quadratic}&D_8\\
\text{irreducible, }\Delta\in K^{\times2}&A_4\\
\text{irreducible, }\Delta\notin K^{\times2}&S_4
\end{array}}
$$

The assumed containment of $V_4$ is essential to reconstruct $G$ from the quotient by this table.
