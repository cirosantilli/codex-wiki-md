<h1 id="9c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The autonomous [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is constant and positive on every trajectory, since $q\ne0$. [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) give $\dot q=2p$, $\dot p=2/q^3$, and hence

$$
\frac d{dt}(pq)=q\dot p+p\dot q=2/q^2+2p^2=2H.
$$

Thus **$u=pq-2Ht$ is conserved**. Put $s=u+2Ht$. Multiplying the energy equation by $q^2$ gives $Hq^2=s^2+1$. The full classical path is therefore

$$
\boxed{q(t)=\eta\sqrt{\frac{(u+2Ht)^2+1}{H}},\qquad p(t)=\frac{u+2Ht}{q(t)},\qquad\eta\in\{-1,1\}.}
$$

The sign $\eta$ labels the two disconnected half-lines and is fixed by initial data. Differentiating verifies both [Hamilton equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md). The inverse-square potential prevents the path from crossing zero.
