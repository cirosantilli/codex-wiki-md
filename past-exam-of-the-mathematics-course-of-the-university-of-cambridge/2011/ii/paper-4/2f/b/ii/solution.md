<h1 id="2f/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If the boundary loop meets zero, the assertion already holds. Otherwise suppose, for contradiction, that $f$ has no zero anywhere in the disc. Then $K(s,t)=f((1-s)e^{2\pi it})$ is a [homotopy](../../../../../../../homotopy.md) through nonvanishing closed loops from the boundary loop to the constant loop $f(0)$. Constant loops have [winding number](../../../../../../../winding-number.md) zero, whereas part (i) gives [winding number](../../../../../../../winding-number.md) one. This contradicts [homotopy invariance of winding number](../../../../../../../homotopy-invariance-of-winding-number.md). Hence **$\boxed{f(z)=0\text{ for some }z\in B}$**.
