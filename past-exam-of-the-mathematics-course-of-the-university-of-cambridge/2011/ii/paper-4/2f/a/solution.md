<h1 id="2f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Normalize the nonvanishing loop to $\gamma(t)/|\gamma(t)|$. A continuous [argument](../../../../../../argument-complex-analysis.md) lift is a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $\theta:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ such that $\gamma(t)/|\gamma(t)|=e^{i\theta(t)}$. Such a lift exists on an interval; any two differ by a constant [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) multiple of $2\pi$. The [winding number](../../../../../../winding-number.md) is

$$
\boxed{w(\gamma;0)=\frac{\theta(1)-\theta(0)}{2\pi}\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

Its value is independent of the lift. This definition applies to continuous loops without assuming a differentiable contour.

The [homotopy invariance of winding number](../../../../../../homotopy-invariance-of-winding-number.md) states: if $H:[0,1]^2\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ is continuous and $H(s,0)=H(s,1)$ for every $s$, then $w(H(s,\cdot);0)$ is independent of $s$. Neither the loops nor their moving basepoints may pass through zero. One may justify invariance by uniformly subdividing the [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) into nearby loops whose normalized values have a continuous argument difference of magnitude less than $\pi$; that argument difference has equal endpoint values, so their [winding numbers](../../../../../../winding-number.md) agree.
