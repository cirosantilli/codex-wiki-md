<h1 id="7c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [nearly Hamiltonian system](../../../../../../nearly-hamiltonian-system.md), the unperturbed [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is conserved and its level curves typically contain a family of [periodic orbits](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md). A weak perturbation changes the energy slowly. The [energy balance method](../../../../../../energy-balance-method.md) integrates $\dot H$ over one unperturbed period to obtain the leading energy change $\Delta H(H)$. A zero of this change selects a possible persistent [periodic orbit](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md). A simple zero persists for sufficiently small perturbation by a [Poincaré return map](../../../../../../poincare-map.md) and the [implicit function theorem](../../../../../../implicit-function-theorem.md). A negative slope of the averaged energy drift gives attraction; a positive slope gives repulsion. A multiple zero requires higher-order analysis, and a zero-free leading drift rules out periodic motion in the corresponding perturbative energy range.
