<h1 id="1i/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose the [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md) were the finite list $p_1,\ldots,p_r$. The [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $N=p_1\cdots p_r+1>1$ has a [prime factor](../../../../../../prime-factor.md), by repeatedly factoring any composite factor until a prime is reached. But division by any $p_j$ leaves remainder one, so that [prime factor](../../../../../../prime-factor.md) is absent from the list. This contradiction proves **there are infinitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md)**. The argument does not require $N$ itself to be prime.
