<h1 id="1i/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For any positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $m$, put $N=(m+1)!$. Each of $N+2,N+3,\ldots,N+m+1$ is composite, since $N+j$ is divisible by $j$ and larger than $j$. Let $p$ be the largest [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) at most $N+1$, and let $q$ be the smallest [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) at least $N+m+2$. Such $q$ exists by part (i), and no prime lies strictly between $p$ and $q$. Therefore these are consecutive [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md) with

$$
\boxed{q-p\ge m+1.}
$$

Since $m$ is arbitrary, **gaps between consecutive [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md) are unbounded**.
