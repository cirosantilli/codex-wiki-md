<h1 id="15e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Differentiate the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) and use cosmological energy conservation:

$$
2H\dot H-2\frac{kc^2}{a^2}H
=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\dot\rho
=-8\pi GH(\rho+P/c^2).
$$

For an expanding universe $H>0$, division by $2H$ and substitution of the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) give the [Friedmann acceleration equation](../../../../../../friedmann-acceleration-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\frac{\ddot a}a=\dot H+H^2=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3P/c^2).}
$$

The [strong energy condition](../../../../../../strong-energy-condition.md) makes $\ddot a\leq0$. Since $aH=\dot a$ and $\dot a>0$,

$$
\frac d{dt}(aH)^2=2\dot a\ddot a\leq0.
$$

Thus the quantity is nonincreasing, with strict decrease when the energy condition is strict. Dividing the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) by $H^2$ yields

$$
\boxed{\Omega-1=\frac{kc^2}{(aH)^2}.}
$$

For fixed nonzero curvature, the magnitude of $\Omega-1$ grows as expansion decelerates. The observed closeness to one therefore requires much tighter early-time closeness to one in the standard decelerating model: this is the [flatness problem](../../../../../../flatness-problem.md). Accelerated expansion during inflation reverses this evolution and suppresses the curvature contribution.
