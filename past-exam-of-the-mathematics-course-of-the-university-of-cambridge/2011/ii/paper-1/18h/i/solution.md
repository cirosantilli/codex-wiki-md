<h1 id="18h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [field homomorphism](../../../../../../field-homomorphism.md) fixing $K$ is injective, since its kernel is an ideal of a field and cannot contain one. It is also $K$-linear. Equal finite [field extension degrees](../../../../../../degree-of-a-field-extension.md) then make it a bijection, hence a $K$-isomorphism.

Equal degrees alone do not ensure that any such homomorphism exists. For example $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ both have degree two, but a $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism would require a square root of two in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. Squaring $a+b\sqrt3$ with rational $a,b$ would give $2ab=0$ and $a^2+3b^2=2$. Neither $a^2=2$ nor $b^2=2/3$ has a rational solution. Thus **the two degree-two extensions are not isomorphic over $\mathbb Q$**.
