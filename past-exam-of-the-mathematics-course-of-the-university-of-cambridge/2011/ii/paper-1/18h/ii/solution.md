<h1 id="18h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An isomorphism fixing $L$ fixes its subfield $K$, so **an $L$-isomorphism is a $K$-isomorphism**. The converse is false because an isomorphism over $K$ may act nontrivially on $L$.

Take $K=\mathbb Q$, $L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, $\alpha=2^{1/4}>0$, $F=L(\alpha)$ and $F'=L(i\alpha)$. Both are degree-two extensions of $L$. The polynomial $X^4-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by the [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md), and both $\alpha$ and $i\alpha$ are roots. Sending $\alpha$ to $i\alpha$ therefore gives a $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism $F\to F'$; on $L$ it sends $\sqrt2$ to $-\sqrt2$.

There is no $L$-isomorphism. The two [quadratic extensions](../../../../../../quadratic-extension.md) adjoin square roots of $\sqrt2$ and $-\sqrt2$. Their square classes would agree only if $-1$ were a square in $L$. To check this criterion directly, a square root of $\sqrt2$ in $L(i\alpha)$ would have form $a+b(i\alpha)$; its square forces $ab=0$. The case $b=0$ would put $\alpha$ in $L$, while $a=0$ would require $b^2=-1$. Both are impossible, the latter since $L\subseteq\mathbb R$. Thus **the extensions are isomorphic over $K$ but not over $L$**.
