<h1 id="30a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In phase-space coordinates $z=(x,v)$, the [characteristic equations for a transport equation](../../../../../../characteristic-equations-for-a-transport-equation.md) are the [Hamilton equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md)

$$
\dot X=\nabla_vH(X,V),\qquad\dot V=-\nabla_xH(X,V),\qquad (X(0),V(0))=(\xi,\eta).
$$

Smoothness of $H$ gives a unique local [Hamiltonian flow](../../../../../../hamiltonian-flow.md) $\Phi_t(\xi,\eta)=(X(t),V(t))$. Along it the chain rule gives $d[f(X,V,t)]/dt=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f(x,v,t)=f_I(\Phi_{-t}(x,v)).}
$$

The inverse is the backward autonomous flow, so this formula defines the unique local classical solution wherever the characteristic reaches the initial hypersurface. A smooth Hamiltonian on all of $\mathbb R^{2n}$ need not have a globally complete flow; the formula is understood on the interval and region where both flows exist. A common global time interval requires the corresponding completeness or growth assumption.
