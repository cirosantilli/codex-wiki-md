<h1 id="19i/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $g$ belongs to the [centre of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md), then $\rho(g)$ commutes with $\rho(h)$ for every $h\in G$. The [Schur lemma](../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) says that an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional complex [irreducible representation](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) commuting with the representation is scalar. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\rho(g)=\lambda_gI\quad(g\in Z).}
$$

Since $g$ has finite order, $\lambda_g$ is a [root of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) and has modulus one.
