<h1 id="11g/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $X=f_1\cdots f_s$ were a product of [irreducible elements](../../../../../../../irreducible-element.md) of $R$. Since [degree of a polynomial](../../../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) is additive under products, exactly one factor has positive degree and all the others are constant. By the preceding classification, the positive-degree factor has constant term $\pm1$, while every constant irreducible is $\pm p$ for a prime $p$. Their product therefore has a nonzero constant term, contradicting $X(0)=0$.

Thus **$X$ is not a product of irreducibles**, so this [integral domain](../../../../../../../integral-domain.md) is not an [atomic domain](../../../../../../../atomic-domain.md) and in particular is not a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md).
