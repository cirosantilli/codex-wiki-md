<h1 id="11g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) states that if

$$
f(X)=a_nX^n+\cdots+a_0\in\mathbb Z[X]
$$

has a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$ such that $p\nmid a_n$, $p\mid a_i$ for every $i<n$, and $p^2\nmid a_0$, then $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

After dividing out the [polynomial content](../../../../../../polynomial-content.md), suppose for contradiction that the resulting primitive polynomial factors as $f=gh$ with positive-degree $g,h\in\mathbb Z[X]$, using [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../../../../gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md). Reduction modulo $p$ gives $\bar f=a_nX^n$, so in the [polynomial ring](../../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) $\mathbb F_p[X]$ both $\bar g$ and $\bar h$ are monomials. Because both factors have positive degree, their constant coefficients are divisible by $p$. Their product $a_0$ is then divisible by $p^2$, a contradiction.
