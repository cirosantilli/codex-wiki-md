<h1 id="3e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3E](../3e.md)

A [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md) is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) if it is not the union of two disjoint nonempty [open sets](../../../../../open-set.md), or equivalently has no nontrivial subset that is both an [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) and a [closed set](../../../../../closed-set.md).

Let $K_x$ be the union of all connected subspaces containing $x$. A union of [connected subsets](../../../../../connected-subset.md) with a common point is connected: if a [separation of a topological space](../../../../../separation-of-a-topological-space.md) existed, every member containing $x$ would lie wholly in the same side. Hence $K_x$ is connected and contains every connected subspace through $x$, so it is the [connected component](../../../../../connected-component.md) of $x$.

If $K_x\cap K_y\ne\varnothing$, their union is connected and maximality gives $K_x=K_y$. Otherwise they are disjoint. Since every $x$ belongs to $K_x$, the connected components **partition $X$**.
