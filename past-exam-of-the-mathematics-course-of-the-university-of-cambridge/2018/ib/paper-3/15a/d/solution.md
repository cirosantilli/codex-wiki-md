<h1 id="15a/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Interpreting the displayed function as

$$
\gamma_n(x)=\frac1\pi\frac{n}{(nx)^2+1},
$$

we have $\gamma_n\geq0$ and, after $u=nx$, $\int_{\mathbb R}\gamma_n=1$. Moreover its mass outside any fixed neighborhood of zero tends to zero. Therefore $(\gamma_n)$ is an [approximate identity](../../../../../../approximate-identity.md), and for every test function $f$,

$$
\int f(x)\gamma_n(x)\,dx=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{f(u/n)}{1+u^2}\,du\longrightarrow f(0).
$$

Thus **$\gamma_n\to\delta$ in the sense of distributions**.
