<h1 id="15a/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Since the odd [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) part of the complex exponential integrates to zero,

$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-n}^ne^{ikx}\,dk
=\frac1\pi\int_0^n\cos(kx)\,dk.
$$

Part (e)(ii) therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-n}^ne^{ikx}\,dk=\delta(x)}
$$

in the sense of distributions. This is the [Fourier representation of the Dirac delta function](../../../../../../fourier-representation-of-the-dirac-delta-function.md).
