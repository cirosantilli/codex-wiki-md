<h1 id="11f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The map $f:U\to\mathbb R^n$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $a$ when there is a linear map $Df(a)$ such that

$$
f(a+h)=f(a)+Df(a)h+o(\|h\|).
$$

It is $C^1$ when it is differentiable everywhere and $a\mapsto Df(a)$ is continuous. The definition immediately gives $\|f(a+h)-f(a)\|\leq(\|Df(a)\|+o(1))\|h\|\to0$, so differentiability implies continuity.

The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) says that if $f$ is $C^1$ near $a$ and $Df(a)$ is invertible, then $f$ restricts to a diffeomorphism between neighbourhoods of $a$ and $f(a)$.
