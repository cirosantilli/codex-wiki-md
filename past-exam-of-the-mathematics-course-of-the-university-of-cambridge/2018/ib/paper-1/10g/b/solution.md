<h1 id="10g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Proceed by induction on $|G|$. If $p\mid|Z(G)|$, [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) gives a central subgroup $C$ of order $p$. By induction $G/C$ has a subgroup of order $p^{n-1}$; its inverse image in $G$ has order $p^n$.

If $p\nmid|Z(G)|$, the [class equation](../../../../../../class-equation.md) shows that some noncentral conjugacy class has size not divisible by $p$. For a representative $x$, its class size is $[G:C_G(x)]$, so $p^n\mid|C_G(x)|$. This centralizer is proper because $x$ is noncentral, and induction gives it, hence $G$, a subgroup of order $p^n$. This proves Sylow's first theorem.
