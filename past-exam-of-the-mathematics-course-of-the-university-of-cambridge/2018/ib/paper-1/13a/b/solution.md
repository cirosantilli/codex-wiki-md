<h1 id="13a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Integrate $ze^{i\pi z}/(z^2+a^2)$ over the upper semicircle. [Jordan lemma](../../../../../../jordan-s-lemma.md) removes the arc, and the pole at $z=ia$ has residue $e^{-\pi a}/2$. Hence

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{x e^{i\pi x}}{x^2+a^2}\,dx
=\pi i e^{-\pi a}.
$$

The cosine contribution is odd, while the sine contribution is even. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\int_0^\infty\frac{x\sin(\pi x)}{x^2+a^2}\,dx
=\frac\pi2e^{-\pi a}.}
$$
