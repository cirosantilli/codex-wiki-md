<h1 id="16b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The equations are

$$
\ddot x+\alpha x^3+\beta xy^2=0,
\qquad
\ddot y+\alpha y^3+\beta x^2y=0.
$$

Hence

$$
\dot J=(\beta-\alpha)xy(x^2-y^2),
$$

which is not generally zero. Conservation requires **$\beta/\alpha=1$**. Then the potential is $\alpha(x^2+y^2)^2/4$, so the action has [rotational symmetry](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md). By [Noether's theorem](../../../../../../noether-conserved-quantity-for-a-mechanical-point-symmetry.md), $J$ is the conserved angular momentum, up to the sign convention used in its definition.
