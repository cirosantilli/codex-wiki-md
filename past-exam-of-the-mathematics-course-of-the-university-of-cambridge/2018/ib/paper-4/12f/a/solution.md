A [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) is one in which every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) converges. On $I=(0,1]$ define

$$
d(x,y)=|x^{-1}-y^{-1}|.
$$

The map $x\mapsto1/x$ is an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) from $(I,d)$ onto the closed subset $[1,\infty)$ of $\mathbb R$, so $d$ is a metric and is complete. That map and its inverse are continuous in the ordinary Euclidean metrics, so it is also a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Therefore $d$ induces exactly the usual subspace topology on $I$.
