<h1 id="9f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let the nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) have degree $d$ and leading coefficient $c$. If $d$ is odd, its limits at $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ have opposite signs, so the [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) shows that its image is $\mathbb R$. If $d$ is even and $c>0$, it tends to $+\infty$ at both ends. Outside a sufficiently large compact interval it exceeds $f(0)$, while on that interval the [extreme value theorem](../../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md) gives a global minimum $a$. Continuity and the intermediate value theorem then give image $[a,\infty)$. For $c<0$, applying this argument to $-f$ gives $(-\infty,a]$. These are **exactly the three possible images**.
