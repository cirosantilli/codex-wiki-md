<h1 id="12c/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Dualize the antisymmetric tensor to a vector,

$$
d_i=\frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}D_{jk},\qquad D_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ijk}d_k.
$$

An isotropic symmetric rank-two tensor depending quadratically on one vector can only be a linear combination of $\delta_{ij}d_kd_k$ and $d_id_j$: after rotating $d$ to the third axis, invariance under all rotations about that axis forces the matrix to be $\operatorname{diag}(a,a,b)$.

Since $D_{ik}D_{jk}=\delta_{ij}d^2-d_id_j$ and $D_{kl}D_{kl}=2d^2$, the most general answer may be written

$$
\boxed{T_{ijklmn}D_{kl}D_{mn}
=a\,\delta_{ij}D_{kl}D_{kl}+b\,D_{ik}D_{jk}.}
$$

The two displayed tensors are independent, for example by taking $d$ along a coordinate axis and comparing the components parallel and perpendicular to it. Hence there are exactly two independent terms.
