<h1 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $(V_n)$ be a [convergent sequence](../../../../../../convergent-sequence.md) in the [Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers](../../../../../../stone-cech-compactification-of-the-natural-numbers.md), with limit $V$. Each fixed $W\ne V$ occurs only finitely often: the [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) property gives a [neighbourhood](../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $V$ excluding $W$, and eventually the sequence is in that [neighbourhood](../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md).

If $(V_n)$ were not eventually equal to $V$, infinitely many terms would differ from $V$. They could not take only finitely many distinct values, because each such value occurs only finitely often. Recursively select a [subsequence](../../../../../../subsequence.md) of pairwise distinct terms, all different from $V$. It still converges to $V$, contradicting the preceding parts. Hence

$$
\boxed{V_n=V\text{ for all sufficiently large }n.}
$$

This establishes [no nontrivial convergent sequences in the Stone-Čech compactification](../../../../../../no-nontrivial-convergent-sequences-in-the-stone-cech-compactification.md) for arbitrary sequences, including ones with repetitions.
