<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The answer is **no**. Let $X_0=\mathbb N$, regarded as one point of the [space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers](../../../../../../space-of-infinite-subsets-of-the-natural-numbers.md), and put

$$
O=[\mathbb N]^\omega\setminus\{X_0\}
=\bigcup_{j\geq1}[\varnothing,\mathbb N\setminus\{j\}].
$$

The displayed union makes $O$ an [open set](../../../../../../open-set.md) in the [Ellentuck topology](../../../../../../ellentuck-topology.md). On the other hand, every basic [neighbourhood](../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) $[s,A]$ of $X_0$ contains a different infinite set: keep $s$ and remove one element from its infinite remaining tail. This other point belongs to $O$. Therefore $X_0$ is in the [closure](../../../../../../closure-topology.md) of $O$ but not in $O$, so

$$
\boxed{O\text{ is star-open but not star-closed}.}
$$

The fact that basic [neighbourhoods](../../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) are [clopen sets](../../../../../../clopen-set.md) does not make arbitrary unions of them closed.
