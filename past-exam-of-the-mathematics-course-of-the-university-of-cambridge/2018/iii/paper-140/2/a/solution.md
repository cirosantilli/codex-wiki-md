<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\dim V=2n$ and let $V_+=\ker(S-I)$ and $V_-=\ker(S+I)$ be the two [eigenspaces](../../../../../../eigenspace.md). The [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) identity gives a [direct sum](../../../../../../direct-sum.md), since

$$
v=\frac{v+Sv}{2}+\frac{v-Sv}{2},\qquad V=V_+\oplus V_-.
$$

For $u,v$ in either one of these [eigenspaces](../../../../../../eigenspace.md), the [anti-symplectic involution](../../../../../../anti-symplectic-involution.md) identity implies

$$
\Omega(u,v)=\Omega(Su,Sv)=-\Omega(u,v),
$$

so both are [isotropic subspaces of a symplectic vector space](../../../../../../isotropic-subspace-of-a-symplectic-vector-space.md). Such a subspace has dimension at most $n$: $W\subset W^\Omega$ and the [nondegenerate bilinear form](../../../../../../nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) $\Omega$ gives $\dim W^\Omega=2n-\dim W$, where $W^\Omega$ is the [symplectic orthogonal complement](../../../../../../symplectic-orthogonal-complement.md). As their dimensions add to $2n$, both have dimension $n$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Fix}(S)=V_+\text{ is a Lagrangian subspace}.}
$$
