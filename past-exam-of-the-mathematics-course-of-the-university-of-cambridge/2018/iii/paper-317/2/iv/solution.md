<h1 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a monatomic [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) of fixed composition, the [adiabatic temperature gradient](../../../../../../adiabatic-temperature-gradient.md) is $\nabla_{\rm ad}=2/5$. The [Schwarzschild criterion](../../../../../../schwarzschild-criterion.md) places the onset of [convection](../../../../../../convection.md) at

$$
-\frac13+\frac{11}{24}\left(\frac T{T_{\rm eff}}\right)^{9/2}=\frac25,
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{T_{\rm eff}}T=\left(\frac58\right)^{2/9}\simeq0.901.}
$$

Thus $T\simeq1.11T_{\rm eff}$. The integrated [power-law opacity radiative envelope](../../../../../../power-law-opacity-radiative-envelope.md) also gives $P/P_{\rm phot}=2^{2/3}\simeq1.59$ there: the radiative layer spans only a small pressure range below the [photosphere](../../../../../../photosphere.md). Its geometrical depth is of order $H_P\log(1.59)$, with $H_P$ a representative [pressure scale height](../../../../../../pressure-scale-height.md), and is small compared with the stellar radius in the assumed thin envelope.

The steep increase of [negative hydrogen ion opacity](../../../../../../negative-hydrogen-ion-opacity.md) with [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) rapidly increases the [radiative temperature gradient](../../../../../../radiative-temperature-gradient.md), so radiation alone soon fails to transport the flux stably. Beyond that point the radiative profile must be replaced by a [convective envelope](../../../../../../convective-envelope.md). Partial [ionization](../../../../../../ionization.md) can lower the actual [adiabatic temperature gradient](../../../../../../adiabatic-temperature-gradient.md) and shift onset; the numerical ratio here uses the fixed-$\gamma$ monatomic approximation.
