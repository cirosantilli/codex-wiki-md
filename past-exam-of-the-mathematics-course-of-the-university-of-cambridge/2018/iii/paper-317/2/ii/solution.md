<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At the [photosphere](../../../../../../photosphere.md), use the stated boundary pressure and the [effective temperature](../../../../../../effective-temperature.md) relation $L=4\pi R^2\sigma T_{\rm eff}^4$. The [radiation constant](../../../../../../radiation-constant.md) obeys $a_{\rm rad}c=4\sigma$ by the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md). Substitution into the [radiative temperature gradient](../../../../../../radiative-temperature-gradient.md) gives

$$
\nabla_{\rm phot}=\frac{3\kappa_{\rm phot}L}{16\pi a_{\rm rad}cGM T_{\rm eff}^4}\frac{2GM}{3\kappa_{\rm phot}R^2}
=\frac{2\sigma}{4a_{\rm rad}c}=\boxed{\frac18}.
$$

This uses the approximate [stellar surface boundary condition](../../../../../../stellar-surface-boundary-condition.md) at optical depth $2/3$; it is not a universal photospheric gradient for arbitrary atmospheres.
