<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A prime is completely split in an extension of degree $n$ exactly when there are $n$ primes above it. Indeed, the [fundamental identity for prime decomposition](../../../../../../fundamental-identity-for-prime-decomposition.md) is $\sum_{\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p}e_{\mathfrak q}f_{\mathfrak q}=n$, and every summand is a positive integer. Thus $n$ primes means that every ramification index and residue degree equals one, and conversely.

By part (a), the number of primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak p$ is the number of right $D$-orbits on $H\backslash G$. This set has $[G:H]=[L:K]$ elements. It has that many orbits exactly when every orbit is a singleton, equivalently when

$$
HgD=Hg\quad\text{for all }g\in G.
$$

This condition says $gDg^{-1}\subseteq H$ for every $g$, or

$$
D\subseteq\bigcap_{g\in G}g^{-1}Hg=\operatorname{Core}_G(H),
$$

the [subgroup core](../../../../../../core-group-theory.md).

Because $M$ is the [Galois closure](../../../../../../galois-closure-of-a-field-extension.md) of $L/K$, it is generated by all the fields $g(L)$. The fixed field of $gHg^{-1}$ is $g(L)$, so the fixed field of the core is their compositum, namely $M$. The [Galois correspondence](../../../../../../galois-correspondence.md) therefore gives $\operatorname{Core}_G(H)=1$. Hence

$$
\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D=1.
$$

In the Galois extension $M/K$, the number of primes above $\mathfrak p$ is $[G:D]$, so $D=1$ is equivalent to complete splitting in $M$. This proves that [complete splitting is preserved by Galois closure](../../../../../../complete-splitting-is-preserved-by-galois-closure.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }M.}
$$

No prior unramifiedness assumption is needed: complete splitting already forces all ramification indices to be one, and the decomposition-group argument includes the ramified case.
