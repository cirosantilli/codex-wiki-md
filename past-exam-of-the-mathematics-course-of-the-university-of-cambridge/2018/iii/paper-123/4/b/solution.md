<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The polynomial is [Eisenstein](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at $3$, so a root $\alpha$ generates a totally ramified cubic field $F=\mathbb Q_3(\alpha)$ and is a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) of $F$. Its cubic discriminant is

$$
\Delta=-4(-3)^3-27\cdot3^2=-135=-5\cdot3^3.
$$

Its $3$-adic valuation is $3$, so it is not a square. The [cubic discriminant criterion](../../../../../../cubic-discriminant-criterion.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q_3)\cong S_3,\qquad[M:\mathbb Q_3]=6.}
$$

The unique quadratic subfield is generated by $\sqrt\Delta$. Since the permitted square root $\sqrt{-5}$ is in $\mathbb Q_3$, this subfield is

$$
E=\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt3).
$$

It is totally ramified of degree two, by the [Eisenstein polynomial](../../../../../../eisenstein-polynomial.md) $T^2-3$. The ramification index of $M/\mathbb Q_3$ is divisible by both the index $3$ of $F$ and the index $2$ of $E$. Since the whole degree is $6$, it follows that

$$
\boxed{e(M/\mathbb Q_3)=6,\qquad k_M=\mathbb F_3.}
$$

In particular $G_0=G$.

We use the following standard facts about totally ramified extensions: every uniformizer generates the valuation ring over the base valuation ring, and for a totally ramified Galois extension

$$
G_i=\{\sigma:v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)\geq i+1\}\quad(i\geq0).
$$

Thus we can use the [uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups](../../../../../../uniformizer-criterion-for-lower-ramification-groups.md). Put $s=\sqrt3$. Since $v_M(\alpha)=2$ and $v_M(s)=3$,

$$
\pi=\frac{\alpha^2}{s},\qquad v_M(\pi)=4-3=1
$$

is a uniformizer of $M$.

Let $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ be the three roots. The [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) acts transitively on their unordered pairs and preserves the valuation, so all differences between distinct roots have the same valuation $d$. The discriminant identity gives

$$
18=v_M(\Delta)=2\sum_{i<j}v_M(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)=6d,
$$

hence $d=3$. Thus, for any $\sigma$, either $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha$ or $\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha$ has valuation $3$.

A nontrivial three-cycle fixes $s$, since it fixes the quadratic subfield, and sends $\alpha$ to a different root. Also $v_M(\sigma(\alpha)+\alpha)=2$: write the sum as $2\alpha+(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)$, whose two terms have unequal valuations $2,3$. Therefore

$$
v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)=3+2-3=2
\quad\text{for either nontrivial three-cycle}.
$$

A transposition changes the sign of $s$. Consequently

$$
\sigma(\pi)-\pi=-\frac{\sigma(\alpha)^2+\alpha^2}{s}.
$$

The numerator has valuation $4$: it is $2\alpha^2$ plus terms of strictly higher valuation if the root changes, and exactly $2\alpha^2$ if the root is fixed. Thus every transposition has $v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)=1$. We conclude that the [ramification groups of the splitting field of T3 minus 3T plus 3 over Q3](../../../../../../ramification-groups-of-the-splitting-field-of-t3-minus-3t-plus-3-over-q3.md) are

$$
\boxed{G_0=S_3,\qquad G_1=A_3\cong C_3,\qquad G_i=\{1\}\quad(i\geq2).}
$$

As an independent check, the cubic field has $\mathcal O_F=\mathbb Z_3[\alpha]$ and different exponent $v_F(g'(\alpha))=v_F(3(\alpha^2-1))=3$. The extension $M/F$ is tamely totally ramified of degree two, so its different exponent is $1$. The [different in a tower](../../../../../../different-in-a-tower.md) gives $d(M/\mathbb Q_3)=1+2\cdot3=7$, agreeing with the ramification sum $(6-1)+(3-1)=7$.
