<h1 id="3/1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Comparison with zero and nonnegativity of the two terms gives

$$
0\leq D(u_\alpha)+\alpha J(u_\alpha)
\leq D(0)+\alpha J(0)=\frac12\|f\|^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{0\leq J(u_\alpha)\leq\frac{\|f\|^2}{2\alpha},\qquad
J(u_\alpha)\longrightarrow0\quad(\alpha\to\infty).}
$$

For a concrete [variational regularization](../../../../../../../variational-regularization.md) example, take a [Hilbert space](../../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) and $J(u)=\|u\|^2$. The bound forces $u_\alpha\to0$ in [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md). Since $K$ is a [bounded linear operator](../../../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md), $Ku_\alpha\to0$, and continuity of the squared [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) gives

$$
\boxed{D(u_\alpha)\longrightarrow\tfrac12\|f\|^2.}
$$

The same conclusion is not forced by $J(u_\alpha)\to0$ for a regulariser with a nontrivial zero set.
