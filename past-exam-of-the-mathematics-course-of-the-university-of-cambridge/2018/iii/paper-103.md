↑ **Parent:** [Paper 103](paper-103.md)

<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

Fix a [Young tableau](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-tableau) $t$ of shape $\lambda$, let $C(t)$ be its column stabilizer, and put

$$
b_t=\sum_{g\in C(t)}\operatorname{sgn}(g)g,\qquad e_t=b_t\{t\}.
$$

These are the [Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#column-antisymmetrizer-of-a-young-tableau) and its [polytabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid). The [Specht module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) is generated by $e_t$ as a [cyclic module](../../../module-theory.md#cyclic-module), so the nonzero [R-module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) $\phi$ has $\phi(e_t)\ne0$. Because its extension $\Phi$ commutes with the [group algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#group-algebra) action,

$$
0\ne\phi(e_t)=\Phi(b_t\{t\})=b_t\Phi(\{t\}).
$$

Expand $\Phi(\{t\})$ in the [tabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) basis of the [Young permutation module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-permutation-module) $M^\mu$. At least one [tabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) $\{u\}$ therefore satisfies $b_t\{u\}\ne0$.

If a row of $u$ contains two entries from the same column of $t$, their transposition $\tau\in C(t)$ fixes $\{u\}$. Pairing the terms indexed by $g$ and $g\tau$ in $b_t\{u\}$ makes them cancel. This remains valid in [characteristic](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-of-a-field) two, since the paired coefficients sum to zero there as well. Thus each row of $u$ meets each column of $t$ in at most one entry.

The first $r$ rows of $u$ consequently contain at most $\min(r,\lambda'_j)$ entries from column $j$ of $t$, where $\lambda'$ is the [conjugate partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#conjugate-partition). Hence

$$
\sum_{i=1}^r\mu_i\leq\sum_j\min(r,\lambda'_j)=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i
\qquad(r\geq1).
$$

By the [dominance order on partitions](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#dominance-order-on-partitions), this proves

$$
\boxed{\lambda\unrhd\mu.}
$$

This is [dominance from a nonzero column antisymmetrizer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#dominance-from-a-nonzero-column-antisymmetrizer). The extension is what allows us to apply $b_t$ to an element of $M^\mu$ without dividing by the column stabilizer's order, which might vanish in the given [field](../../../algebra.md#field).

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

The [Young permutation module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-permutation-module) $M^{(n-k,k)}$ is the [symmetric-group subset permutation representation](../../../representation-theory.md#symmetric-group-subset-permutation-representation) on the $k$-element subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$: the second row of a [tabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) specifies the subset. For $k\geq1$, [restriction of a character](../../../representation-theory.md#restriction-of-a-character) to $S_{n-1}$ fixing $n$ splits these subsets into two [orbits of a group action](../../../group-theory.md#orbit-of-a-group-action), according as they contain $n$.

The corresponding [permutation representations](../../../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation) are on the $k$-subsets and the $(k-1)$-subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$. Their [Young permutation modules](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-permutation-module) have two row sizes $n-1-k,k$ and $n-k,k-1$, respectively. Sort each pair into decreasing order and omit any zero part; swapping the two row labels gives an isomorphic [permutation representation](../../../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation). This sorting matters at $n=2k$.

We use [Young's rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-s-rule): over the [complex numbers](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-number), the multiplicity of $S^\rho$ in $M^\eta$ is the [Kostka number](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#kostka-number) $K_{\rho\eta}$, and it can be nonzero only if $\rho$ dominates $\eta$ in the [dominance order on partitions](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#dominance-order-on-partitions). Since each $\eta$ above has at most two parts and size $n-1$, dominance gives

$$
\rho_1+\rho_2\geq\eta_1+\eta_2=n-1.
$$

Thus $\rho$ has at most two nonzero parts. The [character inner product](../../../representation-theory.md#character-inner-product) measures this constituent multiplicity, so

$$
\boxed{\left\langle\operatorname{Res}_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n}\pi^{(n-k,k)},\chi^\rho\right\rangle=0\quad\text{when }\ell(\rho)\geq3.}
$$

If $k=0$, the original and restricted modules are the [trivial representations](../../../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation), giving the same conclusion. For $n=1$, there is no partition of $n-1=0$ with three parts, so the assertion is vacuous.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

Apply [Young's rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-s-rule), stated as

$$
M^{(n-k,k)}\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\vdash n}(S^\rho)^{\oplus K_{\rho,(n-k,k)}}.
$$

Here the [Kostka number](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#kostka-number) counts [Semistandard Young tableaux](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#semistandard-young-tableau) of shape $\rho$ with $n-k$ entries equal to $1$ and $k$ entries equal to $2$. Their rows weakly increase and their columns strictly increase. A column can therefore have at most two cells, so only a [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer) $\rho=(n-j,j)$ can occur.

In such a [semistandard Young tableau](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#semistandard-young-tableau), all $j$ entries in the second row must be $2$ and all $j$ entries above them must be $1$. The whole first row is then fixed by the content: its first $n-k$ entries are $1$ and its remaining $k-j$ entries are $2$. This is possible exactly when $j\leq k$ and $j\leq n-k$. Because $k\leq n/2$, the second inequality follows from the first. There is exactly one filling for every $0\leq j\leq k$, and none for any other shape.

Consequently each displayed [Specht module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) has multiplicity one, proving the [two-row Young permutation module decomposition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#two-row-young-permutation-module-decomposition)

$$
\boxed{M^{(n-k,k)}\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^k S^{(n-j,j)}.}
$$

For $j=0$, the zero second part is omitted. As a dimension check, the [Hook-length formula](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length-formula) gives $\dim S^{(n-j,j)}=\binom nj-\binom n{j-1}$, with $\binom n{-1}=0$; the sum telescopes to $\binom nk$, the dimension of the original [permutation representation](../../../representation-theory.md#permutation-representation).

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

Use the [hook criterion in a beta set](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-criterion-in-a-beta-set): for a finite [beta set of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#beta-set-of-a-partition) $X$, each [hook length](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) is a difference $b-c$ with $b\in X$, $0\leq c<b$ and $c\notin X$, and every such difference is a hook length.

A hook length $pr$ therefore gives a bead $b$ and a gap $c=b-pr$. Along the progression

$$
b,\ b-p,\ b-2p,\ldots,\ b-rp=c,
$$

the first position is a bead and the last is a gap. Some adjacent positions are consequently a bead followed by a gap. Their difference is $p$, so $p$ is a [hook length](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length). Similarly the progression from $b$ to $c$ in steps of $r$ gives a hook length $r$. Every position used is nonnegative because it lies between $c$ and $b$.

Thus the [divisor closure of hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#divisor-closure-of-hook-lengths) yields

$$
\boxed{pr\in\mathcal H(\lambda)\ \Longrightarrow\ p,r\in\mathcal H(\lambda).}
$$

Neither $p$ nor $r$ needs to be a [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number).

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The converse is **false**. Take $p=2$, $r=3$, $n=6$ and the [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer) $\lambda=(3,3)$. Its [hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length), arranged in its [Young diagram](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram), are

$$
\begin{matrix}
4&3&2\\
3&2&1
\end{matrix}.
$$

Both $2$ and $3$ occur, but $6$ does not, even though $pr=6=n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\lambda=(3,3),\quad p=2,\quad r=3}
$$

is a counterexample with distinct $p,r$.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

Put $L=h_{i,j}=pr$. The [Hook-interval decomposition at a Young-diagram cell](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-interval-decomposition-at-a-young-diagram-cell) is the disjoint union

$$
\{1,\ldots,L\}
=\{h_{i,y}:j\leq y\leq\lambda_i\}
\sqcup\{L-h_{x,j}:i<x\leq\lambda'_j\}.
$$

Here is a proof of the identity using the [hook criterion in a beta set](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-criterion-in-a-beta-set). Keep only the cells weakly southeast of $(i,j)$, translating that cell to $(1,1)$; this gives the [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer)

$$
\nu=(\lambda_i-j+1,\ldots,\lambda_{\lambda'_j}-j+1).
$$

Let $\ell=\lambda'_j-i+1$ and use its [beta set of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#beta-set-of-a-partition) $b_s=\nu_s+\ell-s$. These numbers are precisely the [hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) down the original column from $(i,j)$, with $b_1=L$. The hooks along the first row of $\nu$ are the differences $L-c$ for the gaps $0\leq c<L$. The remaining positions in that interval are the beads $b_2,\ldots,b_\ell$, giving exactly the displayed disjoint decomposition. The first-row and first-column [hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) of $\nu$ agree with those in the original [Young-diagram hook](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-of-a-young-diagram).

Because $p\mid L$, a leg cell satisfies $p\mid h_{x,j}$ exactly when $p\mid L-h_{x,j}$. Thus divisibility by $p$ identifies the relevant cells of the [Young-diagram hook](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-of-a-young-diagram) with the multiples of $p$ in $\{1,\ldots,pr\}$. There are exactly $r$ of those, proving

$$
\boxed{\left|\{(x,y)\in H_{(i,j)}(\lambda):p\mid h_{x,y}(\lambda)\}\right|=r.}
$$

The count includes the corner cell $(i,j)$ itself.

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

The equivalence is **true**. Write $c_r(\eta)$ for the total size at level $r$ of the [core tower of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-tower-of-a-partition) $\eta$, and $q_r(\eta)$ for the corresponding total size in its [quotient tower of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-tower-of-a-partition). We use the size relation and the [P-adic valuation of a symmetric-group character degree from the core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#p-adic-valuation-of-a-symmetric-group-character-degree-from-the-core-tower):

$$
|\eta|=\sum_{r\geq0}c_r(\eta)p^r,
\qquad
\nu_p(\chi^\eta(1))=\frac{\sum_{r\geq0}c_r(\eta)-d_p(|\eta|)}{p-1}.
$$

Here $d_p$ is the [base-p digit sum](../../../number-theory.md#base-p-digit-sum). These imply the [character degree coprime to p from the core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#character-degree-coprime-to-p-from-the-core-tower) criterion: if $|\eta|=\sum_r\alpha_rp^r$, then $p\nmid\chi^\eta(1)$ exactly when $c_r(\eta)=\alpha_r$ at every level. Indeed carrying $p$ units from one position to the next decreases the sum of coefficients by $p-1$; the valuation vanishes precisely when there are no carries.

For $k\geq1$, we also use [power-core truncation of a prime-core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#power-core-truncation-of-a-prime-core-tower): for $\gamma=C_{p^k}(\lambda)$, its core tower agrees with that of $\lambda$ below level $k$ and is empty at every level at least $k$. Moreover $q_k(\lambda)=w_{p^k}(\lambda)$, the [weight of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#weight-of-a-partition) for modulus $p^k$. To see the truncation, removal of a [rim hook](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) of length $p^k$ preserves the first $p$-core and, under the [abacus divisible-hook correspondence](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hooks-divisible-by-the-abacus-modulus), becomes removal of a $p^{k-1}$-hook in one quotient component. Iterating preserves all the lower cores; after all such removals, quotient level $k$ is empty.

If $p\nmid\chi^\lambda(1)$, the criterion gives $c_k(\lambda)=a$, $c_r(\lambda)=0$ for $r>k$, and the lower $c_r(\lambda)$ are the base-$p$ digits of $m$. It follows that $|\gamma|=m$, $p\nmid\chi^\gamma(1)$ and

$$
w_{p^k}(\lambda)=\frac{n-|\gamma|}{p^k}=a.
$$

Conversely, suppose $w_{p^k}(\lambda)=a$ and $p\nmid\chi^\gamma(1)$. Then $|\gamma|=m$ and its lower core-tower sizes are the digits of $m$. Quotient level $k$ of $\lambda$ has total size $a<p$. Each component there has size less than $p$, hence is already a $p$-core and has empty $p$-quotient. Thus $c_k(\lambda)=a$ and every higher level is empty. Combined with truncation, this gives exactly the digits of $n$, so $p\nmid\chi^\lambda(1)$.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{p\nmid\chi^\lambda(1)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ w_{p^k}(\lambda)=a\text{ and }p\nmid\chi^{C_{p^k}(\lambda)}(1).}
$$

If $k=0$, necessarily $m=0$ and $n=a<p$; the $1$-core is empty, its weight is $n=a$, and the [Hook-length formula](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length-formula) shows that every degree is coprime to $p$. Thus the equivalence holds in that boundary case too, with the empty partition having degree one.

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

The implication is **false**. Choose

$$
\boxed{p=3,\quad k=2,\quad a=1,\quad m=4,\quad n=13,\quad\lambda=(12,1).}
$$

The inequalities $0\leq4<9$ and the relation $13=1\cdot9+4$ hold. At cell $(1,4)$, the [hook length](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) is $12-4+1=9=ap^k$.

For the [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer) $(12,1)$, the [Hook-length formula](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length-formula) gives

$$
\chi^{(12,1)}(1)=\frac{13!}{13\cdot11!}=12,
$$

which is divisible by $3$. This also agrees with the degree of the [standard representation of the symmetric group](../../../representation-theory.md#standard-representation-of-the-symmetric-group) $S_{13}$.

The obstruction is visible in the [core of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-of-a-partition): removing the horizontal [rim hook](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) of length nine leaves $\gamma=(3,1)$, which has no hook length nine and hence is the $9$-core. Its degree is $\chi^{(3,1)}(1)=3$. The large hook ensures the required [weight of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#weight-of-a-partition) here, but the core degree is not coprime to $p$.

The implication is **false**. Choose

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 103](paper-103.md)

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

We use the [abacus divisible-hook correspondence](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hooks-divisible-by-the-abacus-modulus) in its multiset form: the [hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) of $\lambda$ that are divisible by $p$, divided by $p$, form exactly the disjoint union of the [multisets](../../../set.md#multiset) of [hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length) of the components of its [quotient of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-of-a-partition) for modulus $p$.

Iterating this correspondence gives, for every $r\geq1$,

$$
\{h/p^r:h\in\mathcal H(\lambda),\ p^r\mid h\}
=\bigsqcup_{\eta\in T^Q(\lambda)_r}\mathcal H(\eta)
$$

as [multisets](../../../set.md#multiset). Each [Young diagram](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram) has one hook for every cell, so the [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) of the right-hand side is $|T^Q(\lambda)_r|$, the sum of the component sizes.

For any positive integer $h$, its [P-adic valuation](../../../number-theory.md#p-adic-valuation) is the number of positive $r$ for which $p^r\mid h$. Summing this identity over hooks and interchanging the finite sums proves

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu_p\!\left(\prod_{h\in\mathcal H(\lambda)}h\right)
&=\sum_{h\in\mathcal H(\lambda)}\sum_{r\geq1}\mathbf1_{p^r\mid h}\\
&=\sum_{r\geq1}|T^Q(\lambda)_r|.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\nu_p\!\left(\prod_{h\in\mathcal H(\lambda)}h\right)=\sum_{r\geq1}|T^Q(\lambda)_r|.}
$$

The sums are finite because hook lengths are bounded by $|\lambda|$, and the total size at each level of the [quotient tower of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-tower-of-a-partition) decreases by at least a factor $p$ until it reaches zero.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

We use the [core-quotient bijection for partitions](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-quotient-bijection-for-partitions): an ordered $p$-tuple $(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{p-1})$ and a fixed $p$-core determine a unique [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer), with

$$
|\lambda|=|C_p(\lambda)|+p\sum_i|\nu_i|.
$$

The [irreducible characters](../../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-character) in both $B$ and $C$ therefore correspond to the same ordered tuples of total size $w$. Replacing the empty [core of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-of-a-partition) by $\gamma$ while keeping the [quotient of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-of-a-partition) fixed gives a [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection) $\chi^\lambda\mapsto\chi^\mu$ from $B$ to $C$. In particular $|B|=|C|$.

For such a pair, all levels of the [core tower of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-tower-of-a-partition) above level zero agree. At level zero, their sizes are $0$ and $m$, respectively. Put $D=\sum_{r\geq1}|T^C(\lambda)_r|=\sum_{r\geq1}|T^C(\mu)_r|$. When $0\leq m<p$, the [base-p digit sum](../../../number-theory.md#base-p-digit-sum) satisfies

$$
d_p(wp)=d_p(w),\qquad d_p(wp+m)=d_p(w)+m.
$$

The [P-adic valuation of a symmetric-group character degree from the core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#p-adic-valuation-of-a-symmetric-group-character-degree-from-the-core-tower) then gives

$$
\nu_p(\chi^\lambda(1))=\frac{D-d_p(w)}{p-1}
=\frac{m+D-[d_p(w)+m]}{p-1}
=\nu_p(\chi^\mu(1)).
$$

Thus the same bijection preserves degree coprimality to $p$ and restricts to a bijection between the indicated sets of [irreducible characters of degree coprime to p](../../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-characters-of-degree-coprime-to-p). Consequently

$$
\boxed{|B|=|C|,\qquad
|B\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(S_{wp})|=|C\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(S_n)|.}
$$

For $m=0$, take $\gamma$ to be the empty partition; the construction becomes the identity.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

For every $\chi^\mu\in C$, level zero of the [core tower of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-tower-of-a-partition) is its [core of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-of-a-partition) $\gamma$, of size $m$. The [character degree coprime to p from the core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#character-degree-coprime-to-p-from-the-core-tower) criterion says that if $p\nmid\chi^\mu(1)$, this level must have size equal to the units digit $\alpha_0$ of $n$ in base $p$.

But $0\leq\alpha_0<p$, whereas $m\geq p$. Hence every character in $C$ has degree divisible by $p$, and

$$
\boxed{|C\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(S_n)|=0.}
$$

Equivalently, the core-tower size coefficient $m$ at position zero needs at least one carry in normalization to the base-$p$ digits. Each carry contributes one to the [P-adic valuation of a symmetric-group character degree from the core tower](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#p-adic-valuation-of-a-symmetric-group-character-degree-from-the-core-tower).
