<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Use the signed-angle convention in which the reflection [grating equation](../../../../../../../grating-equation.md) is $m\lambda=d(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta)$, with positive [diffraction order](../../../../../../../diffraction-order.md) $m$. For fixed incidence,

$$
\frac{d\beta}{d\lambda}=\frac{m}{d\cos\beta},\qquad
q=f_{\rm cam}\frac{d\beta}{d\lambda}=\frac{mf_{\rm cam}}{d\cos\beta}.
$$

The second identity is the local focal-plane scale, measured about the camera axis aligned with the central diffracted ray. Since $A_{\rm cam}=W\cos\beta$, the projected beam size cancels the cosine in the [grating dispersion](../../../../../../../grating-dispersion.md):

$$
\boxed{L=\frac{\lambda qA_{\rm cam}}{f_{\rm cam}}=\frac{Wm\lambda}{d}.}
$$

Writing $N=W/d$ for the number of illuminated grooves also gives $L=mN\lambda$. This connects the geometrical instrument invariant to the phase span of the illuminated [diffraction grating](../../../../../../../diffraction-grating.md).
