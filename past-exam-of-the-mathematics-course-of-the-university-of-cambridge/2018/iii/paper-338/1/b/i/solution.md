A [Newtonian reflector](../../../../../../../newtonian-telescope.md) uses a concave primary generated by a [parabola](../../../../../../../parabola.md), followed by a flat secondary inclined at $45^\circ$ to the [optical axis](../../../../../../../optical-axis.md). Parallel marginal rays converge toward the primary focus; the secondary intercepts that converging beam and folds it sideways to an accessible [focal plane](../../../../../../../focal-plane.md).

**[Figure 1](#1/b/i/image-light-paths-in-a-newtonian-reflector). Light paths in a Newtonian reflector**.

The [Newtonian reflector](../../../../../../../newtonian-telescope.md) needs only one powered mirror, so it is relatively simple to fabricate and inexpensive. A parabolic primary has no on-axis [spherical aberration](../../../../../../../spherical-aberration.md). Its principal wide-field limitation is [coma](../../../../../../../coma-optics.md), accompanied by [field curvature](../../../../../../../petzval-field-curvature.md) and off-axis [astigmatism](../../../../../../../astigmatism-optical-systems.md). The diagonal and its supports obstruct the [entrance pupil](../../../../../../../entrance-pupil.md) and introduce [diffraction](../../../../../../../diffraction.md); the tube is long compared with a folded two-powered-mirror design, and heavy instruments at the side focus can be awkward to support. These comments also answer the unheaded design-comparison clause on the next PDF page.

**Parabolic primary → flat diagonal → side focus.**
