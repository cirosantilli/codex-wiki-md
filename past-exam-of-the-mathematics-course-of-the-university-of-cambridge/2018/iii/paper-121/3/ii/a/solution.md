<h1 id="3/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

We prove the implication from existence of a witness in the extension to a condition forcing the existential statement. If $M[G]\models\exists x\,\varphi(x)$, choose a witness $a\in M[G]$ and a [forcing name](../../../../../../../forcing-name.md) $\sigma\in M$ with $\operatorname{val}(\sigma,G)=a$. The assumed instance of the truth lemma supplies $p\in G$ with

$$
M\models p\Vdash^*\varphi(\sigma).
$$

The [syntactic forcing relation](../../../../../../../syntactic-forcing-relation.md) is monotone under stronger conditions, so every $q\leq p$ also forces this same named instance. In particular, the conditions below $p$ having some forced witness are [dense below a forcing condition](../../../../../../../dense-below-a-forcing-condition.md) $p$. The [existential clause of syntactic forcing](../../../../../../../existential-clause-of-syntactic-forcing.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{M[G]\models\exists x\,\varphi(x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\exists p\in G\;M\models p\Vdash^*\exists x\,\varphi(x).}
$$

Only the truth lemma assumed for individual named instances and the recursive existential clause were used.
