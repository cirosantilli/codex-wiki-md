<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Mostowski collapse theorem](../../../../../../mostowski-collapse-theorem.md) applies when $E$ is a [well-founded relation](../../../../../../well-founded-relation.md) and an [extensional relation](../../../../../../extensional-relation.md) on the set $M$. Extensionality here means that different points have different sets of $E$-predecessors. There is then a unique [transitive set](../../../../../../transitive-set.md) $T$ and a unique [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) $\pi:M\to T$ such that

$$
\boxed{x\,E\,y\iff\pi(x)\in\pi(y).}
$$

The collapsing map is characterized by [well-founded relation](../../../../../../well-founded-relation.md) recursion:

$$
\pi(x)=\{\pi(y):y\in M,\ y\,E\,x\}.
$$

Thus $(M,E)$ is isomorphic to $(T,\in)$. Both well-foundedness and extensionality are hypotheses of the theorem; well-foundedness alone does not make the recursive map injective.
