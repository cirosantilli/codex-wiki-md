<h1 id="1/vi/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the cardinalhood formula $C$ from part (b), and let a finite flag choose between it and its negation:

$$
\boxed{\varphi_3(x,i):\quad(i=0\land C(x))\lor(i=1\land\neg C(x)).}
$$

Here $0,1$ abbreviate their parameter-free definitions as [Finite von Neumann ordinals](../../../../../../../finite-von-neumann-ordinal.md), and both belong to $M$. This is one [first-order formula](../../../../../../../first-order-formula.md) with two free variables.

For $\beta=(\omega_1)^M$, part (b) proved $M\models C(\beta)$ and $N\models\neg C(\beta)$. The shared parameters $(\beta,0)$ therefore make $\varphi_3$ true in $M$ and false in $N$, disproving upward [set-theoretic absoluteness](../../../../../../../set-theoretic-absoluteness.md). The shared parameters $(\beta,1)$ make it false in $M$ and true in $N$, disproving downward [set-theoretic absoluteness](../../../../../../../set-theoretic-absoluteness.md). Hence the same formula fails both directions, with explicit witnesses to each failure.
