<h1 id="1/vi/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $C(x)$ be the formula expressing [cardinal number](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) status:

$$
\boxed{\varphi_2(x)=C(x):\quad x\text{ is an ordinal}\ \land\ \forall\alpha\in x\;\neg\exists f\;(f\text{ is a bijection }\alpha\to x).}
$$

Ordinalhood and the assertion that a given set is a [bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md) between fixed sets are each expressible by a [bounded formula in set theory](../../../../../../../bounded-formula-in-set-theory.md). If $M$ regarded $x$ as a non-cardinal ordinal, a witnessing smaller ordinal and bijection would remain present and valid in $N$. Ordinalhood itself is absolute between the [transitive models](../../../../../../../transitive-model.md). Thus $N\models C(x)$ implies $M\models C(x)$: this is [downward absoluteness of cardinalhood](../../../../../../../downward-absoluteness-of-cardinalhood.md).

For failure in the other direction, put $\beta=(\omega_1)^M$. This ordinal belongs to $M$, and $M\models C(\beta)$ with $\beta>\omega$. Externally $\beta$ is countable, since $\beta\subseteq M$ and $M$ is countable. By the construction of $N$, it contains a countability witness for $\beta$, and hence a bijection $\omega\to\beta$. Therefore $N\models\neg C(\beta)$. This formula is downward absolute and fails to be upward absolute.
