<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a [tiny object](../../../../../../tiny-object.md) $A$, the exponential [functor](../../../../../../functor.md) $E_A=(-)^A$ has a [right adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md), say $R_A$. The defining exponential adjunction gives the natural isomorphism

$$
E_{A\times B}\cong E_AE_B,
$$

since $(X^B)^A\cong X^{A\times B}$. If $A,B$ are [tiny objects](../../../../../../tiny-object.md), the composite on the right is a [left adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) with right adjoint $R_BR_A$. Thus $A\times B$ is tiny. The [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md) is tiny because exponentiation by it is naturally the identity, which is its own right adjoint. Induction, including this empty-product case, proves [tiny objects are closed under finite products](../../../../../../tiny-objects-are-closed-under-finite-products.md):

$$
\boxed{A_1,\ldots,A_n\text{ tiny}\ \Longrightarrow\ \prod_{i=1}^nA_i\text{ tiny}.}
$$
