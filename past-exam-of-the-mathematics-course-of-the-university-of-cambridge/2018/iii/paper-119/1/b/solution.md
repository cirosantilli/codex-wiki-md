<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume (b). A [coproduct in a category](../../../../../../coproduct.md) of [monofunctors](../../../../../../monofunctor.md) in $[\mathcal C,\mathbf{Set}]$ is again a [monofunctor](../../../../../../monofunctor.md): at an object it is the disjoint union of the values, and every arrow acts within the same summand by an [injective function](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Distinct summands cannot become identified, and injectivity within a summand follows from its [monofunctor](../../../../../../monofunctor.md) property.

Apply this to the coproduct of [representable functors](../../../../../../representable-functor.md) constructed in part (a). Its canonical map to $F$ is a [pointwise epimorphism in a functor category](../../../../../../pointwise-epimorphism-in-a-functor-category.md). Thus every $F$ is an epimorphic image of a [monofunctor](../../../../../../monofunctor.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\text{(b)}\Longrightarrow\text{(c)}.}
$$
