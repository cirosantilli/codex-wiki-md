<h1 id="3/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

All [Topological K-theory](../../../../../../topological-k-theory.md) groups below are complex and are indexed modulo two using [Bott periodicity](../../../../../../bott-isomorphism.md). For $n\geq1$, [Complex K-theory of a sphere](../../../../../../complex-k-theory-of-a-sphere.md) gives

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&K^0(S^n)&K^{-1}(S^n)\\ \hline
n\text{ even}&\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z&0\\
n\text{ odd}&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z
\end{array}}
$$

For an even-dimensional [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md), choose a generator $u$ of [Reduced topological K-theory](../../../../../../reduced-topological-k-theory.md). Its square vanishes: the [Chern character](../../../../../../chern-character.md) sends $u$ to a top-degree [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) class, whose square is zero, and the [Chern character](../../../../../../chern-character.md) is injective on this torsion-free [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Thus the [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) answers are

$$
\boxed{K^0(S^{2m})\cong\mathbb Z[u]/(u^2)\quad(m\geq1),\qquad K^0(S^{2m+1})\cong\mathbb Z.}
$$

The exceptional zero-dimensional [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) consists of two points: $K^0(S^0)=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ with coordinatewise multiplication and $K^{-1}(S^0)=0$. In particular its reduced generator is idempotent rather than square-zero.

For the [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) identity, induct over the cells of a [finite CW complex](../../../../../../finite-cw-complex.md). The starting [CW complex](../../../../../../cw-complex.md) consisting of $v$ vertices has $K^0\cong\mathbb Z^v$, $K^{-1}=0$, and $\chi=v$. If $X$ is obtained from $A$ by attaching one $d$-dimensional cell, the quotient is $S^d$, and the [K-theory six-term exact sequence](../../../../../../k-theory-six-term-exact-sequence.md) is

$$
\widetilde K^0(S^d)\longrightarrow K^0(X)\longrightarrow K^0(A)
\longrightarrow\widetilde K^{-1}(S^d)\longrightarrow K^{-1}(X)
\longrightarrow K^{-1}(A)\longrightarrow\widetilde K^0(S^d).
$$

These [abelian groups](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) are finitely generated by induction. Tensoring the [exact sequence](../../../../../../exact-sequence.md) with $\mathbb Q$ preserves exactness. The alternating sum of dimensions in a cyclic six-term [exact sequence](../../../../../../exact-sequence.md) is zero, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}K^0(X)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(X)
&=\operatorname{rank}K^0(A)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(A)\\
&\quad+\operatorname{rank}\widetilde K^0(S^d)
-\operatorname{rank}\widetilde K^{-1}(S^d)\\
&=\chi(A)+(-1)^d=\chi(X).
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\chi(X)=\operatorname{rank}K^0(X)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(X).}
$$

There is a genuine convention issue in the nilpotence assertion. With the usual definition of [Reduced topological K-theory](../../../../../../reduced-topological-k-theory.md) as the kernel of restriction to one basepoint, **the assertion needs $X$ to be connected**. For $X=S^0$ based at its first point, $(0,1)$ lies in $\widetilde K^0(X)$ and satisfies $(0,1)^m=(0,1)$ for every $m\geq1$. Thus it is not a [nilpotent element](../../../../../../nilpotent.md). For an arbitrary [finite CW complex](../../../../../../finite-cw-complex.md), the correct assertion uses the [rank map in topological K-theory](../../../../../../rank-map-in-topological-k-theory.md) on every [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md):

$$
I(X)=\ker\bigl(K^0(X)\longrightarrow H^0(X;\mathbb Z)\bigr).
$$

For a [connected space](../../../../../../connected-space.md), $I(X)=\widetilde K^0(X)$.

Here is an induction proving the corrected assertion. On the vertices, $I(X)=0$. For a positive-dimensional cell attachment $A\subset X$, the ideal $J=\ker(K^0(X)\to K^0(A))$ is square-zero. Indeed, lift two elements of $J$ to [relative topological K-theory](../../../../../../relative-topological-k-theory.md) $K^0(X,A)$. Their product is induced by the reduced diagonal

$$
X/A=S^d\longrightarrow S^d\wedge S^d=S^{2d}.
$$

This map is [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md), since $d<2d$ and $S^{2d}$ is $(2d-1)$-connected. The [relative product in topological K-theory](../../../../../../relative-product-in-topological-k-theory.md) is therefore zero, giving $J^2=0$. If $a\in I(X)$, its restriction belongs to $I(A)$, so induction gives $(a|_A)^m=0$ for some $m$. Then $a^m\in J$ and $a^{2m}=0$. We have proved **every [Topological K-theory](../../../../../../topological-k-theory.md) class of rank zero on every [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md) is a [nilpotent element](../../../../../../nilpotent.md)**, and hence the requested result for [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) $X$.
