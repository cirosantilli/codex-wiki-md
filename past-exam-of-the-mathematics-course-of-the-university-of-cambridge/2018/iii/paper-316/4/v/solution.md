<h1 id="4/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

To first order, the initial-phase correction in $M_0$ can be omitted inside a term already multiplied by $e$. Thus

$$
\frac ra=1-e\cos(nt-\varpi)+O(e^2),\qquad
\delta\lambda=(n-n_g)t+2e\sin(nt-\varpi)+2e\sin\varpi+O(e^2).
$$

For $|a-a_g|/a\ll1$ and $|\delta\lambda|\ll1$, expand the exact [rotating reference frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md) coordinates. Products of the small radial and angular displacements are second order. This gives

$$
\boxed{\frac xa\simeq\frac{a-a_g}{a}-e\cos(nt-\varpi),\qquad
\frac ya\simeq(n-n_g)t+2e\sin(nt-\varpi)+2e\sin\varpi.}
$$

At $t=0$ the two sine terms cancel, as required. Equal [mean motion](../../../../../../mean-motion.md) gives a closed local [epicyclic motion](../../../../../../epicyclic-motion.md) with radial-to-tangential amplitude ratio $1:2$; unequal [mean motion](../../../../../../mean-motion.md) adds along-track drift. The local expansion also requires $|(n-n_g)t|\ll1$, so small frequency mismatch alone does not justify it for arbitrarily large times.
