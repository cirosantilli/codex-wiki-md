<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Measure angles from [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) in the direction of motion. The [true anomaly](../../../../../../true-anomaly.md) $f$ is the angle at the stellar focus between the pericentre ray and the star-particle radius vector. Draw the auxiliary circle of radius $a$ around the [ellipse](../../../../../../ellipse.md)'s center; the point on that circle with the same projected major-axis coordinate as the particle defines the [eccentric anomaly](../../../../../../eccentric-anomaly.md) $E$ at the center.

The [mean anomaly](../../../../../../mean-anomaly.md) $M=n(t-\tau)$ is a uniformly advancing time phase, with $M=0$ at pericentre passage $t=\tau$. It is the angle swept by a fictitious uniformly moving circular phase marker, not generally the actual position angle. The [mean motion](../../../../../../mean-motion.md) is the [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md)

$$
\boxed{n=\sqrt{GM_\star/a^3}=\frac{2\pi}{T},\qquad M=E-e\sin E.}
$$

The second formula is [Kepler's equation](../../../../../../kepler-s-equation.md), which converts the uniform time phase into geometric position.
