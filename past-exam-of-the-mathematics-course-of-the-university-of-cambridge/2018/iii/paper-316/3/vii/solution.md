<h1 id="3/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

For a [phase-mixed orbit](../../../../../../phase-mixed-orbit.md), probability in a short arc is $dt/T=ds/(Tv)$, so the [line density on a Kepler orbit](../../../../../../line-density-on-a-kepler-orbit.md) is inversely proportional to speed. At [true anomaly](../../../../../../true-anomaly.md) $\theta$,

$$
r=\frac{a(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos\theta}=a[1-e\cos\theta]+O(e^2).
$$

Using the [vis-viva equation](../../../../../../vis-viva-equation.md), $v^2=(GM_\star/a)[1+2e\cos\theta]+O(e^2)$ and hence $v=na[1+e\cos\theta]+O(e^2)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\lambda_\ell(\theta)\propto1-e\cos\theta+O(e^2).}
$$

For one particle, the normalized line probability is $(2\pi a)^{-1}(1-e\cos\theta)+O(e^2)$. This is density per arc length; density per unit polar angle has a further factor $ds/d\theta\simeq a(1-e\cos\theta)$ and is proportional to $1-2e\cos\theta$.
