<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Differentiating the [disturbing function](../../../../../../disturbing-function.md) in [Lagrange planetary equations](../../../../../../lagrange-planetary-equations.md) gives

$$
\dot e=\sum_j A_j e_j\sin(\varpi-\varpi_j),\qquad
\dot\varpi=A+\sum_j A_j\frac{e_j}{e}\cos(\varpi-\varpi_j).
$$

For the [complex eccentricity](../../../../../../complex-eccentricity.md) $z=e e^{i\varpi}$,

$$
\dot z=e^{i\varpi}(\dot e+ie\dot\varpi).
$$

Use $\sin\delta+i\cos\delta=i e^{-i\delta}$. Each planetary term becomes $iA_je_je^{i\varpi_j}=iA_jz_j$, so

$$
\boxed{\dot z=iAz+i\sum_{j=1}^N A_jz_j.}
$$

Unlike the polar [orbital element](../../../../../../orbital-element.md) equations, this linear equation is nonsingular at $e=0$.
