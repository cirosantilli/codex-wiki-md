<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $B_i=\sum_j A_j e_{ji}$. For $A\ne g_i$, the solution of the linear [secular forcing of a test particle](../../../../../../secular-forcing-of-a-test-particle.md) equation is

$$
\boxed{z(t)=e_p e^{i(At+\beta_p)}+z_f(t),\qquad
z_f(t)=\sum_i\frac{B_i}{g_i-A}e^{i(g_it+\beta_i)}.}
$$

The initial condition gives $e_p=|z(0)-z_f(0)|$ and $\beta_p=\arg[z(0)-z_f(0)]$. The [proper eccentricity](../../../../../../proper-eccentricity.md) $e_p$ is the constant amplitude of the homogeneous response; its [proper longitude of periapsis](../../../../../../proper-longitude-of-periapsis.md) is $At+\beta_p$. The [forced eccentricity](../../../../../../forced-eccentricity.md) is the driven vector $z_f(t)$, not necessarily a constant magnitude.

On an [Argand diagram](../../../../../../complex-plane.md), $z-z_f$ describes a circle of radius $e_p$. Its center $z_f$ can itself move under the planetary modes, so $z$ need not trace one fixed circle in the inertial complex plane. At a [secular resonance](../../../../../../secular-resonance.md) $A=g_i$, that mode instead produces $iB_i t e^{i(At+\beta_i)}$, and the undamped linear response grows until the approximation fails.
