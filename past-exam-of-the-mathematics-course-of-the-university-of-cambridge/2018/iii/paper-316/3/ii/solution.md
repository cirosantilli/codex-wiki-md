<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The planetary expansion is a decomposition into [secular eigenmodes](../../../../../../secular-eigenmode.md) of conservative linear [Laplace-Lagrange secular theory](../../../../../../laplace-lagrange-secular-theory.md). It assumes small [orbital eccentricities](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md), coplanarity, approximately constant [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) values, and averaging over fast [orbital phases](../../../../../../orbital-phase.md) away from important [mean-motion resonances](../../../../../../mean-motion-resonance.md) and close encounters. Dissipation, migration, and nonlinear secular effects are absent from this constant-coefficient representation.

The $g_i$ are real eigenfrequencies of the [Laplace-Lagrange secular matrix](../../../../../../laplace-lagrange-secular-matrix.md), describing [apsidal precession](../../../../../../apsidal-precession.md). For mode $i$, the signed real coefficients $e_{ji}$ give its amplitude in [planet](../../../../../../planet.md) $j$; their relative magnitudes are fixed by an eigenvector, and their signs distinguish aligned and anti-aligned apsides. The common phase $\beta_i$ specifies that mode's orientation at the chosen time origin. A [planet](../../../../../../planet.md)'s actual [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md) is $|z_j(t)|$, which generally varies through interference of modes; it is not a sum of the positive mode amplitudes.
