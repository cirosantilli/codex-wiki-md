<h1 id="3/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

If the [proper eccentricity](../../../../../../proper-eccentricity.md) is negligible and the [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) ranges over $a\pm\Delta a$, particles lie on nearly aligned forced [Kepler orbits](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md). For a locally constant forced vector, these are geometrically similar [ellipses](../../../../../../ellipse.md), represented to first order by circles of radius $a'$ centered at $-a'\mathbf e_f$.

The boundaries of the [aligned eccentric ring](../../../../../../aligned-eccentric-ring.md) are therefore

$$
r_\pm(\theta)\simeq(a\pm\Delta a)[1-e_f\cos\theta],\qquad
\boxed{W(\theta)\simeq2\Delta a(1-e_f\cos\theta).}
$$

It is narrower at forced [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md) and wider at forced [apoapsis](../../../../../../apoapsis.md), unlike the constant-width [proper-eccentricity annulus](../../../../../../proper-eccentricity-annulus.md). Its centers shift slightly between the two edges, as shown in the right panel of the figure. The condition $e_p/e_f\ll\Delta a/a$ makes the proper radial excursion negligible compared with the variation in forced center location.

The forced vector need not actually be constant across the interval. More generally

$$
W(\theta)\simeq2\Delta a\left|1-\operatorname{Re}\left[(z_f+a\,dz_f/da)e^{-i\theta}\right]\right|.
$$

For aligned apsides this becomes $2\Delta a[1-(e_f+a e_f')\cos\theta]$, provided the orbits remain nested. The radial gradient of [forced eccentricity](../../../../../../forced-eccentricity.md) matters for the density comparison even in a narrow ring.
