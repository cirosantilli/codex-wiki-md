<h1 id="1/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Suppose the inclined population samples its arguments of periapsis and nodal orientations, or that sufficiently slow precession supplies this sampling. Near the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md) the vertical amplitude is $H\simeq a_pI$. For uniformly distributed vertical phase, $z=H\sin\psi$ has [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) $[\pi\sqrt{H^2-z^2}]^{-1}$, so the midplane volume probability is $\Sigma_c/(\pi H)$.

In the genuinely three-dimensional regime $R_p\ll H\ll a_p$, the [geometric collision cross-section](../../../../../../geometric-collision-cross-section.md) is $\pi R_p^2$. The [relative velocity](../../../../../../relative-velocity.md) differs from the coplanar value only at order $I^2$ for the specified high-speed encounters. Therefore the [inclined comet collision time](../../../../../../inclined-comet-collision-time.md) is

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm col,I}\simeq\frac{H}{R_p^2\Sigma_c(a_p)v_{\rm rel}}\simeq\frac{\pi^2I Q^{3/2}a_p^2\sqrt{1-q/a_p}}{R_p^2\sqrt{GM_\star}\sqrt{3-2\sqrt{2q/a_p}}}.}
$$

At fixed $\rho_p,M_\star,I$ the leading scaling is $Q^{3/2}M_p^{-2/3}a_p^2$, with no leading $q$ dependence when $q\ll a_p$. The displayed factor retains the first dependence on $q/a_p$. Also $t_{\rm col,I}/t_{\rm col}\simeq2a_pI/R_p$ in this averaging convention.

**Inclination alone does not specify a collision time for one fixed orbit.** If its [orbital nodes](../../../../../../orbital-node.md) miss the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md)'s orbit, a tilted [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) can have no collisions at all. The finite result is an orientation-averaged population result, or requires precession or scattering to sample those orientations. When $a_pI\lesssim R_p$, the three-dimensional estimate crosses over to the coplanar result instead of tending to zero.
