<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Parameterize the trajectory by the decreasing [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md). The [Poynting–Robertson drag invariant](../../../../../../poynting-robertson-drag-invariant.md) gives

$$
\boxed{q(e)=\frac{Ce^{4/5}}{1+e},\qquad Q(e)=\frac{Ce^{4/5}}{1-e},\qquad
C=\frac{2q_0Q_0}{q_0+Q_0}\left(\frac{Q_0-q_0}{Q_0+q_0}\right)^{-4/5}.}
$$

For $Q_0\gg q_0$, $C\simeq2q_0$. Initially $e\simeq1$, and the orbit moves almost vertically down a plot of $Q/q_0$ against $q/q_0$: the [apocentre distance](../../../../../../apocentre-distance.md) shrinks rapidly while the [pericentre distance](../../../../../../pericentre-distance.md) changes little. Integrating the high-eccentricity slope gives

$$
q_0-q\simeq\frac35q_0^2\left(\frac1Q-\frac1{Q_0}\right),\qquad Q,Q_0\gg q_0.
$$

Once the orbit has moderate [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md), both distances change appreciably. For example $e=1/2$ gives $q\simeq0.766q_0$ and $Q\simeq2.30q_0$ in the $Q_0/q_0\to\infty$ limit. Eventually $e\ll1$, $q\simeq Q\simeq Ce^{4/5}$, and the trajectory approaches the diagonal $Q=q$ before reaching the origin. Thus the two approximate phases are **apocentre contraction at nearly fixed pericentre, followed by nearly circular inward migration**. They are a smooth crossover, not two separate exact solutions.
