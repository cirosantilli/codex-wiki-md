<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Take $\Omega'$ nonempty. If the bound failed, normalization would give nonnegative $v_j$ with supremum one, zero boundary values, $\inf_{\Omega'}v_j\to0$, and $\|Lv_j\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\to0$. The [global Schauder estimate](../../../../../../global-schauder-estimate.md) bounds them in the [Hölder space](../../../../../../holder-space.md) $C^{2,\alpha}$. By [compact embedding of Hölder spaces](../../../../../../compact-embedding-of-holder-spaces.md), a subsequence converges in $C^{2,\beta}$, $0<\beta<\alpha$, to $v\geq0$ with $Lv=0$, zero boundary data and supremum one.

Points approaching the infima have a subsequence converging in $\overline{\Omega'}\subset\Omega$. Uniform convergence gives an interior zero of $v$, and part (c), the [strong minimum principle for elliptic operators](../../../../../../strong-minimum-principle-for-elliptic-operators.md) after replacing $c$ by $\min(c,0)$, gives $v=0$. This contradicts its supremum one, proving

$$
\boxed{\sup_\Omega u\leq C\bigl(\inf_{\Omega'}u+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\bigr).}
$$
