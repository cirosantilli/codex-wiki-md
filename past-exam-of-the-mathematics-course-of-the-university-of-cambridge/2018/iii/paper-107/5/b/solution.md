<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Failure of the bound would give normalized $v_j$ with $\|v_j\|_\infty=1$, zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md), and $\|Lv_j\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\to0$. The [global Schauder estimate](../../../../../../global-schauder-estimate.md) bounds them in $C^{2,\alpha}$. By [compact embedding of Hölder spaces](../../../../../../compact-embedding-of-holder-spaces.md), a subsequence converges in $C^{2,\beta}$ to $v$ with $Lv=0$, zero boundary data and $\|v\|_\infty=1$. The uniform [Hölder seminorm](../../../../../../holder-seminorm.md) bounds pass to the limit, so $v\in C^{2,\alpha}$. This contradicts the trivial [null space](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\|u\|_\infty\leq C_1\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}.}
$$

The [global Schauder estimate](../../../../../../global-schauder-estimate.md) then gives $\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq K\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$, the bounded-inverse estimate used next.
