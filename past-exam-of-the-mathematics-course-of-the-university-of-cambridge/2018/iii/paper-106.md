## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 106](paper-106.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The [weak-star topology](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology) $\sigma(X^*,X)$ is generated by the [seminorms](../../../topological-vector-space.md#seminorm) $p_x(g)=|g(x)|$, $x\in X$. Thus every evaluation is continuous, so $f_n\xrightarrow{w^*}f$ implies $f_n(x)\to f(x)$ for each $x$. Conversely, a basic neighborhood of $f$ imposes finitely many conditions $|(g-f)(x_j)|<\varepsilon$. Pointwise convergence eventually satisfies all of them simultaneously. Hence

$$
\boxed{f_n\xrightarrow{w^*}f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f_n(x)\longrightarrow f(x)\ \text{for every }x\in X.}
$$

The [Uniform boundedness principle](../../../banach-space.md#uniform-boundedness-principle), applied on the [Banach space](../../../banach-space.md) $X$, gives $M=\sup_n\|f_n\|<\infty$. If $x_n\to x$ in the [norm topology](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm-topology), then

$$
|f_n(x_n)-f(x)|\leq M\|x_n-x\|+|f_n(x)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{f_n(x_n)\to f(x)}$. Completeness of $X$ is used in this uniform boundedness step.

If $C\subseteq X^*$ is norm compact, the identity map from $(C,\|\cdot\|)$ to $(C,w^*)$ is a continuous bijection. The target is [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space), since distinct functionals differ at some $x\in X$. The [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../topology.md#compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem) gives

$$
\boxed{\text{the norm and weak-star topologies coincide on }C.}
$$

The [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-alaoglu-theorem) states that the closed dual [unit ball](../../../functional-analysis.md#unit-ball) $B_{X^*}$ is compact in the [weak-star topology](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology); in fact $X$ need only be a [normed vector space](../../../functional-analysis.md#normed-vector-space). To prove it, work over $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ and form

$$
Q=\prod_{x\in X}D_x,\qquad D_x=\{z\in\mathbb F:|z|\leq\|x\|\}.
$$

Each $D_x$ is compact, so $Q$ is compact by the [Tychonoff theorem](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tychonoff-s-theorem). Embed $B_{X^*}$ by $g\mapsto(g(x))_{x\in X}$. Its image consists exactly of those families $(z_x)$ satisfying

$$
z_{x+y}=z_x+z_y,\qquad z_{\alpha x}=\alpha z_x\quad(x,y\in X,\ \alpha\in\mathbb F).
$$

These are closed conditions in the product topology. Conversely, any family satisfying them defines a linear functional bounded by $|z_x|\leq\|x\|$, hence an element of $B_{X^*}$. The image is therefore closed and compact. The induced product topology is precisely the [weak-star topology](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-star-topology), proving the theorem. This is a compactness proof for arbitrary nets, with no separability assumption.

For the [dual map of bounded linear operator](../../../continuous-dual-space.md#transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator) $T^*:Y^*\to X^*$, each $x\in X$ satisfies

$$
(T^*g)(x)=g(Tx).
$$

Evaluation at the fixed vector $Tx$ is weak-star continuous, so $\boxed{T^*\text{ is weak-star-to-weak-star continuous}}$.

Suppose $T$ is a [compact operator](../../../compact-operator.md). By the allowed [Schauder theorem for compact operators](../../../compact-operator.md#schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators), $T^*$ is compact. If $S\subseteq Y^*$ is bounded, the norm closure $C=\overline{T^*(S)}$ is norm compact. We already proved that its norm and weak-star topologies coincide. The weak-star continuous map $T^*|_S$ into $C$ is consequently norm continuous. Conversely, if $T^*$ is weak-star-to-norm continuous on bounded subsets, its restriction to $B_{Y^*}$ maps a weak-star compact set, by [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-alaoglu-theorem), to a norm compact set. Thus $T^*$ is a [compact operator](../../../compact-operator.md), and the [Schauder theorem for compact operators](../../../compact-operator.md#schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators) gives

$$
\boxed{T\text{ compact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T^*|_S:(S,w^*)\to(X^*,\|\cdot\|)\text{ is continuous for every bounded }S\subseteq Y^*.}
$$

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use the usual unital-subalgebra convention $I_H\in A$. The [spectral theorem for a commutative operator algebra](../../../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem-for-a-commutative-operator-algebra) states that the [character space](../../../banach-algebra.md#character-space-of-an-algebra) $\Phi_A$, equipped with the [Gelfand topology](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-topology), is a [compact Hausdorff space](../../../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space), and there is a unique regular [projection-valued measure](../../../hilbert-space.md#projection-valued-measure) $E$ on its [Borel sigma-algebra](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-sigma-algebra) satisfying

$$
\boxed{E(\Phi_A)=I_H,\qquad a=\int_{\Phi_A}\widehat a(\chi)\,dE(\chi)\quad(a\in A),}
$$

where $\widehat a(\chi)=\chi(a)$ is the [Gelfand transform](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-representation). The map $a\mapsto\widehat a$ is an isometric unital star-isomorphism from $A$ onto $C(\Phi_A)$ by the [Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem](../../../banach-algebra.md#commutative-gelfand-naimark-theorem).

More explicitly, every $E(B)$ is an [orthogonal projection](../../../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection), $E(\varnothing)=0$, $E(B\cap C)=E(B)E(C)$, and for disjoint [Borel sets](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-set) $B_n$,

$$
E\left(\bigcup_nB_n\right)v=\sum_nE(B_n)v\quad(v\in H),
$$

with convergence in the norm of the [Hilbert space](../../../hilbert-space.md). Regularity means that each [scalar spectral measure](../../../hilbert-space.md#scalar-spectral-measure) $B\mapsto\langle E(B)v,w\rangle$ is a [regular Borel measure](../../../measure-theory.md#regular-borel-measure). The integral identity then implies $\|a\|=\sup_{\chi\in\Phi_A}|\widehat a(\chi)|$. No separability assumption on $H$ is needed.

<h4 id="2/b/1">1</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/1/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#2/b/1)

First construct the [spectral measure of a normal operator](../../../hilbert-space.md#spectral-measure-of-a-normal-operator) and prove the open-set assertion needed for all three numbered conclusions. The algebra $A=C^*(I,T)$ generated by $T$ and $T^*$ is commutative because $T$ is a [normal operator](../../../hilbert-space.md#normal-operator). The [Gelfand transform](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-representation) identifies $A$ with $C(\Phi_A)$. By [spectral permanence for C-star algebras](../../../banach-algebra.md#spectral-permanence-for-c-star-algebras),

$$
h:\Phi_A\longrightarrow K=\sigma_{\mathcal B(H)}(T),\qquad h(\chi)=\chi(T)
$$

is onto: its image is the spectrum of $T$ in $A$, which is also its spectrum in $\mathcal B(H)$. It is injective because a character is determined by its values on $T,T^*$ and $I$, and $\chi(T^*)=\overline{\chi(T)}$. Thus $h$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism). Apply part (a) and set

$$
P(B)=E(h^{-1}(B))\quad(B\subseteq K\text{ Borel}).
$$

This is a [projection-valued measure](../../../hilbert-space.md#projection-valued-measure) with $P(K)=I$ and

$$
\boxed{T=\int_Kz\,dP(z).}
$$

The associated [continuous functional calculus](../../../banach-algebra.md#continuous-functional-calculus) is isometric: $\|u(T)\|=\|u\|_\infty$ for $u\in C(K)$.

If $U\subseteq K$ is nonempty and relatively open, choose $z_0\in U$ and a nonzero continuous $u$ supported inside $U$, for example a small continuous bump about $z_0$ in the metric of $K\subseteq\mathbb C$. If $P(U)=0$, then $u(T)=\int_Ku\,dP=0$, contradicting $\|u(T)\|=\|u\|_\infty>0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(U)\ne0\quad\text{for every nonempty relatively open }U\subseteq K.}
$$

This proves the claim from faithfulness of the representation rather than assuming it as part of the spectral theorem.

Now take an [isolated point](../../../topological-analysis.md#isolated-point) $\lambda\in K$. Its singleton is relatively open, so $P(\{\lambda\})\ne0$. Multiplication of spectral integrals gives

$$
(T-\lambda I)P(\{\lambda\})=0.
$$

Thus its range is contained in the [eigenspace](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) $\ker(T-\lambda I)$. Conversely, for $v$ in that kernel, the positive [scalar spectral measure](../../../hilbert-space.md#scalar-spectral-measure) $\mu_v(B)=\langle P(B)v,v\rangle$ satisfies

$$
0=\|(T-\lambda I)v\|^2=\int_K|z-\lambda|^2\,d\mu_v(z).
$$

It follows that $\mu_v(K\setminus\{\lambda\})=0$, and hence $\|(I-P(\{\lambda\}))v\|^2=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue},\qquad P(\{\lambda\})\text{ is the orthogonal projection onto }\ker(\lambda I-T).}
$$

The identification of this projection with the [eigenspace](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenspace) holds even for a non-isolated $\lambda$; isolation guarantees that it is nonzero.

If $K=\{\lambda_0\}$, the [projection-valued measure](../../../hilbert-space.md#projection-valued-measure) satisfies $P(\{\lambda_0\})=P(K)=I$. Its integral representation therefore reduces to

$$
\boxed{T=\int_Kz\,dP(z)=\lambda_0P(K)=\lambda_0I.}
$$

Thus a [normal operator](../../../hilbert-space.md#normal-operator) with a singleton spectrum is a scalar multiple of the [identity operator](../../../vector-space.md#identity-operator). Normality is essential: a nonzero nilpotent matrix has spectrum $\{0\}$ but need not be zero.

<h5 id="2/b/3/solution">Solution</h5>

If the spectrum has one point, the preceding part gives $T=\lambda I$, and the [linear span](../../../vector-space.md#linear-span) of any nonzero vector is a closed proper [invariant subspace](../../../representation-theory.md#invariant-subspace) because $\dim H>1$.

Otherwise choose distinct points $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in K$ and disjoint nonempty relatively open neighborhoods $U,V\subseteq K$. The open-set result gives $P(U)\ne0$ and $P(V)\ne0$, while

$$
P(U)P(V)=P(U\cap V)=0.
$$

Thus $P(U)\ne I$. Set $L=\operatorname{ran}P(U)$. As the range of an [orthogonal projection](../../../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection), $L$ is closed, and the preceding observations give $\{0\}\ne L\ne H$. The [Borel functional calculus for a normal operator](../../../banach-algebra.md#borel-functional-calculus-for-a-normal-operator) gives $TP(U)=P(U)T$, so

$$
\boxed{L=\operatorname{ran}P(U)\text{ is a nontrivial closed invariant subspace of }T.}
$$

In fact both $L$ and its orthogonal complement are invariant.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The [Hahn-Banach separation theorem for two convex sets](../../../topological-vector-space.md#hahn-banach-separation-theorem-for-two-convex-sets) states that disjoint nonempty [convex sets](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-set) $A,B$ in a real [locally convex space](../../../topological-vector-space.md#locally-convex-space), with $A$ open, admit a nonzero [continuous linear functional](../../../topological-vector-space.md#continuous-linear-functional) $\ell$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb R$ such that

$$
\boxed{\ell(a)<\alpha\leq\ell(b)\quad(a\in A,\ b\in B).}
$$

There need not be a positive uniform gap between the two sets.

For a proof, set $C=A-B$. It is open and [convex](../../../real-analysis.md#convex-function), and $0\notin C$. Choose $c_0\in C$, let $V=C-c_0$, and let $p$ be the [Minkowski functional](../../../topological-vector-space.md#minkowski-functional) of $V$. Because $V$ is open, convex and contains zero, $p$ is a finite [sublinear functional](../../../functional-analysis.md#sublinear-function), and $V=\{x:p(x)<1\}$. Put $z=-c_0$. Since $z\notin V$, $p(z)\geq1$. On $\mathbb Rz$ define $\ell_0(tz)=tp(z)$. For $t\geq0$ this equals $p(tz)$; for $t<0$ it is nonpositive and hence at most $p(tz)$. The [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem) extends it to a linear $\ell$ with $\ell\leq p$. Both $p(x)$ and $p(-x)$ are bounded near zero, so this domination makes $\ell$ continuous; it is nonzero because $\ell(z)=p(z)\geq1$. For $c\in C$,

$$
\ell(c)=\ell(c-c_0)-p(z)\leq p(c-c_0)-p(z)<1-p(z)\leq0.
$$

Thus $\ell(a)<\ell(b)$ for every $a\in A,b\in B$. Define $\alpha=\sup_{a\in A}\ell(a)$, which is finite because any fixed $b\in B$ bounds it above. Openness of $A$ and nonzeroness of $\ell$ ensure $\ell(a)<\alpha$ for every $a\in A$, and $\alpha\leq\ell(b)$ for every $b\in B$. This proves the stated separation.

For the sequence criterion, suppose $f\in S_{X^*}$ and $f(x_i)\geq\varrho$. If each $t_i\geq0$, then

$$
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^nt_ix_i\right\|\geq f\left(\sum_{i=1}^nt_ix_i\right)\geq\varrho\sum_{i=1}^nt_i.
$$

Conversely, the assumed inequality implies that every [convex combination](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-combination) of the $x_i$ has norm at least $\varrho$. Hence $D=\{x:\|x\|<\varrho\}$ is disjoint from $C=\operatorname{conv}\{x_i:i\geq1\}$. Apply the separation theorem with the open set $D$ to obtain $\ell\ne0$ and

$$
\ell(c)\geq\sup_{d\in D}\ell(d)=\varrho\|\ell\|\quad(c\in C).
$$

Then $f=\ell/\|\ell\|$ has norm one and satisfies $f(x_i)\geq\varrho$ for every $i$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\exists f\in S_{X^*},\ \forall i\ f(x_i)\geq\varrho\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^nt_ix_i\right\|\geq\varrho\sum_{i=1}^nt_i\ \text{for all }n\text{ and }t_i\geq0.}
$$

The PDF confirms the “if and only if” that is garbled in the TeX transcription.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

By the [Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#riesz-markov-kakutani-representation-theorem), the [continuous dual space](../../../continuous-dual-space.md) of $C(K)$ is isometrically the space of finite [regular Borel measures](../../../measure-theory.md#regular-borel-measure), signed for real scalars and complex for complex scalars:

$$
\boxed{C(K)^*\cong M(K),\qquad L_\mu(f)=\int_Kf\,d\mu,\qquad \|L_\mu\|=|\mu|(K).}
$$

Here $|\mu|$ is the [variation measure](../../../measure-theory.md#variation-measure), whose total mass is the [total variation norm of a measure](../../../measure-theory.md#total-variation-norm-of-a-measure).

If $f_n\rightharpoonup0$ in $C(K)$, evaluation at each $t\in K$ gives $f_n(t)\to0$. The [Uniform boundedness principle](../../../banach-space.md#uniform-boundedness-principle) also gives $M=\sup_n\|f_n\|_\infty<\infty$. For any $\mu\in M(K)$, the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) with respect to the finite positive measure $|\mu|$ yields

$$
\left|\int_Kf_n^2\,d\mu\right|\leq\int_K|f_n|^2\,d|\mu|\longrightarrow0,
$$

since $|f_n(t)|^2\to0$ pointwise and $|f_n(t)|^2\leq M^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f_n^2\rightharpoonup0\text{ in }C(K).}
$$

The same proof works for complex squares, because the absolute-value domination remains valid.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

The [Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem](../../../banach-algebra.md#commutative-gelfand-naimark-theorem) states that a complex commutative unital [C-star algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-algebra) $A$ is isometrically star-isomorphic to $C(\Phi_A)$ through its [Gelfand transform](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-representation), with $\Phi_A$ a [compact Hausdorff space](../../../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space). The [l-infinity sequence space](../../../banach-space.md#l-infinity-sequence-space) is such an algebra under coordinatewise multiplication and conjugation, with the [supremum norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#supremum-norm). Therefore

$$
\boxed{K=\Phi_{\ell^\infty},\qquad \Gamma:\ell^\infty\longrightarrow C(K),\quad \Gamma(a)(\chi)=\chi(a)}
$$

gives the required isometric isomorphism.

Uniqueness as a [Banach space](../../../banach-space.md) representation uses the [Banach–Stone theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-stone-theorem), rather than just uniqueness as an algebra representation. To justify the relevant theorem, the [Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#riesz-markov-kakutani-representation-theorem) gives

$$
\operatorname{ext}B_{C(K)^*}=\{\alpha\delta_t:t\in K,\ |\alpha|=1\},
$$

the [extreme points of the dual unit ball of C(K)](../../../functional-analysis.md#extreme-points-of-the-dual-unit-ball-of-c-k). A norm-one measure whose [variation measure](../../../measure-theory.md#variation-measure) is not concentrated at one point splits into two normalized restrictions to disjoint sets of positive variation, and so is not extreme. Conversely, equality in the variation bound shows that any decomposition of $\alpha\delta_t$ into the average of two dual-unit-ball elements forces both to be $\alpha\delta_t$: after removing the phase, the two measures must be positive and their average is concentrated at $t$.

If $U:C(K)\to C(L)$ is a surjective linear [isometry](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry), its [dual map](../../../linear-algebra.md#transpose-of-a-linear-map) preserves these [extreme points](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#extreme-point) and their scalar orbits. Hence $U^*\delta_s=u(s)\delta_{h(s)}$ for a bijection $h:L\to K$ and $|u(s)|=1$. Evaluating at $1$ gives the continuous function $u=U1$, while

$$
f(h(s))=\frac{(Uf)(s)}{u(s)}\quad(f\in C(K))
$$

shows that $h$ is continuous, since continuous functions determine the topology of a [compact Hausdorff space](../../../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space). It is consequently a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism). Thus any other compact space representing $\ell^\infty$ is homeomorphic to this $K$. The [Banach–Stone theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-stone-theorem) is also stated in [Leonard Tomczak's notes on András Zsák's functional analysis lectures](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ltomczak/notes/Lent2023/FuncAna_Notes.pdf).

Embed $\mathbb N$ by the [evaluation characters](../../../banach-algebra.md#evaluation-character) $\iota(n)(a)=a_n$. They are distinct because the coordinate [indicator functions](../../../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) $e_n$ distinguish them. Moreover, $e_n^2=e_n$, so every character has $\chi(e_n)\in\{0,1\}$. If $\chi(e_n)=1$, then

$$
\chi(a)=\chi(ae_n)=a_n\chi(e_n)=a_n,
$$

and hence $\chi=\iota(n)$. It follows that $\{\iota(n)\}=\{\chi:|\chi(e_n)-1|<1/2\}$ is open in $K$. Thus $\iota$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) from the discrete natural numbers onto its image.

To prove density, suppose $D=\overline{\iota(\mathbb N)}\ne K$. The [Urysohn lemma](../../../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) provides a nonzero $v\in C(K)$ vanishing on $D$. Write $v=\Gamma(a)$. Then $a_n=v(\iota(n))=0$ for all $n$, so $a=0$ and $v=0$, a contradiction. Every bounded function $b:\mathbb N\to\mathbb C$ is an element of $\ell^\infty$, and $\Gamma(b)$ is its continuous extension to $K$. Density makes this extension unique. Therefore

$$
\boxed{K\cong\beta\mathbb N,\qquad b\mapsto\Gamma(b)\text{ is the unique continuous extension of every bounded }b.}
$$

This identifies $K$ with the [Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers](../../../set-theory.md#stone-cech-compactification-of-the-natural-numbers).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 106](paper-106.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

The [Mazur theorem](../../../hilbert-space.md#mazur-theorem) states that the [weak closure](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-closure) and norm closure of a [convex set](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-set) $C$ in a [normed vector space](../../../functional-analysis.md#normed-vector-space) coincide:

$$
\boxed{\overline C^{\,w}=\overline C^{\,\|\cdot\|}.}
$$

Norm closure is contained in [weak closure](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-closure) because the [weak topology](../../../weak-topology.md) is coarser. Conversely, if $x\notin\overline C^{\,\|\cdot\|}$, the [Hahn-Banach separation theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-separation-theorem) provides a continuous real linear functional strictly separating $x$ from that closed [convex set](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-set). In a complex space this is the [real part](../../../complex-analysis.md#real-part) of a continuous complex linear functional. A weak neighborhood of $x$ then misses $C$, so $x\notin\overline C^{\,w}$. This proves the equality. It also gives the usual [Mazur lemma](../../../hilbert-space.md#mazur-s-lemma): if $x_n\rightharpoonup x$, then $x$ lies in the norm closure of the [convex hull](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-hull) of each tail, so one can choose tail [convex combinations](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-combination) $y_n$ with $\|y_n-x\|<1/n$.

Now let $K$ be a [weakly compact set](../../../weak-topology.md#weakly-compact-set) in a normed space $X$. Each $\varphi\in X^*$ is bounded on $K$ because it is weakly continuous. The family $\{Jx:x\in K\}$ in $\mathcal B(X^*,\mathbb F)$, where $J$ is the [canonical embedding into the bidual](../../../functional-analysis.md#canonical-embedding-into-the-bidual), is therefore pointwise bounded. The space $X^*$ is Banach even if $X$ is not. Apply the [Uniform boundedness principle](../../../banach-space.md#uniform-boundedness-principle) and use $\|Jx\|=\|x\|$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{\sup_{x\in K}\|x\|<\infty.}
$$

This proves that a [weakly compact set is norm bounded](../../../weak-topology.md#weakly-compact-set-is-norm-bounded) without assuming completeness of the original space.

For the real-valued dual and integral formulas that follow, take $X$ to be real, as in the PDF. In a complex space the norming formula uses [real parts](../../../complex-analysis.md#real-part), and the integral identities use complex-valued functionals instead.

If the [separable Banach space](../../../banach-space.md#separable-banach-space) $X$ is nonzero, choose a norm-dense sequence $(u_n)$ in its unit sphere. By the [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem), choose $\varphi_n\in X^*$ with $\|\varphi_n\|=1$ and $\varphi_n(u_n)=1$. For any unit vector $u$, arbitrarily close $u_n$ satisfy

$$
1-\|u-u_n\|\leq\varphi_n(u)\leq1.
$$

Scaling gives the [countable norming family](../../../functional-analysis.md#countable-norming-family) identity

$$
\boxed{\|x\|=\sup_n\varphi_n(x)\quad(x\in X).}
$$

For $X=\{0\}$ use the constant sequence of zero functionals. If $f:\Omega\to X$ is norm-Borel measurable, every $\varphi_n\circ f$ is measurable, so its countable supremum $\|f(\cdot)\|$ is measurable. Equivalently, this also follows directly from continuity of the [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm).

For any $\varphi\in X^*$, continuity makes $\varphi\circ f$ measurable, and

$$
|\varphi(f(\omega))|\leq\|\varphi\|\,\|f(\omega)\|.
$$

Thus the assumed integrability of the norm implies scalar integrability, and

$$
T_f(\varphi)=\int_\Omega\varphi(f(\omega))\,d\mu(\omega),\qquad
|T_f(\varphi)|\leq\|\varphi\|\int_\Omega\|f\|\,d\mu
$$

defines a bounded linear functional on $X^*$. Use the granted weak-star continuity of $T_f$. By the [continuous dual of a weak-star topology](../../../weak-topology.md#continuous-dual-of-a-weak-star-topology), $T_f$ is evaluation at a vector of $X$. Indeed, continuity gives finitely many $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that $|T_f(\varphi)|<1$ whenever $|\varphi(x_j)|<\varepsilon$ for all $j$. Scaling shows that $T_f$ vanishes on the common kernel of these evaluations. It therefore factors through their finite-dimensional coordinate map, so $T_f(\varphi)=\sum_ja_j\varphi(x_j)=\varphi(\sum_ja_jx_j)$. The [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#hahn-banach-theorem) makes this representing vector unique. Hence

$$
\boxed{v=\int_\Omega f\,d\mu\in X,\qquad \varphi(v)=\int_\Omega\varphi\circ f\,d\mu\quad(\varphi\in X^*),\qquad
\|v\|\leq\int_\Omega\|f\|\,d\mu.}
$$

In this separable setting the vector is the [Bochner integral](../../../measure-theory.md#bochner-integral).

Return to a [weakly compact set](../../../weak-topology.md#weakly-compact-set) $K\subseteq X$ and its inclusion $f:K\to X$. For each fixed $a\in X$, the identity $\|x-a\|=\sup_n\varphi_n(x-a)$ makes $x\mapsto\|x-a\|$ weakly Borel measurable. Norm balls are consequently weakly Borel measurable. Separability gives a countable base of such balls, so every norm-open set is weakly Borel measurable. This proves measurability of $f$, and establishes the equality of the [weak and norm Borel sigma-algebras in a separable Banach space](../../../weak-topology.md#weak-and-norm-borel-sigma-algebras-in-a-separable-banach-space).

Put $M=\sup_{x\in K}\|x\|<\infty$. For every finite signed [Borel measure](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-measure) $\mu$ on $K$,

$$
\boxed{\int_K\|f(x)\|\,d|\mu|(x)\leq M|\mu|(K)<\infty.}
$$

For positive measures this is the integral in the question. For signed measures, the correct integrability condition uses the [variation measure](../../../measure-theory.md#variation-measure); define the integral by taking the difference of the positive and negative integrals. The printed $\Omega$ in this clause should be $K$, the domain of the inclusion.

The [Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#riesz-markov-kakutani-representation-theorem) now defines the bounded linear map

$$
T:C(K)^*\to X,\qquad T(\mu)=\int_Kx\,d\mu(x),\qquad \|T(\mu)\|\leq M\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
$$

For each $\varphi\in X^*$ the restriction $\varphi|_K$ is in $C(K)$, and

$$
\varphi(T(\mu))=\mu(\varphi|_K).
$$

The right side is weak-star continuous in $\mu$. The defining property of the [weak topology](../../../weak-topology.md) therefore proves that $T$ is weak-star-to-weak continuous, for arbitrary nets. For a [Dirac measure](../../../measure-theory.md#dirac-measure), $\boxed{T(\delta_x)=x}$.

If $K\ne\varnothing$, let $\mathcal P(K)$ be its regular [probability measures](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure). This is a weak-star closed subset of $B_{C(K)^*}$: its conditions are $\mu(1)=1$ and $\mu(g)\geq0$ for every nonnegative $g\in C(K)$. It is compact by [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-alaoglu-theorem). Thus $T(\mathcal P(K))$ is weakly compact and [convex](../../../real-analysis.md#convex-function), and contains $K$ because it contains all $T(\delta_x)$. It is weakly closed, hence norm closed, so it contains $C=\overline{\operatorname{conv}K}^{\,\|\cdot\|}$. By [Mazur theorem](../../../hilbert-space.md#mazur-theorem), $C$ is weakly closed. Therefore it is a closed subset of the weakly compact set $T(\mathcal P(K))$, proving

$$
\boxed{\overline{\operatorname{conv}K}^{\,\|\cdot\|}\text{ is weakly compact}.}
$$

The empty case is immediate. In fact $T(\mathcal P(K))=C$: a [barycenter of a measure on a Banach space](../../../measure-theory.md#barycenter-of-a-measure-on-a-banach-space) outside $C$ would be strictly separated by a functional $\varphi$, contradicting $\varphi(T(\mu))=\int_K\varphi\,d\mu\leq\sup_K\varphi$.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 106](paper-106.md)

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

For the first norm-one assertion, use the usual unital [Banach algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md) convention $\|1\|=1$. A [character of an algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md#character-of-an-algebra) is a nonzero complex linear multiplicative functional, initially without any continuity assumption. It satisfies $\varphi(1)=1$, since otherwise $\varphi(a)=\varphi(a1)=0$ for every $a$. Moreover, $a-\varphi(a)1$ cannot be invertible: applying $\varphi$ to an inverse identity would give $0=1$. Thus

$$
\varphi(a)\in\sigma_A(a),\qquad |\varphi(a)|\leq r(a)\leq\|a\|.
$$

This proves [automatic continuity of characters](../../../banach-algebra.md#automatic-continuity-of-characters) and $\|\varphi\|\leq1$. Evaluation at the normalized identity gives the reverse inequality, so

$$
\boxed{\varphi\text{ is continuous and }\|\varphi\|=1.}
$$

If a convention permits $\|1\|>1$, only $\|\varphi\|\leq1$ follows in general: on $\mathbb C$ with norm $2|z|$, the identity character has norm $1/2$. The later completion argument needs only the upper bound.

If $a$ is invertible, $\varphi(a)\varphi(a^{-1})=1$, so every character is nonzero on $a$. Conversely, if $a$ is not invertible, its principal ideal is proper and lies in a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $M$ by [Zorn lemma](../../../set-theory.md#zorn-s-lemma). The ideal $M$ is closed: if its closure were the whole algebra, it would contain $m$ with $\|1-m\|<1$, making $m$ invertible by the [Neumann series](../../../banach-algebra.md#neumann-series), which is impossible for a proper ideal. Maximality therefore gives $\overline M=M$. The quotient $A/M$ is a complex Banach division algebra, hence is $\mathbb C$ by the [Gelfand-Mazur theorem](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-mazur-theorem). The quotient map yields a character vanishing at $a$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{a\text{ is invertible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi(a)\ne0\text{ for every }\varphi\in\Phi_A.}
$$

Now let $A=C(K)$. Its [evaluation characters](../../../banach-algebra.md#evaluation-character) give the map $x\mapsto\delta_x\in\Phi_A$. Every character has this form. Indeed, its kernel is a proper ideal. If the functions in that kernel had no common zero, compactness would give finitely many $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ in the kernel with no common zero. Then $h=\sum_j\overline{f_j}f_j$ belongs to the kernel and is everywhere positive, hence is invertible in $C(K)$, a contradiction. At a common zero $x$, the fact that $f-\varphi(f)1$ belongs to the kernel gives $f(x)=\varphi(f)$ for every $f$. Continuous functions distinguish points of the [compact Hausdorff space](../../../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space) $K$, so the map is bijective. It is continuous for the [Gelfand topology](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-topology), and the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../topology.md#compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem) gives

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{C(K)}\cong K.}
$$

For $K=\varnothing$ the norm comparison is trivial; assume henceforth that $K\ne\varnothing$.

Let $B$ be the completion for the other [algebra norm](../../../banach-algebra.md#algebra-norm) $\|\cdot\|_1$. It remains commutative and unital, with $A$ embedded densely. The restriction of any $\psi\in\Phi_B$ is a nonzero character of $A$, since it takes $1$ to $1$, and is automatically continuous for the original [supremum norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#supremum-norm). Thus $R(\psi)=\psi|_A$ maps into $\Phi_A$. It is injective because two continuous characters agreeing on the dense subalgebra $A$ agree on $B$, and it is continuous because each coordinate $R(\psi)(f)=\psi(f)$ is weak-star continuous.

The [character space](../../../banach-algebra.md#character-space-of-an-algebra) $\Phi_B$ is weak-star compact even if its identity was not normalized: the spectral bound puts all its characters in $B_{B^*}$, and the conditions $\psi(1)=1$, $\psi(bc)=\psi(b)\psi(c)$ define a weak-star closed set. Apply [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../functional-analysis.md#banach-alaoglu-theorem). Consequently $R$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) onto a closed subset $L\subseteq K$.

Suppose $x\in U=K\setminus L$. The [compact Hausdorff space](../../../topology.md#compact-hausdorff-space) $K$ is normal, so choose an open $V$ with $x\in V\subseteq\overline V\subseteq U$. By the [Urysohn lemma](../../../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma), choose $g\in C(K)$ with $g(x)=1$ and $g=0$ on $K\setminus V$, and choose $f\in C(K)$ with $f=0$ on $\overline V$ and $f=1$ on $L$. Then $fg=0$. Every character $\psi$ of $B$ restricts to evaluation at a point of $L$, so $\psi(f)=1$. The invertibility criterion proved above makes $f$ invertible in $B$. Multiplying $fg=0$ by $f^{-1}$ in $B$ gives $g=0$, contradicting $g(x)=1$. Therefore $U=\varnothing$ and $R$ is onto $K$.

For each $x\in K$, choose a character $\psi_x\in\Phi_B$ extending $\delta_x$. Its spectral bound gives

$$
|f(x)|=|\psi_x(f)|\leq\|f\|_1.
$$

Taking the supremum over $x$ proves the [minimality of the supremum norm on C(K)](../../../banach-algebra.md#minimality-of-the-supremum-norm-on-c-k):

$$
\boxed{\|f\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_1\quad(f\in C(K)).}
$$

<h3 id="5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/b)

The [Beurling–Gelfand spectral radius formula](../../../analysis.md#spectral-radius-formula) states that for every element $a$ of a complex [Banach algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md),

$$
\boxed{r(a)=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(a)}|\lambda|
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a^n\|^{1/n}=\inf_{n\geq1}\|a^n\|^{1/n}.}
$$

For a [Hermitian element of a C-star algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md#hermitian-element-of-a-c-star-algebra) $a=a^*$, the [C-star identity](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-identity) gives $\|a^2\|=\|a\|^2$. Iterating,

$$
\|a^{2^m}\|=\|a\|^{2^m}.
$$

The subsequence $n=2^m$ in the spectral-radius limit therefore proves $\boxed{r(a)=\|a\|}$.

Let $\theta:A\to B$ be a unital [C-star homomorphism](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-homomorphism). If $a-\lambda1$ is invertible in $A$, its inverse is sent to an inverse of $\theta(a)-\lambda1$ in $B$. Thus $\sigma_B(\theta(a))\subseteq\sigma_A(a)$ without assuming continuity of $\theta$. For Hermitian $a$, $\theta(a)$ is also Hermitian, so

$$
\|\theta(a)\|=r_B(\theta(a))\leq r_A(a)=\|a\|.
$$

For arbitrary $x\in A$, apply this inequality to $x^*x$ and the [C-star identity](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-identity) in both algebras:

$$
\|\theta(x)\|^2=\|\theta(x)^*\theta(x)\|=\|\theta(x^*x)\|\leq\|x^*x\|=\|x\|^2.
$$

Hence $\boxed{\|\theta(x)\|\leq\|x\|}$ for every $x$.

Now assume $\theta$ is injective. For a fixed $x$, it is enough to prove norm preservation on $C=C^*(1,x^*x)$, a commutative unital [C-star algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-algebra): the preceding square-norm identity will then recover the norm of $x$. By the [Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem](../../../banach-algebra.md#commutative-gelfand-naimark-theorem), identify $C$ with $C(K)$. Let $D=\overline{\theta(C)}\subseteq B$. It is also a commutative unital [C-star algebra](../../../banach-algebra.md#c-star-algebra), and its [character space](../../../banach-algebra.md#character-space-of-an-algebra) $\Phi_D$ is compact. Each $\chi\in\Phi_D$ restricts along $\theta$ to a character of $C(K)$, hence to evaluation at a point $q(\chi)\in K$. The map $q:\Phi_D\to K$ is continuous, so $L=q(\Phi_D)$ is closed.

If $L\ne K$, the [Urysohn lemma](../../../topology.md#urysohn-s-lemma) provides a nonzero $f\in C(K)$ vanishing on $L$. Then $\chi(\theta(f))=f(q(\chi))=0$ for every $\chi\in\Phi_D$. Faithfulness of the [Gelfand transform](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-representation) in $D$ gives $\theta(f)=0$, contradicting injectivity. Thus $q$ is onto. The isometric [Gelfand transform](../../../banach-algebra.md#gelfand-representation) of $D$ now gives

$$
\|\theta(f)\|=\sup_{\chi\in\Phi_D}|\chi(\theta(f))|
=\sup_{t\in K}|f(t)|=\|f\|\quad(f\in C).
$$

Finally,

$$
\|\theta(x)\|^2=\|\theta(x^*x)\|=\|x^*x\|=\|x\|^2,
\qquad\boxed{\|\theta(x)\|=\|x\|.}
$$

This proves that an [injective C-star homomorphism is isometric](../../../banach-algebra.md#injective-c-star-homomorphism-is-isometric) without assuming its image is closed before norm preservation has been established.
