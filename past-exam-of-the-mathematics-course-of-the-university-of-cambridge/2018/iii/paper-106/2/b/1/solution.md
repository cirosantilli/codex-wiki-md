<h1 id="2/b/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

First construct the [spectral measure of a normal operator](../../../../../../../spectral-measure-of-a-normal-operator.md) and prove the open-set assertion needed for all three numbered conclusions. The algebra $A=C^*(I,T)$ generated by $T$ and $T^*$ is commutative because $T$ is a [normal operator](../../../../../../../normal-operator.md). The [Gelfand transform](../../../../../../../gelfand-representation.md) identifies $A$ with $C(\Phi_A)$. By [spectral permanence for C-star algebras](../../../../../../../spectral-permanence-for-c-star-algebras.md),

$$
h:\Phi_A\longrightarrow K=\sigma_{\mathcal B(H)}(T),\qquad h(\chi)=\chi(T)
$$

is onto: its image is the spectrum of $T$ in $A$, which is also its spectrum in $\mathcal B(H)$. It is injective because a character is determined by its values on $T,T^*$ and $I$, and $\chi(T^*)=\overline{\chi(T)}$. Thus $h$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Apply part (a) and set

$$
P(B)=E(h^{-1}(B))\quad(B\subseteq K\text{ Borel}).
$$

This is a [projection-valued measure](../../../../../../../projection-valued-measure.md) with $P(K)=I$ and

$$
\boxed{T=\int_Kz\,dP(z).}
$$

The associated [continuous functional calculus](../../../../../../../continuous-functional-calculus.md) is isometric: $\|u(T)\|=\|u\|_\infty$ for $u\in C(K)$.

If $U\subseteq K$ is nonempty and relatively open, choose $z_0\in U$ and a nonzero continuous $u$ supported inside $U$, for example a small continuous bump about $z_0$ in the metric of $K\subseteq\mathbb C$. If $P(U)=0$, then $u(T)=\int_Ku\,dP=0$, contradicting $\|u(T)\|=\|u\|_\infty>0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(U)\ne0\quad\text{for every nonempty relatively open }U\subseteq K.}
$$

This proves the claim from faithfulness of the representation rather than assuming it as part of the spectral theorem.

Now take an [isolated point](../../../../../../../isolated-point.md) $\lambda\in K$. Its singleton is relatively open, so $P(\{\lambda\})\ne0$. Multiplication of spectral integrals gives

$$
(T-\lambda I)P(\{\lambda\})=0.
$$

Thus its range is contained in the [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) $\ker(T-\lambda I)$. Conversely, for $v$ in that kernel, the positive [scalar spectral measure](../../../../../../../scalar-spectral-measure.md) $\mu_v(B)=\langle P(B)v,v\rangle$ satisfies

$$
0=\|(T-\lambda I)v\|^2=\int_K|z-\lambda|^2\,d\mu_v(z).
$$

It follows that $\mu_v(K\setminus\{\lambda\})=0$, and hence $\|(I-P(\{\lambda\}))v\|^2=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue},\qquad P(\{\lambda\})\text{ is the orthogonal projection onto }\ker(\lambda I-T).}
$$

The identification of this projection with the [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) holds even for a non-isolated $\lambda$; isolation guarantees that it is nonzero.
