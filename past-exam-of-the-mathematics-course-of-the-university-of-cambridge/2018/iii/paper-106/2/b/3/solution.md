<h1 id="2/b/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

If the spectrum has one point, the preceding part gives $T=\lambda I$, and the [linear span](../../../../../../../linear-span.md) of any nonzero vector is a closed proper [invariant subspace](../../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) because $\dim H>1$.

Otherwise choose distinct points $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in K$ and disjoint nonempty relatively open neighborhoods $U,V\subseteq K$. The open-set result gives $P(U)\ne0$ and $P(V)\ne0$, while

$$
P(U)P(V)=P(U\cap V)=0.
$$

Thus $P(U)\ne I$. Set $L=\operatorname{ran}P(U)$. As the range of an [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md), $L$ is closed, and the preceding observations give $\{0\}\ne L\ne H$. The [Borel functional calculus for a normal operator](../../../../../../../borel-functional-calculus-for-a-normal-operator.md) gives $TP(U)=P(U)T$, so

$$
\boxed{L=\operatorname{ran}P(U)\text{ is a nontrivial closed invariant subspace of }T.}
$$

In fact both $L$ and its orthogonal complement are invariant.
