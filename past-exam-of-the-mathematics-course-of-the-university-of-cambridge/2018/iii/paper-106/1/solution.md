The [weak-star topology](../../../../../weak-star-topology.md) $\sigma(X^*,X)$ is generated by the [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md) $p_x(g)=|g(x)|$, $x\in X$. Thus every evaluation is continuous, so $f_n\xrightarrow{w^*}f$ implies $f_n(x)\to f(x)$ for each $x$. Conversely, a basic neighborhood of $f$ imposes finitely many conditions $|(g-f)(x_j)|<\varepsilon$. Pointwise convergence eventually satisfies all of them simultaneously. Hence

$$
\boxed{f_n\xrightarrow{w^*}f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f_n(x)\longrightarrow f(x)\ \text{for every }x\in X.}
$$

The [Uniform boundedness principle](../../../../../uniform-boundedness-principle.md), applied on the [Banach space](../../../../../banach-space-split.md) $X$, gives $M=\sup_n\|f_n\|<\infty$. If $x_n\to x$ in the [norm topology](../../../../../norm-topology.md), then

$$
|f_n(x_n)-f(x)|\leq M\|x_n-x\|+|f_n(x)-f(x)|\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore $\boxed{f_n(x_n)\to f(x)}$. Completeness of $X$ is used in this uniform boundedness step.

If $C\subseteq X^*$ is norm compact, the identity map from $(C,\|\cdot\|)$ to $(C,w^*)$ is a continuous bijection. The target is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), since distinct functionals differ at some $x\in X$. The [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../../../compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\text{the norm and weak-star topologies coincide on }C.}
$$

The [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md) states that the closed dual [unit ball](../../../../../unit-ball.md) $B_{X^*}$ is compact in the [weak-star topology](../../../../../weak-star-topology.md); in fact $X$ need only be a [normed vector space](../../../../../normed-vector-space.md). To prove it, work over $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ and form

$$
Q=\prod_{x\in X}D_x,\qquad D_x=\{z\in\mathbb F:|z|\leq\|x\|\}.
$$

Each $D_x$ is compact, so $Q$ is compact by the [Tychonoff theorem](../../../../../tychonoff-s-theorem.md). Embed $B_{X^*}$ by $g\mapsto(g(x))_{x\in X}$. Its image consists exactly of those families $(z_x)$ satisfying

$$
z_{x+y}=z_x+z_y,\qquad z_{\alpha x}=\alpha z_x\quad(x,y\in X,\ \alpha\in\mathbb F).
$$

These are closed conditions in the product topology. Conversely, any family satisfying them defines a linear functional bounded by $|z_x|\leq\|x\|$, hence an element of $B_{X^*}$. The image is therefore closed and compact. The induced product topology is precisely the [weak-star topology](../../../../../weak-star-topology.md), proving the theorem. This is a compactness proof for arbitrary nets, with no separability assumption.

For the [dual map of bounded linear operator](../../../../../transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator.md) $T^*:Y^*\to X^*$, each $x\in X$ satisfies

$$
(T^*g)(x)=g(Tx).
$$

Evaluation at the fixed vector $Tx$ is weak-star continuous, so $\boxed{T^*\text{ is weak-star-to-weak-star continuous}}$.

Suppose $T$ is a [compact operator](../../../../../compact-operator-split.md). By the allowed [Schauder theorem for compact operators](../../../../../schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators.md), $T^*$ is compact. If $S\subseteq Y^*$ is bounded, the norm closure $C=\overline{T^*(S)}$ is norm compact. We already proved that its norm and weak-star topologies coincide. The weak-star continuous map $T^*|_S$ into $C$ is consequently norm continuous. Conversely, if $T^*$ is weak-star-to-norm continuous on bounded subsets, its restriction to $B_{Y^*}$ maps a weak-star compact set, by [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md), to a norm compact set. Thus $T^*$ is a [compact operator](../../../../../compact-operator-split.md), and the [Schauder theorem for compact operators](../../../../../schauder-theorem-for-compact-operators.md) gives

$$
\boxed{T\text{ compact}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T^*|_S:(S,w^*)\to(X^*,\|\cdot\|)\text{ is continuous for every bounded }S\subseteq Y^*.}
$$
