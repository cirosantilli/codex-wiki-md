<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the [Calabi-Yau threefold](../../../../../../calabi-yau-threefold.md) in the stated convention, the [Hodge decomposition theorem for compact Kähler manifolds](../../../../../../hodge-decomposition-theorem-for-compact-kahler-manifolds.md) gives

$$
0=H^1(M,\mathbb C)=H^{1,0}_{\bar\partial}(M)\oplus H^{0,1}_{\bar\partial}(M).
$$

In particular, the [Dolbeault theorem](../../../../../../dolbeault-theorem.md) yields $H^1(M,\mathcal O_M)=0$. By [Serre duality for compact complex manifolds](../../../../../../serre-duality-for-compact-complex-manifolds.md) in dimension three,

$$
H^2(M,\mathcal O_M)^*\cong H^1(M,K_M).
$$

The [canonical bundle](../../../../../../canonical-bundle.md) is trivial, so the group on the right equals $H^1(M,\mathcal O_M)=0$. Thus $H^2(M,\mathcal O_M)=0$, and part (c) gives

$$
\boxed{\text{Every Calabi-Yau threefold satisfying the given definition is projective}.}
$$
