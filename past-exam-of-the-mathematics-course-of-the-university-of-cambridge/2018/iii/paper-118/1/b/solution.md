<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) interchanges the [Dolbeault operator](../../../../../../dolbeault-operator.md) and [conjugate Dolbeault operator](../../../../../../conjugate-dolbeault-operator.md). Thus $\partial\alpha=0$ implies $\bar\partial\bar\alpha=0$, and $\bar\alpha$ has type $(q,p)$. Its antiholomorphic degree is $p\geq1$, so the allowed [Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma](../../../../../../dolbeault-poincare-lemma.md) on the [polydisc](../../../../../../polydisc.md) gives a form $\gamma\in\mathcal A^{q,p-1}$ satisfying $\bar\partial\gamma=\bar\alpha$.

Conjugate this identity and put $\beta=\bar\gamma$. This proves the [conjugate Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma](../../../../../../conjugate-dolbeault-poincare-lemma.md):

$$
\boxed{\beta\in\mathcal A^{p-1,q}(\mathcal P^n),\qquad\partial\beta=\alpha.}
$$
