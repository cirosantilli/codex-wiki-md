<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Represent the middle-degree class by its unique [harmonic differential form](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) $h$. The [Lefschetz commutator](../../../../../../lefschetz-commutator.md) on degree $k$ is $[\Lambda,L]=(n-k)\operatorname{id}$, so at $k=n$ the operators commute. Since the [adjoint Lefschetz operator](../../../../../../adjoint-lefschetz-operator.md) is the adjoint of the [Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold](../../../../../../lefschetz-operator-of-a-kahler-manifold.md),

$$
\|Lh\|^2=\langle\Lambda Lh,h\rangle
=\langle L\Lambda h,h\rangle=\|\Lambda h\|^2.
$$

Both $Lh$ and $\Lambda h$ are harmonic. A harmonic form represents zero in [de Rham cohomology](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology.md) exactly when the form itself is zero. Consequently $L[\alpha]=0$ if and only if $Lh=0$, which by the norm identity is equivalent to $\Lambda h=0$, hence to $\Lambda[\alpha]=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha\in H^n(M,\mathbb C)\text{ is primitive}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Lambda\alpha=0.}
$$

This identifies the two middle-degree descriptions of a [primitive differential form on a Kähler manifold](../../../../../../primitive-differential-form-on-a-kahler-manifold.md) at the cohomology level.
