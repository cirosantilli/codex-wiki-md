<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\omega$ be the Kähler form associated with the metric. The [Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold](../../../../../../lefschetz-operator-of-a-kahler-manifold.md) and [adjoint Lefschetz operator](../../../../../../adjoint-lefschetz-operator.md) are

$$
\boxed{L\alpha=\omega\wedge\alpha,\qquad\Lambda=L^*.}
$$

Here the adjoint is taken with respect to the pointwise [Hermitian inner product](../../../../../../hermitian-form.md) on complexified forms, or equivalently the global $L^2$ inner product. It lowers degree by two and bidegree by $(1,1)$. In terms of the [Hodge star operator](../../../../../../hodge-star-operator.md), $\Lambda=*^{-1}L*$.

Since $d\omega=0$, $dL=Ld$, so $L$ takes [closed differential forms](../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) to [closed differential forms](../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) and [exact differential forms](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) to [exact differential forms](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md). This already gives its action on [de Rham cohomology](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology.md). For both operators together, use the [Kähler identities](../../../../../../kahler-identities.md): $L$ commutes with the [Hodge Laplacian](../../../../../../hodge-laplacian.md), and taking adjoints shows that $\Lambda$ does too. Thus both preserve [harmonic differential forms](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md).

When $M$ is compact, the [Hodge decomposition theorem](../../../../../../hodge-decomposition-theorem.md) identifies $H^k(M,\mathbb C)$ with its space of harmonic $k$-forms. If $h_\alpha$ is the unique [harmonic differential form](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) representing $[\alpha]$, define

$$
\boxed{L[\alpha]=[Lh_\alpha],\qquad\Lambda[\alpha]=[\Lambda h_\alpha].}
$$

The resulting forms are harmonic and therefore closed, and uniqueness of $h_\alpha$ makes these definitions independent of the initial representative. The cohomological definition of $\Lambda$ uses [harmonic differential forms](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) as representatives: $\Lambda$ need not take an arbitrary closed form to a closed form.
