<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [holomorphic differential form](../../../../../../holomorphic-differential-form.md) $\alpha$ of type $(p,0)$ satisfies $\bar\partial\alpha=0$. The adjoint $\bar\partial^*$ lowers antiholomorphic degree, so $\bar\partial^*\alpha=0$ as well. Therefore $\Delta_{\bar\partial}\alpha=0$.

The [Kähler Laplacian identity](../../../../../../kahler-laplacian-identity.md) gives $\Delta_d\alpha=2\Delta_{\bar\partial}\alpha=0$. [Compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) lets us use the $L^2$ identity for the [Hodge Laplacian](../../../../../../hodge-laplacian.md):

$$
0=\langle\Delta_d\alpha,\alpha\rangle
=\|d\alpha\|^2+\|d^*\alpha\|^2.
$$

Both terms are nonnegative, so

$$
\boxed{d\alpha=0.}
$$

This proves that [holomorphic forms on a compact Kähler manifold are closed](../../../../../../holomorphic-forms-on-a-compact-kahler-manifold-are-closed.md). The original PDF uses $\Omega_M^p$ and $\mathcal A_{M,\mathbb C}^{p+1}$; the extra symbols in the local TeX superscripts are transcription errors.
