<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [nematic order parameter](../../../../../../nematic-order-parameter.md) is a [symmetric second-rank tensor](../../../../../../symmetric-second-rank-tensor.md) and a [traceless second-rank tensor](../../../../../../traceless-second-rank-tensor.md) describing orientational anisotropy. For molecular unit axes $\mathbf s$, $Q_{ij}$ is proportional to $\langle s_i s_j-\delta_{ij}/3\rangle$. It vanishes in an [isotropic phase](../../../../../../isotropic-phase.md) and is unchanged by head-tail reversal $\mathbf s\mapsto-\mathbf s$, unlike a [polar order parameter](../../../../../../polar-order-parameter.md). For [uniaxial nematic order](../../../../../../uniaxial-nematic-order.md), the [nematic director](../../../../../../nematic-director.md) is thus an unoriented axis.

One convention for the [Landau-de Gennes free energy](../../../../../../landau-de-gennes-free-energy.md) through fourth order, respecting [rotational symmetry](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry.md), is

$$
\boxed{f(Q)=\frac a2\operatorname{Tr}Q^2
+\frac c3\operatorname{Tr}Q^3
+\frac b4(\operatorname{Tr}Q^2)^2},\qquad b>0.
$$

For a [traceless second-rank tensor](../../../../../../traceless-second-rank-tensor.md) in three dimensions, the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md) gives $\operatorname{Tr}Q^4=(\operatorname{Tr}Q^2)^2/2$, so there is only one independent quartic [rotational invariant of a symmetric traceless tensor](../../../../../../rotational-invariant-of-a-symmetric-traceless-tensor.md). The only possible linear scalar is $\operatorname{Tr}Q=0$; without an external anisotropy no linear term survives.

The cubic [rotational invariant of a symmetric traceless tensor](../../../../../../rotational-invariant-of-a-symmetric-traceless-tensor.md) distinguishes prolate and oblate forms of [uniaxial nematic order](../../../../../../uniaxial-nematic-order.md). Head-tail reversal leaves $Q$ unchanged and does not impose $Q\mapsto-Q$. Thus a cubic term is allowed in three dimensions and generically produces a [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md), rather than a symmetry-enforced continuous onset. In two dimensions a symmetric [traceless second-rank tensor](../../../../../../traceless-second-rank-tensor.md) has [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $s,-s$, hence $\operatorname{Tr}Q^3=0$: **the cubic invariant vanishes identically in two dimensions**. This is a statement about the [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md); fluctuations in two dimensions require a separate treatment.
