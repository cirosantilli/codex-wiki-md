<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The implication is **false**. Choose

$$
\boxed{p=3,\quad k=2,\quad a=1,\quad m=4,\quad n=13,\quad\lambda=(12,1).}
$$

The inequalities $0\leq4<9$ and the relation $13=1\cdot9+4$ hold. At cell $(1,4)$, the [hook length](../../../../../../hook-length.md) is $12-4+1=9=ap^k$.

For the [partition of an integer](../../../../../../partition-of-an-integer.md) $(12,1)$, the [Hook-length formula](../../../../../../hook-length-formula.md) gives

$$
\chi^{(12,1)}(1)=\frac{13!}{13\cdot11!}=12,
$$

which is divisible by $3$. This also agrees with the degree of the [standard representation of the symmetric group](../../../../../../standard-representation-of-the-symmetric-group.md) $S_{13}$.

The obstruction is visible in the [core of a partition](../../../../../../core-of-a-partition.md): removing the horizontal [rim hook](../../../../../../rim-hook.md) of length nine leaves $\gamma=(3,1)$, which has no hook length nine and hence is the $9$-core. Its degree is $\chi^{(3,1)}(1)=3$. The large hook ensures the required [weight of a partition](../../../../../../weight-of-a-partition.md) here, but the core degree is not coprime to $p$.
