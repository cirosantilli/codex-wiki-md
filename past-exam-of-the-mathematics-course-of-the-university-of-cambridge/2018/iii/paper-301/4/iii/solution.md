<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Insert $\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}=\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}+\alpha\omega^\mu{}_{\nu}$ into the [Lorentz group](../../../../../../lorentz-group.md) condition and keep the coefficient of $\alpha$. It gives

$$
\eta_{\sigma\nu}\omega^\sigma{}_{\mu}+\eta_{\mu\tau}\omega^\tau{}_{\nu}=0.
$$

Define $\omega_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\rho}\omega^\rho{}_{\nu}$. The displayed equation is $\omega_{\nu\mu}+\omega_{\mu\nu}=0$. Raising both indices with the symmetric [Minkowski metric](../../../../../../minkowski-metric.md) preserves this antisymmetry:

$$
\boxed{\omega_{\mu\nu}=-\omega_{\nu\mu},\qquad\omega^{\mu\nu}=-\omega^{\nu\mu}.}
$$

These are the generators of the [Lie algebra of the Lorentz group](../../../../../../lie-algebra-of-the-lorentz-group.md). In particular $\omega^\mu{}_{\mu}=0$. The mixed-index matrix need not be antisymmetric in the ordinary Euclidean transpose; it is antisymmetric after lowering an index with $\eta$.
