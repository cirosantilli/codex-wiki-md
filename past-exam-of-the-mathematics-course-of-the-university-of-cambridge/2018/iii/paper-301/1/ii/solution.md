<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [quantum electrodynamics](../../../../../../quantum-electrodynamics.md) interaction density to be $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=-e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$. The momentum-space [Feynman-gauge photon propagator](../../../../../../feynman-gauge-photon-propagator.md), with Lorentz indices $\mu,\nu$ and momentum $k$, is

$$
\boxed{D_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i\eta_{\mu\nu}}{k^2+i\epsilon}.}
$$

The electron-photon vertex from the [QED Feynman rules](../../../../../../qed-feynman-rules.md) is

$$
\boxed{-ie\gamma^\mu,}
$$

with its spinor indices connecting the incident and outgoing fermion lines. The muon-photon vertex has the same coupling. The [Feynman i-epsilon prescription](../../../../../../feynman-i-epsilon-prescription.md) specifies the pole boundary condition; using a different overall charge-sign convention changes both vertices consistently and leaves the squared amplitude unchanged.

For the [electron-positron annihilation into a muon pair](../../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation-into-a-muon-pair.md), the exchanged [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) has $k=p+q$ and $k^2=s$. Up to the conventional overall phase, the [scattering amplitude](../../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md) is

$$
\mathcal M=\frac{e^2}{s}\,[\bar v_r(q)\gamma^\mu u_s(p)]\,[\bar u_{s'}(p')\gamma_\mu v_{r'}(q')].
$$
