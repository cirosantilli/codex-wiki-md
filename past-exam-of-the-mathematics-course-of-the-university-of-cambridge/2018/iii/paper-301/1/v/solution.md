<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Substitute the preceding [spin average](../../../../../../spin-average.md) into the supplied [differential scattering cross-section](../../../../../../differential-scattering-cross-section.md). Since $e^4=16\pi^2\alpha^2$,

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{\alpha^2}{4s}(1+\cos^2\theta).
$$

The angular integral is $2\pi\int_{-1}^1(1+x^2)\,dx=16\pi/3$. Consequently the [relativistic scattering cross-section](../../../../../../relativistic-scattering-cross-section.md) for the massless [electron-positron annihilation into a muon pair](../../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation-into-a-muon-pair.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}.}
$$

The outgoing particles are distinct, so there is no identical-particle factor. Comparing with the requested monomial form gives

$$
\boxed{B=\frac{4\pi}{3},\qquad j=2,\quad k=-1,\quad l=0,\quad m=0.}
$$

The integrated answer depends only on the incoming invariant $s$; the angular variables $t,u$ have been integrated out.
