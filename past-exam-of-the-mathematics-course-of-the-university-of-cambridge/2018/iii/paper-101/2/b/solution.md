<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The equivalence relation defining the [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) gives

$$
\ker(R\to R_P)=\{r\in R:sr=0\text{ for some }s\notin P\}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{R\longrightarrow R_P\text{ is injective }
\iff\text{ every }s\in R\setminus P\text{ annihilates no nonzero element of }R.}
$$

Equivalently, all [zero divisors](../../../../../../zero-divisor.md) of $R$ must lie in $P$. To prove necessity, if $s\notin P$ and $sr=0$ with $r\ne0$, then $r/1=0$ in $R_P$, contradicting the hypothesis that the map is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Conversely, if every denominator annihilates no nonzero element, the displayed [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) is zero, so the map is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md).

An [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) automatically satisfies this criterion at every [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md), but the criterion itself does not require $R$ to be an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md).
