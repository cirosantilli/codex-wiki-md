<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**No.** For example, take $R=k$, any [field](../../../../../../field.md). It is an [Artinian ring](../../../../../../artinian-ring.md), since its only [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) are zero and itself. In the [formal power series ring](../../../../../../formal-power-series.md) $k[[x]]$, however,

$$
(x)\supsetneq(x^2)\supsetneq(x^3)\supsetneq\cdots
$$

is a strictly descending chain of [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md). The inclusion is strict because $x^n$ has a nonzero coefficient of $x^n$, whereas every element of $(x^{n+1})$ has that coefficient zero. Thus $k[[x]]$ fails the [descending chain condition](../../../../../../descending-chain-condition.md) and is not [Artinian](../../../../../../artinian-ring.md).

The same argument works for any nonzero coefficient [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) $R$: the coefficient $1$ in $x^n$ cannot be produced by a multiple of $x^{n+1}$. Therefore **$R[[x]]$ is never Artinian when $R\ne0$**. The zero [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) is the harmless exception.
