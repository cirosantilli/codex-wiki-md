<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [spectrum of a commutative ring](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md) is the set

$$
\operatorname{Spec}R=\{\mathfrak p\subsetneq R:\mathfrak p\text{ is a prime ideal}\}.
$$

For an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $I\subseteq R$, set $V(I)=\{\mathfrak p:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$. The [Zariski topology](../../../../../../zariski-topology.md) declares precisely these subsets to be [closed sets](../../../../../../closed-set.md). Using arbitrary subsets instead of [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) gives the same definition, because a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) contains a subset exactly when it contains the [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) generated by that subset.

We verify the [closed set](../../../../../../closed-set.md) axioms directly. First, $V(0)=\operatorname{Spec}R$ and $V(R)=\varnothing$. For any family of [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md),

$$
\bigcap_{\lambda}V(I_\lambda)=V\left(\sum_{\lambda}I_\lambda\right),
$$

since containing the sum is equivalent to containing every summand. For two [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md),

$$
V(I)\cup V(J)=V(IJ).
$$

Indeed, containing either $I$ or $J$ implies containing their [product of ideals](../../../../../../product-of-ideals.md). Conversely, if a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p$ contains $IJ$ but does not contain $I$, choose $a\in I\setminus\mathfrak p$. For every $b\in J$, $ab\in\mathfrak p$, so the [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) property forces $b\in\mathfrak p$. Thus $J\subseteq\mathfrak p$.

The displayed identities establish arbitrary intersections and finite unions of [closed sets](../../../../../../closed-set.md), including their empty cases. Therefore **these sets define a topology on $\operatorname{Spec}R$**.
