<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Here the coefficient [fields](../../../../../../field.md) are $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$. A [polynomial ring](../../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) in one variable over a [field](../../../../../../field.md) is a [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md), hence a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md); its nonzero [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) are generated by [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md). The [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) says that all [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) over $\mathbb C$ are linear. Combining it with [complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) shows that the [monic](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) over $\mathbb R$ are exactly the linear polynomials and quadratics with no real root. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R[x]
=\{(0)\}\cup\{(x-a):a\in\mathbb R\}
\cup\{((x-a)^2+b^2):a\in\mathbb R,\ b>0\}.}
$$

Every nonzero [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) in this list is a [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md); $(0)$ is a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) because $\mathbb R[x]$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). Likewise,

$$
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[x]=\{(0)\}\cup\{(x-z):z\in\mathbb C\}.
$$

The map is [contraction of an ideal](../../../../../../contraction-of-an-ideal.md) along the inclusion. It sends $(0)$ to $(0)$. For $z=a+ib$, the contraction of $(x-z)$ consists of the real polynomials vanishing at $z$. If $b=0$, the [factor theorem](../../../../../../factor-theorem.md) gives the [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $(x-a)$. If $b\ne0$, a real polynomial vanishing at $z$ also vanishes at its [complex conjugate](../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) $\overline z$, so it is divisible by $(x-z)(x-\overline z)=(x-a)^2+b^2$. Division by this real quadratic gives the converse and hence

$$
\boxed{f((x-z))=
\begin{cases}
(x-a),&z=a\in\mathbb R,\\
((x-a)^2+b^2),&z=a+ib,\ b\ne0.
\end{cases}}
$$

Thus each real linear [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) has one preimage, each real quadratic [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) has the two conjugate preimages $(x-a-ib)$ and $(x-a+ib)$, and $(0)$ has only $(0)$ above it. In particular, $f$ is surjective.
