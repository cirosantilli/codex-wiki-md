<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The induced map on the [spectrum of a commutative ring](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md) is [contraction of an ideal](../../../../../../contraction-of-an-ideal.md):

$$
\boxed{f(\mathfrak q)=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q).}
$$

To see that it is well defined, let $\mathfrak q$ be a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of $S$. Its inverse image is an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) of $R$, and it is proper because $\varphi(1)=1\notin\mathfrak q$. If $ab\in\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, then $\varphi(a)\varphi(b)\in\mathfrak q$, whence $\varphi(a)\in\mathfrak q$ or $\varphi(b)\in\mathfrak q$. This proves that the inverse image is a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md).

For any [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $I\subseteq R$, its [extension of an ideal](../../../../../../extension-of-an-ideal.md) to $S$ is $IS=\varphi(I)S$. We have

$$
f^{-1}(V_R(I))=\{\mathfrak q:I\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)\}=V_S(IS).
$$

Thus inverse images of [Zariski-closed sets](../../../../../../zariski-closed-set.md) are [Zariski-closed sets](../../../../../../zariski-closed-set.md), which proves that **$f$ is a continuous map**.
