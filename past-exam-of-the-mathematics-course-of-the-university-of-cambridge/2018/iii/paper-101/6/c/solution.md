<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a subring $S\subseteq R$ with the same identity, $R$ is an [integral extension](../../../../../../integral-extension.md) of $S$ if every $r\in R$ is an [integral element](../../../../../../integral-element.md) over $S$: there exist $n\geq1$ and $s_0,\ldots,s_{n-1}\in S$ such that

$$
\boxed{r^n+s_{n-1}r^{n-1}+\cdots+s_1r+s_0=0.}
$$

The requirement that the polynomial be [monic](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) is essential. Merely satisfying some polynomial equation over $S$ is insufficient: for instance $1/2\in\mathbb Q$ satisfies $2T-1=0$ over $\mathbb Z$, but is not integral over $\mathbb Z$. To see this directly, a hypothetical [monic](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) equation for $1/2$, multiplied by $2^n$, would say that an odd integer $1$ plus a sum of even integers is zero.

The degree and coefficients may depend on $r$. Integrality does not by itself assert that the entire [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) $R$ is a finite $S$-[module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md); it does imply that the subalgebra $S[r]$ is generated as an $S$-[module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) by $1,r,\ldots,r^{n-1}$.
