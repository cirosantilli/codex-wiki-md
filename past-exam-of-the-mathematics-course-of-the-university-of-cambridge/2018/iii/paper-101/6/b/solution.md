<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md) $A$,

$$
\boxed{\dim A=1.}
$$

Let $\pi$ be a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md), so its unique nonzero [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) is $\mathfrak m=(\pi)$. We use the defining description of a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md): it is a non-field [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) that is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md), and every nonzero element is $u\pi^n$ for a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $u$ and an integer $n\geq0$.

Every nonzero proper [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is therefore $(\pi^n)$ for some $n\geq1$. Such an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) only when $n=1$: for $n\geq2$, $\pi\pi^{n-1}\in(\pi^n)$ while neither factor belongs to $(\pi^n)$. The [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $(\pi)$ is prime because it is maximal. Also $(0)$ is prime because $A$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). Thus

$$
\operatorname{Spec}A=\{(0),\mathfrak m\},\qquad (0)\subsetneq\mathfrak m,
$$

and the longest strict chain has length one.

The original PDF asks about a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md); the local TeX transcription's phrase “discrete variable $X$” is an extraction error.
