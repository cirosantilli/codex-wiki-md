**No.** In the [Gaussian integers](../../../../../../gaussian-integer.md), choose

$$
P=(2+i)\subset\mathbb Z[i],\qquad Q=P\cap\mathbb Z=(5).
$$

Indeed, substituting $i=-2$ gives $\mathbb Z[i]/(2+i)\cong\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$, so $P$ is a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) and its contraction is $(5)$. Put $\beta=2-i$. Modulo $P$, $\beta$ becomes $4\ne0$ in $\mathbb F_5$, so $\beta\notin P$. Therefore

$$
\alpha=\frac1{2-i}=\frac{2+i}{5}\in\mathbb Z[i]_P.
$$

We show that $\alpha$ is not an [integral element](../../../../../../integral-element.md) over $\mathbb Z_{(5)}$, using a direct reduction argument. Write $B=\mathbb Z_{(5)}[i]$, viewed inside $\mathbb Q(i)$. There is a [ring homomorphism](../../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md)

$$
\rho:B\longrightarrow\mathbb F_5,\qquad i\longmapsto2,
$$

which reduces rational coefficients modulo $5$. It is well defined because denominators in $\mathbb Z_{(5)}$ are not divisible by $5$, and $2^2+1=0$ in $\mathbb F_5$. In particular, $\rho(\beta)=0$ and $\rho(1)=1$.

If $\alpha$ satisfied a [monic](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) equation over $\mathbb Z_{(5)}$, say

$$
\alpha^n+a_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0,
$$

multiplication by $\beta^n$ would give an equality in $B$:

$$
1+a_{n-1}\beta+a_{n-2}\beta^2+\cdots+a_0\beta^n=0.
$$

Applying $\rho$ gives $1=0$ in $\mathbb F_5$, a contradiction. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z[i]_{(2+i)}\text{ is not integral over }\mathbb Z_{(5)}.}
$$

This exhibits why [localization of an integral extension at a prime need not be integral](../../../../../../localization-of-an-integral-extension-at-a-prime-need-not-be-integral.md). Localizing both [rings](../../../../../../ring.md) by the same [multiplicative subset](../../../../../../multiplicatively-closed-set.md) preserves an [integral extension](../../../../../../integral-extension.md), but $\mathbb Z[i]_P$ inverts additional elements, including $2-i$, whose image at the other [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $(2-i)$ is zero. The original extension is integral because $i$ satisfies the [monic](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) equation $T^2+1=0$: the [subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements](../../../../../../subalgebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements.md) property applies to $\mathbb Z[i]$.
