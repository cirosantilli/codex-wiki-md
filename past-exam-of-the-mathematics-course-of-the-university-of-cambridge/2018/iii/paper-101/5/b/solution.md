<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [unique factorization of ideals in a Dedekind domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-of-ideals-in-a-dedekind-domain.md) states that every nonzero proper [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $I$ of a [Dedekind domain](../../../../../../dedekind-domain.md) $D$ has a factorization

$$
\boxed{I=P_1^{n_1}\cdots P_r^{n_r},}
$$

where the $P_j$ are distinct nonzero [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) and the $n_j$ are positive integers. The [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) and their exponents are unique up to reordering. The unit [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $D$ is the empty product; the zero [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is excluded.

Write $v_P(I)$ for the [prime-ideal valuation in a Dedekind domain](../../../../../../prime-ideal-valuation-in-a-dedekind-domain.md), namely the exponent of $P$ in this factorization, with zero assigned when $P$ is absent. The associated containment rule is

$$
I\subseteq J\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_P(I)\geq v_P(J)\text{ for every nonzero prime ideal }P.
$$

Larger exponents give smaller [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md), just as higher divisibility gives smaller [principal ideals](../../../../../../principal-ideal.md) in $\mathbb Z$. The theorem concerns unique factorization of [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md); it does not assert unique factorization of elements or that these [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) are principal.
