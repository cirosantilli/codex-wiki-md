<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A $P$-[primary ideal](../../../../../../primary-ideal.md) is a proper [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $Q$ with [radical of an ideal](../../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) $\sqrt Q=P$, such that $ab\in Q$ and $a\notin Q$ imply $b^n\in Q$ for some $n\geq1$. Equivalently, when $\sqrt Q=P$, the implication is $ab\in Q$, $b\notin P\Rightarrow a\in Q$.

**True for a nonempty finite family.** Let $Q=Q_1\cap\cdots\cap Q_m$, with each $Q_j$ a $P$-[primary ideal](../../../../../../primary-ideal.md). This [intersection of ideals](../../../../../../intersection-of-ideals.md) is proper. Its [radical of an ideal](../../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) is $P$: if $r\in P$, choose $n_j$ with $r^{n_j}\in Q_j$ and put $N=\max_j n_j$. Then $r^N\in Q$. Conversely, $r^N\in Q$ implies $r\in\sqrt{Q_j}=P$.

If $ab\in Q$ and $b\notin P$, the [primary ideal](../../../../../../primary-ideal.md) property gives $a\in Q_j$ for every $j$, so $a\in Q$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{Q_1\cap\cdots\cap Q_m\text{ is }P\text{-primary}\quad(m\geq1).}
$$

As usual, the statement about finite intersections excludes the empty intersection, which is the whole [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) and is not a [primary ideal](../../../../../../primary-ideal.md).
