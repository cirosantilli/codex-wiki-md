<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The vertical acceleration in the [thin disk](../../../../../../thin-disk.md) is of order $\Omega^2H$. Balancing it against the characteristic [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) force gives

$$
\frac{P}{\rho H}\sim\Omega^2H,\qquad c_s\sim\Omega H,
$$

up to factors depending on the [adiabatic exponent](../../../../../../heat-capacity-ratio.md) and the vertical profile. A displacement of size $\delta z$ has a restoring acceleration of order $\Omega^2\delta z$, so $\delta\ddot z\sim-\Omega^2\delta z$. Equivalently the [sound-crossing time](../../../../../../sound-crossing-time.md) is $H/c_s$. Both estimates give the [vertical dynamical timescale of a disk](../../../../../../vertical-dynamical-timescale-of-a-disk.md):

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm dyn}\sim\frac H{c_s}\sim\Omega^{-1}.}
$$

This is a mechanical response time, not an unconditional damping time. An inviscid stable layer can exhibit a [vertical breathing mode of an astrophysical disk](../../../../../../vertical-breathing-mode-of-an-astrophysical-disk.md) without settling; its adiabatic frequency satisfies $\omega_H^2=(\gamma+1)\Omega^2$. Moreover the profile in part (b) need not be convectively stable: its [specific entropy](../../../../../../specific-entropy.md) satisfies

$$
s_{\rm gas}=c_v\log(P/\rho^\gamma)+\text{constant}
=c_v[1-\beta(\gamma-1)]\log T+\text{constant}.
$$

For $z>0$, $T$ decreases upward, so [convective stability](../../../../../../convective-stability.md) requires $\beta>1/(\gamma-1)$, with equality neutrally stratified. Thus interpreting the timescale as re-establishment of a stable equilibrium presupposes suitable stability and damping; the stated equations alone do not guarantee either.
