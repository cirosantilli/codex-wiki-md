<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Distinguish the [cylindrical radius](../../../../../../cylindrical-radius.md) $R$ from the spherical radius $r=\sqrt{R^2+z^2}$. A [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) of the central [gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) for $|z|/R\ll1$ gives

$$
\Phi=-\frac{GM}{R}\left(1+\frac{z^2}{R^2}\right)^{-1/2}
=-\frac{GM}{R}+\frac{GMz^2}{2R^3}+O\left(\frac{GMz^4}{R^5}\right).
$$

Thus the usual [thin-disk expansion of the stellar gravitational potential](../../../../../../thin-disk-expansion-of-the-stellar-gravitational-potential.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Phi(R,z)\simeq-\frac{GM}{R}+\frac12\Omega_K^2(R)z^2,\qquad\Omega_K^2=\frac{GM}{R^3}.}
$$

The vertical acceleration is $-\partial_z\Phi=-\Omega_K^2z$. The first term can be omitted when considering only vertical forces at fixed $R$, but must be retained for radial dynamics.
