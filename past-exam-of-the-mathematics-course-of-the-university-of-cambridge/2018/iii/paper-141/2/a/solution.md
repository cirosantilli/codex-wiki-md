<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Apply the given [skein relation](../../../../../../skein-relation.md) to a positive and a negative kink in a diagram of the [unknot](../../../../../../unknot.md). Both crossed [link diagrams](../../../../../../link-diagram.md) represent the [unknot](../../../../../../unknot.md), whereas the [oriented smoothing](../../../../../../oriented-smoothing.md) represents the two-component [unlink](../../../../../../unlink.md). Thus

$$
q-q^{-1}=(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})V(U_2).
$$

Factor the left-hand side and cancel the nonzero factor in the [Laurent polynomial](../../../../../../laurent-polynomial.md) ring:

$$
\boxed{V(U_2)=q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}.}
$$

The positive sign follows from the [skein relation](../../../../../../skein-relation.md) as printed in this paper. It differs from the frequently used [Jones polynomial](../../../../../../jones-polynomial.md) convention whose two-component [unlink](../../../../../../unlink.md) has value $-(q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2})$. With the printed convention, an $r$-component [link](../../../../../../link.md) has invariant $(-1)^{r-1}$ times that convention's [Jones polynomial](../../../../../../jones-polynomial.md).
