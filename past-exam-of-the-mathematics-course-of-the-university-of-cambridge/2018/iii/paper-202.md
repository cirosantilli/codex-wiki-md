↑ **Parent:** [Paper 202](paper-202.md)

<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

Let $\mathcal P$ be the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra) on $\Omega\times[0,\infty)$, generated by $A\times\{0\}$ with $A\in\mathcal F_0$ and $A\times(s,u]$ with $A\in\mathcal F_s$. A [stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md) $H$ is [previsible](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) precisely when $(\omega,t)\mapsto H_t(\omega)$ is $\mathcal P$-measurable.

**Every left-continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) is [previsible](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process).** Indeed, on intervals $(k2^{-n},(k+1)2^{-n}]$, put $H^{(n)}_t=H_{k2^{-n}}$, and put $H^{(n)}_0=H_0$. Each approximation is a [predictable process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process), since its coefficient is $\mathcal F_{k2^{-n}}$-measurable; left continuity gives $H^{(n)}_t\to H_t$ for every $t$, so measurability passes to the limit.

**An [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) [càdlàg process](../../../stochastic-process.md#cadlag-process) need not be [previsible](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process).** For a concrete example, let $\xi$ take values $0,1$ with equal probabilities, let $\mathcal F_t$ be trivial for $t<1$ and $\mathcal F_t=\sigma(\xi)$ for $t\geq1$, and set $H_t=\xi\mathbf1_{[1,\infty)}(t)$. This is an [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) [càdlàg process](../../../stochastic-process.md#cadlag-process), but $H_1=\xi$ is not $\mathcal F_{1-}$-measurable, where $\mathcal F_{1-}=\sigma(\bigcup_{s<1}\mathcal F_s)$. Sections at time $1$ of the generators of $\mathcal P$ all belong to $\mathcal F_{1-}$, so every [predictable process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) has an $\mathcal F_{1-}$-measurable value there; this proves the counterexample directly.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

Use the standard convention that the [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) $X$ has [càdlàg](../../../calculus.md#cadlag) paths and that the [stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) starts from zero. Write $V(X)$ for its [total-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#total-variation-process). The necessary admissibility condition is $\int_{(0,t]}|H_s|\,dV_s(X)<\infty$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) for each finite $t$; it holds, for example, for locally bounded $H$. Define the pathwise [Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral](../../../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-stieltjes-integration)

$$
\boxed{(H\mathbin\cdot X)_t=\int_{(0,t]}H_s\,dX_s,\qquad(H\mathbin\cdot X)_0=0.}
$$

An atom at $s>0$ contributes $H_s\Delta X_s$, so this convention also fixes the treatment of jumps.

Fix $t$. For a generator $H_s=\mathbf1_A\mathbf1_{(a,b]}(s)$ of the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra), with $A\in\mathcal F_a$, the [Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral](../../../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-stieltjes-integration) is

$$
\mathbf1_A\bigl(X_{b\wedge t}-X_{a\wedge t}\bigr).
$$

It vanishes when $t\leq a$ and is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable otherwise. The [total-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#total-variation-process) is [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process): for a [càdlàg](../../../calculus.md#cadlag) path its value at $t$ is the [supremum](../../../real-analysis.md#supremum) over a countable collection of [partitions of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) using rational interior points and endpoint $t$. Thus the positive and negative [Lebesgue-Stieltjes measures](../../../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-stieltjes-measure) $dX^\pm=(dV(X)\pm dX)/2$ have $\mathcal F_t$-measurable masses on the generator intervals.

The [Monotone class theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#monotone-class-theorem), applied separately to these positive [random measures](../../../measure-theory.md#random-measure) on $[0,t]$, extends this measurability from the generators of the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra) to all nonnegative [predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process). Truncation and positive/negative decomposition then cover every admissible $H$. Consequently **$H\mathbin\cdot X$ is [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process).**

**The integrability condition is essential.** Taken literally, an arbitrary [previsible process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) cannot always be integrated against a [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process): with $X_t=t$ and deterministic $H_s=s^{-1}$ for $s>0$, $H_0=0$, the proposed value at every $t>0$ diverges. The usual [càdlàg](../../../calculus.md#cadlag) convention is also what identifies $dX$ by $dX((a,b])=X_b-X_a$.

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

For deterministic [step functions](../../../measure-theory.md#step-function), define directly from [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) increments

$$
Z_t=\sum_{i=1}^n f_i\bigl(B_{t\wedge s_i}-B_{t\wedge s_{i-1}}\bigr),
$$

with $s_0$ the left endpoint of the first interval and $f=0$ outside the listed intervals. The printed endpoint list omits $s_0$; the intended convention is $0\leq s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_n$, usually $s_0=0$.

Given finitely many observation times, refine the deterministic [partition of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) to include all observation times and all $s_i$. Every $Z_{t_j}$ is a linear combination of the same finite family of [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) centered [normal](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) increments. Thus every linear combination of $(Z_{t_1},\ldots,Z_{t_m})$ is [normal](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution), including possibly a degenerate constant: $Z$ is a [Gaussian process](../../../stochastic-process.md#gaussian-process). By [independence](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), only increments in the common interval $[0,s\wedge t]$ contribute to the [covariance](../../../variance.md#covariance), giving

$$
\boxed{\mathbb EZ_t=0,\qquad C(s,t)=\int_0^{s\wedge t}f(u)^2\,du.}
$$

The same calculation for two [step functions](../../../measure-theory.md#step-function) $f,g$ proves, rather than assumes, the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry)

$$
\mathbb E|Z_t(f)-Z_t(g)|^2=\int_0^t|f(u)-g(u)|^2\,du.
$$

For $f\in L^2(\mathbb R_+)$, choose deterministic [step functions](../../../measure-theory.md#step-function) $f_n\to f$ in the [L2 space](../../../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space). This identity makes $Z_t(f_n)$ a [Cauchy sequence](../../../real-analysis.md#cauchy-sequence) in the [L2 space](../../../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space); the [Riesz-Fischer theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#riesz-fischer-theorem) defines $Z_t(f)$ by [mean-square convergence](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2), independently of the approximation. The [covariance](../../../variance.md#covariance) formula passes to the limit by the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality). For finitely many times and coefficients $a_j$, the [characteristic functions](../../../probability-theory.md#characteristic-function) converge to

$$
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(i\sum_j a_jZ_{t_j}(f)\right)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac12\sum_{j,k}a_ja_k\int_0^{t_j\wedge t_k}f(u)^2\,du\right),
$$

so the extension remains a [Gaussian process](../../../stochastic-process.md#gaussian-process).

For completeness, the construction has a continuous version. Each approximating $Z(f_n)$ is a [martingale](../../../martingale.md), directly by the centered [independent increments](../../../stochastic-process.md#independent-increments) of [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md); the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality) gives

$$
\mathbb E\sup_{u\leq T}|Z_u(f_n)-Z_u(f_m)|^2\leq4\|f_n-f_m\|_{L^2(\mathbb R_+)}^2.
$$

To prove the inequality used here without assuming [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) properties, a continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md) $N$ satisfies $\lambda\mathbb P(N^*_T\geq\lambda)\leq\mathbb E(|N_T|\mathbf1_{\{N^*_T\geq\lambda\}})$, by conditioning at the first crossing on finite deterministic grids and then using continuity; integrating in $\lambda$ and using the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) yields $\|N^*_T\|_2\leq2\|N_T\|_2$. A sufficiently fast subsequence therefore converges uniformly [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) on every compact time interval, giving the continuous version of the [stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral).

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

For a deterministic [partition of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) $0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=t$, put $Q_\pi=\sum_j\Delta_jB\,\Delta_j\widetilde B$. [Independence](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the two [Brownian motions](../../../brownian-motion.md) and their [independent increments](../../../stochastic-process.md#independent-increments) gives

$$
\mathbb EQ_\pi=0,\qquad\mathbb EQ_\pi^2=\sum_j(t_j-t_{j-1})^2\leq t|\pi|\longrightarrow0.
$$

The defining sums for [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) therefore converge to zero in the [L2 space](../../../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) and in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability). Hence, with the continuous versions of the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation),

$$
\boxed{\langle B,\widetilde B\rangle_t=0\quad(t\geq0).}
$$

Equality holds simultaneously for all $t$ by checking rational times and using continuity. For [continuous local martingales](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale), the angle-bracket notation here agrees with [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation).

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The [stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md) $W$ starts from zero and has continuous paths. Its increments on disjoint intervals are [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), since the corresponding pairs of [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) increments are [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), and

$$
W_t-W_s\sim N\bigl(0,(\rho^2+1-\rho^2)(t-s)\bigr)=N(0,t-s).
$$

Thus **$W$ is a standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md), including $\rho=\pm1$.** Here the intended [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) is the joint natural [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory), or another [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) relative to which the pair is a two-dimensional [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md); [independence](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of their path laws alone would not justify arbitrary extra information in a larger [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory).

By bilinearity of [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) and part (i),

$$
\boxed{\langle W,B\rangle_t=\rho\langle B\rangle_t+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\langle\widetilde B,B\rangle_t=\rho t.}
$$

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) applied to $F(t,x)=e^{t/2}\cos x$ gives exact cancellation of the [drift](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#drift-coefficient):

$$
d(e^{t/2}\cos B_t)=-e^{t/2}\sin B_t\,dB_t.
$$

On every finite interval $[0,T]$, the [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) is [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) since $\mathbb E\int_0^Te^s\sin^2B_s\,ds\leq e^T-1$. Therefore **$e^{t/2}\cos B_t$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md), and hence a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale).**

For the other [stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md), $d(B_t-t^2)=dB_t-2t\,dt$. If $B_t-t^2$ were a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), its difference with $B$ would make the continuous [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) $-t^2$ a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), contradicting the fact that a [continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant](../../../martingale.md#continuous-finite-variation-local-martingale-is-constant). Also $\mathbb E(B_t-t^2)=-t^2$ is not constant. Thus **$B_t-t^2$ is neither a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) nor a [martingale](../../../martingale.md).**

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

On the right-hand side, $\mathbb E_x$ means that the reference [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) starts at $B_0=x$; on the left it means the [stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) starts at $X_0=x$. Since $h$ is smooth and compactly supported, $h'$ is bounded and globally [Lipschitz continuous](../../../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity). Define the [stochastic exponential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential)

$$
D_t=\exp\!\left(\int_0^t h'(B_s)\,dB_s-\frac12\int_0^t h'(B_s)^2\,ds\right).
$$

The [Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) holds on every finite horizon $T$, because $\mathbb E\exp(\frac12\int_0^Th'(B_s)^2ds)\leq\exp(T\|h'\|_\infty^2/2)$. Thus $D$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md). Under $d\mathbb Q=D_Td\mathbb P$, the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) makes $\widehat B_t=B_t-x-\int_0^th'(B_s)ds$ a standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) for $t\leq T$. Consequently $B$ under $\mathbb Q$ solves the prescribed [stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation); the [existence and pathwise uniqueness theorem for a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#existence-and-pathwise-uniqueness-theorem-for-a-stochastic-differential-equation) identifies its law with that of $X$.

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives $\int_0^t h'(B_s)dB_s=h(B_t)-h(x)-\frac12\int_0^th''(B_s)ds$. Hence, for any bounded measurable $f$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E_xf(X_t)=\mathbb E_x\!\left[\exp\!\left(h(B_t)-h(x)-\int_0^tV(B_s)\,ds\right)f(B_t)\right],\quad V=\frac12(h')^2+\frac12h''.}
$$

The finite-horizon argument applies separately to every $t\geq0$.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 202](paper-202.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

Let $X,Y$ be two continuous solutions on the same [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space), driven by the same [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and with $X_0=Y_0$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Write $D=X-Y$ and choose a common [Lipschitz constant](../../../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-constant) $L$ for $b,\sigma$. Introduce the [stopping time](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) $\tau_n=\inf\{t\geq0:|X_t|+|Y_t|\geq n\}$. On $\{\tau_n=0\}$ the stopped difference is zero; before $\tau_n$, both coefficients and the difference are bounded.

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) and the zero [expectation](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value) of the stopped [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) yield

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E D_{t\wedge\tau_n}^2
&=\mathbb E\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{s<\tau_n\}}\left(2D_s(b(X_s)-b(Y_s))+(\sigma(X_s)-\sigma(Y_s))^2\right)ds\\
&\leq(2L+L^2)\int_0^t\mathbb E D_{s\wedge\tau_n}^2\,ds.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [Gronwall inequality](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#gronwall-inequality) implies $\mathbb ED_{t\wedge\tau_n}^2=0$. Continuous solution paths are bounded on each compact time interval, so $\tau_n\uparrow\infty$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Letting $n\to\infty$ proves $X_t=Y_t$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) at each fixed time, and a countable dense set of times together with continuity gives [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes). Therefore **the [stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) has [pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness).**

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) verifies the [geometric Brownian motion](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#geometric-brownian-motion) solution

$$
X_t=x\exp\!\left((b-\tfrac12\sigma^2)t+\sigma B_t\right).
$$

This is valid for negative $x$ as well as positive $x$; $x=0$ gives the zero solution. [Pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness) follows from the [Lipschitz continuity](../../../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) of the linear coefficients. The [moment-generating function of a normal distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#moment-generating-function-of-a-normal-distribution) gives $\mathbb Ee^{k\sigma B_t}=e^{k^2\sigma^2t/2}$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E(X_t^k)=x^k\exp\!\left(\left(kb+\frac{k(k-1)}2\sigma^2\right)t\right)\qquad(k\geq1).}
$$

These [moments](../../../probability-theory.md#moment) are finite on every finite horizon. If the convention $0\in\mathbb N$ is used, the zeroth [moment](../../../probability-theory.md#moment) is $1$.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Multiplication by the integrating factor $e^{t/2}$ and the [Itô product rule](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule) give the [Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process](../../../stochastic-process.md#ornstein-uhlenbeck-process)

$$
X_t=e^{-t/2}X_0+\int_0^te^{-(t-u)/2}\,dB_u.
$$

The two summands are [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) centered [normal](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) [random variables](../../../random-variable.md), because $X_0$ is [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and the integrand is deterministic. By the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry), their [variances](../../../variance.md) sum to $e^{-t}+\int_0^te^{-(t-u)}du=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{X_t\sim N(0,1)\quad(t\geq0).}
$$

For $t\geq s$, split the [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) at $s$ to obtain $X_t=e^{-(t-s)/2}X_s+\int_s^te^{-(t-u)/2}dB_u$, where the latter term is centered and [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $X_s$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_s)=e^{-|t-s|/2}.}
$$

In particular this initialization makes the [Gaussian process](../../../stochastic-process.md#gaussian-process) a [stationary process](../../../time-series.md#stationary-process).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 202](paper-202.md)

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

Use the standard definition of a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), including an integrable initial value, and let $T_n\uparrow\infty$ be a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence). If $X\geq0$, the [Fatou lemma](../../../measure-theory.md#fatou-s-lemma) gives $\mathbb EX_t\leq\liminf_n\mathbb EX_{t\wedge T_n}=\mathbb EX_0<\infty$. For $s\leq t$, the [Conditional Fatou lemma](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-fatou-lemma) gives

$$
\mathbb E(X_t\mid\mathcal F_s)\leq\liminf_n\mathbb E(X_{t\wedge T_n}\mid\mathcal F_s)=\liminf_nX_{s\wedge T_n}=X_s.
$$

For clarity, the [Conditional Fatou lemma](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-fatou-lemma) itself follows by setting $U_m=\inf_{n\geq m}U_n$ for nonnegative $U_n$, applying the conditional version of the [monotone convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem) to $U_m\uparrow\liminf U_n$, and observing $\mathbb E(U_m\mid\mathcal F_s)\leq\inf_{n\geq m}\mathbb E(U_n\mid\mathcal F_s)$. Hence **a nonnegative [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) is a [supermartingale](../../../martingale.md#supermartingale).**

If $|X_t|\leq C$ simultaneously for all $t$, with a deterministic $C<\infty$, the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) applied to the stopped [martingale](../../../martingale.md) identity gives, for each $A\in\mathcal F_s$,

$$
\mathbb E(\mathbf1_AX_t)=\lim_n\mathbb E(\mathbf1_AX_{t\wedge T_n})=\lim_n\mathbb E(\mathbf1_AX_{s\wedge T_n})=\mathbb E(\mathbf1_AX_s).
$$

This is exactly the [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) identity, so **a bounded [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md).** The same proof works for deterministic bounds on every finite time interval; merely having a finite random pathwise bound supplies no dominating integrable [random variable](../../../random-variable.md) and is a different convention.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

Let $U_t=(1,0,0)+(B_t^1,B_t^2,B_t^3)$, with three [independent](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) standard [Brownian motions](../../../brownian-motion.md), and set $R_t=|U_t|$ and $M_t=R_t^{-1}$. The radial [stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md) $R$ is a three-dimensional [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process), which never hits zero by the [Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#hitting-zero-classification-for-a-bessel-process). In three dimensions $u\mapsto|u|^{-1}$ is a [harmonic function](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) away from zero: direct differentiation gives $\Delta(|u|^{-1})=0$.

For the [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) $T_n=\inf\{t:R_t\notin(1/n,n)\}$, $n\geq2$, the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives

$$
M_{t\wedge T_n}=1-\sum_{j=1}^3\int_0^{t\wedge T_n}\frac{U_s^j}{R_s^3}\,dB_s^j.
$$

The coefficients are bounded by $n^2$, so each stopped [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) on every finite horizon. The [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) form a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence); as permitted, its divergence need not be proved here. Thus $M$ is a nonnegative [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale).

To see that $M$ is not a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md), integrate the three-dimensional [multivariate normal density](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-density) of $U_t$ in [spherical coordinates](../../../calculus.md#spherical-coordinate-system). The angular integral yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EM_t
&=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\int_0^\infty\left(e^{-(r-1)^2/(2t)}-e^{-(r+1)^2/(2t)}\right)dr\\
&=2\Phi(1/\sqrt t)-1<1=\mathbb EM_0\qquad(t>0),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\Phi$ is the [standard normal distribution function](../../../probability-theory.md#standard-normal-distribution-function). Therefore **the reciprocal of a three-dimensional [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) is a [strict local martingale](../../../martingale.md#strict-local-martingale).**

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

Put $\Delta B_h=B_{t+h}-B_t$ and $R_h=\int_t^{t+h}(H_s-H_t)dB_s$. The constant-in-time coefficient $H_t$ on $(t,t+h]$ is [previsible](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process), so the ratio minus $H_t$ is $R_h/\Delta B_h$. Since $|H|\leq C$, continuity and the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) imply

$$
\eta_h=\mathbb E\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+h}|H_s-H_t|^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

The [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) gives $\mathbb ER_h^2\leq h\eta_h$. For $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, use the [Chebyshev inequality](../../../probability-inequality.md#chebyshev-inequality) and the [normal distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) of $\Delta B_h/\sqrt h$ to obtain

$$
\mathbb P\left(\left|\frac{R_h}{\Delta B_h}\right|>\varepsilon\right)
\leq\mathbb P(|N(0,1)|\leq\delta)+\frac{\eta_h}{\varepsilon^2\delta^2}.
$$

First let $h\downarrow0$, then $\delta\downarrow0$; the [normal distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) has no atom at zero. This proves

$$
\boxed{\frac{\int_t^{t+h}H_s\,dB_s}{B_{t+h}-B_t}\xrightarrow[h\downarrow0]{\mathbb P}H_t.}
$$

No [independence](../../../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) between $R_h$ and the denominator is needed.

The first estimate suggested by the hint is valid: [Brownian scaling](../../../brownian-motion.md#brownian-scaling) and the [Gamma function](../../../complex-analysis.md#gamma-function) give $\mathbb E|\Delta B_h|^{-1/2}=h^{-1/4}2^{-1/4}\Gamma(1/4)/\sqrt\pi$. The second hint requires care about the placement of its [expectation](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value): with $V_h=\int_t^{t+h}(H_s-H_t)^2ds$, the interpretation $\mathbb E|R_h|^{1/2}\leq(\mathbb EV_h)^{1/4}$ is correct by the [Jensen inequality](../../../real-analysis.md#jensen-s-inequality) and the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry), whereas **the interpretation $\mathbb E|R_h|^{1/2}\leq\mathbb E(V_h^{1/4})$ is false with constant $1$**. For the latter interpretation, the [Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality](../../../martingale.md#burkholder-davis-gundy-inequalities) supplies a constant $C_{1/2}$ instead. One can see the obstruction with a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) stopped on exiting $(-1,1)$: if $\tau$ is that [Brownian exit time](../../../brownian-motion.md#brownian-exit-time), then $|B_\tau|=1$, $\mathbb E\tau=1$, and strict [Jensen inequality](../../../real-analysis.md#jensen-s-inequality) gives $\mathbb E\tau^{1/4}<1=\mathbb E|B_\tau|^{1/2}$.

This obstruction also survives the continuity condition on $H$. On a long finite interval approximate $\mathbf1_{\{s<\tau\}}$ by a continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) equal to $1$ until $\tau$ and decreasing linearly to $0$ during the next $\epsilon$ units of time, and take its initial value to be $0$ by inserting a short continuous ramp before starting the exit experiment. The [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) controls these approximations, and letting the interval length tend to infinity recovers the strict inequality above; hence some bounded continuous [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) example also violates the latter interpretation with constant $1$. The direct proof avoids relying on this ambiguous hint.

<h4 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#5/i)

Let $A=\langle M\rangle$ be the [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) and stop at $T_b=\inf\{u\geq0:A_u>b\}$. Its continuity and monotonicity imply $A_{u\wedge T_b}\leq b$ and $A_{T_b}=b$ whenever $T_b<\infty$. The standard [martingale](../../../martingale.md) isometry after stopping by bounded [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) gives $\mathbb E(M_{t\wedge T_b}^2)=\mathbb EA_{t\wedge T_b}\leq b$; in particular the stopped [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) is [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function). This criterion follows by localization and the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality), which bound the second [moment](../../../probability-theory.md#moment) of the stopped supremum by $4b$ and then permit removal of localization.

On $\{A_t\leq b\}$ one has $T_b\geq t$, so the original and stopped paths agree through time $t$. Therefore the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality) and the [Markov inequality](../../../probability-inequality.md#markov-inequality) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{s\leq t}M_s>a\right)
&\leq\mathbb P\left(\sup_{s\leq t}|M_{s\wedge T_b}|>a\right)+\mathbb P(A_t>b)\\
&\leq\frac{4b}{a^2}+\mathbb P(A_t>b).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\sup_{s\leq t}M_s>a)\leq4b/a^2+\mathbb P(\langle M\rangle_t>b).}
$$

Take the [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time)

\boxed{S_n=\inf\{t\geq0:|M_t|\geq n\},\qquad\inf\varnothing=\infty.}

Continuity gives $|M_{t\wedge S_n}|\leq n$, with no overshoot at the first crossing. Stopping preserves the [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) property, and the bounded case proved in question 4(i) makes each $M^{S_n}$ a continuous true [martingale](../../../martingale.md).

Every continuous path is bounded on each compact time interval, so $S_n\uparrow\infty$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Hence this is also a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence) consisting of bounded stopped [martingales](../../../martingale.md).

<h3 id="5/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#5/iii)

For the [dyadic power variation of a continuous local martingale](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#dyadic-power-variation-of-a-continuous-local-martingale), write $\Delta_i^nM=M_{i2^{-n}}-M_{(i-1)2^{-n}}$, $\delta_n=\max_i|\Delta_i^nM|$ and $Q_n=\sum_i(\Delta_i^nM)^2$. [Uniform continuity](../../../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity) on $[0,1]$ gives $\delta_n\to0$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), while the deterministic-partition characterization of [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) gives $Q_n\to\langle M\rangle_1$ in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability). In particular $(Q_n)$ is [bounded in probability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#boundedness-in-probability).

If $p>2$, then $Z_n\leq\delta_n^{p-2}Q_n$, and a factor tending to zero in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability) times a sequence [bounded in probability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#boundedness-in-probability) tends to zero in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability). Thus

$$
\boxed{Z_n\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb P}0\qquad(p>2).}
$$

If $1<p<2$, the reverse comparison is $Q_n\leq\delta_n^{2-p}Z_n$. The assumed finite $\limsup Z_n$ makes $Z_n$ eventually bounded [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), so $Q_n\to0$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). By [uniqueness of a limit in probability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniqueness-of-a-limit-in-probability), $\langle M\rangle_1=0$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Its monotonicity gives $\langle M\rangle_t=0$ for $t\leq1$.

To finish without assuming any integrability of $M$, apply the bound from part (i) to both $M$ and $-M$, with $t=1$, and then let $b\downarrow0$. For each $a>0$ the [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability) of a positive or negative excursion beyond $a$ is zero. Taking countably many $a\downarrow0$ proves **$M$ is indistinguishable from zero on $[0,1]$.** The same argument in fact works for every $0<p<2$ under the stated pathwise boundedness assumption.

<h3 id="5/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#5/iv)

The usual [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) convention includes $\langle M\rangle_0=0$. If $A,C$ are two continuous [finite-variation processes](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) with $A_0=C_0=0$ such that $M^2-A$ and $M^2-C$ are [continuous local martingales](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale), then $A-C$ is a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) of [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function). It must be constant, and its initial value is zero, so $A=C$ up to [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes).

Here is a proof of the fact used. Subtract the initial value of a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) $N$ of [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function), and stop so that both $|N|$ and its [total-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#total-variation-process) are bounded by deterministic constants. The stopped [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) is a bounded true [martingale](../../../martingale.md), so orthogonality of its increments gives, on any deterministic [partition of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) of $[0,t]$,

$$
\mathbb E(N_t^2)=\mathbb E\sum_i(\Delta_iN)^2\leq\mathbb E\left[\max_i|\Delta_iN|\,V_t(N)\right]\longrightarrow0.
$$

Continuity and the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) justify the last limit. Remove stopping and use continuity on a countable dense set of times to conclude $N\equiv0$.

**As literally printed, uniqueness is false without normalization.** If $A$ works then $A+c$ also works for any deterministic constant $c$, since subtracting a constant preserves the [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) property; this is a genuine omitted convention.

**With normalization, existence always holds for a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale).** Its [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) $[M]$, obtained as the limit in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability) of squared increments, is continuous, increasing, starts at zero and satisfies $M^2-[M]$ is a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale), so taking $\langle M\rangle=[M]$ supplies the required [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process).

<h3 id="6/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

Interpret the [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) in its standard extension to continuous [semimartingale](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale) integrands:

$$
\int_0^tY_s\circ dX_s=\int_0^tY_s\,dX_s+\frac12[Y,X]_t.
$$

This extension is needed because $f'(X)$ is generally a [semimartingale](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale) rather than a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). Also the introductory prose in the PDF reverses integrand and integrator relative to its displayed definition; the displayed definition fixes the convention used here.

Apply the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) to $f'(X)$, using $f\in C^3$:

$$
df'(X_s)=f''(X_s)\,dX_s+\frac12f'''(X_s)\,d\langle X\rangle_s.
$$

The continuous [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) in the second term has zero [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) with $X$, while the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) rule for an [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) gives

$$
[f'(X),X]_t=\int_0^tf''(X_s)\,d\langle X\rangle_s.
$$

A second application of the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma), now to $f(X)$, therefore yields

$$
\boxed{f(X_t)-f(X_0)=\int_0^tf'(X_s)\circ dX_s.}
$$

All unbounded coefficients are handled by stopping $X$ on compact spatial intervals, then removing the [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence).

<h3 id="6/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/ii)

Take $X=Y=B$, a standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) with $B_0=0$. The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) and $\langle B\rangle_t=t$ give

$$
\boxed{\int_0^tB_s\circ dB_s=\frac12(B_t^2-t)+\frac12t=\frac12B_t^2.}
$$

If this nonnegative [stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md), which starts from zero, were a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), question 4(i) would make it a [supermartingale](../../../martingale.md#supermartingale) and force its [expectation](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value) to be at most zero. But $\mathbb E(B_t^2/2)=t/2>0$ for $t>0$. Hence **a [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) is in general not a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale).**

<h3 id="6/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6/iii)

The limit is in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability), or more strongly in [uniform convergence on compacts in probability](../../../stochastic-process.md#uniform-convergence-on-compacts-in-probability); a pathwise limit for every [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) is not implicit. The original PDF uses $\lceil t2^n\rceil$, whereas the supplied TeX changes this to $\lfloor t2^n\rfloor$; both versions have the same limit by continuity.

Put $\delta=2^{-n}$ and $m=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor$. For the completed intervals, the algebraic identity

$$
\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{Y_{i\delta}+Y_{(i-1)\delta}}2\,(X_{i\delta}-X_{(i-1)\delta})
=\sum_{i=1}^mY_{(i-1)\delta}\Delta_iX+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m\Delta_iY\,\Delta_iX
$$

separates a left-point sum from a [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) sum.

The results from [stochastic calculus](../../../stochastic-calculus.md) used are these: predictable left-step approximations to a continuous [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) integrand converge in their [Itô integrals](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) in [uniform convergence on compacts in probability](../../../stochastic-process.md#uniform-convergence-on-compacts-in-probability); and products of increments of continuous [semimartingales](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale) on deterministic [partitions of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) with vanishing mesh converge to their [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) in the same sense. The first follows after localization from the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) and the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality), since the integrand approximations converge uniformly on compact intervals; the second is the deterministic-partition characterization of [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation).

Thus the two sums converge to $\int_0^tY_s\,dX_s$ and $\frac12\langle Y,X\rangle_t$. For the ceiling convention, at most one additional full interval extends past $t$; on $[0,T+1]$ its absolute contribution is at most $\sup_{s\leq T+1}|Y_s|$ times the modulus of continuity of $X$ at scale $\delta$, which tends to zero [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil t2^n\rceil}\frac{Y_{i2^{-n}}+Y_{(i-1)2^{-n}}}{2}\,(X_{i2^{-n}}-X_{(i-1)2^{-n}})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb P}\int_0^tY_s\circ dX_s.}
$$

<h3 id="6/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#6/iv)

The [space-time Hermite polynomials](../../../numerical-analysis.md#space-time-hermite-polynomial) obtained by scaling the [Probabilists' Hermite polynomials](../../../numerical-analysis.md#probabilists-hermite-polynomial) are genuine [polynomials](../../../polynomial.md) in both variables, including at $t=0$:

$$
H_n(x,t)=n!\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^j x^{n-2j}t^j}{2^j j!(n-2j)!}.
$$

This expansion follows by differentiating the defining Gaussian exponential, and shows that $H_n(x,0)=x^n$ extends smoothly through $t=0$. Consequently the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) is applicable from time zero. The given backward [heat equation](../../../diffusion-equation.md#heat-equation) cancels the [drift](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#drift-coefficient), and the derivative identity gives

$$
dH_n(B_t,t)=nH_{n-1}(B_t,t)\,dB_t\qquad(n\geq1).
$$

The integrand is continuous and [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process), hence [previsible](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process), and stopping the [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and time makes it bounded. Thus **$H_n(B_t,t)$ is a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) for every $n\geq0$.** The $n=0$ case is the constant $H_0=1$; in fact Gaussian [moments](../../../probability-theory.md#moment) make the displayed [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) on every finite horizon, so these are true [martingales](../../../martingale.md) as well.

The first three [Probabilists' Hermite polynomials](../../../numerical-analysis.md#probabilists-hermite-polynomial) are $h_1(x)=x$, $h_2(x)=x^2-1$ and $h_3(x)=x^3-3x$. Hence

$$
\boxed{H_1(B_t,t)=B_t,\qquad H_2(B_t,t)=B_t^2-t,\qquad H_3(B_t,t)=B_t^3-3tB_t.}
$$
