<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose the [root basis](../../../../../../fundamental-system-of-a-root-system.md)

$$
\boxed{\alpha_1=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2,\qquad
\alpha_2=\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3,\qquad
\alpha_3=\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3.}
$$

In the usual invariant [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md), each [simple root](../../../../../../simple-root.md) has squared length $2$, while $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=(\alpha_1,\alpha_3)=-1$ and $(\alpha_2,\alpha_3)=0$. The labeled [Dynkin diagram](../../../../../../dynkin-diagram.md) is therefore the following chain, with a single bond between adjacent nodes:

$$
\underset{\alpha_2}{\circ}\;\text{---}\;\underset{\alpha_1}{\circ}\;\text{---}\;\underset{\alpha_3}{\circ}.
$$

It is type $D_3$, equivalently the [A3 root system](../../../../../../a3-root-system.md). To check the [root basis](../../../../../../fundamental-system-of-a-root-system.md) explicitly, the corresponding [positive roots](../../../../../../positive-root.md) are $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_3,\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3$; these and their negatives are all twelve roots.
