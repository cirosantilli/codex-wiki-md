<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [derived series](../../../../../../derived-series.md) gives a direct test, with no structural theorem needed. By bilinearity and antisymmetry of the [Lie bracket](../../../../../../lie-bracket.md), every bracket in $\mathfrak h$ is a multiple of $z$, and $z=[x,y]$ actually occurs. Therefore

$$
\mathfrak h^{(1)}=[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]=\mathbb Cz,\qquad
\mathfrak h^{(2)}=[\mathbb Cz,\mathbb Cz]=0.
$$

Consequently this [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../../../../../../heisenberg-lie-algebra.md) is a nonzero [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md). It is an [ideal of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) in itself, since $[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h$. Its [solvable radical](../../../../../../radical-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is therefore all of $\mathfrak h$, contradicting the first criterion in part (a). Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak h\text{ is not semisimple}.}
$$
