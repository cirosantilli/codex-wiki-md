<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the [coroot](../../../../../../coroot.md) normalization $\alpha^\vee=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$, with the pairing understood via the [root system](../../../../../../root-system.md) and its invariant [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md). A [dominant integral weight](../../../../../../dominant-integral-weight.md), called a [dominant weight](../../../../../../dominant-weight.md) here, is a functional $\lambda\in\mathfrak t^*$ such that

$$
\boxed{\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\quad\text{for every simple root }\alpha_i.}
$$

Equivalently, $\lambda=\sum_i n_i\omega_i$ with $n_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$, where the [fundamental weights](../../../../../../fundamental-weight.md) satisfy $\langle\omega_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=\delta_{ij}$. If dominance and integrality are defined separately, dominance means the inequalities and membership in the [weight lattice](../../../../../../weight-lattice.md) means integrality.

A [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) is a nonzero [weight vector](../../../../../../weight-vector.md) $v\in V_\lambda$, so $h\cdot v=\lambda(h)v$ for all $h\in\mathfrak t$, annihilated by every positive [root space](../../../../../../root-space.md):

$$
\boxed{v\ne0,\quad h\cdot v=\lambda(h)v,\quad\mathfrak g_\beta\cdot v=0\quad(\beta\in\Phi^+).}
$$

The last condition is equivalently annihilation by the [raising operators](../../../../../../raising-operator.md) for the [simple roots](../../../../../../simple-root.md).
