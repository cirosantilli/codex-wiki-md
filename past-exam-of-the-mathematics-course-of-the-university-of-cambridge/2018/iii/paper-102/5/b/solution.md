<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [coroot](../../../../../../coroot.md) $\alpha^\vee$ in the pairing. The original PDF retains this mark, which is lost in the local TeX transcription. Fix any [positive root](../../../../../../positive-root.md) $\alpha$, and put $n=\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle$. For a [dominant integral weight](../../../../../../dominant-integral-weight.md), $n$ is a nonnegative integer; positive [coroots](../../../../../../coroot.md) are nonnegative integral combinations of simple [coroots](../../../../../../coroot.md).

Let $v_\lambda$ be a [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md), and restrict to the [sl2 subalgebra associated with a root](../../../../../../sl2-subalgebra-associated-with-a-root.md) $\alpha$. It is killed by its [raising operator](../../../../../../raising-operator.md) $e_\alpha$ and has $h_\alpha$ eigenvalue $n$. If $n\geq1$, its [lowering operator](../../../../../../lowering-operator.md) produces a nonzero vector: otherwise $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]v_\lambda=n v_\lambda$ would vanish. Therefore $\lambda-\alpha$ is a weight.

Choose a [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md) invariant positive-definite [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md) on the real weight space. All weights of a [minuscule representation](../../../../../../minuscule-representation.md) have the same norm, since they belong to $W\lambda$. But

$$
\|\lambda-\alpha\|^2-\|\lambda\|^2
=-2(\lambda,\alpha)+(\alpha,\alpha)
=(1-n)(\alpha,\alpha).
$$

If $n\geq2$, this is negative, a contradiction. Thus for every [positive root](../../../../../../positive-root.md),

$$
\boxed{\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\{0,1\},\quad\text{in particular }\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\leq1.}
$$

When $n=1$, the lower weight is precisely the [Weyl reflection](../../../../../../weyl-reflection.md) $s_\alpha\lambda=\lambda-\alpha$, consistent with the orbit condition.
