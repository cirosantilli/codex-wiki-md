<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [formal character of a weight module](../../../../../../formal-character-of-a-weight-module.md) is $\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_\mu(\dim V_\mu)e^\mu$, where $e^\mu$ are formal basis symbols in the [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) of the [weight lattice](../../../../../../weight-lattice.md), satisfying $e^\mu e^\nu=e^{\mu+\nu}$. For a finite-dimensional irreducible [highest-weight representation](../../../../../../highest-weight-representation.md), the [highest weight](../../../../../../highest-weight-of-a-representation.md) has [weight multiplicity](../../../../../../weight-multiplicity.md) one. Another standard result is that [weight multiplicities](../../../../../../weight-multiplicity.md) are invariant under the [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md): $\dim V_{w\mu}=\dim V_\mu$.

A [minuscule representation](../../../../../../minuscule-representation.md) has all its weights in a single [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md) orbit. Since the [highest weight](../../../../../../highest-weight-of-a-representation.md) $\lambda$ is present, that orbit is $W\lambda$, and the invariance just stated shows every point of it is present with multiplicity one. Consequently the [formal character of a minuscule representation](../../../../../../formal-character-of-a-minuscule-representation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ch}V(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in W\lambda}e^\mu,\qquad\dim V(\lambda)=|W\lambda|.}
$$

The sum is over distinct weights, rather than over all elements of $W$: summing $e^{w\lambda}$ over $w\in W$ would count each weight $|\operatorname{Stab}_W(\lambda)|$ times.
