<h1 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

At fixed [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) and [pressure](../../../../../../../pressure.md), the [atmospheric carbon-to-oxygen ratio](../../../../../../../atmospheric-carbon-to-oxygen-ratio.md) controls the division of the elemental inventory among [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md), [methane](../../../../../../../methane.md), [water](../../../../../../../water.md) and other molecules. For an oxygen-rich mixture, [water](../../../../../../../water.md) can remain after [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) consumes its share of [oxygen](../../../../../../../oxygen.md). Increasing the [atmospheric carbon-to-oxygen ratio](../../../../../../../atmospheric-carbon-to-oxygen-ratio.md) reduces the oxygen available for [water](../../../../../../../water.md), particularly in the hotter layers where [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) is stable. Near or above unity there, [water](../../../../../../../water.md) can become strongly depleted while excess [carbon](../../../../../../../carbon.md) feeds [methane](../../../../../../../methane.md), [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) and [acetylene](../../../../../../../acetylene.md). This familiar hot carbon-rich behavior should not be applied unchanged to every cool layer: at $500\,\mathrm{K}$, [methane](../../../../../../../methane.md) formation can leave substantial [water](../../../../../../../water.md) even at high [atmospheric carbon-to-oxygen ratio](../../../../../../../atmospheric-carbon-to-oxygen-ratio.md).

Increasing the [atmospheric metallicity of a giant planet](../../../../../../../atmospheric-metallicity-of-a-giant-planet.md) at fixed elemental ratios increases the heavy-element inventory relative to [molecular hydrogen](../../../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) and [helium](../../../../../../../helium.md). Abundances of [water](../../../../../../../water.md) and the major carbon-bearing molecules generally rise while [molecular hydrogen](../../../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) remains dominant. In that regime, the [law of mass action](../../../../../../../law-of-mass-action.md) for $\mathrm{CO}+\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CO}_2+\mathrm{H}_2$ gives

$$
f_{\rm CO_2}=K_p\frac{f_{\rm CO}f_{\rm H_2O}}{f_{\rm H_2}}.
$$

Where [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) and [water](../../../../../../../water.md) each scale roughly linearly with enrichment $Z$, [carbon dioxide](../../../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) consequently scales approximately as $Z^2$. This scaling changes when [molecular hydrogen](../../../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) ceases to dominate or chemical partitioning changes. Higher enrichment can also favor [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) over [methane](../../../../../../../methane.md) at fixed conditions, increase [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md), and reduce the [atmospheric scale height](../../../../../../../atmospheric-scale-height.md) and transmission-feature amplitudes. Cloud formation and [atmospheric condensate rainout](../../../../../../../atmospheric-condensate-rainout.md) alter the observable elemental ratios relative to the bulk ones.
