<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The thermal [optical depth](../../../../../../../optical-depth.md) increases inward, so an [atmospheric thermal inversion](../../../../../../../inversion-meteorology.md) requires $dT/d\tau<0$. Differentiating the [semi-grey irradiated atmosphere](../../../../../../../semi-grey-irradiated-atmosphere.md) profile gives

$$
\frac{dT^4}{d\tau}=\frac34\left[T_{\rm int}^4-f_rT_{\rm irr}^4(\gamma^2-1)e^{-\sqrt3\gamma\tau}\right].
$$

Therefore the [inversion criterion with intrinsic planetary flux](../../../../../../../inversion-criterion-with-intrinsic-planetary-flux.md) is

$$
\boxed{f_rT_{\rm irr}^4(\gamma^2-1)e^{-\sqrt3\gamma\tau}>T_{\rm int}^4}.
$$

An inverted layer exists at the top precisely when

$$
\boxed{\gamma^2>1+\frac{T_{\rm int}^4}{f_rT_{\rm irr}^4}}.
$$

For $T_{\rm int}>0$, it extends over

$$
0\leq\tau<\frac{1}{\sqrt3\gamma}\ln\left[\frac{f_rT_{\rm irr}^4(\gamma^2-1)}{T_{\rm int}^4}\right],
$$

provided the logarithm is positive. Otherwise the profile has no inverted interval. For a strongly irradiated [hot Jupiter](../../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md), $T_{\rm int}\ll T_{\rm irr}$ and the threshold is approximately $\gamma>1$; the intrinsic flux eventually restores an inward-increasing [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) at greater depth.

Large visible absorption [opacity](../../../../../../../opacity.md) deposits stellar heat high in the [exoplanet atmosphere](../../../../../../../exoplanet-atmosphere.md). Gas-phase [titanium monoxide](../../../../../../../titanium-monoxide.md) and [vanadium monoxide](../../../../../../../vanadium-ii-oxide.md) are candidate absorbers in sufficiently hot layers. An [atmospheric cold trap](../../../../../../../atmospheric-cold-trap.md) or other condensate loss can remove them, while sufficiently high [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) and vigorous mixing can help keep them in the gas. The condition concerns absorbing [opacity](../../../../../../../opacity.md): highly reflective scattering alone does not deposit the required heat.
