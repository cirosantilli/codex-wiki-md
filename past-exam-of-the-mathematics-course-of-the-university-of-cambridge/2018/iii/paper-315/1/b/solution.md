<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume an [isothermal atmosphere](../../../../../../isothermal-atmosphere.md) in [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md), with constant gravity $g$, [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) $\mu$, and an [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) equation of state. Use $h$ for the [atmospheric scale height](../../../../../../atmospheric-scale-height.md), to distinguish it from the total observable height $H$. Then

$$
\frac{dP}{dz}=-\rho g=-\frac{\mu m_Hg}{k_BT}P,\qquad h=\frac{k_BT}{\mu m_Hg},
$$

so the [cloud-top pressure in an isothermal atmosphere](../../../../../../cloud-top-pressure-in-an-isothermal-atmosphere.md) is

$$
\boxed{P_c=P_s\exp(-H_c/h)}.
$$

An opaque, approximately grey [exoplanet cloud deck](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud-deck.md) can flatten the [exoplanet transmission spectrum](../../../../../../exoplanet-transmission-spectrum.md) if the remaining gas above it has little wavelength-dependent extinction. If its top nearly reaches the observable atmospheric extent, $H_c\simeq H$, this gives $P_c\simeq P_s e^{-H/h}$. More generally, a top at $N$ [atmospheric scale heights](../../../../../../atmospheric-scale-height.md) has $P_c/P_s=e^{-N}$; illustrative values $N=5$ and $10$ give $6.7\times10^{-3}$ and $4.5\times10^{-5}$.

**A flat [exoplanet transmission spectrum](../../../../../../exoplanet-transmission-spectrum.md) and $P_s$ alone do not determine a unique cloud-top [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md).** The [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md), gravity, [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) and cloud altitude, or equivalent opacity information, are also needed. A large [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) can itself suppress spectral features, so the [exoplanet cloud deck](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud-deck.md) interpretation is conditional.
