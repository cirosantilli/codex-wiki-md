<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The time-dependent components of the [second mass moment tensor](../../../../../../../second-mass-moment-tensor.md) contain $\sin(2\Omega t)$ and $\cos(2\Omega t)$. Taking two time derivatives for the [retarded quadrupole field](../../../../../../../retarded-quadrupole-field.md) leaves that frequency unchanged. Hence the [gravitational-wave frequency](../../../../../../../gravitational-wave-frequency.md) is

$$
\boxed{\omega_{\mathrm{GW}}=2|\Omega|,\qquad
f_{\mathrm{GW}}=\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}.}
$$

Here $\omega_{\mathrm{GW}}$ is angular frequency and $f_{\mathrm{GW}}$ counts cycles per unit time. If $I_1=I_2$, the [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) is time independent, so the leading [gravitational wave](../../../../../../../gravitational-wave.md) amplitude vanishes and there is no emitted quadrupole frequency to measure.
