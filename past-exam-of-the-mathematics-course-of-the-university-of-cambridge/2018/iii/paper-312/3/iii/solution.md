<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write $\psi_0=\psi(0,\mathbf k)$. For regular [adiabatic initial conditions](../../../../../../adiabatic-initial-conditions.md) during [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), use $\Theta_0=-\psi_0/2+O(k\eta)$, $\Theta_2=O((k\eta)^2)$, and constant potentials. The dipole equation in the [neutrino Boltzmann hierarchy](../../../../../../neutrino-boltzmann-hierarchy.md) gives

$$
\dot\Theta_1=k(\Theta_0+\psi_0)-\frac{2k}{5}\Theta_2
=\frac12 k\psi_0+O(k^2\eta).
$$

Regularity removes a constant dipole, and integration yields

$$
\boxed{\Theta_1=\frac12\psi_0 k\eta+O((k\eta)^2)}.
$$

Then the quadrupole equation gives

$$
\dot\Theta_2=\frac{2k}{3}\Theta_1-\frac{3k}{7}\Theta_3
=\frac13\psi_0 k^2\eta+O(k^3\eta^2).
$$

Its regular initial value is zero, so

$$
\boxed{\Theta_2=\frac16\psi_0(k\eta)^2+O((k\eta)^3)}.
$$

For the usual regular growing solution, the monopole has no linear correction and parity improves the dipole and quadrupole errors to $O((k\eta)^3)$ and $O((k\eta)^4)$, respectively. The weaker bounds above already follow from the assumptions explicitly given. Although the quadrupole is small on [superhorizon scales](../../../../../../superhorizon-scale.md), the trace-free [Einstein field equations](../../../../../../einstein-field-equations.md) divide its stress by $k^2$, allowing a finite leading potential difference.
