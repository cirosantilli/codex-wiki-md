<h1 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For one [Fourier mode](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md), the trace-free differential operator in the [Einstein field equations](../../../../../../einstein-field-equations.md) is $-k^2Q_{ij}$, with $Q_{ij}=\hat k_i\hat k_j-\delta_{ij}/3$. Part (ii) gives $\Pi_{ij}=(4/5)\bar\rho_\nu\Theta_2Q_{ij}$. The two minus signs in the field equation therefore imply

$$
k^2(\phi-\psi)=\frac{32\pi G}{5}a^2\bar\rho_\nu\Theta_2.
$$

Insert $\Theta_2=\psi_0 k^2\eta^2/6$. During [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), $a\propto\eta$, $\mathcal H=1/\eta$, and the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) gives $8\pi Ga^2\bar\rho=3\mathcal H^2$. Thus

$$
\phi_0-\psi_0=\frac{16\pi G}{15}a^2\bar\rho_\nu\eta^2\psi_0
=\frac25 f_\nu\psi_0,
\qquad
\boxed{\phi_0=\left(1+\frac25f_\nu\right)\psi_0}.
$$

These are the [radiation-era adiabatic potentials with free-streaming neutrinos](../../../../../../radiation-era-adiabatic-potentials-with-free-streaming-neutrinos.md); photons are taken to have negligible [scalar anisotropic stress](../../../../../../scalar-anisotropic-stress.md).

To normalize the potentials, use $\bar P=\bar\rho/3$. The denominator in the [comoving curvature perturbation](../../../../../../comoving-curvature-perturbation.md) becomes

$$
4\pi Ga^2(\bar\rho+\bar P)=\frac{16\pi G}{3}a^2\bar\rho=2\mathcal H^2.
$$

With $\dot\phi=0$, the conserved curvature therefore obeys $\mathcal R=-\phi_0-\psi_0/2$. Combining it with the potential ratio gives

$$
\boxed{\psi_0(\mathbf k)=-\frac{10}{15+4f_\nu}\mathcal R(\mathbf k),\qquad
\phi_0(\mathbf k)=-\frac{10+4f_\nu}{15+4f_\nu}\mathcal R(\mathbf k)}.
$$

As a sign and normalization check, $f_\nu=0$ gives $\phi_0=\psi_0=-2\mathcal R/3$. Increasing the free-streaming radiation fraction increases $\phi_0/\psi_0$ while preserving the supplied curvature convention. The TeX's malformed definition of $\mathcal H$ is restored by the PDF: $\mathcal H=\dot a/a$.
