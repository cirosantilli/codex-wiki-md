<h1 id="3/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

In the regular ingoing chart, the [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) is $k=\partial_v$, with $k^2=-V$. The normal covector to $r=b=r_+$ is $dr$; raising its index gives $\nabla r=\partial_v+V\partial_r=k$ at $r=b$. Thus $k$ is null and normal to that hypersurface, which is a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../killing-horizon.md). It is regular because $R^2(b)=b(b-a)>0$ when $a=r_-<b$.

The defining identity for [surface gravity](../../../../../../surface-gravity.md) on a Killing horizon is $\nabla_a(k^2)=-2\kappa k_a$. Here $k_a=-V(dv)_a+(dr)_a$, so at the horizon this becomes $-V'(b)(dr)_a=-2\kappa(dr)_a$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\frac12V'(r_+)=\frac1{2r_+}.}
$$

This uses the asymptotic normalization $k^2\to-1$. A constant rescaling of $k$ rescales the surface gravity. As a check, the horizon area is $A_H=4\pi r_+(r_+-r_-)$ and $\kappa A_H/(4\pi)=(r_+-r_-)/2=M_{\rm Komar}(r_+)$. The formula is being applied only to the regular strict-inequality case.
