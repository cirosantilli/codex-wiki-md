<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a [Kerr black hole](../../../../../../kerr-black-hole.md), the future horizon generator is $\xi=t+\Omega_H\varphi$. A future-directed causal [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) $p$ and a future-directed null vector $\xi$ satisfy $g(p,\xi)\le0$ in the $(-+++)$ convention. The conserved quantities from the [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) are $E=-p\cdot t$ and $L=p\cdot\varphi$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E-\Omega_HL=-p\cdot\xi\ge0.}
$$

For a massive particle the inequality is strict. Equality for a null particle requires momentum parallel to the horizon generator and describes the limiting tangent case. In a [Penrose process](../../../../../../penrose-process.md) the absorbed particle may have $E<0$; for $\Omega_H>0$ this requires sufficiently negative angular momentum.

In the nonextremal test-particle regime, the [Physical-process first law for a rotating black hole](../../../../../../physical-process-first-law-for-a-rotating-black-hole.md) identifies $\delta M=E$ and $\delta J=L$, so

$$
E-\Omega_HL=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi G}\delta A\ge0
$$

by the [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md). The usual hypotheses of that area law, including the [null energy condition](../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and the required global regularity conditions, are understood. This thermodynamic derivation is first order in the capture process; the causal [horizon flux inequality for causal matter](../../../../../../horizon-flux-inequality-for-causal-matter.md) also applies to the extremal horizon without dividing by $\kappa$.
