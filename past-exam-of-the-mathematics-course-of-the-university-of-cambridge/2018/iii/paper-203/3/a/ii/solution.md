The convention used for planar [Gaussian free field](../../../../../../../gaussian-free-field.md) theory is the [Dirichlet energy space](../../../../../../../dirichlet-energy-space.md):

$$
\boxed{H_0^1(D)=\overline{C_c^\infty(D;\mathbb R)}^{\ \|\cdot\|_\nabla},\qquad
\|f\|_\nabla^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_D|\nabla f|^2.}
$$

It is a real [Hilbert space](../../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) with the extended [Dirichlet inner product](../../../../../../../dirichlet-inner-product.md). A [conformal map](../../../../../../../conformal-map.md) from the [unit disc](../../../../../../../unit-disc.md) onto $D$ identifies the two energy completions, so local representatives can be understood through the disc's zero-boundary space.

For an arbitrary unbounded domain this is the homogeneous energy completion; it should be distinguished from completion in the inhomogeneous norm $\|f\|_{L^2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^2}$ used in another standard definition of the [zero-boundary Sobolev space](../../../../../../../zero-boundary-sobolev-space.md). On bounded domains with a [Poincaré inequality](../../../../../../../poincare-inequality.md), the two norms are equivalent.
