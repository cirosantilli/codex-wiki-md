<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose a [very ample divisor](../../../../../../very-ample-divisor.md) $A$ such that $A-D$ has a nonzero [global section](../../../../../../global-section.md); this is possible by taking a sufficiently high ample twist. Since $X$ is integral, multiplication by its $m$th power injects $H^0(X,mD)$ into $H^0(X,mA)$. By [Serre vanishing](../../../../../../serre-vanishing.md) and the [Hilbert polynomial](../../../../../../hilbert-polynomial.md), $h^0(X,mA)=O(m^n)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{h^0(X,mD)\leq C m^n\quad(m\gg0).}
$$

This is the [polynomial bound for sections of a fixed divisor](../../../../../../polynomial-bound-for-sections-of-a-fixed-divisor.md). The same proof works for a [coherent sheaf](../../../../../../coherent-sheaf.md) of support dimension $d$, by choosing the multiplying section to avoid its [associated points](../../../../../../associated-point-of-a-coherent-sheaf.md); the bound is then $O(m^d)$.
