<h1 id="2/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The relevant invariant of a divisor is its [Iitaka dimension](../../../../../../../iitaka-dimension.md), also called its Kodaira dimension. If two linearly independent [global sections](../../../../../../../global-section.md) $s,t$ occur in $H^0(X,nH)$, their ratio is a nonconstant [rational function](../../../../../../../rational-function.md). The sections $s^q,s^{q-1}t,\ldots,t^q$ are linearly independent, because a nonconstant rational function over an algebraically closed field is transcendental over that field. Thus some [complete linear system of a divisor](../../../../../../../complete-linear-system-of-a-divisor.md) has positive-dimensional image, contradicting $\kappa(H)=0$.

Consequently $h^0(X,nH)=1$ for every $n>0$; the nonemptiness of $|H|$ supplies a nonzero section in every multiple. Its unique divisor is entirely fixed. Hence **every movable part $M_n$ is zero**.
