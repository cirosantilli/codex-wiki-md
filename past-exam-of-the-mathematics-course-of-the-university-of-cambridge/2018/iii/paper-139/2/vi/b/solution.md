<h1 id="2/vi/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The strict statement as printed needs the qualification **if $F\ne0$**. Part (a) already shows $F^2=0$ in the present situation, and the next part proves $F=0$; an unconditional $F^2<0$ would contradict that conclusion.

To prove the needed conditional statement, suppose $F\ne0$. A [fixed part](../../../../../../../fixed-part-of-a-linear-system.md) has $h^0(X,F)=1$. Indeed, a different $F'\in|F|$ has smaller multiplicity along some component of $F$; otherwise $F'-F$ would be a nonzero effective linearly trivial divisor. Adding a member of the [movable part](../../../../../../../movable-part-of-a-linear-system.md) avoiding that component contradicts fixedness.

The [Hodge index theorem for algebraic surfaces](../../../../../../../hodge-index-theorem-for-algebraic-surfaces.md), together with $D\cdot F=0$ and $D^2=0$, gives $F^2\leq0$. If equality held, [Riemann–Roch theorem for algebraic surfaces](../../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem-for-algebraic-surfaces.md) would give $\chi(X,F)=2$, and [Serre duality](../../../../../../../serre-duality.md) would give $h^2(X,F)=h^0(X,-F)=0$. Hence $h^0(X,F)=2+h^1(X,F)\geq2$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ F^2<0.}
$$

This is the [fixed-part elimination on a K3 surface](../../../../../../../fixed-part-elimination-on-a-k3-surface.md) argument.
