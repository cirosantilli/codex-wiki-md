<h1 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Choose an [ample divisor](../../../../../../ample-cartier-divisor.md) $A$. The [Hodge index theorem for algebraic surfaces](../../../../../../hodge-index-theorem-for-algebraic-surfaces.md) implies $D\cdot A>0$: if it were zero, $D^2=0$ would force the [numerical class](../../../../../../real-numerical-divisor-classes.md) of $D$ to be zero. Consequently $-D$ cannot be effective, because every nonzero [effective divisor](../../../../../../effective-cartier-divisor.md) has positive intersection with $A$, while $-D\cdot A<0$.

The [Riemann–Roch theorem for algebraic surfaces](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem-for-algebraic-surfaces.md) and the preceding computation give $\chi(X,\mathcal O_X(D))=2+D^2/2=2$. By [Serre duality](../../../../../../serre-duality.md), $h^2(X,D)=h^0(X,-D)=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{h^0(X,D)=2+h^1(X,D)\geq2.}
$$
