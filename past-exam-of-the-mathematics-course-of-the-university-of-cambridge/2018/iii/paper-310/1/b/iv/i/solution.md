<h1 id="1/b/iv/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

First perturb the radius consistently with the conserved string normalization. If $a_E\mapsto a_E(1+\epsilon)$ while $C_s=\rho_sa^2$ stays at its static value, the density becomes

$$
\rho_E\mapsto\rho_E(1+\epsilon)^{-2}=\rho_E(1-2\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)).
$$

The [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) still has an exact cancellation between the string and curvature terms, and the [Friedmann acceleration equation](../../../../../../../../friedmann-acceleration-equation.md) still vanishes. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{A radius displacement at fixed }C_s\text{ moves to another static solution.}}
$$

The [homogeneous stability of a static cosmic-string universe](../../../../../../../../homogeneous-stability-of-a-static-cosmic-string-universe.md) is neutral in this restricted sense: there is no restoring force and no exponentially growing displacement. Holding the density fixed while changing the radius would instead change $C_s$ and would not be this pure displacement perturbation.
