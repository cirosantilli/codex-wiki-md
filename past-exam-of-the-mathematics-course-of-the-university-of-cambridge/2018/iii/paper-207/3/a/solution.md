<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $Y(1)$ and $Y(0)$ be the [potential outcomes](../../../../../../potential-outcome.md) for the same individual under well-defined interventions setting the binary [exposure](../../../../../../exposure.md) to 1 and 0. The [average treatment effect](../../../../../../average-treatment-effect.md), also called the average [causal effect](../../../../../../causal-effect.md) on the additive scale, is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ACE}=\mathbb E[Y(1)-Y(0)]=\mathbb E[Y(1)]-\mathbb E[Y(0)].}
$$

The average is over the target population. This compares hypothetical outcomes under two interventions, rather than the observed difference $\mathbb E[Y\mid X=1]-\mathbb E[Y\mid X=0]$, which can be distorted by [confounding](../../../../../../confounding.md). For binary $Y$, the displayed contrast is a causal risk difference. [Consistency in causal inference](../../../../../../consistency-in-causal-inference.md) and a well-defined intervention, including an appropriate no-[interference in causal inference](../../../../../../interference-in-causal-inference.md) assumption, give these [potential outcomes](../../../../../../potential-outcome.md) their meaning.
