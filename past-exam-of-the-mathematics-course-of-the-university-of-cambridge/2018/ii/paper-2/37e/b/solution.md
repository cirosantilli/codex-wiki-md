<h1 id="37e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For [pressureless matter](../../../../../../pressureless-matter.md), $w=0$ and part (a) gives $\rho=3\mu/(8\pi a^3)$. With $\Lambda=0$, the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) becomes

$$
\dot a^2+k=\frac{\mu}{a},
$$

or

$$
\boxed{\dot a^2+V(a)=C,\qquad
V(a)=-\frac{\mu}{a},\qquad C=-k.}
$$

This is the $\Lambda=0$ case of the [Friedmann effective potential for pressureless matter](../../../../../../friedmann-effective-potential-for-pressureless-matter.md). The potential rises monotonically from $-\infty$ at $a=0$ to zero from below as $a\to\infty$.

For $k=+1$, $C=-1$ intersects the potential at $a_{\max}=\mu$. An initially expanding [closed matter-dominated Friedmann solution](../../../../../../closed-matter-dominated-friedmann-solution.md) reaches this [turning point](../../../../../../turning-point.md) and recollapses to a [Big Crunch](../../../../../../big-crunch.md). For $k=0$, $C=0$ gives expansion for all time with $\dot a\to0$ and $a\propto t^{2/3}$. For $k=-1$, $C=1$ gives eternal expansion with $\dot a\to1$ in these units. Thus **positive curvature recollapses, whereas zero and negative curvature expand forever**.
