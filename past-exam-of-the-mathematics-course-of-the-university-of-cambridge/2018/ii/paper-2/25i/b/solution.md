<h1 id="25i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a unit-speed curve with nonzero [curvature of a space curve](../../../../../../curvature-of-a-space-curve.md) $\kappa$, define the [Frenet frame](../../../../../../frenet-frame.md)

$$
T=\gamma',
\qquad N=\frac{T'}{\kappa},
\qquad B=T\times N.
$$

The [torsion of a space curve](../../../../../../torsion-of-a-curve.md) is

$$
\boxed{\tau=-B'\mathbin\cdot N}
$$

with this choice of convention.

For the requested example, take the unit-speed circle

$$
\gamma_1(s)=(\cos s,\sin s,0)
$$

and the unit-speed [circular helix](../../../../../../circular-helix.md)

$$
\gamma_2(s)=
\left(\frac12\cos(\sqrt2s),
\frac12\sin(\sqrt2s),
\frac{s}{\sqrt2}\right).
$$

Both have the constant nonzero curvature $\kappa=1$, but their torsions are respectively $0$ and $1$. A [proper Euclidean motion of Euclidean three-space](../../../../../../proper-euclidean-motion-of-euclidean-three-space.md) preserves torsion, so no such rigid motion relates the two curves.
