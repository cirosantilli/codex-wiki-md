<h1 id="25i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Fix $t_0\in[a,b]$ and define

$$
s(t)=\int_{t_0}^t\lVert\gamma'(u)\rVert,du.
$$

Because $\gamma$ is a [regular curve](../../../../../../regular-curve.md), $s'(t)=\lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert>0$. The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) gives a smooth inverse $t=t(s)$ onto the corresponding interval. For $\widetilde\gamma(s)=\gamma(t(s))$,

$$
\left\lVert\frac{d\widetilde\gamma}{ds}\right\rVert
=\lVert\gamma'(t(s))\rVert\frac{dt}{ds}=1.
$$

Thus the [arc-length parametrization](../../../../../../arc-length-parametrization.md) exists.
