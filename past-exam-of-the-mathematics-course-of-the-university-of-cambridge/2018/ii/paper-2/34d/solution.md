<h1 id="34d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [34D](../34d.md)

The [intrinsic parity](../../../../../intrinsic-parity.md) $\eta$ of a particle is the [parity](../../../../../parity.md) eigenvalue of its one-particle state at rest:

$$
\mathcal P|\mathbf0,s,m\rangle
=\eta|\mathbf0,s,m\rangle.
$$

For the particles in this question, $\eta=\pm1$. A two-particle state has [multiparticle parity](../../../../../multiparticle-parity.md) $\eta_1\eta_2(-1)^\ell$, where $\ell$ is its relative [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md).

The pionic-deuterium [ground state](../../../../../ground-state.md) has atomic $\ell=0$. Since the pion has spin zero, its total angular momentum is therefore the deuteron spin, $J=1$, and its parity is $\eta_d\eta_\pi=\eta_\pi$. The two neutrons are identical [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md). Their antisymmetric [spin-one-half singlet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-singlet-state.md) would require even orbital $\ell$, but $S=0$ and $J=1$ would instead require $\ell=1$. Hence they must occupy the symmetric [spin-one-half triplet state](../../../../../spin-one-half-triplet-state.md), so the orbital state is antisymmetric and $\ell$ is odd. The [addition of angular momentum](../../../../../addition-of-angular-momentum.md) with $S=J=1$ permits $\ell=0,1,2$, leaving $\ell=1$. Thus parity conservation gives

$$
\eta_\pi
=\eta_n^2(-1)^1
=-1,
$$

so **the pion has intrinsic parity $\boxed{\eta_\pi=-1}$**.

In $\Delta^-\to\pi^-+n$, the final total spin is $S=1/2$. Coupling it to $\ell$ to obtain $J=3/2$ gives

$$
\left|\ell-\frac12\right|
\leq\frac32
\leq\ell+\frac12,
$$

so **the allowed orbital angular momenta are $\boxed{\ell=1,2}$**. The observed angular probability is $\cos^2\theta=|P_1(\cos\theta)|^2$, where $P_1$ is a [Legendre polynomial](../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md); it therefore selects the $\ell=1$ partial wave rather than $\ell=2$. The [parity selection rule for a two-body decay](../../../../../parity-selection-rule-for-a-two-body-decay.md) now gives

$$
\eta_{\Delta^-}
=\eta_\pi\eta_n(-1)^\ell
=(-1)(+1)(-1)
=+1.
$$

Therefore **the delta particle has intrinsic parity $\boxed{\eta_{\Delta^-}=+1}$**.
