<h1 id="20g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-163})$, the [fundamental discriminant](../../../../../../fundamental-discriminant.md) is $-163$ and the [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is

$$
\frac2\pi\sqrt{163}<9.
$$

Thus every ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm at most eight.

The [ring of integers of a quadratic field](../../../../../../ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field.md) is $\mathbb Z[\theta]$ for $\theta=(1+\sqrt{-163})/2$, whose minimal polynomial is $X^2-X+41$. Its discriminant $-163$ is a quadratic nonresidue modulo each of $3,5,$ and $7$; directly modulo two, $X^2+X+1$ has no root. Hence these primes are inert by the [splitting of rational primes in a quadratic field](../../../../../../splitting-of-rational-primes-in-a-quadratic-field.md). The only prime ideal over one of them with norm at most eight is $(2)$, which has norm four and is principal. Unique [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) now makes every ideal of norm at most eight principal. The Minkowski bound therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\ \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-163}))=1.\ }
$$

This is the [Ideal class group of Q of square root of minus one hundred sixty-three](../../../../../../ideal-class-group-of-q-of-square-root-of-minus-one-hundred-sixty-three.md).
