<h1 id="24i/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Define

$$
\alpha:E\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}^0(E),
\qquad
\alpha(P)=[P-Q].
$$

If $D$ is a [divisor class](../../../../../../divisor-class.md) of degree zero, [Riemann--Roch](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) gives $\ell(D+Q)=1$, because $K_E\sim0$ and the divisor $-D-Q$ has negative degree. A nonzero section therefore yields

$$
D+Q\sim P
$$

for some point $P$, proving that $\alpha$ is surjective. If $\alpha(P)=\alpha(P')$, then $P-P'$ is a [principal divisor](../../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md). For $P\ne P'$ its defining function would have one simple pole and give a degree-one map $E\to\mathbb P^1$, impossible for a genus-one curve. Thus $P=P'$. The map and its inverse are regular, so this proves the [Abel-Jacobi map of a genus-one curve](../../../../../../abel-jacobi-map-of-a-genus-one-curve.md) is an isomorphism.

Transport the addition of the [divisor class group](../../../../../../divisor-class-group.md) through $\alpha$: define $P\boxplus R$ by

$$
[P\boxplus R-Q]=[P-Q]+[R-Q].
$$

Then $Q$ is the identity, and this is the [chord-and-tangent group law](../../../../../../chord-and-tangent-group-law.md) on the plane cubic.

For the displayed equation, inversion is $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$. Thus $P\boxplus P=Q$ exactly when $P=-P$. Besides the point at infinity $Q$, this requires $y=0$. Therefore the complete set of two-torsion points is

$$
\boxed{Q,\quad(\lambda_1,0),\quad(\lambda_2,0),\quad(\lambda_3,0).}
$$
