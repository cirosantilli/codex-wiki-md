<h1 id="26j/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

First intersect $E$ with a sufficiently large bounded interval to obtain a finite positive-measure set. By the [Lebesgue density theorem](../../../../../../lebesgue-s-density-theorem.md), this set has a density-one point $x$. Hence, for every $\varepsilon>0$, some sufficiently small interval $I=(a,b)$ centred at $x$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{m(E\cap(a,b))>(1-\varepsilon)(b-a),}
$$

which is the [high-density interval in a positive-measure subset of the real line](../../../../../../high-density-interval-in-a-positive-measure-subset-of-the-real-line.md).

To obtain the final claim, choose $\varepsilon<1/2$, put $L=b-a$, and translate $A=E\cap(a,b)$ into $[0,L]$. Write

$$
m(A)=\frac L2+\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha>\left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)L>0.
$$

Part (f), after rescaling the interval length, gives

$$
(-2\alpha,2\alpha)\subseteq A-A\subseteq E-E.
$$

Thus **$E-E$ contains an open interval around zero**, which is the [Steinhaus theorem](../../../../../../steinhaus-theorem.md).
