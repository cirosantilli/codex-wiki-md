<h1 id="26j/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [rational numbers](../../../../../../rational-number.md) are countable, so

$$
\mathbb Q=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q}\{q\}
$$

is a countable union of closed sets and hence a [Borel set](../../../../../../borel-set.md); its [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) is zero. Its complement is also Borel and has infinite measure:

$$
\boxed{m(\mathbb Q)=0,\qquad m(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q)=\infty.}
$$

The intervals are nested in the relevant directions, and the strongest lower and upper restrictions both occur at $n=2$:

$$
\bigcap_{n\geq2}[1/n,n]=[1/2,2].
$$

Thus this set is closed, hence Borel, and

$$
\boxed{m\left(\bigcap_{n\geq2}[1/n,n]\right)=\frac32.}
$$
