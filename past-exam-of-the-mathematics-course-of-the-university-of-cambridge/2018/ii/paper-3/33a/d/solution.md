<h1 id="33a/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Put $q=u/x^2$, so $F=xG(q)$ and

$$
F_x=G(q)-2qG'(q),
\qquad
F_u=\frac1xG'(q).
$$

Substitution into the [Lie-symmetry determining equation for a first-order ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../lie-symmetry-determining-equation-for-a-first-order-ordinary-differential-equation.md) and separation with respect to the arbitrary function $G$ gives

$$
\xi_u=0,\qquad
2q\xi-\frac\eta x=0,\qquad
\eta_x=0,\qquad
x(\eta_u-\xi_x)-\xi=0.
$$

These equations imply $\xi=Cx$ and $\eta=2Cu$. Apart from the zero generator, the common symmetry for arbitrary $G$ is therefore generated by

$$
\boxed{V=x\partial_x+2u\partial_u.}
$$

Its [one-parameter group](../../../../../../one-parameter-subgroup.md) is the [scaling symmetry](../../../../../../scaling-symmetry.md)

$$
\boxed{(x,u)\longmapsto(e^s x,e^{2s}u),}
$$

which leaves $u/x^2$ invariant. Special choices of $G$ can have additional symmetries, but this is the complete symmetry valid for an arbitrary smooth $G$.
