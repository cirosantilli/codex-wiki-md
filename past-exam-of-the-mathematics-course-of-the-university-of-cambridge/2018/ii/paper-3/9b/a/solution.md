<h1 id="9b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the very early universe, assume species $i$ is in [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../../thermal-equilibrium.md), is [ultrarelativistic](../../../../../../ultrarelativistic-particle.md) so that $kT\gg m_ic^2$ and $E(p)\simeq pc$, and has negligible [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md), $\mu_i\simeq0$. Its occupation factor is then the [Bose-Einstein distribution](../../../../../../bose-einstein-distribution.md) for a boson or the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) for a fermion.

Substitute $E=pc$ and set $y=pc/(kT)$. Since $p=(kT/c)y$ and $dp=(kT/c)\,dy$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_i
&=\frac{4\pi g_i}{h^3}\int_0^\infty
\frac{pc\,p^2\,dp}{e^{pc/(kT)}\mp1}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{4\pi g_i}{(hc)^3}(kT)^4
\int_0^\infty\frac{y^3}{e^y\mp1}\,dy}.
\end{aligned}
$$
