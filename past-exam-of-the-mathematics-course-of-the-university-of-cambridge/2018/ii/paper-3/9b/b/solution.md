<h1 id="9b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a bosonic degree of freedom, the supplied integral gives

$$
I_B=\int_0^\infty\frac{y^3}{e^y-1}\,dy=\frac{\pi^4}{15}.
$$

For a fermionic degree of freedom, use

$$
\frac1{e^y+1}=\frac1{e^y-1}-\frac2{e^{2y}-1}.
$$

Changing variable $u=2y$ in the second integral gives

$$
I_F=I_B-\frac18I_B=\frac78I_B.
$$

This is the energy-density case of the [fermion-to-boson thermal integral ratio](../../../../../../fermion-to-boson-thermal-integral-ratio.md).

Summing the result of part (a) over all particle species therefore gives

$$
\epsilon
=\frac{4\pi g_*}{(hc)^3}(kT)^4\frac{\pi^4}{15}
=\boxed{\frac{4\pi^5}{15(hc)^3}g_*(kT)^4},
$$

where the [effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom](../../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-energy-degrees-of-freedom.md) is

$$
\boxed{g_*=\sum_{\rm bosons}g_i+\frac78\sum_{\rm fermions}g_i.}
$$
