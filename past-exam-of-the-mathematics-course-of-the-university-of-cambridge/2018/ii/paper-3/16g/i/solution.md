<h1 id="16g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The class of finite groups is **not first-order axiomatizable**. If $S$ axiomatized it, add constants $c_1,c_2,\ldots$ and all inequalities $c_i\ne c_j$. Every finite fragment has a sufficiently large finite cyclic group as a model. The [compactness theorem](../../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) would then give an infinite group satisfying $S$, contradicting the definition of the class. This is the group instance of the fact that [finite structures are not first-order axiomatizable](../../../../../../finite-structures-are-not-first-order-axiomatizable.md).
