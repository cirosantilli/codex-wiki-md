<h1 id="13c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Integrating the continuity equation over state space and applying the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) gives

$$
\frac d{dt}\int P\,dV
=-\int\nabla\cdot\mathbf J\,dV
=-\int_{\partial V}\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf S.
$$

Assume the [probability density function](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) and its derivatives decay sufficiently rapidly at infinity, or impose a [zero-flux boundary condition](../../../../../../zero-flux-boundary-condition.md). The boundary integral then vanishes, so $\int P\,dV$ is constant. Since it is initially one, **the distribution remains normalized for all time.**
