<h1 id="13c/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For

$$
P(\mathbf x)=Z^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac12D_{ij}x_ix_j\right)
$$

with symmetric $D$,

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_j}=-D_{jk}x_kP.
$$

Substitution into the current from part (d) yields

$$
J_i=\left(a_{ik}+\frac12b_{ij}D_{jk}\right)x_kP.
$$

It vanishes for all $\mathbf x$ exactly when

$$
\boxed{a+\frac12bD=0,\qquad D=-2b^{-1}a.}
$$

Thus the stated [multivariate normal distribution](../../../../../../multivariate-normal-distribution.md) is a stationary solution whenever the zero-current assumption makes $D$ symmetric positive definite. This is the [Stationary Gaussian density for a linear Fokker-Planck equation](../../../../../../stationary-gaussian-density-for-a-linear-fokker-planck-equation.md).
