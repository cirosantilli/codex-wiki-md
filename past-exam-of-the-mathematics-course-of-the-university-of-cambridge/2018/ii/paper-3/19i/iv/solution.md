<h1 id="19i/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $|G|=pq$ with primes $p>q$ and $G$ nonabelian. The [Sylow theorems](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) give $n_p\mid q$ and $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$, so $n_p=1$ and the Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is normal. Also $n_q\mid p$ and $n_q\equiv1\pmod q$. If $n_q=1$, both Sylow subgroups are normal and $G$ is their abelian direct product, a contradiction. Hence $n_q=p$, and

$$
\boxed{q\mid p-1.}
$$

Because $G/P$ is abelian, the [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) $G'$ lies in $P$. It is nontrivial because $G$ is nonabelian, and $|P|=p$ is prime, so $G'=P$. Therefore

$$
|G/G'|=q,
$$

and $G$ has exactly $q$ [linear characters](../../../../../../linear-character.md).

Every nonlinear irreducible degree divides $pq$. Its square is at most $pq$, so the only possibility is degree $q$. If there are $b$ such characters, the [sum of squares of irreducible degrees](../../../../../../sum-of-squares-of-irreducible-degrees.md) gives

$$
pq=q+bq^2,
\qquad
b=\frac{p-1}{q}.
$$

The number of irreducible characters equals the number of conjugacy classes, so

$$
\boxed{k(G)=q+\frac{p-1}{q}.}
$$

These are the [irreducible characters of a nonabelian group of order p q](../../../../../../irreducible-characters-of-a-nonabelian-group-of-order-p-q.md).
