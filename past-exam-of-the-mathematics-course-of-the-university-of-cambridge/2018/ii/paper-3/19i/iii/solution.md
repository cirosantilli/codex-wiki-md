<h1 id="19i/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose a finite simple group had an irreducible representation $\rho$ of degree two. Its kernel is normal and cannot be the whole group, so $\rho$ is faithful. A nonabelian simple group has no nontrivial linear character; consequently $\det\rho$ is trivial and $\rho(G)\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$.

Since $2\mid|G|$ by degree divisibility, [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) gives an involution $x\in G$. A nonidentity matrix of order two in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$ has both eigenvalues $-1$, hence equals $-I$. Thus $\rho(x)=-I$ is central in $\rho(G)$. Faithfulness makes $x$ central in $G$, contradicting the trivial centre of a nonabelian simple group. Therefore **no finite simple group has an irreducible character of degree two.**
