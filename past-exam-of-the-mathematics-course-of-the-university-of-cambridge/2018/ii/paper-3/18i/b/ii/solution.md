↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For either $\alpha_j$, the same permutation-of-factors argument shows that every coefficient of $f(t,\alpha_j)$ lies in $L^G$.

They need not generate the whole fixed field. Assume $\operatorname{char}K\ne2$, choose $a_1,a_2\in K$ with independent nonsquare classes, and put

$$
L=K(\sqrt{a_1},\sqrt{a_2}).
$$

Let $G=\{1,\sigma\}$, where

$$
\sigma(\sqrt{a_1})=-\sqrt{a_1},
\qquad
\sigma(\sqrt{a_2})=-\sqrt{a_2}.
$$

Then

$$
f(t,\sqrt{a_1})=t^2-a_1,
\qquad
f(t,\sqrt{a_2})=t^2-a_2,
$$

so all their coefficients generate only $K$. However $\sqrt{a_1a_2}$ is fixed by $\sigma$, and the degree count in part (a) shows

$$
\boxed{L^G=K(\sqrt{a_1a_2})\supsetneq K.}
$$

Thus [separate orbit polynomials can miss mixed invariants](../../../../../../../separate-orbit-polynomials-can-miss-mixed-invariants.md).
