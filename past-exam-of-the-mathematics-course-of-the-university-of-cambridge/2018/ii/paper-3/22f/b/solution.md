<h1 id="22f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $q$ be the [Holder conjugate exponent](../../../../../../conjugate-exponents.md) of $p$. On $\mathbb R^n$ with Lebesgue measure, the [duality of Lp spaces](../../../../../../duality-of-lp-spaces.md) is

$$
\boxed{(L^p)^*\cong L^q\qquad(1\leq p<\infty),}
$$

under the pairing $\Lambda_g(f)=\int fg$. Thus $(L^1)^*=L^\infty$, while for $1<p<\infty$ the dual exponent satisfies $1/p+1/q=1$.

A Banach space is [reflexive](../../../../../../reflexive-banach-space.md) when its canonical map into its bidual is surjective. The spaces $L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ are reflexive exactly for

$$
\boxed{1<p<\infty.}
$$

For these exponents, applying $L^p$ duality twice identifies the bidual with $L^p$. The endpoint spaces $L^1$ and $L^\infty$ are not reflexive; for example, disjoint unit cubes give closed copies of $\ell^1$ and $\ell^\infty$, respectively.

Integration gives an isometric embedding $L^1\hookrightarrow(L^\infty)^*$, but it is not onto. On the subspace

$$
V=\mathbb C1\oplus C_0(\mathbb R^n)\subset L^\infty,
$$

define $\Lambda(c+h)=c$, the limit at infinity. Since $|\Lambda(v)|\leq\lVert v\rVert_\infty$, the [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../../../../hahn-banach-theorem.md) extends $\Lambda$ to all of $L^\infty$. If this extension were integration against some $g\in L^1$, its vanishing on every compactly supported bounded function would force $g=0$ almost everywhere, but $\Lambda(1)=1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(L^\infty)^*\ne L^1.}
$$

This is the [Dual of L infinity is larger than L1](../../../../../../dual-of-l-infinity-is-larger-than-l1.md).
