<h1 id="37e/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $\Delta r=r_2-r_1$. Expanding the quotient $S/T$ consistently to first order gives

$$
v_{\rm eff}
=1+\frac{M}{r_2}
-\frac{M}{\Delta r}\log\frac{r_2}{r_1}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm eff}
=1+\frac{M}{r_2}
-\frac{M}{r_2-r_1}\log\frac{r_2}{r_1}}.
$$

Because $\log(r_2/r_1)/(r_2-r_1)\to1/r_2$ as $r_1\to r_2$,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{r_1\to r_2}v_{\rm eff}=1}.
$$

This local limit is the expected speed of a massless particle measured in a freely falling [local inertial frame](../../../../../../../local-inertial-frame.md); the finite-interval effective velocity combines nonlocal length and clock comparisons.
