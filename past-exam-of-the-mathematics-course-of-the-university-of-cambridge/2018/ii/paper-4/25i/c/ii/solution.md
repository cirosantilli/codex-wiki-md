<h1 id="25i/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an example satisfying $(*)$, consider the [incomplete surface z equals r to three halves](../../../../../../../incomplete-surface-z-equals-r-to-three-halves.md)

$$
S_*=\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,r^{3/2}):r>0\}.
$$

A [meridian of a surface of revolution](../../../../../../../meridian-of-a-surface-of-revolution.md) reaches the missing origin in the finite length

$$
\int_0^{r_0}\sqrt{1+\frac94r}\,dr,
$$

so $S_*$ is [geodesically incomplete](../../../../../../../geodesic-incompleteness.md). The [curvatures of a parametrized surface of revolution](../../../../../../../curvatures-of-a-parametrized-surface-of-revolution.md) give

$$
K(r)=\frac{9}{8r(1+9r/4)^2}\longrightarrow+\infty
\qquad(r\downarrow0).
$$

By [constant speed of an affinely parametrized geodesic](../../../../../../../constant-speed-of-an-affinely-parametrized-geodesic.md), a geodesic with a finite endpoint has finite length and converges in $\mathbb R^3$. The [finite-dimensional continuation criterion](../../../../../../../finite-dimensional-continuation-criterion.md) rules out an endpoint in a compact subset of $S_*$, and infinity is infinitely far away. It must approach the origin, so $(*)$ holds.

The answer to the second question is **no**. The [punctured circular cone](../../../../../../../punctured-circular-cone.md)

$$
S_0=\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,r):r>0\}
$$

is incomplete because a generator reaches the missing vertex in finite time. It is inextendible: a proper connected smooth extension would have to add the origin, but the tangent planes approaching the origin depend on $\theta$ and therefore cannot be the continuous tangent planes of a smooth surface. Yet

$$
K\equiv0
$$

by the [Gaussian curvature of a cone away from its vertex](../../../../../../../gaussian-curvature-of-a-cone-away-from-its-vertex.md). Thus this incomplete inextendible surface does not satisfy $(*)$.
