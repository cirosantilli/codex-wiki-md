<h1 id="25i/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose that a connected smooth surface $\widetilde S\subset\mathbb R^3$ properly contains $S$. Since $S$ and $\widetilde S$ have the same dimension, $S$ is open in $\widetilde S$. Choose a point of its relative boundary. Inside a sufficiently small compact [normal neighbourhood](../../../../../../../normal-neighbourhood.md) in $\widetilde S$, choose $q\in S$ and a closest point $p\in\widetilde S\setminus S$. A minimizing $\widetilde S$-geodesic from $q$ to $p$ stays in $S$ before its endpoint, since encountering the complement earlier would contradict the choice of $p$.

The restriction of this curve is consequently a geodesic of $S$ with a finite maximal endpoint at $p$. On the other hand, the [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) of $S$ is the restriction of the smooth Gaussian curvature of $\widetilde S$, so it remains bounded near $p$. This contradicts condition $(*)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\widetilde S=S},
$$

so $S$ is an [inextendible embedded surface](../../../../../../../inextendible-embedded-surface.md). This is the [curvature-blowup criterion for inextendibility of a surface](../../../../../../../curvature-blowup-criterion-for-inextendibility-of-a-surface.md).
