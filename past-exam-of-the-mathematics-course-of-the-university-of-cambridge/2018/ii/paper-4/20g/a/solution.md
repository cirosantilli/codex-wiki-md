<h1 id="20g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $\alpha=\sqrt[n]{m}$. It is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md) because it satisfies the monic polynomial $X^n-m$. Choose a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $q$ dividing the [square-free integer](../../../../../../square-free-integer.md) $m$, and choose a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathcal O_L$ above $q$. From

$$
(\alpha)^n=(m)
$$

and [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md), the $\mathfrak p$-adic exponents satisfy

$$
n v_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha)=v_{\mathfrak p}(m)=e_{\mathfrak p},
$$

because $q$ occurs to exponent one in $m$. Hence $n$ divides the [ramification index of a prime ideal](../../../../../../ramification-index-of-a-prime-ideal.md) $e_{\mathfrak p}$. If $d=[L:\mathbb Q]$, then $e_{\mathfrak p}\leq d$, while the equation $\alpha^n=m$ gives $d\leq n$. Therefore

$$
n\leq e_{\mathfrak p}\leq d\leq n,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{[L:\mathbb Q]=n}.
$$
