<h1 id="36d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) $M$ is magnetic dipole moment per unit volume. It produces bound volume and [surface current density](../../../../../../surface-current-density.md)

$$
J_{\rm b}=\nabla\times M,
\qquad K_{\rm b}=M\times n.
$$

Starting from the [vector potential](../../../../../../vector-potential.md) of magnetic dipoles and integrating by parts gives

$$
A(x)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\left[
\int_V\frac{\nabla'\times M(x')}{|x-x'|}\,d^3x'
+\int_S\frac{M(x')\times n}{|x-x'|}\,dS'
\right],
$$

which has precisely the form generated by those bound currents.

Write the cylinder radius as $R$ and use cylindrical radius $\rho$. [Ampère's law](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) for the free axial current gives, inside the material,

$$
H(\rho)=\frac{I}{2\pi\rho}\hat\phi,
\qquad
M=\frac B{\mu_0}-H
=\left(\frac\mu{\mu_0}-1\right)
\frac{I}{2\pi\rho}\hat\phi.
$$

At $\rho=R$ this is tangential to the cylinder. Since $n=\hat r$,

$$
\boxed{K_{\rm b}=M\times\hat r
=-\left(\frac\mu{\mu_0}-1\right)
\frac{I}{2\pi R}\hat z},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{M(R)=\left(\frac\mu{\mu_0}-1\right)
\frac{I}{2\pi R}\hat\phi}.
$$

For $\mu>\mu_0$, the bound surface current therefore runs opposite to the free wire current.
