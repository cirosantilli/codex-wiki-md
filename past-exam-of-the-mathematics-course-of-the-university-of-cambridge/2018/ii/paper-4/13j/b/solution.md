<h1 id="13j/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Whenever players 1 and 2 occur, the [linear predictor](../../../../../../linear-predictor.md) contains their parameters only through

$$
\gamma_{12}=\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_{\{1,2\}}.
$$

For any constants $a,b$, replacing

$$
(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_{\{1,2\}})
\longmapsto
(\beta_1+a,\beta_2+b,\beta_{\{1,2\}}-a-b)
$$

leaves every fitted probability and hence the [likelihood function](../../../../../../likelihood-function.md) unchanged. The parameterization is therefore [nonidentifiable](../../../../../../identifiability.md): at most $\gamma_{12}$ can be estimated uniquely. Hence **there is no unique maximum-likelihood estimate of the three separate parameters**.
