<h1 id="2e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2E](../2e.md)

The [group presentation](../../../../../group-presentation.md) gives the [quotient group](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $\mathbb Z^3$ by the subgroup generated by the rows of

$$
 M=\begin{pmatrix}3&6&3\\0&9&9\\-3&3&6\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For the [Smith normal form](../../../../../smith-normal-form.md), the first [determinantal divisor](../../../../../determinantal-divisor.md) is the greatest common divisor of all entries, namely $3$. The nonzero two-by-two [matrix minors](../../../../../minor-linear-algebra.md) are $\pm27$, so the second [determinantal divisor](../../../../../determinantal-divisor.md) is $27$. The third row is the second minus the first, and a nonzero minor shows the [rank](../../../../../rank-one-quadratic-form.md) is exactly two. Consequently the nonzero invariant factors are $3$ and $27/3=9$, with one free generator. The [Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups.md) gives

$$
 \boxed{G\cong\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z.}
$$

An element killed by $3$ has zero free coordinate, arbitrary coordinate in $\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$, and coordinate $0,3$ or $6$ in $\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z$. There are nine such elements. Removing the identity leaves **eight elements of order three**.
