<h1 id="5d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The sign of the [Dirac delta distribution](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) in the equation is positive after the outer minus sign is expanded. Integrating through zero, the nonsingular potential contributes a term tending to zero, whereas the delta term contributes $3k\psi(0)$. The [jump condition for an impulse](../../../../../../jump-condition-for-an-impulse.md) is therefore

$$
 \boxed{\psi'(0^+)-\psi'(0^-)=-3k\psi(0).}
$$

[Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero gives $C_+=C_-=C$. The two one-sided derivatives are $-3kC/2$ and $+3kC/2$, whose difference is exactly $-3kC$. Hence the matching condition introduces no further restriction on $C$, and the globally bounded solution is

$$
 \boxed{\psi(z)=C(1+k|z|)^{-3/2},\qquad C\in\mathbb R\ \text{or}\ \mathbb C.}
$$

This continuous solution has precisely the derivative jump required for its second [distributional derivative](../../../../../../distributional-derivative.md) to cancel the delta term. Its amplitude remains arbitrary because no normalization has been imposed.
