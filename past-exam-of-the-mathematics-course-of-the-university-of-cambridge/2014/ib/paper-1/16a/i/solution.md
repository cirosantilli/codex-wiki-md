<h1 id="16a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An ideal [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md) has zero field in its interior and constant potential on its surface. Grounding fixes that potential to zero. The tangential field is continuous and therefore vanishes; the normal field has the jump fixed by the induced [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md) $\sigma$:

$$
\boxed{E_x=E_y=0\quad(z=0^+),\qquad E_z(0^+)=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0},\qquad \mathbf E(0^-)=0.}
$$

Here the normal points upward out of the conductor. The normal field is not generally zero; grounding prescribes the potential, not the induced charge density.
