↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False.** In $\mathbb R$, let

$$
X=\{n:n\ge1\},\qquad Y=\{-n+1/n:n\ge1\}.
$$

Each is closed: its distinct points escape to infinity, so it has no finite accumulation point. Their [Minkowski sum](../../../../../../minkowski-addition.md) contains $1/n$, tending to zero. However zero is not in the sum. If $m-n+1/n=0$ with positive integers $m,n$, then $n-m=1/n$. For $n>1$ the right side is strictly between zero and one, while the left side is an integer; for $n=1$ the equation would require $m=0$. Thus **the sum of two closed sets need not be closed**. The same counterexample embeds along a coordinate axis in $\mathbb R^n$.
