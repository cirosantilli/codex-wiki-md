<h1 id="12a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define

$$
\boxed{s_{ij}=\frac{t_{ij}+t_{ji}}2,\qquad a_{ij}=\frac{t_{ij}-t_{ji}}2.}
$$

They sum to $t_{ij}$, with $s_{ji}=s_{ij}$ and $a_{ji}=-a_{ij}$. Interchanging two slots of a [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) still produces a [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md), so these are a [symmetric second-rank tensor](../../../../../../symmetric-second-rank-tensor.md) and an [antisymmetric second-rank tensor](../../../../../../antisymmetric-second-rank-tensor.md), not merely symmetric and antisymmetric arrays in a selected frame.

For uniqueness, if $t=s+a=s'+a'$, then $s-s'=a'-a$. The common difference is both symmetric and antisymmetric, so each of its components equals its own negative and vanishes over $\mathbb R$. Thus **the decomposition is unique**. These are the [symmetric and antisymmetric parts of a matrix](../../../../../../symmetric-and-antisymmetric-parts-of-a-matrix.md), with their [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) transformation law preserved.
