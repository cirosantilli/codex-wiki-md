<h1 id="8d/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $G=C_2\times C_2$ and $N=C_2\times\{1\}$. Both are nontrivial, and $N$ is normal because $G$ is abelian. The second-coordinate projection identifies the [quotient group](../../../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G/N$ with $C_2$. Swapping the two coordinates identifies $(G/N)\times N$ with $G$, so

$$
\boxed{G=C_2\times C_2,\quad N=C_2\times\{1\},\quad (G/N)\times N\cong G.}
$$

This is a [direct product of groups](../../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) with its first factor as the specified [normal subgroup](../../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).
