<h1 id="9a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At every regular point of the [double circular cone](../../../../../../double-circular-cone.md), its normal is perpendicular to $\mathbf F$ by part (a). Therefore the integrand of the [flux integral](../../../../../../flux-integral.md) vanishes pointwise:

$$
\boxed{\int_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=0.}
$$

The vertex, if included, is a single point of zero surface area and does not alter the [surface integral](../../../../../../surface-integral.md). The answer is independent of both the subset and its orientation. Tangency, rather than a cancellation between different parts of the surface, explains the zero flux.
