<h1 id="9a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Away from the vertex, use the defining [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) $H=z^2-x^2-y^2$. Its [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) is normal to the smooth part of the [double circular cone](../../../../../../double-circular-cone.md), and

$$
\mathbf F\cdot\nabla H=(x,y,z)\cdot(-2x,-2y,2z)=2H=0
$$

on the surface. Hence **the field is tangent at every regular point of the cone**. At the origin the cone has no unique tangent plane, but $\mathbf F(0)=0$, which belongs to its tangent cone. An equivalent argument, valid also at the vertex, uses the [integral curves of a vector field](../../../../../../integral-curve-of-a-vector-field.md): $\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf x$ has solution $\mathbf x(t)=e^t\mathbf x(0)$, and these dilations preserve $H=0$. This is an example of [radial dilation tangency to homogeneous zero sets](../../../../../../radial-dilation-tangency-to-homogeneous-zero-sets.md).
