<h1 id="11a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On the outward-oriented [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) of radius $R$, $\mathbf n=\mathbf x/R$, so $\mathbf x\cdot d\mathbf S=R\,dS$. Hence

$$
\boxed{I=\frac1{R^2}\operatorname{Area}(S_R)=4\pi.}
$$

Away from the origin, the [divergence and curl of a radial vector field](../../../../../../divergence-and-curl-of-a-radial-vector-field.md) with $f(r)=r^{-3}$ give

$$
\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf x}{r^3}\right)=r(-3r^{-4})+3r^{-3}=0.
$$

If the enclosed volume excludes the origin and its boundary avoids it, this field is smooth throughout the volume. The [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) then gives **$I=0$**. The nonzero [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) flux is compatible with zero [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) away from the origin because the enclosed singularity prevents applying that smooth-volume theorem directly to the full ball.
