<h1 id="8b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Differentiate the [mechanical energy](../../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) and use the [damped pendulum](../../../../../../damped-pendulum.md) equation:

$$
\boxed{\frac{dE}{dt}=\dot\theta(\ddot\theta+\sin\theta)=-c\dot\theta^2\leq0.}
$$

The [energy dissipation of a damped pendulum](../../../../../../energy-dissipation-of-a-damped-pendulum.md) makes $E$ nonincreasing, rather than strictly decreasing at every instant: its derivative vanishes at turning points. It is constant along an equilibrium trajectory. Along every nonstationary trajectory it strictly decreases over any nonzero time interval, because a vanishing integral of $c\dot\theta^2$ over an interval would force an equilibrium there and hence everywhere by uniqueness.
