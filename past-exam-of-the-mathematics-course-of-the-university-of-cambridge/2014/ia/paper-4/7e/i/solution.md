<h1 id="7e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md) if it admits an [injective function](../../../../../../injective-function.md) into the [natural numbers](../../../../../../natural-number.md); this includes [finite sets](../../../../../../finite-set.md) and [countably infinite sets](../../../../../../countably-infinite-set.md). A [countably infinite set](../../../../../../countably-infinite-set.md) can be listed by a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) from $\mathbb N$.

Let $Z$ be the [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) of infinite [binary sequences](../../../../../../bitstream.md). Suppose it were countable and list all its members as $s^{(1)},s^{(2)},\ldots$, writing $s^{(j)}_n$ for the $n$th entry of row $j$. A finite listing could be repeated to make such a list as well. Define the [binary sequence](../../../../../../bitstream.md) $y$ by $y_n=1-s^{(n)}_n$. It differs from the $n$th row in coordinate $n$, for every $n$. Thus it belongs to $Z$ but is missing from the list, a contradiction. **The [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $Z$ is uncountable**, by [Cantor's diagonal argument](../../../../../../cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md).
