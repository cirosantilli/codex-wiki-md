<h1 id="6e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the [odd prime](../../../../../../odd-prime.md) $p$, the [Fermat-Euler theorem](../../../../../../euler-s-theorem.md) gives $x^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$. Therefore the [residue class](../../../../../../residue-class.md) $z=x^{(p-1)/2}$ obeys $(z-1)(z+1)\equiv0\pmod p$. Since a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) dividing a product divides one of its factors,

$$
\boxed{x^{(p-1)/2}\equiv1\text{ or }-1\pmod p.}
$$

If $x$ is a [quadratic residue](../../../../../../quadratic-residue.md), choose $y$ with $y^2\equiv x\pmod p$. Then $y$ is nonzero modulo $p$, and [Fermat's little theorem](../../../../../../fermat-little-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{x^{(p-1)/2}\equiv y^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p.}
$$

This proves the [quadratic residue](../../../../../../quadratic-residue.md) direction of [Euler's criterion](../../../../../../euler-s-criterion.md).
