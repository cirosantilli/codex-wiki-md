<h1 id="6e/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

[Fermat's little theorem](../../../../../../fermat-little-theorem.md) reduces the exponent modulo $22$ because $23$ is a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) and $5$ is [coprime](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md) to it. Compute $5^2\equiv3\pmod{22}$, then $5^4\equiv9\pmod{22}$ and $5^5\equiv45\equiv1\pmod{22}$. Thus $5^5=22k+1$ for an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $k$, and

$$
\boxed{5^{5^5}=5^{22k+1}\equiv(5^{22})^k5\equiv5\pmod{23}.}
$$

The exponent is $5^5=3125$, rather than an iterated left-associated power.
