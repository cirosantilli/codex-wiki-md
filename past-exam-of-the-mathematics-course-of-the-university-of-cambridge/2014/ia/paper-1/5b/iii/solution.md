<h1 id="5b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take points $p=(3,0,4)$ and $q=(-2,3,3)$ with directions $u=(1,3,-1)$ and $v=(0,1,-1)$. A common normal is $u\times v=(-2,1,1)$, of length $\sqrt6$. The [planes](../../../../../../plane.md) through the two lines with this common normal have separation

$$
\frac{|(q-p)\cdot(u\times v)|}{|u\times v|}
=\frac{12}{\sqrt6}=2\sqrt6.
$$

This is a lower bound for the [distance between skew lines](../../../../../../distance-between-skew-lines.md). It is attained at $s=-1$ and $t=-4$: the two points are $(2,-3,5)$ and $(-2,-1,7)$, whose difference $(4,-2,-2)$ is perpendicular to both directions and has length $2\sqrt6$. Thus **the shortest distance is** $\boxed{2\sqrt6}$.
