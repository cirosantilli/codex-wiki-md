<h1 id="10e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) states that a function [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) on $[p,q]$ and differentiable on $(p,q)$ has some $c\in(p,q)$ with $f(q)-f(p)=f'(c)(q-p)$.

Take any $p<q$ inside the given open interval. Differentiability implies the [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) needed on $[p,q]$, and the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is zero throughout it. The [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) therefore gives $f(q)=f(p)$. Since the two points were arbitrary, **the function is constant on the interval**.
