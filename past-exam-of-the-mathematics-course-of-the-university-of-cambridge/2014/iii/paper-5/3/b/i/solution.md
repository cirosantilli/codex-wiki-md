<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [clamped second-order Sobolev space](../../../../../../../clamped-second-order-sobolev-space.md) is $H_0^2(U)=\overline{C_c^\infty(U)}^{H^2}$. On a smooth bounded domain the [Sobolev trace theorem](../../../../../../../sobolev-trace-theorem.md) characterizes it by zero value and zero [normal derivative](../../../../../../../normal-derivative.md) on the boundary. In particular, its whole first-order boundary jet is zero, since tangential derivatives of the zero trace also vanish. As above, assume $f\in L^2(U)$ and classical regularity up to the boundary.

For a [smooth](../../../../../../../smooth-function.md) [weak solution](../../../../../../../weak-solution.md), compactly supported [test functions](../../../../../../../test-function.md) and two [integrations by parts](../../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) give

$$
\int_U(\Delta^2u-f)v=0\quad(v\in C_c^\infty(U)),
$$

so $\Delta^2u=f$ pointwise. Membership in $H_0^2(U)$ supplies $u=\partial_nu=0$ on $\partial U$. Thus it is a classical solution of the [clamped biharmonic problem](../../../../../../../clamped-biharmonic-problem.md).

Conversely, a $C^4(\overline U)$ classical solution with these traces belongs to $H_0^2(U)$. For every compactly supported [test function](../../../../../../../test-function.md), two [integrations by parts](../../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) give $\int_U\Delta u\Delta v=\int_Ufv$. Both sides are continuous for the $H^2$ [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md), so the defining density of $C_c^\infty(U)$ in $H_0^2(U)$ extends this equality to every required test. **The two notions agree under the stated smoothness.**
