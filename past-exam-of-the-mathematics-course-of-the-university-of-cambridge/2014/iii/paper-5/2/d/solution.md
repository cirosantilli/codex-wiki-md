<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Cauchy problem for a partial differential equation](../../../../../../cauchy-problem.md) here prescribes both the value $u|_\Gamma=g$ and the [normal derivative](../../../../../../normal-derivative.md) $\partial_nu|_\Gamma=h$, together with $\Delta u=0$. Only one of these traces would be boundary data for a usual elliptic boundary problem, rather than full [Cauchy data](../../../../../../cauchy-data.md).

Every real hypersurface is a [non-characteristic hypersurface](../../../../../../non-characteristic-hypersurface.md) for the [Laplace equation](../../../../../../laplace-equation.md). In local [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) coordinates flattening the [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) hypersurface $\Gamma$, the coefficient of the second transverse derivative is nonzero: its principal coefficient is the squared length of the conormal. The equation can therefore be solved for that second derivative. The [normal derivative](../../../../../../normal-derivative.md) data determine the transverse first derivative, because the coefficient relating them is nonzero and the tangential first derivatives are already determined by $g$.

The coefficients, flattened [Cauchy data](../../../../../../cauchy-data.md), and coordinate change are all [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md). **The [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) applies**, giving a unique local [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) solution around each point of $\Gamma$. This is a local existence assertion, not a claim of stable dependence in arbitrary [Sobolev space](../../../../../../sobolev-space-split.md) norms.
