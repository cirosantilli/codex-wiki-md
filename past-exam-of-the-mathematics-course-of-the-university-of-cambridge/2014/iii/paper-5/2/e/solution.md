<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**The [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) cannot be applied to merely $C^2$, non-$C^3$ [Cauchy data](../../../../../../cauchy-data.md).** It requires [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) data.

There is also no $C^2$ solution of the [Laplace equation](../../../../../../laplace-equation.md) on a neighborhood of a point where one of these prescribed traces fails to be $C^3$. By interior [elliptic regularity](../../../../../../elliptic-regularity.md), any such solution would be [smooth](../../../../../../smooth-function.md) and [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md). On the [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) hypersurface retained from the preceding part, both its restriction and its [normal derivative](../../../../../../normal-derivative.md) would then be [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md), hence $C^3$. This contradicts the prescribed trace. **There is no solution on a neighborhood of all of $\Gamma$ with the stated non-$C^3$ data.** This does not exclude solutions near other points where the data happen to be [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md).
