<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [Lagrangian submanifold](../../../../../../lagrangian-submanifold.md) of a $2n$-dimensional [symplectic manifold](../../../../../../symplectic-manifold.md) is an embedded $n$-dimensional submanifold on which the [symplectic form](../../../../../../symplectic-form.md) restricts to zero. The dimension requirement distinguishes it from a lower-dimensional [isotropic submanifold](../../../../../../isotropic-submanifold.md).

A [differential one-form](../../../../../../one-form.md) $\sigma$ on $L$ gives an embedded section of its [cotangent bundle](../../../../../../cotangent-bundle.md), since $\pi\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}$. Directly from the [canonical one-form on a cotangent bundle](../../../../../../canonical-one-form-on-a-cotangent-bundle.md),

$$
\sigma^*\lambda=\sigma,\qquad\sigma^*\omega_{\mathrm{can}}=-d\sigma.
$$

Its graph already has half the ambient dimension. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{graph}(\sigma)\text{ is Lagrangian}\iff d\sigma=0.}
$$

This proves the [graph of a closed one-form is Lagrangian](../../../../../../graph-of-a-closed-one-form-is-lagrangian.md) criterion, in both directions.
