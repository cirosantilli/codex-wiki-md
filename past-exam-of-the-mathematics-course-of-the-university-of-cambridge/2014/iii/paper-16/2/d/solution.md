<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [symplectic vector field](../../../../../../symplectic-vector-field.md) satisfies $\mathcal L_X\omega=0$, so its local flow consists of [symplectomorphisms](../../../../../../symplectomorphism.md). By [Cartan's magic formula](../../../../../../cartan-s-magic-formula.md) and $d\omega=0$, this is equivalent to the [differential one-form](../../../../../../one-form.md) $\iota_X\omega$ being closed. In the convention fixed above, a [Hamiltonian vector field](../../../../../../hamiltonian-vector-field.md) satisfies $\iota_X\omega=dH$ for a globally defined smooth function $H$. It is therefore a [symplectic vector field](../../../../../../symplectic-vector-field.md), but the converse requires this closed one-form to be exact.

On the [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) with $\omega=dx\wedge dy$,

$$
\iota_{\partial_x}\omega=dy,\qquad d(dy)=0.
$$

Thus $\partial_x$ is a [symplectic vector field](../../../../../../symplectic-vector-field.md). The closed [differential one-form](../../../../../../one-form.md) $dy$ is not exact on the [torus](../../../../../../torus.md): its integral on the loop $\gamma(s)=[(0,s)]$, $0\leq s\leq1$, equals one, while the integral of an exact one-form around any closed loop is zero. **The vector field is symplectic but not Hamiltonian.** The local candidate $H=y$ does not descend to a single-valued function on $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$.
