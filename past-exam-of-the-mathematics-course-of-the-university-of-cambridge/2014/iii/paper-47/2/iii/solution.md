<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Fix $\epsilon^{01}=+1$ with $x^0=t$, $x^1=x$. The [Noether current](../../../../../../noether-current.md) for the unit shift of $\alpha$ is the derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to $\partial_\mu\alpha$:

$$
\boxed{j^\mu=\frac r2\sin^2\phi\,\partial^\mu\alpha
-\frac{\theta}{4\pi}\epsilon^{\nu\mu}\partial_\nu(\cos\phi).}
$$

The angular field equation is $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. At $\theta=0$ this gives the conserved [Noether charge](../../../../../../noether-charge.md)

$$
\boxed{Q_0=\frac r2\int_{\mathbb R}\sin^2\phi\,\dot\alpha\,dx.}
$$

For nonzero $\theta$, $\epsilon^{10}=-1$, so $j^0$ contains $+\theta\partial_x(\cos\phi)/(4\pi)$. Its integral is a boundary term:

$$
\boxed{Q_\theta=Q_0+\frac{\theta}{4\pi}[\cos\phi]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}
=Q_0-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}T.}
$$

The theta term is locally a total derivative, so it does not change the bulk equations or the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md), but it changes the canonical charge by this topological shift. This is the [theta-angle shift of a sigma-model kink charge](../../../../../../theta-angle-shift-of-a-sigma-model-kink-charge.md). Reversing the convention for $\epsilon^{01}$ reverses the displayed theta shifts. Distinguishing the mechanical charge $Q_0$ from the canonical symmetry charge $Q_\theta$ will be important for quantization.
