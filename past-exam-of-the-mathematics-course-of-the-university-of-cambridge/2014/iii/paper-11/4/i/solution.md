↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [independence graph of events](../../../../../../dependency-graph-of-events.md), also called a [dependency graph of events](../../../../../../dependency-graph-of-events.md), has one vertex for each event $E_i$, with $E_i$ independent of the entire family of events indexed by its nonneighbors. Precisely, $E_i$ is independent of the [sigma-algebra](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) generated by those events. Pairwise independence alone is insufficient.

The [asymmetric Lovász local lemma](../../../../../../asymmetric-lovasz-local-lemma.md) says that if numbers $0\le x_i<1$ satisfy

$$
\mathbb P(E_i)\le x_i\prod_{j\in N(i)}(1-x_j),
$$

then $\mathbb P(\bigcap_iE_i^c)\ge\prod_i(1-x_i)>0$ for a finite family. In the commonly used symmetric [Lovász local lemma](../../../../../../lovasz-local-lemma.md), if each probability is at most $q$ and the maximum graph degree is at most $d$, the sufficient condition is

$$
\boxed{eq(d+1)\le1}.
$$
