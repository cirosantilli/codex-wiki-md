<h1 id="8/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $q:B\to Q$ be the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of $f$ in an [abelian category](../../../../../../abelian-category.md). If $f$ is an [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md), the equality $qf=0f$ implies $q=0$. Since $q$ is also an [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md), $1_Qq=0q$ implies $1_Q=0$. An object with zero identity is a [zero object](../../../../../../zero-object.md): every morphism to or from it is zero. Hence the cokernel object is zero.

Conversely, suppose the cokernel object is zero. If $uf=vf$, additivity gives $(u-v)f=0$. The [categorical cokernel](../../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) property makes $u-v$ factor through the zero object, so $u-v=0$ and $u=v$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f\text{ is epic}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{coker}f=0.}
$$

This is the [zero-cokernel criterion for epimorphisms](../../../../../../zero-cokernel-criterion-for-epimorphisms.md). In a [pushout in a category](../../../../../../pushout-in-a-category.md), the two horizontal morphisms have isomorphic cokernels by part (a). Therefore the lower morphism is epic if and only if the upper morphism is epic. In particular **pushouts reflect epimorphisms** in an [abelian category](../../../../../../abelian-category.md); the same argument also proves preservation.
