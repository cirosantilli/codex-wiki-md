<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [functor](../../../../../../functor.md) $U:\mathcal C\to\mathbf{Set}$ is a [representable functor](../../../../../../representable-functor.md) if some object $R$ admits a [natural isomorphism](../../../../../../natural-isomorphism.md) $\mathcal C(R,-)\cong U$. Here the representable is covariant.

For the identity [functor](../../../../../../functor.md) on the [Category of sets](../../../../../../category-of-sets.md), choose the singleton $1=\{*\}$. The evaluation maps

$$
\mathbf{Set}(1,X)\longrightarrow X,\qquad f\longmapsto f(*)
$$

are [bijections](../../../../../../bijection.md) with inverse $x\mapsto(*\mapsto x)$. For $u:X\to Y$, evaluation of $uf$ is $u(f(*))$, proving naturality. Hence **the identity functor on sets is represented by a singleton**.
