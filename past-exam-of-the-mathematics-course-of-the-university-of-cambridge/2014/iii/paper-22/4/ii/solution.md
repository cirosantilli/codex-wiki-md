<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Classical [Kummer theory](../../../../../../kummer-theory.md) relates extraction of $n$th roots to [Galois cohomology](../../../../../../galois-cohomology.md). In characteristic zero the exact sequence

$$
1\longrightarrow\mu_n\longrightarrow\overline K^{\times}
\xrightarrow{z\mapsto z^n}\overline K^{\times}\longrightarrow1
$$

and [Hilbert theorem 90](../../../../../../hilbert-s-theorem-90.md) identify $H^1(K,\mu_n)$ with $K^{\times}/K^{\times n}$. For an [elliptic curve](../../../../../../elliptic-curve.md), the corresponding [Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../kummer-exact-sequence-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) is

$$
0\longrightarrow E[n]\longrightarrow E(\overline K)
\xrightarrow{[n]}E(\overline K)\longrightarrow0.
$$

Multiplication by $n$ is surjective over the [algebraic closure](../../../../../../algebraic-closure.md). If $nQ=P\in E(K)$, the cocycle $\sigma\mapsto\sigma Q-Q$ takes values in $E[n]$. Changing the choice of $Q$ changes it by a coboundary, and changing $P$ by an element of $nE(K)$ does not change its class. Conversely, a trivial cocycle class allows $Q$ to be adjusted by an $n$-torsion point to become $K$-rational. Thus the [Kummer map of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../kummer-map-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) is an injection

$$
\delta:E(K)/nE(K)\hookrightarrow H^1(K,E[n]).
$$

This is the elliptic form of [Kummer theory](../../../../../../kummer-theory.md). The full cohomology group need not be finite; the arithmetic restriction on these classes is essential.

Choose a finite set $S$ of places containing the archimedean places, the [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md) over $n$, and all [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md) of [bad reduction of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../bad-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md). At a finite place outside $S$, $E$ has [good reduction](../../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) and $n$ is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). A division point of the reduction of $P$ exists over the algebraic closure of the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md). Smooth lifting gives a point over a finite [unramified extension](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) whose multiple differs from $P$ by an element of the [kernel of reduction of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../kernel-of-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md). In the [formal group of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../formal-group-of-an-elliptic-curve.md), $[n](T)=nT+O(T^2)$ is an isomorphism by the [invertible morphism criterion for formal group laws](../../../../../../invertible-morphism-criterion-for-formal-group-laws.md), and its integral inverse converges on the maximal ideal. Correcting that difference produces a division point in the maximal [unramified extension](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md). Hence the [Kummer map of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../kummer-map-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) class is unramified outside $S$.

To prove finiteness explicitly, choose a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md) $L/K$ containing all $E[n]$ and all $n$th roots of unity, and enlarge $S$ to include its ramified places. A basis of $E[n]\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2$ identifies it over $L$ with $\mu_n^2$. Classical [Kummer theory](../../../../../../kummer-theory.md) then identifies

$$
H^1(L,E[n])\cong(L^{\times}/L^{\times n})^2.
$$

The restriction of every class in the image of $\delta$ belongs to $L(S_L,n)^2$, where the [S-unramified power class group](../../../../../../s-unramified-power-class-group.md) is

$$
L(S_L,n)=\{[a]:v_{\mathfrak p}(a)\equiv0\pmod n\text{ for all }\mathfrak p\notin S_L\}.
$$

Indeed an unramified local [Kummer extension](../../../../../../kummer-extension.md) at residue characteristic prime to $n$ has valuation divisible by $n$: in an unramified field containing a root, $n v(a^{1/n})=v(a)$ with integral valuations.

The [finiteness of S-unramified Kummer classes](../../../../../../finiteness-of-s-unramified-kummer-classes.md) follows from the exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{L,S_L}^{\times}/(\mathcal O_{L,S_L}^{\times})^n
\longrightarrow L(S_L,n)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{L,S_L})[n]
\longrightarrow0.
$$

To see the final map, write the ideal of $a$ away from $S_L$ as $\mathfrak a^n$ and take the [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md) of $\mathfrak a$. Its kernel is represented by an [S-unit](../../../../../../s-unit.md), after division by an $n$th power; conversely an $n$-torsion [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md) yields such an $a$. The [S-unit group](../../../../../../s-unit-group.md) is finitely generated by the [Dirichlet unit theorem](../../../../../../dirichlet-s-unit-theorem.md) together with the finitely many inverted [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md). The [ideal class group](../../../../../../ideal-class-group.md) of the localized ring is a quotient of the finite ordinary [ideal class group](../../../../../../ideal-class-group.md). Both outer groups are therefore finite.

Finally restriction has finite kernel: inflation-restriction puts it in the finite group $H^1(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),E[n])$. Thus the image of $\delta$ has finite restriction image and finite kernel, and

$$
\boxed{\#(E(K)/nE(K))<\infty\qquad(n\geq2).}
$$

**This proves the weak Mordell-Weil theorem.** Combining it with the [height descent lemma](../../../../../../height-descent-lemma.md) proves the full [Mordell-Weil theorem](../../../../../../mordell-weil-group.md). Local restrictions at every place refine the finite group used here to the [Selmer group of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../n-selmer-group.md), which is useful for explicit descent calculations.
