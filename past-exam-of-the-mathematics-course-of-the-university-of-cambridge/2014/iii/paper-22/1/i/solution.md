<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the first [algebraic curve](../../../../../../algebraic-curve.md), a [hyperbola](../../../../../../hyperbola.md), use the line $y=1+tx$ through $(0,1)$. Substitution and cancellation of the known intersection give $x((1-t^2)x-2t)=0$. Thus a [rational parametrization of an algebraic curve](../../../../../../rational-parametrization-of-an-algebraic-curve.md) is

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{2t}{1-t^2},\qquad y=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2}.}
$$

The identity $y^2-x^2=1$ follows immediately. Away from $(0,1)$ its inverse is $t=(y-1)/x$; the exceptional point is recovered at $t=0$. The other point with $x=0$, namely $(0,-1)$, corresponds to $t=\infty$, while $t=\pm1$ gives the points at infinity on the projective closure. This explains the exceptional parameters rather than discarding them.

For the second [algebraic curve](../../../../../../algebraic-curve.md), the [rational parametrization of an algebraic curve](../../../../../../rational-parametrization-of-an-algebraic-curve.md)

$$
\boxed{x=t^2,\qquad y=t^3}
$$

has inverse $t=y/x$ where $x\ne0$, and $t=0$ gives the [cusp](../../../../../../cusp-algebraic-geometry.md). Indeed, if $x\ne0$ and $y^2=x^3$, then $(y/x)^2=x$ and $(y/x)^3=y$. **Both curves admit rational parametrizations**, although the second has a singular point.
