<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

The spin-independent term cancels in the signed sum. The linearized stationary equation becomes

$$
M=U\chi_0(T)M+O(M^3),\qquad
\chi_0(T)=\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)[-\partial_\epsilon n_F(\epsilon)]>0.
$$

The even powers vanish because the equation is odd under $M\mapsto-M$. Equivalently, the mean-field action has curvature

$$
\mathcal S_{\rm eff}(M)=\mathcal S_{\rm eff}(0)+\frac{\beta N}{2}[U^{-1}-\chi_0(T)]M^2+O(M^4).
$$

Thus the unpolarized stationary point becomes unstable when

$$
\boxed{U\chi_0(T)>1,\qquad U_c\chi_0(T)=1.}
$$

At low temperature, the derivative of the [Fermi distribution](../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) samples a narrow window around $\mu$, so $\chi_0\simeq\nu(\mu)$ for a smooth [density of states](../../../../../../density-of-states.md). Measuring energy from the Fermi level gives **$U_c\nu(0)\simeq1$**, the [Stoner criterion](../../../../../../stoner-criterion.md) in the spin-summed convention fixed above. This identifies local instability of the paramagnetic state; the detailed transition order also depends on higher terms in the action.

In the isotropic ordered phase of [itinerant ferromagnetism](../../../../../../itinerant-ferromagnetism.md), continuous spin-rotation symmetry leaves a degenerate direction of magnetization. Its transverse fluctuations produce one gapless, quadratically dispersing [mean-field itinerant ferromagnetic Goldstone mode](../../../../../../mean-field-itinerant-ferromagnetic-goldstone-mode.md), $E(\mathbf q)\propto q^2$. The two broken spin generators form a canonical pair, as for the [type-B Goldstone boson](../../../../../../type-b-goldstone-boson.md) of the localized ferromagnet. The system also remains metallic, with exchange-split [Fermi surfaces](../../../../../../fermi-surface.md) and low-energy spin-conserving particle-hole excitations. Longitudinal-amplitude fluctuations and the spin-flip continuum are distinct from the long-wavelength magnon; their energy and damping scales depend on the band structure. Spin anisotropy, if added, could gap the transverse mode, but it is absent from the stated Hamiltonian.
