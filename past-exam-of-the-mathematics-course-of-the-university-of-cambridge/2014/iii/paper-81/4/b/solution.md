<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At $U=0$, use fermionic [Matsubara frequencies](../../../../../../matsubara-frequency.md) $\omega_r=(2r+1)\pi/\beta$. The [fermionic Gaussian integral](../../../../../../grassmann-gaussian-integral.md) has inverse propagator $-i\omega_r+\xi_{\mathbf k}$. With its thermal normalization, the determinant for one single-particle level is $1+e^{-\beta\xi}$, as also follows from its empty and occupied states. Two spin species therefore give

$$
\mathcal Z_0=\prod_{\mathbf k}[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu)}]^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\Omega_0=-\frac1\beta\ln\mathcal Z_0=-2k_BT\sum_{\mathbf k}\ln[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu)}].}
$$

The expression labeled [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md) in the PDF is the [grand potential](../../../../../../grand-potential.md), since the trace contains $H-\mu\hat N$. The thermodynamic [Helmholtz free energy](../../../../../../helmholtz-free-energy.md) at a prescribed electron number $N_e$ is **$F=\Omega_0+\mu N_e$**, with $\mu$ fixed by $N_e=2\sum_{\mathbf k}n_F(\epsilon_{\mathbf k})$. The Matsubara determinant requires a normalization prescription; a raw infinite sum of logarithms is not an ordinary convergent sum.
