<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Repeat the [linear spin-wave approximation](../../../../../../linear-spin-wave-approximation.md) for bonds of lengths one and two. The polarized energy is $E_0=-NS^2(J_1+J_2)$ and each bond length contributes its own lattice Laplacian. Thus

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_k=2S[J_1(1-\cos k)+J_2(1-\cos2k)]
=4S[J_1\sin^2(k/2)+J_2\sin^2k].}
$$

The [next-nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion](../../../../../../next-nearest-neighbour-ferromagnetic-magnon-dispersion.md) remains nonnegative for the stated positive exchanges and has its only zero at $k=0$ modulo $2\pi$ when $J_1>0$. At small $k$, $\varepsilon_k\simeq S(J_1+4J_2)k^2$: longer-range ferromagnetic exchange increases the stiffness.

At this harmonic order, a bosonic occupation configuration has energy $E_0+\sum_k\varepsilon_kn_k$. The one-magnon dispersion is also exact. General multi-magnon energies acquire the interactions omitted in the large-$S$ expansion; the formula does not claim an exact diagonalization of the full interacting many-spin Hamiltonian.
