<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Holstein–Primakoff transformation](../../../../../../holstein-primakoff-transformation.md) acts on the physical number-state subspace $0\leq n\leq2S$. Its operator ordering matters: $S^+=\sqrt{2S-\hat n}\,a$ lowers the number first. Therefore

$$
S^+|n\rangle=\sqrt{n(2S-n+1)}|n-1\rangle,\qquad
S^-|n\rangle=\sqrt{(n+1)(2S-n)}|n+1\rangle,
$$

while $S^z|n\rangle=(S-n)|n\rangle$. The square roots vanish at the appropriate ends, so the spin operators preserve the [Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint](../../../../../../holstein-primakoff-occupation-constraint.md). The [bosonic Fock space](../../../../../../bosonic-fock-space.md) outside this subspace is not part of the spin-$S$ representation.

These matrix elements give $[S^z,S^\pm]=\pm S^\pm$. The two products on $|n\rangle$ are $(n+1)(2S-n)$ and $n(2S-n+1)$, respectively, so their difference is $2(S-n)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{[S^+,S^-]=2S^z,\qquad[S^z,S^\pm]=\pm S^\pm.}
$$

Also $(S^z)^2+\frac12(S^+S^-+S^-S^+)=S(S+1)I$. The transformation therefore has both the correct algebra and the correct spin [Casimir operator](../../../../../../casimir-element.md), not just its small-occupation approximation.
