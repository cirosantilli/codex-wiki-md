<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a properly embedded oriented [topological surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md) $F=\coprod F_j$, put

$$
c(F)=\chi_-(F)=\sum_j\max\{0,-\chi(F_j)\},\qquad \|x\|_T=\min_{[F]=x}c(F).
$$

This defines the [Thurston norm](../../../../../thurston-norm.md) on integral [relative homology](../../../../../relative-homology.md) classes in $H_2(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb Z)$. Homogeneity extends it to rational classes, and continuity extends it to real classes. It is a [seminorm](../../../../../seminorm.md): nonzero classes carried by spheres, disks, [annuli](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) or [tori](../../../../../torus.md) can have zero value.

**Not every integral class has a connected embedded representative.** In $Y=S^1\times S^2$, the class $2[\{*\}\times S^2]$ is a counterexample. A connected embedded oriented surface either separates, in which case it is null-homologous, or has connected complement. In the latter case join its two local sides through its complement to obtain a loop intersecting it exactly once. Its [homology class](../../../../../homology-class.md) is consequently a [primitive lattice element](../../../../../primitive-lattice-element.md). The proposed double has all intersection numbers even, so cannot have such a representative. Two disjoint parallel spheres do represent it.

For the [knot exterior](../../../../../knot-exterior.md) in an [integral homology sphere](../../../../../homology-sphere.md), $H_2(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ is generated by a [Seifert surface](../../../../../seifert-surface.md). Its [Thurston norm](../../../../../thurston-norm.md) is

$$
\|y\|_T=\max\{0,2g(K)-1\},
$$

where $g(K)$ is the [Seifert genus](../../../../../seifert-genus.md) in the ambient [integral homology sphere](../../../../../homology-sphere.md). To justify using a one-boundary-component [Seifert surface](../../../../../seifert-surface.md), simplify a minimizing representative's boundary to parallel essential longitudes. The algebraic sum of these longitudes is one. Join oppositely oriented pairs by boundary annuli and push inward; this preserves [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) and does not increase $\chi_-$. Discard closed components, which represent zero because the ambient [integral homology sphere](../../../../../homology-sphere.md) has $H_2=0$. The component retaining the single boundary is a [Seifert surface](../../../../../seifert-surface.md). This proves the formula, including the disk case.

When $\|y\|_T>0$, $g(K)\geq1$. In zero [Dehn surgery](../../../../../dehn-surgery.md) the longitude bounds a meridional disk in the filling [solid torus](../../../../../solid-torus.md). Cap a minimizing [Seifert surface](../../../../../seifert-surface.md) with that disk. The resulting closed surface $\widehat F$ has the same [genus](../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) and represents a generator of $H_2(Z;\mathbb Z)$: its intersection with the filling core is one. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\|z\|_T\leq\chi_-(\widehat F)=2g(K)-2=\|y\|_T-1.}
$$

The case $g(K)=1$ gives a zero-cost [torus](../../../../../torus.md), rather than a negative value.

Here is a rigorous family of strict examples with $Z=S^1\times S^2$. Use the [excellent knot representative theorem](../../../../../excellent-knot-representative-theorem.md) to choose a [knot](../../../../../knot.md) $J$ representing the generator of $\pi_1(Z)$ with an [excellent three-manifold](../../../../../excellent-three-manifold.md) as exterior $E_0$. Inside $V=\nu J$, choose a [winding number of a satellite pattern](../../../../../winding-number-of-a-satellite-pattern.md) one pattern $P$ whose two-boundary-component exterior $E_1=V\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu P$ is also an [excellent three-manifold](../../../../../excellent-three-manifold.md). Both choices are available because neither ambient manifold has a spherical boundary component. Put $\Gamma=P(J)$ and $E=Z\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\Gamma=E_0\cup_{\partial V}E_1$.

The interface is an [incompressible surface](../../../../../incompressible-surface.md). On a [Thurston norm](../../../../../thurston-norm.md) minimizing surface in $E$ with boundary the meridian $\mu_\Gamma$, arrange all interface intersections to be essential. The [winding number of a satellite pattern](../../../../../winding-number-of-a-satellite-pattern.md) one condition forces the oriented boundary on that interface to have net class $\mu_J$. The piece in $E_0$ has odd meridional boundary sum and costs at least one: a zero-cost representative would require an essential disk or [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md), forbidden by excellence. The piece in $E_1$ has opposite net meridians on its two boundary [tori](../../../../../torus.md). It costs at least two: zero-cost components are boundary-parallel [annuli](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) or [tori](../../../../../torus.md) and carry no such boundary class, and its total meridional boundary count is even, so its nonzero negative [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) has even magnitude. Cutting along the interface adds the costs, since the essential pieces have no disk or sphere components. Thus

$$
\|[\mu_\Gamma\text{-bounding surface}]\|_T\geq1+2=3.
$$

Here the notation denotes the relative class whose boundary is $\mu_\Gamma$, not the meridian curve itself. This is the usual [Thurston norm gluing along an incompressible torus](../../../../../thurston-norm-gluing-along-an-incompressible-torus.md) argument.

Because $\Gamma$ represents the generator of $H_1(Z)$, $H_1(E)=\mathbb Z$, its meridian is null-homologous in $E$, and a longitudinal curve $\lambda_\Gamma$ generates $H_1(E)$. Fill $E$ along $\lambda_\Gamma$ to obtain $X$. The [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) gives $H_1(X)=0$, so $X$ is an [integral homology sphere](../../../../../homology-sphere.md). Let $K$ be the filling core. Its preferred longitude is $\mu_\Gamma$; zero [Dehn surgery](../../../../../dehn-surgery.md) on $K$ consequently recovers $Z$. For this example $\|y\|_T\geq3$, whereas the product sphere generates $H_2(Z)$ with zero cost. Hence

$$
\boxed{0=\|z\|_T<\|y\|_T-1.}
$$

The construction needs a nontrivial winding-one pattern; merely tying a local [knot](../../../../../knot.md) into the product core would not provide this lower bound.
