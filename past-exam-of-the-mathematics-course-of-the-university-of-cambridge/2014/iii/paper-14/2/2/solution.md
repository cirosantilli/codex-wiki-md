<h1 id="2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**The second assertion is true.** Separate the points $p_i$ in the circle direction by a small [isotopy](../../../../../../isotopy.md) if necessary. Cutting $S^1\times S$ at fibers just before and after $p_i\times\gamma_i$ exposes two copies of $S$. The [surface framing](../../../../../../surface-framing.md) is precisely the stated push-off $p_i'\times\gamma_i$: the two normal directions give homotopic nonzero normal fields along the curve.

For a single [fiber-curve surgery](../../../../../../fiber-curve-surgery.md), a cut and regluing of these copies by a [Dehn twist](../../../../../../dehn-twist.md) changes the curve identified with the transverse meridian by one copy of the surface longitude. In the usual convention that negative surface-framed surgery produces a positive [Dehn twist](../../../../../../dehn-twist.md), the filling slope $\mu+\lambda$ implements $\tau_{\gamma_i}^{-1}$. One can see the coefficient locally by tracing a transverse arc across the twist annulus: it acquires one reverse turn along $\gamma_i$, and reversing this cut-and-glue identifies the compressible slope as $\mu+\lambda$. Reversing the circle parameter reverses the monodromy convention, without changing the existence of the [fiber bundle](../../../../../../fiber-bundle-split.md).

Every filled block is therefore a product of $S$ with an interval, with a modified endpoint identification. Their cyclic assembly is the [mapping torus](../../../../../../mapping-torus.md) of the product of these inverse [Dehn twists](../../../../../../dehn-twist.md), in the order of the $p_i$ along the oriented circle. It has the original closed [topological surface](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) $S$ as fiber, hence **fibers over the circle**. Intersections between the $\gamma_i$ do not obstruct this argument: their twists occur in different fibers, and their ordered product need not commute.
