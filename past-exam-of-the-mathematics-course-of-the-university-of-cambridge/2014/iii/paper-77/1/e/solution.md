<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Use the printed [Maxwell-filtered local drag](../../../../../../maxwell-filtered-local-drag.md) law at fixed material arclength, after transients have decayed to a periodic response. Averaging the [force](../../../../../../force.md) equation over a period makes the time-derivative term vanish. Therefore the mean [force](../../../../../../force.md) equals the mean instantaneous [resistive-force theory](../../../../../../resistive-force-theory.md) driving [force](../../../../../../force.md), even though its oscillating part is filtered. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\langle F_y\rangle=-\tfrac12(c_\perp-c_\parallel)L\epsilon^2a\omega\sin\phi+O(\epsilon^4),}
$$

with the same sign convention as before.

At first order the tangent is $\mathbf e_y$ and both [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) components are single harmonics of frequency $\omega$. The [linear Maxwell fluid](../../../../../../linear-maxwell-fluid.md) response multiplies their complex [force](../../../../../../force.md) amplitudes by $(1+i\omega\lambda)^{-1}$. Only its in-phase real part contributes to the mean work. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\langle P\rangle_{\mathrm M}=\frac{c_\perp\epsilon^2\omega^2}{2[1+(\omega\lambda)^2]}
\left(La^2+\frac{L^3}{12}\right)+O(\epsilon^4).}
$$

The leading power is strictly smaller when $\lambda>0$ and $\omega>0$, and equal in the Newtonian limit. The zero-frequency component generating the mean [force](../../../../../../force.md) is unattenuated, whereas elastic storage and phase lag reduce the dissipative harmonic response. This conclusion concerns the specified linear local [force](../../../../../../force.md) law and prescribed kinematics; it is not a claim for every nonlinear viscoelastic constitutive model.
