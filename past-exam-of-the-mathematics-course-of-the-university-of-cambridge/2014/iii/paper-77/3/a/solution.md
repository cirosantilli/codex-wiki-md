<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Stokeslet](../../../../../../stokeslet.md) is the flow produced by a point [force](../../../../../../force.md). With $C=1/(8\pi\mu)$, its tensor is $G_{ij}=C(\delta_{ij}/r+r_ir_j/r^3)$. A separated pair of equal and opposite forces has no [force](../../../../../../force.md) monopole. Expanding their difference in the separation gives a directional derivative of this tensor. For an axial pair, choose the sign of the strength so that

$$
u_i=-S e_j e_k\partial_kG_{ij}
=\frac S{8\pi\mu}\left[-\frac1{r^3}+3\frac{(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf r)^2}{r^5}\right]r_i.
$$

The trace part of $\mathbf e\mathbf e$ does not affect the flow because $\partial_jG_{ij}=0$. The remaining symmetric [force](../../../../../../force.md)-dipole contribution is the [stresslet](../../../../../../force-dipole-flow.md), decaying as $r^{-2}$ instead of the [Stokeslet](../../../../../../stokeslet.md)'s $r^{-1}$. A [force-free](../../../../../../force-free.md) swimming cell has thrust balanced by drag, so this is typically its leading far field. With $S>0$ it is a [pusher microswimmer](../../../../../../pusher-microswimmer.md): axial outflow and transverse inflow. It is distinct from the antisymmetric [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) singularity, the [rotlet](../../../../../../rotlet.md).
