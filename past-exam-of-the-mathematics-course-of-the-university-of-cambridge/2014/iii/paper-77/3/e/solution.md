<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [rotlet](../../../../../../rotlet.md) is the point-[torque](../../../../../../torque.md) fundamental solution of [Stokes flow](../../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md). Its [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) strength $\mathbf R$ is an axial vector, and its circulating [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) decays as $r^{-2}$. A freely swimming cell with no external [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) is [torque-free](../../../../../../torque-free.md). The motor applies opposite internal torques to its body and flagellar apparatus, so the net [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) monopole satisfies

$$
\boxed{\mathbf R_{\mathrm{total}}=0.}
$$

Individual body and flagellar torques need not vanish. A spatially separated pair can therefore leave a [rotlet dipole](../../../../../../rotlet-dipole.md) even though there is no far-field [rotlet](../../../../../../rotlet.md) monopole.
