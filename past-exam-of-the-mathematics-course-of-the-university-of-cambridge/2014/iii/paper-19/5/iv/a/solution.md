<h1 id="5/iv/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

All [cardinalities](../../../../../../../cardinality.md) in this part are first computed in $M$. The [forcing](../../../../../../../forcing-split.md) has size $\kappa$. A family of $\kappa$ finite domains has a $\kappa$-sized [Delta-system](../../../../../../../delta-system.md), since $\kappa$ is regular. There are fewer than $\kappa$ possible value assignments on its finite root: each coordinate $(\alpha,n)$ allows fewer than $\kappa$ values. Regularity lets us thin to two conditions, indeed $\kappa$ many, with identical root assignments. Their union is a condition, proving the $\kappa$-chain condition.

Consequently **every maximal [forcing antichain](../../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md) has [cardinality](../../../../../../../cardinality.md) less than $\kappa$, but there is no one compulsory [cardinality](../../../../../../../cardinality.md)**. For any nonzero [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) $\mu<\kappa$, the single-coordinate conditions assigning the values $\xi<\mu$ at $(\mu,0)$ form an [forcing antichain](../../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md) of size $\mu$. It is maximal: a condition already assigning that coordinate is compatible with its matching value, and a condition not assigning it is compatible with every allowed value. Thus every such size occurs, including singleton maximal [forcing antichains](../../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md). These are the [maximal-antichain sizes in the finite Lévy collapse](../../../../../../../maximal-antichain-sizes-in-the-finite-levy-collapse.md).

The paper writes $p\le q$ for $p\subseteq q$. We use this printed weaker-first convention: $q$ extends $p$ and is stronger. Compatibility and generic meeting arguments below always refer to common extensions, so do not accidentally reverse the convention.
