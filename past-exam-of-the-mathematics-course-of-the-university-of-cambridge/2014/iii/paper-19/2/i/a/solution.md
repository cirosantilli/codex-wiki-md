↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the usual tail-normalized version, an [Ulam matrix on omega-one](../../../../../../../ulam-matrix-on-omega-one.md) is a family $A_{\alpha,n}$ such that, for each $\alpha<\omega_1$, the [sets](../../../../../../../set-split.md) indexed by $n<\omega$ partition the tail $(\alpha,\omega_1)$, while for each fixed $n$ the [sets](../../../../../../../set-split.md) indexed by $\alpha<\omega_1$ are pairwise disjoint. Empty cells are allowed; harmless bounded-tail variants give the same applications.

For a concrete realization, choose [injections](../../../../../../../injective-function.md) $e_\beta:\beta\to\omega$ for every [countable ordinal](../../../../../../../countable-ordinal.md) $\beta$, and [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md)

$$
\boxed{A_{\alpha,n}=\{\beta>\alpha:e_\beta(\alpha)=n\}.}
$$

For fixed $\alpha$ each $\beta>\alpha$ chooses exactly one $n$; for fixed $n$, injectivity of $e_\beta$ prevents one $\beta$ from belonging to two cells. This verifies the two matrix conditions explicitly.
