[König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../../../../../konig-s-theorem-set-theory.md) states that if $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$ for each $i\in I$, then $\sum_i\kappa_i<\prod_i\lambda_i$. Its [cofinality](../../../../../../../cofinality.md) formulation gives, for every infinite [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}>\kappa.}
$$

Taking a cofinal sequence of smaller [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) in $\kappa$ and comparing their sum with the product of their successors gives this inequality. Another standard consequence, for infinite $\lambda$ and $\kappa\ge2$, is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^\lambda)>\lambda,\qquad
\operatorname{cf}(2^\lambda)>\lambda.}
$$

Indeed, if $\mu=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^\lambda)\le\lambda$, the preceding inequality at $\kappa^\lambda$ would contradict $(\kappa^\lambda)^\mu=\kappa^{\lambda\mu}=\kappa^\lambda$.
