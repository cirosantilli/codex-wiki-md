<h1 id="3/iv/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Prune an $\aleph_1$-[Suslin tree](../../../../../../../suslin-tree.md) as in part (a), then use its nodes as [forcing](../../../../../../../forcing-split.md) conditions, with extensions stronger. Two conditions are compatible exactly when comparable, so the absence of uncountable [tree antichains](../../../../../../../tree-antichain.md) is the [forcing](../../../../../../../forcing-split.md) [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../../countable-chain-condition-for-forcing.md). For each $\alpha<\omega_1$, the [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) $D_\alpha$ of nodes of height at least $\alpha$ is dense, by [well-pruned set-theoretic tree](../../../../../../../well-pruned-set-theoretic-tree.md).

If $2^{\aleph_0}>\aleph_1$, full [Martin's axiom](../../../../../../../martin-s-axiom.md) includes $\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}$. It would provide a [filter in an ordered set](../../../../../../../filter-mathematics.md) meeting all these [dense subsets of a forcing order](../../../../../../../dense-subset-of-a-forcing-order.md). Directedness makes that [filter in an ordered set](../../../../../../../filter-mathematics.md) a [chain in a partial order](../../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md), and meeting every $D_\alpha$ makes its heights unbounded, producing an uncountable branch. This contradicts the Suslin-tree property. **A Suslin [set-theoretic tree](../../../../../../../set-theoretic-tree.md) together with failure of [Continuum hypothesis](../../../../../../../continuum-hypothesis.md) therefore implies failure of [Martin's axiom](../../../../../../../martin-s-axiom.md).** This is the [Suslin-tree obstruction to Martin's axiom](../../../../../../../suslin-tree-obstruction-to-martin-s-axiom.md).
