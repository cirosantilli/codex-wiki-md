**A [well-pruned set-theoretic tree](../../../../../../../well-pruned-set-theoretic-tree.md) of height $\kappa$ has the property that every node $t$ extends to every higher level below $\kappa$: if $\operatorname{ht}(t)<\beta<\kappa$, there is $u$ of height $\beta$ with $t<_T u$.** Equivalently, the heights of extensions of every node are unbounded in $\kappa$, since taking predecessors then gives an extension at any prescribed intermediate level. This is stronger than merely having no terminal nodes. For a [kappa-tree](../../../../../../../kappa-tree.md) we use the usual regular uncountable height [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) and levels of size less than that [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md).
