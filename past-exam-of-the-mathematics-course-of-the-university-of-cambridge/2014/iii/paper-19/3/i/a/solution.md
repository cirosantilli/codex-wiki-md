<h1 id="3/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an infinite [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md), the [Gimel function](../../../../../../../gimel-function.md) is $\gimel(\kappa)=\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}$. The [Gimel hypothesis](../../../../../../../gimel-hypothesis.md) asserts, for every [singular cardinal](../../../../../../../singular-cardinal.md) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{\gimel(\kappa)=\max\{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)},\kappa^+\}.}
$$

These are the unavoidable lower bounds supplied by monotonicity of exponentiation and [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../../../../../konig-s-theorem-set-theory.md). The hypothesis imposes the least allowed value at singular [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md); it does not constrain the continuum [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) on regular [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) to their successors. Thus it is weaker than [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../../../../../generalized-continuum-hypothesis.md). In the case $2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}<\kappa$, it says $\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=\kappa^+$, the usual [singular cardinals hypothesis](../../../../../../../singular-cardinals-hypothesis.md) case.
