<h1 id="1/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $f$ be given and choose $Y\subseteq\mathbb R$ of [cardinality](../../../../../../../cardinality.md) $\aleph_1$. Each $f(y)$ is countable, so the [axiom of choice](../../../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) and [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../../../../../../../infinite-cardinal-arithmetic.md) give

$$
\left|Y\cup\bigcup_{y\in Y}f(y)\right|\le\aleph_1.
$$

Since the continuum is larger, choose $x$ outside this union. Then $x\notin f(y)$ for every $y\in Y$. The countable [set](../../../../../../../set-split.md) $f(x)$ cannot contain $Y$, so choose $y\in Y\setminus f(x)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{x\notin f(y),\qquad y\notin f(x).}
$$

The two points are distinct because $x\notin Y$. This proves the [countable-valued free-pair criterion](../../../../../../../freiling-theorem.md) and the required direction of the [Freiling axiom of symmetry](../../../../../../../freiling-axiom-of-symmetry.md) without any measurability assumption on $f$.
