<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the standard [normal set-theoretic tree](../../../../../../normal-set-theoretic-tree.md) convention: a unique root, extensions at every higher level, splitting into at least two successors, and [tree with unique limits](../../../../../../tree-with-unique-limits.md). The small-level and height assumptions already give an $\omega_1$-tree, while the given [tree antichain](../../../../../../tree-antichain.md) condition gives the [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../countable-chain-condition-for-forcing.md). We only need to exclude an uncountable branch.

If such a branch existed, its heights would be unbounded, since each initial segment contains only countably many nodes. Fill in predecessors to obtain its node $b_\alpha$ at every level. At each successor step choose a successor $s_\alpha$ of $b_\alpha$ different from $b_{\alpha+1}$. For $\alpha<\beta$, the node $s_\beta$ extends the branch successor $b_{\alpha+1}$, and so is incompatible with $s_\alpha$. Thus $\{s_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}$ is an uncountable [tree antichain](../../../../../../tree-antichain.md), a contradiction.

Therefore **the [set-theoretic tree](../../../../../../set-theoretic-tree.md) is $\aleph_1$-Suslin**. The splitting part of normality matters: a single [chain in a partial order](../../../../../../chain-in-a-partial-order.md) of height $\omega_1$ would satisfy the [tree antichain](../../../../../../tree-antichain.md) condition but not the conclusion if one used a weakened definition of normality allowing no splitting.
