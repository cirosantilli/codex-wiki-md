<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $\delta\psi_B=\psi_1^{(B)}-\psi_i$ be the first-order scattered correction in the [Born approximation for scalar wave scattering](../../../../../../born-approximation-for-scalar-wave-scattering.md), using the paper's [Green function](../../../../../../green-s-function.md) convention. Comparing its outgoing integral with the preceding logarithmic correction gives

$$
\chi_1=\frac{\delta\psi_B}{\psi_i}.
$$

Therefore the exact algebraic relation between the two first-order approximations is

$$
\boxed{\psi_1^{(R)}=\psi_i\exp\left(\frac{\psi_1^{(B)}-\psi_i}{\psi_i}\right)=\psi_i\exp\left(\frac{\psi_1^{(B)}}{\psi_i}-1\right).}
$$

If $V=O(\varepsilon)$, then $\chi_1=O(\varepsilon)$ on a controlled fixed region, and

$$
\psi_1^{(R)}=\psi_i(1+\chi_1)+O(\varepsilon^2)=\psi_1^{(B)}+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$

Thus **Born and Rytov agree to first order in the [scattering potential](../../../../../../scattering-potential.md)**, but differ as finite approximations. The [Rytov approximation](../../../../../../rytov-approximation.md) exponentiates the first logarithmic correction; the [Born approximation](../../../../../../born-approximation.md) adds the first field correction. The exponential's higher powers are not a calculation of all higher multiple-scattering terms in the [Born series](../../../../../../born-series.md). This distinction explains why a smooth, appreciable [wave phase](../../../../../../phase-waves.md) accumulation can be represented more naturally by the [Rytov approximation](../../../../../../rytov-approximation.md) even when the corresponding linear field expansion is inaccurate.
