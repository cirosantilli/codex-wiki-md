<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Two effects must be distinguished in [Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md). The positive extra [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md) increases the [Hubble parameter](../../../../../../hubble-parameter.md) through the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md). Holding weak rates fixed, faster expansion causes earlier neutron-proton [cosmological weak freeze-out](../../../../../../cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md), leaving more [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md), and leaves less time for neutron decay before nuclear reactions begin. Both tendencies increase the [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md).

Electron-flavor degeneracy also changes the charged-current balance directly. Chemical equilibrium for $n+\nu_e\leftrightarrow p+e^-$ gives, with negligible electron [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md),

$$
\frac{n_n}{n_p}\simeq\exp\!\left[-\frac{m_n-m_p}{T}-\xi_{\nu_e}\right].
$$

This is the [primordial helium response to electron-neutrino degeneracy](../../../../../../primordial-helium-response-to-electron-neutrino-degeneracy.md). Positive $\xi_{\nu_e}$ favors conversion of [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) to [protons](../../../../../../proton.md) and reduces the neutron abundance; negative $\xi_{\nu_e}$ favors the opposite balance. When [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) are the limiting ingredient and nearly all surviving [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) enter helium-4, the [neutron-limited helium synthesis](../../../../../../neutron-limited-helium-synthesis.md) estimate is

$$
Y_p\simeq\frac{2r}{1+r},\qquad r=\frac{n_n}{n_p}\ \text{at nucleosynthesis}.
$$

In this usual proton-rich regime, the direct electron-neutrino effect lowers $Y_p$ for positive degeneracy and raises it for negative degeneracy, other conditions held fixed. At sufficiently large negative degeneracy the equilibrium ratio can instead exceed one; protons then become limiting. The corresponding ideal complete-capture estimate is $Y_p\simeq2\min(r,1)/(1+r)$, so the neutron-limited trend is not a universal monotonic law at arbitrary negative chemical potential. Degeneracy in the other flavors primarily affects helium through the expansion rate in this simplified discussion.

For very large degeneracy the weak reaction rates themselves also change, so a quantitative freeze-out calculation must include both the altered distributions and the altered expansion rate. The two effects can compete for a positive electron-neutrino asymmetry. **The [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md) change is not determined by $|\mu_\nu|$ alone: its flavor and sign matter as well as its [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md).**
