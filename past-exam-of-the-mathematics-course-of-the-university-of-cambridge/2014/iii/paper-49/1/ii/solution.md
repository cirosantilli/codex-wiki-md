<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation](../../../../../../cosmological-perfect-fluid-continuity-equation.md) gives $\rho_r=\rho_{r,0}a^{-4}$ and $\rho_m=\rho_{m,0}a^{-3}$. In [conformal time](../../../../../../conformal-time.md), $\dot a=a'/a$ and $H=a'/a^2$. Thus the flat [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) is

$$
\boxed{(a')^2=H_0^2(\Omega_{r,0}+\Omega_{m,0}a)}.
$$

Choose the expanding branch and put the [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md) at $\tau=0$. Integration gives

$$
\tau(a)=\frac{2}{H_0\Omega_{m,0}}
\left[\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}+\Omega_{m,0}a}-\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}\right].
$$

Inverting it supplies the [radiation-matter scale factor in conformal time](../../../../../../radiation-matter-scale-factor-in-conformal-time.md):

$$
\boxed{a(\tau)=\frac{H_0^2\Omega_{m,0}}4\tau^2
+H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}\,\tau},\qquad
A=\frac{H_0^2\Omega_{m,0}}4,\quad B=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}.
$$

The early linear term gives [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), while the late quadratic term gives [matter domination](../../../../../../matter-domination.md). The solution presumes the stated radiation-plus-matter model, without a [cosmological constant](../../../../../../cosmological-constant.md).

At [matter-radiation equality](../../../../../../matter-radiation-equality.md), $a_{\rm eq}=\Omega_{r,0}/\Omega_{m,0}$. Since $\Omega_{r,0}+\Omega_{m,0}=1$,

$$
\tau_{\rm eq}=\frac{2\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}}{H_0\Omega_{m,0}}(\sqrt2-1),
\qquad
\tau_0=\frac{2}{H_0\Omega_{m,0}}(1-\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}).
$$

In units $c=1$, the comoving [particle horizon](../../../../../../particle-horizon.md) at equality is $\tau_{\rm eq}$; its physical size is $a_{\rm eq}\tau_{\rm eq}$. The [angular diameter distance](../../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) to the equality surface is $a_{\rm eq}(\tau_0-\tau_{\rm eq})$. Therefore the [horizon angle at matter-radiation equality](../../../../../../horizon-angle-at-matter-radiation-equality.md) corresponding to one horizon length is

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\rm hor}\simeq\frac{\tau_{\rm eq}}{\tau_0-\tau_{\rm eq}}
=\frac{(\sqrt2-1)\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}}{1-\sqrt{2\Omega_{r,0}}}}.
$$

For $\Omega_{r,0}=10^{-4}$, this is $4.20\times10^{-3}$ radians, or $0.241^\circ$, about $14.4$ arcminutes. If quoting the full diameter of a horizon-sized patch, the result is twice this, $0.481^\circ$. The small-angle approximation is excellent. Expanding in $\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}}\ll1$ would give $4.14\times10^{-3}$ radians; retaining both cosmic components near equality avoids an inaccurate pure-matter extrapolation there. The [scale factor](../../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) cancels between physical size and [angular diameter distance](../../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md), and $H_0$ cancels from the ratio.

If [cosmological recombination](../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) occurs only a modest expansion after equality, its causal patch still subtends a small part of the sky. Widely separated regions of the observed [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background.md) have nearly the same temperature even though their past [particle horizons](../../../../../../particle-horizon.md) did not overlap in the ordinary decelerating history. Local thermalization after the [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md) therefore cannot explain this large-angle uniformity. This is the [horizon problem](../../../../../../horizon-problem.md); inflation supplies an earlier connected region that can grow to encompass the observed sky.
