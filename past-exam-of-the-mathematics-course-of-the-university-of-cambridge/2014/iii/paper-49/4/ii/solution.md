<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In [matter domination](../../../../../../matter-domination.md), the [Einstein field equations](../../../../../../einstein-field-equations.md) with constant $\Phi$ give the [Newtonian-gauge matter density from a constant gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-gauge-matter-density-from-a-constant-gravitational-potential.md)

$$
-k^2\Phi-3\mathcal H^2\Phi=4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_c\delta_c
=\frac32\mathcal H^2\delta_c,
\qquad
\boxed{\delta_c=-2\Phi-\frac{2k^2}{3\mathcal H^2}\Phi}.
$$

Well inside the [Hubble radius](../../../../../../hubble-radius.md), the first term is negligible. Since $\mathcal H^2\propto a^{-1}$ and $\Phi$ is constant,

$$
\boxed{\delta_{c,\rm grow}\propto a}.
$$

Equivalently, the [matter-era growing and decaying density modes](../../../../../../matter-era-growing-and-decaying-density-modes.md) follow from $\delta_c''+\mathcal H\delta_c'-(3/2)\mathcal H^2\delta_c=0$: they are $\tau^2\propto a$ and $\tau^{-3}\propto a^{-3/2}$.

The lower panel of the preceding figure gives the three requested [density contrast](../../../../../../density-contrast.md) sketches in [Newtonian gauge in cosmology](../../../../../../newtonian-gauge.md). Their early superhorizon density is nearly constant, rather than proportional to $a^2$ in this gauge. A large-$k$ mode enters first and grows only approximately logarithmically during [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md). Once the rapid radiation forcing has subsided, $\delta_c''+\mathcal H\delta_c'\simeq0$ with $\mathcal H\simeq1/\tau$, so $\delta_c\simeq C+D\ln\tau\simeq C+D\ln a$. This is the [Mészáros effect](../../../../../../meszaros-effect.md); forcing around entry determines the coefficients. After equality its growing part becomes proportional to $a$.

A mode near $k_{\rm eq}$ begins substantial growth around equality. A small-$k$ mode keeps its superhorizon constant term until entering during [matter domination](../../../../../../matter-domination.md), then follows the same $a$ growth law. Their entry scale factors satisfy $a_{\rm ent}\propto k^{-1}$ during [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md) and $a_{\rm ent}\propto k^{-2}$ during [matter domination](../../../../../../matter-domination.md). At a common late time, the scaled amplitudes are therefore of order

$$
k^{3/2}\delta_c\propto
\begin{cases}
a\,k^2,&\mathcal H(a)\ll k\ll k_{\rm eq},\\
a\,\ln(k/k_{\rm eq}),&k\gg k_{\rm eq},
\end{cases}
$$

up to common dimensional constants and order-one matching terms. Thus the three late growth curves have the same logarithmic slope one as functions of $a$, but different amplitudes. These amplitude statements require the modes to have entered the [Hubble radius](../../../../../../hubble-radius.md); the full Newtonian-gauge formula above remains available for modes that have not.
