<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Use the following finite-length version of the [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md). Let $S=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}S_n$ be a graded algebra generated over an [Artinian ring](../../../../../artinian-ring.md) $A=S_0$ by finitely many homogeneous elements $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ of positive degrees $e_1,\ldots,e_r$. For a finitely generated nonnegatively graded $S$-module $M$, define its [Poincare series of a graded module](../../../../../poincare-series-of-a-graded-module.md) by

$$
P_M(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}\ell_A(M_n)t^n.
$$

Every component has finite length, and the theorem says

$$
\boxed{P_M(t)=\frac{f(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^r(1-t^{e_i})},\qquad f(t)\in\mathbb Z[t].}
$$

For a [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) whose grading is merely bounded below, the same statement holds with a Laurent-polynomial numerator. When the generators all have degree one, the denominator is $(1-t)^r$.

Here is an induction proof. An [Artinian ring](../../../../../artinian-ring.md) is [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) by question 1, so the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) makes $S$ [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). If there are no positive-degree generators, $S=A$ and a finite homogeneous generating set for $M$ occupies only finitely many degrees. Each component is a finite [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over the [Artinian ring](../../../../../artinian-ring.md) $A$, hence has finite [length of a module](../../../../../length-of-a-module.md), and $P_M$ is a polynomial.

For $r>0$, put $x=x_r$, $e=e_r$, $N=\{m\in M:xm=0\}$ and $C=M/xM$. Both are finitely generated graded [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) annihilated by $x$, hence [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over $S/(x)$, which is generated by the first $r-1$ homogeneous elements. Their multiplication exact sequence is

$$
0\longrightarrow N(-e)\longrightarrow M(-e)\mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{x}}M\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow0.
$$

Here $M(-e)_n=M_{n-e}$. Additivity of length degree by degree gives

$$
\boxed{(1-t^e)P_M(t)=P_C(t)-t^eP_N(t).}
$$

This is the [Hilbert series multiplication exact sequence](../../../../../hilbert-series-multiplication-exact-sequence.md). By induction, the right-hand side has denominator $\prod_{i<r}(1-t^{e_i})$. Division by $1-t^e$ completes the proof of the [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md). The finite-component and finite-generation claims also follow from the finite set of positive-degree algebra and [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) generators; no analytic convergence of a series is involved.

For the local invariant, use the usual [Noetherian local ring](../../../../../noetherian-local-ring.md) hypothesis of [Hilbert–Samuel growth dimension](../../../../../hilbert-samuel-growth-dimension.md). Locality alone does not guarantee finite lengths or polynomial growth; the printed question leaves this finiteness assumption implicit. For instance, in the [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) of the [polynomial ring](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) $k[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$ at $(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$, the [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ has the infinitely many independent classes of the variables, so its length is not finite. Write $\mathfrak m=P$ and $\kappa=R/\mathfrak m$. Its [associated graded ring](../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md)

$$
G=\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1}
$$

is generated over $\kappa$ in degree one, because $\mathfrak m$ is finitely generated. Thus its [Hilbert series](../../../../../hilbert-series.md) is rational with a denominator that is a power of $1-t$. After cancelling factors, write

$$
P_G(t)=\frac{q(t)}{(1-t)^d},\qquad q(1)\neq0.
$$

**Define $d(R)=d$, the pole order at $t=1$**, with $d=0$ when $P_G$ is a polynomial.

Equivalently, the [Hilbert–Samuel function](../../../../../hilbert-samuel-function.md)

$$
H_R(n)=\ell_R(R/\mathfrak m^{n+1})=\sum_{i=0}^n\dim_\kappa(\mathfrak m^i/\mathfrak m^{i+1})
$$

has generating series $P_G(t)/(1-t)$ and eventually agrees with a polynomial of degree $d$. Indeed, coefficients of $(1-t)^{-d-1}$ are $\binom{n+d}{d}$, and multiplication by $q(t)$ leaves leading term $q(1)n^d/d!$. Consequently **$d(R)$ is the degree of the cumulative [Hilbert-Samuel polynomial](../../../../../hilbert-samuel-polynomial.md)**, rather than the degree of the individual graded-component function; the latter has degree $d-1$ when $d>0$. This pole/growth invariant also equals [Krull dimension](../../../../../krull-dimension.md) by the local dimension theorem, although that theorem is not required to define it here.

For an example, take $R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)}$. Its [associated graded ring](../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md) is $k[x,y]$ with the ordinary degree grading: degree $n$ has the $n+1$ monomials $x^iy^{n-i}$ as a basis. Therefore

$$
P_G(t)=\frac1{(1-t)^2},\qquad H_R(n)=\binom{n+2}{2},\qquad\boxed{d\bigl(k[x,y]_{(x,y)}\bigr)=2.}
$$

The length formula also follows directly by counting monomials of total degree at most $n$ in $R/(x,y)^{n+1}$.
