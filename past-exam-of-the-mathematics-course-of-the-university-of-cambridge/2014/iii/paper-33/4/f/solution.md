↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

In the original PDF's probability plot, most points lie close to the diagonal. The quantile plot likewise agrees well over most of the range, but the largest empirical flow, around $68.5$, is well above its fitted quantile, around $66$. The density plot captures the strongly decreasing right-skewed bulk of the excesses. The [return level plot](../../../../../../return-level-plot.md) shows a broadly reasonable fit to most observations, with one conspicuously high extreme and increasingly wide uncertainty at long [return periods](../../../../../../return-period.md). Consequently the [generalized Pareto distribution](../../../../../../generalized-pareto-distribution.md) is a reasonable first approximation, but its most extreme tail is less convincingly represented.

Reading the return-level curve at the 100-year tick gives

$$
\boxed{\widehat z_{100}\approx67\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}},\qquad
95\%\ \mathrm{CI}\ \text{approximately }(65.5,68.5)\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

These are approximate graphical readings, not parameter-based calculations; rounding to whole units would give about $67$ with an interval roughly $65$ to $69$. The two blue curves are uncertainty bounds for the fitted [return level](../../../../../../return-level.md), not prediction bounds for individual observations. The daily-data [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) assumption and the stability of the fit as the threshold changes should be checked, for example by examining clusters of high flows and using [declustering of extremes](../../../../../../declustering-of-extremes.md) where necessary. A 100-year level also extrapolates beyond the 25-year record.
