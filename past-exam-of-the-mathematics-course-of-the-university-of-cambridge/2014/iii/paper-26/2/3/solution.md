<h1 id="2/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For the real-valued variables here, take a $\mathcal G$-measurable version of $X=\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]$. Since the variables are bounded, the [conditional expectation](../../../../../../conditional-expectation.md) defining identity extends to the bounded $\mathcal G$-measurable multiplier $X$, giving

$$
\mathbb E[XY]=\mathbb E\bigl[X\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]\bigr]=\mathbb EX^2.
$$

The [equality case for conditional second moments](../../../../../../equality-case-for-conditional-second-moments.md) now gives

$$
\mathbb E(Y-X)^2=\mathbb EY^2-2\mathbb E[XY]+\mathbb EX^2
=\mathbb EY^2-\mathbb EX^2=0.
$$

A nonnegative [random variable](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md) with zero expectation vanishes with probability one. **Thus $X=Y$ as an [almost sure equality](../../../../../../almost-sure-equality.md).** The same proof works for square-integrable variables; boundedness is more than is needed.
