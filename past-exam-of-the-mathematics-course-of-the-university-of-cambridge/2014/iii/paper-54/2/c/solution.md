<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At the initially resting instant $K_{ij}=0$, and the cold-fluid assumption removes the [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) stress. Sum the $xx$ and $yy$ components of the [magnetized-fluid tensor virial theorem](../../../../../../magnetized-fluid-tensor-virial-theorem.md). The magnetic and gravitational traces are

$$
M_{xx}+M_{yy}=-\frac{B_z^2}{\mu_0},\qquad W_{xx}+W_{yy}=\frac{g_z^2}{4\pi G}.
$$

Since $I_\perp=I_{xx}+I_{yy}$ and $g_z=-\partial_z\Phi$, this yields the [horizontal virial balance of a cold magnetized fluid](../../../../../../horizontal-virial-balance-of-a-cold-magnetized-fluid.md):

$$
\boxed{\frac12\ddot I_\perp=\frac1{4\pi G}\int\left[\frac{4\pi GB_z^2}{\mu_0}-(\partial_z\Phi)^2\right]d\tau.}
$$

Being at rest sets the instantaneous [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) to zero; it does not assert equilibrium or zero acceleration. The fields throughout space contribute to this stress integral, including their vacuum exterior.
