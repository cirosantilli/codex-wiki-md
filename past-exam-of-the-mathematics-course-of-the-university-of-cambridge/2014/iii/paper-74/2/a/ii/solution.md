<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For [velocity-only forcing in averaged oscillator equations](../../../../../../../velocity-only-forcing-in-averaged-oscillator-equations.md), $f=f(\dot y)$, the [oscillation phase](../../../../../../../phase-waves.md) average vanishes: $f(-R\sin\phi)\cos\phi$ is a periodic total [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md), using an antiderivative of $f$. Hence $\theta_T=0$ to first order, although the [amplitude](../../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) may change.

In the cubic case, $f=-R^3\sin^3\phi$, so

$$
R_T=R^3\langle\sin^4\phi\rangle=\frac38R^3.
$$

With $R(0)=R_0>0$, integration gives

$$
\boxed{R(T)=\frac{R_0}{\sqrt{1-3R_0^2T/4}},\qquad \theta(T)=\theta_0}.
$$

For positive $\varepsilon$ this is [cubic velocity anti-damping](../../../../../../../cubic-velocity-anti-damping.md), not damping: the exact [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) is $d(\dot y^2+y^2)/(2dt)=\varepsilon\dot y^4\geq0$. The slow solution grows and formally diverges at $T=4/(3R_0^2)$. The weakly nonlinear approximation loses validity before its [amplitude](../../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) becomes so large that $\varepsilon R^2$ is not small; its divergence is not a controlled prediction of the exact late-time solution. If $R_0=0$, the exact zero solution should be used separately.
