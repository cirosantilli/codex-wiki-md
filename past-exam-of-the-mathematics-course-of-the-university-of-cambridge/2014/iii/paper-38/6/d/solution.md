↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Part (c) gives $\mathbb E M_t\leq0$. Since $Y_tX_t\geq0$, its integrated identity implies

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^tY_sc_sds\leq Y_0X_0
$$

for every finite $t$. The cumulative [consumption](../../../../../../consumption.md) increases with $t$, so the [monotone convergence theorem](../../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E\int_0^\infty Y_tc_tdt\leq Y_0X_0.}
$$

This is the infinite-horizon [state-price budget constraint](../../../../../../state-price-budget-constraint.md). No terminal-wealth convergence or vanishing assumption is needed for this inequality.
