For a [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathfrak g$, write $[A,B]$ for the linear span of brackets with one argument in each indicated subspace. The three definitions are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{abelian:}&\quad[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=0,\\
\text{solvable:}&\quad\mathfrak g^{(r)}=0\text{ for some }r,\quad\mathfrak g^{(0)}=\mathfrak g,\quad\mathfrak g^{(j+1)}=[\mathfrak g^{(j)},\mathfrak g^{(j)}],\\
\text{nilpotent:}&\quad\gamma_s(\mathfrak g)=0\text{ for some }s,\quad\gamma_1=\mathfrak g,\quad\gamma_{j+1}=[\mathfrak g,\gamma_j].
\end{aligned}
$$

These are respectively an [Abelian Lie algebra](../../../../../abelian-lie-algebra.md), a [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md) and a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md). The second and third sequences are the [derived series of a Lie algebra](../../../../../derived-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md) and the [lower central series of a Lie algebra](../../../../../lower-central-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md).

An [Abelian Lie algebra](../../../../../abelian-lie-algebra.md) has $\gamma_2=0$, and a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md) is a [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md): induction gives $\mathfrak g^{(j)}\subseteq\gamma_{j+1}$. Thus **all the implications are generated by**

$$
\boxed{\text{abelian}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{nilpotent}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{solvable}.}
$$

None of the reverse implications holds. The [Heisenberg Lie algebra](../../../../../heisenberg-lie-algebra.md) with basis $x,y,z$ and $[x,y]=z$, all other basic brackets zero, is nonabelian but has $\gamma_2=\mathbb Cz$, $\gamma_3=0$. The two-dimensional [affine Lie algebra of the line](../../../../../affine-lie-algebra-of-the-line.md) with $[h,e]=e$ has $\mathfrak g^{(1)}=\mathbb Ce$ and $\mathfrak g^{(2)}=0$, but $\gamma_j=\mathbb Ce$ for every $j\ge2$. It is solvable and neither nilpotent nor abelian. These two examples answer all six ordered-pair comparisons.

The [Lie theorem](../../../../../lie-s-theorem.md) says that a finite-dimensional [Lie algebra representation](../../../../../lie-algebra-representation.md) of a finite-dimensional [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md) over an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) of [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) zero has a common [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) whenever its representation space is nonzero. Equivalently it admits an invariant [complete flag](../../../../../complete-flag.md), or simultaneous upper triangularization. Here the field may be taken to be $\mathbb C$; these hypotheses are essential.

To prove the common-eigenvector assertion, induct on $\dim\mathfrak g$. The zero algebra is immediate. Since $\mathfrak g$ is nonzero and solvable, its [derived algebra](../../../../../derived-algebra.md) is proper. Choose a codimension-one [Lie algebra ideal](../../../../../ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) $\mathfrak h$ containing it, and write $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathbb Cx$. The [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathfrak h$ is solvable, so induction supplies $v\ne0$ and a [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) $\lambda:\mathfrak h\to\mathbb C$ with $hv=\lambda(h)v$.

Consider the finite-dimensional cyclic subspace $W=\operatorname{span}\{v,xv,x^2v,\ldots\}$. Until the first [linear dependence](../../../../../linear-dependence.md), these powers form a basis. The identity

$$
hx^jv=x(hx^{j-1}v)+[h,x]x^{j-1}v
$$

and $[h,x]\in\mathfrak h$ show by induction, simultaneously for all $h\in\mathfrak h$, that $W$ is $\mathfrak h$-invariant and that $h$ is upper triangular on it with every diagonal entry $\lambda(h)$. It is also $x$-invariant by construction. Hence

$$
0=\operatorname{tr}_W[x,h]=(\dim W)\lambda([x,h]).
$$

The [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of a [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) is zero, and [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) zero gives $\lambda([x,h])=0$.

The simultaneous [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) $E_\lambda=\{w:hw=\lambda(h)w\text{ for every }h\in\mathfrak h\}$ is nonzero and $x$-invariant, since

$$
h(xw)=x(hw)+[h,x]w=\lambda(h)xw.
$$

Over an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md), $x|_{E_\lambda}$ has an [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md), which is therefore a common [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) for all of $\mathfrak g$. This finishes induction. Apply the same assertion to the [quotient representation](../../../../../quotient-representation.md) by its invariant line, and then to successive quotients. A [basis](../../../../../basis.md) adapted to the resulting [complete flag](../../../../../complete-flag.md) gives the stated [simultaneous triangularization of a Lie algebra representation](../../../../../simultaneous-triangularization-of-a-lie-algebra-representation.md), completing the proof of the [Lie theorem](../../../../../lie-s-theorem.md).

A [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md) is a [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md), so the inclusion $\mathfrak g\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ satisfies the [Lie theorem](../../../../../lie-s-theorem.md) and is upper triangular in a suitable [basis](../../../../../basis.md), for finite-dimensional complex $V$.

This is insufficient to prove the [Engel theorem](../../../../../engel-s-theorem.md). Its matrix version starts with a [Lie subalgebra](../../../../../lie-subalgebra.md) of [nilpotent endomorphisms](../../../../../nilpotent-linear-map.md) and concludes that they are simultaneously strictly upper triangular; its abstract version concludes nilpotence from nilpotence of every [Adjoint representation](../../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra.md) endomorphism. Merely [upper triangular matrices](../../../../../upper-triangular-matrix.md) can have nonzero diagonal entries: the one-dimensional algebra $\mathbb CI_V$ is an [Abelian Lie algebra](../../../../../abelian-lie-algebra.md) and a [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md), but acts by a nonnilpotent identity matrix. This is [nilpotent Lie algebras need not act nilpotently](../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebras-need-not-act-nilpotently.md). Moreover, in the abstract [Engel theorem](../../../../../engel-s-theorem.md) nilpotence of the algebra is a conclusion, so assuming it first to invoke the [Lie theorem](../../../../../lie-s-theorem.md) would be circular. **Abstract nilpotence and nilpotence of each representing matrix are different conditions.**
