<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

In the [Lambda-CDM model](../../../../../lambda-cdm-model.md), nearly Gaussian primordial [density contrasts](../../../../../density-contrast.md) grow under gravity within an expanding universe containing [cold dark matter](../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md), ordinary [baryons](../../../../../baryon.md) and a [cosmological constant](../../../../../cosmological-constant.md). The [cold dark matter](../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) is effectively collisionless and has negligible pressure on galactic scales. Before recombination, [baryons](../../../../../baryon.md) are coupled to the photon fluid: [radiation pressure](../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) and acoustic oscillations prevent their perturbations from behaving like pressureless matter. After recombination they can fall into the gravitational potentials already established by [dark matter](../../../../../dark-matter.md), subject to gas pressure and the [Jeans mass](../../../../../jeans-mass.md).

For small [density contrasts](../../../../../density-contrast.md), evolution is linear. On pressure-free scales the growing mode is multiplied by the [linear growth factor](../../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) $D(t)$, with $D\propto a$ during matter domination. The [cosmological density power spectrum](../../../../../matter-power-spectrum.md) can be written

$$
P(k,t)=D^2(t)P_{\rm prim}(k)T^2(k).
$$

Here $T(k)$ is the [cosmological transfer function](../../../../../cosmological-transfer-function.md). For nearly scale-invariant initial conditions, the large-scale matter spectrum behaves approximately as $P\propto k$, whereas well inside the [matter-radiation equality scale](../../../../../matter-radiation-equality-scale.md) it falls approximately as $k^{-3}\log^2 k$, until the microscopic dark-matter cutoff matters. This fall of the dimensional $P(k)$ does not imply less fluctuation power on every smaller mass scale: the power per logarithmic wavenumber is $\Delta^2=k^3P/(2\pi^2)$, and the [smoothed matter density variance](../../../../../smoothed-matter-density-variance.md) is obtained by integrating it against a mass-dependent window. Over the relevant cold-dark-matter hierarchy, smaller mass windows generally have larger variance.

The [hierarchical galaxy formation](../../../../../hierarchical-galaxy-formation.md) picture follows: fluctuations on small mass scales typically reach the nonlinear collapse threshold first, while larger objects assemble later through accretion and [dark-matter halo mergers](../../../../../dark-matter-halo-merger.md). It is a statistical ordering, not a claim that every small object precedes every large rare peak. When $|\delta|$ becomes order unity, the [linear growth factor](../../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) is no longer a solution for the local density. Collisionless [dark matter](../../../../../dark-matter.md) develops multistream regions and bound [dark matter halos](../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md); [phase mixing](../../../../../phase-mixing.md) and [violent relaxation](../../../../../violent-relaxation.md) redistribute orbital energies, and virialized structures approximately obey the [virial theorem](../../../../../virial-theorem.md).

[Baryons](../../../../../baryon.md) have an additional nonlinear route. Infall and shocks convert bulk kinetic energy into thermal energy, with characteristic [virial temperature](../../../../../virial-temperature.md)

$$
k_BT_{\rm vir}\sim\frac{\mu m_pGM_h}{2R_h}.
$$

Unlike collisionless [dark matter](../../../../../dark-matter.md), the gas can lose this energy through [radiative cooling](../../../../../radiative-cooling.md). The [optically thin gas cooling time](../../../../../optically-thin-gas-cooling-time.md) is the thermal-energy density divided by the radiative loss rate, for example

$$
t_{\rm cool}=\frac{3n_{\rm tot}k_BT/2}{n_en_i\Lambda(T)},\qquad
 t_{\rm dyn}\sim(G\rho_h)^{-1/2}.
$$

The density convention in $\Lambda$ must agree with the denominator; the [astrophysical cooling function](../../../../../astrophysical-cooling-function.md) can also be defined using $n_H^2$ instead. The [cooling criterion for galaxy formation](../../../../../cooling-criterion-for-galaxy-formation.md) compares this time with the collapse or supply time. Rapidly cooling gas loses pressure support, contracts and can form stars. Slowly cooling gas remains in a hot atmosphere. Stable virial shocks are not obligatory in every low-mass system: gas can also arrive in cold streams and cool while being accreted.

[Angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) prevents indefinite radial contraction. [Tidal torque theory](../../../../../tidal-torque-theory.md) supplies an initial halo spin, and later mergers change it. If gas radiates energy while retaining much of its [specific angular momentum](../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md), it settles into a rotationally supported [galactic disk](../../../../../galactic-disk.md) rather than reaching the centre. The relation $j\sim Rv_c$ explains why modest halo spin can set a disk radius much smaller than its [virial radius of a dark-matter halo](../../../../../virial-radius-of-a-dark-matter-halo.md). Torques, bars and gravitational encounters can transport [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) outwards and feed central concentrations; [radiative cooling](../../../../../radiative-cooling.md) alone does not remove it.

[Galaxy mergers](../../../../../galaxy-merger.md) alter stellar structure as well as assembling mass. A [major galaxy merger](../../../../../major-galaxy-merger.md) can strongly disturb or destroy an existing [galactic disk](../../../../../galactic-disk.md), randomizing stellar orbits and creating a spheroid through [violent relaxation](../../../../../violent-relaxation.md). A [gas-rich galaxy merger](../../../../../gas-rich-galaxy-merger.md) also permits dissipation, inflow and a burst of [star formation](../../../../../star-formation.md); gas left over or accreted afterwards can rebuild a [galactic disk](../../../../../galactic-disk.md). [Minor galaxy mergers](../../../../../minor-galaxy-merger.md) add stars to outer components, thicken disks and grow bulges. [Dry galaxy mergers](../../../../../dry-galaxy-merger.md) add stellar mass and can increase size without much new [star formation](../../../../../star-formation.md). Halo merging therefore does not imply instantaneous merging of its galaxies: satellite orbital decay requires [Chandrasekhar dynamical friction](../../../../../chandrasekhar-dynamical-friction.md) and can take a substantial time.

The [atomic and molecular cooling thresholds for galaxy formation](../../../../../atomic-and-molecular-cooling-thresholds-for-galaxy-formation.md) supply a lower characteristic scale. Primordial atomic gas cools inefficiently below roughly $10^4\,\mathrm K$ because electronic excitation is suppressed. The corresponding halo mass is of order $10^8M_\odot$ at a redshift of order ten, with approximate dependence $M_{\rm atomic}\propto(1+z)^{-3/2}$ at fixed threshold temperature. [Molecular hydrogen](../../../../../molecular-hydrogen.md) can cool gas at hundreds of kelvin and permit smaller early objects, provided it forms and survives dissociating radiation. [Metal-line cooling](../../../../../metal-line-cooling.md) changes these thresholds after enrichment. Thus the atomic threshold is not an absolute minimum mass for all stellar systems.

At the other end, sufficiently massive [dark matter halos](../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) have high [virial temperatures](../../../../../virial-temperature.md) and low-density hot gas. Above the strong atomic-line-cooling interval, [thermal bremsstrahlung](../../../../../thermal-bremsstrahlung.md) has $\Lambda\propto T^{1/2}$. At comparable halo gas density, $t_{\rm cool}\propto T^{1/2}\propto M_h^{1/3}$, while $t_{\rm dyn}$ depends mainly on formation density. Cooling therefore becomes less able to condense all the gas within the available time. This [upper galaxy mass from gas cooling](../../../../../upper-galaxy-mass-from-gas-cooling.md) argument selects galaxy-sized condensations, broadly halo masses around $10^{12}$–$10^{13}M_\odot$ in simple low-redshift estimates, rather than single luminous galaxies containing every baryon in a group or cluster. Its numerical scale depends on epoch, metallicity and gas profile. Subsequent [galaxy mergers](../../../../../galaxy-merger.md) can build larger stellar systems; cooling is not an absolute upper bound on their final mass.

Finally, a [Press-Schechter halo mass function](../../../../../press-schechter-halo-mass-function.md) has many low-mass objects and a steep high-mass cutoff, while the [luminosity function](../../../../../luminosity-function-astronomy.md) of galaxies also has a faint component and a bright cutoff, often described by a [Schechter function](../../../../../schechter-function.md). The two shapes are related through the [halo-to-galaxy luminosity mapping](../../../../../halo-to-galaxy-luminosity-mapping.md), not by identifying luminosity with total halo mass. In the idealized one-central-galaxy, no-scatter limit,

$$
\Phi(L)=\frac{dn_h}{dM_h}\left|\frac{dM_h}{dL}\right|.
$$

If $dn_h/dM_h\propto M_h^{-a}$ and $L\propto M_h^p$, then $\Phi(L)\propto L^{-1-(a-1)/p}$. A constant conversion gives similar shapes. Actual [stellar feedback](../../../../../stellar-feedback.md), inefficient low-temperature cooling and [reionization](../../../../../reionization.md) suppress faint galaxies relative to small haloes, while long cooling times and [active-galactic-nucleus feedback](../../../../../active-galactic-nucleus-feedback.md) suppress luminosity at large halo masses. Satellites, scatter, stellar populations and dust further affect the correspondence. **The halo hierarchy supplies the gravitational framework; cooling, [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and feedback determine which parts become luminous galaxies.**
