<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At fixed mass, decreasing redshift increases the [linear growth factor](../../../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) and reduces the [halo peak height](../../../../../../halo-peak-height.md) $\nu$. During matter domination $\dot D/D=H$ also falls as the universe expands. As long as $\nu>1$, both effects reduce the positive abundance-growth rate, so **$t_d$ grows towards lower redshift**. In the very rare-tail limit $t_d\propto D^2/H\propto(1+z)^{-7/2}$ provides the approximate trend.

There is a limit to calling this an increase time. At $\nu=1$, the fixed-mass [Press-Schechter halo mass function](../../../../../../press-schechter-halo-mass-function.md) reaches its maximum as a function of time; its logarithmic growth rate vanishes and $t_d$ diverges. For $\nu<1$, the derivative becomes negative, corresponding to net transfer of small objects into more massive systems, so the signed inverse is no longer a positive doubling or increase time. At still later epochs the [cosmological constant](../../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) further suppresses the [linear growth factor](../../../../../../linear-growth-factor.md). These qualifications prevent extrapolating the positive high-redshift growth trend indefinitely.
