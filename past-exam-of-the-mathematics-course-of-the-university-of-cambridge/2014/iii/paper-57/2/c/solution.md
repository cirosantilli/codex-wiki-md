<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For fixed $\omega^2>0$ and weak [gas drag](../../../../../../gas-drag.md), expand a wave root as $s=s_0+\epsilon s_1+O(\epsilon^2)$ with $s_0=\pm i\omega$. At order $\epsilon$, the [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md) yields

$$
(3s_0^2+\omega^2)s_1+2\Omega s_0^2+\Omega(\omega^2-\Omega^2)=0.
$$

Since $s_0^2=-\omega^2$, this reduces to $-2\omega^2s_1-\Omega(\omega^2+\Omega^2)=0$. The [weak-drag dust density waves](../../../../../../weak-drag-dust-density-waves.md) therefore have

$$
\boxed{s_\pm=\pm i\omega-\epsilon\frac{\Omega(\omega^2+\Omega^2)}{2\omega^2}+O(\epsilon^2).}
$$

The first-order real part is negative: the two density waves are **weakly damped**, with their frequencies unchanged to first order. [Gas drag](../../../../../../gas-drag.md) removes their perturbation energy relative to the fixed gas flow. The expansion is an asymptotic result at fixed positive $\omega^2$; it is not uniform close to $\omega^2=0$, where the nominal damping can become comparable to the oscillation frequency and a different scaling is needed.
