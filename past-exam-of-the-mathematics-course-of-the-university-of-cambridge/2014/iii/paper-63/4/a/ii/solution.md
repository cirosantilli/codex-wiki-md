<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an excited eigenstate, $E_i-E_0\geq\Delta$. The matrix-element identity in condition (i), together with the two-sided Fourier cutoff, gives

$$
\langle\phi_i|A^{(Z)}|\phi_0\rangle=\widehat w(E_i-E_0)\langle\phi_i|h_Z|\phi_0\rangle=0.
$$

The reverse [matrix element](../../../../../../../matrix-element.md) has frequency $E_0-E_i\leq-\Delta$ and also vanishes. Equivalently, $A^{(Z)}$ is Hermitian because $h_Z$ is Hermitian and $w$ is real, so the two elements are conjugate. Thus

$$
\boxed{\langle\phi_0|A^{(Z)}|\phi_i\rangle=\langle\phi_i|A^{(Z)}|\phi_0\rangle=0\quad(i>0).}
$$

The [spectral gap](../../../../../../../spectral-gap.md) eliminates precisely the couplings needed to make the unique [ground state](../../../../../../../ground-state.md) an [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of every filtered term. Couplings between excited states with smaller energy differences can remain; the [spectral filter](../../../../../../../spectral-filter.md) need not diagonalize the whole operator.
