<h1 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

One standard form of the [Penrose singularity theorem](../../../../../../../penrose-singularity-theorem.md) assumes a time-oriented [globally hyperbolic spacetime](../../../../../../../globally-hyperbolic-spacetime.md) with a noncompact [Cauchy hypersurface](../../../../../../../cauchy-surface.md), the [null convergence condition](../../../../../../../null-convergence-condition.md) $R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0$ for every [null vector](../../../../../../../null-vector.md) $k$, and a closed future [trapped surface](../../../../../../../trapped-surface.md). It concludes **future null geodesic incompleteness**: some future-inextendible [null geodesic](../../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) has a finite upper endpoint of its [affine parameter](../../../../../../../affine-parameter.md).

The focusing mechanism is the [Null Raychaudhuri equation](../../../../../../../null-raychaudhuri-equation.md). The normal generators have zero [null twist](../../../../../../../null-twist.md), so

$$
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}=-\frac12\theta^2-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}-R_{ab}k^ak^b\leq-\frac12\theta^2.
$$

An initial $\theta_0<0$ therefore gives a [conjugate point to a spacelike surface](../../../../../../../conjugate-point-to-a-spacelike-surface.md) within affine distance at most $2/|\theta_0|$, assuming the generator can be continued that far. Such a generator ceases to lie on the [achronal boundary](../../../../../../../achronal-boundary.md) after its first focal point. Compactness of the [trapped surface](../../../../../../../trapped-surface.md) supplies a uniform bound for all normalized initial null normals. Future completeness would consequently make its future boundary compact. Projection along timelike curves to a connected [Cauchy hypersurface](../../../../../../../cauchy-surface.md) is injective on the [achronal boundary](../../../../../../../achronal-boundary.md) and has open image. Compactness makes the image closed as well; the nonempty image must therefore be the entire hypersurface, contradicting its noncompactness. This explains why the global assumptions supplement local focusing.

For [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](../../../../../../../reissner-nordstrom-spacetime.md), the Maxwell [stress-energy tensor](../../../../../../../stress-energy-tensor.md) satisfies the [null energy condition](../../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md); the [Einstein field equations](../../../../../../../einstein-field-equations.md) imply the required [null convergence condition](../../../../../../../null-convergence-condition.md). The spheres in the band just found are closed and trapped. Apply the theorem to a [maximal Cauchy development](../../../../../../../maximal-cauchy-development.md) with a noncompact [Cauchy hypersurface](../../../../../../../cauchy-surface.md) and containing one such sphere. That [globally hyperbolic spacetime](../../../../../../../globally-hyperbolic-spacetime.md) must be future [null-geodesically incomplete](../../../../../../../null-geodesic-incompleteness.md).

The [Penrose theorem at a Cauchy horizon](../../../../../../../penrose-theorem-at-a-cauchy-horizon.md) needs care: the full maximal analytic [Reissner-Nordstrom spacetime](../../../../../../../reissner-nordstrom-spacetime.md) has inner [Cauchy horizons](../../../../../../../cauchy-horizon.md) and is not globally hyperbolic. It does not satisfy every hypothesis of the displayed theorem. In the exact solution some incomplete geodesics of the globally hyperbolic development reach a smoothly extendible [Cauchy horizon](../../../../../../../cauchy-horizon.md) in finite [affine parameter](../../../../../../../affine-parameter.md). The theorem asserts incompleteness of the development, not that every such endpoint is a curvature singularity. The separate curvature singularity at $r=0$ does not justify silently dropping the theorem's global hypothesis.
