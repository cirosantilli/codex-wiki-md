<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A future [trapped surface](../../../../../../../trapped-surface.md) is a closed spacelike two-surface for which both future orthogonal [null expansions](../../../../../../../null-expansion.md) are strictly negative. Multiplying a future [null vector](../../../../../../../null-vector.md) normal by a positive function multiplies its [null expansion](../../../../../../../null-expansion.md) by that function, so the signs are invariant under allowed normalization changes.

Here $\theta_X<0$ for every $r_0>0$. Thus the [Reissner-Nordstrom trapped spheres](../../../../../../../reissner-nordstrom-trapped-spheres.md) are exactly those with $f(r_0)<0$. Since $r_+>r_->0$,

$$
\boxed{S\text{ is future trapped precisely when }r_-<r_0<r_+.}
$$

At either horizon $\theta_Y=0$, so the spheres are marginal, rather than strictly [trapped surfaces](../../../../../../../trapped-surface.md). For $r_0>r_+$ or $0<r_0<r_-$, $\theta_Y>0$ and this future-trapping condition fails. In particular, being inside the outer [event horizon](../../../../../../../event-horizon.md) alone is insufficient to make every sphere trapped in the charged solution.
