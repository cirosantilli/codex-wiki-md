<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The inverse [Kerr metric](../../../../../../../kerr-metric.md) in [ingoing Kerr coordinates](../../../../../../../ingoing-kerr-coordinates.md) gives the raised normal to a surface of constant $r$:

$$
n^a=g^{ar}=\frac1\Sigma\left(A\,\partial_v+\Delta\,\partial_r+a\,\partial_\chi\right)^a,\qquad n^2=g^{rr}=\frac\Delta\Sigma.
$$

On $r=r_+$, $\Delta=0$, so the normal is null and tangent to the horizon. It reduces to

$$
n^a\big|_H=\frac{r_+^2+a^2}{\Sigma_H}\left(k^a+\frac a{r_+^2+a^2}m^a\right).
$$

A constant linear combination of the two [Killing vector fields](../../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) is again a [Killing vector field](../../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md). Therefore $k+\Omega_Hm$ is normal to this [null hypersurface](../../../../../../../null-hypersurface.md), establishing that it is a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../../killing-horizon.md), with [Kerr horizon angular velocity](../../../../../../../kerr-horizon-angular-velocity.md)

$$
\boxed{\Omega_H=\frac a{r_+^2+a^2}=\frac a{2Mr_+}.}
$$

The second equality uses $\Delta(r_+)=0$. This normal calculation proves hypersurface orthogonality on the horizon, rather than merely proving that the proposed vector happens to have zero norm there.
