<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [Grassmann variable](../../../../../../grassmann-variable.md) is an odd generator of a [Grassmann algebra](../../../../../../grassmann-algebra.md): $\eta_i\eta_j=-\eta_j\eta_i$, so $\eta_i^2=0$. For one generator, any function is $f(\eta)=a+b\eta$. The [Berezin integral](../../../../../../berezin-integral.md) is the linear operation

$$
\boxed{\int d\eta\,1=0,\qquad\int d\eta\,\eta=1},
\qquad \int d\eta\,f(\eta)=b.
$$

Thus integration extracts a coefficient, rather than assigning a length or volume. For many generators it extracts the coefficient of the highest-degree monomial with the sign fixed by the order of the measure. Odd coefficients and [Grassmann derivatives](../../../../../../grassmann-derivative.md) must retain their order; exchanging two odd objects changes the sign.

This operation is invariant under odd translations, because a translation only changes terms of lower degree. For an invertible ordinary matrix $S$ and $\eta=S\xi$, the [Grassmann change-of-variables formula](../../../../../../grassmann-change-of-variables-formula.md) is

$$
D\eta=(\det S)^{-1}D\xi.
$$

The inverse [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md), rather than the ordinary commuting-variable Jacobian, compensates for the factor $\det S$ multiplying the top monomial. Integration agrees with the appropriate ordered [Grassmann derivatives](../../../../../../grassmann-derivative.md), but the orientation must be specified when combining barred and unbarred variables.
