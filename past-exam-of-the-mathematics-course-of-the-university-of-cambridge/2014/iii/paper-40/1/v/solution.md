<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Put $q^a=(E,0,0,k)$ with $E=\sqrt{k^2+m^2}$. An orthonormal real basis of [polarization vectors](../../../../../../polarization-vector.md) is

$$
\boxed{\epsilon_1^a=(0,1,0,0),\quad
\epsilon_2^a=(0,0,1,0),\quad
\epsilon_L^a=\left(\frac{k}{m},0,0,\frac{E}{m}\right)}.
$$

Each [polarization vector](../../../../../../polarization-vector.md) obeys $q_a\epsilon_\lambda^a=0$. The [Minkowski metric](../../../../../../minkowski-metric.md) gives $\epsilon_\lambda\cdot\epsilon_{\lambda'}=\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, since $E^2-k^2=m^2$. The first two are transverse to the spatial [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md); the [longitudinal polarization of a massive vector boson](../../../../../../longitudinal-polarization-of-a-massive-vector-boson.md) is spatially longitudinal but still orthogonal to the full [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md). The [polarization sum for a massive vector boson](../../../../../../polarization-sum-for-a-massive-vector-boson.md) is

$$
\sum_{\lambda=1,2,L}\epsilon_\lambda^a\epsilon_\lambda^b
=\eta^{ab}+\frac{q^aq^b}{m^2}\qquad(q^2=-m^2).
$$

In the rest frame all three [polarization vectors](../../../../../../polarization-vector.md) are spatial unit vectors. Their three independent positive-norm states are the physical [spin](../../../../../../spin.md) states of a massive spin-one particle.
