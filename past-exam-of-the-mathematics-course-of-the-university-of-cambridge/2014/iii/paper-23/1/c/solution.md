<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $t(\tau)=\Delta(\tau)/\Delta(2\tau)$. For $\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_0(2)$, the integral [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $\gamma'=\begin{pmatrix}a&2b\\c/2&d\end{pmatrix}$ has [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) one and satisfies $2\gamma\tau=\gamma'(2\tau)$. Both discriminants acquire the same factor $(c\tau+d)^{12}$, so $t$ is invariant. It has neither zeros nor [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) in the half-plane.

Reduction modulo two gives index three, with two [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) classes, infinity and zero, of [cusp widths](../../../../../../width-of-a-cusp.md) one and two. These can also be found from the orbits of upper triangular [matrices](../../../../../../matrix.md) on primitive columns modulo two. Compactness follows from the finite-index argument in part (a).

At infinity $t=q^{-1}(1+O(q))$, so it has a [simple pole](../../../../../../simple-pole.md). At zero use $S\tau=-1/\tau$ and the [modular discriminant](../../../../../../modular-discriminant.md) inversion law:

$$
t(S\tau)=2^{12}\frac{\Delta(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau/2)}=2^{12}q_0(1+O(q_0)),\qquad q_0=e^{\pi i\tau}.
$$

Thus zero is a simple zero, not a [pole](../../../../../../pole.md), in its width-two [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) coordinate. The [single-pole criterion for a spherical coordinate](../../../../../../single-pole-criterion-for-a-spherical-coordinate.md) now gives

$$
\boxed{t=j_2:X(\Gamma_0(2))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}.}
$$

This [discriminant-ratio coordinate on X0 2](../../../../../../discriminant-ratio-coordinate-on-x0-2.md) is different from a ratio of two $j$-invariants.
