<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

View $j$ as a [meromorphic function](../../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on $X(\Gamma_0(2))$, using its coordinate $t=j_2$. At the infinity [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) its [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) order is one. At the zero [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md), $j(S\tau)=j(\tau)=q^{-1}+O(1)=q_0^{-2}+O(1)$, so its [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) order is two. Part (c) identifies these [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) with $t=\infty$ and $t=0$.

The zero of $j$ on the full modular curve lies at its order-three elliptic point. There are no effective order-three stabilizers in $\Gamma_0(2)$: their lifts have [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $\pm1$, whose [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) modulo two is $X^2+X+1$, whereas an upper triangular [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) over $\mathbb F_2$ has both diagonal entries one. Therefore the degree-three covering has exactly one point over that elliptic point, with [analytic ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index-of-a-holomorphic-map.md) three. Its $t$-coordinate is $a=j_2(\omega)$, and the zero divisor of the pulled-back $j$ is $3[a]$.

A rational function with these zeros and [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) must be $C(t-a)^3/t^2$. At the infinity [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) both $j$ and $t$ have leading coefficient one times $q^{-1}$, giving $C=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{j(\tau)=\frac{(j_2(\tau)-j_2(\omega))^3}{j_2(\tau)^2}.}
$$

The proof fixes the coefficient and the powers using [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) [cusp widths](../../../../../../width-of-a-cusp.md) and elliptic ramification, rather than assuming a formula for the level-two coordinate.
