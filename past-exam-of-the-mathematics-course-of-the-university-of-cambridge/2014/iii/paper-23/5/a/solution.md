<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [cusp form](../../../../../../cusp-form.md), the [invariant norm of a modular form](../../../../../../invariant-norm-of-a-modular-form.md) $|f(\tau)|(\operatorname{Im}\tau)^{k/2}$ is bounded on the entire half-plane. It is invariant under the level group; on a truncated [fundamental region of a modular subgroup](../../../../../../fundamental-region-of-a-modular-subgroup.md) boundedness is compactness, and near each [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) exponential decay of the [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) expansion dominates the power of height. With this bound, Fourier inversion on a horizontal interval gives

$$
|a_n|\le e^{2\pi ny}\int_0^1|f(x+iy)|\,dx\ll e^{2\pi ny}y^{-k/2}.
$$

Choosing $y=1/n$ proves the [Fourier coefficient bound for a cusp form](../../../../../../fourier-coefficient-bound-for-a-cusp-form.md), $a_n=O(n^{k/2})$. Therefore $\sum|a_n|n^{-\sigma}$ converges when $\sigma>k/2+1$, and uniformly on every compact subset of that half-plane. The locally uniform limit of its holomorphic terms is holomorphic, so

$$
\boxed{L(f,s)\text{ is holomorphic for }\operatorname{Re}s>k/2+1.}
$$
