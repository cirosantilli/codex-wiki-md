<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $\varepsilon=n_an^a\in\{-1,+1\}$. The [non-null hypersurface projection](../../../../../../../non-null-hypersurface-projection.md) is $P^a{}_b=\delta^a{}_b-\varepsilon n^an_b$, reproducing both signs in the question. It annihilates the normal and is the identity on tangent vectors to the [hypersurface](../../../../../../../hypersurface.md).

Represent $V$ by the tangent to a curve $\lambda$ through $p$. The curve $\phi\circ\lambda$ lies in $\Sigma$, so its tangent $d\phi_pV$ is tangent to $\Sigma$. Orthogonality therefore gives $n_a(\phi_*V)^a=0$, and

$$
\boxed{P^a{}_b(\phi_*V)^b=(\phi_*V)^a.}
$$

This uses the stated smooth [hypersurface](../../../../../../../hypersurface.md) assumption; the dimension relation alone would not make the image of an arbitrary smooth map a regular hypersurface.
