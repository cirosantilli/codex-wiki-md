Here $I_{ij}$ denotes the unreduced [second mass moment tensor](../../../../../../second-mass-moment-tensor.md), distinct from its trace-free [mass quadrupole moment](../../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md). In coordinates referred to the chosen origin,

$$
\boxed{I_{ij}(t)=\int_{\mathcal V}T^{00}(t,\mathbf y)y_i y_j\,d^3y.}
$$

This expression uses $c=1$. In SI units the leading nonrelativistic mass density is $T^{00}/c^2$. For slowly moving [point masses](../../../../../../point-mass.md), $T^{00}\simeq\sum_A m_{(A)}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_{(A)}(t))$, so

$$
\boxed{I_{ij}(t)=\sum_{A=1}^N m_{(A)}y_{(A)i}(t)y_{(A)j}(t),\qquad
Q_{ij}=I_{ij}-\frac13\delta_{ij}I_{kk}.}
$$

Kinetic corrections to the energy density are higher order in the velocity. The [second mass moment tensor](../../../../../../second-mass-moment-tensor.md) is not the mechanical [moment of inertia](../../../../../../moment-of-inertia.md) tensor, which instead has components $\delta_{ij}I_{kk}-I_{ij}$.
