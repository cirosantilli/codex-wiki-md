<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Power is energy per time. In [geometrized units](../../../../../../geometrized-units.md), energy has the dimension of length and time is converted to length using $c$, so the dimensionless [geometrized luminosity](../../../../../../geometrized-luminosity.md) is

$$
\boxed{L_{\odot,\rm geom}=\frac{G L_\odot}{c^5}\simeq1.06\times10^{-26}.}
$$

Equivalently, $c^5/G$ is the corresponding SI power unit. The lifetime for emitting the entire [rest energy](../../../../../../rest-energy.md) at the given constant [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) is

$$
t_{\rm all}=\frac{M_\odot c^2}{L_\odot}\simeq4.65\times10^{20}\,\mathrm s
=\boxed{1.47\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{yr}}.
$$

The same result follows from $M_\odot^{(\rm time)}/L_{\odot,\rm geom}$. **The stipulated full-mass radiation time is of order $10^{13}$ years**; this is the constant-luminosity energy budget asked for, not a stellar-evolution calculation.
