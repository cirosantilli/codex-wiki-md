<h4 id="1/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/i/a)

In the [Lévy hierarchy](../../../set-theory.md#levy-hierarchy), a [bounded formula in set theory](../../../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory) has only quantifiers of the forms $\forall x\in y$ and $\exists x\in y$. A [Sigma-one formula in set theory](../../../set-theory.md#sigma-one-formula-in-set-theory) is an existential unbounded quantifier block followed by a [bounded formula in set theory](../../../set-theory.md#bounded-formula-in-set-theory), and a [Pi-one formula in set theory](../../../set-theory.md#pi-one-formula-in-set-theory) has a universal unbounded block. **A formula is a [Delta-one formula modulo ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#delta-one-formula-modulo-zfc) if [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) proves it equivalent, with the same free variables, both to a $\Sigma_1$ formula and to a $\Pi_1$ formula.** Equivalence modulo the specified theory is part of the definition; it is not necessary that the original string have both syntactic forms. Such formulas satisfy the usual [Delta-one absoluteness](../../../set-theory.md#delta-one-absoluteness) between [transitive models](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-model) satisfying the relevant axioms. The modulo-ZF analogue is a [Delta-one formula in set theory](../../../set-theory.md#delta-one-formula-in-set-theory).

<h4 id="1/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/i/b)

A [Sierpiński set](../../../measure-theory.md#sierpinski-set) is an uncountable [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $S$ of $\mathbb R$ such that $S\cap N$ is countable for every [set](../../../set.md) $N$ of [Lebesgue measure](../../../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-measure) zero. Equivalently it is enough to test [Borel null sets](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-null-set), because every [null set](../../../measure-theory.md#null-set) is contained in a Borel [null set](../../../measure-theory.md#null-set). **Uncountability and countable intersection with every [null set](../../../measure-theory.md#null-set) are both required.** This is the measure analogue of the category-based [Luzin set](../../../topological-analysis.md#luzin-set) condition; it does not say that $S$ itself is measurable.

<h4 id="1/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/i/c)

The [cumulative-hierarchy reflection principle](../../../set-theory.md#levy-reflection-theorem) says that for every finite collection $\Phi$ of formulas and every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\gamma$, there is an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha>\gamma$ such that for all $\varphi\in\Phi$ and all parameters $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in V_\alpha$,

$$
\boxed{\varphi(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\ \Longleftrightarrow\
(V_\alpha,\in)\models\varphi(a_1,\ldots,a_n).}
$$

Equivalently, all quantifiers on the right are relativized to $V_\alpha$. It is a theorem schema of [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) for finite lists of formulas, with reflecting stages arbitrarily high. It does not assert one set-sized stage elementary for every formula at once. By beginning above the ranks of any specified finite parameter list, the parameters can also be required to belong to the reflecting stage.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

<h5 id="1/ii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/ii/a)

Let $f$ be given and choose $Y\subseteq\mathbb R$ of [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) $\aleph_1$. Each $f(y)$ is countable, so the [axiom of choice](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) and [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../../../set-theory.md#infinite-cardinal-arithmetic) give

$$
\left|Y\cup\bigcup_{y\in Y}f(y)\right|\le\aleph_1.
$$

Since the continuum is larger, choose $x$ outside this union. Then $x\notin f(y)$ for every $y\in Y$. The countable [set](../../../set.md) $f(x)$ cannot contain $Y$, so choose $y\in Y\setminus f(x)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{x\notin f(y),\qquad y\notin f(x).}
$$

The two points are distinct because $x\notin Y$. This proves the [countable-valued free-pair criterion](../../../set-theory.md#freiling-theorem) and the required direction of the [Freiling axiom of symmetry](../../../set-theory.md#freiling-axiom-of-symmetry) without any measurability assumption on $f$.

<h4 id="1/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

<h5 id="1/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/ii/b)

Assume the [Continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#continuum-hypothesis) and enumerate $\mathbb R=\{r_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}$. Define

$$
f(r_\alpha)=\{r_\beta:\beta\le\alpha\}.
$$

Every value is countable. For any two indices, say $\alpha\le\beta$, one has $r_\alpha\in f(r_\beta)$, so the two required nonmembership conditions cannot both hold. Including the endpoint in each initial segment also rules out taking the two points equal. Therefore the free-pair assertion implies the negation of [Continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#continuum-hypothesis). [Cantor theorem](../../../set.md#cantor-s-theorem) and choice already give $2^{\aleph_0}\ge\aleph_1$, so

$$
\boxed{A_{<\aleph_1}(\mathbb R)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 2^{\aleph_0}>\aleph_1\quad\text{in ZFC}.}
$$

<h4 id="1/ii/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

<h5 id="1/ii/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/ii/c)

The [Gödel constructible universe theorem](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe-theorem) gives $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\Rightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{CH})$. The [forcing](../../../forcing.md) independence theorem for the [Continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#continuum-hypothesis) gives $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\Rightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\neg\mathrm{CH})$. For example, the latter can be obtained by first passing to the [constructible universe](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe) and then adding sufficiently many Cohen reals. By the equivalence in part (b), these are respectively models of the negation and affirmation of the free-pair assertion. Thus **if [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) is consistent, the assertion is independent of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice)**. The consistency qualification is essential: an inconsistent theory proves every sentence. These are syntactic relative-consistency implications, not a claim that bare consistency supplies a [countable transitive model](../../../forcing.md#countable-transitive-model).

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

The [axiom of infinity](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-infinity) excludes $V_\omega$ and all smaller stages, so the [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\lambda$ is uncountable. [Full semantics for second-order logic](../../../mathematical-logic.md#full-semantics-for-second-order-logic) is decisive: every externally specified functional relation whose [ordered pairs](../../../set.md#ordered-pair) lie in $V_\lambda$ is an allowed class parameter, even if the relation itself is not an element of $V_\lambda$.

If $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)=\mu<\lambda$, choose an external cofinal [function](../../../function.md) $h:\mu\to\lambda$. Its graph is an allowed class parameter: each input, output and [ordered pair](../../../set.md#ordered-pair) has [rank of a set](../../../set-theory.md#rank-of-a-set) below the infinite [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\lambda$, which is a [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal). The domain $\mu$ belongs to $V_\lambda$. The second-order form of [Axiom schema of replacement](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) would give its range as an element of $V_\lambda$. But a cofinal [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\lambda$ has [rank of a set](../../../set-theory.md#rank-of-a-set) $\lambda$ and cannot belong to $V_\lambda$. Therefore $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)=\lambda$.

If $2^\theta\ge\lambda$ for a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\theta<\lambda$, choice supplies an external [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $q:\mathcal P(\theta)\to\lambda$. The domain belongs to $V_\lambda$ and the [ordered pairs](../../../set.md#ordered-pair) of its graph have ranks below $\lambda$. Applying [Axiom schema of replacement](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) to this external functional class would put its range, the [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\lambda$, in $V_\lambda$, again impossible. Hence $2^\theta<\lambda$ for every $\theta<\lambda$.

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda>\aleph_0,\qquad \operatorname{cf}(\lambda)=\lambda,\qquad
\forall\theta<\lambda\ (2^\theta<\lambda).}
$$

These are exactly the defining conditions for a [strongly inaccessible cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#strongly-inaccessible-cardinal). This [full second-order replacement rank obstruction](../../../set-theory.md#full-second-order-replacement-rank-obstruction) would not follow from [Henkin semantics](../../../mathematical-logic.md#henkin-semantics), where the available class relations may exclude the external [functions](../../../function.md) just used.

<h5 id="2/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/i/a)

In the usual tail-normalized version, an [Ulam matrix on omega-one](../../../set-theory.md#ulam-matrix-on-omega-one) is a family $A_{\alpha,n}$ such that, for each $\alpha<\omega_1$, the [sets](../../../set.md) indexed by $n<\omega$ partition the tail $(\alpha,\omega_1)$, while for each fixed $n$ the [sets](../../../set.md) indexed by $\alpha<\omega_1$ are pairwise disjoint. Empty cells are allowed; harmless bounded-tail variants give the same applications.

For a concrete realization, choose [injections](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $e_\beta:\beta\to\omega$ for every [countable ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#countable-ordinal) $\beta$, and [set](../../../set.md)

$$
\boxed{A_{\alpha,n}=\{\beta>\alpha:e_\beta(\alpha)=n\}.}
$$

For fixed $\alpha$ each $\beta>\alpha$ chooses exactly one $n$; for fixed $n$, injectivity of $e_\beta$ prevents one $\beta$ from belonging to two cells. This verifies the two matrix conditions explicitly.

<h4 id="2/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

<h5 id="2/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/i/b)

[König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../set-theory.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory) states that if $\kappa_i<\lambda_i$ for each $i\in I$, then $\sum_i\kappa_i<\prod_i\lambda_i$. Its [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) formulation gives, for every infinite [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}>\kappa.}
$$

Taking a cofinal sequence of smaller [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) in $\kappa$ and comparing their sum with the product of their successors gives this inequality. Another standard consequence, for infinite $\lambda$ and $\kappa\ge2$, is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^\lambda)>\lambda,\qquad
\operatorname{cf}(2^\lambda)>\lambda.}
$$

Indeed, if $\mu=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^\lambda)\le\lambda$, the preceding inequality at $\kappa^\lambda$ would contradict $(\kappa^\lambda)^\mu=\kappa^{\lambda\mu}=\kappa^\lambda$.

<h4 id="2/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

<h5 id="2/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/i/c)

The predicate is defined by the [constructible hierarchy](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-hierarchy):

$$
\boxed{L_0=\varnothing,\qquad L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha),\qquad
L_\delta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}L_\alpha\quad(\delta\text{ limit}).}
$$

Here $\operatorname{Def}(A)$ consists of [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of $A$ definable in the [set](../../../set.md) structure $(A,\in)$ by a [first-order formula](../../../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) with finitely many parameters from $A$. To express $x\in L_\alpha$ in the language of [set](../../../set.md) theory, assert that $\alpha$ is an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) and there is a hierarchy history of length $\alpha+1$ satisfying this recursion whose last stage contains $x$. Formulas are coded by [natural numbers](../../../arithmetic.md#natural-number) and truth is the definable [satisfaction for a set structure](../../../mathematical-logic.md#satisfaction-for-a-set-structure). [Transfinite recursion](../../../set-theory.md#transfinite-recursion) gives a unique history. The standard coding makes this predicate $\Delta_1$ over [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory); thus the [constructible-level absoluteness over ZF](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-level-absoluteness-over-zf) applies to [transitive models](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-model) of [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory).

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The [bounding number](../../../set-theory.md#bounding-number) $\mathfrak b$ is the least size of an unbounded family in $(\omega^\omega,\le^*)$. The [almost disjointness number](../../../set-theory.md#almost-disjointness-number) $\mathfrak a$ is the least size of an infinite [maximal almost disjoint family on omega](../../../set-theory.md#maximal-almost-disjoint-family-on-omega) of infinite [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega$. Requiring the family to be infinite excludes trivial finite maximal partitions.

A countable family $\{f_i:i<\omega\}$ is bounded by $g(n)=1+\max_{i\le n}f_i(n)$. Thus $\aleph_1\le\mathfrak b$.

Suppose an infinite [almost disjoint family on omega](../../../set-theory.md#almost-disjoint-family-on-omega) $\mathcal A$ has size less than $\mathfrak b$. Choose distinct members $A_n$ and put $C_n=A_n\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}A_i$. These are infinite and pairwise disjoint. For each $B\in\mathcal A$, define $h_B(n)=\max((B\cap C_n)\cup\{0\})$ whenever the intersection is finite. If $B=A_n$ at the exceptional index $n$, [set](../../../set.md) $h_B(n)=0$. These are the only possible infinite intersections. A single $g$ eventually dominates every $h_B$, because there are fewer than $\mathfrak b$ of them. Pick $x_n\in C_n$ with $x_n>g(n)$ and put $X=\{x_n:n<\omega\}$.

For $B$ not among the selected $A_n$, only finitely many $x_n$ can lie in $B$. The same is true for $B=A_j$, with its single exceptional index $j$ ignored. Thus $X$ is infinite and almost disjoint from every member of $\mathcal A$, so the family was not maximal. This [bounding-to-almost-disjointness inequality](../../../set-theory.md#bounding-to-almost-disjointness-inequality) proves $\mathfrak b\le\mathfrak a$.

Finally start with an infinite pairwise disjoint family and use [Zorn's lemma](../../../set-theory.md#zorn-s-lemma) to extend it to a [maximal almost disjoint family on omega](../../../set-theory.md#maximal-almost-disjoint-family-on-omega). It is a family of [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega$, so has [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) at most $2^{\aleph_0}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\aleph_1\le\mathfrak b\le\mathfrak a\le2^{\aleph_0}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

<h5 id="2/iii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/iii/a)

Fix $a,\vec p\in L$ and a formula $\theta(x,\vec p)$. The [reflection theorem for definable hierarchies](../../../set-theory.md#reflection-theorem-for-definable-hierarchies) supplies a stage $L_\beta$ containing these parameters at which the finitely many subformulas of $\theta$ have the same truth values as in $L$, for arguments in $L_\beta$.

This reflection can be proved in the ambient [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) without presupposing [Axiom schema of separation](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification) in $L$: starting at a stage containing the parameters, collect for each relevant existential subformula and every tuple in the current stage the least constructible stage in which a witness occurs, whenever a witness exists in $L$. Ambient [Axiom schema of replacement](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) bounds these [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). Iterate these witness-stage bounds for $\omega$ steps and take their supremum. Induction on subformulas gives the required finite reflection at the resulting union stage. Only the least stage is chosen, so no ambient [axiom of choice](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice) is needed.

Since $L_\beta$ is transitive and contains $a$, the desired separated [subset](../../../set.md#subset) is

$$
b=\{x\in L_\beta:x\in a\text{ and }(L_\beta,\in)\models\theta(x,\vec p)\}.
$$

It is definable over $L_\beta$ with parameters there, so $b\in\operatorname{Def}(L_\beta)=L_{\beta+1}\subset L$. Reflection gives $b=\{x\in a:\theta^L(x,\vec p)\}$. Hence **[ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) proves every [Axiom schema of separation](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-specification) instance relativized to $L$**. This is a direct [Separation proof in the constructible universe](../../../definable-power-set.md#separation-proof-in-the-constructible-universe), not an appeal to the assertion being proved.

<h4 id="2/iii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

<h5 id="2/iii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/iii/b)

Use the transitive-model convention for “standard” in this assertion: the domain is a [transitive set](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-set) and membership is actual membership. A [standard membership model of set theory](../../../set-theory.md#standard-membership-model-of-set-theory) without transitivity is a weaker notion and would not justify the asserted absoluteness. Ordinalhood is bounded-formula absolute, so the internal [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) are precisely $A\cap\operatorname{Ord}$. [Satisfaction for a set structure](../../../mathematical-logic.md#satisfaction-for-a-set-structure) is absolute between transitive [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) models containing that structure: the domain, finite parameter tuples, formula codes and the recursive truth clauses are the same. Equivalently one may use the $\Delta_1$ absoluteness of the predicate defining $L_\alpha$.

Transfinite induction now gives $L_\alpha^{\mathbb A}=L_\alpha$ for each [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha\in A$. At successors both computations use the same definable [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of the same [set](../../../set.md) structure; at limits they take the same union over all smaller [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), which belong to $A$ by transitivity. Thus

$$
\boxed{L^{\mathbb A}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A\cap\operatorname{Ord}}L_\alpha.}
$$

For a set-sized model of [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) height $\theta$, this is $L_\theta$. The restriction to actual [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) makes the printed union precise. Mere well-foundedness of a nontransitive membership substructure would not justify these absoluteness steps; transitivity is the intended standard-model convention.

<h4 id="2/iii/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

<h5 id="2/iii/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/iii/c)

Let $N$ be any transitive class model of [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) containing every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). By part (b), all its internally computed constructible levels are the actual levels, and each belongs to, and is contained in, $N$. Their union therefore gives $L\subseteq N$. Using the permitted fact that $L\models\mathrm{ZF}$, and that $L$ itself contains every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), we conclude

$$
\boxed{L\text{ is the smallest transitive class model of ZF containing all ordinals}.}
$$

The word class matters: a [set](../../../set.md) cannot contain every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). Set-sized standard models instead contain their own truncated [constructible universe](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe) as described in part (b).

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

Let $E=\{\alpha<\kappa:\operatorname{cf}(\alpha)=\omega\}$. This [set](../../../set.md) is stationary: in any [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set), choose a strictly increasing countable sequence and take its supremum, which lies in the [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) and has [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\omega$. For each $\alpha\in E$ choose an increasing cofinal sequence $c_\alpha:\omega\to\alpha$.

Fix $\beta<\kappa$. For every $\alpha\in E$ above $\beta$, some $c_\alpha(n)$ exceeds $\beta$. Partition this stationary tail by the least such $n$. A countable union of nonstationary [sets](../../../set.md) is nonstationary, because fewer than $\kappa$ [club sets](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) have [club filter completeness](../../../set-theory.md#club-filter-completeness), so some cell is stationary. On it the [regressive function](../../../set-theory.md#regressive-function) $\alpha\mapsto c_\alpha(n)$ has, by [Fodor lemma](../../../set-theory.md#fodor-lemma), a stationary fiber at a value $\gamma>\beta$.

Let $I_n=\{\gamma<\kappa:\{\alpha\in E:c_\alpha(n)=\gamma\}\text{ is stationary}\}$. The preceding argument says $\bigcup_n I_n$ is unbounded in $\kappa$. Since $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)>\omega$, at least one $I_n$ is unbounded and therefore has size $\kappa$. Its fibers are pairwise disjoint [stationary sets](../../../set-theory.md#stationary-set). Enumerate $\kappa$ of them as $T_\xi$, $\xi<\kappa$, and define $S_\xi=T_\xi$ for $\xi>0$, while

$$
S_0=T_0\cup\left(\kappa\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\kappa}T_\xi\right).
$$

Adding a remainder preserves stationarity and introduces no overlap. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\bigsqcup_{\xi<\kappa}S_\xi,\qquad S_\xi\text{ stationary for every }\xi.}
$$

This proves the [stationary partition by cofinal-sequence fibers](../../../set-theory.md#stationary-partition-by-cofinal-sequence-fibers) directly for every regular uncountable $\kappa$.

<h5 id="3/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/i/a)

For an infinite [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number), the [Gimel function](../../../set-theory.md#gimel-function) is $\gimel(\kappa)=\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}$. The [Gimel hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#gimel-hypothesis) asserts, for every [singular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#singular-cardinal) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{\gimel(\kappa)=\max\{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)},\kappa^+\}.}
$$

These are the unavoidable lower bounds supplied by monotonicity of exponentiation and [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../set-theory.md#konig-s-theorem-set-theory). The hypothesis imposes the least allowed value at singular [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number); it does not constrain the continuum [function](../../../function.md) on regular [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) to their successors. Thus it is weaker than [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis). In the case $2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}<\kappa$, it says $\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=\kappa^+$, the usual [singular cardinals hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#singular-cardinals-hypothesis) case.

<h4 id="3/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

<h5 id="3/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/i/b)

[Martin's axiom](../../../forcing.md#martin-s-axiom) at $\kappa$, denoted $\mathrm{MA}_\kappa$, says that whenever a [forcing](../../../forcing.md) order satisfies the [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) and $\{D_\xi:\xi<\kappa\}$ is a family of dense [subsets](../../../set.md#subset), there is a [filter in an ordered set](../../../set.md#filter-mathematics) meeting every $D_\xi$. Equivalently, allow any family of at most $\kappa$ dense [subsets](../../../set.md#subset). The [filter in an ordered set](../../../set.md#filter-mathematics) is directed towards stronger common extensions and closed towards weaker conditions. **Full [Martin's axiom](../../../forcing.md#martin-s-axiom) requires $\mathrm{MA}_\kappa$ for every $\kappa<2^{\aleph_0}$.** The bound below the continuum is part of the usual full axiom, explaining its role in part (iv)(b).

<h4 id="3/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

<h5 id="3/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/i/c)

**A [well-pruned set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#well-pruned-set-theoretic-tree) of height $\kappa$ has the property that every node $t$ extends to every higher level below $\kappa$: if $\operatorname{ht}(t)<\beta<\kappa$, there is $u$ of height $\beta$ with $t<_T u$.** Equivalently, the heights of extensions of every node are unbounded in $\kappa$, since taking predecessors then gives an extension at any prescribed intermediate level. This is stronger than merely having no terminal nodes. For a [kappa-tree](../../../set.md#kappa-tree) we use the usual regular uncountable height [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) and levels of size less than that [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number).

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

The [Beth numbers](../../../set-theory.md#beth-number) form a strictly increasing continuous sequence: strictness comes from [Cantor theorem](../../../set.md#cantor-s-theorem), and at a limit $\delta$ one has $\beth_\delta=\sup_{\xi<\delta}\beth_\xi$. A cofinal sequence in $\delta$ of length $\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$ therefore induces a cofinal sequence in $\beth_\delta$, giving $\operatorname{cf}(\beth_\delta)\le\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$.

Conversely, let $\{a_i:i<\operatorname{cf}(\beth_\delta)\}$ be cofinal in $\beth_\delta$. For each $i$ choose $\xi_i<\delta$ with $a_i<\beth_{\xi_i}$. The indices must be cofinal in $\delta$; otherwise all $a_i$ would be bounded by a single smaller Beth number. Thus $\operatorname{cf}(\delta)\le\operatorname{cf}(\beth_\delta)$, and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\beth_\delta)=\operatorname{cf}(\delta).}
$$

This is the [cofinality of a continuous cardinal hierarchy](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality-of-a-continuous-cardinal-hierarchy) at a nonzero limit index; the limit hypothesis is essential to the supremum argument.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

We give an explicit [Gimel recursion for cardinal exponentiation](../../../set-theory.md#gimel-recursion-for-cardinal-exponentiation). First recover the continuum [function](../../../function.md) by induction on infinite [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). At a regular $\kappa$,

$$
2^\kappa=\kappa^\kappa=\gimel(\kappa),
$$

because $2^\kappa\le\kappa^\kappa\le(2^\kappa)^\kappa=2^\kappa$. At a singular $\kappa$, put $\theta=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ and $s=\sup_{\rho<\kappa}2^\rho$, whose value is already known. A cofinal sequence $(\kappa_i)_{i<\theta}$ gives

$$
\boxed{2^\kappa=s^\theta.}
$$

Indeed $\prod_i2^{\kappa_i}=2^{\sum_i\kappa_i}=2^\kappa\le s^\theta$, while $s\le2^\kappa$ gives the reverse inequality after raising to $\theta$.

If the supremum $s$ is attained, the continuum [function](../../../function.md) is eventually constant below $\kappa$, and we may choose $\rho<\kappa$ with $\rho\ge\theta$ and $2^\rho=s$. Then $s^\theta=(2^\rho)^\theta=s$. If it is not attained, the increasing cofinal power values show $\operatorname{cf}(s)=\theta$. To verify the reverse [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) bound, fewer than $\theta$ such lower power values have indices bounded below $\kappa$, and cannot be cofinal in $s$; the upper bound comes from $(\kappa_i)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{2^\kappa=\begin{cases}s,&s\text{ attained below }\kappa,\\
\gimel(s),&s\text{ not attained below }\kappa.
\end{cases}}
$$

Every singular-stage value is therefore determined by the previously computed powers and the given [Gimel function](../../../set-theory.md#gimel-function).

Now fix an infinite exponent $\lambda$ and recurse on the infinite base $\kappa$. If $2\le\kappa\le\lambda$, then $\kappa^\lambda=2^\lambda$, already known. For a successor base $\kappa=\rho^+>\lambda$, every [function](../../../function.md) $\lambda\to\kappa$ has bounded range, and each bounded range has size at most $\rho$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\kappa^\lambda=\max\{\kappa,\rho^\lambda\}.}
$$

This is the [Hausdorff formula for cardinal exponentiation](../../../set-theory.md#hausdorff-formula-for-cardinal-exponentiation). For a limit base $\kappa>\lambda$, put $a=\sup_{\rho<\kappa}\rho^\lambda$ and $\theta=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$. If $\theta>\lambda$, all ranges are bounded and counting over those bounds gives $\kappa^\lambda=a$ (here $a\ge\kappa$).

If $\theta\le\lambda$, then $\kappa^\lambda=a^\theta$. To see the nontrivial upper bound, use a cofinal sequence of bounds $\kappa_i$. A [function](../../../function.md) $\lambda\to\kappa$ is coded by the assignment of each argument to one of these $\theta$ bounds, together with $\theta$ padded [functions](../../../function.md) into the corresponding bounds. The assignment has at most $\theta^\lambda=2^\lambda\le a$ possibilities, and the [functions](../../../function.md) have at most $a^\theta$ possibilities. Conversely $a\le\kappa^\lambda$ and $(\kappa^\lambda)^\theta=\kappa^\lambda$, giving the lower bound. If $a$ is attained as $\rho^\lambda$, then $a^\theta=a$ by currying; otherwise $\operatorname{cf}(a)=\theta$ by the same cofinal-index argument as above. Thus

$$
\boxed{\kappa^\lambda=\begin{cases}
a,&\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)>\lambda,\\
a,&\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\le\lambda\text{ and }a\text{ attained},\\
\gimel(a),&\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\le\lambda\text{ and }a\text{ not attained}.
\end{cases}}
$$

Only smaller-base powers occur in $a$, so this is a genuine recursion, not an implicit appeal to the unknown power.

Finally, finite positive exponents give $\kappa^n=\kappa$ for infinite $\kappa$; finite bases at infinite exponents satisfy $m^\lambda=2^\lambda$ for $m\ge2$. The cases with base $0$ or $1$, exponent $0$, or both arguments finite are elementary, with $0^0=1$ under the empty-function convention. **The [Gimel function](../../../set-theory.md#gimel-function) therefore determines both requested class [functions](../../../function.md) completely.**

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

<h5 id="3/iv/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/iv/a)

For a regular uncountable [kappa-tree](../../../set.md#kappa-tree), keep exactly the nodes whose extensions have unbounded heights:

$$
T^*=\{t\in T:\sup\{\operatorname{ht}(u):u\ge_Tt\}=\kappa\}.
$$

This [set](../../../set.md) is predecessor-closed. At any level $\alpha$, if no node survived, the extension heights above each of its fewer than $\kappa$ nodes would be bounded. Regularity would give a single bound for their union, contradicting the height of the original [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree). Thus every level of $T^*$ is nonempty and still has size less than $\kappa$.

If $t\in T^*$ and $\beta>\operatorname{ht}(t)$, consider its extensions at level $\beta$. If none survived, fewer than $\kappa$ bounded extension [sets](../../../set.md) would again bound every extension of $t$, a contradiction. Therefore a surviving level-$\beta$ extension exists. Hence

$$
\boxed{T^*\text{ is a well-pruned }\kappa\text{-subtree}.}
$$

This [unbounded-extension kernel of a regular tree](../../../set.md#unbounded-extension-kernel-of-a-regular-tree) uses regularity essentially. If one permits singular-height [set-theoretic trees](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) in the term “$\kappa$-tree”, the unrestricted assertion is false: take fewer than $\kappa$ disjoint branches with lengths cofinal in a singular $\kappa$. The levels are small and the height is $\kappa$, but no node has unbounded extensions. A common root can be added without creating a well-pruned subtree. The usual regular-height convention is therefore the one used here.

<h4 id="3/iv/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

<h5 id="3/iv/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/iv/b)

Prune an $\aleph_1$-[Suslin tree](../../../set.md#suslin-tree) as in part (a), then use its nodes as [forcing](../../../forcing.md) conditions, with extensions stronger. Two conditions are compatible exactly when comparable, so the absence of uncountable [tree antichains](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) is the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing). For each $\alpha<\omega_1$, the [set](../../../set.md) $D_\alpha$ of nodes of height at least $\alpha$ is dense, by [well-pruned set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#well-pruned-set-theoretic-tree).

If $2^{\aleph_0}>\aleph_1$, full [Martin's axiom](../../../forcing.md#martin-s-axiom) includes $\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}$. It would provide a [filter in an ordered set](../../../set.md#filter-mathematics) meeting all these [dense subsets of a forcing order](../../../forcing.md#dense-subset-of-a-forcing-order). Directedness makes that [filter in an ordered set](../../../set.md#filter-mathematics) a [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order), and meeting every $D_\alpha$ makes its heights unbounded, producing an uncountable branch. This contradicts the Suslin-tree property. **A Suslin [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) together with failure of [Continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#continuum-hypothesis) therefore implies failure of [Martin's axiom](../../../forcing.md#martin-s-axiom).** This is the [Suslin-tree obstruction to Martin's axiom](../../../set.md#suslin-tree-obstruction-to-martin-s-axiom).

The [partition relation](../../../set-theory.md#partition-relation) $\kappa\to(\lambda)^\alpha_\beta$ means that for every coloring $c:[\kappa]^\alpha\to\beta$, there is $H\subseteq\kappa$ with $|H|=\lambda$ such that $c$ is constant on $[H]^\alpha$. <h4 id="4/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

<h5 id="4/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/i/b)

**The [stationary-indexed club principle](../../../set-theory.md#stationary-indexed-club-principle) $\clubsuit_S$ asserts a sequence $\langle A_\delta:\delta\in S\cap\operatorname{Lim}(\omega_1)\rangle$ with each $A_\delta\subset\delta$ cofinal of order type $\omega$, such that for every uncountable $X\subseteq\omega_1$ some $\delta\in S$ satisfies $A_\delta\subseteq X$.** It predicts a cofinal ladder contained in the target, rather than predicting the entire initial segment of the target as diamond does. For a stationary $S$ with successor members, only its stationary limit-ordinal part is relevant; countable [limit ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal) have [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\omega$. This is the restricted version of the [club principle](../../../set-theory.md#club-principle).

<h4 id="4/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

<h5 id="4/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/i/c)

A [tree with unique limits](../../../set.md#tree-with-unique-limits) has no distinct nodes at a limit level with the same history. Precisely, for a [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal) $\delta$ and $s,t\in T_\delta$,

$$
\boxed{\bigl(\forall\beta<\delta\quad s\upharpoonright\beta=t\upharpoonright\beta\bigr)\Longrightarrow s=t.}
$$

Here $s\upharpoonright\beta$ denotes the unique predecessor of $s$ of height $\beta$. This is uniqueness of a limit node when it exists, not a requirement that every cofinal [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) below a limit level acquire a limit node.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

Use the standard [normal set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#normal-set-theoretic-tree) convention: a unique root, extensions at every higher level, splitting into at least two successors, and [tree with unique limits](../../../set.md#tree-with-unique-limits). The small-level and height assumptions already give an $\omega_1$-tree, while the given [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) condition gives the [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing). We only need to exclude an uncountable branch.

If such a branch existed, its heights would be unbounded, since each initial segment contains only countably many nodes. Fill in predecessors to obtain its node $b_\alpha$ at every level. At each successor step choose a successor $s_\alpha$ of $b_\alpha$ different from $b_{\alpha+1}$. For $\alpha<\beta$, the node $s_\beta$ extends the branch successor $b_{\alpha+1}$, and so is incompatible with $s_\alpha$. Thus $\{s_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}$ is an uncountable [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain), a contradiction.

Therefore **the [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) is $\aleph_1$-Suslin**. The splitting part of normality matters: a single [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) of height $\omega_1$ would satisfy the [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) condition but not the conclusion if one used a weakened definition of normality allowing no splitting.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

Fix a [diamond principle](../../../set-theory.md#diamond-principle) sequence $D_\alpha\subseteq\alpha$. Construct a normal splitting [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) of height $\omega_1$ with countable levels. At successors give every node two successors. At a countable limit stage $\alpha$, the [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) below $\alpha$ is countable. Choose countably many [cofinal branches](../../../set.md#cofinal-branch) through it covering all its nodes, and put one node at level $\alpha$ above each distinct chosen branch. This preserves extension to all higher levels and [tree with unique limits](../../../set.md#tree-with-unique-limits).

Arrange a coding of each level into the [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) block $[\omega\alpha,\omega(\alpha+1))$. On the [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) of limit fixed points of $\alpha\mapsto\omega\alpha$, the nodes coded below $\alpha$ are exactly the nodes of height below $\alpha$. At a limit stage, if $D_\alpha$ codes a maximal [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) of the current [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) below $\alpha$, require every chosen branch to meet it. This is possible: for any starting node $t$, maximality provides a comparable [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) member; if above $t$, first extend to it, and if below $t$, it has already been met. Then extend along a sequence of heights cofinal in $\alpha$. If the prediction is not a maximal [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain), use the ordinary covering branches. Thus every level is countable and the construction remains normal.

Here is the full chain-condition verification. Let $A$ be a maximal [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) in the final [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree). For every node $t$, choose a witness $a_t\in A$ comparable with $t$. There is a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) of countable limit stages $\alpha$ closed under these witness choices: starting from any bound, repeatedly bound the heights of witnesses for all the countably many nodes below the current stage, and take the supremum after countably many steps. At such an $\alpha$, $A\cap T_{<\alpha}$ is already maximal in $T_{<\alpha}$.

View $A$ as a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega_1$ through the coding. Diamond gives stationarily many stages with $D_\alpha=A\cap\alpha$. Choose one also in the witness-closure [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) and the coding [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set). The construction at that stage seals this very [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain): every node of level $\alpha$ extends one of its members below $\alpha$, and so does every node at a later level. No such node can itself belong to $A$, since it is comparable with an earlier member of $A$. Hence

$$
\boxed{A\subseteq T_{<\alpha},\qquad A\text{ is countable}.}
$$

Every [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain) extends to a maximal one, so the [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) has no uncountable [tree antichain](../../../set.md#tree-antichain). Its normal splitting also excludes uncountable branches by part (ii). It is therefore a [Suslin tree](../../../set.md#suslin-tree). By the standard Suslin-tree characterization of [Suslin hypothesis](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#suslin-hypothesis), **diamond implies failure of [Suslin hypothesis](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#suslin-hypothesis)**. The decisive step is [antichain sealing by diamond](../../../set-theory.md#antichain-sealing-by-diamond), with maximality below a correctly guessed [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) stage verified explicitly.

<h3 id="4/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#4/iv)

A [kappa-filtration](../../../set-theory.md#kappa-filtration) is an increasing continuous sequence with union $A$ and $|A_\alpha|<\kappa$ at every stage. Intersect $S$ with the [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) of nonzero [limit ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal). For each remaining $\alpha$, continuity gives $A_\alpha=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}A_\beta$, so choose $r(\alpha)<\alpha$ with $f(\alpha)\in A_{r(\alpha)}$. This is a [regressive function](../../../set-theory.md#regressive-function). [Fodor lemma](../../../set-theory.md#fodor-lemma) gives a stationary [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $S_1$ and a fixed $\beta$ such that all these values lie in $A_\beta$.

Since $|A_\beta|<\kappa$, partition $S_1$ into fewer than $\kappa$ fibers of $f$. If every fiber were nonstationary, choose a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) avoiding each one. Their intersection is [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) by regularity, contradicting stationarity of $S_1$. Thus one fiber is stationary, and

$$
\boxed{\exists a\in A\ \exists S'\subseteq S\text{ stationary}:\ f\upharpoonright S'=a.}
$$

This proves the [filtration form of Fodor lemma](../../../set-theory.md#filtration-form-of-fodor-lemma); continuity at limit stages, the small size of each stage, and regularity of $\kappa$ are all used.

## 5

<h5 id="5/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/i/a)

A [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) is built by well-founded recursion: every member of a $\mathbb P$-name $\tau$ is a pair $(\sigma,p)$ with $p\in\mathbb P$ and $\sigma$ itself a $\mathbb P$-name of lower [forcing name rank](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name-rank). In the ground model all such [forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) form the recursively defined class $V^{\mathbb P}$. Its evaluation by a [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter) is

$$
\boxed{\tau_G=\{\sigma_G:\exists p\in G\ ((\sigma,p)\in\tau)\}.}
$$

The recursion is on [forcing name rank](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name-rank), not on the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) order. [Forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) need not have a unique evaluation across different generics, and many different [forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) may have the same evaluation.

<h4 id="5/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#5/i)

<h5 id="5/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/i/b)

The [forcing truth lemma](../../../forcing.md#forcing-truth-lemma) says that for a formula $\varphi$ and ground-model [forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n$,

$$
\boxed{M[G]\models\varphi((\tau_1)_G,\ldots,(\tau_n)_G)
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\exists p\in G\quad M\models p\Vdash\varphi(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n).}
$$

The [forcing](../../../forcing.md) relation is the ground-model relation furnished by the [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem). The witnessing condition must belong to the particular [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter), not merely to the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) order.

<h4 id="5/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#5/i)

<h5 id="5/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/i/c)

A [forcing](../../../forcing.md) [preserves cofinalities](../../../forcing.md#cofinality-preserving-forcing) over $M$ if every [generic extension](../../../forcing.md#generic-extension) has

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}^{M[G]}(\alpha)=\operatorname{cf}^{M}(\alpha)
\quad\text{for every ordinal }\alpha\in M.}
$$

The two values are compared as [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), using preservation of [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) by [forcing](../../../forcing.md). Equivalently the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) asserts that no ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) acquires a smaller [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality). The assertion concerns all [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) [cofinalities](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality), not just that one specified [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) remains uncountable.

<h3 id="5/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#5/ii)

Let $x=\tau_G$ be any [set](../../../set.md) in the extension. The [set](../../../set.md) $D=\{\sigma:\exists p\ ((\sigma,p)\in\tau)\}$ belongs to $M$. Ground-model choice [well-orders](../../../set.md#well-order) it, so fix an enumeration $\langle\sigma_\xi:\xi<\theta\rangle$ by an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) in $M$. In $M[G]$, take

$$
I=\{\xi<\theta:\exists p\in G\ ((\sigma_\xi,p)\in\tau)\}.
$$

Evaluation gives a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $\xi\mapsto(\sigma_\xi)_G$ from $I$ onto $x$. This is a [set](../../../set.md) [function](../../../function.md) by the [ZF](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory) part of the [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem); no extension choice is needed. For each $y\in x$, take its least preimage [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). Distinct $y$ have distinct least preimages, which [well-order](../../../set.md#well-order) $x$ by their inherited [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) order. Since every extension [set](../../../set.md) has a [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name), every [set](../../../set.md) in $M[G]$ is well-orderable. Therefore

$$
\boxed{M[G]\models\mathrm{AC}.}
$$

This [choice preservation by well-ordered names](../../../forcing.md#choice-preservation-by-well-ordered-names) tolerates repeated evaluations, because the least-index step removes them canonically.

<h3 id="5/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#5/iii)

Work over a ground model of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice)+[Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) and let $\lambda=\aleph_3$. Force with finite binary [partial functions](../../../function.md#partial-function) on $\lambda\times\omega$, with extensions stronger. The [Delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system-lemma) thins any uncountable family of finite domains to an uncountable family with one common root. Only finitely many binary assignments on that root occur, so two conditions agree there and their union is a common extension. Hence the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) has the [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) and preserves [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) and [cofinalities](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality).

The generic union yields $\lambda$ distinct reals. Totality at each coordinate is dense, and for two different coordinates it is dense to assign different values at some unused natural-number position. Thus the extension satisfies $2^{\aleph_0}\ge\lambda$.

A nice [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) for a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega_1$ uses one countable [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) at each [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\omega_1$. Since the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) has size $\lambda$, there are at most $(\lambda^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_1}=\lambda^{\aleph_1}$ such [forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name). Ground [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) gives $\lambda^{\aleph_1}=\lambda$; for instance apply the Hausdorff formula at $\lambda=\aleph_2^+$ and the [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) arithmetic below it. Therefore $2^{\aleph_1}\le\lambda$ in the extension. Combining the bounds gives

$$
\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_3.}
$$

The [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) and [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\aleph_3$ is the same in both models by the [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) condition. The [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem) formalizes this construction as the requested relative-consistency implication. A countable transitive ground model is a convenient presentation, not an additional consequence silently derived from mere consistency. This is the [Cohen forcing two-level continuum plateau](../../../forcing.md#cohen-forcing-two-level-continuum-plateau).

<h3 id="5/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iv/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#5/iv)

<h5 id="5/iv/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/iv/a)

All [cardinalities](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) in this part are first computed in $M$. The [forcing](../../../forcing.md) has size $\kappa$. A family of $\kappa$ finite domains has a $\kappa$-sized [Delta-system](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system), since $\kappa$ is regular. There are fewer than $\kappa$ possible value assignments on its finite root: each coordinate $(\alpha,n)$ allows fewer than $\kappa$ values. Regularity lets us thin to two conditions, indeed $\kappa$ many, with identical root assignments. Their union is a condition, proving the $\kappa$-chain condition.

Consequently **every maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) has [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) less than $\kappa$, but there is no one compulsory [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality)**. For any nonzero [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\mu<\kappa$, the single-coordinate conditions assigning the values $\xi<\mu$ at $(\mu,0)$ form an [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) of size $\mu$. It is maximal: a condition already assigning that coordinate is compatible with its matching value, and a condition not assigning it is compatible with every allowed value. Thus every such size occurs, including singleton maximal [forcing antichains](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain). These are the [maximal-antichain sizes in the finite Lévy collapse](../../../forcing.md#maximal-antichain-sizes-in-the-finite-levy-collapse).

The paper writes $p\le q$ for $p\subseteq q$. We use this printed weaker-first convention: $q$ extends $p$ and is stronger. Compatibility and generic meeting arguments below always refer to common extensions, so do not accidentally reverse the convention.

<h4 id="5/iv/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#5/iv)

<h5 id="5/iv/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/iv/b)

For every infinite $\alpha<\kappa$, the generic union at coordinate $\alpha$ gives a [function](../../../function.md) $g_\alpha:\omega\to\alpha$. The requirement to assign $(\alpha,n)$ is dense for each $n$, and the requirement to use any specified value $\beta<\alpha$ is dense by assigning it at a fresh natural-number position. Therefore $g_\alpha$ is a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function), and every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\kappa$ becomes countable.

The $\kappa$-chain condition preserves the regularity of $\kappa$. For a direct verification, a [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) for a [function](../../../function.md) from some $\mu<\kappa$ into $\kappa$ has fewer than $\kappa$ possible values at each coordinate, using a maximal deciding [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain). The union of these possible-value [sets](../../../set.md) has size less than $\kappa$ by regularity and is bounded in $\kappa$. No such [function](../../../function.md) can be cofinal. In particular $\kappa$ remains uncountable, while every smaller [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) is countable. Hence

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\aleph_1^{M[H]}.}
$$

This is the [finite Lévy collapse to omega-one](../../../forcing.md#finite-levy-collapse-to-omega-one).

<h4 id="5/iv/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#5/iv)

<h5 id="5/iv/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/iv/c)

In $M$, take $T=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}{}^\alpha2$ ordered by extension. Strong inaccessibility gives $|T_\alpha|=2^{|\alpha|}<\kappa$. In $M[H]$, each of these ground levels is therefore countable, while the height $\kappa$ is $\omega_1$. Thus the unchanged ground [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) is an $\aleph_1$-tree in the extension.

Every ground binary [function](../../../function.md) of length $\kappa$ still yields a distinct [cofinal branch](../../../set.md#cofinal-branch) through this [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree). There are $(2^\kappa)^M\ge(\kappa^+)^M$ such branches. The $\kappa$-chain condition preserves [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) at and above $\kappa$, so $(\kappa^+)^M=\aleph_2^{M[H]}$. The ground branch family still has at least that [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality). Consequently

$$
\boxed{M[H]\models\text{Kurepa's Hypothesis}.}
$$

This [Kurepa tree from an inaccessible binary tree](../../../set.md#kurepa-tree-from-an-inaccessible-binary-tree) uses ground-model levels and branches; it does not claim the full binary [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) newly computed in the extension has countable levels.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 19](paper-19.md)

<h3 id="6/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/i/a)

A [forcing](../../../forcing.md) [collapses a cardinal](../../../forcing.md#cardinal-collapse) $\kappa$ over $M$ if it forces that the ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\kappa$ is no longer a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number): in the extension there is a [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection) from some smaller [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) onto $\kappa$. Equivalently there is a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) onto $\kappa$ from an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) whose ground [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality) is smaller. [Forcing](../../../forcing.md) preserves the [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) itself; **collapse changes [cardinality](../../../set-theory.md#cardinality), not the identity of the [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal)**. If the assertion is about one particular [generic extension](../../../forcing.md#generic-extension), the witnessing collapse is interpreted there; a forcing-wide assertion means it is forced by the weakest condition.

<h4 id="6/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/i/b)

**A [set](../../../set.md) $G\subseteq\mathbb P$ is a [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter) over $M$ if it is nonempty, is closed towards weaker conditions, is directed towards stronger common extensions, and meets every dense [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $D\subseteq\mathbb P$ belonging to $M$.** Density means that every condition has a stronger extension in $D$. Genericity is relative to $M$, not to every [dense subset of a forcing order](../../../forcing.md#dense-subset-of-a-forcing-order) in the ambient universe. Under the printed weaker-first convention in Question 5, closure is towards smaller conditions and directedness is towards larger ones; in the standard stronger-first notation these inequalities reverse.

<h4 id="6/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/i/c)

The [Delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system-lemma) states that every uncountable family of finite [sets](../../../set.md) contains an uncountable subfamily $\mathcal D$ and a fixed finite root $r$ such that

$$
\boxed{A\cap B=r\quad\text{for all distinct }A,B\in\mathcal D.}
$$

At a regular uncountable [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\kappa$, a family of $\kappa$ finite [sets](../../../set.md) has a [Delta-system](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system) subfamily of size $\kappa$. The root may be empty. Neither version asserts that all [sets](../../../set.md) in the original family have the same intersection.

<h3 id="6/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/ii)

Use $\kappa$-completeness in its usual [forcing](../../../forcing.md) sense: every decreasing [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) of stronger conditions of length less than $\kappa$ has a common stronger bound. Let $\dot f\in M$ and choose $p\in G$ [forcing](../../../forcing.md) that it [functions](../../../function.md) the ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha<\kappa$ into the ground [set](../../../set.md) $B$. Below any stronger condition $r$, recursively decide each value of $\dot f$ in order. At a successor step the deciding conditions are dense, and at a limit stage use $\kappa$-completeness. After all $\alpha$ steps take another common bound. The recursion and its choices can be performed in $M$, using ground choice and closure, and it records a [function](../../../function.md) $g:\alpha\to B$ in $M$.

Thus below every $r$ stronger than $p$ there is a condition [forcing](../../../forcing.md) $\dot f=\check g$ for some ground $g$. The [set](../../../set.md) $D$ of such whole-function deciding conditions belongs to $M$ and is dense below $p$. Genericity with $p\in G$ makes $G$ meet $D$: adjoin the conditions incompatible with $p$ to obtain a globally [dense subset of a forcing order](../../../forcing.md#dense-subset-of-a-forcing-order), and use directedness to rule out the incompatible alternative. A condition in $G\cap D$ then gives $f=g\in M$.

The reverse inclusion follows because ground [functions](../../../function.md) remain [functions](../../../function.md) with the same domain and values. Therefore

$$
\boxed{({}^\alpha B)^M=({}^\alpha B)^{M[G]}.}
$$

This [closed forcing adds no short ground-valued sequences](../../../forcing.md#closed-forcing-adds-no-short-ground-valued-sequences) argument needs density of complete decisions. A single arbitrarily constructed lower bound need not belong to $G$, and would not by itself prove the claim.

<h3 id="6/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6/iii)

Start with a model of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice)+$V=L$, which also satisfies [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis), and add one Cohen real by the countable [forcing](../../../forcing.md) of finite binary sequences. Its [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) preserves [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). For every infinite ground [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\lambda$, a nice [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) for a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\lambda$ is specified by $\lambda$ countable [forcing antichains](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) of a countable [forcing](../../../forcing.md). The number of such [forcing names](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) is at most

$$
(2^{\aleph_0})^\lambda\le(2^\lambda)^\lambda=2^\lambda=\lambda^+
$$

in the ground model. The ground [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) already supply the preserved lower bound $\lambda^+$. Thus the extension still satisfies $2^\lambda=\lambda^+$ for every infinite [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number), namely [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis).

The Cohen real is not in the ground model: for each ground real it is dense to disagree at a new coordinate. [Forcing](../../../forcing.md) leaves the [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) unchanged, and constructible levels are absolute, so the extension has the same [constructible universe](../../../definable-power-set.md#constructible-universe) as the ground model. The new real is therefore not constructible. We obtain

$$
\boxed{M[G]\models\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{GCH}+V\ne L.}
$$

The [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem) makes this a relative-consistency construction. Thus, if [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) is consistent, [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice)+[Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) does not prove $V=L$. This is the [one-Cohen-real preservation of GCH](../../../forcing.md#one-cohen-real-preservation-of-gch) argument, not a claim that every arbitrary GCH-preserving [forcing](../../../forcing.md) leaves $V=L$ unchanged.

<h3 id="6/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iv/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#6/iv)

<h5 id="6/iv/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/iv/a)

We construct a stronger family: every [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $e_\alpha:\alpha\to\omega$ will have coinfinite range, and coherence means agreement modulo finitely many arguments. Begin with the empty [function](../../../function.md). At a successor, extend $e_\alpha$ by assigning the new argument a value outside its range; the range remains coinfinite, and coherence with earlier [functions](../../../function.md) is unchanged.

At a countable limit $\alpha$, take $0=\alpha_0<\alpha_1<\cdots$ cofinal in $\alpha$. We build [injections](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $p_n:\alpha_n\to\omega$, extending one another exactly, with $p_n$ differing from $e_{\alpha_n}$ at finitely many arguments. Also reserve distinct numbers $k_0,k_1,\ldots$, never used by the eventual union. After constructing $p_n$, choose $k_n$ outside its range and different from the earlier reserved numbers; its range is coinfinite since it differs finitely from that of $e_{\alpha_n}$.

To extend to $\alpha_{n+1}$, first use $e_{\alpha_{n+1}}$ on the new arguments while keeping $p_n$ on the old ones. Only finitely many collisions can arise: $p_n$ and $e_{\alpha_{n+1}}\upharpoonright\alpha_n$ differ finitely by the inductive coherence hypothesis, so their image [sets](../../../set.md) differ finitely. The candidate is otherwise injective. There are also only finitely many new arguments assigned a value among $k_0,\ldots,k_n$. Reassign these finitely many bad arguments to distinct fresh values outside the candidate image and the reserved [finite set](../../../set.md#finite-set). Infinitely many fresh values are available because the candidate image differs only finitely from the coinfinite image of $e_{\alpha_{n+1}}$. The resulting $p_{n+1}$ is injective, extends $p_n$, omits the reserved numbers, and differs finitely from $e_{\alpha_{n+1}}$.

Put $e_\alpha=\bigcup_np_n$. It is injective and omits all $k_n$, so its range is coinfinite. For each $\beta<\alpha$, choose $n$ with $\beta\le\alpha_n$. The restriction $e_\alpha\upharpoonright\beta$ is $p_n\upharpoonright\beta$, which differs finitely from $e_{\alpha_n}\upharpoonright\beta$, and hence from $e_\beta$. [Transfinite recursion](../../../set-theory.md#transfinite-recursion) now gives

$$
\boxed{e_\alpha:\alpha\hookrightarrow\omega,\qquad
e_\alpha\upharpoonright\beta=^*e_\beta\quad(\beta<\alpha<\omega_1).}
$$

These [coherent coinfinite injections into omega](../../../algebra.md#coherent-coinfinite-injections-into-omega) solve the requested problem. Reserving infinitely many omitted values is important: a union of [injections](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) with individually coinfinite ranges need not itself have coinfinite range.

<h4 id="6/iv/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#6/iv)

<h5 id="6/iv/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/iv/b)

Use the displayed family to form the [coherent-injection Aronszajn tree](../../../set.md#coherent-injection-aronszajn-tree). A node at level $\beta$ is a restriction $e_\alpha\upharpoonright\beta$ for some $\alpha\ge\beta$. Every such node differs only finitely from $e_\beta$. There are countably many finite [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of the countable domain $\beta$ and countably many assignments of natural-number values to each, so there are only countably many possible finite modifications. Hence each level is countable. It is nonempty because it contains $e_\beta$.

Every shorter restriction of a node is again a node, and its predecessors have order type its domain [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). Thus this is a [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) of height $\omega_1$. If it had an uncountable [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order), its domain heights would be unbounded in $\omega_1$, since the levels below any countable height contain only countably many nodes. The union of that [chain in a partial order](../../../set.md#chain-in-a-partial-order) would be an [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $\omega_1\to\omega$, impossible. Therefore

$$
\boxed{T\text{ is an }\aleph_1\text{-Aronszajn tree}.}
$$

The countable-level proof uses coherence, whereas the no-branch proof uses injectivity; the two features play different roles.
