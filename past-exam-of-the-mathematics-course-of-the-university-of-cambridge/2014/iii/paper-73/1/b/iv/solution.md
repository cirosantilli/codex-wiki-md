<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Put $\mathbf W_2=\mathbf F_2/\zeta$. To the needed accuracy the [Faxén translation law](../../../../../../../faxen-s-first-law.md) gives $\mathbf U_2=\mathbf W_2+\mathsf M\mathbf V+O(\epsilon^3|\mathbf V|)$. Enforcing its prescribed [velocity](../../../../../../../velocity.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F_2=6\pi\mu a(I-\mathsf M)\mathbf V
+O(6\pi\mu a\epsilon^3|\mathbf V|).}
$$

Here the [force](../../../../../../../force.md) on sphere 1 remains prescribed; no extra second-order inversion term is needed in $\mathbf F_2$. Substitute this [force](../../../../../../../force.md) into the incident field at sphere 1:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf U_1=(I+\mathsf M-\mathsf M^2)\mathbf V
+O(\epsilon^3|\mathbf V|),\qquad
\mathsf M^2=\frac{9a^2}{16R^2}(I+3\mathbf n\mathbf n).}
$$

The negative second-order term reflects the reduced [force](../../../../../../../force.md) required on sphere 2 because sphere 1 already entrains it.
