<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume $|\xi\rangle$ is normalized and the [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) has dimension $d$. The rank-one [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) $P=|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|$ satisfies $P^\dagger=P$ and $P^2=P$.

For $d>1$, $P$ has [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) zero on the orthogonal complement of $|\xi\rangle$, so it cannot be a [unitary operator](../../../../../../unitary-operator.md). The complementary [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) $I-P$ kills $|\xi\rangle$ and is not unitary in any positive dimension. In contrast, the [Householder reflection](../../../../../../householder-transformation.md)

$$
R=I-2P
$$

is Hermitian and obeys $R^\dagger R=R^2=I-4P+4P^2=I$. Its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $-1$ along $|\xi\rangle$ and $+1$ on the orthogonal complement.

**For $d>1$, only $I-2|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|$ is unitary. In the exceptional one-dimensional case, $|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|=I$ is also unitary; its complement remains zero.**
