<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Both displayed matrices have determinant one and integer entries, so the generated [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $M$ lies in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. For any nonidentity matrix $g\in M$, some entry of $g-I$ is a nonzero integer $k$. Choose a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$ not dividing $k$. The [reduction modulo a prime in an integral matrix group](../../../../../../reduction-modulo-a-prime-in-an-integral-matrix-group.md) gives a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md)

$$
M\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
$$

to a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md) in which $g$ is nonidentity. **$\boxed{M\text{ is residually finite}.}$** No determination of the abstract subgroup generated by the two matrices is needed.
