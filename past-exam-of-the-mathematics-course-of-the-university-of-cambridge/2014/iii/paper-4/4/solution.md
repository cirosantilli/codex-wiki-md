<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

A [residually finite group](../../../../../residually-finite-group.md) $G$ has the property that every $1\ne g\in G$ survives under a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) to some [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md). Equivalently, the intersection of its finite-index [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) is trivial. A [Hopfian group](../../../../../hopfian-group.md) is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) for which every surjective [endomorphism](../../../../../endomorphism.md) is an [automorphism](../../../../../automorphism.md).

Suppose $G$ is generated by $d$ elements. A [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $G\to S_n$ is determined by the images of these generators, so there are at most $(n!)^d$ such maps. Every subgroup of index $n$ gives a transitive coset [group action](../../../../../group-action.md) on an $n$-element [set](../../../../../set-split.md), and the subgroup is the stabilizer of a point in that action. There are at most $n$ point stabilizers per action. The [finite-index subgroup count for a finitely generated group](../../../../../finite-index-subgroup-count-for-a-finitely-generated-group.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\#\{H\leq G:[G:H]=n\}\leq n(n!)^d<\infty.}
$$

Now let $\psi:G\to G$ be a surjective [endomorphism](../../../../../endomorphism.md). For any fixed $n$, inverse image under $\psi$ preserves the index of a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). It is also an [injective function](../../../../../injective-function.md) on the finite [set](../../../../../set-split.md) of normal subgroups of index $n$: if $\psi^{-1}(M)=\psi^{-1}(N)$, surjectivity gives $M=N$. It is therefore a [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) of that finite [set](../../../../../set-split.md). Given any finite-index [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N$, there is another such subgroup $M$ with $N=\psi^{-1}(M)$, so $\ker\psi\leq N$. If $G$ is a [residually finite group](../../../../../residually-finite-group.md), intersecting all these $N$ gives $\ker\psi=1$. Hence $\psi$ is an [automorphism](../../../../../automorphism.md). **Every [finitely generated group](../../../../../finitely-generated-group.md) that is a [residually finite group](../../../../../residually-finite-group.md) is a [Hopfian group](../../../../../hopfian-group.md).**

A useful [residual finiteness of semidirect products](../../../../../residual-finiteness-of-semidirect-products.md) theorem is: if $K$ is a [finitely generated group](../../../../../finitely-generated-group.md), then

$$
\boxed{K\rtimes H\text{ is residually finite}\iff K\text{ and }H\text{ are residually finite}.}
$$

More generally, the forward construction only requires that $K$ have a separating family of finite-index [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) invariant under the $H$ action, and that $H$ be a [residually finite group](../../../../../residually-finite-group.md). Necessity follows by restricting finite separating maps to the embedded [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) $K$ and $H$.

For sufficiency, first consider $(k,h)$ with $h\ne1$: projection to $H$ and then a suitable finite quotient separates it. If $h=1$ and $k\ne1$, choose a finite-index [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $U\trianglelefteq K$ with $k\notin U$. Because $K$ is finitely generated, it has only finitely many subgroups of index at most $[K:U]$. Their intersection $C$ is a finite-index [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md) of $K$, is contained in $U$, and is invariant under every automorphism in the $H$ action. The quotient $K/C$ is finite. Let $\rho:H\to\operatorname{Aut}(K/C)$ be the induced action. The map

$$
K\rtimes H\longrightarrow (K/C)\rtimes\rho(H),\qquad (x,y)\longmapsto(xC,\rho(y))
$$

is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) to a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) and separates $(k,1)$. This proves the theorem and the more general invariant-subgroup criterion. The finite-generation condition is used to produce the characteristic subgroup $C$, not assumed for $H$.
