↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Yes: the displayed presentation already has [p-deficiency](../../../../../../p-deficiency.md) exactly one for $p=2$.** The words $x,y,z,xy,yz,zx$ are not proper powers in the [free group](../../../../../../free-group.md). For the length-two words this follows directly from their distinct consecutive letters in a [cyclically reduced word](../../../../../../cyclically-reduced-word.md). Thus each relator's $2$-root exponent is precisely the exponent of $2$ in its displayed power. The relator weights, in the given order, are

$$
\frac12,\quad1,\quad\frac14,\quad\frac1{16},\quad\frac1{16},\quad\frac18.
$$

Their sum is $2$, giving

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{def}_2=3-\left(\frac12+1+\frac14+\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}+\frac18\right)=1.}
$$

In particular the infinitude criterion proves that this group is infinite, although the question only asks for the existence of the presentation and prime.
