↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes, for $p=2$.** The displayed [group presentation](../../../../../../group-presentation.md) is that of the infinite [dihedral group](../../../../../../dihedral-group.md). Put $u=at$ and $v=t$. Then $v^2=1$ and

$$
u^2=atat=a(tat^{-1})t^2=aa^{-1}=1.
$$

Conversely, from $u^2=v^2=1$, set $a=uv$ and $t=v$; then $tat^{-1}=vu=a^{-1}$. These inverse substitutions give

$$
G_2\cong\langle u,v\mid u^2,v^2\rangle\cong C_2*C_2.
$$

Each relator has free-group $2$-root exponent one, so

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{def}_2(\langle u,v\mid u^2,v^2\rangle)=2-\frac12-\frac12=1.}
$$

The change of presentation matters: the original presentation's mixed conjugation relator has weight one and would give a smaller [p-deficiency](../../../../../../p-deficiency.md).
