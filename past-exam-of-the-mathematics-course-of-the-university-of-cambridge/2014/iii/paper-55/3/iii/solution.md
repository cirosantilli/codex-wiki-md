<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $\mathcal R=k_B/m_u$, with $m_u$ the atomic mass constant and $a_r$ the radiation energy-density constant. The [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) and [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) contributions obey $P_g=\rho\mathcal RT/\mu$ and $P_r=a_rT^4/3$. With the constant [stellar gas-pressure fraction](../../../../../../stellar-gas-pressure-fraction.md), eliminate $T=\mu\beta P/(\mathcal R\rho)$ to get

$$
(1-\beta)P=\frac{a_r}{3}\left(\frac{\mu\beta P}{\mathcal R\rho}\right)^4.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{P=K\rho^{4/3},\qquad
K=\left(\frac3{a_r}\right)^{1/3}
\left(\frac{\mathcal R}{\mu}\right)^{4/3}
\left(\frac{1-\beta}{\beta^4}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

Since $\mu$ and $\beta$ are spatially constant, so is $K$. Comparing with $P=K\rho^{1+1/n}$ gives **[polytropic index](../../../../../../polytropic-index.md) $n=3$**. This structural [stellar polytrope](../../../../../../stellar-polytrope.md) relation does not assert that every perturbed fluid parcel has [stellar adiabatic exponent](../../../../../../stellar-adiabatic-exponent.md) $4/3$.
