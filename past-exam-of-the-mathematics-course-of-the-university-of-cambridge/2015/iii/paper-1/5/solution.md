↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

A [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) is a nonzero ring with one [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$. Write $\kappa=R/\mathfrak m$ for its [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md). Its [Krull dimension](../../../../../krull-dimension.md) $\dim R$ is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md); in a [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) chains may be extended to end at $\mathfrak m$.

For the local Hilbert-function convention, define the [Hilbert–Samuel growth dimension](../../../../../hilbert-samuel-growth-dimension.md)

$$
\boxed{d(R)=\deg\bigl(\text{the eventual polynomial }
H_R(t)=\operatorname{length}(R/\mathfrak m^t)\bigr).}
$$

Equivalently it is the order of the pole at $z=1$ of the [Hilbert series](../../../../../hilbert-series.md) of the [associated graded ring](../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md)

$$
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}.
$$

This finite standard graded $\kappa$-algebra is generated by $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. The [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md) makes the cumulative Hilbert function eventually polynomial, which establishes the definition. For an [Artinian local ring](../../../../../artinian-local-ring.md), the polynomial is constant and nonzero, so $d(R)=0$.

For every [prime chain](../../../../../prime-chain.md) of length $s$, the [prime-chain lower bound for local length](../../../../../prime-chain-lower-bound-for-local-length.md) proved in Question 4 gives $H_R(t)\geq c t^s$. A polynomial of degree $d(R)$ cannot satisfy this when $s>d(R)$. Thus **the requested inequality is**

$$
\boxed{\dim R\leq d(R).}
$$

For clarity, the [embedding dimension](../../../../../embedding-dimension.md) is the different invariant

$$
e(R)=\dim_\kappa(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)
=\text{the minimal number of generators of }\mathfrak m.
$$

The equality follows from the [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md). Applying Question 4 to $I=\mathfrak m$ gives $\dim R\leq e(R)$, while the polynomial-algebra surjection below also gives $d(R)\leq e(R)$. In particular, growth dimension and embedding dimension should be kept distinct.

A [regular local ring](../../../../../regular-local-ring.md) is a [Noetherian local ring](../../../../../noetherian-local-ring.md) with $e(R)=\dim R$. Let this common value be $e$ and choose a minimal generating set $x_1,\ldots,x_e$ of $\mathfrak m$. The initial forms give a surjective graded [ring homomorphism](../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md)

$$
\kappa[T_1,\ldots,T_e]\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R.
$$

For $e=0$, the [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md) gives $\mathfrak m=0$, so $R$ is a [field](../../../../../field.md). Assume $e>0$. If the kernel contained a nonzero [homogeneous polynomial](../../../../../homogeneous-polynomial.md) $F$ of degree $q>0$, the cumulative Hilbert function of the target would be bounded by that of $\kappa[T_1,\ldots,T_e]/(F)$. Multiplication by $F$ is injective in the polynomial [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md), so this bound is

$$
\binom{t+e-1}{e}-\binom{t-q+e-1}{e}=O(t^{e-1})\qquad(t>q).
$$

But $\dim R=e$ provides a [prime chain](../../../../../prime-chain.md) of length $e$, and Question 4 gives $H_R(t)\geq ct^e$. This is a contradiction. Hence the [associated graded ring of a regular local ring](../../../../../associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R\cong\kappa[T_1,\ldots,T_e],}
$$

an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md).

Finally the [Krull intersection theorem](../../../../../krull-intersection-theorem.md) gives $\bigcap_j\mathfrak m^j=0$. One can see the needed separatedness directly: for the finitely generated [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $K=\bigcap_j\mathfrak m^j$, the [Artin-Rees lemma](../../../../../artin-rees-lemma.md) gives $K=\mathfrak mK$, and the [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md) gives $K=0$. Therefore each nonzero $a\in R$ has finite $\mathfrak m$-adic order $r$, with nonzero initial form in $\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1}$. For nonzero $a,b$, their initial forms have nonzero product in the graded [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md). It follows that $ab\ne0$, and indeed its order is the sum of their orders. **Thus $R$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md).**
