<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

The [height of a prime ideal](../../../../../height-of-a-prime-ideal.md) is the supremum of the lengths of strict chains of [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) ending at $P$:

$$
\operatorname{ht}P=\sup\{d:P_0\subsetneq P_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_d=P\}
=\dim R_P.
$$

The last equality follows from the [prime ideal correspondence for localization](../../../../../prime-ideal-correspondence-for-localization.md). Here is a length-growth proof of the [Krull height theorem](../../../../../krull-height-theorem.md) that makes the bound explicit.

We first establish a [prime-chain lower bound for local length](../../../../../prime-chain-lower-bound-for-local-length.md). For a [Noetherian local ring](../../../../../noetherian-local-ring.md) $(B,\mathfrak m)$ admitting a [prime chain](../../../../../prime-chain.md) of length $d$, put $H_B(t)=\operatorname{length}(B/\mathfrak m^t)$. Then

$$
H_B(t)\geq c\,t^d\qquad(t\gg0)
$$

for some $c>0$. For $d=0$, use $H_B(t)\geq1$. For $d>0$, quotient by the first [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of the chain; this only decreases $H_B$ and reduces us to a [local integral domain](../../../../../local-integral-domain.md) $B$ with

$$
0\subsetneq P_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_d=\mathfrak m.
$$

Take $0\ne a\in P_1$ and put $C=B/(a)$. It has a [prime chain](../../../../../prime-chain.md) of length $d-1$, so induction bounds $H_C(t)$ below by a positive multiple of $t^{d-1}$.

The [Artin-Rees lemma](../../../../../artin-rees-lemma.md) applied to $(a)\subseteq B$ gives, for a fixed integer $c_0\geq1$,

$$
(a)\cap\mathfrak m^t\subseteq a\mathfrak m^{t-c_0}\qquad(t\geq c_0).
$$

Because $a$ is a [non-zero-divisor](../../../../../non-zero-divisor.md), the first term in the [exact sequence](../../../../../exact-sequence.md)

$$
0\longrightarrow (a)/((a)\cap\mathfrak m^t)
\longrightarrow B/\mathfrak m^t
\longrightarrow B/((a)+\mathfrak m^t)\longrightarrow0
$$

has length at least $H_B(t-c_0)$. Hence

$$
H_B(t)\geq H_B(t-c_0)+H_C(t).
$$

Iterate this inequality about $t/(2c_0)$ times, while its arguments remain at least $t/2$. This gives the desired positive multiple of $t^d$. The particular [Artin-Rees lemma](../../../../../artin-rees-lemma.md) inclusion used here follows directly from finite generation: the [Rees algebra](../../../../../rees-algebra.md) $\bigoplus_{j\geq0}\mathfrak m^jt^j$ is [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), by the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md), and its graded submodule $\bigoplus_j((a)\cap\mathfrak m^j)t^j$ has finitely many homogeneous generators. A bound on their degrees gives $c_0$.

Now localize at the given minimal [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $P$. Write $B=R_P$, $\mathfrak m=PR_P$, and $J=IR_P$. The only [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of $B$ containing $J$ is $\mathfrak m$, so $\sqrt J=\mathfrak m$. Since $\mathfrak m$ is finitely generated, some $\mathfrak m^q\subseteq J$. Thus $B/J$ has finite [module length](../../../../../length-of-a-module.md), say $L$. The [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $J$ is generated by at most $n$ elements.

For $n>0$, products of these generators give a surjection from $\binom{j+n-1}{n-1}$ copies of $B/J$ onto $J^j/J^{j+1}$. Summing the lengths gives the [generator bound for primary-ideal length](../../../../../generator-bound-for-primary-ideal-length.md)

$$
\operatorname{length}(B/J^t)
\leq L\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\binom{j+n-1}{n-1}
=L\binom{t+n-1}{n}=O(t^n).
$$

Since $J\subseteq\mathfrak m$, we have $J^t\subseteq\mathfrak m^t$, and therefore

$$
H_B(t)\leq\operatorname{length}(B/J^t)=O(t^n).
$$

The [prime-chain lower bound for local length](../../../../../prime-chain-lower-bound-for-local-length.md) now excludes any chain of length greater than $n$. **Consequently**

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}P=\dim R_P\leq n.}
$$

For $n=0$, minimality over the zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) says that $P$ is a minimal [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md), so its height is zero. This also handles that boundary case.
