↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [weakly stationary process](../../../../../../weakly-stationary-process.md) has finite [second moments](../../../../../../second-moment.md), a constant [expected value](../../../../../../expected-value.md) $\mu$, and [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md) depending only on the time difference:

$$
\mathbb EX_t=\mu,\qquad\operatorname{Cov}(X_{t+h},X_t)=\gamma(h).
$$

A [strictly stationary process](../../../../../../strictly-stationary-process.md), also called strongly stationary, has every finite-dimensional [probability distribution](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) invariant under a common shift: for every finite choice of times and every shift $h$, $(X_{t_1+h},\ldots,X_{t_m+h})$ and $(X_{t_1},\ldots,X_{t_m})$ have the same law. [strict stationarity](../../../../../../strictly-stationary-process.md) with finite [second moments](../../../../../../second-moment.md) implies [weak stationarity](../../../../../../weakly-stationary-process.md). [weak stationarity](../../../../../../weakly-stationary-process.md) alone does not determine the full joint law, and [strict stationarity](../../../../../../strictly-stationary-process.md) alone does not guarantee finite moments.
