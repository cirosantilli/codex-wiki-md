<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Assume $F$ preserves existing [categorical limits](../../../../../../../categorical-limit.md). For a [diagram in a category](../../../../../../../diagram-category-theory.md) $E:I\to\mathcal B$ with [categorical limit](../../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) cone $\ell_i:L\to E_i$, its image $F\ell_i$ is a [categorical limit](../../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) cone of $FE$. For any $C\in\mathcal C$, a [morphism](../../../../../../../morphism.md) $C\to FL$ is therefore uniquely equivalent to a compatible family of [morphisms](../../../../../../../morphism.md) $C\to FE_i$. In the [Category of sets](../../../../../../../category-of-sets.md), compatible families are exactly the [categorical limit](../../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) of the resulting [hom-sets](../../../../../../../hom-set.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal C(C,FL)\cong\lim_i\mathcal C(C,FE_i).}
$$

The maps are induced by $F\ell_i$, so **$\mathcal C(C,F-)$ preserves the given limit**. Applying this to every existing [categorical limit](../../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) proves the implication, with no completeness hypothesis on $\mathcal B$.
