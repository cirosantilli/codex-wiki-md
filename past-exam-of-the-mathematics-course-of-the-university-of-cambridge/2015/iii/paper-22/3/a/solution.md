<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the orientation $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ and $G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal C$. An [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) $F\dashv G$ is equivalently specified by the [natural transformations](../../../../../../natural-transformation.md)

$$
\eta:1_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow GF,\qquad
\varepsilon:FG\Rightarrow1_{\mathcal D},
$$

called the [unit and counit of an adjunction](../../../../../../unit-and-counit-of-an-adjunction.md), satisfying the [triangle identities for an adjunction](../../../../../../triangle-identities-for-an-adjunction.md)

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_{FC}\circ F(\eta_C)=1_{FC},\qquad
G(\varepsilon_D)\circ\eta_{GD}=1_{GD}.}
$$

The corresponding [natural bijection](../../../../../../natural-bijection.md) is $\mathcal D(FC,D)\cong\mathcal C(C,GD)$, with $f\mapsto G(f)\eta_C$ and inverse $g\mapsto\varepsilon_DF(g)$. **The two triangular equations are the required compatibility conditions.** No proof of equivalence of the formulations is needed here.
