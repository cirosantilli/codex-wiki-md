<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) $C$ of [geometric genus](../../../../../../geometric-genus.md) $g$ and a [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) $D$, write $\ell(D)=h^0(C,\mathcal O_C(D))$ and let $K_C$ be a [canonical divisor](../../../../../../canonical-divisor.md). The curve form of the [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) is

$$
\boxed{\ell(D)-\ell(K_C-D)=\deg D+1-g.}
$$

Here $\deg K_C=2g-2$ and $\ell(K_C)=g$. In [line bundle](../../../../../../line-bundle.md) form,

$$
h^0(C,\mathcal L)-h^0(C,\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})=\deg\mathcal L+1-g.
$$

The usual curve convention is a smooth geometrically integral projective curve; geometric embedding tests may be checked after extending the ground field to an algebraic closure.
