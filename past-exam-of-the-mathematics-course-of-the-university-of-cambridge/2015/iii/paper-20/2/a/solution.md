<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a point $y\in Y$ with [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md) $\kappa(y)$, the [scheme-theoretic fibre](../../../../../../scheme-theoretic-fibre.md) is the [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../../../../base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes.md)

$$
\boxed{X_y=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y).}
$$

The map $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y)\to Y$ is the canonical point map. This [fibre product of schemes](../../../../../../fiber-product-of-schemes.md) retains the [structure sheaf](../../../../../../structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme.md), including any nilpotents, rather than just the set $f^{-1}(y)$. For an [affine scheme](../../../../../../affine-scheme.md) map induced by $A\to B$, at $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A$ it is

$$
X_{\mathfrak p}=\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)).
$$
