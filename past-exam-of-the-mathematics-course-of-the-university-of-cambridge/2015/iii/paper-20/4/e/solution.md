<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Since $g$ is a [separated morphism](../../../../../../separated-morphism.md), the [diagonal morphism](../../../../../../diagonal-morphism.md) $\Delta_g:Y\to Y\times_ZY$ is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md). The square

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
X&\xrightarrow{\Gamma_f}&X\times_ZY\\
{\scriptstyle f}\downarrow&&\downarrow{\scriptstyle(f\circ p_X,p_Y)}\\
Y&\xrightarrow{\Delta_g}&Y\times_ZY
\end{array}
$$

is cartesian. Indeed, on test schemes its pullback condition is $(f(x),y)=(t,t)$, forcing $y=t=f(x)$, and leaving exactly the choice of $x$. The [graph morphism of schemes](../../../../../../graph-morphism-of-schemes.md) $\Gamma_f$ is therefore a [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../../../../base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes.md) of $\Delta_g$. Part (c) shows that **$\Gamma_f$ is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md)**.

Now assume additionally that $h=g\circ f:X\to Z$ is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md). The projection $p_Y:X\times_ZY\to Y$ is its [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../../../../base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes.md) along $g$, so is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md) by part (c). Since

$$
\boxed{f=p_Y\circ\Gamma_f,}
$$

part (b) shows that **$f$ is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md)**. This covers the further assumption in the final unheaded paragraph.
