<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In the [tight-coupling approximation](../../../../../../tight-coupling-approximation.md), the Thomson-scattering rate $\Gamma_T=-\tau'$ is much faster than both spatial streaming and cosmological evolution: $k/\Gamma_T\ll1$ and $\mathcal H/\Gamma_T\ll1$. Frequent scattering locks the photon and baryon velocities and suppresses higher angular multipoles. The [photon dipole](../../../../../../photon-dipole.md) is leading order, while the [photon quadrupole](../../../../../../photon-quadrupole.md) is first order in the mean-free-time expansion.

Use the quadrupole equation. Its time derivative is smaller than its collision term by an additional expansion parameter, and $\Theta_3$ starts one order beyond $\Theta_2$. The leading balance is therefore

$$
-\frac{2k}3\Theta_1=\frac9{10}\tau'\Theta_2.
$$

Thus **for the temperature-only collision operator supplied here**,

$$
\boxed{\Theta_2=-\frac{20}{27}\frac k{\tau'}\Theta_1
=\frac{20}{27}\frac k{\Gamma_T}\Theta_1.}
$$

The [temperature-only tight-coupling quadrupole](../../../../../../temperature-only-tight-coupling-quadrupole.md) is not numerically interchangeable with a hierarchy that includes polarization feedback. The latter changes the leading collision coefficient and gives the existing [photon quadrupole in tight coupling with polarization](../../../../../../photon-quadrupole-in-tight-coupling-with-polarization.md) instead. Here the stated temperature-only approximation is the one being expanded.
