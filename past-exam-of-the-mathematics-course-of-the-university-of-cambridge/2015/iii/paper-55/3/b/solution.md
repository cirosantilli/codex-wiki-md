<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Along the zeroth-order photon path, set $Y=\Theta+\psi$ and $\mathcal S=(3/4)\int d\hat{\mathbf m}\,\Theta(\hat{\mathbf m})[1+(\mathbf e\cdot\hat{\mathbf m})^2]/(4\pi)$. The [scalar photon energy redshift](../../../../../../scalar-photon-energy-redshift.md) and the [Photon Boltzmann equation with Thomson scattering](../../../../../../photon-boltzmann-equation-with-thomson-scattering.md) give

$$
\frac{dY}{d\eta}=\phi'+\psi'+\tau'\left[Y-\psi-\mathcal S-\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_b\right].
$$

The integrating factor is $e^{-\tau}$, because $\tau'<0$ in this convention. Hence

$$
\frac d{d\eta}(e^{-\tau}Y)=e^{-\tau}(\phi'+\psi')
+g(\eta)(\psi+\mathcal S+\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_b),\quad g=-\tau'e^{-\tau}.
$$

[Tight coupling](../../../../../../tight-coupling-approximation.md) before decoupling makes the incident distribution nearly isotropic in the baryon frame. Neglecting its quadrupole, the angular kernel integrates to $\mathcal S=\Theta_0$, since $(3/4)\langle1+(\mathbf e\cdot\hat{\mathbf m})^2\rangle=1$. The primordial term at the optically thick lower endpoint is erased. With instantaneous last scattering, no later scattering and $g=\delta(\eta-\eta_*)$, **the line-of-sight solution is**

$$
\boxed{\Theta(\eta_0,\mathbf x_0,\mathbf e)+\psi(\eta_0,\mathbf x_0)
=\left(\Theta_0+\psi+\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_b\right)(\eta_*,\mathbf x_0-\chi_*\mathbf e)
+\int_{\eta_*}^{\eta_0}d\eta\,(\phi'+\psi')(\eta,\mathbf x_0-(\eta_0-\eta)\mathbf e).}
$$

The approximation is linear, uses an unperturbed ray, neglects the scattering quadrupole and polarization feedback, and idealizes the [cosmological visibility function](../../../../../../cosmological-visibility-function.md) as a delta function. It also excludes [reionization](../../../../../../reionization.md) or any other post-recombination opacity.

The [photon monopole](../../../../../../photon-monopole.md) $\Theta_0$ is the intrinsic emission-temperature perturbation. The emission potential $\psi_*$ gives gravitational redshift on leaving the potential well; together they form the [Sachs-Wolfe combination](../../../../../../sachs-wolfe-combination.md). The baryon [peculiar velocity](../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) supplies the [Doppler CMB anisotropy](../../../../../../doppler-cmb-anisotropy.md). The integral is the [Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](../../../../../../integrated-sachs-wolfe-effect.md), from changes in gravitational potentials during travel. The observer potential is a direction-independent endpoint shift absorbed into the observed monopole. An observer [peculiar velocity](../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md), if included, contributes an additional kinematic dipole; the stated observer is coordinate-comoving.
