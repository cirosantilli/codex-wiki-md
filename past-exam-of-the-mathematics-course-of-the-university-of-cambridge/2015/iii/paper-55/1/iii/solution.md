<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $W=\bar\rho+\bar P\ne0$, the conserved [uniform-density curvature](../../../../../../uniform-density-curvature-perturbation.md) in the spatial-metric convention $1-2\Phi$ is

$$
\boxed{\zeta_{\rm ud}=-\Phi+\frac{\delta\rho}{3W}.}
$$

The source's plus sign in front of both terms is not gauge invariant and cannot yield the claimed conservation. Choosing the opposite overall sign, $\Phi-\delta\rho/(3W)$, is equally valid, but changing only one term is necessary. A passive time displacement $t\mapsto t+T$ gives $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi+\bar NH T$ and $\widetilde{\delta\rho}=\delta\rho+3\bar NHWT$, so their combination above is unchanged. This is the [uniform-density curvature sign test](../../../../../../uniform-density-curvature-sign-test.md).

The lapse perturbation in the perturbed energy-conservation source is $\Psi$, not the spatial-curvature perturbation $\Phi$. Indeed the perturbation of the proper-time density derivative is $\mathcal D\delta\rho-\Psi\mathcal D\bar\rho$, while the normal expansion changes by $-\kappa$. The consistent equation is

$$
\mathcal D\delta\rho=-3H(\delta\rho+\delta P)+W(\kappa-3H\Psi)-\Delta u.
$$

For a constant [barotropic equation of state](../../../../../../barotropic-equation-of-state.md), $\delta P=w\delta\rho$ and $\mathcal DW=-3H(1+w)W$. Differentiate $\zeta_{\rm ud}$ and cancel the density terms:

$$
\mathcal D\zeta_{\rm ud}=-\mathcal D\Phi+\frac\kappa3-H\Psi-\frac{\Delta u}{3W}
=\frac13\Delta\chi_+-\frac{\Delta u}{3W}.
$$

More generally, $\delta P_{\rm nad}=\delta P-(\mathcal D\bar P/\mathcal D\bar\rho)\delta\rho$ gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal D\zeta_{\rm ud}=-\frac{H}{W}\delta P_{\rm nad}
+\frac13\Delta\left(\chi_+-\frac uW\right).}
$$

Regular scalar modes have gradient terms suppressed by $(k/aH)^2$. Thus **adiabatic [uniform-density curvature](../../../../../../uniform-density-curvature-perturbation.md) is conserved to leading order on [superhorizon scales](../../../../../../superhorizon-scale.md)**, $\dot\zeta_{\rm ud}\simeq0$. This is a long-wavelength limit, not exact finite-wavenumber conservation; it assumes a regular gradient expansion and excludes $W=0$, where this variable is undefined. In contrast the literal printed variable obeys $\mathcal D\zeta_{\rm printed}=2\mathcal D\Phi+O(k^2)$ even after repairing the energy equation, and generally evolves across changes of the background equation of state.

There are three practical benefits. First, [uniform-density curvature](../../../../../../uniform-density-curvature-perturbation.md) is gauge invariant, so matching it does not depend on coordinate slicing. Second, [superhorizon conservation of uniform-density curvature](../../../../../../superhorizon-conservation-of-uniform-density-curvature.md) transports primordial amplitudes through changes in the expansion history without solving each short-scale dynamical equation. Third, when a mode re-enters the horizon it supplies initial conditions for density growth and [Cosmic microwave background anisotropy](../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-anisotropy-split.md); non-adiabatic pressure is also an explicit diagnostic of when that simple transport fails.
