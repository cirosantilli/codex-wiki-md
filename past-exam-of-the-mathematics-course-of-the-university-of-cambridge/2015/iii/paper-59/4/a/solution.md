<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The usual major carbon/oxygen reservoirs in a hydrogen-rich [hot Jupiter](../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md) are **[water](../../../../../../water.md), [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) and [methane](../../../../../../methane.md)**, with their relative importance set by [thermochemical equilibrium](../../../../../../thermochemical-equilibrium.md). This is not a universal ranking for every temperature and composition: nitrogen molecules or [carbon dioxide](../../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) can exceed a strongly depleted member of this trio.

At about one bar, the useful net reaction is

$$
\mathrm{CO}+3\mathrm{H}_2\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{CH}_4+\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm O.
$$

The rightward reaction is exothermic. Cooler gas favors [methane](../../../../../../methane.md) and [water](../../../../../../water.md); warming favors [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) and suppresses [methane](../../../../../../methane.md). The CO/CH4 crossover is of order $10^3\,\mathrm K$ and shifts with pressure, elemental inventory and metallicity; it is not a universal temperature. [Water](../../../../../../water.md) remains an important oxygen reservoir for oxygen-rich compositions but can dissociate at sufficiently high temperature.

Increasing the [atmospheric metallicity of a giant planet](../../../../../../atmospheric-metallicity-of-a-giant-planet.md) raises the available carbon and oxygen. In a dilute, H2-dominated regime, major CO and H2O abundances roughly increase with the enrichment factor, and cool-regime CH4 does likewise. CO2 can rise faster, approximately quadratically in suitable warm regimes. At very high enrichment, the H2 fraction and [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) also change, invalidating simple linear scalings.

At high temperature and [atmospheric carbon-to-oxygen ratio](../../../../../../atmospheric-carbon-to-oxygen-ratio.md) below unity, CO binds much of the carbon, leaving excess oxygen for H2O. As C/O approaches or exceeds one, CO consumes nearly all available oxygen and **H2O is strongly depleted**; excess carbon enhances CH4, [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) and [acetylene](../../../../../../acetylene.md). Cooler CH4-dominated chemistry uses less oxygen in CO and can leave more [water](../../../../../../water.md) even at the same elemental ratio. These trends are [carbon partition and atmospheric water abundance](../../../../../../carbon-partition-and-atmospheric-water-abundance.md), and assume equilibrium rather than vertical or horizontal quenching.
