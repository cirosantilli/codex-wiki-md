- **Vertical chemical quenching:** when the [eddy mixing time](../../../../../../eddy-mixing-time.md) becomes shorter than the [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md), transported gas retains a deeper abundance rather than reaching local [thermochemical equilibrium](../../../../../../thermochemical-equilibrium.md). For example, [carbon monoxide–methane quenching](../../../../../../carbon-monoxide-methane-quenching.md) can maintain excess [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md) in cool upper giant-planet gas where equilibrium favors [methane](../../../../../../methane.md).
- **[Atmospheric photochemistry](../../../../../../atmospheric-photochemistry.md):** ultraviolet photons dissociate molecules and drive reaction networks away from thermal equilibrium. For example, irradiation of CH4-containing gas can produce [acetylene](../../../../../../acetylene.md) and hydrocarbon precursors of an [atmospheric haze](../../../../../../haze.md).
- **[Horizontal chemical quenching](../../../../../../horizontal-chemical-quenching.md):** winds cross a day-night temperature gradient faster than reactions can reset the composition. For example, CO-rich dayside gas can reach a cooler [hot Jupiter](../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md) nightside without converting most of its carbon to CH4.

These are three distinct ways to maintain [disequilibrium chemistry in an exoplanet atmosphere](../../../../../../disequilibrium-chemistry-in-an-exoplanet-atmosphere.md); each compares chemistry with a different transport or irradiation process.
