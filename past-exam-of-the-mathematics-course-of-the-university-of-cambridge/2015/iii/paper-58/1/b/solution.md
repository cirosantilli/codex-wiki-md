<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Evaluate [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) on the gas-supported [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) solution. At the centre,

$$
\frac{P_{{\rm rad},c}}{P_{{\rm gas},c}}=\frac{aT_c^4}{3P_c}
=\boxed{\frac{125\pi aG^3}{15552\mathcal R^4}\,M^2}.
$$

The factors $R^{-4}$ cancel between $T_c^4$ and $P_c$, so at fixed [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) the ratio depends only on mass. Equivalently it is $(M/M_{\rm eq})^2$, with

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm eq}=\sqrt{\frac{15552}{125\pi a}}\,\frac{\mathcal R^2}{G^{3/2}}=\sqrt{\frac{15552}{125\pi a}}\,\frac{k^2}{G^{3/2}\mu^2H^2}.}
$$

For $H\simeq1.6605\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ this is approximately $14.6\mu^{-2}M_\odot$, or $40.5M_\odot$ if $\mu=0.6$. If $H$ denotes hydrogen-atom mass instead, use the corresponding [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md) convention consistently; the coefficient is about $14.4\mu^{-2}M_\odot$. **Equality marks the breakdown of the gas-pressure approximation.** It is a diagnostic based on the gas-only model, not an exact mixed-pressure stellar solution.
