<h1 id="2/2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since the species decouples while relativistic, its comoving particle number is conserved. Once it becomes nonrelativistic, its energy density is $\rho_{\nu,0}\simeq m_\nu n_{\nu,0}$. With $R_n:=n_{\nu,0}/n_{\gamma,0}$,

$$
\frac{\Omega_\nu}{\Omega_\gamma}\simeq\frac{m_\nu R_n}{\langle E_\gamma\rangle},\qquad m_\nu\lesssim\frac{0.1}{\Omega_\gamma}\frac{\langle E_\gamma\rangle}{R_n}.
$$

This is a [density bound on a relativistically decoupled massive relic](../../../../../../../density-bound-on-a-relativistically-decoupled-massive-relic.md). Using literally the printed energy-density coefficient, the supplied number-density coefficient gives $\langle E_\gamma\rangle=(3.3/0.12)T_0=27.5T_0$. For $g_\nu=2$ and $g_d=100$,

$$
\boxed{m_\nu\lesssim2000\,\frac{27.5\times2\times10^{-13}}{0.0293}\,\mathrm{GeV}\simeq3.8\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{GeV}\simeq4\times10^2\,\mathrm{eV}\quad\text{(printed normalization)}.}
$$

The estimate scales as $(2/g_\nu)(g_d/100)$. At the bound the particles are safely nonrelativistic today, so neglecting their kinetic energy is consistent.

There is a physical normalization defect in the printed coefficient. Direct integration of the [Bose-Einstein distribution](../../../../../../../bose-einstein-distribution.md) gives

$$
\frac{\rho}{g}=\frac{T^4}{2\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3\,dx}{e^x-1}=\frac{\pi^2}{30}T^4\simeq0.329T^4,
$$

which is also the coefficient consistent with the supplied $1.66$ expansion-rate formula. It implies $\langle E_\gamma\rangle=\pi^4T_0/(30\zeta(3))\simeq2.70T_0$, rather than $27.5T_0$. With this physically normalized [photon](../../../../../../../photon.md) gas, the same [density bound on a relativistically decoupled massive relic](../../../../../../../density-bound-on-a-relativistically-decoupled-massive-relic.md) becomes **$m_\nu\lesssim3.7\times10^1\,\mathrm{eV}$**, or about **$4\times10^1\,\mathrm{eV}$** using the rounded coefficients with $0.33$ in place of $3.3$. The two estimates differ by the printed factor of ten; the entropy-dilution result is unaffected.
