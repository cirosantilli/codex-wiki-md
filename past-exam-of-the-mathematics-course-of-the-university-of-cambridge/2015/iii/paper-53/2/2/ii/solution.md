<h1 id="2/2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The preceding bound applies to a species that freezes out while relativistic, retaining an unsuppressed thermal number density apart from entropy dilution. It need not apply to a species that is already massive while reactions remain rapid.

For $m_\nu\gg T_{\rm dec}$, the interaction strength is sufficient to keep the species in [thermal equilibrium](../../../../../../../thermal-equilibrium.md) through its relativistic-to-nonrelativistic transition. At $T\sim m_\nu$, the relativistic scaling would give $\Gamma/H\sim(m_\nu/T_{\rm dec})^3\gg1$. Subsequent annihilation removes most particle-antiparticle pairs while equilibrium remains possible. At negligible [chemical potential](../../../../../../../chemical-potential.md), the nonrelativistic equilibrium number density is

$$
n_{\rm eq}\simeq g_\nu\left(\frac{m_\nu T}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_\nu/T}.
$$

This [Boltzmann suppression of a nonrelativistic relic](../../../../../../../boltzmann-suppression-of-a-nonrelativistic-relic.md) can make the eventual abundance much smaller than the relativistically decoupled value. Freeze-out now occurs when $n_{\rm eq}\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim H$, with a nonrelativistic cross-section, rather than by extrapolating the massless $T^5$ rate indefinitely. A large mass can therefore have an acceptably small energy density: **a hot-relic mass bound is not a universal bound on a heavy thermal relic**.

Compatibility depends on the annihilation rate and any conserved particle-antiparticle asymmetry; the supplied relativistic rate alone does not determine the heavy relic's final abundance. Efficient annihilation is the mechanism that permits large masses within the standard expansion and thermal history.
