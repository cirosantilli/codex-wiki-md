<h1 id="2/1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A collisionless relativistic species redshifts with $T_\nu\propto a^{-1}$. The coupled plasma instead obeys [cosmological entropy conservation](../../../../../../../cosmological-entropy-conservation.md), $g_{*s}T^3a^3=\mathrm{constant}$, and is heated relative to a decoupled species as massive particles annihilate. The [effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom](../../../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-energy-degrees-of-freedom.md) $g_*$ and [effective number of relativistic entropy degrees of freedom](../../../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-degrees-of-freedom.md) $g_{*s}$ coincide while all species share a common relativistic temperature. Use $g_d:=g_{*s}(T_{\rm dec})\simeq100$ for the [Standard Model](../../../../../../../standard-model-split.md) bath, excluding the already decoupled extra species.

Just before ordinary [neutrino decoupling](../../../../../../../neutrino-decoupling.md), the coupled bath contains two [photon](../../../../../../../photon.md) states, four [Electron](../../../../../../../electron.md)/[Positron](../../../../../../../positron.md) states and six ordinary [neutrino](../../../../../../../neutrino.md)/[antineutrino](../../../../../../../antineutrino.md) states. Therefore

$$
g_{*s}=2+\frac78(4+6)=\frac{43}{4}.
$$

The extra-neutrino temperature relative to that bath is then $(43/(4g_d))^{1/3}$. After the ordinary [neutrinos](../../../../../../../neutrino.md) decouple, [Electron](../../../../../../../electron.md)/[Positron](../../../../../../../positron.md) annihilation heats only the [photon](../../../../../../../photon.md) bath: its entropy count changes from $2+(7/8)4=11/2$ to $2$. Both [neutrino](../../../../../../../neutrino.md) populations miss that last heating, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_{\nu,0}}{T_0}=\left(\frac{43}{4g_d}\right)^{1/3}\left(\frac4{11}\right)^{1/3}=\left(\frac{43}{11g_d}\right)^{1/3}\simeq0.34.}
$$

This is the [two-stage entropy dilution of an early-decoupled neutrino](../../../../../../../two-stage-entropy-dilution-of-an-early-decoupled-neutrino.md). Numerically **$T_{\nu,0}\simeq6.8\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm{GeV}$**. Relative to ordinary relic [neutrinos](../../../../../../../neutrino.md) it is cooler by $(43/(4g_d))^{1/3}\simeq0.48$.

Let $g_\nu$ count the extra [neutrino](../../../../../../../neutrino.md)'s populated helicity and antiparticle states. For one conventional [neutrino](../../../../../../../neutrino.md) plus [antineutrino](../../../../../../../antineutrino.md) species $g_\nu=2$, the same state count as [photons](../../../../../../../photon.md). The [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) supplies the $3/4$ number-density factor, so

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_{\nu,0}}{n_{\gamma,0}}=\frac34\frac{g_\nu}{2}\left(\frac{T_{\nu,0}}{T_0}\right)^3=\frac{3g_\nu}{8}\frac{43}{11g_d}\simeq0.029\frac{g_\nu}{2}.}
$$

Thus a two-state species has about **$2.9\%$** of the [photon](../../../../../../../photon.md) number density. Stating $g_\nu$ explicitly also covers a different choice of populated states.
