<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose $S$ and $R$ are [model companions](../../../../../../model-companion.md) of $T$. Their [universal consequences of a theory](../../../../../../universal-consequences-of-a-theory.md) agree, so the [diagram embedding criterion for universal theories](../../../../../../diagram-embedding-criterion-for-universal-theories.md) permits embeddings in both directions between their model classes.

Starting from any $M_0\models S$, alternately take such extensions, identifying each model with its image under the embedding:

$$
M_0\subseteq N_0\subseteq M_1\subseteq N_1\subseteq M_2\subseteq\cdots,
\qquad M_i\models S,\quad N_i\models R.
$$

Since $S$ is [model-complete](../../../../../../model-complete-theory.md), $M_i\preccurlyeq M_{i+1}$; since $R$ is [model-complete](../../../../../../model-complete-theory.md), $N_i\preccurlyeq N_{i+1}$. The two subsequences have the same union $U$. The [elementary chain theorem](../../../../../../elementary-chain-theorem.md) gives

$$
U\models S\cup R,\qquad M_0\preccurlyeq U.
$$

Every axiom of $R$, as a sentence true in $U$, is therefore true in $M_0$. Thus every model of $S$ is a model of $R$. Reversing their roles proves the converse. **The [model companion](../../../../../../model-companion.md) is unique up to logical equivalence.** If $T$ is inconsistent, its only possible companion is likewise inconsistent, so the same uniqueness conclusion holds.
