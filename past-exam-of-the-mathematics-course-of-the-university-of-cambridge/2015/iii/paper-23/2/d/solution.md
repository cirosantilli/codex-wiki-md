<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**Both assertions are false.** For [simple closure](../../../../../../simple-closure.md), take the inclusion

$$
\mathcal M=(\{0\},+,-,0)\subseteq\mathcal N=(\mathbb Q,+,-,0).
$$

Both satisfy [DAG prime](../../../../../../theory-of-torsion-free-divisible-abelian-groups-allowing-the-trivial-group.md), but the [quantifier-free formula](../../../../../../quantifier-free-formula.md) $x\ne0$ has a witness in $N$ and none in $M$. The same example works in the reduced language $\{+,0\}$.

For [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md), the [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) $\exists x(x\ne0)$ distinguishes these two models. Every closed group term is zero, so every atomic closed equality is true in both models. Every [quantifier-free sentence](../../../../../../quantifier-free-sentence.md), being a Boolean combination of such equalities, has the same truth value in both. The distinguishing sentence therefore has no equivalent [quantifier-free sentence](../../../../../../quantifier-free-sentence.md) modulo [DAG prime](../../../../../../theory-of-torsion-free-divisible-abelian-groups-allowing-the-trivial-group.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{DAG}'\text{ does not have quantifier elimination}.}
$$

Including the trivial group is exactly what makes the proposed [simple closure](../../../../../../simple-closure.md) condition fail.
