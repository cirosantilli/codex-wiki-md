<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the same two-index [filter on a set](../../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) $F=\{\{0,1\}\}$, with a unary predicate $P$ true only in the first one-element factor. In the [reduced product](../../../../../../reduced-product.md), $P(a)$ is false, so $\neg P(a)$ is true. But

$$
\{i:\mathcal M_i\models\neg P(a_i)\}=\{1\}\notin F.
$$

**[Logical negation](../../../../../../negation.md) also breaks the equivalence.** For a general proper [filter on a set](../../../../../../filter-set-theory.md), a set and its complement can both be absent. In an [ultrafilter](../../../../../../ultrafilter.md), exactly one belongs, which is the step used to transfer [logical negation](../../../../../../negation.md) in the [Łoś theorem](../../../../../../los-theorem.md).
