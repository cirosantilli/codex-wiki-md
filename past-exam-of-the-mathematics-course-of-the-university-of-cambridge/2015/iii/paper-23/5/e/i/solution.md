<h1 id="5/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [first-order language](../../../../../../../first-order-language.md) with one constant $c$, take $\mathcal M=(\mathbb N,c=0)$ with equality only, and let $A=\varnothing$. Set

$$
P=\operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal M}(0/\varnothing).
$$

This [complete type](../../../../../../../complete-type.md) contains $x=c$ and is isolated by that formula. In any [first-order structure](../../../../../../../first-order-structure.md), at most one element can satisfy $x=c$. In every $\mathcal N\equiv\mathcal M$, its interpretation of $c$ realizes $P$, by [elementary equivalence](../../../../../../../elementary-equivalence.md). Hence **no elementarily equivalent model has infinitely many realizations of $P$**. An [algebraic type](../../../../../../../algebraic-type.md) can have a fixed finite number of realizations in every model.
