<h1 id="5/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A counterexample can even use a nonalgebraic [isolated type](../../../../../../../isolated-type.md). Let $L=\{<\}$, $\mathcal M=(\mathbb Q,<)$, $A=\varnothing$, and $P=\operatorname{tp}^{\mathcal M}(0/\varnothing)$. The [quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../../../quantifier-elimination-for-dense-linear-orders-without-endpoints.md) shows that all elements have the same [complete type](../../../../../../../complete-type.md) over the empty set: a one-variable [quantifier-free formula](../../../../../../../quantifier-free-formula.md) has no order information except $x=x$ and $x<x$.

Thus $x=x$ isolates $P$. Every $\mathcal N\equiv\mathcal M$ is a [dense linear order without endpoints](../../../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md), and every element of $N$ realizes $P$. Such a model cannot omit it. **Even a type with infinitely many realizations need not be omittable; isolation is the relevant obstruction.**
