<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

On the unit ball take the [uniformly elliptic operator](../../../../../../uniformly-elliptic-operator.md) $L=\Delta-2n$ and the smooth function $u(x)=-1-|x|^2$. The zeroth-order coefficient is negative and

$$
Lu=-2n+2n(1+|x|^2)=2n|x|^2\geq0.
$$

Nevertheless

$$
\boxed{\sup_{B_1}u=-1>-2=\sup_{\partial B_1}u.}
$$

This is consistent with the [weak maximum principle](../../../../../../weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators.md), whose upper bound here is $\sup_{\partial B_1}u^+=0$. At a negative interior maximum the term $cu$ can be positive; this is why a negative boundary maximum cannot replace its [positive part](../../../../../../positive-part-of-a-real-valued-function.md). If $c=0$, subtracting the boundary maximum preserves the differential inequality and gives the stronger bound. That subtraction is unavailable for general $c\leq0$.
