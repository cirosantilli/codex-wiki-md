<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The missing hypothesis is **[uniform ellipticity](../../../../../../uniformly-elliptic-operator.md) up to the boundary**. The principal coefficient matrix and its [quadratic form](../../../../../../quadratic-form.md) are

$$
a(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&y^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad a^{ij}\xi_i\xi_j=\xi_1^2+y^2\xi_2^2.
$$

Although this is [positive-definite](../../../../../../positive-definite-bilinear-form.md) at every interior point, no single positive [uniform ellipticity](../../../../../../uniformly-elliptic-operator.md) constant works on $R$: taking $\xi=(0,1)$ requires $y^2\geq\lambda$ for arbitrarily small positive $y$. The normal second-derivative coefficient vanishes on the lower side. This [degenerate ellipticity](../../../../../../degenerate-elliptic-operator.md) allows the equation and boundedness to force a boundary trace, instead of allowing arbitrary continuous [Dirichlet data](../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-data.md). The coefficient matrix is [positive-definite](../../../../../../positive-definite-bilinear-form.md) at every interior point, but lacks the uniform lower bound used by the usual bounded-domain existence theorem.
