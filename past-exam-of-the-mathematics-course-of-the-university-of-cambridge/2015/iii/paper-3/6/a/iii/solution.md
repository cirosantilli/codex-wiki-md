<h1 id="6/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

An indecomposable of dimension vector $\mathbf n$ is a [brick module](../../../../../../../brick-module.md) and has $q_Q(\mathbf n)=1$ by the previous parts. Its [automorphism group of a quiver representation](../../../../../../../automorphism-group-of-a-quiver-representation.md) has dimension one. Therefore

$$
\dim\mathcal O_X=\sum_i n_i^2-1=\sum_{\rho:i\to j}n_in_j=\dim\operatorname{Rep}_Q(\mathbf n).
$$

It follows that its orbit is open and dense in the irreducible [quiver representation space](../../../../../../../quiver-representation-space.md). Two different indecomposables of the same dimension vector would give two disjoint nonempty open orbits. Nonempty open subsets of an irreducible space must intersect, so this is impossible. Hence **an indecomposable is uniquely determined by its dimension vector, up to isomorphism**. This is the [positive definite Tits form indecomposable classification](../../../../../../../positive-definite-tits-form-indecomposable-classification.md).
