<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A representation of the [one-arrow quiver](../../../../../../kronecker-quiver-with-one-arrow.md) is an $n_2\times n_1$ matrix $F$. If $r=\operatorname{rank}F$, changes of basis put it in the form with an $r\times r$ identity block and all other entries zero. Since multiplication by invertible matrices preserves [matrix rank](../../../../../../matrix-rank.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_X=\{M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n_2\times n_1}(k):\operatorname{rank}M=r\}.}
$$

This describes the orbit for every $0\leq r\leq\min(n_1,n_2)$, including the zero orbit. Its dimension is

$$
\boxed{\dim\mathcal O_X=r(n_1+n_2-r).}
$$

Indeed, choosing its $r$-dimensional image contributes $r(n_2-r)$ parameters in a [Grassmannian](../../../../../../grassmannian.md), and choosing a surjective map from $k^{n_1}$ onto that image contributes $rn_1$. This is a [rank orbit of a matrix under left-right multiplication](../../../../../../rank-orbit-of-a-matrix-under-left-right-multiplication.md).
