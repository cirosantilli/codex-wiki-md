<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $\gamma_X(s)$ be the affinely parametrized [geodesic](../../../../../../../geodesic.md) of the [affine connection](../../../../../../../affine-connection.md) with $\gamma_X(0)=p$ and $\dot\gamma_X(0)=X$. For fixed $t$, the curve $\eta(u)=\gamma_X(tu)$ has initial velocity $tX$. The [geodesic](../../../../../../../geodesic.md) equation is homogeneous of degree two in the velocity, so its left-hand side for $\eta$ is $t^2$ times the left-hand side for $\gamma_X$, and is zero. Uniqueness of the [geodesic](../../../../../../../geodesic.md) [initial value problem](../../../../../../../initial-value-problem.md) gives $\eta=\gamma_{tX}$.

By the definition of the [affine exponential map](../../../../../../../affine-exponential-map.md), **rescaling the initial velocity rescales affine parameter**:

$$
\boxed{\exp_p(tX)=\gamma_{tX}(1)=\gamma_X(t).}
$$

At $t=0$ both sides equal $p$. The statement is made where the geodesics exist; without [geodesic completeness](../../../../../../../geodesic-completeness.md), the [affine exponential map](../../../../../../../affine-exponential-map.md) is defined only on a suitable neighbourhood of zero in $T_pM$, not necessarily all of $T_pM$. An affine parameter distance need not be a metric length.
