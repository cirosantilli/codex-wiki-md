<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [normalized Killing one-form](../../../../../../../normalized-killing-one-form.md) $\eta=F^{-1}V_\mu dx^\mu$ is a [closed differential form](../../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md). The [Poincaré lemma](../../../../../../../poincare-lemma.md) gives a local scalar potential $\phi$ with $\eta=d\phi$, hence $V_\mu=F\partial_\mu\phi$. Choose the adapted chart of part (i), so $V=\partial_0$. Then

$$
\partial_0\phi=\eta(V)=1,\qquad
\phi=x^0+f(x^1,x^2,x^3),\qquad
\partial_i f=\frac{g_{0i}}{g_{00}}.
$$

The [closed differential form](../../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) condition supplies the integrability of these spatial derivatives. For a fixed $g$ and $V$, $f$ is determined locally up to an additive constant; its derivatives cannot be chosen freely despite the wording in the paper.

Use $\bar x^0=\phi$, $\bar x^i=x^i$. The [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) is one, so this is a local coordinate change. Holding $\bar x^i$ fixed gives $V=\partial_{\bar0}$, while its metric-dual [one-form](../../../../../../../one-form.md) is $v=F\,d\bar x^0$. Thus $\bar g_{00}=F<0$ and $\bar g_{0i}=0$. The [Killing equation](../../../../../../../killing-equation.md) still holds, so $\partial_{\bar0}\bar g_{\mu\nu}=0$. **The metric is locally static**:

$$
\boxed{\bar g_{0i}=0,\qquad\partial_{\bar0}\bar g_{\mu\nu}=0.}
$$

A [closed differential form](../../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) need not be globally exact; neither a global potential nor a global static chart follows without additional topological assumptions.
