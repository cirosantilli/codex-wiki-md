<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write $F=V_\rho V^\rho<0$ and $A_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu V_\nu$. The [Killing equation](../../../../../../../killing-equation.md) makes $A_{\mu\nu}$ antisymmetric. Expanding [hypersurface orthogonality](../../../../../../../hypersurface-orthogonality.md) and contracting with $V^\alpha$ gives

$$
V_\alpha A_{\mu\nu}+V_\mu A_{\nu\alpha}+V_\nu A_{\alpha\mu}=0,
\qquad
F A_{\mu\nu}+\frac12V_\mu\partial_\nu F-\frac12V_\nu\partial_\mu F=0.
$$

Here [metric compatibility](../../../../../../../metric-compatibility.md) gives $\partial_\mu F=2V^\rho A_{\mu\rho}$. Therefore

$$
2F A_{\mu\nu}=V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F,
$$

and the [normalized Killing one-form](../../../../../../../normalized-killing-one-form.md) satisfies

$$
\nabla_\mu(F^{-1}V_\nu)-\nabla_\nu(F^{-1}V_\mu)
=\frac{2A_{\mu\nu}}F-\frac{V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F}{F^2}=0.
$$

Thus **the required exponent is** $\boxed{n=-2}$. The notation $|V|^2$ in this paper denotes the negative scalar $F$, rather than a positive squared norm; the answer uses only its real reciprocal. More generally, for a multiplier $h(F)$ the antisymmetric derivative is $(h/F+h')(V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F)$, so $h=C/F$ is the universally valid choice. If $F$ is constant, other exponents can work in that particular metric.
