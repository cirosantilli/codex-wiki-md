<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For each fixed coordinate function $x^\alpha$, the expression $\Box x^\alpha$ is a scalar: $\alpha$ labels four chosen functions and is not a free [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) index. The equation is therefore coordinate invariant when those functions are held fixed. However, the demand that the currently chosen coordinates themselves be [harmonic coordinates](../../../../../../harmonic-coordinate.md) is a chart condition, not a four-vector equation invariant under arbitrary changes of coordinate functions.

Indeed, for new functions $\bar x^\alpha=f^\alpha(x)$ the [d'Alembert operator](../../../../../../d-alembert-operator.md) gives

$$
\Box\bar x^\alpha=f^\alpha{}_{,\beta}\Box x^\beta+g^{\beta\gamma}f^\alpha{}_{,\beta\gamma}.
$$

The second term prevents a vector transformation law. An affine redefinition preserves [harmonic coordinates](../../../../../../harmonic-coordinate.md); an arbitrary nonlinear one does not. For example, a Cartesian spatial coordinate $x$ in [Minkowski spacetime](../../../../../../minkowski-spacetime.md) has $\Box x=0$, whereas $\Box(x^2)=2$, and $x^2$ is a valid new coordinate on $x>0$. **The equations are scalar equations for specified functions, while harmonic gauge is chart dependent.**
