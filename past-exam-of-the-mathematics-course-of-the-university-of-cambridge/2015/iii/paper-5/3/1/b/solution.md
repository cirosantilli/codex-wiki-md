<h1 id="3/1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Reflect $u$ across the two endpoints of $I$ into a slightly larger interval, giving an $H^1$ extension, and multiply by a smooth [cutoff function](../../../../../../../cutoff-function.md) equal to one near $\overline I$. Convolve the resulting function on $\mathbb R$ with a [mollifier](../../../../../../../mollifier.md). The convolutions $u_\varepsilon$ are smooth; differentiation commutes with convolution of the [weak derivative](../../../../../../../weak-derivative.md). Continuity of translations in $L^2$ proves both $u_\varepsilon\to u$ and $u_\varepsilon'\to u'$ in $L^2(I)$, hence convergence in the [Sobolev norm](../../../../../../../sobolev-norm.md).

To obtain convergence [almost everywhere](../../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), select a sequence with $\|u_n-u\|_2\le2^{-n}$. Then $\int\sum_n|u_n-u|^2<\infty$, so the summands tend to zero almost everywhere. Thus **a smooth approximating sequence converges both in $H^1$ and almost everywhere**. This is [density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space](../../../../../../../density-of-smooth-functions-in-a-sobolev-space.md); smooth functions need not vanish at the endpoints.
