<h1 id="2/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Throughout this question the [Banach spaces](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md) and [Hilbert spaces](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) are complex. Separability is an additional standing hypothesis, not part of the general definition of a [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md). Here and below [unit ball](../../../../../../unit-ball.md) means the closed unit ball. An open unit ball in positive finite dimension is not compact. In finite dimension, equivalence of [norms](../../../../../../norm.md) identifies the closed unit ball with a closed bounded subset of Euclidean space, so the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md) gives compactness in the [norm topology](../../../../../../norm-topology.md).

In an infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), the [Gram-Schmidt process](../../../../../../gram-schmidt-process.md) constructs an infinite [orthonormal sequence](../../../../../../orthonormal-sequence.md) $(e_n)$. Put $f_n=e_n/\sqrt2$. Then $\|f_n\|=1/\sqrt2\le1$ and, for $m\ne n$, $\|f_n-f_m\|^2=(1+1)/2=1$. No subsequence is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), contradicting [sequential compactness](../../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md). Thus **the closed unit ball is norm compact exactly in finite dimension**.
