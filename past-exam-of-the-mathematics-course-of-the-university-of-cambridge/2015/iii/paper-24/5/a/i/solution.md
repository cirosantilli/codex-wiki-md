<h1 id="5/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [countable-condition collapse](../../../../../../../countable-condition-collapse.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P=\operatorname{Col}(\omega_1,\kappa)
=\{p:p\text{ is a countable partial function from }\omega_1\text{ to }\kappa\},}
$$

ordered by reverse inclusion: an extension of a function is a stronger condition. All sizes and conditions here are computed in the ground model $M$.

This forcing is [countably closed](../../../../../../../countably-closed-forcing.md): the union of a descending countable sequence is a countable partial function. Thus it adds no countable ordinal sequences and preserves $\omega_1$. For each $\xi<\omega_1$, the conditions whose domains contain $\xi$ are dense; for each $\eta<\kappa$, those whose ranges contain $\eta$ are dense. The union of the [generic filter](../../../../../../../generic-filter.md) is therefore a surjection from $\omega_1$ onto $\kappa$, and $|\kappa|^{M[G]}=\aleph_1$.

Inaccessibility gives $\kappa^{\aleph_0}=\kappa$: every countable sequence is bounded below the regular $\kappa$, and the [strong limit cardinal](../../../../../../../strong-limit-cardinal.md) property bounds the number of sequences at each bound below $\kappa$. Hence $|\mathbb P|=\kappa$. The forcing satisfies the $\kappa^+$-[chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../../chain-condition-for-forcing.md), so it preserves every cardinal above $\kappa$. **This gives precisely the requested collapse and preservation.** The assertion that $\kappa$ is collapsed to $\aleph_1$ concerns its new cardinality; the ordinal $\kappa$ itself does not change.
