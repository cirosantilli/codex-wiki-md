<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose $\aleph_2$ distinct [cofinal branches](../../../../../../../cofinal-branch.md) of the [Kurepa tree](../../../../../../../kurepa-tree.md) and regard them as subsets of its underlying set $\omega_1$. Let $\mathcal F$ be this family. Fix an ordinal $\alpha<\omega_1$. The countably many node codes below $\alpha$ have heights bounded by some $\delta<\omega_1$. A [cofinal branch](../../../../../../../cofinal-branch.md) meets level $\delta$, and its node there determines all its predecessors, hence all its nodes with codes below $\alpha$. Since level $\delta$ is countable,

$$
\boxed{|\mathcal F|=\aleph_2,\qquad
|\{X\cap\alpha:X\in\mathcal F\}|\leq\aleph_0\quad(\alpha<\omega_1).}
$$

This is the [Kurepa-family hypothesis](../../../../../../../kurepa-family-hypothesis.md). The given compatibility between the ordinal codes and the tree order ensures in particular that the branches are consistently viewed as subsets of $\omega_1$; boundedness of the heights of the countably many codes is what the argument uses.
