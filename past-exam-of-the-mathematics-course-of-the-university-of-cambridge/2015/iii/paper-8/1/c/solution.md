<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For an autonomous [Hamiltonian function](../../../../../../hamiltonian-function.md), the [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) and [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) give

$$
\frac d{dt}H(X(t),V(t))
=\nabla_xH\cdot\nabla_vH-\nabla_vH\cdot\nabla_xH=0.
$$

Thus **the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) is constant along each characteristic**:

$$
\boxed{H(X(t),V(t))=H(x,v).}
$$

The time-independent hypothesis is implicit in the displayed expression in this subpart. For the time-dependent [Hamiltonian function](../../../../../../hamiltonian-function.md) allowed earlier, the correct [Hamiltonian energy balance](../../../../../../hamiltonian-energy-balance.md) is instead

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}H(t,X(t),V(t))=\partial_tH(t,X(t),V(t)).}
$$

For example $H(t,x,v)=t+|v|^2/2$ has a unique global [Hamiltonian flow](../../../../../../hamiltonian-flow.md), but its value along a curve increases at unit rate. Thus conservation cannot be claimed for arbitrary time-dependent $H$.
