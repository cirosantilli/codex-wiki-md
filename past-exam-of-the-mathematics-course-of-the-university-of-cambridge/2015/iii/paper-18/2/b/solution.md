<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [differential form of type (p, q)](../../../../../../differential-form-of-type-p-q.md), $d\alpha=0$ is equivalent to both $\partial\alpha=0$ and $\bar\partial\alpha=0$, because the two resulting types are distinct. Moreover $\partial\bar\partial\beta$ has type $(p,q)$ when $\beta$ has type $(p-1,q-1)$, and

$$
d(\partial\bar\partial\beta)=0
$$

by the square-zero and anticommutation identities. Thus the denominator defining [Bott-Chern cohomology](../../../../../../bott-chern-cohomology.md) is a [vector subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of its numerator, making the quotient well defined.

[Complex conjugation of differential-form type](../../../../../../complex-conjugation-of-differential-form-type.md) sends a $d$-closed $(p,q)$-form to a $d$-closed $(q,p)$-form. For a representative in the denominator,

$$
\overline{\partial\bar\partial\beta}
=\bar\partial\partial\bar\beta=-\partial\bar\partial\bar\beta.
$$

The minus sign does not change the denominator [vector subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md). Conjugation consequently induces a conjugate-linear bijection $[\alpha]\mapsto[\bar\alpha]$ between the two [Bott-Chern cohomology](../../../../../../bott-chern-cohomology.md) spaces; applying it twice is the identity. Equivalently, **$\boxed{H^{p,q}_{BC}(X)\cong\overline{H^{q,p}_{BC}(X)}}$** as complex [vector spaces](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md).
