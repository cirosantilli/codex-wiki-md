<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A $d$-closed [differential form of type (p, q)](../../../../../../differential-form-of-type-p-q.md) is $\bar\partial$-closed, and a [Bott-Chern cohomology](../../../../../../bott-chern-cohomology.md) coboundary is a [Dolbeault cohomology](../../../../../../dolbeault-cohomology.md) coboundary because

$$
\partial\bar\partial\beta=-\bar\partial(\partial\beta).
$$

Hence taking the same representative defines a [complex-linear map](../../../../../../complex-linear-map.md) $\phi:H^{p,q}_{BC}(X)\to H^{p,q}_{\bar\partial}(X)$.

If $X$ is compact and Kähler, the [Dolbeault Hodge decomposition](../../../../../../dolbeault-hodge-decomposition-on-a-compact-hermitian-manifold.md) supplies a $\bar\partial$-harmonic representative $\alpha$ of every [Dolbeault cohomology](../../../../../../dolbeault-cohomology.md) class. The scalar [Kähler Laplacian identity](../../../../../../kahler-laplacian-identity.md) is $\Delta_d=2\Delta_{\bar\partial}$, so $\alpha$ is also $d$-harmonic. Consequently

$$
0=\langle\Delta_d\alpha,\alpha\rangle
=\|d\alpha\|^2+\|d^*\alpha\|^2,
$$

and $d\alpha=0$. It therefore defines a [Bott-Chern cohomology](../../../../../../bott-chern-cohomology.md) class mapping to the original class. Thus **$\boxed{\phi\text{ is surjective on a compact Kähler manifold}.}$**

For clarity, the scalar [Kähler Laplacian identity](../../../../../../kahler-laplacian-identity.md) follows from the [Kähler identities](../../../../../../kahler-identities.md): they make the mixed anticommutators $\{\partial,\bar\partial^*\}$ and $\{\bar\partial,\partial^*\}$ zero, while the curvature-zero instance of the identity proved in 4(b) gives $\Delta'=\Delta''$. Expanding $\Delta_d$ yields $\Delta'+\Delta''=2\Delta''$.
