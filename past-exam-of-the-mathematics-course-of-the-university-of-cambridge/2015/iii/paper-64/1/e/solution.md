<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For a slowly rotating central star, the [accretion-disk boundary layer](../../../../../../accretion-disk-boundary-layer.md) radiates a [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) $L_{\rm BL}\sim GM_*\dot M/(2r_*)$. A layer of thickness comparable to the [disk scale height](../../../../../../disk-scale-height.md) has emitting area $A_{\rm BL}\sim r_*H$ times a geometric constant. Applying the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) to this [blackbody](../../../../../../blackbody.md) area gives

$$
\sigma T_{\rm BL}^4\sim\frac{GM_*\dot M}{r_*^2H}.
$$

Comparing with $\sigma T_{\rm in}^4=3GM_*\dot M/(8\pi r_*^3)$ gives **the boundary-layer [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) scaling**

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm BL}\sim\left(\frac{r_*}{H}\right)^{1/4}T_{\rm in}.}
$$

Only the scaling is fixed: for a two-faced annulus of area $4\pi r_*H$, for example, $T_{\rm BL}^4/T_{\rm in}^4=r_*/(3H)$. A surface belt gives a different order-one coefficient.

For a [thin disk](../../../../../../thin-disk.md), $H/r_*\ll1$, so a comparable [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) emerges from a smaller emitting area at a higher [effective temperature](../../../../../../effective-temperature.md). **The boundary-layer emission is harder than the disk emission**, with its [Planck law](../../../../../../planck-s-law.md) peak shifted to higher [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md). It is also closer to a single-[temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) component than the broad [multitemperature blackbody disk](../../../../../../multitemperature-blackbody-disk.md) spectrum, under the adopted uniform-[temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) approximation. Rapid stellar rotation weakens the heating and this conclusion's [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) contrast.
