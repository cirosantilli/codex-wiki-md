<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Integrating the [standard thin-disk dissipation flux](../../../../../../standard-thin-disk-dissipation-flux.md) over annular area, with both faces already included in $\mathcal H$, gives

$$
L_D=\frac{3GM_*\dot M}{2}\int_{r_*}^{r_{\rm out}}\left(r^{-2}-r_*^{1/2}r^{-5/2}\right)dr
=\frac{GM_*\dot M}{2r_*}\left[1-3\frac{r_*}{r_{\rm out}}+2\left(\frac{r_*}{r_{\rm out}}\right)^{3/2}\right].
$$

Thus **the disk [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md)** for $r_{\rm out}\gg r_*$ is

$$
\boxed{L_D\simeq\frac{GM_*\dot M}{2r_*}.}
$$

The [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) decreases by approximately $GM_*/r_*$ per unit mass from a distant outer edge to the surface, giving a potential-energy release rate $GM_*\dot M/r_*$. The [standard thin-disk luminosity](../../../../../../standard-thin-disk-luminosity.md) is half of this.

The missing half remains as [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) of nearly circular orbital motion: at the inner edge $v_K^2=GM_*/r_*$, so the specific orbital [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is $GM_*/(2r_*)$. Equivalently, circular motion has total specific [mechanical energy](../../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) $-GM_*/(2r_*)$. Matter joining a slowly rotating star must shed this orbital motion in an [accretion-disk boundary layer](../../../../../../accretion-disk-boundary-layer.md), producing approximately another $GM_*\dot M/(2r_*)$ of [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md). A rotating star can retain some energy in spin, so equal disk and [accretion-disk boundary layer](../../../../../../accretion-disk-boundary-layer.md) luminosities assume slow stellar rotation. For a central [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md), there is no material surface, and energy can instead be carried inward.
