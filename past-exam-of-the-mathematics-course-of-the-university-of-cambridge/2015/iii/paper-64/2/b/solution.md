<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a local axisymmetric [Fourier mode](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md), the [Toomre stability criterion](../../../../../../toomre-s-stability-criterion.md) balances three contributions to the squared oscillation [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md):

$$
\omega^2=\kappa^2+c_s^2k^2-2\pi G\Sigma_0|k|.
$$

The [radial epicyclic frequency](../../../../../../radial-epicyclic-frequency.md) supplies rotational restoration at long wavelengths; [isothermal sound speed](../../../../../../isothermal-sound-speed.md) and [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) stabilize short wavelengths; disk [self-gravity](../../../../../../self-gravity.md) destabilizes intermediate wavelengths. Minimizing over $|k|$ gives $|k|=\pi G\Sigma_0/c_s^2$ and $\omega_{\min}^2=\kappa^2(1-Q^{-2})$. **Axisymmetric gravitational instability occurs when $Q<1$.**

In a centrally dominated [Keplerian disk](../../../../../../keplerian-disk.md), $\kappa=\Omega$ and vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) gives $c_s\simeq H\Omega$. Using $\Omega^2=GM_*/r^3$ and a local disk-mass estimate $M_D\sim\pi r^2\Sigma_0$,

$$
\boxed{Q\sim\frac Hr\frac{M_*}{M_D},\qquad\text{instability when }\frac{M_D}{M_*}\gtrsim\frac Hr.}
$$

This is the [disk mass form of the Toomre criterion](../../../../../../disk-mass-form-of-the-toomre-criterion.md). An actual enclosed mass depends on the radial [surface density](../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) profile and changes an order-one coefficient. In particular, a profile proportional to $r^{-3/2}$ has $M_D(<r)\simeq4\pi r^2\Sigma_0$ when its inner cutoff is negligible.

For the protosolar estimate, take $M_*\simeq M_\odot\simeq2\times10^{33}\,\mathrm g$. This [solar mass](../../../../../../solar-mass.md) is implicit in identifying the central star with the young Sun. The supplied constant [disk aspect ratio](../../../../../../disk-aspect-ratio.md) gives

$$
Q(r)=\frac{0.1M_\odot}{\pi\Sigma_0(r)r^2}\simeq\frac{2\times10^{32}}{\pi\times10^3\times10^{26}}\left(\frac r{\mathrm{AU}}\right)^{-1/2}
\simeq6.4\times10^2\left(\frac r{\mathrm{AU}}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

Using the printed approximate [astronomical unit](../../../../../../astronomical-unit.md) and [gravitational constant](../../../../../../gravitational-constant.md), one can also obtain $\Omega(1\,\mathrm{AU})\simeq3.7\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $c_s\simeq3.7\times10^5\,\mathrm{cm\,s}^{-1}$; they give the same $Q$. The [gravitational constant](../../../../../../gravitational-constant.md) cancels from the mass form.

**At one astronomical unit the disk is very stable**, with $Q\simeq640$. Formal extrapolation gives

$$
\boxed{r_{Q=1}\simeq(6.4\times10^2)^2\,\mathrm{AU}\simeq4\times10^5\,\mathrm{AU}.}
$$

This is far beyond the planetary region and any plausible extent of the [minimum-mass solar nebula](../../../../../../minimum-mass-solar-nebula.md). Moreover, the extrapolated enclosed disk mass is already a substantial fraction of a [solar mass](../../../../../../solar-mass.md), so the centrally dominated approximation becomes questionable. The formal [radius](../../../../../../radius.md) is not a prediction of a real unstable outer nebula. **Direct gas fragmentation by gravitational instability is unlikely to have formed Solar System planets in this model.** [Core accretion](../../../../../../core-accretion.md) is the more natural route; an earlier substantially more massive or colder disk would be a different model. Even $Q<1$ alone does not guarantee fragmentation, because sufficiently rapid cooling is also needed.
