[Uniqueness in law](../../../../../../uniqueness-in-law.md) means that any two [weak stochastic solutions](../../../../../../weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) with the same prescribed initial distribution have the same distribution as random elements of the continuous-path space. Their probability spaces and driving [Brownian motions](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) may differ.

[Pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md) means that if two solutions are defined on the same filtered probability space, driven by the same [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md), and have the same initial variable almost surely, then

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(X_t=\widetilde X_t\text{ for every }t\geq0)=1.}
$$

This is [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../../../../indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes.md), a statement about coupled sample paths. It is stronger than requiring only equality of their distributions.
