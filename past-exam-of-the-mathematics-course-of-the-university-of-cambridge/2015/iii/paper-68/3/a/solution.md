<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the [quadratic variational principle for a symmetric positive operator](../../../../../../quadratic-variational-principle-for-a-symmetric-positive-operator.md) below, use a [symmetric operator](../../../../../../symmetric-operator.md) $L$ on the real [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) $H$. Strict positive definiteness means

$$
\langle Lv,v\rangle>0\qquad(v\ne0).
$$

A [uniformly positive definite symmetric operator](../../../../../../uniformly-positive-definite-symmetric-operator.md) satisfies the stronger [coercive operator](../../../../../../coercive-operator.md) condition

$$
\boxed{\langle Lv,v\rangle\geq\gamma\|v\|^2\quad\text{for some }\gamma>0.}
$$

In this variational setting, “positive definite” is often used for a symmetric operator with this uniform bound. We will state explicitly where the [coercive operator](../../../../../../coercive-operator.md) bound is needed. For a [bounded linear operator](../../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) defined on all of $H$, symmetry means that the operator is [self-adjoint](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md). For an unbounded operator, positivity is imposed on its [operator domain](../../../../../../operator-domain.md), and the variational formulation is made on its form domain.

Symmetry is essential in a real [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md): positivity of the quadratic expression alone does not imply symmetry. For example, $L=I+K$ with nonzero real [skew-symmetric matrix](../../../../../../skew-symmetric-matrix.md) $K$ satisfies $\langle Lv,v\rangle=\|v\|^2$, but its quadratic functional has derivative involving $I$, not $I+K$. A [positive definite symmetric operator](../../../../../../positive-definite-symmetric-operator.md) supplies both the positivity and symmetry needed in part (b).
