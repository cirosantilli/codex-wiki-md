<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

First consider an [M-M-K queue](../../../../../m-m-s-queue.md) with $K$ servers, arrival rate $\lambda$, and service rate $\beta$ at each server. Assume $\lambda<K\beta$ so that a [stationary distribution](../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) exists. Its population is a [birth-death process](../../../../../birth-death-process.md) with rates $q_{n,n+1}=\lambda$ and $q_{n,n-1}=\beta\min(n,K)$. The equilibrium probabilities satisfy [detailed balance](../../../../../detailed-balance.md)

$$
\pi_n\lambda=\pi_{n+1}\beta\min(n+1,K).
$$

Thus the population process is a [reversible Markov chain](../../../../../reversible-markov-chain.md). In its [time reversal of a continuous-time Markov chain](../../../../../time-reversal-of-a-continuous-time-markov-chain.md), forward departures become upward jumps whose rate is the constant $\lambda$, [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the state. They can be generated by a rate-$\lambda$ [Poisson process](../../../../../poisson-process.md) [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the initial reversed state. Reversing time preserves the [Poisson process](../../../../../poisson-process.md) law, proving **the equilibrium departure process is Poisson with rate $\lambda$**, the [Burke theorem for a multi-server queue](../../../../../burke-theorem-for-a-multi-server-queue.md).

The same argument gives the departure component of [quasireversibility](../../../../../quasireversibility.md) needed for the tandem calculation: **the present upstream population is [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of its entire past departure process**. Indeed, these departures become future arrivals in the reversed chain, [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of its initial population. First check this on finite time windows, then extend to the full past. A Poisson output alone, without this [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), would not justify multiplying queue-empty probabilities.

Now let $a=\nu\mu$, where $\mu$ denotes the mean service time at passport control, so the service rate at each of its two servers is $1/\mu$. The [stationary distribution](../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) of the [M-M-s queue](../../../../../m-m-s-queue.md) with $s=2$ has normalizing sum

$$
\pi_0^{-1}=1+a+\frac{a^2}{2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty(a/2)^k
=\frac{2+a}{2-a}.
$$

Hence its empty probability is $(2-a)/(2+a)$. Its output feeds a stable [M/D/1 queue](../../../../../m-d-1-queue.md) with rate $\nu$ and deterministic service time $\tau$. In a stationary work-conserving single-server queue, the busy fraction is the rate of completed work, $\nu\tau$, so the empty probability is $1-\nu\tau$.

The downstream stationary workload is a causal function of past upstream departures. For example, it is the limit of workloads constructed from arrivals in increasingly long finite past intervals; stability makes this stationary construction well defined. The backward [independence](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) just proved therefore makes the two present queue populations [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\text{both queues empty})=\frac{2-\nu\mu}{2+\nu\mu}(1-\nu\tau).}
$$

With walking time $\sigma$, the downstream arrival stream is the upstream departure stream shifted by $\sigma$. Its present workload depends only on departures up to time $-\sigma$. The number walking at time zero is the number of departures in $(-\sigma,0]$, which has [Poisson distribution](../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) of mean $\nu\sigma$ and is [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of the earlier departures. The upstream present population is [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of both parts of its past departure stream. Thus the three empty events factor, giving

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\text{both queues empty and none walking})=\frac{2-\nu\mu}{2+\nu\mu}(1-\nu\tau)e^{-\nu\sigma}.}
$$

The factor $e^{-\nu\sigma}$ is the empty probability of the deterministic-travel infinite-server stage. Both printed stability inequalities ensure the stationary calculations apply.
