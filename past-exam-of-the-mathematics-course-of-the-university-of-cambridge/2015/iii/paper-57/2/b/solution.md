<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In [magnetostatics](../../../../../../magnetostatics.md), $-\nabla p-\rho\nabla\Phi+\mathbf f_L=0$. The plane-parallel [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) and all thermodynamic fields are independent of $y$, so the $y$ component of the [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) must vanish:

$$
\nabla\psi\times\nabla B_y=0.
$$

Away from a null of $\nabla\psi$, this says that $B_y$ is constant along each connected contour of the [Cartesian magnetic flux function](../../../../../../cartesian-magnetic-flux-function.md). Thus, on a regular flux region,

$$
\boxed{B_y=b(\psi).}
$$

This statement is local; disconnected contours with the same numerical flux label need not share one global function without the usual flux-tube connectivity assumption.

Since $\nabla B_y=b'(\psi)\nabla\psi$, the transverse [magnetostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../magnetostatic-equilibrium.md) equation becomes

$$
\boxed{\frac1{\mu_0}
\left[\nabla^2\psi+b(\psi)b'(\psi)\right]\nabla\psi
+\nabla p+\rho\nabla\Phi=0.}
$$

This is a [Cartesian magnetostatic flux-function equilibrium](../../../../../../cartesian-magnetostatic-flux-function-equilibrium.md). Projecting it along a transverse [magnetic field line](../../../../../../magnetic-field-line.md) gives hydrostatic balance along that line; projecting across the line balances the gas pressure and weight against [magnetic pressure](../../../../../../magnetic-pressure.md) and [magnetic tension](../../../../../../magnetic-tension.md).
