↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For an [independent subgroup contrast in meta-analysis](../../../../../../independent-subgroup-contrast-in-meta-analysis.md), the difference of disjoint [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) subgroup estimates has estimated [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) equal to the sum of the two variances. The subgroup contrast and [standard error](../../../../../../standard-error.md) are

$$
D=0.45-0.16=0.29,\qquad
\operatorname{SE}(D)=\sqrt{0.11+0.05}=0.4.
$$

The [Wald test](../../../../../../wald-test.md) of equal subgroup mean [log odds ratios](../../../../../../log-odds-ratio.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{Z=\frac{0.29}{0.4}=0.725,\qquad
Z^2=0.525625,\qquad p\approx0.468.}
$$

Under the null, $Z$ is approximately [standard normal](../../../../../../standard-normal-distribution.md) and $Z^2$ has a [chi-squared distribution](../../../../../../chi-squared-distribution.md) with one degree of freedom. A quantile-2 [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md) for the difference is $(0.29-0.8,0.29+0.8)=(-0.51,1.09)$. **There is no statistically persuasive evidence of a subgroup difference.** The result does not establish equal effects. Nor does a between-trial subgroup comparison, by itself, identify a causal advantage of one technique: other study characteristics may differ.
