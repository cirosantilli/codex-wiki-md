<h1 id="20f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [20F](../20f.md)

Two [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) elements represent the same [germ of a holomorphic function](../../../../../germ-of-a-holomorphic-function.md) at $z$ if they agree on some neighbourhood of $z$. For a holomorphic $f$ on an open $D\subseteq G$, set $U(f,D)=\{[f]_z:z\in D\}$. These sets form a [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) on the [space of germs of holomorphic functions](../../../../../space-of-germs-of-holomorphic-functions.md): every germ lies in one, and if a germ lies in two, equality of the representatives on a smaller neighbourhood supplies a [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) set in their intersection. Declaring arbitrary unions of [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) sets open therefore gives a topology.

Different base points are separated by disjoint small discs in $G$. If two different germs have the same base point, choose representatives on one connected small disc. Their sheets are disjoint: any common germ elsewhere on that disc would force equality throughout the disc by the [identity theorem](../../../../../identity-theorem.md). This proves the Hausdorff property.

The projection $\pi([f]_z)=z$ maps each sheet $U(f,D)$ homeomorphically onto $D$. Use these maps as complex charts. On overlaps their transition functions are the identity, hence holomorphic; thus each connected component is a [Riemann surface](../../../../../riemann-surfaces.md), and $\pi$ is a local [biholomorphism](../../../../../biholomorphism.md), an [analytic germ projection](../../../../../analytic-germ-projection.md). For completeness, second countability of a component follows by following continuation chains through a countable [basis of a topology](../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md) of rational discs: starting at one germ, finite chains of such discs give only countably many continuation sheets, by uniqueness on overlaps, and they cover every germ in that component.

A [complete analytic function](../../../../../complete-analytic-function.md) consists of all germs reachable from one initial germ by [analytic continuation](../../../../../analytic-continuation.md). The supplied path-lifting equivalence identifies these with its path component. Since the germ space is locally path connected, connected components and path components agree. Thus its continuation surface is exactly one component $\mathcal G_{\mathcal F}$.

A local [biholomorphism](../../../../../biholomorphism.md) need not evenly cover the whole base; the germ projection is an analytic covering in this local sense. The examples below distinguish a genuine [regular covering map](../../../../../regular-covering-map.md) from a surjective nonregular projection, with the meaning of regular stated explicitly.
