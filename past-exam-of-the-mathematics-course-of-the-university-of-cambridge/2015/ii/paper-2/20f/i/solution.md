<h1 id="20f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $G=\mathbb C^*$ and the [complete analytic function](../../../../../../complete-analytic-function.md) generated by a logarithm germ. Its continuation surface identifies with $\mathbb C$ via $w\mapsto[\log]_z$ with $z=e^w$ and logarithm value $w$. Thus $\pi(w)=e^w$. It is a [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md); the deck transformations $w\mapsto w+2\pi in$ act transitively on each fibre, so it is also a [regular covering map](../../../../../../regular-covering-map.md) in the deck-transitive sense. **It is not bijective**, since every nonzero $z$ has infinitely many logarithm values. It also meets the alternative convention in which “regular” merely requires an evenly covered neighbourhood at each base point.
