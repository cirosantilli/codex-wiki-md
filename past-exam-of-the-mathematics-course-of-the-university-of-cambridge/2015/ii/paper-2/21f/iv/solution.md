↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**The equality is false.** In $A=k[x,y]$, take $I=(y)$ and $J=(y-x^2)$. Both are [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md), since their quotient rings are isomorphic to $k[x]$, so both are radical. But

$$
\sqrt I+\sqrt J=(y,x^2),\qquad\sqrt{I+J}=(x,y).
$$

The element $x$ belongs to the latter ideal and not the former. This also shows why taking the [radical of an ideal](../../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) can enlarge a sum of already radical ideals.
