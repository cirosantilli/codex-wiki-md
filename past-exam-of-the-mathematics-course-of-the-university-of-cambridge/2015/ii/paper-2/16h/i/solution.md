<h1 id="16h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the customary squarefree convention for the quadratic radicand $d$. For $d\equiv2,3\pmod4$, the [ring of integers of a number field](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathbb Z[\sqrt d]$, so

$$
\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)}/(p)\cong\mathbb F_p[X]/(X^2-d).
$$

The ideal $(p)$ is prime exactly when this quotient is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). A [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) quotient of a field is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) exactly when the defining [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is irreducible. This proves the assertion, including the possibility of ramified primes where the reduction has a repeated root.

Squarefreeness is necessary for the printed assertion as written: $d=18\equiv2\pmod4$ defines the same field as $d=2$. The prime $3$ is inert in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ because $X^2-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_3$, although $X^2-18=X^2$ is reducible. Thus **the result requires a squarefree quadratic radicand**.
