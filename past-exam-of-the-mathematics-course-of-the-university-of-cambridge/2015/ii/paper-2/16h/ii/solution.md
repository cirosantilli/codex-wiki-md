<h1 id="16h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $s=\sqrt{-14}$. Then $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[s]$ and the [field discriminant](../../../../../../field-discriminant.md) is $-56$. Reduction of $X^2+14$ modulo $2$ and $3$ gives

$$
\boxed{(2)=\mathfrak p_2^2,\ \mathfrak p_2=(2,s);\qquad(3)=\mathfrak p_3\overline{\mathfrak p}_3,\ \mathfrak p_3=(3,s-1),\ \overline{\mathfrak p}_3=(3,s+1).}
$$

Their [ideal norms](../../../../../../ideal-norm.md) are $2,3,3$, respectively. The [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{56}<5$, so every ideal class has an integral representative of [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) at most $4$. The only possible representatives are the unit ideal, $\mathfrak p_2$, $\mathfrak p_3$, $\overline{\mathfrak p}_3$, and the norm-$4$ ideal $\mathfrak p_2^2=(2)$. Thus there are at most four classes.

The class of $\mathfrak p_2$ is nontrivial, since a [principal ideal](../../../../../../principal-ideal.md) of [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) $2$ would require $x^2+14y^2=2$. Also $\mathfrak p_3^2$ is not principal: [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) $9$ forces $x+ys=\pm3$, whose ideal is $\mathfrak p_3\overline{\mathfrak p}_3$, not $\mathfrak p_3^2$.

Now $N(2+s)=18$. It is divisible by $\mathfrak p_2$ and $\mathfrak p_3$, but not by $\overline{\mathfrak p}_3$, as substitution of $s=-1$ modulo $3$ gives $1$. Unique ideal factorization and [norms](../../../../../../norm.md) therefore give $(2+s)=\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3^2$. Consequently $[\mathfrak p_3]^2=[\mathfrak p_2]^{-1}=[\mathfrak p_2]$, a nontrivial class of order $2$. The class of $\mathfrak p_3$ has order $4$, and the four-class bound is attained:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-14}))\cong C_4,\text{ generated by }[(3,\sqrt{-14}-1)].}
$$
