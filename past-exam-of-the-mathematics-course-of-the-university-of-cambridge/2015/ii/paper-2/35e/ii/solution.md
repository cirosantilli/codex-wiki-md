<h1 id="35e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Reflection about the closest point leaves the gap geometry unchanged and reverses the direction of translation. The linear Stokes equations make the excess [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) change sign when the imposed [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) changes sign. Combining reflection with this linearity shows that [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) is odd in $X$. Its leading normal force on the stationary plane therefore cancels between the two sides, giving **$\mathbf e_y\cdot\mathbf F=0$ in the lubrication limit**. Normal viscous stresses are of smaller order in the aspect ratio. This uses symmetry only, rather than a [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) calculation.
