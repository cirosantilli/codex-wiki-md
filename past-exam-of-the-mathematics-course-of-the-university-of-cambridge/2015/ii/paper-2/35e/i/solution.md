<h1 id="35e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Near the closest point, in $X=x-Ut$, the gap is $h(X)\simeq h_0+X^2/(2a)$. The horizontal length scale is **$\ell=\sqrt{2ah_0}$**, up to an inessential constant. Thus the aspect ratio $h_0/\ell\ll1$ follows from $h_0\ll a$. The [lubrication approximation](../../../../../../lubrication-theory.md) assumes an incompressible [Newtonian fluid](../../../../../../newtonian-fluid.md), no slip on the surfaces, slow variation of the gap, and negligible inertia relative to transverse viscous stress:

$$
\boxed{\frac{\rho U h_0^2}{\mu\ell}\ll1.}
$$

This is the reduced Reynolds-number condition; it is weaker than requiring a [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) based on $\ell$ to be small. In the cylinder frame the geometry and flow are steady. [Pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) is uniform across the narrow gap to leading order, and equal ambient [pressures](../../../../../../pressure.md) far to either side close the model. The calculation assumes a full liquid film; [cavitation](../../../../../../cavitation.md) would change that [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) condition.
