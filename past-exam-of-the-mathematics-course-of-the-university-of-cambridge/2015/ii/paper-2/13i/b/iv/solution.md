<h1 id="13i/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For every countable [ordinal](../../../../../../../ordinal.md) $\beta$, $\omega^\beta$ is countable: successor stages use a countable product, and countable limit stages a countable union. Hence $\omega^{\omega_1}=\sup_{\beta<\omega_1}\omega^\beta\leq\omega_1$. Conversely $\omega^\beta\geq\beta$ by [transfinite induction](../../../../../../../transfinite-induction.md), so these values are cofinal in $\omega_1$. Therefore **$\omega^{\omega_1}=\omega_1$**.

It is not the least fixed point. Set $a_0=1$, $a_{n+1}=\omega^{a_n}$ and define [epsilon zero](../../../../../../../epsilon-zero.md) as $\varepsilon_0=\sup_na_n$. This is countable, and continuity of [ordinal exponentiation](../../../../../../../ordinal-exponentiation.md) at limits gives $\omega^{\varepsilon_0}=\sup_n\omega^{a_n}=\varepsilon_0$. Any fixed point of $\alpha\mapsto\omega^\alpha$ dominates every $a_n$ by induction, hence dominates $\varepsilon_0$. Thus **the least fixed point is $\varepsilon_0<\omega_1$**.
