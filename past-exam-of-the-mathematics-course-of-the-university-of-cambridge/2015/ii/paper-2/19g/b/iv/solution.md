<h1 id="19g/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The integration functional satisfies $|Tf|\leq\|f\|_\infty\int_0^1|h|$. If $h$ never vanishes, its sign is constant and $f=\operatorname{sgn}h$ attains equality. In general use the [continuous functions](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f_\varepsilon=h/\sqrt{h^2+\varepsilon}$, of supremum [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) at most $1$. Then

$$
Tf_\varepsilon=\int_0^1\frac{h^2}{\sqrt{h^2+\varepsilon}}\,dt\longrightarrow\int_0^1|h(t)|\,dt
$$

by the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md). Therefore **$\|T\|=\int_0^1|h|$**. Every bounded subset of its finite-dimensional target $\mathbb R$ is relatively compact, so **$T$ is compact**, including when $h=0$.
