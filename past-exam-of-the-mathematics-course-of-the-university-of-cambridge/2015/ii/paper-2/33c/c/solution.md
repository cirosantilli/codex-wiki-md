<h1 id="33c/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For fixed particle number, [Bose-Einstein condensation](../../../../../../bose-einstein-condensation.md) means that a macroscopic fraction occupies the single-particle [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md). As $\mu\uparrow0$, the excited-state population reaches its maximum

$$
N_{\rm ex}=B(k_BT)^3\int_0^\infty\frac{x^2}{e^x-1}\,dx=2B\zeta(3)(k_BT)^3.
$$

At the transition this equals $N$, so

$$
\boxed{T_c=\frac1{k_B}\left(\frac{N}{2B\zeta(3)}\right)^{1/3}\propto N^{1/3}.}
$$

Below $T_c$, the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) is approximately zero in the large-$N$ continuum description. Additional particles occupy the [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md), giving

$$
\boxed{\frac{N_0}{N}\simeq1-\left(\frac T{T_c}\right)^3,\qquad\gamma=3.}
$$

For finite $N$, the actual [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) remains slightly negative; the saturation statement is the macroscopic approximation used here.
