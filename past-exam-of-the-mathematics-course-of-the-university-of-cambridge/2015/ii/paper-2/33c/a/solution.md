<h1 id="33c/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Bose-Einstein distribution](../../../../../../bose-einstein-distribution.md) gives $n_i=[\exp((E_i-\mu)/(k_BT))-1]^{-1}$, with [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) below the lowest [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) when its discrete occupation is finite. In the continuum approximation,

$$
\boxed{N=\int_0^\infty\frac{g(E)}{e^{(E-\mu)/(k_BT)}-1}\,dE,\qquad U=\int_0^\infty\frac{Eg(E)}{e^{(E-\mu)/(k_BT)}-1}\,dE.}
$$

If the zero-energy [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) is macroscopically occupied, its discrete occupation $N_0$ must be added to the first integral; it contributes no [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) to the second. This distinction is essential when applying the formulas to [Bose-Einstein condensation](../../../../../../bose-einstein-condensation.md).
