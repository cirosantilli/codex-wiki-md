<h1 id="23j/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

By the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md), the integral is bounded by $\|f\|_2\|k_x\|_2$, where $k_x(y)=|x-y|^{-\alpha}$. For $0\leq x\leq1$,

$$
\int_0^1|x-y|^{-2\alpha}\,dy=\frac{x^{1-2\alpha}+(1-x)^{1-2\alpha}}{1-2\alpha}<\infty.
$$

For $x$ outside the interval, the integral is bounded by the value at the nearest endpoint; the endpoint value is finite as well. Since $2\alpha<1$, the kernel is square-integrable for every real $x$, proving **[absolute convergence](../../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) for every $x$**.
