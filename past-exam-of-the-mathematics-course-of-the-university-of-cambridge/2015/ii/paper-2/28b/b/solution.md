<h1 id="28b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the nearly conserved oscillator [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) $E=(\dot x^2+x^2)/2$. The [differential equation](../../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) gives

$$
\dot E=-\varepsilon(2\dot x^4+2x^3\dot x-4x^4\dot x^2-\dot x^2).
$$

For a leading-order orbit $x=A\cos\theta$, $\dot x=-A\sin\theta$, the cycle averages are $\langle\dot x^4\rangle=3A^4/8$, $\langle x^3\dot x\rangle=0$, $\langle x^4\dot x^2\rangle=A^6/16$, and $\langle\dot x^2\rangle=A^2/2$. The [energy balance method](../../../../../../energy-balance-method.md) therefore gives

$$
\langle\dot E\rangle=\frac\varepsilon4A^2(A^2-1)(A^2-2),\qquad\dot A=\frac\varepsilon4A(A^2-1)(A^2-2).
$$

The two nonzero simple roots give the leading [limit cycles](../../../../../../limit-cycle.md) **$x\simeq\cos(t+\theta_0)$ and $x\simeq\sqrt2\cos(t+\theta_0)$**. Below amplitude $1$ the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) increases, between $1$ and $\sqrt2$ it decreases, and above $\sqrt2$ it increases. Hence **the amplitude-$1$ cycle is stable and the amplitude-$\sqrt2$ cycle unstable** for positive small $\varepsilon$.

The linearized averaged radial exponents are $-\varepsilon/2$ and $\varepsilon$, respectively, consistent with nontrivial [Floquet multipliers](../../../../../../floquet-multiplier.md) $e^{-\pi\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)}$ and $e^{2\pi\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)}$. The nonzero derivatives at these averaged zeros also justify persistence of nearby cycles for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.
