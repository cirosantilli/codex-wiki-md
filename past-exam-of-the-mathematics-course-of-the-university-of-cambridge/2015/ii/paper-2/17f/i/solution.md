<h1 id="17f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-galois-theory.md) gives an inclusion-reversing bijection between [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $H\leq\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ and intermediate fields $M$, by $H\mapsto L^H$ and $M\mapsto\operatorname{Gal}(L/M)$, for a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md). One has $[L:L^H]=|H|$ and $[L^H:K]=[\operatorname{Gal}(L/K):H]$. The field $L^H$ is Galois over $K$ exactly when $H$ is normal; then its Galois group is the quotient by $H$.

Let $\alpha=\sqrt[3]2$ and $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$. The [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta)$. A [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) in [characteristic zero](../../../../../../characteristic-zero.md) is normal and separable, hence Galois. [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at $2$ gives $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=3$. This field is real, whereas $\zeta$ is not, and $\zeta$ satisfies a quadratic [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Thus $[L:\mathbb Q]=6$. The faithful permutation action on the three roots identifies the Galois group with $S_3$. The [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) fixing $\alpha$ is generated by the transposition interchanging the other two roots. Its conjugates are different transposition [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md), so **it is not normal**.
