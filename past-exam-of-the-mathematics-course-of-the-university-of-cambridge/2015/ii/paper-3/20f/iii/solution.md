<h1 id="20f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the two charts of the [blowup of the affine plane at the origin](../../../../../../blowup-of-the-affine-plane-at-the-origin.md). In the chart $y=xv$, the total transform is

$$
x^2-y^3=x^2(1-xv^3),
$$

so the [proper transform](../../../../../../strict-transform.md) has equation $1-xv^3=0$. It misses the exceptional divisor $x=0$ and is smooth, since its derivative with respect to $x$ is $-v^3\ne0$ there.

In the chart $x=yu$, the total transform is $y^2(u^2-y)$, so the proper transform is

$$
\boxed{y=u^2,\qquad x=u^3.}
$$

Its equation $u^2-y=0$ has partial derivative $-1$ with respect to $y$, so it is smooth even at its unique intersection $(u,y)=(0,0)$ with the exceptional divisor. The two chart descriptions agree on their overlap. Thus **one blowup makes the cusp nonsingular**, although it is tangent to the exceptional divisor there.
