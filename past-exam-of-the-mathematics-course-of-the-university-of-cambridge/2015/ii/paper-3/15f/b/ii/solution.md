<h1 id="15f/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For finite nonempty $\Omega$, let $e_\omega$ be the basis vectors of the [permutation representation](../../../../../../../permutation-representation.md). Put

$$
u=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}e_\omega,\qquad V=\left\{\sum_\omega a_\omega e_\omega:\sum_\omega a_\omega=0\right\}.
$$

The line $\mathbb Cu$ is fixed by $G$, and $V$ is the [augmentation subrepresentation of a permutation representation](../../../../../../../augmentation-subrepresentation-of-a-permutation-representation.md). Since $|\Omega|\ne0$ in $\mathbb C$, they have zero intersection and their dimensions add to $|\Omega|$. Hence **$\boxed{\mathbb C\Omega\cong\mathbb C_G\oplus V}$**.

The printed formulation does not explicitly require $\Omega$ to be finite. For an infinite nonempty $G$-set, interpret $\mathbb C\Omega$ as finite-support vectors. Choose one finite orbit $O$ (all orbits are finite because $G$ is finite), replace $u$ by $\sum_{\omega\in O}e_\omega$, and use the same augmentation kernel. This again splits off a trivial summand. The nonempty-set assumption is needed; the empty permutation module contains no trivial summand.
