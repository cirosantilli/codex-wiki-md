<h1 id="16f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $\alpha=\sqrt[5]3$ and let $\zeta$ be a primitive fifth root of unity. Then $L=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\zeta)$. The field degrees $[\mathbb Q(\alpha):\mathbb Q]=5$ and $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=4$ both divide $[L:\mathbb Q]$, while adjoining the two generators gives $[L:\mathbb Q]\leq20$. Consequently $[L:\mathbb Q]=20$.

The [cyclotomic field](../../../../../../cyclotomic-field.md) $\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q$ is Galois with cyclic group of order $4$. The [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-galois-theory.md) gives a normal subgroup

$$
N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q(\zeta)),\qquad |N|=5,\qquad G/N\cong C_4.
$$

Thus $N\cong C_5$ and **the chain** $\boxed{G\triangleright N\triangleright\{1\}}$ has abelian successive quotients, proving that $G$ is a [solvable group](../../../../../../solvable-group.md). One may refine it by inserting the inverse image of the order-two subgroup of $C_4$, giving cyclic factors $C_2,C_2,C_5$.
