<h1 id="16f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $f$ is an [irreducible polynomial](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md), any two roots $\alpha,\beta$ have the same [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md). Thus the map $\mathbb Q(\alpha)\to\mathbb Q(\beta)$ carrying $\alpha$ to $\beta$ is a field embedding. The [extension theorem for field embeddings](../../../../../../extension-theorem-for-field-embeddings.md) extends it to an embedding of the [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) $L$ into an algebraic closure. Since $L/\mathbb Q$ is normal, its image is $L$, so it is an element of the [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md).

Conversely, factor $f$ into irreducible factors over $\mathbb Q$. An automorphism preserves the vanishing of each factor, so it cannot send a root of one factor to a root of another. Since there are no repeated roots, different factors have disjoint root sets. Transitivity on all roots therefore forces a single irreducible factor. This proves **the equivalence**.
