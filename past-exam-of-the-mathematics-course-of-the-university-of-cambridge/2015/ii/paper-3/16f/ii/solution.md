<h1 id="16f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Label the distinct roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$. Define $\tau(\sigma)$ by $\sigma(\alpha_i)=\alpha_{\tau(\sigma)(i)}$. Composition respects this rule, so it is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md). If its image permutation is the identity, $\sigma$ fixes all the roots; they generate $L$, so $\sigma$ is the identity. Thus **$\tau$ is injective**.

For $f=t(t^2-2)$, label the roots $0,\sqrt2,-\sqrt2$. The [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and its nontrivial automorphism swaps precisely roots $2,3$. Hence $\boxed{\operatorname{im}\tau=\langle(2\ 3)\rangle}$.

For $f=t^3-2$, [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) gives irreducibility. The real root $\alpha=\sqrt[3]2$ generates a degree-three real field. Adjoining a primitive cube root of unity $\zeta_3$ gives a further extension of degree two, since it is nonreal and satisfies $t^2+t+1=0$. Thus its splitting field has degree $6$. In characteristic zero this is a [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md), so its Galois group has order $6$. The injection into $S_3$ is therefore **an isomorphism**.
