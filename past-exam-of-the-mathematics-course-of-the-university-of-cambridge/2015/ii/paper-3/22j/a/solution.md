A [measure-preserving transformation](../../../../../../measure-preserving-transformation.md) is a measurable map $T:E\to E$ satisfying $\mu(T^{-1}A)=\mu(A)$ for every measurable $A$. An [invariant set](../../../../../../invariant-set-of-a-measure-preserving-transformation.md) satisfies $T^{-1}A=A$ (or equality modulo null sets, in the measure-theoretic convention).

Preimages preserve complements and countable unions. Therefore the whole space is invariant, the complement of an invariant set is invariant, and a countable union of invariant sets is invariant. Thus **the invariant sets form a $\sigma$-algebra**. The same proof works modulo null sets because a countable union of null exceptional sets is null.
