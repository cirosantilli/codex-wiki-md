<h1 id="35d/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The preceding condition is symmetric in $b,d$, so $\ell_{[b;d]}=0$. On a patch where the potential representation supplied in the question holds, write $\ell_a=\partial_a\phi$. Define

$$
\boxed{k_a=e^\phi\ell_a=\partial_a(e^\phi).}
$$

Then $k_{a;b}=e^\phi(\ell_{a;b}+\ell_a\ell_b)$. Since $e^\phi\ne0$, the condition on $\ell$ is exactly **$\boxed{k_{a;b}=0}$**, so $k$ is a [parallel covector field](../../../../../../parallel-covector-field.md). Closed covectors have this scalar-potential representation locally by the [Poincaré lemma](../../../../../../poincare-lemma.md); a global potential additionally requires exactness, for example on a simply connected domain.
