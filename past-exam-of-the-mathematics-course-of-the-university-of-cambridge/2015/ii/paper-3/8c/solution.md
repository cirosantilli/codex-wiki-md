<h1 id="8c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [8C](../8c.md)

The [flatness problem](../../../../../flatness-problem.md) is the extreme closeness to spatial flatness required in the early universe if ordinary decelerating expansion is to produce the observed near-flat universe. From the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../friedmann-equations.md), with $c=1$ and curvature constant $k$,

$$
H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{k}{a^2},\qquad \Omega-1=\frac{k}{a^2H^2}=\frac{k}{\dot a^2}.
$$

During decelerating expansion $|\Omega-1|$ grows. The [Friedmann acceleration equation](../../../../../friedmann-acceleration-equation.md) gives $\ddot a/a=-4\pi G(\rho+3P)/3$. Thus $\rho+3P<0$ makes an expanding universe accelerate, increases $\dot a$ and suppresses $|\Omega-1|$: this is the [inflationary solution of the flatness problem](../../../../../inflationary-solution-of-the-flatness-problem.md).

The [slow-roll approximation](../../../../../slow-roll-approximation.md) neglects $\dot\phi^2/2$ compared with $V$ and $\ddot\phi$ compared with $3H\dot\phi$. For the positive-field branch, taking $m>0$,

$$
H\simeq\frac{m\phi}{\sqrt3},\qquad 3H\dot\phi\simeq-2m^2\phi,\qquad \dot\phi\simeq-\frac{2m}{\sqrt3}.
$$

Integrating the field equation and $\dot a/a=H$ gives **the slow-roll solution**

$$
\boxed{\phi(t)=\phi_i-\frac{2mt}{\sqrt3},\qquad a(t)=a_i\exp\left(\frac{m\phi_i t}{\sqrt3}-\frac{m^2t^2}{3}\right)=a_i\exp\frac{\phi_i^2-\phi(t)^2}{4}.}
$$

The [potential slow-roll parameters](../../../../../potential-slow-roll-parameter.md) are $\epsilon_V=\eta_V=2/\phi^2$. They cease to be small when $\phi$ is of order $1$ in these units; the customary endpoint $\epsilon_V=1$ is **$\boxed{\phi_{\rm end}\simeq\sqrt2}$**. Equating the approximate kinetic and potential energies instead gives $\sqrt{2/3}$, another order-one indicator, not a precise endpoint of an approximation that is already failing.
