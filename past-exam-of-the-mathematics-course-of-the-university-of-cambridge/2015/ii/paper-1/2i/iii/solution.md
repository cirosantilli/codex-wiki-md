<h1 id="2i/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $\eta>0$, use the [weak maximum principle for elliptic operators](../../../../../../weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators.md) perturbation $\phi_\eta(x)=\phi(x)+\eta|x|^2$. Its [Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian.md) is at least $4\eta$, so the preceding argument puts its maximum on the boundary. If $R^2=\max_{\bar\Omega}|x|^2$, then, for any $x\in\bar\Omega$,

$$
\phi(x)\leq\max_{\partial\Omega}\phi+\eta R^2.
$$

Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives $\phi(x)\leq\max_{\partial\Omega}\phi$. The boundary is compact, so this maximum is attained. This proves the weak [weak maximum principle for elliptic operators](../../../../../../weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators.md) even when the [Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian.md) may vanish.
