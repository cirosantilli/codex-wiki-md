<h1 id="16h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $c_1,\ldots,c_d$ be the coefficients of $f$. Each is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md). The ring $R=\mathbb Z[c_1,\ldots,c_d]$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-module: reduce powers of each generator by its monic integral equation. As $f$ is monic, $R[\alpha]$ is generated over $R$ by $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$, so is a finite $\mathbb Z$-module. It is torsion-free as a subgroup of a characteristic-zero field, hence free of finite rank. Multiplication by $\alpha$ on it is represented by an integer [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md). Its monic [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md), by [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md), annihilates $\alpha$. Thus **$\alpha$ is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md)**. This proves the required transitivity of integrality in this setting.

Every root of the monic $h$ is also a root of the monic [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $h^n\in\mathcal O_K[x]$, and is therefore an [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md). The coefficients of $h$ are elementary symmetric [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in these roots, hence [algebraic integers](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md) because [algebraic integers](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md) form a ring. They also belong to $K$, so belong to $\mathcal O_K$. Consequently **$h\in\mathcal O_K[x]$**.
