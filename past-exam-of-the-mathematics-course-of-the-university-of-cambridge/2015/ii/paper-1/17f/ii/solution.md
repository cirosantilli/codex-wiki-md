<h1 id="17f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) of $f$ over $K$ is an extension generated over $K$ by all its roots in which $f$ splits into linear factors. A nonconstant irreducible [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is [separable polynomial](../../../../../../separable-polynomial.md) when its roots in a [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) are distinct, equivalently when it is coprime to its formal [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md). For a general [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), the convention compatible with the printed assertion is that all its irreducible factors are separable; repeated occurrences of the same factor are allowed under this convention.

For a finite field of characteristic $p$, the [finite-field Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../../finite-field-frobenius-automorphism.md) $x\mapsto x^p$ is injective and hence surjective. If an irreducible [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $g$ had zero [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md), every nonzero exponent would be divisible by $p$. Taking $p$th roots of its coefficients would give $g=h^p$, contradicting irreducibility. Thus $g'\ne0$, and irreducibility gives $\gcd(g,g')=1$. Every irreducible factor is therefore separable: **finite fields are perfect**.

If instead one defines a separable [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) to mean that the entire [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has no repeated roots, the printed claim for every $f$ is false: $(t-1)^2$ is a counterexample over any finite field. The proof above establishes the precise intended perfect-field assertion; a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has entirely distinct roots if and only if it is also squarefree.
