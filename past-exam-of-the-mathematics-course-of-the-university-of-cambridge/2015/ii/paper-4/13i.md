# 13I

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 4](../paper-4-split.md)

The [Axiom of foundation](../../../../axiom-of-regularity.md) states that every nonempty set $A$ has a member $a$ with $a\cap A=\varnothing$. The principle of [epsilon induction](../../../../epsilon-induction.md) states that a formula $\phi$ satisfying $\forall x[(\forall y\in x\;\phi(y))\Rightarrow\phi(x)]$ holds for every set $x$.

Assume [Axiom of foundation](../../../../axiom-of-regularity.md) and suppose a progressive formula has a counterexample $a$. In the [transitive closure](../../../../transitive-closure.md) of $\{a\}$, use [axiom schema of separation](../../../../axiom-schema-of-specification.md) to collect the counterexamples into a nonempty set $B$. Foundation supplies $b\in B$ having no member in $B$. Since the closure is transitive, every member of $b$ satisfies $\phi$, contradicting progressiveness. Conversely, if a nonempty set $A$ had no foundation-minimal member, every $x\in A$ would have a member $y\in x\cap A$. The formula $x\notin A$ is then progressive: if every member of $x$ lies outside $A$, $x$ cannot belong to $A$. [Epsilon induction](../../../../epsilon-induction.md) would make $A$ empty, a contradiction. This proves equivalence in the presence of the other [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](../../../../zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory.md).

To obtain the [cumulative hierarchy](../../../../cumulative-hierarchy.md), perform [epsilon induction](../../../../epsilon-induction.md) on the assertion that $x$ belongs to some $V_\alpha$. If this holds for each member $y$, [Axiom schema of replacement](../../../../axiom-schema-of-replacement.md) collects their least such ordinals into a set. Choose an ordinal $\beta$ above them all. Then $x\subseteq V_\beta$ and $x\in V_{\beta+1}$. Thus every set occurs in the hierarchy. Use the [rank of a set](../../../../rank-of-a-set.md) convention $\operatorname{rk}(x)=\sup_{y\in x}(\operatorname{rk}(y)+1)$, equivalently the least $\alpha$ with $x\subseteq V_\alpha$.
