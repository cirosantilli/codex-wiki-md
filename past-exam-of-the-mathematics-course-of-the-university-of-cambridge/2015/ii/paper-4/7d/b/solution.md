<h1 id="7d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [normal mode](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) write $x_j=A_j e^{i\omega t}$. The [generalized eigenvalue problem](../../../../../../generalized-eigenvalue-problem.md) is

$$
k\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}A
=\omega^2m\operatorname{diag}(1,2,1)A.
$$

The three independent [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) and corresponding squared frequencies are

$$
\boxed{(1,1,1):0;\qquad(1,0,-1):k/m;\qquad(1,-1,1):2k/m.}
$$

The last two have zero mass-weighted displacement and are [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) in the mass inner product. The lowest frequency is zero: it represents a rigid translation with both springs unchanged. Its general motion includes uniform centre-of-mass velocity, not just a constant displacement. Superpositions of the two oscillatory [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md) and this translation give the general solution.
