<h1 id="17f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In characteristic two, let $K=\mathbb F_2(t)$ and $L=K(u)$ with $u^2+u=t$. The [Artin–Schreier polynomial](../../../../../../artin-schreier-polynomial.md) is irreducible: if $r\in K$ solved it, a finite pole of $r$ would create a pole of even order in $r^2+r$, impossible for $t$; at infinity a pole of $r$ likewise gives even order, while $t$ has order one. A constant $r$ cannot solve it either. Its derivative is one, so this degree-two extension is [separable field extension](../../../../../../separable-extension.md). Adjoining $\sqrt\alpha$ in characteristic two is either trivial or [purely inseparable field extension](../../../../../../purely-inseparable-extension.md), since the derivative of $X^2-\alpha$ vanishes. Hence $\boxed{L\ne K(\sqrt\alpha)\text{ for every }\alpha\in K}$.

For the second example take $\boxed{K=\mathbb F_{11}}$. Every extension still has characteristic eleven, and $X^{11}-1=(X-1)^{11}$ in it. Thus the only eleventh [root of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) is one, whose order is one, and there is no primitive eleventh root in any extension.
