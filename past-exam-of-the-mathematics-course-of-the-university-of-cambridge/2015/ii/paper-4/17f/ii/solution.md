<h1 id="17f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [Vandermonde determinant](../../../../../../vandermonde-determinant.md) $\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)$. Its square is a [symmetric polynomial](../../../../../../symmetric-polynomial.md), hence a polynomial in the [elementary symmetric polynomials](../../../../../../elementary-symmetric-polynomial.md) and belongs to $K$. It is nonzero because the variables are algebraically independent. A transposition of two variables fixes every $e_i$ and therefore fixes $K$, but sends $\Delta$ to $-\Delta\ne\Delta$ in characteristic zero. Thus $\boxed{\alpha=\Delta\notin K,\quad\alpha^2\in K}$.
