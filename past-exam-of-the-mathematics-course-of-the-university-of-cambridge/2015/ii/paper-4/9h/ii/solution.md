↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** Since $N$ is not a square, some exponent $e_i$ is odd. Choose a [quadratic nonresidue](../../../../../../quadratic-nonresidue.md) modulo that $p_i$ and choose residue one at every other prime. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) produces a unit $b$ modulo $N$ with $(b/N)=-1$. Multiplication by $b$ permutes the residue classes modulo $N$. Thus, by multiplicativity of the [Jacobi symbol](../../../../../../jacobi-symbol.md),

$$
\sum_{a=1}^N(a/N)=\sum_{a=1}^N(ba/N)=-\sum_{a=1}^N(a/N).
$$

The sum is therefore $\boxed{0}$. Nonunits contribute zero, so the permutation argument covers every term.
