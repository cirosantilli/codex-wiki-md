<h1 id="32c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The single-spin average in the effective field is $\tanh(\beta B_{\rm eff})$. Requiring it to equal the assumed average gives the [mean-field self-consistency equation](../../../../../../self-consistency-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{m=\tanh[\beta(B+2DJm)].}
$$

At $B=0$, the slope of its right side at the origin is $2DJ/(k_BT)$. Thus $\boxed{T_c=2DJ/k_B}$. For $T>T_c$, $m=0$ is the only solution and is the stable minimum: thermal fluctuations dominate. For $T<T_c$, two stable nonzero solutions $\pm m(T)$ represent [spontaneous magnetization](../../../../../../spontaneous-magnetization.md), while the zero solution is unstable. At $T_c$ they merge continuously. Expanding $\tanh x=x-x^3/3+\cdots$ near the transition yields $m^2\sim3(T_c-T)/T_c$, so the mean-field [critical exponent](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) is one-half. These are predictions of the approximation, not a proof of the exact Ising transition in every dimension.
