<h1 id="5e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The delayed positive term models recruitment depending on the population a time $T$ earlier; the quadratic negative term represents instantaneous density-dependent losses. Thus this [delay differential equation](../../../../../../delay-differential-equation.md) delays recruitment rather than the strength of crowding acting on the present population.

With $x=1+y$, the [linearized equation](../../../../../../linearized-equation.md) is $\dot y=\alpha y(t-T)-2\alpha y(t)$. Its [characteristic equation of a delay differential equation](../../../../../../characteristic-equation-of-a-delay-differential-equation.md) is $\lambda+2\alpha=\alpha e^{-\lambda T}$. A root with nonnegative real part would satisfy $|\lambda+2\alpha|\geq2\alpha$, whereas $|\alpha e^{-\lambda T}|\leq\alpha$. This is impossible. Consequently **the equilibrium is locally exponentially asymptotically stable for every $\alpha>0$ and $T>0$**. There is no upper bound on $\alpha T$ in this model.
