<h1 id="7a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $Dx=\lambda x$ with $x\ne0$. Commutation gives

$$
D(Cx)=C(Dx)=\lambda Cx.
$$

Thus $Cx$ belongs to the [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) $E_\lambda(D)$, with the zero vector allowed. Since $D$ has $n$ distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), each [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is one-dimensional. Hence **$Cx=\mu x$ for some scalar $\mu$**. This is the fact that [commuting maps preserve eigenspaces](../../../../../../commuting-maps-preserve-eigenspaces.md).

The standard [linear independence](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) theorem for [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) belonging to distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) supplies a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ of [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $D$. The preceding argument makes every one an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $C$ as well. Their column matrix therefore simultaneously diagonalizes $C$ and $D$. This is [simple spectrum gives simultaneous diagonalization](../../../../../../simple-spectrum-gives-simultaneous-diagonalization.md).

For real matrices, this conclusion always holds over $\mathbb C$; a real common [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) follows when the distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $D$ are real. Real matrix entries alone do not ensure a real [eigenbasis](../../../../../../eigenbasis.md): a quarter-turn of $\mathbb R^2$, with $C=D$, has [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\pm i$. The matrices in this question have real spectra, so their common [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) is real.
