<h1 id="1e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Subtracting twice the first [linear congruence](../../../../../../linear-congruence.md) from the second gives $5x\equiv1\pmod{53}$. Since $5\cdot32=3\cdot53+1$, the [modular inverse](../../../../../../modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) of $5$ modulo $53$ is $32$. Thus $x\equiv32\pmod{53}$. Substitution in the first [linear congruence](../../../../../../linear-congruence.md) then gives $2y\equiv23\pmod{53}$, whence $y\equiv38\pmod{53}$. **All integer solutions are**

$$
\boxed{x=32+53r,\qquad y=38+53t,\qquad r,t\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

The two free [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) are independent. Conversely, these values give $6x+2y\equiv268\equiv3$ and $17x+4y\equiv696\equiv7\pmod{53}$, so no additional restriction is needed.
