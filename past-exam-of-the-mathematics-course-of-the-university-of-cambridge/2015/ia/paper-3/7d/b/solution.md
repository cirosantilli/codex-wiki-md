<h1 id="7d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Every $g\in G$ lies in $HgK$, since both [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) contain the identity. If two [double cosets](../../../../../../double-coset.md) intersect, choose

$$
h_1gk_1=h_2g'k_2.
$$

Then $g'=h_2^{-1}h_1gk_1k_2^{-1}\in HgK$, so every element of $Hg'K$ lies in $HgK$. Reversing the argument gives equality. Thus **the double cosets partition the group**.

Unlike single [cosets](../../../../../../coset.md), [double cosets](../../../../../../double-coset.md) can have different cardinalities. For finite $G$, decompose $HgK$ into left [cosets](../../../../../../coset.md) of $K$, or apply the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) to the action of $H$ on $gK$. Its [stabiliser subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is $H\cap gKg^{-1}$, giving

$$
\boxed{|HgK|=\frac{|H||K|}{|H\cap gKg^{-1}|}.}
$$

This formula explains the counterexamples in the following parts.
