<h1 id="8d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The identity belongs to the [center of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md). If $z,w$ commute with every $g$, then $zw$ does too; rearranging $gz=zg$ also shows that $z^{-1}$ commutes with $g$. Hence $Z(G)$ is a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Moreover $gzg^{-1}=z$ for $z\in Z(G)$, so it is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).

Suppose $|G|=p^n>1$. The [conjugation action](../../../../../../conjugation-action.md) partitions $G$ into [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). The [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) gives class size $[G:C_G(x)]$, where $C_G(x)$ is the [centralizer](../../../../../../centralizer.md) of $x$. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), this size is a power of $p$; it is one exactly when $x$ is central. Thus the [class equation](../../../../../../class-equation.md) has the form

$$
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{\text{noncentral classes}}p^{m_i},\qquad m_i\ge1.
$$

It follows that $p\mid |Z(G)|$. Since the identity is central, $|Z(G)|\ge1$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{|Z(G)|\ge p,\qquad Z(G)\ne\{e\}.}
$$

This is the nontrivial-centre property of a [finite p-group](../../../../../../finite-p-group.md).
