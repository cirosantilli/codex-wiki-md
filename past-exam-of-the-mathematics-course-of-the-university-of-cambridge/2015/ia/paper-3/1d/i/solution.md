<h1 id="1d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [two-involution characterization of a dihedral group](../../../../../../two-involution-characterization-of-a-dihedral-group.md) uses the extended convention that includes $C_2$ and the [infinite dihedral group](../../../../../../infinite-dihedral-group.md); the two generators need not be distinct. Put $a=xy$ and $b=y$. The inversion relation gives

$$
a^2=xyxy=x(yxy^{-1})y^2=xx^{-1}=e,\qquad b^2=e,\qquad x=ab.
$$

If $a\ne e$, both $a,b$ are [involutions](../../../../../../involution.md) and generate the original [group](../../../../../../group-split.md). If $a=e$, then $x=y$ and the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is $C_2$; choose the same nonidentity element twice. Thus **two elements of order two generate the group**, including the degenerate case allowed here. The distinction between square equal to the identity and [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) exactly two is essential.
