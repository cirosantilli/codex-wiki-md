<h1 id="7d/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If one [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) contains the other, their union is the larger [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md). Conversely, if neither contains the other, choose $h\in H\setminus K$ and $k\in K\setminus H$. If the union were a [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) it would contain $hk$. If $hk\in H$, then $k=h^{-1}(hk)\in H$, a contradiction; if $hk\in K$, then $h=(hk)k^{-1}\in K$, again a contradiction. Thus

$$
\boxed{H\cup K\text{ is a subgroup}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ H\subseteq K\text{ or }K\subseteq H.}
$$

The [union of two subgroups](../../../../../../../union-of-two-subgroups.md) cannot generally be treated like their generated [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md).
