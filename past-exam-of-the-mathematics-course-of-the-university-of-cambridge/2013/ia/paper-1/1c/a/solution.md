<h1 id="1c/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[De Moivre's theorem](../../../../../../de-moivre-s-theorem.md) states that $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n=\cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta)$ for integer $n$. For $z\ne0$, write its [polar form](../../../../../../polar-form-of-a-complex-number.md) as $z=re^{i\theta}$, where $r=|z|>0$. If $w=se^{i\varphi}$ and $w^n=z$, equality of moduli gives $s=r^{1/n}$ and equality of arguments gives $n\varphi=\theta+2\pi k$. Thus the distinct [roots of a complex number](../../../../../../root-of-a-complex-number.md) are

$$
\boxed{w_k=r^{1/n}\left(\cos\frac{\theta+2\pi k}{n}+i\sin\frac{\theta+2\pi k}{n}\right),\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.}
$$

Changing the chosen argument of $z$ only permutes these roots. For $z=0$ the only root is zero. Taking $r=8$ and $\theta=\pi$ gives the cube roots

$$
\boxed{1+i\sqrt3,\quad-2,\quad1-i\sqrt3.}
$$

Cubing any of them returns $-8$; geometrically they are equally spaced on the circle of radius two.
