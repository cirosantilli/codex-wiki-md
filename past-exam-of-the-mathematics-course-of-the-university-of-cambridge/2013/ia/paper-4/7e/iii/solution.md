<h1 id="7e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the [parabola obstruction to countable line coverings](../../../../../../parabola-obstruction-to-countable-line-coverings.md). The curve

$$
\mathcal P=\{(t,t^2):t\in\mathbb R\}
$$

is in [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) with the real line and hence is an [uncountable set](../../../../../../uncountable-set.md). A vertical line meets $\mathcal P$ in one point. A nonvertical line $y=ax+b$ meets it only where $t^2-at-b=0$, so it meets $\mathcal P$ in at most two points.

If countably many lines covered the plane, their intersections with $\mathcal P$ would cover $\mathcal P$. That would express $\mathcal P$ as a [countable union of countable sets](../../../../../../countable-union-of-countable-sets.md), indeed of sets with at most two elements, contradicting its uncountability. Therefore **no countable collection of lines covers the plane**.
