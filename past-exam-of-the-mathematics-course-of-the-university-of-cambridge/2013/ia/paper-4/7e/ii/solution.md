<h1 id="7e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the [cardinality of integer-periodic function spaces](../../../../../../cardinality-of-integer-periodic-function-spaces.md), fix a positive period $n$ first. A [periodic function](../../../../../../periodic-function.md) $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ with that period is completely and uniquely determined by its $n$ values at $0,\ldots,n-1$, so this class is in [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) with $\mathbb Z^n$. The integers are a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md), and repeated pairing shows that a finite [Cartesian product](../../../../../../cartesian-product.md) of countable sets is countable. Taking the union over positive $n$ and using the [countable union of countable sets](../../../../../../countable-union-of-countable-sets.md) proves that **the integer-domain periodic [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) form a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md)**.

For the rational domain, even period one leaves infinitely many independent choices. Let $q_k=1/(k+2)$ for $k\in\mathbb N_0$; these are distinct members of $\mathbb Q\cap[0,1)$. For every subset $A\subseteq\mathbb N_0$, define

$$
f_A(q)=
\begin{cases}
1,&q-\lfloor q\rfloor=q_k\text{ for some }k\in A,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

The [fractional part](../../../../../../fractional-part.md) is unchanged by adding one, so $f_A$ is an integer-valued [periodic function](../../../../../../periodic-function.md) on $\mathbb Q$. Distinct subsets give distinct [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) because $f_A(q_k)$ records whether $k\in A$. Thus the uncountable [power set](../../../../../../power-set.md) of $\mathbb N_0$ injects into these [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md). **The rational-domain periodic [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) form an [uncountable set](../../../../../../uncountable-set.md).** The difference is that $\mathbb Z$ has only finitely many residue classes modulo a fixed integer period, whereas $\mathbb Q$ has infinitely many.
