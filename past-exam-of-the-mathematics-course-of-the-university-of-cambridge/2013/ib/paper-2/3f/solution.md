↑ **Parent:** [3F](../3f.md)

Both maxima exist by continuity on the [compact set](../../../../../compact-space.md) $[a,b]$. Their sum is nonnegative and vanishes only for $f=0$. The identities $\|cf\|_1=|c|\|f\|_1$ and the [triangle inequality](../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) follow by applying the corresponding [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) facts to $f$ and $f'$. This proves that $\|\cdot\|_1$ is a [norm](../../../../../norm.md).

For $m=(a+b)/2$, $|\Phi(f)|=|f'(m)|\leq\max|f'|\leq\|f\|_1$. Hence **the first unit ball has image contained in $[-1,1]$**: [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) evaluation is a [bounded linear functional](../../../../../continuous-linear-functional.md) for this [norm](../../../../../norm.md).

For the [supremum norm](../../../../../supremum-norm.md) alone, take $f_N(x)=\sin(N(x-m))$. These smooth functions satisfy $\|f_N\|_0\leq1$, but $\Phi(f_N)=N$. Thus **the second image is unbounded**. The distinction is the [unboundedness of derivative evaluation in the supremum norm](../../../../../unboundedness-of-derivative-evaluation-in-the-supremum-norm.md), not a failure of differentiability of the individual functions.
