<h1 id="11g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The annihilating polynomial excludes any free $\mathbb C[X]$-summand. In a cyclic [primary decomposition](../../../../../../primary-decomposition.md) summand $\mathbb C[X]/(P^a)$, the same condition requires $P^a$ to divide $(X-2)^4$. Thus $P=X-2$, up to a scalar unit, and $1\leq a\leq4$. The complex [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) of this summand is $a$.

Write $A_j=\mathbb C[X]/((X-2)^j)$. The exponents must form a [partition of an integer](../../../../../../partition-of-an-integer.md) $4$, giving exactly

$$
\boxed{A_4,\quad A_3\oplus A_1,\quad A_2\oplus A_2,\quad A_2\oplus A_1\oplus A_1,\quad A_1^{\oplus4}.}
$$

They are distinct [module isomorphism](../../../../../../module-isomorphism.md) types by uniqueness in the [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../../../../../../structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md); equivalently they are the five possible [Jordan block](../../../../../../jordan-block.md) patterns for eigenvalue $2$ in dimension four.
