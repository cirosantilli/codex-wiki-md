↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../../../../../../structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) applies because a [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md). For a finitely generated $R$-[module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md),

$$
\boxed{M\cong R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_s),\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_s,}
$$

where the $d_i$ are nonzero nonunits. The free rank $r$ and the [invariant factors of a finitely generated module](../../../../../../invariant-factor-of-a-finitely-generated-module.md) $d_i$ are unique, with each $d_i$ determined up to multiplication by a unit. Equivalently, the [torsion submodule](../../../../../../torsion-submodule.md) decomposes into cyclic [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $R/(P^a)$ for irreducible $P$, with uniquely determined prime-power elementary factors.
