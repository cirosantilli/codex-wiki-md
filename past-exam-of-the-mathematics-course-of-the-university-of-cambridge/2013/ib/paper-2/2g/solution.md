<h1 id="2g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2G](../2g.md)

Let $R$ be a [Euclidean domain](../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) and $I$ a nonzero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md). Choose $d\in I\setminus\{0\}$ minimizing the Euclidean size. For any $a\in I$, [Euclidean division](../../../../../euclidean-division.md) gives $a=qd+r$, where either $r=0$ or $r$ has smaller size than $d$. But $r=a-qd\in I$, so minimality forces $r=0$. Consequently $I=(d)$. The zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is $(0)$, proving that **every [Euclidean domain](../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md)**.

A commutative ring is [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) if every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is finitely generated; equivalently, every ascending chain of [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) stabilizes. To see the equivalence, the union of an ascending chain is an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md). Finitely many generators for that union already belong to one member of the chain, after which the chain is constant. Conversely, an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) which is not finitely generated permits successive choices outside the [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) generated by previous choices, producing a strictly ascending chain.

Every [principal ideal domain](../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) is therefore [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). The given [Euclidean domain](../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) property of the [Gaussian integers](../../../../../gaussian-integer.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z[i]\text{ is Noetherian}.}
$$
