<h1 id="1e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1E](../1e.md)

The [adjugate matrix](../../../../../adjugate-matrix.md) is the transpose of the cofactor [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md): $\operatorname{adj}(A)_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\det A_{\widehat j,\widehat i}$. Cofactor expansion gives the [adjugate identity](../../../../../adjugate-identity.md)

$$
A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\operatorname{adj}(A)A=(\det A)I.
$$

For an invertible [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) this yields $A^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}(A)/\det A$. Integer entries of $A$ imply integer cofactors, so $\det A=\pm1$ makes its inverse integral. Conversely, if $A$ and $A^{-1}$ both have integer entries, their [determinants](../../../../../determinant.md) are integers and $(\det A)(\det A^{-1})=1$. The only integer units are $\pm1$. Hence **an invertible integer [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) has an integer inverse exactly when it is a [unimodular matrix](../../../../../unimodular-matrix.md)**, with [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) $\pm1$. Invertibility is implicit whenever $A^{-1}$ is used.
