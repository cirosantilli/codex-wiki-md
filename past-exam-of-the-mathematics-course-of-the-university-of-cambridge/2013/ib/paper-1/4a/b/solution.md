<h1 id="4a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [Legendre transform](../../../../../../convex-conjugate.md), maximize $px-f(x)$ over $x>0$. Its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $p-1-\log x$, and strict concavity makes the stationary point $x=e^{p-1}$ the unique maximizer. Substitution gives the [convex conjugate of x log x](../../../../../../convex-conjugate-of-x-log-x.md)

$$
\boxed{f^*(p)=e^{p-1},\qquad p\in\mathbb R.}
$$

The range of $f'$ is all of $\mathbb R$, so all real dual arguments have this finite value. Every point of the original graph is supported by its tangent of slope $f'(x)$; the conjugate supremum therefore recovers $f$ from these supporting lines. Equivalently, the inverse [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) relation for this strictly convex differentiable [Legendre transform](../../../../../../convex-conjugate.md) gives **$f^{**}=f$ on $x>0$**. If an extended-real function on all of $\mathbb R$ is wanted, its closed convex extension has value zero at $x=0$ and infinity for $x<0$; biconjugation includes that endpoint closure.
