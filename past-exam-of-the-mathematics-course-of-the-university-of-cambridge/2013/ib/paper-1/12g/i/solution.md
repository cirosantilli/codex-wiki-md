<h1 id="12g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The sphere is the inverse image of $\{1\}$ under the continuous map $u\mapsto\|u\|^2$, so it is closed in $\mathbb R^3$. It is bounded. The [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md) therefore makes it compact. Every [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md): distinct points have disjoint open balls of radii smaller than half their distance. The Euclidean subspace metric supplies that property here. Hence **$S^2$ is compact and Hausdorff**.
