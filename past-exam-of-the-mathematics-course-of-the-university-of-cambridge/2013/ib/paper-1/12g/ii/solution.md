<h1 id="12g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The quotient map $q:S^2\to X$ is continuous and surjective by the [quotient topology](../../../../../../quotient-topology.md), so $X$ is compact as a continuous image of a compact space. To prove the Hausdorff property explicitly, let $[u]\ne[v]$ and set $d=\min(\|u-v\|,\|u+v\|)>0$. Choose $r<d/3$. The saturated open sets

$$
U=(B_r(u)\cup B_r(-u))\cap S^2,\qquad
V=(B_r(v)\cup B_r(-v))\cap S^2
$$

are disjoint by the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md). Each contains both antipodes of every one of its points, so $q^{-1}(qU)=U$ and similarly for $V$. Consequently $qU,qV$ are disjoint open quotient neighborhoods separating the classes. Thus **the quotient is compact and Hausdorff**. It is the [real projective plane](../../../../../../real-projective-plane.md); equivalently the [metric on the antipodal sphere quotient](../../../../../../metric-on-the-antipodal-sphere-quotient.md) is $d_X([u],[v])=\min(\|u-v\|,\|u+v\|)$ and induces this topology.
