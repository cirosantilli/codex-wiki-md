<h1 id="10g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The coordinate axes are exactly the simultaneous eigenspaces of all invertible diagonal [matrices](../../../../../../matrix.md) in $T$. A [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) normalizing $T$ must permute these common eigenspaces: if $tv=\chi(t)v$, then $t(gv)=g(g^{-1}tg)v$ is again a scalar multiple of $gv$. Thus a normalizer element has exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column, a [monomial matrix](../../../../../../monomial-matrix.md). In dimension two it is either diagonal or diagonal times $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Conversely $J$ swaps the diagonal entries and normalizes $T$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{N=T\rtimes\langle J\rangle=T\cup TJ,\qquad N/T\cong C_2.}
$$

This is the [normalizer of a diagonal subgroup of GL2](../../../../../../normalizer-of-a-diagonal-subgroup-of-gl2.md); the quotient records the permutation of the two axes.
