<h1 id="10g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a prime $p>2$, a diagonal [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) with two distinct nonzero [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) exists. The same common-eigenspace argument makes the [normalizer of a diagonal subgroup of GL2](../../../../../../normalizer-of-a-diagonal-subgroup-of-gl2.md) precisely the monomial [matrices](../../../../../../matrix.md). There are $(p-1)^2$ choices of nonzero entries for each of two permutation patterns, giving

$$
\boxed{|N|=2(p-1)^2\quad(p>2).}
$$

At $p=2$, however, the invertible diagonal [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) is trivial. Every invertible [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) normalizes it, so

$$
\boxed{N=GL_2(\mathbb F_2),\qquad |N|=(2^2-1)(2^2-2)=6.}
$$

The first factor chooses a nonzero first column and the second chooses a column outside its one-dimensional span. The exceptional answer is necessary because the printed question permits every prime.
