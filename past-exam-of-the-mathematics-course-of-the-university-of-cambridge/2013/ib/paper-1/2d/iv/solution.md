<h1 id="2d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

At $z=1$, numerator and denominator both have simple zeroes. Their [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) give the removable value

$$
\boxed{\lim_{z\to1}\frac{\sqrt z-1}{\sin\pi z}=-\frac1{2\pi}.}
$$

At each positive integer $n\ge2$, the numerator is nonzero and the denominator has a simple zero, so these are simple [poles](../../../../../../pole.md). At zero the expansion on the principal sheet is

$$
\frac{\sqrt z-1}{\sin\pi z}=-\frac1{\pi z}+\frac1{\pi z^{1/2}}+O(z),
$$

which contains a fractional power. Thus **zero is a [branch point](../../../../../../branch-point.md) with [pole](../../../../../../pole.md)-like leading growth**, not an ordinary isolated [pole](../../../../../../pole.md). The negative real axis is the chosen [branch cut](../../../../../../branch-cut.md). On local continuations across its nonzero points, negative integers also give simple poles because the continued square root is $\pm i\sqrt{|n|}$, never one. They lie outside the open principal branch domain; noninteger negative points are merely cut points, not intrinsic singularities.
