<h1 id="2d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

On the stated branch of the [principal complex logarithm](../../../../../../principal-complex-logarithm.md), the only denominator zero is $z=1$, and $\log z=(z-1)+O((z-1)^2)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{z=1\text{ is a simple pole, with residue }1.}
$$

Zero is a logarithmic [branch point](../../../../../../branch-point.md), not a removable isolated singularity, even though $1/\log z$ tends to zero as $z\to0$ within the cut plane. Continuation around zero changes $\log z$ by $2\pi i$. The nonpositive real axis is the chosen [branch cut](../../../../../../branch-cut.md); its nonzero points are not intrinsically branch points and admit local analytic continuation. There are no other finite singularities inside the specified branch domain.
