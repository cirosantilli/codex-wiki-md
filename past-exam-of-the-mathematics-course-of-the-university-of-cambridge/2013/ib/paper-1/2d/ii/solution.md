<h1 id="2d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $z\ne0$, the denominator zeroes are $z_n=1/[\pi(n+\tfrac12)]$. Its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $\sin(1/z)/z^2$, nonzero at each such point. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{simple poles at }z_n=\frac1{\pi(n+\tfrac12)},\quad n\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

These poles accumulate at zero. Consequently **zero is a [non-isolated singularity](../../../../../../non-isolated-singularity.md)**, as described by [reciprocal cosine with accumulating poles](../../../../../../reciprocal-cosine-with-accumulating-poles.md). It is not an isolated essential singularity: no punctured disc about zero is a domain of holomorphy for this reciprocal function. Although $\cos(1/z)$ itself has an [essential singularity](../../../../../../essential-singularity.md) at zero, taking its reciprocal introduces this accumulation of poles.
