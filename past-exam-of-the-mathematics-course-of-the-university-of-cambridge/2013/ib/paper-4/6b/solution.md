<h1 id="6b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6B](../6b.md)

Applying the [angular momentum operator](../../../../../angular-momentum-operator.md) directly gives

$$
\hat L_z\psi=\hbar xz+i\hbar y f'(x)z.
$$

For an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$, comparison with $\lambda(f(x)+iy)z$ gives $\hbar x=\lambda f(x)$ and $\hbar f'(x)=\lambda$. The first identity excludes $\lambda=0$; differentiating it and using the second gives $\lambda^2=\hbar^2$. No additive constant in $f$ is permitted. **The possibilities are $f(x)=x$, $\lambda=\hbar$, and $f(x)=-x$, $\lambda=-\hbar$.**

For $f=x$, the polynomial $\psi=(x+iy)z$ is homogeneous of degree two and satisfies $\Delta\psi=0$. Expanding the three squared [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) operators gives, with $D=x\partial_x+y\partial_y+z\partial_z$,

$$
\hat L^2=-\hbar^2\left(r^2\Delta-D^2-D\right).
$$

Here $D\psi=2\psi$ and $D^2\psi=4\psi$. Therefore **$\hat L^2\psi=6\hbar^2\psi$**. This is the degree-two case of [angular momentum of a homogeneous harmonic polynomial](../../../../../angular-momentum-of-a-homogeneous-harmonic-polynomial.md); its angular labels are $l=2$, $m=1$.
