<h1 id="13g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $C$ and $D$ be the [connected components](../../../../../../connected-component.md) of $x$ and $y$. First, $C\times D$ is connected in the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md). For each $c\in C$, the connected slice $\{c\}\times D$ meets the connected slice $C\times\{y\}$. Their union over $c$ is connected by the common-intersection argument, and is exactly $C\times D$.

Now let $K$ be the [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md) of $(x,y)$ in $X\times Y$. The coordinate projections are continuous, so their images of $K$ are connected and contain $x$ and $y$. Maximality therefore gives $K\subseteq C\times D$. Conversely, the connected set $C\times D$ contains $(x,y)$, so it lies in $K$. **$K=C\times D$.** This is [connected components of a product space](../../../../../../connected-components-of-a-product-space.md); it requires neither local connectedness nor path connectedness.
