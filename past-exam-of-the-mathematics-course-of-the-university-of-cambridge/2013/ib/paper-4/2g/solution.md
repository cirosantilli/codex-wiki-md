<h1 id="2g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2G](../2g.md)

The [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) of $x$ is its orbit under the [conjugation action](../../../../../conjugation-action.md), and its stabilizer is the [centralizer](../../../../../centralizer.md) $C_G(x)$. The [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) gives

$$
|\operatorname{Cl}_G(x)|=[G:C_G(x)].
$$

By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), the order of the [centralizer](../../../../../centralizer.md) divides $p^n=|G|$, so the index is a power of $p$. A [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) has size one exactly when its element belongs to the [center of a group](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md).

Separate these singleton classes in the [class equation](../../../../../class-equation.md):

$$
p^n=|Z(G)|+\sum_{x\notin Z(G)}[G:C_G(x)],
$$

where the sum takes one representative of each nonsingleton [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). Every summand is divisible by $p$. Thus $p\mid |Z(G)|$, and the identity ensures $|Z(G)|>0$. **Consequently $|Z(G)|\ge p$.** This is the [nontrivial center of a finite p-group](../../../../../nontrivial-center-of-a-finite-p-group.md) property.
