<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

For the [Sheaf of relative Kähler differentials](../../../../../sheaf-of-relative-kahler-differentials.md) on $X=\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}$, the cotangent form of the [Euler sequence](../../../../../euler-sequence.md) is

$$
0\longrightarrow\Omega_{X/\mathbb C}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X(-1)^{\oplus4}
\xrightarrow{(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3)}\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow0.
$$

The last arrow is onto because at least one homogeneous coordinate is invertible on each standard affine chart. Locally its kernel is the rank-three module generated by the differentials of the three affine coordinates, yielding the displayed [Sheaf of relative Kähler differentials](../../../../../sheaf-of-relative-kahler-differentials.md). The [cohomology of twisting sheaves on projective space](../../../../../cohomology-of-twisting-sheaves-on-projective-space.md) gives $H^q(X,\mathcal O_X(-1))=0$ for every $q$, while $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb C$ and its positive-degree groups vanish. The [long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology](../../../../../long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology.md) therefore gives the [cotangent-sheaf cohomology of projective space](../../../../../cotangent-sheaf-cohomology-of-projective-space.md) in this dimension:

$$
\boxed{H^q(X,\Omega_{X/W})\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb C,&q=1,\\0,&q\ne1,\end{cases}
\qquad\chi(X,\Omega_{X/W})=-1.}
$$

The isomorphism for $q=1$ is the connecting map from the constant global sections of $\mathcal O_X$. The [Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf](../../../../../euler-characteristic-of-a-coherent-sheaf.md) is the alternating sum of dimensions, explaining the minus sign.

For the curve, put $S=\mathbb C[t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3]$. It is a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md), so irreducibility of $F$ makes $(F)$ a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md). Also $F$ does not divide $G$: otherwise irreducibility of $G$ would make them associates, contrary to their distinct degrees. Thus the image of $G$ is a nonzero element of the [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md) $S/(F)$. It follows that $(F,G)$ is a [regular sequence](../../../../../regular-sequence.md). Its [Koszul complex](../../../../../koszul-complex.md) is an [exact sequence](../../../../../exact-sequence.md), and graded sheafification gives

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X(-12)
\xrightarrow{(-G,F)}\mathcal O_X(-5)\oplus\mathcal O_X(-7)
\xrightarrow{(F,G)}\mathcal O_X
\longrightarrow i_*\mathcal O_Z\longrightarrow0,
$$

where $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ is the [closed immersion](../../../../../closed-immersion.md). The signs make the composite $F(-G)+GF=0$. The shifts come respectively from $5+7$, $5$, and $7$.

Let $\mathcal I_Z$ be the [ideal sheaf of a closed subscheme](../../../../../ideal-sheaf-of-a-closed-subscheme.md). Split this [Koszul resolution](../../../../../koszul-resolution.md) into

$$
0\to\mathcal O_X(-12)\to\mathcal O_X(-5)\oplus\mathcal O_X(-7)
\to\mathcal I_Z\to0,
\qquad
0\to\mathcal I_Z\to\mathcal O_X\to i_*\mathcal O_Z\to0.
$$

For all integers $d$, $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X(d))=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X(d))=0$. Also $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$. The two [long exact sequences in sheaf cohomology](../../../../../long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology.md), together with [sheaf cohomology under a closed inclusion](../../../../../sheaf-cohomology-under-a-closed-inclusion.md), consequently identify

$$
H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z)
\cong H^2(X,\mathcal I_Z)
\cong\ker\bigl(H^3(X,\mathcal O_X(-12))
\longrightarrow H^3(X,\mathcal O_X(-5))\oplus H^3(X,\mathcal O_X(-7))\bigr).
$$

By [Serre duality](../../../../../serre-duality.md), $H^3(X,\mathcal O_X(-m))\cong S_{m-4}^*$ for $m\geq4$. Counting degree-$d$ monomials in four variables gives $\dim S_d=\binom{d+3}{3}$. Thus the source has dimension $\binom{11}{3}=165$, and the two target spaces have dimensions $\binom{4}{3}=4$ and $\binom{6}{3}=20$. The [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md) proves the requested bound:

$$
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z)\geq165-4-20=141.}
$$

In fact equality holds. Under the [Serre duality](../../../../../serre-duality.md) pairings, the dual of this map is

$$
S_1\oplus S_3\longrightarrow S_8,\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto-Ga+Fb.
$$

If $Ga=Fb$, primeness of $F$ and $F\nmid G$ imply $F\mid a$. But $a$ has degree one and $F$ has degree five, forcing $a=0$, and then $b=0$. The dual map is injective, so the original map is surjective and its kernel has dimension $141$. **Thus the lower bound is attained for every pair allowed in the question.** No smoothness assumption is needed. The [regular sequence](../../../../../regular-sequence.md) cuts out a [projective complete intersection](../../../../../projective-complete-intersection.md) of dimension one. Also $H^0(X,\mathcal I_Z)=H^1(X,\mathcal I_Z)=0$, by the negative twists and the intermediate cohomology vanishing in the first short exact sequence, so the second gives $H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z)=\mathbb C$. This is the [genus of a complete-intersection space curve](../../../../../genus-of-a-complete-intersection-space-curve.md): the first cohomology has dimension $141$, equal to its [arithmetic genus](../../../../../arithmetic-genus.md).
