<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

It is important to distinguish loss of forward-going [surface-gravity-wave energy](../../../../../../surface-gravity-wave-energy.md) from conversion of mechanical [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) into [heat](../../../../../../heat.md). [Scattering attenuation by ice floes](../../../../../../scattering-attenuation-by-ice-floes.md) redirects energy; it can attenuate a coherent transmitted wave without dissipating the total energy.

For fixed floe geometry, increasing frequency usually increases attenuation over the relevant swell range: shorter [wavelengths](../../../../../../wavelength.md) respond more strongly to the contrast between water and the [elastic plate](../../../../../../elastic-plate.md), and to repeated floe edges. Long [surface gravity waves](../../../../../../surface-gravity-wave.md) have weak curvature and often penetrate much farther. This is a trend over a specified frequency range, not a theorem excluding resonances.

The diameter dependence is governed by $d/\lambda$. An [ice floe](../../../../../../ice-floe.md) much smaller than the [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) moves nearly with the water and scatters weakly. Scattering becomes appreciable when floe size is comparable with the [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md), and interference between its two edges can give maxima and minima. At fixed ice concentration, larger [ice floes](../../../../../../ice-floe.md) also mean fewer edges per unit propagation distance, roughly proportional to $p/d$. Consequently the attenuation coefficient need not increase monotonically with diameter: the single-floe reflection and the number of encounters must both be considered. Thickness increases areal inertia as $\rho_i h$ and [bending stiffness](../../../../../../bending-stiffness.md) as $h^3$, generally increasing wave mismatch and reflection, although detailed frequency-dependent resonances again prevent a universal monotonic law.

When $d\ll\lambda$, particularly for [frazil ice](../../../../../../frazil-ice.md) and [pancake ice](../../../../../../pancake-ice.md), weak individual scattering leaves other processes dominant. Relative crystal and water motion causes [viscous dissipation](../../../../../../viscous-dissipation.md); an aggregate layer can behave as a viscous or viscoelastic material, and [pancake ice](../../../../../../pancake-ice.md) collisions, rubbing and overwash remove energy. Their importance depends on concentration and wave amplitude.

For a uniform continuous sheet with horizontal dimensions much greater than the [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md), there are no repeated floe edges in its interior. A perfectly elastic sheet over inviscid water supports undamped [flexural-gravity waves](../../../../../../flexural-gravity-wave.md), so internal scattering is not an explanation of decay there. Real attenuation can instead arise from internal ice anelasticity or [viscoelasticity](../../../../../../viscoelasticity.md), a dissipative sub-ice [viscous boundary layer](../../../../../../viscous-boundary-layer.md), turbulence, cracks and brine-related processes. **Small-floe mixtures and continuous sheets require dissipation models beyond the isolated-floe scattering picture.**
