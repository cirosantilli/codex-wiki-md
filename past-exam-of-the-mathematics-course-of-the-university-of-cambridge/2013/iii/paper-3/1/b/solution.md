<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $H\leq G$, induction gives $H^{(i)}\leq G^{(i)}$, because commutators of elements of a subgroup are also commutators in the larger group. Thus termination of the [derived series](../../../../../../derived-series.md) of $G$ forces termination for $H$.

For a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N$, the quotient map sends $[x,y]$ to the commutator of their images. Surjectivity then gives

$$
(G/N)^{(i)}=G^{(i)}N/N.
$$

Consequently **subgroups and [quotient groups](../../../../../../quotient-group.md) of a soluble group are soluble**. The assertion about a quotient uses a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md); it is not a quotient by an arbitrary subgroup.
