[Iwasawa's simplicity lemma](../../../../../../iwasawa-simplicity-lemma.md) states the following. Suppose $G$ acts faithfully and primitively on a set, and a [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) $H$ has an abelian [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $A$ whose $G$-conjugates generate $G$. Then every nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N$ contains $G'$. In particular, **if $G$ is nontrivial and perfect, then $G$ is simple**.

Indeed a nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) in a faithful primitive action is transitive, so $G=NH$. Since $H$ normalizes $A$, all conjugates of $A$ have the same image in $G/N$. Those images generate the quotient, which is therefore abelian. This gives $G'\leq N$ and proves the stated conclusion.
