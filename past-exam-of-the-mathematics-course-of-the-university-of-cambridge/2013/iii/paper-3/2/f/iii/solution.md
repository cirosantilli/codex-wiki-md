<h1 id="2/f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the projective-line realization from part (ii). The subgroup $K$ of translations is abelian and normal in the [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) $G$ of infinity. We verify both remaining conditions of [Iwasawa's simplicity lemma](../../../../../../../iwasawa-simplicity-lemma.md), instead of concluding simplicity from transitivity alone.

Let $R$ be generated by all conjugates of $K$ in $G^+$. It contains the matrices

$$
T_t=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U_t=xT_tx=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\t&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Here matrices act by fractional linear transformations. For $t\ne0$,

$$
w(t)=T_tU_{t^{-1}}T_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\t^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

In particular $w(1)=x\in R$, and $w(t)w(1)=\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})$ acts as multiplication by $t^2$. Squaring is a bijection of $F^*$, so every $M_s$ belongs to $R$. Hence $R$ contains $G$ and $x$, and $\boxed{R=G^+}$.

Choose $s\ne1$. The commutator, with convention $[u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$, is

$$
[M_s,T_t]=T_{(s-1)t}.
$$

As $t$ varies, this gives all translations. Thus $(G^+)'$ contains $K$, and normality makes it contain every conjugate of $K$. Since those generate $G^+$, the group is a [perfect group](../../../../../../../perfect-group.md).

A sharply three-transitive action on nine points is a [primitive group action](../../../../../../../primitive-group-action.md), and this permutation action is a [faithful group action](../../../../../../../faithful-group-action.md). All Iwasawa hypotheses now hold, so

$$
\boxed{G^+\text{ is simple, of order }504.}
$$

It may be identified with $\operatorname{PSL}_2(8)=\operatorname{PGL}_2(8)$: the generators are fractional linear transformations and $|\operatorname{PGL}_2(8)|=8(8^2-1)=504$, while all nonzero field elements are squares. The simplicity proof above does not rely on assuming simplicity of that named family.
