<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If either vector vanishes, both arguments are zero. Otherwise choose a [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) $R$ sending $x/|x|$ to $y/|y|$. The [surface measure on a sphere](../../../../../../surface-measure-on-a-sphere.md) is rotation invariant. Changing variables $\sigma=R\omega$ gives

$$
\int_{\mathbb S^2}\phi(|x|y\cdot\sigma)\,d\sigma
=\int_{\mathbb S^2}\phi(|x||y|R(x/|x|)\cdot R\omega)\,d\omega
=\int_{\mathbb S^2}\phi(|y|x\cdot\omega)\,d\omega.
$$

Thus the two spherical [integrals](../../../../../../integral.md) are equal. The property extends to complex continuous $\phi$ by treating real and imaginary parts separately, as needed for the Fourier exponential.
