<h4 id="1/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/i/a)

**An [inner model](../../../set-theory.md#inner-model) of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) is a transitive class containing every [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) and satisfying all its axioms.** Write this class as $A$ with its inherited membership relation. Satisfaction is interpreted by restricting all quantifiers to $A$. In a first-order formulation this is a schema for a definable class, possibly with fixed parameters. The class may equal the whole universe. Transitivity means $x\in y\in A\Rightarrow x\in A$; containing all [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) rules out treating an arbitrary [transitive set](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-set) model as an [inner model](../../../set-theory.md#inner-model).

<h4 id="1/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/i/b)

A [strongly inaccessible cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#strongly-inaccessible-cardinal) is an uncountable [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\lambda$ that is both regular and a strong limit:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)=\lambda,\qquad
2^\mu<\lambda\text{ for every cardinal }\mu<\lambda.}
$$

Regularity excludes expressing $\lambda$ as the supremum of a shorter increasing sequence, while the strong-limit requirement concerns all smaller [power sets](../../../set.md#power-set). Merely being an uncountable regular limit [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) is the weaker notion of a [weakly inaccessible cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#weakly-inaccessible-cardinal).

<h4 id="1/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

<h5 id="1/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/i/c)

A [first-order formula](../../../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) $\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is an [absolute formula](../../../set-theory.md#absolute-formula) for membership structures $A$ and $B$ if, for every tuple $\vec a\in(A\cap B)^n$,

$$
\boxed{(A,\in)\models\varphi(\vec a)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(B,\in)\models\varphi(\vec a).}
$$

The same elements interpret the free variables in both structures; bound variables range over their respective domains. Thus absoluteness requires both directions, rather than only preservation of truth from the smaller structure to the larger one.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

<h5 id="1/ii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/ii/a)

Use a definable cumulative hierarchy of set-sized stages: $A_\alpha\subseteq A_\beta$ for $\alpha<\beta$, $A_\eta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\eta}A_\alpha$ at nonzero limits, and $A=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}A_\alpha$. Class parameters and the hierarchy are fixed definable data. The [reflection theorem for definable hierarchies](../../../set-theory.md#reflection-theorem-for-definable-hierarchies) says that for every finite collection $\Phi$ of formulas there is a [closed unbounded class of ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#closed-unbounded-class-of-ordinals) $C$ such that

$$
\boxed{\alpha\in C\ \Longrightarrow\
\bigl[A_\alpha\models\varphi(\vec a)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A\models\varphi(\vec a)\bigr]
\quad(\varphi\in\Phi,\ \vec a\in A_\alpha).}
$$

This is a schema of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) for each finite collection and class definition, not a purported truth predicate for all formulas over the universe at once.

Close $\Phi$ under subformulas. For each existential subformula $\exists y\,\psi(y,\vec x)$ and each tuple in $A_\alpha$, if a witness exists in $A$, take the least stage index containing a witness. There are only set-many parameter tuples and finitely many formulas. The [Axiom schema of replacement](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-schema-of-replacement) therefore bounds all these least indices by an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $h(\alpha)$, chosen larger than $\alpha$. No definable selection of the witnesses themselves is needed.

Above any prescribed bound choose $\alpha_0<\alpha_1<\cdots$ with $\alpha_{m+1}>h(\alpha_m)$, and put $\eta=\sup_m\alpha_m$. Every tuple in $A_\eta$ lies in some $A_{\alpha_m}$, and each true existential instance for that tuple has a witness in $A_{\alpha_{m+1}}\subseteq A_\eta$. Induction over the subformulas now proves agreement between $A_\eta$ and $A$: atomic formulas use the same membership relation, Boolean operations preserve agreement, and the existential step uses this witness property. Thus reflecting stages are unbounded.

For closedness, suppose reflecting stages have limit $\eta$. Every tuple in $A_\eta$ lies in a reflecting stage below $\eta$, and every true existential instance has a witness there. The same subformula induction proves reflection at $\eta$. Hence the reflecting stages for the subformula-closed collection form a closed unbounded class, proving the [Lévy reflection theorem](../../../set-theory.md#levy-reflection-theorem) in this relative form.

<h4 id="1/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

<h5 id="1/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/ii/b)

**The printed inclusion-and-elementarity assertion is false if transitivity is required of the same submodel.** Take the theorem of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) that combines the [axiom of infinity](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-infinity) and the [Axiom of power set](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-power-set). Whenever $V_\delta$ satisfies this sentence, it contains $\omega$, every [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega$, and their actual [power set](../../../set.md#power-set) $\mathcal P(\omega)$. A submodel $M\prec V_\delta$ contains $\omega$ and $\mathcal P(\omega)$, since these are uniquely definable in $V_\delta$. If $M$ were transitive, it would contain every element of $\mathcal P(\omega)$, contradicting countability by [Cantor theorem](../../../set.md#cantor-s-theorem).

The corrected conclusion uses an [elementary embedding](../../../set-theory.md#elementary-embedding) rather than elementary inclusion. By [Lévy reflection theorem](../../../set-theory.md#levy-reflection-theorem), choose $\delta>\omega+2$ with $V_\delta\models\varphi$ and with Extensionality true there. The [Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../mathematical-logic.md#downward-lowenheim-skolem-theorem) says that an infinite structure in a countable language has a countable [elementary substructure](../../../mathematical-logic.md#elementary-substructure). Apply it to obtain a countable $N\prec(V_\delta,\in)$. The membership relation on $N$ is externally well-founded, and elementarity makes it extensional. The [Mostowski collapse theorem](../../../set-theory.md#mostowski-collapse-theorem) gives an isomorphism $\pi:N\to M$ onto a countable [transitive set](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-set). Hence

$$
\boxed{M\models\varphi,\qquad j=\pi^{-1}:M\longrightarrow V_\delta
\text{ is elementary}.}
$$

Indeed $M\subseteq V_\delta$: rank induction gives $\operatorname{rank}(\pi(x))\le\operatorname{rank}(x)$ for every $x\in N$. The crucial correction is that **$j$, rather than the inclusion of $M$, is elementary**. For a formula with free variables, apply this argument to its universal closure.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

Let $\theta=\xi^*$, and work with the set structure $V_\theta\models\mathrm{ZFC}$. Its [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) height is an infinite limit: a [transitive model](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-model) of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) has no largest [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), since it can take the successor of each [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) it contains. Thus $\operatorname{cf}(\theta)\ge\omega$.

Suppose instead that $\operatorname{cf}(\theta)>\omega$. Enumerate all [first-order formulas](../../../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula), and close each finite initial collection under subformulas. Applying [Lévy reflection theorem](../../../set-theory.md#levy-reflection-theorem) inside $V_\theta$, choose a strictly increasing sequence $\beta_m<\theta$ such that $V_{\beta_m}$ agrees with $V_\theta$ on the first $m$ collections. Take $\eta=\sup_m\beta_m<\theta$. The internal rank levels here are the actual rank levels, because $V_\theta$ is a transitive rank model.

If $V_\theta\models\exists x\,\psi(x,\vec a)$ with $\vec a\in V_\eta$, choose $m$ large enough to include this formula and all the parameters in $V_{\beta_m}$. Reflection supplies a witness in $V_{\beta_m}\subseteq V_\eta$. The [Tarski-Vaught test](../../../mathematical-logic.md#tarski-vaught-test) therefore gives $V_\eta\prec V_\theta$. In particular $V_\eta$ satisfies all of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice), contrary to the minimality of $\theta$.

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\xi^*)=\omega.}
$$

The countable enumeration is of formulas, not merely axioms: witness closure is what makes the union a model of the entire theory.

<h3 id="1/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/iv)

The structure $H_\kappa$ of [hereditarily small sets](../../../set-theory.md#hereditarily-small-set) is transitive. If $\kappa\le\omega$, all its sets are hereditarily finite, so it cannot satisfy Infinity. Therefore the assumed model has $\kappa>\omega$.

Let $\mu<\kappa$ be a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). The [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\mu$ belongs to $H_\kappa$, and every actual [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\mu$ also belongs to $H_\kappa$: its [transitive closure](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-closure) has size at most $\max(\mu,\omega)<\kappa$. The [Power set](../../../set.md#power-set) axiom inside $H_\kappa$ therefore produces the actual $\mathcal P(\mu)$, since all [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) relevant to the internal definition are present. This [power set](../../../set.md#power-set) itself belongs to $H_\kappa$, so

$$
2^\mu=|\mathcal P(\mu)|<\kappa.
$$

Thus $\kappa$ is a strong limit. Its regularity was assumed, and we have proved uncountability. Hence **$\kappa$ is strongly inaccessible**.

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

<h5 id="2/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/i/a)

The [axiom of constructibility](../../../definable-power-set.md#axiom-of-constructibility) asserts **every set belongs to the constructible universe**, or $V=L$. Define $L_0=\varnothing$, $L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha)$, and $L_\eta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\eta}L_\alpha$ at limit stages. Here $\operatorname{Def}(X)$ consists of [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of $X$ first-order definable over $(X,\in)$ with finitely many parameters from $X$. Then $L=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}L_\alpha$. The assertion is $\forall x\,\exists\alpha\,(x\in L_\alpha)$, rather than a statement that every set is parameter-free definable.

<h4 id="2/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

<h5 id="2/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/i/b)

The [Bukovský-Hechler theorem](../../../set-theory.md#bukovsky-hechler-theorem) states: if $\kappa$ is singular and there are a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\rho<\kappa$ and a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\tau$ such that $2^\mu=\tau$ for every [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\mu$ with $\rho\le\mu<\kappa$, then

$$
\boxed{2^\kappa=\tau.}
$$

Thus an eventual plateau of the power-set [function](../../../function.md) below a [singular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#singular-cardinal) continues at that [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). One can see the mechanism by decomposing $\kappa$ into $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$ bounded pieces: $2^\kappa=(2^{<\kappa})^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}$. On the plateau choose $\mu\ge\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$, so $\tau^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=(2^\mu)^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=2^\mu=\tau$.

<h4 id="2/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

<h5 id="2/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/i/c)

The [singular cardinals hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#singular-cardinals-hypothesis) is the assertion that for every infinite [singular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#singular-cardinal) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}<\kappa
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}=\kappa^+.}
$$

In particular, for a singular strong-limit [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) it gives $2^\kappa=\kappa^+$. This is a restriction on singular-cardinal exponentiation; it does not assert the full [Generalized continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-continuum-hypothesis) at all [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number).

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

Put $\theta=\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$. There is a strictly increasing cofinal map $a:\theta\to\delta$. If $\operatorname{cf}(\theta)=\nu<\theta$, choose a cofinal map $b:\nu\to\theta$. Then $a\circ b$ is cofinal in $\delta$, contradicting the definition of $\theta$ as its least cofinal [order type](../../../set-theory.md#order-type). Since always $\operatorname{cf}(\theta)\le\theta$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cf}(\operatorname{cf}(\delta))=\operatorname{cf}(\delta).}
$$

For a nonzero [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal) this is an infinite [regular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#regular-cardinal). If the convention includes zero among [limit ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal), its [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) is zero and the identity is immediate there.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

First the increasing sequence is cofinal in $\kappa$. Write $\mu=\sup_{\xi<\delta}\kappa_\xi$. Strict increase gives $|\delta|\le\mu$ when the sequence is infinite, so its [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) sum is $\max(|\delta|,\mu)=\mu$. The given sum therefore implies $\mu=\kappa$ and $|\delta|\le\kappa$.

For any [function](../../../function.md) $f:\lambda\to\kappa$, choose an index $\xi_t$ with $f(t)<\kappa_{\xi_t}$ for every $t<\lambda$. There are at most $\lambda<\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$ such indices, so they are bounded below $\delta$. Enlarging the bound if needed gives an index $\xi$ with $\operatorname{ran}(f)\subseteq\kappa_\xi$. Thus

$$
{}^\lambda\kappa=\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}{}^\lambda\kappa_\xi,
\qquad \kappa^\lambda\le\sum_{\xi<\delta}\kappa_\xi^\lambda.
$$

Conversely each summand is at most $\kappa^\lambda$, and $|\delta|\le\kappa\le\kappa^\lambda$ because $\lambda\ne0$. Infinite [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) multiplication consequently gives

$$
\sum_{\xi<\delta}\kappa_\xi^\lambda
\le|\delta|\cdot\kappa^\lambda=\kappa^\lambda.
$$

Combining the inequalities proves **$\kappa^\lambda=\sum_{\xi<\delta}\kappa_\xi^\lambda$**.

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

<h5 id="2/iv/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/iv/a)

For the intended infinite [regular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#regular-cardinal) $\kappa$, set $S_0(\delta)=0$. Suppose $0<\zeta<\kappa^+$ and the [functions](../../../function.md) for its predecessors have been constructed. Since $|\zeta|\le\kappa$, choose a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $e_\zeta:\kappa\to\zeta$ and define

$$
\boxed{S_\zeta(\delta)=\sup_{\eta<\delta}\bigl(S_{e_\zeta(\eta)}(\delta)+1\bigr).}
$$

The supremum is below $\kappa$: it uses fewer than $\kappa$ [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\kappa$, and $\kappa$ is regular. This defines a [function](../../../function.md) $\kappa\to\kappa$ at each stage of the recursion.

For any $\alpha<\zeta$, choose $\eta$ with $e_\zeta(\eta)=\alpha$. Whenever $\delta>\eta$, the displayed supremum includes $S_\alpha(\delta)+1$, so $S_\alpha(\delta)<S_\zeta(\delta)$. Therefore the recursion yields a [long chain under eventual domination](../../../set-theory.md#long-chain-under-eventual-domination) of length $\kappa^+$.

<h4 id="2/iv/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

<h5 id="2/iv/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/iv/b)

Take the graphs $G_\zeta=\{(\delta,S_\zeta(\delta)):\delta<\kappa\}$. Each has size $\kappa$, and any two meet in fewer than $\kappa$ points, because their [functions](../../../function.md) are eventually strictly ordered. Fix a [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection) $b:\kappa\times\kappa\to\kappa$ and let $A_\zeta=b[G_\zeta]\in[\kappa]^\kappa$.

Given $I\in[\kappa^+]^\kappa$, enumerate it as $\{\zeta_\eta:\eta<\kappa\}$ and trim the sets in this order:

$$
A_{\zeta_\eta}^*=A_{\zeta_\eta}\setminus\bigcup_{\nu<\eta}A_{\zeta_\nu}.
$$

The deleted part is a union of fewer than $\kappa$ pairwise intersections, each of size less than $\kappa$. Regularity ensures its size is less than $\kappa$. If $\nu<\eta$, then $A_{\zeta_\eta}^*$ is disjoint even from the full earlier $A_{\zeta_\nu}$, hence from its trimmed version. Thus all the retained sets are pairwise disjoint and each lost fewer than $\kappa$ elements. This proves

$$
\boxed{*(\kappa,\kappa).}
$$

The argument is the [essential disjointness of small subfamilies](../../../set-theory.md#essential-disjointness-of-small-subfamilies) of a regular-cardinal almost disjoint family.

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

<h5 id="3/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/i/a)

For a nonzero [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal) $\delta$, a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $C\subseteq\delta$ is a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) if it is unbounded in $\delta$ and contains every one of its limit points below $\delta$. A [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $S\subseteq\delta$ is stationary if

$$
\boxed{S\cap C\ne\varnothing\quad\text{for every club }C\subseteq\delta.}
$$

This defines [stationarity in a limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#stationarity-in-a-limit-ordinal). The regular uncountable case is the usual [stationary set](../../../set-theory.md#stationary-set) setting; results such as [Fodor lemma](../../../set-theory.md#fodor-lemma) require that additional hypothesis, rather than an arbitrary [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal).

<h4 id="3/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

<h5 id="3/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/i/b)

A [set-theoretic tree](../../../set.md#set-theoretic-tree) is a partial order for which the strict predecessors of every node are well-ordered by the tree order. The height of a node is that predecessor [order type](../../../set-theory.md#order-type), and $T_\alpha$ is the set of nodes of height $\alpha$. Under the ordinary height-and-width definition, a [kappa-tree](../../../set.md#kappa-tree) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}(T)=\kappa,\qquad 0<|T_\alpha|<\kappa\quad(\alpha<\kappa).}
$$

For an arbitrary [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) in this definition one must distinguish it from additional conventions such as being well-pruned, normal or splitting. Splitting means that every node has two incompatible extensions; it is not implied by the height-and-width clauses. This distinction is material in Question 5(ii)(a).

<h4 id="3/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

<h5 id="3/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/i/c)

**The [club principle](../../../set-theory.md#club-principle) predicts a cofinal countable ladder contained in every uncountable subset of $\omega_1$.** More precisely, $\clubsuit$ asserts that there is a sequence $\langle A_\delta:\delta\in\lim(\omega_1)\rangle$ such that each $A_\delta$ is cofinal in $\delta$ with [order type](../../../set-theory.md#order-type) $\omega$, and every uncountable $X\subseteq\omega_1$ contains at least one entire $A_\delta$. It predicts a countable ladder contained in $X$, rather than predicting $X\cap\delta$ exactly. The latter is the stronger kind of guessing in the [diamond principle](../../../set-theory.md#diamond-principle).

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/a">a</h4>

For every $\beta<\omega_1$, choose an [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $e_\beta:\beta\to\omega$. Define the [Ulam matrix on omega-one](../../../set-theory.md#ulam-matrix-on-omega-one) by

$$
\boxed{A_{\alpha,n}=\{\beta<\omega_1:\alpha<\beta\text{ and }e_\beta(\alpha)=n\}.}
$$

For a fixed $\alpha$, every $\beta>\alpha$ belongs to exactly one of these sets, so their union is the entire tail $(\alpha,\omega_1)$. Its complement is the countable [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha+1$. For distinct $\alpha,\alpha'$ and fixed $n$, membership in both sets would give $e_\beta(\alpha)=e_\beta(\alpha')=n$ for some $\beta$ above both, contradicting injectivity. This verifies both requested properties.

<h4 id="3/ii/b">b</h4>

<h5 id="3/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

To prove unboundedness, begin above any prescribed [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) with $\gamma_0<\lambda$ and choose a strictly increasing sequence so that

$$
\gamma_{m+1}>\sup\bigcup_{\alpha<\gamma_m}B(\alpha).
$$

This upper bound is below $\lambda$: there are fewer than $\lambda$ countable sets in the union, and $\lambda$ is regular and uncountable. Put $\delta=\sup_m\gamma_m<\lambda$. For any $\alpha<\delta$, choose $m$ with $\alpha<\gamma_m$; then $B(\alpha)\subseteq\gamma_{m+1}\subseteq\delta$. Thus $\delta\in E$.

For closedness, suppose $\delta<\lambda$ is a limit point of $E$. Given $\alpha<\delta$, choose $\eta\in E$ with $\alpha<\eta<\delta$. Then $B(\alpha)\subseteq\eta\subseteq\delta$. Therefore $\delta\in E$ as well. Hence **$E$ is a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) in $\lambda$**. This is the [club of closure points for countable set-valued functions](../../../set-theory.md#club-of-closure-points-for-countable-set-valued-functions).

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Write $\Diamond'_S$ for the countable-family assertion in the PDF; its prime is not a superscript $c$. A single [stationary diamond at a regular cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#stationary-diamond-at-a-regular-cardinal) sequence gives such families by taking singletons, so $\Diamond_S\Rightarrow\Diamond'_S$.

Conversely enumerate each countable family as $E_\alpha(n)$, $n<\omega$, padding finite families and allowing the empty set as a default. Fix a [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection) $\pi:\lambda\times\omega\to\lambda$. The previous part applied to $B(\xi)=\{\pi(\xi,n):n<\omega\}$ gives a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) $D$ on which $\pi[\alpha\times\omega]\subseteq\alpha$. Define, for each $n$, a single candidate sequence

$$
D^n_\alpha=\{\xi<\alpha:\pi(\xi,n)\in E_\alpha(n)\}\qquad(\alpha\in S).
$$

Suppose no candidate sequence witnesses $\Diamond_S$. For each $n$ choose $X_n\subseteq\lambda$ and a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) $C_n$ such that $D^n_\alpha\ne X_n\cap\alpha$ for every $\alpha\in S\cap C_n$. Code all the counterexamples into

$$
X=\{\pi(\xi,n):n<\omega,\ \xi\in X_n\}.
$$

The countable-family hypothesis guesses $X$ on a stationary set. Choose a guessing $\alpha$ in the [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) $D\cap\bigcap_n C_n$, and choose $n$ with $E_\alpha(n)=X\cap\alpha$. For every $\xi<\alpha$, closure under $\pi$ gives

$$
\xi\in D^n_\alpha\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \pi(\xi,n)\in X
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \xi\in X_n.
$$

Thus $D^n_\alpha=X_n\cap\alpha$, contradicting $\alpha\in C_n$. At least one candidate is a diamond sequence, proving

$$
\boxed{\Diamond'_S\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Diamond_S.}
$$

This is the [countable-family diamond equivalence](../../../set-theory.md#countable-family-diamond-equivalence), for every regular uncountable $\lambda$ and stationary $S\subseteq\lambda$.

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

**There is a [cofinal branch](../../../set.md#cofinal-branch).** We give a proof that does not require distinct limit-level nodes to have different predecessor chains.

The set $S=\{\alpha<\lambda:\operatorname{cf}(\alpha)=\kappa\}$ is stationary. Indeed a strictly increasing continuous $\kappa$-sequence in any [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) has supremum in that [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set), below $\lambda$, of [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\kappa$. At each $\alpha\in S$, there are fewer than $\kappa$ pairs of nodes on $T_\alpha$. For every pair whose predecessor chains below $\alpha$ differ, choose a height where they differ. Since $\operatorname{cf}(\alpha)=\kappa$, all these heights are bounded by some $\beta_\alpha<\alpha$. Consequently nodes in $T_\alpha$ having the same predecessor at $\beta_\alpha$ have identical predecessor chains below $\alpha$.

The [Fodor lemma](../../../set-theory.md#fodor-lemma) states that a regressive [function](../../../function.md) on a stationary [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of a regular uncountable [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) is constant on a stationary [subset](../../../set.md#subset). Applying it here, $\beta_\alpha$ has a constant value $\beta$ on a stationary [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $S'$. Choose $t_\alpha\in T_\alpha$ for each $\alpha\in S'$. The level $T_\beta$ has fewer than $\kappa<\lambda$ nodes. Partitioning $S'$ according to the predecessor of $t_\alpha$ at height $\beta$, one fiber $S''$ is stationary, since the union of fewer than $\lambda$ nonstationary sets is nonstationary. Let its common predecessor be $t$.

For $\alpha<\alpha'$ in $S''$, the predecessor of $t_{\alpha'}$ at level $\alpha$ and $t_\alpha$ have the same predecessor $t$ at level $\beta$. Their chains below $\alpha$ therefore agree. For each $\nu<\lambda$, choose $\alpha\in S''$ above $\nu$ and let $b_\nu$ be the predecessor of $t_\alpha$ at level $\nu$. The preceding comparison makes $b_\nu$ independent of that choice. The nodes $b_\nu$ form a chain through every level:

$$
\boxed{\{b_\nu:\nu<\lambda\}\text{ is a cofinal branch of }T.}
$$

This proves the [uniformly narrow regular-height tree branch theorem](../../../set.md#uniformly-narrow-regular-height-tree-branch-theorem). The uniform bound below the smaller regular $\kappa$ is stronger than merely bounding each level below $\lambda$.

A [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name) for $\mathbb P$ is a set of ordered pairs $(\sigma,p)$ where $p\in P$ and $\sigma$ is itself a [forcing name](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name). <h4 id="4/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

<h5 id="4/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/i/b)

With this paper's order convention, larger conditions are stronger. A [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter) $G\subseteq P$ over $M$ is nonempty, closed toward weaker conditions, and directed toward stronger conditions: if $p,q\in G$, some $r\in G$ satisfies $r\ge p,q$. **It also meets every $D\in M$ which $M$ regards as dense in $\mathbb P$.**

For a countable transitive $M$, such a filter containing any prescribed condition can be built by enumerating its dense sets and successively choosing stronger conditions in them. The definition does not require $G\notin M$: for an atomic [forcing](../../../forcing.md) a [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter) may already belong to $M$.

A [subset](../../../set.md#subset) $E\subseteq P$ is [dense above a forcing condition](../../../forcing.md#dense-above-a-forcing-condition) $p$ if

\boxed{\forall q\ge p\ \exists r\ge q\ (r\in E).}

All displayed strengthenings use the PDF convention. This only requires density in the cone of extensions of $p$; it need not be dense throughout $P$, nor downward or upward closed.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/a">a</h4>

<h5 id="4/ii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/ii/a)

The [generic extension](../../../forcing.md#generic-extension) is $M[G]=\{\tau^G:\tau\in M\text{ is a forcing name}\}$. It is transitive: if $x\in\tau^G$, an active pair in $\tau$ supplies a subname $\sigma\in M$ with $x=\sigma^G$. Ground sets belong to it by their canonical names. Without assuming a weakest condition, use $\check a=\{(\check b,p):b\in a,\ p\in P\}$; nonemptiness of $G$ gives $\check a^G=a$.

Induction on [forcing name rank](../../../forcing.md#forcing-name-rank) gives $\operatorname{rank}(\tau^G)\le\operatorname{nrk}(\tau)$. The right side is an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) of $M$. If $\alpha\in M[G]$ is an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), its rank equals $\alpha$, so it is at most a ground-model [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). Transitivity of $M$ then implies $\alpha\in M$. Conversely every ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) remains the same [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) in the transitive extension, since membership is unchanged. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Ord}\cap M[G]=\mathrm{Ord}\cap M.}
$$

This proves [forcing preserves ordinals](../../../forcing.md#forcing-preserves-ordinals) directly from ranks, without assuming [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) preservation.

We use only the definability and truth clauses of the [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem). For every formula, its [forcing](../../../forcing.md) relation on names is definable inside $M$; [forcing](../../../forcing.md) is preserved by strengthening; and $M[G]\models\varphi(\vec\tau^G)$ exactly when some condition in $G$ forces $\varphi(\vec\tau)$. These clauses do not presuppose the Separation axiom we are proving.

Let $a=\tau^G$ and let $\vec\sigma^G$ be the parameters in the desired instance. In $M$, form the name

$$
\rho=\{(\nu,q):\exists p\,[(\nu,p)\in\tau\ \land\ q\ge p
\ \land\ q\Vdash\varphi(\nu,\vec\sigma)]\}.
$$

This is a set in $M$ by its Separation axiom and [forcing](../../../forcing.md) definability, using the subnames of $\tau$ and $P$ as a set-sized bound.

If $x\in\rho^G$, some $q\in G$ activates $(\nu,q)$ and $q\ge p$ activates $(\nu,p)\in\tau$. Thus $x=\nu^G\in a$, and the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) theorem gives $\varphi(x,\vec\sigma^G)$. Conversely, if $x\in a$ satisfies that formula, choose an active pair $(\nu,p)\in\tau$ with $x=\nu^G$ and a condition $r\in G$ [forcing](../../../forcing.md) the formula. Directedness gives $q\in G$ stronger than both $p$ and $r$. Monotonicity puts $(\nu,q)\in\rho$, so $x\in\rho^G$.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\rho^G=\{x\in a:M[G]\models\varphi(x,\vec\sigma^G)\}.}
$$

Every requested instance of [separation in a generic extension](../../../forcing.md#separation-in-a-generic-extension) follows.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

For a maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) $A\in M$, the set $D_A=\{q:\exists a\in A\ (q\ge a)\}$ is dense. Maximality ensures each condition is compatible with some $a\in A$, and a common strengthening lies in $D_A$. A [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter) meets $D_A$ and hence, by closure toward weaker conditions, meets $A$. Directedness makes any two of its conditions compatible, so it contains at most one member of an [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain). Thus **$|G\cap A|=1$**.

Conversely, suppose this equality holds for every ground-model maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain). For any dense $D\in M$, use Choice in $M$ to select an [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) $A\subseteq D$ maximal among [forcing antichains](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) contained in $D$. It is maximal in $P$ as well: a condition incompatible with every member of $A$ would have an extension in $D$ still incompatible with every member, enlarging $A$. The hypothesis gives a member of $G\cap A\subseteq G\cap D$. Thus $G$ meets every ground dense set and is generic.

This proves the [maximal-antichain criterion for genericity](../../../forcing.md#maximal-antichain-criterion-for-genericity) in both directions.

Let $\kappa=(\omega_2)^M$ be the fixed ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). We prove that $S$ remains stationary in this [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). The [chain condition for forcing](../../../forcing.md#chain-condition-for-forcing) ensures that $\kappa$ remains regular: possible values of each coordinate of an ordinal-valued name form a ground set of size less than $\kappa$, by a maximal deciding [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain). A hypothetical cofinal map with domain below $\kappa$ would have its range covered by fewer than $\kappa$ such small sets, hence bounded by regularity in $M$.

Let $\dot C$ name a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) in $\kappa$, and take $p\in G$ [forcing](../../../forcing.md) this. For each $\alpha<\kappa$, choose in $M$ a maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) above $p$ deciding the least point of $\dot C$ strictly above $\alpha$. Its set of possible values has size less than $\kappa$, so choose a ground bound $h(\alpha)<\kappa$ larger than all of them. The ground set

$$
D=\{\delta<\kappa:\delta\text{ is limit and }(\forall\alpha<\delta)\ h(\alpha)<\delta\}
$$

is a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) by the usual countable closure iteration and regularity. For every $\delta\in D$, $p$ forces $\dot C\cap\delta$ unbounded in $\delta$, and closedness forces $\delta\in\dot C$. Hence $p\Vdash\check D\subseteq\dot C$. In $M$, choose $\delta\in S\cap D$; that same [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) belongs to $S\cap C$ in the extension. **Stationarity at the fixed ground $\kappa$ is preserved.**

There is an important qualification to the printed $\omega_2$ notation. If it is recomputed internally as $(\omega_2)^{M[G]}$, the assertion is false without preservation of smaller [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). Finite partial maps from $\omega$ to $(\omega_1)^M$ form a [forcing](../../../forcing.md) of size $(\aleph_1)^M$, hence have the $(\aleph_2)^M$-chain condition, but collapse $(\omega_1)^M$ to countable. Then $(\omega_2)^M=(\omega_1)^{M[G]}$, while $(\omega_2)^{M[G]}$ is larger. The old $S$ is bounded in this new $\omega_2$, so cannot be stationary there. The proved statement uses the fixed ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), or alternatively requires the lower-cardinal preservation needed to retain its aleph index.

The [valuation of a forcing name](../../../forcing.md#evaluation-of-a-forcing-name) is defined recursively by

A [nice forcing name](../../../forcing.md#nice-forcing-name) for a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of a ground-model set $a$, often an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), has the form

<h3 id="5/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/ii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#5/ii)

<h5 id="5/ii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/ii/a)

**Under the ordinary height-and-width definition of an $\aleph_1$-tree, the printed claim is false.** The chain $(\omega_1,\le)$ has one node at each level, every antichain has size at most one, and it is itself an uncountable chain.

Here is the intended argument under the additional splitting convention. Any uncountable chain in a tree with countable levels is unbounded in height, because a bounded set of levels below $\omega_1$ has only countably many nodes. Its predecessor closure therefore gives a [cofinal branch](../../../set.md#cofinal-branch) $B$. At each node on $B$, splitting supplies an extension off $B$, incompatible with a later node on $B$. Recursively for $\xi<\omega_1$, choose such an off-branch node $s_\xi$, then move sufficiently far along $B$ that all subsequent choices are above a branch node incompatible with $s_\xi$. At a limit stage the previous countably many heights are bounded below $\omega_1$, so the recursion continues. The $s_\xi$ are pairwise incompatible, an uncountable antichain.

Thus **a splitting $\aleph_1$-tree with only countable antichains has no uncountable chains**. A splitting convention must be stated; the raw tree hypothesis alone does not suffice.

<h4 id="5/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#5/ii)

<h5 id="5/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/ii/b)

Use the equivalent tree formulation of the [Suslin hypothesis](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#suslin-hypothesis): there is no normal, well-pruned [Suslin tree](../../../set.md#suslin-tree) of height $\omega_1$, with countable levels and no uncountable chains or antichains. This is equivalent to the linear-order formulation that every complete dense order without endpoints satisfying the [countable chain condition for a linear order](../../../set.md#countable-chain-condition-for-a-linear-order) is separable.

If such a tree $T$ existed, use its nodes as [forcing](../../../forcing.md) conditions, with a higher extension stronger. It is [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) because its antichains are countable. For every $\alpha<\omega_1$, the set $D_\alpha=\{t:\operatorname{ht}(t)\ge\alpha\}$ is dense because the tree is well-pruned. The assertion $\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}$ is precisely that a [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) [forcing](../../../forcing.md) and a family of at most $\aleph_1$ dense sets admit a filter meeting all of them.

Apply it to these dense sets. A directed filter in a tree is a chain: two compatible nodes are comparable, since both lie among the well-ordered predecessors of a common extension. Meeting every $D_\alpha$ makes this chain cofinal, contrary to the defining absence of uncountable chains in a Suslin tree. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{Suslin's Hypothesis}.}
$$

<h3 id="5/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#5/iii)

Start with a ground model containing a normal splitting [Suslin tree](../../../set.md#suslin-tree) $T$, and force with $\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_2)$, the finite partial [functions](../../../function.md) from $\omega_2\times\omega$ to $2$. Such a starting model is relatively consistent with [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice): the constructible universe has $\Diamond$, and $\Diamond$ implies the existence of a Suslin tree.

This [Cohen forcing](../../../forcing.md#cohen-forcing) is Knaster. In an uncountable family of finite conditions, the [delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system-lemma) yields an uncountable family whose domains have one common root; thinning to common values on that finite root makes its members pairwise compatible. If $P$ is Knaster and $T$ is [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing), the product $P\times T$ is [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing): thin any uncountable family to pairwise compatible $P$ coordinates, then use [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) to find two compatible $T$ coordinates. Thus $P$ forces that the old $T$ is still [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) A name for an uncountable antichain would otherwise produce an uncountable antichain in this product by deciding its nodes.

Countable levels and the tree's splitting property persist, and the splitting argument from the previous part rules out a new [cofinal branch](../../../set.md#cofinal-branch) in a [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) tree. Therefore the old $T$ is still Suslin. Meanwhile the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) adds at least $\aleph_2$ distinct reals, with all [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) preserved, so $2^{\aleph_0}\ge\aleph_2$.

We obtain **a model with a Suslin tree and $\neg\mathrm{CH}$**. Hence existence of a Suslin tree does not imply the [Continuum hypothesis](../../../set-theory.md#continuum-hypothesis). This uses [Knaster forcing preserves Suslin trees](../../../forcing.md#knaster-forcing-preserves-suslin-trees), not a claim that arbitrary [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) [forcing](../../../forcing.md) preserves them.

<h3 id="5/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iv/a">a</h4>

<h5 id="5/iv/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/iv/a)

For uncountable regular $\kappa$, let $\theta=\operatorname{otp}(C_\zeta)$. Since $C_\zeta$ is a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) in $\zeta$, $\operatorname{cf}(\theta)=\operatorname{cf}(\zeta)=\kappa$, so $\theta\ge\kappa$.

If $\theta>\kappa$, its [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) ensures $\theta>\kappa+\omega$: [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) strictly between $\kappa$ and $\kappa+\omega$ are successors, and $\kappa+\omega$ has [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\omega$. In the continuous increasing enumeration $c$ of $C_\zeta$, take $\gamma=c(\kappa+\omega)$. It is a limit point of $C_\zeta$ and has [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\omega<\kappa$. Coherence gives $C_\gamma=C_\zeta\cap\gamma$, with [order type](../../../set-theory.md#order-type) $\kappa+\omega$ and size $\kappa$. This contradicts the size clause for [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) of [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) below $\kappa$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{otp}(C_\zeta)=\kappa\quad\text{for uncountable regular }\kappa.}
$$

**The printed hypotheses need this qualification.** At $\kappa=\omega$, choose $C_\zeta=\zeta$ for every nonzero countable [limit ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal). These [club sets](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) are coherent, and the small-cofinality clause is vacuous. But at $\zeta=\omega^2$ their [order type](../../../set-theory.md#order-type) is $\omega^2$, not $\omega$. The standard [square principle](../../../set-theory.md#square-principle) includes an order-type bound $\operatorname{otp}(C_\zeta)\le\kappa$, which also repairs this countable case.

<h4 id="5/iv/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#5/iv)

<h5 id="5/iv/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/iv/b)

Fix $\delta<\kappa^+$ with $\operatorname{cf}(\delta)>\omega$. The limit points of $C_\delta$ form a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) in $\delta$. For each such point $\gamma$, coherence gives

$$
\operatorname{otp}(C_\gamma)=\operatorname{otp}(C_\delta\cap\gamma).
$$

As $\gamma$ increases through those limit points, these order types strictly increase. Hence the [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) contains at most one member of a fixed fiber $E_\xi$. Removing an initial segment past that member leaves a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) disjoint from $E_\xi$. Therefore $E_\xi\cap\delta$ is not stationary. This proves **each $E_\xi$ is non-reflecting**, by the [nonreflection of fixed order-type fibers](../../../set-theory.md#nonreflection-of-fixed-order-type-fibers).

For uncountable regular $\kappa$, part (a) gives type $\kappa$ at [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality) $\kappa$, and the size clause gives type less than $\kappa$ at smaller cofinalities. All nonzero [limit ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal) below $\kappa^+$ fall into one of these cases. Thus

$$
\lim(\kappa^+)=\bigsqcup_{\xi\le\kappa}E_\xi.
$$

The index set $\kappa+1$ has cardinality $\kappa$, so this is a disjoint union of $\kappa$ non-reflecting [subsets](../../../set.md#subset). Empty fibers can be omitted; if nonempty pieces are required, a fiber of size $\kappa^+$ can be split further, since nonreflection is inherited by [subsets](../../../set.md#subset). Such a fiber exists because $\kappa$ sets of size at most $\kappa$ cannot cover $\kappa^+$ points.

At $\kappa=\omega$, the nonreflection definition is vacuous for every [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\omega_1$, since no smaller [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) has uncountable [cofinality](../../../set-theory.md#cofinality). The partition conclusion is therefore still true by an arbitrary countable partition, although the particular fibers from the weak printed square assumptions need not cover all [limit ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#limit-ordinal). Under the standard order-type-bounded square definition, the displayed fiber partition works in this case too.

<h3 id="6/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/i/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/i/a)

The [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem) has two central clauses. **Definability:** for each [first-order formula](../../../mathematical-logic.md#first-order-formula) $\varphi$, the relation $p\Vdash\varphi(\vec\tau)$ on conditions and ground-model names is first-order definable in $M$. **Truth lemma:** for every $M$-generic $G$ and every tuple of names in $M$,

$$
\boxed{M[G]\models\varphi(\vec\tau^G)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists p\in G\ (p\Vdash\varphi(\vec\tau)).}
$$

[Forcing](../../../forcing.md) is monotone under strengthening and agrees with the generic-extension semantics. Together with the generic model construction, $M[G]$ is a [transitive model](../../../set-theory.md#transitive-model) of [ZFC](../../../set-theory.md#zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice) containing $M$ with the same [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal). The definability and truth clauses are the auxiliary parts used when proving individual extension axioms.

<h4 id="6/i/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/i/b)

The [canonical forcing name](../../../forcing.md#canonical-forcing-name) $\dot a=\check a$ is defined recursively by

$$
\boxed{\check a=\{(\check b,p):b\in a,\ p\in P\}.}
$$

If a weakest condition $1_P$ is provided, one may instead pair each $\check b$ only with $1_P$. The all-conditions version works for an arbitrary nonempty [forcing](../../../forcing.md) without that extra convention. Induction on rank gives $\check a^G=a$ for every [generic filter](../../../forcing.md#generic-filter), since each element $b\in a$ is activated by some condition of the nonempty $G$. Thus all ground-model objects have canonical names.

<h4 id="6/i/c">c</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6/i)

<h5 id="6/i/c/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/i/c)

A precise form of the [generalized delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-delta-system-lemma) is this. Let $\mu<\theta$ be infinite [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number), with $\theta$ regular and uncountable, and suppose $|[\alpha]^{<\mu}|<\theta$ for every $\alpha<\theta$. Every family $\mathcal A$ of $\theta$ distinct sets, each of cardinality less than $\mu$, contains a subfamily $\mathcal B$ of size $\theta$ and a set $r$ such that

$$
\boxed{x\ne y\in\mathcal B\ \Longrightarrow\ x\cap y=r.}
$$

The members form a [delta-system](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system) with root $r$. A sufficient usual arithmetic hypothesis is $\alpha^\mu<\theta$ for all infinite [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\alpha<\theta$. The finite-set case $\mu=\omega$ needs only regular uncountable $\theta$, since finite [subsets](../../../set.md#subset) of each $\alpha<\theta$ form a family of size less than $\theta$. This is the form used for the finite-support collapse below.

<h3 id="6/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/ii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/ii)

<h5 id="6/ii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/ii/a)

Choose a name $\dot f\in M$ for $f$ and a condition $p\in G$ [forcing](../../../forcing.md) that it is a [function](../../../function.md) $\alpha\to\beta$. The [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha,\beta$ belong to $M$, by [forcing preserves ordinals](../../../forcing.md#forcing-preserves-ordinals). For each $\zeta<\alpha$, choose in $M$ a maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) $A_\zeta$ in the cone above $p$, every member deciding $\dot f(\zeta)$ as an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\beta$. Conditions deciding an ordinal-valued name are dense, by the [forcing theorem](../../../forcing.md#forcing-theorem).

Let $y(\zeta)$ be the set of values decided by members of $A_\zeta$. The chain condition gives $|y(\zeta)|^M<\kappa$, and Choice and Replacement in $M$ assemble all these sets into a [function](../../../function.md) $y\in M$ with domain $\alpha$. Since $p\in G$, genericity ensures that $G$ meets each deciding [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) above $p$; equivalently enlarge it to a global maximal [forcing antichain](../../../forcing.md#forcing-antichain) by conditions incompatible with $p$. Its chosen value is the actual $f(\zeta)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{dom}(y)=\alpha,\quad
M\models|y(\zeta)|<\kappa,\quad
M[G]\models f(\zeta)\in y(\zeta)\text{ for all }\zeta<\alpha.}
$$

This is the [possible-values lemma for chain-condition forcing](../../../forcing.md#possible-values-lemma-for-chain-condition-forcing). All size bounds in its construction are internal to the ground model.

<h4 id="6/ii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6/ii)

<h5 id="6/ii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/ii/b)

Suppose a ground [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) $\lambda\ge\kappa$ became smaller in the extension. There would be a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $f:\alpha\to\lambda$ for some [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha<\lambda$. A finite domain cannot map onto an infinite [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal), so use part (a) to cover $f$ by a ground [function](../../../function.md) $y$ of small value sets. Its range is contained in $U=\bigcup_{\zeta<\alpha}y(\zeta)$.

If $\lambda>\kappa$, the ground cardinality of $U$ is at most $|\alpha|^M\cdot\kappa<\lambda$. If $\lambda=\kappa$, regularity bounds a union of fewer than $\kappa$ sets of size less than $\kappa$ by a [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) less than $\kappa$. In either case $U$ is a proper ground [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\lambda$, and the same missing [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) remains missing in the extension, contradicting surjectivity.

Hence **all ground cardinalities at least $\kappa$ are preserved**. Ground bijections also remain bijections, so old noncardinals cannot turn into [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). The assertion does not prevent collapse of smaller [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number), and consequently does not by itself preserve the aleph index of $\kappa$.

<h3 id="6/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iii/a">a</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6/iii)

<h5 id="6/iii/a/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#6/iii/a)

Let $p\in G$ force that $\dot D$ is a diamond sequence. For each $\alpha<\omega_1$, construct in $M$ the family

$$
E_\alpha=\{x\subseteq\alpha:\exists q\ge p\ (q\Vdash\dot D_\alpha=\check x)\}.
$$

This family is countable in $M$: choose a witnessing condition for each distinct value; different values require incompatible conditions, and the [forcing](../../../forcing.md) is [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) It is not being asserted that every generic value of $\dot D_\alpha$ is a ground [subset](../../../set.md#subset). Only values forced equal to a ground [subset](../../../set.md#subset) enter $E_\alpha$.

For any ground $X\subseteq\omega_1$ and ground [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) $C\subseteq\omega_1$, the [CCC](../../../forcing.md#countable-chain-condition-for-forcing) preserves $\omega_1$ and $C$ remains a [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set). Since $p$ forces diamond, some strengthening $q\ge p$ forces $\dot D_\alpha=\check X\cap\check\alpha$ for an [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) $\alpha\in C$, deciding the [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) witness if necessary. Thus $X\cap\alpha\in E_\alpha$. The ground set of such $\alpha$ meets every ground [club set](../../../set-theory.md#club-set) and is stationary in $M$.

Consequently $\langle E_\alpha\rangle$ witnesses the countable-family diamond assertion in $M$. By the fully proved [countable-family diamond equivalence](../../../set-theory.md#countable-family-diamond-equivalence), with $\lambda=\omega_1$ and its stationary index set, it follows that

$$
\boxed{M\models\Diamond.}
$$

Thus [CCC forcing cannot create diamond](../../../set-theory.md#ccc-forcing-cannot-create-diamond).

<h4 id="6/iii/b">b</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6/iii)

<h5 id="6/iii/b/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/iii/b)

A diamond sequence on a regular uncountable $\lambda$ implies $2^{\aleph_0}\le\lambda$. For every $x\subseteq\omega$, regard $x$ as a [subset](../../../set.md#subset) of $\lambda$. A correct guess above $\omega$ is exactly $x$, so the sequence's at most $\lambda$ guesses include every real.

Force with $\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\lambda^+)$ using finite conditions. The [delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#delta-system-lemma) and agreement on the finite root prove the countable chain condition. It preserves [cardinals](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) and the regularity of $\lambda$, while adding at least $\lambda^+$ distinct reals. The reals on distinct coordinates differ on a dense set of conditions, so their number is indeed at least this ground [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number). In the extension,

$$
\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}\ge\lambda^+>\lambda,\qquad\neg\Diamond_\lambda.}
$$

This gives the requested relative consistency over a model with the indicated regular uncountable [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number), with the usual forcing-theoretic consistency interpretation.

<h3 id="6/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#6/iv)

Work internally in the ground model and write $P=\operatorname{Levy}(\aleph_0,\lambda)$. Conditions are finite partial [functions](../../../function.md) with the coordinate-wise value bounds in the PDF, ordered by inclusion, so compatible conditions agree on their common domain and their union is a common strengthening.

Suppose there were $\lambda$ many pairwise incompatible conditions. Each fixed finite domain carries fewer than $\lambda$ possible [functions](../../../function.md): it uses finitely many [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\lambda$, and each coordinate has fewer than $\lambda$ choices. Regularity therefore lets us select $\lambda$ distinct domains. The finite-set form of the [generalized delta-system lemma](../../../set-theory.md#generalized-delta-system-lemma) gives a subfamily of size $\lambda$ with common intersection $r$. There are fewer than $\lambda$ possible value assignments to the finite root $r$. Regularity gives a further subfamily of size $\lambda$ agreeing on the root. Any two of its conditions have a union in $P$, contradicting incompatibility. Thus **$P$ has the $\lambda$-chain condition**. Strong inaccessibility is more than is needed for this finite-support argument; regular uncountability suffices.

For each $0<\alpha<\lambda$ and $n<\omega$, requiring $(n,\alpha)$ to be in the domain is dense. For each $\xi<\alpha$, requiring $\xi$ to occur as a value at some $(n,\alpha)$ is also dense: choose a fresh $n$ and extend the condition. Hence the generic union defines, for each such $\alpha$, a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $g_\alpha:\omega\to\alpha$. Every ground [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) below $\lambda$ is therefore countable in $M[G]$.

By the chain condition and the cardinal-preservation argument, $\lambda$ remains an uncountable [cardinal](../../../set-theory.md#cardinal-number) in the extension; the same small-value argument preserves its regularity as well. All [ordinals](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) are unchanged. Since every smaller [ordinal](../../../set-theory.md#ordinal) is countable and $\lambda$ is not,

$$
\boxed{\aleph_1^{M[G]}=\lambda.}
$$

This is the [finite Lévy collapse to omega-one](../../../forcing.md#finite-levy-collapse-to-omega-one). The dense-set construction proves the collapse below $\lambda$, while the chain condition is what prevents collapsing $\lambda$ itself.
