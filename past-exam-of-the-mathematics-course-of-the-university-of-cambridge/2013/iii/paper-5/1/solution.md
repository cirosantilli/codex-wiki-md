<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

We take algebras to be unital and [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) to be unital. A finite-dimensional [semisimple algebra](../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) over the [complex numbers](../../../../../complex-number.md) is one whose regular left [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a [semisimple module](../../../../../semisimple-module.md), that is, a direct sum of [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md). We use the basic finite-dimensional equivalences: this is equivalent to a zero [Jacobson radical](../../../../../jacobson-radical.md), and the [Jacobson radical](../../../../../jacobson-radical.md) of any finite-dimensional algebra is a nilpotent ideal.

The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) here says that there are positive integers $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ with

$$
\boxed{A\cong\prod_{i=1}^r M_{d_i}(\mathbb C).}
$$

The nonisomorphic [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) are the standard column [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) of the factors, of dimensions $d_i$, and the factor sizes are unique up to reordering.

Here is a proof. Decompose the regular left [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) as ${}_AA=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r S_i^{\oplus n_i}$ with pairwise nonisomorphic [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) $S_i$. These are all the [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md): any [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) is generated by a nonzero vector and is therefore a quotient of the regular [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md). The [Schur lemma](../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) gives $\operatorname{Hom}_A(S_i,S_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$ and $\operatorname{End}_A(S_i)=\mathbb C$. For the latter assertion, an endomorphism has an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) over $\mathbb C$, and the kernel of its difference from that scalar is a nonzero [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), hence the whole [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md). Consequently

$$
\operatorname{End}_A({}_AA)\cong\prod_i M_{n_i}(\mathbb C).
$$

Every regular-module endomorphism is right multiplication by its value at $1$, so $\operatorname{End}_A({}_AA)\cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$. Taking opposite algebras and using matrix transposition gives the asserted decomposition with $d_i=n_i$.

For a [matrix algebra](../../../../../matrix-algebra.md) $M_d(\mathbb C)$, the [matrix units](../../../../../matrix-unit.md) show that its only [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) is $\mathbb C^d$: the spaces $E_{jj}S$ are isomorphic through $E_{ij}$, and a nonzero vector in one of them generates one copy of the column [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md). Simplicity makes that copy all of $S$. In a product algebra, the mutually orthogonal central [idempotents](../../../../../idempotent.md) decompose every [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) into its factor [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md); a [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) uses exactly one factor. This proves the [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) assertion and also uniqueness, since the primitive central [idempotents](../../../../../idempotent.md) and the dimensions of their [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) determine the factors.
