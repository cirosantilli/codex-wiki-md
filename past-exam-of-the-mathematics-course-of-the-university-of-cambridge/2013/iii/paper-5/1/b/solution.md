<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Maschke's theorem](../../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md) makes the [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) $\mathbb CG$ semisimple, since $G$ is a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md) and the ground field has [characteristic zero](../../../../../../characteristic-zero.md). By the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md), write $\mathbb CG\cong\prod_{i=1}^r M_{d_i}(\mathbb C)$. Its [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) give exactly the $r$ nonisomorphic irreducible complex [group representations](../../../../../../group-representation.md).

The center of each [matrix algebra](../../../../../../matrix-algebra.md) consists of scalar matrices, so $\dim_{\mathbb C}Z(\mathbb CG)=r$. On the other hand, an element $\sum_g a_g g$ is central precisely when its coefficients are constant on [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). The sums of the elements in the separate [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) are therefore a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of the center. Hence **the number of irreducible complex representations equals the number of conjugacy classes**.
