<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume first that $S\ne0$, as required for the irreducible-module conclusion. If $B\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(S)$ is surjective, any nonzero invariant subspace contains every image of one of its nonzero vectors under all endomorphisms, hence is all of $S$. Thus $S$ is irreducible.

Conversely an irreducible $B$-module is finite dimensional: for $s\ne0$, $S=Bs$ is a quotient of the finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $B$. Let $D$ be the image of $B$ in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(S)$. It acts faithfully and irreducibly. The subspace $J(D)S$ is a [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md), so is zero or all of $S$. The latter alternative would imply $J(D)^kS=S$ for every $k$, contradicting nilpotence of the [Jacobson radical](../../../../../../jacobson-radical.md). Therefore $J(D)S=0$, and faithfulness gives $J(D)=0$.

The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) makes $D$ a product of [matrix algebras](../../../../../../matrix-algebra.md). A faithful [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) forces there to be just one factor, because all other factors would annihilate that [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md). Thus $D\cong M_d(\mathbb C)$ and $S\cong\mathbb C^d$, so the action is the full endomorphism algebra. This is the [Burnside matrix-algebra theorem](../../../../../../burnside-matrix-algebra-theorem.md).

**For nonzero $S$, surjectivity is equivalent to irreducibility.** The zero [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a literal exception if it is admitted: its endomorphism algebra is zero, so the action map is surjective, whereas the zero [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is not irreducible.
