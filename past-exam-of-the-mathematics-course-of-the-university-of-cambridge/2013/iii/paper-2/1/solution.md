↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and let $B$ be the [integral closure](../../../../../integral-closure.md) of $A$ in $L$. We first establish the elementary [integral extension](../../../../../integral-extension.md) machinery, and then use the [trace pairing](../../../../../trace-pairing.md) to put $B$ inside a finite $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md).

An [integral element](../../../../../integral-element.md) $x$ satisfies a [monic polynomial](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) over the base [ring](../../../../../ring.md). If its equation has degree $r$, the powers $1,x,\ldots,x^{r-1}$ generate $A[x]$ as an $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md). More generally, adjoining finitely many [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md) gives a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md): reduce the exponent of each generator using its monic equation. Conversely, if an $A$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) $M$ of an extension algebra contains $1$, is finitely generated, and is stable under multiplication by $x$, choose generators $m_j$ and write $xm_j=\sum_i a_{ij}m_i$. Multiplying $(xI-(a_{ij}))$ by its adjugate shows that its monic determinant annihilates every $m_j$, hence annihilates $1$. This [finite-module criterion for integrality](../../../../../finite-module-criterion-for-integrality.md) is the [determinant trick](../../../../../determinant-trick.md).

If $x,y$ are [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md), the finite $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $A[x,y]$ is stable under $x+y$, $xy$ and $-x$. The criterion proves that these are [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md). Thus the [integral closure](../../../../../integral-closure.md) really is a subring. It also proves [integral dependence is transitive](../../../../../integral-dependence-is-transitive.md): if $x$ is integral over an integral $A$-algebra $C$, take the finitely many coefficients of its equation, form their finite $A$-subalgebra $C_0$, and observe that $C_0[x]$ is finite over $C_0$ and therefore finite over $A$. The criterion applies to $x$. In particular, an algebra generated by finitely many [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md) is a finite [integral extension](../../../../../integral-extension.md), even though an arbitrary [integral extension](../../../../../integral-extension.md) need not be finite.

Choose a $K$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ of $L$. Each basis element is algebraic over $K$. If

$$
v_i^{r_i}+c_{i1}v_i^{r_i-1}+\cdots+c_{ir_i}=0,
$$

choose a nonzero $a_i\in A$ clearing all coefficient denominators. Then $e_i=a_iv_i$ satisfies a monic equation with coefficients $a_i^jc_{ij}\in A$. Hence all $e_i$ belong to $B$ and still form a $K$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md). This is [integral field basis by denominator clearing](../../../../../integral-field-basis-by-denominator-clearing.md).

We next prove the [trace of an integral element over a normal domain](../../../../../trace-of-an-integral-element-over-a-normal-domain.md) property. For $z\in B$, its images under all $K$-embeddings of $L$ into an algebraic closure are [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md) over $A$, because each satisfies the same monic equation. Their sum is integral by the subring property just proved. Since $L/K$ is a [separable field extension](../../../../../separable-extension.md), that sum is the [field trace](../../../../../field-trace.md) $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(z)$ and lies in $K$. The normality assumption means that $A$ is a [normal domain](../../../../../integrally-closed-domain.md), equivalently an [integrally closed domain](../../../../../integrally-closed-domain.md), so

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(z)\in A\qquad(z\in B).
$$

The same conclusion applies to $ze_i$, since $B$ is a ring.

The [trace pairing](../../../../../trace-pairing.md) of a finite [separable field extension](../../../../../separable-extension.md) is nondegenerate. One can see this directly using the allowed [Galois theory](../../../../../galois-theory-split.md): for a primitive element $u$, the embedding matrix of $1,u,\ldots,u^{d-1}$ is a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct conjugates of $u$. Its determinant is nonzero, and the trace Gram matrix is its transpose times itself. Nondegeneracy is unchanged by a change of [basis](../../../../../basis.md).

Let $M=\sum_iAe_i$. Its [trace-dual lattice](../../../../../trace-dual-lattice.md) is

$$
M^\vee=\{x\in L:\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(xM)\subseteq A\}.
$$

Nondegeneracy supplies a trace-dual $K$-[basis](../../../../../basis.md) $e_1^*,\ldots,e_d^*$, with $\operatorname{Tr}(e_i e_j^*)=\delta_{ij}$. The coefficient formula gives

$$
M^\vee=\bigoplus_iAe_i^*,\qquad B\subseteq M^\vee.
$$

Equivalently, the matrix $T=(\operatorname{Tr}(e_ie_j))$ has entries in $A$ and nonzero determinant $D$; if $b=\sum_i c_i e_i\in B$, then $Tc\in A^d$ and the adjugate formula gives $Dc\in A^d$. Thus $B\subseteq D^{-1}M$ as well. Both descriptions exhibit a finite free ambient $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md).

A finite $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a [Noetherian module](../../../../../noetherian-module.md) when $A$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), and every [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of a [Noetherian module](../../../../../noetherian-module.md) is finitely generated. Since $B$ is an $A$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of $M^\vee$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{B\text{ is a ring, integral over }A,\text{ and finite as an }A\text{-module}.}
$$

This proves [finiteness of integral closure in a finite separable extension](../../../../../finiteness-of-integral-closure-in-a-finite-separable-extension.md). The hypotheses have distinct roles: separability makes the [trace pairing](../../../../../trace-pairing.md) nonsingular, normality puts integral traces back in $A$, and [Noetherianity](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) makes the contained [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) finite. Also $\operatorname{Frac}(B)=L$, since the integral basis elements span $L$ over $K$, and $B$ is itself [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) because its ideals are $A$-[submodules](../../../../../submodule.md) of a finite $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md).
