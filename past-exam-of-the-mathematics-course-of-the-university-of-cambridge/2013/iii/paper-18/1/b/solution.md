↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [representation of a functor](../../../../../../representation-of-a-functor.md) $F$ is $(A,x)$ with $x\in F(A)$ such that $h\mapsto F(h)(x)$ is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) $\mathcal C(A,B)\to F(B)$ for every $B$. These [bijections](../../../../../../bijection.md) are natural by functoriality. The element $x$ is its [universal element](../../../../../../universal-element-of-a-set-valued-functor.md).

If $(B,y)$ is another representation, universality gives unique arrows $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to A$ satisfying $F(f)x=y$ and $F(g)y=x$. Consequently $F(gf)x=x=F(1_A)x$, so $gf=1_A$ by injectivity of the representing [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md). Similarly $fg=1_B$. Therefore $f$ is the unique compatible [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md). This proves [uniqueness of functor representations](../../../../../../uniqueness-of-functor-representations.md); uniqueness refers to an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) carrying the specified [universal elements](../../../../../../universal-element-of-a-set-valued-functor.md) to one another, rather than to every [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) between the underlying objects.
