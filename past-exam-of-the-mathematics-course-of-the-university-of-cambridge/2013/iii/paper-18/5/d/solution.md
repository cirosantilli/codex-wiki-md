<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

If $G$ is [representable](../../../../../../representable-functor.md), it is a [limit-preserving functor](../../../../../../limit-preserving-functor.md), and its [universal element](../../../../../../universal-element-of-a-set-valued-functor.md) gives an [initial object](../../../../../../initial-object.md) of $(1\downarrow G)$, hence a weakly initial singleton.

Conversely, a limit-preserving $G$ makes $(1\downarrow G)$ a [complete category](../../../../../../complete-category.md). For a small diagram $(A_j,x_j)$, take $L=\lim_jA_j$ in $\mathcal C$. Its distinguished elements form a compatible family in $\lim_jGA_j$. [Categorical limit](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) preservation gives a unique $x\in GL$ with $Gp_j(x)=x_j$. The underlying [categorical limit](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) factorization of a [categorical cone](../../../../../../cone-over-a-diagram.md) preserves this element, proving the comma-category [universal property](../../../../../../universal-property.md). For the empty diagram, this uses $G1\cong1$. This is the construction of [limits in a comma category of a limit-preserving functor](../../../../../../limits-in-a-comma-category-of-a-limit-preserving-functor.md).

The comma [category](../../../../../../category-split.md) is [locally small](../../../../../../locally-small-category.md) since its arrows are subsets of the hom-sets in $\mathcal C$. By the previous part, its [weakly initial set](../../../../../../weakly-initial-set.md) therefore yields an [initial object](../../../../../../initial-object.md). Part (b) then yields a representation of $G$. Thus [representability from a solution set](../../../../../../representability-from-a-solution-set.md) follows with all small [categorical limits](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md), rather than finite [categorical limits](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) alone.
