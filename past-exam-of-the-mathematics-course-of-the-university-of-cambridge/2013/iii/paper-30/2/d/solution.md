<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Test $H_0:\gamma_d=0$ for both nonreference days, against at least one nonzero day [fixed effect](../../../../../../fixed-effect.md), conditional on chocolate. There are two added [regression coefficients](../../../../../../regression-coefficient.md) and $24-6=18$ residual [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md). The [nested-model F-test](../../../../../../nested-model-f-test.md) statistic is

$$
\boxed{F=\frac{9.750/2}{107.083/18}=\frac{4.875}{5.9491}\simeq0.8195.}
$$

Under $H_0$ and the [normal linear model](../../../../../../normal-linear-model.md) assumptions, $F\sim F_{2,18}$. Its 5% upper critical value is $3.555$, so **do not reject the absence of day effects**; the [p-value](../../../../../../p-value.md) is approximately $0.456$. Thus the missing row has two [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md), mean square $4.875$, and the [F-test](../../../../../../f-test.md) value above. These observations do not provide evidence that including day improves the chocolate-adjusted mean model. They do not prove that every possible day effect or chocolate–day [interaction term](../../../../../../interaction-term.md) is absent.
