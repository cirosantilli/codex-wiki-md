↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [exponential dispersion family](../../../../../../exponential-dispersion-model.md) has density or mass function, relative to a fixed measure,

$$
f(y;\theta,\phi)=\exp\left\{\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{\phi}+c(y,\phi)\right\},\qquad \phi>0.
$$

Here $\theta$ is the [natural parameter](../../../../../../natural-parameter-of-an-exponential-family.md), $b$ is the [cumulant function](../../../../../../cumulant-function-of-an-exponential-family.md), and $\phi$ is the [dispersion parameter](../../../../../../dispersion-parameter.md). Assume the [natural parameter](../../../../../../natural-parameter-of-an-exponential-family.md) lies in the interior of its domain and derivatives can pass through the normalizing integral. Differentiating normalization once and twice gives

$$
\boxed{\mu=E(Y)=b'(\theta),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\phi b''(\theta)=\phi V(\mu).}
$$

The [variance function](../../../../../../variance-function.md) is $V(\mu)=b''((b')^{-1}(\mu))$, where the mean-to-natural-parameter inverse exists. The [dispersion parameter](../../../../../../dispersion-parameter.md) may be fixed, as in a [Poisson distribution](../../../../../../poisson-distribution.md), rather than estimated.
