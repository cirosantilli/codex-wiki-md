<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Under the more plausible [Quasi-Poisson regression](../../../../../../quasi-poisson-regression.md), the year [Wald statistic](../../../../../../wald-test.md) is $-1.686$, with reported [p-value](../../../../../../p-value.md) $0.0922$. An approximate 95% [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md) is

$$
\beta_1\in-0.09816\pm1.96(0.05821)=(-0.21225,0.01593),
\qquad e^{\beta_1}\in(0.8088,1.0161).
$$

Thus **the estimated fall is about 9.35%, but the data do not establish a reduction at the 5% level after allowing for [overdispersion](../../../../../../overdispersion.md)**. The interval allows both a sizeable reduction and a small increase. The before–after comparison also lacks a contemporaneous randomized control, so [causal inference](../../../../../../causal-inference-split.md) about the campaign would require addressing other changes between years. The small [Poisson regression](../../../../../../poisson-regression.md) [p-value](../../../../../../p-value.md) is not sufficient evidence of a campaign effect when its [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) assumption is unsuitable.
