<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let the vertical-velocity amplitude $W$ be real. A convenient [inertia-gravity wave](../../../../../../inertia-gravity-wave.md) polarization, avoiding pressure denominators, is

$$
\hat w=W,\quad\hat u=-\frac mkW,\quad
\hat v=i\frac{fm}{\omega k}W,\quad\hat b=i\frac{N^2}{\omega}W.
$$

The physical velocities and buoyancy are their real parts. The period-mean [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) density and [available potential energy](../../../../../../available-potential-energy.md) density are

$$
\boxed{\langle K\rangle=\frac{\rho_0W^2}{4}\left[1+\frac{m^2}{k^2}+\frac{f^2m^2}{\omega^2k^2}\right],\qquad
\langle A\rangle=\frac{\rho_0W^2N^2}{4\omega^2}.}
$$

The latter follows from $A=\rho_0b^2/(2N^2)$, or from $A=\rho_0N^2\xi_z^2/2$ for vertical [fluid displacement](../../../../../../lagrangian-displacement-fluid-mechanics.md). The factor $1/4$ includes both the energy definition and the mean of a squared harmonic.

Using the [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md),

$$
\boxed{\langle K\rangle-\langle A\rangle
=\frac{\rho_0W^2f^2m^2}{2\omega^2k^2}=2\langle K_y\rangle.}
$$

This [energy partition of rotating internal waves](../../../../../../energy-partition-of-rotating-internal-waves.md) shows that the printed request for ordinary kinetic/potential equipartition in the rotating case is false in general. An explicit counterexample is $f=1$, $N=2$, $k=m=1$, $W=1$, giving $\omega^2=5/2$, $\langle K\rangle=3\rho_0/5$ and $\langle A\rangle=2\rho_0/5$.

The valid modified oscillator balance is

$$
\boxed{\langle K_{xz}\rangle=\langle A\rangle+\langle K_y\rangle.}
$$

The transverse rotational velocity is in quadrature with the in-plane motion, so it supplies an additional positive energy term on the displacement side of this oscillator balance. It remains physically [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), not gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md). Ordinary kinetic/potential equipartition is recovered when $f=0$ or when the transverse amplitude vanishes. For a real harmonic wave the instantaneous total $K+A$ is constant at a fixed point: the coefficient of $\cos^2(kx+mz-\omega t)$ from in-plane motion equals the coefficient of $\sin^2$ from transverse motion and buoyancy. This consistency does not imply equality of their separate period means.
