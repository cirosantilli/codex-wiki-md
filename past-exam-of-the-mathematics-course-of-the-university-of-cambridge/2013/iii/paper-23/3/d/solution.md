<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Under the [Riemann hypothesis](../../../../../../riemann-hypothesis.md), every $\beta=1/2$, so for $\sigma>1/2$ the [absolutely convergent](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) formula gives

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}\log|\xi(\sigma+it)|
=\sum_\rho\frac{\sigma-1/2}{(\sigma-1/2)^2+(t-\gamma)^2}>0.
$$

The zero set is nonempty: otherwise [Hadamard factorization](../../../../../../hadamard-factorization-theorem.md) would make $\xi$ an exponential of a linear [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), and its functional symmetry would force it to be constant, contrary to gamma growth on the positive real axis. Thus the inequality is strict in the open right half-plane. [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $\sigma=1/2$, including at boundary zeros, proves the claimed increasing modulus on the closed half-line.

Conversely, suppose the modulus is nondecreasing for every fixed $t$. If a zero $\beta+i\gamma$ had $\beta>1/2$, then nonnegativity and monotonicity would force $|\xi(\sigma+i\gamma)|=0$ throughout $1/2\le\sigma\le\beta$. The [identity theorem](../../../../../../identity-theorem.md) would make $\xi$ identically zero, a contradiction. A zero left of the line reflects to one right of the line by the [functional equation](../../../../../../functional-equation.md) and [complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) symmetry. Hence every zero lies on the [critical line](../../../../../../critical-line.md). This proves the [xi modulus criterion for the Riemann hypothesis](../../../../../../xi-modulus-criterion-for-the-riemann-hypothesis.md). The two following roman headers refer to supplied asymptotic assumptions, not further questions, and require no Solution sections.
