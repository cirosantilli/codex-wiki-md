<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

At fixed masses, the [equal-mass circular-binary quadrupole luminosity](../../../../../../equal-mass-circular-binary-quadrupole-luminosity.md) grows as $R^{-5}$. The binary's Newtonian binding energy is $-GM^2/(4R)$, so reducing the separation increases both the binding and the radiated power. Equivalently, its luminosity scaling is

$$
P=\frac25\frac{c^5}{G}\left(\frac{GM}{Rc^2}\right)^5,
$$

which makes the importance of [orbital compactness](../../../../../../orbital-compactness.md) explicit.

Ordinary extended stars cannot remain separate at very small orbital radii: contact, mass transfer and [tidal disruption](../../../../../../tidal-disruption.md) intervene. A [neutron star](../../../../../../neutron-star.md) or [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) can remain a compact orbiting object down to separations of order a few gravitational radii, allowing high orbital speeds, rapidly changing [mass quadrupole moments](../../../../../../mass-quadrupole-moment.md) and strong [gravitational waves](../../../../../../gravitational-wave.md). Thus compact, tightly bound binaries are especially efficient emitters. The Newtonian [quadrupole formula](../../../../../../quadrupole-formula.md) explains the scaling; precision predictions near merger require relativistic dynamics, where that approximation itself ceases to be reliable.
