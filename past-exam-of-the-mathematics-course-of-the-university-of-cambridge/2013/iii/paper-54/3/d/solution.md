<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a fixed satellite mass on an adiabatically changing [circular orbit](../../../../../../circular-orbit.md), $L_s=M_s\sqrt{GM_*r_0}=M_sr_0^2\Omega$. The satellite [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) is minus the disc [torque](../../../../../../torque.md). Hence

$$
\frac12M_sr_0\Omega\frac{dr_0}{dt}=-\Gamma,\qquad
\frac{dr_0}{dt}=-\frac{8\chi}{9}\,\beta q\frac{\Sigma_0r_0^2}{M_*}\frac{r_0\Omega}{(H/r_0)^2}.
$$

The [circular-orbit migration rate from disk torque](../../../../../../circular-orbit-migration-rate-from-disk-torque.md), in the normalization supplied by the question, is

$$
\boxed{\frac{dr_0}{dt}=-\frac49\,\beta q\frac{\Sigma_0r_0^2}{M_*}\frac{r_0\Omega}{(H/r_0)^2}.}
$$

It is inward for a positive [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) slope and outward for a negative one. The complete-flyby normalization makes its magnitude four times larger, with the same direction. This is a local [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) estimate; the hypothesis of a nearly circular slowly migrating orbit is needed when using the derivative of the circular [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md).
