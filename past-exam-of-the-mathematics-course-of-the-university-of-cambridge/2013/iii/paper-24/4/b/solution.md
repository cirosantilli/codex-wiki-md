<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For each nonnegative rational $q$, equality of the two versions gives $\mathbb P(X_q=Y_q)=1$. Intersect these countably many full-probability events with the full-probability event on which both paths are [càdlàg](../../../../../../cadlag.md). Call the resulting event $A$; then $\mathbb P(A)=1$.

Fix $\omega\in A$ and any real $t\geq0$. Choose rational numbers $q_n>t$ decreasing to $t$. [Right continuity](../../../../../../right-continuous-function.md) gives

$$
X_t(\omega)=\lim_nX_{q_n}(\omega)
=\lim_nY_{q_n}(\omega)=Y_t(\omega).
$$

The same event $A$ works for every $t$, because the argument is pathwise after $\omega$ is fixed. **The [stochastic processes](../../../../../../stochastic-process-split.md) are therefore [indistinguishable](../../../../../../indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes.md).** This proves that [càdlàg versions are indistinguishable](../../../../../../cadlag-versions-are-indistinguishable.md); the left limits are not needed for this implication, since [right continuity](../../../../../../right-continuous-function.md) alone suffices.
