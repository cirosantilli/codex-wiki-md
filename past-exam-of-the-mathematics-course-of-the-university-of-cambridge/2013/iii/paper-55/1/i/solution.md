<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If gas cannot radiate enough energy to fall below the [virial temperature](../../../../../../virial-temperature.md), infall converts gravitational energy into heat through shocks and compression. Thermal pressure can support an extended atmosphere in the [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md). It need not have the same density profile as the collisionless [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md), because its entropy and pressure matter.

A useful quantitative comparison is the [radiative gas cooling time](../../../../../../optically-thin-gas-cooling-time.md) $t_{\rm cool}\sim(3/2)nk_BT/(n_en_i\Lambda)$ against the halo dynamical time $t_{\rm dyn}\sim r_{\rm vir}/V_{\rm vir}$. If cooling remains slow or a heating source balances it, the gas stays predominantly hot and diffuse, rather than forming a compact, cold, self-gravitating stellar system. **Without sufficient cooling, most baryons remain pressure-supported halo gas.** Very slow cooling can still feed gradual central condensation; the condition is about energy loss relative to the evolution time, not an absolute prohibition on any inward motion.
