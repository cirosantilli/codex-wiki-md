<h1 id="1/ii/ii/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In sufficiently hot ionized gas, electrons radiate when accelerated in ion Coulomb fields: [thermal bremsstrahlung](../../../../../../../../thermal-bremsstrahlung.md) is the main continuum cooling process. In the optically thin nonrelativistic limit its emission rate scales approximately as

$$
\mathcal L_{\rm ff}\propto n_e\sum_i Z_i^2n_iT^{1/2}.
$$

For hot metal-poor gas this is the principal high-temperature channel. Metal ions still give important [metal-line cooling](../../../../../../../../metal-line-cooling.md) near $10^6\,\mathrm K$, and in enriched gas can remain important up to several million kelvin; one should not infer that crossing $10^6\,\mathrm K$ instantly eliminates all lines. At sufficiently high temperatures most ions are stripped and free-free emission dominates. [Inverse Compton cooling](../../../../../../../../inverse-compton-cooling.md) can also matter for ionized, diffuse gas in a strong radiation field, especially the high-redshift [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background.md). **The high-temperature asymptote is bremsstrahlung cooling, with metal-line and Compton qualifications where appropriate.**
