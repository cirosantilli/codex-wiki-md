<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the asymptotically free [beta function](../../../../../../beta-function.md), separation of variables gives

$$
\boxed{g(t)=\frac{g}{\sqrt{1+2bg^2t}}.}
$$

The anomalous-dimension integral is elementary:

$$
\log f(t)=2c\int_0^t\frac{g^2}{1+2bg^2u}\,du=\frac cb\log(1+2bg^2t),\qquad
\boxed{f(t)=(1+2bg^2t)^{c/b}\sim(2bg^2)^{c/b}t^{c/b}.}
$$

For a reference two-point factor regular and nonzero at the free coupling, $C(r,g(t))\to C(r,0)$, so the large-momentum correction is a power $c/b$ of $\log(p^2/\mu^2)$. These are [asymptotic freedom](../../../../../../asymptotic-freedom.md) and [logarithmic two-point scaling from a cubic beta function](../../../../../../logarithmic-two-point-scaling-from-a-cubic-beta-function.md). If the supplied beta and gamma expressions are leading small-coupling terms rather than exact functions, they fix the leading logarithmic exponent, while subleading corrections and the prefactor depend on higher orders.
