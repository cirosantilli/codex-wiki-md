<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $0<g<g_*$, the positive [beta function](../../../../../../beta-function.md) makes the [running coupling](../../../../../../running-coupling.md) increase toward the [ultraviolet fixed point](../../../../../../ultraviolet-fixed-point.md) $g_*$. Assume a continuous locally Lipschitz [beta function](../../../../../../beta-function.md), a continuous [anomalous dimension](../../../../../../anomalous-dimension.md) at the fixed point, and a finite nonzero reference value $C(r,g_*)$. Then $g(t)\to g_*$ and

$$
\log f(t)=2\gamma(g_*)t+o(t),\qquad C(e^{2t}r,g)=e^{2\gamma(g_*)t+o(t)}C(r,g_*).
$$

Thus the high-momentum factor has exponent $\gamma(g_*)$ in $p^2$, and the propagator scales as $(p^2)^{-1+\gamma(g_*)}$ up to slower corrections. If the fixed point is simple and attractive, $\beta'(g_*)<0$, the approach is exponential; suitable smoothness then gives $f(t)\sim A e^{2\gamma(g_*)t}$ with finite $A$. The stated beta-function information alone supplies no numerical value for the [anomalous dimension](../../../../../../anomalous-dimension.md) and does not exclude slower corrections at a nonsimple fixed point. This is [two-point scaling at an ultraviolet fixed point](../../../../../../two-point-scaling-at-an-ultraviolet-fixed-point.md).
