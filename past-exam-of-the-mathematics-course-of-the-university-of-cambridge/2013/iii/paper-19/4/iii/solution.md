<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a maximal [forcing antichain](../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md) $A\in M$, the set $D_A=\{q:\exists a\in A\ (q\ge a)\}$ is dense. Maximality ensures each condition is compatible with some $a\in A$, and a common strengthening lies in $D_A$. A [generic filter](../../../../../../generic-filter.md) meets $D_A$ and hence, by closure toward weaker conditions, meets $A$. Directedness makes any two of its conditions compatible, so it contains at most one member of an [forcing antichain](../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md). Thus **$|G\cap A|=1$**.

Conversely, suppose this equality holds for every ground-model maximal [forcing antichain](../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md). For any dense $D\in M$, use Choice in $M$ to select an [forcing antichain](../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md) $A\subseteq D$ maximal among [forcing antichains](../../../../../../forcing-antichain.md) contained in $D$. It is maximal in $P$ as well: a condition incompatible with every member of $A$ would have an extension in $D$ still incompatible with every member, enlarging $A$. The hypothesis gives a member of $G\cap A\subseteq G\cap D$. Thus $G$ meets every ground dense set and is generic.

This proves the [maximal-antichain criterion for genericity](../../../../../../maximal-antichain-criterion-for-genericity.md) in both directions.
