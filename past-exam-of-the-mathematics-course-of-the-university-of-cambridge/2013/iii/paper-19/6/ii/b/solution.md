<h1 id="6/ii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose a ground [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) $\lambda\ge\kappa$ became smaller in the extension. There would be a [surjection](../../../../../../../surjective-function.md) $f:\alpha\to\lambda$ for some [ordinal](../../../../../../../ordinal.md) $\alpha<\lambda$. A finite domain cannot map onto an infinite [ordinal](../../../../../../../ordinal.md), so use part (a) to cover $f$ by a ground [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) $y$ of small value sets. Its range is contained in $U=\bigcup_{\zeta<\alpha}y(\zeta)$.

If $\lambda>\kappa$, the ground cardinality of $U$ is at most $|\alpha|^M\cdot\kappa<\lambda$. If $\lambda=\kappa$, regularity bounds a union of fewer than $\kappa$ sets of size less than $\kappa$ by a [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) less than $\kappa$. In either case $U$ is a proper ground [subset](../../../../../../../subset.md) of $\lambda$, and the same missing [ordinal](../../../../../../../ordinal.md) remains missing in the extension, contradicting surjectivity.

Hence **all ground cardinalities at least $\kappa$ are preserved**. Ground bijections also remain bijections, so old noncardinals cannot turn into [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md). The assertion does not prevent collapse of smaller [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md), and consequently does not by itself preserve the aleph index of $\kappa$.
