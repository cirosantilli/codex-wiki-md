<h1 id="6/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A diamond sequence on a regular uncountable $\lambda$ implies $2^{\aleph_0}\le\lambda$. For every $x\subseteq\omega$, regard $x$ as a [subset](../../../../../../../subset.md) of $\lambda$. A correct guess above $\omega$ is exactly $x$, so the sequence's at most $\lambda$ guesses include every real.

Force with $\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\lambda^+)$ using finite conditions. The [delta-system lemma](../../../../../../../delta-system-lemma.md) and agreement on the finite root prove the countable chain condition. It preserves [cardinals](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) and the regularity of $\lambda$, while adding at least $\lambda^+$ distinct reals. The reals on distinct coordinates differ on a dense set of conditions, so their number is indeed at least this ground [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md). In the extension,

$$
\boxed{2^{\aleph_0}\ge\lambda^+>\lambda,\qquad\neg\Diamond_\lambda.}
$$

This gives the requested relative consistency over a model with the indicated regular uncountable [cardinal](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md), with the usual forcing-theoretic consistency interpretation.
