<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use the [Minkowski metric](../../../../../minkowski-metric.md) with signature $(+,-)$, so the [Sine-Gordon equation](../../../../../sine-gordon-equation.md) is $\phi_{tt}-\phi_{xx}+\sin\phi=0$. The [light-cone coordinates](../../../../../light-cone-coordinates.md) here satisfy

$$
\partial_+=\partial_x+\partial_t,\qquad\partial_-=\partial_x-\partial_t,\qquad\partial_+\partial_-=\partial_x^2-\partial_t^2.
$$

In particular, there is no extra factor of four with this coordinate normalization. The [Bäcklund transformation](../../../../../backlund-transformation.md) requires $a\ne0$, because one of its equations contains $a^{-1}$.

Put $F=(\psi+\phi)/2$ and $G=(\psi-\phi)/2$. In the parameter convention of this paper the [Sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformation](../../../../../sine-gordon-backlund-transformation.md) gives $G_+=a\sin F$ and $F_-=a^{-1}\sin G$. Differentiating, for a twice differentiable transformed field, gives

$$
G_{+-}=\cos F\sin G,\qquad F_{+-}=\cos G\sin F.
$$

Since $\psi=F+G$, the addition formula yields $\psi_{+-}=\sin(F+G)=\sin\psi$. Therefore **the transformed field satisfies the same [Sine-Gordon equation](../../../../../sine-gordon-equation.md):**

$$
\boxed{\psi_{tt}-\psi_{xx}+\sin\psi=0.}
$$

Subtracting the two differentiated equations also gives $\phi_{+-}=\sin\phi$, showing the compatibility with the seed equation. This proves the unheaded request before the numbered parts, without assuming a particular [soliton](../../../../../soliton.md) form.
