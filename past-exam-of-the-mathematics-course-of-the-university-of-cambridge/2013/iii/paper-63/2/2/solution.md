<h1 id="2/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use the [stability function](../../../../../../stability-function.md) just calculated. Its denominator has its two zeros at $3\pm i\sqrt3$, so there is no pole in the closed left half-plane. Put $z=x+iy$, $N=1+z/2+z^2/12$ and $D=1-z/2+z^2/12$. A direct calculation yields

$$
|D|^2-|N|^2=-2x\left(1+\frac{|z|^2}{12}\right).
$$

For $x\leq0$ this is nonnegative, and therefore $|R(z)|\leq1$. **The method is A-stable.** Equality holds on the imaginary axis, consistently with the absence of numerical damping for those scalar oscillatory modes. Since $R(z)\to1$ as $z\to\infty$, the [A-stability](../../../../../../a-stability.md) established here does not imply strong damping of very stiff decaying modes.
