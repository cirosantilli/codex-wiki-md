<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use two crossed sets of [blocks in experimental design](../../../../../../blocks-in-experimental-design.md): volunteers as rows and afternoons as columns. Volunteer blocks control persistent ability, drawing style and prior experience; afternoon blocks control conditions shared that day, including common task difficulty and general practice over time. Neither partition is nested in the other.

**Give every volunteer each program twice, and use each program twice each afternoon.** The [row-column design](../../../../../../row-column-design.md) is then balanced for both block systems. In the additive model

$$
\mathbb E[Y_{vt}]=\mu+\alpha_v+\beta_t+\tau_{d(v,t)},
$$

the program contrasts are [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to both centered block spaces. This controls additive volunteer and afternoon effects; it does not automatically eliminate individual learning differences or [carryover effects](../../../../../../carryover-effect.md).
