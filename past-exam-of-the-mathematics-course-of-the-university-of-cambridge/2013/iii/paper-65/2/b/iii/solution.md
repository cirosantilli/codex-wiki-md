<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For large $\alpha$, the evanescent interaction between the two interfaces is proportional to $E=e^{-2\alpha}$. Ignoring it first gives isolated [interfacial gravity waves](../../../../../../../interfacial-gravity-wave.md) with phase speeds

$$
C=-1\pm\sqrt q\quad\text{at the lower interface},\qquad
C=1\pm\sqrt q\quad\text{at the upper interface}.
$$

The lower wave propagating positively relative to its local current and the upper wave propagating negatively have equal laboratory speed when

$$
-1+\sqrt q=1-\sqrt q,\qquad\boxed{q=1\ \text{or}\ J=2\alpha.}
$$

Their common speed is zero. This is [counterpropagating wave instability](../../../../../../../counterpropagating-wave-instability.md): the interfaces communicate through their decaying velocity fields, and near resonance their wave phases can lock while extracting energy from the mean shear.

The instability interval has asymptotic endpoints $J_\pm=2\alpha[1\pm E+O(E^2)]$ and width

$$
\boxed{J_+-J_-\sim4\alpha e^{-2\alpha}.}
$$

Near exact resonance the interaction is weak but destabilizing: at $q=1$, $C^2_-=2-\sqrt{4+E^2}=-E^2/4+O(E^4)$, so $\operatorname{Im}C\sim E/2$. Thus the resonant band narrows and growth weakens exponentially with separation measured in decay lengths. The resonance here is between two density interfaces in globally uniform shear, not a resonance modified by additional [vorticity](../../../../../../../vorticity.md) jumps.
