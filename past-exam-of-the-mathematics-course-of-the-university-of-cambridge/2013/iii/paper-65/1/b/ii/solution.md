<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the positive-frequency [internal gravity wave](../../../../../../../internal-wave.md) branch, $\omega=NK_h/K$. Differentiating with respect to the components of the [wave vector](../../../../../../../wavevector.md) gives the [group velocity](../../../../../../../group-velocity.md),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf c_g=\left(\frac{Nkm^2}{K_hK^3},\frac{Nlm^2}{K_hK^3},-\frac{NK_hm}{K^3}\right).}
$$

The wavefront-normal [phase velocity](../../../../../../../phase-velocity.md) is $\mathbf c_p=\omega\mathbf k/K^2$. Directly,

$$
\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_g
=\frac{Nm^2(k^2+l^2)}{K_hK^3}-\frac{NK_hm^2}{K^3}=0,
\qquad\boxed{\mathbf c_p\cdot\mathbf c_g=0.}
$$

The negative-frequency branch changes both propagation signs but not their orthogonality. Another proof is that $\omega$ is homogeneous of degree zero in the [wave vector](../../../../../../../wavevector.md), so Euler's homogeneous-function identity gives $\mathbf k\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf k}\omega=0$. **Energy propagates along constant-phase surfaces, rather than normal to them.** When $m=0$, $\omega=N$ and $\mathbf c_g=0$; the scalar-product result remains true, but there is no nonzero group-velocity direction to describe geometrically.
