<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A [separated dual pair](../../../../../dual-pair.md) consists of real [vector spaces](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $E,F$ and a [bilinear form](../../../../../bilinear-form.md) $\langle x,y\rangle$ that separates both variables: each nonzero $x\in E$ pairs nontrivially with some $y\in F$, and conversely. Thus $F$ embeds in the algebraic dual of $E$, and $E$ embeds in the algebraic dual of $F$.

The [weak topology](../../../../../weak-topology-split.md) $\sigma(E,F)$ is the coarsest topology making all maps $x\mapsto\langle x,y\rangle$, $y\in F$, continuous. It makes $E$ a Hausdorff [locally convex space](../../../../../locally-convex-space.md), with topology generated by the [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md) $p_y(x)=|\langle x,y\rangle|$. A neighbourhood base at zero consists of finite intersections

$$
\{x:|\langle x,y_j\rangle|<\varepsilon_j,\ 1\leq j\leq m\},
\qquad\varepsilon_j>0.
$$

Separation ensures that the common zero set of all these seminorms is just zero, giving Hausdorffness.

We determine the [continuous dual of a weak topology](../../../../../continuous-dual-of-a-weak-topology.md). Suppose a [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) $\ell$ is continuous. A basic neighbourhood as above is contained in $\{x:|\ell(x)|<1\}$. If $x$ is in the common kernel of the finitely many evaluations, every scalar multiple of $x$ lies in that neighbourhood, so $\ell(x)=0$. Hence $\ell$ factors through the image of

$$
T:E\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m,\qquad Tx=(\langle x,y_1\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,y_m\rangle).
$$

Extend the resulting [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) on $T(E)$ to $\mathbb R^m$. It has the form $z\mapsto\sum_j a_jz_j$, giving $\ell(x)=\langle x,\sum_ja_jy_j\rangle$. Conversely every such evaluation is continuous by the definition of the [weak topology](../../../../../weak-topology-split.md). Since the pairing separates $F$, this identification is injective, and

$$
\boxed{(E,\sigma(E,F))'=F.}
$$

For the remaining parts every topological assertion about $E'$ or its subsets uses the stipulated [weak-star topology](../../../../../weak-star-topology.md) $\sigma(E',E)$.
