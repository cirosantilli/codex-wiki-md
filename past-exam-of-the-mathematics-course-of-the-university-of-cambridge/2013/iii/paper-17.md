**The divisor determines the bundle through its local equations.** Choose an [open cover](../../../topology.md#open-cover) on which $V$ has a reduced [holomorphic](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-differentiability-at-a-point) defining equation $f_i$, using $f_i=1$ on sets disjoint from $V$. Because $V$ is a [complex submanifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-submanifold) of [codimension of a submanifold](../../../topology.md#codimension-of-a-submanifold) one, its ideal is locally generated by a coordinate; consequently $f_i/f_j$ is a nowhere-zero [holomorphic function](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-function) on each overlap. Inside the [sheaf of meromorphic functions on a complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#sheaf-of-meromorphic-functions-on-a-complex-manifold), consider the [locally free sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-free-sheaf) of rank one

$$
\mathcal O_M(V)|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}.
$$

These local descriptions agree on overlaps. With $e_i=1/f_i$, its [transition functions of a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#transition-function-of-a-vector-bundle) are specified by

$$
e_i=\frac{f_j}{f_i}e_j.
$$

The ratios satisfy the [Čech cocycle condition](../../../ringed-space.md#cech-cocycle-condition), and the associated [holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-line-bundle-associated-to-a-divisor) is $[V]$. The section $1=f_i e_i$ is [holomorphic](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-differentiability-at-a-point) and vanishes to order one along $V$. Replacing $f_i$ by $u_i f_i$, with $u_i$ a [holomorphic](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-differentiability-at-a-point) unit, replaces the frame by $u_i^{-1}e_i$ without changing the subsheaf of meromorphic functions. Refining the cover changes no local sections either. This proves **choice independence up to isomorphism** rather than merely producing a bundle for one cover.

For a [complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-manifold) of dimension $n$, the [canonical bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#canonical-bundle) is

$$
\boxed{K_M=\bigwedge\nolimits^n(T^{1,0}M)^*.}
$$

It is the [holomorphic line bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-line-bundle) of holomorphic top-degree [differential forms](../../../differential-form.md). To compute it on [Complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-space), let $S=\mathcal O(-1)$ be the [complex tautological line bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#complex-tautological-line-bundle), so that $S^*=[H]=\mathcal O(1)$ is the [hyperplane line bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#hyperplane-line-bundle). At a line $\ell\subset\mathbb C^{n+1}$, the [tangent space](../../../differential-geometry.md#tangent-space) is $\operatorname{Hom}(\ell,\mathbb C^{n+1}/\ell)$. Tensoring the tautological quotient sequence by $S^*$ gives the [Euler sequence on complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#euler-sequence-on-complex-projective-space)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O\longrightarrow
\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus(n+1)}
\longrightarrow T^{1,0}\mathbb P^n
\longrightarrow0.
$$

Taking the top [exterior power](../../../linear-algebra.md#exterior-power) gives $\det T^{1,0}\mathbb P^n=\mathcal O(n+1)$; taking its [dual bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#dual-bundle) yields

$$
\boxed{K_{\mathbb P^n}\cong[H]^{\otimes(-n-1)}.}
$$

Here a negative [tensor power](../../../linear-algebra.md#tensor-power) means the corresponding positive [tensor power](../../../linear-algebra.md#tensor-power) of the [dual bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#dual-bundle).

The proposed sections over $U$ form a [sheaf](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#sheaf-mathematics): compatible maps into $L$ glue uniquely, and their projections remain $\phi$. Addition and multiplication by [holomorphic functions](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-function) are defined in the fibres of $L$, making this a sheaf of $\mathcal O_X$-modules. If $e_i$ is a [holomorphic local frame](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-local-trivialization) of $L$ on $W_i\subset Y$, every section over $U\subset\phi^{-1}(W_i)$ has the unique expression

$$
\theta(x)=a(x)e_i(\phi(x)),\qquad a\in\mathcal O_X(U).
$$

Thus the sheaf is a [locally free sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-free-sheaf) of rank one. Its [transition functions of a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#transition-function-of-a-vector-bundle) are those of $L$ composed with $\phi$, so the corresponding [pullback vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#pullback-vector-bundle) is precisely $\phi^*L$. This proves **invertibility locally and compatibility globally**.

For the local [blowup of a complex manifold at a point](../../../complex-geometry.md#blowup-of-a-complex-manifold-at-a-point), work on the projective chart $Y_i\ne0$ and write

$$
u=x_i,\qquad t_j=Y_j/Y_i\quad(j\ne i).
$$

All the incidence equations reduce to $x_j=ut_j$. Therefore $(u,(t_j)_{j\ne i})$ gives a [holomorphic coordinate](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-coordinate) chart isomorphic to $\mathbb C^n$. On an overlap with $Y_\ell\ne0$,

$$
u'=ut_\ell,\qquad t'_i=1/t_\ell,\qquad
t'_j=t_j/t_\ell\quad(j\ne i,\ell),
$$

which is a [biholomorphism](../../../complex-analysis.md#biholomorphism) where $t_\ell\ne0$. The incidence subset is closed in the product of two Hausdorff spaces and is covered by these charts; hence it is a [complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-manifold) of dimension $n$. In each chart the exceptional fibre is $u=0$, so it is a smooth [complex submanifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-submanifold), with its induced projective coordinates giving

$$
\boxed{E\cong\mathbb P^{n-1}.}
$$

Away from the origin the inverse of the projection is $x\mapsto(x,[x])$, which is [holomorphic](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-differentiability-at-a-point); the projection is therefore a [biholomorphism](../../../complex-analysis.md#biholomorphism) outside $E$.

To find the [canonical bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#canonical-bundle), pull back the nowhere-zero top form on $\mathbb C^n$. In the chart above, its [Jacobian determinant](../../../calculus.md#jacobian-determinant) gives

$$
\pi_1^*(dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_n)
=\pm u^{n-1}\,du\wedge\bigwedge_{j\ne i}dt_j.
$$

The sign depends only on the ordering of the coordinates. This is a section of $K_X$ with [divisor on a complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#divisor-on-a-complex-manifold) exactly $(n-1)E$: it has that vanishing order in every exceptional chart and no zeros elsewhere. A nonzero [meromorphic section of a holomorphic line bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#meromorphic-section-of-a-holomorphic-line-bundle) with divisor $D$ identifies its [holomorphic line bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-line-bundle) with $[D]$, by sending the local generator $1/f_i$ of $\mathcal O(D)$ to the nowhere-zero frame obtained by dividing the section by $f_i$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{K_X\cong[E]^{\otimes(n-1)}.}
$$

This also includes $n=1$, when the local map is an isomorphism and the exponent is zero.

For a general [complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-manifold), choose a coordinate neighbourhood $U$ of $P$, with $P$ sent to $0$, and replace $U$ by the inverse image of this coordinate neighbourhood in the local [blowup of a complex manifold at a point](../../../complex-geometry.md#blowup-of-a-complex-manifold-at-a-point). Glue this space to $M\setminus\{P\}$ along $U\setminus\{P\}$ using the preceding [biholomorphism](../../../complex-analysis.md#biholomorphism). The resulting charts give a [complex manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-manifold) $\widetilde M$ and a [holomorphic map](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-map) $\phi$; the exceptional fibre is $\mathbb P^{n-1}$ and the map is a [biholomorphism](../../../complex-analysis.md#biholomorphism) off that fibre. For completeness, the local projection is proper because its inverse image over a compact subset is closed in that subset times compact [Complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-space). This ensures the gluing is Hausdorff: separate points with distinct images downstairs, and points over $P$ inside the local blowup. The atlas is second countable as well.

The top [exterior power](../../../linear-algebra.md#exterior-power) of the differential defines a global [holomorphic bundle map](../../../fiber-bundle.md#holomorphic-bundle-map) $\phi^*K_M\to K_{\widetilde M}$. Equivalently, it is a section of $K_{\widetilde M}\otimes(\phi^*K_M)^{-1}$. The same local [Jacobian determinant](../../../calculus.md#jacobian-determinant) has vanishing order $n-1$ on $E$, and the differential is invertible elsewhere. Applying the [holomorphic line bundle associated to a divisor](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-line-bundle-associated-to-a-divisor) construction gives the [canonical bundle formula for a point blowup](../../../complex-geometry.md#canonical-bundle-formula-for-a-point-blowup)

$$
\boxed{K_{\widetilde M}\cong\phi^*K_M\otimes[E]^{\otimes(n-1)}.}
$$

A [connection on a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-vector-bundle) is a complex-linear map from smooth sections to vector-bundle-valued one-forms satisfying

$$
D(fs)=df\otimes s+fDs.
$$

Choose a locally finite trivializing cover, its componentwise flat [connections on a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-vector-bundle) $D_i$, and a subordinate smooth [partition of unity](../../../differential-geometry.md#partition-of-unity) $\rho_i$. The formula $D=\sum_i\rho_iD_i$ is globally meaningful: each weighted term extends by zero outside its chart. Its [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule) follows from $\sum_i\rho_i=1$, proving **every smooth complex vector bundle admits a connection**.

Extend the [connection on a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-vector-bundle) to bundle-valued [differential forms](../../../differential-form.md) by

$$
D(\beta\otimes s)=d\beta\otimes s+(-1)^{\deg\beta}\beta\wedge Ds.
$$

The [curvature form of a connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#curvature-form) is $\Theta_D=D^2$. Applying this twice to $fs$ shows that the $df$ terms cancel, so $D^2$ is tensorial and defines an $\operatorname{End}(E)$-valued two-form. With column coordinates for sections and [connection one-form](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-one-form) $A$, the local expression is $D=d+A\wedge$. Direct expansion on an arbitrary bundle-valued form gives

$$
(d+A\wedge)^2=dA\wedge+A\wedge A\wedge.
$$

The terms containing a derivative of the argument cancel by the graded [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule). Thus [Cartan curvature matrix equation](../../../fiber-bundle.md#cartan-curvature-matrix-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\Theta=dA+A\wedge A.}
$$

Here multiplication includes matrix multiplication and the [exterior product](../../../linear-algebra.md#exterior-product) of form entries. For a frame change $e'=eg$, the [connection one-form](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-one-form) and [curvature form of a connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#curvature-form) transform as

$$
A'=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg,\qquad
\Theta'=g^{-1}\Theta g.
$$

The [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) is consequently frame independent. Also

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(A\wedge A)=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\wedge A_{ji}=0:
$$

the diagonal terms vanish and the off-diagonal terms cancel in pairs. Locally $\operatorname{Tr}\Theta=d\operatorname{Tr}A$, and hence $d\operatorname{Tr}\Theta=0$. The local primitives need not agree, but the two-form does. We obtain **a global closed two-form** $\operatorname{Tr}\Theta_D$.

The [determinant connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#determinant-connection) on $\det E=\bigwedge^rE$ is defined intrinsically by

$$
D^{(r)}(s_1\wedge\cdots\wedge s_r)
=\sum_{j=1}^r s_1\wedge\cdots\wedge Ds_j\wedge\cdots\wedge s_r,
$$

where the differential-form coefficient of $Ds_j$ is placed first. In a local frame, only the diagonal components contribute to the derivative of $e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_r$, so its [connection one-form](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-one-form) is $\operatorname{Tr}A$. A line-bundle connection has curvature $d\operatorname{Tr}A$, because a scalar one-form wedges with itself to zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Theta_{D^{(r)}}=\operatorname{Tr}\Theta_D.}
$$

To see independence of the [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology) class, write $D_1-D_0=a$, a globally defined endomorphism-valued one-form. The [curvature difference formula](../../../fiber-bundle.md#curvature-difference-formula) and the same trace cancellations give the [trace curvature transgression](../../../fiber-bundle.md#trace-curvature-transgression)

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\Theta_{D_1}-\operatorname{Tr}\Theta_{D_0}
=d\operatorname{Tr}a.
$$

Their difference is globally an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form), so their [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology) classes coincide.

For the [Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#hermitian-metric-on-a-holomorphic-vector-bundle), use the displayed ordering of the local matrices and put $B=(\partial h)h^{-1}$. The identity $\bar\partial h^{-1}=-h^{-1}(\bar\partial h)h^{-1}$ gives

$$
\bar\partial B
=(\bar\partial\partial h)h^{-1}
+(\partial h)h^{-1}\wedge(\bar\partial h)h^{-1}.
$$

The plus sign comes from applying the graded [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule) to the one-form $\partial h$. By the derivative formula for a [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant),

$$
\operatorname{Tr}B=\partial\log\det h.
$$

The [Hermitian positive-definite matrix](../../../linear-algebra.md#hermitian-positive-definite-matrix) $h$ has positive real determinant, so this logarithm is an ordinary smooth real function. The [trace of Chern curvature](../../../complex-geometry.md#trace-of-chern-curvature) in these conventions is therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\bar\partial\partial\log\det h
=d(\partial\log\det h)=-\partial\bar\partial\log\det h.}
$$

It is locally an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form) and in particular closed.

A [holomorphic local frame](../../../complex-geometry.md#holomorphic-local-trivialization) change multiplies $\det h$ by $|\det g|^2$. A nowhere-zero [holomorphic function](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-function) has a local [holomorphic logarithm](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-logarithm), so $\bar\partial\partial\log|\det g|^2=0$. Thus the preceding expression for $\alpha$ is independent of the frame and glues globally. For two [Hermitian metrics on a holomorphic vector bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#hermitian-metric-on-a-holomorphic-vector-bundle), the function

$$
f=\log\frac{\det h_1}{\det h_0}
$$

is globally defined because the frame-change factors cancel. Their two-forms differ by $d(\partial f)$. Hence **the class of $\alpha$ is independent of the metric**. The two-form is generally imaginary-valued; the metric independence statement is in complex [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology), or equivalently for the real form $i\alpha$.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

Use the normalization in which a [Hodge metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-metric) is a [Kähler metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-metric) whose [Kähler form](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-form) $\omega$ represents the image of an [integral cohomology](../../../cohomology.md#integral-cohomology) class in real [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology). Equivalently, all its periods on integral two-cycles are integers. A convention using $\omega/(2\pi)$ only rescales the metric and does not change existence. An invariant metric on a [complex torus](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-torus) is one preserved by every translation; its pullback to $\mathbb C^n$ has constant coefficients.

Let $T=\mathbb C^n/\Lambda$ carry a [Hodge metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-metric). Average its [Kähler form](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-form) over translations using normalized [Haar measure](../../../measure-theory.md#haar-measure):

$$
\omega_0=\int_T t_a^*\omega\,da.
$$

This [averaging Kähler forms over a complex torus](../../../complex-geometry.md#averaging-kahler-forms-over-a-complex-torus) preserves reality, type $(1,1)$, closedness and positivity. Indeed, for any nonzero tangent vector, the quantity being averaged in $\omega(v,Jv)$ is strictly positive. Every translation is homotopic to the identity, since a path from $0$ to $a$ supplies such a homotopy. Thus $[\omega_0]=[\omega]$, as is seen either on [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology) or by integrating over two-cycles. The average is translation invariant by invariance of [Haar measure](../../../measure-theory.md#haar-measure) and determines a [Kähler metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-metric) $g_0(v,w)=\omega_0(v,Jw)$. Its class is still integral. The reverse implication is immediate. Therefore **a Hodge metric exists exactly when an invariant one does**.

Here is the [Riemann bilinear criterion for a period matrix](../../../complex-geometry.md#riemann-bilinear-criterion-for-a-period-matrix), with the signs kept explicit. For the [period matrix of a complex torus](../../../complex-geometry.md#period-matrix-of-a-complex-torus), use real coordinates $t_1,\ldots,t_{2n}$ along the lattice basis, so $z=\Omega t$. An invariant real two-form is

$$
\omega=\frac12\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}\,dt_i\wedge dt_j,\qquad Q^t=-Q.
$$

Its period on the coordinate two-torus $(i,j)$ is $Q_{ij}$; these tori generate integral second homology. Thus integrality of the class is exactly $Q\in M_{2n}(\mathbb Z)$. Positivity of a [Kähler form](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-form) makes $Q$ nonsingular. Because the lattice basis is a real basis of $\mathbb C^n$, the complex matrix

$$
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}\Omega\\\bar\Omega\end{pmatrix}
$$

is invertible: its rows recover the real and imaginary parts of the coordinates. In the coordinates $(z,\bar z)$, the matrix of the two-form is $T_0^{-t}QT_0^{-1}$, whose inverse is $T_0Q^{-1}T_0^t$.

The [differential form of type (p, q)](../../../complex-geometry.md#differential-form-of-type-p-q) condition $(1,1)$ says that the two diagonal blocks of the form matrix vanish. Since the form is nonsingular, this is equivalent to the diagonal blocks of its inverse vanishing. Because $Q$ is real, these inverse blocks vanish exactly when

$$
\Omega Q^{-1}\Omega^t=0.
$$

Set $H=-i\Omega Q^{-1}\bar\Omega^t$. Skew-symmetry and reality of $Q$ give $H^*=H$, and the full inverse matrix is

$$
T_0Q^{-1}T_0^t=
\begin{pmatrix}0&iH\\-iH^t&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Inverting these blocks identifies the two-form explicitly:

$$
\omega=i\sum_{a,b}(H^{-1})_{ba}\,dz_a\wedge d\bar z_b.
$$

For a vector with complex coordinate column $v$, evaluation yields $\omega(v,Jv)=2v^*H^{-1}v$. Hence this two-form is positive exactly when $H$ is a [Hermitian positive-definite matrix](../../../linear-algebra.md#hermitian-positive-definite-matrix). This proves both directions: an invariant [Hodge metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-metric) gives such an integral $Q$, and any such $Q$ produces a constant positive real closed $(1,1)$-form of integral periods. In particular,

$$
\boxed{T\text{ admits a Hodge metric}\iff
\exists Q\in M_{2n}(\mathbb Z),\
Q^t=-Q,\ \det Q\ne0,\
\Omega Q^{-1}\Omega^t=0,\
-i\Omega Q^{-1}\bar\Omega^t>0.}
$$

As a sign check, for $\Omega=(I,iI)$ and $Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}$ the last matrix is $2I$, and the associated form is $(i/2)\sum dz_a\wedge d\bar z_a$.

For the specified two-dimensional [complex torus](../../../complex-geometry.md#complex-torus), put

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
s=\sqrt2,\qquad A=I+sJ.
$$

The real and imaginary period vectors are independent because $\det A=3$, so they do form a full lattice. Suppose a polarization matrix $Q$ existed. Its inverse $R=Q^{-1}$ is a rational skew-symmetric matrix, and hence has the block expression

$$
R=\begin{pmatrix}aJ&B\\-B^t&cJ\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,c\in\mathbb Q,\quad B\in M_2(\mathbb Q).
$$

The first condition of the [Riemann bilinear criterion for a period matrix](../../../complex-geometry.md#riemann-bilinear-criterion-for-a-period-matrix) becomes

$$
0=\Omega R\Omega^t=(a-3c)J+i(BA^t-AB^t).
$$

Separating real and imaginary parts gives $a=3c$ and

$$
B-B^t=s(BJ+JB^t).
$$

The matrices on both sides have rational entries except for the factor $\sqrt2$. Irrationality of $\sqrt2$ therefore implies $B=B^t$ and $BJ+JB=0$. For a symmetric two-by-two matrix the latter expression is $(\operatorname{Tr}B)J$, so $\operatorname{Tr}B=0$. The candidate [Hermitian matrix](../../../hilbert-space.md#hermitian-operator) is now

$$
H=-i\Omega R\bar\Omega^t
=AB+BA^t-6icJ.
$$

Since $A=I+sJ$, $B$ is symmetric and traceless, and $J$ is skew-symmetric, $\operatorname{Tr}(AB+BA^t)=2\operatorname{Tr}B=0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}J=0$. Thus $\operatorname{Tr}H=0$. A [Hermitian positive-definite matrix](../../../linear-algebra.md#hermitian-positive-definite-matrix) has strictly positive [eigenvalues](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue), hence strictly positive [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace). This contradiction proves

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C^2/\Lambda\text{ admits no Hodge metric}.}
$$

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

Let $\mathcal A^{p,q}$ denote the [sheaf of differential forms of type (p, q)](../../../differential-form.md#sheaf-of-differential-forms-of-type-p-q) printed in the PDF. Take the pointwise [Hermitian inner product](../../../linear-algebra.md#hermitian-form) to be linear in its first argument, and use $\mathrm{vol}=\omega^n/n!$. The bidegrees in the question specify the [conjugate-linear Hodge star](../../../differential-form.md#conjugate-linear-hodge-star): it is uniquely characterized by

$$
\alpha\wedge *\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol},
\qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A^{p,q}.
$$

Thus $*(c\beta)=\bar c*\beta$, and $*$ maps bidegree $(p,q)$ to $(n-p,n-q)$. If $S$ is the complex-linear extension of the real [Hodge star operator](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-star-operator), then $*=S\circ\text{complex conjugation}$. The operator $S$ is real and $S^2=(-1)^{k(2n-k)}$ on degree $k=p+q$, so

$$
\boxed{**\psi=(-1)^{p+q}\psi.}
$$

Using the complex-linear star instead would give a different bidegree, $(n-q,n-p)$; keeping the convention explicit prevents that ambiguity.

The [Hermitian Lefschetz operator](../../../complex-geometry.md#lefschetz-operator-on-a-hermitian-manifold) is exterior multiplication by $\omega$. Define $\Lambda$ pointwise by

$$
\langle L\alpha,\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha,\Lambda\beta\rangle.
$$

It lowers bidegree by $(1,1)$. In a unitary real coframe with $\omega=\sum_j e^j\wedge f^j$, it is $\Lambda=\sum_j\iota_{f_j}\iota_{e_j}$, the corresponding sum of [interior product of a differential form](../../../differential-form.md#interior-product) operators. This gives its existence and identifies it as a smooth operator. On a compact manifold, integrate the pointwise equality against $\mathrm{vol}$ to obtain

$$
(L\alpha,\beta)_{L^2}=(\alpha,\Lambda\beta)_{L^2}.
$$

No integration by parts is needed for this order-zero operator. Thus $\Lambda$ is both the pointwise and the global [formal adjoint](../../../hilbert-space.md#formal-adjoint).

Write $[A,B]=AB-BA$, and let $k$ denote the degree of the input form. The given [Lefschetz commutator](../../../complex-geometry.md#lefschetz-commutator) is $[L,\Lambda]=(k-n)I$. For $r=1$ the required formula is exactly this identity. If it holds for $r$, the [commutator derivation identity](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator-derivation-identity) gives

$$
[L^{r+1},\Lambda]=L[L^r,\Lambda]+[L,\Lambda]L^r.
$$

The second commutator acts on degree $k+2r$, so the coefficient on the right is

$$
r(k-n)+r(r-1)+(k+2r-n)
=(r+1)(k-n)+(r+1)r.
$$

This proves the [commutator formula for powers of the Lefschetz operator](../../../complex-geometry.md#commutator-formula-for-powers-of-the-lefschetz-operator)

$$
\boxed{[L^r,\Lambda]=r(k-n+r-1)L^{r-1}.}
$$

For [injectivity of powers of the Lefschetz operator](../../../complex-geometry.md#injectivity-of-powers-of-the-lefschetz-operator), $r=0$ is immediate. Suppose $r\ge1$ and $r+k\le n$. If $k=0$ or $1$, then $\Lambda\psi=0$, and $L^r\psi=0$ implies

$$
r(k-n+r-1)L^{r-1}\psi=0.
$$

The scalar is nonzero, since $k+r\le n$ makes $k-n+r-1\le-1$. Repeating with the smaller power shows $\psi=0$. For $k\ge2$, induct on $r+k$, treating all $r=0$ cases as already established. The same commutator calculation yields, with $c=r(k-n+r-1)\ne0$,

$$
L^{r-1}(L\Lambda\psi-c\psi)=0.
$$

The induction hypothesis applies to $L^{r-1}$ on degree $k$, since $(r-1)+k<r+k$ and $(r-1)+k\le n$. Hence $\psi=L\alpha$ with $\alpha=c^{-1}\Lambda\psi$ of degree $k-2$. Moreover $L^{r+1}\alpha=L^r\psi=0$. Since $(r+1)+(k-2)=r+k-1$, induction also makes $L^{r+1}$ injective on that degree, so $\alpha=0$ and $\psi=0$. All operators preserve restriction to open sets; the argument applies on every open set. Therefore **$L^r$ is an injective morphism of sheaves whenever $r+k\le n$**.

On a [Kähler manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-manifold), the [Kähler identities](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-identities), with the positive form convention used above, are

$$
[\Lambda,\bar\partial]=-i\partial^*,\qquad
[\Lambda,\partial]=i\bar\partial^*.
$$

Taking [formal adjoints](../../../hilbert-space.md#formal-adjoint) conjugates the scalar and reverses the order in the commutator. Since $\Lambda^*=L$, this gives

$$
\boxed{[L,\bar\partial^*]=-i\partial,\qquad
[L,\partial^*]=i\bar\partial.}
$$

To derive the [Kähler Laplacian identity](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-laplacian-identity), put $P=\partial$ and $B=\bar\partial$, so $P^2=B^2=0$ and $PB+BP=0$. Write $\{A,B\}=AB+BA$ for an [anticommutator](../../../vector-space.md#anticommutator). The [Kähler identities](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-identities) say $B^*=-i[\Lambda,P]$ and $P^*=i[\Lambda,B]$. Therefore

$$
\{P,B^*\}=-i\{P,[\Lambda,P]\}=0,\qquad
\{B,P^*\}=i\{B,[\Lambda,B]\}=0,
$$

because expansion leaves only terms containing $P^2$ or $B^2$. Furthermore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_\partial&=i\{P,[\Lambda,B]\},\\
\Delta_{\bar\partial}&=-i\{B,[\Lambda,P]\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Expanding these expressions and using $PB=-BP$ makes them equal. Since $d=P+B$ and $d^*=P^*+B^*$, the mixed [anticommutators](../../../vector-space.md#anticommutator) already vanish, and consequently

$$
\boxed{\Delta_d=2\Delta_\partial=2\Delta_{\bar\partial}.}
$$

Closedness of the [Kähler form](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-form) implies $[L,P]=[L,B]=0$. Using the adjoint identity above,

$$
[L,\Delta_{\bar\partial}]
=B[L,B^*]+[L,B^*]B
=-i(BP+PB)=0.
$$

The equality of the three [Laplacians](../../../calculus.md#laplacian) therefore proves **each Laplacian commutes with $L$**.

For a compact [Kähler manifold](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-manifold), the [Dolbeault Hodge decomposition](../../../complex-geometry.md#dolbeault-hodge-decomposition-on-a-compact-hermitian-manifold) is the orthogonal decomposition

$$
\mathcal A^{p,q}(M)=
\mathcal H^{p,q}\oplus
\operatorname{im}\bar\partial\oplus
\operatorname{im}\bar\partial^*.
$$

Here harmonicity for the [Dolbeault Laplacian](../../../complex-geometry.md#dolbeault-laplacian) and for the [Hodge Laplacian](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-laplacian) agrees by the preceding identity. If $\bar\partial\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=h+\bar\partial u+\bar\partial^*v$, then $\bar\partial\bar\partial^*v=0$. Taking its inner product with $v$ gives $\|\bar\partial^*v\|^2=0$. Thus $\alpha$ is cohomologous to $h$. Conversely, a harmonic form is $\bar\partial$-closed, and a harmonic exact form $h=\bar\partial u$ satisfies $\|h\|^2=(\bar\partial^*h,u)=0$. This proves existence and uniqueness of the harmonic representative and hence

$$
\boxed{H_{\bar\partial}^{p,q}(M)\cong\mathcal H^{p,q}(M).}
$$

These spaces are finite dimensional by the ellipticity of the [Dolbeault Laplacian](../../../complex-geometry.md#dolbeault-laplacian) on the compact manifold.

Complex conjugation commutes with the real operator $\Delta_d$ and interchanges the bidegrees $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$. It is therefore a conjugate-linear bijection of the harmonic spaces, proving [Hodge symmetry](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-symmetry). The real [Hodge star operator](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-star-operator) commutes with $\Delta_d$, as does complex conjugation; hence their composition, our [conjugate-linear Hodge star](../../../differential-form.md#conjugate-linear-hodge-star), sends harmonic $(p,q)$-forms bijectively to harmonic $(n-p,n-q)$-forms. Its square is the nonzero scalar established above. This proves [Hodge duality](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-duality), and gives

$$
\boxed{h^{p,q}=h^{q,p},\qquad h^{p,q}=h^{n-p,n-q}.}
$$

For the final [hard Lefschetz isomorphism on Dolbeault cohomology](../../../complex-geometry.md#hard-lefschetz-isomorphism-on-dolbeault-cohomology), take $k=p+q\le n$ and $r=n-k$. This restriction is necessary to make the displayed power nonnegative. The [Hermitian Lefschetz operator](../../../complex-geometry.md#lefschetz-operator-on-a-hermitian-manifold) raises bidegree by $(1,1)$, and the [Lefschetz operator preserves harmonic forms](../../../complex-geometry.md#lefschetz-operator-preserves-harmonic-forms); therefore

$$
L^{n-k}:\mathcal H^{p,q}\longrightarrow
\mathcal H^{n-q,n-p}
$$

is well defined. It is injective by [injectivity of powers of the Lefschetz operator](../../../complex-geometry.md#injectivity-of-powers-of-the-lefschetz-operator), since $r+k=n$. [Hodge symmetry](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-symmetry) and [Hodge duality](../../../complex-geometry.md#hodge-duality) give $h^{n-q,n-p}=h^{q,p}=h^{p,q}$, so it is a bijection between finite-dimensional spaces of equal dimension. Because $[L,\bar\partial]=0$, the map on harmonic representatives agrees with exterior multiplication by $[\omega]^{n-k}$ on [Dolbeault cohomology](../../../complex-geometry.md#dolbeault-cohomology). We conclude

$$
\boxed{L^{n-p-q}:H_{\bar\partial}^{p,q}(M)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H_{\bar\partial}^{n-q,n-p}(M),
\qquad p+q\le n.}
$$

For $p+q>n$, the corresponding valid statement is the inverse of the positive-power isomorphism from bidegree $(n-q,n-p)$ to $(p,q)$; a negative exterior-multiplication power is not defined.
