<h1 id="10e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Since $f\prime\prime\prime\lt 0$, $f\prime\prime$ decreases from $0$, so $f\prime$ strictly decreases from $1$ and $f(x)\lt x$. If $f\prime$ never vanished, its decreasing positive [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) would make $f$ stay positive, but $f\prime\prime\lt 0$ bounded away from zero after any fixed point forces $f\prime$ negative eventually. Thus $f$ rises to one maximum and then decreases strictly to $-\infty$, crossing zero exactly once; it is positive before that crossing.
