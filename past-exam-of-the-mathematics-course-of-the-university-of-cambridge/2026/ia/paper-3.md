## 2D

↑ **Parent:** [2D](#2d)

<h4 id="2d/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2d/a)

$Z(G)=\{z:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$; it is closed under products and inverses, abelian, and invariant under conjugation. If $K=\{e,k\}$ is normal, conjugation fixes its unique nonidentity element $k$, so $k\in Z(G)$. Thus the statement is true.

## 3A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="3a/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3A](#3a)

$s(t)=\int^t|\dot x(u)|du$, $t=dx/ds$, $\kappa=|dt/ds|$, and $n=\kappa^{-1}dt/ds$. Differentiating $t\cdot t=1$ proves $t\perp n$. Here $|\dot x|=2e^t$ and

$$
t=\tfrac12(\cos t-\sin t,\sin t+\cos t,\sqrt2),\quad n=\frac{(-2\sin t,2\cos t,\sqrt2)}{\sqrt{10}},\quad \kappa=\frac{\sqrt{10}}{4e^t}.
$$

## 4A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="4a/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4A](#4a)

[Divergence theorem](../../../calculus.md#divergence-theorem) says $\int_V\nabla\cdot F\,dV=\int_{\partial V}F\cdot n\,dS$ for the outward normal. Here $\nabla\cdot(xe^{-r^2})=(3-2r^2)e^{-r^2}$, so radial integration gives $4\pi a^3e^{-a^2}$. On the sphere $F\cdot n=ae^{-a^2}$, whose [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) is the same $4\pi a^3e^{-a^2}$.

## 5D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="5d/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5D](#5d)

<h4 id="5d/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5d/a)

The order of $g$ is the least positive $r$ with $g^r=e$. Division $n=qr+s$ shows $g^n=e$ forces $s=0$. [Lagrange theorem](../../../group-theory.md#lagrange-s-theorem) follows because left cosets of a [subgroup](../../../group.md#subgroup) partition $G$ into equal blocks; applying it to $\langle g\rangle$ shows $|g|\mid|G|$. Coset multiplication makes $G/N$ a [group](../../../group.md) when $N\triangleleft G$. If $x\in N$, then $x^n=e$. Conversely, if $x^n=e$, the order of $xN$ divides both $m$ and $n$, hence is one, so $x\in N$.

<h3 id="5d/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5D](#5d)

<h4 id="5d/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5d/b)

Every $b^m$ lies in $N$ because $(bN)^m=N$. Conversely choose $r$ with $mr\equiv1\pmod n$; for $a\in N$, $a=(a^r)^m$ because $a^n=e$.

## 6D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="6d/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6D](#6d)

<h4 id="6d/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#6d/i)

$GL_2(\mathbb C)$ consists of invertible $2\times2$ complex [matrices](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) and $SL_2(\mathbb C)=\ker\det$. It is normal, and the first isomorphism theorem gives $GL_2/SL_2\cong\mathbb C^*$.

<h3 id="6d/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6D](#6d)

<h4 id="6d/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#6d/ii)

The Möbius [group](../../../group.md) consists of maps $z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)$ with $ad-bc\ne0$, including the usual action on $\infty$. [Scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar) [matrices](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) act trivially and are the whole kernel, so $M\cong GL_2(\mathbb C)/(\mathbb C^*I)$.

<h3 id="6d/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6D](#6d)

<h4 id="6d/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#6d/iii)

The map $z\mapsto z-w$ sends $w$ to $0$, proving transitivity (with inversion handling $\infty$). Fixed points satisfy the quadratic $cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0$, with multiplicity and $\infty$ included, so a nonidentity has one or two. The stabiliser of $\infty$ is $S=\{z\mapsto az+b:a\ne0\}$.

<h3 id="6d/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6D](#6d)

<h4 id="6d/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#6d/iv)

Choose a fixed point $p$ of a nonidentity transformation and conjugate by a Möbius map carrying $p$ to $\infty$; the conjugate lies in $S$. The identity already lies there.

## 7D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="7d/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7D](#7d)

An action is a map $G\times X\to X$ satisfying $ex=x$ and $(gh)x=g(hx)$; it induces $G\to S_X$. Acting on the $n$ left cosets of $H$ gives a homomorphism with kernel $K\triangleleft G$ contained in $H$. If $K$ were trivial, $|G|$ would divide $n!$, so under the stated hypothesis $K$ is nontrivial. For $[G:L]=p$ with $p$ the least prime divisor of $|G|$, the transitive image in $S_p$ has order divisible by $p$; its other possible prime divisors are smaller, hence absent, so its order is $p$. Thus the kernel has index $p$ and, being contained in $L$, equals $L$. Prime index alone is insufficient: a [subgroup](../../../group.md#subgroup) generated by a transposition in $S_3$ has index $3$ and is not normal.

## 8D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="8d/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8D](#8d)

Disjoint-cycle decomposition follows by partitioning the set into permutation orbits. Every cycle $(a_1\ldots a_r)$ equals $(a_1a_r)\cdots(a_1a_2)$, so transpositions generate $S_n$; $A_n$ is the kernel of the sign map. For even $m$, an $m$-cycle is odd. Products of pairs of $m$-cycles generate $A_n$ (express the standard generators $(ab)(ac)$ using the hinted overlapping cycles), while adjoining any one $m$-cycle yields all of $S_n$. Thus the set of all $m$-cycles generates $S_n$. For odd $m$, every $m$-cycle is even, so they cannot generate $S_n$.

## 9A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="9a/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9A](#9a)

<h4 id="9a/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#9a/a)

Using $\varepsilon_{ijk}\varepsilon_{klm}=\delta_{il}\delta_{jm}-\delta_{im}\delta_{jl}$,  
$[\nabla\times(F\times G)]_i=\partial_j(F_iG_j)-\partial_j(F_jG_i)$; expanding the [derivatives](../../../calculus.md#derivative) gives the stated four terms.

<h3 id="9a/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9A](#9a)

<h4 id="9a/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#9a/b)

Irrotational means $\nabla\times E=0$. For $E=\nabla f$, its $i$th curl component is $\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial_j\partial_kf=0$ because mixed partials are symmetric.

<h3 id="9a/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9A](#9a)

<h4 id="9a/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#9a/c)

<h5 id="9a/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#9a/c/i)

On $C$, $dx=a e_\phi d\phi$, so $I=\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi=2\pi$. Locally $f=\phi$, but no single-valued global potential exists on the punctured domain; this is why irrotationality does not force this closed [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) to vanish.

<h4 id="9a/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#9a/c)

<h5 id="9a/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#9a/c/ii)

The field is $\nabla f$ for $f=\rho\sin\phi\,e^{-\rho^2-z^2}$. Hence $I=0$, either directly or by the [fundamental theorem for line integrals](../../../calculus.md#fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals).

[Stokes theorem](../../../calculus.md#stokes-theorem) is $\int_S(\nabla\times F)\cdot n\,dS=\oint_{\partial S}F\cdot dx$, with the boundary orientation given by the right-hand rule relative to $n$.

↑ **Parent:** [10A](#10a)

<h4 id="10a/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#10a/b)

$\nabla\times F=(3y^2,3x^2,3x^2+3y^2)$, whose divergence is $0$.

<h3 id="10a/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10A](#10a)

<h4 id="10a/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#10a/c)

Take the outward normal on the cylindrical side and the upward normal on the top disc. The induced boundary direction on the bottom circle is clockwise as viewed from above.

<h3 id="10a/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10A](#10a)

<h4 id="10a/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#10a/d)

The clockwise boundary [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) at $z=0$ is  
$-\int_0^{2\pi}a^4(\sin^4\phi+\cos^4\phi)d\phi=-3\pi a^4/2$. Direct integration of the curl over the side and top gives the same value (the $h$-dependent terms cancel), verifying Stokes.

<h3 id="10a/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [10A](#10a)

<h4 id="10a/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#10a/e)

$S\prime$ has the same bottom boundary and induced orientation, so Stokes’ theorem makes the answer independent of its shape: $-3\pi a^4/2$.

## 11A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="11a/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [11A](#11a)

<h4 id="11a/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#11a/a)

For two solutions let $u$ be their difference. Green’s identity and $\partial_nu=0$ give $0=\int_Vu(\nabla^2u-m^2u)=-\int_V(|\nabla u|^2+m^2u^2)$, so $u=0$ when $m\ne0$. At $m=0$, solutions are unique only up to an additive constant.

<h3 id="11a/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [11A](#11a)

<h4 id="11a/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#11a/b)

<h5 id="11a/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#11a/b/i)

On a simply connected region, $\nabla\times E=0$ gives $E=-\nabla\phi$. Then [Gauss's law](../../../electromagnetism.md#gauss-s-law) gives $\nabla^2\phi=-\rho/\varepsilon_0$.

<h4 id="11a/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#11a/b)

<h5 id="11a/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#11a/b/ii)

Writing $r=|x|$ and imposing continuity gives

$$
\phi(r)=\frac{\rho_0}{2\varepsilon_0}\begin{cases}2(b-a),&r\lt a,\\2b-r-a^2/r,&a\le r\le b,\\(b^2-a^2)/r,&r\gt b.\end{cases}
$$

<h4 id="11a/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#11a/b)

<h5 id="11a/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#11a/b/iii)

[Gauss's law](../../../electromagnetism.md#gauss-s-law) gives $E=Q\hat r/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)$ outside any spherically symmetric charge distribution. The shell in (ii) has $Q=2\pi\rho_0(b^2-a^2)$, and differentiating its exterior potential gives exactly this field.

## 12A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="12a/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](#12a)

<h4 id="12a/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#12a/a)

A rank-$k$ Cartesian [tensor](../../../linear-algebra.md#tensor) has components $T_{i_1\ldots i_k}$ transforming under an orthogonal change of [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $R$ as $T\prime_{i_1\ldots i_k}=R_{i_1j_1}\cdots R_{i_kj_k}T_{j_1\ldots j_k}$.

<h3 id="12a/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](#12a)

<h4 id="12a/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#12a/b)

Contracting one index from each tensor means summing them, for example $C_{i_1\ldots i_{p-1}j_1\ldots j_{q-1}}=A_{i_1\ldots i_{p-1}r}B_{rj_1\ldots j_{q-1}}$. Its rank is $p+q-2$. Substitution of the transformation laws for $p=q=3$ and $R_{rs}R_{rt}=\delta_{st}$ proves the tensor law.

<h3 id="12a/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](#12a)

<h4 id="12a/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#12a/c)

Isotropic means invariant under every proper rotation. The general ranks $0,1,2,3$ are respectively a [scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar) $a$, zero, $b\delta_{ij}$, and $c\varepsilon_{ijk}$ (for full orthogonal invariance the last must also vanish).

<h3 id="12a/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](#12a)

<h4 id="12a/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#12a/d)

<h5 id="12a/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

Rotational invariance makes this an isotropic [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector), hence it is $0$; oddness gives the same result.

<h4 id="12a/d/ii">ii</h4>

It equals $C\delta_{ij}$. Taking the trace gives $3C=4\pi a^4e^{-a^2}$, hence $C=4\pi a^4e^{-a^2}/3$.

<h4 id="12a/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#12a/d)

<h5 id="12a/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#12a/d/iii)

This isotropic rank-three [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) is $0$ by oddness under $x\mapsto-x$.

<h3 id="12a/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](#12a)

<h4 id="12a/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#12a/e)

Expanding the integrand, the terms linear in $x$ integrate to zero. Therefore

$$
T_{ij}=\frac{4\pi a^4e^{-a^2}}3\delta_{ij}+4\pi a^2e^{-a^2}u_iv_j,
$$

so these are $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
