<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For $\psi_0=(r^2+z^2)/2$,

$$
\psi_{0,r}=r,
\qquad
\psi_{0,z}=z,
\qquad
D=1+r^2+z^2.
$$

The prescribed function is $g=\sqrt D$, so part c gives $u_z=-z$. Hence the [non-characteristic condition](../../../../../../non-characteristic-hypersurface.md) reduces to

$$
1-(1-z)^2(r^2+z^2)\ne0.
$$

The [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) therefore guarantees a unique local [real-analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) solution at exactly those points

$$
(t_0,r_0,z_0)
=\left(\frac{r_0^2+z_0^2}{2},r_0,z_0\right),
\qquad r_0>0,
$$

for which

$$
1-(1-z_0)^2(r_0^2+z_0^2)\ne0.
$$
