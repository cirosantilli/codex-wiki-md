## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Take the [weighted projective plane](../../../toric-geometry.md#weighted-projective-plane) $X=\mathbb P(1,1,2)$. Its [fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#fan-in-toric-geometry) in $N=\mathbb Z^2$ has rays generated by

$$
v_0=(-1,-2),\qquad v_1=(1,0),\qquad v_2=(0,1),
$$

and all three two-dimensional cones spanned by adjacent rays. Their union is $N_{\mathbb R}$, so the fan is [complete](../../../toric-geometry.md#complete-fan) and the [toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-variety) $X$ is [proper](../../../toric-geometry.md#proper-toric-variety).

The determinants of the cones $\langle v_1,v_2\rangle$ and $\langle v_2,v_0\rangle$ have absolute value one, while

$$
|\det(v_0,v_1)|=2.
$$

The [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety) therefore shows that the affine chart for $\langle v_0,v_1\rangle$ is singular; it is the cyclic quotient singularity of type $\frac12(1,1)$. Thus $X$ is a singular proper toric surface.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Insert the primitive ray

$$
v_3=(0,-1)=\frac12(v_0+v_1)
$$

inside the singular cone. The corresponding [star subdivision](../../../toric-geometry.md#star-subdivision) replaces $\langle v_0,v_1\rangle$ by $\langle v_0,v_3\rangle$ and $\langle v_3,v_1\rangle$. Both new determinants have absolute value one, as do the two unchanged cones, so the subdivided fan is smooth. The induced proper birational [toric morphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-morphism) $\widetilde X\to X$ is therefore a [toric resolution of singularities](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-resolution-of-singularities). Its exceptional invariant curve has self-intersection $-2$, and $\widetilde X$ is the [Hirzebruch surface](../../../toric-geometry.md#hirzebruch-surface) $\mathbb F_2$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

On $\mathbb F_2$, let $S$ be the [negative section of a Hirzebruch surface](../../../toric-geometry.md#negative-section-of-a-hirzebruch-surface), so $S^2=-2$, and let $F$ be the [fiber class of a Hirzebruch surface](../../../toric-geometry.md#fiber-class-of-a-hirzebruch-surface), with $F^2=0$ and $S\mathbin{\cdot}F=1$. The [line bundle](../../../ringed-space.md#line-bundle) $\mathcal O(S+3F)$ is very ample. Indeed, the toric ampleness criterion for $aS+bF$ is

$$
a>0,\qquad b-2a>0,
$$

and on a smooth complete toric variety every ample line bundle is [very ample](../../../ringed-space.md#very-ample-line-bundle). These inequalities hold for $(a,b)=(1,3)$.

Explicitly, after choosing the standard lattice coordinates for $\mathbb F_2$, its [lattice polytope of a toric divisor](../../../toric-geometry.md#lattice-polytope-of-a-toric-divisor) is

$$
P_{S+3F}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,0),(1,1),(0,1)\}.
$$

The monomials indexed by the lattice points of this polygon separate torus orbits and tangent directions, so their [Kodaira map](../../../ringed-space.md#kodaira-map) is a closed embedding. This directly verifies that the resolved proper toric surface is projective.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Write the invariant prime divisors in the order of the rays

$$
v_1=(1,0),\quad v_2=(0,1),\quad v_3=(-1,k),\quad v_4=(0,-1)
$$

as $D_1,\ldots,D_4$. The [toric divisor class sequence](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-divisor-class-sequence) is

$$
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^4
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb F_k)\longrightarrow0,
$$

and the two characters in the standard basis of $M$ give

$$
D_1-D_3=0,
\qquad
D_2+kD_3-D_4=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb F_k)
\cong\mathbb Z[S]\oplus\mathbb Z[F],
$$

where $S=D_2$, $F=D_1=D_3$, and $D_4=S+kF$. Since the fan is smooth, this is also the [Picard group](../../../ringed-space.md#picard-group).

The [orbit-cone correspondence](../../../toric-geometry.md#orbit-cone-correspondence) decomposes $\mathbb F_k$ into one two-dimensional torus, four one-dimensional torus orbits, and four fixed points. Only the zero-dimensional orbits contribute to the compactly supported [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic), so

$$
\boxed{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb F_k)=4.}
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The [Cox construction](../../../toric-geometry.md#cox-construction) uses coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_4$ of degrees

$$
\deg x_1=\deg x_3=F,
\qquad
\deg x_2=S,
\qquad
\deg x_4=S+kF.
$$

Its irrelevant locus is

$$
V(x_1,x_3)\cup V(x_2,x_4),
$$

so put $U=\mathbb A^4\setminus\bigl(V(x_1,x_3)\cup V(x_2,x_4)\bigr)$. The [algebraic torus](../../../toric-geometry.md#algebraic-torus) $G=(\mathbb G_m)^2$ acts by

$$
(\lambda,\mu)\mathbin{\cdot}(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
=(\mu x_1,\lambda x_2,\mu x_3,\lambda\mu^k x_4).
$$

Every point of $U$ has a nonzero coordinate from each opposing pair, so the action has the expected closed orbits and trivial stabilizers. The [geometric quotient](../../../toric-geometry.md#geometric-quotient) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb F_k=U/G.}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Take the invariant divisor $D_1$, whose class is $F$. Its global sections are represented in the [Cox ring](../../../toric-geometry.md#cox-ring) by $x_1$ and $x_3$. They have no common zero on $U$, so $F$ is [basepoint-free](../../../cartier-divisor.md#basepoint-free-divisor). Its [Kodaira map](../../../ringed-space.md#kodaira-map) is

$$
[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\longmapsto[x_1:x_3]\in\mathbb P^1.
$$

This is exactly the ruling $\mathbb F_k=\mathbb P_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(k))\to\mathbb P^1$, and its fibers have divisor class $F$.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The toric morphism $\mathbb A^2_{x,y}\to\mathbb A^1_t$ with $t=xy$ is induced on cocharacter lattices by

$$
\varphi:N=\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad
(a,b)\longmapsto a+b.
$$

The fan of $\mathbb A^2$ is the cone $\langle e_1,e_2\rangle$ and its faces, while the fan of $\mathbb A^1$ is $\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ and its zero face. The [blowup of the affine plane at the origin](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#blowup-of-the-affine-plane-at-the-origin) is the [star subdivision](../../../toric-geometry.md#star-subdivision) obtained by inserting the ray through $e_1+e_2$; its maximal cones are

$$
\langle e_1,e_1+e_2\rangle,
\qquad
\langle e_1+e_2,e_2\rangle.
$$

Because $\varphi(e_1)=\varphi(e_2)=1$ and $\varphi(e_1+e_2)=2$, every cone maps into $\mathbb R_{\geq0}$, giving the composite toric morphism $f:X\to\mathbb A^1$.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For a torus character $\chi^m$, its [principal divisor on a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#principal-divisor-on-a-toric-variety) is

$$
\operatorname{div}(\chi^m)=\sum_\rho\langle m,v_\rho\rangle D_\rho.
$$

Here $t=xy=\chi^{(1,1)}$, so on the blown-up fan

$$
\operatorname{div}(f^*t)=D_{e_1}+2D_{e_1+e_2}+D_{e_2}.
$$

The [scheme-theoretic fiber](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#scheme-theoretic-fiber) $X_0=V(f^*t)$ therefore contains the exceptional divisor $D_{e_1+e_2}$ with multiplicity two. Its defining ideal is not radical along that divisor, so $X_0$ is a [nonreduced scheme](../../../ringed-space.md#nonreduced-scheme).

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

The two standard [affine](../../../ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) charts of the blowup restrict to an affine open cover of $X_0$. On the $x$-chart write $y=xu$. Since $xy=x^2u$, the fiber is

$$
U_x\cap X_0=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,u]/(x^2u).
$$

On the $y$-chart write $x=yv$, giving

$$
U_y\cap X_0=\operatorname{Spec}k[y,v]/(y^2v).
$$

On their overlap, $u$ and $v$ are invertible with $v=u^{-1}$ and $y=xu$. The squared factors $x^2$ and $y^2$ display the doubled exceptional component explicitly.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Let $q:N_{\mathbb R}\to N(\tau)_{\mathbb R}=N_{\mathbb R}/\operatorname{span}(\tau)$ be the quotient map. The images $q(\sigma)$ for $\tau\preceq\sigma$ satisfy the face and intersection axioms, so $\operatorname{Star}(\tau)$ is a fan.

To prove completeness, take $\bar v\in N(\tau)_{\mathbb R}$, choose a lift $v$, and choose $w$ in the relative interior of $\tau$. Since $\Sigma$ is complete, each $v+mw$ lies in some cone of $\Sigma$. There are finitely many cones, so one cone $\sigma$ contains $v+m_jw$ for an unbounded sequence $m_j$. Closedness gives $w\in\sigma$. Because $w$ lies in the relative interior of the cone $\tau$ and $\sigma\cap\tau$ is a face of $\tau$, this forces $\tau\preceq\sigma$. Finally,

$$
\bar v=q(v+m_jw)\in q(\sigma).
$$

**Thus every point of the quotient lies in the support of $\operatorname{Star}(\tau)$, proving that it is a [complete fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#complete-fan).**

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

Let a complete fan in a $d$-dimensional real vector space have ray generators $v_1,\ldots,v_r$. Every cone of the fan lies in the positive hull $\operatorname{cone}(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$. If $r<d$, these vectors do not span the space. If $r=d$, either they still fail to span, or they are linearly independent and their positive hull is a proper strictly convex cone. In either case their positive hull cannot be the whole vector space, contrary to completeness. Hence $r\geq d+1$.

The bound is attained: take rays through

$$
e_1,\ldots,e_d,-(e_1+\cdots+e_d)
$$

and cones generated by every proper subset of these rays. This is the [simplex fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#simplex-fan), which is complete and has $d+1$ rays. Therefore the minimal number is $d+1=\dim N_{\mathbb R}+1$.
