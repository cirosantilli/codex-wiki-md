<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose a small ball $B$ whose translates by distinct lattice elements are disjoint. The restrictions of the quotient map $\pi:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^n/\Lambda$ to translates of $B$ supply holomorphic charts, because every transition map is a complex translation. A closed fundamental parallelepiped is compact and surjects onto the quotient, so the resulting [complex torus](../../../../../../complex-torus.md) $X$ is compact.

The standard form

$$
\omega_0=\frac i2\sum_{j=1}^ndz_j\wedge d\overline z_j
$$

is translation invariant and therefore descends uniquely to a form $\omega_X$ with $\pi^*\omega_X=\omega_0$. It remains closed, of type $(1,1)$, and positive, so it is a [Kähler form](../../../../../../kahler-manifold.md).
