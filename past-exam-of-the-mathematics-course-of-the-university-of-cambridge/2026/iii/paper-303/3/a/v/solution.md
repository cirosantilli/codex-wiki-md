<h1 id="3/a/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

For $d>4$, the quartic coupling has $y_g=4-d<0$, but setting it to zero removes the stabilizing term. It is a [dangerously irrelevant coupling](../../../../../../../dangerously-irrelevant-coupling.md). At $t=0$, minimizing $g\phi^4-B\phi$ gives $\phi\sim(B/g)^{1/3}$ and $\delta=3$. This is consistent with Gaussian scaling because

$$
\frac{\Delta_B-y_g}{3}
=\frac{(d+2)/2-(4-d)}3
=\frac{d-2}{2}=\Delta_\phi.
$$

For $d<4$, $g$ flows to a nonzero [Wilson-Fisher fixed point](../../../../../../../wilson-fisher-fixed-point.md), so no dangerously irrelevant zero-coupling limit spoils the ordinary [scaling relations](../../../../../../../scaling-relation-for-critical-exponents.md).
