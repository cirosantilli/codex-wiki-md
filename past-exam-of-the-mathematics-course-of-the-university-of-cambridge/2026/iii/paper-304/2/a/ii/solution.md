<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use [dimensional regularization](../../../../../../../dimensional-regularization.md) with $d=4-\epsilon$ and choose [Feynman gauge](../../../../../../../feynman-gauge.md). Combining the electron and photon denominators with a [Feynman parameter](../../../../../../../feynman-parameter.md), shifting the loop momentum, and discarding the odd term leaves the numerator

$$
(2-d)(1-x)\gamma^\mu p_\mu+dm.
$$

The pole of the rotationally symmetric integral consequently gives

$$
\Sigma_{\rm pole}(p)
=\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}
\int_0^1dx\,[-2(1-x)\gamma^\mu p_\mu+4m]
=\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}(-\gamma^\mu p_\mu+4m).
$$

With the inverse-propagator convention

$$
S^{-1}(p)=\gamma^\mu p_\mu-m+\delta_2\gamma^\mu p_\mu-\delta_m-\Sigma(p),
$$

the [minimal subtraction scheme](../../../../../../../minimal-subtraction-scheme.md) chooses the counterterm pole to equal $\Sigma_{\rm pole}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\delta_2=-\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon},
\qquad
\delta_m=-\frac{me^2}{2\pi^2\epsilon}.}
$$
