<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Regard $\lambda$ as a [running coupling](../../../../../../running-coupling.md) $\lambda(M)$. At fixed $s,t,u$ and to order $\lambda^2$, differentiating the one-loop answer with respect to $\log M$ gives

$$
0=\frac{d\Gamma_4}{d\log M}
=\frac{d\lambda}{d\log M}
-\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\int_0^1 6\,dx+O(\lambda^3),
$$

because $m\ll M$ implies $F(M^2)\simeq-x(1-x)M^2$. Thus the [beta function](../../../../../../beta-function-physics.md) is

$$
\beta(\lambda)=\frac{d\lambda}{d\log M}
=\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2}+O(\lambda^3).
$$

Solving this [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) with $\lambda(M_0)=\lambda_0$ gives

$$
\frac1{\lambda(M)}=\frac1{\lambda_0}
-\frac3{16\pi^2}\log\frac{M}{M_0},
\qquad
\lambda(M)=\frac{\lambda_0}{1-\dfrac{3\lambda_0}{16\pi^2}\log(M/M_0)}
$$

to leading-logarithmic order.
