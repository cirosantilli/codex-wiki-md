<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

For

$$
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_1^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

the two basic particles have bosonic self-statistics and full mutual-braid phase $-1$. Moreover $|\det K_1|=4$. These are the electric and magnetic particles of the [surface code](../../../../../../surface-code.md).

For

$$
K_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_2^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

the first basic particle is a fermion, the second is a boson, and they are [mutual semions](../../../../../../mutual-semion.md); again $|\det K_2|=4$. They can be identified with $f=e\times m$ and $m$, so this is merely another integral basis for the same surface-code phase.

Finally,

$$
|\det K_3|
=\det\begin{pmatrix}4&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}
=12,
$$

which already rules it out. Therefore

$$
\boxed{K_1\ \text{and}\ K_2\ \text{describe the surface code};\quad K_3\ \text{does not}.}
$$
