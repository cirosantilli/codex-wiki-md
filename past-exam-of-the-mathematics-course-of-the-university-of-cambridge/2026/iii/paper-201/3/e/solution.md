<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

By [Brownian scaling](../../../../../../brownian-scaling.md),

$$
|B_t|^{-1}\stackrel d=
t^{-1/2}|Z+t^{-1/2}B_0|^{-1},
$$

where $Z$ has the standard three-dimensional [multivariate normal distribution](../../../../../../multivariate-normal-distribution.md). The right-hand side tends to zero in [probability](../../../../../../convergence-in-probability.md), since $Z$ has no atom at the origin. Part (d) gives almost-sure convergence to $Y$, which also implies convergence in probability to $Y$. [Uniqueness of a limit in probability](../../../../../../uniqueness-of-a-limit-in-probability.md) therefore gives $Y=0$ almost surely. Hence $|B_t|\to\infty$ almost surely, proving the [transience of Brownian motion in dimension at least three](../../../../../../transience-of-brownian-motion-in-dimension-at-least-three.md) in dimension three.
