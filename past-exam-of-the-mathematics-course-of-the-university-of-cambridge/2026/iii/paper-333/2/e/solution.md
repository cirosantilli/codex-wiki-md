<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Because $v=\psi_x=(g/f_0)\eta_x$, the mean boundary-current velocity is exactly related to the height change by

$$
\boxed{
\bar v=\frac1\delta\int_0^\delta v\,dx
=\frac g{f_0\delta}\Delta\eta_{\rm WBC}}.
$$

For the e-folding convention used in part d,

$$
\bar v
=A\left[\frac L\delta(1-e^{-1})-1\right].
$$

Its robust narrow-layer scaling is

$$
\boxed{
\bar v\sim
\frac{L\,\tau'(y)}{\rho H_0^2\gamma}}.
$$

Thus weaker drag makes the current proportionally narrower and faster. Their product is independent of $\gamma$ to leading order:

$$
H_0\delta\bar v
\sim\frac{f_0L}{\beta\rho H_0}\tau'(y)
=-T_{\rm int}.
$$

[Mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) requires the narrow return transport to cancel the broad [Sverdrup balance](../../../../../../sverdrup-balance.md) transport. The meridional gradient of planetary [potential vorticity](../../../../../../potential-vorticity.md) makes a frictional closure possible on the western side and produces western intensification; bottom drag supplies the vorticity sink that permits fluid parcels to cross potential-vorticity contours there.
