<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the cover $\mathcal U=\{X_1,X_2\}$ of the [affine plane with doubled origin](../../../../../../affine-plane-with-doubled-origin.md), the overlap is the [punctured affine plane](../../../../../../punctured-affine-plane.md) $U$. Since $H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)=k[x,y]$, the Čech complex begins

$$
k[x,y]\oplus k[x,y]\longrightarrow k[x,y],
\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto b-a.
$$

This map is surjective, and the normalized complex has no terms in degrees at least two. Consequently

$$
\check H^p(\mathcal U,\mathcal O_Y)=0\qquad(p>0).
$$

The [Mayer-Vietoris sequence for sheaf cohomology](../../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence-for-sheaf-cohomology.md) also gives $H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0$, but its next part gives

$$
H^2(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\cong H^1(U,\mathcal O_U).
$$

Part b with $f=1$ shows that the group on the right is infinite-dimensional. Thus

$$
\check H^2(\mathcal U,\mathcal O_Y)=0
\ne H^2(Y,\mathcal O_Y).
$$

This does not contradict the [acyclic cover theorem](../../../../../../leray-s-theorem.md). Although $X_1$ and $X_2$ are affine, their intersection $U$ is not acyclic: it has nonzero first structure-sheaf cohomology. Equivalently, this affine cover does not satisfy the theorem's hypotheses; the doubled-origin plane is not a [semi-separated scheme](../../../../../../semi-separated-scheme.md).
