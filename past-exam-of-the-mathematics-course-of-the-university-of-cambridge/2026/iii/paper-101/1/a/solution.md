<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Tensor-hom adjunction](../../../../../../tensor-hom-adjunction.md) is the natural isomorphism

$$
\Phi_{M,N,P}:\operatorname{Hom}_R(M\otimes_RN,P)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_R\bigl(M,\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,P)\bigr).
$$

For an [R-module homomorphism](../../../../../../module-homomorphism.md) $f$, it is given explicitly by

$$
\Phi(f)(m)(n)=f(m\otimes n).
$$

Conversely, an $R$-linear map $g:M\to\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,P)$ determines the [balanced map](../../../../../../balanced-map.md) $(m,n)\mapsto g(m)(n)$, so the [universal property of the tensor product of modules](../../../../../../universal-property-of-the-tensor-product-of-modules.md) gives

$$
\Phi^{-1}(g)(m\otimes n)=g(m)(n).
$$

These formulas are inverse to each other because pure tensors generate the [tensor product of modules](../../../../../../tensor-product-of-modules.md).
