<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Second uniqueness theorem for primary decomposition](../../../../../../second-uniqueness-theorem-for-primary-decomposition.md) says that in a minimal primary decomposition of an ideal in a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), every primary component belonging to an isolated prime is unique. Here an isolated prime is a minimal member of the set $\{\sqrt{Q_i}\}$.

Let $\mathfrak p=\sqrt{Q_i}$ be isolated and apply [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md). If $j\ne i$, minimality of $\mathfrak p$ gives $\sqrt{Q_j}\nsubseteq\mathfrak p$, so some element of $Q_j$ becomes a unit in $R_{\mathfrak p}$. Consequently

$$
IR_{\mathfrak p}=Q_iR_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

Because $Q_i$ is $\mathfrak p$-primary, multiplication by any $s\notin\mathfrak p$ cannot carry an element outside $Q_i$ into $Q_i$. Therefore

$$
Q_i=Q_iR_{\mathfrak p}\cap R=IR_{\mathfrak p}\cap R.
$$

The right side depends only on $I$ and $\mathfrak p$, proving uniqueness.
