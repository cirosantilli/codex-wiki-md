<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Pass to $A=R/I$. It is enough to prove that the zero ideal of $A[t]$ is primary. The zero ideal of $A$ is primary, so every [zero divisor](../../../../../../zero-divisor.md) of $A$ is [nilpotent element](../../../../../../nilpotent.md).

Suppose $fg=0$ in $A[t]$ with $g\ne0$. By [McCoy theorem](../../../../../../mccoy-theorem.md), some nonzero $a\in A$ satisfies $af=0$. Hence every coefficient of $f$ is a zero divisor and therefore nilpotent. There are only finitely many coefficients, so the ideal they generate is nilpotent; consequently some power of $f$ is zero. This proves that $(0)$ is primary in $A[t]$, and the [coefficientwise quotient of a polynomial ring](../../../../../../coefficientwise-quotient-of-a-polynomial-ring.md)

$$
R[t]/I^e\cong(R/I)[t]
$$

shows that $I^e$ is primary.
