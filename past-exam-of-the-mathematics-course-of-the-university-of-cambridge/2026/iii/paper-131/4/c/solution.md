<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose for a contradiction that $Y\times\mathbb R$ carries a complete [Ricci-flat metric](../../../../../../ricci-flat-riemannian-manifold.md). Since $Y$ is closed, the two subsets $Y\times(A,\infty)$ and $Y\times(-\infty,-A)$ are different unbounded components outside the compact set $Y\times[-A,A]$. Thus $Y\times\mathbb R$ is [disconnected at infinity](../../../../../../disconnected-at-infinity.md) and, by part (a), contains a [line in a Riemannian manifold](../../../../../../line-in-a-riemannian-manifold.md).

Its [Ricci curvature](../../../../../../ricci-curvature.md) is zero, so the [Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem](../../../../../../cheeger-gromoll-splitting-theorem.md) gives an isometry

$$
Y\times\mathbb R\cong N^3\times\mathbb R.
$$

The product Ricci tensor shows that $N$ is a complete three-dimensional Ricci-flat manifold. By the allowed fact, $N$ is [flat](../../../../../../flat-manifold.md), and hence so is $Y\times\mathbb R$.

The [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) of a complete flat manifold is complete, simply connected, and has zero [sectional curvature](../../../../../../sectional-curvature.md). The [Hadamard-Cartan theorem](../../../../../../cartan-hadamard-theorem.md) therefore identifies it diffeomorphically with $\mathbb R^4$, so it is contractible. On the other hand, the universal cover of the product is

$$
\widetilde{Y\times\mathbb R}=\widetilde Y\times\mathbb R,
$$

which deformation retracts onto $\widetilde Y$. It is contractible only if $\widetilde Y$ is contractible, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence no such complete Ricci-flat metric exists.
