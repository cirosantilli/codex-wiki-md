<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Give $S^2\times\mathbb R$ the [Riemannian product](../../../../../../riemannian-product.md) of the unit round metric and the Euclidean metric. It is complete and has infinite [diameter](../../../../../../diameter.md). The round sphere has [scalar curvature](../../../../../../scalar-curvature.md) $2$, while the line has scalar curvature $0$; scalar curvature is additive under Riemannian products, so

$$
\operatorname{Scal}_{S^2\times\mathbb R}=2.
$$

Thus this manifold has a strictly positive uniform lower bound on scalar curvature but violates the conclusion of the [Bonnet-Myers theorem](../../../../../../myers-s-theorem.md). Its [Ricci curvature](../../../../../../ricci-curvature.md) vanishes in the $\mathbb R$ direction, showing precisely why a scalar-curvature bound is insufficient.
