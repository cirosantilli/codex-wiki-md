<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $A,B,C$ represent $a,b,c$. Since $c$ is central, $C$ commutes with $A$ and $B$. Over the [complex number](../../../../../../complex-number.md) field $\mathbb C$, $C$ has an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\gamma$, and its corresponding [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is invariant under all three operators. The [irreducibility](../../../../../../irreducible-lie-algebra-representation.md) of $V$ therefore makes this eigenspace all of $V$, so $C=\gamma I$. Taking the [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) of

$$
C=[A,B]=AB-BA
$$

gives $(\dim V)\gamma=0$ by the [cyclic property of the trace](../../../../../../cyclic-property-of-the-trace.md); hence $C=0$.

The remaining operators $A$ and $B$ commute. Two commuting operators on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) have a common [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md), whose span is invariant. Irreducibility therefore forces $\dim V=1$. Conversely, every pair $(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb C^2$ defines a one-dimensional irreducible representation by

$$
a\longmapsto\alpha,
\qquad b\longmapsto\beta,
\qquad c\longmapsto0.
$$

These are all the finite-dimensional irreducible representations.
