<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The luminosity from both disk faces is the radial integral of the given viscous heating rate:

$$
L=\int_{r_{\rm in}}^{r_{\rm out}}
2\pi r\,\frac94\bar\nu\Sigma\Omega^2\,dr.
$$

Substituting the steady profile and $\Omega^2=GM/r^3$ gives

$$
L=GM\dot M\left[
\frac{\sqrt{r_{\rm out}}}{r_{\rm in}^{3/2}}
-\frac{3}{2r_{\rm in}}+\frac{1}{2r_{\rm out}}
\right].
$$

Using $\Theta=\dot M\sqrt{GM}(\sqrt{r_{\rm out}}-\sqrt{r_{\rm in}})$ turns this into

$$
\boxed{L=\Theta\Omega_{\rm in}
+\frac{GM\dot M}{2}
\left(\frac1{r_{\rm out}}-\frac1{r_{\rm in}}\right)}.
$$

The first term is mechanical power injected by the stellar magnetic torque. The second is $\dot M(E_{\rm in}-E_{\rm out})$, where $E=-GM/(2r)$ is the specific energy of a circular orbit; it is negative because outward motion makes the material less tightly bound and consumes some torque power. The remainder is radiated by viscous dissipation.

Matter reaching the outer edge still has binding energy $GM/(2r_{\rm out})$ per unit mass. A wind that reaches infinity with negligible terminal energy must therefore receive

$$
\boxed{\dot E_{\rm unbind}=\frac{GM\dot M}{2r_{\rm out}}}
$$

from the stellar radiation field. In a signed disk-energy balance, the material removed at the edge carries orbital energy flux $\dot M E_{\rm out}=-GM\dot M/(2r_{\rm out})$.
