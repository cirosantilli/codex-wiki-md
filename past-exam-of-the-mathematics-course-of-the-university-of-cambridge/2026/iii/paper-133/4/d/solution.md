<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Fix a hyperbolicity constant $\delta_0>0$ for $\mathbb H^2$. For a prescribed $\delta>0$, scale its distance by

$$
d_\delta=\frac{\delta}{\delta_0}d_{\mathbb H^2}.
$$

All distances in every geodesic triangle, including its thinness constant, scale by $\delta/\delta_0$. Hence

$$
X_\delta=(\mathbb H^2,d_\delta)
$$

is $\delta$-hyperbolic. Multiplication of a metric by a fixed positive constant is a [bilipschitz equivalence](../../../../../../bilipschitz-equivalence.md) and hence a [quasi-isometry](../../../../../../quasi-isometry.md). Therefore $X_\delta$ is quasi-isometric to $\mathbb H^2$ and cannot be a quasi-tree. This supplies an example for every $\delta>0$.
