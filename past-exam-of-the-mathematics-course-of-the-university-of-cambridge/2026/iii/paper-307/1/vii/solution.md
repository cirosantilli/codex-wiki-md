<h1 id="1/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

The three Weyl components form one fundamental representation of the global $SU(3)$, so coupling $G$ to background gauge fields reveals a nonzero $SU(3)^3$ ['t Hooft anomaly](../../../../../../t-hooft-anomaly.md). This does not destroy the global symmetry in flat space or make the theory inconsistent; it constrains any infrared description through ['t Hooft anomaly matching](../../../../../../t-hooft-anomaly-matching.md). If $G$ were instead intended as a dynamical gauge symmetry, the same uncancelled [gauge anomaly](../../../../../../gauge-anomaly.md) would make the model inconsistent and additional chiral matter would be required. Ordinary quantum corrections cannot generate the forbidden superpotential, and the [non-renormalization theorem](../../../../../../non-renormalization-theorem.md) protects its absence.
