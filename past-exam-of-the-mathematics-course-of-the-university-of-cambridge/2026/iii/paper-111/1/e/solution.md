<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

By the assumed transitivity on [fundamental systems](../../../../../../fundamental-system-of-a-root-system.md), some $w_0\in W$ sends $\Delta$ to $-\Delta$. It therefore sends the entire [positive system of a root system](../../../../../../positive-system-of-a-root-system.md) $\Pi$ to $-\Pi$. Part d then gives

$$
\ell(w_0)=|\Pi|=\frac{|\Phi|}{2}.
$$

For every $w\in W$, its [inversion set](../../../../../../inversion-set-of-a-weyl-group-element.md) is contained in $\Pi$, so $\ell(w)\leq|\Pi|$ and $w_0$ has maximal length.

If $u$ also has maximal length, then $N(u)=\Pi$, so $u(\Pi)=-\Pi$. Hence $w_0^{-1}u$ preserves $\Pi$ and has no inversions. Part d makes its [Coxeter length](../../../../../../coxeter-length.md) zero, so it is the identity. Thus $u=w_0$, proving that the [longest element of a finite Coxeter group](../../../../../../longest-element-of-a-coxeter-group.md) is unique and has length $|\Phi|/2$.
