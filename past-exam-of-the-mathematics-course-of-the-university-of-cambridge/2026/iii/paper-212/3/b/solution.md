<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Run [Wilson algorithm rooted at infinity](../../../../../../wilson-algorithm-rooted-at-infinity.md) with $w_1,\ldots,w_K$ first in the enumeration. Couple its first $K$ walks with the independent walks in the hypothesis. On the positive-probability event that their ranges are pairwise disjoint, no walk from $w_j$ hits any earlier loop-erased range. Wilson's algorithm therefore creates $K$ distinct trees, so

$$
\mathbb P(\text{the wired uniform spanning forest has at least $K$ trees})>0.
$$

The [component-number zero-one law for the wired uniform spanning forest](../../../../../../component-number-zero-one-law-for-the-wired-uniform-spanning-forest.md) says that its number of trees is almost surely constant; it follows from tail triviality of the wired uniform spanning forest and the fact that all its trees are infinite. The displayed positive probability must consequently equal one.
