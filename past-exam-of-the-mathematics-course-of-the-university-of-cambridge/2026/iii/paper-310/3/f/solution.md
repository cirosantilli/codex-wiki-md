**Yes.** [Synchronous gauge in cosmology](../../../../../../synchronous-gauge-in-cosmology.md) uses freely falling time lines and sets the lapse and shift perturbations to zero, so no gravitational-potential force appears explicitly in this component of momentum conservation. Metric perturbations still affect the other field equations, the evolution of matter variables, and the relation between coordinate-dependent variables and gauge-invariant observables; synchronous gauge also retains residual gauge modes.

In [Newtonian gauge in cosmology](../../../../../../newtonian-gauge.md), the time-time metric perturbation is a Newtonian gravitational potential. Its spatial [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) therefore appears explicitly as a force term in the [Euler equation](../../../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md). The difference is a coordinate representation of the same covariant conservation law.
