One quantitative form of [Halász theorem](../../../../../../halasz-theorem.md) is the following. If $f$ is [multiplicative](../../../../../../multiplicative-function.md) and $|f(n)|\leq1$, put

$$
M(f;x,T)=\min_{|t|\leq T}\mathbb D(f,n^{it};x)^2,
\qquad
\mathbb D(f,g;x)^2
=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-\Re(f(p)\overline{g(p)})}{p}.
$$

Then, uniformly for $2\leq T\leq x$,

$$
\frac1x\left|\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)\right|
\ll (1+M(f;x,T))e^{-M(f;x,T)}+\frac1{\sqrt T}.
$$

**Thus a bounded [multiplicative arithmetic function](../../../../../../multiplicative-function.md) can have a large mean only when it has small [pretentious distance](../../../../../../pretentious-distance.md) from some Archimedean character $n^{it}$.**
