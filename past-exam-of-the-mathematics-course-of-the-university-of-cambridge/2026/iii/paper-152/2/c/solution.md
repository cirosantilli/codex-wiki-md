<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take the invariant divisor $D_1$, whose class is $F$. Its global sections are represented in the [Cox ring](../../../../../../cox-ring.md) by $x_1$ and $x_3$. They have no common zero on $U$, so $F$ is [basepoint-free](../../../../../../basepoint-free-divisor.md). Its [Kodaira map](../../../../../../kodaira-map.md) is

$$
[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\longmapsto[x_1:x_3]\in\mathbb P^1.
$$

This is exactly the ruling $\mathbb F_k=\mathbb P_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(k))\to\mathbb P^1$, and its fibers have divisor class $F$.
