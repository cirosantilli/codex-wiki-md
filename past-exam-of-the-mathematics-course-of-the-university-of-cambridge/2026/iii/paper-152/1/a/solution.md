<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take the [weighted projective plane](../../../../../../weighted-projective-plane.md) $X=\mathbb P(1,1,2)$. Its [fan](../../../../../../fan-in-toric-geometry.md) in $N=\mathbb Z^2$ has rays generated by

$$
v_0=(-1,-2),\qquad v_1=(1,0),\qquad v_2=(0,1),
$$

and all three two-dimensional cones spanned by adjacent rays. Their union is $N_{\mathbb R}$, so the fan is [complete](../../../../../../complete-fan.md) and the [toric variety](../../../../../../toric-variety.md) $X$ is [proper](../../../../../../proper-toric-variety.md).

The determinants of the cones $\langle v_1,v_2\rangle$ and $\langle v_2,v_0\rangle$ have absolute value one, while

$$
|\det(v_0,v_1)|=2.
$$

The [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../../../../smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety.md) therefore shows that the affine chart for $\langle v_0,v_1\rangle$ is singular; it is the cyclic quotient singularity of type $\frac12(1,1)$. Thus $X$ is a singular proper toric surface.
