<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Ideal flux freezing anchors a field line in the highly conducting disc, so its [field-line angular velocity](../../../../../../field-line-angular-velocity.md) naturally equals the angular velocity of its footpoint. A circular orbit in the central potential is Keplerian:

$$
\omega(\psi)=\Omega_0
=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r_0^3}}.
$$

Put $r=r_0+\delta r$ and expand

$$
\Phi_{cg}=-\frac{GM}{\sqrt{r^2+z^2}}
-\frac12\Omega_0^2r^2.
$$

The linear radial term vanishes because $GM/r_0^2=\Omega_0^2r_0$. The radial and vertical second derivatives are respectively $-3\Omega_0^2$ and $\Omega_0^2$, so

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{cg}
\simeq\text{constant}
+\frac12\Omega_0^2[z^2-3(r-r_0)^2]}.
$$

If the field line makes angle $\theta$ from the vertical, then locally $\delta r=z\tan\theta$. Its quadratic potential change is

$$
\frac12\Omega_0^2z^2(1-3\tan^2\theta),
$$

which is negative exactly when

$$
\boxed{\theta>30^\circ}.
$$

This is the local [magnetocentrifugal acceleration](../../../../../../magnetocentrifugal-acceleration.md) launching criterion.
