<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The flux-function form solves $\nabla\mathbin\cdot\mathbf B=0$ identically. With $k=k(\psi)$, the poloidal relation $\rho\mathbf u_p=k\mathbf B_p$ and axisymmetry solve steady mass conservation. The ideal induction equation is solved by the velocity representation with the [field-line angular velocity](../../../../../../field-line-angular-velocity.md) $\omega=\omega(\psi)$. The azimuthal momentum equation integrates once to the [magnetohydrodynamic angular-momentum invariant](../../../../../../magnetohydrodynamic-angular-momentum-invariant.md) $\ell=\ell(\psi)$.

What remains is the pressure or entropy equation and the two poloidal components of momentum. Projecting poloidal momentum along a field line produces the Bernoulli invariant in part (c); projecting across magnetic surfaces produces the transfield or Grad-Shafranov equation that determines their shape. An equation of state and boundary conditions complete the problem. The stellar gravitational potential is prescribed, so no self-gravity Poisson equation remains to solve.
