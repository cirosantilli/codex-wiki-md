<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write the [power-law size distribution](../../../../../../power-law-size-distribution.md) as $n(D)=kD^{-\alpha}$. The total geometric cross-section of the optically thin [debris disk](../../../../../../debris-disk.md) is

$$
\sigma_{\rm tot}=\int_{D_{\min}}^{D_{\max}}
\frac{\pi D^2}{4}kD^{-\alpha}\,dD
\simeq\frac{\pi k}{4(\alpha-3)}D_{\min}^{3-\alpha}.
$$

Because blackbody grains at radius $r$ intercept the fraction $\sigma_{\rm tot}/(4\pi r^2)$ of the stellar luminosity, the [fractional luminosity of a debris disk](../../../../../../fractional-luminosity-of-a-debris-disk.md) gives

$$
k=16r^2f(\alpha-3)D_{\min}^{\alpha-3}.
$$

Another [integral](../../../../../../integral.md) then yields

$$
\boxed{n(>D)=\frac{16r^2f(\alpha-3)}{\alpha-1}
D_{\min}^{\alpha-3}D^{1-\alpha}},
$$

for $D_{\min}\ll D\ll D_{\max}$.
