Here $a_p$ is the planet's [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md), while $a,e,I$ are the particle's [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md), [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md), and [orbital inclination](../../../../../../orbital-inclination.md) relative to the planet's plane. The formula assumes the [circular restricted three-body problem](../../../../../../circular-restricted-three-body-problem.md): the planet-to-star mass ratio is small, the particle has negligible mass, and its motion is approximately heliocentric and Keplerian away from brief encounters. The planet's own orbit is circular.

The [Jacobi constant](../../../../../../jacobi-constant.md) is exactly conserved in that ideal rotating problem. Expressing it in heliocentric [orbital elements](../../../../../../orbital-element.md) away from the planet gives the approximately conserved [Tisserand parameter](../../../../../../tisserand-parameter.md)

$$
T_p=\frac{a_p}{a}+2\sqrt{\frac a{a_p}(1-e^2)}\cos I.
$$

The first term measures the particle's normalized binding energy. The second is twice the component of its [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) normal to the planet's plane, normalized by $\sqrt{GM_*a_p}$.
