<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) and [Fourier's law](../../../../../../fourier-s-law.md) give

$$
\rho c_p T_t=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
\left(k(T)T_z\right).
$$

At steady state $kT_z=F_c$ is constant. Since $k=a/T$,

$$
\frac{d\log T}{dz}=\frac{F_c}{a}.
$$

The two temperature [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) therefore give

$$
\boxed{T(z)=T_s\exp\left(\frac{F_c z}{a}\right)
=T_s\left(\frac{T_m}{T_s}\right)^{z/h}},
\qquad
\boxed{F_c=\frac ah\log\frac{T_m}{T_s}}.
$$

At the basal [phase boundary](../../../../../../phase-boundary.md), the [Stefan condition](../../../../../../stefan-condition.md) is

$$
\rho L\dot h=F_c-F_w.
$$

The first term removes [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md) released by freezing, while the oceanic flux $F_w$ supplies heat to the interface. A steady shell has $\dot h=0$, hence $F_c=F_w$ and

$$
\boxed{h_\infty=\frac a{F_w}\log\frac{T_m}{T_s}}.
$$

Here $T_s$ must simultaneously satisfy the surface balance from part a with $F_c=F_w$.
