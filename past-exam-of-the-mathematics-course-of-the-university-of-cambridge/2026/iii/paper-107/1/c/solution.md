<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Iteration gives the [interior derivative estimate for a harmonic function](../../../../../../interior-derivative-estimate-for-a-harmonic-function.md)

$$
|D^{k+1}u(x_0)|\leq C(n,k)R^{-(k+1)}\sup_{B_R(x_0)}|u|.
$$

The growth hypothesis bounds the right side by $C'R^{\alpha-1}+o(1)$, which tends to zero as $R\to\infty$. Thus every derivative of order $k+1$ vanishes everywhere. The [Taylor theorem](../../../../../../taylor-theorem.md) makes $u$ a polynomial of degree at most $k$, proving the [polynomial-growth Liouville theorem for harmonic functions](../../../../../../polynomial-growth-liouville-theorem-for-harmonic-functions.md).
