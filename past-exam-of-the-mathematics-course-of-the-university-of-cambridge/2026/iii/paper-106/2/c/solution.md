<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a unital [Banach algebra](../../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md), $\varphi(a)$ belongs to $\sigma_A(a)$: otherwise $a-\varphi(a)1$ would be invertible, while applying $\varphi$ to its inverse identity would give $0=1$. Therefore

$$
|\varphi(a)|\leq r(a)\leq\lVert a\rVert,
$$

so every [character of an algebra](../../../../../../character-of-an-algebra.md) is continuous and has norm one. The nonunital case follows by extending the character to the [unitization of an algebra](../../../../../../unitization-of-an-algebra.md).

The [Gelfand topology](../../../../../../gelfand-topology.md) on $\Phi_A$ is the [weak-star topology](../../../../../../weak-star-topology.md) inherited from $A^*$: a net $\varphi_i$ converges to $\varphi$ exactly when $\varphi_i(a)\to\varphi(a)$ for every $a\in A$. If $A$ is unital, $\Phi_A$ lies in the weak-star compact dual unit ball by the [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md). The equations

$$
\varphi(1)=1,
\qquad
\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)
$$

define a weak-star closed subset, so $\Phi_A$ is compact.
