<h1 id="2/f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $B$ be the completion under the assumed [algebra norm](../../../../../../../algebra-norm.md). Restriction sends every $\psi\in\Phi_B$ to a character of the dense subalgebra $C(\mathbb R)$, hence by part i to $\delta_x$ for some $x$. Continuity of the restriction says exactly that $x\in K$. Conversely, every $\delta_x$ continuous for the algebra norm extends uniquely through the completion, and continuity of multiplication makes the extension a character of $B$. Restriction and extension therefore give a bijection

$$
\Phi_B\longleftrightarrow K.
$$

For $b\in B$, choose $h_n\in C(\mathbb R)$ converging to $b$ in the algebra norm. Characters on the unital [Banach algebra](../../../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md) $B$ are uniformly norm bounded, so $h_n|_K$ converges uniformly to the function $x\mapsto\widetilde\delta_x(b)$. This function is continuous. Hence $x\mapsto\widetilde\delta_x$ is continuous into the [Gelfand topology](../../../../../../../gelfand-topology.md). Its inverse is again $\psi\mapsto\psi(t)$, so the bijection is a homeomorphism. In particular, $K$ is compact.
