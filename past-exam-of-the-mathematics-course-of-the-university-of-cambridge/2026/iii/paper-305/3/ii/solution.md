<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Classically the $p$ antifundamentals admit $U(p)$ flavour rotations and $\lambda$ admits an independent phase rotation, giving $SU(p)\times U(1)_\psi\times U(1)_\lambda$ up to finite quotients. A $U(1)$ with charges $q_\lambda,q_\psi$ is free of the mixed $SU(N)^2U(1)$ anomaly when

$$
(N-2)q_\lambda+p q_\psi=0.
$$

Using $p=N-4$, choose

$$
q_\lambda=N-4,
\qquad
q_\psi=-(N-2).
$$

The orthogonal axial phase has a [chiral anomaly](../../../../../../chiral-anomaly.md), while this combination survives. The continuous quantum global symmetry is therefore $SU(p)\times U(1)$, again up to possible finite central quotients.
