<h1 id="24f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [24F](../24f.md)

The degree is the sum of divisor coefficients; principal divisors are $(f)$ for nonzero rational [functions](../../../../../function-split.md). $\operatorname{Cl}(C)=\operatorname{Div}(C)/\operatorname{Prin}(C)$ and $\operatorname{Cl}^0$ is its degree-zero [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md). On $\mathbb P^1$, $K\sim-2P$; on a hyperelliptic genus-$g$ curve with degree-two fibre $H$, $K\sim(g-1)H$. If $\operatorname{Cl}^0=0$, any point divisor has $\ell(P)=2$ by Riemann-Roch, giving a degree-one map to $\mathbb P^1$; the converse follows because every degree-zero divisor on $\mathbb P^1$ is principal. For $g\ge1$, $[p-p_0]=[q-p_0]$ would give a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) with divisor $p-q$, hence a degree-one map unless $p=q$, proving injectivity. If $p+q\sim r+s$ and the pairs differ, the resulting degree-two pencil makes $C$ hyperelliptic; therefore on a nonhyperelliptic curve the unordered pairs coincide.
