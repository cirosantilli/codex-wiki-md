<h1 id="38a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

[Differentiation](../../../../../../differentiation.md) gives $u''+u=\frac32r_su^2$. With $u=b^{-1}(v_0+\varepsilon v_1+\cdots)$,

$$
v_0''+v_0=0,\qquad v_1''+v_1=\frac32v_0^2.
$$

The symmetry selects $v_0=\sin\phi$, the straight line at closest distance $b$. Since  
$\frac32\sin^2\phi=\frac34(1-\cos2\phi)$,

$$
v_1=\frac34+\frac14\cos2\phi.
$$

The two asymptotic zeros shift from $0,\pi$ to $-\varepsilon,\pi+\varepsilon$, so the deflection is $2\varepsilon=2r_s/b=4GM/(bc^2)$, the [Schwarzschild light deflection](../../../../../../schwarzschild-light-deflection.md). It is tested by stellar deflection near the Sun and gravitational lensing.
