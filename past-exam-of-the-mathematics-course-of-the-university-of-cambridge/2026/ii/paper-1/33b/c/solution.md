<h1 id="33b/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Rotational invariance gives $\{m_y,H\}=\{m_z,H\}=0$. Direct calculation gives

$$
m_x=\{m_y,m_z\}=yp_z-zp_y.
$$

Take $G=m_x^2+m_y^2+m_z^2$; angular-momentum brackets imply $\{G,m_x\}=0$, and central symmetry gives $\{G,H\}=0$. Hence $(H,G,m_x)$ are in involution.

At $\mathbf x=(1,0,0)$, $m_y=-p_z$, $m_z=p_y$. The differentials of $G$ and $m_x$ have independent components in the $p_y,p_z$ and transverse position directions when $p_y,p_z\ne0$; $dH$ supplies an independent radial/[momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) component. Thus they are locally independent and the system is Liouville integrable.
