<h1 id="18f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [18F](../18f.md)

Orbits of the root action are precisely roots belonging to the same irreducible factor, proving transitivity iff irreducibility.

For a same-degree counterexample, let $L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$,

$$
f=(X^2-2)(X^2-3),\qquad
g=\min_{\mathbb Q}(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)=X^4-10X^2+1.
$$

Both split in $L$, but the first action has two orbits of size two while the second is transitive, so the [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) are not conjugate in $S_4$.

Over a finite field, [Frobenius cycle type](../../../../../frobenius-cycle-type.md) proves the assertion. The given quartic reduces over $\mathbb F_2$ to $X^4+X^3+1$, which is irreducible, so its [group](../../../../../group-split.md) there is cyclic of order four generated by a $4$-cycle.

Over $\mathbb Q$ the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is irreducible by the same reduction. Modulo $3$ it factors as linear times irreducible cubic, so [Dedekind factorization cycle test](../../../../../dedekind-factorization-cycle-test.md) gives a $3$-cycle; modulo $2$ it gives a $4$-cycle. Its discriminant is $229$, and modulo $23$ its factor degrees are $1,1,2$, giving a transposition. These cycle types force the transitive [group](../../../../../group-split.md) to be $S_4$.
