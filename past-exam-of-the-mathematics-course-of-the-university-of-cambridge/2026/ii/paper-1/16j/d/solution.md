<h1 id="16j/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Choice well-orders every infinite $X$, so Hessenberg gives $X^2\cong X$. Conversely assume this square identity for every infinite set. For arbitrary $X$, form the disjoint union $Y=X\sqcup h(X)$. A pairing $Y^2\cong Y$, together with the well-order on $h(X)$, yields by the standard tracing/comparison argument an injection either $h(X)\to X$ or $X\to h(X)$. The first is forbidden by [Hartogs theorem](../../../../../../hartogs-theorem.md), so the second well-orders $X$. This is [Tarski cardinal-square theorem](../../../../../../tarski-cardinal-square-theorem.md), and hence choice follows.
