## 1G

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="1g/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1G](#1g)

The [möbius function](../../../number-theory.md#mobius-function) is $mu(1)=1$, $mu(n)=0$ if a prime square divides $n$, and $mu(n)=(-1)^r$ when $n$ is a product of $r$ distinct primes. The [Riemann zeta function](../../../analytic-number-theory.md#riemann-zeta-function) is $zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}$ for $\Re s>1$.

Both sides of the proposed identity are multiplicative. At $n=p^a$, the right side is $1$ for $a=0,1$ and $1+\mu(p)=0$ for $a\ge2$, exactly $mu(p^a)^2$. Absolute convergence permits rearrangement, so

$$
\boxed{\sum_n\frac{\mu(n)^2}{n^s}=\sum_d\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2s}}\sum_m\frac1{m^s}=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2s)}.}
$$

## 2I

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="2i/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2I](#2i)

[Runge theorem](../../../complex-analysis.md#runge-s-theorem) says that if $K\subset\mathbb C$ is compact and $\mathbb C\setminus K$ is connected, every [function](../../../function.md) holomorphic near $K$ is uniformly approximable on $K$ by [polynomials](../../../polynomial.md).

Choose a compact exhaustion $K_j$ of the slit disc $\Delta\setminus L^+$, rounding the two sides of the slit so that $0\notin K_j$ and $\mathbb C\setminus K_j$ is connected. Runge applied to $1/z$ gives a [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) $p_j$ with $\sup_{K_j}|p_j-1/z|<1/j$. Every compact subset of the slit disc lies in all sufficiently large $K_j$, proving locally [uniform convergence](../../../real-analysis.md#uniform-convergence).

<h3 id="3h/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3H](#3h)

<h4 id="3h/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3h/d)

The [asymptotic equipartition property](../../../information-theory.md#asymptotic-equipartition-property) is $-n^{-1}\log_2p(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\to H$ in probability. For an i.i.d. source this is the weak law applied to $-\log_2p(X_i)$, and independence gives $H(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=nH(X)$. Thus its [information rate](../../../information-theory.md#information-rate) and AEP constant are both $H(X)$.

## 4I

↑ **Parent:** [4I](#4i)

<h4 id="4i/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4i/a)

A state $q$ is inaccessible when no word $w\in\Sigma^*$ has $\widehat\delta(q_0,w)=q$.

<h3 id="4i/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4I](#4i)

<h4 id="4i/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4i/b)

Set $p\sim q$ when $\widehat\delta(p,w)\in F\iff\widehat\delta(q,w)\in F$ for every word $w$. Put $\delta'([q],a)=[\delta(q,a)]$ and $F'=\{[q]:q\in F\}$. If $p\sim q$, then testing continuations $aw$ shows $\delta(p,a)\sim\delta(q,a)$, so $\delta'$ is well-defined.

<h3 id="4i/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4I](#4i)

<h4 id="4i/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4i/c)

The [Myhill-Nerode theorem](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#myhill-nerode-theorem) says this quotient is, up to isomorphism, the unique accessible DFA with the least possible number of states accepting the language.

<h3 id="4i/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4I](#4i)

<h4 id="4i/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4i/d)

Take states $0,\ldots,2n-1$, transition $j\mapsto j+1\pmod{2n}$, initial state $0$, and make every even state accepting. Every state is reached by some $a^j$, and two states are equivalent exactly when they have the same parity. The quotient therefore has two states and accepts the even-length words.

## 5K

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="5k/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5K](#5k)

[Logistic regression](../../../statistical-modelling.md#logistic-regression) assumes independent $Y_i\mid X_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi_i)$ with

$$
\log\frac{\pi_i}{1-\pi_i}=\beta_0+X_i^T\beta.
$$

For the binomial exponential family, $\ell=y\theta-m\log(1+e^\theta)+\text{constant}$, so the mean is $m e^\theta/(1+e^\theta)$ and the canonical parameter is its logit. It may be fitted by [iteratively reweighted least squares](../../../statistical-modelling.md#iteratively-reweighted-least-squares).

Under LDA, Bayes' rule gives

$$
\log\frac{P(Y=1\mid x)}{P(Y=0\mid x)}=log\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_0}+(\mu_1-\mu_0)^T\Sigma^{-1}x-	frac12(\mu_1^T\Sigma^{-1}\mu_1-\mu_0^T\Sigma^{-1}\mu_0),
$$

which is a logistic model with an intercept.

## 7D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="7d/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [7D](#7d)

The [laplace transform](../../../analysis.md#laplace-transform) is $\widehat f(p)=\int_0^\infty e^{-pt}f(t),dt$, and inversion is

$$
f(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}e^{pt}\widehat f(p),dp,
$$

where the vertical [Bromwich contour](../../../complex-analysis.md#bromwich-contour) lies to the right of all singularities. For $t>0$ it is closed leftwards, and for $t<0$ rightwards, where the exponential decays.

Transforming the PDE and imposing boundedness as $x\to\infty$ gives

$$
U(x,p)=\frac1{p(p+1)}+\frac{p-1}{p(p+1)}e^{-x\sqrt{p/D}},
$$

with the square root cut conventionally along $(-\infty,0]$. Therefore

$$
u(x,t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}e^{pt}U(x,p),dp.
$$

There are poles at $p=0,-1$ (with cancellation assessed in the complete expression) and a [branch point](../../../complex-analysis.md#branch-point) at $0$; close left for $t>0$ and wrap the cut while including the relevant residues.

## 8A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="8a/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8A](#8a)

<h4 id="8a/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#8a/a)

With $x=\ell\sin\theta$ and $y=-at^2/2-\ell\cos\theta$, discard a total [derivative](../../../calculus.md#derivative) from the [kinetic energy](../../../classical-mechanics.md#kinetic-energy) to obtain

$$
L=\tfrac12m\ell^2\dot\theta^2+ m\ell(g-a)\cos\theta.
$$

**Thus $\ddot\theta+(g-a)\sin\theta/\ell=0$. If $a=g$, then $\ddot\theta=0$: the pendulum is weightless in the lift and rotates uniformly (or remains fixed).**

<h3 id="8a/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [8A](#8a)

<h4 id="8a/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#8a/b)

The equilibria are $\theta=0,\pi$ modulo $2\pi$ unless $a=g$, when every angle is an equilibrium. Linearisation shows $	heta=0$ stable for $a<g$ and $\theta=\pi$ stable for $a>g$; the other is unstable.

## 9D

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="9d/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9D](#9d)

<h4 id="9d/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#9d/a)

Since $p\propto L^{-1}=V^{-1/3}$, $dp/dV=-p/(3V)$. At fixed occupations, $dE=\int \mathcal E'(p)(dp/dV)\bar n(p),dp,dV$. Equating this to $-P,dV$ yields $P=(3V)^{-1}\int p\mathcal E'(p)\bar n(p),dp$.

<h3 id="9d/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9D](#9d)

<h4 id="9d/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#9d/b)

For $\mathcal E=pc$, $p\mathcal E'=\mathcal E$, so $P=E/(3V)=\rho/3$. Adiabatic energy conservation gives $\rho\propto a^{-4}$, equivalently conserved particle number gives $n\propto a^{-3}$; with $\rho\propto T^4$ or $n\propto T^3$, both imply $T\propto a^{-1}$.

<h3 id="9d/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [9D](#9d)

<h4 id="9d/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#9d/c)

Here $\mathcal E=mc^2+p^2/(2m)+\cdots$, so $p\mathcal E'=p^2/m$. Consequently $P=(3V)^{-1}\int p^2/m,\bar n,dp=2U/(3V)$. For an adiabatic monatomic gas, $u\propto a^{-5}$ and $n\propto a^{-3}$, hence $T\propto u/n\propto a^{-2}$.

## 13D

<h3 id="13d/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [13D](#13d)

A [branch point](../../../complex-analysis.md#branch-point) is a point around which analytic continuation changes a value; a branch cut removes curves so continuation becomes single-valued, and a branch is one such single-valued analytic choice. Define

$$
\operatorname{Arcsin}z=\int_0^z(1-t^2)^{-1/2}dt
$$

on $\mathbb C\setminus((-∞,-1]\cup[1,∞))$, choosing the square root equal to $1$ at zero. Monodromy around $\pm1$ explains multivaluedness.

On the upper lip $0+$ of the cut convention, the branch remains positive on $(0,1)$, so $\int_{0+}^1dt/\sqrt{1-t^2}=\pi/2$. Define $\operatorname{Arccos}z=\pi/2-\operatorname{Arcsin}z$ on the same slit domain.

Also

$$
\arctan z=\frac1{2i}[\log(1+iz)-\log(1-iz)].
$$

Changing logarithm branches changes the value by multiples of $\pi$ (already visible at $z=1$). Principal logarithms give a single branch on the plane cut from $\pm i$ outward. Differentiating both sides and checking the value at zero proves

$$
\operatorname{Arctan}z=\operatorname{Arcsin}\frac z{\sqrt{1+z^2}}
$$

where the compatible principal branches are defined.

For fixed $y$, Riesz gives a unique $Sy$ with $\theta(x,y)=\langle x,Sy\rangle$. Conjugate [linearity](../../../vector-space.md#linearity) in $y$ and the inner-product convention make $S$ linear. Moreover $\|Sy\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}|\theta(x,y)|\le\|\theta\|\|y\|$, and taking both suprema proves $\|S\|=\|\theta\|$.

<h3 id="22i/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [22I](#22i)

<h4 id="22i/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#22i/c)

The adjoint is the unique $T^*$ satisfying $\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^*y\rangle$. Apply Riesz to $x\mapsto\langle Tx,y\rangle$. The preceding norm calculation gives $\|T^*\|\le\|T\|$, while $\|Tx\|=\sup_{\|y\|=1}|\langle x,T^*y\rangle|$ gives the reverse inequality.

<h3 id="22i/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [22I](#22i)

<h4 id="22i/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#22i/d)

Hermitian means $T=T^*$, unitary $T^*T=TT^*=I$, and normal $T^*T=TT^*$. If $\|(T-\lambda)x_n\|\to0$ for unit $x_n$, then $\langle Tx_n,x_n\rangle\to\lambda$ and each term is real, so $\lambda\in\mathbb R$. ↑ **Parent:** [35A](#35a)

<h4 id="35a/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#35a/c)

**No.** A pure product state has a pure reduced density [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix), whereas part (b) gives $I/2$.

<h3 id="35a/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [35A](#35a)

<h4 id="35a/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#35a/d)

The values are $+1,+1,-1,-1$ for $\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_4$. Thus $z$-spin outcomes are perfectly correlated for the first pair and perfectly anticorrelated for the second.

<h3 id="35a/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [35A](#35a)

<h4 id="35a/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#35a/e)

Using $T=2\,\mathrm{SWAP}-I$, the symmetric triplet [Bell state](../../../bell-state.md) have [eigenvalue](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $1$, while the antisymmetric singlet has [eigenvalue](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $-3$. With the ordering shown, $\mathcal B_1,\mathcal B_2,\mathcal B_3$ are triplet combinations and $\mathcal B_4$ is the singlet.

<h3 id="35a/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [35A](#35a)

<h4 id="35a/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#35a/f)

Applying $I\otimes I,X,Z,$ or $XZ$ to one qubit permutes the Bell [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) up to phases. Composing two such Pauli operations maps any chosen Bell state to any other, proving local equivalence.

## 36E

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="36e/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [36E](#36e)

<h4 id="36e/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#36e/a)

For any normalized trial state $\psi$, the spectral expansion gives $\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle\ge E_0$. Minimize this Rayleigh quotient over a trial family to obtain the best upper bound.

<h3 id="36e/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [36E](#36e)

<h4 id="36e/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#36e/b)

Write the normalized trial state as $\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2}|0\rangle+\epsilon|\eta\rangle$ with $\eta\perp0$. The cross term vanishes because $H|0\rangle=E_0|0\rangle$, so the energy error is $\epsilon^2(\langle\eta|H|\eta\rangle-E_0)=O(\epsilon^2)$.

<h3 id="36e/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [36E](#36e)

<h4 id="36e/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#36e/c)

The unperturbed ground state is even about $a/2$, while $V_{\mathcal E}=q\mathcal E(x-a/2)$ is odd. Its first-order expectation therefore vanishes.

<h3 id="36e/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [36E](#36e)

<h4 id="36e/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#36e/d)

The correction is odd, obeys the well [boundary conditions](../../../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition), and is linear in the field, exactly the parity and order of the first-order [wavefunction](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#wave-function) correction.

<h3 id="36e/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [36E](#36e)

<h4 id="36e/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#36e/e)

Put $y=x-a/2$ and $\epsilon=q\mathcal E$. To quadratic order the Rayleigh quotient contains $\epsilon^2(A\alpha^2+2B\alpha)$, where $A=\hbar^2/(2m)$ and $B=\langle y^2\rangle=a^2(\pi^2-6)/(12\pi^2)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\alpha_{\rm opt}=-\frac BA=-\frac{ma^2(\pi^2-6)}{6\pi^2\hbar^2}.}
$$

The first law and Gibbs-Duhem relation give $d\Gamma=-S,dT+V,dp-N,d\mu$. Extensivity gives Euler's relation $E=TS-pV+\mu N$, hence $\Gamma=E-TS+pV-\mu N=0$.

<h3 id="37e/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [37E](#37e)

<h4 id="37e/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#37e/b)

<h5 id="37e/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#37e/b/i)

Two polarizations and periodic wavevectors give $g(\omega)=A\omega/(\pi c_s^2)$.

<h4 id="37e/b/ii">ii</h4>

Requiring $\int_0^{\omega_D}g(\omega)d\omega=2N$ gives $\omega_D=2c_s\sqrt{\pi N/A}$.

<h4 id="37e/b/iii">iii</h4>

<h5 id="37e/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#37e/b/iii)

For $k_BT\ll\hbar\omega_D$, extend the [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) to infinity:

$$
\log Z=-\int_0^\infty g(\omega)\log(1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega})d\omega
=\frac{A k_B^2T^2}{\pi c_s^2\hbar^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^3}.
$$

**Thus $X=\zeta(3)/\pi$.**

Since $F=-k_BT\log Z$, $S=-\partial F/\partial T=3\zeta(3)A k_B^3T^2/(\pi c_s^2\hbar^2)$.

## 38A

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="38a/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [38A](#38a)

<h4 id="38a/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#38a/a)

$G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}$. The contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}=0$ is required to match $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$.

<h3 id="38a/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [38A](#38a)

<h4 id="38a/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#38a/b)

Computing $R=6(\ddot a/a+(\dot a^2+k)/a^2)$ gives

$$
\dot a^2=\frac{8\pi\rho}{3}a^2-k,\qquad \ddot a=-\frac{4\pi}{3}(\rho+3p)a.
$$

**Thus $\alpha=8\pi\rho/3$, $\beta=-k$, and $\gamma=-4\pi(\rho+3p)/3$ in the stated notation.**

<h3 id="38a/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [38A](#38a)

<h4 id="38a/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#38a/c)

Staticity requires $\rho_0+3p_0=0$, so $\rho_m=\Lambda/(4\pi)$. The first Friedmann equation then requires $k=1$ and $a_0=\Lambda^{-1/2}$.

<h3 id="38a/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [38A](#38a)

<h4 id="38a/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#38a/d)

Linearizing the constraint gives $\delta\rho=-2\rho_0\delta a/a_0$. Since $\delta p=\delta\rho/3$, the [acceleration](../../../classical-mechanics.md#acceleration) equation gives $\delta\ddot a=2\Lambda\delta a$. Hence perturbations contain $e^{\sqrt{2\Lambda}t}$: the Einstein static universe is unstable.

## 39C

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="39c/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [39C](#39c)

<h4 id="39c/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#39c/a)

[Stokes flow](../../../stokes-flow.md) obeys $-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2u=0$, $\nabla\cdot u=0$. Reversing all applied forces reverses $u$ while leaving [streamlines](../../../fluid-mechanics.md#streamline) unchanged. Reflection in the vertical plane containing the cylinder axis rules out rotation normal to that plane; reflection combined with reversibility rules out rotation within it and spin about the axis. Thus its orientation remains fixed.

<h3 id="39c/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [39C](#39c)

<h4 id="39c/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#39c/b)

Lubrication gives $u_r=(p_r/2\mu)z(z-h)$ and continuity gives $\int_0^h u_rdz=-Vr/2$. Hence $p_r=6\mu Vr/h^3$ and $p-p_0=3\mu V(r^2-a^2)/h^3$. Balancing the upward [pressure](../../../thermodynamics.md#pressure) force with $W$ yields

$$
\dot h=-\frac{2Wh^3}{3\pi\mu a^4}.
$$

**Therefore $h^{-2}=h_0^{-2}+4Wt/(3\pi\mu a^4)$ and $h\sim(3\pi\mu a^4/4Wt)^{1/2}$.**

## 40C

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="40c/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [40C](#40c)

<h4 id="40c/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#40c/a)

Substitution gives the displayed relation with $c_P^2=(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho$ and $c_S^2=\mu/\rho$. Resolving $A$ parallel and perpendicular to $k$ gives a longitudinal P mode of speed $c_P$ and two transverse polarizations of speed $c_S$, conventionally SV in and SH normal to the propagation plane.

<h3 id="40c/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [40C](#40c)

<h4 id="40c/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#40c/b)

In the lower solid $l^2+m^2=\omega^2/c_S^2$. Put $m'^2=\omega^2/c_S'^2-l^2$ and $Z=\mu'm'\cot(m'h)$. Matching displacement and shear stress at $y=0$, with zero displacement at $h$, gives

$$
R=-\frac{Z+i\mu m}{Z-i\mu m}.
$$

For real propagating parameters $|R|=1$: the rigidly backed lossless layer transmits no mean energy, so all incident energy is reflected (though with a phase shift).

## 41B

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="41b/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [41B](#41b)

<h4 id="41b/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#41b/a)

With $x=\cos\theta$, $T_k(x)=\cos k\theta$, proving orthogonality. In the convention $f\sim c_0+\sum_{k\ge1}c_kT_k$, $c_0=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi f(\cos\theta)d\theta$ and $c_k=2\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi f(\cos\theta)\cos k\theta d\theta$. For $f=\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $c_0=2/\pi$, odd coefficients vanish, and $c_{2m}=-4/[\pi(4m^2-1)]$.

<h3 id="41b/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [41B](#41b)

<h4 id="41b/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#41b/b)

The substitution $x=\cos\theta$ identifies the weighted norm with the $L^2(0,\pi)$ norm of $f(\cos\theta)$. Completeness of cosine Fourier [series](../../../real-analysis.md#series-mathematics) therefore makes the Chebyshev partial sums converge in $\|\cdot\|_w$.

<h3 id="41b/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [41B](#41b)

<h4 id="41b/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#41b/c)

Parseval's identity for the cosine [series](../../../real-analysis.md#series-mathematics), with $\|T_0\|_w^2=\pi$ and $\|T_k\|_w^2=\pi/2$ for $k\ge1$, gives the claimed equality.

<h3 id="41b/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [41B](#41b)

<h4 id="41b/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#41b/d)

Repeated integration by parts in the cosine-coefficient [integral](../../../calculus.md#integral) shows $|c_k|\le C_rk^{-r}$ for every $r$ when $f$ is smooth (endpoint terms vanish after using the even periodic extension). Parseval then bounds the squared tail by $C_r\sum_{k>N}k^{-2r}$, which is $O(N^{-2r+1})$. Choosing $r$ arbitrarily large proves spectral convergence.
