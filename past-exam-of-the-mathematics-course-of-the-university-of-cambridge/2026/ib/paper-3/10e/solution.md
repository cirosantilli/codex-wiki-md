<h1 id="10e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10E](../10e.md)

Generators define a surjection $R^k\to M$, and conversely images of the standard [basis](../../../../../basis.md) generate any quotient. Writing $\phi(m_i)=\sum_j a_{ij}m_j$ with $a_{ij}\in I$ and applying the adjugate to $XI-A$ gives a monic annihilating [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) whose lower coefficients lie in $I$. Taking $\phi=1$ when $IM=M$ yields $xM=0$ with $x-1\in I$; for $M=\mathbb Z/3$ and $I=(2)$ take $x=3$. The Jacobson radical criterion follows by placing a nonunit $1-rs$ in a maximal [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md). It yields Nakayama’s lemma. Applying the [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) trick to preimages of a finite generating set constructs a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) right inverse to any surjective endomorphism, proving injectivity.
