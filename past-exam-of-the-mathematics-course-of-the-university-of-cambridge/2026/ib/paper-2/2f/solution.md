<h1 id="2f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2F](../2f.md)

Differentiability at $p$ means $f(p+h)=f(p)+Lh+o(|h|)$ for a [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) $L=Df(p)$; [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) are its values on coordinate [vectors](../../../../../vector.md). Integrating the [partial derivatives](../../../../../partial-derivative.md) along the two coordinate segments and using continuity at $p$ proves the stated sufficient condition. Here $f(x,y)=g(x)+g(y)$ with $g(t)=t^2\sin(1/t)$ and $g(0)=0$. Since $g\prime(0)=0$, $f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) everywhere. But $g\prime(t)=2t\sin(1/t)-\cos(1/t)$ does not approach $0$ at zero, so $f$ is $C^1$ exactly where $xy\ne0$.
