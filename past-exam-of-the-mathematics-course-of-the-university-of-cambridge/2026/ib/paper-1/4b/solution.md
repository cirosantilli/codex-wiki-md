<h1 id="4b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4B](../4b.md)

Fermat’s principle makes $\int ds/c$ stationary. Writing the ray as $x=x(y)$ gives a [Lagrangian](../../../../../lagrangian.md) independent of $y$, and its first [integral](../../../../../integral.md) is $\sin\theta/c=K$. At the origin $K=(1/\sqrt2)/(1/\sqrt3)=\sqrt{3/2}$. At $x=1$, $c=1/\sqrt2$, so $\sin\theta=\sqrt3/2$ and the forward angle is $\theta=\pi/3$.
