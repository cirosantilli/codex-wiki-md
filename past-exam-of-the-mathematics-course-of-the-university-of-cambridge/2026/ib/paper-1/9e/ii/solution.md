<h1 id="9e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

One direction follows because finitely many nilpotent coefficients generate a nilpotent [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md). Conversely, reducing a [nilpotent polynomial](../../../../../../nilpotent-polynomial.md) modulo $N$ gives a nilpotent [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) over the reduced [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) $R/N$; comparing its highest nonzero coefficient shows it must be zero, so every coefficient lay in $N$.
