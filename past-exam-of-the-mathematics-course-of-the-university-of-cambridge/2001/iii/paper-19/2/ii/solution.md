<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The $q$-power [Frobenius isogeny](../../../../../../frobenius-isogeny-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) sends affine coordinates to $(x^q,y^q)$, has degree $q$, and fixes exactly the points defined over $\mathbb F_q$, including the origin. Consequently

$$
E(\mathbb F_q)=\ker(1-\phi)(\overline{\mathbb F}_q).
$$

The degree is $q$ because the $q$-power Frobenius on a smooth curve has function-field inseparable degree $q$. By the permitted separability of $1-\phi$, this [separable isogeny](../../../../../../separable-isogeny.md) has exactly $\deg(1-\phi)$ geometric kernel points; there is no inseparable multiplicity to remove. Part(i) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\#E(\mathbb F_q)=\deg(1-\phi)=q+1-\operatorname{tr}(\phi).}
$$

This relates the [elliptic-curve point count over a finite field](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-point-count-over-a-finite-field.md) to the integer trace, rather than treating the point count as an independently assumed definition of the trace.
