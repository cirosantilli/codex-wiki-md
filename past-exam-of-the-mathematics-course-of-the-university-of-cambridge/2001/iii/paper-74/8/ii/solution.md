<h1 id="8/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $H_k$ be the ordinary [Hilbert class field](../../../../../../hilbert-class-field.md) of $k$. Its real places split completely, so $H_k$ is totally real. Since $K/k$ is quadratic and $K$ is totally imaginary, $H_k\cap K$ cannot equal $K$; hence it equals $k$. Therefore the [compositum](../../../../../../field-compositum.md) $KH_k$ has

$$
[KH_k:K]=[H_k:k]=h_k,
$$

and is an abelian extension of $K$, by restriction of the [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) to $H_k$. At finite primes it is unramified because [unramified extensions remain unramified under base change](../../../../../../unramified-extensions-remain-unramified-under-base-change.md). The field $K$ has no real places, so there is no infinite-place ramification condition left. Thus $KH_k\subseteq H_K$, where $H_K$ is the [Hilbert class field](../../../../../../hilbert-class-field.md) of $K$. Since $[H_K:K]=h_K$, the tower degree formula proves [class number divisibility for a CM extension](../../../../../../class-number-divisibility-for-a-cm-extension.md):

$$
\boxed{h_k\mid h_K.}
$$

This argument uses the ordinary class field with split real places; replacing it silently by the narrow class field would invalidate the claim that $H_k$ is totally real.
