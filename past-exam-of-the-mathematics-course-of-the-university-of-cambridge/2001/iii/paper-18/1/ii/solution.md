<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose the [incline](../../../../../../incline.md) is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_r$. Repeated [distributivity](../../../../../../distributive-property.md), followed by [commutativity](../../../../../../commutativity.md) and additive [idempotence](../../../../../../idempotence.md), expresses every element as a finite nonempty sum

$$
f_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}x_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots x_r^{\alpha_r},\qquad A\subseteq\mathbb N^r\setminus\{0\}\text{ finite and nonempty}.
$$

No multiplicative identity is assumed: a zero exponent means that the corresponding generator is omitted, and every displayed [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) has at least one factor. Different supports may represent the same element; uniqueness is unnecessary.

Associate to $A$ its upward-closed exponent set

$$
U_A=\{\beta\in\mathbb N^r:\exists\alpha\in A\ (\alpha\le\beta\text{ coordinatewise})\}.
$$

If $U_A\supseteq U_B$, each [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) of $f_B$ is either equal to, or a multiplicative extension of, a [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) of $f_A$. It is absorbed by that earlier [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md), and hence by the full sum $f_A$. Adding the terms of $f_B$ one at a time gives

$$
\boxed{U_A\supseteq U_B\ \Longrightarrow\ f_A+f_B=f_A\ \Longrightarrow\ f_A\preceq f_B.}
$$

For equal exponent vectors the first absorption is additive [idempotence](../../../../../../idempotence.md); for strictly larger vectors it is the incline's multiplicative absorption. Thus the issue is to prove that exponent upsets are [well-quasi-ordered](../../../../../../well-quasi-ordering.md) by reverse inclusion, not merely to apply [Dickson's lemma](../../../../../../dickson-s-lemma.md) to individual exponents.
