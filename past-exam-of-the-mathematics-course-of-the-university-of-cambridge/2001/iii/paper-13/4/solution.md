<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Use integral [singular chains](../../../../../singular-chain.md). The inclusion $Y\subseteq X$ identifies $C_k(Y)$ with the subgroup of $C_k(X)$ generated by the [singular simplices](../../../../../singular-simplex.md) whose images lie in $Y$. It is a chain subcomplex. Define

$$
C_k(X,Y)=C_k(X)/C_k(Y),\qquad
\bar\partial(c+C_k(Y))=\partial c+C_{k-1}(Y).
$$

This boundary is well defined because $\partial C_k(Y)\subseteq C_{k-1}(Y)$, and its square is zero because $\partial^2=0$. Its [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) is, by definition, $H_k(X,Y)$. A [relative cycle](../../../../../relative-cycle.md) has a representative $c$ with $\partial c\in C_{k-1}(Y)$; representatives of the same [relative homology](../../../../../relative-homology.md) class differ by $\partial b+y$, where $b\in C_{k+1}(X)$ and $y\in C_k(Y)$.

Inclusion and quotient induce homomorphisms $i_*:H_k(Y)\to H_k(X)$ and $j_*:H_k(X)\to H_k(X,Y)$. Define the [relative homology connecting homomorphism](../../../../../relative-homology-connecting-homomorphism.md) by

$$
\delta:H_k(X,Y)\longrightarrow H_{k-1}(Y),
\qquad \delta[c]=[\partial c].
$$

Here $\partial c$ is a cycle in $Y$ since $\partial^2c=0$. If $c$ is changed to $c+\partial b+y$, its boundary changes by $\partial y$, a boundary in $Y$, proving independence of representatives. The formula is additive, so $\delta$ is a homomorphism.

The [long exact sequence in relative homology](../../../../../long-exact-sequence-in-relative-homology.md) is

$$
\boxed{\cdots\longrightarrow H_k(Y)
\xrightarrow{i_*}H_k(X)
\xrightarrow{j_*}H_k(X,Y)
\xrightarrow{\delta}H_{k-1}(Y)
\xrightarrow{i_*}H_{k-1}(X)\longrightarrow\cdots}
$$

and ends in

$$
H_0(Y)\xrightarrow{i_*}H_0(X)
\xrightarrow{j_*}H_0(X,Y)\longrightarrow0.
$$

We prove all three types of exactness directly, so no unproved abstract lemma about [chain complexes](../../../../../chain-complex.md) is required.

At $H_k(X)$, $j_*i_*=0$ because a chain in $Y$ is zero in the relative chain [group](../../../../../group-split.md). Conversely, let an absolute cycle $c$ have $j_*[c]=0$. The definition of a relative boundary gives $c=\partial b+y$ with $y\in C_k(Y)$. Since $c$ is a cycle, $\partial y=0$. Thus $[c]=i_*[y]$, proving $\ker j_*=\operatorname{im}i_*$.

At $H_k(X,Y)$, $\delta j_*=0$ because an absolute cycle has zero boundary. Conversely, suppose $\delta[c]=0$. Then $\partial c=\partial y$ for some $y\in C_k(Y)$. Therefore $c-y$ is an absolute cycle and $j_*[c-y]=[c]$. Hence $\ker\delta=\operatorname{im}j_*$.

At $H_{k-1}(Y)$, $i_*\delta=0$ because $\partial c$ is a boundary in $X$. Conversely, if a cycle $y\in C_{k-1}(Y)$ becomes zero in $H_{k-1}(X)$, then $y=\partial c$ for some $c\in C_k(X)$. That $c$ is a [relative cycle](../../../../../relative-cycle.md), and $\delta[c]=[y]$. Thus $\ker i_*=\operatorname{im}\delta$.

These arguments also prove the assertions at shifted indices. Finally, every zero-dimensional [relative cycle](../../../../../relative-cycle.md) lifts to a chain in $C_0(X)$, and every zero-chain is an absolute cycle; this proves surjectivity onto $H_0(X,Y)$. All maps, representative changes, compositions and exactness assertions have consequently been justified from the chain definitions.
