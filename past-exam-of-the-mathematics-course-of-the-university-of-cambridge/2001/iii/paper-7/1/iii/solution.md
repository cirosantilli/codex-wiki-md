<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $\lambda\in\partial\sigma_B(x)$. If $\lambda\in\rho_A(x)$, choose a connected [open](../../../../../../open-set.md) disc about it contained in $\rho_A(x)$. On that disc the [clopen](../../../../../../clopen-set.md) subset $\rho_B(x)$ is either the whole disc or empty. Neither possibility permits a [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) point of $\sigma_B(x)$, so $\lambda\in\sigma_A(x)$. But $\sigma_A(x)\subseteq\sigma_B(x)$, so a point in the interior of $\sigma_A(x)$ is in the interior of $\sigma_B(x)$. Our point cannot be such an interior point. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\partial\sigma_B(x)\subseteq\partial\sigma_A(x)}.
$$

This is [boundary inclusion of spectra in a closed unital subalgebra](../../../../../../boundary-inclusion-of-spectra-in-a-closed-unital-subalgebra.md), established from the preceding arguments rather than quoted.
