<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

An [algebra involution](../../../../../algebra-involution.md) is a conjugate-linear map $a\mapsto a^*$ satisfying $(ab)^*=b^*a^*$ and $(a^*)^*=a$; on a [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) algebra it fixes $1$. In the Banach-star convention it is continuous, although the following spectral identity uses only its algebraic properties. An element $a$ is invertible precisely when $a^*$ is: its inverse is $(a^{-1})^*$. Applying this to $a=\lambda1-x$ proves

$$
\boxed{\sigma_A(x^*)=\{\overline\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma_A(x)\}}.
$$

For a nonunital algebra, extend the involution to its [unitization of an algebra](../../../../../unitization-of-an-algebra.md) by $(a,\lambda)^*=(a^*,\overline\lambda)$ and use the spectrum in that unitization; the same argument proves the identity.

A [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) [C-star algebra](../../../../../c-star-algebra.md) is a complex [Banach algebra](../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md) with an involution satisfying the [C-star identity](../../../../../c-star-identity.md) $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$. A [Normal element of a C-star algebra](../../../../../normal-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) satisfies $x^*x=xx^*$. The [norm](../../../../../norm.md) identity for the nonzero identity implies $\|1\|=1$.
