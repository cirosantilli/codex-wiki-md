<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Work over $\mathbb C$ with a finite-dimensional [semisimple Lie algebra](../../../../../../semisimple-lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathfrak g$. Choose a [Cartan subalgebra](../../../../../../cartan-subalgebra.md) $\mathfrak h$, its [root system](../../../../../../root-system.md) $R\subset\mathfrak h^*$, a positive system $R^+$ and its [simple roots](../../../../../../simple-root.md). Let $W$ be the [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md), generated by the corresponding root reflections, and put $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha\in R^+}\alpha$, the [Weyl vector](../../../../../../half-sum-of-positive-roots.md). The finite-dimensional irreducible [highest-weight representation](../../../../../../highest-weight-representation.md) $L(\lambda)$ is indexed by a [dominant integral weight](../../../../../../dominant-integral-weight.md) $\lambda$.

If $P$ is the [weight lattice](../../../../../../weight-lattice.md), its [group ring](../../../../../../group-ring.md) has formal symbols $e^\mu$ with multiplication $e^\mu e^\nu=e^{\mu+\nu}$. The [formal character](../../../../../../formal-character-of-a-weight-module.md) is

$$
\operatorname{ch}L(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in P}(\dim L(\lambda)_\mu)e^\mu,
$$

where $L(\lambda)_\mu=\{v:Hv=\mu(H)v\text{ for all }H\in\mathfrak h\}$ is a [weight space](../../../../../../weight-space.md). For a weight $\nu$, define the [Weyl alternant](../../../../../../weyl-alternant.md) $A_\nu=\sum_{w\in W}\det(w)e^{w\nu}$; the sign $\det(w)=(-1)^{\ell(w)}$ is the determinant of $w$ on the real root span, equivalently the parity of its expression in simple reflections.

The [Weyl character formula](../../../../../../weyl-character-formula.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ch}L(\lambda)=\frac{A_{\lambda+\rho}}{A_\rho}
=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}\det(w)e^{w(\lambda+\rho)}}{e^\rho\prod_{\alpha\in R^+}(1-e^{-\alpha})}.}
$$

The second denominator is the [Weyl denominator formula](../../../../../../weyl-denominator-formula.md). The quotient can initially be taken in the fraction field of the group ring; the formula says it is the finite character on the left, so its apparent singularities are removable. The shifts by $\rho$ make $\lambda+\rho$ strictly dominant.
