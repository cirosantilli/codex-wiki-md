## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 16](paper-16.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#schoenflies-theorem) says that a simple closed piecewise linear curve in $S^2$ separates it into two components whose closures are [piecewise linear balls](../../../topology.md#piecewise-linear-ball) of dimension two. In particular, a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) of the curve to the equator extends to a piecewise linear [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) of the whole [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere).

A [piecewise linear ball](../../../topology.md#piecewise-linear-ball) is a space piecewise linearly [homeomorphic](../../../topology.md#local-homeomorphism) to a standard [simplex](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex). Write $D=B_1\cap B_2$. The complementary [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) to $D$ in each $\partial B_j$ is a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) by the two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#schoenflies-theorem). Thus each pair $(\partial B_j,D)$ is piecewise linearly [homeomorphic](../../../topology.md#local-homeomorphism) to a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) with a chosen hemisphere. Choose these [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) maps to agree on $D$. Extend them to the interiors by coning from an interior point, after compatible subdivisions. The resulting maps identify $B_1$ and $B_2$ with the two halves of a standard ball, glued along its equatorial [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). Consequently

$$
\boxed{B_1\cup_D B_2\cong_{mathrm{PL}} B^3.}
$$

The same [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) parametrization proves a useful companion fact: if a ball is indented by removing a smaller ball attached along a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), the closure of what remains is again a ball. In the standard boundary-disk model, move the indented [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) through a [collar neighbourhood](../../../differential-geometry.md#collar-neighbourhood) to a flat [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics).

A [handle decomposition](../../../topology.md#handle-decomposition) is a filtration obtained by attaching [handles](../../../topology.md#handle) $D^r\times D^{n-r}$ along $\partial D^r\times D^{n-r}$ in the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of the preceding stage. The number $r$ is the [handle](../../../topology.md#handle) index; index-zero [handles](../../../topology.md#handle) are disjoint balls, and index-$n$ [handles](../../../topology.md#handle) close the remaining spherical [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) components. Starting with a [triangulation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#triangulation) of $M$, thicken its vertices, then its edges, and successively its higher-dimensional [simplexes](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex). In a [barycentric subdivision](../../../homology.md#barycentric-subdivision) these thickenings have precisely the indicated [handle](../../../topology.md#handle) and attaching-region product structures. Adding collars between successive attachments gives a collared [handle decomposition](../../../topology.md#handle-decomposition).

For a [critical level handle embedding](../../../topology.md#critical-level-handle-embedding), assign successive distinct heights $t_j$ to the [handles](../../../topology.md#handle). At height $t_j$ the horizontal slice contains the $j$th [handle](../../../topology.md#handle) and its attaching region. Between [handle](../../../topology.md#handle) heights, the embedded [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of the previously attached [handles](../../../topology.md#handle) travels along its product collar; there is no topological change there. The lower [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) collar meets the attaching region of the next [handle](../../../topology.md#handle), and the upper collar leaves its new [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). The stated [ambient isotopy](../../../differential-geometry.md#ambient-isotopy) therefore separates all topology changes into horizontal [handles](../../../topology.md#handle) and all intervening motion into collars. For an embedded [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface), the local changes are the appearance of a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), attachment of a band, and disappearance of a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics).

Here is the three-dimensional conclusion, with the distinction between the bounded and unbounded regions made explicit: **a piecewise linear embedded $S^2$ in $\mathbb R^3$ bounds a piecewise linear three-ball; in the one-point compactification $S^3$, both complementary closures are three-balls.** The unbounded closure in $\mathbb R^3$ itself is not a [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) ball.

Put the embedded [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in [critical level handle embedding](../../../topology.md#critical-level-handle-embedding) position. At a regular horizontal level its intersection with the plane is a finite disjoint union of simple closed curves. An innermost such curve bounds a planar [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) whose interior misses the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Cut the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) along this curve and insert two slightly displaced copies of the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). This replaces one [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) by two [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Repeat at regular levels between the critical heights, using innermost curves first. The number of curves at these chosen levels decreases at each operation, so the process stops. Each resulting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) then lies in a slab containing at most one horizontal [handle](../../../topology.md#handle).

These terminal [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bound balls directly. With no [handle](../../../topology.md#handle) the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) transported through a collar and capped at its ends, the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of $D^2\times I$. A minimum or maximum merely supplies one of those caps. At a band level there are two planar [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circles on one side and one on the other. Cap the two circles, straighten each planar [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) using the two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#schoenflies-theorem), and straighten the joining band in its planar collar. If the circles are not nested, this gives two balls joined along a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics); if they are nested, it gives a ball with one boundary-attached ball removed. The [gluing three-balls along a boundary disk](../../../topology.md#gluing-three-balls-along-a-boundary-disk) argument and its indentation version show in both cases that the terminal [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bounds a ball. This also explains why the planar position of the band matters: no assertion about an arbitrary knotted band is being made.

Reverse the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) surgeries. Suppose the two resulting [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bound balls $A$ and $B$. Their disjoint boundaries imply that the balls are disjoint or one contains the other. Shrink the small displacement of the two surgery caps back to the original planar [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). In the disjoint case, the pre-surgery [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bounds the union of two balls along that [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). In the nested case, it bounds the larger ball with the smaller boundary-attached ball removed. Both are balls by the preceding elementary facts. Induction through the finitely many surgeries proves the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem). To treat the other region in $S^3$, remove a point inside the ball just constructed and apply the same argument in the resulting copy of $\mathbb R^3$.

A [3-manifold](../../../topology.md#3-manifold) is an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold) when every piecewise linear embedded [two-sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#two-sphere) bounds a ball. Suppose $p:\mathbb R^3\to M$ is a [universal cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#universal-cover), and lift an embedded [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $S$ to $\widetilde S$. Since $S$ is [simply connected](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space), its lift maps homeomorphically to $S$. By the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem), $\widetilde S$ bounds a [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) ball. The inverse image of $S$ is locally finite, so only finitely many other lifted [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) lie inside this ball. Choose an innermost one, bounding a ball $B$ whose interior meets no lift of $S$.

The interior of $B$ maps to one component $U$ of $M\setminus S$. This restriction is a [covering map](../../../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) onto $U$: a path in $U$ starting in the image lifts into $\operatorname{int}B$, since it cannot cross a lifted [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). The covering is proper, because a [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) subset of $U$ has preimage closed in the [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) ball and cannot meet $\partial B$. Near $S$, the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map $\partial B\to S$ is one-to-one, so this [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) covering has one sheet. The map on $B$ is therefore a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) onto $U\cup S$. Thus **every embedded [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in $M$ bounds a ball**, and $M$ is an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold).

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

We prove the [Loop theorem](../../../topology.md#loop-theorem) by a finite tower of double [covering spaces](../../../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space), keeping track of the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) class during the descent. This works without assuming [orientability](../../../topology.md#orientability) or [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold).

Let $F$ be the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component containing the basepoint. A nontrivial element in the kernel of $\pi_1(F)\to\pi_1(M)$ is represented by the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of a singular [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) $u:D^2\to M$. Arrange that the interior of the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) maps into the interior of $M$, and make $u$ simplicial after [simplicial subdivision](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplicial-subdivision). Let $V_0$ be a small regular neighbourhood of its image, retaining a subsurface $B_0\subset\partial V_0$ in $F$ containing the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) loop. More generally, during the construction we require the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) loop to stay outside the [normal subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup) which is the kernel of the map from the current [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) subsurface to $\pi_1(F)$. This records exactly the information needed at the end.

If $V_0$ has a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) double cover, lift $u$ to that cover and take a regular neighbourhood $V_1$ of the lifted [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) image. Repeat this whenever the current neighbourhood has a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) double cover. This procedure is finite. Indeed, let $C_i$ be the simplicial image of the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) at stage $i$. The full inverse image of $C_i$ in a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) double cover is [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), since the cover retracts to this inverse image. It is the union of the lifted [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) image $C_{i+1}$ and its translate under the nontrivial deck transformation. These two images must meet. At least one image [simplex](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex) downstairs consequently has two distinct preimages represented in $C_{i+1}$. No image [simplex](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex) disappears, so the number of distinct image [simplexes](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex) strictly increases. This number is at most the number of [simplexes](../../../algebraic-topology.md#simplex) of the fixed domain [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). Thus the [tower proof of the loop theorem](../../../topology.md#tower-proof-of-the-loop-theorem) terminates.

At the top, $V_r$ has no [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) double cover. Equivalently, $H^1(V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0$. Mod-two [Poincare-Lefschetz duality](../../../cohomology.md#lefschetz-duality) and the relative [homology](../../../homology.md) sequence give

$$
H_2(V_r,\partial V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0,
\qquad
H_1(\partial V_r;\mathbb F_2)\hookrightarrow H_1(V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0.
$$

The [classification of compact surfaces](../../../topology.md#classification-of-compact-surfaces) now makes every component of $\partial V_r$ a [two-sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#two-sphere). The lifted [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) subsurface $B_r$ is planar. Its [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) is normally generated by its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circles. Since the lifted loop is outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup), one [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circle of $B_r$ is outside that subgroup. That circle bounds a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) on its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere); push the interior of this [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) into $V_r$. We have obtained an embedded [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) with the required boundary-class property at the top of the tower.

We next descend one double cover. Projecting an embedded [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) gives a singular [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) with only double curves: triple points and branch points cannot arise from a two-sheeted cover. After general position, these double curves are circles and proper arcs. The following cut-and-paste procedure eliminates them while preserving a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) class outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup).

First eliminate the circular double curves. Take a double circle innermost on one of its preimage sheets. If its two preimage circles bound disjoint subdisks, cut out the two subdisks and interchange small displaced copies of them; discard any resulting closed component and retain the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) carrying the original outer [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). If the circles are nested, replace the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) bounded by the outer preimage circle by the innermost [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), pushed slightly onto the appropriate sheet. Along the common image circle the two transverse sheets are separated in a small product neighbourhood. In the nested case the intervening [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) may have its sides interchanged under projection; separating the two sheets on its double cover and then rounding the two opposite corners makes the same replacement downstairs. The outer [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is unchanged, no new double curve is introduced, and the chosen double circle disappears. Repeating leaves only double arcs.

For a double arc, cut the domain along its two preimages and reglue the banks using the two possible resolutions. There are two [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) choices. At least one has [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup). Here is the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) calculation that proves this, rather than assuming that an arbitrary resolution preserves essentiality. With connecting paths absorbed into four [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) words $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$, the old word is $W=\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$. In one configuration the two possible new words are

$$
u=\alpha\gamma,\qquad v=\alpha\beta^{-1}\gamma\delta^{-1},\qquad
W=u\delta^{-1}v^{-1}u\delta.
$$

In the other configuration they are

$$
u=\alpha\gamma^{-1},\qquad v=\alpha\delta\gamma\beta,\qquad
W=u(\gamma\delta)^{-1}u^{-1}v(\gamma\delta).
$$

Changing the connecting paths merely conjugates these words. In either configuration, if both new [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) words belonged to a [normal subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#normal-subgroup), so would the old word. Choose a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) whose word stays outside the subgroup. The double arc used in the resolution disappears, and the remaining double curves are inherited from the old [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), so their number decreases. Remove any circular double curves again, and repeat. The resulting [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) is embedded.

Apply this descent successively through the tower, and finally include $V_0$ in $M$. Its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) lies in $F$ and represents a nontrivial element of $\pi_1(F)$; a simple closed curve in a [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) precisely when it bounds a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) in that [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface). We have therefore proved

$$
\boxed{\exists\ e:(D^2,\partial D^2)\hookrightarrow(M,\partial M)
\quad\text{with }e(\partial D^2)\text{ not bounding a disk in }\partial M.}
$$

The argument also permits a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) subsurface in place of the whole [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component. In particular, if an embedded simple closed [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) curve $c$ bounds a singular [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), use a small [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) about $c$ as the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) subsurface. The embedded [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) produced has [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) an essential simple closed curve in that [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics), hence [isotopic](../../../differential-geometry.md#isotopy) to $c$. Extend that [isotopy](../../../differential-geometry.md#isotopy) through a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) collar. This proves the fixed-boundary form of [Dehn's lemma](../../../topology.md#dehn-s-lemma): the original curve $c$ itself bounds an embedded [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) with interior in the manifold.

Now consider the knot map. Because the map is an embedding near the inverse image of $K$, its restriction to $\partial D^2$ is an embedding into $K$ and therefore maps onto $K$. No interior point can map to $K$, since it would duplicate a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) image. [Compactness](../../../topology.md#compact-space) away from a small domain collar permits a sufficiently small tubular neighbourhood of $K$ to be chosen whose inverse image lies in the embedded collar. Its intersection with this collar cuts off an [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics). The rest of the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) maps into the [knot exterior](../../../knot-theory.md#knot-exterior), with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) a simple closed curve $c$ on the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [torus](../../../topology.md#torus).

The [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) shows that $c$ runs once along $K$. The mapped [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) in the exterior gives zero meridional coefficient, equivalently zero [linking number](../../../knot-theory.md#linking-number) with $K$. Thus $c$ is a longitude. Apply the fixed-boundary [Dehn's lemma](../../../topology.md#dehn-s-lemma) just proved to obtain an embedded [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) in the exterior bounded by $c$. Attach the embedded collar [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) between $c$ and $K$. This gives an embedded spanning [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) for $K$. A regular neighbourhood of that [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) is a standard [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) with $K$ its equatorial curve, so **$K$ is the unknot**.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

A [normal surface](../../../topology.md#normal-surface) meets each [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron) in disjoint [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) of the four triangular and three quadrilateral types. Its arcs in a face join distinct edges, each [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) meets an edge at most once, and at most one quadrilateral type occurs in a [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron). These last requirements exclude, for example, a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) with an octagonal [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set).

We first establish the surgery operation needed for the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) system. Suppose a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) $D$ disjoint from the other [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) compresses one [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $S$. It produces [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $S'$ and $S''$. Small parallel copies of the three [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bound a three-times [punctured three-sphere](../../../topology.md#punctured-three-sphere) $P$. Denote the old piece on the uncompressed side by $A$, and the two pieces on the compressed side by $B'$ and $B''$. If both $B'$ and $B''$ were punctured three-spheres, then $P$ glued to them would also be a punctured three-sphere, contrary to the hypothesis on the original cut manifold. Choose, say, $B'$ which is not of this type, and retain $S'$ alone in place of $S$.

The other new piece is $A\cup P\cup B''$. It too cannot be a punctured three-sphere. If it were, cap its external [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) to make $S^3$. All the [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) separating $A$ from the attached pieces then bound [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball) by the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem); the component containing $A$ would be a punctured three-sphere, contradicting the hypothesis on $A$. Thus the [sphere surgery preserving nontrivial complementary pieces](../../../topology.md#sphere-surgery-preserving-nontrivial-complementary-pieces) keeps exactly $k$ [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) and keeps every complementary piece nontrivial. The assumption that all [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) separate ensures that the old and new pieces used here really are distinct sides.

Apply general position to the whole [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) system, avoiding the vertices and making it transverse to edges and faces. We describe a terminating normalization, allowing the replacement operation just proved as well as [isotopy](../../../differential-geometry.md#isotopy). If some component of its intersection with a [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron) is not a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), choose a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circle innermost among circles not bounding such [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) components. A [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) just inside the tetrahedral [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) gives a compression; if intervening [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) components occur, first push them into collars of their [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). Perform the compression and retain one new [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) as above. This replaces a non-disk tetrahedral piece by [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) pieces without increasing edge intersections. Repetition is finite: every compression divides the original [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) along a separating circle, and the sum of $1-\chi$ over the non-disk planar tetrahedral pieces decreases; [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) components can then be put in [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) collars. A closed [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) component wholly in a [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron) is impossible, because it would cut off a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball), contrary to the complementary-piece condition.

Now each tetrahedral piece is a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). A face circle innermost in a face allows its incident [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) to be moved through that face [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), removing the circle. This reduces the number of face intersections without increasing the edge weight. A returning face arc similarly gives an edge bigon across which the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) can be pushed, reducing the number of edge intersections by two. Restore tetrahedral [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) pieces by the preceding operations whenever necessary.

It remains to exclude multiple intersections of a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) with an edge, which the absence of returning face arcs alone does not exclude. Suppose a tetrahedral [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) meets an edge twice. Among such [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) choose a pair of intersections consecutive on the edge and with no other [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) intersection between them. Such a pair exists: an intervening [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) crosses the edge interval an even number of times, so passing to an innermost intervening pair eventually gives consecutive points. Join them by an arc in the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). The [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) pieces divide the [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron) into [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball), and this arc together with the edge interval lies in the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of one of these [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball). It therefore bounds a bigon with interior disjoint from the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface). Push across the bigon to reduce edge weight by two. At fixed weight the previous moves reduce face and non-disk complexity; the edge weight itself is a nonnegative integer. Hence the whole process terminates. A final tetrahedral [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) meets each edge at most once, and its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) separates either one vertex from three or two vertices from two. It is respectively a normal triangle or quadrilateral. Thus **a normal system of exactly $k$ [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) with the required complementary-piece property exists**.

For the finite decomposition, we need a bound on such normal systems, not merely an assertion that they have been normalized. Let $f_2$ be the number of faces of the fixed [triangulation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#triangulation). In a triangular face cut by normal arcs, all but at most four complementary regions are rectangles between adjacent parallel arcs. Thus at most $4f_2$ complementary three-dimensional components contain a nonrectangular face region. Every remaining component is assembled from product regions between parallel normal [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), so is an interval bundle whose [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) components are [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Its [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) base is either $S^2$ or $\mathbb{RP}^2$. The product $S^2\times I$ is a [punctured three-sphere](../../../topology.md#punctured-three-sphere), and is excluded. The other possibility is the twisted interval bundle over $\mathbb{RP}^2$, namely $\mathbb{RP}^3$ with a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) removed. Each such piece contributes a distinct $\mathbb F_2$ summand to $H_1(M;\mathbb F_2)$: gluing along [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) does not identify first-homology classes, by the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mayer-vietoris-sequence). Since $k$ separating [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) give $k+1$ components, we obtain the explicit finite bound

$$
\boxed{k+1\leq 4f_2+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_1(M;\mathbb F_2).}
$$

For an arbitrary closed [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) $X$, first cut out nonseparating [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). A neighbourhood of one such [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) together with an arc joining its two sides is a punctured $S^2$-bundle over $S^1$; the bundle is the product or the twisted bundle according to the [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of the attaching identification. Its complement is [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), so this extracts a [connected sum](../../../differential-geometry.md#connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds) factor. The mod-two first Betti number of the remaining capped manifold decreases by one. Hence only finitely many such extractions are possible. The remaining manifolds have only separating [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere).

Whenever a remaining factor is not a [prime three-manifold](../../../topology.md#prime-three-manifold), split it along a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) into two factors neither [homeomorphic](../../../topology.md#local-homeomorphism) to $S^3$. Viewed in the original remaining manifold, all these successive splitting [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) form a disjoint system whose pieces are not punctured three-spheres. The normalization and bound just proved prohibit infinitely many such splittings. This proves **existence of a finite prime decomposition**, including for $X$ without assuming [orientability](../../../topology.md#orientability).

For completeness, both [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bundles extracted above are [prime three-manifolds](../../../topology.md#prime-three-manifold). Their [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) is $\mathbb Z$, so a separating [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) and the [van Kampen theorem](../../../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem) express $\mathbb Z$ as a [free product](../../../algebraic-topology.md#free-product) in which one capped factor has trivial [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group). Its punctured piece lifts homeomorphically to the [universal cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#universal-cover) $S^2\times\mathbb R$, identified with $\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}$. That [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) lifted piece has one spherical [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) and must be the bounded side of this [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in $\mathbb R^3$. It misses the origin, so the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem) makes it a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball). The corresponding capped factor is $S^3$, as required.

Suppose now that $X$ is [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface). A prime factor which is not an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold) contains a nonseparating [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere): an essential separating [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) would already split it nontrivially. Extracting the corresponding [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) bundle shows that the factor must be $S^2\times S^1$. Thus the other nontrivial primes are irreducible.

Consider two prime decompositions. Represent each by a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) system, including one nonseparating [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in each $S^2\times S^1$ factor. The capped pieces are now the irreducible factors and copies of $S^3$. Make the two [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) systems transverse. To remove their intersections, choose a circle innermost on a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) of the second system and surger the first system along its [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). Retain both surgery [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) temporarily. The old [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) and the new pair bound a three-times punctured [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere); adding the pair and then deleting the old [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) only inserts or removes spherical punctures.

This operation cannot alter an irreducible capped piece: every new [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in that piece bounds a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball), so it can only split off a punctured $S^3$ piece. The other pieces were already punctured $S^3$, where the same conclusion follows from the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../topology.md#three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem). After small displacements the surgery removes the chosen intersection circle and introduces none. Repeat until the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) systems are disjoint. Their union is a common refinement, and its non-$S^3$ capped irreducible pieces are precisely those of each original decomposition. Hence these factors agree, up to order and orientation-preserving [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism).

Finally, the number $r$ of $S^2\times S^1$ factors is determined by

$$
r=b_1(X;\mathbb Q)-\sum_P b_1(P;\mathbb Q),
$$

where the sum runs over the common irreducible factors. This uses additivity of first [homology](../../../homology.md) under [connected sum](../../../differential-geometry.md#connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds), not a claim that the [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) determines a manifold. Therefore **the [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) prime decomposition is unique up to order and insertion or deletion of $S^3$ factors**.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

For $t$ [tetrahedra](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron), a [normal surface](../../../topology.md#normal-surface) has a vector $x\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{7t}$ counting four triangle types and three quadrilateral types in each [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron). On every glued face, the numbers of arcs of each of its three types must agree on the two sides. These give homogeneous integer [linear equations](../../../linear-algebra.md#linear-equation)

$$
Ax=0.
$$

For closed [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) the coordinates of [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) meeting an unglued [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) face must also vanish. The nonnegativity and matching equations are not the whole admissibility condition: in each [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron) at most one of its three quadrilateral coordinates can be nonzero. Conversely, for an admissible vector, arrange the prescribed parallel [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) and glue their face arcs in order. This reconstructs an embedded [normal surface](../../../topology.md#normal-surface), unique up to normal [isotopy](../../../differential-geometry.md#isotopy).

A [fundamental normal surface](../../../topology.md#fundamental-normal-surface) corresponds to a nonzero admissible vector which cannot be written as $y+z$ with both $y$ and $z$ nonzero admissible vectors. These indecomposable vectors form a fundamental set: every admissible vector is their finite sum. To find them, fix which quadrilateral type is allowed in each [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron). This leaves the integer points of a rational polyhedral cone $Ax=0,\ x\geq0$, with some coordinates set to zero. Compute its [Hilbert basis of a rational cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#hilbert-basis-of-a-rational-cone), for example by subdividing into rational simplicial cones and enumerating the lattice points in their fundamental parallelepipeds along with their integral ray generators, then removing decomposable generators. Repeat the finite list of quadrilateral choices, remove duplicates, and remove vectors decomposable in their compatible sector. Finiteness follows because each of these pointed rational cones has a finite Hilbert basis.

Let $E$ be the [link exterior](../../../knot-theory.md#link-exterior). By [Alexander duality](../../../cohomology.md#alexander-duality), for a two-component link

$$
H_2(E;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2.
$$

An embedded [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) closed [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) separates $S^3$; its mod-two class is nonzero exactly when the two link components lie on different sides. The connectedness qualification matters: two parallel splitting [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) form a disconnected [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) with zero total class. We use the criterion only for [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface).

Normalize a splitting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) by [isotopy](../../../differential-geometry.md#isotopy), [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) compressions and removal of components lying inside [tetrahedra](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron). A compression replaces a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) by two [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) whose mod-two classes add to the old class; at least one is still a splitting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). The discarded tetrahedral [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) have zero class. Thus there is a normal splitting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Choose one, $S$, of least [normal surface weight](../../../topology.md#normal-surface-weight).

Suppose its vector is decomposable. Realize $S=A+B$ as a [Haken sum](../../../topology.md#haken-sum), choosing a decomposition with the least possible number of intersection curves. Two elementary exchange observations are useful. First, $A$ and $B$ can be taken [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space). If their total union has more than two components, perform regular exchanges connecting components until exactly two embedded groups remain, resolving any intersections within each group. Since the final sum is [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space), this can be done before the last joining exchange; it uses up at least one of the old intersection curves. The resulting two [normal surfaces](../../../topology.md#normal-surface) still have total vector $x(S)$, contradicting minimality. Second, no intersection curve separates both $A$ and $B$. If it did, resolve that curve first: the two separated sides give two groups. Resolve any remaining self-intersections within each group and continue exchanges between groups only while the result remains disconnected. Just before the exchange making the final [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface), there are again exactly two embedded normal groups with fewer intersection curves. Both assertions follow from the local four-bank resolution at an intersection circle; internal crossings are resolved before regarding a group as one embedded summand.

Every closed embedded [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) in $S^3$ is [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface). Additivity of [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic) in the [Haken sum](../../../topology.md#haken-sum) gives

$$
2=\chi(S)=\chi(A)+\chi(B).
$$

For [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) closed [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) the positive [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic) is $2$, and the only zero [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic) is that of a [torus](../../../topology.md#torus). Thus one summand, say $A$, is a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) and the other $B$ is a [torus](../../../topology.md#torus). Both have smaller weight than $S$. The [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $A$ cannot split the link by minimality, so

$$
[A]=0,\qquad [B]=[S]\neq0.
$$

Each intersection circle separates the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $A$, so the second exchange observation makes it nonseparating on $B$. In particular, these circles are essential parallel curves on the [torus](../../../topology.md#torus).

Cut $A$ along the intersection circles. There are at least two innermost [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) patches. Choose one of smallest weight, $D$; then $2w(D)\leq w(A)$. Its interior misses $B$, and its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is essential on $B$. Compress the [torus](../../../topology.md#torus) along two slightly separated copies of $D$. The result is a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) $C$, and the compression takes place in the exterior, so

$$
[C]=[B]\neq0,\qquad w(C)\leq w(B)+2w(D)\leq w(S).
$$

Thus $C$ is a splitting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere).

This alone is not quite a contradiction if the last weight inequality is equality: $C$ need not yet be normal. At the compression seam it uses the alternative, rather than regular, exchange between the [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) patch and the [torus](../../../topology.md#torus). An intersection circle of normal [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) sheets must cross a tetrahedral face; it cannot be a closed curve entirely in two [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) interiors in one [tetrahedron](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#tetrahedron), which can be put in standard position with only intersection arcs. In a face, parallel arcs of the same type do not cross. At a crossing of different normal arc types, the alternative exchange joins two endpoints on the same edge, producing a returning arc. Pushing across its edge bigon strictly reduces weight. Normalize the resulting [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface), retaining a nonzero-class [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) at each compression as before. We obtain a normal splitting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) of weight strictly less than $w(S)$, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{a least-weight normal splitting sphere is fundamental}.}
$$

The [algorithm](../../../computer-science.md#algorithm) is now finite. Triangulate the exterior from a link diagram, enumerate its fundamental admissible vectors with the closed-surface [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) condition, reconstruct their [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface), and inspect the [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) components and [Euler characteristics](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic). A fundamental vector yields a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface), since otherwise its component vectors decompose it. A [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) closed [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) in $S^3$ with [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic) two is a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Compute its mod-two class in the finite simplicial [chain complex](../../../homology.md#chain-complex), or equivalently check which link [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) tori lie on either side. Answer yes exactly when such a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) has nonzero class. The argument proves that every split link is detected, and every detected [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) witnesses splitting. For more than two components, $H_2(E;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2^{\ell-1}$ and a nonzero [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) class separates a nonempty proper subset of the components; the same least-weight argument and finite enumeration apply.

For the example, take a once-punctured [torus](../../../topology.md#torus) $P$, an essential nonseparating simple closed curve $c\subset P$, and

$$
M=P\times I,\qquad A=c\times I.
$$

The inclusion of the [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) $A$ induces the [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $\langle c\rangle\hookrightarrow\pi_1(P)$, so $A$ is an [incompressible surface](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface). Cutting gives $(P\text{ cut along }c)\times I$, a [pair of pants](../../../topology.md#pair-of-pants-mathematics) times an interval. A [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) times an interval is a [handlebody](../../../topology.md#handlebody) of [genus](../../../topology.md#genus-of-a-surface) $1-\chi(P)$: cut the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) to a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) by proper arcs and reverse those cuts as one-handle attachments. Both the punctured [torus](../../../topology.md#torus) and the pair of pants have [Euler characteristic](../../../homology.md#euler-characteristic) $-1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{M\text{ and }M\text{ cut along }A\text{ are both genus-two handlebodies}.}
$$

This [self-reproducing incompressible annulus cut](../../../topology.md#self-reproducing-incompressible-annulus-cut) also shows why arbitrary incompressible cutting choices need not terminate.

A [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy) is a finite sequence of cuts along properly embedded two-sided [incompressible surfaces](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface), with no spherical components, whose final manifold is a disjoint union of [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball). [Disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) are permitted. In an [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) ambient manifold two-sided [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) are [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface); for an ambient manifold which is not [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) we permit two-sided [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) without assuming their [orientability](../../../topology.md#orientability) as well.

For $S^1\times F$, first cut along $\{p\}\times F$, obtaining $I\times F$. This [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is two-sided and its [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) injects as a direct factor. Next choose essential two-sided curves in $F$ until the cut [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) has [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), and cut along their products with $I$. For [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) $F$ one nonseparating curve suffices. For $F$ which is not [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) and has [genus](../../../topology.md#genus-of-a-surface) at least two, the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of an embedded [Möbius band](../../../topology.md#mobius-band) is a two-sided essential curve; neither side is a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), so its [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) injects. Cutting this curve leaves pieces with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), including the [Möbius band](../../../topology.md#mobius-band). For each resulting [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) choose a finite system of proper arcs cutting it into [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). The corresponding vertical [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) give the remaining [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy) cuts. Essential [annuli](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) have [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group), and [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) have trivial [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group), so all cuts are incompressible. The terminal products are $D^2\times I$, hence [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball). This construction covers the possibilities without [orientability](../../../topology.md#orientability) in the stated range as well as the [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) ones.

Here is a terminating construction for a [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) [irreducible three-manifold](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold) $M$ with nonempty [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). We use the permitted general results in the following precise forms: a boundary-incompressible non-ball component has a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) two-sided nonseparating [incompressible surface](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) with nonempty [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set); and a sufficiently large collection of disjoint closed [incompressible surfaces](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) has two parallel components with no intervening member. These are respectively the existence and finiteness results for [incompressible surfaces](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface).

First perform essential [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) compressions as long as possible. Their number is finite: each nonseparating compression decreases [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [genus](../../../topology.md#genus-of-a-surface), while a separating compression divides a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component into two of smaller [genus](../../../topology.md#genus-of-a-surface). More explicitly, the sum of $\max(0,2g-1)$ over the closed boundary components decreases at every essential compression. Each cut preserves [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold). To see this for any incompressible cutter, a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) in the cut manifold bounds a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) in the uncut manifold. A closed nonspherical incompressible component of the cutter cannot lie in this [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball), since its [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) could not inject into the trivial group. A component with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) cannot lie in an interior [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) either. Thus the [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) misses the cutter and survives in the cut manifold. Fundamental-group [injectivity](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) of two-sided [incompressible surfaces](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) follows from the [Loop theorem](../../../topology.md#loop-theorem), applied to their two [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) copies after cutting.

Push the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of each non-ball residual component slightly into the original $M$ to make a closed frontier $F_1$, omitting spherical components. This frontier is incompressible on its residual side, since all [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) compressions there have been exhausted. It is also incompressible on the side already cut off. Indeed, a hypothetical compression [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) on that side can be made transverse to the earlier cutting [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface). An innermost intersection circle bounds a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) on its cutting [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) by incompressibility; replacing the corresponding subdisk removes the circle, without introducing intersections with earlier cutters. Repeat in the order of the cuts. The [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) would finally lie in a product collar of the frontier, where its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is fundamental-group-injective, a contradiction. Thus $F_1$ is incompressible in $M$.

If a residual component is not a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball), choose the permitted nonseparating [incompressible surface](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), cut it, and compress the new [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) fully again. Push that [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) in to obtain a new closed frontier $F_2$ disjoint from $F_1$. Continue. [Three-manifold irreducibility](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold) persists, and the same innermost-circle proof makes every nonspherical frontier incompressible in the original $M$.

We explain why the parallel frontiers forced by the finiteness theorem cannot occur in this construction. The required product fact is: **a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) nonseparating [incompressible surface](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) in $G\times I$ cannot be proper and disjoint from $G\times\{1\}$**. To prove it, intersect the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) with vertical [annuli](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) $c\times I$ for essential curves $c$ in a closed $G$. Inessential intersection circles can be removed using incompressibility and the [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball) supplied by [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold). An outermost intersection arc has both endpoints on the lower [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set); [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) compression along its annular bigon reduces the intersections. At least one resulting component stays nonseparating, since the mod-two relative class of the original [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is the sum of the resulting classes. Essential intersection circles can likewise be removed by surgery along the [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) running to its lower [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). Retain a nonseparating incompressible component after each surgery. These operations reduce the intersection number and cut the base [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) to one with [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). Now intersect with the vertical [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) of a proper arc system cutting that base to a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). The same innermost-circle and outermost-arc operations make the [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) disjoint from these [disks](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics). It then lies in a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) $D^2\times I$, where any proper two-sided [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) separates. This is the contradiction proving the product fact.

If two frontiers in the construction were parallel with no other frontier between, their product region would contain one of the intervening nonseparating cutters, disjoint from the later frontier. The product fact forbids this. Consequently the closed-frontier construction has only finitely many stages. Once a residual irreducible component has only spherical [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), push one [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) inward. The [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) it bounds cannot be on the collar side, which contains a [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component of the manifold. The [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) and the collar therefore exhaust that component, making it a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball). We have constructed **a finite [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy), and can choose all its cutting [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) to have nonempty [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set)**.

We will use one further consequence in the map argument: these manifolds are [aspherical](../../../algebraic-topology.md#aspherical-space). Work backwards through the [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy). A [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) is [contractible](../../../algebraic-topology.md#contractible-space). At one gluing stage, the [van Kampen theorem](../../../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem) normal form identifies the lifts of the cut pieces and cutting [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) in the [universal cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#universal-cover) with a tree of spaces. The lifted pieces are [contractible](../../../algebraic-topology.md#contractible-space) by induction, and the lifted cutting [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) are [contractible](../../../algebraic-topology.md#contractible-space) because the [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) have [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) and are fundamental-group-injective. A finite subtree is [contractible](../../../algebraic-topology.md#contractible-space) by removing terminal vertices; every [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) map meets only finitely many pieces. All higher [homotopy groups](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) therefore vanish at this stage, and induction proves [asphericity from a three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#asphericity-from-a-three-manifold-hierarchy). Apply this tree argument separately at each gluing stage.

The following three entries complete a single proof of the relative-boundary [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) assertion.

<h3 id="5/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#5/i)

First show that the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map is onto and has degree $\pm1$, and that the fundamental-group [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) is actually an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism). An [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) map between closed [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) onto an open-and-closed subset by [invariance of domain](../../../topology.md#invariance-of-domain). Thus each component of $\partial M$ maps homeomorphically to a distinct component of $\partial N$.

Orient $M$ and $N$, and write

$$
f_*[M,\partial M]=d[N,\partial N].
$$

Apply the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map in the relative [homology](../../../homology.md) sequence. The [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of the right side has coefficient $d$ on every [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component of $N$. On the left, an image [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) component has coefficient $+1$ or $-1$, and an omitted component has coefficient zero. Since at least one component is present, $d=\pm1$; then no component can be omitted and all signs agree. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f|_{\partial M}:\partial M\longrightarrow\partial N\text{ is a homeomorphism},\qquad\deg f=\pm1.}
$$

Let $H=f_*\pi_1(M)$ and lift $f$ to the [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) cover $p:N_H\to N$ corresponding to $H$. If the cover were noncompact, then $H_3(N_H,\partial N_H;\mathbb Z)=0$. The lifted relative fundamental class would have zero [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), but its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is a nonzero sum of distinct [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) fundamental classes, since the original [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) and the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) components are [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space). This is impossible. The cover therefore has a finite number $r$ of sheets. Degree multiplicativity gives

$$
\pm1=\deg f=r\,\deg\widetilde f.
$$

Hence $r=1$. The image of $f_*$ is the whole target [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group), and the assumed [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) makes **$f_*$ an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism)**.

<h3 id="5/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#5/ii)

Choose a [connected](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) proper [incompressible surface](../../../topology.md#incompressible-surface) $S$ with nonempty [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) as the first cut in a [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy) of $N$. Homotope $f$ relative to $\partial M$ into transverse position and let $F=f^{-1}(S)$.

We first make $F$ incompressible. If $D$ is a compression [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) for $F$, its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) maps to a loop in $S$ which is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) in $N$. Since $S$ is fundamental-group-injective, this loop is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) in $S$. Fill it in $S$ and alter $f$ in a small neighbourhood of $D$ so that its inverse image is obtained by compressing $F$ along $D$. The alteration is a [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy): the filling and the old [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) give a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) map in $N$, which is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) by the asphericity proved above. A product collar of $S$ specifies the two sides of the modification. The integer $\sum_j\max(0,1-\chi(F_j))$ decreases at every essential compression; sphere components can be removed separately. A resulting [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) component of $F$ bounds a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) in $M$ by [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../topology.md#irreducible-three-manifold); move the map of that [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) to one side of $S$ to remove it. The required extension and [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) exist because the higher [homotopy groups](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) of $N$ vanish. Continuing leaves an incompressible $F$ with no spherical components.

For each component $F_j$, the map on [fundamental groups](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) to $S$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). Indeed, its composition with the [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $\pi_1(S)\to\pi_1(N)$ equals the composition of $\pi_1(F_j)\to\pi_1(M)$ with the [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism) $f_*$. Both maps in the latter composition are [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). A closed nonspherical [orientable](../../../differential-geometry.md#orientable-surface) $F_j$ cannot occur: its [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) would inject into $\pi_1(S)$, a [free group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group), whereas every subgroup of a [free group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) is free and a closed positive-genus [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) group is not free. Thus every component of $F$ has [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set).

Apply the permitted surface-map [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) result to $F_j\to S$, fixing its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set). A fundamental-group-injective map of [compact](../../../topology.md#compact-space) [surfaces](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) is [homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) either to a covering or, in the [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) case here, to a map into one target [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circle. The exceptional [annulus](../../../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) case is impossible: its two distinct [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circles would map onto the same circle of $\partial S$, contradicting the [injectivity](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) of the original [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map. If $S$ is a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), [injectivity](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) makes each $F_j$ a [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics), and the unique [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circle directly gives a single [disk](../../../topology.md#disk-mathematics) [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism). Otherwise each component is [homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) to a cover of $S$.

Every point of $\partial S$ has exactly one preimage on $\partial F$, since $f|_{\partial M}$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism). Every cover component contributes its positive degree to the number of preimages on every target [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) circle. Thus there is exactly one component and its covering degree is one. We have proved that **$F\to S$ can be homotoped to a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism), relative to its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set)**.

Extend this [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) to $M$, and use the two-sided collars to make $f$ a product [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) on a neighbourhood of $F$. The transverse normal direction is a degree-one local crossing, so the collars on its two sides match the two sides of $S$; any superfluous folds can be removed in the normal interval. We may now cut $M$ along $F$ and $N$ along $S$, obtaining a map whose restriction to the entire cut [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism). The other hypotheses also survive: the cut manifolds are irreducible, have nonempty [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), and the inclusion of every cut component into its uncut manifold is fundamental-group-injective by the normal form of the [van Kampen theorem](../../../algebraic-topology.md#seifert-van-kampen-theorem). A loop killed by the cut map is consequently killed by $f_*$ and hence is trivial in its source cut component.

<h3 id="5/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#5/iii)

Apply the boundary-degree argument to each source cut component. It maps to one target cut component and is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) on each of its [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) components onto distinct target [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) components. Its relative degree must be $\pm1$, so it meets the whole [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) of that target component. Since the global cut [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) map is one-to-one, two source components cannot map to the same target component; since it is onto, every target component is accounted for. Thus the cut components correspond bijectively, and each paired map satisfies the original hypotheses.

Induct on the remaining number of cuts in the chosen target [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy), keeping fixed all [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) pieces already straightened. At the final stage the target components are [three-balls](../../../topology.md#three-ball). Each corresponding source component is irreducible with a single spherical [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set), and is therefore a [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) by the inward-pushed-boundary argument above. Extend its prescribed [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set) [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) over the [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) by coning. The original [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball) map and this [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) are [homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) relative to the [boundary](../../../topology.md#boundary-of-a-set): after identifying the target with a convex [three-ball](../../../topology.md#three-ball), take their straight-line [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy). This establishes the induction base.

Reverse the cuts. On both copies of every cutting [surface](../../../topology.md#topological-surface) the two constructed maps agree with the fixed product [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) of its collar, so the component [homeomorphisms](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) glue. Their [homotopies](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) also glue, because all cut boundaries have stayed fixed. Repeating through the [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy) gives

$$
\boxed{f\simeq h\ \text{relative to }\partial M,\qquad h:M\longrightarrow N\text{ a homeomorphism}.}
$$

The proof derives the three-dimensional rigidity from the [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../topology.md#three-manifold-hierarchy) and the permitted surface-map straightening; it does not use the desired three-dimensional [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) assertion as a quoted theorem.
