<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) $A,B$ write $[A,B]$ for the [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by their [group commutators](../../../../../group-commutator.md). A [nilpotent group](../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) has a [central series](../../../../../central-series.md): a finite chain $1=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_c=G$ with $[G_i,G]\leq G_{i-1}$. Equivalently its [upper central series](../../../../../upper-central-series.md), defined by $Z_0=1$ and $Z_{i+1}/Z_i=Z(G/Z_i)$, reaches $G$. Indeed every [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) term $G_i$ lies in $Z_i$ by induction, and the [upper central series](../../../../../upper-central-series.md) itself is central. A third equivalent definition is that the [lower central series](../../../../../lower-central-series.md) $\gamma_1=G$, $\gamma_{i+1}=[\gamma_i,G]$ terminates at $\gamma_{c+1}=1$: reversing this series gives a [central series](../../../../../central-series.md), and induction along any [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) gives $\gamma_{c+1}=1$.

The least such $c$ is the [nilpotency class](../../../../../nilpotency-class.md). Nontrivial [abelian groups](../../../../../abelian-group.md) have class one. Upper-unitriangular $3\times3$ [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) have class two: their [group commutators](../../../../../group-commutator.md) lie in the central top-right-entry [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md), and further [group commutators](../../../../../group-commutator.md) vanish. The same example works over a [finite field](../../../../../finite-field.md) or over the integers, so nilpotence is not a finiteness property. [Subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) and quotients inherit nilpotence by intersecting or mapping a [central series](../../../../../central-series.md); finite [direct products of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) have class at most the largest factor class. Every [nilpotent group](../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) is soluble, since its [derived series](../../../../../derived-series.md) is contained successively in the [lower central series](../../../../../lower-central-series.md). The converse fails: $S_3$ is soluble but its center is trivial, so it cannot be a nontrivial [nilpotent group](../../../../../nilpotent-group.md). Extensions of [nilpotent groups](../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) need not be nilpotent, as the extension $C_3\triangleleft S_3$ with quotient $C_2$ shows.

To prove the finite classification, first note that every nontrivial [finite p-group](../../../../../finite-p-group.md) has nontrivial center. In its [class equation](../../../../../class-equation.md), every noncentral [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) has size divisible by $p$, so the center's order is divisible by $p$. Induct on the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) order: the smaller p-group $P/Z(P)$ is nilpotent, and lifting its [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) and adjoining $Z(P)$ proves $P$ nilpotent. A finite [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) of prime-power-order [groups](../../../../../group-split.md) is consequently nilpotent.

Conversely suppose $G$ is finite and nilpotent. We need the [normalizer condition for nilpotent groups](../../../../../normalizer-condition-for-nilpotent-groups.md). For any proper [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H<G$, choose the first upper-central term $Z_i$ not contained in $H$. Then $Z_{i-1}\leq H$. Every $z\in Z_i$ commutes with $H$ modulo $Z_{i-1}$, so it normalizes $H$. Choosing $z\notin H$ proves $H<N_G(H)$.

Let $P$ be a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) and put $M=N_G(P)$. Since $P$ is normal in $M$, it is the unique Sylow p-subgroup of $M$. Any element normalizing $M$ must therefore normalize $P$, so $N_G(M)=M$. If $M$ were proper, the [normalizer](../../../../../normalizer.md) condition would contradict this equality. Hence $N_G(P)=G$: every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) is normal. For [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P,Q$ belonging to different primes, $[P,Q]\leq P\cap Q=1$, so they commute. Their product has order the product of their orders, namely $|G|$, and has trivial intersections between each factor and the product of the others. Multiplication therefore gives

$$
\boxed{G\cong\prod_{p\mid |G|}P_p.}
$$

This proves [finite nilpotent group decomposition](../../../../../finite-nilpotent-group-decomposition.md) in both directions without assuming the classification in the [normalizer](../../../../../normalizer.md) argument. It also shows that a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) is nilpotent exactly when all its [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) are normal. The finite hypothesis is essential to the prime-power direct-product statement; an infinite [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is nilpotent but is not such a finite product.
