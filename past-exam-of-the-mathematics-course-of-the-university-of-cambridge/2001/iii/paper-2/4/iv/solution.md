<h1 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Use $[g]$ for the [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) element of the [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) $A=\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p]$, so $[g][h]=[g+h]$ and $[0]=1$. The [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of the augmentation map is the [augmentation ideal](../../../../../../augmentation-ideal.md) $I$. This algebra must not initially be identified with a [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../../laurent-polynomial-ring.md): the additive [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $\mathbb Z_p$ is not abstractly generated by the integer $1$.

For $r\ge1$, map $A$ onto $\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p/p^r\mathbb Z_p]$ and call its [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $J_r$. It is generated by $[g]-1$ for $g\in p^r\mathbb Z_p$: differences of [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) elements in one coset have the form $[h]([g]-1)$. Since each such $g=p^rh$,

$$
[g]-1=([h]-1)^{p^r}\in I^{p^r}
$$

by the characteristic-$p$ binomial identity. Thus $J_r\subseteq I^{p^r}$.

The [quotient group](../../../../../../quotient-group.md) is a [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order $p^r$, generated by the image of $1$. Its [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) is

$$
\mathbb F_p[u]/(u^{p^r}-1)=\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^{p^r}),\qquad T=u-1.
$$

The image of $I$ is $(T)$ and its $p^r$th power vanishes, proving the reverse inclusion. Hence

$$
\boxed{J_r=I^{p^r},\qquad A/I^{p^r}\cong\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^{p^r})}.
$$

This is [cyclic finite quotients of the p-adic augmentation filtration](../../../../../../cyclic-finite-quotients-of-the-p-adic-augmentation-filtration.md).

For any $n\ge0$, choose $p^r>n+1$. The finite quotient then identifies $I^n/I^{n+1}$ with $(T^n)/(T^{n+1})$, a one-dimensional space generated by the image of $([1]-1)^n$. The degree-one symbol $X$ of $[1]-1$ consequently gives a surjective graded map $\mathbb F_p[X]\to\operatorname{gr}_IA$, and no power of $X$ vanishes in its degree. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{gr}_I\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p]\cong\mathbb F_p[X]}.
$$

The same finite quotients show that the [I-adic completion](../../../../../../adic-completion-of-a-module.md) is $\mathbb F_p[[T]]$, and its [associated graded ring](../../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md) is identical. The proof therefore covers the [completed group algebra](../../../../../../completed-group-algebra.md) interpretation too, while proving the literal ordinary-algebra statement without assuming completion. Applying part ii to a [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) would require its actual completeness, not just this [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [associated graded ring](../../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md).
