<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $x=I+E_{12}$, $y=I+E_{23}$ and $z=I+E_{13}$. The multiplication of matrix-coordinate triples is $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b'+ac',c+c')$. In particular $z$ is central, $xy=zyx$, and every element has the unique normal form $y^cz^bx^a$. Consequently

$$
kG\cong B[x,x^{-1};\sigma],\qquad B=k[y,y^{-1},z,z^{-1}],\qquad\sigma(y)=zy,\quad\sigma(z)=z.
$$

The distinct normal-form [monomials](../../../../../../monomial.md) are the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) [basis](../../../../../../basis.md), so this is an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md), not merely a [surjection](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) from a presented algebra. This is the [integral Heisenberg group algebra as a skew Laurent ring](../../../../../../integral-heisenberg-group-algebra-as-a-skew-laurent-ring.md).

Here is the needed [skew Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../../skew-hilbert-basis-theorem.md), including its proof. If $A$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../../../../left-noetherian-ring.md) and $\sigma$ an [automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism.md), let $J$ be a [left ideal](../../../../../../left-ideal.md) of $A[t;\sigma]$, where $ta=\sigma(a)t$. Define

$$
I_n=\{\sigma^{-n}(a_n):\ \sum_{i=0}^na_it^i\in J\}.
$$

These are [left ideals](../../../../../../left-ideal.md) of $A$: adding lifts adds the coefficient, and left multiplication by $\sigma^n(r)$ multiplies its normalized value by $r$. Multiplication of a lift by $t$ proves $I_n\subseteq I_{n+1}$. The chain stabilizes at some $N$. Choose finite generators $u_{nj}$ for each $I_n$, $0\le n\le N$, and lifts $f_{nj}\in J$ with $t^n$ coefficient $\sigma^n(u_{nj})$.

For $f\in J$ of degree $m\ge N$, write its normalized [leading coefficient](../../../../../../leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial.md) as $\sigma^{-m}(\operatorname{lc}f)=\sum_j r_ju_{Nj}$. Then

$$
f-\sum_j\sigma^m(r_j)t^{m-N}f_{Nj}
$$

has smaller degree: the displayed summands have leading coefficients $\sigma^m(r_ju_{Nj})$. For $m<N$ use the generators for $I_m$ without the shift. Induction on degree expresses every element of $J$ in terms of the finitely many chosen lifts. Thus $A[t;\sigma]$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../../../../left-noetherian-ring.md). Passing to the [opposite ring](../../../../../../opposite-ring.md) replaces $\sigma$ by $\sigma^{-1}$ and proves the corresponding right-sided result.

This proof includes the ordinary [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) when $\sigma$ is the identity. Starting with the [field](../../../../../../field.md) $k$, it makes $k[y,z]$ [Noetherian](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md); [localization](../../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) at powers of $y,z$ makes $B$ [Noetherian](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) as well. Finally, for a [left ideal](../../../../../../left-ideal.md) $L$ in $B[x,x^{-1};\sigma]$, its intersection $J$ with $B[x;\sigma]$ has finitely many generators. For each $f\in L$, some $x^qf$ lies in $J$, so $f=x^{-q}(x^qf)$ is generated by those same elements in the [skew Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../../skew-laurent-polynomial-ring.md). Right multiplication clears powers for a [right ideal](../../../../../../right-ideal.md). Therefore **$kG$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../../../../left-noetherian-ring.md) and a [right Noetherian ring](../../../../../../right-noetherian-ring.md).**
