<h1 id="4/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Measure the cluster's baryonic mass, dominated by its [intracluster medium](../../../../../../intracluster-medium.md) with a smaller stellar contribution, and divide it by an independently inferred total mass. If this cluster baryon fraction $f_b$ is representative of the [cosmic baryon fraction](../../../../../../cosmic-baryon-fraction.md), then $f_b=\Omega_b/\Omega_m$. Thus the [cluster baryon-fraction estimate of matter density](../../../../../../cluster-baryon-fraction-estimate-of-matter-density.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Omega_m=\frac{\Omega_b}{f_b}.}
$$

[Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md) constrains the [baryon-to-photon ratio](../../../../../../baryon-to-photon-ratio.md) and hence $\Omega_bh^2$ through primordial light-element abundances. A specified $h$ converts this to $\Omega_b$; the cluster mass and gas-mass estimates also have distance-dependent $h$ factors, which must be used consistently. This method estimates the total nonrelativistic matter density, including baryonic and [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md). It does not add radiation or vacuum energy to that inferred $\Omega_m$; in a matter-dominated interpretation the question's $\Omega$ is this matter parameter.

[Primordial deuterium as a baryon-density indicator](../../../../../../primordial-deuterium-as-a-baryon-density-indicator.md) works because increasing baryon density makes the destruction of [deuterium](../../../../../../deuterium.md) into heavier nuclei more efficient during [Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md). Smaller inferred primordial D/H therefore implies larger $\Omega_b$, with the other nucleosynthesis assumptions fixed. **For a fixed measured cluster baryon fraction, a smaller primordial deuterium abundance raises the inferred matter density.** A low abundance caused instead by stellar processing is not a lower primordial abundance; depletion, gas ejection and the representativeness of clusters must also be assessed before identifying the cluster fraction with the cosmic one.
