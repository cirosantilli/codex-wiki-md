<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For approximately circular motion in a spherically dominated [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md), $v_c^2(r)=GM(<r)/r$. If most gravitating mass followed the concentrated luminous [galaxies](../../../../../../galaxy-split.md), then beyond their main light distribution the enclosed mass would nearly saturate, giving a declining Keplerian [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md), $v_c\propto r^{-1/2}$.

Instead, extended [galaxy rotation curves](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) commonly remain roughly flat. The [spherical mass profile for a flat rotation curve](../../../../../../spherical-mass-profile-for-a-flat-rotation-curve.md) then requires

$$
\boxed{M(<r)\simeq\frac{v_0^2r}{G},\qquad
\rho(r)\simeq\frac{v_0^2}{4\pi Gr^2}.}
$$

The inferred mass continues growing where the luminous contribution is small, so the [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) increases outward. Under the assumed gravitational dynamics this is evidence for an extended [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md). Measurements of neutral-gas motion can probe radii beyond the bright stellar disk. A realistic disk contribution must be computed using its flattened geometry; the spherical formula describes a halo-dominated region and does not turn every disk [rotation curve](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) into a spherical mass profile.
