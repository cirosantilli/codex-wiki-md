<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [luminosity distance](../../../../../../luminosity-distance.md) is defined by $F=L/(4\pi d_L^2)$, where $L$ is the [bolometric luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) and $F$ the received [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md). In the [FRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) a sphere through the observer around a comoving source has area $4\pi a_0^2r_e^2$. The [cosmological redshift](../../../../../../cosmological-redshift.md) reduces each photon's [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) by $(1+z)^{-1}$, and [cosmological time dilation](../../../../../../cosmological-time-dilation.md) reduces their arrival rate by another such factor. Hence

$$
F=\frac{L}{4\pi a_0^2r_e^2(1+z)^2},
\qquad \boxed{d_L=(1+z)a_0r_e=(1+z)r_e.}
$$

The positive radial lookback integral is $\chi_e=c\int_{t_e}^{t_0}dt/a(t)=\int_0^{r_e}dr/\sqrt{1-kr^2}$. The printed time limits are reversed; also the factor $c$ is required unless units $c=1$ are adopted. The areal coordinate $r_e$ and the radial [comoving distance](../../../../../../comoving-radial-distance.md) $\chi_e$ agree only when $k=0$.

For an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md), the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) gives $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3/2}$. Using the [redshift-time relation](../../../../../../redshift-time-relation.md) in the radial [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) integral yields

$$
r_e=c\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}
=\frac{2c}{H_0}\left[1-(1+z)^{-1/2}\right].
$$

Thus the [Einstein-de Sitter luminosity-distance relation](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-luminosity-distance-relation.md) is

$$
\boxed{d_L(z)=\frac{2c}{H_0}\left[(1+z)-\sqrt{1+z}\right].}
$$

Its small-[redshift](../../../../../../redshift.md) expansion $d_L=(c/H_0)[z+z^2/4+O(z^3)]$ recovers [Hubble's law](../../../../../../hubble-s-law.md).
