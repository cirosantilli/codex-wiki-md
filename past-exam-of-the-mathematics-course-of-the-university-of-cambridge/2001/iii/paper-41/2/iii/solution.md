<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md), $a\propto t^{2/3}$ and $H=2/(3t)$. The [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) gives

$$
\rho_m=\frac{3H^2}{8\pi G}=\frac1{6\pi Gt^2},
\qquad
\ddot\delta+\frac4{3t}\dot\delta-\frac2{3t^2}\delta=0.
$$

This is an [Euler differential equation](../../../../../../cauchy-euler-equation.md). Substitution of $\delta=t^p$ gives $p(p-1)+(4/3)p-2/3=0$, namely $(p-2/3)(p+1)=0$. The [Einstein-de Sitter density-growth modes](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-density-growth-modes.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\delta(t)=C_+t^{2/3}+C_-t^{-1}
=A_+a(t)+A_-a(t)^{-3/2}.}
$$

The [linear growth factor](../../../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) for the growing mode is proportional to $a$, rather than an exponential in cosmic time. These expressions apply while the [density contrast](../../../../../../density-contrast.md) is small enough for [linear cosmological density perturbations](../../../../../../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation-split.md).
