<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

In a two-person [Nash bargaining problem](../../../game-theory.md#nash-bargaining-problem), an agreement specifies a jointly feasible pair of [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) $(u,v)$, measured in each player's own [utility function](../../../utility-function.md). For a finite game, binding agreements can randomize jointly among action pairs, so the feasible [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) region $F$ is the [convex hull](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-hull) of the pure-action [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff). This permits correlated joint actions; it is generally larger than the [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) region generated by independent [mixed strategies](../../../game-theory.md#mixed-strategy). A [disagreement point](../../../game-theory.md#disagreement-point) $d=(d_1,d_2)$ specifies what each player receives if negotiations fail. Assume a [compact convex set](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#compact-convex-set) of feasible agreements and at least one agreement strictly improving both disagreement [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff).

A [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) $(u',v')$ jointly dominates $(u,v)$ if $u'\ge u$, $v'\ge v$, and at least one inequality is strict. This is [joint dominance of payoff vectors](../../../game-theory.md#joint-dominance-of-payoff-vectors); strict improvement for both is unnecessary. A [Pareto optimal](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency) agreement is one that has no feasible joint dominator. A jointly dominated agreement wastes a possible gain, but [Pareto efficiency](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency) alone does not settle how gains should be divided. The [negotiation set in two-person bargaining](../../../game-theory.md#negotiation-set-in-two-person-bargaining) is

$$
B_d=\{(u,v)\in F:u\ge d_1,\ v\ge d_2,\ (u,v)\text{ is Pareto optimal}\}.
$$

Its individual-rationality constraints keep either player from accepting less than disagreement; its [Pareto efficiency](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency) constraint rules out wasted opportunities. This [negotiation set](../../../game-theory.md#negotiation-set-in-two-person-bargaining) is distinct from objection-and-counterobjection bargaining sets for coalitional games.

The [Nash arbitration procedure](../../../game-theory.md#nash-bargaining-solution) selects the [Nash bargaining solution](../../../game-theory.md#nash-bargaining-solution) by maximizing gains relative to disagreement:

$$
\boxed{(u_*,v_*)=\operatorname*{argmax}_{(u,v)\in F,\ u\ge d_1,\ v\ge d_2}
(u-d_1)(v-d_2).}
$$

The [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) is the [Nash product](../../../game-theory.md#nash-product). Essentiality makes its maximum positive; [compactness](../../../topology.md#compact-space) gives attainment. On positive gains, its logarithm is $\log(u-d_1)+\log(v-d_2)$, whose [Hessian](../../../calculus.md#hessian-matrix) is diagonal and [negative definite](../../../linear-algebra.md#negative-definite-matrix). Thus [strict concavity](../../../real-analysis.md#strict-concavity) on the feasible [convex set](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-set) makes the maximizing [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) unique. Positive gain in either coordinate increases the product, so the solution is [Pareto optimal](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency).

The procedure respects feasibility, [bargaining individual rationality](../../../game-theory.md#bargaining-individual-rationality), [Pareto efficiency](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency), [bargaining symmetry](../../../game-theory.md#bargaining-symmetry), [positive affine invariance in bargaining](../../../game-theory.md#positive-affine-invariance-in-bargaining), and [bargaining independence of irrelevant alternatives](../../../game-theory.md#bargaining-independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives). In particular, replacing each player's utility by a separate positive affine rescaling multiplies the product by a positive constant; it does not compare one player's raw utility scale to the other's. Deleting other feasible agreements cannot change a unique maximizer that remains feasible. Symmetry under interchange of the players gives equal selected coordinates when the bargaining problem itself is symmetric.

These properties also explain why the product rule is distinguished. Normalize a positive-gain maximizer to $(1,1)$ and disagreement to $(0,0)$ by positive [affine maps](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#affine-map). The first-order inequality along a segment from $(1,1)$ to any feasible $(a,b)$ gives $a+b\le2$. A sufficiently large symmetric triangle, bounded below in each coordinate and above by $a+b=2$, contains the normalized feasible region. Symmetry and [Pareto efficiency](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-efficiency) select $(1,1)$ in that triangle. Independence of irrelevant alternatives then selects the same point in the original normalized region, and affine invariance transforms it back. This is the [supporting triangle for Nash bargaining](../../../game-theory.md#supporting-triangle-for-nash-bargaining) argument; the assumptions of convexity and a strict joint improvement are important. Without strict improvement, a zero product need not select a unique agreement.

For a finite game, a natural disagreement convention is each player's [security level payoff](../../../game-theory.md#security-level-payoff):

$$
d_1=\max_p\min_q p^TAq,\qquad
d_2=\max_q\min_p p^TBq,
$$

where $A,B$ are the two [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) [matrices](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) and $p,q$ are [mixed strategies](../../../game-theory.md#mixed-strategy). Each player can independently guarantee at least its own security level. The [maximin bargaining solution](../../../game-theory.md#maximin-bargaining-solution) applies the [Nash product](../../../game-theory.md#nash-product) rule using this $d$. In this convention, “maximin” determines the disagreement [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff); it does not replace the arbitration [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) by $\min(u,v)$, which would depend improperly on interpersonal utility scales. The resulting agreement can strictly benefit both players relative to their guarantees, yet need not benefit both relative to a particular noncooperative [Nash equilibrium](../../../game-theory.md#nash-equilibrium).

Choose the sign convention

$$
L_b(x,\lambda)=f(x)-\lambda^T(h(x)-b),\qquad x\in X,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb R^m.
$$

This is the [optimization Lagrangian](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#optimization-lagrangian); equality-constraint [Lagrange multipliers](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-multiplier) are unrestricted in sign. The [Lagrangian sufficiency theorem](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#lagrange-sufficiency-theorem) for minimization says: if $x_*\in X$ satisfies $h(x_*)=b$ and globally minimizes $L_b(\,\cdot\,,\lambda_*)$ on $X$ for some $\lambda_*$, then it globally minimizes the constrained [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function). Indeed, for every feasible $x$,

$$
f(x)=L_b(x,\lambda_*)\ge L_b(x_*,\lambda_*)=f(x_*).
$$

This is a full global certificate, not merely stationarity. No convexity is needed for the implication, although convexity commonly supplies the required global [optimization Lagrangian](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#optimization-lagrangian) minimum.

Let $\phi(u)=\inf\{f(x):x\in X,\ h(x)=u\}$, setting $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$, and assume $\phi(b)$ is finite. The [Strong Lagrangian property](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#strong-lagrangian-property) at $b$ means that a finite multiplier attains the constrained value as an unconstrained lower bound:

$$
\boxed{\inf_{x\in X}L_b(x,\lambda_*)=\phi(b).}
$$

This includes dual attainment, but need not include primal attainment. A [non-vertical supporting hyperplane of a value function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#non-vertical-supporting-hyperplane-of-a-value-function) at $b$ has a finite slope $\lambda_*$ and supports the [epigraph](../../../calculus-of-variations.md#epigraph) from below:

$$
\phi(u)\ge\phi(b)+\lambda_*^T(u-b)\quad\text{for every }u.
$$

If this inequality holds, then for every $x\in X$,

$$
f(x)\ge\phi(h(x))\ge\phi(b)+\lambda_*^T(h(x)-b),
$$

so $\inf_XL_b(x,\lambda_*)\ge\phi(b)$. Conversely restricting the infimum to $h(x)=b$ gives $\inf_XL_b\le\phi(b)$, even if the primal infimum is approached only by a sequence. Thus equality holds. In the reverse direction, the Strong [optimization Lagrangian](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#optimization-lagrangian) identity implies $f(x)-\lambda_*^T(h(x)-b)\ge\phi(b)$ for every $x$. Taking the infimum over $h(x)=u$ yields the supporting inequality, with empty fibres causing no problem. This proves both implications without a convexity or attainment assumption beyond finite $\phi(b)$.

For the advertising calculation, measure the budget in thousands of pounds by $B=a/1000$. Requiring the full budget to be spent gives $3x+y=B$, with $x,y\ge0$. Substitute $y=B-3x$ to reduce the [maximization problem](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#maximization-problem) to

$$
F_B(x)=-14x^2+(7B-1)x+3B-B^2,\qquad 0\le x\le B/3.
$$

Its [second derivative](../../../calculus.md#second-derivative) is $-28$, so the unique constrained maximum is obtained by clipping the [stationary point](../../../calculus-of-variations.md#stationary-point):

$$
\boxed{(x_*,y_*)=
\begin{cases}
(0,B),&0\le B\le1/7,\\
(B/4-1/28,\ B/4+3/28),&B\ge1/7.
\end{cases}}
$$

The upper constraint never clips the interior formula because $B/4-1/28<B/3$ for $B\ge0$. The maximal revenue in thousands of pounds is

$$
R(B)=
\begin{cases}
3B-B^2,&0\le B\le1/7,\\
-B^2/8+11B/4+1/56,&B\ge1/7.
\end{cases}
$$

Equivalently the revenue [Hessian](../../../calculus.md#hessian-matrix) is $\begin{pmatrix}-4&1\\1&-2\end{pmatrix}$, [negative definite](../../../linear-algebra.md#negative-definite-matrix) since its leading minor is negative and its [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant) is seven. Thus the full-budget affine feasible set has a unique global maximum.

At the two requested budgets,

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
a&x_*&y_*&R(a/1000)\\ \hline
1000&3/14&5/14&37/14\\
10000&69/28&73/28&841/56
\end{array}
$$

The units in the middle columns are minutes; the last column is thousands of pounds. Revenue measured in pounds is $G(a)=1000R(a/1000)$, so

$$
\boxed{G'(1000)=\frac52,\qquad G'(10000)=\frac14.}
$$

An additional advertising pound therefore increases optimized revenue locally by £2.50 at the smaller budget and £0.25 at the larger budget. These are marginal rates, not exact finite-difference increments: on the interior branch $G(a+1)-G(a)=G'(a)-1/8000$.

The maximization multiplier in $f(x,y)-\mu(3x+y-B)$ equals $R'(B)=(11-B)/4$ on the interior branch. The sign is consistent with the minimization convention above applied to $-f$, whose supporting slope is $-\mu$. If spending is only capped by the budget, rather than required to equal it, these same answers apply at the two budgets. Beyond £11,000 the unconstrained maximum $(x,y)=(19/7,20/7)$ is affordable, so further allowed spending is unused and the capped-budget value is constant; forced full spending would instead lower revenue.

A reduction must encode primal solutions and infeasibility or unboundedness information; merely declaring a known optimum or subtracting an unknown optimum from the [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) is not a construction. The following [homogeneous self-dual embedding of a linear program](../../../convex-optimization.md#homogeneous-self-dual-embedding-of-a-linear-program) works without assuming the input problem has an optimum.

First remove linearly dependent equality rows by [Gaussian elimination](../../../numerical-analysis.md#gaussian-elimination). If an eliminated row has an inconsistent right-hand side, the original problem is already certified infeasible. Return the one-variable special-form program $\min 0$ with $A^*=0$, $y=1$, and retain that certificate; no original [mathematical optimization](../../../mathematical-optimization.md) remains. Otherwise the remaining [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) has [full row rank](../../../vector-space.md#full-row-rank); write it as $A$ again. Put $e=\mathbf1\in\mathbb R^n$ and

$$
\bar b=b-Ae,\qquad \bar c=c-e,\qquad \bar z=c^Te+1,\qquad N=n+1.
$$

Use nonnegative variables $x,s\in\mathbb R^n$ and $\tau,\kappa,\theta,\rho\in\mathbb R$, with a temporary unrestricted multiplier $\lambda\in\mathbb R^m$. Impose the [homogeneous linear system](../../../linear-algebra.md#homogeneous-linear-system)

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax-b\tau+\bar b\theta&=0,\\
-A^T\lambda+c\tau-s-\bar c\theta&=0,\\
b^T\lambda-c^Tx-\kappa+\bar z\theta&=0,\\
-\bar b^T\lambda+\bar c^Tx-\bar z\tau+N\rho&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

All coefficients are computable from the input. The temporary free multiplier can be eliminated without introducing cancelling positive/negative variables. Define

$$
H=(AA^T)^{-1}A,\quad q=c\tau-s-\bar c\theta,\quad
\lambda=Hq.
$$

Replace the second equation by $(I-A^TH)q=0$, and substitute $Hq$ in the other equations. Because $A$ has [full row rank](../../../vector-space.md#full-row-rank), $A^T\lambda=q$ is equivalent to these conditions. For no remaining equality rows, simply omit $\lambda$ and its empty equations.

Let $y=(x,s,\tau,\kappa,\theta,\rho)$ and $n^*=2n+4$. Collect these substituted equations into $A^*y=0$, impose $\mathbf1^Ty=1$, and let $c^*$ select the coordinate $\theta$. The resulting [Karmarkar standard form](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#karmarkar-standard-form) is

$$
\boxed{\min\theta,\qquad A^*y=0,\quad\mathbf1^Ty=1,\quad y\ge0.}
$$

The next two parts prove its known positive starting point and zero optimal value. Notice that normalization is legitimate for any nonzero nonnegative embedding [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) because all the equations are homogeneous; it does not require a prior bound on the original optimizer.

In the unnormalized embedding, take

$$
x=s=e,\qquad \tau=\kappa=\theta=\rho=1,\qquad\lambda=0.
$$

Then the first equation is $Ae-b+\bar b=0$, the second is $c-e-\bar c=0$, and the third is $-c^Te-1+\bar z=0$. The fourth becomes

$$
\bar c^Te-\bar z+N
=(c^Te-n)-(c^Te+1)+(n+1)=0.
$$

The multiplier elimination preserves this point because $q=c-e-\bar c=0$ and hence $Hq=0$. Every coordinate of $y$ is one. Dividing by $n^*=2n+4$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{y^{(0)}=(1/n^*,\ldots,1/n^*)^T,}
$$

which satisfies $A^*y^{(0)}=0$, $\mathbf1^Ty^{(0)}=1$, and strict positivity. Its existence is independent of feasibility of the original input problem.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

The [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) $\theta$ is nonnegative. We now construct a feasible zero-objective point, distinguishing the input problem's possible states.

If the primal is feasible and bounded below, [strong duality](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#strong-duality) and attainment for a [linear program](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#linear-programming) give $x_*\ge0$, $\lambda_*$ and $s_*=c-A^T\lambda_*\ge0$ with

$$
Ax_*=b,\qquad c^Tx_*=b^T\lambda_*.
$$

Set $\theta=\kappa=0$, $\tau=1$, $x=x_*$, $s=s_*$, $\lambda=\lambda_*$, and

$$
\rho=\frac{e^Tx_*+e^Ts_*+1}{N}>0.
$$

The first three embedding equations hold. Substituting their equalities into the fourth gives precisely $-e^Tx_*-e^Ts_*-1+N\rho=0$.

If the primal is infeasible, the [Farkas alternative](../../../convex-optimization.md#farkas-lemma) supplies $\lambda$ with $A^T\lambda\le0$ and $b^T\lambda>0$. Take $x=\tau=\theta=0$, $s=-A^T\lambda\ge0$, $\kappa=b^T\lambda>0$, and $\rho=(e^Ts+\kappa)/N$. These satisfy the same equations. If the primal is feasible and unbounded below, it has a direction in the [recession cone](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#recession-cone) $x\ge0$ with $Ax=0$ and $c^Tx<0$. Set $\lambda=s=\tau=\theta=0$, $\kappa=-c^Tx>0$, and $\rho=(e^Tx+\kappa)/N$. This is again a feasible nonzero embedding [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector). Normalize the constructed [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) by the sum of its nonnegative coordinates. In every case,

$$
\boxed{\min c^{*T}y=\min\theta=0.}
$$

The facts about optimal pairs and recession directions follow from the polyhedral alternatives of [linear programming duality](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#linear-programming-duality); they do not require knowing their values or witnesses to write the transformed coefficients.

To recover information from the transformed optimum, multiply the first three original embedding equations by $\lambda^T$, $x^T$, and $\tau$, respectively, and add. Their cross terms cancel; the fourth equation gives

$$
x^Ts+\tau\kappa=N\theta\rho.
$$

At $\theta=0$, nonnegativity forces $x^Ts=0$ and $\tau\kappa=0$. The equations also imply

$$
e^Tx+e^Ts+\tau+\kappa=N\rho.
$$

Thus any normalized zero-objective point has $\rho>0$: if $\rho=0$, all these coordinates vanish, and then $q=\lambda=\theta=0$, contradicting normalization.

If $\tau>0$, division by $\tau$ gives a primal-feasible point $x/\tau$ and dual-feasible point $\lambda/\tau$ with equal [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) values; hence they are optimal. If $\kappa>0$, then $\tau=0$, $Ax=0$, $A^T\lambda=-s\le0$, and

$$
b^T\lambda-c^Tx=\kappa>0.
$$

Therefore either $b^T\lambda>0$ certifies primal infeasibility, or $c^Tx<0$ is an improving recession direction certifying dual infeasibility and unboundedness whenever the primal is feasible. If only the latter certificate is obtained and primal feasibility is unknown, apply the same construction to the feasibility problem with [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function) $c=0$; it gives either a [feasible point](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#feasible-point) or a direct primal infeasibility certificate.

A degenerate optimal point can have $\tau=\kappa=0$ and convey neither conclusion. Choose an optimum in the [relative interior](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#relative-interior) of the [optimal face of a linear program](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#optimal-face-of-a-linear-program). The explicit existence argument above gives a point of this face with $\tau+\kappa>0$. A nonnegative coordinate which is positive somewhere on a [face of a convex set](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#face-of-a-convex-set) is positive throughout its [relative interior](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#relative-interior), so the chosen point has $\tau+\kappa>0$ and can be decoded. The assertion follows because a small relative-interior displacement in the direction opposite a positive-coordinate point would otherwise make that coordinate negative. Equivalently one may obtain a maximum-support optimum; choosing an arbitrary degenerate boundary optimum is insufficient.

This reduction is useful because an [interior-point method](../../../convex-optimization.md#interior-point-method) now has a known strictly feasible start, a compact [standard simplex](../../../algebraic-topology.md#standard-simplex) slice, and a known target [objective function](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#objective-function). Projective rescalings can recenter positive iterates, and the transformed problem supplies optimality or infeasibility information without a separate guessed optimum. Polynomial bit-complexity claims need rational data with a finite encoding length. The printed bound $U$ on arbitrary real coefficients by itself does not bound denominators or the size of nonzero [determinants](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant); it is not a substitute for that encoding assumption.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

The all-to-one [shortest path problem](../../../graph-theory.md#shortest-path-problem) asks for the minimum length $v_i$ of a [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) from every [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $i$ to the root $n$, with $v_n=0$ and $v_i=\infty$ if no such [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) exists. Positive [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) lengths allow [directed cycles](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-cycle) to be deleted, so a shortest [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path), when one exists, uses at most $n-1$ [directed edges](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge). Its optimality equations are

$$
v_i=\min_{(i,j)\in A}(c_{ij}+v_j),\qquad i\ne n.
$$

A [Bellman-Ford algorithm](../../../graph-theory.md#bellman-ford-algorithm) initializes $d_n^{(0)}=0$ and other labels to infinity, then repeats

$$
d_i^{(k)}=\min\left\{d_i^{(k-1)},\min_{(i,j)\in A}(c_{ij}+d_j^{(k-1)})\right\},
$$

keeping the root label zero. Induction on $k$ proves that $d_i^{(k)}$ is the least length among [directed paths](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) using at most $k$ [directed edges](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge): either the old best [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) remains, or the first [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) is followed by a [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) with at most $k-1$ [directed edges](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge). Thus $n-1$ rounds give the exact distances. An in-place variant repeatedly relaxes $d_i\leftarrow\min(d_i,c_{ij}+d_j)$; each decreased label triggers further incoming-arc relaxations. It terminates when no finite label can improve. The [Bellman inequalities](../../../graph-theory.md#bellman-inequalities) then telescope along each [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) to show the labels are lower bounds on all [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) lengths, while every finite label represents a discovered [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) and is an upper bound on the optimum. This proves correctness. The method is a [label-correcting shortest-path algorithm](../../../graph-theory.md#label-correcting-shortest-path-algorithm): a processed label can be revised many times. Full scans cost $O(n|A|)$.

Let $d=\min_{i\ne n}c_{in}$ and choose $j$ attaining it. Every [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) from a nonroot [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) to $n$ has a final [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) costing at least $d$, and all preceding [directed edges](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) have positive lengths. Therefore every $v_k\ge d$. The direct [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) from $j$ to $n$ has length $d$, so

$$
\boxed{v_j=c_{jn}=d\le v_k\quad(k\ne n).}
$$

If no incoming root [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) exists, all these distances are infinite and there is no finite next [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) to settle.

The all-to-one [Dijkstra algorithm](../../../graph-theory.md#dijkstra-algorithm) sets the root's label to zero and all others to infinity. Repeatedly choose an unsettled [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $j$ with least finite label, make that label permanent, and relax every incoming [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) $(i,j)$ of an unsettled [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory):

$$
d_i\leftarrow\min\{d_i,c_{ij}+d_j\}.
$$

Store the corresponding successor $j$ whenever the label improves. Stop when all [vertices](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) are settled or every remaining label is infinite. This applies the usual source version to the reversed [graph](../../../graph.md); relaxing outgoing original [directed edges](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) would solve the wrong direction of the problem.

For its correctness, inductively assume every settled distance is exact. Any [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path) from an unsettled [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) to the root must first enter the settled set at some [vertex](../../../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $w$, through an [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) $(z,w)$. That [directed edge](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-edge) has already been relaxed, so $d_z\le c_{zw}+v_w$. The newly selected label $d_j$ is no greater than $d_z$. The [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path)'s preceding nonnegative lengths therefore make its total length at least $d_j$. On the other hand, $d_j$ itself is the length of a discovered [directed path](../../../graph-theory.md#directed-path). Hence $d_j=v_j$, and it can never need correction. This is a [label-setting shortest-path algorithm](../../../graph-theory.md#label-setting-shortest-path-algorithm). Straight array selection costs $O(n^2+|A|)$.

Use the two-by-two [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) entries visible in the original PDF. If $q$ is the probability that II uses its first action, I's expected [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) from its two [pure strategies](../../../game-theory.md#pure-strategy) are $4-q$ and $2q$. Their difference $4-3q$ is strictly positive for every $q\in[0,1]$. Thus I's first action [strict dominance](../../../game-theory.md#strict-dominance) its second, and every [Nash equilibrium](../../../game-theory.md#nash-equilibrium) has I use the first action with probability one. Against that action, II receives 8 from its first action and 4 from its second, so its first action is uniquely optimal. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{the only equilibrium pair is }(I_1,II_1),\quad\text{with payoffs }(3,8).}
$$

This excludes nontrivial mixed equilibria as well as the other pure pairs.

For the [maximin bargaining solution](../../../game-theory.md#maximin-bargaining-solution), first compute the two [security level payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#security-level-payoff). If I uses its first action with probability $p$, its [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) against II's two [pure strategies](../../../game-theory.md#pure-strategy) are $2+p$ and $4p$. Both increase with $p$, so its best worst-case [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) is attained at $p=1$ and is $d_1=3$. If II uses its first action with probability $q$, its [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) against I's two actions are $4+4q$ and $6-6q$. The lower of these is maximized at their intersection,

$$
q=\frac15,\qquad d_2=\frac{24}{5}.
$$

These are separate security calculations, not the expected [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) from a pair of security strategies.

Cooperation permits jointly chosen lotteries, giving the [convex hull](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#convex-hull) of the four [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff). The [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) $(3,8)$ dominates $(2,0)$ and $(0,6)$, so the [Pareto frontier](../../../mathematical-optimization.md#pareto-frontier) is the segment from $(3,8)$ to $(4,4)$, with equation $v=20-4u$. Individual rationality relative to $d=(3,24/5)$ restricts this to

$$
3\le u\le\frac{19}{5},\qquad v=20-4u.
$$

On this [negotiation set](../../../game-theory.md#negotiation-set-in-two-person-bargaining), the [Nash product](../../../game-theory.md#nash-product) is

$$
P(u)=(u-3)\left(\frac{76}{5}-4u\right),\qquad
P'(u)=\frac{136}{5}-8u,\quad P''(u)=-8.
$$

Its unique maximizer is interior, giving

$$
\boxed{(u_*,v_*)=\left(\frac{17}{5},\frac{32}{5}\right).}
$$

One implementing binding agreement always uses I's first action and uses II's first action with probability $3/5$, its second with probability $2/5$. This agreement raises both [payoffs](../../../game-theory.md#payoff) above their security levels, but II receives $32/5<8$, less than at the noncooperative equilibrium. Thus **player II prefers the noncooperative game under the specified maximin bargaining rule**. The cooperative feasible set contains the equilibrium [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff); it is the particular arbitration rule, not an inability to cooperate at that [payoff](../../../game-theory.md#payoff), that produces II's lower allocation.

Writing $z_1=x_1-1$, $z_2=x_2-2$, $z_3=x_3-3$ identifies it with a scaled [standard simplex](../../../algebraic-topology.md#standard-simplex) $z_1+z_2+z_3=6$. Its three vertices are $(7,2,3)$, $(1,8,3)$ and $(1,2,9)$.

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

The [core of a cooperative game](../../../game-theory.md#core-game-theory) is the set of efficient allocations for which every [coalition](../../../game-theory.md#coalition-game-theory) receives at least its own value: $x(S)\ge v(S)$ for every $S\subseteq N$. The singleton constraints give individual rationality, and the remaining proper-coalition inequalities are

$$
x_1+x_2\ge3,\qquad x_1+x_3\ge10,\qquad x_2+x_3\ge6.
$$

Efficiency turns the second into $x_2\le2$; together with $x_2\ge2$ this forces $x_2=2$. Put $x_1=t$ and $x_3=10-t$. The remaining constraints give $t\ge1$, $t\le6$, and $t\le7$ from $x_3\ge3$. The first pair constraint adds only $t\ge1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{C(v)=\{(t,2,10-t):1\le t\le6\}.}
$$

Each point in this segment satisfies all singleton, pair, and grand-coalition constraints, so the description is sufficient as well as necessary. Its endpoints are $(1,2,9)$ and $(6,2,4)$.

<h3 id="6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/c)

For a [coalition](../../../game-theory.md#coalition-game-theory) $S$, its [coalition excess](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) at an allocation is $e(S,x)=v(S)-x(S)$, measuring its complaint. Sort all these [excesses of a coalition](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) in decreasing order. The [nucleolus](../../../game-theory.md#nucleolus) is the [imputation](../../../game-theory.md#imputation-in-a-coalitional-game) whose sorted [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) is smallest in [lexicographic order](../../../extremal-set-theory.md#lexicographic-order): minimize the greatest complaint first, then the next among ties, and continue. Including the empty and grand [coalitions](../../../game-theory.md#coalition-game-theory) merely inserts two fixed zero entries and does not change the minimizing allocation.

For every efficient allocation, the [excesses of a coalition](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) of $\{2\}$ and $\{1,3\}$ sum to zero:

$$
e(\{2\},x)+e(\{1,3\},x)
=2-x_2+10-(12-x_2)=0.
$$

The largest proper-coalition [coalition excess](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) is therefore at least zero. Since the [core of a cooperative game](../../../game-theory.md#core-game-theory) from part (b) is nonempty, this first-stage minimum is exactly zero and its minimizers are precisely that [core of a cooperative game](../../../game-theory.md#core-game-theory). Restrict to $x=(t,2,10-t)$, $1\le t\le6$. The six proper-coalition [excesses of a coalition](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) are

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
S&1&2&3&12&13&23\\ \hline
e(S,x)&1-t&0&t-7&1-t&0&t-6
\end{array}
$$

The two zero [excesses of a coalition](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) are fixed. Among the remaining [excesses of a coalition](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition), $t-7<t-6$, so the next largest complaint is $\max\{1-t,t-6\}$. It is minimized by equalizing those two affine expressions:

$$
1-t=t-6,\qquad t=\frac72,\qquad
\max\{1-t,t-6\}=-\frac52.
$$

This minimizer is unique, so later lexicographic stages cannot change it. Thus

$$
\boxed{\nu(v)=\left(\frac72,2,\frac{13}{2}\right).}
$$

Its sorted proper-coalition [coalition excess](../../../game-theory.md#excess-of-a-coalition) [vector](../../../vector-space.md#vector) is $(0,0,-5/2,-5/2,-5/2,-7/2)$. The calculation both identifies the [nucleolus](../../../game-theory.md#nucleolus) and proves uniqueness for this game without replacing the requested computation by the general uniqueness theorem.
