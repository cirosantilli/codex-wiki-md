<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Here a [universal element](../../../../../../universal-element-of-a-set-valued-functor.md) means a pair $(A,u)$, with $u\in X(A)$, such that for every $B$ and every $x\in X(B)$ there is a unique [morphism](../../../../../../morphism.md) $f:B\to A$ satisfying $X(f)(u)=x$. Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\mathcal C(B,A)\longrightarrow X(B),\quad f\longmapsto X(f)(u)
\text{ is bijective for every }B.}
$$

By the [Yoneda lemma](../../../../../../yoneda-lemma.md) these component maps constitute a [natural transformation](../../../../../../natural-transformation.md) $h_A\to X$. If $u$ is universal, every component is bijective, so this is a [natural isomorphism](../../../../../../natural-isomorphism.md) and $X$ is a [representable presheaf](../../../../../../representable-functor.md). Conversely a [natural isomorphism](../../../../../../natural-isomorphism.md) $h_A\to X$ takes $1_A$ to an element $u$ with exactly this property. In the [category of elements](../../../../../../category-of-elements.md) of the contravariant [functor](../../../../../../functor.md) $X$, $(A,u)$ is a [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md): every $(B,x)$ has a unique arrow to it.
