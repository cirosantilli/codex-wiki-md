<h1 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $F\dashv U$, let $1$ denote a singleton [set](../../../../../../set-split.md). The [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) gives the natural [bijections](../../../../../../bijection.md)

$$
U(C)\cong\mathbf{Set}(1,U(C))\cong\mathcal C(F1,C).
$$

Thus **$U$ is represented by $F1$**, proving $A\Rightarrow R$.

If $U\cong\mathcal C(R,-)$ is a covariant [representable functor](../../../../../../representable-functor.md), a [morphism](../../../../../../morphism.md) $R\to\lim_jC_j$ is exactly a compatible family of [morphisms](../../../../../../morphism.md) $R\to C_j$. Consequently the comparison map

$$
\mathcal C(R,\lim_jC_j)\longrightarrow\lim_j\mathcal C(R,C_j)
$$

is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) for every existing small [categorical limit](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md). This proves $R\Rightarrow L$ by the [universal property](../../../../../../universal-property.md) of a limit. In particular,

$$
\boxed{A\Rightarrow R\Rightarrow L.}
$$
