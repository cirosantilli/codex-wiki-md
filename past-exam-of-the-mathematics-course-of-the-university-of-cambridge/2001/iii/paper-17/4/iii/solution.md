<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Given $A,B$, their [product in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md) is the [pullback](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md) of the unique arrows $A\to1\leftarrow B$, where $1$ is a [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md). Having constructed this [product in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md), define the [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md) of $f,g:A\rightrightarrows B$ as the [pullback](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md)

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
E&\longrightarrow&B\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\scriptstyle\delta_B\\
A&\xrightarrow{(f,g)}&B\times B,
\end{array}
$$

where $\delta_B$ is the [categorical diagonal](../../../../../../categorical-diagonal.md). A map $x:X\to A$ factors through this [pullback](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md) exactly when $fx=gx$: the accompanying arrow to $B$ must equal this common composite. The factorization is unique, proving the [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md) property.

Thus the hypotheses give a [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md), binary [products in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md) and all [equalizers](../../../../../../equaliser.md). Part (i) now gives **all finite limits**.
