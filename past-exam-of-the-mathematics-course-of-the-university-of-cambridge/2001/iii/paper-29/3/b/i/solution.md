<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The one-day slaughter-inspection yield is

$$
\widehat p_{\mathrm{inspection}}=\frac{15}{30000}=0.0005,
$$

or 500 detections per million inspected adult cattle. The routine field detections correspond, using a stable herd size of five million, to

$$
\widehat r_{\mathrm{field}}=\frac{14600}{5\times10^6\times365}=8.0\times10^{-6}
$$

per adult-cattle day, or 8 detections per million adult-cattle days. Therefore the numerical one-day comparison is

$$
\boxed{\frac{0.0005}{8.0\times10^{-6}}=62.5.}
$$

The annual field rate is $14600/(5\times10^6)=0.00292$ per cattle-year; comparing the inspection [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md) directly with this annual proportion would mix observation periods. Applying the random inspection-sample proportion to all 150000 slaughtered adults would predict about 75 detected cases in that slaughter cohort. These arithmetic comparisons describe detection yields, not a 62.5-fold biological disease-risk difference: a cross-sectional inspection estimates a [prevalence](../../../../../../../prevalence.md) yield, whereas daily field reporting is an ascertainment rate over animal-time.
