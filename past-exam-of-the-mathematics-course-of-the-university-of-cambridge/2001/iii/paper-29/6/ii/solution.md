↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The individuals' [haplotypes](../../../../../../haplotype.md) are not identifiable from the supplied source: the pedigree drawings are missing.** The original PDF has a blank figure area, and the accompanying TeX contains no family [genotypes](../../../../../../genotype.md). Even the two-locus [genotypes](../../../../../../genotype.md) of individuals 3 and 4 are not stated elsewhere, so numerical [allele](../../../../../../allele.md) pairs cannot be supplied honestly.

The method, once the drawings are available, is to use parental or grandparental [genotypes](../../../../../../genotype.md) to assign the [allele](../../../../../../allele.md) inherited at each locus on the same chromosome. A double heterozygote with [alleles](../../../../../../allele.md) $A_1,A_2$ and $B_1,B_2$ has the two candidate [haplotype](../../../../../../haplotype.md) pairs $A_1B_1/A_2B_2$ and $A_1B_2/A_2B_1$; ancestor and offspring transmissions determine or restrict its phase. These symbols describe the general phasing problem, not invented [genotypes](../../../../../../genotype.md) for the missing individuals. If pedigree transmissions leave more than one phase possible, retain all compatible phases rather than selecting one without evidence.
