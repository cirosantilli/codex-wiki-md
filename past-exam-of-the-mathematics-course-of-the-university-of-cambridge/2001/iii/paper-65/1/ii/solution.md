<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [BPS state](../../../../../../bps-state.md) **saturates a supersymmetry energy bound and is annihilated by some supercharges**. For a massive state with one relevant [central charge in supersymmetry](../../../../../../central-charge-in-supersymmetry.md), normalize the [supersymmetry algebra](../../../../../../super-poincare-algebra.md) by $\{Q^A_\alpha,Q^B_\beta\}=2\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{AB}Z$. In its rest frame, appropriate linear combinations of [supercharges](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md) have anticommutators $2(M+|Z|)$ and $2(M-|Z|)$. Positivity therefore gives the [BPS bound in supersymmetry](../../../../../../bps-bound-in-supersymmetry.md) $M\geq|Z|$. At equality, the latter combinations have zero norm and annihilate the state, producing a shortened [supermultiplet](../../../../../../supermultiplet.md) and preserved [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md). With the alternative convention omitting the factor $2$ in the central-charge anticommutator, the bound reads $M\geq|Z|/2$. The mass is tied to the charge, but this does not imply unconditional stability against decay as parameters vary.
