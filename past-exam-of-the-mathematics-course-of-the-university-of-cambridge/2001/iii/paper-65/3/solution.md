<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Take canonical kinetic terms, [gauge coupling](../../../../../gauge-coupling.md) $g>0$, and define the [Fayet–Iliopoulos term](../../../../../fayet-iliopoulos-term.md) by $+\xi D$. If $\phi$ is the scalar component of the charge-$q$ [chiral superfield](../../../../../chiral-superfield.md), the relevant [auxiliary field](../../../../../auxiliary-field.md) Lagrangian is

$$
\mathcal L_D=\frac12D^2+D\bigl(gq|\phi|^2+\xi\bigr),\qquad D=-\bigl(gq|\phi|^2+\xi\bigr).
$$

The algebraic equation eliminates $D$ and gives the [D-term scalar potential](../../../../../d-term-scalar-potential.md)

$$
\boxed{V_D=\frac12\bigl(\xi+gq|\phi|^2\bigr)^2.}
$$

Since the [superpotential](../../../../../superpotential.md) vanishes, $F=0$. In a homogeneous vacuum, the [supersymmetry transformation](../../../../../supersymmetry-transformation.md) of the [gaugino](../../../../../gaugino.md) includes

$$
\delta\lambda_\alpha=i\epsilon_\alpha D+(\sigma^{\mu\nu}\epsilon)_\alpha F_{\mu\nu}=i\epsilon_\alpha D_0.
$$

Thus $D_0\ne0$ gives an inhomogeneous shift: no nonzero constant [supersymmetry](../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) parameter leaves this vacuum invariant. **The gaugino $\lambda$ is the goldstino**, because it is the field shifting under the broken [supercharge](../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md); there is no $F$ contribution here.

For $q\ne0$, minimizing over $x=|\phi|^2\geq0$ separates three cases. If $q\xi<0$, the allowed value $x=-\xi/(gq)$ gives $D_0=V_0=0$: [supersymmetry](../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) is unbroken while the internal [gauge symmetry](../../../../../gauge-invariance.md) is Higgsed. If $q\xi>0$, the unconstrained zero of $D$ would have negative $x$, so the minimum is $\phi=0$ with $D_0=-\xi$ and $V_0=\xi^2/2$. If $\xi=0$, the minimum $\phi=0$ is [supersymmetric](../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md). Consequently, with this explicitly stated sign convention,

$$
\boxed{q\xi>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{supersymmetry breaking}\qquad(q\ne0).}
$$

Reversing the sign used to define the [Fayet–Iliopoulos term](../../../../../fayet-iliopoulos-term.md) reverses the printed sign criterion, not the physics. If $q=0$, the scalar is decoupled and flat: any $\xi\ne0$ still breaks [supersymmetry](../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) through $D_0=-\xi$, but the charged-field criterion does not apply.

On the broken branch, expand about $\phi=0$:

$$
V=\frac12\xi^2+gq\xi|\phi|^2+\frac12g^2q^2|\phi|^4.
$$

Writing $\phi=(\phi_1+i\phi_2)/\sqrt2$ gives both real scalar modes the squared mass $gq\xi$. There is no [superpotential](../../../../../superpotential.md) [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) mass and no [vacuum expectation value](../../../../../vacuum-expectation-value.md) to induce [gaugino](../../../../../gaugino.md)–matter-fermion mixing. The Abelian [gauge symmetry](../../../../../gauge-invariance.md) is unbroken, so its vector is also massless. The complete [mass spectrum of single charged-field D-term breaking](../../../../../mass-spectrum-of-single-charged-field-d-term-breaking.md) is

$$
\boxed{m_{\phi_1}^2=m_{\phi_2}^2=gq\xi,\qquad m_\psi=m_\lambda=m_A=0.}
$$

The original [chiral multiplet](../../../../../chiral-multiplet.md) therefore has the squared-mass splitting

$$
\boxed{\Delta m_{\rm chiral}^2=m_\phi^2-m_\psi^2=gq\xi=-gqD_0.}
$$

The original [vector multiplet](../../../../../supersymmetric-vector-multiplet.md) has zero mass splitting, although the nonzero $D_0$ means it is not an unbroken-[supersymmetry](../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) representation. As a check, the [tree-level supertrace mass sum rule](../../../../../tree-level-supertrace-mass-sum-rule.md) gives $\operatorname{Str}M^2=2gq\xi$ when the two real scalar degrees of freedom are counted. On the $q=0$ broken branch all the propagating fields are massless.

There is a necessary scope qualification: one nonzero-charge [Weyl fermion](../../../../../weyl-spinor.md) has an Abelian cubic [gauge anomaly](../../../../../gauge-anomaly.md) proportional to $q^3$ and a mixed gravitational [gauge anomaly](../../../../../gauge-anomaly.md) proportional to $q$. These are the classical vacuum and spectrum calculations requested by the model; a consistent quantum [gauge theory](../../../../../gauge-theory.md) needs an anomaly-cancelling completion, whose additional matter must also be included in its vacuum analysis.
