<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [capital asset pricing model](../../../../../../capital-asset-pricing-model.md) gives

$$
\mathbb Er_i=r_f+\beta_i(\mathbb Er_M-r_f),\qquad
\beta_i=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(r_i,r_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(r_M)}.
$$

Thus it has the same affine relation between [expected returns](../../../../../../expected-return.md) and a single systematic exposure. When the factor is the [market portfolio](../../../../../../market-portfolio.md)'s excess return, the loadings are the [beta of an asset](../../../../../../beta-of-an-asset.md), $\lambda_0=r_f$, and $\lambda_1=\mathbb Er_M-r_f$. More generally, for a nondegenerate exact one-factor model and a [market portfolio](../../../../../../market-portfolio.md) with loading $b_M\ne0$, $\beta_i=b_i/b_M$, and the pricing relation can be rewritten using that exposure.

The interpretations and assumptions differ. The [CAPM](../../../../../../capital-asset-pricing-model.md) is an equilibrium relation obtained with a [mean-variance optimization](../../../../../../modern-portfolio-theory.md) framework, common beliefs, and suitable borrowing/lending and market-clearing assumptions. [Arbitrage pricing theory](../../../../../../arbitrage-pricing-theory.md) uses absence of [arbitrage](../../../../../../arbitrage.md), factor structure, and in realistic models [diversification](../../../../../../portfolio-diversification.md); it does not identify a generic factor with the [market portfolio](../../../../../../market-portfolio.md). Moreover the [CAPM](../../../../../../capital-asset-pricing-model.md) permits asset-specific risk, whereas the exact model in part (a) has no residual risk. **The two formulas coincide for a suitable market factor, but no-arbitrage factor pricing by itself does not establish the CAPM's equilibrium assumptions.**
