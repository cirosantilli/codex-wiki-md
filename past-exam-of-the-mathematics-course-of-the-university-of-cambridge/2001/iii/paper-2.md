<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

Put $x=I+E_{12}$, $y=I+E_{23}$ and $z=I+E_{13}$. The multiplication of matrix-coordinate triples is $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b'+ac',c+c')$. In particular $z$ is central, $xy=zyx$, and every element has the unique normal form $y^cz^bx^a$. Consequently

$$
kG\cong B[x,x^{-1};\sigma],\qquad B=k[y,y^{-1},z,z^{-1}],\qquad\sigma(y)=zy,\quad\sigma(z)=z.
$$

The distinct normal-form [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) are the [group](../../../group.md) [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis), so this is an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism), not merely a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) from a presented algebra. This is the [integral Heisenberg group algebra as a skew Laurent ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-heisenberg-group-algebra-as-a-skew-laurent-ring).

Here is the needed [skew Hilbert basis theorem](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#skew-hilbert-basis-theorem), including its proof. If $A$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring) and $\sigma$ an [automorphism](../../../algebra.md#automorphism), let $J$ be a [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) of $A[t;\sigma]$, where $ta=\sigma(a)t$. Define

$$
I_n=\{\sigma^{-n}(a_n):\ \sum_{i=0}^na_it^i\in J\}.
$$

These are [left ideals](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) of $A$: adding lifts adds the coefficient, and left multiplication by $\sigma^n(r)$ multiplies its normalized value by $r$. Multiplication of a lift by $t$ proves $I_n\subseteq I_{n+1}$. The chain stabilizes at some $N$. Choose finite generators $u_{nj}$ for each $I_n$, $0\le n\le N$, and lifts $f_{nj}\in J$ with $t^n$ coefficient $\sigma^n(u_{nj})$.

For $f\in J$ of degree $m\ge N$, write its normalized [leading coefficient](../../../polynomial.md#leading-coefficient-of-a-polynomial) as $\sigma^{-m}(\operatorname{lc}f)=\sum_j r_ju_{Nj}$. Then

$$
f-\sum_j\sigma^m(r_j)t^{m-N}f_{Nj}
$$

has smaller degree: the displayed summands have leading coefficients $\sigma^m(r_ju_{Nj})$. For $m<N$ use the generators for $I_m$ without the shift. Induction on degree expresses every element of $J$ in terms of the finitely many chosen lifts. Thus $A[t;\sigma]$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring). Passing to the [opposite ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#opposite-ring) replaces $\sigma$ by $\sigma^{-1}$ and proves the corresponding right-sided result.

This proof includes the ordinary [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) when $\sigma$ is the identity. Starting with the [field](../../../algebra.md#field) $k$, it makes $k[y,z]$ [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring); [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) at powers of $y,z$ makes $B$ [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) as well. Finally, for a [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) $L$ in $B[x,x^{-1};\sigma]$, its intersection $J$ with $B[x;\sigma]$ has finitely many generators. For each $f\in L$, some $x^qf$ lies in $J$, so $f=x^{-q}(x^qf)$ is generated by those same elements in the [skew Laurent polynomial ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#skew-laurent-polynomial-ring). Right multiplication clears powers for a [right ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#right-ideal). Therefore **$kG$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring) and a [right Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#right-noetherian-ring).**

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

The commutative [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#laurent-polynomial-ring) $B$ is an [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) and $\sigma$ is invertible. Write nonzero elements as

$$
r=\sum_{i=m_0}^{m}a_ix^i,\qquad s=\sum_{j=n_0}^{n}b_jx^j,\qquad a_m,b_n\ne0.
$$

The largest exponent in their product is $m+n$, with coefficient

$$
\boxed{a_m\sigma^m(b_n)\ne0}.
$$

There is only one pair of exponents contributing at this degree, and the coefficient cannot vanish in the [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $B$. Hence $rs\ne0$. Equivalently, **$rs=0$ forces $r=0$ or $s=0$.** This is [skew Laurent extensions of domains are domains](../../../commutative-algebra.md#skew-laurent-extensions-of-domains-are-domains), and the argument works over the original [field](../../../algebra.md#field) in any characteristic.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

For a [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain) $R$, the right [Ore condition](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-condition) on its nonzero elements requires that for every $a\in R$ and nonzero $s$, there exist $t\ne0$ and $b\in R$ with $at=sb$. The [Ore theorem](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-theorem) says that this is precisely the condition for a [division ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring) of right fractions in which $R$ embeds and every element is $as^{-1}$. The left-handed condition similarly gives left fractions.

For completeness, a [right Noetherian domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#right-noetherian-domain) satisfies the condition. If $aR\cap bR=0$ with $a,b\ne0$, the [right ideals](../../../associative-algebra.md#right-ideal)

$$
bR\subset bR+abR\subset bR+abR+a^2bR\subset\cdots
$$

form a strict chain of direct sums. Indeed, in a relation $\sum_{i=0}^na^ibr_i=0$, the first term lies in $bR$ and the rest in $aR$. Their zero intersection makes $br_0=0$, hence $r_0=0$; cancel $a$ and repeat. Every new summand is nonzero in a [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain), contradicting the [ascending chain condition](../../../algebra.md#ascending-chain-condition). Thus $au=bv\ne0$ exists, with $u,v\ne0$, giving right common multiples. The opposite-ring argument gives left common multiples when $R$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring). This proves [right Noetherian domains satisfy the Ore condition](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#right-noetherian-domains-satisfy-the-ore-condition).

A sketch of the fraction construction also explains the theorem. Pairs $(a,s)$ represent $as^{-1}$. They represent the same fraction when a common right denominator $su=tv\ne0$ also satisfies $au=bv$. Addition uses $(au+bv)(su)^{-1}$. For multiplication choose $sc=bd$ with $d\ne0$; then

$$
(as^{-1})(bt^{-1})=ac(td)^{-1}.
$$

Further common denominators prove well-definedness and the [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) identities. The original map is injective because all nonzero denominators are regular; a zero fraction numerator would satisfy $au=0$ for $u\ne0$. Every nonzero fraction has inverse $sa^{-1}$, so the [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) is a [division ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring). This construction has the [universal property](../../../category-theory.md#universal-property) of inverting all nonzero elements.

Apply this to the two-sided [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain) $kG$. Its left Ore property expresses any right fraction as a left one: choose $u\ne0$ with $ua=bs$, yielding $as^{-1}=u^{-1}b$. Hence **one [division ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring) is simultaneously the left and right classical [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) of quotients of $kG$**; the two constructions agree by their [universal property](../../../category-theory.md#universal-property).

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 2](paper-2.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

Work over $\mathbb C$, or another [field](../../../algebra.md#field) of [characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). The [Weyl algebra](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#weyl-algebra) $A_n$ is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_n,\partial_1,\ldots,\partial_n$ with commuting $x$'s, commuting $\partial$'s and $[\partial_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$. Its [ordered monomial basis of a Weyl algebra](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ordered-monomial-basis-of-a-weyl-algebra) consists of the [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) $x^\alpha\partial^\beta$. One way to verify independence is to apply a finite operator relation to the formal exponential $e^{\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf x}$: after canceling that exponential the relation becomes a [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) identity $\sum c_{\alpha\beta}\mathbf x^\alpha\mathbf t^\beta=0$, so every coefficient vanishes. The relations already reorder every word into that [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis).

Give both generators degree one. The [Bernstein filtration](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#bernstein-filtration) is

$$
F_mA_n=\operatorname{span}\{x^\alpha\partial^\beta:|\alpha|+|\beta|\le m\},\qquad\operatorname{gr}A_n\cong\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n].
$$

Thus $\dim F_mA_n=\binom{m+2n}{2n}$. [Commutator](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) terms have lower degree, accounting for the commutative symbol algebra. Since that algebra is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), leading-symbol generation followed by finite downward degree cancellation proves that $A_n$ is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) on both sides.

A left [finitely generated module](../../../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module) $M$ has a [good filtration](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module), for example $M_m=\sum_jF_mA_nm_j$ for a finite generating set of the [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). More generally one allows fixed degree shifts of the generators. The [associated graded module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-module) is finite over the symbol algebra. Define

$$
\operatorname{Ch}(M)=\operatorname{Supp}(\operatorname{gr}M)=V(\operatorname{Ann}\operatorname{gr}M)\subseteq\mathbb A^{2n}\cong T^*\mathbb A^n.
$$

This is the [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module) for the Bernstein algebra filtration. It records where the [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics)'s leading symbols remain nonzero and makes noncommutative [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) growth accessible to [commutative algebra](../../../commutative-algebra.md).

For a fixed algebra filtration it is independent of the [good filtration](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module). Any two such filtrations differ by bounded shifts $M_i\subseteq\widetilde M_{i+c}$ and conversely, since each finite set of generators occurs in bounded degree in the other filtration. If a degree-$d$ operator has symbol annihilating $\operatorname{gr}M$, its $r$th power maps $M_i$ into $M_{i+rd-r}$. For $r>2c$, bounded-shift comparison forces the power of that symbol to annihilate $\operatorname{gr}\widetilde M$. Reversing the argument gives equal [radicals of ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#radical-of-an-ideal) of [annihilators](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory). This is [independence of characteristic support from a good module filtration](../../../associative-algebra.md#independence-of-characteristic-support-from-a-good-module-filtration). Changing the algebra filtration itself, for example to the [order filtration of a Weyl algebra](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#order-filtration-of-a-weyl-algebra) with the $x_i$ in degree zero, need not preserve the same variety; the convention matters.

The [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../commutative-algebra.md#hilbert-serre-theorem) gives an eventual cumulative [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md)

$$
\dim M_m=\frac{e(M)}{d!}m^d+O(m^{d-1}),\qquad d=\dim\operatorname{Ch}(M).
$$

Here $d$ is also the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../associative-algebra.md#gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module), and $e(M)>0$ is an integer [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module) for nonzero $M$. Finite generation bounds $d\le2n$.

Here is a proof sketch of the essential lower bound, rather than an appeal to its name. For a nonzero $f\in F_mA_n$, choose a nonzero [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) of greatest total degree in its ordered expansion, say $x^\alpha\partial^\beta$. The operations $\operatorname{ad}\partial_i$ and $\operatorname{ad}(-x_i)$ differentiate this expansion with respect to $x_i$ and $\partial_i$. Apply them $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ times. Lower-degree terms die, and every other [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) of the same degree also dies unless it has exactly those exponents. The result is a nonzero scalar multiple of the chosen coefficient. [Characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero) is essential for the factorials. Expanding the repeated [commutators](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) therefore gives

$$
1=\sum_j u_jfv_j,\qquad u_j,v_j\in F_mA_n.
$$

Choose $0\ne v\in M_0$. If $f$ acted as zero on all $M_m$, every $fv_jv$ would vanish, contrary to $v=\sum_ju_jfv_jv$. Hence the linear action map

$$
F_mA_n\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(M_m,M_{2m})
$$

is injective. It follows that $\dim F_mA_n\le(\dim M_m)(\dim M_{2m})$. Comparing [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) degrees gives $2n\le2d$. Thus

$$
\boxed{n\le d(M)\le2n}\qquad(M\ne0).
$$

This proves [Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules](../../../associative-algebra.md#bernstein-inequality-for-weyl-algebra-modules) through the [faithful finite-step action of a Weyl algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#faithful-finite-step-action-of-a-weyl-algebra).

The bound is sharp. The [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) representation $\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\cong A_n/\sum_iA_n\partial_i$ has the [zero section](../../../fiber-bundle.md#zero-section-of-a-vector-bundle) as its [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module), namely $\xi=0$ and dimension $n$. It is a [simple module](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module): differentiate a nonzero [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) sufficiently to obtain a nonzero constant, then multiply by the $x_i$ to generate the whole [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). The [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $A_n/\sum_iA_nx_i$ similarly has [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module) $x=0$, a fiber of the [cotangent bundle](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#cotangent-bundle). A nonzero [free module](../../../module-theory.md#free-module) has the whole [cotangent bundle](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#cotangent-bundle) and dimension $2n$. No nonzero finite-dimensional [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) exists for $n\ge1$, also seen by taking the [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of $[\partial_1,x_1]=I$.

[Modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) attaining dimension $n$ are [holonomic Weyl algebra modules](../../../associative-algebra.md#holonomic-weyl-algebra-module) (with the zero [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) included conventionally). Every nonzero subquotient remains of dimension $n$ by the lower bound and monotonicity of growth. Compatible filtrations in a [short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence) make the leading [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module) additive. Since each nonzero factor has positive integer [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module), the number of factors in any strict [submodule](../../../module-theory.md#submodule) chain is bounded by $e(M)$. Together with Noetherianity, this proves finite [composition length](../../../finite-group-theory.md#composition-length). It illustrates why [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module) and [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module) provide useful structure beyond a list of presentations. In positive characteristic there are finite-dimensional Weyl [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), so the characteristic-zero inequality and this discussion must not be asserted without its [field](../../../algebra.md#field) hypothesis.

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

Whenever the graded pieces $N_i$ are finitely generated over $S_0$, define the [Poincare series of a graded module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#poincare-series-of-a-graded-module) by

$$
P_{N,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{i\ge0}\lambda(N_i)t^i.
$$

Additivity means $\lambda(B)=\lambda(A)+\lambda(C)$ for every [short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence) $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ in the indicated category. Dimension over a [field](../../../algebra.md#field) is a basic example; length is another when the [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) have finite length.

The [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../commutative-algebra.md#hilbert-serre-theorem) needs finite-generation hypotheses: take $S_0$ commutative [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) and $S$ generated over it by homogeneous $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ of positive degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_r$. For finitely generated graded $N$, every $N_i$ is then finite over $S_0$, and

$$
\boxed{P_{N,\lambda}(t)=\frac{Q(t)}{\prod_{j=1}^r(1-t^{d_j})},\qquad Q(t)\in\mathbb Z[t]}.
$$

If negative grading indices are allowed for the [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), the numerator is a [Laurent polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#laurent-polynomial) instead. In standard degree one, the coefficients are eventually [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) functions of the index.

To prove this, regard $N$ as finite over the surjective [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) presentation $S_0[X_1,\ldots,X_r]\to S$. Induct on $r$. At $r=0$ only finitely many homogeneous generator degrees occur, so the series is a [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md). For the last variable $x$ of degree $d$, put $K=(0:_Nx)$ and $Q=N/xN$. The [polynomial ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring) is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) by the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem), hence $K$ is finite; both $K$ and $Q$ are finite [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) over the [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) with that variable removed. The degree-$i$ exact sequence is

$$
0\longrightarrow K_{i-d}\longrightarrow N_{i-d}\xrightarrow{x}N_i\longrightarrow Q_i\longrightarrow0.
$$

Additivity, followed by summing over $i$, gives

$$
(1-t^d)P_{N,\lambda}=P_{Q,\lambda}-t^dP_{K,\lambda}.
$$

The induction hypothesis supplies the remaining denominator factors. This proves [Hilbert-Serre theorem for an additive coefficient function](../../../commutative-algebra.md#hilbert-serre-theorem-for-an-additive-coefficient-function). If all $d_i=1$, expanding $(1-t)^{-r}$ proves eventual [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) coefficients directly.

An arbitrary positively [graded ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#graded-ring) in the introductory wording is not enough by itself. For example $S=k[X_1,X_2,\ldots]$ with every variable of degree one and $N=S$ is a cyclic [graded module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#graded-module), but $N_1$ is not finite-dimensional over $k$, so the dimension-valued series is not even defined. The theorem above states the standard hypotheses that make the requested definition and rationality valid.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

Choose a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $x_1,x_2,x_3$ of the [Lie algebra](../../../lie-algebra.md) and filter its [universal enveloping algebra](../../../lie-algebra.md#universal-enveloping-algebra) by word length: $F_0R=\mathbb C$ and $F_dR$ is spanned by products of at most $d$ [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) elements. The relations $x_jx_i-x_ix_j=[x_j,x_i]$ replace a degree-two [commutator](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) by degree one, so the degree-one symbols commute.

The [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../../../lie-algebra.md#poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem) says the ordered [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) $x_1^{a_1}x_2^{a_2}x_3^{a_3}$ are a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis). Its relevant proof can be seen by reordering an inverted neighboring pair using the displayed relation. Reordering terminates because same-length terms have fewer inversions and bracket terms have smaller word length. Disjoint swaps commute; the only overlapping three-letter reorderings differ by $[x_i,[x_j,x_k]]+[x_j,[x_k,x_i]]+[x_k,[x_i,x_j]]=0$. The [Jacobi identity](../../../lie-algebra.md#jacobi-identity) therefore makes the reduced ordered expression independent of the reordering path. Ordered [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) have no remaining relations and form the [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis). Their degree-$d$ symbols give

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{gr}R\cong\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak g)\cong\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3]},\qquad\dim F_dR=\binom{d+3}{3}.
$$

For generators $m_1,\ldots,m_q$ of the [finitely generated module](../../../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module) $M$, put $M_d=\sum_jF_dRm_j$. This is a [good filtration](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module), and $\operatorname{gr}M$ is finite over the three-variable [polynomial ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring). Define the [dimension of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#dimension-of-a-filtered-module) as the [Krull dimension](../../../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension) of its [associated graded module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-module), equivalently the degree of the eventual cumulative [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) $\dim_{\mathbb C}M_d$, or the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../associative-algebra.md#gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module). Two [good filtrations](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module) have bounded shifts, so the growth degree is independent of the generators. Since

$$
\dim M_d\le q\binom{d+3}{3},
$$

its [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) degree is at most three. Therefore **every nonzero finitely generated $R$-module has dimension at most $3$**. The zero [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) may be assigned the usual separate empty-support convention. This bound also follows immediately from its [support of a module](../../../module-theory.md#support-of-a-module) inside affine three-space.

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

Let $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_s)$ and form the [Rees algebra](../../../module-theory.md#rees-algebra) $\mathcal R=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}I^jt^j$. It is generated over $R$ by $a_1t,\ldots,a_st$, so the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) makes it [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring). The [Rees module](../../../module-theory.md#rees-module) $\mathcal M=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}I^jMt^j$ is finite over it, generated by finitely many generators of the [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $M$ in degree zero. Hence its graded [submodule](../../../module-theory.md#submodule)

$$
\mathcal N=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}(N\cap I^jM)t^j
$$

has finitely many homogeneous generators, all in degrees at most some $k$.

For $i\ge k$, each degree-$d$ generator contributes a multiple from $I^{i-d}(N\cap I^dM)$. This is contained in $I^{i-k}(N\cap I^kM)$, because multiplication by $I^{k-d}$ puts its coefficient both in $N$ and in $I^kM$. Thus $N\cap I^iM\subseteq I^{i-k}(N\cap I^kM)$. The reverse inclusion follows directly by multiplication: it stays in $N$ and in $I^iM$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{N\cap I^iM=I^{i-k}(N\cap I^kM)\qquad(i\ge k)}.
$$

This is the [Artin-Rees lemma](../../../module-theory.md#artin-rees-lemma), proved through finite graded generation rather than an assumption that the intersection filtration initially has that form.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

Write the negative filtration as $F_0R=R\supseteq F_{-1}R\supseteq F_{-2}R\supseteq\cdots$, with $F_iRF_jR\subseteq F_{i+j}R$. Completeness is used in the separated sense $R\cong\varprojlim R/F_{-n}R$; in particular the intersection of all filtration terms is zero.

For any [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) $J$, its induced associated graded ideal is a [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) of $\operatorname{gr}R$. Choose finite homogeneous generators and lift them to $j_1,\ldots,j_r\in J$, with orders $n_i\ge0$, meaning $j_i\in F_{-n_i}R\setminus F_{-n_i-1}R$. Let $j\in J$. At each order $s$, the leading symbol of the current residual is a sum of the generator symbols with homogeneous coefficients. Lift these coefficients to $c_{i,s}\in F_{-(s-n_i)}R$ (zero when $n_i>s$) and subtract $\sum_i c_{i,s}j_i$. The new residual lies in $F_{-(s+1)}R$.

Iterating expresses $j$ as partial sums of $\sum_i c_i j_i$, with residuals tending to zero. For fixed $i$ the successive coefficient increments have orders tending to infinity. Separated completeness therefore supplies $c_i=\sum_s c_{i,s}\in R$. Multiplication is continuous by the multiplicative filtration, so

$$
\boxed{j=\sum_{i=1}^r c_i j_i}.
$$

The sum on the right is finite, despite the convergent process used to obtain its coefficients. It belongs to the actual [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) generated by the lifts; no assumption that $J$ is closed was made. Every [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) is consequently finitely generated, and **$R$ is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring)**. This is the left-handed [complete negative filtered-graded transfer of Noetherianity](../../../module-theory.md#complete-negative-filtered-graded-transfer-of-noetherianity); the separated-completeness hypothesis is essential to the limiting argument.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

A [uniform pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#uniform-pro-p-group) is a finitely generated [powerful pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#powerful-pro-p-group) whose [lower p-series](../../../finite-group-theory.md#lower-p-series) $P_1=G$, $P_{i+1}=\overline{P_i^p[P_i,G]}$ has constant successive indices. For odd $p$, the [powerful pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#powerful-pro-p-group) condition means $[G,G]\subseteq G^p$, with closed generated powers understood. For $p=2$ it means $[G,G]\subseteq G^4$. For finitely generated [powerful pro-p groups](../../../topological-group.md#powerful-pro-p-group), uniformity is equivalent to being a [torsion-free group](../../../group.md#torsion-free-group).

It is convenient to use general [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) size $d$, then put $d=p$. Let $U_m=1+p^mM_d(\mathbb Z_p)$. The required [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) is $U_1$, the [inverse limit](../../../module-theory.md#inverse-limit) of its finite p-group quotients. For odd $p$, the convergent [matrix logarithm](../../../vector-space.md#matrix-logarithm) and [matrix exponential](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#matrix-exponential) series

$$
\log(1+A)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{r+1}A^r}{r},\qquad\exp(B)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{B^r}{r!}
$$

are inverse maps between $U_m$ and $p^mM_d(\mathbb Z_p)$. The valuation bounds for $A,B\in pM_d$ ensure convergence and integrality. They give $\log(u^p)=p\log u$ and hence a bijective power map $U_m\to U_{m+1}$: the unique root of $v\in U_{m+1}$ is $\exp(p^{-1}\log v)$.

Direct [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) multiplication gives $[U_i,U_j]\subseteq U_{i+j}$, so $[U_1,U_1]\subseteq U_2=U_1^p$ and it is a [powerful pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#powerful-pro-p-group). Moreover

$$
U_m/U_{m+1}\cong(M_d(\mathbb F_p),+),\qquad\boxed{[U_m:U_{m+1}]=p^{d^2}}.
$$

The $d^2$ elements $1+pE_{ij}$ topologically generate: their $p^{m-1}$th powers give all [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) directions in $U_m/U_{m+1}$, since $(1+pE_{ij})^{p^{m-1}}\equiv1+p^mE_{ij}\pmod{p^{m+1}}$. Successive correction of each [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) entry, followed by completeness, approximates every element by words in these generators.

Its [lower p-series](../../../finite-group-theory.md#lower-p-series) is exactly $U_m$ and the indices are constant; thus the [group](../../../group.md) is a [uniform pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#uniform-pro-p-group). Alternatively, the logarithm excludes nontrivial p-power torsion. Setting $d=p$ gives **a [uniform pro-p group](../../../topological-group.md#uniform-pro-p-group) [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) of dimension and generator number $p^2$**, with indices $p^{p^2}$. This is the [odd-prime principal p-adic congruence subgroup is uniform](../../../topological-group.md#odd-prime-principal-p-adic-congruence-subgroup-is-uniform) result. The odd-prime condition matters: $-I$ is nontrivial order-two torsion in $1+2M_d(\mathbb Z_2)$.

<h3 id="4/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#4/iv)

Use $[g]$ for the [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) element of the [group algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#group-algebra) $A=\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p]$, so $[g][h]=[g+h]$ and $[0]=1$. The [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) of the augmentation map is the [augmentation ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#augmentation-ideal) $I$. This algebra must not initially be identified with a [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#laurent-polynomial-ring): the additive [group](../../../group.md) $\mathbb Z_p$ is not abstractly generated by the integer $1$.

For $r\ge1$, map $A$ onto $\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p/p^r\mathbb Z_p]$ and call its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) $J_r$. It is generated by $[g]-1$ for $g\in p^r\mathbb Z_p$: differences of [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) elements in one coset have the form $[h]([g]-1)$. Since each such $g=p^rh$,

$$
[g]-1=([h]-1)^{p^r}\in I^{p^r}
$$

by the characteristic-$p$ binomial identity. Thus $J_r\subseteq I^{p^r}$.

The [quotient group](../../../group-theory.md#quotient-group) is a [cyclic group](../../../group.md#cyclic-group) of order $p^r$, generated by the image of $1$. Its [group algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#group-algebra) is

$$
\mathbb F_p[u]/(u^{p^r}-1)=\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^{p^r}),\qquad T=u-1.
$$

The image of $I$ is $(T)$ and its $p^r$th power vanishes, proving the reverse inclusion. Hence

$$
\boxed{J_r=I^{p^r},\qquad A/I^{p^r}\cong\mathbb F_p[T]/(T^{p^r})}.
$$

This is [cyclic finite quotients of the p-adic augmentation filtration](../../../commutative-algebra.md#cyclic-finite-quotients-of-the-p-adic-augmentation-filtration).

For any $n\ge0$, choose $p^r>n+1$. The finite quotient then identifies $I^n/I^{n+1}$ with $(T^n)/(T^{n+1})$, a one-dimensional space generated by the image of $([1]-1)^n$. The degree-one symbol $X$ of $[1]-1$ consequently gives a surjective graded map $\mathbb F_p[X]\to\operatorname{gr}_IA$, and no power of $X$ vanishes in its degree. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{gr}_I\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z_p]\cong\mathbb F_p[X]}.
$$

The same finite quotients show that the [I-adic completion](../../../commutative-algebra.md#adic-completion-of-a-module) is $\mathbb F_p[[T]]$, and its [associated graded ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring) is identical. The proof therefore covers the [completed group algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#completed-group-algebra) interpretation too, while proving the literal ordinary-algebra statement without assuming completion. Applying part ii to a [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) would require its actual completeness, not just this [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) [associated graded ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring).
