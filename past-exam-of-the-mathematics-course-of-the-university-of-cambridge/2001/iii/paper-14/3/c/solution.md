<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose one [projective line](../../../../../../projective-line.md) from each of two different pairs. They are [skew lines](../../../../../../skew-lines.md) by the preceding disjointness result. After a projective linear coordinate change call them

$$
L=\{x_2=x_3=0\},\qquad M=\{x_0=x_1=0\}.
$$

For $A\in L$, $B\in M$, their joining line meets the [cubic surface](../../../../../../cubic-surface.md) at $A,B$ and a third point. This defines a [rational map](../../../../../../rational-map-complex-analysis.md) $L\times M\dashrightarrow S$ on the [Zariski-open set](../../../../../../zariski-open-set.md) where that third intersection is distinct and the joining line is not contained in $S$.

More explicitly, because $F$ vanishes on both [projective lines](../../../../../../projective-line.md),

$$
F(\alpha A+\beta B)=\alpha\beta\bigl(\alpha U(A,B)+\beta V(A,B)\bigr).
$$

The third point is $[VA-UB]$. Both [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $U,V$ are nonzero: if either vanished identically, one of the two [projective lines](../../../../../../projective-line.md) would lie in the [singular locus](../../../../../../singular-locus.md). Thus $UV\ne0$ supplies a nonempty [Zariski-open set](../../../../../../zariski-open-set.md).

Conversely, a point $P=[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]$ outside $L\cup M$ lies on the unique joining line whose endpoints have coordinates $[x_0:x_1]$ on $L$ and $[x_2:x_3]$ on $M$. This is a rational inverse on the same generic locus. The [rational parametrization of a cubic surface from two skew lines](../../../../../../rational-parametrization-of-a-cubic-surface-from-two-skew-lines.md) gives

$$
S\ \text{birational to}\ L\times M\cong\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1.
$$

The latter contains the [affine plane](../../../../../../affine-plane.md) as a dense open subset, as does $\mathbb P^2$. Hence $S$ is [birational](../../../../../../birational-variety.md) to $\mathbb P^2$.
