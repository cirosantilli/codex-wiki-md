<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Blow up the distinct singular points of the [integral projective curve](../../../../../../integral-projective-curve.md), once at each original point, and let $C'$ be its [strict transform of an algebraic subvariety](../../../../../../strict-transform-of-an-algebraic-subvariety.md). Write $H$ for the pullback of a [projective line](../../../../../../projective-line.md) and $E_i$ for the [algebraic exceptional divisors](../../../../../../algebraic-exceptional-divisor.md). The [multiplicity of a plane curve at a point](../../../../../../multiplicity-of-a-plane-curve-at-a-point.md) and the [intersection formula for blowing up a surface](../../../../../../intersection-formula-for-blowing-up-a-surface.md) give

$$
[C']=dH-\sum_i m_iE_i,\qquad
H^2=1,\qquad H\cdot E_i=0,\qquad E_i\cdot E_j=-\delta_{ij}.
$$

The [canonical divisor formula for a surface blowup](../../../../../../canonical-divisor-formula-for-a-surface-blowup.md) gives $K=-3H+\sum_iE_i$. Applying the [arithmetic adjunction formula on a smooth surface](../../../../../../arithmetic-adjunction-formula-on-a-smooth-surface.md), rather than a formula requiring a smooth strict transform, yields

$$
2p_a(C')-2=d^2-3d-\sum_i m_i(m_i-1),
$$

and hence

$$
p_a(C')=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}2-\frac12\sum_i m_i(m_i-1).
$$

The [strict transform of an algebraic subvariety](../../../../../../strict-transform-of-an-algebraic-subvariety.md) is still an [integral projective curve](../../../../../../integral-projective-curve.md). Its [arithmetic genus](../../../../../../arithmetic-genus.md) is $h^1(C',\mathcal O_{C'})\ge0$, even if some singularities remain. Therefore the [plane curve singularity multiplicity bound](../../../../../../plane-curve-singularity-multiplicity-bound.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sum_i m_i(m_i-1)\le(d-1)(d-2).}
$$

The argument uses only one [blowup of a smooth algebraic surface](../../../../../../blowup-of-a-smooth-algebraic-surface.md) at each original singular point; it does not assume these blowups resolve every singularity.
