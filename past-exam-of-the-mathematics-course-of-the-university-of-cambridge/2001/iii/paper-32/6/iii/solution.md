<h1 id="6/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A finite-dimensional [exponential family](../../../../../../exponential-family-split.md) has common dominating measure and density

$$
f(y;\theta)=h(y)\exp\{\eta(\theta)^{\mathsf T}T(y)-\kappa(\eta(\theta))\}.
$$

Here $\eta$ is the [natural parameter of an exponential family](../../../../../../natural-parameter-of-an-exponential-family.md), and normalization determines the [cumulant function of an exponential family](../../../../../../cumulant-function-of-an-exponential-family.md) $\kappa$. A full regular family lets $\eta$ vary over an open natural parameter domain. A [curved exponential family](../../../../../../curved-exponential-family.md) restricts $\eta$ to a lower-dimensional submanifold, while a minimal representation removes redundant affine combinations of the statistic.

Differentiating the normalization under regularity gives

$$
\nabla\kappa(\eta)=\mathbb E_\eta T(Y),\qquad
\kappa''(\eta)=\operatorname{cov}_\eta(T(Y)).
$$

Consequently $\kappa$ is convex and its Hessian is the [Fisher information matrix](../../../../../../fisher-information-matrix.md) in natural coordinates. For an independent size-$n$ sample, $S=\sum_iT(Y_i)$ is a [sufficient statistic](../../../../../../sufficient-statistic.md): the joint density factors into $\prod_i h(y_i)$ times $\exp\{\eta^{\mathsf T}S-n\kappa(\eta)\}$, so the conditional law given $S$ loses its parameter dependence. An interior [maximum-likelihood estimator](../../../../../../maximum-likelihood-estimator.md) solves

$$
S/n=\nabla\kappa(\widehat\eta),
$$

matching empirical and theoretical sufficient-statistic means. Under minimality the Hessian is positive definite, so the [log-likelihood](../../../../../../log-likelihood.md) is strictly concave; existence of an interior maximizer still needs the observed statistic to be in the appropriate interior convex support.

For a full open natural family, the canonical [sufficient statistic](../../../../../../sufficient-statistic.md) is a [complete statistic](../../../../../../complete-statistic.md): if $\mathbb E_\eta g(S)=0$ throughout the open domain, exponential tilting about an interior point makes the Laplace transform of the integrable signed measure $g(s)P_{\eta_0}(ds)$ zero on a neighborhood. Transform uniqueness makes that measure zero, so $g(S)=0$ almost surely. This requires integrability under the family. It is not an automatic property of curved restrictions. The sufficient-statistic reduction, explicit information and cumulant identities make these families especially useful for exact and higher-order inference. [Normal distributions](../../../../../../normal-distribution.md), [Poisson distributions](../../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) and [gamma distributions](../../../../../../gamma-distribution.md) supply familiar examples.

A [transformation model](../../../../../../transformation-model.md) is built differently: a [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) acts on the sample and parameter spaces, and its [probability laws](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) satisfy

$$
Y\sim P_\theta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad gY\sim P_{g\theta}.
$$

In a transitive model all laws are transforms of a fixed reference law. The [location family](../../../../../../location-family.md) is generated by translations, with density $f_\mu(y)=f_0(y-\mu)$. The positive-scale family has density $f_\sigma(y)=\sigma^{-1}f_0(y/\sigma)$, and the [location-scale family](../../../../../../location-scale-family.md) combines them as

$$
f_{\mu,\sigma}(y)=\sigma^{-1}f_0((y-\mu)/\sigma),\qquad \sigma>0.
$$

The Jacobian factor is essential. For independent observations its power is $\sigma^{-n}$. The base density need not have moments, and a [transformation model](../../../../../../transformation-model.md) need not have fixed support.

An [equivariant estimator](../../../../../../equivariant-estimator.md) respects the parameter action. With a well-defined equivariant fit $T(y)$ valued in the transformation group, the normalized sample

$$
a(y)=T(y)^{-1}y
$$

is a [maximal invariant](../../../../../../maximal-invariant.md). Equivariance gives $a(gy)=a(y)$; if normalized samples agree, $y'$ is obtained from $y$ by $T(y')T(y)^{-1}$. In a transitive [transformation model](../../../../../../transformation-model.md) its law is parameter-free, so it is an [ancillary statistic](../../../../../../ancillary-statistic.md). For location and positive scale, centered standardized residuals provide this construction on nondegenerate samples. If the parameter action has a nontrivial stabilizer, normalization must retain its quotient action rather than pretending that a unique group element is determined.

This geometry motivates conditioning on an ancillary and using the [P-star approximation](../../../../../../p-star-approximation.md) for the fitted parameter. Ancillarity means parameter-free distribution; it does not by itself assert independence from the estimator. In a normal location model with known scale, the [sample mean](../../../../../../sample-mean.md) and centered residuals are independent by their joint Gaussian law and zero [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md). In an exponential scale model, the sample sum and its normalized proportions are independent, again an additional model property.

The two classes overlap but are distinct. A normal location-scale family is both an [exponential family](../../../../../../exponential-family-split.md) and a [transformation model](../../../../../../transformation-model.md); an exponential mean family is both a one-dimensional natural [exponential family](../../../../../../exponential-family-split.md) and a scale model. A general [Cauchy distribution](../../../../../../cauchy-distribution.md) location family is a [transformation model](../../../../../../transformation-model.md) without a fixed finite-dimensional regular exponential-family sufficient-statistic reduction. Exponential-family structure emphasizes sufficient statistics and natural-parameter convexity; transformation structure emphasizes equivariance, [group orbits](../../../../../../orbit-of-a-group-action.md) and ancillary reduction.
