<h1 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Every [unitary matrix](../../../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) is unitarily diagonalizable. Part (ii) therefore proves the stronger [matrix](../../../../../../../matrix.md) statement $\phi(A(t))=I$, not merely a statement about its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). The [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), and it contains every upper unipotent. By part (iii) their [normal closure](../../../../../../../normal-closure.md) is the entire [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\phi(g)=I\quad\text{for every }g\in SL_2(\mathbb R).}
$$

This proves the [triviality of finite-dimensional unitary representations of SL2R](../../../../../../../triviality-of-finite-dimensional-unitary-representations-of-sl2r.md). In fact the argument used only the algebraic homomorphism property and finite-dimensional unitarity; it did not need continuity.
