<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $U=\phi(A(t))$. Applying the [group homomorphism](../../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $\phi$ to the identity just proved shows that $U$ is conjugate to $U^{m^2}$ for every positive integer $m$. Thus the multiset of its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is unchanged by taking their $m^2$th powers.

Take $m=2$. The fourth-power operation permutes this finite multiset. For each [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $z$, iterating its permutation cycle gives $z^{4^r}=z$ for some positive integer $r$. Since $U$ is unitary, $z\ne0$, so $z^{4^r-1}=1$: every [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is a [root of unity](../../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). Choose a positive integer $d$ divisible by all their orders. Every [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $U^{d^2}$ is then one. Because its [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) multiset is also the original one, we obtain

$$
\boxed{\text{every eigenvalue of }\phi(A(t))\text{ equals }1.}
$$

This is [square-power rigidity of a finite nonzero spectrum](../../../../../../../square-power-rigidity-of-a-finite-nonzero-spectrum.md). Finiteness of the dimension is essential to the permutation-cycle argument.
