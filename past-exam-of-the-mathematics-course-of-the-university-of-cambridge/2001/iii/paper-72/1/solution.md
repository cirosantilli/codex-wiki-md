<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

[Spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md) occurs when the [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) has a symmetry that an equilibrium [pure thermodynamic phase](../../../../../pure-thermodynamic-phase.md) does not share. For a ferromagnetic [XY model](../../../../../xy-model.md), a nonzero [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) selects an orientation even though the zero-field [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is rotation invariant. The order of limits matters: with $V$ the volume and $h$ a field selecting the orientation, the spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) is

$$
\mathbf m_0=\lim_{h\to0^+}\lim_{V\to\infty}\langle\mathbf m\rangle_{V,h}.
$$

A finite system at zero field instead averages over symmetry-related orientations and has zero vector [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md). Below an ordered transition, that symmetric mixture must be distinguished from a selected [pure thermodynamic phase](../../../../../pure-thermodynamic-phase.md).

A [Goldstone mode](../../../../../goldstone-boson.md) is a soft fluctuation tangent to the continuously degenerate family of equilibrium orientations. For the [XY model](../../../../../xy-model.md), the uniform change $\theta\mapsto\theta+\epsilon$ costs no energy. Local changes cost only gradients, so the [Goldstone-mode effective free energy](../../../../../goldstone-mode-effective-free-energy.md) starts with $\frac12\rho_s\int(\nabla\theta)^2$, with no restoring term proportional to $\theta^2$. A [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform.md) mode therefore has a stiffness proportional to $q^2$, becoming arbitrarily soft at long wavelength. The dimensionless [phase stiffness](../../../../../phase-stiffness.md) here is $\bar K=\beta\rho_s$. These are the [spin waves](../../../../../spin-wave.md) of the angle field. Their large thermal fluctuations can invalidate the proposed ordered phase in low dimension; the existence of a symmetry of the [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) alone does not guarantee its spontaneous breaking.
