<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The physical angle takes values on a circle, so it is defined modulo $2\pi$. Around a closed curve enclosing a [phase vortex](../../../../../../phase-vortex.md),

$$
\oint\nabla\theta\cdot d\mathbf l=2\pi n,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

The integer [winding number](../../../../../../winding-number.md) cannot change through a smooth single-valued deformation unless a defect crosses the curve. Thus a globally smooth Gaussian [spin wave](../../../../../../spin-wave.md) field omits whole topological sectors of the [XY model](../../../../../../xy-model.md).

Outside a vortex core of radius $a$, an isolated charge-$n$ [phase vortex](../../../../../../phase-vortex.md) has $\theta=n\varphi$ and $|\nabla\theta|=|n|/r$. Its dimensionless energy in a sample of size $L$ is

$$
\beta E_n=\frac{\bar K}{2}\int_a^L2\pi r\,dr\,\frac{n^2}{r^2}
=\pi\bar Kn^2\log(L/a)+\beta E_{\rm core}.
$$

There are of order $(L/a)^2$ positions for its core, so the positional [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) contributes $2\log(L/a)$. The competition for unit charge is therefore $(\pi\bar K-2)\log(L/a)$, apart from core terms. Opposite charges have a logarithmic attraction and form a [vortex Coulomb gas](../../../../../../vortex-coulomb-gas.md).

At low temperature, [vortex-antivortex binding](../../../../../../vortex-antivortex-binding.md) leaves no free charges on large scales. [Spin waves](../../../../../../spin-wave.md) still destroy true [long-range order](../../../../../../long-range-order.md), but the [correlation function](../../../../../../correlation-function.md) decays algebraically with the renormalized [phase stiffness](../../../../../../phase-stiffness.md). At the [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](../../../../../../berezinskii-kosterlitz-thouless-transition.md), pairs unbind; free vortices screen the interaction and produce a finite [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md) and exponential decay above the transition. The energy-entropy estimate identifies the threshold stiffness $K_R=2/\pi$, where $K_R$ is the long-distance dimensionless stiffness, not necessarily the bare $\bar K$. It agrees with the [universal stiffness jump](../../../../../../universal-stiffness-jump.md) and the limiting algebraic exponent $1/(2\pi K_R)=1/4$. **The two-dimensional transition is vortex unbinding between quasi-long-range order and disorder, rather than the onset of nonzero spontaneous magnetization.**
