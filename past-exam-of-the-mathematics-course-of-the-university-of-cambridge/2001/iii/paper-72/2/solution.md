<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [scaling hypothesis for critical phenomena](../../../../../scaling-hypothesis-for-critical-phenomena.md) says that the singular part of the [free-energy density](../../../../../free-energy-density.md) near a continuous [thermodynamic critical point](../../../../../thermodynamic-critical-point.md) becomes a generalized homogeneous function of the relevant thermal and magnetic variables. The growing [correlation length](../../../../../correlation-length.md) removes sensitivity to most microscopic details. Systems flowing towards the same [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) have the same [universality class](../../../../../universality-class.md), up to nonuniversal normalizations of the fields and lengths.

A change of length scale by $b>1$ gives, when ordinary [hyperscaling](../../../../../hyperscaling-relation.md) holds,

$$
f_s(t,h)=b^{-d}f_s(b^{y_t}t,b^{y_h}h).
$$

Choosing $b=|t|^{-1/y_t}$ gives $f_s=|t|^{d/y_t}g_\pm(h/|t|^{y_h/y_t})$. More generally the specified homogeneous form uses exponents $2-\alpha$ and $\Delta$ without presupposing [hyperscaling](../../../../../hyperscaling-relation.md). Its thermodynamic derivatives determine the [critical exponents](../../../../../critical-exponent.md). Analytic backgrounds are separated from this singular part.

Throughout the calculation, fractional powers of the thermal variable mean $|t|$, with different scaling functions above and below the transition. The spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) exponent uses the ordered branch $t<0$ with $h\to0^+$ after the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md). Marginal variables can generate logarithmic corrections, and a [dangerously irrelevant coupling](../../../../../dangerously-irrelevant-coupling.md) can invalidate the simple hyperscaling identification with a correlation volume.
