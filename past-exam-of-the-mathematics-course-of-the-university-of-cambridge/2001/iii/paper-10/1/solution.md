Write $\chi$ for the red-blue [finite colouring](../../../../../finite-coloring.md) of unordered pairs. The two alternatives concern one [arithmetic progression](../../../../../arithmetic-progression.md) or a pair of disjoint [arithmetic progressions](../../../../../arithmetic-progression.md); they need not be mutually exclusive. For $m=1$, a singleton already has no non-blue edge, so the first alternative holds. Assume henceforth $m\ge2$.

If the first alternative fails, every $m$-term [arithmetic progression](../../../../../arithmetic-progression.md) contains a red pair. Label each $(a,d)\in\mathbb N^2$ by one such pair's positions $0\le r<s<m$, choosing the first in a fixed ordering when there are several. This is a [finite colouring](../../../../../finite-coloring.md) with at most $\binom m2$ colours. The following two branches establish the [arithmetic-progression bipartite Ramsey dichotomy](../../../../../arithmetic-progression-bipartite-ramsey-dichotomy.md).
