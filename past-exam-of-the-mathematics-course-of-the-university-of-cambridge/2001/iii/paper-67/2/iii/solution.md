<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At a fixed [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md), the [deuterium equilibrium abundance](../../../../../../deuterium-equilibrium-abundance.md) contains an explicit factor $n_B$ through $\eta$. Two nucleons have to encounter each other to make one bound nucleus. Their capture rate per volume is proportional to $n_nn_p$, whereas [nuclear photodisintegration](../../../../../../nuclear-photodisintegration.md) competes through the very numerous thermal [photons](../../../../../../photon.md). The small [baryon-to-photon ratio](../../../../../../baryon-to-photon-ratio.md) therefore delays stable [deuterium](../../../../../../deuterium.md) formation until $T\ll\varepsilon_D$, producing the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md). Changing the [cosmological baryon density](../../../../../../cosmological-baryon-density.md) changes both its release time and the subsequent efficiency of nuclear burning. The residual primordial [deuterium](../../../../../../deuterium.md) abundance is particularly sensitive to these rates: on the usual nucleosynthesis branch, increasing $n_B$ burns more [deuterium](../../../../../../deuterium.md) into heavier nuclei and lowers the remaining D/H. The positive dependence of the instantaneous equilibrium expression and the negative dependence of the final relic D/H are different statements.

By contrast, once nuclear reactions are efficient, nearly every surviving [neutron](../../../../../../neutron.md) ends in [helium-4](../../../../../../helium-4.md). Two [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) make each [helium-4](../../../../../../helium-4.md) nucleus, so $n_{\rm He}\simeq n_n/2$ and

$$
\boxed{Y_P\simeq\frac{4n_{\rm He}}{n_B}
=\frac{2n_n}{n_n+n_p}=\frac{2r}{1+r},\qquad r=\frac{n_n}{n_p}.}
$$

This [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md) is mainly neutron limited: $r$ is set by weak conversion, [cosmological weak freeze-out](../../../../../../cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md) and [free-neutron decay after freeze-out](../../../../../../free-neutron-decay-after-freeze-out.md), rather than by the absolute baryon density. There is a weaker dependence because a higher $\eta$ releases the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md) earlier and allows fewer [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) to decay. Thus $Y_P$ is comparatively insensitive, not exactly independent, of $n_B$. At such exceptionally small baryon densities that nuclei never form efficiently, the neutron-limited approximation would fail.
