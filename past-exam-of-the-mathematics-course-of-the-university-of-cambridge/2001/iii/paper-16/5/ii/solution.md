<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose a [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) proper [incompressible surface](../../../../../../incompressible-surface.md) $S$ with nonempty [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) as the first cut in a [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../../../../three-manifold-hierarchy.md) of $N$. Homotope $f$ relative to $\partial M$ into transverse position and let $F=f^{-1}(S)$.

We first make $F$ incompressible. If $D$ is a compression [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) for $F$, its [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) maps to a loop in $S$ which is [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) in $N$. Since $S$ is fundamental-group-injective, this loop is [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) in $S$. Fill it in $S$ and alter $f$ in a small neighbourhood of $D$ so that its inverse image is obtained by compressing $F$ along $D$. The alteration is a [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md): the filling and the old [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) give a [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) map in $N$, which is [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) by the asphericity proved above. A product collar of $S$ specifies the two sides of the modification. The integer $\sum_j\max(0,1-\chi(F_j))$ decreases at every essential compression; sphere components can be removed separately. A resulting [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) component of $F$ bounds a [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md) in $M$ by [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../../../../irreducible-three-manifold.md); move the map of that [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md) to one side of $S$ to remove it. The required extension and [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) exist because the higher [homotopy groups](../../../../../../homotopy-group.md) of $N$ vanish. Continuing leaves an incompressible $F$ with no spherical components.

For each component $F_j$, the map on [fundamental groups](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) to $S$ is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Indeed, its composition with the [injection](../../../../../../injective-function.md) $\pi_1(S)\to\pi_1(N)$ equals the composition of $\pi_1(F_j)\to\pi_1(M)$ with the [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) $f_*$. Both maps in the latter composition are [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). A closed nonspherical [orientable](../../../../../../orientable-surface.md) $F_j$ cannot occur: its [fundamental group](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) would inject into $\pi_1(S)$, a [free group](../../../../../../free-group.md), whereas every subgroup of a [free group](../../../../../../free-group.md) is free and a closed positive-genus [surface](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) group is not free. Thus every component of $F$ has [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md).

Apply the permitted surface-map [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) result to $F_j\to S$, fixing its [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md). A fundamental-group-injective map of [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) [surfaces](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) is [homotopic](../../../../../../homotopy.md) either to a covering or, in the [annulus](../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) case here, to a map into one target [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circle. The exceptional [annulus](../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) case is impossible: its two distinct [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circles would map onto the same circle of $\partial S$, contradicting the [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md) of the original [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) map. If $S$ is a [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md) makes each $F_j$ a [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), and the unique [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circle directly gives a single [disk](../../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Otherwise each component is [homotopic](../../../../../../homotopy.md) to a cover of $S$.

Every point of $\partial S$ has exactly one preimage on $\partial F$, since $f|_{\partial M}$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Every cover component contributes its positive degree to the number of preimages on every target [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circle. Thus there is exactly one component and its covering degree is one. We have proved that **$F\to S$ can be homotoped to a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md), relative to its [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md)**.

Extend this [surface](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) to $M$, and use the two-sided collars to make $f$ a product [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) on a neighbourhood of $F$. The transverse normal direction is a degree-one local crossing, so the collars on its two sides match the two sides of $S$; any superfluous folds can be removed in the normal interval. We may now cut $M$ along $F$ and $N$ along $S$, obtaining a map whose restriction to the entire cut [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). The other hypotheses also survive: the cut manifolds are irreducible, have nonempty [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md), and the inclusion of every cut component into its uncut manifold is fundamental-group-injective by the normal form of the [van Kampen theorem](../../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md). A loop killed by the cut map is consequently killed by $f_*$ and hence is trivial in its source cut component.
