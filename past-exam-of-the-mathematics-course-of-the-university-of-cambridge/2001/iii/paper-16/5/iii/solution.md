<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Apply the boundary-degree argument to each source cut component. It maps to one target cut component and is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) on each of its [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) components onto distinct target [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) components. Its relative degree must be $\pm1$, so it meets the whole [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of that target component. Since the global cut [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) map is one-to-one, two source components cannot map to the same target component; since it is onto, every target component is accounted for. Thus the cut components correspond bijectively, and each paired map satisfies the original hypotheses.

Induct on the remaining number of cuts in the chosen target [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../../../../three-manifold-hierarchy.md), keeping fixed all [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) pieces already straightened. At the final stage the target components are [three-balls](../../../../../../three-ball.md). Each corresponding source component is irreducible with a single spherical [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md), and is therefore a [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md) by the inward-pushed-boundary argument above. Extend its prescribed [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) over the [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md) by coning. The original [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md) map and this [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) are [homotopic](../../../../../../homotopy.md) relative to the [boundary](../../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md): after identifying the target with a convex [three-ball](../../../../../../three-ball.md), take their straight-line [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md). This establishes the induction base.

Reverse the cuts. On both copies of every cutting [surface](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) the two constructed maps agree with the fixed product [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) of its collar, so the component [homeomorphisms](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) glue. Their [homotopies](../../../../../../homotopy.md) also glue, because all cut boundaries have stayed fixed. Repeating through the [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../../../../three-manifold-hierarchy.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f\simeq h\ \text{relative to }\partial M,\qquad h:M\longrightarrow N\text{ a homeomorphism}.}
$$

The proof derives the three-dimensional rigidity from the [three-manifold hierarchy](../../../../../../three-manifold-hierarchy.md) and the permitted surface-map straightening; it does not use the desired three-dimensional [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) assertion as a quoted theorem.
