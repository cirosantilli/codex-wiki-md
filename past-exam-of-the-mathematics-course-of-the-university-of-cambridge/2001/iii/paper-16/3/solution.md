<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [normal surface](../../../../../normal-surface.md) meets each [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) in disjoint [disks](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) of the four triangular and three quadrilateral types. Its arcs in a face join distinct edges, each [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) meets an edge at most once, and at most one quadrilateral type occurs in a [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md). These last requirements exclude, for example, a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) with an octagonal [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md).

We first establish the surgery operation needed for the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) system. Suppose a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) $D$ disjoint from the other [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) compresses one [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) $S$. It produces [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) $S'$ and $S''$. Small parallel copies of the three [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) bound a three-times [punctured three-sphere](../../../../../punctured-three-sphere.md) $P$. Denote the old piece on the uncompressed side by $A$, and the two pieces on the compressed side by $B'$ and $B''$. If both $B'$ and $B''$ were punctured three-spheres, then $P$ glued to them would also be a punctured three-sphere, contrary to the hypothesis on the original cut manifold. Choose, say, $B'$ which is not of this type, and retain $S'$ alone in place of $S$.

The other new piece is $A\cup P\cup B''$. It too cannot be a punctured three-sphere. If it were, cap its external [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) to make $S^3$. All the [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) separating $A$ from the attached pieces then bound [three-balls](../../../../../three-ball.md) by the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem.md); the component containing $A$ would be a punctured three-sphere, contradicting the hypothesis on $A$. Thus the [sphere surgery preserving nontrivial complementary pieces](../../../../../sphere-surgery-preserving-nontrivial-complementary-pieces.md) keeps exactly $k$ [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) and keeps every complementary piece nontrivial. The assumption that all [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) separate ensures that the old and new pieces used here really are distinct sides.

Apply general position to the whole [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) system, avoiding the vertices and making it transverse to edges and faces. We describe a terminating normalization, allowing the replacement operation just proved as well as [isotopy](../../../../../isotopy.md). If some component of its intersection with a [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) is not a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), choose a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circle innermost among circles not bounding such [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) components. A [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) just inside the tetrahedral [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) gives a compression; if intervening [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) components occur, first push them into collars of their [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [disks](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). Perform the compression and retain one new [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) as above. This replaces a non-disk tetrahedral piece by [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) pieces without increasing edge intersections. Repetition is finite: every compression divides the original [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) along a separating circle, and the sum of $1-\chi$ over the non-disk planar tetrahedral pieces decreases; [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) components can then be put in [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) collars. A closed [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) component wholly in a [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) is impossible, because it would cut off a [three-ball](../../../../../three-ball.md), contrary to the complementary-piece condition.

Now each tetrahedral piece is a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). A face circle innermost in a face allows its incident [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) to be moved through that face [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), removing the circle. This reduces the number of face intersections without increasing the edge weight. A returning face arc similarly gives an edge bigon across which the [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md) can be pushed, reducing the number of edge intersections by two. Restore tetrahedral [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) pieces by the preceding operations whenever necessary.

It remains to exclude multiple intersections of a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) with an edge, which the absence of returning face arcs alone does not exclude. Suppose a tetrahedral [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) meets an edge twice. Among such [disks](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) choose a pair of intersections consecutive on the edge and with no other [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md) intersection between them. Such a pair exists: an intervening [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) crosses the edge interval an even number of times, so passing to an innermost intervening pair eventually gives consecutive points. Join them by an arc in the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). The [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) pieces divide the [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) into [three-balls](../../../../../three-ball.md), and this arc together with the edge interval lies in the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of one of these [three-balls](../../../../../three-ball.md). It therefore bounds a bigon with interior disjoint from the [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md). Push across the bigon to reduce edge weight by two. At fixed weight the previous moves reduce face and non-disk complexity; the edge weight itself is a nonnegative integer. Hence the whole process terminates. A final tetrahedral [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) meets each edge at most once, and its [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) separates either one vertex from three or two vertices from two. It is respectively a normal triangle or quadrilateral. Thus **a normal system of exactly $k$ [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) with the required complementary-piece property exists**.

For the finite decomposition, we need a bound on such normal systems, not merely an assertion that they have been normalized. Let $f_2$ be the number of faces of the fixed [triangulation](../../../../../triangulation.md). In a triangular face cut by normal arcs, all but at most four complementary regions are rectangles between adjacent parallel arcs. Thus at most $4f_2$ complementary three-dimensional components contain a nonrectangular face region. Every remaining component is assembled from product regions between parallel normal [disks](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), so is an interval bundle whose [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) components are [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md). Its [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) base is either $S^2$ or $\mathbb{RP}^2$. The product $S^2\times I$ is a [punctured three-sphere](../../../../../punctured-three-sphere.md), and is excluded. The other possibility is the twisted interval bundle over $\mathbb{RP}^2$, namely $\mathbb{RP}^3$ with a [three-ball](../../../../../three-ball.md) removed. Each such piece contributes a distinct $\mathbb F_2$ summand to $H_1(M;\mathbb F_2)$: gluing along [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) does not identify first-homology classes, by the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md). Since $k$ separating [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) give $k+1$ components, we obtain the explicit finite bound

$$
\boxed{k+1\leq 4f_2+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_1(M;\mathbb F_2).}
$$

For an arbitrary closed [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) $X$, first cut out nonseparating [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md). A neighbourhood of one such [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) together with an arc joining its two sides is a punctured $S^2$-bundle over $S^1$; the bundle is the product or the twisted bundle according to the [orientation](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) of the attaching identification. Its complement is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md), so this extracts a [connected sum](../../../../../connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds.md) factor. The mod-two first Betti number of the remaining capped manifold decreases by one. Hence only finitely many such extractions are possible. The remaining manifolds have only separating [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md).

Whenever a remaining factor is not a [prime three-manifold](../../../../../prime-three-manifold.md), split it along a [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) into two factors neither [homeomorphic](../../../../../local-homeomorphism.md) to $S^3$. Viewed in the original remaining manifold, all these successive splitting [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) form a disjoint system whose pieces are not punctured three-spheres. The normalization and bound just proved prohibit infinitely many such splittings. This proves **existence of a finite prime decomposition**, including for $X$ without assuming [orientability](../../../../../orientability.md).

For completeness, both [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) bundles extracted above are [prime three-manifolds](../../../../../prime-three-manifold.md). Their [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) is $\mathbb Z$, so a separating [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) and the [van Kampen theorem](../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) express $\mathbb Z$ as a [free product](../../../../../free-product.md) in which one capped factor has trivial [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md). Its punctured piece lifts homeomorphically to the [universal cover](../../../../../universal-cover.md) $S^2\times\mathbb R$, identified with $\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}$. That [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) lifted piece has one spherical [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) and must be the bounded side of this [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in $\mathbb R^3$. It misses the origin, so the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem.md) makes it a [three-ball](../../../../../three-ball.md). The corresponding capped factor is $S^3$, as required.

Suppose now that $X$ is [orientable](../../../../../orientable-surface.md). A prime factor which is not an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../../../irreducible-three-manifold.md) contains a nonseparating [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md): an essential separating [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) would already split it nontrivially. Extracting the corresponding [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) bundle shows that the factor must be $S^2\times S^1$. Thus the other nontrivial primes are irreducible.

Consider two prime decompositions. Represent each by a [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) system, including one nonseparating [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in each $S^2\times S^1$ factor. The capped pieces are now the irreducible factors and copies of $S^3$. Make the two [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) systems transverse. To remove their intersections, choose a circle innermost on a [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) of the second system and surger the first system along its [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). Retain both surgery [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) temporarily. The old [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) and the new pair bound a three-times punctured [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md); adding the pair and then deleting the old [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) only inserts or removes spherical punctures.

This operation cannot alter an irreducible capped piece: every new [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in that piece bounds a [three-ball](../../../../../three-ball.md), so it can only split off a punctured $S^3$ piece. The other pieces were already punctured $S^3$, where the same conclusion follows from the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem.md). After small displacements the surgery removes the chosen intersection circle and introduces none. Repeat until the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) systems are disjoint. Their union is a common refinement, and its non-$S^3$ capped irreducible pieces are precisely those of each original decomposition. Hence these factors agree, up to order and orientation-preserving [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md).

Finally, the number $r$ of $S^2\times S^1$ factors is determined by

$$
r=b_1(X;\mathbb Q)-\sum_P b_1(P;\mathbb Q),
$$

where the sum runs over the common irreducible factors. This uses additivity of first [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) under [connected sum](../../../../../connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds.md), not a claim that the [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) determines a manifold. Therefore **the [orientable](../../../../../orientable-surface.md) prime decomposition is unique up to order and insertion or deletion of $S^3$ factors**.
