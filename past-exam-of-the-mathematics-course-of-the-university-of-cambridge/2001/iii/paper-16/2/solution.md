<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

We prove the [Loop theorem](../../../../../loop-theorem.md) by a finite tower of double [covering spaces](../../../../../covering-space.md), keeping track of the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) class during the descent. This works without assuming [orientability](../../../../../orientability.md) or [three-manifold irreducibility](../../../../../irreducible-three-manifold.md).

Let $F$ be the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) component containing the basepoint. A nontrivial element in the kernel of $\pi_1(F)\to\pi_1(M)$ is represented by the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of a singular [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) $u:D^2\to M$. Arrange that the interior of the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) maps into the interior of $M$, and make $u$ simplicial after [simplicial subdivision](../../../../../simplicial-subdivision.md). Let $V_0$ be a small regular neighbourhood of its image, retaining a subsurface $B_0\subset\partial V_0$ in $F$ containing the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) loop. More generally, during the construction we require the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) loop to stay outside the [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) which is the kernel of the map from the current [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) subsurface to $\pi_1(F)$. This records exactly the information needed at the end.

If $V_0$ has a [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) double cover, lift $u$ to that cover and take a regular neighbourhood $V_1$ of the lifted [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) image. Repeat this whenever the current neighbourhood has a [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) double cover. This procedure is finite. Indeed, let $C_i$ be the simplicial image of the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) at stage $i$. The full inverse image of $C_i$ in a [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) double cover is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md), since the cover retracts to this inverse image. It is the union of the lifted [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) image $C_{i+1}$ and its translate under the nontrivial deck transformation. These two images must meet. At least one image [simplex](../../../../../simplex.md) downstairs consequently has two distinct preimages represented in $C_{i+1}$. No image [simplex](../../../../../simplex.md) disappears, so the number of distinct image [simplexes](../../../../../simplex.md) strictly increases. This number is at most the number of [simplexes](../../../../../simplex.md) of the fixed domain [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). Thus the [tower proof of the loop theorem](../../../../../tower-proof-of-the-loop-theorem.md) terminates.

At the top, $V_r$ has no [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) double cover. Equivalently, $H^1(V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0$. Mod-two [Poincare-Lefschetz duality](../../../../../lefschetz-duality.md) and the relative [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) sequence give

$$
H_2(V_r,\partial V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0,
\qquad
H_1(\partial V_r;\mathbb F_2)\hookrightarrow H_1(V_r;\mathbb F_2)=0.
$$

The [classification of compact surfaces](../../../../../classification-of-compact-surfaces.md) now makes every component of $\partial V_r$ a [two-sphere](../../../../../two-sphere.md). The lifted [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) subsurface $B_r$ is planar. Its [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) is normally generated by its [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circles. Since the lifted loop is outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), one [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circle of $B_r$ is outside that subgroup. That circle bounds a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) on its [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md); push the interior of this [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) into $V_r$. We have obtained an embedded [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) with the required boundary-class property at the top of the tower.

We next descend one double cover. Projecting an embedded [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) gives a singular [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) with only double curves: triple points and branch points cannot arise from a two-sheeted cover. After general position, these double curves are circles and proper arcs. The following cut-and-paste procedure eliminates them while preserving a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) class outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).

First eliminate the circular double curves. Take a double circle innermost on one of its preimage sheets. If its two preimage circles bound disjoint subdisks, cut out the two subdisks and interchange small displaced copies of them; discard any resulting closed component and retain the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) carrying the original outer [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md). If the circles are nested, replace the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) bounded by the outer preimage circle by the innermost [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), pushed slightly onto the appropriate sheet. Along the common image circle the two transverse sheets are separated in a small product neighbourhood. In the nested case the intervening [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) may have its sides interchanged under projection; separating the two sheets on its double cover and then rounding the two opposite corners makes the same replacement downstairs. The outer [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) is unchanged, no new double curve is introduced, and the chosen double circle disappears. Repeating leaves only double arcs.

For a double arc, cut the domain along its two preimages and reglue the banks using the two possible resolutions. There are two [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) choices. At least one has [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) outside the prescribed [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). Here is the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) calculation that proves this, rather than assuming that an arbitrary resolution preserves essentiality. With connecting paths absorbed into four [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) words $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$, the old word is $W=\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$. In one configuration the two possible new words are

$$
u=\alpha\gamma,\qquad v=\alpha\beta^{-1}\gamma\delta^{-1},\qquad
W=u\delta^{-1}v^{-1}u\delta.
$$

In the other configuration they are

$$
u=\alpha\gamma^{-1},\qquad v=\alpha\delta\gamma\beta,\qquad
W=u(\gamma\delta)^{-1}u^{-1}v(\gamma\delta).
$$

Changing the connecting paths merely conjugates these words. In either configuration, if both new [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) words belonged to a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so would the old word. Choose a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) whose word stays outside the subgroup. The double arc used in the resolution disappears, and the remaining double curves are inherited from the old [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), so their number decreases. Remove any circular double curves again, and repeat. The resulting [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) is embedded.

Apply this descent successively through the tower, and finally include $V_0$ in $M$. Its [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) lies in $F$ and represents a nontrivial element of $\pi_1(F)$; a simple closed curve in a [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md) is [null-homotopic](../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) precisely when it bounds a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) in that [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md). We have therefore proved

$$
\boxed{\exists\ e:(D^2,\partial D^2)\hookrightarrow(M,\partial M)
\quad\text{with }e(\partial D^2)\text{ not bounding a disk in }\partial M.}
$$

The argument also permits a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) subsurface in place of the whole [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) component. In particular, if an embedded simple closed [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) curve $c$ bounds a singular [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), use a small [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) about $c$ as the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) subsurface. The embedded [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) produced has [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) an essential simple closed curve in that [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md), hence [isotopic](../../../../../isotopy.md) to $c$. Extend that [isotopy](../../../../../isotopy.md) through a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) collar. This proves the fixed-boundary form of [Dehn's lemma](../../../../../dehn-s-lemma.md): the original curve $c$ itself bounds an embedded [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) with interior in the manifold.

Now consider the knot map. Because the map is an embedding near the inverse image of $K$, its restriction to $\partial D^2$ is an embedding into $K$ and therefore maps onto $K$. No interior point can map to $K$, since it would duplicate a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) image. [Compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) away from a small domain collar permits a sufficiently small tubular neighbourhood of $K$ to be chosen whose inverse image lies in the embedded collar. Its intersection with this collar cuts off an [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md). The rest of the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) maps into the [knot exterior](../../../../../knot-exterior.md), with [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) a simple closed curve $c$ on the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [torus](../../../../../torus.md).

The [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) shows that $c$ runs once along $K$. The mapped [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) in the exterior gives zero meridional coefficient, equivalently zero [linking number](../../../../../linking-number.md) with $K$. Thus $c$ is a longitude. Apply the fixed-boundary [Dehn's lemma](../../../../../dehn-s-lemma.md) just proved to obtain an embedded [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) in the exterior bounded by $c$. Attach the embedded collar [annulus](../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md) between $c$ and $K$. This gives an embedded spanning [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) for $K$. A regular neighbourhood of that [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) is a standard [three-ball](../../../../../three-ball.md) with $K$ its equatorial curve, so **$K$ is the unknot**.
