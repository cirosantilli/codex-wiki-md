<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../schoenflies-theorem.md) says that a simple closed piecewise linear curve in $S^2$ separates it into two components whose closures are [piecewise linear balls](../../../../../piecewise-linear-ball.md) of dimension two. In particular, a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) of the curve to the equator extends to a piecewise linear [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) of the whole [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md).

A [piecewise linear ball](../../../../../piecewise-linear-ball.md) is a space piecewise linearly [homeomorphic](../../../../../local-homeomorphism.md) to a standard [simplex](../../../../../simplex.md). Write $D=B_1\cap B_2$. The complementary [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) to $D$ in each $\partial B_j$ is a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) by the two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../schoenflies-theorem.md). Thus each pair $(\partial B_j,D)$ is piecewise linearly [homeomorphic](../../../../../local-homeomorphism.md) to a [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) with a chosen hemisphere. Choose these [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) maps to agree on $D$. Extend them to the interiors by coning from an interior point, after compatible subdivisions. The resulting maps identify $B_1$ and $B_2$ with the two halves of a standard ball, glued along its equatorial [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{B_1\cup_D B_2\cong_{mathrm{PL}} B^3.}
$$

The same [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) parametrization proves a useful companion fact: if a ball is indented by removing a smaller ball attached along a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), the closure of what remains is again a ball. In the standard boundary-disk model, move the indented [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) through a [collar neighbourhood](../../../../../collar-neighbourhood.md) to a flat [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md).

A [handle decomposition](../../../../../handle-decomposition.md) is a filtration obtained by attaching [handles](../../../../../handle.md) $D^r\times D^{n-r}$ along $\partial D^r\times D^{n-r}$ in the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of the preceding stage. The number $r$ is the [handle](../../../../../handle.md) index; index-zero [handles](../../../../../handle.md) are disjoint balls, and index-$n$ [handles](../../../../../handle.md) close the remaining spherical [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) components. Starting with a [triangulation](../../../../../triangulation.md) of $M$, thicken its vertices, then its edges, and successively its higher-dimensional [simplexes](../../../../../simplex.md). In a [barycentric subdivision](../../../../../barycentric-subdivision.md) these thickenings have precisely the indicated [handle](../../../../../handle.md) and attaching-region product structures. Adding collars between successive attachments gives a collared [handle decomposition](../../../../../handle-decomposition.md).

For a [critical level handle embedding](../../../../../critical-level-handle-embedding.md), assign successive distinct heights $t_j$ to the [handles](../../../../../handle.md). At height $t_j$ the horizontal slice contains the $j$th [handle](../../../../../handle.md) and its attaching region. Between [handle](../../../../../handle.md) heights, the embedded [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of the previously attached [handles](../../../../../handle.md) travels along its product collar; there is no topological change there. The lower [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) collar meets the attaching region of the next [handle](../../../../../handle.md), and the upper collar leaves its new [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md). The stated [ambient isotopy](../../../../../ambient-isotopy.md) therefore separates all topology changes into horizontal [handles](../../../../../handle.md) and all intervening motion into collars. For an embedded [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md), the local changes are the appearance of a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md), attachment of a band, and disappearance of a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md).

Here is the three-dimensional conclusion, with the distinction between the bounded and unbounded regions made explicit: **a piecewise linear embedded $S^2$ in $\mathbb R^3$ bounds a piecewise linear three-ball; in the one-point compactification $S^3$, both complementary closures are three-balls.** The unbounded closure in $\mathbb R^3$ itself is not a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) ball.

Put the embedded [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in [critical level handle embedding](../../../../../critical-level-handle-embedding.md) position. At a regular horizontal level its intersection with the plane is a finite disjoint union of simple closed curves. An innermost such curve bounds a planar [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) whose interior misses the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md). Cut the [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) along this curve and insert two slightly displaced copies of the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). This replaces one [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) by two [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md). Repeat at regular levels between the critical heights, using innermost curves first. The number of curves at these chosen levels decreases at each operation, so the process stops. Each resulting [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) then lies in a slab containing at most one horizontal [handle](../../../../../handle.md).

These terminal [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) bound balls directly. With no [handle](../../../../../handle.md) the [surface](../../../../../topological-surface.md) is a [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) transported through a collar and capped at its ends, the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of $D^2\times I$. A minimum or maximum merely supplies one of those caps. At a band level there are two planar [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) circles on one side and one on the other. Cap the two circles, straighten each planar [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) using the two-dimensional [Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../schoenflies-theorem.md), and straighten the joining band in its planar collar. If the circles are not nested, this gives two balls joined along a [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md); if they are nested, it gives a ball with one boundary-attached ball removed. The [gluing three-balls along a boundary disk](../../../../../gluing-three-balls-along-a-boundary-disk.md) argument and its indentation version show in both cases that the terminal [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) bounds a ball. This also explains why the planar position of the band matters: no assertion about an arbitrary knotted band is being made.

Reverse the [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md) surgeries. Suppose the two resulting [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) bound balls $A$ and $B$. Their disjoint boundaries imply that the balls are disjoint or one contains the other. Shrink the small displacement of the two surgery caps back to the original planar [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). In the disjoint case, the pre-surgery [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) bounds the union of two balls along that [disk](../../../../../disk-mathematics.md). In the nested case, it bounds the larger ball with the smaller boundary-attached ball removed. Both are balls by the preceding elementary facts. Induction through the finitely many surgeries proves the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem.md). To treat the other region in $S^3$, remove a point inside the ball just constructed and apply the same argument in the resulting copy of $\mathbb R^3$.

A [3-manifold](../../../../../3-manifold.md) is an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../../../irreducible-three-manifold.md) when every piecewise linear embedded [two-sphere](../../../../../two-sphere.md) bounds a ball. Suppose $p:\mathbb R^3\to M$ is a [universal cover](../../../../../universal-cover.md), and lift an embedded [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) $S$ to $\widetilde S$. Since $S$ is [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md), its lift maps homeomorphically to $S$. By the [Three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem](../../../../../three-dimensional-schoenflies-theorem.md), $\widetilde S$ bounds a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) ball. The inverse image of $S$ is locally finite, so only finitely many other lifted [spheres](../../../../../sphere.md) lie inside this ball. Choose an innermost one, bounding a ball $B$ whose interior meets no lift of $S$.

The interior of $B$ maps to one component $U$ of $M\setminus S$. This restriction is a [covering map](../../../../../covering-space.md) onto $U$: a path in $U$ starting in the image lifts into $\operatorname{int}B$, since it cannot cross a lifted [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md). The covering is proper, because a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) subset of $U$ has preimage closed in the [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) ball and cannot meet $\partial B$. Near $S$, the [boundary](../../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) map $\partial B\to S$ is one-to-one, so this [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) covering has one sheet. The map on $B$ is therefore a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) onto $U\cup S$. Thus **every embedded [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) in $M$ bounds a ball**, and $M$ is an [irreducible three-manifold](../../../../../irreducible-three-manifold.md).
