<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The common [Neumann boundary conditions](../../../../../../neumann-boundary-condition.md) define a $(p+1)$-dimensional timelike [worldvolume](../../../../../../worldvolume.md): one time direction and $p$ spatial directions. The remaining [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) fix its transverse position. An open string therefore ends on a [D-brane](../../../../../../d-brane.md) of spatial dimension $p$, a D$p$-brane. For the critical [bosonic string theory](../../../../../../bosonic-string-theory.md) there are $25-p$ transverse directions.

If the Dirichlet constants agree at the two endpoints, both ends lie on the same planar [D-brane](../../../../../../d-brane.md). If they differ, the string stretches between two parallel [D-branes](../../../../../../d-brane.md) with the same orientation but different positions. **Neumann directions are tangent to the brane; Dirichlet directions are transverse to it.** The brane is dynamical: tangential massless open-string polarizations supply a gauge field, while transverse ones describe its displacement, as in [worldvolume fields from open-string massless states](../../../../../../worldvolume-fields-from-open-string-massless-states.md). The unprojected bosonic brane also has a [tachyon](../../../../../../tachyon.md); the boundary-condition interpretation does not make that ground state supersymmetrically stable.
