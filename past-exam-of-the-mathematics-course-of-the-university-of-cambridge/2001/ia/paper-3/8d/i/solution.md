<h1 id="8d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Compose [permutations](../../../../../../permutation.md) from right to left. The indicated cycle lengths immediately give $X^3=P^2=Q^2=e$. Under [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md), $X^{-1}(ab)X=(X^{-1}a\ X^{-1}b)$. Since $X^{-1}$ sends $1,2,3$ to $3,1,2$ and fixes $4$,

$$
\boxed{X^{-1}PX=(31)(24)=Q,\qquad
X^{-1}QX=(32)(14)=(14)(23)=PQ.}
$$

Direct composition also gives $PQ=QP=(14)(23)$. Thus $V=\{e,P,Q,PQ\}$ is a [Klein four-group](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md). [Conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) by $X$ cycles its three nonidentity elements, so $X$ normalizes $V$.

The [cosets](../../../../../../coset.md) $V,VX,VX^2$ have four elements each and are distinct. If two coincided, a nontrivial power of $X$ would belong to $V$, impossible because it has order three whereas every nonidentity element of $V$ has order two. Hence the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by $X,P,Q$ contains at least twelve elements. These generators are [even permutations](../../../../../../even-permutation.md), so the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) lies in the [alternating group](../../../../../../alternating-group.md) $A_4$, whose order is $4!/2=12$; multiplying by one fixed transposition pairs even and odd [permutations](../../../../../../permutation.md) to justify this count. Therefore

$$
\boxed{A_4=\langle X,P,Q\rangle.}
$$

The calculation also describes $A_4$ as the [semidirect product](../../../../../../semidirect-product.md) $V\rtimes\langle X\rangle$, with the order-three factor permuting the three involutions.
