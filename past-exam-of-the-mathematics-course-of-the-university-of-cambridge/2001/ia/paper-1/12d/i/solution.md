<h1 id="12d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose the continuous nonnegative function has $f(t_0)>0$ somewhere. [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) supplies a positive-length interval within $[a,b]$ on which $f\ge f(t_0)/2$; at an endpoint use a one-sided interval. By [monotonicity of the Riemann integral](../../../../../../monotonicity-of-the-riemann-integral.md), its contribution to the integral is strictly positive, a contradiction. Therefore **$\boxed{f\equiv0}$** under the stated continuous-function assumptions.

For the general integrable case, choose $c\in(a,b)$ and let $f(c)=1$ while $f(t)=0$ for $t\ne c$. This nonnegative [indicator function](../../../../../../indicator-function.md) is [Riemann integrable](../../../../../../riemann-integrable-function.md) with integral zero: isolate $c$ in partition intervals of arbitrarily small total length, making the upper sum arbitrarily small while the lower sum stays zero. Yet $f(c)=1$. This is the [single-point changes preserve Riemann integrals](../../../../../../single-point-changes-preserve-riemann-integrals.md) phenomenon and supplies the counterexample.
