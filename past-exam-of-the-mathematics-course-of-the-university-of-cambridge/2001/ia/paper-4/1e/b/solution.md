<h1 id="1e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**No such set exists.** If $R$ had precisely the [sets](../../../../../../set-split.md) $x$ satisfying $x\notin x$ as its elements, applying that membership criterion to $R$ itself would give

$$
R\in R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\notin R,
$$

a [contradiction](../../../../../../contradiction.md). This is [Russell's paradox](../../../../../../russell-s-paradox.md). It rules out this unrestricted universal collection; a [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) whose elements happen not to contain themselves is perfectly possible, for example the [empty set](../../../../../../empty-set.md).
