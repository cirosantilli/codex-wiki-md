<h1 id="1e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Suppose $F:X\to\mathcal P(X)$ were a [surjection](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) onto the [power set](../../../../../../power-set.md) of the [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $X$. Form the [subset](../../../../../../subset.md)

$$
D=\{x\in X:x\notin F(x)\}.
$$

Surjectivity would give $a\in X$ with $F(a)=D$. But then $a\in D$ holds exactly when $a\notin F(a)=D$, a [contradiction](../../../../../../contradiction.md). Hence no such [surjection](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) exists, and in particular **there is no bijection between a set and its power set**. This is the diagonal construction in [Cantor's theorem](../../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md); it also covers the [empty set](../../../../../../empty-set.md), whose [power set](../../../../../../power-set.md) has one element.
