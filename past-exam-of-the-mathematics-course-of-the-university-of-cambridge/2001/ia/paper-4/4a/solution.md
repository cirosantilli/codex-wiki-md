<h1 id="4a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4A](../4a.md)

Put $M=m_1+m_2$, define the [center of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) $\mathbf R=(m_1\mathbf x_1+m_2\mathbf x_2)/M$, and let $\mathbf r=\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2$ be relative [position](../../../../../position.md). Adding the two [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) equations gives

$$
M\ddot{\mathbf R}=\mathbf F_1+\mathbf F_2=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf R(t)=\mathbf R(0)+t\dot{\mathbf R}(0).}
$$

The [center of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) has constant [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md), by conservation of total [momentum](../../../../../momentum.md).

Subtracting the particle [accelerations](../../../../../acceleration.md) instead gives

$$
\ddot{\mathbf r}=\frac{\mathbf f(\mathbf r)}{m_1}+\frac{\mathbf f(\mathbf r)}{m_2}
=\frac{\mathbf f(\mathbf r)}\mu,
\qquad
\boxed{\mu\ddot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf f(\mathbf r),\quad
\mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}.}
$$

Thus relative motion is a one-particle problem with the [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$, while [center-of-mass motion](../../../../../center-of-mass-motion.md) is uniform. The same [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) appears in the [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) decomposition $T=\tfrac12M|\dot{\mathbf R}|^2+\tfrac12\mu|\dot{\mathbf r}|^2$.
