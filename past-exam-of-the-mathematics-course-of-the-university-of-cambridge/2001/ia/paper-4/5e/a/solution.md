<h1 id="5e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$, the nonzero [residue classes](../../../../../../residue-class.md) form the multiplicative [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of the [finite field](../../../../../../finite-field.md) $\mathbb F_p$. Every class has a unique [multiplicative inverse](../../../../../../multiplicative-inverse.md). A class equals its own inverse precisely when $x^2=1$, so $(x-1)(x+1)=0$ in the [field](../../../../../../field.md) and $x=1$ or $x=-1$.

For an [odd prime](../../../../../../odd-prime.md), pair each remaining class with its distinct inverse. Each pair contributes one to the product, leaving

$$
\boxed{(p-1)!\equiv1\cdot(-1)=-1\pmod p.}
$$

For $p=2$, the same congruence is $1\equiv-1\pmod2$. This proves [Wilson's theorem](../../../../../../wilson-s-theorem.md).
