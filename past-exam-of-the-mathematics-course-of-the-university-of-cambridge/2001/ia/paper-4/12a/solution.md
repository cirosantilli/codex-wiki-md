<h1 id="12a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [12A](../12a.md)

A coaxial shell of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$ has [mass](../../../../../mass.md) $dm=2\pi\rho l r\,dr$. Integrating squared radius against [mass](../../../../../mass.md), the [moment of inertia of a uniform solid cylinder](../../../../../moment-of-inertia-of-a-uniform-solid-cylinder.md) about its axis is

$$
I=\int_0^a r^2dm=2\pi\rho l\int_0^a r^3dr
=\frac{\pi\rho l a^4}2=\boxed{\frac12Ma^2},
\qquad M=\pi\rho a^2l.
$$

On the smooth incline, no tangential contact [force](../../../../../force.md) produces spin, so the initially stationary cylinder simply slides with [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) $g\sin\theta$. Therefore $t_{\rm slide}=\sqrt{2L/(g\sin\theta)}$.

For [rolling without slipping](../../../../../rolling-without-slipping.md), let $F$ be uphill [static friction](../../../../../static-friction.md) and $A$ the downward translational [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md). [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md), the rotational [torque](../../../../../torque.md) equation, and the contact constraint give

$$
MA=Mg\sin\theta-F,\qquad
Fa=I\alpha,\qquad A=a\alpha.
$$

With $I=Ma^2/2$, these give $F=MA/2$ and $A=2g\sin\theta/3$. The [rolling-time factor on a straight incline](../../../../../rolling-time-factor-on-a-straight-incline.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{t_{\rm roll}=\sqrt{\frac{3L}{g\sin\theta}}
=\sqrt{\frac32}\,t_{\rm slide}.}
$$

The required friction is $Mg\sin\theta/3$; the stated no-slip motion presupposes sufficient [static friction](../../../../../static-friction.md). At an instantaneously stationary contact point it does no work. Gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md) is shared between translational and rotational [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), explaining why rolling accelerates more slowly than the smooth slide.
