<h1 id="3b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3B](../3b.md)

A field is irrotational when $\nabla\times F=0$. In $\mathbb R^3$ define

$$
V(x)=-\int_\gamma F\cdot d\ell,
$$

where $\gamma$ is any path from $x_0$ to $x$. The difference between two choices is the [circulation](../../../../../circulation-physics.md) around a closed loop. By the [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) it is the flux of $\nabla\times F$ through a spanning surface and hence zero. Thus $V$ is well-defined, $V(x_0)=0$, and [differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) gives $F=-\nabla V$.

If $\nabla\times A_0=B$, every solution is

$$
A=A_0+\nabla\chi
$$

for an arbitrary [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) $\chi$, since the difference is curl-free. A necessary condition is $\nabla\cdot B=0$, because the divergence of a curl vanishes.
