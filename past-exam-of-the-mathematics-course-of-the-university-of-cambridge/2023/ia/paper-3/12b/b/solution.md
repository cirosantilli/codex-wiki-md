<h1 id="12b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The measure, Gaussian factor, $r^{-1}$, coordinates, and [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) all transform covariantly under rotations, so $T_{ijkl}$ is an [isotropic tensor integral](../../../../../../isotropic-tensor-integral.md). Symmetry gives $\beta=\gamma$.

Contracting $i=j$ and using $\nabla^2(1/r)=0$ away from the origin gives

$$
3\alpha+2\beta=0.
$$

Next contract $i=k$ and $j=l$. Since

$$
\sum_i x_i\partial_i\partial_j(r^{-1})=\frac{2x_j}{r^3},
$$

one obtains

$$
3\alpha+12\beta
=2\int_{\mathbb R^3}\frac{e^{-r^2}}r,dV
=4\pi.
$$

Solving yields

$$
\boxed{\alpha=-\frac{4\pi}{15},\qquad
\beta=\gamma=\frac{2\pi}{5}}.
$$
