<h1 id="10c/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

With signature $(+,-,-,-)$, the four-momenta are

$$
P_{\rm massive}=m\gamma(c,v),
\qquad
P_\gamma=\frac{\hbar\omega}{c}(1,e).
$$

In the rest frame of a future timelike $P_1$, one has $P_1=(Mc,0)$ and $P_2^0>0$, so $P_1\cdot P_2=McP_2^0>0$; Lorentz invariance proves the assertion in every frame.

The [impossibility of photon decay into two massive particles](../../../../../../impossibility-of-photon-decay-into-two-massive-particles.md) follows because a photon has squared [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) zero. If it decayed into an electron and positron, conservation would give

$$
0=(P_-+P_+)^2=2m^2c^2+2P_-\cdot P_+>0,
$$

a contradiction.
