↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**No.** The [Darboux theorem](../../../../../../darboux-s-theorem-analysis.md) says that every [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) has the intermediate-value property. Choose $\epsilon<1/3$. In a deleted neighborhood of $a$, the assumed [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) puts $\phi(x)$ within $\epsilon$ of $\phi(a)+1$. Darboux's theorem applied between $a$ and any such $x$ would require values near $\phi(a)+1/2$, which that neighborhood excludes. This contradiction shows that $\phi$ cannot be a [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md).
