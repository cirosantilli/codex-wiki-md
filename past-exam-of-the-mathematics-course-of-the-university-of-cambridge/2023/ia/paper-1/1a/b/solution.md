The [Fourier transform of a derivative](../../../../../../fourier-transform-of-a-derivative.md) gives

$$
\widetilde{p'}(k)=ik\widetilde p(k).
$$

Since $p'=g$,

$$
\boxed{\widetilde p(k)=\frac{\widetilde g(k)}{ik}},
\qquad k\neq0.
$$

An arbitrary additive constant in $p$ affects only the zero-frequency [Dirac delta function](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) when transforms are interpreted as distributions.

The values of $\log i$ are $i(\pi/2+2\pi n)$. Hence

$$
i^{-2i}=\exp\{-2i\log i\}
=\exp((4n+1)\pi),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

These are infinitely many distinct positive real numbers.
