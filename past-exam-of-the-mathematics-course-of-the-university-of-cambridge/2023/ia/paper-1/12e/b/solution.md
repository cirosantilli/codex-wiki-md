<h1 id="12e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Changing variables gives

$$
\frac1h\int_a^x(f(t+h)-f(t))dt
=\frac1h\left(\int_x^{x+h}f(s)ds-\int_a^{a+h}f(s)ds\right).
$$

The average of a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) over $[y,y+h]$ tends to $f(y)$ as $h\to0$. The two terms therefore tend to $f(x)$ and $f(a)$, proving the stated [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md).
