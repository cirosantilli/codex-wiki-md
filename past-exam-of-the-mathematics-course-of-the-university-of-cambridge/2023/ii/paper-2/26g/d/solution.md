**No.** For sufficiently smooth curves,

$$
Q(\gamma,s)=\kappa_\gamma'(s)
$$

is unchanged by proper Euclidean motions and transforms correctly under translations of the arc-length parameter, so it is a [pointwise Euclidean invariant of a curve](../../../../../../pointwise-euclidean-invariant-of-a-curve.md). It is not determined by the two numbers $\kappa(s)$ and $\tau(s)$.

For a concrete comparison near $s=0$, use the [plane curve reconstructed from curvature](../../../../../../plane-curve-reconstructed-from-curvature.md) construction with

$$
\kappa_1(s)=1,
\qquad
\kappa_2(s)=1+s.
$$

The resulting unit-speed planar curves both have $\kappa(0)=1$ and $\tau(0)=0$, but their curvature derivatives at zero are respectively $0$ and $1$. Thus no single function $f$ can satisfy $Q(\gamma,s)=f(\kappa(s),\tau(s))$ for all curves.

Indeed, the covariance conditions alone even permit nonlocal examples such as $Q(\gamma,s)=\kappa_\gamma(s+1)$. This is the distinction recorded by [pointwise Euclidean invariants need not depend only on current curvature and torsion](../../../../../../pointwise-euclidean-invariants-need-not-depend-only-on-current-curvature-and-torsion.md).
