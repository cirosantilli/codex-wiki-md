<h1 id="40c/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The reflected P-wave is propagating only while its normal wavenumber is real, equivalently while $|\sin\phi|\leq1$. Since

$$
\sin\phi=\frac{c_P}{c_S}\sin\theta,
$$

it is [evanescent](../../../../../../../evanescent-wave.md) when

$$
\boxed{\sin\theta>\frac{c_S}{c_P}}.
$$

Put

$$
s=\frac{c_P}{c_S}\sin\theta>1,
\qquad
q=\sqrt{s^2-1},
\qquad
\cos\phi=iq.
$$

Then its spatial factor is

$$
e^{ik_Psx+k_Pqz},
$$

which decays as $z\to-\infty$. The reflected SV amplitude becomes

$$
R=-\frac{iq\cos\theta-s\sin\theta}
{iq\cos\theta+s\sin\theta}.
$$

The numerator and denominator have equal [complex modulus](../../../../../../../modulus.md), so

$$
\boxed{|R|=1}.
$$

**Thus evanescent mode conversion changes the reflected SV phase but not its amplitude, as described by [Unit-modulus SV reflection with evanescent P conversion](../../../../../../../unit-modulus-sv-reflection-with-evanescent-p-conversion.md).**
