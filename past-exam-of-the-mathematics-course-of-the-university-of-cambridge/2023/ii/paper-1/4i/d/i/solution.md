<h1 id="4i/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take

$$
G'=(\{a,b\},\{S,A,B\},P',S),
$$

where

$$
P'=\{S\to ABA,\ A\to AB,\ B\to BA,\ A\to a,\ B\to b\}.
$$

This grammar is in [Weak Chomsky normal form](../../../../../../../weak-chomsky-normal-form.md). In the original grammar, $C$ occurs only in $S\to AC$ and has the sole production $C\to BA$. Thus every use of

$$
S\to AC\to ABA
$$

is replaced in $G'$ by $S\to ABA$, while every later production is unchanged. Conversely, the ternary production can always be expanded into those two original steps. Therefore $G'$ is equivalent to $G$ in the sense of an [equivalent grammar](../../../../../../../equivalent-grammar.md).
