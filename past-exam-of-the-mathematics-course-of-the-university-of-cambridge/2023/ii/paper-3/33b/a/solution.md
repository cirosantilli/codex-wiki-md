<h1 id="33b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For distinguishable particles with one-particle [Hilbert spaces](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md) $\mathcal H_1,\ldots,\mathcal H_N$, the [multiparticle quantum state](../../../../../../multiparticle-quantum-state.md) belongs to

$$
\mathcal H_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_N.
$$

A product of one-particle states gives a product vector, and arbitrary linear combinations give entangled states. A noninteracting Hamiltonian is the sum of one-particle Hamiltonians acting on their respective tensor factors, so product-state energies add.

For two identical particles, the [particle exchange operator](../../../../../../particle-exchange-operator.md) $P_{12}$ swaps every degree of freedom. Since $P_{12}^2=I$, its eigenvalues are $\pm1$. Particles whose allowed total states satisfy

$$
P_{12}|\Psi\rangle=+|\Psi\rangle
$$

are [bosons](../../../../../../boson.md), while those satisfying

$$
P_{12}|\Psi\rangle=-|\Psi\rangle
$$

are [fermions](../../../../../../fermion.md). Thus bosonic states are symmetric and fermionic states antisymmetric under interchange. The same condition applies to every transposition in a system of more than two [identical particles](../../../../../../indistinguishable-particles.md).
