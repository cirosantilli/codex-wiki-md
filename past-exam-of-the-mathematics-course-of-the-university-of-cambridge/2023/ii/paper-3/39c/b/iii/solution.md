<h1 id="39c/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $V<U$,

$$
\psi'(k)=V-U-k^8<0
$$

for every real $k$, so there is no real stationary point. The ray $x=Vt$ travels more slowly than the minimum group velocity $U$ and receives no leading stationary-phase wave packet. Under the usual smoothness and decay assumptions on $A$, the [nonstationary oscillatory integral](../../../../../../../nonstationary-oscillatory-integral.md) can be integrated by parts repeatedly, so

$$
\boxed{\phi(Vt,t)=O(t^{-N})}
$$

for every fixed $N$ for which the needed derivatives are integrable. In particular it is asymptotically much smaller than the $t^{-1/2}$ signal when $V>U$.
