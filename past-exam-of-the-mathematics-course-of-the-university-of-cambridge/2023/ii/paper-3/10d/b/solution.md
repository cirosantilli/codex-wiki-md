<h1 id="10d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Fermat-Euler theorem](../../../../../../euler-s-theorem.md) gives

$$
a^{\varphi(N)}\equiv1\pmod N,
$$

so the [multiplicative order](../../../../../../multiplicative-order.md) $r=\operatorname{ord}_N(a)$ exists. For every [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $k$,

$$
f(k+r)=a^{k+r}\equiv a^ka^r\equiv a^k=f(k)\pmod N.
$$

Thus the modular exponential is [periodic](../../../../../../periodic-modular-exponentiation.md) with period $r$. If $t>0$ were a smaller period, then $f(t)=f(0)$ would give $a^t\equiv1\pmod N$, contradicting the minimality of $r$. Hence $r$ is its least positive period.
