<h1 id="1g/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose that the positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $n$ is a [decisive number](../../../../../../decisive-number.md). For every [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p<\sqrt n$, the [composite number](../../../../../../composite-number.md) $p^2$ satisfies $1<p^2<n$. It therefore cannot be [coprime](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md) to $n$, so $p\mid n$.

The product of all [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md) below $\sqrt n$ consequently divides $n$. Taking [natural logarithms](../../../../../../natural-logarithm.md) gives

$$
\log n\geq\sum_{p<\sqrt n}\log p.
$$

Changing the strict endpoint can remove at most one prime term. By part b, for every sufficiently large $n$ the right-hand side is at least, for example, $\sqrt n/13$. This is impossible for sufficiently large $n$, since

$$
\frac{\sqrt n}{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

**Thus every [decisive number](../../../../../../decisive-number.md) lies below one fixed bound, and only finitely many [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) can do so.**
