<h1 id="38c/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The displacement must have small relative amplitude and small slope,

$$
\frac{|\eta|}{h}\ll1,\qquad |\eta_x|\ll1.
$$

Write the potentials as perturbations of the base flow,

$$
\phi_\pm=Ux+\varphi_\pm,\qquad \phi_0=\varphi_0,
$$

with $|\nabla\varphi_\pm|/U$ and $|\nabla\varphi_0|/U$ small. A [linearization](../../../../../../linearization.md) evaluates the conditions at the unperturbed lines $y=\pm h$ and discards products of perturbations. At $y=h$ this gives

$$
\varphi_{+,y}=\eta_t+U\eta_x,\qquad
\varphi_{0,y}=\eta_t,\qquad
\varphi_{+,t}+U\varphi_{+,x}=\varphi_{0,t},
$$

whereas at $y=-h$ it gives

$$
\varphi_{-,y}=-\eta_t-U\eta_x,\qquad
\varphi_{0,y}=-\eta_t,\qquad
\varphi_{-,t}+U\varphi_{-,x}=\varphi_{0,t}.
$$

The perturbation potentials remain [harmonic functions](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md), and their gradients decay at the corresponding far fields.
