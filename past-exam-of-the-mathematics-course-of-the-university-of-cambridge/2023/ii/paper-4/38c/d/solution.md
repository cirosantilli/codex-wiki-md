<h1 id="38c/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The linearized equations are homogeneous and have constant coefficients in $x$ and $t$. Consequently, spatial [Fourier modes](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md) evolve independently, and the [superposition principle](../../../../../../superposition-principle.md) reconstructs arbitrary sufficiently regular initial disturbances from them. It is therefore enough to seek the [normal modes](../../../../../../normal-mode.md)

$$
\eta(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}\{\eta_0e^{ikx+\sigma t}\}.
$$

Here the real $k$ is the [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md), $\sigma$ is the possibly complex temporal exponent, and taking the [real part](../../../../../../real-part.md) produces a physical displacement.
