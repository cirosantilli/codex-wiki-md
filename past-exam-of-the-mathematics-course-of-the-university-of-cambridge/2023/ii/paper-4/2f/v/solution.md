↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

**No.** Take

$$
B=[0,1]\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb R^2.
$$

It contains more than one point and has the [fixed-point property of a closed interval](../../../../../../fixed-point-property-of-a-closed-interval.md), but it is not homeomorphic to the closed unit disc. Indeed, deleting any point $(t,0)$ with $0<t<1$ disconnects $B$, so such a point is a [cut point](../../../../../../cut-point.md). Deleting one point from a two-dimensional closed disc leaves a [path-connected space](../../../../../../path-connected-space.md). Since being a cut point is preserved by [homeomorphisms](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md), the two spaces cannot be homeomorphic. This gives the required [counterexample](../../../../../../counterexample.md).
