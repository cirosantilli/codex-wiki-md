<h1 id="2f/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The closed unit disc has the [fixed-point property](../../../../../../fixed-point-property.md) by the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](../../../../../../brouwer-fixed-point-theorem.md). The annulus admits the [fixed-point-free rotation of an annulus](../../../../../../fixed-point-free-rotation-of-an-annulus.md), for example

$$
R(x,y)=(-x,-y),
$$

which is continuous, maps the annulus to itself, and has no fixed point because the origin is not in the annulus. If the disc and annulus were [homeomorphic](../../../../../../local-homeomorphism.md), the [homeomorphism invariance of the fixed-point property](../../../../../../homeomorphism-invariance-of-the-fixed-point-property.md) would give the annulus the fixed-point property, a contradiction. Therefore they are not homeomorphic.
