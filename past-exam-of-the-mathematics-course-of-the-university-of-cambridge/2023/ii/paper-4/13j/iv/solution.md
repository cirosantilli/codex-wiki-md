↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Under $H_1$, the slope in this through-origin [linear regression](../../../../../../linear-regression-split.md) is $1$. Its observed [Wald statistic](../../../../../../wald-test.md) is

$$
t=\frac{1.15790-1}{0.08643}=1.83.
$$

The corresponding two-sided [p-value](../../../../../../p-value.md) is about $0.07$, so the fit does not give evidence against $H_1$ at the $5\%$ [significance level](../../../../../../significance-level.md).

The usual least-squares [standard error](../../../../../../standard-error.md) assumes constant conditional [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md). Goal counts are counts, and under the natural [Poisson distribution](../../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) model their variance equals their mean $x_i$, so observations with different expected goals are [heteroscedastic](../../../../../../heteroscedastic.md). The reported homoscedastic standard error is therefore doubtful; a heteroscedasticity-robust calculation or a suitable count model would be more credible.
