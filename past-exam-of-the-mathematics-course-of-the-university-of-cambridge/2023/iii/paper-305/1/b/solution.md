<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $P^2=m^2>0$, choose the future-pointing rest momentum $p^\mu=(m,0,0,0)$. Its [little group](../../../../../../little-group.md) is the rotation group $SO(3)$, or $SU(2)$ on the double cover. Its finite-dimensional unitary irreducible representations are labelled by

$$
j=0,\frac12,1,\frac32,\ldots
$$

and have dimension $2j+1$. These are the spin states of a massive particle.

For $P^2=0$, choose $p^\mu=(E,0,0,E)$. Its little group is the two-dimensional Euclidean group

$$
ISO(2)=SO(2)\ltimes\mathbb R^2.
$$

Nontrivial unitary action of the translation subgroup produces infinite-dimensional [continuous-spin representations](../../../../../../continuous-spin-representation.md). For the finite-helicity particles observed in ordinary relativistic field theory, that subgroup acts trivially, leaving a one-dimensional representation of rotations about $\mathbf p$. Such representations are labelled by helicity.
