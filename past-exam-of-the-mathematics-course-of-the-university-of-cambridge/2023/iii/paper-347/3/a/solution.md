<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [maximal radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion](../../../../../../maximal-radiative-efficiency-of-black-hole-accretion.md) is the fraction of rest-mass energy available if all binding energy released before capture escapes as radiation. In a Newtonian disk ending at $R_{\rm in}$,

$$
\eta_{\max}=\frac{GM}{2R_{\rm in}c^2},
$$

because a circular orbit has specific binding energy $GM/(2R)$. In relativity, $\eta_{\max}=1-E_{\rm ISCO}$. Black-hole spin moves the [innermost stable circular orbit](../../../../../../innermost-stable-circular-orbit.md) inward for prograde flow and outward for retrograde flow, increasing or decreasing this maximum respectively.

Since $L=\eta\dot M c^2$, a source of fixed luminosity requires $\dot M=L/(\eta c^2)$, while black-hole mass grows at approximately $(1-\eta)\dot M$. The actual [radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion](../../../../../../radiative-efficiency-of-black-hole-accretion.md) can lie below the maximum when energy is advected through the horizon or carried away mechanically. A low-density, optically thin [advection-dominated accretion flow](../../../../../../advection-dominated-accretion-flow.md) stores dissipated energy in ions, while a high-rate [slim accretion disk](../../../../../../slim-accretion-disk.md) traps photons and advects their energy inward; both are [radiatively inefficient flows](../../../../../../radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow.md).
