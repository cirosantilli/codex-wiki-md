<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $\mathcal R=k_B/(\mu m_u)$. At fixed density,

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\log P}{\partial\log T}\right)_\rho
=4-3\beta,
\qquad
\left(\frac{\partial\log P}{\partial\log\rho}\right)_T
=\beta.
$$

The specific internal energy is

$$
u=\frac32\mathcal RT+\frac{a_{\rm rad}T^4}{\rho}.
$$

Applying the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics.md) adiabatically gives

$$
\left(\frac{d\log T}{d\log\rho}\right)_s
=\frac{8-6\beta}{24-21\beta}.
$$

Combining these logarithmic derivatives yields

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_{\rm ad}(\beta)
=\frac{8-6\beta}{32-24\beta-3\beta^2}}.
$$

As gas pressure dominates,

$$
\boxed{\lim_{\beta\to1}\nabla_{\rm ad}=\frac25},
$$

the result for a monatomic perfect gas. In the radiation-pressure limit it tends to $1/4$.
