<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose for a contradiction that $b_0\ne0$. The [Eisenstein series](../../../../../../eisenstein-series.md) in the question has constant term $2\zeta(k)$, so

$$
h=g-\frac{b_0}{2\zeta(k)}G_k
$$

has zero constant term and is therefore a level-one [cusp form](../../../../../../cusp-form.md) of weight $k$. Part (b) gives the coefficient bound $[q^n]h=O(n^{k/2})$. Since $b_n=O(n^{k/2})$ as well, the displayed Fourier expansion of $G_k$ would imply

$$
\sigma_{k-1}(n)=O(n^{k/2}).
$$

Take $n=\ell$ through the [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md). Then

$$
\sigma_{k-1}(\ell)=1+\ell^{k-1},
$$

which cannot be $O(\ell^{k/2})$ because $k-1>k/2$ for $k\geq4$. Therefore $b_0=0$, so $g$ vanishes at the only cusp of $\Gamma(1)$ and belongs to $S_k(\Gamma(1))$. This is the [Fourier coefficient growth criterion for a level-one cusp form](../../../../../../fourier-coefficient-growth-criterion-for-a-level-one-cusp-form.md).
