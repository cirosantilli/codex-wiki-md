<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Matching the flat film to a cylindrical interface gives the local [parabolic lubrication gap](../../../../../../parabolic-lubrication-gap.md)

$$
h-h_0\sim\frac{(x-L)^2}{a}.
$$

The thickness changes by $O(h_0)$ across the transition, so

$$
\boxed{\delta\sim(ah_0)^{1/2}}.
$$

In the extensional-force equation, viscous and capillary terms have scales

$$
\frac{\mu h_0U}{\delta^2},
\qquad
\frac{\gamma h_0^2}{\delta^3}.
$$

Their balance gives

$$
\boxed{U\sim\frac\gamma\mu\left(\frac{h_0}{a}\right)^{1/2}}.
$$

The transition adjusts on time $\delta/U$, whereas the flat film drains on time $L/U$. Since $\delta\ll L$, the transition is quasi-steady. Its [thin-film mass flux](../../../../../../thin-film-mass-flux.md) therefore satisfies

$$
\boxed{uh\simeq Uh_0=\text{constant}}.
$$
