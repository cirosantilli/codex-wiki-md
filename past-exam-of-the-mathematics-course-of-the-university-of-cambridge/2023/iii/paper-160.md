## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 160](paper-160.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [symmetric group](../../../finite-group-theory.md#symmetric-group) acts transitively on the [Young tableaux](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-tableau) of a fixed shape. Moreover, $g e(t)=e(gt)$ for every permutation $g$, so every [polytabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid) is a translate of any fixed one. Hence the [Specht module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) is a [cyclic module](../../../module-theory.md#cyclic-module) generated by $e(t)$.

It remains to see that $e(t)\ne0$. In its expansion, the coefficient of the [tabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid) $\{t\}$ is one: if $\pi\in C(t)$ and $\{\pi t\}=\{t\}$, then $\pi\in R(t)\cap C(t)=\{1\}$. Thus the generator, and therefore the module, is nonzero.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Write the [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) as $\tau=rc$ with $r\in R(t)$ and $c\in C(t)$. If $x$ is not one of the two entries moved by $\tau$, then $r(c(x))=x$. The entries $x$ and $c(x)$ lie in one column of $t$, while $c(x)$ and $r(c(x))=x$ lie in one row. A [Young diagram](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#young-diagram) has only one cell at the intersection of a specified row and column, so $c(x)=x$ and then $r(x)=x$.

Consequently both $r$ and $c$ fix every entry outside the support of $\tau$. On the two remaining entries each is either the identity or their transposition. They cannot both transpose them, since then $rc=1$, and they cannot both be the identity. Exactly one of $r,c$ is therefore $\tau$, proving that $\tau$ lies in exactly one of $R(t)$ and $C(t)$.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

The transpositions in the row stabilizer number

$$
\sum_i\binom{\lambda_i}{2}
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(j-1),
$$

because a cell in column $j$ has $j-1$ cells before it in its row. Similarly, the transpositions in the column stabilizer number

$$
\sum_j\binom{\lambda'_j}{2}
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(i-1).
$$

Their difference is

$$
\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(j-i)
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}c_{i,j}(\lambda),
$$

the sum of the [Young-diagram cell contents](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#content-of-a-young-diagram-cell).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

Conjugation by a permutation merely permutes the transpositions. Their sum $C$ is therefore a [conjugacy class](../../../group-theory.md#conjugacy-class) sum and belongs to the [center of an associative algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#center-of-an-associative-algebra) $\mathbb C S_n$. Since the complex [Specht module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) is an [irreducible representation](../../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation), [Schur lemma](../../../representation-theory.md#schur-s-lemma) says that $C$ acts on it as a [scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar), say $c(\lambda)$.

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Compare the coefficient of $\{t\}$ in $C e(t)$. For a transposition $\tau$, a summand $\tau\pi\{t\}$ with $\pi\in C(t)$ equals $\{t\}$ exactly when $\tau\pi\in R(t)$, equivalently $\tau\in R(t)C(t)$. Part b(i) then leaves two cases: a row transposition contributes $+1$ through $\pi=1$, while a column transposition contributes $-1$ through $\pi=\tau$. Every other transposition contributes zero.

The coefficient is consequently the number of row transpositions minus the number of column transpositions, which part b(ii) identifies with $\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}c_{i,j}(\lambda)$. The coefficient of $\{t\}$ in $c(\lambda)e(t)$ is $c(\lambda)$, so

$$
\boxed{c(\lambda)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}c_{i,j}(\lambda).}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

In a [Young-diagram hook](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-of-a-young-diagram), the cell $(i,j)$ has $\lambda_i-j$ cells to its right and $\lambda'_j-i$ below it. Summing the arm lengths row by row and the leg lengths column by column gives

$$
\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\bigl((j-1)+(i-1)+1\bigr)
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(i+j-1).
$$

Adding the content $c_{i,j}=j-i$ turns the summand on the right into $2j-1$. Since $\sum_{j=1}^m(2j-1)=m^2$, summing each row proves

$$
\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(h_{i,j}+c_{i,j})
=\sum_i\lambda_i^2.
$$

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

The [conjugate partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#conjugate-partition) $\lambda'$ has the same hook lengths as $\lambda$ and the opposite contents. Applying part d(i) to both diagrams and adding yields

$$
\sum_i\bigl(\lambda_i^2+(\lambda'_i)^2\bigr)
=2\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}.
$$

Now sum the first identity of part d(i) over all partitions $\mu\vdash n$. Conjugation is a bijection on those partitions, so the total of $\sum_i(\mu'_i)^2$ equals the total of $\sum_i\mu_i^2$. Dividing the summed displayed identity by two gives

$$
\boxed{\sum_{\mu\vdash n}\sum_i\mu_i^2
=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mu}h_{i,j}(\mu).}
$$

<h4 id="1/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/d/iii)

We use [mathematical induction](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on $n$. The identity is immediate for the empty partition. Add a removable corner $(r,s)$ to a partition of $n-1$, and put $c=s-r$. Only the new hook and the hooks to its left in row $r$ or above it in column $s$ change. Each old affected hook length increases by one. If $S$ is the sum of those old hook lengths, direct substitution in the hook formula gives

$$
S=\sum_{j<s}(s-j+\lambda'_j-r)
+\sum_{i<r}(\lambda_i-s+r-i)
=n-\frac{r+s}{2}+\frac{(s-r)^2}{2}.
$$

There are $r+s-2$ affected old hooks, so the increase in the sum of squared hook lengths is

$$
1+(r+s-2)+2S=2n-1+c^2.
$$

The increase in $n^2+\sum c_{i,j}^2$ is likewise $n^2-(n-1)^2+c^2=2n-1+c^2$. The induction closes and proves

$$
\boxed{\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}^2
=n^2+\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}c_{i,j}^2.}
$$

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 160](paper-160.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule) states that if a permutation has a $k$-cycle and remaining cycle type $\rho$, then

$$
\chi^\lambda(k,\rho)
=\sum_H(-1)^{\operatorname{leg}(H)}\chi^{\lambda\setminus H}(\rho),
$$

where $H$ runs over the removable [rim hooks](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#rim-hook) of length $k$. Iterating removes rim hooks whose lengths are the cycle lengths, and the character value is the signed sum over all complete removal sequences.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

Let $s_i=(i\ i+1)$. Since $\mu-\mathrm{id}=s_i(\lambda-\mathrm{id})$, reindex the alternating sum defining $\psi^\mu$ by $\pi\mapsto s_i\pi$. This preserves all permutation-character terms and reverses every [sign of a permutation](../../../finite-group-theory.md#sign-of-a-permutation), because $\operatorname{sgn}(s_i\pi)=-\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)$. Hence $\psi^\mu=-\psi^\lambda$.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

For each $m$, the shifted sequence $\beta_{i,m+1}-\mathrm{id}$ is obtained from $\beta_{i,m}-\mathrm{id}$ by swapping the adjacent entries in positions $i+m$ and $i+m+1$. Part a(ii) therefore gives

$$
\psi^{\beta_{i,m+1}}=-\psi^{\beta_{i,m}}.
$$

This is the usual [character straightening](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#straightening-of-a-symmetric-group-character-indexed-by-a-composition) procedure: the distinguished entry $\alpha_i-i-k$ is moved successively to the right. Either it reaches the unique place that makes some $\beta_{i,m}$ a [partition of an integer](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#partition-of-an-integer), or it meets an equal shifted entry. Under the hypothesis the first alternative never occurs, so two entries of some $\beta_{i,m}-\mathrm{id}$ coincide. The corresponding alternating sum is fixed by swapping those entries but changes sign by part a(ii), and is therefore zero. Repeatedly applying the displayed relation gives $\psi^{\beta_{i,0}}=0$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Write $\chi=\chi^\lambda$. By the [Hook-length formula](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hook-length-formula),

$$
\frac{|S_n|}{\chi(1)}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}(\lambda).
$$

Suppose the [order of a group element](../../../group-theory.md#order-of-a-group-element) $g$ did not divide this product. Then for some [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) $p$, the highest [prime power](../../../number-theory.md#prime-power) $p^a$ dividing $|g|$ would not divide the hook product. One cycle of $g$ has length divisible by $p^a$, whereas no hook length of $\lambda$ is divisible by $p^a$. In particular there is no removable rim hook having that cycle length. Applying the [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule) first to this cycle gives $\chi(g)=0$, a contradiction. This is the [symmetric-group character co-degree vanishing criterion](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#symmetric-group-character-co-degree-vanishing-criterion), and its contrapositive proves

$$
\boxed{|g|\mid\frac{|S_n|}{\chi(1)}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Choose $g$ whose [disjoint permutation cycles](../../../finite-group-theory.md#disjoint-permutation-cycles) have lengths equal to the [principal hook lengths](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#principal-hook-lengths-of-a-partition) of $\lambda$. Those lengths are distinct and sum to $n$, so this is a permutation in $S_n$. In the iterated [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule), there is a unique complete sequence that removes the corresponding principal rim hooks. Its contribution is one sign, and hence the [Principal-hook character value of a symmetric group](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#principal-hook-character-value-of-a-symmetric-group) gives $\chi^\lambda(g)\in\{1,-1\}$.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/d/i)

Take $g$ to be an $n$-cycle. For $n\geq2$, the [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule) gives $\chi^{(n)}(g)=1$ and $\chi^{(n-1,1)}(g)=-1$; every remaining two-row diagram contains a $2\times2$ square and is not a rim hook, so its value at $g$ is zero. Therefore

$$
F(g)=1-(-1)=2\ne0.
$$

The exhibited cycle has length $k=n$, so $k\equiv n\pmod2$. For $n=1$, the sole character has value one at the identity and the same conclusion holds with $k=1$.

<h4 id="2/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/d/ii)

Apply the [Frobenius characteristic map](../../../combinatorics.md#frobenius-characteristic-map). For even $n$, the [Jacobi–Trudi identity](../../../combinatorics.md#jacobi-trudi-identity) and cancellation of consecutive terms give

$$
\operatorname{ch}(F)
=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^r h_{n-r}h_r
=[t^n]H(t)H(-t).
$$

The generating function for the [complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials](../../../combinatorics.md#complete-homogeneous-symmetric-polynomial) now gives

$$
H(t)H(-t)
=\exp\left(\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{p_jt^j}{j}\right)
 \exp\left(\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{(-1)^jp_jt^j}{j}\right)
=\exp\left(\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{p_{2j}t^{2j}}{j}\right).
$$

This expansion contains only products $p_\mu$ for which every part of $\mu$ is even. The coefficient of $p_\mu$ in $\operatorname{ch}(F)$ is $F(\mu)/z_\mu$, so $F(\mu)=0$ whenever the cycle type $\mu$ has an odd part. Equivalently, $F(g)=0$ whenever $g$ contains an odd cycle. This is the [Two-row alternating character cancellation](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#two-row-alternating-character-cancellation).

<h4 id="2/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

<h5 id="2/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/d/iii)

For a nonzero example, take $n=3$ and let $g$ be a transposition. The two terms are the trivial and standard characters of $S_3$, whose values at a transposition are respectively $1$ and $0$. Thus $F(g)=1$ although $g$ contains a cycle of even length.

For a zero example, take $n=5$ and again let $g$ be a transposition, of cycle type $(2,1^3)$. The values of $\chi^{(5)},\chi^{(4,1)},\chi^{(3,2)}$ there are $1,2,1$, respectively. Consequently

$$
\boxed{F(g)=1-2+1=0.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 160](paper-160.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

Use a [beta set](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#beta-set-of-a-partition) $X$ on an $e$-runner [partition abacus](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#abacus-of-a-partition). A hook of length divisible by $e$ corresponds to a bead $x$ and a gap $x-eh$ on the same runner. Divide both runner positions by $e$. They become a bead and a gap at distance $h$ in the runner partition $\lambda^{(s)}$, and hence determine a hook there. This gives the required [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection), with $|H|=e|f(H)|$.

Removing $H$ replaces the bead $x$ by the gap $x-eh$. On its runner this is exactly the bead move that removes $f(H)$, while every other runner is unchanged. Thus hook removal commutes with the construction and

$$
Q_e(\lambda\setminus H)=Q_e(\lambda)\setminus f(H).
$$

This is the [abacus divisible-hook correspondence](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hooks-divisible-by-the-abacus-modulus).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

For $\lambda=(6,5,3)$ use the three-bead [beta set](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#beta-set-of-a-partition) $\{8,6,3\}$. Its runners give the 3-[quotient of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-of-a-partition)

$$
Q_3(\lambda)=\bigl((1,1),\varnothing,(2)\bigr).
$$

The hook lengths divisible by three and their images are

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(H_{1,3}(\lambda))&=H_{1,1}(\lambda^{(2)}),&6&=3\cdot2,\\
f(H_{1,5}(\lambda))&=H_{1,2}(\lambda^{(2)}),&3&=3\cdot1,\\
f(H_{2,1}(\lambda))&=H_{1,1}(\lambda^{(0)}),&6&=3\cdot2,\\
f(H_{3,1}(\lambda))&=H_{2,1}(\lambda^{(0)}),&3&=3\cdot1.
\end{aligned}
$$

There are no others, as the complete hook-length rows are $(8,7,6,4,3,1)$, $(6,5,4,2,1)$, and $(3,2,1)$.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

**True.** A hook length divisible by $ef$ is divisible by $e$. If $\lambda$ is its own [Core of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-of-a-partition) $C_e(\lambda)$, it has no such hook, so it is also its own $ef$-core.

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

**False.** Let $\lambda=(3)$, $e=2$, and $f=3$. Then

$$
C_2(C_3(\lambda))=C_2(\varnothing)=\varnothing,
\qquad
C_3(C_2(\lambda))=C_3((1))=(1).
$$

**Thus core operations for different moduli need not commute.**

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

**True.** On an $f$-runner [partition abacus](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#abacus-of-a-partition), taking the $e$-quotient of each runner refines its positions by their residue modulo $e$. The resulting $ef$ runners are indexed by pairs of residues. Ordering those pairs may differ from the conventional residue order modulo $ef$, but only by a permutation. Hence $Q_e(Q_f(\lambda))$ is a permutation of $Q_{ef}(\lambda)$.

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

**True.** Slide beads upward separately in each of the refined residue classes modulo $ef$. Grouping the refined runners first by residue modulo $f$ and then packing each component modulo $e$ produces $C_e(Q_f(\lambda))$. Packing modulo $ef$ before forgetting the refinement produces $Q_f(C_{ef}(\lambda))$. These are the same runner configurations, so

$$
\boxed{C_e(Q_f(\lambda))=Q_f(C_{ef}(\lambda)).}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Apply the [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#murnaghan-nakayama-rule) successively to the $w$ disjoint $e$-cycles. A complete term requires a sequence of $w$ removable $e$-hooks. If $w>w_e(\lambda)$, no such sequence exists after the [Weight of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#weight-of-a-partition) is exhausted, so the character value is zero.

Suppose $w=w_e(\lambda)$. Every complete sequence ends at the [Core of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#core-of-a-partition) $C_e(\lambda)$. Under the [abacus divisible-hook correspondence](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#hooks-divisible-by-the-abacus-modulus), a removal chooses one cell from one component $\lambda^{(i)}$ of the [quotient of a partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#quotient-of-a-partition). The choices of which runner is used occur in

$$
\binom{w}{|\lambda^{(0)}|,\ldots,|\lambda^{(e-1)}|}
$$

orders. Within runner $i$, the signed complete removal sum is the degree $\chi^{\lambda^{(i)}}(1)$, and all inter-runner removal orders have the common [Sign of an abacus hook-removal sequence](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#sign-of-an-abacus-hook-removal-sequence) $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. The remaining permutation $\gamma$ acts on the core, giving

$$
\boxed{\chi^\lambda(\rho\gamma)
=\varepsilon
\binom{w}{|\lambda^{(0)}|,\ldots,|\lambda^{(e-1)}|}
\chi^{C_e(\lambda)}(\gamma)
\prod_{i=0}^{e-1}\chi^{\lambda^{(i)}}(1).}
$$

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 160](paper-160.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Let $b_t$ be the [Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#column-antisymmetrizer-of-a-young-tableau). For a tabloid $\{u\}$, if two entries from one column of $t$ lie in one row of $u$, their column transposition fixes $\{u\}$ and pairs every term of $b_t\{u\}$ with its negative. Thus $b_t\{u\}=0$.

Otherwise each row of $u$ meets each column of $t$ at most once. Matching entries within columns then gives a column permutation $c\in C(t)$ for which $\{cu\}=\{t\}$. Reindexing the antisymmetrizer gives $b_t\{u\}=\operatorname{sgn}(c)e(t)$. Hence every basis tabloid maps into $\mathbb F e(t)$, while $b_t\{t\}=e(t)$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{b_tM^\lambda=\mathbb F e(t).}
$$

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

<h5 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/b/i)

The coefficient $\langle e(t),\omega\rangle$ is nonzero exactly when every column of $t$ meets every row of $\omega$ in at most one entry. Permuting equal-length rows of $\omega$ preserves this condition, and the same holds with $u$ in place of $t$. Therefore membership in $A_{tu}$ is constant on each class of the stated [equivalence relation](../../../set-theory.md#equivalence-relation), so $A_{tu}$ is a union of equivalence classes.

<h4 id="4/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

<h5 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/b/ii)

Each equivalence class has size $\prod_{j=1}^n a_j!$, because its $a_j$ rows of length $j$ may be permuted freely. Swapping two length-$j$ rows multiplies each of $\langle e(t),\omega\rangle$ and $\langle e(u),\omega\rangle$ by the same sign $(-1)^j$, so their product is constant on the class. Since

$$
\langle e(t),e(u)\rangle
=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega^\lambda}
\langle e(t),\omega\rangle\langle e(u),\omega\rangle,
$$

the contribution of every nonzero class is a multiple of $\prod_j a_j!$. The whole inner product is therefore such a multiple.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

<h5 id="4/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/c/i)

The partition is $p$-regular precisely when

$$
a_j<p\qquad\text{for every }j,
$$

that is, when no part occurs $p$ or more times. This is the definition of a [regular partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#regular-partition) in [characteristic of a field](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-of-a-field) $p$.

<h4 id="4/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

<h5 id="4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/c/ii)

If some $a_j\geq p$, then $a_j!=0$ in $\mathbb F$. Part b(ii) makes every pairing $\langle e(t),e(u)\rangle$ zero, so the [Tabloid bilinear form](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#tabloid-bilinear-form) vanishes identically on $S^\lambda$ and $S^\lambda\subseteq(S^\lambda)^\perp$.

Conversely, if $\lambda$ is $p$-regular, every $a_j!$ is nonzero. Fact 1 supplies tableaux $t,u$ with

$$
\langle e(t),e(u)\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^n(a_j!)^j\ne0.
$$

**Thus the restriction of the form is not identically zero and $S^\lambda\not\subseteq(S^\lambda)^\perp$.**

<h3 id="4/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

<h5 id="4/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/d/i)

Because $\lambda$ is $p$-regular, Fact 1 gives a tableau $u$ for which

$$
\alpha:=\langle e(t),e(u)\rangle
=\prod_{j=1}^n(a_j!)^j\ne0.
$$

Part a, applied linearly to the tabloid expansion of $e(u)$, gives

$$
\boxed{b_t e(u)=\langle e(t),e(u)\rangle e(t)=\alpha e(t).}
$$

<h4 id="4/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

<h5 id="4/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/d/ii)

The nonzero homomorphism $\theta$ cannot kill $e(t)$, because the [Specht module](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#specht-module) is generated by the translates of this [polytabloid](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#polytabloid). Equivariance and part d(i) therefore give

$$
b_t\theta(e(u))=\theta(b_t e(u))=\alpha\theta(e(t))\ne0.
$$

**Thus $b_t$ acts nontrivially on $M^\mu/V$, and hence on $M^\mu$. Some $\mu$-tabloid $\{v\}$ must satisfy $b_t\{v\}\ne0$. Fact 2 now says that $\lambda$ dominates $\mu$ in the [dominance order on partitions](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#dominance-order-on-partitions).**

<h4 id="4/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

<h5 id="4/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/d/iii)

Assume $\lambda=\mu$. Part a says that the image of $b_t$ on $M^\lambda/V$ is contained in the one-dimensional space spanned by $e(t)+V$. The identity from part d(ii) then shows that $\theta(e(t))$ itself is a scalar multiple of $e(t)+V$.

Apply this with $V=S^\lambda\cap(S^\lambda)^\perp$ and with $\theta$ obtained by composing the quotient map $S^\lambda\to D^\lambda$ with any endomorphism of the [Simple symmetric-group module from a regular partition](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#simple-symmetric-group-module-from-a-regular-partition) $D^\lambda$. Every endomorphism is scalar on the generating polytabloid and hence on all its translates. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb F}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F S_n}(D^\lambda)=1.}
$$

<h3 id="4/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#4/e)

If the two displayed simple quotients $D^\alpha$ and $D^\beta$ are isomorphic, compose the quotient map $S^\alpha\to D^\alpha$ with an isomorphism to $D^\beta\subseteq M^\beta/(S^\beta\cap(S^\beta)^\perp)$. Part d(ii) gives $\alpha\succeq\beta$. Applying the inverse isomorphism gives $\beta\succeq\alpha$. Antisymmetry of the [dominance order on partitions](../../../representation-theory-of-the-symmetric-group.md#dominance-order-on-partitions) forces $\alpha=\beta$. Conversely, equality of the partitions makes the quotients identical, hence isomorphic.
