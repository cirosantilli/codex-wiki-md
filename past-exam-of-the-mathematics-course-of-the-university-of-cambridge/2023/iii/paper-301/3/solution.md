↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [Dirac spinor](../../../../../dirac-spinor.md) representation is the four-dimensional representation $(\tfrac12,0)\oplus(0,\tfrac12)$ of the connected [Lorentz group](../../../../../lorentz-group.md). It is characterized by

$$
S[\Lambda]^{-1}\gamma^\mu S[\Lambda]
=\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}\gamma^\nu.
$$

For

$$
\Lambda=\exp\!\left(\frac12\Omega_{\rho\sigma}M^{\rho\sigma}\right),
$$

one may take

$$
S[\Lambda]
=\exp\!\left(\frac18\Omega_{\rho\sigma}[\gamma_\rho,\gamma_\sigma]\right),
$$

with the signs of $\Omega_{\rho\sigma}$ fixed by the displayed vector-representation convention. This formula follows by differentiating the covariance relation and using the [Clifford algebra](../../../../../clifford-algebra.md) $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$; moving both gamma indices upstairs changes the apparent parameter signs according to the metric.

Use the [Weyl representation of the gamma matrices](../../../../../weyl-representation-of-the-gamma-matrices.md),

$$
\gamma^0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\I_2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\gamma^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^i\\-\sigma^i&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
