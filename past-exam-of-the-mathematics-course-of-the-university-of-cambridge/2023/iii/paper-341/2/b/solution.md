<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Every member of the family has order at least three, so it is [consistent](../../../../../../consistency-of-a-numerical-method.md). By the [Dahlquist equivalence theorem](../../../../../../dahlquist-equivalence-theorem.md), convergence is therefore equivalent to [zero-stability](../../../../../../zero-stability.md), which is characterized by the [root condition for a multistep method](../../../../../../root-condition-for-a-multistep-method.md).

The roots of $\rho$ are $1$ and $a$. They lie in the closed unit disk exactly when $|a|\leq1$. At $a=1$, however, the unit root $1$ is repeated, whereas at $a=-1$ the distinct unit roots $1$ and $-1$ are both simple. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{the method is convergent exactly for }-1\leq a<1.}
$$
