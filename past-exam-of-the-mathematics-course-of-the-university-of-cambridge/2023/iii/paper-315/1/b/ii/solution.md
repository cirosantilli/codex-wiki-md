<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A homogeneous interpretation assigns the planet a [brightness temperature](../../../../../../../brightness-temperature.md) $T_b$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{
B_\nu(T_b)=fB_\nu(T_1)+(1-f)B_\nu(T_2),
\qquad f=\sin^2\theta_T}.
$$

At a fixed wavelength let $x=hc/(\lambda k_B)$ and

$$
S=\frac{f}{e^{x/T_1}-1}
+\frac{1-f}{e^{x/T_2}-1}.
$$

Inverting the [Planck law](../../../../../../../planck-s-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{T_b=\frac{x}{\log(1+S^{-1})}}.
$$

At $\lambda=20\,\mu{\rm m}$, $x=719.4\,{\rm K}$. With $\theta_T=\pi/4$, $T_1=1500\,{\rm K}$, and $T_2=1000\,{\rm K}$, one obtains

$$
\boxed{T_b\simeq1251\,{\rm K}}.
$$

The [Rayleigh-Jeans law](../../../../../../../rayleigh-jeans-law.md) would give the nearly identical area-weighted estimate $1250\,{\rm K}$.
