<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose $u$ were positive somewhere. Since it tends to zero at infinity and to $-\infty$ at its vortex zero, it would attain a positive interior maximum away from the origin. At such a maximum the [second-derivative test](../../../../../../second-derivative-test.md) gives $\Delta u\leq0$, whereas the smooth Taubes equation gives

$$
\Delta u=e^u-1>0.
$$

This contradiction with the [maximum principle for subharmonic functions](../../../../../../maximum-principle-for-subharmonic-functions.md) proves

$$
\boxed{u\leq0\quad\text{on }\mathbb R^2}.
$$

**Consequently the Higgs magnitude of a vortex satisfies $|\phi|=e^{u/2}\leq1$ everywhere.**
