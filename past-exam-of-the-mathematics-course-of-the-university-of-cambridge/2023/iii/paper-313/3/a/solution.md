<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On an oriented Euclidean four-space with its metric volume form, the [Hodge star operator](../../../../../../hodge-star-operator.md) is defined by

$$
\alpha\wedge{}^\star\beta
=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\operatorname{vol}
$$

for forms $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of the same degree. In $n$ Euclidean dimensions it satisfies

$$
{}^\star({}^\star\alpha)=(-1)^{p(n-p)}\alpha
\qquad(\alpha\in\Lambda^p).
$$

Hence on two-forms in four dimensions,

$$
\boxed{{}^\star{}^\star=1\quad\text{on }\Lambda^2(\mathbb R^4)}.
$$

The normalization of $\operatorname{vol}$ as the metric volume form is essential; reversing its orientation reverses a single Hodge star but leaves its square unchanged.
