<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The matter part of the [massless closed-string vertex operator](../../../../../../massless-closed-string-vertex-operator.md) is

$$
V_\epsilon=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}:\!\partial X^\mu\bar\partial X^\nu e^{ik\cdot X}\!:,
\qquad k^2=0,
\qquad k^\mu\epsilon_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}k^\nu=0.
$$

Its symmetric trace-free polarization is the graviton, while its antisymmetric polarization is the [Kalb–Ramond field](../../../../../../kalb-ramond-field.md), or B-field. The [mass-shell condition](../../../../../../string-mass-shell-condition.md) $k^2=0$ and transversality make $V_\epsilon$ a [primary operator](../../../../../../primary-field.md) of [conformal weights](../../../../../../conformal-weight.md) $(1,1)$, as required for an [integrated string vertex operator](../../../../../../integrated-string-vertex-operator.md).

The graviton polarization has the linearized [gauge redundancy](../../../../../../gauge-redundancy.md)

$$
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\sim\epsilon_{\mu\nu}+k_\mu\xi_\nu+k_\nu\xi_\mu,
$$

while the antisymmetric polarization obeys

$$
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\sim\epsilon_{\mu\nu}+k_\mu\Lambda_\nu-k_\nu\Lambda_\mu.
$$

In either case the change in the integrated vertex is a worldsheet [total derivative](../../../../../../total-derivative.md), hence vanishes on a closed worldsheet; in covariant language it is [BRST-exact](../../../../../../brst-cohomology.md). This [string-state gauge redundancy](../../../../../../string-state-gauge-redundancy.md) is the vertex-operator form of linearized target-space diffeomorphism or two-form gauge invariance.
