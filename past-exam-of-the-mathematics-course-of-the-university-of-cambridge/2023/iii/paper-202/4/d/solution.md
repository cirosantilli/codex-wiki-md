<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The lifetime of the time-changed process is

$$
\mathcal T=C_\infty=\int_0^\infty e^{2(B_t+at)}dt.
$$

By the [strong law for Brownian motion](../../../../../../strong-law-for-brownian-motion.md), $B_t/t\to0$ almost surely. If $a<0$, the exponent is eventually at most $at<0$, so the integral is finite. If $a>0$, it is eventually positive and grows linearly; if $a=0$, the [recurrence of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../../../../recurrence-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion.md) makes $B$ spend infinite total time in, for example, $[-1,1]$, so the integral is infinite. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\mathcal T<\infty)=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d<2.}
$$
