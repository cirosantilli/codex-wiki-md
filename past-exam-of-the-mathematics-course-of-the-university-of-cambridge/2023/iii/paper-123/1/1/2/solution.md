<h1 id="1/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Let $L/K$ be a finite abelian extension and let $\mathfrak m$ be an admissible modulus, so every ramified prime divides $\mathfrak m_0$ and the [Artin reciprocity map](../../../../../../../artin-reciprocity-law.md)

$$
\theta_{L/K}:I_K(\mathfrak m)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
$$

is defined. If $\mathfrak p\nmid\mathfrak m_0$, then the [ideal-theoretic decomposition law](../../../../../../../ideal-theoretic-decomposition-law.md) says that its residue degree is

$$
f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)
=\operatorname{ord}\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p),
$$

and, since $\mathfrak p$ is unramified, it has

$$
g(\mathfrak p)=\frac{[L:K]}{\operatorname{ord}\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p)}
$$

prime factors in $L$.

Indeed, $\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p)$ is the [Artin symbol](../../../../../../../artin-symbol.md), whose restriction to the residue field is $x\mapsto x^{N\mathfrak p}$. The Galois group of the finite residue-field extension is generated by this Frobenius automorphism and has order $f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)$. The fundamental identity $efg=[L:K]$, with $e=1$, gives the formula for $g$.
