<h1 id="1/1/5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$, whose discriminant is $-24$, and put

$$
H=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6},\sqrt2)
=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3}).
$$

This is a biquadratic extension of $\mathbb Q$ whose three quadratic subfields have discriminants $-24$, $8$, and $-3$. The [discriminant of a biquadratic field](../../../../../../../discriminant-of-a-biquadratic-field.md) is therefore

$$
d_H=(-24)\cdot8\cdot(-3)=576=d_K^2.
$$

The relative discriminant formula gives

$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{H/K})
=\frac{|d_H|}{|d_K|^{[H:K]}}=1,
$$

so $H/K$ is unramified at every finite prime. Both fields are totally imaginary, so no infinite place ramifies. Since $[H:K]=2=h_K$, the extension has the full degree of the [Hilbert class field](../../../../../../../hilbert-class-field.md), and hence

$$
\boxed{H_K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6},\sqrt2).}
$$
