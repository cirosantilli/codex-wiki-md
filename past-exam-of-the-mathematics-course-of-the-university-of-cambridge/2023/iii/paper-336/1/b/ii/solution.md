<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Since $\Phi_\theta=-A\sin\theta$ and the average of $\sin^2\theta$ over one period is $1/2$, the leading [averaged Lagrangian](../../../../../../../averaged-lagrangian.md) is

$$
\boxed{
\overline L^{(0)}(k,\omega,A)
=\frac14A^2(\omega^2-c^2k^2).}
$$

Its [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) for the slowly varying amplitude is algebraic:

$$
\frac{\partial\overline L^{(0)}}{\partial A}
=\frac12A(\omega^2-c^2k^2)=0.
$$

For a nonzero wave this again gives the [dispersion relation](../../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md). It does not determine $A$ because the original wave equation is linear and homogeneous: the leading amplitude is fixed only by the next-order transport equation and by initial or boundary data.
