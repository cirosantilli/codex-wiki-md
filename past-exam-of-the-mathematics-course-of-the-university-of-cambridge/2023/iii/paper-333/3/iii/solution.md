<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

When $\mu\gg1$, the boundary coupling $B/H=\operatorname{csch}\mu/\mu$ is exponentially small. The two eigenvalues are therefore the diagonal boundary-wave speeds through order $1/\mu$:

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde c_{\rm lower}
=\frac1\mu-i\epsilon+O(e^{-\mu}),
\qquad
\widetilde c_{\rm upper}
=1-\frac1\mu+O(e^{-\mu})}.
$$

The lower wave is damped at the imposed rate, while the upper wave is exponentially isolated from the lower boundary and remains neutral to algebraic order. Damping destroys the phase-locked counterpropagating-wave interaction at short horizontal wavelength rather than damping both waves equally.
