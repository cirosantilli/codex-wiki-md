<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The claim fails for a general extension. If $k$ is an infinite [field](../../../../../../field.md), all the infinitely many maximal ideals $(x-a)$ of $k[x]$ contract to $(0)$ in $k$.

It still fails for an [integral extension](../../../../../../integral-extension.md). Take a finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$ and

$$
B=\prod_{j\geq1}k.
$$

Every $b\in B$ satisfies the monic equation $b^q-b=0$, so $B$ is integral over the diagonal copy of $k$. The coordinate kernels are infinitely many distinct [maximal ideals](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md), all lying over $(0)$.

**The claim is true for a [module-finite ring extension](../../../../../../module-finite-ring-extension.md).** The primes above $\mathfrak p$ correspond to the primes of the [fiber ring](../../../../../../fiber-ring.md)

$$
B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p).
$$

This is a finite-dimensional algebra over the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md) $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, hence an [Artinian ring](../../../../../../artinian-ring.md), and an Artinian ring has only finitely many prime ideals.
