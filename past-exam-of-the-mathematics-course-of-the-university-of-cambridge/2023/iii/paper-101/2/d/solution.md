<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Form the finite-dimensional $k$-algebra

$$
C=B\otimes_{A,f}k.
$$

Because a finite extension is integral, the [Lying-over theorem](../../../../../../lying-over-theorem.md) supplies a prime of $B$ above $\ker f$; after localization and extension of the residue field to $k$, this shows $C\ne0$. Therefore $C$ has at least one [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md).

As an [Artinian ring](../../../../../../artinian-ring.md), $C$ has only finitely many maximal ideals. For each such ideal $\mathfrak n$, the quotient $C/\mathfrak n$ is a finite [field extension](../../../../../../field-extension.md) of the algebraically closed field $k$, so it equals $k$. Consequently the quotient maps $C\to k$ are in bijection with the required extensions $g:B\to k$. The set of extensions is therefore finite and nonempty.
