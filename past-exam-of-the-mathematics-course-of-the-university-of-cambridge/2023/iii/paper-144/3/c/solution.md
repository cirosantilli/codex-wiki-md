<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the [Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../../downward-lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) to choose a small elementary submodel $M\preceq N$. By hypothesis, $M$ meets every $E$-class. If there were infinitely many classes, the type

$$
q(x)=\{\neg E(x,m):m\in M\}
$$

would be finitely satisfiable: finitely many parameters meet only finitely many classes, so choose $x$ from another class. Its parameter set is small, so [saturation](../../../../../../homogeneity-plus-realization-of-empty-set-types-implies-saturation.md) would realize $q$ in $N$. That realization would lie in an $E$-class disjoint from $M$, contradicting the hypothesis. This proves the [small elementary submodel meeting every definable equivalence class](../../../../../../small-elementary-submodel-meeting-every-definable-equivalence-class.md) criterion: $E$ has only finitely many classes.
