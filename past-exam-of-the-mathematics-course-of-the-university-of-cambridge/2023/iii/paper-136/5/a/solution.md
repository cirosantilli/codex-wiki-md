<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the [discrete valuation](../../../../../../discrete-valuation.md) $v$ corresponding to $|\mathord\cdot|$, the [valuation ring](../../../../../../valuation-ring.md) and its maximal ideal are

$$
\mathcal O_K=\{x\in K:v(x)\geq0\},
\qquad
\mathfrak m=\{x\in K:v(x)>0\}.
$$

The first is a subring of the field $K$, hence an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). An element of $\mathcal O_K$ is a unit exactly when its valuation is zero, so every nonunit lies in $\mathfrak m$ and $\mathfrak m$ is the unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md).

Choose a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $\pi$ with $v(\pi)=1$. If $I\ne0$ is an ideal, the set of valuations of its nonzero elements has a least member $n$. Choose $x\in I$ with $v(x)=n$. Then $x=u\pi^n$ for a unit $u$, so $(x)=(\pi^n)$. Every $y\in I$ has $v(y)\geq n$, hence $y/x\in\mathcal O_K$, and therefore $I=(x)$. Thus $\mathcal O_K$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md), in particular a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md).
