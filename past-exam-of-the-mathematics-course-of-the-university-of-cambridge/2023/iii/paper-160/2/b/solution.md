<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $\chi=\chi^\lambda$. By the [Hook-length formula](../../../../../../hook-length-formula.md),

$$
\frac{|S_n|}{\chi(1)}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}h_{i,j}(\lambda).
$$

Suppose the [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $g$ did not divide this product. Then for some [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$, the highest [prime power](../../../../../../prime-power.md) $p^a$ dividing $|g|$ would not divide the hook product. One cycle of $g$ has length divisible by $p^a$, whereas no hook length of $\lambda$ is divisible by $p^a$. In particular there is no removable rim hook having that cycle length. Applying the [Murnaghan–Nakayama rule](../../../../../../murnaghan-nakayama-rule.md) first to this cycle gives $\chi(g)=0$, a contradiction. This is the [symmetric-group character co-degree vanishing criterion](../../../../../../symmetric-group-character-co-degree-vanishing-criterion.md), and its contrapositive proves

$$
\boxed{|g|\mid\frac{|S_n|}{\chi(1)}.}
$$
