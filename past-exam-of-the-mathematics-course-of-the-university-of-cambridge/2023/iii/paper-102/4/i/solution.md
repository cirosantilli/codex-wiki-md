↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a dominant integral weight $\lambda$, the [Weyl character formula](../../../../../../weyl-character-formula.md) is

$$
\operatorname{ch}L_\lambda=
\frac{\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w(\lambda+\rho)}}
{\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w\rho}}.
$$

Here $W$ is the [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md), $\ell(w)$ its [Coxeter length](../../../../../../coxeter-length.md), $\rho$ the [Weyl vector](../../../../../../half-sum-of-positive-roots.md), and $\operatorname{ch}$ the [formal character of a weight module](../../../../../../formal-character-of-a-weight-module.md). Taking the limit at the identity gives the [Weyl dimension formula](../../../../../../weyl-dimension-formula.md)

$$
\boxed{\dim L_\lambda=
\prod_{\alpha\in R^+}
\frac{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}
{\langle\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}.}
$$
