Let $Z$ indicate a recent birthday event, $A$ indicate a social gathering, and $Y$ denote subsequent household COVID-19 infection. The standard [instrumental variable](../../../../../../instrumental-variable.md) conditions are:

- [Instrument relevance](../../../../../../instrument-relevance.md): $Z$ changes the probability or intensity of $A$.
- [Instrumental-variable independence](../../../../../../instrumental-variable-independence.md): conditional on chosen baseline covariates, birthday timing is independent of unmeasured causes of infection and of the relevant [potential outcomes](../../../../../../potential-outcome.md).
- The [exclusion restriction](../../../../../../exclusion-restriction.md): $Z$ affects $Y$ only through the social gathering $A$.
- For a [local average treatment effect](../../../../../../local-average-treatment-effect.md), [instrumental-variable monotonicity](../../../../../../instrumental-variable-monotonicity.md) excludes households that would hold a gathering without a birthday event but would suppress it because of one.

The usual [consistency in causal inference](../../../../../../consistency-in-causal-inference.md), well-defined exposure, and absence of relevant [interference in causal inference](../../../../../../interference-in-causal-inference.md) are also needed to interpret the result causally.
