<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the injective [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $F_2\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ from part (a). If $1\ne g\in F_2$, its image is an integral matrix $M\ne I$. Choose a [prime number](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$ that does not divide one nonzero entry of $M-I$. The [reduction modulo a prime in an integral matrix group](../../../../../../reduction-modulo-a-prime-in-an-integral-matrix-group.md) homomorphism

$$
\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
$$

then sends $M$ to a nonidentity element. Its target is a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md), so the composite map separates $g$ from the identity. Hence $F_2$ is a [residually finite group](../../../../../../residually-finite-group.md).
