<h1 id="1/e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

When $r_-=0$, $\Omega=0$ and $f=(r^2-r_+^2)/L^2$. A circle $S$ at fixed $v,r$ has circumference $2\pi r$. Choose its future null normals as

$$
\ell=\partial_v+\frac f2\partial_r,
\qquad
n=-\partial_r,
\qquad
\ell\mathbin\cdot n=-1.
$$

For a one-dimensional transverse surface, each [null expansion](../../../../../../../null-expansion.md) is the logarithmic derivative of its length:

$$
\theta_{(\ell)}=\frac1r\ell(r)=\frac{f}{2r},
\qquad
\theta_{(n)}=\frac1r n(r)=-\frac1r.
$$

Inside the horizon, $0<r<r_+$ implies $f<0$, so both expansions are negative. Every such circle is therefore a [trapped surface](../../../../../../../trapped-surface.md), specifically a [Trapped circle inside a nonrotating BTZ black hole](../../../../../../../trapped-circle-inside-a-nonrotating-btz-black-hole.md).
