<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The normal to a surface of constant $r$ is $n_a=(dr)_a$, and the inverse ingoing metric gives

$$
n^an_a=g^{rr}=f(r).
$$

Hence $r=r_+$ is a [null hypersurface](../../../../../../null-hypersurface.md). Let

$$
\xi=k+\Omega_+m=\partial_v+\Omega_+\partial_\chi,
\qquad \Omega_+=\Omega(r_+)=\frac{r_-}{Lr_+}.
$$

This constant linear combination of [Killing vector fields](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) is Killing. Its squared norm is

$$
\xi^2=-f+r^2(\Omega-\Omega_+)^2,
$$

which vanishes at $r_+$. More strongly, lowering its index in the ingoing metric gives $\xi_a=(dr)_a$ on the horizon. It is therefore the null normal and generator, so the surface is a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../killing-horizon.md).

Using $\nabla_a(\xi^2)=-2\kappa\xi_a$ on a Killing horizon, the squared term contributes no first derivative at $r_+$ and

$$
\kappa=\frac12f'(r_+).
$$

Differentiating $f$ at its simple outer root gives

$$
f'(r_+)=\frac{2(r_+^2-r_-^2)}{L^2r_+},
$$

so the [surface gravity](../../../../../../surface-gravity.md) is

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\frac{r_+^2-r_-^2}{L^2r_+}}.
$$

It vanishes in the extremal case $r_+=r_-$.
