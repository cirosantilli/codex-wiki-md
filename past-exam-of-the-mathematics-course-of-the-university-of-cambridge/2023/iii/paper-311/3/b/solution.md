<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) states that, assuming the [null energy condition](../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and the usual predictability hypotheses, the area of cross-sections of a future [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) never decreases toward the future.

Indeed, the horizon generators form a hypersurface-orthogonal null congruence, so their twist vanishes. If the expansion were negative anywhere, the [null focusing theorem](../../../../../../null-focusing-theorem.md) would make it diverge to $-\infty$ within finite affine parameter, provided the generator extends that far. This produces a [conjugate point](../../../../../../conjugate-point.md), after which the generator cannot remain on an [achronal set](../../../../../../achronal-set.md) such as an event horizon. Future completeness rules out the alternative that the generator simply ends first. Therefore $\theta\geq0$ everywhere, and

$$
\boxed{\frac{dA}{d\lambda}=\theta A\geq0.}
$$
