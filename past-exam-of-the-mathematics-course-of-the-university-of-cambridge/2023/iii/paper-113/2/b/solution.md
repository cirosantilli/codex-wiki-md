<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $f:U=\mathbb A_\mathbb C^1\setminus\{1\}\to\mathbb A_\mathbb C^1$ for $f(t)=t^2$. For any closed point $a\in\mathbb C$, the fiber is the zero-dimensional closed subscheme of $U$ cut out by $t^2-a$. It is finite over $\mathbb C$, and every [finite morphism](../../../../../../finite-morphism.md) is [proper](../../../../../../proper-morphism.md). This includes $a=1$, whose fiber in $U$ consists only of $t=-1$.

The morphism itself fails the [valuative criterion for properness](../../../../../../valuative-criterion-for-properness.md). Take the [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md) $R=\mathbb C[[s]]$ with [fraction field](../../../../../../field-of-fractions.md) $K=\mathbb C((s))$. The $K$-point $t=1+s$ lies in $U(K)$ and lies over the $R$-point of the target defined by

$$
y=(1+s)^2.
$$

Any extension $\operatorname{Spec}R\to\mathbb A^1$ of that $K$-point must still be given by $t=1+s$, whose closed point maps to the deleted point $t=1$. It therefore cannot factor through $U$. The required lift does not exist, so $f$ is not proper. This is an instance of the fact that [proper closed-point fibers do not imply properness](../../../../../../proper-closed-point-fibers-do-not-imply-properness.md).
