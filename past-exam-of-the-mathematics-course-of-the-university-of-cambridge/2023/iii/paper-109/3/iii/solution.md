↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**Yes.** For every prime power $q\geq2$, a [finite projective plane](../../../../../../finite-projective-plane.md) of order $q$ has

$$
n=q^2+q+1
$$

points and the same number of lines; every two lines meet in exactly one point, and the intersection of all lines is empty. Its lines therefore form one required family on $n$ points. The near-pencil from part (ii) is another. They are non-isomorphic because every projective-plane line has size $q+1$, whereas the near-pencil has a member of size $n-1$. There are infinitely many prime powers, so infinitely many such $n$.
