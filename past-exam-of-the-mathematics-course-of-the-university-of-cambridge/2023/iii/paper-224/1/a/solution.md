<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Because $X$ and $Y$ are [independent random variables](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), conditioning on $Y$ merely translates $X$. The [conditional entropy under a deterministic change of variables](../../../../../../conditional-entropy-under-a-deterministic-change-of-variables.md) and the fact that [conditioning reduces entropy](../../../../../../conditioning-reduces-entropy.md) therefore give

$$
H(X+Y)\geq H(X+Y\mid Y)=H(X\mid Y)=H(X).
$$

Interchanging $X$ and $Y$ similarly gives $H(X+Y)\geq H(Y)$, and hence

$$
H(X+Y)\geq\max\{H(X),H(Y)\}.
$$

The proof applies to countable alphabets whenever the displayed [information entropies](../../../../../../information-entropy.md) are finite.
