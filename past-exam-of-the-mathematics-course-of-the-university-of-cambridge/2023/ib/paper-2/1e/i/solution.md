<h1 id="1e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $e^2=e$ with $e\ne0,1$. Then $e(1-e)=0$, and the [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) $eR$ and $(1-e)R$ are nonzero [rings](../../../../../../ring.md) with identities $e$ and $1-e$. Define

$$
\Phi:R\longrightarrow eR\times(1-e)R,
\qquad
\Phi(r)=(er,(1-e)r).
$$

This is a [ring homomorphism](../../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md). Its inverse is $(x,y)\mapsto x+y$, because $ex=x$, $(1-e)y=y$, and the cross terms vanish. Hence $R\cong eR\times(1-e)R$, proving (ii).
