<h1 id="1e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1E](../1e.md)

For

$$
R=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a\equiv b\pmod2\},
$$

the parity condition is preserved by componentwise addition, negation, and multiplication, and $(1,1)$ is the identity. Thus $R$ is a [ring](../../../../../ring.md).

It is not an [integral](../../../../../integral.md) domain, since $(2,0)$ and $(0,2)$ are nonzero elements of $R$ whose product is zero. It is also not a product of two nontrivial [rings](../../../../../ring.md). Indeed, an [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) in $\mathbb Z^2$ has each coordinate in $\{0,1\}$, and the parity condition leaves only $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. The proved equivalence then excludes a nontrivial product decomposition.
