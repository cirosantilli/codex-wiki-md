<h1 id="4d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a particle in the scalar [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) $U(x)$,

$$
H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+U(x).
$$

The [canonical commutation relation](../../../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) $[x,p]=i\hbar$ gives

$$
[H,x]=\frac1{2m}[p^2,x]=-\frac{i\hbar}{m}p.
$$

The [Ehrenfest theorem](../../../../../../ehrenfest-theorem.md) with $O=x$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{m\frac d{dt}\langle x\rangle_\psi=\langle p\rangle_\psi}.
$$

Since $[p^2,p]=0$ and direct action on a [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function.md) gives $[U(x),p]=i\hbar U'(x)$,

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\langle p\rangle_\psi
=-\langle U'(x)\rangle_\psi}.
$$

Finally $H$ has no explicit time dependence and $[H,H]=0$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\langle H\rangle_\psi=0}.
$$

The first two equations are the expectation-value counterparts of momentum equals mass times velocity and [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md); the last is [conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md). They become the classical equations directly when the force varies negligibly across the wave packet, so that $\langle U'(x)\rangle\simeq U'(\langle x\rangle)$. These conclusions are summarized by the [classical equations from Ehrenfest theorem](../../../../../../classical-equations-from-ehrenfest-theorem.md).
