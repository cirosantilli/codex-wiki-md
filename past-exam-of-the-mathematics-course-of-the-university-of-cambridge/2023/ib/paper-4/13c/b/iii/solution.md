<h1 id="13c/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the one-dimensional limit, $v=0$ and $u=u(x)$. The first component of the [Navier-Cauchy equation](../../../../../../../navier-cauchy-equation.md) reduces to

$$
(\lambda+2\mu)u''(x)=0.
$$

For a [nondegenerate one-dimensional elastic material](../../../../../../../nondegenerate-one-dimensional-elastic-material.md), $\lambda+2\mu\ne0$, so $u''=0$ and $u(x)=Ax+B$. The [boundary conditions](../../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) $u(0)=0$ and $u(L)=\Delta$ give $B=0$ and $A=\Delta/L$. Therefore the [uniform extension of a one-dimensional elastic body](../../../../../../../uniform-extension-of-a-one-dimensional-elastic-body.md) is

$$
\boxed{u(x)=\frac{\Delta}{L}x}.
$$
