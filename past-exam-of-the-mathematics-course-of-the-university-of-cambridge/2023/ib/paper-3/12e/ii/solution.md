<h1 id="12e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The plane

$$
\boxed{y=0}
$$

meets the [one-sheet hyperboloid](../../../../../../one-sheet-hyperboloid.md) in the two disjoint curves

$$
(x,y,z)=(\pm\sqrt{1+z^2},0,z).
$$

They are the two opposite meridians of the [surface of revolution](../../../../../../surface-of-revolution.md). Parametrize the generating profile by [arc length](../../../../../../arc-length.md). Its [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) lies in the meridian plane and is orthogonal to the profile tangent; it is also orthogonal to the azimuthal tangent. It is therefore normal to the surface, so both meridians are geodesics. This is the disjoint case in [plane-section geodesics of the unit one-sheet hyperboloid](../../../../../../plane-section-geodesics-of-the-unit-one-sheet-hyperboloid.md).
