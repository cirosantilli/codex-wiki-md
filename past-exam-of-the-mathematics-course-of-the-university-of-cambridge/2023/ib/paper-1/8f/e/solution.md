<h1 id="8f/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**True.** Suppose $A,B$ are real and $AP=PB$ for an invertible complex [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $P=X+iY$, with $X,Y$ real. Taking real and imaginary parts gives

$$
AX=XB,\qquad AY=YB.
$$

The [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $q(t)=\det(X+tY)$ is not identically zero, since $q(i)=\det P\ne0$. Choose a real $t$ outside its finite zero set. Then $Q=X+tY$ is real and invertible, and $AQ=QB$. Thus $A$ and $B$ are conjugate over $\mathbb R$, as stated by [complex similarity of real matrices implies real similarity](../../../../../../complex-similarity-of-real-matrices-implies-real-similarity.md).
