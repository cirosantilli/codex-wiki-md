<h1 id="12g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Near each $a\in D$, the local branch from part (a), chosen to have value $\log|a|+i\theta(a)$ at $a$, agrees with

$$
L(z)=\log|z|+i\theta(z).
$$

Indeed, two logarithms of the same nonzero number differ by $2\pi i$ times an integer, and the integer is locally constant; it is zero at $a$. Hence $L$ is locally analytic and therefore analytic on $D$. This is the [analytic logarithm on the positive-axis slit plane](../../../../../../analytic-logarithm-on-the-positive-axis-slit-plane.md).

For $-1<\alpha<1$, an analytic branch is

$$
\boxed{z^\alpha=\exp(\alpha L(z)).}
$$
