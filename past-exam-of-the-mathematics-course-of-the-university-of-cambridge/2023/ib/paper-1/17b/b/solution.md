<h1 id="17b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [function](../../../../../../function-split.md)

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
y_0-t,&0\leq t\leq y_0,\\
0,&y_0\leq t\leq T
\end{cases}
$$

is continuous, satisfies the equation away from $\widetilde t=y_0$, and has unequal one-sided [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) there.

It is unique in the stated class. While a solution is positive its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $-1$, so continuity forces it to follow $y_0-t$ until it first reaches zero at $t=y_0$. It cannot subsequently make a positive excursion: on any connected interval $(a,b)$ where $y>0$ and $y(a)=0$, integration of $y'=-1$ gives $y(t)=-(t-a)<0$. A negative excursion similarly contradicts $y'=1$. Hence the solution remains zero. This is the [sign-decay differential equation](../../../../../../sign-decay-differential-equation.md).
