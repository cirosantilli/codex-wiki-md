<h1 id="9b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9B](../9b.md)

For a relativistic species the supplied relation $P=\epsilon/3$ makes its [entropy density](../../../../../entropy-density.md) proportional to $T^3$. The [fermion-to-boson thermal integral ratio](../../../../../fermion-to-boson-thermal-integral-ratio.md) gives the factor $7/8$ for each fermionic degree of freedom, so the electromagnetic plasma immediately before [electron-positron annihilation in cosmology](../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation-in-cosmology.md) has

$$
g_{*s,{\rm before}}
=g_\gamma+\frac78(g_{e^-}+g_{e^+})
=2+\frac78(2+2)
=\frac{11}{2}.
$$

After annihilation only the two photon polarizations remain, so $g_{*s,{\rm after}}=2$.

The neutrinos have already undergone [neutrino decoupling](../../../../../neutrino-decoupling.md). They therefore receive none of the electron-positron entropy and cool as $T_\nu\propto a^{-1}$. In the still-coupled electromagnetic plasma, [cosmological entropy conservation](../../../../../cosmological-entropy-conservation.md) gives $g_{*s}T_\gamma^3a^3={\rm constant}$. If $T_d$ and $a_d$ denote the common temperature and scale factor just before annihilation, then at a later scale factor $a$,

$$
2T_\gamma^3a^3=\frac{11}{2}T_d^3a_d^3,
\qquad
T_\nu^3a^3=T_d^3a_d^3.
$$

Dividing gives the [Cosmic neutrino background](../../../../../cosmic-neutrino-background.md) temperature ratio

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_\nu}{T_\gamma}=\left(\frac4{11}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$
