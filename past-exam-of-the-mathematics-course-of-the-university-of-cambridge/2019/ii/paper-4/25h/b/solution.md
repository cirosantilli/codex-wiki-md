<h1 id="25h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A regular surface is a [minimal surface](../../../../../../minimal-surface.md) when its [mean curvature](../../../../../../mean-curvature.md) vanishes identically, equivalently when its two [principal curvatures](../../../../../../principal-curvature.md) sum to zero everywhere.

The [catenoid](../../../../../../catenoid.md)

$$
\phi(u,v)=(\cosh v\cos u,\cosh v\sin u,v)
$$

is an example. In part (a), take $f(v)=\cosh v$ and $g(v)=v$, so $q=\cosh v$. The formula there gives

$$
H=\frac12\left(\operatorname{sech}^2v-\operatorname{sech}^2v\right)=0,
\qquad
K=-\operatorname{sech}^4v<0.
$$

A plane and a [circular cylinder](../../../../../../circular-cylinder.md) have zero [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md), as does a [flat cone](../../../../../../flat-cone.md) away from its vertex. Because a [local isometry](../../../../../../local-isometry.md) preserves Gaussian curvature, **the catenoid is minimal and is not locally isometric to any of those surfaces**; this is the [catenoid is a minimal surface](../../../../../../catenoid-is-a-minimal-surface.md).
