<h1 id="20g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Taking norms in

$$
p\mathcal O_K=Q_1\cdots Q_s
$$

gives

$$
p^n=N(p\mathcal O_K)=\prod_{i=1}^sN(Q_i)=p^s,
$$

so $s=n$.

Suppose for contradiction that $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, and let $f$ be the degree-$n$ minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. Part (a) identifies the prime ideals above $p$ with the distinct irreducible factors of $\overline f$. Since there are $n$ distinct prime ideals, all with norm $p$, the norm formula forces $\overline f$ to have $n$ distinct irreducible factors of degree one. But $\mathbb F_p[X]$ has only the $p$ distinct monic linear polynomials

$$
X-a\qquad(a\in\mathbb F_p).
$$

This is impossible when $n>p$. The [completely split small-prime obstruction to monogenicity](../../../../../../completely-split-small-prime-obstruction-to-monogenicity.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{n>p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal O_K\ne\mathbb Z[\alpha]\text{ for every }\alpha\in\mathcal O_K.}
$$
