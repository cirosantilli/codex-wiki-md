<h1 id="26k/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [quantile function](../../../../../../../quantile-function.md) $X_\mu=F_\mu^{-1}$ is nondecreasing, hence a [Borel measurable function](../../../../../../../borel-measurable-function.md). More explicitly, for every $x\in\mathbb R$,

$$
\{\omega\in(0,1):X_\mu(\omega)\leq x\}
=(0,F_\mu(x)]\cap(0,1).
$$

Thus $X_\mu$ is a [random variable](../../../../../../../random-variable-split.md), and because Lebesgue measure is being used on $(0,1)$,

$$
\mathbb P(X_\mu\leq x)=F_\mu(x).
$$

Its [cumulative distribution function](../../../../../../../cumulative-distribution-function.md) is therefore $F_\mu$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L(X_\mu)=\mu.}
$$

This is [inverse transform sampling](../../../../../../../inverse-transform-sampling.md).
