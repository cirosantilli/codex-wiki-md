<h1 id="37a/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Applying the [Laplacian](../../../../../../../laplacian.md) to $\mathbf u$ and using $\nabla^2\boldsymbol\Phi=\mathbf0$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla^2\mathbf u
&=\nabla\left[\nabla^2
(\mathbf x\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol\Phi+\chi)\right]
-2\nabla^2\boldsymbol\Phi\\
&=2\nabla(\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol\Phi).
\end{aligned}
$$

For

$$
p=2\mu\nabla\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol\Phi,
$$

one therefore has

$$
-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u=\mathbf0.
$$

Together with part (i), these are the [Stokes equations](../../../../../../../stokes-equation.md), so the fields form the [Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation](../../../../../../../unscaled-papkovich-neuber-representation.md).
