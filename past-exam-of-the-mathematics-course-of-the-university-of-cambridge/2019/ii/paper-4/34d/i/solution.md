<h1 id="34d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[Landau theory](../../../../../../landau-theory.md) represents the appropriate thermodynamic potential as an analytic function of a small [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md), retaining every low-order term allowed by the system's [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md). At fixed control parameters, the equilibrium phase is the global minimum of the [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md); local minima describe metastable phases. A [continuous phase transition](../../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md) occurs when a minimum changes continuously and loses stability, whereas a [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md) occurs when distinct minima exchange global stability.

For

$$
G(\phi)=\frac14\phi^4+\frac12\varepsilon\phi^2,
$$

the stationary points satisfy

$$
G'(\phi)=\phi(\phi^2+\varepsilon)=0.
$$

If $\varepsilon>0$, $\phi=0$ is the unique minimum. If $\varepsilon<0$, $\phi=0$ is a maximum and the two degenerate minima are

$$
\phi_\pm=\pm\sqrt{-\varepsilon},
\qquad
G(\phi_\pm)=-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}.
$$

Thus the reflection symmetry $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ is spontaneously broken below $\varepsilon=0$, and

$$
\boxed{\text{the transition at }\varepsilon=0\text{ is continuous, with }|\phi|\sim(-\varepsilon)^{1/2}.}
$$
