<h1 id="34d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the [sextic even Landau potential](../../../../../../sextic-even-landau-potential.md)

$$
G(\phi)=\frac16\phi^6+\frac14g\phi^4+\frac12\varepsilon\phi^2,
$$

put $q=\phi^2$. The stationary-point equation is

$$
G'(\phi)=\phi(q^2+gq+\varepsilon)=0,
$$

so the nonzero stationary points have

$$
q_\pm=\frac{-g\pm\sqrt{g^2-4\varepsilon}}2.
$$

At such a point, $\varepsilon=-q^2-gq$, and hence

$$
G=-\frac13q^3-\frac14gq^2
=-q^2\left(\frac q3+\frac g4\right).
$$

A first-order transition occurs when a nonzero minimum has the same free energy as the disordered minimum $G(0)=0$. Therefore

$$
q=-\frac{3g}{4},
\qquad
\boxed{\varepsilon=\frac{3g^2}{16},\quad g<0.}
$$

At $g=0,\varepsilon=0$, the first-order line meets the continuous line and forms the [tricritical point](../../../../../../tricritical-point.md). For $g>0$, the transition at $\varepsilon=0$ is continuous.

For $g<0$, the disordered state $\phi=0$ is a local minimum when $\varepsilon>0$ and loses stability at the disordered [spinodal point](../../../../../../spinodal-point.md) $\varepsilon=0$. The ordered minima exist while

$$
g^2-4\varepsilon\geq0,
$$

and merge with the intervening maxima at the ordered spinodal $\varepsilon=g^2/4$. Consequently the two spinodals enclose a region of [metastability](../../../../../../metastability.md). If increasing $\varepsilon$ corresponds to heating, the ordered phase can persist above the coexistence curve throughout

$$
\boxed{\text{superheating:}\qquad
g<0,\quad \frac{3g^2}{16}<\varepsilon<\frac{g^2}{4},}
$$

while the disordered phase can persist below it throughout

$$
\boxed{\text{supercooling:}\qquad
g<0,\quad 0<\varepsilon<\frac{3g^2}{16}.}
$$
