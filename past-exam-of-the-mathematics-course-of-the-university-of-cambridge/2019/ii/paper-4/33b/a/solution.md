<h1 id="33b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the classical experiment, particles whose [impact parameter](../../../../../../impact-parameter.md) lies between $b$ and $b+db$ occupy an incident annulus of area

$$
d\sigma=2\pi b\,|db|.
$$

For a [spherically symmetric potential](../../../../../../spherically-symmetric-potential.md), azimuthal symmetry scatters this annulus into the solid-angle strip

$$
d\Omega=2\pi\sin\theta\,|d\theta|.
$$

Thus the [central-potential classical differential cross-section](../../../../../../central-potential-classical-differential-cross-section.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=\frac{b}{\sin\theta}
\left|\frac{db}{d\theta}\right|.}
$$

If the relation between $b$ and $\theta$ has several branches, one sums this expression over every branch. The corresponding diagram consists of an incoming line offset by $b$ from the centre, an outgoing asymptote making angle $\theta$ with the incident direction, and the annulus and solid-angle strip just described.

For quantum scattering by a [central potential](../../../../../../central-potential.md), the far-field [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function.md) is

$$
\psi(\mathbf r)\sim e^{ikz}
+f(\theta)\frac{e^{ikr}}r,
\qquad r\to\infty,
$$

where $f(\theta)$ is the [scattering amplitude](../../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md). The incident plane wave has [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md)

$$
\mathbf J_{\rm in}=\frac{\hbar k}{m}\,\widehat{\mathbf z}.
$$

To leading order in $1/r$, the scattered spherical wave has radial current

$$
\mathbf J_{\rm sc}
=\frac{\hbar k}{m}\frac{|f(\theta)|^2}{r^2}
\,\widehat{\mathbf r}.
$$

The scattered rate through area $r^2d\Omega$ divided by the incident flux is therefore the [quantum differential cross-section from a scattering amplitude](../../../../../../quantum-differential-cross-section-from-a-scattering-amplitude.md):

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=|f(\theta)|^2.}
$$
