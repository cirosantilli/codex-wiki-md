<h1 id="27k/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For every [Borel set](../../../../../../borel-set.md) $A\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, let

$$
N(A)=\#(\Pi\cap A)
$$

be its random point count. A [Non-homogeneous Poisson point process](../../../../../../non-homogeneous-poisson-point-process.md) with [intensity function of a point process](../../../../../../intensity-function-of-a-point-process.md) $\lambda$ is characterized by:


- $N(A)$ is finite almost surely for every bounded Borel set $A$;
- for every bounded Borel set $A$,


$$
N(A)\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda(A)),
\qquad
\Lambda(A)=\int_A\lambda(x)\,dx;
$$


- the counts $N(A_1),\ldots,N(A_m)$ are [independent random variables](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) whenever $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ are pairwise disjoint bounded Borel sets.

Because $\Lambda$ has a density with respect to [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md), it is atomless, so the process is simple: almost surely no location occurs more than once.
