↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [mapping theorem for Poisson point processes](../../../../../../mapping-theorem-point-process.md) applies provided $f$ is a [measurable function](../../../../../../measurable-function.md) and its [pushforward measure](../../../../../../pushforward-measure.md)

$$
f_*\Lambda(B)=\Lambda(f^{-1}(B))
$$

is locally finite, meaning

$$
\Lambda(f^{-1}(B))<\infty
$$

for every bounded Borel set $B\subseteq\mathbb R^s$. If “Poisson process” means a simple point process, one also requires $f_*\Lambda$ to be atomless, equivalently

$$
\Lambda(f^{-1}(\{y\}))=0
\qquad(y\in\mathbb R^s).
$$

Under these conditions, $f(\Pi)$ is a [Poisson point process](../../../../../../poisson-point-process.md) with mean measure

$$
\boxed{\Lambda_f(B)=f_*\Lambda(B)=\Lambda(f^{-1}(B)).}
$$
