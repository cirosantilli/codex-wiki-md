<h1 id="7a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put

$$
s(t)=\sqrt{1-t^2}
$$

and take the [branch cut](../../../../../../branch-cut.md) $[-1,1]$. On $\mathbb C\setminus[-1,1]$, choose the [analytic branch of a square root](../../../../../../analytic-branch-of-a-square-root.md) whose upper boundary value is

$$
s(x+i0)=+\sqrt{1-x^2},
\qquad -1<x<1.
$$

Its lower boundary value is $s(x-i0)=-\sqrt{1-x^2}$. This fixes the integrand to be $+1$ at $0^+$ as required.

For $0\leq\arg z\leq\pi$, start at $0^+$ and use any path to $z$ lying above the cut; path deformations within that region do not alter the integral by the [Cauchy integral theorem](../../../../../../cauchy-s-integral-theorem.md). For $\pi<\arg z<2\pi$, start in the same way, continue counterclockwise around the branch point $-1$ from the upper bank to the lower bank, and then continue to $z$ through the lower half-plane. This second prescription is the continuation obtained as $arg z$ increases through $\pi$.
