<h1 id="24f/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The inclusion $L((n-1)p)\subseteq L(np)$ allows one additional coefficient: the coefficient of $t^{-n}$ in the local Laurent expansion at $p$, where $t$ is a [uniformizer](../../../../../../../uniformizer.md). The resulting linear map

$$
L(np)/L((n-1)p)\longrightarrow\mathbb C
$$

is injective, so the dimension can increase by at most one.

It increases precisely when some [rational function](../../../../../../../rational-function.md) has a pole of exact order $n$ at $p$ and no other poles. By the definition of the [Weierstrass gap](../../../../../../../weierstrass-gap.md) set $G(p)$, this occurs exactly when $n\notin G(p)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{
\ell(np)=
\begin{cases}
\ell((n-1)p),&n\in G(p),\\
\ell((n-1)p)+1,&n\notin G(p).
\end{cases}}
$$
