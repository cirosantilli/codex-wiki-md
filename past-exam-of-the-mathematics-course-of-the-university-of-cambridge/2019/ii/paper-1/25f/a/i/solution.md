<h1 id="25f/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write

$$
f_1=xy,
\qquad
f_2=y^2-z^3+xz,
\qquad
f_3=x(x+y+2z+1).
$$

The equation $xy=0$ splits the analysis into two cases. If $x=0$, the remaining equation is $y^2=z^3$, giving the [cuspidal cubic](../../../../../../../cuspidal-cubic.md)

$$
C=V(x,y^2-z^3).
$$

It is irreducible because $y^2-z^3$ is irreducible in $k[y,z]$: as a quadratic in $y$, it could factor only if $z^3$ were a square in $k[z]$.

If $y=0$, the other equations become

$$
z(x-z^2)=0,
\qquad
x(x+2z+1)=0.
$$

Their solutions are $(0,0,0)$, which already lies on $C$, and the two points

$$
P=(-1,0,0),
\qquad
Q=(1,0,-1).
$$

Thus the [irreducible components](../../../../../../../irreducible-component.md) are

$$
\boxed{C,\quad\{P\},\quad\{Q\}}.
$$

Equivalently, the [radical ideal](../../../../../../../radical-ideal.md) of the [cusp with two isolated-point components](../../../../../../../cusp-with-two-isolated-point-components.md) is

$$
\boxed{I(V)
=(x,y^2-z^3)
\cap(x+1,y,z)
\cap(x-1,y,z+1).}
$$
