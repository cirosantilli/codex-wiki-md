<h1 id="25f/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $(a,b,c)$ be a tangent vector in the $x,y,z$ directions. At a point $(0,y,z)\in C$, the two isolated components are absent in a Zariski neighbourhood, so the [Zariski tangent space](../../../../../../../zariski-tangent-space.md) is that of $C$. Differentiating $x$ and $y^2-z^3$ gives

$$
\boxed{
T_{(0,y,z)}V
=\{(a,b,c):a=0,\ 2yb-3z^2c=0\}}.
$$

For $(y,z)\ne(0,0)$ this is a line. At the cusp,

$$
\boxed{T_{(0,0,0)}V=\{(0,b,c):b,c\in k\}},
$$

which is two-dimensional.

Each of $P$ and $Q$ is a reduced isolated component with local ring $k$, so

$$
\boxed{T_PV=T_QV=\{0\}}.
$$

This last conclusion must use all functions in $I(V)$, as required by the definition of the Zariski tangent space. The Jacobian of only the three displayed defining polynomials has an extra spurious tangent direction at $Q$, because those polynomials do not generate the radical ideal there; for example, $x(x^2-1)\in I(V)$ removes that direction.
