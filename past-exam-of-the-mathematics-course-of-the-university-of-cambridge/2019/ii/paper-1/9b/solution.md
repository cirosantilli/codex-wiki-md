<h1 id="9b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9B](../9b.md)

For an infinitesimal test mass $\delta m$ on the surface of a homogeneous sphere, its Newtonian [mechanical energy](../../../../../energy.md) is

$$
E=\frac12\delta m\,\dot R^2-\frac{GM\delta m}{R}.
$$

The spatially flat case corresponds to zero specific energy. Using

$$
M=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3
$$

and dividing by $\delta mR^2/2$ gives

$$
\left(\frac{\dot R}{R}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho.
$$

Since $R$ is proportional to the [scale factor](../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) $a$, this is the [Newtonian derivation of the flat Friedmann equation](../../../../../newtonian-derivation-of-the-flat-friedmann-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{H^2=\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho}.
$$

Now take $a(t_0)=1$ and $\rho=\rho_0a^{-4}$. Put

$$
C=\sqrt{\frac{8\pi G\rho_0}{3}}.
$$

The contracting branch of the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) is

$$
\frac{\dot a}{a}=-Ca^{-2},
\qquad
\dot a=-\frac Ca.
$$

Integrating $a\,da=-C\,dt$ from $t_0$ gives

$$
\frac12(a(t)^2-1)=-C(t-t_0).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{a(t)=\sqrt{1-2C(t-t_0)}}
$$

on the contracting interval, reaching $a=0$ at $t=t_0+1/(2C)$.
