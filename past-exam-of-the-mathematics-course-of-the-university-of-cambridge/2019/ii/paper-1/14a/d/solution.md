<h1 id="14a/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the hypergeometric symbol, select the exponent $1-c$ at zero and factor out its local power by writing

$$
y(z)=z^{1-c}v(z).
$$

The [Exponential shifting of a Papperitz symbol](../../../../../../dependent-variable-rescaling-of-a-papperitz-symbol.md) makes the exponents of $v$ equal to $0,c-1$ at zero, $0,c-a-b$ at one, and $a-c+1,b-c+1$ at infinity. These are the hypergeometric parameters

$$
A=a-c+1,
\qquad
B=b-c+1,
\qquad
C=2-c,
$$

because $1-C=c-1$ and $C-A-B=c-a-b$. The solution for $v$ that is analytic at zero and equals one there is therefore the [Gauss hypergeometric function](../../../../../../hypergeometric-function.md) $F(A,B,C;z)$. Thus the [second local hypergeometric solution](../../../../../../second-local-hypergeometric-solution.md) is

$$
\boxed{y(z)=z^{1-c}F(a-c+1,b-c+1,2-c;z)}.
$$

For noninteger $c$ this is linearly independent from $F(a,b,c;z)$; at resonant values, the usual limiting or logarithmic modification may be required.
