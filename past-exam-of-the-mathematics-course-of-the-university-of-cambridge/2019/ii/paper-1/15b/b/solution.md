<h1 id="15b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $V(\phi)=\lambda\phi^4$, the [slow-roll approximation](../../../../../../slow-roll-approximation.md) gives

$$
H^2\simeq\frac{8\pi G}{3}\lambda\phi^4,
\qquad
3H\dot\phi\simeq-4\lambda\phi^3.
$$

Taking the expanding branch $H>0$ and using $H=\sqrt{8\pi G\lambda/3}\,\phi^2$ gives the [first-order linear differential equation](../../../../../../first-order-linear-differential-equation.md)

$$
\dot\phi=-\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{3\pi G}}\,\phi.
$$

Thus the [quartic-potential slow-roll solution](../../../../../../quartic-potential-slow-roll-solution.md) with the normalization used in this question is

$$
\boxed{
\phi(t)=\phi_0
\exp\left[-\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{3\pi G}}(t-t_0)\right]}.
$$

If $\phi_0<0$, the field remains negative and $\dot\phi>0$, so it rolls toward the potential minimum at zero.
