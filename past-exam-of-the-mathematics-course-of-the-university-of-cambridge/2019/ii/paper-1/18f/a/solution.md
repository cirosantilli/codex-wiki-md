<h1 id="18f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Suppose otherwise and choose a relation

$$
\lambda_1\sigma_1(x)+\cdots+\lambda_m\sigma_m(x)=0
\qquad(x\in K)
$$

with the least possible number $m$ of nonzero coefficients. After relabelling and scaling, take every $\lambda_i\ne0$ and $\lambda_m=1$. Since the [field embeddings](../../../../../../field-embedding.md) $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_m$ are distinct, there is a $y\in K$ such that $\sigma_1(y)\ne\sigma_m(y)$.

Apply the relation to $xy$ and subtract $\sigma_m(y)$ times the relation at $x$. Because each [field embedding](../../../../../../field-embedding.md) is multiplicative, this gives

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}
\lambda_i\bigl(\sigma_i(y)-\sigma_m(y)\bigr)\sigma_i(x)=0
\qquad(x\in K).
$$

The $m$th term has disappeared, while the coefficient of $\sigma_1$ is nonzero. This is a shorter nontrivial relation, contradicting minimality. Hence distinct embeddings are linearly independent as functions. This is the [Artin independence theorem](../../../../../../linear-independence-of-distinct-field-embeddings.md).
