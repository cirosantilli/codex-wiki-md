<h1 id="31e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At an [equilibrium point](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md), $x^2-1=0$ and $kxy=0$. If $k\ne0$, the only equilibria are

$$
(1,0),\qquad(-1,0).
$$

Their [Jacobian matrix](../../../../../../jacobian-matrix.md) is

$$
DF(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}2x&0\\ky&kx\end{pmatrix}.
$$

At $(1,0)$ its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $2,k$, and at $(-1,0)$ they are $-2,-k$. Hence for $k>0$, $(1,0)$ is an [unstable node](../../../../../../unstable-node.md) and $(-1,0)$ is a [stable node](../../../../../../stable-node.md); for $k<0$, both points are [saddles](../../../../../../saddle-equilibrium.md).

If $k=0$, every point on each line $x=1$ and $x=-1$ is an equilibrium. The line $x=1$ is transversely repelling because the nonzero eigenvalue is $2$, while $x=-1$ is transversely attracting because the nonzero eigenvalue is $-2$; the tangential eigenvalue is zero on both nonisolated equilibrium lines.
