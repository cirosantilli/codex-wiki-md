<h1 id="22h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $x=(x_n)\in\ell^p$ with $1\leq p<\infty$, let

$$
x^{(N)}=(x_1,\ldots,x_N,0,0,\ldots)\in F.
$$

Then

$$
\lVert x-x^{(N)}\rVert_p^p
=\sum_{n>N}|x_n|^p\longrightarrow0
$$

because the defining nonnegative series converges. Thus $F=c_{00}$ is dense in the [l-p sequence space](../../../../../../l-p-sequence-space.md) for every finite $p$; this is the [density of finitely supported sequences in l-p](../../../../../../density-of-finitely-supported-sequences-in-l-p.md).

It is not dense in the [l-infinity sequence space](../../../../../../l-infinity-sequence-space.md). For the constant sequence $\mathbf1=(1,1,\ldots)$ and any finitely supported $y$, some tail of $y$ is zero, so

$$
\lVert\mathbf1-y\rVert_\infty=1.
$$

**Hence $\mathbf1$ does not belong to the closure of $F$.**
