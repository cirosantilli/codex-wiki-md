<h1 id="20g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Dirichlet unit theorem](../../../../../../dirichlet-s-unit-theorem.md) states that for a [number field](../../../../../../number-field.md) with $r$ real embeddings and $s$ conjugate pairs of complex embeddings,

$$
\mathcal O_K^\times\cong\mu(K)\times\mathbb Z^{r+s-1},
$$

where $\mu(K)$ is its finite group of [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). Here $K$ is real quadratic, so $(r,s)=(2,0)$ and $\mu(K)=\{\mathord\pm1\}$. Thus its unit group has rank one.

The element

$$
\varepsilon=1+\sqrt2
$$

is a unit because its [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) is

$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\varepsilon)
=(1+\sqrt2)(1-\sqrt2)=-1.
$$

It is the smallest unit greater than one in the chosen real embedding. Indeed, write such a unit as $u=a+b\sqrt2$ and let $u'=a-b\sqrt2=N(u)/u$ be its [algebraic conjugate](../../../../../../conjugate-element-field-theory.md). Since $|u'|<1<u$, both $a=(u+u')/2$ and $b=(u-u')/(2\sqrt2)$ are positive integers. Hence $a,b\geq1$ and $u\geq1+\sqrt2$. The rank-one logarithmic lattice in Dirichlet's theorem is therefore generated by $\log\varepsilon$. Consequently the [units of Q of square root two](../../../../../../units-of-q-of-square-root-two.md) are

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_K^\times
=\{\mathord\pm(1+\sqrt2)^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}}.
$$
