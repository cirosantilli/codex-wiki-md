↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Schur lemma](../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) over $\mathbb C$ states that an [intertwining operator](../../../../../../intertwining-operator.md) $T:V\to W$ between finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations is either zero or an isomorphism. In particular, every intertwining endomorphism of $V$ is a scalar multiple of the identity.

Indeed, $\ker T\subseteq V$ and $\operatorname{im}T\subseteq W$ are [invariant subspaces](../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md). If $T\ne0$, irreducibility gives $\ker T=0$ and $\operatorname{im}T=W$, so $T$ is an isomorphism. For an endomorphism $T:V\to V$, the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) gives an eigenvalue $\lambda\in\mathbb C$. Then $T-\lambda I$ is an intertwiner with nonzero kernel, so the first part forces $T-\lambda I=0$. Hence $T=\lambda I$, completing the [Proof of Schur lemma](../../../../../../proof-of-schur-lemma.md).
