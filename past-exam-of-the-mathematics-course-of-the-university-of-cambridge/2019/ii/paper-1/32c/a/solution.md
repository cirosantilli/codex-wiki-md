In canonical coordinates, [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) are

$$
\boxed{\dot q_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad
\dot p_i=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}\quad(1\leq i\leq n).}
$$

A function $F(q,p)$ is an [integral of motion](../../../../../../integral-of-motion.md) if it is constant along every [Hamiltonian flow](../../../../../../hamiltonian-flow.md), equivalently

$$
\dot F=\{F,H\}=0
$$

for a time-independent $F$. The system is an [integrable Hamiltonian system](../../../../../../integrable-hamiltonian-system.md) when it has $n$ first integrals $F_1,\ldots,F_n$ whose [differentials](../../../../../../total-differential.md) are linearly independent almost everywhere and which are in mutual [Poisson involution](../../../../../../poisson-bracket.md): $\{F_i,F_j\}=0$ for all $i,j$.
