<h1 id="7a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

One possible open contour begins at $t=0$ along a ray with $\operatorname{Re}(1/t)>0$, avoids the [essential singularity](../../../../../../essential-singularity.md) at the origin, and ends at infinity along a ray with $\operatorname{Re}(zt)<0$. For example, when $z>0$ is real, it may leave the origin along the positive real axis, bend through the upper half-plane, and approach the negative real axis as $|t|\to\infty$. At its initial end, $e^{-1/t}\to0$; at its final end, $e^{zt}\to0$. More generally, the terminal ray may be chosen with

$$
\arg t=\pi-\arg z,
$$

so that $zt$ is negative real. This contour satisfies the endpoint condition from part (a) and therefore produces a solution of the differential equation.
