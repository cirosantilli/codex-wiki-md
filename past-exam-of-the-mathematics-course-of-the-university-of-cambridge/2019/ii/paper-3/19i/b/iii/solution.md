<h1 id="19i/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Work with the selected transitive orbit $X\cong G/H$. By [Maschke's theorem](../../../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md), decompose its permutation character as

$$
1_H\mathbin{\uparrow}^G
=1_G+\sum_jm_j\chi_j,
$$

where the $\chi_j$ are distinct nontrivial [irreducible characters](../../../../../../../irreducible-character.md). [Character orthogonality](../../../../../../../character-orthogonality.md) gives

$$
\left\langle1_H\mathbin{\uparrow}^G,
1_H\mathbin{\uparrow}^G\right\rangle_G
=1+\sum_jm_j^2.
$$

Consequently the augmentation summand $V$ is [irreducible](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) exactly when this inner product equals $2$.

The [double cosets](../../../../../../../double-coset.md) $H\backslash G/H$ are the $H$-orbits on $G/H$. Because $G$ acts transitively on $X$, these in turn correspond to the $G$-orbits on $X\times X$: move the first coordinate to the base point $H$, after which its stabilizer is $H$. One orbit is the diagonal. There are exactly two orbits precisely when $H$ is transitive on $X\setminus\{H\}$, equivalently when the action is [two-transitive](../../../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md). Part (ii) therefore proves

$$
\boxed{V\text{ is irreducible}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G\text{ acts two-transitively on }X.}
$$

This is the [irreducible augmentation criterion for a transitive group action](../../../../../../../irreducible-augmentation-criterion-for-a-transitive-group-action.md).

Finally, transitivity implies that the fixed-vector space $\mathbb C[X]^G$ consists only of constant vectors and has dimension one. If the nonzero representation $V$ were a [trivial representation](../../../../../../../trivial-representation.md), it would contribute another fixed vector, a contradiction. Thus $V$ is not the trivial representation.
