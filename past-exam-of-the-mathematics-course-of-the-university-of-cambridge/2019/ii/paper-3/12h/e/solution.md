**No.** The property

$$
\mathcal S=\{n:|W_n|\text{ is a finite perfect square}\}
$$

is an [index set](../../../../../../index-set.md): it depends only on the domain $W_n$ of the partial function computed, not on the syntax of its program. It is nontrivial. For example, an index whose domain is a singleton belongs to $\mathcal S$, whereas an index whose domain has two elements does not. The [Rice theorem](../../../../../../rice-s-theorem.md) therefore shows that $\mathcal S$ is not a [computable set](../../../../../../computable-set.md), so no algorithm can decide the requested property for arbitrary $n$.

Invoking the [Church–Turing thesis](../../../../../../church-turing-thesis.md), this also rules out any effective procedure in the informal sense, because such a procedure would be represented by a computable function.
