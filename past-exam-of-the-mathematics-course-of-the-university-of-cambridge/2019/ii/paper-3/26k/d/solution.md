<h1 id="26k/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Set

$$
U=e^{-X},
\qquad
V=e^{-Y}.
$$

These are bounded random variables with values in $(0,1]$, and for every positive integer $n$,

$$
\mathbb E[U^n]=\psi_X(n)=\psi_Y(n)=\mathbb E[V^n].
$$

Part c shows that $U$ and $V$ have the same law. Since $x\mapsto e^{-x}$ is injective on $[0,\infty)$ with inverse $u\mapsto-\log u$, taking the [pushforward measure](../../../../../../pushforward-measure.md) by this inverse shows that $X$ and $Y$ have the same law. This proves the [uniqueness theorem for Laplace transforms of nonnegative random variables](../../../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-laplace-transforms-of-nonnegative-random-variables.md).
