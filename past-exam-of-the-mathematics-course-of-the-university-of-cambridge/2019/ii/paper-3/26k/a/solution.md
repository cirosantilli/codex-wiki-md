<h1 id="26k/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\mu_X$ and $\mu_Y$ be the [probability laws](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) of $X$ and $Y$. The hypothesis says

$$
\int_{\mathbb R}f\,d\mu_X
=\mathbb E[f(X)]
=\mathbb E[f(Y)]
=\int_{\mathbb R}f\,d\mu_Y
$$

for every compactly supported [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f$. The theorem that [compactly supported continuous functions determine a finite Borel measure](../../../../../../compactly-supported-continuous-functions-determine-a-finite-borel-measure.md) gives $\mu_X=\mu_Y$. Hence $X$ and $Y$ have the same law.
