<h1 id="21h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose for contradiction that the [vector space of univariate polynomials](../../../../../../vector-space-of-univariate-polynomials.md) $P$ were complete in the given [norm](../../../../../../norm.md). For $n\geq0$, let

$$
P_n=\{p\in P:\deg p\leq n\}.
$$

Each $P_n$ is finite-dimensional and therefore closed by the fact that a [finite-dimensional subspace is closed](../../../../../../finite-dimensional-subspace-is-closed.md). It is a proper linear subspace of $P$, so it has empty interior: if a linear subspace contained any open ball, translation and scalar multiplication would make it contain every vector. Hence every $P_n$ is a [nowhere dense set](../../../../../../nowhere-dense-set.md).

But every polynomial has finite degree, so

$$
P=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}P_n.
$$

This writes the supposed complete metric space $P$ as a countable union of nowhere-dense closed subsets, contradicting the [Baire category theorem](../../../../../../baire-category-theorem.md). Thus no norm can make $P$ complete. Equivalently, this is the countable-basis obstruction that a [Banach space has uncountable Hamel dimension](../../../../../../banach-space-has-uncountable-hamel-dimension.md).
