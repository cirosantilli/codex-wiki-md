<h1 id="8e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [adiabatic invariance of the action](../../../../../../adiabatic-invariance-of-the-action.md) applies when the frequency changes by only a small fraction during one oscillation and no [separatrix](../../../../../../separatrix.md) is encountered. A convenient condition is

$$
\left|\frac{\dot\omega}{\omega^2}\right|\ll1,
\qquad \omega(t)=\sqrt{\frac{k(t)}m}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{I=\frac E\omega\simeq\text{constant}.}
$$

Consequently

$$
E=I\omega\propto\sqrt{k}.
$$

If $A$ is the displacement amplitude, $E=\tfrac12kA^2$, and hence

$$
A=\sqrt{\frac{2I}{m\omega}}
\propto\omega^{-1/2}
\propto k^{-1/4}.
$$

**Thus a slowly stiffened spring raises the energy as $k^{1/2}$ while reducing the amplitude as $k^{-1/4}$.**
