↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**False.** Take $x=\mathcal P(\omega)$, the [power set](../../../../../../power-set.md) of the first infinite [ordinal](../../../../../../ordinal.md) $\omega$. Every subset of $\omega$ has rank at most $\omega$, and an infinite cofinal subset has rank exactly $\omega$, so

$$
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\mathcal P(\omega)\bigr)=\omega+1,
$$

which is a [countable ordinal](../../../../../../countable-ordinal.md). But [Cantor theorem](../../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) shows that $\mathcal P(\omega)$ is an [uncountable set](../../../../../../uncountable-set.md).
