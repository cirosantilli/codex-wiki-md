<h1 id="16i/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**True.** If $x$ is a nonempty [finite set](../../../../../../finite-set.md), then

$$
\operatorname{rank}(x)
=\sup_{y\in x}\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(y)+1\bigr)
=\max_{y\in x}\bigl(\operatorname{rank}(y)+1\bigr).
$$

This maximum is a [successor ordinal](../../../../../../successor-ordinal.md), whereas the [empty set](../../../../../../empty-set.md) has rank $0$. Hence a set of nonzero [limit ordinal](../../../../../../limit-ordinal.md) rank cannot be finite and must be an [infinite set](../../../../../../infinite-set.md).
