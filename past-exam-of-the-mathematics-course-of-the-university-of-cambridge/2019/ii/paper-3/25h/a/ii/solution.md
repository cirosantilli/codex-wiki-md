<h1 id="25h/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [first fundamental form of a surface of revolution](../../../../../../../first-fundamental-form-of-a-surface-of-revolution.md) is

$$
I=f(v)^2\,du^2+q(v)^2\,dv^2,
\qquad
q(v)^2=f'(v)^2+g'(v)^2.
$$

The [geodesic Lagrangian](../../../../../../../geodesic-lagrangian.md) is consequently

$$
L=\frac12\left(f(v)^2u'^2+q(v)^2v'^2\right).
$$

The rotation angle $u$ is a [cyclic coordinate](../../../../../../../cyclic-coordinate.md), so its [conjugate momentum](../../../../../../../canonical-momentum.md) is conserved by the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md):

$$
\frac d{ds}\frac{\partial L}{\partial u'}
=\frac d{ds}\left(f(v)^2u'\right)=0.
$$

Thus $f(v(s))^2u'(s)$ is constant.

The positively oriented unit tangent to the [parallel of a surface of revolution](../../../../../../../parallel-of-a-surface-of-revolution.md) through $\gamma(s)$ is $e_u=\phi_u/f(v)$. Since $\gamma$ has unit speed and $\theta$ is its angle with that parallel,

$$
\cos\theta(s)
=\langle\gamma'(s),e_u\rangle
=f(v(s))u'(s).
$$

Multiplication by $f(v(s))$ yields

$$
\boxed{f(v(s))\cos\theta(s)=f(v(s))^2u'(s)=\text{constant},}
$$

which is [Clairaut's relation](../../../../../../../clairaut-first-integral-for-a-surface-of-revolution.md).
