<h1 id="25h/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let

$$
c=f(v(s))^2u'(s)=f(v(s))\cos\theta(s)
$$

be the [Clairaut first integral for a surface of revolution](../../../../../../../clairaut-first-integral-for-a-surface-of-revolution.md) from part a(ii). If $c\ne0$, then $|\cos\theta|\leq1$ gives

$$
f(v(s))\geq|c|
$$

for every $s$. Since $f$ tends to zero at both poles, the geodesic avoids nonempty open neighbourhoods of the poles.

If $c=0$, then $u'(s)=0$ whenever the geodesic is away from a pole. It consequently lies in one [meridian of a surface of revolution](../../../../../../../meridian-of-a-surface-of-revolution.md); after crossing a pole, the angular coordinate changes by $\pi$ but remains in the same plane through the rotation axis. A small surface neighbourhood of any point outside that meridian is disjoint from the geodesic. In either case there is a nonempty open set $U$ with

$$
\gamma(\mathbb R)\cap U=\varnothing.
$$

This proves that the [geodesic image on a compact two-pole surface of revolution is not dense](../../../../../../../geodesic-image-on-a-compact-two-pole-surface-of-revolution-is-not-dense.md).
