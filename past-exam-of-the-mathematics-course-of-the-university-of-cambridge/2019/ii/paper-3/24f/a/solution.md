<h1 id="24f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Homogenizing the affine equation gives the [projective closure](../../../../../../projective-completion.md)

$$
X=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^2,
\qquad
F(X,Y,Z)=Y^3Z-X^4-Z^4.
$$

Its partial derivatives are

$$
F_X=-4X^3,
\qquad
F_Y=3Y^2Z,
\qquad
F_Z=Y^3-4Z^3.
$$

If all three vanished at a point of $X$, then $X=0$ and either $Y=0$ or $Z=0$. If $Y=0$, the equation $F=0$ forces $Z=0$; if $Z=0$, the equation $F_Z=0$ forces $Y=0$. Either conclusion gives the forbidden zero triple of [homogeneous coordinates](../../../../../../homogeneous-coordinate.md). The [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md) therefore shows that $X$ is a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md); more specifically, it is the [projective closure of y cubed equals x to the fourth plus one](../../../../../../projective-closure-of-y-cubed-equals-x-to-the-fourth-plus-one.md), a [smooth plane quartic](../../../../../../smooth-plane-quartic.md).
