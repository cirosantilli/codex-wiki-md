<h1 id="19i/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $T$ commute with the given representation. Since $A$ and $C$ are diagonal and their four joint pairs of eigenvalues are distinct, commuting with both forces $T$ to be diagonal. Commuting with the permutation matrix $B$, which exchanges coordinates $1\leftrightarrow2$ and $3\leftrightarrow4$, equates the first two and last two diagonal entries. Commuting with $D$, which exchanges $1\leftrightarrow3$ and $2\leftrightarrow4$, makes all four entries equal. Thus the [commutant](../../../../../../../centralizer.md) consists only of scalar matrices. Since the representation is unitary, a proper invariant subspace would have an orthogonal projection in the commutant. No such nonscalar projection exists, so the representation is irreducible.
