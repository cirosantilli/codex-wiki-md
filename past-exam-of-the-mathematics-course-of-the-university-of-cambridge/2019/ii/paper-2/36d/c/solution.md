<h1 id="36d/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The function $f$ tends to $-\infty$ both as $r\to0^+$ and as $r\to\infty$. Its only positive stationary point is

$$
r_0=\left(\frac m{H^2}\right)^{1/3},
$$

and it is a maximum with

$$
f(r_0)=1-3(mH)^{2/3}.
$$

Therefore $f$ has two positive simple roots exactly when

$$
mH<\frac1{3\sqrt3}=\frac1{\sqrt{27}}.
$$

At $r_c=1/(\sqrt3H)$,

$$
f(r_c)=\frac23-2\sqrt3mH>0.
$$

Since $f$ is positive precisely between its two positive roots, this proves

$$
\boxed{r_-<\frac1{\sqrt3H}<r_+.}
$$
