<h1 id="5j/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For participants with the same recorded history, the first [linear regression](../../../../../../linear-regression-split.md) estimates that one additional average mile cycled per week is associated with $0.04917$ fewer asthma episodes. The corresponding $p$-value is $0.00761$, giving evidence against a zero conditional association.

The second model estimates that, among participants with the same history, receiving the incentive is associated with $0.91387$ more episodes. The third estimates that receiving the incentive is associated with $1.73282$ more miles cycled per week. Both incentive coefficients have very small [p-values](../../../../../../p-value.md). Because the incentive was assigned in a [randomized controlled trial](../../../../../../randomized-controlled-trial.md), these last two comparisons estimate a [causal effect](../../../../../../causal-effect.md) of the incentive.
