<h1 id="21f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Writing the square coordinates modulo one, the curve has the parametrization

$$
t\longmapsto(2t,3t)\pmod1.
$$

It is therefore the $(2,3)$ [torus knot](../../../../../../torus-knot.md), or trefoil, on the common boundary of the two solid tori. Applying the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) to the complements on the two sides gives one generator $a$ for the meridian of one solid torus and one generator $b$ for the other; the boundary slope identifies two turns of the first with three turns of the second. Hence

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(S-K)\cong\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^3\rangle.}
$$

This group is nonabelian: imposing the additional relation $a^2=b^3=1$ gives a quotient

$$
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^3=1\rangle\cong C_2*C_3,
$$

which maps onto the nonabelian group $S_3$ by sending $a$ to a transposition and $b$ to a three-cycle. An abelian group cannot have a nonabelian quotient.
