Saying that $U$ has law $\mu$ means that $\mu$ is the [pushforward measure](../../../../../../pushforward-measure.md)

$$
\mu=\mathbb P\circ U^{-1},
\qquad
\mu(A)=\mathbb P(U\in A).
$$

For a real random variable, $\mu$ is a probability measure on $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$.

The pair $U,V$ consists of [independent random variables](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) when

$$
\mathbb P(U\in A,V\in B)
=\mathbb P(U\in A)\mathbb P(V\in B)
$$

for every pair of Borel sets $A,B$; equivalently, their joint law is the product of their marginal laws.
