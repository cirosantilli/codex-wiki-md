<h1 id="22h/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For $F_1$, the functions satisfy $\|\sin(\pi nx)/n\|_\infty\leq1/n$, so the sequence converges uniformly to zero. Its closure is the sequence together with its limit and is compact. The same argument applies to $F_2$, since $\|\sin(\pi nx)/\sqrt n\|_\infty\leq n^{-1/2}$. Thus $F_1$ and $F_2$ are relatively compact.

The family $F_3$ is not [equicontinuous](../../../../../../equicontinuity.md). At $x=0$ its members vanish, but for $x_n=1/(2n)\to0$,

$$
\sin(\pi n x_n)=1.
$$

By the [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](../../../../../../arzela-ascoli-theorem.md), a relatively compact subset of $C[0,1]$ must be equicontinuous. Hence $F_3$ is not relatively compact.
