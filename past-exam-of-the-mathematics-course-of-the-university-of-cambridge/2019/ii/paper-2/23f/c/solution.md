<h1 id="23f/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The map $f$ is bounded by one near the puncture, so the [Riemann removable singularity theorem](../../../../../../riemann-removable-singularity-theorem.md) extends it holomorphically to a map $\widetilde f:\Delta\to\mathbb C$. The [maximum modulus principle](../../../../../../maximum-modulus-principle.md) shows that its image still lies in $\Delta$: if $|\widetilde f(0)|=1$, the extension would be constant, contradicting its values in $\Delta\setminus\{0\}$ unless that constant also had modulus less than one.

If $f$ is biholomorphic, its inverse $g:\Delta\setminus\{0\}\to\Delta\setminus\{0\}$ also extends holomorphically. Were $a=\widetilde f(0)\ne0$, continuity of $\widetilde g\circ\widetilde f$ and the identity $g(f(z))=z$ on the punctured disk would give $g(a)=0$. This is impossible because $a\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}$ and $g$ takes values in the punctured disk. Hence

$$
\boxed{\widetilde f(0)=0.}
$$
