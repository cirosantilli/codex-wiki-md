## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 143](paper-143.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [Nielsen–Schreier theorem](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#nielsen-schreier-theorem) says that every [subgroup](../../../group.md#subgroup) of a [free group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#free-group) is free. Realize $F_n$ as the [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) of the rose $R_n$, the graph with one vertex and $n$ oriented loops. A subgroup $H\leq F_n$ of finite [index of a subgroup](../../../group.md#index-of-a-subgroup) $d$ corresponds to a connected $d$-sheeted [covering graph](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#covering-graph) $X\to R_n$. The graph $X$ has $d$ vertices and $dn$ unoriented edges. Choosing a [spanning tree](../../../combinatorics.md#spanning-tree) leaves

$$
dn-(d-1)=1+d(n-1)
$$

edges outside the tree, and these freely generate $\pi_1(X)\cong H$. Thus the [Nielsen–Schreier formula](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#nielsen-schreier-formula) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(H)=1+d(n-1).}
$$

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Take the [free abelian group](../../../group-theory.md#free-abelian-group) $G=\mathbb Z^2$, whose [rank of a group](../../../group.md#rank-of-a-group) is two, and its [finite-index subgroup](../../../group.md#finite-index-subgroup) $H=2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$. The subgroup has index two and is again isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^2$, so its rank is two. The [Nielsen–Schreier formula](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#nielsen-schreier-formula) would instead give $1+2(2-1)=3$. Hence

$$
\boxed{G=\mathbb Z^2,\qquad H=2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z}
$$

is a counterexample.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $r=d(G)$ be the [rank of a group](../../../group.md#rank-of-a-group), let $S$ be a generating set of size $r$, and suppose $[G:H]=d$. Choose a Schreier transversal adapted to a spanning tree in the [Schreier coset graph](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#schreier-coset-graph). By [Schreier's lemma](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#schreier-s-lemma), $H$ is generated by the elements

$$
ts\overline{ts}^{-1}
\qquad(t\in T, s\in S).
$$

There are $dr$ candidates. The $d-1$ oriented edges in the spanning tree give trivial candidates, leaving at most $dr-(d-1)$. This proves the [Schreier index-rank inequality](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#schreier-index-rank-inequality)

$$
\boxed{d(H)\leq1+d\bigl(d(G)-1\bigr).}
$$

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Let $G$ have a [generating set of a group](../../../group.md#generating-set-of-a-group) with $r$ elements. Every subgroup $H$ of index $n$ gives a [transitive group action](../../../group-theory.md#transitive-group-action) of $G$ on the $n$ left cosets of $H$, hence a [group homomorphism](../../../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $\rho_H:G\to S_n$ after the cosets are labelled. Conversely, $H$ is the [stabilizer subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#stabilizer-subgroup) of one point in this action.

A homomorphism $G\to S_n$ is determined by the images of the $r$ generators, so there are at most $(n!)^r$ such homomorphisms and at most $n(n!)^r$ point stabilizers. Therefore the [finiteness of subgroups of a fixed finite index](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#finiteness-of-subgroups-of-a-fixed-finite-index) gives **only finitely many subgroups of index $n$**.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For a finite generating set $S$ of $G$, the [Cayley graph](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#cayley-graph) $\operatorname{Cay}_S(G)$ has vertex set $G$ and an edge from $g$ to $gs$ for every $g\in G$ and $s\in S\cup S^{-1}$; one may retain orientations and labels, or forget them.

As unlabelled undirected graphs, the Cayley graph of the [infinite cyclic group](../../../group.md#infinite-cyclic-group) $\mathbb Z$ with generator $1$ and the Cayley graph of the [infinite dihedral group](../../../representation-theory.md#infinite-dihedral-group)

$$
D_\infty=C_2*C_2=\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle
$$

with generators $a,b$ are both the two-way infinite line. The first group is an [abelian group](../../../group.md#abelian-group) and the second is not, so they are not isomorphic. Thus the requested example is the [infinite line as a Cayley graph](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#infinite-line-as-a-cayley-graph) of **$\mathbb Z$ and $D_\infty$**.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

A map $f:X\to Y$ is a [quasi-isometry](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#quasi-isometry) if there are $\lambda\geq1$ and $\varepsilon,R\geq0$ such that

$$
\lambda^{-1}d_X(x,x')-\varepsilon\leq d_Y(fx,fx')\leq\lambda d_X(x,x')+\varepsilon
$$

for all $x,x'\in X$, and every point of $Y$ lies within distance $R$ of $f(X)$. The [Milnor–Švarc lemma](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#milnor-svarc-lemma) says that a group acting properly discontinuously, cocompactly and isometrically on a proper geodesic metric space is finitely generated, and each orbit map from a [word metric](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#word-metric) is a quasi-isometry.

Now let $S,T$ be finite generating sets of $G$. Put

$$
L=\max_{s\in S}|s|_T,
\qquad
M=\max_{t\in T}|t|_S.
$$

Replacing each letter in an $S$-word by a $T$-word and conversely gives

$$
M^{-1}d_S(g,h)\leq d_T(g,h)\leq Ld_S(g,h).
$$

Thus the identity map is a [bilipschitz equivalence](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#bilipschitz-equivalence), hence a quasi-isometry. **All finite generating sets of a finitely generated group give quasi-isometric Cayley graphs.**

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Suppose first that $\phi:G\to H$ is a $(\lambda,\varepsilon)$-quasi-isometry. If $k\in\ker\phi$, the lower quasi-isometry inequality gives

$$
\lambda^{-1}|k|_G-\varepsilon\leq d_H(1,\phi(k))=0,
$$

so the [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../group-theory.md#kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism) lies in the finite word-metric ball of radius $\lambda\varepsilon$ and is finite. Coarse surjectivity gives an $R$ such that every $h\in H$ is within $R$ of $\phi(G)$. The finite ball $B_H(1,R)$ therefore contains representatives for every coset of $\phi(G)$, so $H/\phi(G)$ is finite.

Conversely, suppose $\ker\phi$ is finite and $phi(G)$ is a [finite-index subgroup](../../../group.md#finite-index-subgroup) of $H$. The map factors as

$$
G\longrightarrow G/\ker\phi\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\phi(G)\hookrightarrow H.
$$

The first arrow is a [finite-kernel quotient quasi-isometry](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#finite-kernel-quotient-quasi-isometry), the middle arrow is an isomorphism of finitely generated groups, and the last arrow is a [finite-index subgroup quasi-isometry](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#finite-index-subgroup-quasi-isometry). Their composition is a quasi-isometry. Hence the [quasi-isometry criterion for a group homomorphism](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#quasi-isometry-criterion-for-a-group-homomorphism) is

$$
\boxed{\phi\text{ is a quasi-isometry}\iff |\ker\phi|<\infty\text{ and }[H:\phi(G)]<\infty.}
$$

Yes. Take $G=F_2$. The [free product](../../../algebraic-topology.md#free-product) satisfies

$$
G*G\cong F_2*F_2\cong F_4.
$$

A connected three-sheeted cover of the two-petal rose has [fundamental group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#fundamental-group) of rank $1+3(2-1)=4$ by the [Nielsen–Schreier formula](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#nielsen-schreier-formula). Thus $F_4$ is isomorphic to an index-three subgroup of $F_2$. A [finite-index subgroup quasi-isometry](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#finite-index-subgroup-quasi-isometry) then gives

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 143](paper-143.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For a locally finite [discrete metric](../../../topological-analysis.md#discrete-metric) space $(X,d)$ with basepoint $x_0$, the [growth function of a discrete metric space](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#growth-function-of-a-discrete-metric-space) is

$$
\beta_{X,x_0}(r)=|B(x_0,r)|
=\#\{x\in X:d(x,x_0)\leq r\}.
$$

In a [Cayley graph](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#cayley-graph), left multiplication by $gx_0^{-1}$ is a graph isometry carrying $x_0$ to any other vertex $g$. It therefore gives a bijection $B(x_0,r)\to B(g,r)$ for every $r$. Thus **the growth function of a fixed Cayley graph is exactly independent of the basepoint**.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Choose any finite generating set $T$ of the [finitely generated group](../../../group.md#finitely-generated-group) $H$. Starting from a finite generating set $S_0$ of $G$, set

$$
S=S_0\cup T.
$$

Every $T$-word in $H$ of length at most $r$ is also an $S$-word in $G$ of length at most $r$. The inclusion of the corresponding word-metric balls is injective, so the [growth function of a finitely generated group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#growth-function-of-a-finitely-generated-group) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\beta_{H,T}(r)\leq\beta_{G,S}(r)\qquad(r\geq0).}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

A finitely generated group has [polynomial growth of a group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#polynomial-growth-of-a-group) if its growth function is bounded above by $C(1+r)^D$ for some constants $C,D$.

Let $G$ be generated by $s_1,\ldots,s_k$. Since $G$ is a [two-step nilpotent group](../../../group-theory.md#two-step-nilpotent-group), every [group commutator](../../../group.md#group-commutator) $[s_i,s_j]$ is central. Commuting letters past one another therefore collects every word of length at most $n$ into the form

$$
s_1^{a_1}\cdots s_k^{a_k}
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}[s_i,s_j]^{b_{ij}}.
$$

The generator exponents satisfy $|a_i|\leq n$. At most $n(n-1)/2$ exchanges are needed during collection, so $|b_{ij}|\leq n^2$ is a sufficient bound. With $m=k(k-1)/2$, the number of possible collected expressions is at most

$$
(2n+1)^k(2n^2+1)^m=O\!\left(n^{k+2m}\right).
$$

Relations can only reduce this count. This proves the [polynomial growth of a finitely generated two-step nilpotent group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#polynomial-growth-of-a-finitely-generated-two-step-nilpotent-group), so **every finitely generated two-step nilpotent group has polynomial growth**.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Consider the matrices

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z).
$$

Their projective action on the positive real line is

$$
A(x)=x+1\in(1,\infty),
\qquad
B(x)=\frac{x}{x+1}\in(0,1).
$$

These disjoint images give the monoid version of the [ping-pong lemma](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#ping-pong-lemma): after removing a common initial letter, two different positive words act differently. Thus $A,B$ generate a [free monoid](../../../algebra.md#free-monoid). There are $2^n$ distinct positive words of length $n$, all lying in the radius-$n$ word ball for any generating set enlarged to contain $A,B$. Therefore the [Exponential growth of SL2 of Z](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#exponential-growth-of-sl2-of-z) gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\text{ has exponential growth}.}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 143](paper-143.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

An [amenable group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#amenable-group) is a group $G$ admitting a left-invariant [finitely additive probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#finitely-additive-probability-measure) $m:\mathcal P(G)\to[0,1]$. For a $G$-set $X$, subsets $A,B\subseteq X$ are [equidecomposable subsets under a group action](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#equidecomposable-subsets-under-a-group-action) if there are finite partitions $A=\bigsqcup_iA_i$, $B=\bigsqcup_iB_i$ and elements $g_i\in G$ such that $g_iA_i=B_i$. The action is a [paradoxical group action](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#paradoxical-group-action) if $X$ contains two disjoint subsets, each $G$-equidecomposable with $X$.

To prove the [nonamenability of a nonabelian free group](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#nonamenability-of-a-nonabelian-free-group), write $F_2=\langle a,b\rangle$ and let $W(s)$ be the set of nonempty reduced words beginning with $s\in\{a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\}$. Cancellation of the first letter gives the disjoint decompositions

$$
F_2=W(a)\sqcup aW(a^{-1}),
\qquad
F_2=W(b)\sqcup bW(b^{-1}).
$$

If an invariant measure $m$ existed, these would imply

$$
1=m(W(a))+m(W(a^{-1})),
\qquad
1=m(W(b))+m(W(b^{-1})).
$$

Every singleton has measure zero: invariance gives all singletons the same measure, and finite additivity over arbitrarily many distinct points forces that measure to vanish. The four sets $W(s)$ partition $F_2\setminus\{1\}$, so their measures sum to one. The two displayed equations say that the same sum is two, a contradiction. Hence

$$
\boxed{F_2\text{ is not amenable}.}
$$

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

For a finite generating set $S$, the [Følner condition](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#folner-condition) requires that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there be a nonempty finite $F\subseteq G$ such that

$$
\frac{|sF\mathbin\triangle F|}{|F|}<\varepsilon
\qquad(s\in S),
$$

where $\mathbin\triangle$ denotes [symmetric difference](../../../set.md#symmetric-difference). Choose a [Følner sequence](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#folner-sequence) $(F_n)$ and define normalized counting functions on all subsets $A\subseteq G$ by

$$
\mu_n(A)=\frac{|A\cap F_n|}{|F_n|}.
$$

By compactness of the product $[0,1]^{\mathcal P(G)}$, some subnet converges pointwise to a function $m$. The identities $\mu_n(G)=1$ and finite additivity on disjoint subsets pass to the limit, so $m$ is a [finitely additive probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#finitely-additive-probability-measure).

For a fixed $g=s_1\cdots s_\ell$, the triangle inequality for symmetric differences gives

$$
\frac{|gF_n\mathbin\triangle F_n|}{|F_n|}
\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}
\frac{|s_jF_n\mathbin\triangle F_n|}{|F_n|}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Consequently

$$
|\mu_n(gA)-\mu_n(A)|
\leq\frac{|g^{-1}F_n\mathbin\triangle F_n|}{|F_n|}\longrightarrow0,
$$

so $m(gA)=m(A)$. The limit is left invariant and the [Følner condition implies amenability](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#folner-condition-implies-amenability). Thus **every finitely generated group satisfying the Følner condition is amenable**.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

For the standard generator $1$ of the additive group $\mathbb Z$, take the intervals

$$
F_n=\{-n,-n+1,\ldots,n\}.
$$

Only the two endpoints change under translation by $1$, and hence

$$
\frac{|(F_n+1)\mathbin\triangle F_n|}{|F_n|}
=\frac{2}{2n+1}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The same follows for every fixed integer translation by iteration. Therefore these sets are a [Følner sequence for the integers](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#folner-sequence-for-the-integers), and **$\mathbb Z$ satisfies the Følner condition**.

<h3 id="4/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

Yes, but there is only one nontrivial isomorphism type that works. If $G=C_2$, then

$$
G*G=C_2*C_2\cong D_\infty.
$$

The [infinite dihedral group](../../../representation-theory.md#infinite-dihedral-group) contains its infinite cyclic rotation subgroup with index two, so it is amenable by [virtually abelian groups are amenable](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#virtually-abelian-groups-are-amenable).

Suppose now that $G\not\cong C_2$. The action of $G*G$ on its [Bass-Serre tree](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#bass-serre-tree) is non-elementary: one vertex degree is greater than two, so there are hyperbolic elements with disjoint pairs of endpoints. Suitable powers satisfy the [ping-pong lemma](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#ping-pong-lemma) on the boundary and generate a copy of $F_2$. Since [a subgroup of an amenable group is amenable](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#a-subgroup-of-an-amenable-group-is-amenable), an amenable group cannot contain this nonamenable subgroup. The [amenability of a free product](../../../geometric-group-theory.md#amenability-of-a-free-product) therefore yields

$$
\boxed{G*G\text{ is amenable}\iff G\cong C_2}
$$

for nontrivial finitely generated $G$.
