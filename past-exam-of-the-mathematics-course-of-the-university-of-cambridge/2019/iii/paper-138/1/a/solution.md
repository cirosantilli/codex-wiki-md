<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An $A$-module is a [semisimple module](../../../../../../semisimple-module.md) when it is a direct sum of [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md), equivalently when every submodule has a complementary submodule. The finite-dimensional algebra $A$ is a [semisimple algebra](../../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) when its left regular module ${}_AA$ is semisimple.

Let $C_{p^n}=\langle g\rangle$, put $N=p^n$, and suppose $\operatorname{char}k=p$. The [freshman's dream](../../../../../../freshman-s-dream.md) gives

$$
g^N-1=(g-1)^N,
$$

so, with $u=g-1$,

$$
kC_{p^n}\cong k[u]/(u^N).
$$

The [indecomposable modules of a cyclic p-group in characteristic p](../../../../../../indecomposable-modules-of-a-cyclic-p-group-in-characteristic-p.md) are precisely

$$
\boxed{M_r=k[u]/(u^r),\qquad1\leq r\leq N.}
$$

Indeed, a module is a vector space with a nilpotent operator $u$, and its decomposition into [Nilpotent Jordan blocks](../../../../../../nilpotent-jordan-block.md) gives these modules. Each $M_r$ is a [uniserial module](../../../../../../uniserial-module.md), with unique chain

$$
M_r\supset uM_r\supset\cdots\supset u^{r-1}M_r\supset0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{J(M_r)=uM_r,\qquad\operatorname{Soc}(M_r)=u^{r-1}M_r.}
$$
