<h1 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

For equal logarithmic width $d\log D$, the number of bodies is $n(D)D\,d\log D$. Their mass is therefore

$$
dM=\frac{\pi\rho K}{6}D^{4-\alpha}\,d\log D.
$$

The mass removed by [catastrophic planetesimal collisions](../../../../../../catastrophic-planetesimal-collision.md) per logarithmic bin per unit time scales as

$$
\dot M_{\rm loss}(D)\propto D^{4-\alpha}R_{\rm cc}(D).
$$

Assume a steady [collisional cascade](../../../../../../collisional-cascade.md) with supply only at the large end, loss only at the small end, negligible reassembly or cratering contributions, and self-similar fragmentation that transfers mass through comparable logarithmic intervals. [Mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) requires the downward mass flux across every intermediate size boundary to be the same; otherwise mass would accumulate or deplete there. Under the scale-invariant bin-transfer assumption, this is proportional by a size-independent factor to $\dot M_{\rm loss}$, so

$$
\boxed{D^{4-\alpha}R_{\rm cc}(D)=\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

This is [constant mass flux in a collisional cascade](../../../../../../constant-mass-flux-in-a-collisional-cascade.md). Steady state alone fixes the net flux, while the identification with the destruction rate of each bin additionally uses the stated fragmentation assumptions.
