<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the [gravitational focusing](../../../../../../gravitational-focusing.md) regime, take $d\ll D$ and $v_{\rm rel}\ll v_{\rm esc}$ at large separation. Mutual gravitational acceleration then makes the impact speed approximately the target's [escape velocity](../../../../../../escape-velocity.md):

$$
v_{\rm imp}^2\simeq v_{\rm esc}^2
=\frac{2G(\pi\rho D^3/6)}{D/2}
=\frac{2\pi}{3}G\rho D^2.
$$

Setting the [specific impact energy](../../../../../../specific-impact-energy.md) equal to the [catastrophic disruption threshold](../../../../../../catastrophic-disruption-threshold.md) gives

$$
\boxed{D_{\rm cc}\simeq\left(\frac{3Q_b}{\pi G\rho}\right)^{1/3}D^{(1+b)/3}.}
$$

The size-dependent far-field encounter speed drops out of this leading threshold because gravitational acceleration dominates at impact. [Gravitational focusing](../../../../../../gravitational-focusing.md) also increases the collision cross-section by approximately $1+v_{\rm esc}^2/v_{\rm rel}^2$, but this concerns the rate rather than the energy threshold. These formulas assume the disruption-energy convention remains per unit target mass and that gravitationally accelerated impact energy couples to disruption as specified by $Q_D^*$.
