<h1 id="2/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Combining [constant mass flux in a collisional cascade](../../../../../../constant-mass-flux-in-a-collisional-cascade.md) with the rate in part (iv) gives

$$
\dot M_{\rm loss}(D)\propto
D^{6+p-\alpha+(1-\alpha)(3+b-2p)/3}.
$$

Set the exponent to zero and multiply by three:

$$
3(6+p-\alpha)+(1-\alpha)(3+b-2p)=0,
$$

or $21+b+p-\alpha(6+b-2p)=0$. Therefore the [velocity-dependent collisional cascade](../../../../../../velocity-dependent-collisional-cascade.md) has

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{21+b+p}{6+b-2p}.}
$$

For $b=p=0$, this recovers the [Dohnanyi collisional cascade](../../../../../../dohnanyi-collisional-cascade.md) slope $\alpha=7/2$. The denominator must be nonzero, and the resulting slope and destructive cutoff must satisfy the assumptions used for the collision-rate integral. A size-dependent vertical belt thickness would supply an additional volume factor and change this result.
