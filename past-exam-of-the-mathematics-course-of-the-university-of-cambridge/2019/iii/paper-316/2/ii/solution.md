<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $d$ be an impactor diameter and assume it is much smaller than target diameter $D$, with equal material [mass density](../../../../../../density.md). Neglect [gravitational focusing](../../../../../../gravitational-focusing.md) and take the encounter speed to be the stated target-size law $v_{\rm rel}=v_pD^p$. The [specific impact energy](../../../../../../specific-impact-energy.md) is

$$
Q=\frac12\left(\frac dD\right)^3v_p^2D^{2p}.
$$

A [catastrophic planetesimal collision](../../../../../../catastrophic-planetesimal-collision.md) requires $Q\ge Q_D^*=Q_bD^b$. Therefore the [catastrophic projectile diameter](../../../../../../catastrophic-projectile-diameter.md) is

$$
\boxed{D_{\rm cc}=\left(\frac{2Q_b}{v_p^2}\right)^{1/3}D^{1+(b-2p)/3}.}
$$

Use $\max(D_{\rm cc},D_{\min})$ as the actual lower size cutoff when integrating a rate. If this exceeds $D_{\max}$, the specified population contains no destructive impactors. The small-impactor approximation itself requires $D_{\rm cc}\ll D$.
