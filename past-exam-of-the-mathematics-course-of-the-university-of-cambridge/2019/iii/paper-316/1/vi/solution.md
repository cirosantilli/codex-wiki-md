<h1 id="1/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

The [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) is $h=\sqrt{GM_\star a(1-e^2)}$, so the [periapsis angular speed](../../../../../../periapsis-angular-speed.md) is

$$
\dot\theta_{\rm peri}=\frac h{a^2(1-e)^2}
=n\frac{\sqrt{1+e}}{(1-e)^{3/2}}.
$$

At the [exterior mean-motion resonance](../../../../../../exterior-mean-motion-resonance.md), neglecting [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md), $n/n_p=p/(p+q)$. Thus at the limiting [orbital eccentricity](../../../../../../orbital-eccentricity.md) $e=\sqrt{2q/(5p-q)}$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\dot\theta_{\rm peri}}{n_p}
=\frac p{p+q}\frac{\sqrt{1+\sqrt{2q/(5p-q)}}}{[1-\sqrt{2q/(5p-q)}]^{3/2}}.}
$$

The condition for overtaking the planet in angle is

$$
\boxed{\frac{p^2}{(p+q)^2}(1+e)>(1-e)^3,\qquad e=\sqrt{\frac{2q}{5p-q}}<1.}
$$

For the formal second-order equilibrium, it holds for every physical bound value $0<q<5p/3$. To see this, rewrite $q/p=5e^2/(2+e^2)$, so $p/(p+q)=(2+e^2)/(2+6e^2)$. For $0<e<1$, $\sqrt{1+e}/(1-e)^{3/2}>1+2e$, while

$$
(2+e^2)(1+2e)-(2+6e^2)=e(4-5e+2e^2)>0.
$$

The instantaneous angular motion is therefore prograde relative to the planet near [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md), although its mean relative motion is retrograde. The range $q\ll p$ is the regime where the eccentricity expansion is most reliable.
