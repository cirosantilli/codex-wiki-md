<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

At [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md), the particle's [mean longitude](../../../../../../mean-longitude.md) equals its [longitude of periapsis](../../../../../../longitude-of-periapsis.md): $\lambda=\varpi$. Hence the [pericentre geometry of an exterior resonance](../../../../../../pericentre-geometry-of-an-exterior-resonance.md) gives

$$
\phi=p(\varpi-\lambda_p),\qquad
\boxed{\Delta\theta_{\rm peri}=\frac{\phi+2\pi j}{p},\quad j\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

The [resonant argument](../../../../../../resonant-argument.md) therefore fixes the angular separation from the planet whenever the particle reaches [periapsis](../../../../../../periapsis.md). With $r_{\rm peri}=a(1-e)$ and planetary radius $a_p$, the actual separation is

$$
\boxed{d_{\rm peri}^2=a_p^2+r_{\rm peri}^2-2a_pr_{\rm peri}\cos\Delta\theta_{\rm peri}.}
$$

Thus a restricted libration phase can prevent close encounters even when the radial ranges of the two orbits overlap: this is [resonance protection](../../../../../../resonance-protection.md).
