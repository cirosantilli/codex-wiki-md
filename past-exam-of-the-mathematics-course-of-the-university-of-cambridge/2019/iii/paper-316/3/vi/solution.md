<h1 id="3/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Put $\epsilon=m_2/m_1\ll1$ and $t_{s2}/t_{s1}=\epsilon^k$, with $0<k<1$. The [gas-driven contraction of a planetesimal binary](../../../../../../gas-driven-contraction-of-a-planetesimal-binary.md) and [gas-driven migration of a binary centre of mass](../../../../../../gas-driven-migration-of-a-binary-centre-of-mass.md) times obey

$$
\frac\tau{\tau_b}=\eta\frac{m_1/t_{s1}+m_2/t_{s2}}{m_1/t_{s2}+m_2/t_{s1}}
=\eta\frac{1+\epsilon^{1-k}}{\epsilon^{-k}+\epsilon}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac\tau{\tau_b}\simeq\eta\epsilon^k\ll1.}
$$

The low-mass secondary has the shorter [aerodynamic stopping time](../../../../../../aerodynamic-stopping-time.md) and chiefly controls internal damping, whereas the primary chiefly controls [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) drag. The pair can contract substantially, potentially merging, before its stellar orbital radius changes appreciably. This conclusion is restricted to a pair that remains bound and within the adopted linear-drag regime.
