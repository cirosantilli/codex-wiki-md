<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

With $\hat{\mathbf r}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, project the relative [gas drag](../../../../../../gas-drag.md) to obtain

$$
\boxed{f_r=\Delta v_g\sin\theta,\qquad
f_\theta=-\kappa v+\Delta v_g\cos\theta.}
$$

The [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) is $\varepsilon=v^2/2-\mu/r=-\mu/(2a)$. Differentiation cancels the conservative gravitational work and gives $\dot\varepsilon=\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf f$. For the circular orbit,

$$
\dot\varepsilon=vf_\theta=-\kappa v^2+\Delta v_gv\cos\theta.
$$

Using $v^2=\mu/a$, the [orbit-averaged drag work](../../../../../../orbit-averaged-drag-work.md) relation therefore gives the instantaneous leading-order change

$$
\boxed{\dot a=-2\kappa a+\frac{2\Delta v_ga^{3/2}}{\sqrt\mu}\cos\theta.}
$$

The differential headwind term can change sign during the orbit; the damping term always removes orbital energy.
