<h1 id="4/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Initially the two inner orbital normals coincide, so their combined [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is $L_{12}=L_1+L_2$ along one direction. Use the outer orbital normal as the vertical axis. The inner pair's vector then has perpendicular component $L_{12}\sin\Delta I$ and parallel component $L_{12}\cos\Delta I$. Adding the outer [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) gives

$$
L_{\perp}=L_{12}\sin\Delta I,\qquad
L_{\parallel}=L_3+L_{12}\cos\Delta I.
$$

The [invariable plane](../../../../../../invariable-plane.md) is perpendicular to this total vector. Thus the outer orbit's [orbital inclination](../../../../../../orbital-inclination.md) to that plane equals the angle between its own normal and the total normal. The [inclination to the invariable plane of two orbital planes](../../../../../../inclination-to-the-invariable-plane-of-two-orbital-planes.md) is

$$
\boxed{I_3=\arctan\left(\frac{\sin\Delta I}{A+\cos\Delta I}\right),\qquad A=\frac{L_3}{L_1+L_2}.}
$$

This uses the small, prograde inclinations for which the denominator is positive; in general use the corresponding two-argument arctangent to choose the quadrant. If $A\gg1$, $I_3\simeq\Delta I/(A+1)$ for small $\Delta I$: the distant planet carries most of the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and lies close to the [invariable plane](../../../../../../invariable-plane.md).
