<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

[Adaptive-optics sky coverage](../../../../../../../adaptive-optics-sky-coverage.md) is limited by the need for a [guide star](../../../../../../../guide-star-adaptive-optics.md) bright enough to measure the [wavefront error](../../../../../../../wavefront-error.md) on the atmospheric evolution time. A faint guide gives noisy measurements; a guide too far from the target samples a different turbulent column. That angular mismatch is [anisoplanatism](../../../../../../../anisoplanatism.md), with useful separation characterized by the [isoplanatic angle](../../../../../../../isoplanatic-angle.md). Thus a conventional single-guide system cannot provide equally good correction at every target position.

A [laser guide star](../../../../../../../laser-guide-star.md) supplies an artificial bright reference and improves coverage. However, its finite-distance beam does not sample the full stellar turbulence column, and conventional laser systems need a natural reference for absolute image motion. The required reference brightness, angular separation, and desired correction quality therefore still constrain observations.
