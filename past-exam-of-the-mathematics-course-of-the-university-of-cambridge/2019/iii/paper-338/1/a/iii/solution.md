<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the simple slit-image approximation, the projected slit width is $w=x f_{\rm cam}/f_{\rm coll}$. Equating this width to the separation $q\Delta\lambda$ of barely resolved features, using the [grating dispersion](../../../../../../../grating-dispersion.md), gives

$$
\Delta\lambda\simeq\frac{xd\cos\beta}{mf_{\rm coll}},\qquad
\boxed{R\simeq\frac{mf_{\rm coll}\lambda}{xd\cos\beta}.}
$$

This recovers the stated [spectral resolving power](../../../../../../../spectral-resolving-power.md) under the assumption that the grating has unit anamorphic magnification.

For arbitrary distinct $\alpha$ and $\beta$, the [anamorphic magnification of a grating](../../../../../../../anamorphic-magnification-of-a-grating.md) must be included. At fixed [wavelength](../../../../../../../wavelength.md), the [grating equation](../../../../../../../grating-equation.md) gives $|d\beta/d\alpha|=\cos\alpha/\cos\beta$, so the slit image instead has width $w=x f_{\rm cam}\cos\alpha/(f_{\rm coll}\cos\beta)$. Thus the general [slit-limited resolving power of a grating](../../../../../../../slit-limited-resolving-power-of-a-grating.md) is

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{mf_{\rm coll}\lambda}{xd\cos\alpha}.}
$$

The two expressions agree in the [Littrow configuration](../../../../../../../littrow-configuration.md), $\alpha=\beta$. Without that condition or the unit-magnification approximation, the quoted $\cos\beta$ expression is not the general slit-limited result. Finite grating size, detector sampling, and [optical aberrations](../../../../../../../optical-aberration.md) can lower the actual resolution further.
