The traditional [infrared photometric bands](../../../../../../../infrared-photometric-band.md) extending the optical [photometric system](../../../../../../../photometric-system.md) into this range are approximately

$$
\boxed{J:1.25\,\mu{\rm m},\quad H:1.65\,\mu{\rm m},\quad K:2.2\,\mu{\rm m},\quad L:3.5\,\mu{\rm m},\quad M:4.8\,\mu{\rm m}.}
$$

Their [photometric passbands](../../../../../../../photometric-passband.md) lie mainly in [atmospheric windows](../../../../../../../atmospheric-window.md) between strong molecular absorption bands. This makes ground-based observations practical. The exact centers depend on the system: $L'$ is near $3.8\,\mu{\rm m}$, and shortened $K_s$ and $M'$ bands reduce unwanted background or absorption. At the longest wavelengths, atmospheric and instrumental [thermal radiation](../../../../../../../thermal-radiation.md) make [sky brightness](../../../../../../../sky-brightness.md) especially important. A modern $Y$ band near $1.0\,\mu{\rm m}$ is an additional choice, rather than one of the traditional $JHKLM$ extensions.
