<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For identical [luminosities](../../../../../../../luminosity.md) in a uniform Euclidean distribution, the [inverse-square law](../../../../../../../inverse-square-law.md) gives flux $F\propto r^{-2}$, while the number of sources within distance $r$ is proportional to $r^3$. Therefore ten times as many objects require $r_{10}/r_1=10^{1/3}$ and $F_{10}/F_1=10^{-2/3}$. Applying the definition of [apparent magnitude](../../../../../../../apparent-magnitude.md) gives

$$
\boxed{m_{10}-m_1=-2.5\log_{10}(10^{-2/3})=\frac53,\qquad m_{10}=m_1+\frac53.}
$$

This is the [Euclidean magnitude-limited source count](../../../../../../../euclidean-magnitude-limited-source-count.md) relation. It assumes no extinction, spatial variation of density, or cosmological change in the flux-distance relation.
