<h1 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let the two internal angles be $r=\theta_2$ and $\phi-r=\theta_3$, since the [optical prism](../../../../../../../prism-optics.md) geometry gives $\theta_2+\theta_3=\phi$. By [Snell's law](../../../../../../../snell-s-law.md), the corresponding external angles are $F(r)$ and $F(\phi-r)$, where $F(r)=\arcsin(n\sin r)$. The deviation is

$$
\gamma(r)=F(r)+F(\phi-r)-\phi.
$$

On the transmitted branch with $n>1$ and $0<r<\arcsin(1/n)$,

$$
F''(r)=\frac{n(n^2-1)\sin r}{(1-n^2\sin^2r)^{3/2}}>0.
$$

Thus $F'$ is strictly increasing. The stationary condition $\gamma'(r)=F'(r)-F'(\phi-r)=0$ forces $r=\phi-r$, and the positive second [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) makes this the unique [minimum deviation](../../../../../../../minimum-deviation.md). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\theta_2=\theta_3=\frac\phi2,\qquad\theta_1=\theta_4.}
$$

The argument assumes a transmitted ray is possible; the symmetric internal angles must lie below the critical angle.
