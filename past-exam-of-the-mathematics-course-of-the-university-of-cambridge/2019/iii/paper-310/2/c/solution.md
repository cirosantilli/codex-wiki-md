<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $Y_{\rm He}\simeq0.25$ be the present [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md). If $n_{\rm He}$ is the helium-nucleus density and $n_H$ the hydrogen-nucleus density, then

$$
n_b=n_H+4n_{\rm He},
\qquad
n_p=n_H+2n_{\rm He}.
$$

Since $Y_{\rm He}=4n_{\rm He}/n_b$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_p}{n_b}=1-\frac{Y_{\rm He}}2\simeq0.875}.
$$

This retains the correction from [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) bound in helium. [Charge neutrality](../../../../../../charge-neutrality.md) requires one [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) per proton, including bound electrons, so with the [baryon-to-photon ratio](../../../../../../baryon-to-photon-ratio.md) $\eta=n_b/n_\gamma\simeq6\times10^{-10}$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_e}{n_\gamma}
=\frac{n_p}{n_b}\eta
\simeq5.3\times10^{-10}}.
$$
