<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The reaction maintaining chemical equilibrium is

$$
\boxed{e^-+e^+\leftrightarrow\gamma+\gamma}.
$$

For $T<m_e$ and negligible [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md), the [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) and [Positron](../../../../../../positron.md) are dilute and obey the [nonrelativistic equilibrium number density](../../../../../../nonrelativistic-equilibrium-number-density.md). Including two spin states for each particle,

$$
n_{e^-}+n_{e^+}=4\left(\frac{m_eT}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_e/T}.
$$

The [photon number density](../../../../../../photon-number-density.md) is $n_\gamma=2\zeta(3)T^3/\pi^2$, so the [electron-positron equilibrium pair abundance below the electron mass](../../../../../../electron-positron-equilibrium-pair-abundance-below-the-electron-mass.md) is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{n_{e^-}+n_{e^+}}{n_\gamma}
=\frac{2\pi^2}{\zeta(3)}
\left(\frac{m_e}{2\pi T}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_e/T}}.
$$

If $n_e$ denotes only one of the charge-conjugate species, this expression is divided by two.
