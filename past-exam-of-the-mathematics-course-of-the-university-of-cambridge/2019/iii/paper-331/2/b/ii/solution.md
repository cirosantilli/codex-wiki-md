<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $y=\widetilde c^2$ and $s=J/(2\alpha)>0$. The [dispersion relation for two density interfaces in uniform shear](../../../../../../../dispersion-relation-for-two-density-interfaces-in-uniform-shear.md) is

$$
y^2-2(1+s)y+(1-s)^2-s^2e^{-4\alpha}=0.
$$

Its [discriminant](../../../../../../../discriminant.md) is $16s+4s^2e^{-4\alpha}>0$, so both roots $y_\pm$ are real. Their sum is positive; therefore one is negative precisely when their product is negative. That condition is

$$
(1-s)^2<s^2e^{-4\alpha},\qquad |1-s|<se^{-2\alpha}.
$$

Solving for $J$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{2\alpha}{1+e^{-2\alpha}}<J<\frac{2\alpha}{1-e^{-2\alpha}}.}
$$

In this interval $y_-<0$, so $\widetilde c=i\sqrt{-y_-}$ is the growing [normal mode](../../../../../../../normal-mode.md). At either endpoint the corresponding root is zero and the mode is neutral.
