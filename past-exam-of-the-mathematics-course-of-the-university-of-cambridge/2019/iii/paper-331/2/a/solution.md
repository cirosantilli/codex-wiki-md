<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\widehat\eta$ be the displacement amplitude of an interface. Its [kinematic boundary condition](../../../../../../kinematic-boundary-condition.md) is $ik(U-c)\widehat\eta=\widehat w$, on either side, so the first of the [jump conditions for stratified inviscid shear flow](../../../../../../jump-conditions-for-stratified-inviscid-shear-flow.md) is

$$
\boxed{\left[\frac{\widehat w}{U-c}\right]_-^+=0.}
$$

For zero [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension.md), pressure is continuous at the displaced interface. Since the background [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) has derivative $-g\rho$, linearization gives $[\widehat p-g\rho\widehat\eta]_-^+=0$. Using $\widehat p=\rho_0[(U-c)\widehat w'-U'\widehat w]/(ik)$ and the kinematic condition gives

$$
\boxed{\left[(U-c)\widehat w'-U'\widehat w-\frac{g\rho}{\rho_0}\frac{\widehat w}{U-c}\right]_-^+=0.}
$$

This derivation also works when $U$ jumps: the displacement is shared, while the vertical velocity need not be. It avoids ambiguous products of [distributions](../../../../../../distribution-mathematical-analysis.md) at a discontinuous velocity profile.
