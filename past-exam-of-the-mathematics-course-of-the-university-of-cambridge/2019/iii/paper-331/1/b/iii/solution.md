<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose $a=1$. The [power-transformed Taylor–Goldstein energy identity](../../../../../../../power-transformed-taylor-goldstein-energy-identity.md) reduces to

$$
\int_{-L}^L(U-c)^2(|q'|^2+k^2|q|^2)dz=\int_{-L}^LN^2|q|^2dz.
$$

The right side is real. For an unstable mode, $c_i>0$, so taking the [imaginary part](../../../../../../../imaginary-part.md) gives

$$
\int_{-L}^L(U-c_r)(|q'|^2+k^2|q|^2)dz=0.
$$

Consequently the [phase velocity](../../../../../../../phase-velocity.md) is a weighted average:

$$
\boxed{c_r=\frac{\int_{-L}^LU(|q'|^2+k^2|q|^2)dz}{\int_{-L}^L(|q'|^2+k^2|q|^2)dz},\qquad U_{\min}<c_r<U_{\max}.}
$$

The weights are nonnegative and not identically zero. For a nonconstant shear profile, equality at an extremum would force $q$ to vanish on an interval where $U$ differs from that extremum; uniqueness for the regular [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md) would then give the zero mode. This is the [phase-speed bound for unstable stratified shear modes](../../../../../../../phase-speed-bound-for-unstable-stratified-shear-modes.md).
