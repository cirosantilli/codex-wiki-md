<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../../../../../../poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem.md) shows that the weights of the [Verma module](../../../../../../verma-module.md) $M(\omega_2)$ are

$$
\boxed{\omega_2-Q_+
=\{\omega_2-n_1\alpha_1-n_2\alpha_2:n_1,n_2\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\},}
$$

with multiplicities given by the corresponding [Kostant partition function](../../../../../../kostant-partition-function.md).

The [Dynkin labels](../../../../../../dynkin-label.md) of $\omega_2$ are $(0,1)$. Hence the two simple-root [singular vectors](../../../../../../singular-vector.md) are $f_1v_{\omega_2}$, of weight $\omega_2-\alpha_1$, and $f_2^2v_{\omega_2}$, of weight $\omega_2-2\alpha_2$. They generate the [Maximal proper submodule of a dominant integral Verma module](../../../../../../maximal-proper-submodule-of-a-dominant-integral-verma-module.md). Its set of weights is consequently

$$
\boxed{(\omega_2-\alpha_1-Q_+)\ \cup\ (\omega_2-2\alpha_2-Q_+).}
$$
