<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [simple Lie algebra](../../../../../../simple-lie-algebra.md) is a nonabelian [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) whose only [ideals of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) are $0$ and the whole algebra.

Use the standard basis $e,f,h$ of the [sl2 Lie algebra](../../../../../../sl2-lie-algebra.md), with

$$
[h,e]=2e,\qquad [h,f]=-2f,\qquad [e,f]=h.
$$

Let $I\ne0$ be an ideal and choose $0\ne x=ae+bf+ch\in I$. Since $I$ is invariant under the [Adjoint representation](../../../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra.md), it is invariant under the [linear operator](../../../../../../linear-operator.md) $\operatorname{ad}h$. The three basis vectors are [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $\operatorname{ad}h$ with distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $2,-2,0$. Applying the corresponding polynomial spectral projections to $x$ shows that $I$ contains at least one nonzero multiple of $e$, $f$, or $h$.

If $e\in I$, then $[f,e]=-h\in I$ and $[f,h]=2f\in I$; the cases $f\in I$ and $h\in I$ are identical after taking brackets with the other basis vectors. Hence $e,f,h\in I$, so $I=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$. Therefore **$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ is simple**.
