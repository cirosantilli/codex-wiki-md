<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [ideal of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is a [vector subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) $I\subseteq\mathfrak g$ such that $[\mathfrak g,I]\subseteq I$. The [derived series of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../derived-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is defined by $\mathfrak g^{(0)}=\mathfrak g$ and $\mathfrak g^{(r+1)}=[\mathfrak g^{(r)},\mathfrak g^{(r)}]$.

Suppose that $I$ is an ideal and $x\in\mathfrak g$, $u,v\in I$. The [Jacobi identity](../../../../../../jacobi-identity.md) gives

$$
[x,[u,v]]=[[x,u],v]+[u,[x,v]].
$$

Both terms on the right lie in $[I,I]$, because $[x,u],[x,v]\in I$. Thus $[I,I]$ is again an ideal. Starting from the ideal $\mathfrak g$ and applying this observation inductively proves that **every term $\mathfrak g^{(r)}$ of the derived series is an ideal of $\mathfrak g$**.
