<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Apply force balance to one hemisphere. The pressure force is $\pi R^2(\Pi_{\rm in}-\Pi_{\rm out})$, while the [interfacial tension](../../../../../../interfacial-tension.md) around its equator gives $2\pi R\gamma$. The [Young–Laplace equation](../../../../../../young-laplace-equation.md) therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\Pi_{\rm in}-\Pi_{\rm out}=\frac{2\gamma}{R}.}
$$

To leading order in curvature, let the two bulk compositions be $\phi_+=\phi_B+\delta_+$ and $\phi_-=-\phi_B+\delta_-$. Their curvatures agree, $\alpha=f''(\pm\phi_B)=-2a$, so equal [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) require $\alpha\delta_+=\alpha\delta_-$ and hence a common shift $\delta$.

Since $d\Pi/d\phi=\phi f''(\phi)$, expansion about the flat coexistence compositions gives $\Pi_+-\Pi_-=2\phi_B\alpha\delta+O(\delta^2)$. Matching the capillary pressure proves the [Gibbs--Thomson relation](../../../../../../gibbs-thomson-relation.md) in these variables:

$$
\boxed{\delta=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha\phi_BR},\qquad\mu_s=\alpha\delta=\frac{\gamma}{\phi_BR}.}
$$

The pressure jump balances interfacial forces; it does not permit unequal [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md). A common shift in both compositions supplies a common interfacial [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) while their pressures differ. The expansion assumes $R$ large compared with the [phi-four diffuse interface](../../../../../../phi-four-diffuse-interface.md) width.
