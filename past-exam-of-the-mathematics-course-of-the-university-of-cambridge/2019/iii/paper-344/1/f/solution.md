<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

In the usual three-dimensional isotropic setting, the transition is expected to be **discontinuous**, by the [Brazovskii fluctuation-induced first-order transition](../../../../../../brazovskii-fluctuation-induced-first-order-transition.md) mechanism.

If $\bar\kappa<0$, write $q_*^2=-\bar\kappa/(2\gamma)$ and $r=\bar a-\bar\kappa^2/(4\gamma)$. Then $J(q)=r+\gamma(q^2-q_*^2)^2$. A continuous finite-wavevector transition would require $r\to0$ with $q_*>0$. But the [soft-mode shell integral](../../../../../../soft-mode-shell-integral.md) behaves as

$$
\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3J(q)}\sim\frac{q_*}{4\pi\sqrt{\gamma r}},
\qquad
\int\frac{q^2\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^3J(q)}\sim\frac{q_*^3}{4\pi\sqrt{\gamma r}}.
$$

The positive fluctuation corrections in the [self-consistency equations](../../../../../../self-consistency-equation.md) diverge in that limit. In particular $\bar\kappa=\kappa+2BI_0$ cannot stay at a finite negative value while $I_0$ diverges. Thus the isotropic disordered state cannot lose stability through a zero-mass mode on a fixed nonzero shell in this approximation.

Ordering is instead expected through a free-energy crossing with a finite-amplitude [smectic phase](../../../../../../smectic-phase.md), giving a [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md). This is a reasoned fluctuation prediction for the finite-wavevector transition; the disordered [Gaussian variational approximation](../../../../../../gaussian-variational-approximation.md) alone does not locate the coexistence point or calculate the amplitude jump. A zero-wavevector instability or a lower-dimensional setting would require a separate analysis.
