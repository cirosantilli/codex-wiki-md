<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take $\gamma>0$ so that the short-wavelength quadratic energy is stable. For $B=0$, a [Fourier mode](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md) with wavevector magnitude $q$ has inverse [Gaussian field theory](../../../../../../gaussian-field-theory.md) covariance

$$
G(q)=a+\kappa q^2+\gamma q^4
=a-\frac{\kappa^2}{4\gamma}+\gamma\left(q^2+\frac\kappa{2\gamma}\right)^2.
$$

For $\kappa<0$, its minimum is at

$$
\boxed{q_0=\sqrt{-\frac\kappa{2\gamma}},\qquad a_c=\frac{\kappa^2}{4\gamma}.}
$$

The zero-average constraint excludes the uniform mode but allows $\phi=A\cos(q_0z)$. At $a=a_c$ the modes on the [nonzero-wavevector soft-mode sphere](../../../../../../nonzero-wavevector-soft-mode-sphere.md) become soft; below it they have negative quadratic energy, so the homogeneous state loses stability. A modulation along any chosen direction is the simplest [smectic phase](../../../../../../smectic-phase.md) pattern. The quadratic instability selects its wavelength; with $B=0$, a nonlinear stabilizing interaction would still be needed to determine its amplitude.
