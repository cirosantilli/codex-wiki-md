<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [quantum electrodynamics](../../../../../../quantum-electrodynamics.md) interaction is $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-e j^\mu A_\mu$, where the [Dirac electromagnetic current](../../../../../../dirac-electromagnetic-current.md) is $j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$. Under [parity symmetry in quantum field theory](../../../../../../parity-symmetry-in-quantum-field-theory.md),

$$
j^\mu(x)\mapsto {\Lambda_P^\mu}_\nu j^\nu(x_P),
\qquad \Lambda_P=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
$$

Invariance of the interaction therefore requires the [electromagnetic four-potential](../../../../../../electromagnetic-four-potential.md) to transform as the same [Lorentz four-vector](../../../../../../four-vector.md):

$$
\boxed{\widehat P A^\mu(x)\widehat P^{-1}={\Lambda_P^\mu}_\nu A^\nu(x_P)},
$$

so $A^0$ is parity even and $\mathbf A$ is parity odd. Under [charge conjugation](../../../../../../charge-conjugation.md), the current is odd, $j^\mu\mapsto-j^\mu$, so invariance requires

$$
\boxed{\widehat C A^\mu(x)\widehat C^{-1}=-A^\mu(x)}.
$$
