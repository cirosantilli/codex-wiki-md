<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Treat each grain as an isolated compact sphere and take its wavelength-independent [radiation-pressure efficiency](../../../../../../radiation-pressure-efficiency.md) to be $Q_{\rm pr}=1$. At distance $R$, its mass and radiation-force cross-section are

$$
m_d=\frac43\pi r_d^3\rho_d,\qquad A_d=\pi r_d^2.
$$

The inward gravitational force and outward [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) force are

$$
F_G=\frac{GM_{\rm BH}m_d}{R^2},\qquad
F_{\rm rad}=\frac{L}{4\pi R^2c}A_d.
$$

Thus the opacity per unit dust mass and the [dust-grain Eddington limit](../../../../../../dust-grain-eddington-limit.md) are

$$
\kappa_d=\frac{A_d}{m_d}=\frac{3}{4\rho_dr_d},\qquad
L_{\rm Edd,d}=\frac{4\pi GM_{\rm BH}c}{\kappa_d}.
$$

To equal the [Eddington luminosity](../../../../../../eddington-luminosity.md) based on [electron-scattering opacity](../../../../../../electron-scattering-opacity.md), $\kappa_d$ must equal $\kappa_{\rm es}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{r_{d,\rm crit}=\frac{3}{4\rho_d\kappa_{\rm es}}.}
$$

For fully ionized hydrogen, $\kappa_{\rm es}=\sigma_T/m_p\simeq0.40\,\mathrm{cm^2\,g^{-1}}$, and the stated $\rho_d=1\,\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}$ gives

$$
\boxed{r_{d,\rm crit}\simeq1.9\,\mathrm{cm}.}
$$

Using $0.34\,\mathrm{cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ for a roughly solar-composition gas instead gives $2.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. This composition-dependent normalization should be stated because the problem does not fix it. For a different constant [radiation-pressure efficiency](../../../../../../radiation-pressure-efficiency.md), the critical radius is multiplied by $Q_{\rm pr}$.

At $L=L_{\rm Edd,es}$, the grain's [radiation-pressure coefficient](../../../../../../radiation-pressure-coefficient.md) is

$$
\frac{F_{\rm rad}}{F_G}=\frac{\kappa_d}{\kappa_{\rm es}}
=\frac{r_{d,\rm crit}}{r_d}.
$$

Consequently, **smaller grains experience a net outward force, larger grains remain gravitationally attracted, and critical-size grains have zero net radial force**. Grains released at rest are therefore size-sorted into expelled and infalling populations. With an initial inward velocity, an outward force does not guarantee reversal before the grain reaches the hole; trajectories also depend on initial conditions. Gas drag and dust destruction have been omitted from this isolated-grain calculation. Ordinary interstellar grains are much smaller than a centimetre, so their direct radiative acceleration is very strong in this idealized model.
