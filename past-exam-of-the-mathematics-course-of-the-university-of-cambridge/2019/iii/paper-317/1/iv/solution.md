<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Retain the same [stellar homology](../../../../../../stellar-homology.md) and gas-pressure assumptions, but use the specified [CNO cycle](../../../../../../cno-cycle.md) law $\epsilon\propto\rho T^{16}$ and constant [electron-scattering opacity](../../../../../../electron-scattering-opacity.md). The nuclear luminosity now scales as

$$
L_{\rm nuc}\propto M\rho_cT_c^{16}\propto M^{18}R^{-19}.
$$

The [radiative diffusion in a star](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion-in-a-star.md) scaling becomes

$$
L_{\rm rad}\propto RT_c^4/\rho_c\propto M^3.
$$

Equating the two yields $R^{19}\propto M^{15}$. Therefore [radiative homology with CNO burning and electron scattering](../../../../../../radiative-homology-with-cno-burning-and-electron-scattering.md) gives

$$
\boxed{M\propto R^{19/15},\qquad L\propto M^3.}
$$

Since $R\propto L^{5/19}$, the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) gives

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm eff}\propto L^{9/76},\qquad
\frac{d\log L}{d\log T_{\rm eff}}=\frac{76}{9}\simeq8.44.}
$$

This is a model comparison using the exponent 16 prescribed here. Actual massive stars may have appreciable [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) and convective cores, which violate the assumptions of this purely radiative gas-pressure family.
