<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Melting raises [permeability of a porous medium](../../../../../../permeability-of-a-porous-medium.md). An advancing protrusion therefore offers a less resistive path to hot liquid, attracting larger [Darcy flux](../../../../../../darcy-velocity.md) and receiving more heat. The resulting faster melting reinforces the protrusion: this is a [reactive infiltration instability](../../../../../../reactive-infiltration-instability.md). A permeability decrease would reverse this feedback, while zero contrast gives no growth in this ideal model.

For positive contrast, the [growth rate](../../../../../../growth-rate.md) is proportional to transverse [wavenumber](../../../../../../wavenumber.md), so **the model has no finite fastest-growing wavelength**. The [latent-to-sensible heat ratio](../../../../../../latent-to-sensible-heat-ratio.md) slows the front but supplies no short-wave cutoff. Finite thermal transport and a resolved melting zone can introduce a length scale; pore geometry eventually limits the continuum description. If interface curvature changes the equilibrium melting temperature through the [Gibbs--Thomson relation](../../../../../../gibbs-thomson-relation.md), that can also oppose short-wave corrugations. The modified [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md) must include such effects before a preferred wavelength can be calculated; a length scale alone does not guarantee that every added mechanism selects one.
