<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a steady field in the apparatus frame, the time derivative in the translating crystal frame is $-V\partial_z$. The given solute equation therefore becomes

$$
[u_z-V(1-\varphi)]C_z=-rVC\varphi_z.
$$

Substituting the [density-change flow in a pulled mushy layer](../../../../../../../density-change-flow-in-a-pulled-mushy-layer.md) reduces this to $(1-\varphi)C_z=C\varphi_z$. Thus the [solute conservation in a steadily pulled mush](../../../../../../../solute-conservation-in-a-steadily-pulled-mush.md) integrates to $(1-\varphi)C=C_0$, using the zero [solid fraction](../../../../../../../solid-fraction.md) at the top boundary.

Set $\mathcal C=mC_0/\Delta T$. The [liquidus](../../../../../../../liquidus.md) gives $C=C_0(1-\theta/\mathcal C)$, so

$$
\boxed{\varphi=-\frac{\theta}{\mathcal C-\theta},\qquad 1-\varphi=\frac{\mathcal C}{\mathcal C-\theta}.}
$$

In particular, $\varphi=0$ at $\theta=0$, and immediately above the [eutectic temperature](../../../../../../../eutectic-temperature.md), where $\theta=-1$, the mush has $\varphi=1/(\mathcal C+1)$. It becomes fully solid across the eutectic front below.
