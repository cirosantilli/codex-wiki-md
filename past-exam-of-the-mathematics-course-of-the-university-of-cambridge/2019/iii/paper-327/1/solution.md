The [Schwartz space](../../../../../schwartz-space.md) consists of the [smooth functions](../../../../../smooth-function.md) $\varphi$ for which

$$
p_{\alpha,\beta}(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^n}|x^\alpha\partial^\beta\varphi(x)|<\infty
$$

for all [multi-indices](../../../../../multi-index-notation.md) $\alpha,\beta$. Its topology is generated by these [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md); $\varphi_j\to\varphi$ means convergence in every seminorm. A [tempered distribution](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md) is a continuous linear functional on this space. Convergence in $\mathcal S'$ here means weak convergence: $\langle u_j,\varphi\rangle\to\langle u,\varphi\rangle$ for every Schwartz function.

Use the unnormalized angular-frequency [Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform.md)

$$
\widehat\varphi(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-ix\cdot\lambda}\varphi(x)\,dx,
\qquad \langle\widehat u,\varphi\rangle=\langle u,\widehat\varphi\rangle.
$$

The transform is a continuous automorphism of the [Schwartz space](../../../../../schwartz-space.md), so this duality defines a [tempered distribution](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md). Put $D_j=-i\partial_j$, as required by the stated identities. [Integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) and differentiation under the integral give

$$
D_x^\alpha\widehat\varphi(x)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\widehat{\lambda^\alpha\varphi}(x),
\qquad \widehat{D_\lambda^\beta\varphi}(x)=x^\beta\widehat\varphi(x).
$$

Thus, keeping the transpose factor of $D=-i\partial$ explicit,

$$
\langle\widehat{D_j u},\varphi\rangle
=i\langle u,\partial_{x_j}\widehat\varphi\rangle
=\langle\widehat u,\lambda_j\varphi\rangle,
$$

and

$$
\langle\widehat{x_j u},\varphi\rangle
=\langle u,x_j\widehat\varphi\rangle
=\langle\widehat u,D_j\varphi\rangle
=\langle-D_j\widehat u,\varphi\rangle.
$$

Iterating gives the [Fourier derivative identities for distributions](../../../../../fourier-derivative-identities-for-distributions.md)

$$
\boxed{\widehat{D^\alpha u}=\lambda^\alpha\widehat u,
\qquad \widehat{x^\beta u}=(-1)^{|\beta|}D^\beta\widehat u.}
$$

The logarithm defines a [tempered distribution](../../../../../tempered-distribution.md) because it is locally integrable and grows more slowly than a polynomial. Its derivative is $u'(x)=x/(1+x^2)$. The standard transform pair $\widehat{(1+x^2)^{-1}}=\pi e^{-|\lambda|}$, obtainable by the [residue theorem](../../../../../residue-theorem.md), gives

$$
\widehat{u'}(\lambda)=i\partial_\lambda(\pi e^{-|\lambda|})
=-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda)e^{-|\lambda|}.
$$

As $\widehat{u'}=i\lambda\widehat u$, we have $\lambda\widehat u=-\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda)e^{-|\lambda|}$. Define

$$
\langle T,\varphi\rangle
=-\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\varphi(\lambda)-\varphi(0)}{|\lambda|}e^{-|\lambda|}\,d\lambda.
$$

This integral is absolutely convergent: the numerator is $O(|\lambda|)$ near zero, and the exponential controls the tails. It defines a tempered distribution satisfying the same multiplication equation. The [kernel of multiplication by a coordinate](../../../../../kernel-of-multiplication-by-a-coordinate.md) therefore gives $\widehat u=T+c\delta_0$.

To determine $c$, use the supplied [Gaussian Fourier transform](../../../../../fourier-transform-of-a-gaussian.md) pair with $\varphi_n(0)=1/(2\sqrt\pi)$. Then

$$
\langle\widehat u,\varphi_n\rangle
=\sqrt n\int_{\mathbb R}\tfrac12\log(1+x^2)e^{-nx^2}\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R}\tfrac12\log(1+y^2/n)e^{-y^2}\,dy\longrightarrow0.
$$

Also $\langle T,\varphi_n\rangle\to0$ by the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md): the relevant integrand is bounded by a constant times $|\lambda|e^{-|\lambda|}$ for $n\geq1$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{c=0,\qquad
\langle\widehat u,\varphi\rangle
=-\pi\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{|\lambda|>\varepsilon}
\frac{\varphi(\lambda)-\varphi(0)}{|\lambda|}e^{-|\lambda|}\,d\lambda.}
$$

The cutoff limit agrees with the absolutely convergent integral; this particular subtraction convention fixes the otherwise ambiguous delta term. The reusable identity is [Fourier transform of the logarithm of one plus x squared](../../../../../fourier-transform-of-the-logarithm-of-one-plus-x-squared.md).
