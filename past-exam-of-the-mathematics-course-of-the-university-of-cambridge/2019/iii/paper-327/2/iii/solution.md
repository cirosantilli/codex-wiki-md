<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Since $(x^2-1)(-\delta_0)=\delta_0$, write $u'=-\delta_0+w$, where $(x^2-1)w=0$. The polynomial has two simple zeros. The [distribution annihilated by a function with simple zeros](../../../../../../distribution-annihilated-by-a-function-with-simple-zeros.md) lemma gives

$$
w=a\delta_{-1}+b\delta_1.
$$

Integrating with the [Heaviside step function](../../../../../../heaviside-step-function.md) yields

$$
\boxed{u=-H(x)+aH(x+1)+bH(x-1)+c,\qquad a,b,c\in\mathbb C.}
$$

This includes every solution: its derivative has exactly the required delta terms, and any two primitives differ only by a constant distribution.
