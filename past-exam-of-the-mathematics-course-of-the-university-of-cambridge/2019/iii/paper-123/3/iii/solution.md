<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$. Its [fundamental discriminant](../../../../../../fundamental-discriminant.md) is $-24$. The [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is

$$
\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{24}<4,
$$

so every [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md) contains an integral ideal of norm $1$, $2$ or $3$. The primes above $2$ and $3$ are ramified, their classes have order at most two, and

$$
(\sqrt{-6})=\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3
$$

shows that they represent the same class. This class is nontrivial because the [norm form](../../../../../../norm-form.md) $a^2+6b^2$ does not represent $2$. Hence

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(K)\cong C_2.
$$

Now let $H=K(\sqrt2)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3})$. The [discriminant of a biquadratic field](../../../../../../discriminant-of-a-biquadratic-field.md) is the product of the discriminants of its three quadratic subfields, so

$$
d_H=8\cdot(-3)\cdot(-24)=576=d_K^2.
$$

The [relative discriminant](../../../../../../relative-discriminant.md) $\mathfrak D_{H/K}$ therefore has norm one, proving that $H/K$ is unramified at every finite prime. Since $[H:K]=2=|\operatorname{Cl}(K)|$, the [Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus six](../../../../../../hilbert-class-field-of-q-of-square-root-minus-six.md) is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3}).}
$$
