<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. The [decomposition group](../../../../../../decomposition-group.md) and [inertia group](../../../../../../inertia-group.md) at $q$ are

$$
D_{q/p}=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma(q)=q\},
\qquad
I_{q/p}=\ker\left(D_{q/p}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(B/q\,/\,R/p)\right).
$$

The [transitivity of the Galois action on primes](../../../../../../transitivity-of-the-galois-action-on-primes.md) gives $G/D_{q/p}$ as the set of primes above $p$. Completion induces the canonical isomorphism

$$
D_{q/p}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(L_q/K_p),
$$

and reduction gives the exact sequence

$$
1\longrightarrow I_{q/p}\longrightarrow D_{q/p}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(B/q\,/\,R/p)\longrightarrow1.
$$

Consequently $|I_{q/p}|=e_{q/p}$, $|D_{q/p}/I_{q/p}|=f_{q/p}$ and, if $g$ is the number of primes above $p$,

$$
\boxed{|G|=g e_{q/p}f_{q/p}.}
$$
