<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Align the polar axis with the [magnetic dipole moment](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-moment.md). The [magnetic dipole field](../../../../../../magnetic-dipole-field.md) has

$$
B_r=\frac{2m\cos\theta}{r^3},\qquad B_\theta=\frac{m\sin\theta}{r^3}.
$$

The [magnetic-field-line equation](../../../../../../magnetic-field-line-equation.md) gives $dr/(r\,d\theta)=2\cot\theta$, hence the [dipole magnetic-field line](../../../../../../dipole-magnetic-field-line.md) is $r=C\sin^2\theta$. Normalize the loaded bundle by its small surface angular radius $\theta_\star$ at $r=R_\star$. Its boundary obeys

$$
\frac{\sin^2\theta(r)}{\sin^2\theta_\star}=\frac r{R_\star},
\qquad
\theta(r)^2\simeq\theta_\star^2\frac r{R_\star}.
$$

The [dipolar flux-tube area](../../../../../../dipolar-flux-tube-area.md) for one polar cap is consequently

$$
\boxed{A(r)\simeq\pi\theta_\star^2\frac{r^3}{R_\star}.}
$$

Equivalently, $BA$ is constant by conservation of [magnetic flux](../../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) and $B\propto r^{-3}$ near the axis. Two equal loaded caps double the total area. The $r^3$ scaling is independent of the normalization; if the loading angle is specified at another radius $r_L$, use $A(r)=\pi\theta_L^2r^3/r_L$ instead.
