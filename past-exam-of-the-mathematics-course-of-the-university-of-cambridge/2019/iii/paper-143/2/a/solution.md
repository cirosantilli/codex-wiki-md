<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a finite generating set $S$ of $G$, the [Cayley graph](../../../../../../cayley-graph.md) $\operatorname{Cay}_S(G)$ has vertex set $G$ and an edge from $g$ to $gs$ for every $g\in G$ and $s\in S\cup S^{-1}$; one may retain orientations and labels, or forget them.

As unlabelled undirected graphs, the Cayley graph of the [infinite cyclic group](../../../../../../infinite-cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z$ with generator $1$ and the Cayley graph of the [infinite dihedral group](../../../../../../infinite-dihedral-group.md)

$$
D_\infty=C_2*C_2=\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^2=1\rangle
$$

with generators $a,b$ are both the two-way infinite line. The first group is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) and the second is not, so they are not isomorphic. Thus the requested example is the [infinite line as a Cayley graph](../../../../../../infinite-line-as-a-cayley-graph.md) of **$\mathbb Z$ and $D_\infty$**.
