<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $\lambda\geq\kappa$ be a [cardinal number](../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) of $M$. If forcing collapsed $\lambda$, some condition would force a [surjective function](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) $\dot f:\mu\to\lambda$ for an ordinal $\mu<\lambda$. Replacing $\mu$ by its ground-model cardinality lets us assume that $\mu$ is a cardinal.

For each $\xi<\mu$, choose in $M$ a maximal [antichain in a forcing order](../../../../../../antichain-in-a-forcing-order.md) deciding $\dot f(\xi)$. The [chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../chain-condition-for-forcing.md) makes its size less than $\kappa$, so the set $B_\xi$ of possible values of $\dot f(\xi)$ has size below $\kappa$. This is the [possible-values lemma for chain-condition forcing](../../../../../../possible-values-lemma-for-chain-condition-forcing.md). Every interpreted range is contained in $B=\bigcup_{\xi<\mu}B_\xi$. If $\mu<\kappa$, the fact that $\kappa$ is a [regular cardinal](../../../../../../regular-cardinal.md) gives $|B|<\kappa\leq\lambda$; if $\kappa\leq\mu<\lambda$, [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../../../../../../infinite-cardinal-arithmetic.md) gives $|B|\leq\mu<\lambda$. Either way $B$ cannot contain the range of a surjection onto $\lambda$, a contradiction. By [cardinal preservation by chain-condition forcing](../../../../../../cardinal-preservation-by-chain-condition-forcing.md), **$\mathbb P$ preserves every cardinal at least $\kappa$.**
