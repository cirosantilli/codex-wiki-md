<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $M$ be the given [transitive model](../../../../../../transitive-model.md) and suppose that its [ordinal height of a model of set theory](../../../../../../ordinal-height-of-a-model-of-set-theory.md) $\operatorname{Ord}\cap M$ were a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md). For every $x\in M$, the internal [Axiom of choice](../../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) gives a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) from $x$ to an ordinal of $M$; transitivity makes this an actual bijection, and the ordinal is externally countable. Thus every element of $M$ is externally countable.

For each $\alpha\in\operatorname{Ord}\cap M$, the internal rank $V_\alpha^M$ belongs to $M$ and is therefore countable. Every $x\in M$ lies in one of these ranks, so $M$ is a countable union of countable sets and is itself countable, contrary to the hypothesis. By [uncountable transitive set model has uncountable ordinal height](../../../../../../uncountable-transitive-set-model-has-uncountable-ordinal-height.md), **$M$ contains uncountably many ordinals.**
