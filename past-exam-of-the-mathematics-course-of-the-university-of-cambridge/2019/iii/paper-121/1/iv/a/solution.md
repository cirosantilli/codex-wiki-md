<h1 id="1/iv/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $N$ be a [well-founded model of set theory](../../../../../../../well-founded-model-of-set-theory.md) of the complete theory $T$, and suppose that a [Paris model](../../../../../../../paris-model.md) $M\models T$ were ill-founded. Its internal [ordinals](../../../../../../../ordinal.md) would then contain an external descending sequence

$$
\alpha_0\ni^M\alpha_1\ni^M\alpha_2\ni^M\cdots.
$$

For every $n$, choose a [first-order formula](../../../../../../../first-order-formula.md) $\varphi_n$ that uniquely defines $\alpha_n$ in $M$. The sentences asserting that $\varphi_n$ uniquely defines an ordinal and that the object defined by $\varphi_{n+1}$ belongs to the object defined by $\varphi_n$ are true in $M$. Since $T$ is complete, all its models satisfy the same [first-order sentences](../../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md), so the corresponding uniquely defined ordinals in $N$ form an external descending membership sequence. This contradicts the well-foundedness of $N$. Thus [Paris models are well-founded when their complete theory has a well-founded model](../../../../../../../paris-models-are-well-founded-when-their-complete-theory-has-a-well-founded-model.md) proves that **every Paris model of $T$ is well-founded.**
