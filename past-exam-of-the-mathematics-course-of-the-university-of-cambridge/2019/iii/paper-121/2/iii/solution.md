<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [definable power set](../../../../../../definable-power-set-split.md) $\mathcal D(A)$ performs one definability step over the single structure $(A,\in)$, whereas the [constructible power set](../../../../../../constructible-power-set.md) $\mathcal P^L(A)$ contains subsets of $A$ created at arbitrarily late stages of the [constructible hierarchy](../../../../../../constructible-hierarchy.md).

For the concrete case $A=\omega$, there are only countably many [first-order formulas](../../../../../../first-order-formula.md) and finite tuples of natural-number parameters, so $\mathcal D(\omega)$ is a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md). In contrast, the [constructible universe](../../../../../../constructible-universe.md) satisfies [ZFC](../../../../../../zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory-with-choice.md), and [Cantor theorem](../../../../../../cantor-s-theorem.md) makes its full power set $\mathcal P^L(\omega)$ uncountable inside $L$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathcal D(\omega)\subsetneq\mathcal P^L(\omega),}
$$

so the two notions do not agree in general.
