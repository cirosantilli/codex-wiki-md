↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Choose homogeneous algebra generators $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ of positive degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ for $S$; they exist because $S$ is Noetherian. For a finitely generated graded module $V=\bigoplus_jV_j$, its [Poincare series of a graded module](../../../../../poincare-series-of-a-graded-module.md) is

$$
P_V(t)=\sum_j(\dim_kV_j)t^j.
$$

The [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md) states that

$$
\boxed{P_V(t)=\frac{Q(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^r(1-t^{d_i})}}
$$

for a Laurent polynomial $Q(t)\in\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$.

We prove this by induction on $r$. For $r=0$, $S=k$ and $V$ is finite-dimensional, so its series is a Laurent polynomial. For $x=x_r$ of degree $d=d_r$, multiplication gives the exact sequence of graded modules

$$
0\longrightarrow(0:_Vx)(-d)\longrightarrow V(-d)
\xrightarrow{x}V\longrightarrow V/xV\longrightarrow0.
$$

Additivity of the [Hilbert series](../../../../../hilbert-series.md) yields

$$
(1-t^d)P_V(t)=P_{V/xV}(t)-t^dP_{(0:_Vx)}(t).
$$

Both modules on the right are finitely generated over $k[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}]$, so the induction hypothesis proves the formula.

For the finitely generated commutative algebra $R$, let $R_j$ be the stated degree filtration. Its [associated graded ring](../../../../../associated-graded-ring.md)

$$
\operatorname{gr}R=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}R_j/R_{j-1}
$$

is a [standard graded algebra](../../../../../standard-graded-algebra.md) generated by the initial forms of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. Since

$$
\dim_kR_j=\sum_{i=0}^j\dim_k(R_i/R_{i-1}),
$$

Hilbert–Serre shows that **the quantity $\dim_kR_j$ agrees with a polynomial for all sufficiently large $j$**. The [growth of a finitely generated commutative algebra](../../../../../growth-of-a-finitely-generated-commutative-algebra.md) has polynomial degree $\dim R$, independently of the chosen finite generating set.

For

$$
R=k[Y_1,Y_1^{-1},Y_2,Y_2^{-1}]
$$

with the four displayed generators, a basis of $R_j$ consists of the Laurent monomials $Y_1^aY_2^b$ satisfying $|a|+|b|\leq j$. There are $4r$ points with $|a|+|b|=r>0$, and one point at the origin. Hence the [growth of the two-variable Laurent polynomial algebra](../../../../../growth-of-the-two-variable-laurent-polynomial-algebra.md) is

$$
\boxed{\dim_kR_j=1+4\sum_{r=1}^jr=2j^2+2j+1.}
$$
