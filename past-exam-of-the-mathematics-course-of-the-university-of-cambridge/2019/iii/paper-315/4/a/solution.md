<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A close-in giant is often in [synchronous rotation](../../../../../../synchronous-rotation.md) after [tidal locking](../../../../../../tidal-locking.md), giving persistent dayside heating and nightside cooling. Its contrast is controlled by the competition between [radiative relaxation time in a planetary atmosphere](../../../../../../radiative-relaxation-time-in-a-planetary-atmosphere.md), wind transport characterized by the [atmospheric advection time](../../../../../../atmospheric-advection-time.md), wave adjustment, and drag. When heat transport is fast compared with radiation, [day-night heat redistribution](../../../../../../day-night-heat-redistribution.md) lowers the contrast; when radiation is fast, each hemisphere stays closer to its local radiative balance.

For a rough atmospheric column estimate,

$$
\tau_{\rm rad}\sim\frac{c_pp}{4g\sigma_{\rm SB}T^3},\qquad
\tau_{\rm adv}\sim\frac{R_p}{U}.
$$

At comparable [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md), higher [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) sharply shortens radiative relaxation, tending to increase the day-night contrast. Wind speeds, rotation, and magnetic drag can modify this trend; dissociation and recombination can carry additional heat in very hot atmospheres.

At higher altitude, lower [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) generally means shorter radiative relaxation and a larger contrast. Infrared bands with larger [opacity](../../../../../../opacity.md) probe these higher layers, while lower-opacity windows sample deeper layers with longer cooling times and more effective redistribution. A wavelength-dependent [exoplanet thermal phase curve](../../../../../../exoplanet-thermal-phase-curve.md) can therefore reveal how the contrast and hot-region displacement change with pressure.
