<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume all three planets retain approximately their original hydrogen-helium bulk composition. Stellar encounters change orbital energy and irradiation, not automatically the planet's elemental abundances. Their atmospheric structures can become approximately stationary long before their interiors finish cooling.

For the unperturbed [Jupiter](../../../../../../jupiter.md)-like planet at $a\simeq5.2\,\mathrm{au}$, the zero-albedo globally averaged [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) is about $122\,\mathrm K$. Stellar light heats the outer atmosphere, while internal cooling supports a deeper temperature gradient and convection. At cooler pressures, [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md) favors methane and ammonia; condensate clouds can include ammonia at high levels and water deeper down. [Disequilibrium chemistry in an exoplanet atmosphere](../../../../../../disequilibrium-chemistry-in-an-exoplanet-atmosphere.md) can preserve carbon monoxide or other species from deeper layers.

For the inward-migrated planet at $a\simeq0.052\,\mathrm{au}$, the irradiation-only [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) is ten times higher, about $1220\,\mathrm K$, because $T_{\rm eq}\propto a^{-1/2}$. Its [irradiated planetary atmosphere](../../../../../../irradiated-planetary-atmosphere.md) has a heated radiative exterior, potentially strong day-night differences, and a deep [radiative-convective boundary](../../../../../../radiative-convective-boundary.md). At suitable pressures, [chemical equilibrium](../../../../../../chemical-equilibrium.md) increasingly favors carbon monoxide over methane; water remains important, while alkali absorption and high-temperature condensates can matter. An [atmospheric thermal inversion](../../../../../../inversion-meteorology.md) depends on absorbers, clouds, and irradiation and is not guaranteed simply by migration.

The ejected object is a [rogue planet](../../../../../../rogue-planet.md). Its irradiation-based [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) becomes very small, but its actual emitting [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) is set primarily by internal cooling and [Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction](../../../../../../kelvin-helmholtz-mechanism.md). After only several million years it can remain warm and self-luminous, with deep convection and an outward-cooling radiative atmosphere. Its photospheric chemistry and clouds depend on that cooling temperature; they cannot be inferred from the absence of a host star alone. None of these cases fixes an exact pressure-temperature profile without a cooling model, opacities, and atmospheric composition.
