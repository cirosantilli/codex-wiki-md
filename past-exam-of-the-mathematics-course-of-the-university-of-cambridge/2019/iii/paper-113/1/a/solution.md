<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write the [affine scheme](../../../../../../affine-scheme.md) as $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$. A point $x\in U$ corresponds to a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p\subset B$, and its [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) is $\mathcal O_{X,x}\cong B_{\mathfrak p}$ with [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$. Hence

$$
f_x\notin\mathfrak m_x
\iff \bar f/1\notin\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}
\iff \bar f\notin\mathfrak p.
$$

The last condition defines the [principal open subscheme](../../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) $D(\bar f)$, so

$$
\boxed{U\cap X_f=D(\bar f).}
$$

Every point of the [scheme](../../../../../../scheme.md) $X$ has such an affine neighbourhood. Thus $X_f$ is locally open, and hence is open in the [Zariski topology](../../../../../../zariski-topology.md).
