<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

First prove that $A$ is an [integrally closed domain](../../../../../../integrally-closed-domain.md). If $u\in\operatorname{Frac}A$ is integral over $A$, then it is integral over $A_x$. Since the [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md) $A_x$ is integrally closed, write $u=a/x^n$ with $a\in A$ and $n\geq0$ minimal. If $n>0$, an integral equation for $u$ gives, after multiplication by a suitable power of $x$,

$$
a^m+c_1a^{m-1}x^n+\cdots+c_mx^{nm}=0.
$$

Hence $a^m\in(x)$. The ideal $(x)$ is prime, so $a\in(x)$, contradicting minimality of $n$. Therefore $n=0$ and $u\in A$.

Now apply the [Nagata theorem for divisor class groups](../../../../../../nagata-theorem-for-divisor-class-groups.md). The class group of $A_x$ vanishes by the [divisor-class criterion for unique factorization](../../../../../../divisor-class-criterion-for-unique-factorization.md). Hence $\operatorname{Cl}(A)$ is generated by height-one primes that meet $\{1,x,x^2,\ldots\}$. Such a prime contains $x$ and must equal $(x)$, because the [Krull principal ideal theorem](../../../../../../krull-principal-ideal-theorem.md) makes the nonzero prime $(x)$ itself height one. Its divisor class is principal, so $\operatorname{Cl}(A)=0$. A second application of the divisor-class criterion yields

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is a unique factorization domain}.}
$$

This argument is the [Nagata criterion for unique factorization domains](../../../../../../nagata-criterion-for-unique-factorization-domains.md).
