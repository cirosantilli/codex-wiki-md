<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $R=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^3)$ and let $X=\operatorname{Spec}R$. The ideal $P=(x,z)$ is a height-one [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md), because $R/P\cong k[y]$ and $\dim R=2$. It therefore defines a [prime Weil divisor](../../../../../../prime-weil-divisor.md) $D=V(x,z)$.

Localizing at $x$ eliminates $y$, giving

$$
R_x\cong k[x,x^{-1},z],
$$

a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md). The [Nagata theorem for divisor class groups](../../../../../../nagata-theorem-for-divisor-class-groups.md) says that $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is generated by the height-one primes containing $x$. Since

$$
R/(x)\cong k[y,z]/(z^3),
$$

the only such prime is $P$, and hence $[D]$ generates. At the generic point of $D$, $y$ is a [unit in a ring](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) and $x=z^3/y$, so the order of vanishing is three:

$$
\operatorname{div}(x)=3D.
$$

Thus $3[D]=0$.

This relation has exact order three. Indeed, if a [principal divisor](../../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) were supported on $D$, its defining rational function would be a unit on $D(x)$. The units of $R_x=k[z][x,x^{-1}]$ are precisely $c x^m$ with $c\in k^\times$ and $m\in\mathbb Z$, whose divisors are $3mD$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}\bigl(k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^3)\bigr)\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,}
$$

generated by $[V(x,z)]$. This is the $n=3$ case of the [Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity](../../../../../../divisor-class-group-of-an-a-type-surface-singularity.md).
