<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On every open set $U$, define $F_U^\#(a)=a^p$. In [characteristic of a ring](../../../../../../characteristic-of-a-ring.md) $p$, the [binomial theorem](../../../../../../binomial-theorem.md) gives $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$, so these are [ring homomorphisms](../../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md); they commute with restrictions and hence define a morphism of [sheaves of rings](../../../../../../sheaf-of-rings.md).

On an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}A$, the inverse image of a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p$ under the [Frobenius endomorphism](../../../../../../frobenius-endomorphism.md) is

$$
\{a:a^p\in\mathfrak p\}=\mathfrak p,
$$

by primality. The induced continuous map is therefore the identity. These local morphisms agree on overlaps, giving the [Absolute Frobenius morphism](../../../../../../absolute-frobenius-morphism.md) $F_X:X\to X$. Its action on the underlying space and on every local section was prescribed, so the morphism is unique.
