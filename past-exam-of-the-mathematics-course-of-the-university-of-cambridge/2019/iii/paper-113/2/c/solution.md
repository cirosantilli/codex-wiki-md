<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take

$$
X=\mathbb P_{\mathbb F_p}^1.
$$

The [global regular functions on projective space](../../../../../../global-regular-functions-on-projective-space.md) give $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=\mathbb F_p$, on which the [Absolute Frobenius morphism](../../../../../../absolute-frobenius-morphism.md) is the identity. On the standard [affine line](../../../../../../affine-line.md) $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p[t]$, however, its map on functions is $t\mapsto t^p$, which is not surjective. Thus $F_X$ is not an [isomorphism of schemes](../../../../../../isomorphism-of-schemes.md).

Choose the rational closed point $x$ given by $t=0$. The affine formula for a [fibre product of schemes](../../../../../../fiber-product-of-schemes.md) gives its [scheme-theoretic fiber](../../../../../../scheme-theoretic-fiber.md):

$$
F_X^{-1}(x)
=\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathbb F_p[t]\otimes_{\mathbb F_p[t],\,t\mapsto t^p}\mathbb F_p\right)
\cong\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^p)}.
$$

This is the [Fibre of absolute Frobenius over a rational point of the affine line](../../../../../../fibre-of-absolute-frobenius-over-a-rational-point-of-the-affine-line.md): it is a one-point, length-$p$ [nonreduced scheme](../../../../../../nonreduced-scheme.md), rather than a reduced point.
