<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a dilute [particle suspension](../../../../../../suspension-chemistry.md), take $W_s>0$ as the downward speed relative to the carrier fluid. The [particle deposition flux](../../../../../../particle-deposition-flux.md) through a horizontal absorbing bed is $W_s\phi_b$, where $\phi_b$ is the near-bed [particle volume fraction](../../../../../../particle-volume-fraction.md). Without stirring, settling can create a particle-free upper layer and a descending concentration front, so uniform concentration throughout the remaining fluid is not automatic. Weak [turbulence](../../../../../../turbulence-split.md) can maintain a nearly uniform suspended concentration while leaving the mean [settling velocity](../../../../../../settling-velocity.md) approximately equal to the isolated-particle value; this justifies using $W_s\phi$ in a mixed-layer balance. Strong stirring can produce resuspension and requires a separate erosion model.

For a bed inclined at angle $\vartheta$ to the horizontal, the normal settling speed is $W_s\cos\vartheta$, giving deposition flux $W_s\phi_b\cos\vartheta$ per unit actual bed area. There is also downslope particle motion, and near-wall effects or resuspension can alter this simplest absorbing-boundary model. Integrating the normal projection over a sloping bed is equivalent to integrating $W_s\phi_b$ over its horizontal projection.

The mixture [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) is $\rho=\rho_0+(\rho_p-\rho_0)\phi$, so its [reduced gravity](../../../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md) is

$$
\boxed{g'=d\phi,\qquad d=\frac{g(\rho_p-\rho_0)}{\rho_0}.}
$$

The [Boussinesq approximation](../../../../../../boussinesq-approximation.md) requires $(\rho_p-\rho_0)\phi/\rho_0\ll1$, in addition to the dilution assumption.
