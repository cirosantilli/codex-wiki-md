<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The stationary [Killing vector field](../../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) is $k=\partial_t=\partial_v$, with $k^2=-f$. The normal to $r=\text{constant}$ has squared norm $g^{rr}=f$, so $r=r_+$ is a [null hypersurface](../../../../../../../null-hypersurface.md); there $k$ is both tangent and normal. It is therefore a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../../killing-horizon.md).

For a static metric of this form, the [surface gravity](../../../../../../../surface-gravity.md) is $\kappa=f'(r_+)/2$. Since $r_+^2r_-^2=Q^2$ and $r_+^2+r_-^2=2M$,

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\frac{r_+^2-r_-^2}{r_+^3}=\frac{2\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}}{r_+^3}}.
$$

For the [extremal black hole](../../../../../../../extremal-black-hole.md) $M=Q$, the horizons coincide and **$\kappa=0$**.
