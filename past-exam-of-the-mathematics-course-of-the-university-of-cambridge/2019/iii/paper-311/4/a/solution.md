<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a real free [scalar field](../../../../../../scalar-field.md) on a [globally hyperbolic spacetime](../../../../../../globally-hyperbolic-spacetime.md), take

$$
S=-\frac12\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left(\nabla_a\phi\nabla^a\phi+(m^2+\xi R)\phi^2\right).
$$

The [Klein-Gordon equation](../../../../../../klein-gordon-equation.md) has a well-posed initial-value problem on every [Cauchy hypersurface](../../../../../../cauchy-surface.md). Its complex solution space carries the conserved [Klein-Gordon inner product](../../../../../../klein-gordon-inner-product.md). Choose modes with

$$
(u_i,u_j)_{KG}=\delta_{ij},\qquad (u_i^*,u_j^*)_{KG}=-\delta_{ij},\qquad (u_i,u_j^*)_{KG}=0,
$$

and expand

$$
\widehat\phi=\sum_i(a_i u_i+a_i^\dagger u_i^*),\qquad [a_i,a_j^\dagger]=\delta_{ij}.
$$

The [canonical commutation relations](../../../../../../canonical-commutation-relation.md) produce the [bosonic Fock space](../../../../../../bosonic-fock-space.md), and $a_i|0\rangle=0$ defines the chosen vacuum.

The choice of [positive-frequency solutions](../../../../../../positive-frequency-solution.md) is extra structure. In a time-dependent geometry there is no preferred time translation, so different choices mix $u_i$ with $u_i^*$ and give the [vacuum ambiguity in a nonstationary spacetime](../../../../../../vacuum-ambiguity-in-a-nonstationary-spacetime.md). In a [stationary spacetime](../../../../../../stationary-spacetime.md), a timelike [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) defines positive frequency by $i\mathcal L_Ku_i=\omega_i u_i$, $\omega_i>0$, and hence the preferred [vacuum state in a stationary spacetime](../../../../../../vacuum-state-in-a-stationary-spacetime.md).

If the spacetime is stationary in the remote past and future, define normalized in- and out-modes there. Their [Bogoliubov transformation](../../../../../../bogoliubov-transformation.md) is

$$
u_i^{\rm out}=\sum_j(\alpha_{ij}u_j^{\rm in}+\beta_{ij}u_j^{{\rm in}*}).
$$

The expected out-particle number in the in-vacuum is

$$
\boxed{\langle0_{\rm in}|N_i^{\rm out}|0_{\rm in}\rangle=\sum_j|\beta_{ij}|^2}.
$$

Thus nonzero beta coefficients measure particle creation by the intervening time dependence.
