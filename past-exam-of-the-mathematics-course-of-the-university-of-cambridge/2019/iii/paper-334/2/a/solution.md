<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The first term is the local viscous force per unit length from [resistive-force theory](../../../../../../resistive-force-theory.md). With [unit tangent vector](../../../../../../unit-tangent-vector.md) $\mathbf t=\mathbf r_s$, the drag tensor gives $-c_\parallel\mathbf v_\parallel-c_\perp\mathbf v_\perp$, where $\mathbf v=\mathbf r_t$ is velocity relative to the fluid.

The second term is the bending force of an [inextensible filament](../../../../../../inextensible-filament.md). Varying its energy $E_b=(B/2)\int|\mathbf r_{ss}|^2ds$ gives bulk force density $-B\mathbf r_{ssss}$. The final term, $\partial_s(T\mathbf r_s)$, is the force density from [filament tension](../../../../../../filament-tension.md); $T$ is the [Lagrange multiplier](../../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) enforcing $|\mathbf r_s|=1$. At low [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), these forces balance without a filament acceleration term.

With mass, length, and time dimensions $M,\mathsf L,\mathsf T$, the coefficients have

$$
\boxed{[c_\perp]=[c_\parallel]=M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-1},\qquad [B]=M\mathsf L^3\mathsf T^{-2},\qquad [T]=M\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}.}
$$

Thus the drag coefficients have the dimensions of [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md), the [filament bending modulus](../../../../../../filament-bending-modulus.md) has dimensions force times length squared, and tension has dimensions force.
