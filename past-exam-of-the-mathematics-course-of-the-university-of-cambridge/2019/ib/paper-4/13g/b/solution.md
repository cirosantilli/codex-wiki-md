<h1 id="13g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [closed set](../../../../../../closed-set.md) $C\subseteq X$ is compact because it is closed in the compact space $X$. Its image $f(C)$ is compact by the fact that a [continuous image of a compact space](../../../../../../continuous-image-of-a-compact-space.md) is compact, and every compact subset of the [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) $Y$ is closed. Thus the continuous bijection $f$ is a [closed map](../../../../../../closed-map.md). Its inverse $f^{-1}:Y\to X$ is consequently continuous, so $f$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). This is the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../../../../compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md).
