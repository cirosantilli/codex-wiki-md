<h1 id="2g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $g\in G$. Since $K$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), $gPg^{-1}\leq K$; it has the same order as $P$, so it is also a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $K$. The conjugacy assertion in the [Sylow theorems](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) supplies $k\in K$ such that

$$
k(gPg^{-1})k^{-1}=P.
$$

Consequently $kg\in N_G(P)$, and therefore

$$
g=k^{-1}(kg)\in K N_G(P)=N_G(P)K.
$$

Thus $G\subseteq N_G(P)K$, while the reverse inclusion is immediate because both factors are subgroups of $G$. Hence

$$
\boxed{G=N_G(P)K}.
$$
