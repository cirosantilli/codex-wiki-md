**No.** For each $x>0$, $e^{-nx}\to0$, whereas $e^{-n0}=1$ for every $n$. The [pointwise limit](../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md) on $[0,1]$ is therefore

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x=0,\\0,&x>0.\end{cases}
$$

This limit is not [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $0$. Since every $f_n(x)=e^{-nx}$ is continuous, the [uniform limit theorem](../../../../../../uniform-limit-theorem.md) rules out uniform convergence. Directly, $|f_n(x)-f(x)|=e^{-nx}$ for $x>0$, whose [supremum](../../../../../../supremum.md) over $(0,1]$ is $1$ for every $n$.
