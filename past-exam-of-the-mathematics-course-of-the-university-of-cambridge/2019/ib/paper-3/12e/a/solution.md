<h1 id="12e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A local form of the [Picard-Lindelöf theorem](../../../../../../picard-lindelof-theorem.md) is as follows. Let $F(t,y)$ be continuous on a rectangle about $(t_0,y_0)$ and uniformly [Lipschitz](../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) in $y$ there. Then some interval about $t_0$ admits a unique continuously differentiable solution of

$$
y'(t)=F(t,y(t)),
\qquad y(t_0)=y_0.
$$

The solution is the unique fixed point of the associated integral operator. If $F$ is continuous on $[a,b]\times\mathbb R^n$ and globally Lipschitz in $y$ with one constant, the solution exists uniquely throughout $[a,b]$.
