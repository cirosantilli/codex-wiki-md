<h1 id="12e/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose a [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) of the finite-dimensional vector space and let $|x|_\infty$ be the maximum absolute coordinate. For any norm $N$, the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
N(x)\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^nN(e_i)\right)|x|_\infty,
$$

so $N$ is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) in the coordinate topology. On the compact set $\{x:|x|_\infty=1\}$, the positive continuous function $N$ attains a positive minimum $m$ and a finite maximum $M$. By [absolute homogeneity](../../../../../../../norm.md),

$$
m|x|_\infty\leq N(x)\leq M|x|_\infty.
$$

This proves the [equivalence of norms in finite dimensions](../../../../../../../equivalence-of-norms-in-finite-dimensions.md): every norm is equivalent to the maximum norm, and [transitivity](../../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) of these inequalities proves that any two norms on a [finite-dimensional vector space](../../../../../../../finite-dimensional-vector-space.md) are Lipschitz equivalent.
