<h1 id="3e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At a point with $xy\ne0$, the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) of the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is

$$
\det Df(x,y)=\frac{5y^4}{3x^{2/3}}\ne0.
$$

The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) therefore supplies neighbourhoods on which $f$ has a continuously differentiable, hence [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md), inverse. Concretely, if $(u,v)=f(x,y)$, the local inverse is the restriction of

$$
\boxed{y=v^{1/5},\qquad x=(u-v^{2/5})^3},
$$

which is continuously differentiable near the image whenever $y\ne0$.
