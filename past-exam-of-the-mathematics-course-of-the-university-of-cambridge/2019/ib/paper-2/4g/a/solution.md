<h1 id="4g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Suppose for contradiction that the [continuous image of a connected space](../../../../../../continuous-image-of-a-connected-space.md) $Y=f(X)$ is disconnected. Then there are disjoint nonempty sets $U,V$, open in the [subspace topology](../../../../../../subspace-topology.md) on $Y$, with $Y=U\cup V$. By [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), $f^{-1}(U)$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ are disjoint open subsets of $X$; by [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) both are nonempty, and their union is $X$. This is a [separation of a topological space](../../../../../../separation-of-a-topological-space.md) of $X$, contradicting that $X$ is a [connected space](../../../../../../connected-space.md). Hence $Y$ is connected.
