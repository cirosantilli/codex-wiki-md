<h1 id="11a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $E=|\mathbf E|$ and $\widehat{\mathbf E}=\mathbf E/E$. The constant electric [force](../../../../../../force.md) gives [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md)

$$
\mathbf p(t)=q\mathbf E t.
$$

Combining $\mathbf p=\gamma m\dot{\mathbf x}$ with the [relativistic energy-momentum relation](../../../../../../energy-momentum-relation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf x}(t)=\frac{q\mathbf E t/m}{\sqrt{1+(qEt/mc)^2}}}.
$$

The [dimensional analysis](../../../../../../dimensional-analysis.md) from part (a) confirms that $qEt$ has the dimensions of [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md), so $qEt/(mc)$ is dimensionless and the expression has the dimensions of [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md).

The [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) increases monotonically from zero and approaches the [speed of light](../../../../../../speed-of-light.md) asymptotically:

$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}|\dot{\mathbf x}(t)|=c.
$$

Integrating in the direction of the field gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf x(t)=\frac{mc^2}{qE}\left[\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{qEt}{mc}\right)^2}-1\right]\widehat{\mathbf E}}.
$$

At small times, the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) of the square root gives the [nonrelativistic limit](../../../../../../nonrelativistic-limit.md)

$$
\mathbf x(t)=\frac{q\mathbf E}{2m}t^2+O(t^4),
$$

which is motion from rest with the constant [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) $q\mathbf E/m$.
