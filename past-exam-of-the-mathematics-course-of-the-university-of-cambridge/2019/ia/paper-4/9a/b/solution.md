<h1 id="9a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $C$ be the centre of the Earth and let $M$ be its mass. Since $CW=R$ and $CS=R+l$, the [law of cosines](../../../../../../law-of-cosines.md) in triangle $CSB$ gives the ball's initial distance from $C$ as

$$
r_B=CB=\sqrt{(R+l)^2+l^2-2l(R+l)\cos\theta_0}.
$$

The surface value of the [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md) satisfies $g=GM/R^2$. Using the [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md) $-GMm/r$, [conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) now gives

$$
\frac12mv_2^2=GMm\left(\frac1R-\frac1{r_B}\right).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{v_2=\left[2gR^2\left(\frac1R-\frac1{\sqrt{(R+l)^2+l^2-2l(R+l)\cos\theta_0}}\right)\right]^{1/2}}.
$$
