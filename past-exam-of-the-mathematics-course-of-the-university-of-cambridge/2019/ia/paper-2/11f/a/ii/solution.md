<h1 id="11f/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For every $\lambda\in\mathbb R^2$, the scalar $\lambda^TX$ is normal with mean $\lambda^T\mu$ and variance $\lambda^TV\lambda$. The [moment-generating function of a normal distribution](../../../../../../../moment-generating-function-of-a-normal-distribution.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{M_X(\lambda)=\exp\left(\lambda^T\mu+\frac12\lambda^TV\lambda\right)}.
$$

**Thus $\mu$ and $V$ determine the moment-generating function on all of $\mathbb R^2$. By the [uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions](../../../../../../../uniqueness-theorem-for-moment-generating-functions.md), they uniquely determine the [probability distribution](../../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) of $X$.**
