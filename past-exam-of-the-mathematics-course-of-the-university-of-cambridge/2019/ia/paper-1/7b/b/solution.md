<h1 id="7b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Eigenvectors belonging to distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md). Under the hypothesis they therefore form a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$. For every $j$,

$$
\chi_A(A)v_j=\chi_A(\lambda_j)v_j=0
$$

because $\chi_A(\lambda_j)=0$. A linear map that vanishes on a basis is zero, hence

$$
\boxed{\chi_A(A)=0}.
$$

This proves the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md) in the distinct-eigenvalue case.
