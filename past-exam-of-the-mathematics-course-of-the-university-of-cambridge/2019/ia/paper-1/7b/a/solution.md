<h1 id="7b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose

$$
\boxed{\chi_A(z)=\det(A-zI)}.
$$

The [Leibniz formula for determinants](../../../../../../leibniz-formula-for-determinants.md) shows that its leading term is $(-z)^n$, as required. A scalar $\lambda$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) exactly when $A-\lambda I$ has a nonzero kernel, equivalently when it is singular, so

$$
\boxed{\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of }A\iff\chi_A(\lambda)=0}.
$$

The [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) gives a complex root of the degree-$n$ polynomial $\chi_A$, so every nonempty complex square matrix has at least one complex eigenvalue.
