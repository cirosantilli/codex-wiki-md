<h1 id="11h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $P=p_1p_2\cdots p_{k+1}$ be the product of the first $k+1$ [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md), and take $n=P^a$ for positive integers $a$. The [prime factorization](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) gives

$$
\tau(P^a)=(a+1)^{k+1},
\qquad
(\log P^a)^k=a^k(\log P)^k.
$$

Their ratio is

$$
\frac{(a+1)^{k+1}}{a^k(\log P)^k}
=\frac{a(1+1/a)^{k+1}}{(\log P)^k}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

**Thus every sufficiently large $a$ gives $\tau(P^a)\geq(\log P^a)^k$, producing infinitely many such $n$.**
