<h1 id="24f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [24F](../24f.md)

Apply a [projective linear transformation](../../../../../projective-linear-transformation.md) sending the first $n+1$ points to the [coordinate points](../../../../../coordinate-point.md)

$$
e_0=[1:0:\cdots:0],\ldots,e_n=[0:\cdots:0:1].
$$

The next point has every [homogeneous coordinate](../../../../../homogeneous-coordinate.md) nonzero by [general linear position](../../../../../general-linear-position.md), so a diagonal projective transformation fixing the $e_i$ sends it to

$$
e=[1:\cdots:1].
$$

Write the last point as $a=[a_0:\cdots:a_n]$. Again general linear position implies that every $a_i$ is nonzero. It also implies $a_i\ne a_j$ for $i\ne j$: otherwise $a$, $e$, and the $n-1$ coordinate points other than $e_i,e_j$ would lie in the [hyperplane](../../../../../hyperplane.md) $x_i-x_j=0$.

Put $t_i=a_i^{-1}$ and define the degree-$n$ [homogeneous polynomials](../../../../../homogeneous-polynomial.md)

$$
P_i(S,T)=\prod_{j\ne i}(S-t_jT),\qquad 0\leq i\leq n.
$$

The associated map $\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^n$ is a [rational normal curve](../../../../../rational-normal-curve.md). It sends $[t_i:1]$ to $e_i$, sends $[1:0]$ to $e$, and sends $[0:1]$ to $a$, because

$$
P_i(0,1)=(-1)^n\prod_{j\ne i}t_j
$$

is one common nonzero scalar times $t_i^{-1}=a_i$. This proves existence.

For uniqueness, parametrize any rational normal curve through these points and choose a coordinate $[S:T]$ on its source $\mathbb P^1$ so that the preimages of $e$ and $a$ are respectively $[1:0]$ and $[0:1]$. If the preimage of $e_i$ is $[s_i:1]$, then its $i$th coordinate polynomial must have all the other $s_j$ as roots. It is therefore

$$
Q_i(S,T)=c_i\prod_{j\ne i}(S-s_jT).
$$

The value at $[1:0]$ is $e$, so all $c_i$ are equal and may be removed by a common rescaling. The value at $[0:1]$ is $a$, so $s_i^{-1}$ is proportional to $a_i$. Thus all $s_i$ are obtained from the $t_i$ by one common rescaling of the coordinate on $\mathbb P^1$. This merely reparametrizes the curve, proving uniqueness.

For $n=3$, choose the [monomial basis](../../../../../monomial-basis.md) $S^3,S^2T,ST^2,T^3$. The resulting curve is the [twisted cubic](../../../../../twisted-cubic.md)

$$
[S:T]\longmapsto[S^3:S^2T:ST^2:T^3].
$$

Its [homogeneous ideal](../../../../../homogeneous-ideal.md) is generated by the three quadratic [matrix minors](../../../../../minor-linear-algebra.md)

$$
\boxed{x_0x_2-x_1^2,\qquad x_0x_3-x_1x_2,\qquad x_1x_3-x_2^2},
$$

the $2\times2$ minors of

$$
\begin{pmatrix}x_0&x_1&x_2\\x_1&x_2&x_3\end{pmatrix}.
$$
