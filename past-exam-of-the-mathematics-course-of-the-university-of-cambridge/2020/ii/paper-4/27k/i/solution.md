↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [M/M/1 queue](../../../../../../m-m-1-queue.md) has [Poisson process](../../../../../../poisson-process.md) arrivals of rate $\lambda$, independent [exponential](../../../../../../exponential-distribution.md) service times of rate $\mu$, and one server. The two letters $M$ indicate the [memoryless property](../../../../../../memorylessness-of-the-exponential-distribution.md) of the interarrival and service-time distributions. Its queue length is a [birth-death process](../../../../../../birth-death-process.md) with birth rate $\lambda$ and death rate $\mu$ away from zero. When $\rho=\lambda/\mu<1$, its normalized [stationary distribution](../../../../../../stationary-distribution.md) is

$$
\pi_n=(1-\rho)\rho^n;
$$

the powers $\rho^n$ quoted in the question form the corresponding unnormalized invariant measure.
