<h1 id="39b/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For

$$
p(\rho)=A-\frac{B^2}{\rho},
$$

the [adiabatic sound speed](../../../../../../../adiabatic-sound-speed.md) is

$$
c^2=\frac{dp}{d\rho}=\frac{B^2}{\rho^2},
\qquad c=\frac B\rho.
$$

Since $\int(c/\rho)d\rho=-B/\rho=-c$, choose invariants that vanish in the equilibrium state $(u,c)=(0,c_0)$:

$$
\boxed{R_+=u-c+c_0,
\qquad R_-=u+c-c_0}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{u+c=c_0+R_-,
\qquad u-c=-c_0+R_+}.
$$

In a right-moving [simple wave](../../../../../../../simple-wave.md) with $R_-=0$, the $R_+$ characteristic speed is therefore $u+c=c_0$, independent of amplitude. The transport equation becomes $(\partial_t+c_0\partial_x)R_+=0$, so the whole velocity profile translates rigidly at the equilibrium [sound speed](../../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) $c_0$ without nonlinear distortion.
