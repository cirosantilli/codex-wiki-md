<h1 id="32e/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For $0<\mu<1$, all orbits converge to one fixed point, and for $\mu=1$ every orbit is eventually fixed or period two, so no iterate has a horseshoe. For the symmetric [tent map](../../../../../../../tent-map.md) with slope magnitude $\mu>1$, the [topological entropy](../../../../../../../topological-entropy.md) is $\log\mu>0$. The [interval-map positive-entropy horseshoe theorem](../../../../../../../interval-map-positive-entropy-horseshoe-theorem.md) says that a continuous [interval map](../../../../../../../interval-map.md) has [positive topological entropy](../../../../../../../positive-topological-entropy.md) exactly when some [iterate](../../../../../../../iterated-function.md) has a [horseshoe](../../../../../../../horseshoe-for-an-interval-map.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{F\text{ is chaotic in Glendinning's sense exactly when }1<\mu\leq2}.
$$

For $\mu\geq\sqrt2$ this already follows from part (iii); for $1<\mu<\sqrt2$, a higher iterate supplies the horseshoe.
