<h1 id="26k/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $n>m$, [independence](../../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) and $\mathbb EX_k=0$ give

$$
\lVert S_n-S_m\rVert_2^2
=\mathbb E\left|\sum_{k=m+1}^na_kX_k\right|^2
=\sum_{k=m+1}^na_k^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus $(S_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the complete [L2 space](../../../../../../../l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space.md) and converges in $L^2$ to some random variable $S$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) gives $\lVert Z\rVert_1\leq\lVert Z\rVert_2$ on a probability space, so the convergence also holds in $L^1$. Finally, convergence in $L^2$ implies [convergence in probability](../../../../../../../convergence-in-probability.md), which implies [convergence in distribution](../../../../../../../convergence-in-distribution.md).
