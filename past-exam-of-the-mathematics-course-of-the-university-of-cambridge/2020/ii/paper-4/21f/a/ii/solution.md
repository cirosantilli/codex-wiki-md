<h1 id="21f/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write the closed [oriented surface](../../../../../../../oriented-surface.md) of [genus](../../../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) $g$ as $\Sigma_g=A\cup B$, where $B$ is a [closed disk](../../../../../../../closed-disc.md), $A$ is a slightly enlarged once-punctured surface, and $A\cap B$ is an [annulus](../../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md). Thus $B$ is [contractible](../../../../../../../contractible-space.md), $A\cap B$ is [homotopy equivalent](../../../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) to the [circle](../../../../../../../circle.md), and $A$ is homotopy equivalent to the [wedge sum](../../../../../../../wedge-sum.md) of $2g$ circles. The boundary circle represents the product of the $g$ commutators in the [fundamental group](../../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) of $A$, so its image in the [first homology group](../../../../../../../first-homology.md), which is the [abelianization](../../../../../../../abelianization.md) of that group, is zero.

The relevant part of the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) is consequently

$$
0\longrightarrow H_2(\Sigma_g)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb Z^{2g}\longrightarrow H_1(\Sigma_g)\longrightarrow0.
$$

The degree-zero part says that the connected space $\Sigma_g$ has $H_0(\Sigma_g)\cong\mathbb Z$, while the groups above degree two vanish. Hence

$$
\boxed{H_i(\Sigma_g;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z^{2g},&i=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$
