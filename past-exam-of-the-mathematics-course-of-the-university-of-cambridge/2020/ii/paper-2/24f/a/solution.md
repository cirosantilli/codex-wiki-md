<h1 id="24f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Because $k$ is [algebraically closed](../../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md), the [smooth quadric surface](../../../../../../smooth-quadric-surface.md) $V$ has a $k$-point $p$. Choose a plane $\Pi\cong\mathbb P_k^2$ not containing $p$ and project from $p$:

$$
\pi_p:V\dashrightarrow\Pi.
$$

For a general point $q\in\Pi$, the line $\overline{pq}$ meets the degree-two surface $V$ in $p$ and exactly one further point. Sending $q$ to that second intersection is a [rational map](../../../../../../rational-map-complex-analysis.md) inverse to $\pi_p$ on dense open subsets. Therefore

$$
\boxed{V\text{ is birational to }\mathbb P_k^2}.
$$
