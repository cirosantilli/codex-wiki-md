<h1 id="5j/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Two packs contain $40$ cigarettes. Holding height fixed, increasing average consumption from zero to two packs per day changes the [log odds](../../../../../../log-odds.md) by

$$
40(0.02313)=0.9252.
$$

The corresponding [odds ratio](../../../../../../odds-ratio.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{e^{0.9252}\approx2.52}.
$$

Thus the model estimates that a two-pack-per-day smoker has about $2.52$ times the odds of heart disease of an otherwise comparable nonsmoker. This is an association in the fitted observational model, rather than by itself a causal conclusion.
