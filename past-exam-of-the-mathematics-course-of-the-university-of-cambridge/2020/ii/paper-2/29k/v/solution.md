<h1 id="29k/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Suppose the claim $H=(S_1)^2$, payable at time $1$, were replicable. Holdings chosen at time zero are constants, so the time-$1$ value of any [self-financing portfolio](../../../../../../self-financing-portfolio.md) has the affine form

$$
aS_1^0+bS_1=a(1+r)+bS_1.
$$

But $S_1=S_0Z_1$ has a continuous positive density on $(0,\infty)$. No affine function of $S_1$ can equal the quadratic function $S_1^2$ almost surely. Therefore $H$ is not attainable and the market is not [complete](../../../../../../complete-market.md).

The same argument applies if $H$ is viewed as a time-$T$ claim: once $H$ is known at time $1$, its required discounted value at time $1$ is fixed, and the first-period portfolio value would still have to reproduce a nonaffine function of $S_1$.
