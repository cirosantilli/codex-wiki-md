<h1 id="37d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The momentum conjugate to $x^\mu$ is

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu}
=mc\frac{\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}+qA_\mu,
$$

while

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu}
=q(\partial_\mu A_\nu)\dot x^\nu.
$$

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) therefore gives

$$
mc\frac d{d\lambda}
\left(\frac{\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}\right)
=q(\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu)\dot x^\nu
=qF_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu.
$$

Since $dot x^\mu=(d\tau/d\lambda)u^\mu$ and $\sqrt{-\dot x^2}=c,d\tau/d\lambda$, division by $d\tau/d\lambda$ gives the [Lorentz-force equation from the worldline action](../../../../../../lorentz-force-equation-from-the-worldline-action.md)

$$
\boxed{m\frac{du_\mu}{d\tau}=qF_{\mu\nu}u^\nu.}
$$

Raising the free index yields exactly

$$
\boxed{m\frac{du^\mu}{d\tau}=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu.}
$$
