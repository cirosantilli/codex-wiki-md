<h1 id="37d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $A^\mu=(0,0,Bx,0)$, the only independent nonzero component of the [electromagnetic field tensor](../../../../../../electromagnetic-field-tensor.md) is

$$
F^{12}=B,
\qquad F^{21}=-B.
$$

The temporal and $z$ components of the [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) show that $u^0$ and $u^3$ are constant. Taking $u^3=0$, the transverse equations are

$$
\ddot x=\frac{qB}{m}\dot y,
\qquad
\ddot y=-\frac{qB}{m}\dot x,
$$

where dots now denote [proper time](../../../../../../proper-time.md) derivatives. With

$$
\boxed{\omega=\frac{qB}{m}},
$$

a solution is

$$
\boxed{
x(\tau)=x_c+R\cos\bigl(\omega(\tau-\tau_0)\bigr),
\qquad
y(\tau)=y_c-R\sin\bigl(\omega(\tau-\tau_0)\bigr).
}
$$

Direct differentiation verifies both equations. Hence the trajectory is a [relativistic cyclotron orbit](../../../../../../relativistic-cyclotron-orbit.md) with proper angular frequency $omega=qB/m$; the sign fixes its orientation.
