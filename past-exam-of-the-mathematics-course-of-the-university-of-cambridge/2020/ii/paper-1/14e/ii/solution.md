<h1 id="14e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The solution of $y''+k^2y=0$ with $y(0)=1$ and $y'(0)=0$ is $y(x)=\cos(kx)$. Near $x=0$, the map $z=\sin^2x$ has the local inverse $x=\arcsin\sqrt z$, so this solution is [analytic](../../../../../../space-of-holomorphic-functions.md) in $z$, corresponds to exponent zero at $z=0$, and has value one there. Uniqueness of the normalized local solution of the [Gauss hypergeometric equation](../../../../../../gauss-hypergeometric-equation.md) therefore gives the [hypergeometric cosine identity](../../../../../../hypergeometric-cosine-identity.md)

$$
\boxed{
F\left(\frac{k}{2},-\frac{k}{2};\frac12;z\right)
=\cos\!\left(k\arcsin\sqrt z\right),
}
$$

or, when $z=\sin^2x$ on the chosen local branch,

$$
\boxed{F\left(\frac{k}{2},-\frac{k}{2};\frac12;\sin^2x\right)=\cos(kx)}.
$$
