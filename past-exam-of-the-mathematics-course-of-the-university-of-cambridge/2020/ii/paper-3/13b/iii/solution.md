<h1 id="13b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Each larva undergoes [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion-equation-split.md) over its exponentially distributed lifetime of mean $1/\mu$ and deposits eggs at rate $\lambda$ wherever it is found. The characteristic [diffusion length](../../../../../../diffusion-length.md) before death is $\sqrt{D/\mu}$, which explains the exponential spatial decay. Integrating the remnant over space gives

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e(x,\infty)\,dx=\frac{N\lambda}{\mu}.
$$

This is also immediate physically: each of the $N$ larvae lives for mean time $1/\mu$ and therefore lays an expected $\lambda/\mu$ eggs. The eggs remain as a stationary record because they neither die nor diffuse.
