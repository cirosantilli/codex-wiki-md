<h1 id="35a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In a box of volume $V$, the [electromagnetic mode density in a box](../../../../../../electromagnetic-mode-density-in-a-box.md), including the two transverse [polarizations](../../../../../../polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md), is

$$
dN=2\frac{V}{(2\pi)^3}d^3k
=\frac{V}{\pi^2}k^2\,dk.
$$

Each [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) has energy $\hbar\omega=\hbar ck$, and the given [mean occupation number](../../../../../../planck-photon-distribution.md) is $(e^{\hbar ck/T}-1)^{-1}$. Omitting the temperature-independent [zero-point energy](../../../../../../zero-point-energy.md), the total energy is therefore

$$
E=\frac{V\hbar c}{\pi^2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{k^3}{e^{\hbar ck/T}-1}\,dk.
$$

With the [change of variables](../../../../../../change-of-variables-formula.md) $x=\hbar ck/T$,

$$
E=\frac{VT^4}{\pi^2\hbar^3c^3}
\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}\,dx.
$$
