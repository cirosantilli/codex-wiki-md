<h1 id="26k/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Kolmogorov zero-one law](../../../../../../kolmogorov-s-zero-one-law.md) says that for [independent random variables](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) $(Y_n)_{n\geq0}$, every event in the [tail sigma-algebra](../../../../../../tail-sigma-algebra.md)

$$
\mathcal T=\bigcap_{N\geq0}\sigma(Y_N,Y_{N+1},\ldots)
$$

has [probability](../../../../../../probability-measure.md) zero or one.

In the [canonical model of an independent and identically distributed sequence](../../../../../../canonical-model-of-an-independent-and-identically-distributed-sequence.md), the sample space is a sequence space $E^{\mathbb N}$ with the product law, $Y_n$ is the $n$th coordinate, and the left [Bernoulli shift](../../../../../../bernoulli-shift.md) $S$ satisfies $Y_n\circ S=Y_{n+1}$. If $A$ is shift-invariant, then for every $N$,

$$
A=S^{-N}A\in\sigma(Y_N,Y_{N+1},\ldots),
$$

so $A\in\mathcal T$. The zero-one law therefore says that the canonical one-sided i.i.d. shift is [ergodic](../../../../../../ergodicity.md).
