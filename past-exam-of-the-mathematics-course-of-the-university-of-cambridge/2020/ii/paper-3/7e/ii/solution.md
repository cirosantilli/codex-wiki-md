<h1 id="7e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set

$$
F(z)=\wp'(z)^2-4\wp(z)^3+g_2\wp(z)+g_3.
$$

This is an [elliptic function](../../../../../../elliptic-function.md). Write $a=g_2/20$ and $b=g_3/28$ in the expansion from part (i). At a lattice point, the coefficient of $z^{-2}$ in $F$ is $g_2-20a=0$, and its constant coefficient is $g_3-28b=0$; there are no other singular terms. Every apparent pole is therefore a [removable singularity](../../../../../../removable-singularity.md), and $F$ extends to an entire elliptic function with value zero at the lattice points.

An entire elliptic function is bounded on a [fundamental parallelogram](../../../../../../fundamental-parallelogram-of-a-period-lattice.md) and hence everywhere. By [Liouville theorem](../../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) it is constant, and its value at zero is zero. Thus $F\equiv0$, proving the [Weierstrass elliptic differential equation](../../../../../../weierstrass-elliptic-differential-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{\wp'(z)^2=4\wp(z)^3-g_2\wp(z)-g_3.}
$$
