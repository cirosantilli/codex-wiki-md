<h1 id="31e/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

On the two vertical sides of the square, the $x$ component of the [vector field](../../../../../../../vector-field.md) satisfies

$$
\dot x\big|_{x=1}=-1+\sin(2y)\leq0,
\qquad
\dot x\big|_{x=-1}=1+\sin(2y)\geq0.
$$

On the horizontal sides,

$$
\dot y\big|_{y=1}=-x-1\leq0,
\qquad
\dot y\big|_{y=-1}=1-x\geq0.
$$

Thus the vector field never points outward through the boundary, so the closed square is a [positively invariant set](../../../../../../../positively-invariant-set.md) and trajectories cannot leave $S$.

At a fixed point, $\dot y=0$ gives $x=-y^3$. Substitution into $\dot x=0$ gives

$$
2y^3-y^9+\sin(2y)=0.
$$

For $0<y<1$, both $y^3(2-y^6)$ and $\sin(2y)$ are positive; for $-1<y<0$, both are negative. Hence $y=0$, and then $x=0$, is the unique [equilibrium point](../../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) in $S$.

This alone does not prove that $S$ lies in the [domain of stability](../../../../../../../basin-of-attraction.md). A compact trapping region containing a unique equilibrium can also contain a [periodic orbit](../../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) or another nontrivial [omega-limit set](../../../../../../../omega-limit-set.md).
