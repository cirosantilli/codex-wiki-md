<h1 id="31e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\phi_t(x_0)$ denote the [flow map](../../../../../../flow-map.md) generated by $\dot x=f(x)$.


- The fixed point $0$ is [asymptotically stable](../../../../../../asymptotic-stability.md) if it is [Lyapunov stable](../../../../../../lyapunov-stability.md) and there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $0$ such that $\phi_t(x_0)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ for every $x_0\in U$.
- Its [domain of stability](../../../../../../basin-of-attraction.md), or [basin of attraction](../../../../../../basin-of-attraction.md), is $\{x_0:\phi_t(x_0)\to0\text{ as }t\to\infty\}$, with forward existence understood.
- A [Lyapunov function](../../../../../../lyapunov-function.md) on a neighbourhood $E$ of $0$ is a continuous function $V$, differentiable away from the origin, such that $V(0)=0$, $V(x)>0$ for $x\ne0$, and its [orbital derivative](../../../../../../orbital-derivative.md)


$$
\dot V(x)=\nabla V(x)\mathbin{\cdot}f(x)
$$

is nonpositive on $E$.

The [First Lyapunov theorem](../../../../../../first-lyapunov-theorem.md) says that the existence of such a function makes the origin Lyapunov stable. To prove it, choose a sufficiently small closed ball $\overline B_\varepsilon\subset E$. By [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) and positive definiteness,

$$
\alpha=\min_{\lVert x\rVert=\varepsilon}V(x)>0.
$$

By [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), some $\delta>0$ satisfies $V(x)<\alpha$ whenever $\lVert x\rVert<\delta$. Along a trajectory beginning in $B_\delta$, $V$ cannot increase. Such a trajectory therefore cannot reach the sphere $\lVert x\rVert=\varepsilon$, where $V\geq\alpha$. Hence it remains in $B_\varepsilon$ for every $t\geq0$, which is Lyapunov stability.

The [LaSalle invariance principle](../../../../../../lasalle-s-invariance-principle.md) says that if a trajectory remains in a compact [positively invariant set](../../../../../../positively-invariant-set.md) on which $\dot V\leq0$, then it approaches the largest invariant subset of $\{x:\dot V(x)=0\}$.
