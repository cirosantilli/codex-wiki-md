<h1 id="10c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $p=1/4$, put $|\psi_{\rm in}\rangle=\tfrac12|g\rangle+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}|b\rangle$. After the first phase rotation the state is

$$
|v\rangle=\frac{e^{i\phi}}2|g\rangle+\frac{\sqrt3}2|b\rangle,
\qquad
\langle\psi_{\rm in}|v\rangle=\frac{e^{i\phi}+3}{4}.
$$

Since $UR_{00}^\pi U^\dagger=I-2|\psi_{\rm in}\rangle\langle\psi_{\rm in}|$, the requested state is

$$
\frac{e^{i\phi}-3}{4}|g\rangle
-\frac{\sqrt3(e^{i\phi}+1)}4|b\rangle.
$$

It is proportional to $|g\rangle$ exactly when $e^{i\phi}=-1$, hence when $\phi=\pi$. The state is then $-|g\rangle$, whose [global phase](../../../../../../global-phase.md) has no physical effect.
