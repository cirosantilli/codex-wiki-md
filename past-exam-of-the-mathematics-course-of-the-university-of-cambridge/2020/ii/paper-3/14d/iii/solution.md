<h1 id="14d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Under the stated approximation the perturbation equation is

$$
\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta=0,
\qquad
\dot\delta\propto a^{-2}.
$$

During [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), $a\propto t^{1/2}$, so $\dot\delta\propto t^{-1}$ and

$$
\delta=C_1+C_2\log t=C_1+2C_2\log a.
$$

Matter perturbations therefore have only [logarithmic growth of matter perturbations during radiation domination](../../../../../../logarithmic-growth-of-matter-perturbations-during-radiation-domination.md).

In a [curvature-dominated universe](../../../../../../curvature-dominated-universe.md), $a\propto t$, so $\dot\delta\propto t^{-2}$ and

$$
\delta=C_1+C_2t^{-1}=C_1+C_2a^{-1}.
$$

The nondecaying mode is constant: curvature domination gives [freezing of matter perturbations during curvature domination](../../../../../../freezing-of-matter-perturbations-during-curvature-domination.md), rather than continued growth.
