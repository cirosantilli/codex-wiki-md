<h1 id="14d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Expand the [density contrast](../../../../../../density-contrast.md) in [Fourier modes](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md), $\delta(\mathbf x,t)=\int\delta_{\mathbf k}(t)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\,d^3k$. In the static universe each mode satisfies

$$
\ddot\delta_{\mathbf k}+c_s^2(k^2-k_J^2)\delta_{\mathbf k}=0.
$$

For $k>k_J$ there are [sound waves](../../../../../../acoustic-wave.md)

$$
\delta_{\mathbf k}=A_{\mathbf k}e^{i\omega_k t}+B_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\omega_k t},
\qquad
\omega_k=c_s\sqrt{k^2-k_J^2}.
$$

For $k<k_J$, [Jeans instability](../../../../../../jeans-instability.md) gives growing and decaying exponentials with rate $c_s\sqrt{k_J^2-k^2}$. At the [Jeans wavenumber](../../../../../../jeans-wavenumber.md) the two solutions are constant and linear in time.
