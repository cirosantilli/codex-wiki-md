<h1 id="24f/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Choose a point $p\in C$ and project from $p$ to a line not containing $p$. Every line through $p$ meets the quartic in four points counted with [intersection multiplicity](../../../../../../intersection-multiplicity.md) by [Bézout theorem](../../../../../../bezout-s-theorem.md); removing the fixed intersection at $p$ leaves a generic fibre of three points. This [projection from a point on a plane curve](../../../../../../projection-from-a-point-on-a-plane-curve.md) therefore defines a nonconstant morphism

$$
C\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1
$$

of degree three, so the [gonality](../../../../../../gonality.md) is at most three.

It cannot be one, because a degree-one morphism between [smooth projective curves](../../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) is an [isomorphism of algebraic varieties](../../../../../../isomorphism-of-algebraic-varieties.md), whereas $C$ has genus three and the [projective line](../../../../../../projective-line.md) has genus zero. It cannot be two either: that would make $C$ a [hyperelliptic curve](../../../../../../hyperelliptic-curve.md). The [canonical map of a hyperelliptic curve](../../../../../../canonical-map-of-a-hyperelliptic-curve.md) is not a [closed embedding](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md), but for a [smooth plane quartic](../../../../../../smooth-plane-quartic.md) the [canonical map of a smooth plane curve](../../../../../../canonical-map-of-a-smooth-plane-curve.md) is exactly its given embedding $C\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^2$, since $K_C\cong\mathcal O_C(1)$. This contradiction proves

$$
\boxed{d=\operatorname{gon}(C)=3.}
$$
