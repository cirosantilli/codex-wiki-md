<h1 id="24f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put

$$
F=X_0^4-X_1^4-X_2^4.
$$

By the [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md), a [projective plane curve](../../../../../../projective-plane-curve.md) is singular only at a common projective zero of $F$ and all its [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md). Here

$$
F_{X_0}=4X_0^3,\qquad
F_{X_1}=-4X_1^3,\qquad
F_{X_2}=-4X_2^3.
$$

Their simultaneous vanishing forces $X_0=X_1=X_2=0$, which is not a point of [projective space](../../../../../../projective-space-split.md). Hence the Fermat quartic $C$ is [nonsingular](../../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md). In particular it is a [smooth plane quartic](../../../../../../smooth-plane-quartic.md) and has [geometric genus](../../../../../../geometric-genus.md) $(4-1)(4-2)/2=3$.
