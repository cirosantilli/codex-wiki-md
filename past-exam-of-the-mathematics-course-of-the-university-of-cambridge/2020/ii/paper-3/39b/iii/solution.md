<h1 id="39b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The stationary contribution travels with [group velocity](../../../../../../group-velocity.md)

$$
V=\omega'(k_0)=\frac{3S}{2}\sqrt{k_0},
$$

so $k_0=4V^2/(9S^2)$. The condition

$$
\frac{\epsilon V^2}{S^2}\ll1
$$

is therefore equivalent, up to a constant factor, to $k_0\epsilon\ll1$: the dominant [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) is much longer than the impacted interval. Its Fourier transform can then be replaced by

$$
-\frac{2W\sin(k\epsilon)}k\sim-2W\epsilon,
$$

which is the total initial vertical velocity integrated over space. The far field cannot resolve $W$ and $\epsilon$ separately and depends only on their product $W\epsilon$.
