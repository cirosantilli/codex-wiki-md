<h1 id="18g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Because $L/K$ is a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md),

$$
[L:K]=|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)|=|A_{10}|=\frac{10!}{2}=1\,814\,400.
$$

Suppose the irreducible quadratic $f$ became reducible over $L$. It would then have a root $\alpha\in L$, and $K(\alpha)/K$ would be a quadratic intermediate extension. By the [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-galois-theory.md), $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K(\alpha))$ would be a subgroup of index two in $A_{10}$ and hence a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) by the [index-two subgroup is normal](../../../../../../index-two-subgroup-is-normal.md) result. But the [Simplicity of alternating groups](../../../../../../simplicity-of-alternating-groups.md) says that $A_{10}$ is simple, while this subgroup would be proper and nontrivial. This contradiction proves that $f$ remains irreducible in $L[x]$.
