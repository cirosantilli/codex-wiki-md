<h1 id="3e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Define $F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$. By the [fundamental theorem of calculus](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus.md), $F'=f$. The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives

$$
\frac d{du}F(g(u))=f(g(u))g'(u).
$$

Integrating from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ and using $g(\alpha)=a$, $g(\beta)=b$ yields the [change of variables formula](../../../../../../change-of-variables-formula.md)

$$
\int_\alpha^\beta f(g(u))g'(u)\,du
=F(b)-F(a)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.
$$

The argument also covers a decreasing $g$ because both integrals are oriented.
