<h1 id="9f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) $m_\alpha$ is the unique monic polynomial of least positive degree such that $m_\alpha(\alpha)=0$. The [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md) says that $\alpha$ satisfies its characteristic polynomial, so such polynomials exist. Dividing any two monic candidates of least degree shows uniqueness.

If $m_\alpha(x)=x^m$, then $\alpha^m=0$ but $\alpha^{m-1}\ne0$. The least $r$ for which $(\alpha^3)^r=0$ is the least integer satisfying $3r\geq m$. Hence

$$
\boxed{m_{\alpha^3}(x)=x^{\lceil m/3\rceil}}.
$$
