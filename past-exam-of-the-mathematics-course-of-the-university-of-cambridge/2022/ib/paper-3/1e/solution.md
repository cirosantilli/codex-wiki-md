<h1 id="1e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1E](../1e.md)

The [first isomorphism theorem for rings](../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem-for-rings.md) states that a ring homomorphism $\phi:R\to S$ induces an isomorphism

$$
R/\ker\phi\cong\operatorname{im}\phi.
$$

Because $J$ is an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md), sums and products of elements of $R+J$ remain in $R+J$, so it is a subring. The surjective homomorphism

$$
R\longrightarrow(R+J)/J,
\qquad r\longmapsto r+J
$$

has kernel $R\cap J$. The theorem gives

$$
\boxed{R/(R\cap J)\cong(R+J)/J}.
$$

Evaluation at $-2$ gives

$$
\mathbb Q[X]/(X+2)\cong\mathbb Q,
$$

so this quotient is a [field](../../../../../field.md) of [characteristic](../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) zero. The other quotient is

$$
\mathbb Z[X]/(3,X^2+X+1)
\cong\mathbb F_3[X]/((X-1)^2).
$$

It has characteristic $3$ but is not a field, since the nonzero class of $X-1$ is [nilpotent](../../../../../nilpotent.md).
