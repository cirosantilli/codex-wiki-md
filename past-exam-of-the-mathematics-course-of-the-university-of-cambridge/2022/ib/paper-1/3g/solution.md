<h1 id="3g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3G](../3g.md)

The [Taylor series](../../../../../taylor-series.md) of the complex sine is

$$
\sin z=z-\frac{z^3}{3!}+\frac{z^5}{5!}-\cdots.
$$

Therefore, for $z\ne0$,

$$
\frac{\sin z}{z}
=1-\frac{z^2}{3!}+\frac{z^4}{5!}-\cdots,
$$

and the right side defines a [holomorphic function](../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) at $z=0$ with value $1$. Thus the apparent singularity is a [removable singularity](../../../../../removable-singularity.md).

The resulting power series is

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^nz^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}.
$$

The [ratio test](../../../../../ratio-test.md) shows convergence for every $z\in\mathbb C$, so its [radius of convergence](../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is

$$
\boxed{\infty}.
$$
