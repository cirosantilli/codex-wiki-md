<h1 id="12g/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Because $\cos0=1$, continuity gives a disc $U$ about zero on which $\cos z\ne0$. The function $-\tan z=(\log\cos z)'$ has a holomorphic primitive $G$ on a sufficiently small disc, normalized by $G(0)=0$. Then

$$
\boxed{f(z)=e^{G(z)/2}}
$$

is holomorphic and satisfies $f(0)=1$ and $f(z)^2=\cos z$ on $U$.

**No such square root exists throughout $|z|<2$.** The point $z=\pi/2$ lies in that disc and is a [simple zero](../../../../../../simple-zero.md) of $\cos z$, since $-\sin(\pi/2)=-1$. Every zero of the square of a holomorphic function has even [order of a zero of a holomorphic function](../../../../../../order-of-a-zero-of-a-holomorphic-function.md), contradicting this simple zero.
