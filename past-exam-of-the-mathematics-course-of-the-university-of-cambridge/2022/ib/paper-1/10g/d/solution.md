This is always true. Uniform convergence makes $(f_n)$ a [uniformly Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../uniformly-cauchy-sequence.md): for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $N$ such that

$$
d_Y(f_n(x),f_m(x))<\varepsilon
$$

for every $x\in A$ and $m,n\geq N$. Letting $x\to x_0$ through points of $A\setminus\{x_0\}$ and using the assumed limits gives

$$
d_Y(y_n,y_m)\leq\varepsilon.
$$

**Thus $(y_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md). Since $Y$ is a [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md), $(y_n)$ converges in $Y$.**
