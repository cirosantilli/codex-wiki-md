↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Bromwich inversion integral](../../../../../../bromwich-contour.md) closes to the left for $t>0$. Equivalently, partial fractions at the two double poles give

$$
\frac1{s^2(s+2)^2}
=-\frac1{4s}+\frac1{4s^2}
+\frac1{4(s+2)}+\frac1{4(s+2)^2}.
$$

Taking residues therefore yields

$$
\boxed{f(t)=\frac14\bigl(t-1+(t+1)e^{-2t}\bigr)}.
$$
