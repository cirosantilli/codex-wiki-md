<h1 id="9e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Regard $f,g$ as polynomials in $Y$ over $\mathbb C(X)$. Their coprimality and the preceding argument let Euclid's algorithm produce

$$
A(X,Y)f+B(X,Y)g=1
$$

with coefficients in $\mathbb C(X)$. Clearing denominators gives a nonzero $h(X)\in(f,g)$, so $I\cap\mathbb C[X]\ne0$. Interchanging $X,Y$ gives a nonzero $k(Y)\in I$.

In the quotient, $h(X)=k(Y)=0$. Reducing powers by these two univariate relations shows that the finitely many monomials

$$
X^iY^j,
\qquad 0\leq i<\deg h,
\quad0\leq j<\deg k,
$$

span. Therefore $\mathbb C[X,Y]/I$ is finite-dimensional.
