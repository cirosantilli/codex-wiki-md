<h1 id="2e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2E](../2e.md)

A [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $G$ is [abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md) when $xy=yx$ for every $x,y\in G$. It is [cyclic](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) when some [generator of a group](../../../../../generator-of-a-group.md) $g$ satisfies

$$
G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}.
$$

If $x=g^r$ and $y=g^s$ belong to a cyclic group, then

$$
xy=g^{r+s}=g^{s+r}=yx,
$$

so every cyclic group is abelian.

The [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md) $C_2\times C_2$ is abelian, but every nonidentity element has order two, so no element generates all four elements. Thus an abelian group need not be cyclic.

The condition on proper [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) does not force $G$ to be abelian. The [quaternion group](../../../../../quaternion-group.md)

$$
Q_8=\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}
$$

is nonabelian because $ij=k$ whereas $ji=-k$. Its proper subgroups are the trivial subgroup, $\{\pm1\}$, and the three cyclic subgroups

$$
\langle i\rangle,\qquad\langle j\rangle,\qquad\langle k\rangle,
$$

each of order four. They are all cyclic, so

$$
\boxed{\text{the answer is no}}.
$$
