<h1 id="26i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Gauss map](../../../../../../gauss-map.md) sends $p$ to the chosen [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) $N(p)$. Differentiating $\langle N,N\rangle=1$ shows that $dN_p$ maps $T_pS$ into itself. The [shape operator](../../../../../../shape-operator.md) $S_p=-dN_p$ is self-adjoint because its associated [second fundamental form](../../../../../../second-fundamental-form-split.md) is symmetric. Its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $k_1,k_2$ are the [principal curvatures](../../../../../../principal-curvature.md). A point is [umbilical](../../../../../../umbilical-point.md) when $k_1=k_2$, and the surface is [minimal](../../../../../../minimal-surface.md) when its [mean curvature](../../../../../../mean-curvature.md) $H=(k_1+k_2)/2$ vanishes everywhere.

In an orthonormal principal basis, $dN_p$ has diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(-k_1,-k_2)$. It is conformal exactly when

$$
\langle dN_pu,dN_pv\rangle=\lambda(p)\langle u,v\rangle,
\qquad \lambda(p)>0,
$$

which is equivalent to $k_1^2=k_2^2\ne0$. At a nonumbilical point this says $k_1=-k_2$, exactly the condition $H=0$. Hence, when there are no umbilical points, the surface is minimal if and only if its Gauss map is conformal.
