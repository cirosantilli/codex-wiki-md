<h1 id="6c/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use enzyme conservation to write

$$
e=e_0(1-v_1-v_2).
$$

Since $\tau=k_1e_0t$, $u=s/s_0$, $v_i=c_i/e_0$, and $\epsilon=e_0/s_0$, substitution into the mass-action equations gives

$$
\boxed{
f(u,v_1,v_2)
=-u(1-v_1-v_2)+\alpha v_1-\beta uv_1
},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
g_1(u,v_1,v_2)
&=u(1-v_1-v_2)-\alpha v_1-\beta uv_1+\gamma v_2,\\
g_2(u,v_1,v_2)
&=\beta uv_1-\gamma v_2,
\end{aligned}}
$$

where

$$
\alpha=\frac{k_{-1}}{k_1s_0},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{k_2}{k_1},
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{k_3}{k_1s_0}.
$$

The factors of $\epsilon$ multiplying $dv_i/d\tau$ identify the complex concentrations as fast variables in a [singular perturbation](../../../../../../singular-perturbation.md).
