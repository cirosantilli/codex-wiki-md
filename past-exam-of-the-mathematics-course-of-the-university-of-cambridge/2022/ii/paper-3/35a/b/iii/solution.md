<h1 id="35a/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The zero-temperature [internal energy](../../../../../../../internal-energy.md) is

$$
E=\int_0^{E_F}\varepsilon g(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon
=\frac{VE_F^4}{4\pi^2\hbar^3c^3}
=\frac34NE_F.
$$

Using part (i),

$$
p=\frac{NE_F}{4V}
=\frac D4N^{4/3}V^{-4/3}.
$$

Consequently the [adiabatic equation of state](../../../../../../../adiabatic-equation-of-state.md) is

$$
\boxed{
pV^a=K,
\qquad
a=\frac43,
\qquad
K=\frac{\hbar c(3\pi^2)^{1/3}}4N^{4/3}
}.
$$

The pressure is [degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../../degeneracy-pressure.md): it remains nonzero at zero temperature because of the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md). By comparison, a classical ultrarelativistic [ideal gas](../../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) at fixed temperature obeys $pV=Nk_BT$, so its corresponding volume exponent is one and its pressure vanishes as $T\to0$.
