<h1 id="39c/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Re}=\frac{\mathcal U\mathcal L}{\nu}}.
$$

When it is large, viscosity is negligible in most of the flow, but the inviscid outer solution cannot generally satisfy the [no-slip boundary condition](../../../../../../no-slip-boundary-condition.md) at a rigid wall. A thin [boundary layer](../../../../../../boundary-layer.md) resolves this mismatch: tangential gradients remain of outer scale $\mathcal L$, normal gradients have the much shorter scale $\delta$, and the small viscous coefficient is offset by $u_{yy}=O(\mathcal U/\delta^2)$. Matching a viscous inner solution to an inviscid outer solution retains the leading viscous effects without solving the full equations uniformly everywhere.
