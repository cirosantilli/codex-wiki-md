<h1 id="22g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $(T_m)$ be a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the [operator norm](../../../../../../operator-norm.md). For each $v\in V$,

$$
\|T_mv-T_nv\|\leq\|T_m-T_n\|\,\|v\|,
$$

so $(T_mv)$ is a Cauchy sequence. [Completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md) of the [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md) $V$ permits the definition  
$Tv=\lim_mT_mv$. Pointwise passage to the [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) proves that $T$ is a [linear](../../../../../../linear-map.md). A norm-Cauchy sequence is a [bounded sequence](../../../../../../bounded-sequence.md), say $\|T_m\|\leq C$, and therefore  
$\|Tv\|\leq C\|v\|$, so $T$ is bounded. Finally, letting $m\to\infty$ in  
$\|(T_n-T_m)v\|\leq\varepsilon\|v\|$ gives  
$\|T_n-T\|\leq\varepsilon$. Thus $T_n\to T$ in operator norm and  
$\mathcal B(V,V)$ is Banach.
