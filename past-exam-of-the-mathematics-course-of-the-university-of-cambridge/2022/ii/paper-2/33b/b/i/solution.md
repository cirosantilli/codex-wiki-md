<h1 id="33b/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let

$$
I_0=[x_0,x_1],\qquad I_1=[x_1,x_2],\qquad I_2=[x_2,x_3].
$$

The [covering relations](../../../../../../../interval-covering-relation.md) are

$$
I_0\longrightarrow I_1,\qquad
I_1\longrightarrow I_2,\qquad
I_2\longrightarrow I_0,I_1,I_2.
$$

Thus the [covering graph](../../../../../../../directed-covering-graph-of-an-interval-map.md) has [adjacency matrix](../../../../../../../adjacency-matrix-of-a-directed-graph.md)

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The two closed length-three walks

$$
I_2\to I_2\to I_2\to I_2,
\qquad
I_2\to I_0\to I_1\to I_2
$$

give a [horseshoe from two closed covering walks](../../../../../../../horseshoe-from-two-closed-covering-walks.md) for $F^3$, so $F$ must be [chaotic](../../../../../../../glendinning-chaos.md). Moreover,

$$
\operatorname{tr}A=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(A^3)=7.
$$

By [counting cycles in an interval covering graph](../../../../../../../counting-cycles-in-an-interval-covering-graph.md), the number of primitive length-three cyclic itineraries is

$$
\frac{7-1}{3}=2.
$$

Hence $F$ must have at least, and the connect-the-dots realization shows that it need have only, $\boxed{2}$ distinct 3-cycles.
