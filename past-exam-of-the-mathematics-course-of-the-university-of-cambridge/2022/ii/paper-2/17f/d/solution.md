<h1 id="17f/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Form the [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) whose [vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) are the points $x_i$, joining two when their [Euclidean distance](../../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) is one. Two distinct points have at most two common unit-distance neighbours, because two [unit circles](../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) intersect in at most two points. The graph is therefore $K_{3,2}$-free. Part (c) bounds its [unordered edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) by $2n^{3/2}$, so the number of [ordered](../../../../../../ordered-pair.md) unit-distance pairs is at most

$$
\boxed{4n^{3/2}}.
$$
