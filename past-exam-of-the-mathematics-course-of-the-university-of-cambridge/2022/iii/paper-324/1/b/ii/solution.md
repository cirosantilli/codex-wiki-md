<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $a=\lambda_P$ and $b=\lambda_Q$. The [Pauli operators](../../../../../../../pauli-operator.md) are Hermitian and satisfy $P^2=Q^2=I$ and $PQ+QP=0$, so

$$
V^\dagger=V,
\qquad
V^2=\frac12(aP+bQ)^2=I.
$$

Thus $V$ is a [unitary operator](../../../../../../../unitary-operator.md). For every Pauli operator $R$, according as $R$ commutes or anticommutes with $P$ and $Q$, expansion of $VRV$ gives one of $\pm R$ or $\pm RPQ$, up to the phase that makes it Hermitian. It is therefore another Pauli operator, so $V$ normalizes the [Pauli group](../../../../../../../pauli-group.md) and is a [Clifford operation](../../../../../../../clifford-gate.md). Finally,

$$
V|\psi\rangle
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(I+bQ)|\psi\rangle,
$$

which is the normalized projection onto the eigenvalue-$b$ [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) of $Q$. Hence $V$ maps the eigenvalue-$\lambda_P$ eigenspace of $P$ onto the eigenvalue-$\lambda_Q$ eigenspace of $Q$.
