<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The differential-forms version of the [Frobenius theorem](../../../../../../frobenius-theorem.md) says that a constant-rank [distribution](../../../../../../distribution-differential-geometry.md) $D=\bigcap_i\ker\alpha^i$ is [integrable](../../../../../../integrable-distribution.md) exactly when

$$
d\alpha^i=\sum_j\beta^i{}_j\wedge\alpha^j
$$

for suitable 1-forms $\beta^i{}_j$. For a [plane distribution](../../../../../../plane-distribution.md) $D=\ker\alpha$ on $\mathbb R^3$, this reduces to the [integrability criterion for a plane distribution](../../../../../../integrability-criterion-for-a-plane-distribution.md) $\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$.

For example, $\ker dz$ is integrable: its integral surfaces are the horizontal planes $z=\text{constant}$. In contrast, for $\alpha=dz-x\,dy$,

$$
\alpha\wedge d\alpha=(dz-x\,dy)\wedge(-dx\wedge dy)=-dz\wedge dx\wedge dy\ne0.
$$

**Thus $\ker(dz-x\,dy)$ is not integrable; it is the standard [contact structure](../../../../../../contact-structure.md) on $\mathbb R^3$.**
