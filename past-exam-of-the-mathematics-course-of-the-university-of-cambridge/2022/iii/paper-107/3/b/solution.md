<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [method of continuity](../../../../../../method-of-continuity.md) with

$$
L_t=(1-t)\Delta+tL,
\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
$$

Every $L_t$ has the same uniform ellipticity and Hölder coefficient bounds, so the estimates in part (a) hold with one constant independent of $t$. They imply injectivity. The set of $t$ for which $L_t:C_0^{2,\alpha}(B)\to C^{0,\alpha}(B)$ is onto is open by the [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md) and a Neumann-series perturbation, and closed by the uniform a priori estimate. It contains $0$ because the Dirichlet Laplacian is bijective. Connectedness of $[0,1]$ therefore gives surjectivity at $t=1$, and $L$ is bijective.
