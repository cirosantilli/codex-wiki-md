<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The first equation is vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md): the pressure gradient balances the vertical gravity of the central mass, $\Omega^2z$, and the disk's own potential $\Phi_d$. The second is the plane-parallel [Poisson equation](../../../../../../poisson-equation.md) for disk self-gravity. The third balances the vertical increase of [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md) against local viscous heating in a Keplerian [alpha disk](../../../../../../alpha-disk.md), and the fourth is the optically thick [radiative diffusion](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) law.

With $P/\rho\sim c_s^2$, $\Sigma\sim\rho H$, and all terms in hydrostatic balance comparable,

$$
\frac{c_s^2}{H}\sim\Omega^2H\sim G\Sigma.
$$

The first comparison gives $H\sim c_s/\Omega$; inserting it into the second gives $\Omega c_s\sim G\Sigma$. Hence

$$
\boxed{Q=\frac{\Omega c_s}{\pi G\Sigma}\sim1},
$$

up to the order-one constants deliberately omitted by the scaling argument.
