<h1 id="section-a/4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\Omega=(-1,1)^2$ and use the clamped energy space $V=H_0^2(\Omega)$, whose traces satisfy $u=\partial_nu=0$ on $\partial\Omega$. Two applications of [integration by parts](../../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) give

$$
\langle\Delta^2u,u\rangle_{L^2}
=\int_\Omega(\Delta u)^2\,dx\,dy\geq0.
$$

If equality holds, then $\Delta u=0$. The [maximum principle for harmonic functions](../../../../../../../maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions.md) and the zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) imply $u=0$. The [biharmonic operator](../../../../../../../biharmonic-operator.md) is therefore positive definite. The same identity and conclusion hold for the simply supported conditions $u=\Delta u=0$; either standard interpretation of the paper's phrase “zero boundary conditions” gives the result.
