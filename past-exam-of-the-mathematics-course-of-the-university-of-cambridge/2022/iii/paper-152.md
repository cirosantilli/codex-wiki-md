## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

For $m=(u,v)\in M\cong\mathbb Z^2$, membership in the [dual cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#dual-cone) is equivalent to

$$
u\geq0,
\qquad -u+3v\geq0.
$$

The [Hilbert basis of a rational cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#hilbert-basis-of-a-rational-cone) is $(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)$. Therefore the [coordinate ring of an affine toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#coordinate-ring-of-an-affine-toric-variety) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C[S_\sigma]
=\mathbb C[t_2,t_1t_2,t_1^2t_2,t_1^3t_2]
\subseteq\mathbb C[t_1^{\pm1},t_2^{\pm1}].}
$$

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

The diagonal action of $\mu_3$ on $\mathbb A^2_{x,y}$ fixes precisely the monomials whose total degree is divisible by three. Its invariant ring is generated by

$$
x^3,\quad x^2y,\quad xy^2,\quad y^3.
$$

The assignment

$$
x^{3-j}y^j\longmapsto t_1^jt_2,
\qquad0\leq j\leq3,
$$

identifies this invariant ring with the ring in part i. Taking spectra gives the [diagonal cyclic quotient singularity of order three](../../../toric-geometry.md#diagonal-cyclic-quotient-singularity-of-order-three)

$$
\boxed{X_\sigma\cong\mathbb A^2/\mu_3.}
$$

<h4 id="1/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/a/iii)

Insert the ray generated by $w=(0,1)$ between $v_1=(1,0)$ and $v_2=(-1,3)$. Since

$$
\det(v_1,w)=1,
\qquad
\det(w,v_2)=1,
$$

the subdivided fan is smooth and gives a [toric resolution of singularities](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-resolution-of-singularities). The unique exceptional divisor is $D_w$. As

$$
v_1+v_2=(0,3)=3w,
$$

the [self-intersection formula for a toric surface divisor](../../../toric-geometry.md#self-intersection-formula-for-a-toric-surface-divisor) gives $D_w^2=-3$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Let $w$ be the primitive generator of the new ray $\tau$, and let $u,v$ be its adjacent primitive rays in the smooth subdivided fan. Smoothness gives a unique integer $a$ with

$$
u+v=aw,
\qquad D_\tau^2=-a.
$$

Because $\tau$ is new, it lies in the interior of an original two-dimensional cone. Its adjacent refined rays lie on opposite sides of it inside that cone, which forces $a>0$. Therefore $D_\tau^2<0$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $v_1=(1,0)$ and $v_2=(1,m)$ generate the distinguished cone. If a complete fan had exactly one further ray with primitive generator $w=(a,b)$ and were smooth away from $x_\sigma$, the other two cones would be smooth. Completeness puts $w$ on the other side of the two boundary rays, so after choosing cyclic orientation $b=-1$. Smoothness would require

$$
\det(v_2,w)=-1-ma=1,
$$

hence $ma=-2$. This has no integer solution for $m\geq3$. Thus no proper toric variety can satisfy the three conditions from the PDF.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

For the rays $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, and $v_3=(-a,-b)$, the determinants of consecutive pairs have absolute values

$$
1,\qquad a,\qquad b.
$$

By the [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety), $\mathbb P(1,a,b)$ is smooth exactly when $a=b=1$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

The [toric divisor class sequence](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-divisor-class-sequence) is

$$
M\longrightarrow\mathbb ZD_1\oplus\mathbb ZD_2\oplus\mathbb ZD_3
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(X)\longrightarrow0.
$$

The two characters give

$$
D_1-aD_3=0,
\qquad
D_2-bD_3=0.
$$

Thus

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P(1,a,b))\cong\mathbb Z,
$$

generated by $[D_3]$.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

An invariant Weil divisor is Cartier precisely when its local linear support functions are integral on every maximal cone. Applying this to the three cones shows that a class $n[D_3]$ is Cartier exactly when both $a$ and $b$ divide $n$. Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, this means $ab\mid n$. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P(1,a,b))\cong\mathbb Z,
$$

and its map to the class group identifies it with $ab\mathbb Z\subseteq\mathbb Z$. This is the [divisor class and Picard groups of a weighted projective plane](../../../toric-geometry.md#divisor-class-and-picard-groups-of-a-weighted-projective-plane).

<h4 id="2/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/a/iv)

The [Cox ring](../../../toric-geometry.md#cox-ring) has one variable for each ray and is $\mathbb C[x,y,z]$, graded by

$$
\deg x=1,\qquad\deg y=a,\qquad\deg z=b.
$$

The irrelevant locus is the common zero of all three variables. The quasitorus associated with the class group is $\mathbb C^*$ and acts by

$$
t\cdot(x,y,z)=(tx,t^ay,t^bz).
$$

The [Cox construction](../../../toric-geometry.md#cox-construction) therefore identifies the closed points of $\mathbb P(1,a,b)$ with

$$
\boxed{(\mathbb A^3\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb C^*.}
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

A toric morphism is induced by a lattice map carrying each source cone into a target cone. Compose a hypothetical fan map from the fan of $\mathbb P^2$ to the product fan with either coordinate projection to the fan of $\mathbb P^1$. If the three primitive source rays are $r_1,r_2,r_3$ with $r_1+r_2+r_3=0$, their scalar images must have each adjacent pair in one half-line. This is impossible for three numbers summing to zero unless all are zero. Both coordinate projections of the lattice map vanish, so the map itself is zero and the toric morphism is constant. This proves [no nonconstant toric morphism from the projective plane to the product of projective lines](../../../toric-geometry.md#no-nonconstant-toric-morphism-from-the-projective-plane-to-the-product-of-projective-lines).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For $D=kD_1$, the [lattice polytope of a toric divisor](../../../toric-geometry.md#lattice-polytope-of-a-toric-divisor) consists of $(m_1,m_2)$ satisfying

$$
m_1\geq-k,\qquad m_2\geq0,\qquad-m_1+m_2\geq0,\qquad-m_2\geq0.
$$

Thus $m_2=0$ and $-k\leq m_1\leq0$. Its lattice points index a basis of global sections, so

$$
\dim\Gamma(\mathbb F_1,\mathcal O(kD_1))=
\begin{cases}
k+1,&k\geq0,\\
0,&k<0.
\end{cases}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For every maximal cone, one endpoint of the interval of lattice points from part a realizes the corresponding Cartier datum. The toric basepoint-free criterion therefore makes $\mathcal O(kD_1)$ basepoint-free for $k\geq0$. Equivalently, part d identifies it with the pullback of the globally generated bundle $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(k)$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

The divisor $D_1$ is a fiber of the ruling and has $D_1^2=0$. Hence $(kD_1)^2=0$ for every $k$. An ample divisor on a complete surface has positive self-intersection, so no $\mathcal O(kD_1)$ with $k\geq0$ is ample. These results are summarized by [multiples of a fiber on the first Hirzebruch surface](../../../toric-geometry.md#multiples-of-a-fiber-on-the-first-hirzebruch-surface).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Let $E_1$ be the invariant point divisor of $\mathbb P^1$ corresponding to the ray $\mathbb R_{\geq0}$. Under $(u_1,u_2)\mapsto u_1$, the ray $(1,0)$ maps primitively to that ray, while no other ray maps into its interior. The toric pullback formula gives

$$
\pi^*E_1=D_1,
$$

equivalently $D_1=\pi^{-1}(E_1)$ as divisors.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

**False.** The Picard group of the [Hirzebruch surface](../../../toric-geometry.md#hirzebruch-surface) is freely generated by the negative section $S$ and a fiber $F=D_1$. Pullbacks from $\mathbb P^1$ form only the subgroup $\mathbb ZF$. For example, $\mathcal O(S)$ cannot be a pullback: its restriction to a fiber has degree $S\mathbin\cdot F=1$, whereas every pullback from the base restricts trivially to every fiber.
