↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For the [uniform distribution](../../../../../../continuous-uniform-distribution.md) $\mathcal P(q)=1$, substitute $q=Q\sin\theta$ into part d:

$$
\mathcal P(Q)
=Q\int_0^{\pi/2}
\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-Q^2\sin^2\theta}}
=\boxed{QK(Q)},
$$

where $K$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the first kind](../../../../../../complete-elliptic-integral-of-the-first-kind.md). Both $Q$ and $K(Q)$ increase on $(0,1)$; indeed $\mathcal P(Q)\sim\pi Q/2$ near zero and diverges logarithmically as $Q\uparrow1$. Thus the apparent distribution is strongly skewed toward higher $Q$: projection makes many intrinsically flattened systems look round.
