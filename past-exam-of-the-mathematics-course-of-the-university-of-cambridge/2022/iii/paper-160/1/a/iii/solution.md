<h1 id="1/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a tabloid $U$, let $r_U(k)$ be the row containing $k$, and order tabloids lexicographically by

$$
(r_U(1),r_U(2),\ldots,r_U(n)).
$$

If $t$ is standard and $1\ne\pi\in C(t)$, let $k$ be the least number moved by $\pi$. Every smaller entry is fixed, while $\pi^{-1}(k)$ is a larger entry below $k$ in the same column. Hence $\{\pi t\}$ agrees with $\{t\}$ before $k$ and has $r_{\pi t}(k)>r_t(k)$. Thus $\{t\}$ is the unique least tabloid in $e(t)$ and has coefficient one.

Distinct standard tableaux have distinct tabloids, since increasing each row recovers the tableau from its tabloid. A linear relation among standard polytabloids now has a least leading tabloid, which cannot cancel. They are therefore linearly independent, as in the [linear independence of standard polytabloids](../../../../../../../linear-independence-of-standard-polytabloids.md).
