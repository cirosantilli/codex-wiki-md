<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $n\geq4$, the only partitions dominating $(n-2,1,1)$ are

$$
(n),\quad(n-1,1),\quad(n-2,2),\quad(n-2,1,1).
$$

The entries of content $(n-2,1,1)$ consist of $n-2$ copies of $1$ and one copy each of $2$ and $3$. There is one semistandard tableau of shape $(n)$; two of shape $(n-1,1)$, according as the lower cell contains $2$ or $3$; one of shape $(n-2,2)$; and one of shape $(n-2,1,1)$. Therefore

$$
m_{(n)}=1,\qquad
m_{(n-1,1)}=2,\qquad
m_{(n-2,2)}=1,\qquad
m_{(n-2,1,1)}=1,
$$

and every other multiplicity is zero. For $n=3$, the coincident middle shapes combine to give $m_{(3)}=1$, $m_{(2,1)}=2$, and $m_{(1,1,1)}=1$.
