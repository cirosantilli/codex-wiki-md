<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Goldstine theorem](../../../../../../goldstine-theorem.md) states that the canonical image $J(B_X)$ of the closed unit ball of a [normed vector space](../../../../../../normed-vector-space.md) $X$ is weak-star dense in $B_{X^{**}}$.

The [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md) states that $B_{X^*}$ is compact in the [weak-star topology](../../../../../../weak-star-topology.md). To prove it, map each $f\in B_{X^*}$ to its values in

$$
P=\prod_{x\in X}\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\leq\lVert x\rVert\}.
$$

Every factor is compact, so [Tychonoff theorem](../../../../../../tychonoff-s-theorem.md) makes $P$ compact. The image of $B_{X^*}$ is cut out by the closed linearity conditions

$$
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y),qquad f(\alpha x)=\alpha f(x),
$$

and is therefore closed in $P$. The product topology restricted to this image is exactly pointwise convergence on $X$, namely the weak-star topology. Hence the ball is compact.
