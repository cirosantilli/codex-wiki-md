<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $Z=\epsilon\zeta$ and seek the slowly attenuating [wave envelope](../../../../../../envelope-waves.md) $A(Z)$ in

$$
\psi=A(Z)e^{i(k\xi-\omega t)}.
$$

At order $\epsilon^0$, the equation gives the [internal gravity wave](../../../../../../internal-wave.md) [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md)

$$
\omega^2=N^2\sin^2\theta.
$$

At order $\epsilon^1$, retaining one derivative of the slowly varying amplitude gives

$$
2i\omega k^4A+2iN^2k\sin\theta\cos\theta\,A_Z=0.
$$

Using $\omega=N\sin\theta$ therefore yields

$$
A_Z=-\frac{k^3}{N\cos\theta}A.
$$

Hence the leading [viscous attenuation of an internal-wave beam](../../../../../../viscous-attenuation-of-an-internal-wave-beam.md) is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde\psi(\zeta)=A_0
\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon k^3}{N\cos\theta}\zeta\right)
=A_0e^{-k\zeta/\operatorname{Re}},}
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Re}=\frac{N\cos\theta}{\epsilon k^2}.}
$$

The wave [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md), being quadratic in the amplitude, decays twice as rapidly in the exponent.
