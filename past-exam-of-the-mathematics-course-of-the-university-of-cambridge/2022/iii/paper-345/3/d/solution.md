<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The source supplies the kinematic [buoyancy flux](../../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md) $B_0=F_0/\rho_0$. The unstratified [line plume](../../../../../../line-plume.md) velocity scale is $B_0^{1/3}$. Comparing this with the stratification time scale $N_0^{-1}$ gives the [stratified line-plume height scale](../../../../../../stratified-line-plume-height-scale.md)

$$
\boxed{z_s\sim\frac{B_0^{1/3}}{N_0}
=\left(\frac{F_0}{\rho_0N_0^3}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

If $z_s\gg H$, the initial plume is only weakly affected by the [stable density stratification](../../../../../../stable-density-stratification.md) and reaches the ceiling much like an unstratified wall plume. If $z_s\sim H$, its [buoyancy flux](../../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md) falls substantially during the rise; it approaches neutral buoyancy near the upper room, overshoots because of its [momentum flux](../../../../../../momentum-flux.md), and spreads as a horizontal [buoyant intrusion](../../../../../../buoyant-intrusion.md). If $z_s\ll H$, the plume reaches neutral buoyancy low in the room and forms a low intrusion after a modest overshoot. In each sketch the plume widens by [entrainment](../../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md), while increasing $N_0$ lowers the neutral-buoyancy and overshoot heights.
