<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Whenever $\alpha$ and $\beta$ bound a bigon, isotope one side across the bigon. The two removed crossings have opposite local signs: the induced directions around the two corners of an oriented disc are opposite. Thus the move reduces the [geometric intersection number](../../../../../../geometric-intersection-number.md) by two while leaving the [algebraic intersection number of curves on an oriented surface](../../../../../../algebraic-intersection-number-of-curves-on-an-oriented-surface.md) unchanged.

Repeatedly remove bigons. The process terminates because the intersection count is a nonnegative integer, and the [bigon criterion](../../../../../../bigon-criterion.md) says that the resulting curves $\alpha_0$ and $\beta_0$ are in minimal position. Since every removal cancelled one positive and one negative crossing,

$$
\boxed{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha_0,\beta_0\rangle.}
$$
