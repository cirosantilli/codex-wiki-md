<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since $\delta_{1/t}\varphi(x)=\varphi(x/t)$ maps the [Schwartz space](../../../../../../../schwartz-space.md) continuously to itself, the formula

$$
\langle\delta_tu,\varphi\rangle=t^{-n}\langle u,\delta_{1/t}\varphi\rangle
$$

defines a continuous [linear functional](../../../../../../../linear-functional.md) on $\mathcal S$, hence a [tempered distribution](../../../../../../../tempered-distribution.md). For a [locally integrable function](../../../../../../../locally-integrable-function.md) $u$, the [change of variables formula](../../../../../../../change-of-variables-formula.md) $y=tx$ gives

$$
\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(tx)\varphi(x)\,dx
=t^{-n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(y)\varphi(y/t)\,dy,
$$

so this [dilation of a distribution](../../../../../../../dilation-of-a-distribution.md) agrees with ordinary function dilation.
