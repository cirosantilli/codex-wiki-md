<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In an isothermal hydrostatic atmosphere, pressure falls as $P(z)=P_0e^{-z/H}$. If a water-band line core becomes optically thick at pressure $P_{\rm line}$ and an opaque cloud fixes the nearby continuum at $P_{\rm cl}$, the [exoplanet transmission spectrum](../../../../../../exoplanet-transmission-spectrum.md) feature spans

$$
\frac{\Delta z}{H}=\log\frac{P_{\rm cl}}{P_{\rm line}}.
$$

A two-scale-height feature therefore requires

$$
\boxed{P_{\rm cl}=e^2P_{\rm line}}.
$$

Taking a representative near-infrared water-band pressure $P_{\rm line}\sim1\,{\rm mbar}$ gives $P_{\rm cl}\sim7\,{\rm mbar}$, so the appropriate estimate is an [exoplanet cloud deck](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud-deck.md) top near $10\,{\rm mbar}$. The numerical value scales directly with the assumed line-core pressure.

Other explanations include a high [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md), subsolar water abundance, a colder terminator, [atmospheric haze](../../../../../../haze.md), patchy two-limb clouds, stellar contamination, or instrumental systematics. Optical scattering slopes, broader [James Webb Space Telescope](../../../../../../james-webb-space-telescope.md) molecular coverage, repeated transits, secondary-eclipse spectra, phase curves, and precise mass and radius measurements can distinguish these possibilities.
