<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Comparable hydrostatic thermal escape requires comparable [Jeans escape parameter](../../../../../../jeans-escape-parameter.md) $\lambda=GM_pm/(k_BTR_{\rm exo})$. For the same escaping species and $R_{\rm exo}\simeq R_p$,

$$
\frac{T_{\rm J}}{T_\oplus}
\gtrsim\frac{M_{\rm J}/R_{\rm J}}{M_\oplus/R_\oplus}
\simeq\frac{318}{11.2}\simeq28.
$$

Thus Jupiter at 5 au needs an exobase temperature at least about thirty times Earth's at the same irradiation to have comparable [Jeans escape flux](../../../../../../jeans-escape-flux.md).

For the inner planet, the usable EUV power is $\eta\pi R_{\rm exo}^2F_{\rm EUV}$. If the binding energy per unit escaping mass is $GM_p/R_p$, [energy-limited atmospheric escape](../../../../../../energy-limited-atmospheric-escape.md) gives

$$
\dot M=\frac{\eta\pi R_{\rm exo}^2F_{\rm EUV}R_p}{GM_p}.
$$

The time to lose a fraction $x$ of the planetary mass is therefore

$$
\boxed{t_x=\frac{xGM_p^2}
{\eta\pi R_{\rm exo}^2R_pF_{\rm EUV}}}.
$$

This neglects Roche-lobe reduction, radiative cooling, changes in radius and flux, and the planet's orbital evolution. If the gas is lifted only from $R_{\rm exo}$, replace $R_p$ in the denominator by $R_{\rm exo}$.
