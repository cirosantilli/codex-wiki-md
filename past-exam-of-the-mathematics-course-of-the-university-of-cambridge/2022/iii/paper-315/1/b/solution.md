<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\mu=\cos\theta$. For $I_\nu=A_\nu+B_\nu\mu^3$, the [mean intensity](../../../../../../mean-intensity.md) is

$$
J_\nu=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}I_\nu\,d\mu=A_\nu,
$$

because the cubic term is odd. The K-integral, or second angular moment, is

$$
K_\nu=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\mu^2I_\nu\,d\mu
=\frac{A_\nu}{3},
$$

because the $\mu^5$ contribution is also odd. Hence

$$
\boxed{K_\nu=\frac13J_\nu}.
$$

This intensity obeys the [Eddington closure approximation](../../../../../../eddington-closure-approximation.md) even though it is not isotropic.
