## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

A [morphism of schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) is [proper](../../../ringed-space.md#proper-morphism) when it is a [finite type morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#morphism-of-finite-type), a [separated morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#separated-morphism), and a [universally closed morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism). The [valuative criterion for properness](../../../ringed-space.md#valuative-criterion-for-properness) says, under the usual finite-type and Noetherian hypotheses, that $f:X\to Y$ is proper exactly when every commutative square

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\operatorname{Spec}K&\longrightarrow&X\\
\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow f\\
\operatorname{Spec}R&\longrightarrow&Y
\end{array}
$$

with $R$ a [discrete valuation ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#discrete-valuation-ring) and $K=\operatorname{Frac}R$ has a unique diagonal lift $\operatorname{Spec}R\to X$.

For $\mathbb P_k^2\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, a $K$-point is $[x_0:x_1:x_2]$ with not all $x_i$ zero. If $\pi$ is a [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer), multiply all coordinates by one power of $\pi$ so that $\min_i v(x_i)=0$. The resulting coordinates lie in $R$ and at least one is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), so they define an $R$-point extending the given $K$-point. If two extensions exist, on a chart where one coordinate is a unit their affine coordinate ratios agree in $K$ and therefore in the [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $R$; hence the extensions agree. This verifies existence and uniqueness directly.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Take the [projective line with a doubled point](../../../ringed-space.md#projective-line-with-a-doubled-point) over $\mathbb C$, obtained by gluing two copies of $\mathbb P_{\mathbb C}^1$ along the complement of one point. After every [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes), a closed subset has closed image from each of the two projective-line charts, so its total image, the union of those two images, is closed. The structure morphism is therefore [universally closed](../../../ringed-space.md#universally-closed-morphism). The two doubled points have no disjoint neighborhoods, so the scheme is not [separated](../../../ringed-space.md#separated-scheme) and hence is not [proper](../../../ringed-space.md#proper-morphism).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Because $\mathcal A_\bullet$ is coherent and locally generated by $\mathcal A_1$, there is locally a finite-rank coherent sheaf $\mathcal E$ and a surjection of graded algebras

$$
\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal E)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal A_\bullet.
$$

The [Relative Proj construction](../../../ringed-space.md#relative-proj-construction) turns this into a [closed immersion](../../../ringed-space.md#closed-immersion)

$$
Y=\operatorname{Proj}_X\mathcal A_\bullet\hookrightarrow\mathbb P_X(\mathcal E).
$$

**Consequently $Y\to X$ is a [projective morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#projective-morphism) and therefore a [proper morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#proper-morphism). Since $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is proper and proper morphisms are closed under composition, $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is proper.**

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

The [coincidence locus of two scheme morphisms](../../../ringed-space.md#coincidence-locus-of-two-scheme-morphisms) is the fibre product

$$
E=X\times_{(f,g),\,Y\times_kY,\,\Delta_Y}Y.
$$

The [diagonal morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#diagonal-morphism) $\Delta_Y$ is a [locally closed immersion](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-closed-immersion), and this property is stable under [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes), so $E\hookrightarrow X$ is locally closed. The [universal property of a fibre product](../../../ringed-space.md#universal-property-of-a-fibre-product) says that a morphism $T\to X$ factors through $E$ exactly when $f|_T=g|_T$. Thus $E$ is the largest locally closed subscheme on which they coincide. If $Y$ is [separated](../../../ringed-space.md#separated-scheme), $\Delta_Y$ is a [closed immersion](../../../ringed-space.md#closed-immersion), and its base change $E\hookrightarrow X$ is closed.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

A [Weil divisor](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#weil-divisor) on $X$ is a finite formal sum

$$
D=\sum_Zn_Z[Z],\qquad n_Z\in\mathbb Z,
$$

over integral codimension-one closed subschemes $Z$. Since $X$ is [regular in codimension one](../../../ringed-space.md#regular-in-codimension-one), the local ring at the generic point of each $Z$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#discrete-valuation-ring). A nonzero [rational function](../../../isolated-singularity.md#rational-function) $f\in k(X)^\times$ therefore defines the principal divisor $\operatorname{div}(f)=\sum_Zv_Z(f)[Z]$. The [divisor class group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-class-group) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(X)=\operatorname{Div}(X)/\{\operatorname{div}(f):f\in k(X)^\times\}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The scheme $\mathbb P_k^3$ is Noetherian, integral, separated, and regular. Every open subscheme inherits these properties, so $U=\mathbb P_k^3\setminus C$ satisfies $(\star)$. Because $C$ has dimension one, it has codimension two in $\mathbb P_k^3$ and contains no [prime Weil divisor](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#prime-weil-divisor). The [localization sequence for the divisor class group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#localization-sequence-for-the-divisor-class-group) therefore makes restriction an isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P_k^3)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Cl}(U).
$$

The [hyperplane divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md#hyperplane-divisor) generates the class group of projective space, so

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(U)\cong\mathbb Z.}
$$

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

**False.** For $n\geq2$, take the affine hypersurface

$$
X_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^n)\subset\mathbb A_k^3.
$$

It is an [integral scheme](../../../ringed-space.md#integral-scheme), and its only possible singular point is the origin, which has codimension two. Hence it is [regular in codimension one](../../../ringed-space.md#regular-in-codimension-one); as a hypersurface it satisfies Serre's condition $(S_2)$, so the [Serre criterion for normality](../../../ringed-space.md#serre-s-criterion-for-normality) also makes it a [normal scheme](../../../ringed-space.md#normal-scheme). The [Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-class-group-of-an-a-type-surface-singularity) is

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
$$

generated by $V(x,z)$. Thus a closed affine subscheme satisfying $(\star)$ can have nonzero torsion in its class group.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

The standard affine charts of $\mathbb P^3$ are copies of $\mathbb A^3$, and the product charts of $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ are copies of $\mathbb A^2$. Their local rings are localizations of [polynomial rings](../../../commutative-algebra.md#polynomial-ring) over $k$ and hence are [regular local rings](../../../commutative-algebra.md#regular-local-ring). Both schemes are therefore [regular](../../../ringed-space.md#regular-scheme).

On a regular integral scheme every [Weil divisor](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#weil-divisor) is [Cartier](../../../cartier-divisor.md), so the [divisor class group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-class-group) is naturally the [Picard group](../../../ringed-space.md#picard-group). Pullback of [line bundles](../../../ringed-space.md#line-bundle) along the [Segre embedding](../../../projective-space.md#segre-embedding) therefore defines

$$
\iota^*: \operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P^3)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1).
$$

The two groups are

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P^3)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal O(1)],
\qquad
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal O(1,0)]\oplus\mathbb Z[\mathcal O(0,1)].
$$

The Segre coordinates are bihomogeneous of bidegree $(1,1)$, so $\iota^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(1)=\mathcal O(1,1)$. In these bases the map is $m\mapsto(m,m)$ and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{im}\iota^*=\mathbb Z(1,1)\subset\mathbb Z^2.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Write $A=k[x,y]$. If $\dim Z=1$, its prime ideal has height one and is generated by an [irreducible polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#irreducible-polynomial) $f$, because $A$ is a [unique factorization domain](../../../algebra.md#unique-factorization-domain). Then $U=D(f)$ is a [principal open subscheme](../../../ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) and

$$
H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)=A_f=k[x,y,f^{-1}].
$$

If $\dim Z=0$, its complement has codimension two. Since $\mathbb A_k^2$ is [normal](../../../ringed-space.md#normal-scheme), regular functions extend across that codimension-two subset, giving

$$
H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)=k[x,y].
$$

This includes the calculation for the [punctured affine plane](../../../ringed-space.md#punctured-affine-plane). If $Z=\mathbb A_k^2$ has dimension two, then $U$ is empty and its ring of sections is the zero ring.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For an open cover $\mathcal U=(U_i)$ and a sheaf $\mathcal F$, the [Čech cochain complex](../../../ringed-space.md#cech-cochain-complex) is

$$
\check C^p(\mathcal U,\mathcal F)=\prod_{i_0<\cdots<i_p}\mathcal F(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_p}),
$$

with the alternating sum of restriction maps as differential. Its cohomology is the [Čech cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#cech-cohomology) $\check H^p(\mathcal U,\mathcal F)$.

Cover $\mathbb P_k^1$ by $U_0=D_+(x_0)$ and $U_1=D_+(x_1)$, and write $t=x_1/x_0$. This is an [acyclic affine cover](../../../ringed-space.md#leray-s-theorem) for the [twisting sheaf on projective space](../../../ringed-space.md#twisting-sheaf-on-projective-space) $\mathcal O(-1)$. In compatible trivializations, its degree-one Čech quotient is

$$
H^1(\mathbb P_k^1,\mathcal O(-1))
\cong
\frac{k[t,t^{-1}]}{k[t]+t^{-1}k[t^{-1}]}.
$$

The first summand contains every nonnegative power of $t$ and the second every negative power, so

$$
\boxed{H^1(\mathbb P_k^1,\mathcal O(-1))=0.}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $A=k[x_1,x_2,x_3]$ and cover the [punctured affine three-space](../../../ringed-space.md#punctured-affine-three-space) $X$ by the three [principal opens](../../../ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) $D(x_i)$. Every finite intersection is affine, so the [acyclic cover theorem](../../../ringed-space.md#leray-s-theorem) identifies [sheaf cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#sheaf-cohomology) with the cohomology of

$$
0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_iA_{x_i}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i<j}A_{x_ix_j}\longrightarrow A_{x_1x_2x_3}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The augmented complex has zeroth cohomology $A$ and first cohomology zero. Its second cohomology is

$$
\frac{A_{x_1x_2x_3}}{A_{x_1x_2}+A_{x_1x_3}+A_{x_2x_3}},
$$

with $k$-basis represented by $x_1^{-a}x_2^{-b}x_3^{-c}$ for $a,b,c\geq1$. There are no higher Čech terms. Hence

$$
\boxed{H^q(X,\mathcal O_X)=
\begin{cases}
A,&q=0,\\
0,&q=1\text{ or }q\geq3,\\
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a,b,c\geq1}k\,x_1^{-a}x_2^{-b}x_3^{-c},&q=2.
\end{cases}}
$$

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Multiplication by the homogeneous equation $f_d$ and restriction to its zero scheme give the [structure-sheaf sequence of a hypersurface](../../../ringed-space.md#structure-sheaf-sequence-of-a-hypersurface)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}(-d)\xrightarrow{\cdot f_d}\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^3}\longrightarrow i_*\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow0.
$$

By [cohomology under a closed immersion](../../../ringed-space.md#cohomology-under-a-closed-immersion), $H^q(\mathbb P^3,i_*\mathcal O_X)\cong H^q(X,\mathcal O_X)$. The associated [long exact sequence in cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology) contains

$$
H^1(\mathbb P^3,\mathcal O)\longrightarrow H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\longrightarrow H^2(\mathbb P^3,\mathcal O(-d)).
$$

Both outer groups vanish by the [cohomology of twisting sheaves on projective space](../../../projective-space.md#cohomology-of-twisting-sheaves-on-projective-space), because they are intermediate cohomology groups on $\mathbb P^3$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0.}
$$
