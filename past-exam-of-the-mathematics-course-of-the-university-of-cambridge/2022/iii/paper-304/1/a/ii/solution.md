<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is $m\ddot x=0$. The unique path with the prescribed endpoints is therefore

$$
x_{\rm cl}(t)=x_i+\frac{x_f-x_i}{T}t.
$$

It is a minimum of the Euclidean action and a stationary point of the real-time action. Its classical action is

$$
S_{\rm cl}=\int_0^T\frac m2\dot x_{\rm cl}^{,2},dt
=\frac{m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2T}.
$$

The [principle of stationary action](../../../../../../../principle-of-stationary-action.md) consequently fixes the position-dependent phase of the [semiclassical propagator](../../../../../../../semiclassical-propagator.md) as

$$
K(x_f,T;x_i,0)=C(T)e^{iS_{\rm cl}/\hbar}.
$$

Because the action is quadratic, the stationary-phase evaluation of the [path integral](../../../../../../../path-integral.md) is exact. Composition of propagators, or the [Van Vleck determinant](../../../../../../../van-vleck-determinant.md), gives $C(T)=\sqrt{m/(2\pi i\hbar T)}$, reproducing part i.
