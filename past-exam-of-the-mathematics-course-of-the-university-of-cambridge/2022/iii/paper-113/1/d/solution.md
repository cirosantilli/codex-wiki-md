<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [coincidence locus of two scheme morphisms](../../../../../../coincidence-locus-of-two-scheme-morphisms.md) is the fibre product

$$
E=X\times_{(f,g),\,Y\times_kY,\,\Delta_Y}Y.
$$

The [diagonal morphism](../../../../../../diagonal-morphism.md) $\Delta_Y$ is a [locally closed immersion](../../../../../../locally-closed-immersion.md), and this property is stable under [base change of a morphism of schemes](../../../../../../base-change-of-a-morphism-of-schemes.md), so $E\hookrightarrow X$ is locally closed. The [universal property of a fibre product](../../../../../../universal-property-of-a-fibre-product.md) says that a morphism $T\to X$ factors through $E$ exactly when $f|_T=g|_T$. Thus $E$ is the largest locally closed subscheme on which they coincide. If $Y$ is [separated](../../../../../../separated-scheme.md), $\Delta_Y$ is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md), and its base change $E\hookrightarrow X$ is closed.
