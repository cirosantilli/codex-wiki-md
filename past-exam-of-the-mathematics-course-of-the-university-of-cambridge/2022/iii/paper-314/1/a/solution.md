<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A magnetostatic [force-free magnetic field](../../../../../../force-free-magnetic-field.md) is in equilibrium when its electric current exerts no [Lorentz force density](../../../../../../lorentz-force-density.md):

$$
\mathbf J\times\mathbf B=0.
$$

Thus $\mathbf J$ is pointwise parallel to $\mathbf B$, so for some scalar $\alpha(\mathbf r)$,

$$
\mathbf J=\alpha\mathbf B.
$$

Using the magnetostatic [Ampère's law](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md), $\nabla\times\mathbf B=(4\pi/c)\mathbf J$, gives

$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\mathbf B=\frac{4\pi}{c}\alpha\mathbf B}.
$$

Taking the divergence and using $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B\mathbin\cdot\nabla\alpha=0}.
$$

The [force-free parameter](../../../../../../force-free-parameter.md) is therefore constant along each magnetic field line.
