↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Here

$$
q(x)=\frac{a^2x}{(x^2+d^2)^2},
\qquad
\theta(x)=-\frac{a^2}{2(x^2+d^2)}
$$

after choosing the integration constant so that $\theta(\pm\infty)=0$. The condition $B_z\to0$ at both ends removes the cosine solution. Up to an overall sign and magnitude, one may write

$$
B_z=B_0\sin I(x),\qquad
B_y=B_0\cos I(x),\qquad
I(x)=\frac{a^2}{2(x^2+d^2)}.
$$

The angle ranges from $0$ to $I_{\max}=a^2/(2d^2)$. Neither nonzero component changes sign when the entire range lies in the first quadrant, namely $I_{\max}\leq\pi/2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac d a\geq\frac1{\sqrt\pi}}
$$

for positive length scales $a,d$. Equality allows $B_y$ to touch zero at $x=0$ without changing sign.
