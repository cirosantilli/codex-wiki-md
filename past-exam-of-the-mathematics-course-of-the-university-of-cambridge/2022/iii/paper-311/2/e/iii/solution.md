<h1 id="2/e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In the reversible limit the particle is released arbitrarily close to the horizon, so

$$
dM=\Phi_H\,dQ=\frac{Q}{r_+^{d-3}}\,dQ.
$$

Holding $r_+$ fixed and using $Q^2=r_+^{d-3}r_-^{d-3}$,

$$
M=\frac12\left(r_+^{d-3}+\frac{Q^2}{r_+^{d-3}}\right).
$$

Discharging to $Q=0$ therefore extracts at most

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm ext}^{\rm max}
=M-\frac12r_+^{d-3}
=\frac{Q^2}{2r_+^{d-3}}
=\frac12r_-^{d-3}}.
$$

[Hawking's area theorem](../../../../../../../hawking-s-area-theorem.md) forbids the horizon area, and hence $r_+$, from decreasing. The smallest possible final neutral mass is consequently $r_+^{d-3}/2$, giving exactly the same upper bound.
