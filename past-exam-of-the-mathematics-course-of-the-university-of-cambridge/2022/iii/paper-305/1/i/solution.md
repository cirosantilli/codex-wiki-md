<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The range of a force is controlled by the lightest state that can carry it. Exchange of a particle of mass $m$ produces a [Yukawa potential](../../../../../../yukawa-potential.md) proportional to $e^{-mr}/r$, with range $\hbar/(mc)$. The [electromagnetic interaction](../../../../../../electromagnetic-interaction.md) is long range because its carrier, the [photon](../../../../../../photon.md), is massless. The [weak interaction](../../../../../../weak-interaction.md) is short range because the [W bosons](../../../../../../w-boson.md) and [Z boson](../../../../../../z-boson.md) are massive. Although the [gluons](../../../../../../gluon.md) of the [strong interaction](../../../../../../strong-interaction.md) are massless in the Lagrangian, [Quantum chromodynamics](../../../../../../quantum-chromodynamics.md) confines color and has a [mass gap](../../../../../../mass-gap.md); only color-singlet hadrons propagate over macroscopic distances. The residual nuclear force is consequently controlled at long distance by massive pion exchange.
