<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

After replacing $X_j$ by $X_j-\mu$, the maximal inequality for independent averages gives

$$
\left\|\sup_{m\geq1}\frac{|S_m|}{m}\right\|_{L^p}
\leq C_p\|X_1\|_{L^p},
\qquad p>1.
$$

This follows from the [Doob Lp maximal inequality](../../../../../../doob-lp-maximal-inequality.md) by dyadically grouping the partial sums. The strong law makes

$$
\sup_{m\geq n}\frac{|S_m|}{m}\longrightarrow0
$$

almost surely. The displayed maximal function is in $L^p$, so [dominated convergence](../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) applied to its $p$th power proves convergence in $L^p$.
