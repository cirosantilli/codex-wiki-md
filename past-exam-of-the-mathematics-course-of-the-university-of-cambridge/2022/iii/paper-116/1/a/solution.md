<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An uncountable [cardinal number](../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa$ is [weakly compact](../../../../../../weakly-compact-cardinal.md) when every $\kappa$-satisfiable theory in an [infinitary language](../../../../../../infinitary-language.md) $L_{\kappa,\kappa}$ with at most $\kappa$ nonlogical symbols is satisfiable. A cardinal is [inaccessible](../../../../../../strongly-inaccessible-cardinal.md) when it is uncountable, [regular](../../../../../../regular-cardinal.md), and a [strong limit cardinal](../../../../../../strong-limit-cardinal.md).

Two standard results supply the proof. First, every weakly compact cardinal is inaccessible. Second, every weakly compact $\kappa$ has the [Keisler extension property](../../../../../../keisler-extension-property.md): there is a transitive set $X\supsetneq V_\kappa$ such that

$$
(V_\kappa,\in)\prec(X,\in)
$$

and $\kappa\in X$. Since $\kappa$ is inaccessible, the relevant [downward absoluteness](../../../../../../set-theoretic-absoluteness.md) makes $X\models$ “$\kappa$ is inaccessible”. Hence $X$ satisfies “there is an inaccessible cardinal”. By [elementarity](../../../../../../elementary-substructure.md), $V_\kappa$ satisfies the same sentence, so it contains some inaccessible $\lambda$. Every [ordinal](../../../../../../ordinal.md) in $V_\kappa$ is below $\kappa$, and inaccessibility is absolute here, giving

$$
\boxed{\lambda<\kappa\text{ and }\lambda\text{ is inaccessible}.}
$$
