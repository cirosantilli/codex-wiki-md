<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

A [Wirtinger presentation](../../../../../wirtinger-presentation.md) from a connected [knot diagram](../../../../../knot-diagram.md) has one generator per arc and one relator per crossing, with one relator redundant. Its presentation complex is a finite two-dimensional [CW complex](../../../../../cw-complex.md) $X$ with one zero-cell, $n$ one-cells, and $n-1$ two-cells, and the usual diagrammatic construction gives a [homotopy equivalence](../../../../../homotopy-equivalence.md) $X\simeq E_K$.

The [abelianization](../../../../../abelianization.md) of the [knot group](../../../../../knot-group.md) is $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, generated by a [meridian of a knot](../../../../../meridian-of-a-knot.md). Every homomorphism to the [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/2$ factors through this abelianization, and reduction modulo two is its unique surjection. Thus the requested map $\alpha$ is unique.

Its kernel determines a two-sheeted [covering space](../../../../../covering-space.md) $\widehat X$. The nontrivial [deck transformation](../../../../../deck-transformation.md) acts on cellular chains and homology, giving them module structures over the [group ring](../../../../../group-ring.md)

$$
\widehat R=\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z/2]\cong\mathbb Z[t]/(t^2-1).
$$

Lift one copy of every cell of $X$; its two deck translates form a free $\widehat R$-basis. Hence $C_0\cong\widehat R$, $C_1\cong\widehat R^n$, and $C_2\cong\widehat R^{n-1}$. If $\widetilde X$ is the [infinite cyclic cover](../../../../../infinite-cyclic-cover-of-a-knot-exterior.md), its cellular chains are free over $R=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$, and imposing $t^2=1$ gives

$$
C_*^{\mathrm{cell}}(\widehat X)\cong C_*^{\mathrm{cell}}(\widetilde X)\otimes_R\widehat R.
$$

For an odd prime $p$, the two [idempotents](../../../../../idempotent.md) $e_+=(1+t)/2$ and $e_-=(1-t)/2$ split the [group algebra](../../../../../group-algebra.md)

$$
\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z/2]\cong R_+\oplus R_-,
\qquad R_+=\mathbb F_p[t^{\pm1}]/(t-1),\quad R_-=\mathbb F_p[t^{\pm1}]/(t+1).
$$

The plus summand is the cellular chain complex of $X$ with $\mathbb F_p$ coefficients, while the minus summand is $C_-=C_*^{\mathrm{cell}}(\widetilde X)\otimes_RR_-$. Therefore

$$
H_*(\widehat X;\mathbb F_p)\cong H_*(E_K;\mathbb F_p)\oplus H_*(C_-).
$$

On the minus summand, the boundary $t-1:C_1\to C_0$ becomes multiplication by $-2$, which is invertible in $\mathbb F_p$, so $H_0(C_-)=0$. After the corresponding cancellation, the remaining square boundary matrix is an [Alexander matrix](../../../../../alexander-matrix.md) specialized at $t=-1$. It is singular over $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when

$$
\Delta_K(-1)\equiv0\pmod p,
$$

or equivalently when $p$ divides the [knot determinant](../../../../../knot-determinant.md) $\det K$. Thus $H_*(C_-)$ is nonzero exactly in that case.

Finally, $\det K=|\Delta_K(-1)|$ is a nonzero odd integer, so the minus complex is acyclic over $\mathbb Q$. Since a knot exterior has the rational homology of a circle,

$$
\boxed{H_i(\widehat X;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&i=0,1,\\
0,&i\geq2.
\end{cases}}
$$
