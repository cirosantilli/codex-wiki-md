<h1 id="18h/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The two tests have the same size, but their powers cross. At $\theta=0.1$,

$$
\pi_1(0.1)=0.15,
\qquad
\pi_2(0.1)=\frac12\left(\frac1{\sqrt{10}}+0.2\right)^2<0.15.
$$

At $\theta=0.5$,

$$
\pi_1(0.5)=0.55,
\qquad
\pi_2(0.5)=1-\frac12\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{10}}\right)^2>0.55.
$$

Thus Test 2 is a same-size test that beats Test 1 at one alternative, while Test 1 beats Test 2 at another. By the [crossing-power obstruction to a uniformly most powerful test](../../../../../../crossing-power-obstruction-to-a-uniformly-most-powerful-test.md),

$$
\boxed{\text{neither Test 1 nor Test 2 is uniformly most powerful}.}
$$
