<h1 id="2e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2E](../2e.md)

After the $2n$ sides are identified in pairs, the [polygonal-schema Euler count](../../../../../polygonal-schema-euler-count.md) gives one face, $n$ edges and some number $V\geq1$ of vertices. The [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) of a closed orientable genus-$g$ surface is $2-2g$, so

$$
2-2g=V-n+1,
\qquad
n=V-1+2g\geq2g.
$$

For genus two, take a regular octagon in the [Poincare disc model](../../../../../poincare-disk-model.md) and identify each side with its opposite side, with the orientation reversed along the boundary. One cyclic labelling is

$$
a,b,c,d,a^{-1},b^{-1},c^{-1},d^{-1}.
$$

The quotient has $V=1$, $E=4$, and $F=1$, hence Euler characteristic $-2$ and genus two. All eight vertices become one point, so smoothness requires their angles to sum to $2\pi$. Each interior angle is consequently

$$
\boxed{\frac{2\pi}{8}=\frac\pi4}.
$$

Equivalently, the [hyperbolic polygon area](../../../../../hyperbolic-polygon-area.md) formula shows that the [regular hyperbolic octagon fundamental polygon](../../../../../regular-hyperbolic-octagon-fundamental-polygon.md) has area

$$
(8-2)\pi-8\left(\frac\pi4\right)=4\pi,
$$

as required by the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](../../../../../gauss-bonnet-theorem.md) for a genus-two surface of curvature $-1$.
