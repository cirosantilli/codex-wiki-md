<h1 id="1e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Regard

$$
F=X^2+Y^2-1
$$

as a monic quadratic in $X$ over $\mathbb Q[Y]$. By the [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../../../../gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md), reducibility in $\mathbb Q[X,Y]$ would imply reducibility in $\mathbb Q(Y)[X]$, so $1-Y^2$ would be a square in $\mathbb Q(Y)$. This is impossible: its zero at $Y=1$ has odd order one, whereas every zero or pole of a square in a rational-function field has even order. Hence

$$
\boxed{X^2+Y^2-1\text{ is irreducible over }\mathbb Q}.
$$

The same [odd-valuation obstruction to a rational-function square](../../../../../../odd-valuation-obstruction-to-a-rational-function-square.md) works over every field of characteristic different from two, since the zeros $Y=1$ and $Y=-1$ are then distinct. It does not work over every field. In characteristic two,

$$
X^2+Y^2-1=(X+Y+1)^2,
$$

so the [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is reducible.
