<h1 id="1e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

By the finite case of the structure theorem, a finite abelian [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is a direct sum of cyclic [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) of prime-power order. If it is indecomposable, this decomposition can have only one nonzero summand, so the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is cyclic of order $p^n$ for some prime $p$.

Conversely, every nontrivial [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of the cyclic [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $C_{p^n}$ contains its unique [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order $p$. Hence two nontrivial [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) cannot have trivial intersection. They therefore cannot be the two summands of an internal direct sum. Thus the [indecomposable finite abelian groups](../../../../../../indecomposable-finite-abelian-groups.md) are precisely

$$
\boxed{C_{p^n}\quad(n\geq1)}.
$$
