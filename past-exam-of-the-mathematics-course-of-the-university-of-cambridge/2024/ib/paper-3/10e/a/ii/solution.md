<h1 id="10e/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In a Boolean [ring](../../../../../../../ring.md), $(r+1)^2=r+1$. Using $r^2=r$ and $1^2=1$ gives

$$
r+2r+1=r+1,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{2r=0\quad\text{for every }r\in R}.
$$

If $R$ is also a nonzero [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) domain, then

$$
r(r-1)=r^2-r=0
$$

forces every $r$ to equal $0$ or $1$. Hence $R\cong\mathbb F_2$.

If $I$ is prime in a Boolean [ring](../../../../../../../ring.md), then $R/I$ is a nonzero Boolean [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) domain by part (i), and hence is $\mathbb F_2$. Since the quotient is a field,

$$
\boxed{I\text{ is maximal}}.
$$

This is the [prime ideals of a Boolean ring are maximal](../../../../../../../prime-ideals-of-a-boolean-ring-are-maximal.md) property.
