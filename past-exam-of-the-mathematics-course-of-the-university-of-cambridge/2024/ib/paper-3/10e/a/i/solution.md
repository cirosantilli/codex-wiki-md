<h1 id="10e/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose $I$ is prime. If

$$
(a+I)(b+I)=0+I,
$$

then $ab\in I$, so $a\in I$ or $b\in I$. Thus one factor is zero and $R/I$ is an [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) domain.

Conversely, if $R/I$ is an [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) domain and $ab\in I$, then

$$
(a+I)(b+I)=0+I,
$$

so $a+I=0+I$ or $b+I=0+I$. Therefore $a\in I$ or $b\in I$, and $I$ is prime. This proves the [prime ideal quotient criterion](../../../../../../../prime-ideal-quotient-criterion.md).
