<h1 id="9e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An $R$-module is free if it has a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md): a subset $B$ such that every element has a unique expression as a finite $R$-linear combination of elements of $B$.

Choose a maximal [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$ of the nonzero [ring](../../../../../../ring.md) $R$. If $R^n\cong R^m$, quotienting by $\mathfrak m$ gives

$$
(R/\mathfrak m)^n\cong(R/\mathfrak m)^m.
$$

These are [vector spaces](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) over the field $R/\mathfrak m$, so equality of dimension gives $n=m$. This proves [invariant basis number for a commutative ring](../../../../../../invariant-basis-number-for-a-commutative-ring.md).

A direct summand of a free module need not be free. Take $R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$. Its [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) $P=(2)$ and $Q=(3)$ satisfy

$$
P\cap Q=0,
\qquad P+Q=R,
$$

so $P\oplus Q\cong R$. But $P$ has three elements, whereas a finite-rank free $R$-module has $6^r$ elements; hence $P$ is not free.
