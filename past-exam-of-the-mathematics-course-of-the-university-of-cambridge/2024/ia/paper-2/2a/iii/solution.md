<h1 id="2a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Since

$$
\frac{f'}{1+f^2}=C,
$$

integration gives

$$
\arctan f(x)=Cx+D.
$$

Part (i) gives $D=0$, so the [tangent addition functional equation](../../../../../../tangent-addition-functional-equation.md) has the solutions

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\tan(Cx)},
$$

for constants $C$ such that no pole lies in $(-a,a)$. Conversely, the tangent addition formula verifies the functional equation. If $f'(0)=2$, then $C=2$, and

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\tan(2x)}
$$

on any such interval, necessarily with $a\leq\pi/4$.
