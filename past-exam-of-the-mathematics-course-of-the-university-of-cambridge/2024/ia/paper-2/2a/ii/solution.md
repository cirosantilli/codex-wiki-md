<h1 id="2a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Using the addition law with $y=h$,

$$
\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h
=\frac{f(h)}h
\frac{1+f(x)^2}{1-f(x)f(h)}.
$$

Differentiability makes $f$ continuous, so $f(h)\to f(0)=0$, and

$$
\frac{f(h)}h\to f'(0).
$$

Writing $C=f'(0)$ and taking the [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f'(x)=C(1+f(x)^2)}.
$$
