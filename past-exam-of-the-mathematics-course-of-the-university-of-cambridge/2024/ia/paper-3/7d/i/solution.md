[Cayley theorem](../../../../../../cayley-s-theorem.md) states that every [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $G$ is isomorphic to a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of the symmetric [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) on its underlying set. For $g\in G$, define

$$
L_g:G\to G,
\qquad L_g(x)=gx.
$$

Each $L_g$ is a permutation, and $L_gL_h=L_{gh}$, so $g\mapsto L_g$ is a homomorphism. If $L_g$ is the identity permutation, then $g=L_g(1)=1$; hence the homomorphism is injective. For finite $G$ of order $n$, this embeds $G$ in $S_n$.
