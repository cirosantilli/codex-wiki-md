<h1 id="6d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Five examples are

$$
C_{12},\qquad C_6\times C_2,
\qquad A_4,\qquad D_{12},\qquad \operatorname{Dic}_3,
$$

where $D_{12}$ denotes the symmetry [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of a hexagon and $\operatorname{Dic}_3$ is the $3$-dicyclic [group](../../../../../../group-split.md).

The first two are abelian, while the last three are not. Of the first two, only $C_{12}$ is cyclic. Among the nonabelian [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md), $A_4$ has no element of order $6$; both of the others do. The dihedral [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $D_{12}$ has seven involutions, whereas $\operatorname{Dic}_3$ has the unique involution $a^3$ and also has elements outside $\langle a\rangle$ of order four. These invariants prove that all five [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) are pairwise non-isomorphic.
