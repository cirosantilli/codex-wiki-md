**No.** Take $a=0$ and

$$
f(x)=x,\qquad g(x)=1+|x|.
$$

Then $f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) and

$$
(fg)(x)=x+x|x|.
$$

Since $x|x|/x=|x|\to0$, the product is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at zero with

$$
(fg)'(0)=1\ne0.
$$

However, $g$ has one-sided [derivatives](../../../../../../../derivative.md) $-1$ and $1$ and is not [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at zero. This is a [nondifferentiable factor with a differentiable nondegenerate product](../../../../../../../nondifferentiable-factor-with-a-differentiable-nondegenerate-product.md).
