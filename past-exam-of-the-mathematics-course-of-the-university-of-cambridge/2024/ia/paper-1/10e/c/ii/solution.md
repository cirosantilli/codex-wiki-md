**No.** At $a=0$, let

$$
f(x)=\sqrt[3]x,\qquad g(y)=y^3.
$$

The [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) $g$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $f(0)=0$, and

$$
(g\circ f)(x)=x,
$$

so the composite has [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) one at zero. But

$$
\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=|h|^{-2/3}
$$

is unbounded, so $f$ is not [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md). This is a [nondifferentiable inner function with a differentiable nondegenerate composite](../../../../../../../nondifferentiable-inner-function-with-a-differentiable-nondegenerate-composite.md).
