<h1 id="10e/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

To say that $f$ is twice [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $a$ means that $f'$ is defined in a neighbourhood of $a$ and is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $a$; its [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) is $f''(a)$.

By the [chain rule](../../../../../../../chain-rule.md), near $a$,

$$
(g\circ f)'=(g'\circ f)f'.
$$

Since $f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) and $g'$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $f(a)$, another use of the chain rule shows that $g'\circ f$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $a$. The [product rule](../../../../../../../product-rule.md) then proves that the displayed product is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md). Hence $g\circ f$ is twice [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md), with the [second derivative chain rule](../../../../../../../second-derivative-chain-rule.md)

$$
\boxed{(g\circ f)''(a)
=g''(f(a))[f'(a)]^2+g'(f(a))f''(a)}.
$$
