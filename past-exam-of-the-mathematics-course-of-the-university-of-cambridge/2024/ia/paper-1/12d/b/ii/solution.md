**No.** Enumerate the rationals in $[0,1]$ as

$$
A=\{q_1,q_2,\ldots\},
$$

and define

$$
f(q_n)=n,\qquad f(x)=0\quad(x\notin A).
$$

The [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is nonnegative and unbounded, while it is identically zero on $[0,1]\setminus A$. For any $r>0$, however, $f_r$ is positive on the dense set of rationals and zero on the dense set of irrationals. Every interval therefore has positive oscillation, and the upper and lower Darboux [integrals](../../../../../../../integral.md) differ. Thus even the truncations need not be Riemann integrable.
