<h1 id="8b/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The proof is by induction on $n$. Choose a unit [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$ of $A$, which exists by part (a), and extend it to an orthonormal [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md). In that [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md),

$$
U_1^\dagger AU_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&*\\
0&C
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

By induction, a unitary change of [basis](../../../../../../../basis.md) on the last $n-1$ coordinates makes $C$ upper triangular. Extending that change by the identity on the first coordinate leaves the displayed zero block intact. Their product is unitary and makes all of $A$ upper triangular. This proves [Schur triangularization](../../../../../../../schur-decomposition.md).
