<h1 id="31c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Near $x=\lambda$,

$$
1-\frac{\lambda^2}{x^2}=\frac{2(x-\lambda)}{\lambda}+O((x-\lambda)^2).
$$

With

$$
t=\left(\frac2\lambda\right)^{1/3}
\frac{x-\lambda}{\epsilon^{2/3}},
$$

the leading equation is $y_{tt}-ty=0$. Decay for $t>0$ selects $y=C\operatorname{Ai}(t)$. The [Airy turning-point connection formula](../../../../../../airy-turning-point-connection-formula.md) gives on the oscillatory side

$$
y\sim C' p(x)^{-1/2}
\sin\left(\frac1\epsilon\int_x^\lambda p(s)\,ds+\frac\pi4\right).
$$

For this to be proportional to the inner expression from part (a), the two phases must differ by an integer multiple of $\pi$. Thus the [WKB quantization condition](../../../../../../wkb-quantization-condition.md) is

$$
\boxed{\int_1^\lambda\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{x^2}-1\right)^{1/2}dx
=\epsilon\left(n+\frac34\right)\pi,
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$
