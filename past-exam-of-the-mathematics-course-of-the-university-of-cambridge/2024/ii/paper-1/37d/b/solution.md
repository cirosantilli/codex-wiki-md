<h1 id="37d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Lorentz invariance requires $A^\mu$ and $J^\mu$ to transform as four-vectors, $\chi$ as a [scalar](../../../../../../scalar.md), $m$ and $\mu_0$ as [scalars](../../../../../../scalar.md), and the integration domain and [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) to respect the transformation. Then every contracted term and $d^4x$ are Lorentz invariant.

Under $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi$, the field strength is unchanged. The source term changes by a boundary term plus

$$
-\int\chi\,\partial_\mu J^\mu,d^4x,
$$

which vanishes for a conserved current and suitable boundary behaviour of $\chi$. The mass term is not gauge invariant. Thus the action has the usual local gauge invariance when

$$
\boxed{m=0,\qquad \partial_\mu J^\mu=0,}
$$

with the gauge [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) or its boundary contribution suitably controlled. For $m>0$, the mass term breaks this gauge symmetry.
