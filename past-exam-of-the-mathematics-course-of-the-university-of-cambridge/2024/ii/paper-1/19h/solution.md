<h1 id="19h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [19H](../19h.md)

A complex representation of $G$ is a complex [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $V$ together with a homomorphism

$$
\rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V).
$$

It is faithful when $\ker\rho=\{e\}$, equivalently when $\rho$ is injective. Every finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) has a faithful complex representation: in the [regular representation](../../../../../regular-representation.md), $G$ permutes the [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $\{e_h:h\in G\}$ by

$$
\rho(g)e_h=e_{gh},
$$

and a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) element fixing every [basis](../../../../../basis.md) [vector](../../../../../vector.md) must be the identity.

If $g$ is conjugate to $g^k$, then $\rho(g)$ is similar to $\rho(g^k)=\rho(g)^k$. Thus the two [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) have the same [spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md), while an [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ of $\rho(g)$ gives the [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda^k$ of $\rho(g)^k$. Hence

$$
\lambda\in S(g)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda^k\in S(g).
$$

Now take a $p$-cycle $g\in S_p$. It is conjugate to $g^k$ for every $1\leq k<p$. Faithfulness makes $\rho(g)$ have order $p$, so its [spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md) contains a nontrivial $p$th root of unity $\lambda$. The preceding implication puts all the $p-1$ distinct values

$$
\lambda,\lambda^2,\ldots,\lambda^{p-1}
$$

in the [spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md). Therefore $\dim V\geq p-1$. This is the [spectrum orbit bound for a faithful symmetric-group representation](../../../../../spectrum-orbit-bound-for-a-faithful-symmetric-group-representation.md).

Finally let $G$ be the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of all permutations of $\mathbb N$. It has no faithful finite-dimensional complex representation. For every $n$, $G$ contains a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $(C_2)^n$ generated by $n$ disjoint transpositions. In a $d$-dimensional complex representation, commuting involutions are simultaneously diagonalizable and hence map into the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of diagonal sign [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md), which has order $2^d$. A faithful restriction would require $2^n\leq2^d$ for every $n$, an impossibility.
