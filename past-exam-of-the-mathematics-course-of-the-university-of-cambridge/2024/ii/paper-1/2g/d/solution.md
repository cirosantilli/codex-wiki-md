<h1 id="2g/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The $n$ roots of $T_n$ are distinct and lie in $(-1,1)$. Repeated use of Rolle's theorem shows that every root of $T_n^{(k)}$, for $0\leq k<n$, also lies in $(-1,1)$. Its leading coefficient is positive, so

$$
T_n^{(k)}(x)>0\qquad(x\geq1).
$$

Taking $k=r+1$ shows that

$$
\frac d{dx}T_n^{(r)}(x)=T_n^{(r+1)}(x)>0
$$

for $0\leq r\leq n-1$; when $r=n-1$, the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is the positive constant $T_n^{(n)}$. This is the [positivity of Chebyshev derivatives beyond the unit interval](../../../../../../positivity-of-chebyshev-derivatives-beyond-the-unit-interval.md).
