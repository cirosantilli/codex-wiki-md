<h1 id="40d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\widetilde\rho$, $\widetilde p$, and $u$ be first-order perturbations. Linearized mass and [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) conservation and the homentropic relation are

$$
\partial_t\widetilde\rho+\rho_0\nabla\cdot u=0,
\qquad
\rho_0\partial_tu=-\nabla\widetilde p,
\qquad
\widetilde p=c_0^2\widetilde\rho.
$$

Differentiate the first equation in time and use the divergence of the second to obtain

$$
\boxed{\partial_t^2\widetilde p-c_0^2\nabla^2\widetilde p=0.}
$$

The perturbation is irrotational, so write $u=\nabla\phi$. Integrating the [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) equation spatially, with a purely time-dependent constant absorbed into $\phi$, gives

$$
\boxed{\widetilde p=-\rho_0\partial_t\phi.}
$$
