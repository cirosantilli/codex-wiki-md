<h1 id="34b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Set $c=\langle\psi|\phi\rangle$. The off-diagonal Hermiticity identity holds automatically, while

$$
\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle=gc
$$

must be real. Thus

$$
\boxed{g\langle\psi|\phi\rangle\in\mathbb R}
$$

is necessary and sufficient. For $g\ne0$, put $r=gc/|g|\in\mathbb R$. Since the two normalized spanning [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are linearly independent, $|r|<1$.

The normalized [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) and [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are

$$
\boxed{
|\pm\rangle=
\frac{(g^*/|g|)|\psi\rangle\pm|\phi\rangle}
 {\sqrt{2(1\pm r)}},
\qquad E_\pm=\pm|g|.}
$$

Direct application of $H$ verifies the [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) equations. Their unnormalized [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md) is

$$
-\frac g{|g|}c+\frac{g^*}{|g|}c^*=-r+r=0,
$$

so the distinct [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) are orthogonal, as required for a [Hermitian operator](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md). If $g=0$, $H=0$ and every [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) is an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md).
