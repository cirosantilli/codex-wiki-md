<h1 id="9d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Because $X_i=n_i/n_B$,

$$
\frac{X_D}{X_pX_n}=n_B\frac{n_D}{n_pn_n}.
$$

Using $n_B=\eta n_\gamma$ and the given photon density in the [deuterium equilibrium abundance](../../../../../../deuterium-equilibrium-abundance.md) formula gives

$$
\boxed{
\frac{X_D}{X_pX_n}
\simeq\frac{16\zeta(3)}{\sqrt\pi}\eta
\left(\frac{k_BT}{m_pc^2}\right)^{3/2}
e^{B_D/(k_BT)}}.
$$

Although the exponential favors deuterium once $k_BT<B_D$, the very small factor $\eta$ expresses the enormous excess of photons. The high-energy tail therefore photodissociates newly formed deuterium until the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) is well below $B_D/k_B$; this is the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md).
