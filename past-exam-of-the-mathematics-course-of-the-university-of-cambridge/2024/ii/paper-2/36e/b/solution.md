<h1 id="36e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $\psi=\sum_n\alpha_n\phi_n$ and an orthonormal trial set,

$$
\frac{\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi|\psi\rangle}
=\frac{\alpha^\dagger\mathcal H\alpha}{\alpha^\dagger\alpha},
\qquad
\mathcal H_{nm}=\langle\phi_n|H|\phi_m\rangle.
$$

Stationarity subject to $\alpha^\dagger\alpha=1$ gives

$$
\mathcal H\alpha=E\alpha.
$$

The [finite-subspace variational method](../../../../../../finite-subspace-variational-method.md), equivalently the [Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle](../../../../../../rayleigh-ritz-variational-principle.md), says that the minimum quotient is the smallest [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $\mathcal H$. It is therefore the optimal upper bound on $E_0$ available from this span.
