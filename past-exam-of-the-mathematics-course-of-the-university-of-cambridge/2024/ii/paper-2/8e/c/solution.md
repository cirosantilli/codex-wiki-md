<h1 id="8e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)$ and $n=(1,1,1)$. Since

$$
q_1q_2+q_2q_3+q_3q_1
=\frac12\{(q\cdot n)^2-|q|^2\},
$$

the [Lagrangian](../../../../../../lagrangian.md) depends on $q$ only through $|q|^2$ and $(q\cdot n)^2$, and on the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) through $|\dot q|^2$. Rotations about the $n$-axis preserve all three quantities, proving the invariance.

The [axially symmetric isotropic-mass Lagrangian](../../../../../../axially-symmetric-isotropic-mass-lagrangian.md) therefore conserves the axial [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md)

$$
J_n=n\cdot(q\times\dot q)
=(q_2\dot q_3-q_3\dot q_2)
 +(q_3\dot q_1-q_1\dot q_3)
 +(q_1\dot q_2-q_2\dot q_1),
$$

up to an irrelevant normalization of $n$. Since $L$ has no explicit time dependence, [conservation of energy from time-translation invariance](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy-from-time-translation-invariance.md) gives the second conserved quantity

$$
\boxed{E=\frac12|\dot q|^2+\frac12|q|^2
+\alpha(q_1q_2+q_2q_3+q_3q_1).}
$$
