**Yes.** Your public and private exponents reveal $ed-1$, a nonzero multiple of $\lambda(N)$. The standard [RSA private exponent reveals the factorization](../../../../../../../rsa-private-exponent-reveals-the-factorization.md) algorithm uses repeated squaring of random residues to obtain a nontrivial square root of one and hence factors $N$ with a greatest common divisor. Once $p$ and $q$ are known, compute $\phi(N)$ and invert every other customer's public exponent to obtain that customer's private exponent.
