Lévy's convergence theorem says that probability measures $\mu_n$ on $\mathbb R^d$ converge weakly to $\mu$ if and only if their characteristic [functions](../../../../../../../function-split.md) converge pointwise to the characteristic [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) of $\mu$. More generally, if the characteristic [functions](../../../../../../../function-split.md) converge pointwise to a [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) continuous at zero, that [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) is the characteristic [function](../../../../../../../function-split.md) of a probability measure and the corresponding weak convergence holds.
