<h1 id="16i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Zorn lemma](../../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md) says that a nonempty partially ordered set in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element. The [axiom of choice](../../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) says that every family of nonempty sets has a choice [function](../../../../../../function-split.md). The [well-ordering theorem](../../../../../../well-ordering-theorem.md) says that every set admits a well-order.

Choice gives the Hausdorff maximal principle by repeatedly choosing an element extending a chain; the union at [limit](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) stages is again a chain. A maximal chain has an upper bound, and that upper bound is maximal, proving Zorn. Conversely, apply Zorn to partial choice [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) ordered by extension. A maximal partial choice [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) must have the full family as domain, proving choice.

Choice also well-orders a set by recursively choosing from the unchosen remainder; [Hartogs theorem](../../../../../../hartogs-theorem.md) forces this recursion to exhaust the set before it reaches the Hartogs ordinal. Conversely, from a well-order on the union of a family, choose the least element of each member. Thus all three principles are equivalent.
