<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $D:\mathcal J\to\mathbf{Fld}$ be connected and nonempty. The limit of the underlying diagram of [commutative rings](../../../../../../commutative-ring.md) is the subring

$$
L=\left\{(x_j)\in\prod_jD(j):
D(u)(x_i)=x_j\text{ for every }u:i\to j\right\}.
$$

If $x\in L$ is nonzero in one component, it is nonzero in every component: field homomorphisms are injective, and connectedness propagates this fact along zigzags. Hence the componentwise inverses $(x_j^{-1})$ are defined and compatible. Thus $L$ is a field. Since the inclusion $\mathbf{Fld}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CRng}$ is full, the same cone is limiting in the [category of fields](../../../../../../category-of-fields.md).

If $\mathcal J$ is disconnected, choose two components and use the constant field $\mathbb F_2$ on one and $\mathbb F_3$ on the other. There is no cone in fields because its apex would map to fields of two different characteristics. Hence $\mathbf{Fld}$ does not have all limits of any disconnected shape.
