<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose a nonzero solution $w$ did not change sign. Replacing it by $-w$ if necessary gives $w\geq0$. The argument of part (b.ii) is local and invariant under translation and scaling: it applies on every ball whose concentric double lies in $B_2$. If the zero set of $w$ had positive measure, a density point and this local result would make $w$ vanish on one ball. Applying the same result successively on overlapping balls would then give $w\equiv0$ throughout the connected ball $B_2$, a contradiction. Thus the zero set has measure zero in $B_2$. Applying part (a.i) to $u=w$ gives

$$
\int_{B_2}q\zeta^2\leq\int_{B_2}|D\zeta|^2
$$

for every compactly supported $C^1$ function $\zeta$; the omitted zero set has measure zero. But $\inf q>\lambda_1$, while the variational characterization of the [First Dirichlet eigenvalue](../../../../../../../first-dirichlet-eigenvalue.md) provides a $\zeta$ with

$$
\int|D\zeta|^2<\int q\zeta^2.
$$

This contradiction proves that every nonzero solution takes both positive and negative values.
