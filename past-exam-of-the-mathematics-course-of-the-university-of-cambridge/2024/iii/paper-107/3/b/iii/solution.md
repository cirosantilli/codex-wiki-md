<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The function $u$ solves the linear nondivergence equation

$$
b^{ij}(x)D_{ij}u=0.
$$

The coefficient matrix is uniformly elliptic, and part (ii) makes it $C^{0,\alpha}$ on every smaller ball. Solve the corresponding linear Dirichlet problem on $B_r\Subset B_1$ with boundary value $u$. Existence gives a $C^{2,\alpha}$ solution, and the [weak maximum principle for elliptic operators](../../../../../../../weak-maximum-principle-for-elliptic-operators.md) gives uniqueness, so that solution is $u$. Hence $u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_1)$.

The local boundedness estimate for uniformly elliptic equations first controls $\|u\|_{C^0(B_r)}$ by $\|u\|_{L^2(B_1)}$. Applying the [interior Schauder estimate](../../../../../../../interior-schauder-estimate.md) and covering $B_\theta$ by finitely many balls gives

$$
\boxed{\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(B_\theta)}
\leq C(n,L,\theta)\|u\|_{L^2(B_1)}.}
$$
