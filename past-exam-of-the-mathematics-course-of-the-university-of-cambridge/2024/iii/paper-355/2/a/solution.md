<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a cylinder of radius $r$ and length $L$, the [mean curvature](../../../../../../mean-curvature.md) is $H=1/(2r)$ and the area is $2\pi rL$. Its [Helfrich membrane energy](../../../../../../thermal-membrane-undulation.md) per unit length is therefore

$$
\frac{E_m}{L}=2\pi r\left(\sigma+\frac{k_c}{2r^2}\right)
=2\pi\sigma r+\frac{\pi k_c}{r}.
$$

The first term favors a narrow tube and the second penalizes its curvature. Setting the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) with respect to $r$ to zero gives

$$
2\pi\sigma-\frac{\pi k_c}{r^2}=0,
\qquad
\boxed{r_0=\sqrt{\frac{k_c}{2\sigma}}}.
$$

The positive second derivative confirms a minimum.
