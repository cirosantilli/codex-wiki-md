<h1 id="1/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

Coplanarity requires both $I=I_d$ and $\Omega=\phi_d$, up to the [orbital-plane orientation degeneracy](../../../../../../orbital-plane-orientation-degeneracy.md). Under that hypothesis the measured planet angle must obey

$$
\tan(\phi-\phi_d)=\cos I_d\tan f.
$$

For a nearly edge-on belt this offset is small except close to the projected crossings where $\cos f=0$. Measurements at several [orbital phases](../../../../../../orbital-phase.md) can therefore test the entire projected ellipse: a systematic offset from the predicted relation implies a nonzero [mutual inclination](../../../../../../mutual-inclination.md). A single projected location generally cannot establish coplanarity because $f$, the front-back branch, and the nodal orientation can be degenerate.
