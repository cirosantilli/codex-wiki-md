<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At the exterior $(p+q):p$ [mean-motion resonance](../../../../../../mean-motion-resonance.md),

$$
\frac n{n_1}=\frac p{p+q}.
$$

Using the [Kepler third law](../../../../../../kepler-s-third-law.md) for $M_*\gg M_1$ gives

$$
\boxed{a=a_1\left(\frac{p+q}{p}\right)^{2/3}}.
$$

The [disturbing function](../../../../../../disturbing-function.md) is a Fourier series in integer combinations of the orbital angles. The [D'Alembert characteristic](../../../../../../d-alembert-characteristic.md) permits the eccentric term

$$
\mathcal R_{\rm res}=C_{p,q}(a)e^q\cos\phi_1+O(e^{q+2}),
\qquad
\phi_1=(p+q)\lambda-p\lambda_1-q\varpi.
$$

Away from resonance, terms with rapidly circulating angles average away. Here, however,

$$
\dot\phi_1=(p+q)n-pn_1-q\dot\varpi\simeq0,
$$

so $\phi_1$ is a slow [resonant argument](../../../../../../resonant-argument.md). Successive [astronomical conjunctions](../../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md) then act coherently, making this term dominate the long-period resonant dynamics even though a $q$th-order resonance has coefficient proportional to $e^q$ at small eccentricity.
