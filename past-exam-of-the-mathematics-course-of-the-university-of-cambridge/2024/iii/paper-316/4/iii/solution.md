<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the 8:5 resonance, $p=5$ and $q=3$. Five planetesimal periods equal eight planetary periods, so the curve closes after

$$
\boxed{5\text{ planetesimal orbits}=8\text{ planetary orbits}}.
$$

Starting at conjunction at [apoapsis](../../../../../../apoapsis.md) gives $\phi_1=q\pi=3\pi\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}$. In the frame rotating with the planet, let $\theta$ be the planetesimal's longitude relative to the fixed planet. At periapsis, $5\theta=\phi_1$ modulo $2\pi$, so the five possible periapsis directions are

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\rm peri}=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{5},\qquad k=0,1,2,3,4}.
$$

At apoapsis, $\phi_1=5\theta+3\pi$, giving

$$
\boxed{\theta_{\rm apo}=\frac{2k\pi}{5},\qquad k=0,1,2,3,4}.
$$

A sketch should therefore show a five-lobed rotating-frame rosette centered on the star, with the fixed planet on the $\theta=0$ ray, five outer turning points separated by $72^\circ$, and five inner turning points halfway between their rays. Successive orbits visit these points in resonance order before the fifth orbit closes the pattern.
