↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At $L_3$, the ordering is $L_3,M_1,O,M_2$. Since $r_1=1+\beta$,

$$
x_{L_3}=-\mu_2-(1+\beta),
\qquad
r_2=2+\beta.
$$

The equation $F=0$ becomes

$$
\mu_1\left[(1+\beta)-(1+\beta)^{-2}\right]
+\mu_2\left[(2+\beta)-(2+\beta)^{-2}\right]=0.
$$

Writing $\epsilon=\mu_2/\mu_1\ll1$ and retaining first order in $\beta$ and $\epsilon$ gives

$$
3\beta+\frac74\epsilon=0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\beta\simeq-\frac7{12}\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1}=\alpha}.
$$

The negative sign says that $L_3$ is slightly less than one binary separation from the dominant mass $M_1$.
