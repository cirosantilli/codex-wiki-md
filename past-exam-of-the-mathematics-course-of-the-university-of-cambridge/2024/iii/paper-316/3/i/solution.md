<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since the binary separation and [mean motion](../../../../../../mean-motion.md) are both one, the [Kepler third law](../../../../../../kepler-s-third-law.md) gives

$$
\mu_1+\mu_2=G(M_1+M_2)=1.
$$

The [center of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) condition puts the bodies at

$$
\mathbf r_{M_1}=(-\mu_2,0),
\qquad
\mathbf r_{M_2}=(\mu_1,0)
$$

in the [rotating reference frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md). Thus the sketch has $M_1$, $O$, and $M_2$ in that order along the $x$ axis, with $P=(x,y)$, and

$$
\boxed{OM_1=\mu_2,\qquad OM_2=\mu_1},
$$



$$
\boxed{M_1P=r_1=\sqrt{(x+\mu_2)^2+y^2}},
$$



$$
\boxed{M_2P=r_2=\sqrt{(x-\mu_1)^2+y^2}}.
$$

In the inertial sketch this entire configuration rotates uniformly about $O$.
