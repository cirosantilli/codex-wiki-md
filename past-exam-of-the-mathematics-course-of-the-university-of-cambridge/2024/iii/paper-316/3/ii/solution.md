<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At a [Lagrange point](../../../../../../lagrange-point.md), the test particle is stationary in the [rotating reference frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md), so $F=G=0$. On the $x$ axis, $y=0$ makes $G=0$ automatically. The singularities at the two masses split the axis into three intervals, and the balance between gravity and [centrifugal acceleration](../../../../../../centrifugal-acceleration.md) gives one root of $F=0$ in each interval. These are the three [Collinear Lagrange points](../../../../../../collinear-lagrange-point.md) $L_1,L_2,L_3$.

For an off-axis equilibrium, $y\ne0$. Put $A_i=1-r_i^{-3}$. Then

$$
\frac Gy=\mu_1A_1+\mu_2A_2=0,
$$

while

$$
F-x\frac Gy=\mu_1\mu_2(A_1-A_2)=0.
$$

Hence $A_1=A_2=0$ and therefore $r_1=r_2=1$. The two intersections of unit circles centered at $M_1$ and $M_2$ form equilateral triangles with the binary, giving the [Triangular Lagrange points](../../../../../../triangular-lagrange-point.md)

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{\mu_1-\mu_2}{2},qquad y=\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}}.
$$

Together with the three collinear points, these are the five equilibria of the [circular restricted three-body problem](../../../../../../circular-restricted-three-body-problem.md).
