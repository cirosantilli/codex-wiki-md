<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let

$$
\Lambda(L)=m^*L\otimes\operatorname{pr}_1^*L^\vee\otimes\operatorname{pr}_2^*L^\vee.
$$

If $L\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$, then its restriction to $X\times\{y\}$ is $T_y^*L\otimes L^\vee=\phi_L(y)$, up to a constant one-dimensional factor, and is therefore trivial. Its restriction to $\{e\}\times X$ is also trivial. The [Seesaw theorem](../../../../../../seesaw-theorem.md) now implies that $\Lambda(L)$ is trivial on $X\times X$.

Conversely, if $\Lambda(L)$ is trivial, restricting it to $X\times\{y\}$ shows that $\phi_L(y)$ is trivial for every $y\in X(k)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{L\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)\iff\Lambda(L)\simeq\mathcal O_{X\times X}.}
$$
