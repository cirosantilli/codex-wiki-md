<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The given velocity is a [homologous spherical flow](../../../../../../homologous-spherical-flow.md),

$$
\mathbf u=q(t)\mathbf r,
\qquad q(t)=\frac{u_0(t)}{R(t)},
$$

with the spatially uniform [velocity divergence](../../../../../../velocity-divergence.md)

$$
\nabla\mathbin\cdot\mathbf u=3q>0.
$$

Every shocked [fluid element](../../../../../../fluid-element.md) therefore expands rather than undergoing continued compression, and the velocity fills the remnant smoothly instead of concentrating its mass in a thin cooling shell. In the absence of radiative losses, the [entropy advection equation](../../../../../../entropy-advection-equation.md) then makes each element follow an [adiabatic process](../../../../../../adiabatic-process.md) after its one entropy-producing passage through the shock. This broad expanding structure is the expected [adiabatic phase of a supernova remnant](../../../../../../adiabatic-phase-of-a-supernova-remnant.md).
