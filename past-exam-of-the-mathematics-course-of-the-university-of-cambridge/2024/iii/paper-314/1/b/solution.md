<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put

$$
q(R)=\frac{4\pi\alpha(R)}c.
$$

For an [axisymmetric vector field](../../../../../../axisymmetric-vector-field.md) depending only on the [cylindrical radius](../../../../../../cylindrical-radius.md) $R$, the [solenoidal vector field](../../../../../../solenoidal-vector-field.md) condition is

$$
\frac1R\frac{d}{dR}(RB_R)=0.
$$

Hence $RB_R$ is constant. The hypothesis $B_R\to0$ on the axis forces

$$
\boxed{B_R=0}.
$$

The azimuthal and axial components of $\nabla\times\mathbf B=q\mathbf B$ are then

$$
-\frac{dB_z}{dR}=qB_\phi,
\qquad
\frac1R\frac{d}{dR}(RB_\phi)=qB_z.
$$

Eliminating $B_\phi=-B_z'/q$ gives the closed [ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../ordinary-differential-equation.md)

$$
\boxed{B_z''+\left(\frac1R-\frac{q'}q\right)B_z'+q^2B_z=0},
\qquad q=\frac{4\pi\alpha}{c}.
$$

Once $B_z$ is known, $B_\phi=-B_z'/q$ and $B_R=0$ determine the full [cylindrical force-free magnetic field](../../../../../../cylindrical-force-free-magnetic-field.md).
