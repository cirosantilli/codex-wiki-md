<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Varying the quadratic action gives

$$
(z^2\mathcal R')'-z^2\nabla^2\mathcal R=0.
$$

For a Fourier mode,

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R_k''+2\frac{z'}z\mathcal R_k'
+k^2\mathcal R_k=0}.
$$

At late times $\epsilon\propto a^{-6}$, so

$$
z=a\sqrt{2\epsilon}\propto a^{-2}\propto\eta^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{z'}z\simeq\frac2\eta}.
$$

The equation becomes $\mathcal R_k''+4\mathcal R_k'/\eta+k^2\mathcal R_k=0$. At $k=0$, its independent solutions are a constant and $\eta^{-3}$. The leading late-time solution in the form given in the question is therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal R_k(\eta)
=A_k\frac{1+ik\eta}{\eta^3}e^{-ik\eta}},
\qquad
\boxed{q=3}.
$$

This is the [growing curvature perturbation in ultra-slow-roll inflation](../../../../../../growing-curvature-perturbation-in-ultra-slow-roll-inflation.md).
