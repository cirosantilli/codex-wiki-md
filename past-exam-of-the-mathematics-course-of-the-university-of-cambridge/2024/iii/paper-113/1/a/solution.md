<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A morphism $f:X\to S$ is a [separated morphism](../../../../../../separated-morphism.md) when its [diagonal morphism](../../../../../../diagonal-morphism.md)

$$
\Delta_{X/S}:X\longrightarrow X\times_SX
$$

is a [closed immersion](../../../../../../closed-immersion.md).

For the requested example, take the [affine plane with doubled origin](../../../../../../affine-plane-with-doubled-origin.md): glue two copies $U,V\cong\mathbb A_k^2$ by the identity on

$$
U\cap V\cong\mathbb A_k^2\setminus\{0\}.
$$

The opens $U$ and $V$ are affine, while their intersection is the [punctured affine plane](../../../../../../punctured-affine-plane.md), which is not affine. Indeed, its regular functions are still $k[x,y]$; if it were affine, the canonical map to $\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y]=\mathbb A_k^2$ would be an isomorphism, contrary to the missing origin. The resulting scheme is not separated: in a separated scheme, the intersection of two affine opens is the inverse image of the closed diagonal inside their affine product and is therefore affine.
