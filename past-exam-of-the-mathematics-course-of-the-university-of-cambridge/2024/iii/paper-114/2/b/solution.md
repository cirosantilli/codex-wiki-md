<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume for contradiction that the [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) $\phi$ is fixed-point-free. The finite group action is then a [covering space action](../../../../../../covering-space-action.md), so the quotient map

$$
q:\mathbb R^n\longrightarrow Y=\mathbb R^n/\langle\phi\rangle
$$

is a double covering and $Y$ is an $n$-manifold.

Apply the [long exact sequence in homology](../../../../../../long-exact-sequence-in-homology.md) to the [transfer chain map of a double covering](../../../../../../transfer-chain-map-of-a-double-covering.md). Since $\mathbb R^n$ is contractible, its positive-dimensional mod-two homology vanishes. The degree-zero portion, together with the fact that $q_*:H_0(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb F_2)\to H_0(Y;\mathbb F_2)$ is an isomorphism, gives

$$
H_1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong H_0(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2.
$$

In every higher degree the same exact sequence gives

$$
H_i(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong H_{i-1}(Y;\mathbb F_2).
$$

**Thus $H_i(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2$ for every $i\geq0$. This contradicts [homology above the dimension of a manifold](../../../../../../homology-above-the-dimension-of-a-manifold.md), which gives $H_i(Y;\mathbb F_2)=0$ for $i>n$. Therefore $\phi$ has a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md).**
