<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define the [Lubin–Tate torsion](../../../../../../lubin-tate-torsion.md) by

$$
\mu_{f,n}=\{x\in\overline{\mathfrak m}:[\pi^n]_f(x)=f^{\circ n}(x)=0\}.
$$

The scalar endomorphisms make this a module over $\mathcal O_K/\pi^n\mathcal O_K$.

The [Newton polygon](../../../../../../newton-polygon.md) or [Weierstrass preparation theorem](../../../../../../weierstrass-preparation-theorem.md) applied to the Lubin–Tate congruences shows that $[\pi^n]_f$ has exactly $q^n$ distinct roots in $\overline{\mathfrak m}$. More precisely, the quotient of the distinguished factors for $[\pi^n]_f$ and $[\pi^{n-1}]_f$ has degree $q^{n-1}(q-1)$, and its roots are precisely the points killed by $\pi^n$ but not by $\pi^{n-1}$.

Choose such a point $\omega_n$. If $a\in\mathcal O_K$, write $a=\pi^ru$ with $u$ a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). Since $[u]_f$ is an automorphism, $[a]_f(\omega_n)=0$ exactly when $r\geq n$. Thus

$$
\mathcal O_K/\pi^n\mathcal O_K\longrightarrow\mu_{f,n},
\qquad
a\longmapsto[a]_f(\omega_n),
$$

is injective. Both sides have $q^n$ elements, so it is an isomorphism of modules. Therefore $\mu_{f,n}$ is a [free module](../../../../../../free-module.md) of rank one.
