<h1 id="4/c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The variance of an estimated hazard increment is large when its group has few individuals in the [risk set](../../../../../../../risk-set.md). The [log-rank weights](../../../../../../../log-rank-weight.md) are near zero when either $r_j^{(0)}$ or $r_j^{(1)}$ is small and are largest when both groups retain substantial information. They therefore suppress noisy late-event comparisons and weight each observed-minus-expected event by its available information. Unit weights would instead give equal influence to unstable increments from depleted risk sets.
