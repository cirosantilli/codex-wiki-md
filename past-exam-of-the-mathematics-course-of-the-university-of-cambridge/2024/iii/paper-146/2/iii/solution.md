<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let

$$
(M,\omega)=(T^2,dx\wedge dy),
\qquad
L=\{y=0\}.
$$

For any sufficiently small nonzero $\delta$, translation

$$
\phi_t(x,y)=(x,y+t\delta)
$$

is a [symplectic isotopy](../../../../../../symplectic-isotopy.md) with $\phi_1(L)\cap L=\varnothing$. Choosing $|\delta|$ arbitrarily small makes the isotopy arbitrarily small in every $C^k$ norm.

This isotopy has nonzero [flux homomorphism](../../../../../../flux-homomorphism.md), represented by $\delta\,dx$. In contrast, every [Hamiltonian isotopy](../../../../../../hamiltonian-isotopy.md) has zero flux. If a Hamiltonian image $L'$ were disjoint from $L$, the two homologous essential circles would bound an annulus $A$, and evaluation of the flux on $[L]$ would equal the signed symplectic area

$$
\int_A\omega,
$$

which is nonzero. This contradicts vanishing Hamiltonian flux, so $L$ is not Hamiltonian displaceable.
