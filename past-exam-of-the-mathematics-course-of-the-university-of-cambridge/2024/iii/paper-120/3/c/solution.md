<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**No such first-order theory exists.** Suppose $T$ axiomatized the Heyting algebras having only finitely many [regular elements](../../../../../../regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra.md). Expand the language by constants $c_n$ and add

$$
\neg\neg c_n=c_n,\qquad c_n\neq c_m\quad(n\neq m).
$$

Every finite subset has a model: take a sufficiently large finite Boolean algebra, in which every element is regular. By the [compactness theorem](../../../../../../compactness-theorem.md), the entire expanded theory has a model. Its reduct is a model of $T$ with infinitely many distinct regular elements, contradicting the proposed axiomatization.
