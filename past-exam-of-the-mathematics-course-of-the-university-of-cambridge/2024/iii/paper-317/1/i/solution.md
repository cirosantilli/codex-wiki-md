<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a family in [stellar homology](../../../../../../stellar-homology.md), dimensionless radial profiles are fixed. [Mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) and [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) then give the central scalings

$$
\rho_c\propto\frac{M}{R^3},
\qquad
\frac{P_c}{R}\sim\frac{GM\rho_c}{R^2},
\qquad
\boxed{P_c\propto\frac{GM\rho_c}{R}\propto\frac{GM^2}{R^4}}.
$$

The [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) equation of state, with fixed [mean molecular weight](../../../../../../mean-molecular-weight.md), consequently gives

$$
\boxed{T_c\propto\frac{P_c}{\rho_c}\propto\frac{M}{R}},
$$

where fixed dimensional constants such as $G$ are suppressed in homology relations.

The [proton–proton chain](../../../../../../proton-proton-chain.md) law $\epsilon\propto\rho T^{7/2}$ gives the nuclear luminosity

$$
L_{\rm nuc}\sim M\epsilon_c
\propto M\rho_cT_c^{7/2}
\propto M^{11/2}R^{-13/2}.
$$

On the other hand, [radiative diffusion in a star](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion-in-a-star.md) gives

$$
L_{\rm rad}\propto\frac{RT_c^4}{\kappa_c\rho_c}.
$$

Using the [Kramers' opacity law](../../../../../../kramers-opacity-law.md) $\kappa_c\propto\rho_cT_c^{-7/2}$,

$$
L_{\rm rad}\propto\frac{RT_c^{15/2}}{\rho_c^2}
\propto M^{11/2}R^{-1/2}.
$$

Thermal equilibrium requires $L_{\rm nuc}=L_{\rm rad}$. Their common mass factor cancels, leaving $R^{-13/2}\propto R^{-1/2}$, and hence

$$
\boxed{R=\text{constant}},
\qquad
\boxed{L\propto M^{11/2}}.
$$

This model captures the gas-pressure support, pp-chain burning, and strongly mass-dependent luminosity of the [lower main sequence](../../../../../../lower-main-sequence.md), including the Sun approximately. Its fully radiative assumption is an idealization: the Sun has a convective envelope, and sufficiently low-mass [red dwarfs](../../../../../../red-dwarf.md) become largely or fully convective, so real radii are not exactly constant.
