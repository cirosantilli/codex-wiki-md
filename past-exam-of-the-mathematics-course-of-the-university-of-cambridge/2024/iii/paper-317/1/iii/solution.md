<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At fixed hydrogen abundance, the ratio of the two rates near the transition scales as

$$
\frac{\epsilon_{\rm CNO}}{\epsilon_{pp}}
\propto X_{\rm CNO}T^{23/2-7/2}
=X_{\rm CNO}T^8.
$$

Reducing $X_{\rm CNO}$ by $256=2^8$ therefore requires twice the temperature to recover equality:

$$
\boxed{T_{\rm tr}'=2(2\times10^7\,\mathrm K)
=4\times10^7\,\mathrm K}.
$$

The metal-poor stars remain on the same pp-chain homology sequence up to this point because the question holds opacity and mean molecular weight fixed. Since that sequence has $T_c\propto M$,

$$
\boxed{M_{\rm tr}'\simeq M_\odot
\frac{4.0\times10^7}{1.5\times10^7}
=\frac83M_\odot}.
$$

The low-CNO pp branch therefore overlaps the solar-composition pp branch and simply continues farther upward and leftward before turning onto a CNO-dominated branch. The CNO branch retains $R\propto M^{4/7}$ and $L\propto M^{73/14}$, but its normalization changes. Since $R^{14}\propto\epsilon_0M^8$, reducing the CNO coefficient by 256 gives, at fixed mass,

$$
\frac{R'}R=256^{-1/14}=2^{-4/7},
\qquad
\frac{L'}L=256^{1/28}=2^{2/7}.
$$

It is consequently somewhat smaller, hotter, and more luminous than the solar-CNO homology model of the same mass. The sketch should show its delayed break at $8M_\odot/3$ and its CNO branch displaced upward and leftward.
