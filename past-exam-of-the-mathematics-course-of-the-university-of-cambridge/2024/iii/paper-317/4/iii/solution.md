<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $g=|\nabla\Psi|$ on the equipotential $S(\Psi)$. The volume between neighboring equipotentials separated by $d\Psi$ is

$$
dV=\int_S\frac{d\Psi}{g}\,dS
=S\langle g^{-1}\rangle d\Psi.
$$

Because density is constant on each equipotential,

$$
dm=\rho,dV.
$$

The [volume-equivalent radius](../../../../../../volume-equivalent-radius.md) is defined by $V=4\pi r'^3/3$, so $dV=4\pi r'^2dr'$. It follows immediately that

$$
\boxed{\frac{dm}{dr'}=4\pi r'^2\rho}.
$$

Hydrostatic balance gives $dP/d\Psi=-\rho$, while

$$
\frac{dm}{d\Psi}=\rho S\langle g^{-1}\rangle.
$$

Consequently

$$
\frac{dP}{dm}=-\frac1{S\langle g^{-1}\rangle}.
$$

Defining the geometric correction

$$
\boxed{f_P=\frac{4\pi r'^4}{GmS\langle g^{-1}\rangle}}
$$

puts this in the one-dimensional stellar-structure form

$$
\boxed{\frac{dP}{dm}=-f_P\frac{Gm}{4\pi r'^4}}.
$$

For a sphere, $S=4\pi r'^2$ and $g=Gm/r'^2$, so $f_P=1$ as required.
