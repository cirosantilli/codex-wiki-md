<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $\mu=\cos\theta$ and $S=j/\kappa$. Integrating the [radiative transfer equation](../../../../../../radiative-transfer-equation.md) over solid angle gives

$$
\frac d{d\tau}\int_{4\pi}\mu I\,d\Omega
=\int_{4\pi}I\,d\Omega-4\pi S.
$$

The first integral is the [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md) $F$. A stationary atmosphere with no local energy source has $dF/d\tau=0$, so

$$
\boxed{4\pi\frac j\kappa
=\int_{4\pi}I\,d\Omega}.
$$

By definition, the [mean intensity](../../../../../../mean-intensity.md) is $J=(4\pi)^{-1}\int I\,d\Omega$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac j\kappa=J},
\qquad
\boxed{4\pi\frac j\kappa=4\pi J},
$$

which is radiative equilibrium: the angle-integrated emission and absorption rates are equal.
