<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because $H\ll R$ and $M_{\rm env}\ll M$, the envelope has nearly constant area $4\pi R^2$ and nearly constant [surface gravity of a star](../../../../../../surface-gravity-of-a-star.md) $g=GM/R^2$. Plane-parallel [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) is

$$
\frac{dP}{dz}=-\rho g.
$$

Since $d\Sigma/dz=-\rho$, one has $dP/d\Sigma=g$. Taking the pressure to vanish with $\Sigma$ at the surface gives

$$
\boxed{P=g\Sigma}.
$$

The spherical stellar energy equation is $dL_r/dr=4\pi r^2\rho\epsilon$. With $r\simeq R$ and $F=L_r/(4\pi R^2)$,

$$
\frac{dF}{dr}=\rho\epsilon.
$$

Division by $d\Sigma/dr=-\rho$ gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{dF}{d\Sigma}=-\epsilon}.
$$

Likewise, [radiative diffusion in a star](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion-in-a-star.md) gives

$$
\frac{dT}{dr}=-\frac{3\kappa\rho L_r}{16\pi ac r^2T^3}
\simeq-\frac{3\kappa\rho F}{4acT^3},
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{dT}{d\Sigma}=\frac{3\kappa F}{4acT^3}}.
$$
