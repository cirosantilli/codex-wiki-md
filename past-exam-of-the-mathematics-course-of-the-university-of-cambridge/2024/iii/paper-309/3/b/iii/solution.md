<h1 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Because $R=O(\epsilon)$ and

$$
f(0)=0,
\qquad f'(0)=1,
\qquad f''(0)=0,
$$

the [Taylor series](../../../../../../../taylor-series.md) gives $f(R)=R+O(\epsilon^3)$ and $f'(R)=1+O(\epsilon^2)$. The extra derivative terms in the f(R) equation are therefore at least second order. At first order the theory reduces to the [Linearized Einstein equations](../../../../../../../linearized-einstein-equations.md)

$$
G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\mathcal T_{\mu\nu}.
$$

In [harmonic coordinates](../../../../../../../harmonic-coordinate.md), equivalently the [Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity](../../../../../../../lorenz-gauge-in-linearized-gravity.md) $\partial_\mu\bar h^\mu{}_\nu=0$, the [trace-reversed metric perturbation](../../../../../../../trace-reversed-metric-perturbation.md)

$$
\bar h_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h
$$

satisfies

$$
\boxed{\partial^\rho\partial_\rho\bar h_{\mu\nu}
=-16\pi\mathcal T_{\mu\nu}},
\qquad
\boxed{\partial_\mu\bar h^\mu{}_\nu=0}.
$$

Thus [Linearized f(R) gravity about Minkowski spacetime](../../../../../../../linearized-f-r-gravity-about-minkowski-spacetime.md) has the same weak-field predictions as general relativity under these assumptions. Differences can first appear through nonlinear curvature terms or in backgrounds about which $f''(R)$ is nonzero.
