<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume late-stage [bicontinuous phase separation](../../../../../../bicontinuous-phase-separation.md) has one characteristic domain size $L(t)$ and statistically self-similar morphology. The [dynamical scaling of binary-fluid coarsening](../../../../../../dynamical-scaling-of-binary-fluid-coarsening.md) then estimates

$$
|\mathbf v|\sim\dot L,
\qquad
|\partial_t\mathbf v|\sim\ddot L,
\qquad
|(\mathbf v\mathbin\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v|
\sim\frac{\dot L^2}{L},
\qquad
|\nabla^2\mathbf v|\sim\frac{\dot L}{L^2}.
$$

The interface has [surface tension](../../../../../../surface-tension.md) $\sigma$. Its curvature pressure is of order $\sigma/L$, so its coarse-grained gradient, equivalently the transverse part of $-\phi\nabla\mu$, scales as $\sigma/L^2$. Diffusive composition transport is neglected because hydrodynamic advection controls the late stage, and $\rho,\eta,\sigma$ are treated as constants.

The pressure cannot simply be discarded: in an [incompressible flow](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) it cancels the longitudinal part of the other forces and contains both capillary and dynamic contributions. Applying the divergence-free projection to the momentum equation eliminates $\nabla P$ while retaining a solenoidal force with the same scaling. Geometry, projection, signs, and correlations are absorbed into dimensionless order-one coefficients. The result is

$$
\boxed{\rho\left(\alpha\ddot L+\beta\frac{\dot L^2}{L}\right)
=\eta\gamma\frac{\dot L}{L^2}
+\sigma\frac{\delta}{L^2}.}
$$
