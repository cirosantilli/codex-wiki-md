<h1 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The primitive ray generators of $\sigma$ are

$$
v_0=(3,-2),\qquad v_3=(0,1),
$$

and $|\det(v_0,v_3)|=3$. They do not form a lattice basis, so the [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../../../../../smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety.md) shows that $U_\sigma$ is singular. The presentation from part (i) gives the same conclusion by the [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md): all derivatives of $B^3-AC$ vanish at $A=B=C=0$.

Insert the primitive rays

$$
v_1=(2,-1),\qquad v_2=(1,0).
$$

Let $\Sigma$ consist of the cones

$$
\operatorname{Cone}(v_0,v_1),\quad
\operatorname{Cone}(v_1,v_2),\quad
\operatorname{Cone}(v_2,v_3)
$$

and all their faces. Since

$$
\det(v_0,v_1)=\det(v_1,v_2)=\det(v_2,v_3)=1,
$$

every cone of $\Sigma$ is smooth. Its support is $\sigma$, so this [fan subdivision](../../../../../../../fan-subdivision.md) induces a proper birational [toric morphism](../../../../../../../toric-morphism.md)

$$
p:X_\Sigma\longrightarrow U_\sigma.
$$

The source is smooth, and $p$ is an isomorphism over the dense [algebraic torus](../../../../../../../algebraic-torus.md); it is therefore a [toric resolution of singularities](../../../../../../../toric-resolution-of-singularities.md).
