<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $\Gamma=\Gamma_1\sqcup\Gamma_2$ is a nontrivial [disjoint union of graphs](../../../../../../disjoint-union-of-graphs.md), no defining relation mixes the two vertex sets, and hence

$$
A_\Gamma\cong A_{\Gamma_1}*A_{\Gamma_2}.
$$

On the topological side, every clique lies in one component, so

$$
S_\Gamma=S_{\Gamma_1}\vee S_{\Gamma_2},
$$

a one-point union of [Salvetti complexes](../../../../../../salvetti-complex.md).

If $\Gamma=\Gamma_1*\Gamma_2$ is a nontrivial [join of graphs](../../../../../../join-graph-theory.md), every generator from the first part commutes with every generator from the second. Therefore

$$
A_\Gamma\cong A_{\Gamma_1}\times A_{\Gamma_2}.
$$

Every clique of the join is the union of a clique in each factor, which gives the cubical identity

$$
\boxed{S_\Gamma\cong S_{\Gamma_1}\times S_{\Gamma_2}.}
$$
