<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A group $G$ is [residually finite](../../../../../../residually-finite-group.md) if, for every $1\ne g\in G$, there are a finite group $Q$ and a homomorphism $\phi:G\to Q$ such that $\phi(g)\ne1$.

Let $F(S)$ be a [free group](../../../../../../free-group.md) and let $1\ne w\in F(S)$ be a reduced word. In the rose with one oriented loop for each $s\in S$, the word $w$ determines a nonclosed reduced path from a chosen vertex in the universal covering tree. Its finite image path can be completed to a finite [covering graph](../../../../../../covering-graph.md) of the rose: for each label, pair the still unmatched incoming and outgoing edge germs, adding finitely many vertices if necessary. The lift of $w$ remains nonclosed in this finite cover.

Let $H\le F(S)$ be the finite-index subgroup represented by this based cover. Then $w\notin H$. The action of $F(S)$ on the finite set of right cosets $H\backslash F(S)$ gives a homomorphism to a finite [symmetric group](../../../../../../symmetric-group.md), and $w$ does not fix the coset $H$. Thus $w$ survives in a finite quotient. Since $w$ was arbitrary, every free group is residually finite.
