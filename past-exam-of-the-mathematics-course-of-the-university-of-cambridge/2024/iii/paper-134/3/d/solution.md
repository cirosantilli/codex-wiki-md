<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Because $X$ is special, the [fundamental group of a special cube complex](../../../../../../fundamental-group-of-a-special-cube-complex.md) embeds in a [Right-angled Artin group](../../../../../../right-angled-artin-group.md). Right-angled Artin groups are residually finite by the [residual finiteness of a right-angled Artin group](../../../../../../residual-finiteness-of-a-right-angled-artin-group.md), and a [subgroup of a residually finite group](../../../../../../subgroup-of-a-residually-finite-group.md) is residually finite. Hence $\pi_1X$ is residually finite.

If $\pi_1X$ were simple, choose $1\ne g\in\pi_1X$. A finite quotient in which $g$ survives has a proper normal kernel. Simplicity would force that kernel to be trivial, embedding $\pi_1X$ into a finite group, contrary to the assumption that $\pi_1X$ is infinite. This is precisely the obstruction that an [infinite residually finite group is not simple](../../../../../../infinite-residually-finite-group-is-not-simple.md).
