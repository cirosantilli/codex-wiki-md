<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define the indoor-to-outdoor [reduced gravity](../../../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md)

$$
g'_V=\frac{g(\rho_1-\rho_H)}{\rho_0}>0.
$$

Because the whole opening lies above the interface, its indoor side contains upper-layer fluid of density $\rho_H$. Under [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md), the pressure difference varies linearly about a [neutral pressure level](../../../../../../neutral-pressure-level.md). Equal opening geometry and equal [discharge coefficients](../../../../../../discharge-coefficient.md) for inflow and outflow put that level at the vent midpoint. At vertical distance $y$ from it, the ideal [Bernoulli equation](../../../../../../bernoulli-equation.md) gives speed $\sqrt{2g'_V|y|}$.

Writing $Q_V$ for either one-way [volumetric flow rate](../../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md), integration over one half of the opening gives

$$
Q_V
=C_dL\int_0^{H'/2}\sqrt{2g'_Vy}\,dy
=\frac{C_d}{3}LH'^{3/2}\sqrt{g'_V}.
$$

The total unsigned exchange is $2Q_V$. The ideal sharp-edged inviscid model has $C_d=1$; an empirical $C_d<1$ represents contraction and losses.
