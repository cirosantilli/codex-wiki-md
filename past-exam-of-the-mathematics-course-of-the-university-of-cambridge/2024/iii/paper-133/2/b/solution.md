<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $g$ be an isometry of the [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) $T$. After subdividing edges if necessary, it has no edge inversion. If $g$ fixes a vertex, it is an [elliptic isometry of a tree](../../../../../../elliptic-isometry-of-a-tree.md). Otherwise choose a vertex $v$ minimizing the positive integer $d(v,gv)$. The geodesic segments $g^n[v,gv]$ concatenate without backtracking: any backtracking would produce a vertex with smaller displacement. Their union over $n\in\mathbb Z$ is therefore a bi-infinite geodesic, the [axis of a tree isometry](../../../../../../axis-of-a-tree-isometry.md), and $g$ translates it by $d(v,gv)$. This proves the elliptic-hyperbolic classification of an [isometry of a tree](../../../../../../isometry-of-a-tree.md).

An element of finite order cannot translate a line through a positive distance, since its powers would have unbounded displacement. Every finite-order element of $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ therefore fixes a vertex of its [Bass-Serre tree](../../../../../../bass-serre-tree.md). Vertex stabilizers are conjugates of $\langle A\rangle$ and $\langle B\rangle$, so the element is conjugate to a power of $A$ or a power of $B$.
