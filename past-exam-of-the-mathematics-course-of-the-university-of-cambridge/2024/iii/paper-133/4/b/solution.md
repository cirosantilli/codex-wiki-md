<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $p$ be the midpoint of a geodesic $[y_1,y_2]$. Since $Y$ is a [convex subset of a geodesic metric space](../../../../../../convex-subset-of-a-geodesic-metric-space.md), $p\in Y$. In the geodesic triangle with vertices $x,y_1,y_2$, the point $p$ lies within $\delta$ of $[x,y_1]$ or $[x,y_2]$. By symmetry suppose $q\in[x,y_1]$ and $d(p,q)\le\delta$. Put $L=d(y_1,y_2)$. Then

$$
d(q,y_1)\ge d(p,y_1)-d(p,q)\ge L/2-\delta,
$$

and consequently

$$
d(x,p)\le d(x,q)+\delta
=d(x,y_1)-d(q,y_1)+\delta
\le d(x,y_1)-L/2+2\delta.
$$

Because $y_1$ is a closest point of $Y$ to $x$ and $p\in Y$, $d(x,y_1)\le d(x,p)$. Combining the inequalities gives $L\le4\delta$. This is the [coarse uniqueness of a closest point in a hyperbolic metric space](../../../../../../coarse-uniqueness-of-a-closest-point-in-a-hyperbolic-metric-space.md).
