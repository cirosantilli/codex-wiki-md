<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $[u,v]$ be one side of the geodesic quadrilateral, and draw a diagonal from $u$ to the opposite vertex. A point $p\in[u,v]$ lies, by $\delta$-thinness of the first [geodesic triangle](../../../../../../geodesic-triangle.md), within $\delta$ either of an adjacent side or of the diagonal. In the latter case, $\delta$-thinness of the second triangle places the nearby point of the diagonal within another $\delta$ of one of the other two sides. The [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) then places $p$ within $2\delta$ of the remaining three sides. The argument applies to every side, proving the [geodesic quadrilateral in a hyperbolic metric space](../../../../../../geodesic-quadrilateral-in-a-hyperbolic-metric-space.md) bound.
