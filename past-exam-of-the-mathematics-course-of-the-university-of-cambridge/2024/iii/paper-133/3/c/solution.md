<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The relations $aka^{-1}k=1$ and $bkb^{-1}k=1$ say that conjugation by either $a$ or $b$ sends $k$ to $k^{-1}$. Thus $K=\langle k\rangle$ is a normal subgroup of order at most $3$. Quotienting by it gives

$$
\Gamma/K
\cong\langle a,b\mid a^4=b^4=(ab)^4=1\rangle,
$$

the orientation-preserving [hyperbolic triangle group](../../../../../../hyperbolic-triangle-group.md) $\Delta(4,4,4)$. This group acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly by isometries on the [hyperbolic plane](../../../../../../hyperbolic-plane.md). The [Milnor–Švarc lemma](../../../../../../milnor-svarc-lemma.md) therefore makes $\Delta(4,4,4)$ quasi-isometric to $H^2$.

The quotient map $\Gamma\to\Gamma/K$ has finite kernel, so part (b), equivalently the [finite-kernel quotient quasi-isometry](../../../../../../finite-kernel-quotient-quasi-isometry.md), makes $\Gamma$ quasi-isometric to $\Delta(4,4,4)$. By transitivity of [quasi-isometry](../../../../../../quasi-isometry.md),

$$
\boxed{\Gamma\simeq_{\mathrm{qi}}H^2.}
$$
