<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Pass to the integral extension

$$
A/(I\cap A)\subseteq B/I.
$$

The ring $B/I$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md), and the ideal $J/I$ has zero contraction to the base. If $J/I$ contained a nonzero element $b$, choose an integral equation for $b$ of least degree:

$$
b^r+a_{r-1}b^{r-1}+\cdots+a_1b+a_0=0.
$$

Its constant term is nonzero, since otherwise the domain property would let us cancel $b$ and obtain an equation of lower degree. But

$$
a_0=-b(b^{r-1}+a_{r-1}b^{r-2}+\cdots+a_1)
$$

is a nonzero element of $(J/I)\cap(A/(I\cap A))$, a contradiction. Therefore $J/I=0$ and $I=J$. This proves [ideal contraction rigidity under an integral extension](../../../../../../ideal-contraction-rigidity-under-an-integral-extension.md).
