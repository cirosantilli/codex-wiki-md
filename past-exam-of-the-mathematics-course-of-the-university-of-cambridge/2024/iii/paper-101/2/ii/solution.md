<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a commutative ring $R$, the [Jacobson radical](../../../../../../jacobson-radical.md) is

$$
J(R)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in\operatorname{MaxSpec}R}\mathfrak m.
$$

Let $A\subseteq B$ be integral. If $\mathfrak n$ is maximal in $B$, then its contraction $\mathfrak n\cap A$ is maximal in $A$. Therefore every $a\in J(A)$ belongs to every $\mathfrak n$, and

$$
J(A)\subseteq J(B)\cap A.
$$

Conversely, the [Lying-over theorem](../../../../../../lying-over-theorem.md) puts a maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $B$ above every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $A$. Hence an element of $J(B)\cap A$ lies in every $\mathfrak m$, proving

$$
\boxed{J(A)=J(B)\cap A}.
$$

This is the [Jacobson radical under an integral extension](../../../../../../jacobson-radical-under-an-integral-extension.md) formula.
