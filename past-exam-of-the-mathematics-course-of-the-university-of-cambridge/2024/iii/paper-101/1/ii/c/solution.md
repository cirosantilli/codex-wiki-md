↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**True.** Fix an isomorphism $\phi:M\otimes_RN\to R^n$, and write the inverse image of the first standard basis vector as

$$
\phi^{-1}(e_1)=\sum_{j=1}^r m_j\otimes n_j.
$$

Define

$$
\psi:N^r\longrightarrow R,
\qquad
(y_j)_j\longmapsto
\operatorname{pr}_1\!\left(\phi\left(\sum_jm_j\otimes y_j\right)\right).
$$

Since $\psi((n_j)_j)=1$, this is a split surjection. Tensor its splitting with $M$. The resulting split surjection has the form

$$
M\otimes_RN^r
\cong(M\otimes_RN)^r
\cong R^{nr}	woheadrightarrow M.
$$

**Thus $M$ is a direct summand of a finite [free module](../../../../../../../free-module.md), so it is a [projective module](../../../../../../../projective-module.md). Symmetry gives the same conclusion for $N$. This is [projectivity of factors of a nonzero finite free tensor product](../../../../../../../projectivity-of-factors-of-a-nonzero-finite-free-tensor-product.md).**
