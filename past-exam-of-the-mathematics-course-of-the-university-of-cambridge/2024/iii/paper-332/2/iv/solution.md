<h1 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The stagnant layer transports [heat](../../../../../../heat.md) by [thermal conduction](../../../../../../thermal-conduction.md), so its upward [heat flux](../../../../../../heat-flux-density.md) is

$$
F_s=\frac{k(T_h-T_s)}h.
$$

At the maximum-flux interface from part iii,

$$
T_h-T_s=(T_m-T_s)-\frac35(T_w-T_m).
$$

Equating $F_s$ to $F_{\max}$ gives

$$
\boxed{
h=\frac1{C_0}
\left(\frac{\operatorname{Ra}_c\nu\kappa}{g\alpha}\right)^{1/3}
\frac{(T_m-T_s)-\frac35(T_w-T_m)}
{(T_w-T_m)^{5/3}}}.
$$

The stagnant layer has positive thickness only if

$$
T_w<T_m+\frac53(T_m-T_s).
$$

For an ice-covered freshwater lake, take $T_s\simeq0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $T_m\simeq4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The largest interior temperature compatible with this [penetrative convection in an ice-covered lake](../../../../../../penetrative-convection-in-an-ice-covered-lake.md) model is therefore

$$
\boxed{T_{w,\max}\simeq4+\frac53(4)
=10.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C}.
$$
