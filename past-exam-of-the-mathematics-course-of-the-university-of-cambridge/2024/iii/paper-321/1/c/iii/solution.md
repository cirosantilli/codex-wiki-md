<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

As $\lambda\to0$, the [exponential function](../../../../../../../exponential-function.md) satisfies

$$
1-e^{\lambda(1-\sqrt x)}
=\lambda(\sqrt x-1)+O(\lambda^2).
$$

Consequently

$$
\nu\Sigma\longrightarrow
\frac{\dot M}{3\pi}(1-x^{-1/2}),
$$

which is the purely viscous result from part (c)(i).

For $\lambda(\sqrt x-1)\gg1$, the exponential is negligible and

$$
\nu\Sigma\sim\frac{\dot M}{3\pi\lambda}x^{-1/2}
=-\frac{\dot M\nu}{2\pi\beta r^{1/2}}.
$$

Since the magnetic prescription is $v_m=\beta r^{-1/2}$, this is precisely $\nu$ times the magnetically driven profile in part (b)(i).

The dimensionless cumulative competition in the exact profile is $\lambda(\sqrt x-1)$. The [surface magnetic torque](../../../../../../../surface-magnetic-torque-on-an-accretion-disk.md) dominates where

$$
\boxed{\lambda(\sqrt x-1)\gg1},
$$

whereas the [viscous torque in an accretion disk](../../../../../../../viscous-torque-in-an-accretion-disk.md) controls the inner transition where this quantity is at most order one. Thus the crossover is approximately

$$
\boxed{r\sim r_{\rm in}(1+\lambda^{-1})^2}.
$$

For $\lambda\ll1$ this is $r/r_{\rm in}\sim\lambda^{-2}$; for $\lambda\gg1$, magnetic transport dominates outside a narrow inner layer of fractional width $O(\lambda^{-1})$.
