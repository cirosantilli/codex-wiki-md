↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Now the mass per unit radius satisfies the nonlinear [scalar conservation law](../../../../../../../scalar-conservation-law.md)

$$
\partial_tM_r+\partial_rq(M_r)=0,
\qquad q(M_r)=M_rv_m(M_r).
$$

Its [characteristic curves](../../../../../../../characteristic-curve.md) have speed

$$
\boxed{\frac{dr}{dt}=q'(M_r)=\frac{d(M_rv_m)}{dM_r}}.
$$

Because $v_m<0$ and $|q'|$ increases with $M_r$, the high central part of a localized pulse travels inward faster than its low-density edges. On the inner, rising side, faster high-density characteristics catch slower low-density ones, causing [characteristic crossing](../../../../../../../characteristic-crossing.md) and an inward-facing [shock wave](../../../../../../../shock-wave.md). On the outer, falling side, the characteristics separate and form a [rarefaction wave](../../../../../../../rarefaction-wave.md). The initial bump therefore develops a sharp inner front and a broad outer tail while moving toward the star.
