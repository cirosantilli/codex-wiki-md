<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Denote the four terms by

$$
I=(\mathbf v_p\mathbin\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v_p,
\qquad
P=-\frac1\rho\nabla p,
\qquad
G=-\nabla\Phi,
\qquad
C=\Omega^2\mathbf R,
$$

so the radial or poloidal momentum equation is $I=P+G+C$.

i) In a thin [Keplerian accretion disk](../../../../../../keplerian-accretion-disk.md), radial inertia and pressure are higher-order in $H/R$, leaving $G+C\simeq0$.

ii) In a nearly static stellar atmosphere, $I=C=0$ and [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) balance gives $P+G=0$.

iii) In pressureless gravitational collapse, rotation and pressure are negligible, so $I=G$; this is free fall.

iv) A [slim accretion disk](../../../../../../slim-accretion-disk.md) retains radial inertia and radial pressure together with gravity and centrifugal support, so all four terms generally survive: $I=P+G+C$.

v) A stationary geometrically thick disk or torus has negligible poloidal inertia but order-one pressure support, giving $P+G+C=0$.

vi) Nonrotating [Bondi accretion](../../../../../../bondi-accretion.md) has $C=0$ and $I=P+G$. In a highly supersonic Bondi--Hoyle limit the pressure term is also negligible, reducing this to ballistic $I=G$.

vii) A sub-Keplerian [advection-dominated accretion flow](../../../../../../advection-dominated-accretion-flow.md) has significant pressure support and radial inflow as well as rotation, so again $I=P+G+C$, with $C$ smaller than the Keplerian value and the remaining inward gravity balanced by $P$ and $I$.
