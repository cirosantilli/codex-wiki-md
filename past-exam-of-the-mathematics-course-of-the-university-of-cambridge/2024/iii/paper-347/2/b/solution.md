<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Shakura--Sunyaev thin disk](../../../../../../shakura-sunyaev-thin-disk.md) model replaces poorly resolved turbulent angular-momentum transport by a stress proportional to pressure,

$$
|T_{R\phi}|=\alpha p,
\qquad 0<\alpha\lesssim1.
$$

Equivalently, an eddy viscosity has $\nu\sim v_{\rm eddy}\ell_{\rm eddy}$. Turbulent motions much faster than the [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) $c_s$ would shock, while eddies much larger than the [disk scale height](../../../../../../disk-scale-height.md) $H$ would not fit within the disk. Writing $v_{\rm eddy}\ell_{\rm eddy}=\alpha c_sH$ therefore gives the [alpha disk](../../../../../../alpha-disk.md) prescription

$$
\boxed{
\nu=\alpha c_sH
\simeq\alpha\frac{c_s^2}{\Omega_K}
},
$$

where vertical hydrostatic balance gives $H\simeq c_s/\Omega_K$. The parameter $\alpha$ summarizes the correlation and efficiency of turbulent or magnetic stresses; it is not a molecular viscosity.
