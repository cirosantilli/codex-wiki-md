<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $\varepsilon=H/R\ll1$. Vertical hydrostatic balance gives $c_s\sim H\Omega_K$, whereas leading radial force balance gives $u_\phi\sim R\Omega_K$. Hence

$$
\frac{u_\phi}{c_s}\sim\frac RH=\varepsilon^{-1}\gg1.
$$

Using part (a) and $\nu=\alpha c_sH$ far from the inner edge,

$$
\frac{|u_R|}{c_s}
\simeq\frac32\frac{\nu}{Rc_s}
=\frac32\alpha\frac HR
=O(\alpha\varepsilon)\ll1.
$$

Thus [subsonic radial drift in a thin disk](../../../../../../subsonic-radial-drift-in-a-thin-disk.md) coexists with highly supersonic orbital motion. Gas follows nearly circular, pressure-coherent orbits and loses angular momentum only slowly, completing of order $(\alpha\varepsilon^2)^{-1}$ revolutions during its viscous inflow.
