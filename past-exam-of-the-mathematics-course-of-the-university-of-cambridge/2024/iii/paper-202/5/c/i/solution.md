↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**False.** For a common sequence, each term of which is a refining deterministic [partition of an interval](../../../../../../../partition-of-an-interval.md), standard Brownian paths have [quadratic variation](../../../../../../../quadratic-variation.md) $t$ almost surely, whereas the paths $t\mapsto B_{2t}$ have quadratic variation $2t$ almost surely. These two path properties define disjoint measurable subsets of $C([0,1])$, so the two laws are [mutually singular measures](../../../../../../../mutually-singular-measures.md). In particular, the law of $B_{2\cdot}$ is not absolutely continuous with respect to [Wiener measure](../../../../../../../wiener-measure.md). This is the [Pathwise quadratic variation distinguishes Brownian speeds](../../../../../../../pathwise-quadratic-variation-distinguishes-brownian-speeds.md) argument.
