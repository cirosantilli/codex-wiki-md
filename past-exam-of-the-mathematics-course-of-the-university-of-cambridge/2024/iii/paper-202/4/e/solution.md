<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For the equation actually produced by the preceding construction, namely the [Bessel process](../../../../../../bessel-process.md) equation of dimension $2-1/\delta$, the drift has [Lipschitz continuity](../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) on every compact subset of $(0,\infty)$. Starting at any positive time and position, [pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md) therefore makes the time-changed process agree until its first hit of zero with the [maximal local solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../maximal-local-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md). For $\delta>1$, its dimension lies in $(1,2)$, so it can hit zero; the time-change construction then supplies further excursions, whereas the maximal local solution on $(0,\infty)$ stops at that first hit.

For $\delta=1$, $X_s=|B_s|$ is [Reflected Brownian motion](../../../../../../reflected-brownian-motion.md); away from zero it agrees with the maximal local solution of $dX=d\widetilde B$. For the dimension-$\delta$ equation printed in the paper, the preceding construction does not agree with the maximal local solution unless $\delta=1$, for the coefficient mismatch established in part (d).
