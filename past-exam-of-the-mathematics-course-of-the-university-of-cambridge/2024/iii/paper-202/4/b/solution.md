<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let

$$
A_t=\int_0^t\delta^2Y_u^{2(\delta-1)/\delta}du=\int_0^t\delta^2|B_u|^{2\delta-2}du.
$$

The clock $A$ is an [absolutely continuous function](../../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) and is strictly increasing: its derivative is positive away from the [Brownian zero set](../../../../../../brownian-zero-set.md), which has zero [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md). It also tends to infinity. This is immediate for $\delta=1$; for $\delta>1$, recurrence and the [Strong Markov property](../../../../../../strong-markov-property.md) imply that the [Brownian occupation time](../../../../../../brownian-occupation-time.md) of, for example, $\{x:1<|x|<2\}$ is unbounded, while the integrand is bounded below there by $\delta^2$.

**Thus $A$ is continuous, strictly increasing, and maps $[0,\infty)$ onto itself. Its [inverse function](../../../../../../inverse-function.md) $\tau_s=A^{-1}(s)$ is finite, continuous, and strictly increasing.**
