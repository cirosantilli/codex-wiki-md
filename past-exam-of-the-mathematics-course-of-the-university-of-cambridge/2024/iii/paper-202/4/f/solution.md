<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Let $Z=\{t:B_t=0\}$. Since $A$ is the clock from part (b) and $\tau=A^{-1}$,

$$
\{s:X_s=0\}=A(Z).
$$

For $\delta>1$, the [absolutely continuous function](../../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md) $A$ has derivative $A'(t)=\delta^2|B_t|^{2\delta-2}=0$ on $Z$. The one-dimensional area bound for an absolutely continuous function therefore gives

$$
\lambda(A(Z))\leq\int_ZA'(t)dt=0.
$$

For $\delta=1$, $A_t=t$ and the conclusion follows directly because the [Brownian zero set](../../../../../../brownian-zero-set.md) has zero [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md). Hence the zero set of $X$ has zero Lebesgue measure almost surely.
