<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose $V=L$. If $x\in L_{\omega_1}$, then $x\in L_\alpha$ for some [countable ordinal](../../../../../../countable-ordinal.md) $\alpha$. The set $L_\alpha$ is transitive and countable, so the [transitive closure](../../../../../../transitive-closure.md) of $x$ lies in a countable set. Thus $x$ is [hereditarily countable](../../../../../../hereditarily-countable-set.md), proving

$$
L_{\omega_1}\subseteq H_{\aleph_1}.
$$

Conversely, let $x\in H_{\aleph_1}$ and choose a sufficiently large $L_\theta$ containing $x$. By the [Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../../downward-lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md), there is a countable elementary substructure $N\prec L_\theta$ that contains every member of $\operatorname{TC}(\{x\})$. The [Mostowski collapse theorem](../../../../../../mostowski-collapse-theorem.md) gives a transitive collapse of $N$, and the [condensation lemma for the constructible universe](../../../../../../condensation-lemma-for-the-constructible-universe.md) identifies it with $L_\beta$ for a countable ordinal $\beta$. Because $N$ contains the transitive closure of $x$ pointwise, the collapse fixes $x$. Thus $x\in L_\beta\subseteq L_{\omega_1}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{L_{\omega_1}=H_{\aleph_1}.}
$$
