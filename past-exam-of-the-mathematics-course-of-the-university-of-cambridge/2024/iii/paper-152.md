## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Let $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ be the primitive lattice generators of the rays of the strongly convex rational cone $\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb R}$. The [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety) says that the [affine toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#affine-toric-variety) $U_\sigma$ is smooth exactly when $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ form part of a $\mathbb Z$-basis of the [cocharacter lattice of an algebraic torus](../../../toric-geometry.md#cocharacter-lattice-of-an-algebraic-torus) $N$. Equivalently, there is a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $N$ such that

$$
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,\ldots,e_r).
$$

In particular, the cone must be a [simplicial polyhedral cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#simplicial-polyhedral-cone) and its ray generators must be primitive; for a full-dimensional cone the criterion says that those generators form a lattice basis.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The [orbit-cone correspondence](../../../toric-geometry.md#orbit-cone-correspondence) is an inclusion-reversing bijection

$$
\{\sigma\in\Sigma\}\longleftrightarrow
\{T_N\text{-orbits in }X_\Sigma\},
\qquad \sigma\longmapsto O(\sigma).
$$

If $n=\operatorname{rank}N$, then

$$
\dim O(\sigma)=n-\dim\sigma,
\qquad
O(\sigma)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(M\cap\sigma^\perp,\mathbb C^\times).
$$

The affine chart and [orbit closure in a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#orbit-closure-in-a-toric-variety) are

$$
U_\sigma=\bigcup_{\tau\preceq\sigma}O(\tau),
\qquad
V(\sigma)=\overline{O(\sigma)}
=\bigcup_{\tau\supseteq\sigma}O(\tau).
$$

In particular, the zero cone corresponds to the dense [algebraic torus](../../../toric-geometry.md#algebraic-torus), while maximal cones correspond to torus-fixed points.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For every $t\in T_N$, the torus action map

$$
x\longmapsto t\cdot x
$$

is an [automorphism](../../../algebra.md#automorphism) of $X_\Sigma$. It induces isomorphisms of [local rings](../../../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring), so it carries smooth points to smooth points and singular points to singular points. The singular locus is consequently invariant under $T_N$. If it contains one point $x$, it contains the entire orbit $T_N\cdot x$; hence the [torus-invariance of the singular locus of a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#torus-invariance-of-the-singular-locus-of-a-toric-variety) proves that $X_\Sigma^{\mathrm{sing}}$ is a union of the orbits in the [orbit-cone correspondence](../../../toric-geometry.md#orbit-cone-correspondence).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

Write $m=ae_1^*+be_2^*\in M$. The inequalities defining the [dual cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#dual-cone) are

$$
\langle m,3e_1-2e_2\rangle=3a-2b\geq0,
\qquad
\langle m,e_2\rangle=b\geq0.
$$

Thus

$$
\sigma^\vee\cap M
=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:b\geq0, 3a\geq2b\}.
$$

Its [Hilbert basis of a rational cone](../../../toric-geometry.md#hilbert-basis-of-a-rational-cone) is

$$
(1,0),\qquad(1,1),\qquad(2,3).
$$

Indeed, after subtracting copies of $(2,3)$ one reduces to $b=0,1,$ or $2$, and the remaining point is generated by $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$. The [coordinate ring of an affine toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#coordinate-ring-of-an-affine-toric-variety) is therefore

$$
\mathbb C[\sigma^\vee\cap M]
=\mathbb C[x,xy,x^2y^3]
\subseteq\mathbb C[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}].
$$

Putting $A=x$, $B=xy$, and $C=x^2y^3$ gives the alternative presentation

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C[U_\sigma]
\cong\mathbb C[A,B,C]/(B^3-AC).}
$$

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

The primitive ray generators of $\sigma$ are

$$
v_0=(3,-2),\qquad v_3=(0,1),
$$

and $|\det(v_0,v_3)|=3$. They do not form a lattice basis, so the [smoothness criterion for a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#smoothness-criterion-for-a-toric-variety) shows that $U_\sigma$ is singular. The presentation from part (i) gives the same conclusion by the [Jacobian criterion](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#jacobian-criterion): all derivatives of $B^3-AC$ vanish at $A=B=C=0$.

Insert the primitive rays

$$
v_1=(2,-1),\qquad v_2=(1,0).
$$

Let $\Sigma$ consist of the cones

$$
\operatorname{Cone}(v_0,v_1),\quad
\operatorname{Cone}(v_1,v_2),\quad
\operatorname{Cone}(v_2,v_3)
$$

and all their faces. Since

$$
\det(v_0,v_1)=\det(v_1,v_2)=\det(v_2,v_3)=1,
$$

every cone of $\Sigma$ is smooth. Its support is $\sigma$, so this [fan subdivision](../../../toric-geometry.md#fan-subdivision) induces a proper birational [toric morphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-morphism)

$$
p:X_\Sigma\longrightarrow U_\sigma.
$$

The source is smooth, and $p$ is an isomorphism over the dense [algebraic torus](../../../toric-geometry.md#algebraic-torus); it is therefore a [toric resolution of singularities](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-resolution-of-singularities).

<h4 id="1/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/d/iii)

Let $D_1$ and $D_2$ be the [toric divisors](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-divisor) corresponding respectively to the primitive ray generators $v_1=(3,-2)$ and $v_2=(0,1)$ of the original cone. The [principal divisor on a toric variety](../../../toric-geometry.md#principal-divisor-on-a-toric-variety) formula applied to the characters $x=\chi^{e_1^*}$ and $y=\chi^{e_2^*}$ gives

$$
\operatorname{div}(x)=3D_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{div}(y)=-2D_1+D_2.
$$

The [toric divisor class sequence](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-divisor-class-sequence) therefore presents the [divisor class group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-class-group) as

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(U_\sigma)
\cong\frac{\mathbb ZD_1\oplus\mathbb ZD_2}
{\langle3D_1,-2D_1+D_2\rangle}
\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
$$

The class of $D_1$ is a generator and $[D_2]=2[D_1]$.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 152](paper-152.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [product fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#product-fan) for $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1$ has rays generated by

$$
e_1,qquad e_2,qquad-e_1
$$

and maximal cones $\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,e_2)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(e_2,-e_1)$. The given fan is obtained by the [star subdivision](../../../toric-geometry.md#star-subdivision) of the first maximal cone through

$$
e_1+e_2.
$$

By the [toric blowup at a torus-fixed point](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-blowup-at-a-torus-fixed-point), this subdivision blows up the torus-fixed point corresponding to $\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,e_2)$. Hence

$$
X_\Sigma\cong\operatorname{Bl}_{p}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1)
$$

for that torus-fixed point $p$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

The [fan of the affine line](../../../toric-geometry.md#fan-of-the-affine-line) consists of the zero cone and $\mathbb R_{\geq0}e'$. Under $\varphi(e_1)=0$ and $\varphi(e_2)=e'$, the four primitive source rays map as

$$
e_1+e_2\mapsto e',\qquad
e_1\mapsto0,\qquad
e_2\mapsto e',\qquad
-e_1\mapsto0.
$$

Every cone of the source fan consequently maps into $\mathbb R_{\geq0}e'$. The defining criterion for a [toric morphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-morphism) is satisfied, so $\varphi$ induces

$$
f:X_\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1.
$$

Under the blowup description from part (i), this is the blowup map followed by the projection $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1\to\mathbb A^1$.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

Before blowing up, the fiber over zero is the smooth curve

$$
F_0=\mathbb P^1\times\{0\},
$$

and the blowup center $p$ lies on it. Its total transform is the union of the [strict transform](../../../complex-geometry.md#strict-transform) $\widetilde F_0$ and the [exceptional divisor](../../../complex-geometry.md#exceptional-divisor) $E$. Blowing up a smooth point of a smooth curve does not change the curve, so $\widetilde F_0\cong\mathbb P^1$, while $E\cong\mathbb P^1$. They meet transversely in the single point recording the tangent direction of $F_0$ at $p$. Therefore

$$
f^{-1}(0)=\widetilde F_0\cup E
$$

is two copies of the [complex projective line](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-line) meeting at one point.

<h4 id="2/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/a/iv)

The inverse image of the dense torus $\mathbb A^1\setminus\{0\}=\mathbb G_m$ is represented by the subfan formed from cones mapping to the zero cone of the target fan. These are

$$
\Sigma_0=\{0,\operatorname{Cone}(e_1),\operatorname{Cone}(-e_1)\}.
$$

This is the [fan of the projective line](../../../toric-geometry.md#fan-of-the-projective-line) in the $e_1$-axis, together with the zero fan in the complementary $e_2$-direction. By the [product fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#product-fan) construction,

$$
X_{\Sigma_0}\cong\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb G_m
=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb C^\times.
$$

It also follows geometrically because the blowup center lies over zero, so the blowup is unchanged over $\mathbb A^1\setminus\{0\}$.

<h4 id="2/a/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#2/a/v)

Part (iii) gives

$$
f^{-1}(0)=\widetilde F_0\cup E,
$$

two [complex projective lines](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-line) meeting at one point. If $x\ne0$, the fiber $\mathbb P^1\times\{x\}$ misses the blowup center. The blowup map is an [isomorphism of algebraic varieties](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#isomorphism-of-algebraic-varieties) away from its center, so

$$
f^{-1}(x)\cong\mathbb P^1
$$

for every nonzero closed point $x\in\mathbb A^1$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

The cone $\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ and all its faces form the standard fan of affine three-space, so

$$
U_\sigma\cong\mathbb A^3.
$$

The complete target fan has primitive rays

$$
f_1,qquad f_2,qquad-f_1-f_2
$$

and every cone generated by a proper subset of them. This is the two-dimensional [simplex fan](../../../toric-geometry.md#simplex-fan), hence

$$
\boxed{X_\Sigma\cong\mathbb P^2.}
$$

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

The images of the three rays of $\sigma$ are

$$
\widehat\varphi(e_1)=f_1,qquad
\widehat\varphi(e_2)=f_2,qquad
\widehat\varphi(e_3)=-f_1-f_2.
$$

No cone of the complete fan of $\mathbb P^2$ contains all three rays, so $\widehat\varphi(\sigma)$ is not contained in one target cone. Thus $\widehat\varphi$ is not a morphism of fans and does not define a regular [toric morphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-morphism) on all of $U_\sigma$.

On the dense [algebraic torus](../../../toric-geometry.md#algebraic-torus), the pullbacks of the two standard target characters are

$$
u\longmapsto x_1/x_3,
\qquad
v\longmapsto x_2/x_3.
$$

Under the standard torus chart $[u:v:1]\in\mathbb P^2$, the resulting [rational map of toric varieties from a lattice homomorphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#rational-map-of-toric-varieties-from-a-lattice-homomorphism) is

$$
f:\mathbb A^3\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^2,
\qquad
(x_1,x_2,x_3)\longmapsto[x_1:x_2:x_3].
$$

Its homogeneous coordinates vanish simultaneously exactly at the origin. Hence its [indeterminacy locus](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#indeterminacy-locus) is $\{0\}$.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

Let

$$
v=e_1+e_2+e_3
$$

and let $\widetilde\Sigma$ be the [star subdivision](../../../toric-geometry.md#star-subdivision) of $\sigma$ through the ray $\mathbb R_{\geq0}v$. Its maximal cones are

$$
\operatorname{Cone}(v,e_2,e_3),\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,v,e_3),\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(e_1,e_2,v).
$$

Since $\widehat\varphi(v)=0$, their images lie respectively in

$$
\operatorname{Cone}(f_2,-f_1-f_2),\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(f_1,-f_1-f_2),\qquad
\operatorname{Cone}(f_1,f_2).
$$

Thus $\widehat\varphi$ is a morphism of fans from $\widetilde\Sigma$ to $\Sigma$.

The [toric blowup at a torus-fixed point](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-blowup-at-a-torus-fixed-point) identifies

$$
X_{\widetilde\Sigma}\cong\operatorname{Bl}_0\mathbb A^3.
$$

More explicitly, the [blowup of affine space at the origin](../../../toric-geometry.md#blowup-of-affine-space-at-the-origin) is the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)$. The induced [toric morphism](../../../toric-geometry.md#toric-morphism)

$$
g:X_{\widetilde\Sigma}\longrightarrow X_\Sigma\cong\mathbb P^2
$$

is its bundle projection: it sends a nonzero vector to its direction $[x_1:x_2:x_3]$ and restricts to the identity on the [exceptional divisor](../../../complex-geometry.md#exceptional-divisor) $\mathbb P^2$. Therefore $g$ resolves the [indeterminacy locus](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#indeterminacy-locus) of $f$.
