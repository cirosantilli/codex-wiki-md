<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [Weyl representation of the gamma matrices](../../../../../../weyl-representation-of-the-gamma-matrices.md),

$$
\gamma^\mu=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^\mu\\\bar\sigma^\mu&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\gamma^5=\begin{pmatrix}-I&0\\0&I\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\psi=\binom{u_L}{u_R}.
$$

A [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) acts as $\psi'(x')=S(\Lambda)\psi(x)$ with $S(\Lambda)=\exp[-\tfrac i4\omega_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}]$. In this basis a boost is block diagonal and gives $u_L\mapsto e^{\boldsymbol\chi\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2}u_L$ and $u_R\mapsto e^{-\boldsymbol\chi\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2}u_R$.

For boosts, the two exponentials cancel in $u_L^\dagger u_R$; for rotations, the unitary rotation and its inverse cancel. Hence this bilinear is a [Lorentz scalar](../../../../../../lorentz-scalar.md). The [Pauli matrices](../../../../../../pauli-matrices.md) obey the identity

$$
i\sigma^2(\sigma^i)^*=-\sigma^i i\sigma^2
$$

shows that $i\sigma^2u_L^*$ acquires the right-handed boost matrix and the usual rotation matrix, so it is right-handed.
