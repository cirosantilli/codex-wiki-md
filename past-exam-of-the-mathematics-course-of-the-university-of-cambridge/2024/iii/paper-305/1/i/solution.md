<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $m=|m|e^{i\theta}$. [Parity](../../../../../../parity.md) reverses the spatial coordinates and must exchange the two chiral [Weyl spinors](../../../../../../weyl-spinor.md). One convenient choice is

$$
\boxed{
P:\quad
\psi_L(t,\mathbf x)\mapsto e^{-i\theta}\psi_R(t,-\mathbf x),
\qquad
\psi_R(t,\mathbf x)\mapsto e^{i\theta}\psi_L(t,-\mathbf x)}.
$$

The identities $\sigma^0=\bar\sigma^0$ and $\sigma^i=-\bar\sigma^i$ exchange the two kinetic terms after $\mathbf x\mapsto-\mathbf x$. The mass bilinear $m\bar\psi_R\psi_L$ maps to its [complex conjugate](../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md) $m^*\bar\psi_L\psi_R$, so the two mass terms are exchanged and the action is invariant. More generally, independent unit phases $a,b$ may multiply the two transformations provided $b^*a=m^*/m$.
