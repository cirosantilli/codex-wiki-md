<h1 id="2/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

For a [damped ferromagnetic spin wave](../../../../../../damped-ferromagnetic-spin-wave.md), shifting both poles into the lower half-plane preserves [causality](../../../../../../causality.md):

$$
G_R(\omega,k)=\frac{\omega_k/s}{(\omega+i\Gamma_k)^2-\omega_k^2}.
$$

Its [spectral function](../../../../../../spectral-function.md) is

$$
\varrho(\omega,k)=
\frac{4(\omega_k/s)\omega\Gamma_k}
{(\omega^2-\omega_k^2-\Gamma_k^2)^2+4\omega^2\Gamma_k^2}.
$$

The classical [Fluctuation-dissipation theorem](../../../../../../fluctuation-dissipation-theorem.md) then gives

$$
S(\omega,k)=
\frac{4T(\omega_k/s)\Gamma_k}
{(\omega^2-\omega_k^2-\Gamma_k^2)^2+4\omega^2\Gamma_k^2}.
$$

Hence the zero-frequency [static structure factor](../../../../../../static-structure-factor.md) is

$$
S(k)=\frac{4T\omega_k\Gamma_k}
{s(\omega_k^2+\Gamma_k^2)^2},
$$

which has the stated proportionality after absorbing the normalization $4/s$.
