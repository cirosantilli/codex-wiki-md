<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Put $q=\delta\theta$, $p=\delta\phi$, so $\theta=\pi/2-q$ and $\phi=p$. The [quadratic Lagrangian](../../../../../../quadratic-lagrangian.md) is obtained by taking

$$
\mathcal L_2=sq\dot p-
\frac{\rho_s}{2}\left[(\nabla q)^2+(\nabla p)^2\right].
$$

The two real fluctuations form a coordinate and its [canonical momentum](../../../../../../canonical-momentum.md) rather than two independent modes. Their [Euler-Lagrange equations](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) combine to give

$$
\ddot q+\left(\frac{\rho_s}{s}\right)^2\nabla^4q=0,
$$

and similarly for $p$. The [ferromagnetic magnon](../../../../../../ferromagnetic-magnon.md) therefore has the quadratic [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md)

$$
\boxed{\omega_k=\frac{\rho_s}{s}k^2.}
$$
