<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Subtract the zero-temperature term by using $\coth(x/2)=1+2/(e^x-1)$. In three dimensions the thermal part is

$$
\langle\theta^2\rangle_T=
\frac1{\chi v}\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{1}{k}\frac1{e^{vk/T}-1}
=\frac{T^2}{12\chi v^3},
$$

where the last integral uses the [Bose-Einstein distribution](../../../../../../bose-einstein-distribution.md). Gaussian phase fluctuations reduce the [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) by $\langle e^{-i\theta}\rangle=e^{-\langle\theta^2\rangle/2}$. Taking symmetry restoration to occur when the thermal variance is of order one gives

$$
T_*\sim\sqrt{12\chi v^3}.
$$

The effective action in part c has $\chi=1/(2\lambda)$, and hence $T_*\sim\sqrt{6v^3/\lambda}$. This is an order-of-magnitude estimate because near restoration the phase-only [effective field theory](../../../../../../effective-field-theory.md) omits large amplitude fluctuations and sensitivity to its [ultraviolet cutoff](../../../../../../ultraviolet-cutoff.md).
