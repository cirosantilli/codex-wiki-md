<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Strictly, the normalized functions form the unit sphere rather than a vector space; let $V=L^2(\mathbb R)$ and restrict to normalized states when interpreting wavefunctions. The group law gives

$$
D(q,r,s)D(q',r',s')\psi(x)
=e^{-i(s+s'+qr')}e^{i(r+r')x}\psi(x-q-q')
=D(gg')\psi(x).
$$

Translation preserves Lebesgue measure and both exponential factors have unit modulus, so $D(g)$ preserves the inner product and is a [unitary representation](../../../../../../unitary-representation.md). This is the [Schrödinger representation of the Heisenberg group](../../../../../../schrodinger-representation-of-the-heisenberg-group.md): $q$ translates position, $r$ translates momentum, and $s$ contributes the physically irrelevant overall phase.
