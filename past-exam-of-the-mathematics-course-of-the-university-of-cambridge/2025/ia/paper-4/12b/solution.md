<h1 id="12b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [12B](../12b.md)

With $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$,

$$
U^\mu=\gamma(c,v),\qquad U_\mu U^\mu=\gamma^2(c^2-v^2)=c^2.
$$

[Differentiation](../../../../../differentiation.md) with respect to [proper time](../../../../../proper-time.md) gives

$$
A^\mu=\left(\frac{\gamma^4v\cdot a}{c},\ \gamma^2a+\frac{\gamma^4(v\cdot a)v}{c^2}\right).
$$

Orthogonality $U\cdot A=0$ implies that in the instantaneous rest frame $A'^\mu=(0,a')$. For collinear motion a Lorentz boost gives

$$
a'=\gamma^3a=\frac{a}{(1-v^2/c^2)^{3/2}}.
$$

For constant positive [proper acceleration](../../../../../proper-acceleration.md) $a'$,

$$
v(t)=\frac{a't}{\sqrt{1+(a't/c)^2}},\qquad
x(t)=\frac{c^2}{a'}\left(\sqrt{1+(a't/c)^2}-1\right).
$$

This hyperbola has future null asymptote $x=ct-c^2/a'$. A right-moving signal emitted from $x_0$ at $t=0$ follows $x=ct+x_0$; if $x_0<-c^2/a'$, it lies beyond that [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) horizon and never intersects the worldline.
