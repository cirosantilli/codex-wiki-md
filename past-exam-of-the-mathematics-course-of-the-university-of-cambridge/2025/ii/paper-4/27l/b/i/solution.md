↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $r=(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{1/2}$, so $f(x,y,z)=r^3$. For $t\geq0$,

$$
f^{-1}([0,t])
=\{(x,y,z):r\leq t^{1/3}\},
$$

whose volume is

$$
\frac{4\pi}{3}\bigl(t^{1/3}\bigr)^3
=\frac{4\pi}{3}t.
$$

Thus the pushforward intensity of $[0,t]$ is

$$
\lambda\,\operatorname{vol}(f^{-1}([0,t]))
=\frac{4\pi\lambda}{3}t.
$$

The pushforward is locally finite and diffuse, so the [radial volume transform of a homogeneous Poisson point process](../../../../../../../radial-volume-transform-of-a-homogeneous-poisson-point-process.md) and the mapping theorem show that

$$
\boxed{f(\Pi)\text{ is a homogeneous Poisson process of rate }
\rho=\frac{4\pi\lambda}{3}}.
$$
