<h1 id="34b/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Part (a) gives

$$
[K_i,K_j]=-ie\hbar\varepsilon_{ijk}B_k.
$$

For

$$
M=\frac i\hbar r\cdot K,
\qquad
N=\frac i\hbar r'\cdot K,
$$

their commutator is the scalar

$$
[M,N]
=\frac{ie}{\hbar}(r\times r')\cdot B.
$$

It therefore commutes with both $M$ and $N$. Applying the stated Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff identity in both orders yields the [magnetic translation algebra](../../../../../../magnetic-translation-algebra.md)

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal T_r\mathcal T_{r'}
=\exp\left[
\frac{ie}{\hbar}(r\times r')\cdot B
\right]
\mathcal T_{r'}\mathcal T_r}.
$$

Writing $\Phi=B\cdot(r\times r')$, the translations commute exactly when their phase is one:

$$
\frac{e\Phi}{\hbar}=2\pi n,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Thus the cell flux must be an integral multiple of the [magnetic flux quantum](../../../../../../magnetic-flux-quantum.md):

$$
\boxed{\Phi=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{e}n=\frac he n}.
$$
