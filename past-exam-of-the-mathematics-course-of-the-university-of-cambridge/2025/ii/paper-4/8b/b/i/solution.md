<h1 id="8b/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $Q=\lambda q$ and $P=\lambda p$,

$$
\{Q,P\}_{q,p}=\lambda^2.
$$

The one-dimensional criterion for a [canonical transformation](../../../../../../../canonical-transformation.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda=1\text{ or }\lambda=-1}.
$$

For either value, the [type-two generating function for a canonical transformation](../../../../../../../type-two-generating-function-for-a-canonical-transformation.md)

$$
\boxed{S(q,P;\lambda)=\lambda qP}
$$

works, because $p=S_q=\lambda P$ and $Q=S_P=\lambda q$; when $\lambda^2=1$, the first equation is equivalent to $P=\lambda p$. For every other $\lambda$, the Poisson bracket is not one and the map is not canonical.
