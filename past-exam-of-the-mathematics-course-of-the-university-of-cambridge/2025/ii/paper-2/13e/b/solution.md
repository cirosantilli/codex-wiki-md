<h1 id="13e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [Papperitz symbol](../../../../../../papperitz-symbol.md) records the three regular singular points of a second-order Fuchsian equation and the two characteristic exponents at each point. Near $z=1$, substituting $w\sim(z-1)^\rho$ into the leading terms gives

$$
\rho(2\rho-1)=0,
$$

so the exponents are $0$ and $1/2$. The same calculation at $z=-1$ gives $0$ and $1/2$. At infinity, $w\sim z^s$ gives

$$
-s^2+n^2=0,
$$

so $s=\pm n$. Since an exponent $\rho$ at infinity denotes behavior $z^{-\rho}$, these are recorded as $-n,n$. Thus the equation has P-symbol

$$
\boxed{
P\left\{\begin{matrix}
1&-1&\infty&\\
0&0&-n&z\\
\tfrac12&\tfrac12&n&
\end{matrix}\right\}.}
$$

The exponent sum is $1$, as required by the [Fuchs relation](../../../../../../fuchs-relation.md) for three singular points.
