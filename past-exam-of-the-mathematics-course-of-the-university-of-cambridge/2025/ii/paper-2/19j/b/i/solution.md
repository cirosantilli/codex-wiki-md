<h1 id="19j/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An invariant bilinear form $B$ defines a linear map

$$
\Phi_B:V\longrightarrow V^*,
\qquad
\Phi_B(v)(w)=B(v,w).
$$

Invariance gives

$$
\Phi_B(gv)(w)=B(gv,w)=B(v,g^{-1}w)
=(g\Phi_B(v))(w),
$$

so $\Phi_B$ is an [$G$-homomorphism](../../../../../../../intertwining-operator.md). Its [kernel](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is a $G$-subrepresentation of [irreducible](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) $V$. Hence either $\Phi_B=0$, so $B=0$, or $\Phi_B$ is [injective](../../../../../../../injective-function.md) and therefore a [linear isomorphism](../../../../../../../linear-isomorphism.md) $V\to V^*$. In the latter case $B$ is a [nondegenerate](../../../../../../../nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md).

If $B$ and $C$ are two nonzero invariant forms, then

$$
\Phi_C^{-1}\Phi_B\in\operatorname{End}_G(V).
$$

By [Schur lemma](../../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md), this endomorphism is scalar, so $B=\lambda C$. Cases involving the zero form are immediate. Thus any two invariant bilinear forms are proportional.
