<h1 id="16h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose first that $M\models T$. By the [soundness theorem for first-order logic](../../../../../../soundness-theorem-for-first-order-logic.md), every theorem of $T$ is true in $M$. A contradiction cannot be true in a structure, so $T$ is consistent.

Conversely, the [Godel completeness theorem](../../../../../../godel-s-completeness-theorem.md) says that every semantically valid consequence is derivable. Its equivalent model-existence form says directly that every consistent first-order theory has a model. Hence

$$
\boxed{T\text{ is consistent}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\text{ has a model}.}
$$
