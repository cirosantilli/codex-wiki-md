<h1 id="1g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $N=3p$, its nontrivial difference-of-squares representation is

$$
3p=\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right)^2-\left(\frac{p-3}{2}\right)^2.
$$

As $p$ is prime, every earlier square difference would give another nontrivial factorization, which is impossible. Thus the first successful value is $r=(p+3)/2$, and

$$
\boxed{k=\frac{p+3}{2}-\lfloor\sqrt{3p}\rfloor.}
$$
