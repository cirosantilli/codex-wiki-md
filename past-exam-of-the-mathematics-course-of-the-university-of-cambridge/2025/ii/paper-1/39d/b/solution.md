<h1 id="39d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Reflect the configuration in the horizontal plane through the cylinder axis. The wall and cylinder geometry are unchanged. Downward translation becomes upward translation, a possible wall-normal migration [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) is unchanged, and the axial angular [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) changes sign because it is an axial [vector](../../../../../../vector.md).

On the other hand, reversing the original [Stokes flow](../../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md) makes all three [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) change sign: translation becomes upward, wall-normal migration reverses, and rotation reverses. The reflected and reversed problems therefore have the same forcing and geometry except that their wall-normal [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) have opposite signs. Uniqueness of [Stokes flow](../../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md) forces that [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) to equal its own negative, so it is zero. The rotational [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) already agree under the two operations, so this argument places no restriction on rotation.
