<h1 id="35b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put

$$
G_k(x-x')=e^{ik|x-x'|}.
$$

Away from $x=x'$, this satisfies $(d^2/dx^2+k^2)G_k=0$. Its first [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) has jump $2ik$, so, distributionally,

$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+k^2\right)G_k(x-x')=2ik\delta(x-x').
$$

Applying $d^2/dx^2+k^2$ to the proposed [integral](../../../../../../integral.md) equation therefore gives

$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+k^2\right)\psi(x)
=\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}V(x)\psi(x).
$$

Since $k^2=2mE/\hbar^2$, this is precisely the stated [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md). The term $e^{ikx}$ is the incident wave and the choice $e^{ik|x-x'|}$ gives outgoing waves on both sides of each source point.
