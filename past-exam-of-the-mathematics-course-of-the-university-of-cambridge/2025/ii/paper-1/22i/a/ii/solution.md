<h1 id="22i/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $1<p<\infty$ and $q=p/(p-1)$. Every $y\in\ell^q$ defines

$$
f_y(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}x_ny_n.
$$

Hölder's inequality gives $\lVert f_y\rVert\leq\lVert y\rVert_q$, and equality follows by using a normalized [sequence](../../../../../../../sequence.md) proportional to $\operatorname{sgn}(y_n)|y_n|^{q-1}$.

Conversely, for $f\in(\ell^p)^*$ put $y_n=f(e_n)$. On the first $N$ coordinates, choose the same norming [vector](../../../../../../../vector.md) to obtain

$$
\left(\sum_{n=1}^N|y_n|^q\right)^{1/q}\leq\lVert f\rVert.
$$

Letting $N\to\infty$ shows $y\in\ell^q$. On finitely supported [sequences](../../../../../../../sequence.md), [linearity](../../../../../../../linearity.md) gives $f=f_y$; density of those [sequences](../../../../../../../sequence.md) in $\ell^p$ extends this identity to all $x$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(\ell^p)^*\cong\ell^q}
$$

isometrically.
