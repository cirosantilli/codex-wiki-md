<h1 id="6a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let the conserved population be $S>0$. At equilibrium $Y'=-\beta YN=0$, so $Y$ and $N$ cannot both be positive. The other equations then force $U=0$. The only equilibria are

$$
E_Y=(S,0,0),\qquad E_N=(0,S,0).
$$

Near $E_Y$, using $(N,U)$ as coordinates gives the linearization

$$
\binom{N'}{U'}=
\begin{pmatrix}(\beta-\alpha)S&\zeta\\ \alpha S&-\zeta\end{pmatrix}
\binom NU.
$$

Its [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is $-\beta S\zeta<0$, so $E_Y$ is a saddle and is unstable. Near $E_N$, coordinates $(Y,U)$ give the triangular linearization with [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-\beta S$ and $-\zeta$. Hence $E_N$ is asymptotically stable.
