<h1 id="23h/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Since $\nu\ll\mu$ and both measures are finite, the Radon--Nikodym theorem supplies a measurable $w:E\to[0,\infty]$ such that

$$
\nu(A)=\int_Aw\,d\mu
$$

for every measurable $A$. Moreover,

$$
\int_Ew\,d\mu=\nu(E)<\infty,
$$

so $w$ is finite $\mu$-almost everywhere and is $\mu$-integrable. The reverse absolute continuity implies $w>0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere: if $w=0$ on $A$, then $\nu(A)=0$, hence $\mu(A)=0$.
