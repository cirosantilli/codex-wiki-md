**No.** Take

$$
X=L^1(\mathbb R^n)\cap L^2(\mathbb R^n),
\qquad
\|f\|_X=\|f\|_1+\|f\|_2.
$$

This is a Banach space, and $X$-convergence implies $L^2$ convergence, which has an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence. Thus the hypotheses hold.

However, let

$$
f(x)=|x|^{-3n/4}1_{\{|x|<1\}}.
$$

The [power singularity integrability](../../../../../../../power-singularity-integrability.md) test gives $f\in L^1$ but $f\notin L^2$. Its truncations $f_m=\min(f,m)$ belong to $X$ and converge to $f$ in $L^1$ by dominated convergence. Hence $(f_m)$ is $L^1$-Cauchy in $X$ but has no $L^1$ limit belonging to $X$. Therefore $X$ need not be complete in the $L^1$ norm.
