<h1 id="10e/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If a prime divided every coefficient of $fg$, reducing modulo that prime would make the product of the two nonzero reduced primitive [polynomials](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) zero in a domain, impossible. Thus a product of primitive [polynomials](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is primitive. Factoring the contents from arbitrary $f,g$ then gives

$$
c(fg)=u,c(f)c(g)
$$

for a unit $u$.
