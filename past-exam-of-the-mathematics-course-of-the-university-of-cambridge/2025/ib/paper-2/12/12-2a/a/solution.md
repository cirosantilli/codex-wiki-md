<h1 id="12/12-2a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

If $f$ had a pole at one of the finitely many candidate points, choose a [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) vanishing to sufficiently high order at all the other candidates. [Hermite interpolation](../../../../../../../hermite-interpolation.md) lets its remaining Taylor coefficients be chosen so that the residue of $p^2f$ at the selected pole is nonzero. A small contour around that pole would then have nonzero [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) by the [residue theorem](../../../../../../../residue-theorem.md), contradicting the hypothesis. Hence none of the candidate points is a pole and $f$ is entire.
