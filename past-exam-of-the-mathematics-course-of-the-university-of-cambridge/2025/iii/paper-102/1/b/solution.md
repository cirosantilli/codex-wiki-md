↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A vector subspace $I\subseteq\mathfrak g$ is an [ideal of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) when $[\mathfrak g,I]\subseteq I$. A [nilpotent Lie algebra](../../../../../../nilpotent-lie-algebra.md) is one whose lower central series

$$
\mathfrak g=\gamma_1(\mathfrak g),\qquad
\gamma_{r+1}(\mathfrak g)=[\mathfrak g,\gamma_r(\mathfrak g)]
$$

eventually vanishes.

By the [Engel theorem](../../../../../../engel-s-theorem.md), the operators $\operatorname{ad}_x$ for a nilpotent complex Lie algebra can be represented simultaneously by strictly upper triangular matrices. Their products are strictly upper triangular and have zero trace. Hence

$$
\boxed{\kappa(x,y)=0\quad\text{for every }x,y\in\mathfrak g}.
$$
