<h1 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At an arbitrary point choose [normal coordinates](../../../../../../../normal-coordinates.md), so $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=0$ there. Torsion freedom and commuting partial derivatives immediately give the algebraic [first Bianchi identity](../../../../../../../first-bianchi-identity.md)

$$
R^a{}_{[bcd]}=0.
$$

Differentiating the coordinate curvature formula at that point and cyclically antisymmetrizing gives the differential identity

$$
\nabla_{[e}R^a{}_{|b|cd]}=0.
$$

Both equations are tensorial, so validity in normal coordinates at every point proves them in every coordinate system.

Contract the differential identity on its first and third curvature indices and use the algebraic symmetries of the Riemann tensor. One obtains the [contracted Bianchi identity](../../../../../../../contracted-bianchi-identity.md)

$$
\boxed{\nabla_aR^a{}_b-\frac12\nabla_bR=0}.
$$
