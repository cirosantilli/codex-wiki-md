<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At secondary eclipse the removed monochromatic planetary flux is $\pi B_\lambda(T_p)R_p^2/d^2$, while the stellar flux is $\pi B_\lambda(T_s)R_s^2/d^2$. The thermal [exoplanet secondary eclipse](../../../../../../exoplanet-secondary-eclipse.md) depth is therefore

$$
\boxed{\delta_{\rm ecl}(\lambda)
=\left(\frac{R_p}{R_s}\right)^2
\frac{B_\lambda(T_p)}{B_\lambda(T_s)}}.
$$

A hot Jupiter has tiny visible thermal contrast, rapidly improving contrast toward the infrared, and molecular absorption or emission features superposed on its continuum. Reflected light may add a visible component. In the [Rayleigh-Jeans law](../../../../../../rayleigh-jeans-law.md) limit, $B_\lambda(T)\propto T/\lambda^4$, so

$$
\boxed{\delta_{\rm ecl}\longrightarrow
\left(\frac{R_p}{R_s}\right)^2\frac{T_p}{T_s}},
$$

a wavelength-independent asymptote for ideal blackbodies.
