<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $c:\mathbb N\to[k]$ be the given [finite coloring](../../../../../../../finite-coloring.md). Color each $t$-element subset $\{y_1,\ldots,y_t\}$ of $\mathbb N$ by

$$
c(y_1^2+\cdots+y_t^2).
$$

By [Ramsey's theorem](../../../../../../../ramsey-s-theorem.md) there is an infinite set $Y$ whose $t$-element subsets all receive the same induced color. Enumerate it increasingly as $x_1<x_2<\cdots$. Then every sum $x_{i_1}^2+\cdots+x_{i_t}^2$ with $i_1<\cdots<i_t$ has that color. The argument works for every positive integer $t$; primality is not needed for this part.
