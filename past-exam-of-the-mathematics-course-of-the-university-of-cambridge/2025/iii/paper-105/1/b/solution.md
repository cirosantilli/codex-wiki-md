<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose a $C^1$ [classical solution](../../../../../../classical-solution.md) existed and set $w=u+iv$. The system is precisely

$$
u_x=v_t,
\qquad
u_t=-v_x,
$$

which are the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) for $w$ as a function of the [complex number](../../../../../../complex-number.md) $z=x+it$. Hence $w$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) near $z=0$. Every holomorphic function is [real analytic](../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md), so its restriction

$$
w(x)=w(0,x)=f(x)+ig(x)
$$

to the real axis is real analytic near zero. Its [real part](../../../../../../real-part.md) and [imaginary part](../../../../../../imaginary-part.md) show that both $f$ and $g$ must be real analytic, contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore no such $C^1$ solution exists.
