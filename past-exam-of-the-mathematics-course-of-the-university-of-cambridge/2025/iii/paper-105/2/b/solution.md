<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $g=Du\in L^p(0,1)$ be the [weak derivative](../../../../../../weak-derivative.md) and define

$$
F(x)=\int_0^xg(s)\,ds.
$$

The [fundamental theorem of calculus for Lebesgue integration](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-lebesgue-integration.md) makes $F$ an [absolutely continuous function](../../../../../../absolutely-continuous-function.md), differentiable [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), with $F'=g$ almost everywhere. The [distributional derivative](../../../../../../distributional-derivative.md) of $u-F$ is zero. A locally integrable function with zero distributional derivative on a connected interval is equal almost everywhere to a constant $C$. Consequently

$$
\widetilde u(x)=C+\int_0^xg(s)\,ds
$$

is an absolutely continuous representative of $u$, and $\widetilde u'=g\in L^p(0,1)$ almost everywhere.
