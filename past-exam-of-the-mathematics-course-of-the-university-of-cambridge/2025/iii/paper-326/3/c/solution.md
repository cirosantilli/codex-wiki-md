<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [normal equation for a linear inverse problem](../../../../../../normal-equation-for-a-linear-inverse-problem.md) is

$$
\boxed{A^*Au=A^*f}.
$$

It has a solution exactly when

$$
f\in\operatorname{ran}A\oplus(\operatorname{ran}A)^\perp
=\operatorname{dom}(A^\dagger).
$$

When solutions exist they form

$$
A^\dagger f+\ker A.
$$

They are unique exactly when $\ker A=\{0\}$, while $A^\dagger f$ is always the unique solution in $(\ker A)^\perp$ and the solution of minimum norm. Here $A^\dagger$ is the [Moore-Penrose inverse](../../../../../../moore-penrose-inverse.md).
