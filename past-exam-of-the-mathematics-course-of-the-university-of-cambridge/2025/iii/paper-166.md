↑ **Parent:** [Paper 166](paper-166.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

For the general result, choose logarithms of nonzero [algebraic numbers](../../../algebra.md#algebraic-number) $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s$ and algebraic coefficients $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_s$, and put

$$
\Lambda=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^s\beta_j\log\alpha_j.
$$

Let

$$
A_j=\max\{H(f_{\alpha_j}),e^{|\log\alpha_j|},10\},
$$

where $f_{\alpha_j}$ is the [minimal polynomial](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) and its $H$ is the [naive polynomial height](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#naive-polynomial-height), and let

$$
B=\max\{H(f_{\beta_0}),\ldots,H(f_{\beta_s}),
\log A_1,\ldots,\log A_s\}.
$$

The [general lower bound for a linear form in logarithms](../../../number-theory.md#general-lower-bound-for-a-linear-form-in-logarithms) states that, if $\Lambda\ne0$, then

$$
|\Lambda|>
\exp\!\left(-C\log A_1\cdots\log A_s\log B\right),
$$

where the effective constant $C$ depends only on $s$ and the degree of the [number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#number-field) generated by all the data.

For the improved homogeneous result, take $b_1,\ldots,b_s\in\mathbb Z$ and

$$
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^sb_j\log\alpha_j.
$$

With the same $A_j$, after ordering the terms set

$$
B^*=\max\left\{
\frac{|b_1|}{\log A_s},\ldots,
\frac{|b_{s-1}|}{\log A_s},|b_s|,10
\right\}.
$$

The [Baker lower bound for a homogeneous linear form in logarithms](../../../number-theory.md#baker-lower-bound-for-a-homogeneous-linear-form-in-logarithms) gives

$$
\Lambda\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\Lambda|>
\exp\!\left(-C\log A_1\cdots\log A_s\log B^*\right).
$$

Both constants are effective. The division by $\log A_s$ in $B^*$ is the improvement that matters when $\alpha_s$ has variable height.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Put $\Lambda=n\log2-m\log3$. It is nonzero by [unique prime factorization](../../../number-theory.md#fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic). If $3^m\leq2^{n-1}$ or $3^m\geq2^{n+1}$, the claimed inequality follows immediately after increasing the effective constant. We may therefore assume

$$
2^{n-1}<3^m<2^{n+1},
$$

which implies $m\asymp n$.

The [Baker lower bound for a homogeneous linear form in logarithms](../../../number-theory.md#baker-lower-bound-for-a-homogeneous-linear-form-in-logarithms), with the fixed algebraic numbers $2$ and $3$, gives

$$
|\Lambda|\geq n^{-C_0}
$$

for an effective absolute constant $C_0$. If $|\Lambda|>1$, the desired conclusion is again immediate. Otherwise, the [mean value theorem](../../../calculus.md#mean-value-theorem) applied to the [exponential function](../../../calculus.md#exponential-function) on $[-1,1]$ gives $|e^u-1|\geq c|u|$. Hence

$$
|2^n-3^m|
=3^m|e^\Lambda-1|
\geq c3^m|\Lambda|
\geq c2^{n-1}n^{-C_0}.
$$

Absorbing the fixed factor into a larger exponent proves $|2^n-3^m|\geq2^n/n^C$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let

$$
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
\qquad
\psi=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}=-\phi^{-1}.
$$

Suppose $F_n=x^k$ is a [perfect power](../../../number-theory.md#perfect-power) with $x\geq2$. The finitely many small $n$ can be absorbed into the final effective constant. The [Binet formula](../../../real-analysis.md#binet-formula) gives $x^k\asymp\phi^n$ and

$$
\left|\frac{\phi^n}{\sqrt5x^k}-1\right|
=\frac{|\psi|^n}{\sqrt5x^k}
\ll\phi^{-2n}.
$$

Thus, for

$$
\Lambda=n\log\phi-\log\sqrt5-k\log x,
$$

the local Lipschitz equivalence of $u$ and $e^u-1$ at zero yields

$$
0<|\Lambda|\ll\phi^{-2n}.
$$

The form cannot vanish: applying the nontrivial [field automorphism](../../../galois-theory.md#field-automorphism) of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ to $\phi^n=\sqrt5x^k$ would give $\psi^n=-\sqrt5x^k$, whose absolute values are incompatible.

Apply the [Baker lower bound for a homogeneous linear form in logarithms](../../../number-theory.md#baker-lower-bound-for-a-homogeneous-linear-form-in-logarithms) with the variable-height number $x$ placed last. The parameters belonging to $\phi$ and $\sqrt5$ are absolute constants, while $\log A_x\asymp\log x$. Moreover,

$$
k\log x=n\log\phi+O(1),
$$

so $n/\log A_x\ll k$ and therefore $B^*\ll k$. The refined lower bound becomes

$$
|\Lambda|>\exp(-C_1\log x\log k)
>\exp\!\left(-C_2\frac nk\log k\right).
$$

Comparison with the exponential upper bound gives $k\leq C_3\log k$. Since $k/\log k$ tends to infinity, this bounds $k$ by an effective absolute constant. Enlarging it to cover the discarded small indices proves the claim.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 166](paper-166.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Let $K$ be a [number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#number-field). Its [places](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#place-of-a-number-field) consist of its real embeddings, conjugate pairs of complex embeddings, and the finite places associated with nonzero [prime ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak p$ of its [ring of integers of a number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ring-of-integers). At a real or complex place use the usual absolute value. If $\mathfrak p$ lies over the [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) $p$ with [ramification index](../../../arithmetic.md#ramification-index) $e_{\mathfrak p}$, normalize its [absolute value](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#non-archimedean-place-of-a-number-field) by

$$
|x|_{\mathfrak p}=p^{-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)/e_{\mathfrak p}}.
$$

These normalizations extend the standard absolute values on $\mathbb Q$.

Write $d_v=[K_v:\mathbb Q_v]$ for the [local degree of a place](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#local-degree-of-a-place). Thus $d_v$ is $1$ at a real place, $2$ at a complex place, and $e_vf_v$ at a finite place. The [Absolute multiplicative Weil height](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#absolute-multiplicative-weil-height) is

$$
H(\alpha)=
\prod_{v\in M_K}\max\{1,|\alpha|_v\}^{d_v/[K:\mathbb Q]}.
$$

The [product formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#product-formula) shows that this is unchanged when $K$ is replaced by a larger number field containing $\alpha$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The [product formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#product-formula) says that every $x\in K^\times$ satisfies

$$
\prod_{v\in M_K}|x|_v^{d_v}=1.
$$

First suppose that $x$ is an [algebraic integer](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#algebraic-integer). Its [principal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#principal-ideal) has the [prime ideal factorization](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#prime-ideal-factorization)

$$
(x)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)}.
$$

Taking the [ideal norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-norm) gives

$$
|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x)|
=\prod_{\mathfrak p}p^{f_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)}
=\prod_{v\text{ finite}}|x|_v^{-d_v}.
$$

On the other hand, the [field norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) is the product over embeddings, so

$$
|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x)|
=\prod_{v\text{ infinite}}|x|_v^{d_v}.
$$

Equating these expressions proves the formula for algebraic integers. Every nonzero element of $K$ is a quotient of two algebraic integers, and multiplicativity completes the proof.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

For every $k\in\mathbb Z$,

$$
H(\alpha^k)=H(\alpha)^{|k|}.
$$

For $k\geq0$, this follows directly from

$$
\max(1,|\alpha^k|_v)=\max(1,|\alpha|_v)^k
$$

at each [place of a number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#place-of-a-number-field). The [product formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#product-formula) gives $H(\alpha^{-1})=H(\alpha)$, because

$$
\max(1,|\alpha|_v^{-1})
=\frac{\max(1,|\alpha|_v)}{|\alpha|_v},
$$

and this handles negative $k$ as well.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

If $\alpha\ne0$ has [degree](../../../algebra.md#field-extension) $d$, then at every Archimedean embedding

$$
H(\alpha)^{-d}\leq|\alpha|\leq H(\alpha)^d.
$$

Indeed, the factor of the defining product belonging to that [Archimedean place](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#archimedean-place) shows $\max(1,|\alpha|)^{1/d}\leq H(\alpha)$; using the exact local degree only improves this estimate. This proves the upper bound. Apply it to $\alpha^{-1}$ and use $H(\alpha^{-1})=H(\alpha)$ to obtain the lower bound. This is the [Liouville height inequality](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#liouville-height-inequality).

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

Write $\mathcal L(R)$ for the [polynomial length](../../../polynomial.md#polynomial-length). The [height bound for a polynomial evaluation](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#height-bound-for-a-polynomial-evaluation) is

$$
H\!\left(\frac{P(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)}
{Q(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)}\right)
\leq
\max\{\mathcal L(P),\mathcal L(Q)\}
\prod_{j=1}^kH(\alpha_j)^{n_j},
$$

provided the denominator is nonzero. At non-Archimedean places the integral coefficients and [ultrametric inequality](../../../arithmetic.md#ultrametric-inequality) give the local estimate without an extra constant; at Archimedean places the [triangle inequality](../../../topological-analysis.md#triangle-inequality) gives the polynomial length. Multiplication over every [place of a number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#place-of-a-number-field) and the [product formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#product-formula) produce the displayed bound.

Taking $P(X,Y)=X+Y$ and $Q=1$ gives

$$
H(\alpha+\beta)\leq2H(\alpha)H(\beta).
$$

Taking $P(X)=X+1$ gives

$$
\boxed{H(\alpha+1)\leq2H(\alpha).}
$$

<h3 id="2/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#2/f)

For coprime integers $p,q$ with $q>0$,

$$
H(p/q)=\max\{|p|,q\}.
$$

This follows either directly from the real and [p-adic absolute values](../../../arithmetic.md#p-adic-absolute-value), or from the [height-Mahler measure formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#height-mahler-measure-formula) applied to the primitive [minimal polynomial](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) $qX-p$.

<h3 id="2/g">g</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/g/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [G](#2/g)

Choose distinct [prime numbers](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) $p,q$ so large that $pq>C(p+q)$, and set

$$
\alpha=\frac1p,
\qquad
\beta=\frac1q.
$$

The fraction $(p+q)/(pq)$ is reduced, because neither $p$ nor $q$ divides $p+q$. The [height of a rational number](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#height-of-a-rational-number) therefore gives

$$
H(\alpha+\beta)=pq,
\qquad
H(\alpha)+H(\beta)=p+q.
$$

The choice of $p,q$ proves the required strict inequality.

<h3 id="2/h">h</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/h/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [H](#2/h)

For an integer $m>1$, let $\alpha_m$ be a root of

$$
f_m(X)=X^2-2mX+m.
$$

Its roots are

$$
R_m=m+\sqrt{m^2-m}>1,
\qquad
r_m=m-\sqrt{m^2-m}\in(0,1).
$$

The polynomial is [irreducible](../../../polynomial.md#irreducible-polynomial) over $\mathbb Q$: its discriminant is $4m(m-1)$, and the product of the coprime consecutive integers $m$ and $m-1$ cannot be a square unless both are squares, which is impossible for consecutive positive squares beyond $0,1$.

The [height-Mahler measure formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#height-mahler-measure-formula) gives

$$
H(\alpha_m)^2=R_m.
$$

Both [algebraic conjugates](../../../algebra.md#conjugate-element-field-theory) of $\alpha_m+1$ exceed $1$, so

$$
H(\alpha_m+1)^2
=(R_m+1)(r_m+1)
=f_m(-1)=3m+1.
$$

Consequently

$$
\frac{H(\alpha_m+1)}{H(\alpha_m)}
=\sqrt{\frac{3m+1}{R_m}}
\longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac32}>1.
$$

Fix, for example, $\delta=1/10$. For all sufficiently large $m$, the ratio is larger than $1+\delta$ by a fixed margin, while $H(\alpha_m)\to\infty$. Hence, for every $C>0$, some sufficiently large $m$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{H(\alpha_m+1)>(1+\delta)H(\alpha_m)+C.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 166](paper-166.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For a [linear form](../../../linear-algebra.md#linear-functional) $L=a_1X_1+\cdots+a_NX_N$ over a [number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#number-field) $K$, define its height to be the [projective height](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#projective-height) of its coefficient vector:

$$
H_p(L)=\prod_{v\in M_K}\max_j|a_j|_v^{d_v/[K:\mathbb Q]}.
$$

The [product formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#product-formula) makes this independent of multiplying $L$ by a nonzero scalar.

The [Siegel lemma](../../../number-theory.md#siegel-s-lemma) says the following. Let $D=[K:\mathbb Q]$, let $L_1,\ldots,L_M$ be linear forms in $N$ variables with $DM<N$, and suppose $H_p(L_i)\leq\mathcal H$ for every $i$, where $\mathcal H\geq1$. Then there is a nonzero $\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z^N$ annihilated by all the forms and satisfying

$$
\boxed{\lVert\mathbf x\rVert_\infty
\leq(N\mathcal H)^{DM/(N-DM)}.}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Write

$$
P(X)=\sum_{j=0}^np_jX^j,
\qquad
Q(X)=\sum_{j=0}^nq_jX^j,
$$

so there are $N=2(n+1)$ unknown integer coefficients. Let $t$ be the number of integers $r$ with

$$
0\leq r<\frac{(2-\kappa)n}{d}-1.
$$

For each such $r$, impose the linear equation over $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$

$$
L_r(\mathbf p,\mathbf q)
=D_r(P+\alpha Q)(\alpha)
=0,
$$

where $D_r$ is the [normalized derivative of a polynomial](../../../number-theory.md#normalized-derivative-of-a-polynomial). The coefficient of $p_j$ is $\binom jr\alpha^{j-r}$ and that of $q_j$ is $\binom jr\alpha^{j-r+1}$. The local definition of [projective height](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#projective-height), together with $\binom jr\leq2^n$, gives

$$
H_p(L_r)\leq C_0^n
$$

for a constant $C_0$ depending only on $\alpha$.

There are $M=t$ forms over the degree-$d$ field $K$, and

$$
dM<(2-\kappa)n-d<N.
$$

Moreover $N-dM\geq\kappa n+2$. Applying [Siegel lemma](../../../number-theory.md#siegel-s-lemma) gives a nonzero integral coefficient vector with

$$
\max\{H(P),H(Q)\}
\leq(NC_0^n)^{dM/(N-dM)}
\leq C_1^n,
$$

because the exponent is bounded in terms of $\kappa$ and every fixed power of $n$ is at most exponential in $n$. These $P,Q$ have all the required vanishing normalized derivatives.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Suppose $P$ and $Q$ were linearly dependent over $\mathbb Q$. Then $P=cQ$ for some $c\in\mathbb Q$, with the zero-polynomial cases included. Since $d\geq3$, $c+\alpha\ne0$, and therefore

$$
P+\alpha Q=(c+\alpha)Q.
$$

If this has multiplicity $t$ at $\alpha$, then the [minimal polynomial](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) of $\alpha$ to the power $t$ divides $Q$ in $\mathbb Q[X]$. Hence $dt\leq\deg Q\leq n$.

The construction in part (b) gives

$$
t>\frac{(2-\kappa)n}{d}-2.
$$

When $\kappa<1$, this exceeds $n/d$ for all sufficiently large $n$ depending only on $\kappa$ and $d$, contradicting $dt\leq n$. Thus $P,Q$ are linearly independent.

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

The determinant in the hint is independent of $Y$ and equals the [Wronskian](../../../differential-equation.md#wronskian)

$$
W(X)=
\begin{vmatrix}
F&F_Y\\
F_X&F_{XY}
\end{vmatrix}
=P(X)Q'(X)-P'(X)Q(X).
$$

It is nonzero because $P,Q$ are linearly independent. Also $\deg W<2n$, and coefficient convolution gives

$$
H(W)\leq2n(n+1)C^{2n}\leq C_0^n
$$

for a constant $C_0$ depending only on $C$.

Fix $y$, and suppose $F(X,y)$ has multiplicity $r$ at $a=p/q$. In

$$
W=F(X,y)Q'-F_X(X,y)Q,
$$

the two terms vanish to orders at least $r$ and $r-1$, so $W$ has multiplicity at least $r-1$ at $a$. The primitive polynomial $(qX-p)^{r-1}$ therefore divides $W$ in $\mathbb Z[X]$ by [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../commutative-algebra.md#gauss-lemma-for-polynomials). Comparing leading coefficients gives

$$
q^{r-1}\leq H(W)\leq C_0^n.
$$

If $r>\delta n+1$, this implies $q^{\delta n}<C_0^n$ and hence $q<C_0^{1/\delta}$. Choosing $q$ larger than this bound proves that $r\leq\delta n+1$, uniformly in $y$.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

It is enough to prove the result for $0<\kappa<1$, since a construction for a smaller positive value gives the weaker vanishing requirement for any larger one. Apply part (b) with $\kappa/2$. For large $n$, part (c) gives linearly independent polynomials $P_0,Q_0$, and

$$
G(X,Y)=P_0(X)+YQ_0(X)
$$

satisfies $H(G)\leq C_1^n$ and has order at least

$$
\frac{(2-\kappa/2)n}{d}-2
$$

at $X=\alpha$ after setting $Y=\alpha$.

Set $\delta=\kappa/(4d)$. By the [rational multiplicity bound for a linear auxiliary polynomial](../../../number-theory.md#rational-multiplicity-bound-for-a-linear-auxiliary-polynomial), once $q_1$ is sufficiently large, the one-variable polynomial $G(X,y)$ has multiplicity at most $\delta n+1$ at $p_1/q_1$. Consequently there is some integer $j\leq\delta n+1$ such that

$$
D_j^XG(p_1/q_1,y)\ne0.
$$

Put $F=D_j^XG$. The [normalized derivative of a polynomial](../../../number-theory.md#normalized-derivative-of-a-polynomial) preserves integral coefficients and multiplies height by at most $2^n$, so $H(F)\leq C_2^n$. Differentiation lowers the vanishing order at $(\alpha,\alpha)$ by at most $j$; for sufficiently large $n$,

$$
\frac{(2-\kappa/2)n}{d}-2-j
\geq\frac{(2-\kappa)n}{d}.
$$

Finally write $F=P-YQ$ by replacing the coefficient of $Y$ by its negative. Then $F(p_1/q_1,y)\ne0$ and all the claimed bounds hold.

<h3 id="3/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#3/f)

If $\alpha$ is nonreal, its positive distance from $\mathbb R$ makes the assertion immediate, so assume $\alpha\in\mathbb R$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and put

$$
\mu=\frac d2+1+\varepsilon.
$$

Choose $\kappa>0$ so small that

$$
\eta:=\frac{(\mu-1)(2-\kappa)}d-1>0.
$$

Suppose for a contradiction that infinitely many reduced fractions satisfy $|\alpha-p/q|<q^{-\mu}$. Their denominators are unbounded.

Choose one such $p_1/q_1$ with $q_1$ arbitrarily large, and then a later one $p_2/q_2$ with $q_2$ arbitrarily large. Choose the integer $n$ so that

$$
q_1^{(2-\kappa)n/d}
\leq q_2
<q_1^{(2-\kappa)(n+1)/d}.
$$

Then $n$ can be made sufficiently large for part (e). Apply it with $y=p_2/q_2$. Since $F$ has integral coefficients, degree at most $n$ in $X$, and degree at most one in $Y$, its nonzero rational value satisfies the denominator bound

$$
|F(p_1/q_1,p_2/q_2)|\geq q_1^{-n}q_2^{-1}.
$$

The supplied upper estimate and the two approximation inequalities give, with $a=(2-\kappa)n/d$,

$$
|F(p_1/q_1,p_2/q_2)|
<C_3^n(q_1^{-\mu a}+q_2^{-\mu})
\leq2C_3^nq_1^{-\mu a}.
$$

Using the upper bound $q_2<q_1^{a+(2-\kappa)/d}$ in the denominator estimate and comparing yields

$$
q_1^{(\mu-1)a-n-(2-\kappa)/d}<2C_3^n.
$$

After taking $n$th roots and letting the choice of $q_2$ make $n$ large, this bounds $q_1^\eta$ by a constant depending only on $\alpha,\kappa$. That contradicts the ability to choose $q_1$ arbitrarily large. Hence only finitely many such rational approximations exist. This is the [Thue-Siegel rational approximation bound](../../../number-theory.md#thue-siegel-rational-approximation-bound).
