<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Write $\mathcal L(R)$ for the [polynomial length](../../../../../../polynomial-length.md). The [height bound for a polynomial evaluation](../../../../../../height-bound-for-a-polynomial-evaluation.md) is

$$
H\!\left(\frac{P(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)}
{Q(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)}\right)
\leq
\max\{\mathcal L(P),\mathcal L(Q)\}
\prod_{j=1}^kH(\alpha_j)^{n_j},
$$

provided the denominator is nonzero. At non-Archimedean places the integral coefficients and [ultrametric inequality](../../../../../../ultrametric-inequality.md) give the local estimate without an extra constant; at Archimedean places the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives the polynomial length. Multiplication over every [place of a number field](../../../../../../place-of-a-number-field.md) and the [product formula](../../../../../../product-formula.md) produce the displayed bound.

Taking $P(X,Y)=X+Y$ and $Q=1$ gives

$$
H(\alpha+\beta)\leq2H(\alpha)H(\beta).
$$

Taking $P(X)=X+1$ gives

$$
\boxed{H(\alpha+1)\leq2H(\alpha).}
$$
