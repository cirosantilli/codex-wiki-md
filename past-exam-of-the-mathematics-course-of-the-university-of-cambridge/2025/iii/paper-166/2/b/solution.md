<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [product formula](../../../../../../product-formula.md) says that every $x\in K^\times$ satisfies

$$
\prod_{v\in M_K}|x|_v^{d_v}=1.
$$

First suppose that $x$ is an [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md). Its [principal ideal](../../../../../../principal-ideal.md) has the [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md)

$$
(x)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)}.
$$

Taking the [ideal norm](../../../../../../ideal-norm.md) gives

$$
|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x)|
=\prod_{\mathfrak p}p^{f_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)}
=\prod_{v\text{ finite}}|x|_v^{-d_v}.
$$

On the other hand, the [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) is the product over embeddings, so

$$
|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x)|
=\prod_{v\text{ infinite}}|x|_v^{d_v}.
$$

Equating these expressions proves the formula for algebraic integers. Every nonzero element of $K$ is a quotient of two algebraic integers, and multiplicativity completes the proof.
