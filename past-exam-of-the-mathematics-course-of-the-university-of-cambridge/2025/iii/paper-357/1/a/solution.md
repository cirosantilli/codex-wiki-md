<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [inverse metric](../../../../../../inverse-metric.md) condition is

$$
(\eta^{\alpha\rho}+k^{\alpha\rho})
(\eta_{\rho\beta}+h_{\rho\beta})
=\delta^\alpha{}_\beta.
$$

Keeping only terms linear in the [metric perturbation](../../../../../../linearized-gravity.md) gives

$$
k^\alpha{}_\beta+h^\alpha{}_\beta=0.
$$

Thus the [linearized inverse metric](../../../../../../linearized-inverse-metric.md) is

$$
\boxed{k^{\alpha\beta}=-h^{\alpha\beta}},
\qquad
\boxed{g^{\alpha\beta}
=\eta^{\alpha\beta}-h^{\alpha\beta}+O(\epsilon^2)},
$$

where indices on $h_{\alpha\beta}$ are raised with the Minkowski metric.
