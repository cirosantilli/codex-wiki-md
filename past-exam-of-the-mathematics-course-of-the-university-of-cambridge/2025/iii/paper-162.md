## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 162](paper-162.md)

<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

At the generic point $\eta_V$ of $V$, the local ring $R=\mathcal O_{W,\eta_V}$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian local domain. Write $r=a/b$ with nonzero $a,b\in R$ and define the [order of vanishing](../../../isolated-singularity.md#order-of-vanishing)

$$
\operatorname{ord}_V(r)=\operatorname{length}_R(R/(a))-\operatorname{length}_R(R/(b)).
$$

This is independent of the representation. The associated principal $k$-cycle is

$$
[\operatorname{div}(r)]=\sum_{\substack{V\subseteq W\\\dim V=k}}\operatorname{ord}_V(r)[V]\in Z_k(X),
$$

where only finitely many terms are nonzero.

The subgroup $\operatorname{Rat}_k(X)\subseteq Z_k(X)$ is generated by these cycles as $W$ ranges over integral $(k+1)$-dimensional subvarieties and $r$ over $R(W)^\times$. Two $k$-cycles are [rationally equivalent](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#rational-equivalence) when their difference lies in this subgroup, and the [Chow group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#chow-group) is

$$
\boxed{A_k(X)=Z_k(X)/\operatorname{Rat}_k(X).}
$$

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

Restriction of cycles from $X$ to $U$ is surjective: every integral subvariety of $U$ is the restriction of its closure in $X$. Its kernel on cycle groups consists exactly of cycles supported on $Z$, hence is the image of $Z_k(Z)\to Z_k(X)$.

This descends to the [Chow group](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#chow-group) level. If a cycle $\alpha$ restricts to zero in $A_k(U)$, express its restriction as a sum of principal divisors on $(k+1)$-dimensional subvarieties of $U$. Taking their closures in $X$ and the same rational functions gives a rationally equivalent cycle whose difference from $\alpha$ is supported on $Z$. Thus $[\alpha]$ lies in the image of $A_k(Z)$. Since principal divisors restrict to principal divisors, the other composite is zero. This proves the [localization sequence for Chow groups](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#localization-sequence-for-chow-groups)

$$
\boxed{A_k(Z)\xrightarrow{j_*}A_k(X)\xrightarrow{i^*}A_k(U)\to0.}
$$

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

Homotopy invariance gives

$$
A_k(\mathbb A^n)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z[\mathbb A^n],&k=n,\\
0,&k\ne n,
\end{cases}
$$

or, in cohomological grading, $A^*(\mathbb A^n)=\mathbb Z$ concentrated in degree zero.

Apply the localization sequence successively to $X_{i-1}\subseteq X_i$. Since the Chow group of each stratum is freely generated in the dimensions of its affine components, induction shows that $A_*(X_i)$ is generated by the closures of the cells appearing up to stage $i$. Hence $A_*(X)$ is generated by the classes $[V_{i,j}]$ of a [cellular decomposition of a scheme](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#cellular-decomposition-of-a-scheme).

The standard cells of $\mathbb P^r$ have one affine space in each dimension $0,\ldots,r$. Consequently

$$
A_k(\mathbb P^r)\cong\mathbb Z
$$

for $0\leq k\leq r$, generated by a linear $\mathbb P^k$, and zero otherwise. Equivalently, the [Chow ring of projective space](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#chow-ring-of-projective-space) is $\mathbb Z[H]/(H^{r+1})$.

<h3 id="1/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/iv)

The [exceptional divisor](../../../complex-geometry.md#exceptional-divisor) is $E\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}$, so its Chow groups are generated by the linear subspaces $[\Gamma_k]$. The complement $X\setminus E$ is isomorphic through the blow-up map to $\mathbb P^r\setminus\{p\}$. The localization sequence for $\{p\}\subseteq\mathbb P^r$ shows that the latter's Chow groups are generated by the restrictions of linear spaces $H_j$ through $p$ for $1\leq j\leq r$; its zero-dimensional Chow group vanishes.

Under the complement isomorphism, $H_j\setminus\{p\}$ corresponds to the open part of the [strict transform](../../../complex-geometry.md#strict-transform) $\widetilde H_j$. A second localization sequence, now for $E\subseteq X$, shows that $A_*(X)$ is generated by the lifts $[\widetilde H_j]$ together with the images $[\Gamma_k]$ from $A_*(E)$, exactly as claimed.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 162](paper-162.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

Let $\zeta=c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E)}(1))$. The [projective bundle formula for Chow groups](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#projective-bundle-formula-for-chow-groups) is the map

$$
\Theta_E:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{e}A_{k-e+i}(X)\longrightarrow A_k(\mathbb P(E)),
\qquad
(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_e)\longmapsto
\sum_{i=0}^{e}\zeta^i\cap p^*\alpha_i.
$$

Flat pullback raises dimension by $e$, and intersection with $\zeta^i$ lowers it by $i$, so every summand has dimension $k$.

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

Compactify the total space of $E$ as $\overline E=\mathbb P(E\oplus\mathcal O_X)$; its hyperplane at infinity is $\mathbb P(E)$ and its open complement is $E$. Under the projective bundle formula for $\overline E$, the pushforward from that hyperplane spans the summands containing positive powers of $\zeta$. The localization quotient therefore retains the zeroth summand

$$
p^*A_k(X)\subseteq A_{k+e+1}(\overline E),
$$

whose restriction to $E$ is precisely the vector-bundle pullback $\pi^*:A_k(X)\to A_{k+e+1}(E)$. It survives injectively. In fact this proves the stronger [homotopy invariance of Chow groups](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#homotopy-invariance-of-chow-groups): $\pi^*$ is an isomorphism.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

From the tautological exact sequence and the Whitney formula in the [Chern class](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#chern-class) formalism,

$$
c(\xi)=\frac{c(p^*E)}{c(\mathcal O(-1))}
=p^*c(E)(1+\zeta+\zeta^2+\cdots).
$$

Thus $c_i(\xi)$ is a sum of terms $\zeta^j p^*c_{i-j}(E)$ with $0\leq j\leq i$. The projective-bundle pushforward satisfies

$$
p_*(\zeta^j\cap p^*\gamma)=0\quad(j<e),
\qquad
p_*(\zeta^e\cap p^*\gamma)=\gamma.
$$

The projection formula now makes every term vanish when $i<e$. For $i=e$, only the term $\zeta^e p^*\alpha$ survives, giving

$$
p_*(c_i(\xi)\cap p^*\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
\alpha,&i=e,\\
0,&i<e.
\end{cases}
$$

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

Write $S(\zeta)=\sum_{j\geq0}\zeta^j$. By the projective bundle formula, it is enough to verify the identity on a basis element $\beta=\zeta^q\cap p^*\alpha$, $0\leq q\leq e$. Expand

$$
p_*(S(\zeta)\cap\beta)
=\sum_{i\geq0}p_*(\zeta^{i+q}\cap p^*\alpha)
$$

and use the pushforward identities obtained from part iii. Multiplication by $c(p^*E)S(\zeta)$ is the inverse triangular operation, because

$$
c(\xi)=c(p^*E)S(\zeta).
$$

It recovers $\zeta^q p^*\alpha$ in dimension $k$. By linearity, every $\beta\in A_k(\mathbb P(E))$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\left\{c(p^*E)\cap\sum_{j\geq0}\zeta^j\cap
p^*p_*\left(\sum_{i\geq0}\zeta^i\cap\beta\right)\right\}_k=\beta.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 162](paper-162.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

The [Euler sequence](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#euler-sequence) on $\mathbb P^m$ is

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}(1)^{\oplus(m+1)}
\longrightarrow T\mathbb P^m\longrightarrow0.
$$

The Whitney product formula for the [Total Chern class](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#total-chern-class) therefore gives

$$
c(T\mathbb P^m)=\frac{(1+H)^{m+1}}1=(1+H)^{m+1}
$$

in $A^*(\mathbb P^m)=\mathbb Z[H]/(H^{m+1})$.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

If $P_X(t)=\chi(X,\mathcal O_X(t))$ is the [Hilbert polynomial](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#hilbert-polynomial) of a pure $k$-dimensional closed subscheme, then

$$
P_X(t)=\frac{\deg X}{k!}t^k+O(t^{k-1}).
$$

Equivalently, if $H=c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, the [degree of a projective scheme](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#degree-of-a-projective-scheme) is

$$
\deg(X\subseteq\mathbb P^m)=\int_{\mathbb P^m}H^k\cap[X]=\int_XH^k.
$$

A generic complementary linear subspace meets $X$ in a nonempty zero-dimensional scheme whose length is this number. It is therefore a positive integer.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Let $N=N_{X/\mathbb P^m}$ be the [normal bundle](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#normal-bundle), of rank $m-n$. Since $TX$ is trivial, its exact sequence and the Euler-sequence calculation give

$$
c(N)=c(T\mathbb P^m|_X)=(1+H)^{m+1}.
$$

If $m<2n$, put $i=m-n+1$, so $1\leq i\leq n$. Since $i$ exceeds the rank of $N$, $c_i(N)=0$, yet the formula gives

$$
0=c_i(N)=\binom{m+1}{i}H^i.
$$

Intersecting with $H^{n-i}$ and integrating over $X$ gives

$$
0=\binom{m+1}{i}\deg X,
$$

contradicting positivity of the degree. Hence $m\geq2n$.

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

Now $m=2n$ and $N$ has rank $n$. Write $d=\deg X$, so the pushforward of the fundamental class is

$$
i_*[X]=dH^n\in A_n(\mathbb P^{2n}).
$$

The [self-intersection formula](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#self-intersection-formula) and the calculation of $c(N)$ give

$$
i^*i_*[X]=c_n(N)\cap[X]
=\binom{2n+1}{n}H^n\cap[X].
$$

On the other hand, pulling back $dH^n$ makes the left side $dH^n\cap[X]$. Integrating over $X$ gives

$$
d^2=\binom{2n+1}{n}d.
$$

Since $d>0$, cancellation yields

$$
\boxed{\deg(X\subseteq\mathbb P^{2n})=\binom{2n+1}{n}.}
$$
