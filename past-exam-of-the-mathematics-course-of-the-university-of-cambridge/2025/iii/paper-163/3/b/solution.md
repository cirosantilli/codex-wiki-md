<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $t_j=j/3$, the three central radial directions are proportional to $(1,t_j,t_j^2)$. Their determinant is the nonzero Vandermonde product

$$
(t_2-t_1)(t_3-t_1)(t_3-t_2).
$$

Choose $c>0$ small enough that every triple $\omega_j\in\Sigma_j$ still has determinant bounded away from zero. An invertible linear transformation sends the three central directions to the standard basis; it sends the given tubes to comparable tubes with directions in fixed small neighborhoods of $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and distorts volume and dimensions by fixed factors.

Applying part a after this transformation gives, uniformly in the three families,

$$
\left\|\prod_{j=1}^3
\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_j}\chi_T\right)^{1/3}
\right\|_{3/2}
\lesssim_\epsilon R^{1+\epsilon}
\prod_{j=1}^3|\mathcal T_j|^{1/3}.
$$

This is the required moment-curve version; its uniformity is precisely the transversality supplied by the [moment curve](../../../../../../moment-curve.md) and the Vandermonde determinant.
