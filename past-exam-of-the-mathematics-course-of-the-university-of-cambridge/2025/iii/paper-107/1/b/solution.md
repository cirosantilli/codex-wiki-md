<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose $u_1$ and $u_2$ are [weak solutions](../../../../../../weak-solution.md) with the same [trace](../../../../../../sobolev-trace-theorem.md), and put $w=u_1-u_2\in H_0^1(\Omega)$. The [weak formulation](../../../../../../weak-formulation.md) permits $w$ itself as a [test function](../../../../../../test-function.md), giving

$$
0=\int_\Omega Dw\mathbin\cdot Dw=\int_\Omega|Dw|^2.
$$

Thus $w$ is [almost everywhere](../../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) constant, and its zero trace makes that constant zero. This proves uniqueness.

The weak identity also says that $\Delta u=0$ in the sense of [distributions](../../../../../../distribution-theory-split.md). The [Weyl lemma](../../../../../../weyl-lemma.md) therefore gives $u\in C^\infty(\Omega)$ and $\Delta u=0$ pointwise. The assumed [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) on $\overline\Omega$ retains the prescribed boundary values, so the weak solution is the unique classical solution in $C^\infty(\Omega)\cap C^0(\overline\Omega)$.
