<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Suppose $v_k\to v$ in $C^{1,\beta}$ and write $u_k=T(v_k)$. The preceding uniform [Schauder estimate](../../../../../../schauder-estimates.md) bounds $(u_k)$ in $C^{2,\alpha\beta}$. Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence converging in $C^{1,\beta}$ by the [compact embedding of Hölder spaces](../../../../../../compact-embedding-of-holder-spaces.md). The composed coefficients converge uniformly, and lower-exponent Hölder compactness provides enough convergence of the second derivatives to pass to the linear equation. Every subsequential limit solves the problem defining $T(v)$.

Uniqueness of that linear [Dirichlet problem](../../../../../../dirichlet-problem.md) forces every such limit to equal $T(v)$. Since every subsequence has a further subsequence with this same limit, the whole sequence converges to $T(v)$ in $C^{1,\beta}$. Thus $T$ is continuous.
