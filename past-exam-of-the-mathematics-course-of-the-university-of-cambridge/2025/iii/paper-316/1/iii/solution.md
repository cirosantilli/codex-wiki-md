<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For equal [mass density](../../../../../../density.md), the [specific impact energy](../../../../../../specific-impact-energy.md) is

$$
Q=\frac{\tfrac12m_{\rm im}v_{\rm rel}^2}{m}
=\frac12\left(\frac{D_{\rm im}}D\right)^3v_{\rm rel}^2.
$$

Thus the smallest catastrophic impactor has $D_{\rm im}=X_cD$, where

$$
\boxed{X_c=\left(\frac{2Q_D^*}{v_{\rm rel}^2}\right)^{1/3}}.
$$

Integrating the collision rate gives

$$
R_{\rm cc}(D)=A\left[
\frac{D_{\rm im}^{3-\alpha}}{3-\alpha}
+\frac{2D D_{\rm im}^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}
+\frac{D^2D_{\rm im}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
\right]_{X_cD}^{D_{\max}}.
$$

For $X_c\ll1$, the last term is the dominant lower-limit contribution, so the [catastrophic planetesimal collision rate](../../../../../../catastrophic-planetesimal-collision-rate.md) is

$$
\boxed{R_{\rm cc}(D)\simeq\frac{A}{\alpha-1}X_c^{1-\alpha}D^{3-\alpha}}.
$$
