<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $k=\mu(1-\beta)$. Taking the [cross product](../../../../../../cross-product.md) of the equation of motion with $\mathbf r$ gives

$$
\frac d{dt}(\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r})=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf h=\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}}.
$$

Since $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf h=0$ at every time, the orbit lies in the fixed plane perpendicular to the conserved [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) $\mathbf h$. Taking the [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md) with $\dot{\mathbf r}$ gives the conserved [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md)

$$
\boxed{C=\frac12|\dot{\mathbf r}|^2-\frac{\mu(1-\beta)}r}.
$$

For $\beta>1$, the effective inverse-square force is repulsive and the potential term is positive.
