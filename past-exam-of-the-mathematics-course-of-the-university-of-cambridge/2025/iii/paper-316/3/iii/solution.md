<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Insert the planets' two [secular eigenmodes](../../../../../../secular-eigenmode.md) and define their forcing strengths

$$
\nu_k=\sum_{j=1}^2A_je_{jk}.
$$

Solving the first-order [linear ordinary differential equation](../../../../../../linear-ordinary-differential-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{z(t)=e_{\rm free}e^{i(At+\beta_{\rm free})}
+\sum_{k=1}^2\frac{\nu_k}{g_k-A}e^{i(g_kt+\beta_k)}}.
$$

The first term is the particle's freely precessing eccentricity. The remaining terms are its [forced eccentricity](../../../../../../forced-eccentricity.md), phase-locked to the planets' modes. In the complex plane, their vector sum makes the eccentricity and longitude of periapsis oscillate. A denominator becomes small at a [secular resonance](../../../../../../secular-resonance.md) $A=g_k$; the nonresonant formula then ceases to be uniform.
