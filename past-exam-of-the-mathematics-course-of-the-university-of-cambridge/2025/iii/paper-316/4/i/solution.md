<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Treat the just-bound orbit as a [parabolic Kepler orbit](../../../../../../parabolic-trajectory.md) with $q\simeq a_p$. Its specific angular momentum is $h=\sqrt{2GM_*q}$, so its instantaneous angular speed is $h/r^2$. Equating this to the planet's mean motion $n_p=\sqrt{GM_*/a_p^3}$ gives

$$
\boxed{r=(2qa_p^3)^{1/4}\simeq2^{1/4}a_p}.
$$

The parabolic orbit equation $r=2q/(1+\cos f)$ then gives $f\simeq47^\circ$, or $50^\circ$ to the nearest ten degrees.

If the bodies were in conjunction at periapsis, [Barker equation](../../../../../../barker-equation.md) gives

$$
n_pt=\sqrt2\left[\tan\frac f2+\frac13\tan^3\frac f2\right]
\simeq37^\circ.
$$

The planetesimal is therefore at its maximum lead, approximately

$$
\boxed{f-n_pt\simeq10^\circ}
$$

ahead of the planet as seen from the star.
