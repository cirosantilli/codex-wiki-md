<h1 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose $x\notin\mathcal O_K$, so $v(x)<0$. The [ultrametric inequality](../../../../../../../ultrametric-inequality.md) gives $v(1+x)=v(x)$. If $1+x=y^n$, then

$$
n,v(y)=v(x).
$$

For a [discrete valuation](../../../../../../../discrete-valuation.md), only finitely many positive integers $n$ divide the fixed nonzero integer $v(x)$. Therefore $x$ cannot belong to $S$, proving $S\subseteq\mathcal O_K$.
