<h1 id="4/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

One direction follows by restriction. Conversely, suppose $|\cdot|$ is a [Non-Archimedean absolute value](../../../../../../../non-archimedean-absolute-value.md). Then $|m|_L=|m|\leq1$ for every integer $m$. For $x,y\in L$, the binomial theorem and the ordinary triangle inequality give

$$
|x+y|_L^n
\leq\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj|x|_L^j|y|_L^{n-j}
\leq(n+1)\max(|x|_L,|y|_L)^n.
$$

Taking $n$th roots and letting $n\to\infty$ yields the [ultrametric inequality](../../../../../../../ultrametric-inequality.md) for $|\cdot|_L$. Thus an [extension of an absolute value](../../../../../../../extension-of-an-absolute-value.md) is non-Archimedean exactly when its restriction is.
