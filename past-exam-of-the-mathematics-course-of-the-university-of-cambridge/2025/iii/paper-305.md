## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 305](paper-305.md)

<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

Use [unitary gauge](../../../standard-model.md#unitary-gauge) and write $\phi=v+h/\sqrt2$. The quadratic Lagrangian contains

$$
\frac12(\partial h)^2-\lambda v^2h^2+e^2v^2A_\mu A^\mu.
$$

Thus the radial Higgs excitation is a spin-zero particle with $m_h^2=2\lambda v^2$, while the gauge boson is a massive spin-one particle with $m_A^2=2e^2v^2$. The phase of $\phi$ is the [Goldstone boson](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#goldstone-boson) eaten by the [Higgs mechanism](../../../standard-model.md#higgs-mechanism) to supply the longitudinal polarization of $A_\mu$.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

Varying $A_\nu$ gives the [Maxwell equations](../../../electromagnetism.md#maxwell-equations)

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=J^\nu,
\qquad
J^\nu=ie\bigl[(D^\nu\phi)^\dagger\phi-\phi^\dagger D^\nu\phi\bigr].
$$

On the vacuum manifold, choose unitary gauge so that $\phi=v$. Then $\mathbf J=-2e^2v^2\mathbf A$ and the [London equation](../../../electromagnetism.md#london-equations) is

$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf J=-2e^2v^2\mathbf B.
$$

For a static configuration, $\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mathbf J$ and $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$, so

$$
\nabla^2\mathbf B=2e^2v^2\mathbf B=\frac1{\xi^2}\mathbf B,
\qquad
\boxed{\xi=\frac1{\sqrt2ev}=\frac1{m_A}}.
$$

The magnetic field therefore decays exponentially inside the condensate. This is the [Meissner effect](../../../electromagnetism.md#meissner-effect), with penetration depth equal to the inverse gauge-boson mass.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

Writing $\Phi=(\phi_1,\phi_2)^T$, the scalar sector has a global $SU(2)$ flavour symmetry in addition to the gauged common $U(1)$ phase. A vacuum can be chosen as $\Phi_0=(0,v)^T$. A diagonal combination of the gauge phase and the $SU(2)$ rotation generated by $\sigma_3$ leaves it invariant.

The radial scalar has $m_h^2=2\lambda v^2$, the gauge boson has $m_A^2=2e^2v^2$, and two real scalars remain massless. The four real scalar directions consist of one radial mode and three angular modes; one angular mode is eaten, leaving the two [Goldstone bosons](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#goldstone-boson) expected from the two broken physical global generators. The vacuum set before quotienting is $S^3$, and the manifold of gauge-inequivalent vacua is the [complex projective line](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-line)

$$
\boxed{S^3/U(1)=\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2.}
$$

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 305](paper-305.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

For $q=(u,d)^T$, massless two-flavour [Quantum chromodynamics](../../../standard-model.md#quantum-chromodynamics) has

$$
\mathcal L=-\frac12\operatorname{tr}(G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu})
+i\bar q\not Dq.
$$

Classically its continuous global symmetry is $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_V\times U(1)_A$, up to finite central quotients. The [Adler-Bell-Jackiw anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#chiral-anomaly) destroys the continuous axial $U(1)_A$ in the quantum theory, leaving $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times U(1)_V$, together with an anomaly-preserved discrete axial subgroup.

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The condensate transforms as $\langle\bar q_Lq_R\rangle\mapsto L\langle\bar q_Lq_R\rangle R^\dagger$, so its value proportional to the identity produces [chiral symmetry breaking](../../../standard-model.md#chiral-symmetry-breaking)

$$
SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\longrightarrow SU(2)_V.
$$

The vacuum manifold is the coset $(SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R)/SU(2)_V\cong SU(2)$ and has three Goldstone modes. A field $U(x)\in SU(2)$ transforms as $U\mapsto LUR^\dagger$. Lorentz invariance and chiral symmetry leave, at two-derivative order, one invariant metric on this coset, so

$$
\mathcal L_\pi=\frac{f_\pi^2}{4}\operatorname{tr}(\partial_\mu U^\dagger\partial^\mu U).
$$

Since the Lagrangian density has dimension four and $U$ is dimensionless, the [pion decay constant](../../../standard-model.md#pion-decay-constant) $f_\pi$ has mass dimension one.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

For $U=e^{2i\pi/f_\pi}$, the [Maurer-Cartan form](../../../lie-theory.md#maurer-cartan-form) expands as

$$
U^\dagger\partial_\mu U
=\frac{2i}{f_\pi}\partial_\mu\pi
+\frac12\left[\frac{-2i\pi}{f_\pi},\frac{2i\partial_\mu\pi}{f_\pi}\right]+\cdots.
$$

Substituting, using cyclicity of the trace, and collecting even powers gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_\pi=operatorname{tr}(\partial_\mu\pi)^2
-\frac{2}{3f_\pi^2}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\pi^2(\partial_\mu\pi)^2-(\pi\partial_\mu\pi)^2\right)+O(\pi^6)}.
$$

Odd terms cancel because the sigma-model metric is invariant under $U\leftrightarrow U^\dagger$.

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

Electromagnetism gauges the vector flavour transformation $U\mapsto e^{ie\alpha Q}Ue^{-ie\alpha Q}$, so covariance requires $D_\mu U=\partial_\mu U-ieA_\mu[Q,U]$. The identity part of $Q=I/6+\sigma_3/2$ drops out of the commutator. Writing

$$
\pi=\begin{pmatrix}\pi^0/2&\pi^+/\sqrt2\\\pi^-/\sqrt2&-\pi^0/2\end{pmatrix}
$$

gives $[Q,\pi]_{12}=\pi_{12}$ and $[Q,\pi]_{21}=-\pi_{21}$. Hence $\pi^+$ has electric charge $+e$, $\pi^-$ has charge $-e$, and $\pi^0$ is neutral.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 305](paper-305.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

A [gauge anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-anomaly) makes gauge redundancy inconsistent and must cancel. A [chiral anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#chiral-anomaly) is the quantum nonconservation of a classically conserved axial current and may be a physical effect. A ['t Hooft anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#t-hooft-anomaly) is an obstruction to gauging a global symmetry; it is preserved by [renormalization-group flow](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#renormalization-group-flow) and must be reproduced by the infrared theory, through massless degrees of freedom, symmetry breaking, or topological order.

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

The axial $U(1)$ is anomalous, while vector fermion number survives. In a left-handed convention, put $\widetilde\psi_R=(\psi_R)^c$. The continuous quantum symmetry and charges are

$$
SU(N_f)_L\times SU(N_f)_R\times U(1)_V,
$$



$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
&SU(2)_{\rm gauge}&SU(N_f)_L&SU(N_f)_R&U(1)_V\\\hline
\psi_L&\mathbf2&\mathbf{N_f}&\mathbf1&+1\\
\widetilde\psi_R&\mathbf2&\mathbf1&\overline{\mathbf{N_f}}&-1
\end{array}
$$

up to finite quotients and the surviving discrete axial symmetry.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

Normalize a fundamental cubic anomaly to $A(\mathbf{N_f})=1$ and its Dynkin index to $I(\mathbf{N_f})=1$. The two gauge colours give

$$
\mathcal A[SU(N_f)_L^3]=2,
\qquad
\mathcal A[SU(N_f)_L^2U(1)_V]=2.
$$

The left and charge-conjugated right fermions have opposite vector charges and equal multiplicities, so

$$
\mathcal A[U(1)_V^3]=0,
\qquad
\mathcal A[U(1)_V\text{-gravity}]=0.
$$

If the theory confined with unbroken chiral symmetry, gauge-invariant operators contain an even number of $SU(2)$ fundamentals and are bosonic, so no massless composite fermions could match the nonzero chiral anomalies. ['t Hooft anomaly matching](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#t-hooft-anomaly-matching) therefore forces chiral symmetry breaking, with its infrared Goldstone theory carrying the anomaly.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

There is no perturbative $SU(2)$ gauge anomaly because both the fundamental and adjoint representations are real or pseudoreal and have vanishing cubic gauge index. The [Witten SU(2) anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#witten-su-2-anomaly) also vanishes: the $N_f$ Dirac fermions give $2N_f$ fundamental Weyl doublets, an even number, while the adjoint Weyl fermion is in an integer-isospin real representation.

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

In left-handed variables, both $\psi_L$ and $\widetilde\psi_R$ have $U(1)_X$ charge $-1$. Cancellation of the mixed $SU(2)_{\rm gauge}^2U(1)_X$ anomaly requires

$$
-2N_f I(\mathbf2)+qI(\mathrm{adj})=-2N_f+4q=0,
$$

where $I(\mathbf2)=1$ and $I(\mathrm{adj})=4$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{q=\frac{N_f}{2}}.
$$

<h3 id="3/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#3/v)

The adjoint fermion is a flavour singlet, so

$$
\mathcal A[SU(N_f)_L^3]=2,
\qquad
\mathcal A[SU(N_f)_L^2U(1)_X]=-2.
$$

There are $4N_f$ fundamental Weyl components of charge $-1$ and three adjoint components of charge $q=N_f/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A[U(1)_X^3]=-4N_f+\frac{3N_f^3}{8}},
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal A[U(1)_X\text{-gravity}]=-4N_f+\frac{3N_f}{2}=-\frac{5N_f}{2}}.
$$

<h3 id="3/vi">vi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/vi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#3/vi)

For $N_f=3$, $q=3/2$. Under $SU(3)_L\times SU(3)_R\times U(1)_X$, the proposed left-handed composites transform as

$$
\chi:\ (\mathbf3,\overline{\mathbf3})_{-1/2},
\qquad
\eta:\ (\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1)_{-1/2},
\qquad
\widetilde\eta:\ (\mathbf1,\mathbf3)_{-1/2}.
$$

For example, the charge of $\chi=\bar\psi_R\lambda_L\psi_L$ is $-1+3/2-1=-1/2$, and the antisymmetric product of two $SU(3)$ fundamentals is an antifundamental.

<h3 id="3/vii">vii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/vii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](#3/vii)

For $SU(3)_L^3$, the three right-flavour copies of $\chi$ contribute $3$, while $\eta$ contributes $-1$, giving $2$. For $SU(3)_L^2U(1)_X$, these fields contribute $3(-1/2)+(-1/2)=-2$. All fifteen components of $\chi$, $\eta$, and $\widetilde\eta$ have charge $-1/2$, so

$$
\mathcal A[U(1)_X^3]=15\left(-\frac12\right)^3=-\frac{15}{8},
\qquad
\mathcal A[U(1)_X\text{-gravity}]=15\left(-\frac12\right)=-\frac{15}{2}.
$$

The ultraviolet formulas at $N_f=3$ give $2$, $-2$, $-15/8$, and $-15/2$ respectively. Every ['t Hooft anomaly](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#t-hooft-anomaly) therefore matches.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 305](paper-305.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Under $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$, one generation consists of

$$
Q_L:(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1/6},\quad
u_R:(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{2/3},\quad
d_R:(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-1/3},
$$



$$
L_L:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2},\quad
e_R:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{-1},\quad
\nu_R:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_0.
$$

These are the fundamental fermions of the [Standard Model](../../../standard-model.md) augmented by a right-handed neutrino.

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

A Dirac mass term directly pairs a left-handed weak doublet with a right-handed weak singlet and is therefore not electroweak gauge invariant. Introduce the [Higgs doublet](../../../standard-model.md#higgs-field) $H:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{1/2}$ and $\widetilde H=i\sigma_2H^*:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2}$. The gauge-invariant [Yukawa terms](../../../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) are

$$
-\bar Q_LY_dHd_R-\bar Q_LY_u\widetilde Hu_R
-\bar L_LY_eHe_R-\bar L_LY_\nu\widetilde H\nu_R+\text{h.c.}
$$

When [electroweak symmetry breaking](../../../standard-model.md#electroweak-symmetry-breaking) gives $\langle H\rangle=(0,v_H/\sqrt2)^T$, these become Dirac mass matrices $M_f=Y_fv_H/\sqrt2$.

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

Two complex $N\times N$ quark Yukawa matrices contain $4N^2$ real parameters. Flavour-basis transformations $U(N)_Q\times U(N)_u\times U(N)_d$ remove $3N^2-1$, because the common baryon-number phase remains unbroken. Thus there are

$$
N^2+1=2N+\frac{N(N-1)}2+\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}2
$$

physical parameters: $2N$ quark masses, $N(N-1)/2$ mixing angles, and $(N-1)(N-2)/2$ complex phases. A physical phase exists only for $N\geq3$ and produces [CP violation](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#cp-violation), equivalently time-reversal violation when CPT is conserved.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

Biunitary diagonalization uses independent left rotations for the up and down Yukawa matrices. Their mismatch is the [Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix](../../../standard-model.md#cabibbo-kobayashi-maskawa-matrix) $V=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}$. For two generations it can be made real and is

$$
V=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_c&\sin\theta_c\\-\sin\theta_c&\cos\theta_c\end{pmatrix}.
$$

In the mass basis,

$$
\mathcal L_{\rm weak}\propto
W_\mu^+\bar u_{Li}\bar\sigma^\mu V_{ij}d_{Lj}
+W_\mu^-\bar d_{Lj}\bar\sigma^\mu V_{ij}^*u_{Li}.
$$

The off-diagonal entries permit charged-current flavour change, for example $W^+\to u\bar s$ or semileptonic kaon decay; both vanish when $\theta_c=0$.
