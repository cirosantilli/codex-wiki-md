<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The theory $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z,+,0)$ is not [aleph-zero-categorical](../../../../../../../omega-categorical-theory.md). Introduce a constant $c$ and add the formulas

$$
c\ne0,
\qquad
\exists y\;(ny=c)quad(n=1,2,\ldots).
$$

Every finite subset is realized in $\mathbb Z$ by taking $c$ to be a nonzero common multiple of the finitely many displayed integers. The [compactness theorem](../../../../../../../compactness-theorem.md) therefore gives a model of $\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb Z,+,0)$ containing a nonzero [infinitely divisible element of an abelian group](../../../../../../../infinitely-divisible-element-of-an-abelian-group.md). The [Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem](../../../../../../../downward-lowenheim-skolem-theorem.md) gives such a model that is countable.

No nonzero integer is divisible by every positive integer, so this countable model is not isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$. This is the [nonstandard model of the additive integers](../../../../../../../nonstandard-model-of-the-additive-integers.md) obstruction to categoricity.
