<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the finite-partial-isomorphism criterion for [quantifier elimination](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination.md). Let $M,N\models\mathrm{DLO}$ and let $h:A\to B$ be an isomorphism between finite suborders. For $a\in M\setminus A$, its position relative to $A$ is one of the finitely many open intervals determined by $A$, or one of the two exterior rays. The corresponding interval or ray determined by $B$ is nonempty because the orders are dense and have no endpoints. Choose $b$ there. Then $h\cup\{(a,b)\}$ remains a partial order isomorphism.

The same argument extends in the other direction. The [back-and-forth method](../../../../../../back-and-forth-method.md) criterion therefore applies, proving [quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints](../../../../../../quantifier-elimination-for-dense-linear-orders-without-endpoints.md). Hence [DLO](../../../../../../dense-linear-order-without-endpoints.md) eliminates quantifiers.
