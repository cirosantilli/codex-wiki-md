<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Varying $A_\nu$ gives the [Maxwell equations](../../../../../../maxwell-equations.md)

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=J^\nu,
\qquad
J^\nu=ie\bigl[(D^\nu\phi)^\dagger\phi-\phi^\dagger D^\nu\phi\bigr].
$$

On the vacuum manifold, choose unitary gauge so that $\phi=v$. Then $\mathbf J=-2e^2v^2\mathbf A$ and the [London equation](../../../../../../london-equations.md) is

$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf J=-2e^2v^2\mathbf B.
$$

For a static configuration, $\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mathbf J$ and $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$, so

$$
\nabla^2\mathbf B=2e^2v^2\mathbf B=\frac1{\xi^2}\mathbf B,
\qquad
\boxed{\xi=\frac1{\sqrt2ev}=\frac1{m_A}}.
$$

The magnetic field therefore decays exponentially inside the condensate. This is the [Meissner effect](../../../../../../meissner-effect.md), with penetration depth equal to the inverse gauge-boson mass.
