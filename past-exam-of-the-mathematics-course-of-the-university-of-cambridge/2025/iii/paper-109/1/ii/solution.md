<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $c(A)=[n]\setminus A$ and reverse the order of the ground set by $j\mapsto n+1-j$. The resulting bijection sends [lexicographic order](../../../../../../lexicographic-order.md) on $[n]^{(r)}$ to [colexicographic order](../../../../../../colexicographic-order.md) on $[n]^{(n-r)}$. It also sends the iterated [upper shadow](../../../../../../upper-shadow.md) of an $r$-uniform family at level $r+1$ to the [lower shadow](../../../../../../lower-shadow.md) of the corresponding $(n-r)$-uniform family, up to the same harmless reversal of the ground set.

The [Kruskal-Katona theorem](../../../../../../kruskal-katona-theorem.md) says that this lower shadow is smallest for an initial colex segment. Undoing the complement and reversal therefore says that the upper shadow of a family of fixed size in $[n]^{(r)}$ is smallest for the initial lex segment.
