<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $\mu^2<0$ and $g>0$, minimizing the $O(N)$-invariant potential gives

$$
\phi^2=v^2=-\frac{\mu^2}{4g}.
$$

The free energy has the full $O(N)$ symmetry, but choosing one point on this sphere leaves only the rotations $O(N-1)$ fixing that point. This is [spontaneous symmetry breaking](../../../../../../../spontaneous-symmetry-breaking.md), $O(N)\to O(N-1)$. The $N-1$ tangent directions along the sphere cost no potential energy and are the massless [Goldstone modes](../../../../../../../goldstone-boson.md) predicted by the [Goldstone theorem](../../../../../../../goldstone-theorem.md).
