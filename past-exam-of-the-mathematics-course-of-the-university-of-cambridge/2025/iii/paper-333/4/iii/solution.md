<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose the phase so that the trapped meridional structure $\widehat v(y)$ is real. The linear wave equations then make $\widehat\phi$ and $\widehat u$ purely imaginary relative to $\widehat v$, so

$$
\overline{u'v'}=\frac12\operatorname{Re}(\widehat u\widehat v^*)=0,
\qquad
\boxed{F^{(y)}=0.}
$$

For every equatorial mode, zonal momentum, hydrostatic balance, and buoyancy evolution give

$$
\widehat u=\frac{k}{\omega}\widehat\phi
+i\frac{\beta y}{\omega}\widehat v,
\qquad
\widehat\sigma=im\widehat\phi,
\qquad
\widehat w=-\frac{\omega m}{N^2}\widehat\phi.
$$

Substitution into the transformed flux shows that the terms involving $\widehat v\widehat\phi^*$ cancel between momentum and buoyancy transport. Hence

$$
\boxed{
F^{(z)}=-\overline{u'w'}
+\frac{\beta y}{N^2}\overline{v'\sigma'}
=\frac12\frac{km}{N^2}|\widehat\phi|^2.}
$$

For upward-propagating waves $m<0$, so $F^{(z)}$ has the opposite sign to $k$. Where a wave dissipates, its flux tends toward zero with height: eastward modes with $k>0$ have $F^{(z)}<0$ and exert an eastward force, while westward modes with $k<0$ have $F^{(z)}>0$ and exert a westward force. Thus dissipation deposits each wave's signed zonal pseudomomentum in the stratospheric mean flow. The formula also shows why $F^{(z)}$ is a wave-activity or pseudomomentum flux rather than merely $-\overline{u'w'}$: the eddy buoyancy transport supplies the correction required by the transformed circulation.
