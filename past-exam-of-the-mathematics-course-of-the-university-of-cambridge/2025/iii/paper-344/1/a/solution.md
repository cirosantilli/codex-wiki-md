<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [binary fluid mixture](../../../../../../binary-fluid-mixture.md), the [compositional order parameter](../../../../../../compositional-order-parameter.md) $\phi(\mathbf r)$ may be taken as the local concentration difference between the two species. In a closed system

$$
\int_V\phi(\mathbf r),d\mathbf r
$$

is fixed by the total amount of each species. This is the sense in which $\phi$ is conserved; locally it changes through a current and obeys a continuity equation.

For a symmetric mixture, the [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md) free energy is

$$
F[\phi]=\int_V\left[\frac a2\phi^2+\frac b4\phi^4+\frac{\kappa_1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^2\right]d\mathbf r,
\qquad b,\kappa_1>0.
$$

A term $h\int_V\phi,d\mathbf r$ is constant on the allowed configurations and therefore changes neither equilibrium probabilities nor the dynamics. Equivalently, it merely shifts the [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) by a spatial constant, whose gradient vanishes.
