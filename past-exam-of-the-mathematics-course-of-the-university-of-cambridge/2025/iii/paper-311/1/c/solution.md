<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The normal to a surface of constant $r$ has squared norm

$$
g^{ab}(\partial_ar)(\partial_br)=g^{rr}=f(r),
$$

which vanishes at $r=r_+$. Thus this surface is a [null hypersurface](../../../../../../null-hypersurface.md). The stationary [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md)

$$
K=\partial_t
$$

has $K^2=g_{tt}=-f$, so it becomes null there and generates a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../killing-horizon.md). Since $f$ has a simple zero, its [surface gravity](../../../../../../surface-gravity.md) is

$$
\boxed{\kappa=\frac12f'(r_+)=\frac1{r_+}}.
$$

A horizon cross-section has topology $S^3\times S^1$, where the circle is the periodic $z$ direction. Including a complete generator, the null hypersurface has topology

$$
\boxed{\mathbb R\times S^3\times S^1}.
$$
