<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) states that the area of spatial cross-sections of a future [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) cannot decrease toward the future, provided the [null energy condition](../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and the standard global assumptions hold.

Let $k^a$ be an affinely parametrized horizon generator. It is hypersurface-orthogonal, so its [null twist](../../../../../../null-twist.md) vanishes. The [Null Raychaudhuri equation](../../../../../../null-raychaudhuri-equation.md) in $D$ dimensions and the Einstein equation give

$$
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}
=-\frac{\theta^2}{D-2}
-\widehat\sigma_{ab}\widehat\sigma^{ab}
-R_{ab}k^ak^b
\leq-\frac{\theta^2}{D-2}.
$$

If $\theta(\lambda_0)<0$, integration implies that $\theta$ diverges to $-\infty$ within affine distance at most $(D-2)/|\theta(\lambda_0)|$. The resulting focal point would make the generator leave the achronal boundary that defines the event horizon, contradicting its assumed future completeness. Hence $\theta\geq0$ everywhere. Since [null expansion](../../../../../../null-expansion.md) obeys

$$
\frac{dA}{d\lambda}=\theta A,
$$

every horizon area element, and therefore every complete cross-section area, is nondecreasing.
