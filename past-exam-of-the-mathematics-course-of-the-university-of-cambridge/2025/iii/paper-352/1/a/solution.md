<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [small-amplitude oscillatory shear](../../../../../../small-amplitude-oscillatory-shear.md), expand the measured [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md) as

$$
\tau=\epsilon a\sin(\omega t+\delta)
=\epsilon\left(a\cos\delta\,\sin\omega t+a\sin\delta\,\cos\omega t\right).
$$

Comparison with the definition

$$
\tau=\epsilon\left(G'\sin\omega t+G''\cos\omega t\right)
$$

of the [storage modulus](../../../../../../storage-modulus.md) and [loss modulus](../../../../../../loss-modulus.md) gives

$$
\boxed{G'=a\cos\delta,\qquad G''=a\sin\delta},
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{a=\sqrt{(G')^2+(G'')^2},\qquad \tan\delta=\frac{G''}{G'}}.
$$

Thus the measured phase directly gives the [loss tangent](../../../../../../loss-tangent.md).

At low [angular frequency](../../../../../../angular-frequency.md), a standard [viscoelastic fluid](../../../../../../viscoelasticity.md) has time to relax and responds mainly as a viscous liquid: stress is approximately in phase with strain rate, so $\delta\to\pi/2$ as $\omega\to0$. At high frequency it cannot relax during one cycle and responds mainly as an elastic solid, so stress is approximately in phase with strain and $\delta\to0$ as $\omega\to\infty$.

For a material dominated by one [viscoelastic relaxation time](../../../../../../viscoelastic-relaxation-time.md) $\lambda$, the crossover between these regimes occurs when $\omega\lambda$ is of order one. Measure the frequency $\omega_c$ at which $G'=G''$, equivalently $\delta=\pi/4$, and estimate

$$
\boxed{\lambda\simeq\omega_c^{-1}}.
$$

A broad or multi-peaked crossover instead indicates a spectrum of relaxation times.
