<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Kolchin theorem](../../../../../../kolchin-theorem.md) conjugates a faithful representation of a [unipotent algebraic group](../../../../../../unipotent-algebraic-group.md) $U$ into the upper unitriangular group $U_n$. Let $U_n^{(r)}$ consist of matrices whose first $r-1$ superdiagonals vanish. Matrix multiplication gives

$$
[U_n^{(r)},U_n^{(s)}]\subseteq U_n^{(r+s)}.
$$

Since $U_n^{(n)}=1$, this filtration is a finite [central series](../../../../../../central-series.md), so $U_n$ and every subgroup of it are [nilpotent groups](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md). Hence $U$ is nilpotent.

The converse fails: $\mathbb G_m$ is abelian, hence nilpotent, but its nonidentity points are semisimple rather than unipotent.
