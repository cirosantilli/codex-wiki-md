<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [derived subgroup of an affine algebraic group](../../../../../../derived-subgroup-of-an-affine-algebraic-group.md) $[G,G]$ is the closed subgroup generated by the commutators $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. If $G$ is connected, the image of every finite product of commutator maps is connected and contains the identity. The closures of these images form an increasing sequence; once their dimensions stabilize, the stable member is closed under products and inverses and equals $[G,G]$. Hence $[G,G]$ is connected.

Now suppose the connected group $G$ is solvable. The [Lie-Kolchin theorem](../../../../../../lie-kolchin-theorem.md) conjugates a faithful representation of $G$ into the upper triangular matrices. Every commutator then has all diagonal entries equal to one, so every element of $[G,G]$ is [unipotent](../../../../../../unipotent-element-of-an-affine-algebraic-group.md). Moreover $[G,G]$ lies in the upper unitriangular group, whose superdiagonal filtration is a central series. It is therefore a [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md).

A [diagonalizable algebraic group](../../../../../../diagonalizable-group.md) is a closed subgroup of a product of copies of $\mathbb G_m$. A [unipotent algebraic group](../../../../../../unipotent-algebraic-group.md) has only unipotent elements, while a semisimple algebraic group here means one all of whose elements are semisimple. A [reductive algebraic group](../../../../../../reductive-group.md) is smooth, connected, affine, and has trivial connected normal unipotent radical.
