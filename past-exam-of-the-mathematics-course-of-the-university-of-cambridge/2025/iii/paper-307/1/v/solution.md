<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

With canonical normalization, the renormalizable [supersymmetric gauge theory](../../../../../../supersymmetric-gauge-theory.md) has

$$
\mathcal L=
\int d^4\theta\,\Phi^\dagger e^{2V}\Phi
+\left[\frac1{16g^2}\int d^2\theta\,
\operatorname{Tr}(W^\alpha W_\alpha)+\mathrm{h.c.}\right].
$$

The first term is the gauge-covariant [Kähler potential](../../../../../../kahler-potential.md) and the second is the [Supersymmetric Yang-Mills action](../../../../../../supersymmetric-yang-mills-action.md). A holomorphic [superpotential](../../../../../../superpotential.md) would also be integrated as $\int d^2\theta\,\mathcal W(\Phi)+\mathrm{h.c.}$, but one commuting field in the fundamental representation of $SU(3)$ admits no nonconstant renormalizable gauge-invariant superpotential: the apparent cubic $\epsilon_{ijk}\Phi^i\Phi^j\Phi^k$ vanishes.
