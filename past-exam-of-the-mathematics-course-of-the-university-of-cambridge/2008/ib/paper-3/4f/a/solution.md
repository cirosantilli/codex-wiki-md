<h1 id="4f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the continuous slope map $(x,y)\mapsto x/y$ on the given set, where $y>0$. The attained ratios are positive rational numbers. Choosing the irrational cut $\sqrt2$, the two subsets

$$
A=\{x/y<\sqrt2\},\qquad B=\{x/y>\sqrt2\}
$$

are disjoint, relatively open, and cover the entire set. Both are nonempty: ratios one and two are attained inside the square. This [irrational-cut separation of rational slopes](../../../../../../irrational-cut-separation-of-rational-slopes.md) is a separation, so

$$
\boxed{\text{the subspace is not connected}.}
$$

The individual rational-slope rays are connected, but their common origin is excluded; their separate connectivity does not imply connectivity of their union.
