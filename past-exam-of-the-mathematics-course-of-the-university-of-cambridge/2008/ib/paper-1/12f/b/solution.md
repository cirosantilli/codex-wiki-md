**A [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) compact [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md).** The empty set omits zero and $\mathbb Z$ has finite complement. An arbitrary union omitting zero belongs to the family. If a union contains zero, one of its constituent sets contains zero and is cofinite, so the union is also cofinite. A finite intersection either omits zero or is an intersection of finitely many cofinite sets and is therefore cofinite. This proves all [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md) axioms.

For distinct nonzero integers $a,b$, the disjoint open singletons $\{a\},\{b\}$ separate them. To separate $0$ and $n\ne0$, use the disjoint [open sets](../../../../../../open-set.md) $\mathbb Z\setminus\{n\}$ and $\{n\}$. Thus this is a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). Every [open cover](../../../../../../open-cover.md) has a member containing zero, which leaves only finitely many integers uncovered; choose one member for each of those points. This gives a finite subcover and proves [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md). The space is the [one-point compactification](../../../../../../alexandroff-extension.md) of the [discrete space](../../../../../../discrete-space.md) $\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, with zero as the added point.
