<h1 id="4c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4C](../4c.md)

Choose the transverse axis so that the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) travels in the $xy$ plane. Its energy-momentum [four-vector](../../../../../four-vector.md) is $p^\mu=(E/c,E\cos\theta/c,E\sin\theta/c,0)$. A [Lorentz boost](../../../../../lorentz-boost.md) of speed $v$ along $x$, with $\beta=v/c$ and $\gamma=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$, gives

$$
E'/c=\gamma(E/c-\beta p_x),\qquad p_x'=\gamma(p_x-\beta E/c).
$$

Because a [photon](../../../../../photon.md) has zero invariant mass, $|\mathbf p'|=E'/c$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E'=\frac{E(1-\beta\cos\theta)}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}},\qquad\cos\theta'=\frac{cp_x'}{E'}=\frac{\cos\theta-\beta}{1-\beta\cos\theta}.}
$$

The first is the [relativistic Doppler effect](../../../../../relativistic-doppler-effect.md) in angular form, and the second is [relativistic aberration](../../../../../relativistic-aberration.md).
