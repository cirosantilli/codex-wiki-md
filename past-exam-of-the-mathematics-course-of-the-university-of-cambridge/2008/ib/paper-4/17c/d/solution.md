<h1 id="17c/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

With light signals, $u=c$, the train-frame [relativistic velocity-addition formula](../../../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md) gives signal velocities $+c,-c$. The endpoints are equally distant from the source in that frame, and

$$
\boxed{t_A=t_B=\frac L{2c},\qquad t'_B-t'_A=-\frac{\gamma Lv}{c^2}\neq0.}
$$

The two reception events are simultaneous in $S$ but not in $S'$: in the station frame the rear moves toward its light signal while the front moves away. Directly, $\Delta x=x_B-x_A=-L$ and $\Delta t=0$, so the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) gives $\Delta t'=\gamma(\Delta t+v\Delta x/c^2)=-\gamma Lv/c^2$. This is [relativity of simultaneity](../../../../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md), concerning spatially separated events; it does not contradict the invariant light speed or the causal ordering of events on a single observer's [world line](../../../../../../world-line.md).
