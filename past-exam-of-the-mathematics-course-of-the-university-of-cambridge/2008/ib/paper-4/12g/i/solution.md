<h1 id="12g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The coordinate curves are assumed to be [geodesics](../../../../../../geodesic.md) as geometric curves; use their [arc-length parametrization](../../../../../../arc-length-parametrization.md) in the [geodesic equations](../../../../../../geodesic-equation.md). On $u=c$, choose increasing arc length $s$, so $\dot u=0$ and $\dot v=1/\sqrt G$. The transverse equation becomes

$$
\frac d{ds}\left(\frac F{\sqrt G}\right)=\frac{G_u}{2G},\qquad \frac1{\sqrt G}\left(\frac F{\sqrt G}\right)_v=\frac{G_u}{2G}.
$$

Thus $(F/\sqrt G)_v=G_u/(2\sqrt G)=(\sqrt G)_u$. On $v=d$, similarly $\dot v=0$, $\dot u=1/\sqrt E$, and the transverse equation gives $(F/\sqrt E)_u=E_v/(2\sqrt E)=(\sqrt E)_v$. Hence the [geodesic coordinate grid](../../../../../../geodesic-coordinate-grid.md) identities are

$$
\boxed{(F/\sqrt G)_v=(\sqrt G)_u,\qquad(F/\sqrt E)_u=(\sqrt E)_v.}
$$
