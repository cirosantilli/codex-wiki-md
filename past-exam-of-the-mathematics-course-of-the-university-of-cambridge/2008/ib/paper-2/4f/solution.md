<h1 id="4f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4F](../4f.md)

Use three compactness facts: a closed subset of a compact space is compact; a continuous image of a compact space is compact; and a compact subset of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) is closed. For a closed $C\subseteq X$, these facts show that $f(C)$ is closed in $Y$. Therefore the bijection $f$ is a closed map, and $(f^{-1})^{-1}(C)=f(C)$ proves [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) of its inverse. **Thus $f$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md).** This proves the [compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem](../../../../../compact-to-hausdorff-continuous-bijection-theorem.md) without requiring $X$ itself to be Hausdorff.

Let $q:[0,1]\to[0,1]/\sim$ be the quotient map and $h(t)=(\cos2\pi t,\sin2\pi t)$. The map $h$ is continuous and identifies precisely the equal parameters and the endpoint pair. Hence it induces a bijection $\bar h$ from the [quotient space](../../../../../quotient-topological-space.md) to the unit circle, with $h=\bar h\circ q$; the definition of the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) makes $\bar h$ continuous. The quotient is compact as a continuous image of $[0,1]$, while the circle is Hausdorff as a subspace of $\mathbb R^2$. Applying the preceding theorem gives

$$
\boxed{[0,1]/\sim\ \cong S^1.}
$$
