<h1 id="6b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6B](../6b.md)

Take the [electric dipole moment](../../../../../electric-dipole-moment.md) along the polar axis, $\mathbf p=p\hat{\mathbf z}$. The [electric field](../../../../../electric-field.md) is minus the gradient of the [electric potential](../../../../../electric-potential.md); in spherical polar coordinates,

$$
E_r=-\partial_r\phi=\frac{2p\cos\theta}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3},\qquad
E_\theta=-\frac1r\partial_\theta\phi=\frac{p\sin\theta}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3},\qquad E_\varphi=0.
$$

Since $\mathbf p=p(\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r-\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta)$ and $\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_r=p\cos\theta$, the radial and angular components combine as

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac{3(\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_r)\hat{\mathbf e}_r-\mathbf p}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^3},\qquad r>0.}
$$

The origin is excluded because the point-dipole potential is singular there.
