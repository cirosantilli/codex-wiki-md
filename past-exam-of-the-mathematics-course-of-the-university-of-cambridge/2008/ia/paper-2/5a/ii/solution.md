<h1 id="5a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Integrate the first equation over $(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)$. The integral of the ordinary cooling term tends to zero as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, while the [Dirac delta function](../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) integrates to one. Thus the [jump condition for an impulse](../../../../../../jump-condition-for-an-impulse.md) is

$$
\boxed{T_1(1^+)-T_1(1^-)=-1.}
$$

After the jump there is no further forcing, so [Newton's law of cooling](../../../../../../newton-s-law-of-cooling.md) gives

$$
T_1(t)-T_\infty=[(T_0-T_\infty)e^{-a}-1]e^{-a(t-1)}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{T_1(t)=T_\infty+(T_0-T_\infty)e^{-at}-e^{-a(t-1)},\qquad t>1.}
$$

The last term has jump minus one at time one and then decays at the same exponential rate as the unforced temperature.
