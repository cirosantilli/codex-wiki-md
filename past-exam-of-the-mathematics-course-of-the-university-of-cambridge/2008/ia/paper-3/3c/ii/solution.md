<h1 id="3c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Divide the velocity by the [speed](../../../../../../speed.md) calculated above. The [unit tangent vector](../../../../../../unit-tangent-vector.md) is

$$
\boxed{T(t)=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\frac{2t}{1+t^2},1\right).}
$$

Its derivative is

$$
T'(t)=\frac1{\sqrt2(1+t^2)^2}\bigl(-4t,2(1-t^2),0\bigr),
\qquad |T'(t)|=\frac{\sqrt2}{1+t^2}>0.
$$

Because $ds/dt=\sqrt2(1+t^2)>0$, the [principal normal vector](../../../../../../principal-normal-vector.md) has the direction of $T'(t)$, giving

$$
\boxed{N(t)=\left(-\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},0\right).}
$$

This is a [unit vector](../../../../../../unit-vector.md), and $N\cdot e_z=0$ for every real $t$. The [curvature of a space curve](../../../../../../curvature-of-a-space-curve.md) is $|T'|/(ds/dt)=1/(1+t^2)^2>0$, so the [principal normal vector](../../../../../../principal-normal-vector.md) is defined everywhere on this [curve](../../../../../../curve.md). The same perpendicularity follows from [constant tangent component forces perpendicular principal normals](../../../../../../constant-tangent-component-forces-perpendicular-principal-normals.md): $T\cdot e_z=1/\sqrt2$ is constant, and differentiating this identity forces the normal component along $e_z$ to vanish.
