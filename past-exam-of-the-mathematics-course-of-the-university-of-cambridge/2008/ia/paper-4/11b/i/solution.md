<h1 id="11b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\mathbf e'_1(t),\mathbf e'_2(t),\mathbf e'_3(t)$ be an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) fixed in the [rotating reference frame](../../../../../../rotating-reference-frame.md) $S'$. A small rotation through $\boldsymbol\omega\,dt$ changes each basis vector by $\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf e'_j\,dt$, so its derivative in the [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md) is

$$
\left(\frac{d\mathbf e'_j}{dt}\right)_S=\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf e'_j.
$$

Write the arbitrary [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) as $\mathbf a=\sum_j a_j(t)\mathbf e'_j(t)$. Differentiating the components and the moving basis separately gives

$$
\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_S=\sum_j\dot a_j\mathbf e'_j+\sum_j a_j\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf e'_j
=\left(\frac{d\mathbf a}{dt}\right)_{S'}+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf a.
$$

This proves the **[rotating-frame derivative formula](../../../../../../rotating-frame-derivative-formula.md)**, including when the [angular velocity](../../../../../../angular-velocity.md) varies with time.
