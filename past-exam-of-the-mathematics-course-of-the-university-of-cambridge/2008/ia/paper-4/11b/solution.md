<h1 id="11b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11B](../11b.md)

Let $\Omega$ be the Earth's [angular velocity](../../../../../angular-velocity.md) magnitude. Use the local east, north and upward unit [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf e_E,\mathbf e_N,\mathbf e_U$, with $\mathbf e_E\times\mathbf e_N=\mathbf e_U$. At latitude $\lambda$, the Earth's [angular velocity](../../../../../angular-velocity.md) and the train's ground-relative [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) are

$$
\boldsymbol\Omega=\Omega\cos\lambda\,\mathbf e_N+\Omega\sin\lambda\,\mathbf e_U,\qquad \mathbf v'=V\mathbf e_N.
$$

Thus the east component of the [Coriolis force](../../../../../coriolis-force.md) is

$$
-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v'=2m\Omega V\sin\lambda\,\mathbf e_E.
$$

For a north–south track, the train has no eastward [acceleration](../../../../../acceleration.md) relative to Earth. Its path curvature lies in the local north–vertical plane. The [centrifugal acceleration](../../../../../centrifugal-acceleration.md) and [Newtonian gravity](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) also lie in that plane, and the Earth's constant [angular velocity](../../../../../angular-velocity.md) gives no [Euler acceleration](../../../../../euler-acceleration.md). Hence the sideways [force](../../../../../force.md) from the track must cancel the eastward [Coriolis force](../../../../../coriolis-force.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F_{\rm track,side}=-2m\Omega V\sin\lambda\,\mathbf e_E.}
$$

**Its magnitude is $2m\Omega V\sin\lambda$, and its direction is west** in the Northern hemisphere. The eastward tendency is the apparent [Coriolis force](../../../../../coriolis-force.md); the requested track [force](../../../../../force.md) has the opposite direction.
