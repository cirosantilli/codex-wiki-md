<h1 id="10b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose the [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) to vanish at infinity. The total [mechanical energy](../../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) is

$$
E=\frac m2\bigl(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2\bigr)-\frac{GMm}{r}=\frac{mh^2}2\bigl((u')^2+u^2\bigr)-GMm\,u.
$$

Put $\gamma=GM/h^2$ and $\varphi=\theta-\theta_0$. From part (i), $u=\gamma(1+e\cos\varphi)$ and $u'=-\gamma e\sin\varphi$. Consequently

$$
(u')^2+u^2=\gamma^2\bigl(1+2e\cos\varphi+e^2\bigr),
$$

and the angular terms cancel against the [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md):

$$
\boxed{E=\frac{mG^2M^2}{2h^2}(e^2-1).}
$$

For a nonradial [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md), negative, zero and positive [mechanical energy](../../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) correspond respectively to $e<1$, $e=1$ and $e>1$.
