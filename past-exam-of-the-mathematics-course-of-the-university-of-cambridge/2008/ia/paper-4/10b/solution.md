<h1 id="10b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10B](../10b.md)

At infinity, the meteorite's incoming asymptote has [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $V$ and perpendicular distance $b$ from the Earth’s centre. The magnitude of its [specific angular momentum](../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) is the magnitude of $\mathbf r\times\mathbf v$. On the asymptote, this is $bV$, so [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) gives $\boxed{h=bV}$. Its [mechanical energy](../../../../../mechanical-energy.md) at infinity is $E=mV^2/2$. Comparing with part (ii) gives

$$
\boxed{e=\sqrt{1+\frac{b^2V^4}{G^2M^2}}}.
$$

For $b>0$ this [Kepler orbit](../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) is a [hyperbola](../../../../../hyperbola.md). The smallest $r=1/u$ occurs at [periapsis](../../../../../periapsis.md), where $\cos(\theta-\theta_0)=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{r_{\min}=\frac{b^2V^2}{GM(1+e)}=\frac{GM}{V^2}(e-1)
=\frac{\sqrt{G^2M^2+b^2V^4}-GM}{V^2}.}
$$

The middle equality follows by substituting $e^2-1=b^2V^4/(G^2M^2)$. A trajectory entering the Earth satisfies $r_{\min}<R$, equivalently

$$
e<1+\frac{RV^2}{GM}\iff b^2<R^2+\frac{2GMR}{V^2}.
$$

Therefore the sufficient collision condition is

$$
\boxed{b<\sqrt{R^2+\frac{2GMR}{V^2}}.}
$$

This is [gravitational focusing](../../../../../gravitational-focusing.md): [Newtonian gravity](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) increases the collision radius measured at infinity. Equality is the ideal grazing trajectory, which touches the surface; a strict inequality penetrates it. For $b=0$ the incoming motion is radial and collides, with $r_{\min}=0$ as the limiting value of the above expression.
