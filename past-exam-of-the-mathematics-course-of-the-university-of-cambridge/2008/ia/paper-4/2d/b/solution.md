<h1 id="2d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For positive [integers](../../../../../../integer.md), $m/m=2^0$, establishing reflexivity. If $m/n=2^k$, then $n/m=2^{-k}$, establishing symmetry. If also $n/\ell=2^j$, then $m/\ell=2^{k+j}$, establishing transitivity. Hence this is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).

Every positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) can be written uniquely as $m=2^r d$, where $r\geq0$ is an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) and $d$ is an odd positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md), its [odd part of a positive integer](../../../../../../odd-part-of-a-positive-integer.md). Existence follows by repeatedly dividing by two while the result is even; the process terminates because the positive [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) strictly decrease. For uniqueness, $2^r d=2^s e$ with $d,e$ odd would make one of $d,e$ even if $r\ne s$; therefore $r=s$ and $d=e$.

Multiplying by a power of two preserves the [odd part of a positive integer](../../../../../../odd-part-of-a-positive-integer.md), and two positive [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) with the same odd part have ratio a power of two. The [equivalence class](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md) indexed by odd $d$ is consequently $\{2^r d:r\geq0\}$. **The required representative set is $\boxed{A=\{1,3,5,\ldots\}}$.**
