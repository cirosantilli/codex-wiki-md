<h1 id="11d/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $f(x)=0$ and $f(x+h)/\sqrt{|h|}\to0$. Then

$$
\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h
=\operatorname{sgn}(h)\left(\frac{f(x+h)}{\sqrt{|h|}}\right)^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{g'(x)=0.}
$$

This proves sufficiency of the second alternative even if $f$ itself is not [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at $x$. For example, $f(t)=|t|^{3/4}$ is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and not [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at zero, while $g(t)=|t|^{3/2}$ is [differentiable](../../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) there with derivative zero.
