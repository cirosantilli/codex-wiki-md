<h1 id="11d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

By [differentiability implies continuity](../../../../../../differentiability-implies-continuity.md), $f(x+h)\to f(x)$. Add and subtract $f(x+h)g(x)$ in the difference quotient:

$$
\frac{f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)}h
=f(x+h)\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}h
+g(x)\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h.
$$

The two quotient limits exist by the given [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md). Taking the limit and using [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) yields the [product rule](../../../../../../product-rule.md):

$$
\boxed{(fg)'(x)=f(x)g'(x)+g(x)f'(x).}
$$

Thus the product is [differentiable](../../../../../../differentiable-function.md) at the specified point; differentiability elsewhere is not needed.
