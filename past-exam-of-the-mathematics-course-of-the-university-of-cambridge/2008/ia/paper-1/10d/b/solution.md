<h1 id="10d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $u,v\in f(I)$ with $u<y<v$. Choose preimages $x_u,x_v\in I$, and let $a=\min(x_u,x_v)$, $b=\max(x_u,x_v)$. The defining property of a [real interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) ensures $[a,b]\subseteq I$. The restriction of $f$ to this [closed interval](../../../../../../closed-real-interval.md) is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), and its endpoint values are $u,v$ in one order or the other. The [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) supplies $c\in[a,b]\subseteq I$ with $f(c)=y$. Thus every value between two image values lies in the image, proving

$$
\boxed{f(I)\text{ is a real interval}.}
$$

The [continuous image of a real interval](../../../../../../continuous-image-of-a-real-interval.md) result does not require $I$ itself to be closed or bounded; only the segment between the chosen preimages is used. Empty and single-point images also satisfy the interval property.
