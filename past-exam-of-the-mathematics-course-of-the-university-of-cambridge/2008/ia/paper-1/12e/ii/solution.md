<h1 id="12e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $|z|=r<1$, the inequality $n!\geq n$ for $n\geq1$ gives

$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty|z|^{n!}
\leq r+\sum_{n=1}^\infty r^n<\infty.
$$

The first $r$ comes from $0!=1$; both initial terms have exponent one. Thus the [factorial-gap power series](../../../../../../factorial-gap-power-series.md) converges absolutely inside the unit circle. For $|z|>1$, its terms have magnitudes $|z|^{n!}\to\infty$, so the [term test for divergence](../../../../../../term-test-for-divergence.md) proves divergence and $R=1$.

At every point with $|z|=1$, each term has magnitude one, so the terms cannot tend to zero, regardless of their phases. Hence

$$
\boxed{R=1;\quad\text{divergence at every point of }|z|=1.}
$$

The large gaps between exponents make this a [lacunary power series](../../../../../../lacunary-power-series.md); cancellation cannot overcome the necessary condition that the terms of a convergent [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) tend to zero.
