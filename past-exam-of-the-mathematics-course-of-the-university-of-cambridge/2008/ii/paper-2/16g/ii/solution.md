<h1 id="16g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For each $n$, let $\sigma_n$ assert the existence of $n$ distinct elements. If a theory $T$ has arbitrarily large finite models, every finite subset of $T\cup\{\sigma_n:n\ge1\}$ has a model. By the [compactness theorem](../../../../../../compactness-theorem.md), this extended theory has a model, necessarily infinite. Since fields of size $2^j$ are arbitrarily large, no [first-order theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) can have exactly the finite fields of characteristic two as its models.

Suppose instead a finite conjunction $\chi$ axiomatized exactly the infinite fields of characteristic two. Let $T_2$ be the field axioms with $1+1=0$. Then $T_2\cup\{\neg\chi\}$ would have exactly the finite characteristic-two fields as models, contradicting the preceding conclusion. Equivalently, compactness of $T_2\cup\{\sigma_n\}\models\chi$ would force some finite size threshold, but arbitrarily large finite fields still fail $\chi$. **The infinite characteristic-two fields are not finitely axiomatizable.**
