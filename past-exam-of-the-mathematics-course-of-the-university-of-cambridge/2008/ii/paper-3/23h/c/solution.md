<h1 id="23h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**There is no compact boundaryless minimal surface in $\mathbb R^3$.** For an immersion $X$ of a minimal surface, the coordinate functions are harmonic: $\Delta_SX=0$. Consequently $\Delta_S|X|^2=2|\nabla_SX|^2=4$, because the immersion has a two-dimensional orthonormal tangent frame. Integrating over a compact surface without boundary gives zero on the left by the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) and $4\operatorname{Area}(S)>0$ on the right, a contradiction. If surfaces with boundary are allowed, a closed planar disk is a compact minimal example; the boundaryless convention matters.
