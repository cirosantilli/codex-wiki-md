<h1 id="24h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $n=1$, the singular set is the point $\{0\}$, a zero-dimensional [submanifold](../../../../../../submanifold.md). For $n\geq2$, every elementary matrix $E_{ij}$ is singular and the smooth curve $t\mapsto tE_{ij}$ lies in $X$ through zero. If $X$ were a [submanifold](../../../../../../submanifold.md), all $E_{ij}$ would belong to $T_0X$ and span $M(n)$. Its dimension at zero would therefore be $n^2$, making $X$ locally open there. But arbitrarily small matrices $tI$ are invertible, a contradiction. Thus

$$
\boxed{X\text{ is a submanifold exactly when }n=1.}
$$

The failure is at the zero matrix; fixed-rank pieces of the singular set can separately be smooth [submanifolds](../../../../../../submanifold.md).
