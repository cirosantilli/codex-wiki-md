<h1 id="29c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a first-order [quasilinear partial differential equation](../../../../../../quasilinear-partial-differential-equation.md), take $C^1$ coefficients $a_j,b$, $C^1$ prescribed data $u_0$ on a smooth $C^2$ [hypersurface](../../../../../../hypersurface.md) $S$, and a point $x_0\in S$. The data are non-characteristic there if

$$
a(x_0,u_0(x_0))\cdot n(x_0)\ne0,
$$

where $n$ is any nonzero normal to $S$. Then there is a neighborhood of $x_0$ on which a unique $C^1$ solution realizes the data. Uniqueness is local, after restricting to a neighborhood swept out by the nearby characteristics.

The [method of characteristics](../../../../../../method-of-characteristics.md) solves $\dot x=a(x,u)$, $\dot u=b(x,u)$ from points of $S$. In local surface coordinates $\eta$, the map $(\eta,s)\mapsto x(\eta,s)$ has nonsingular derivative at $s=0$ exactly under the stated normal condition. The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) makes these variables local coordinates, giving the asserted solution and uniqueness.
