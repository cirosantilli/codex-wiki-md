<h1 id="15e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let a comoving test particle have physical radius $R=a(t)\chi$, where $\chi$ is fixed. Assume [Newtonian gravity](../../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md), spherical homogeneity, negligible pressure, and no mass flux through this sphere, so its enclosed mass $M=(4\pi/3)\rho R^3$ is constant. The [shell theorem](../../../../../../spherical-shell-theorem.md) gives a conserved specific [energy](../../../../../../energy.md)

$$
\frac12\dot R^2-\frac{GM}{R}=\mathcal E.
$$

Substitute $R=a\chi$, divide by $R^2/2$, and write $2\mathcal E/\chi^2=-kc^2$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}.}
$$

Homogeneity makes the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) scale as $\chi^2$, leaving the same $k$ at every comoving radius. The sign of $k$ encodes the binding-energy sign. This Newtonian derivation applies to pressure-free matter; interpreting $k$ as spatial curvature and extending the equation to relativistic pressure components requires [general relativity](../../../../../../general-relativity-split.md), rather than conservation of this Newtonian shell [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) alone.
