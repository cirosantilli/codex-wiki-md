<h1 id="15e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Separate fluid conservation gives $\dot\rho_M=-3H\rho_M$, hence $\rho_M=\rho_{M0}a^{-3}$ after normalization at today. For $P_\Lambda=-\rho_\Lambda c^2$, the conservation equation gives $\dot\rho_\Lambda=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\rho=\rho_{M0}a^{-3}+\rho_{\Lambda0}.}
$$

Assume both densities are positive and set $A=8\pi G\rho_{M0}/3$, $B=8\pi G\rho_{\Lambda0}/3$. On the expanding flat branch, $\dot a^2=A/a+Ba^2$. With $b=a^{3/2}$ this becomes $\dot b=(3/2)\sqrt{A+Bb^2}$. Choosing the [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md) at $t=0$ and integrating gives

$$
\boxed{a(t)=\alpha\sinh^{2/3}(\beta t),\qquad
\alpha=(\rho_{M0}/\rho_{\Lambda0})^{1/3},\quad
\beta=\frac32\sqrt{B}=\sqrt{6\pi G\rho_{\Lambda0}}.}
$$

The normalization $a(t_0)=1$ requires $\sinh(\beta t_0)=\sqrt{\rho_{\Lambda0}/\rho_{M0}}$; equivalently $\alpha=\sinh^{-2/3}(\beta t_0)$. Thus the age $t_0$ and the two positive densities are not independent once this normalization and bang time are fixed.

As $t\downarrow0$, $a\sim\alpha(\beta t)^{2/3}$, the matter-dominated law. As $t\to\infty$, $a\sim\alpha2^{-2/3}e^{\sqrt B\,t}$ and $\ddot a/a\to B>0$, giving accelerated dark-energy expansion. Direct differentiation gives $\ddot a/a=B-A/(2a^3)$, so acceleration begins once $a^3>A/(2B)$. If [dark energy](../../../../../../dark-energy.md) vanishes, the limiting matter-only solution must be taken instead; negative dark-energy density would not have the stated late-time accelerating form.
