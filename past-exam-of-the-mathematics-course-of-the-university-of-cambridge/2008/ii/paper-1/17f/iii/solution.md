↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**True** for finite connected simple graphs. [average degree of a graph](../../../../../../average-degree-of-a-graph.md) at most two implies $e\leq v$. A [spanning tree](../../../../../../spanning-tree.md) has $v-1$ edges, leaving at most one extra edge. Hence the graph is a [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) or unicyclic. [trees](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) are planar, and a unicyclic graph is a cycle with [trees](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) attached to its vertices, which can also be embedded in the plane. Connectedness is essential to this argument: a disconnected nonplanar component can be diluted by isolated vertices.
