↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [integral basis](../../../../../../integral-basis.md) of a degree-$d$ [number field](../../../../../../number-field.md) is a $\mathbb Z$-basis of its [ring of integers](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md): each [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md) has a unique integer linear combination of those $d$ elements. For $k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, the standard quadratic-integer criterion gives $\mathcal O_k=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$, since $2$ is squarefree and is not congruent to one modulo four. Thus

$$
\boxed{1,\sqrt2\text{ is an integral basis of }k.}
$$
