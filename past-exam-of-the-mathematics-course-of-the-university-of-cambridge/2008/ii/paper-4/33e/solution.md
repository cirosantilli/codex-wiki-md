<h1 id="33e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [33E](../33e.md)

A periodic lattice [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) commutes with lattice translations. The [Bloch theorem](../../../../../bloch-s-theorem.md) therefore classifies its states by characters of the translation group:

$$
\psi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf x)=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf x),
$$

where $u$ is lattice periodic. For each [crystal momentum](../../../../../crystal-momentum.md) $\mathbf k$, solving on a primitive cell with the corresponding quasiperiodic boundary condition gives discrete [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) indexed by $n$. Their continuous dependence on $\mathbf k$ produces the [energy bands](../../../../../energy-band.md) $E_n(\mathbf k)$ as $\mathbf k$ ranges through the [Brillouin zone](../../../../../brillouin-zone.md). Bragg mixing opens gaps at suitable degeneracies, although bands can overlap in three dimensions and gaps are not guaranteed between every pair.

The quantum numbers are **band index, wavevector modulo reciprocal lattice vectors, and [spin](../../../../../spin.md) where [spin](../../../../../spin.md) is conserved**. In a finite crystal the allowed wavevectors are discrete; in the thermodynamic limit they become dense and the band description emerges. Spin-orbit coupling can replace a separately conserved [spin](../../../../../spin.md) label by an appropriate internal band label.

Dilute randomly placed impurities break exact translation symmetry, so [crystal momentum](../../../../../crystal-momentum.md) is no longer an exact quantum number. Most extended states retain approximately the host band structure but acquire impurity scattering and disorder broadening. An impurity potential can also produce [impurity levels in a crystal](../../../../../impurity-level-in-a-crystal.md), often in a band gap: levels associated with a [donor impurity](../../../../../donor-impurity.md) lie near the conduction-band edge and can supply electrons, while those associated with an [acceptor impurity](../../../../../acceptor-impurity.md) lie near the valence-band edge and can create holes. The impurity's species and potential determine whether such levels exist and how deep they are; not every impurity necessarily makes a gap state. At low density these levels are nearly isolated, while overlap at increasing density can broaden them into an impurity band. Added or removed carriers shift the [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential.md) as well.
