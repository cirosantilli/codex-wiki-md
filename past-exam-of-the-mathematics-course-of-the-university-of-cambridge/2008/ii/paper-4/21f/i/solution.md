<h1 id="21f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $fg\simeq1_Y$ and $hf\simeq1_X$, composing homotopies gives

$$
h\simeq h(fg)\simeq(hf)g\simeq g.
$$

Thus $gf\simeq hf\simeq1_X$, while $fg\simeq1_Y$ already holds, so $g$ is a [homotopy inverse](../../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) of $f$.

More generally let $a:Y\to Y$ be a [homotopy inverse](../../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) of $fg$ and $b:X\to X$ one of $hf$. Then $g'=ga$ satisfies $fg'\simeq1_Y$, and $h'=bh$ satisfies $h'f\simeq1_X$. The first argument applies. Hence **the separate one-sided hypotheses imply that $f$ is a [homotopy equivalence](../../../../../../homotopy-equivalence.md)**, as expressed by [separate homotopy inverses combine into a homotopy equivalence](../../../../../../separate-homotopy-inverses-combine-into-a-homotopy-equivalence.md).
