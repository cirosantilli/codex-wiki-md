<h1 id="24h/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**The assertion is false without further global hypotheses.** For a concrete [non-spherical surface of constant Gaussian curvature one](../../../../../../non-spherical-surface-of-constant-gaussian-curvature-one.md), fix $a=1/2$ and set

$$
f(u)=a\cos u,\qquad g(u)=\int_0^u\sqrt{1-a^2\sin^2 t}\,dt,
$$



$$
X(u,v)=(f(u)\cos v,f(u)\sin v,g(u)),\qquad |u|<\pi/4,\quad v\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z).
$$

Here $f>0$, $g'>0$ and $f'^2+g'^2=1$, so this is a connected regular embedded [surface of revolution](../../../../../../surface-of-revolution.md) with unit-speed meridian. Its metric is $du^2+f(u)^2dv^2$, whose [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) is $-f''/f=1$. This formula also follows by multiplying its [principal curvatures](../../../../../../principal-curvature.md) $-f''/g'$ and $g'/f$.

At $u=0$ these [principal curvatures](../../../../../../principal-curvature.md) are $a=1/2$ and $1/a=2$. Every point of a radius-one sphere is umbilical with both principal curvature magnitudes one. The displayed surface therefore cannot lie in any such sphere, despite having $K=1$ everywhere.
