<h1 id="24h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A simple closed curve on the sphere bounds two topological disks. Equal areas give area $2\pi$ for each, since the unit sphere has area $4\pi$. Apply [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](../../../../../../gauss-bonnet-theorem.md) to either disk $D$, with $K=1$ and $\chi(D)=1$:

$$
2\pi+\int_\gamma k_g\,ds=2\pi,\qquad\int_\gamma k_g\,ds=0.
$$

A continuous [geodesic curvature](../../../../../../geodesic-curvature.md) that were strictly positive everywhere, or strictly negative everywhere, would have a correspondingly strict sign for this integral. Therefore **neither everywhere strictly positive nor everywhere strictly negative [geodesic curvature](../../../../../../geodesic-curvature.md) is possible**. Orientation reversal changes its sign but not this conclusion.
