<h1 id="30c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose the [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) convention

$$
\boxed{\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx,\qquad
f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat f(\xi)\,d\xi.}
$$

It maps the [Schwartz space](../../../../../../schwartz-space.md) to itself. For a [tempered distribution](../../../../../../tempered-distribution.md) $u$, define the transform by bilinear distributional duality:

$$
\boxed{\langle\widehat u,\phi\rangle=\langle u,\widehat\phi\rangle\qquad(\phi\in\mathcal S).}
$$

For an ordinary integrable function this agrees with the first formula by interchanging its integrals. The absence of complex conjugation is the distribution-pairing convention.
