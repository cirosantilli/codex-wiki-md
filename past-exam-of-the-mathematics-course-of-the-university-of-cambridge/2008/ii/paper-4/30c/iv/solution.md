<h1 id="30c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the difference of two solutions the equation is $w_{tt}+w_t-\Delta w=0$ with zero initial data. The same shrinking-ball energy now obeys

$$
E'(t)=-\int_{B_t}w_t^2\,dx
+\int_{\partial B_t}\left[w_t\partial_nw-\tfrac12(w_t^2+|\nabla w|^2)\right]dS\leq0.
$$

Again $E(0)=0$ forces the difference to vanish at every chosen spacetime point. Therefore **the two $C^2$ solutions agree for every $t>0$**. Damping contributes an additional negative energy term and does not weaken the local uniqueness proof.
