<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The first similarity hypothesis of [Kolmogorov 1941 theory](../../../../../../kolmogorov-1941-theory.md) states that at sufficiently large [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), the small-scale statistics in the [equilibrium range](../../../../../../equilibrium-range.md) are locally universal and determined by the mean [turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy-dissipation-rate.md) $\epsilon$ and [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md) $\nu$, together with the separation or wavenumber being measured. Large-scale forcing details are excluded only at scales well below the [integral scale of turbulence](../../../../../../integral-scale-of-turbulence.md).

These parameters define the [Kolmogorov microscales](../../../../../../kolmogorov-microscales.md)

$$
\eta=(\nu^3/\epsilon)^{1/4},\qquad
u_\eta=(\nu\epsilon)^{1/4},\qquad
\tau_\eta=(\nu/\epsilon)^{1/2}.
$$

[Dimensional analysis](../../../../../../dimensional-analysis.md) gives universal functions $F$ and $G$ with

$$
\boxed{S_2(r)=u_\eta^2F(r/\eta)=(\nu\epsilon)^{1/2}F(r/\eta),}
$$



$$
\boxed{E(k)=u_\eta^2\eta\,G(k\eta)
=(\epsilon\nu^5)^{1/4}G(k\eta).}
$$

The dimensional factors follow because $S_2$ has units of [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) squared, whereas $E$ has units of [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) squared times length. The universal equilibrium forms include viscous effects; they do not impose a power law through the [dissipation range](../../../../../../dissipation-range.md).

The second similarity hypothesis asserts independence of [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md) at scales large compared with $\eta$ but still small compared with the integral scale. In this [inertial range](../../../../../../inertial-range.md), $S_2$ can depend only on $\epsilon$ and $r$. Balancing time and length dimensions forces powers $2/3$ and $2/3$. The same calculation for $E$, using $k$ instead of $r$, forces powers $2/3$ and $-5/3$. Hence

$$
\boxed{S_2(r)=C_2\epsilon^{2/3}r^{2/3},\qquad\eta\ll r\ll\ell,}
$$



$$
\boxed{E(k)=C_K\epsilon^{2/3}k^{-5/3},\qquad\ell^{-1}\ll k\ll\eta^{-1}.}
$$

The constants $C_2$ and $C_K$ are universal within the hypotheses and the stated normalization. They are related by the exact [structure-function spectral filter](../../../../../../structure-function-spectral-filter.md), rather than by assigning all energy to one sharp scale.
