<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

After [cosmological horizon crossing](../../../../../../cosmological-horizon-crossing.md), the rapidly oscillating radiation source can be averaged away as specified. Well inside [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), the remaining [cold dark matter](../../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) self-gravity is also subleading because $\rho_C/\rho_R\ll1$. To leading order the [linear cosmological density perturbation](../../../../../../linear-cosmological-density-perturbation-split.md) equation is therefore

$$
\delta''+\frac1\tau\delta'\simeq0,
\qquad (\tau\delta')'\simeq0.
$$

Two integrations give

$$
\boxed{\delta(\tau)\simeq A+B\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_H}\right),\qquad \tau_H\sim k^{-1}.}
$$

The reference time makes the logarithm dimensionless. A growing superhorizon mode reaches entry with a nonzero derivative, so matching generally excites $B\ne0$. For example, an abrupt leading-order match from $\delta\propto\tau^2$ gives $B=\tau_H\delta'_H\simeq2\delta_H$, whereas its exact numerical coefficient depends on the transition and the radiation forcing.

This logarithmic growth is the [Mészáros effect](../../../../../../meszaros-effect.md). It is an asymptotic radiation-era result: after dropping the oscillating radiation perturbation, the original equation still contains $4\pi Ga^2\rho_C\delta$. Retaining that term and the changing background leads to the [Mészáros equation](../../../../../../meszaros-equation.md), which provides the interpolation near [matter-radiation equality](../../../../../../matter-radiation-equality.md). It would be incorrect to claim that a pure logarithm solves the complete mixed-background equation exactly.
