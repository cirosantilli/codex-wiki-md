<h1 id="3/v/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A longer [neutron](../../../../../../../neutron.md) [half-life](../../../../../../../half-life.md) means a larger mean lifetime $\tau_n=\tau_{1/2}/\ln2$. Holding the initial [neutron-to-proton ratio](../../../../../../../neutron-to-proton-ratio.md) and the nuclear-burning delay fixed, the derivative of the [neutron](../../../../../../../neutron.md) survival factor is positive:

$$
\frac{\partial\ln Y_4}{\partial\tau_n}=\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_n^2}>0.
$$

Thus fewer [neutrons](../../../../../../../neutron.md) disappear during the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md), and **the final helium-4 mass fraction increases**.

There can be a second effect if the increased lifetime represents a uniform reduction of the relevant [weak interaction](../../../../../../../weak-interaction.md) strength. With fixed masses and other couplings, [neutron](../../../../../../../neutron.md) decay and the conversion reactions both scale as $G_F^2$. Thus $\tau_n\propto G_F^{-2}$, whereas $T_F\propto G_F^{-2/3}\propto\tau_n^{1/3}$. Weaker conversion then freezes out earlier and increases $r_F$, reinforcing the survival effect. The survival argument alone already establishes the requested [neutron half-life effect on primordial helium](../../../../../../../neutron-half-life-effect-on-primordial-helium.md); linking lifetime to all conversion rates additionally requires this microscopic assumption.
