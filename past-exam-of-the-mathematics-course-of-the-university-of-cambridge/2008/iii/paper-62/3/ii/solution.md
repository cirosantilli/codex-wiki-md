<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

During [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), the [effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom](../../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-energy-degrees-of-freedom.md), denoted $g_*$, gives the [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md)

$$
\rho_R=\frac{\pi^2}{30}g_*T^4.
$$

The paper's $\mathcal N_{\rm eff}$ is this $g_*$; it is not the conventionally normalized effective number of [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) species. With the specified [reduced Planck mass](../../../../../../reduced-planck-mass.md), the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) reads $H^2=\rho_R/(3M_{\rm Pl}^2)$, whence

$$
H=\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2g_*}{90}}\frac{T^2}{M_{\rm Pl}}.
$$

Write the [weak interaction](../../../../../../weak-interaction.md) conversion rate as $\Gamma=A G_F^2T^5$. The ratio $\Gamma/H$ scales as $T^3$, so it falls during cooling: conversion eventually becomes too slow to maintain [neutron-proton chemical equilibrium](../../../../../../neutron-proton-chemical-equilibrium.md). Equating the conversion and expansion rates gives

$$
T_F=\left[\frac{\sqrt{\pi^2g_*/90}}{A G_F^2M_{\rm Pl}}\right]^{1/3}.
$$

On suppressing the numerical coefficient, the [cosmological weak freeze-out](../../../../../../cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md) [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) is consequently

$$
\boxed{T_F\sim G_F^{-2/3}\mathcal N_{\rm eff}^{1/6}M_{\rm Pl}^{-1/3}.}
$$

The one-sixth power reflects a balance between a conversion rate proportional to $T^5$ and a [Hubble parameter](../../../../../../hubble-parameter.md) proportional to $\sqrt{g_*}\,T^2$.
