<h1 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The nucleons are nonrelativistic, so the [Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md) gives

$$
n_i=g_i\left(\frac{m_iT}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{(\mu_i-m_i)/T},\qquad i=n,p.
$$

Both spin degeneracies are two. Taking the ratio and using the specified negligible [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) yields the [neutron-to-proton ratio](../../../../../../neutron-to-proton-ratio.md)

$$
r_F=\left(\frac{m_n}{m_p}\right)^{3/2}e^{-(m_n-m_p)/T_F}
\simeq\boxed{e^{-Q/T_F}}.
$$

The mass prefactor differs from unity only at the per-mille level. The dependence on $T_F$ is much stronger: $d\ln r_F/d\ln T_F=Q/T_F>0$. Thus **earlier, hotter weak freeze-out leaves more [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) relative to [protons](../../../../../../proton.md)**. For illustration, $T_F=1\,\mathrm{MeV}$ gives $r_F\simeq0.28$; $T_F=0.8\,\mathrm{MeV}$ gives $r_F\simeq0.20$. [Free-neutron decay after freeze-out](../../../../../../free-neutron-decay-after-freeze-out.md) lowers the ratio further before most [Big Bang nucleosynthesis](../../../../../../big-bang-nucleosynthesis.md) takes place.
