<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Before [electron-positron annihilation in cosmology](../../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation-in-cosmology.md), [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) contribute two [helicity](../../../../../../helicity.md) states; [Electrons](../../../../../../electron.md) and positrons contribute two spin states each. Each of the three light [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) flavours contributes one active [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) [helicity](../../../../../../helicity.md) and one antineutrino [helicity](../../../../../../helicity.md). At a common [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) the [effective number of relativistic energy degrees of freedom](../../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-energy-degrees-of-freedom.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{g_*=2+\frac78(4+6)=\frac{43}{4}=10.75.}
$$

Using $G_F=1.17\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$ and $M_{\rm Pl}\simeq2.435\times10^{18}\,\mathrm{GeV}$ in the scaling formula alone gives $T_F\sim2\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Including the explicit expansion coefficient from (ii), but setting the unknown rate coefficient $A$ to one, gives $T_F\simeq1.5\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Thus the warranted conclusion is **a freeze-out [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) of order $1\,\mathrm{MeV}$**, above the $0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$ [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) mass. The omitted weak-rate coefficient prevents a more precise prediction from this estimate.

[Neutrino decoupling](../../../../../../neutrino-decoupling.md) occurs before most electron-positron pairs annihilate. Afterwards [neutrinos](../../../../../../neutrino.md) redshift freely, $T_\nu a=\mathrm{constant}$, while the [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) receive the pair [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md). For the still-coupled photon-pair gas, [cosmological entropy conservation](../../../../../../cosmological-entropy-conservation.md) gives $g_{*s}T_\gamma^3a^3=\mathrm{constant}$. Its [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) degrees of freedom drop from $2+(7/8)4=11/2$ to $2$. Comparing this with the freely cooling [neutrinos](../../../../../../neutrino.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_\nu}{T_\gamma}=\left(\frac4{11}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
\frac{T_\gamma}{T_\nu}=\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^{1/3}.}
$$

After annihilation, [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) and [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) [temperatures](../../../../../../temperature.md) differ. To write the total [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md) as $(\pi^2/30)g_*T_\gamma^4$, the [neutrino](../../../../../../neutrino.md) contribution must include that [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) ratio:

$$
\boxed{g_*=2+\frac78\,6\left(\frac4{11}\right)^{4/3}\simeq3.36.}
$$

This is the paper's post-annihilation $\mathcal N_{\rm eff}$. Merely deleting the [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) term while keeping all species at the same [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) would be incorrect. For comparison the [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) count, normalized to $T_\gamma^3$, is $g_{*s}=2+(7/8)6(4/11)=43/11\simeq3.91$. These results assume instantaneous [neutrino decoupling](../../../../../../neutrino-decoupling.md) and relativistic [neutrinos](../../../../../../neutrino.md); the two counts represent different thermal moments.
