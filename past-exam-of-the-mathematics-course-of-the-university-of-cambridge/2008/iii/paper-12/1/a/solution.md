<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**The assertion is true.** The assumed finite [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) means that $f_0-w_1$ is not identically zero and has an isolated zero of order $k$ at $z_1$. Choose $\rho>0$ so that $\overline{D(z_1,\rho)}\subset D(0,r)$ and $f_0-w_1$ has no other zeros in this closed disc. On its boundary,

$$
\eta=\min_{|z-z_1|=\rho}|f_0(z)-w_1|>0.
$$

The joint [holomorphy](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) established above gives $f_\alpha\to f_0$ uniformly on that circle as $\alpha\to0$. For sufficiently small $|\alpha|$, therefore, $|f_\alpha-f_0|<\eta$. By [Rouché's theorem](../../../../../../rouche-s-theorem.md), $f_\alpha-w_1$ has exactly $k$ zeros in $D(z_1,\rho)$, counted with [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md). In particular it has at least one zero in $D(0,r)$. This preserves the total local zero count, without asserting that the zeros remain together.
