<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**The assertion is false for $k\ge2$, but true for $k=1$.** For $k\ge2$, take the jointly [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) right-hand side $A_\alpha(z,w)=kz^{k-1}+\alpha$. Its solution with zero initial value is

$$
f_\alpha(z)=z^k+\alpha z=z(z^{k-1}+\alpha).
$$

At $\alpha=0$, the value zero has [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) $k$ at the origin. For $\alpha\ne0$, however, every zero is a [simple zero](../../../../../../simple-zero.md): $f_\alpha'(0)=\alpha$, and at a nonzero zero $z^{k-1}=-\alpha$,

$$
f_\alpha'(z)=kz^{k-1}+\alpha=-(k-1)\alpha\ne0.
$$

Thus there is no zero of [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) $k$ anywhere, even though the $k$ simple zeros stay close to zero for small $\alpha$. When $k=1$, part (a) gives exactly one zero counted with [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) in a small disc, so that zero is simple. Alternatively, the [holomorphic implicit function theorem](../../../../../../holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem.md) gives its [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) location as a function of $\alpha$.
