<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Let $F$ be the generator of the [smooth flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#smooth-flow). The [Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#anosov-flow) property requires $F$ to be nowhere zero and a continuous splitting of the [tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#tangent-bundle)

$$
TN=E^s\oplus\mathbb RF\oplus E^u
$$

which is invariant under $D\phi_t$. There must be constants $C\ge1$ and $\kappa>0$ such that, for every $x$, every $t\ge0$ and every vector in the indicated fibre,

$$
\boxed{\|D\phi_t\xi_s\|\le Ce^{-\kappa t}\|\xi_s\|,
\qquad
\|D\phi_{-t}\xi_u\|\le Ce^{-\kappa t}\|\xi_u\|.}
$$

The first inequality defines the [Anosov stable bundle](../../../dynamical-systems.md#stable-bundle-of-an-anosov-flow); the second defines the [Anosov unstable bundle](../../../dynamical-systems.md#unstable-bundle-of-an-anosov-flow) and is equivalent to uniform forward expansion there. The remaining one-dimensional direction is the flow direction. The [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) can come from any [Riemannian metric](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-metric), since norms are uniformly equivalent on a [closed manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#closed-manifold). The essential requirements are a continuous invariant splitting and estimates uniform in both the base point and time, rather than separate exponential rates along individual orbits.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Here is the [quadratic-form criterion for an Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#quadratic-form-criterion-for-an-anosov-flow). Assume that the generator $F$ is nowhere zero and form the transverse [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle)

$$
\mathcal E=TN/\mathbb RF.
$$

Since $D\phi_tF(x)=F(\phi_t x)$, the derivative induces a linear cocycle $P_t:\mathcal E_x\to\mathcal E_{\phi_t x}$. Suppose there is a continuous nondegenerate indefinite [quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#quadratic-form) $Q_x$ on each fibre, with constant [signature of a quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#signature-of-a-quadratic-form), such that $t\mapsto Q_{\phi_t x}(P_t\xi)$ is differentiable and

$$
\mathcal DQ_x(\xi)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}Q_{\phi_t x}(P_t\xi)
$$

is continuous and **positive definite on every transverse fibre**. Then $\phi_t$ is an [Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#anosov-flow). An equivalent way to specify $Q$ is a [quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#quadratic-form) on $TN$ whose radical is exactly $\mathbb RF$ and whose induced form and derivative satisfy these conditions on the quotient.

On the [closed manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#closed-manifold), compactness makes the positivity uniform: for some $c>0$, $\mathcal DQ_x(\xi)\ge c\|\xi\|^2$. Thus the positive and negative cones are strictly separated by the linearized flow, yielding uniform unstable and stable transverse subbundles and their invariant lifts. The positive index is the unstable dimension and the negative index is the stable dimension. Strict positivity must be imposed transversely; imposing it on the flow direction would be impossible because that direction is carried into itself.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Use the [Levi-Civita connection](../../../general-relativity.md#levi-civita-connection) to represent a tangent vector to the [unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#unit-tangent-bundle) at $(x,v)$ by the initial data $(J(0),D_tJ(0))$ of the [Jacobi field](../../../general-relativity.md#jacobi-field) generated by a variation of the unit-speed [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic). The unit-speed constraint gives $\langle D_tJ(0),v\rangle=0$. Along the [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic), write

$$
J(t)=a\dot\gamma(t)+y(t)i\dot\gamma(t),\qquad D_tJ(t)=\dot y(t)i\dot\gamma(t).
$$

Here $a$ is constant, while the perpendicular [Jacobi field](../../../general-relativity.md#jacobi-field) equation is

$$
\ddot y+K(\gamma(t))y=0.
$$

The flow direction is represented by $(a,y,\dot y)=(1,0,0)$. Consequently $(y,z)=(y,\dot y)$ are coordinates on the transverse quotient. Define its nondegenerate [quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#quadratic-form) by $Q(y,z)=yz$. Its [signature of a quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#signature-of-a-quadratic-form) is $(1,1)$, and

$$
\frac{d}{dt}Q(y(t),z(t))=z(t)^2-K(\gamma(t))y(t)^2.
$$

Since the [Gaussian curvature](../../../second-fundamental-form.md#gaussian-curvature) is strictly negative and the surface is a [closed manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#closed-manifold), there is $k_0>0$ with $K\le-k_0$. Hence

$$
\dot Q\ge\min(1,k_0)(y^2+z^2).
$$

The [quadratic-form criterion for an Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#quadratic-form-criterion-for-an-anosov-flow) now proves **the geodesic flow is Anosov**. This is the [geodesic hyperbolicity from negative Gaussian curvature](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-hyperbolicity-from-negative-gaussian-curvature) argument. If the surface is not oriented, the local choice of perpendicular unit vector changes both $y$ and $z$ in sign; their product is unchanged, so the [quadratic form](../../../linear-algebra.md#quadratic-form) still exists globally.

When $K=-1$, the perpendicular solutions are $y=Ae^t+Be^{-t}$. The pure contracting data have $(y,z)=(B,-B)$ and the pure expanding data have $(y,z)=(A,A)$. In the [canonical coframe of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#canonical-coframe-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle), $H$ represents $(iv,0)$ and $V$ represents $(0,iv)$, so

$$
\boxed{E^s(x,v)=\mathbb R(H-V),\qquad E^u(x,v)=\mathbb R(H+V).}
$$

Equivalently the [Anosov stable bundle](../../../dynamical-systems.md#stable-bundle-of-an-anosov-flow) consists of pairs $(\eta,-\eta)$ with $\eta\perp v$, and the [Anosov unstable bundle](../../../dynamical-systems.md#unstable-bundle-of-an-anosov-flow) consists of $(\eta,\eta)$. The derivative multiplies these data by $e^{-t}$ and $e^t$, respectively, using parallel transport along the [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic) to identify the perpendicular directions. Together with $\mathbb RX$ they give the required invariant splitting.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [Livsic theorem](../../../dynamical-systems.md#livsic-theorem) says that for a [topologically transitive](../../../dynamical-systems.md#topological-transitivity) [Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#anosov-flow) on a [closed manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#closed-manifold), a Hölder continuous function $f$ is a [flow coboundary](../../../dynamical-systems.md#flow-coboundary) if and only if it has zero integral over every periodic orbit. In the integral formulation there is a Hölder continuous function $u$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{u(\phi_t x)-u(x)=\int_0^t f(\phi_sx)\,ds\quad\text{for all }x,t.}
$$

Equivalently, $u$ is differentiable along the flow and $Fu=f$. The periodic obstruction is

$$
\phi_Tx=x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int_0^T f(\phi_sx)\,ds=0.
$$

It is necessary because the left-hand side of the coboundary identity is zero at a period. The substantive theorem is its sufficiency. For a smooth flow and smooth $f$, the smooth regularity version gives a smooth $u$. Two such solutions differ by a constant: their difference is flow invariant and hence constant on a dense orbit and, by continuity, everywhere. This smooth version is the one needed in part (b).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Evaluate the smooth one-form on unit velocities to obtain $f(x,v)=\theta_x(v)$. Its integral along a periodic orbit of the [geodesic flow](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-flow) is exactly the one-form integral along the corresponding closed [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic). Negative curvature makes this geodesic flow an [Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#anosov-flow); the geodesic flow of a connected closed negatively curved surface is also [topologically transitive](../../../dynamical-systems.md#topological-transitivity). Apply the smooth [Livsic theorem](../../../dynamical-systems.md#livsic-theorem) to obtain $u\in C^\infty(SM)$ with

$$
Xu=\theta_x(v).
$$

If there is more than one connected component, apply this argument to each component separately.

The [vertical vector field of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vertical-vector-field-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle) differentiates rotation of $v$, so

$$
VXu=\theta_x(iv),\qquad V^2Xu=-\theta_x(v).
$$

For each $x$, writing $\theta_x(v)=A\cos\vartheta+B\sin\vartheta$ shows explicitly that

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\theta_x(v)^2\,d\vartheta
=\pi(A^2+B^2)
=\int_0^{2\pi}\theta_x(iv)^2\,d\vartheta.
$$

The fibre disintegration of [Liouville volume of a surface geodesic flow](../../../fiber-bundle.md#liouville-volume-of-a-surface-geodesic-flow) is $d\mu=dA_g\,d\vartheta$. Integrating the displayed equality over the base therefore gives $\|VXu\|_{L^2}^2=\|Xu\|_{L^2}^2$. Substitution into the supplied [Pestov identity for a geodesic flow](../../../inverse-problem.md#pestov-identity-for-a-geodesic-flow) yields

$$
\|XVu\|_{L^2}^2+\int_{SM}(-K)(Vu)^2\,d\mu=0.
$$

Both terms are nonnegative and $-K\ge k_0>0$. Thus $Vu=0$ everywhere. Since every unit-circle fibre is connected, $u$ is independent of velocity and descends to a smooth function $h$ on $M$: $u=h\circ\pi$. Its geodesic derivative is

$$
Xu(x,v)=dh_x(v).
$$

Comparison with $Xu=\theta_x(v)$, first on unit vectors and then on all vectors by linearity, proves

$$
\boxed{\theta=dh.}
$$

Thus the one-form is an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form). This proves the degree-one [geodesic X-ray transform](../../../inverse-problem.md#geodesic-x-ray-transform) conclusion directly from the given integral identity and the stated smooth coboundary theorem.

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

**No: the round sphere supplies a counterexample.** On the unit round $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$, restrict the smooth one-form

$$
\theta=x_3^2\,dx_1.
$$

The [antipodal map](../../../homology.md#antipodal-map) $A(x)=-x$ satisfies $A^*\theta=-\theta$, since $x_3^2$ is unchanged and $dx_1$ changes sign. Every unit-speed closed [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic) is a great circle, with $\gamma(t+\pi)=A(\gamma(t))$. Therefore its two half-circle integrals cancel:

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\theta_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t))\,dt
=\int_0^\pi\bigl(\theta+A^*\theta\bigr)_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t))\,dt=0.
$$

The same holds for all repeated traversals and either orientation.

However, its [exterior derivative](../../../differential-form.md#exterior-derivative) is

$$
d\theta=2x_3\,dx_3\wedge dx_1,
$$

which is not zero on the sphere. For example, at $p=(0,1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)$ the tangent vectors $u=(1,0,0)$ and $w=(0,1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2)$ give $d\theta_p(u,w)=1$. Hence $\theta$ is not a [closed differential form](../../../differential-form.md#closed-differential-form), and cannot be an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form), because $d(dh)=0$. This is the [counterexample to geodesic one-form injectivity on the round sphere](../../../inverse-problem.md#counterexample-to-geodesic-one-form-injectivity-on-the-round-sphere); periodic geodesic integrals alone do not imply exactness without an additional geometric hypothesis.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A [simple Riemannian manifold](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#simple-riemannian-manifold) has strictly convex boundary, no [conjugate points](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#conjugate-points), and a unique connecting [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic) depending smoothly on its endpoints. In particular its unit-speed geodesics are nontrapping. Write $\nu$ for the inward unit normal, and identify inward and outward parts of its boundary [unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#unit-tangent-bundle) by

$$
\partial_+SM=\{(x,v):x\in\partial M,\ |v|_g=1,\ \langle v,\nu\rangle\ge0\},
\qquad
\partial_-SM=\{(x,v):\langle v,\nu\rangle\le0\}.
$$

The signs are a convention; here $+$ means inward. If $(x,v)$ is strictly inward, let $\tau(x,v)>0$ be its first exit time. The [geodesic scattering relation](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-scattering-relation) is

$$
\boxed{\alpha(x,v)=\phi_{\tau(x,v)}(x,v)
=\bigl(\gamma_{x,v}(\tau),\dot\gamma_{x,v}(\tau)\bigr)\in\partial_-SM.}
$$

Thus it records both the exit point and the outward unit velocity, not just the exit point. On strictly outward vectors extend $\alpha$ by following the same [geodesic flow](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-flow) backwards to its previous entry point, retaining the velocity orientation. On tangential vectors set $\alpha(x,v)=(x,v)$. The extended [scattering relation](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-scattering-relation) is an involution on the full boundary unit bundle, since its forward and backward constructions undo one another. On a [simple Riemannian manifold](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#simple-riemannian-manifold), the travel time equals the [boundary distance function](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-distance-function) between the two endpoints; travel time is often recorded separately as lens data.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

First recover the induced boundary metric from the [boundary distance function](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-distance-function). If $c(t)$ is a smooth boundary curve with $c(0)=x$ and $\dot c(0)=w\in T_x\partial M$, the local metric expansion of distance gives

$$
\lim_{t\to0}\frac{d_{g_i}(x,c(t))^2}{t^2}=g_i(w,w).
$$

One can obtain this expansion in local coordinates from the fact that the metric tends uniformly to its value at $x$; distances to nearby points have that constant metric's first-order norm. Equality of the two [boundary distance functions](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-distance-function) makes the two quadratic values equal for every boundary-tangent $w$. Polarization therefore recovers the full bilinear induced metric:

$$
h=g_1|_{T\partial M}=g_2|_{T\partial M}.
$$

This does not yet identify the metrics on normal vectors or mixed tangent-normal pairs.

Let $\nu_i$ be the inward unit normal for $g_i$. The [boundary normal coordinates](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-normal-coordinates)

$$
c_i(x,r)=\exp_x^{g_i}(r\nu_i(x)),\qquad 0\le r<\varepsilon,
$$

are [collar neighborhoods](../../../differential-geometry.md#collar-neighbourhood) for a common sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. The [Gauss lemma](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry) gives $c_i^*g_i=dr^2+h_i(r)$ with $h_i(0)=h$. Hence the local boundary-fixing map $c_2\circ c_1^{-1}$ already satisfies

$$
g_1=(c_2\circ c_1^{-1})^*g_2\quad\text{on }TM|_{\partial M},
$$

because both pulled-back metrics there are $dr^2+h$.

To justify a global [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism), rather than assume a local collar map extends, interpolate the positive metrics by $g_s=(1-s)g_1+sg_2$, $0\le s\le1$. Their inward unit normals and exponential maps give smoothly varying collars

$$
C_s(x,r)=\exp_x^{g_s}(r\nu_s(x)).
$$

Compactness of $[0,1]\times\partial M$ gives a uniform collar size after shrinking $\varepsilon$. On the image of each collar define the time-dependent [vector field](../../../calculus.md#vector-field)

$$
Z_s(C_s(x,r))=\partial_s C_s(x,r).
$$

Multiply it by a smooth cutoff which is one for small $r$ and zero before the collar's outer edge, and extend it by zero to the rest of $M$. This gives a smooth global time-dependent [vector field](../../../calculus.md#vector-field). It vanishes on the boundary, because $C_s(x,0)=x$ for all $s$. Its flow $\Psi_s$ exists for the full interval and fixes the boundary. For sufficiently small fixed $r$, the curve $s\mapsto C_s(x,r)$ solves this flow equation, so uniqueness gives

$$
\Psi_s(c_1(x,r))=C_s(x,r).
$$

Here $C_0=c_1$ and $C_1=c_2$. Thus the final map $\psi=\Psi_1$ agrees with the local map from the $g_1$ collar to the $g_2$ collar near the boundary. In particular $D\psi$ fixes tangential vectors and sends $\nu_1$ to $\nu_2$ there. Since the tangent-normal splittings are orthogonal and the normals have unit length,

$$
\boxed{\psi|_{\partial M}=\operatorname{id},\qquad g_1=\psi^*g_2\text{ on }TM|_{\partial M}.}
$$

The construction only needs equality of the induced boundary metrics; the equal-distance hypothesis supplies that equality.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $\rho(x,y)$ be the common [boundary distance function](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-distance-function), with $x\ne y$. For each metric, the [simple Riemannian manifold](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#simple-riemannian-manifold) property gives a unique unit-speed [geodesic](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic) from $x$ to $y$, smoothly depending on the endpoint pair. Denote its initial and terminal velocities by $v_i,w_i$.

The [first variation of geodesic energy](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#first-variation-of-geodesic-energy), or equivalently length variation at unit speed, gives the endpoint formula

$$
d_x\rho(\xi)=-g_i(v_i,\xi),\qquad
d_y\rho(\eta)=g_i(w_i,\eta)
\quad(\xi\in T_x\partial M,\ \eta\in T_y\partial M).
$$

For completeness, if $J$ is an endpoint variation along a geodesic, differentiating its length and integrating the [covariant derivative](../../../general-relativity.md#covariant-derivative) by parts gives

$$
\delta L=\langle J,\dot\gamma\rangle\big|_0^L
-\int_0^L\langle J,D_t\dot\gamma\rangle\,dt.
$$

The integral vanishes by the [geodesic equation](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation), leaving precisely these two boundary terms and their opposite signs.

Because the full metrics agree on the boundary tangent spaces $T_xM$, the induced boundary metric $h$, unit spheres and inward unit normals agree there. The displayed covectors therefore determine the same tangential velocity components for the two metrics:

$$
(v_i)_T=-\operatorname{grad}_x^h\rho,\qquad
(w_i)_T=\operatorname{grad}_y^h\rho.
$$

Their normal components are fixed by unit length and the entry or exit sign. With inward unit normals $\nu_x,\nu_y$,

$$
v_i=(v_i)_T+\sqrt{1-| (v_i)_T|_h^2}\,\nu_x,
\qquad
w_i=(w_i)_T-\sqrt{1-| (w_i)_T|_h^2}\,\nu_y.
$$

Strict convexity makes these signs strict for distinct endpoints. Thus $v_1=v_2$ and $w_1=w_2$ for the same endpoint pair.

Now start with any strictly inward $(x,v)$ and let its $g_1$ exit point be $y$. The $g_2$ geodesic joining $x$ to this same $y$ has the same initial velocity $v$, so uniqueness of the [geodesic equation](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-equation) identifies it with the $g_2$ trajectory launched from $(x,v)$. Its exit velocity is also the same. Hence

$$
\boxed{\alpha_{g_1}=\alpha_{g_2}.}
$$

This is the [boundary distance determines geodesic scattering](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#boundary-distance-determines-geodesic-scattering) argument. The extension to outward vectors follows by the inverse scattering relation, and tangential vectors are fixed for both metrics. Equality of the travel times follows as well, since both equal $\rho(x,y)$.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Let $\pi:SM\to M$ be the [unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#unit-tangent-bundle) projection. Write $X$ for the [geodesic flow](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-flow) generator and $V$ for the [vertical vector field of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vertical-vector-field-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle), positively rotating $v$ toward $iv$. The acceleration prescribed in the question changes only this velocity angle, so the [surface thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#generalized-thermostat-on-a-riemannian-surface) generator is

$$
F=X+\lambda V.
$$

It never vanishes: $d\pi(F)=v\ne0$. Also $F$ and $V$ are linearly independent, since $d\pi(V)=0$.

The [vertical Maslov cycle of a surface thermostat](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#vertical-maslov-cycle-of-a-surface-thermostat) is

$$
\boxed{\Lambda_V=\{((x,v),L):V(x,v)\in L\}
=\{((x,v),\mathbb RF(x,v)\oplus\mathbb RV(x,v))\}.}
$$

There is exactly one such plane in each fibre, so $\Lambda_V$ is the image of a smooth section. A two-plane containing $F$ corresponds to a line in the rank-two quotient $T(SM)/\mathbb RF$; thus $\Lambda(SM)$ is a [projective bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#projective-bundle) with fibre $\mathbb{RP}^1$. Its total dimension is four and the cycle has dimension three, making it a smooth hypersurface. In this two-dimensional transverse space the [Maslov cycle](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#maslov-cycle) means the projective line meeting the distinguished vertical line, which here is just that distinguished line itself in each fibre.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

Use the derivative to transport the planes:

$$
\boxed{\phi_t^*((x,v),L)=\bigl(\phi_t(x,v),D\phi_t(x,v)L\bigr).}
$$

The derivative is an invertible linear map, so it sends a two-dimensional subspace to a two-dimensional subspace. The flow identity $\phi_t\circ\phi_s=\phi_{t+s}$ gives

$$
D\phi_tF(x,v)=F(\phi_t(x,v)).
$$

Hence a plane containing the generator is sent to another plane containing the generator; the displayed formula is a well-defined smooth lift to $\Lambda(SM)$. The [chain rule](../../../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives $\phi_{t+s}^*=\phi_t^*\circ\phi_s^*$, and $\phi_0^*$ is the identity. Thus it is a genuine lifted [smooth flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#smooth-flow), with inverse $\phi_{-t}^*$, rather than merely a family of maps of individual fibres. Its generator is $F^*=\left.\partial_t\phi_t^*\right|_{t=0}$.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

Let $H$ be the horizontal lift of $iv$ supplied by the [canonical coframe of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#canonical-coframe-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle). The frame $(X,H,V)$ has commutators

$$
[V,X]=H,\qquad [V,H]=-X,\qquad [X,H]=KV.
$$

Since $F=X+\lambda V$, the [Lie bracket](../../../lie-algebra.md#lie-bracket) product rule gives

$$
[F,V]=-H-(V\lambda)V,\qquad
[F,H]=-\lambda F+(K-H\lambda+\lambda^2)V.
$$

These formulas hold for every smooth $\lambda$, with no curvature or Anosov assumption.

Follow a tangent variation $\xi(t)=D\phi_t\xi(0)$ and express it in the moving frame as

$$
\xi(t)=a(t)F+y(t)H+z(t)V.
$$

Pulling this identity back by $D\phi_{-t}$ makes its left side constant. The derivative of the pulled-back frame vector $B$ is the pulled-back bracket $[F,B]$, so the coefficients satisfy

$$
\dot a=\lambda y,\qquad
\dot y=z,\qquad
\dot z=-(K-H\lambda+\lambda^2)y+(V\lambda)z.
$$

This derives the [linearized transverse equation for a surface thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#linearized-transverse-equation-for-a-surface-thermostat); its first equation in $a$ disappears on the quotient by $F$.

Near a vertical plane, $z\ne0$, and the smooth projective coordinate $r=y/z$ describes the plane as

$$
L=\mathbb RF+\mathbb R(V+rH).
$$

The [vertical Maslov cycle of a surface thermostat](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#vertical-maslov-cycle-of-a-surface-thermostat) is exactly $r=0$. Differentiating the quotient with the derived equations yields

$$
\dot r=1-(V\lambda)r+(K-H\lambda+\lambda^2)r^2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{F^*r=1\quad\text{on }\Lambda_V.}
$$

Since $r$ is a local defining function for the hypersurface, the nonzero normal derivative proves that **the lifted vector field is transverse to the Maslov cycle everywhere**. In particular, the sign follows from the forward derivative lift and the positive vertical-rotation convention, rather than from an unspecified projective orientation.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

Fix an orientation and a positive [volume form](../../../differential-form.md#volume-form) $\Omega$. Define the [divergence](../../../calculus.md#divergence) by the [Lie derivative](../../../differential-form.md#lie-derivative-of-a-differential-form)

$$
\mathcal L_F\Omega=(\operatorname{div}_\Omega F)\Omega.
$$

A smooth function is a [flow coboundary](../../../dynamical-systems.md#flow-coboundary) when it equals $Fu$ for some smooth $u$. Every other positive [volume form](../../../differential-form.md#volume-form) is $e^h\Omega$ for a smooth $h$. The product rule for the [Lie derivative](../../../differential-form.md#lie-derivative-of-a-differential-form) gives

$$
\mathcal L_F(e^h\Omega)=e^h\bigl(Fh+\operatorname{div}_\Omega F\bigr)\Omega,
\qquad
\operatorname{div}_{e^h\Omega}F=\operatorname{div}_\Omega F+Fh.
$$

Thus changing the reference [volume form](../../../differential-form.md#volume-form) changes its [divergence](../../../calculus.md#divergence) by a [flow coboundary](../../../dynamical-systems.md#flow-coboundary). If volume forms of either orientation are allowed, take the logarithm of the absolute ratio on each connected component; multiplying a reference form by a constant sign leaves its divergence unchanged.

If $\operatorname{div}_\Omega F=Fu$, the preceding product rule shows

$$
\mathcal L_F(e^{-u}\Omega)=0.
$$

Conversely, if $e^h\Omega$ is invariant, then $\operatorname{div}_\Omega F=-Fh=F(-h)$ is a [flow coboundary](../../../dynamical-systems.md#flow-coboundary). Vanishing of the [Lie derivative](../../../differential-form.md#lie-derivative-of-a-differential-form) is equivalent to flow invariance, since $\partial_t\phi_t^*\Omega=\phi_t^*(\mathcal L_F\Omega)$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\phi_t\text{ preserves a smooth volume form}
\iff\operatorname{div}_\Omega F\text{ is a smooth flow coboundary}.}
$$

The equivalence holds for every choice of reference [volume form](../../../differential-form.md#volume-form), proving the [invariant volume criterion for a smooth flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#invariant-volume-criterion-for-a-smooth-flow). The smoothness requirement matters: a merely measurable transfer function does not give a smooth invariant volume form.

<h3 id="5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/b)

This is a [Gaussian thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gaussian-thermostat), with generator and angular acceleration

$$
F=X+\lambda V,\qquad \lambda(x,v)=\langle E(x),iv\rangle.
$$

The canonical fields $X$ and $V$ preserve [Liouville volume of a surface geodesic flow](../../../fiber-bundle.md#liouville-volume-of-a-surface-geodesic-flow). To check this directly, use the [canonical coframe of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#canonical-coframe-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle) $(\alpha,\beta,\omega)$, with dual frame $(X,H,V)$ and

$$
d\alpha=\omega\wedge\beta,\qquad d\beta=\alpha\wedge\omega,\qquad d\omega=-K\alpha\wedge\beta.
$$

For the positive volume $\mu=\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\omega$, contraction gives $\iota_X\mu=\beta\wedge\omega$ and $\iota_V\mu=\alpha\wedge\beta$. Both two-forms are closed by the displayed structure equations, so the [Cartan formula for the Lie derivative](../../../differential-form.md#cartan-s-magic-formula) gives $\mathcal L_X\mu=\mathcal L_V\mu=0$.

The [product rule for divergence](../../../calculus.md#product-rule-for-divergence) now yields

$$
\operatorname{div}_\mu F=\operatorname{div}_\mu X+
\lambda\operatorname{div}_\mu V+V\lambda=V\lambda.
$$

The [vertical vector field of a surface unit tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vertical-vector-field-of-a-surface-unit-tangent-bundle) fixes the base point and satisfies $V(v)=iv$, $V(iv)=-v$. Since $E$ depends only on the base point,

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{div}_\mu F(x,v)=-\langle E(x),v\rangle.}
$$

This is the [divergence of a Gaussian thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#divergence-of-a-gaussian-thermostat). Reversing the overall sign of the chosen volume form does not change its divergence, so the answer is also valid if Liouville volume is written as the oppositely oriented contact volume.

<h3 id="5/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/c)

Use the following permitted kernel result, stated in the form needed here. For an [Anosov flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#anosov-flow) generated by a smooth [surface thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#generalized-thermostat-on-a-riemannian-surface) on a closed oriented [Riemannian surface](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#riemannian-surface), if a smooth function $u$ on $SM$ satisfies

$$
Fu=h\circ\pi+\eta_x(v),
$$

where $h$ is a smooth base function and $\eta$ is a smooth one-form, then $h=0$ and $\eta$ is an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form). Conversely these two conditions give a smooth solution by taking a base primitive. This is the [kernel of the thermostat X-ray transform in degrees zero and one](../../../inverse-problem.md#kernel-of-the-thermostat-x-ray-transform-in-degrees-zero-and-one). In particular it applies to [Gaussian thermostats](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gaussian-thermostat) without an additional curvature condition; it is stronger than the magnetic or geodesic special case.

Put $\theta=E^\flat$, so $\theta_x(v)=\langle E(x),v\rangle$. Suppose the thermostat preserves a smooth [volume form](../../../differential-form.md#volume-form). Write it as $\rho\mu$ with smooth $\rho>0$, choosing its sign separately on each component if necessary. By the [invariant volume criterion for a smooth flow](../../../dynamical-systems.md#invariant-volume-criterion-for-a-smooth-flow) and the divergence computation in part (b),

$$
0=\operatorname{div}_{\rho\mu}F=-\theta_x(v)+F\log\rho.
$$

Thus $u=\log\rho$ is a smooth solution of $Fu=\theta_x(v)$. Apply the stated kernel theorem with $h=0$ to obtain $\theta=df$ for a smooth base function $f$. The definition of the [gradient](../../../calculus.md#gradient) then gives $E=\operatorname{grad}_g f$.

Conversely, suppose $E=\operatorname{grad}_g f$. Since $d\pi(F)=v$, the vertical part has no effect on a base function and

$$
F(f\circ\pi)=df_x(v)=\langle E(x),v\rangle.
$$

The [divergence of a Gaussian thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#divergence-of-a-gaussian-thermostat) and the volume-change formula therefore give

$$
\operatorname{div}_{e^{f\circ\pi}\mu}F
=-\langle E,v\rangle+F(f\circ\pi)=0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{E=\operatorname{grad}_g f
\iff\phi_t\text{ preserves a smooth volume form},
\qquad \Omega=e^{f\circ\pi}\mu\text{ is invariant}.}
$$

Only the necessary direction uses the Anosov kernel theorem; the displayed invariant density works for any potential [Gaussian thermostat](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gaussian-thermostat). In particular its exponent is positive, as required to cancel the negative divergence found in part (b).
