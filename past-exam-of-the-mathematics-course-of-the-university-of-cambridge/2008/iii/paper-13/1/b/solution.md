<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [strong maximum principle for harmonic functions](../../../../../../strong-maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions.md) states that a $C^2$ [harmonic function](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md) on a connected domain which attains its supremum at an interior point is constant. Applying it to $-u$ gives the corresponding assertion for an attained infimum.

Let $M=u(x_0)=\sup_\Omega u$. The ball [mean value property for harmonic functions](../../../../../../mean-value-property-for-harmonic-functions.md) gives, for every sufficiently small ball about $x_0$,

$$
\int_{B_r(x_0)}(M-u)\,dx=0.
$$

The integrand is continuous and nonnegative. If it were positive at any point, it would be bounded below by a positive number on a smaller ball, contradicting the zero integral. Therefore $u=M$ on the whole ball. The set $A=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=M\}$ is relatively closed by [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and relatively open by this same argument at each of its points. It is nonempty. [Connected space](../../../../../../connected-space.md) forces $A=\Omega$, proving the principle. If the open set is disconnected, constancy is asserted on the relevant [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md).
