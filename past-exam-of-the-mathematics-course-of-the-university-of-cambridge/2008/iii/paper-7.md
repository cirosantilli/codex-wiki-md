<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Set $S=A\setminus\mathfrak m$, a [multiplicative subset](../../../commutative-algebra.md#multiplicatively-closed-set) because $\mathfrak m$ is a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal). The [quotient module](../../../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $B=A/\mathfrak a$ is a [cyclic module](../../../module-theory.md#cyclic-module) generated by $\bar1$, so its [localization of a module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-module) $S^{-1}B$ is zero precisely when $\bar1/1=0$. By the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization), this is equivalent to the existence of $s\in S$ with $s\bar1=0$ in $B$. The latter condition says $s\in\mathfrak a$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{B_{\mathfrak m}=0\iff\mathfrak a\cap(A\setminus\mathfrak m)\ne\varnothing\iff\mathfrak a\not\subseteq\mathfrak m.}
$$

Explicitly, if $s\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak m$, multiplication by $s$ annihilates $B$ but becomes invertible after [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring), forcing every localized element to be zero.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

The forward implication is immediate: the [localization of a module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-module) that is zero remains zero. Conversely, suppose $M\ne0$ and choose $0\ne x\in M$. Its [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory)

$$
J=\operatorname{Ann}_A(x)=\{a\in A:ax=0\}
$$

is a proper [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal), since $1\notin J$. Choose a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$ containing $J$. Then $x/1$ cannot be zero in $M_{\mathfrak m}$: otherwise the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) would give $sx=0$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak m$, contradicting $s\in J\subseteq\mathfrak m$. Thus some maximal [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) is nonzero, proving

$$
\boxed{M=0\iff M_{\mathfrak m}=0\text{ for every maximal ideal }\mathfrak m.}
$$

In the suggested cyclic description, $Ax\cong A/J$ and part (a) gives $(Ax)_{\mathfrak m}\ne0$; the same fraction argument embeds it in $M_{\mathfrak m}$. This is [localization detects zero elements](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-detects-zero-elements), applied to an arbitrary [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics); finite generation is unnecessary.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Suppose $f$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). If $f_{\mathfrak m}(x/s)=0$, some $t\notin\mathfrak m$ satisfies $tf(x)=f(tx)=0$. Injectivity gives $tx=0$, and the [vanishing criterion in a module localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#vanishing-criterion-in-a-module-localization) gives $x/s=0$. Hence every localized [module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function).

Conversely, suppose all $f_{\mathfrak m}$ are [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). For $x\in\ker f$, its image $x/1$ in $M_{\mathfrak m}$ maps to zero and therefore is zero for every [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$. The [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) argument in part (b) implies $x=0$. Thus $\ker f=0$ and $f$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). We have proved that [injectivity of a module map is detected at maximal localizations](../../../commutative-algebra.md#injectivity-of-a-module-map-is-detected-at-maximal-localizations):

$$
\boxed{f\text{ injective}\iff f_{\mathfrak m}\text{ injective for every maximal }\mathfrak m.}
$$

Equivalently, [exactness of localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#exactness-of-localization) gives $(\ker f)_{\mathfrak m}\cong\ker(f_{\mathfrak m})$, and part (b) detects whether this [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) is zero.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Use the defining criterion that a [flat module](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module) $E$ makes $u\otimes1_E$ [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) for every [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) [module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) $u$. By [right exactness of the tensor product](../../../module-theory.md#right-exactness-of-the-tensor-product), preserving injections is equivalent to preserving every [short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence).

First assume $E$ is [flat](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module) over $A$, and fix a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$. Let $u:U\hookrightarrow V$ be an [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) of $A_{\mathfrak m}$-modules. Regard it as an [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) of $A$-modules. There are natural [module isomorphisms](../../../module-theory.md#module-isomorphism)

$$
U\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}E_{\mathfrak m}\cong U\otimes_AE,\qquad V\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}E_{\mathfrak m}\cong V\otimes_AE.
$$

For example, $u_0\otimes(e/s)$ maps to $s^{-1}u_0\otimes e$, with inverse $u_0\otimes e\mapsto u_0\otimes(e/1)$. These are well-defined because every $s\notin\mathfrak m$ already acts invertibly on $U$. Under these [isomorphisms](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism) the tensor map is $u\otimes_A1_E$, which is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) by flatness. Thus $E_{\mathfrak m}$ is [flat](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module) over $A_{\mathfrak m}$.

For the converse, assume every $E_{\mathfrak m}$ is [flat](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module) over $A_{\mathfrak m}$ and take any [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $u:U\hookrightarrow V$ of $A$-modules. By part (c), each $u_{\mathfrak m}$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). Since [localization commutes with tensor products](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-commutes-with-tensor-products), the [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) of $u\otimes_A1_E$ identifies with

$$
u_{\mathfrak m}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}1_{E_{\mathfrak m}}:U_{\mathfrak m}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}E_{\mathfrak m}\longrightarrow V_{\mathfrak m}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}E_{\mathfrak m}.
$$

It is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) by the assumed local flatness. Apply part (c) again, now to $u\otimes_A1_E$, to conclude that it is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) globally. Since $u$ was arbitrary, $E$ is [flat](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module). Therefore

$$
\boxed{E\text{ is <flat> over }A\iff E_{\mathfrak m}\text{ is <flat> over }A_{\mathfrak m}\text{ for every maximal }\mathfrak m.}
$$

This proves [flatness is local](../../../module-theory.md#flatness-is-local) without any finite-generation hypothesis.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Take $B=\mathbb Z$, $N=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, and the [short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

Multiplication by two is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function), and the reduction map has image all of $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ and [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) $2\mathbb Z$, proving exactness. Any [module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) from $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z$ is zero: its generator must map to an integer killed by two. On the other hand, a homomorphism from $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ to itself is determined arbitrarily by the image of its generator, so its Hom group is $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Applying the [Hom functor](../../../algebra.md#hom-functor) therefore gives

$$
0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

**This sequence is not exact at $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$:** the incoming image is zero while the outgoing [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) is the whole nonzero group. In particular, the identity of $N$ cannot lift through the reduction map to a map $N\to\mathbb Z$.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For any [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $u:U\hookrightarrow V$, the [direct sum](../../../vector-space.md#direct-sum) decomposition $F=P\oplus Q$ gives natural [module isomorphisms](../../../module-theory.md#module-isomorphism)

$$
U\otimes_AF\cong(U\otimes_AP)\oplus(U\otimes_AQ),\qquad V\otimes_AF\cong(V\otimes_AP)\oplus(V\otimes_AQ).
$$

Under these, $u\otimes1_F$ is the block-diagonal map $(u\otimes1_P)\oplus(u\otimes1_Q)$. It is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) because $F$ is a [flat module](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module). If an element lies in the [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) of $u\otimes1_P$, insert zero in the other component; injectivity of the whole map forces that element to be zero. Hence $u\otimes1_P$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) for every $u$, which proves $\boxed{P\text{ is flat}}$. This establishes that [direct summands of flat modules are flat](../../../module-theory.md#direct-summands-of-flat-modules-are-flat), without a finite-generation assumption.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Here the Hom groups must be taken over $A$; the printed subscript $B$ in this part has no defined coefficient ring. For an arbitrary [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $P$, form the [free module](../../../module-theory.md#free-module)

$$
F=\bigoplus_{p\in P}Ae_p,
$$

and define a [module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) $\pi:F\to P$ by $e_p\mapsto p$. Each element of $F$ has finite support, so this prescription extends uniquely and is well-defined. It is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) because every $p\in P$ is the image of $e_p$. Thus every module is a quotient of a [free module](../../../module-theory.md#free-module).

Let $K=\ker\pi$. The [short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence) $0\to K\to F\xrightarrow{\pi}P\to0$ and the defining exactness condition for a [projective module](../../../module-theory.md#projective-module) make

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,F)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,P),\qquad s\longmapsto\pi s
$$

[surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function). Lift $1_P$ to a map $s:P\to F$. Then $\pi s=1_P$, so $s$ is [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function). Every $f\in F$ has the decomposition

$$
f=(f-s\pi(f))+s\pi(f),
$$

whose first term lies in $K$ and second lies in $s(P)$. If $s(p)\in K$, applying $\pi$ gives $p=0$, so the intersection is zero. Consequently

$$
\boxed{F=K\oplus s(P)\cong K\oplus P.}
$$

This is a [split short exact sequence](../../../module-theory.md#split-short-exact-sequence) and proves that [projective modules are direct summands of free modules](../../../module-theory.md#projective-modules-are-direct-summands-of-free-modules), including modules that are not finitely generated.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

First show that [free modules are flat](../../../module-theory.md#free-modules-are-flat). If $F=\bigoplus_{j\in J}Ae_j$, distributivity of the [tensor product of modules](../../../module-theory.md#tensor-product-of-modules) gives

$$
U\otimes_AF\cong\bigoplus_{j\in J}U.
$$

Explicitly, $u\otimes\sum_j a_je_j$ maps to $(a_ju)_j$, and the inverse sends a finitely supported tuple $(u_j)_j$ to $\sum_j u_j\otimes e_j$. For an [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $u:U\hookrightarrow V$, the tensor map is therefore the coordinatewise [injection](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $\bigoplus_JU\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_JV$. This proves flatness for any free rank.

By part (c), a [projective module](../../../module-theory.md#projective-module) $P$ is a [direct summand](../../../vector-space.md#direct-summand) of a [free module](../../../module-theory.md#free-module) $F$. The latter is flat by the preceding argument, and part (b) proves that its summand $P$ is [flat](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module). Therefore $\boxed{\text{every projective }A\text{-module is flat}}$. This proves [projective modules are flat](../../../module-theory.md#projective-modules-are-flat) directly from the exactness and splitting definitions.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 7](paper-7.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Choose $a_1,\ldots,a_d\in\mathfrak m$ whose classes form a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) of the [cotangent space of a local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#cotangent-space-of-a-local-ring) $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, and put $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)$. Spanning gives $\mathfrak m=J+\mathfrak m^2$. Thus the [quotient module](../../../module-theory.md#quotient-module) $N=\mathfrak m/J$ satisfies $N=\mathfrak mN$. It is a [finitely generated module](../../../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module), since $A$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring).

Here is the needed form of the [Nakayama lemma](../../../mathematics.md#nakayama-lemma), with proof. Choose generators $n_1,\ldots,n_r$ of $N$ and write $n_i=\sum_jc_{ij}n_j$ with $c_{ij}\in\mathfrak m$. For the column vector $n$, $(I-C)n=0$. Multiply by the adjugate to obtain $\det(I-C)n=0$. Since $\det(I-C)\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}$, this determinant is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) in the [local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring) $A$, so every $n_i=0$. Therefore $N=0$, proving $\mathfrak m=J$. Conversely, any generators of $\mathfrak m$ must span its cotangent space, so at least $d$ are needed. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mu(\mathfrak m)=d,}
$$

Here $\mu(M)$ denotes the [minimal number of generators of a module](../../../module-theory.md#minimal-number-of-generators-of-a-module) $M$.

For the additional minimum, the [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m$ is itself $\mathfrak m$-primary, so it supplies an eligible [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) generated by $d$ elements. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\min\{\mu(Q):Q\text{ is }\mathfrak m\text{-primary}\}\leq d.}
$$

The minimum here is over [primary ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideal). **A bound $\mu(Q)\leq d$ for every $\mathfrak m$-primary [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) would be false.** Indeed, for $A=k[[u,v]]$ and $\mathfrak m=(u,v)$, $d=2$ but $Q=\mathfrak m^2=(u^2,uv,v^2)$ is primary and needs three generators. To verify primaryness, if $ab\in Q$ and $a\notin Q$, then $b$ cannot be a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), so $b\in\mathfrak m$ and $b^2\in Q$. The three displayed monomials form a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) of $Q/\mathfrak mQ=\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^3$, proving the lower bound of three, while they already generate $Q$. This illustrates that [primary ideals can need more generators than the maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#primary-ideals-can-need-more-generators-than-the-maximal-ideal).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

A [formal power series](../../../commutative-algebra.md#formal-power-series) $f=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nx^n$ is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) exactly when its constant coefficient is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) in $A$. If $fg=1$, constant coefficients give $a_0b_0=1$. Conversely, for invertible $a_0$, set

$$
b_0=a_0^{-1},\qquad b_n=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^na_ib_{n-i}\quad(n\geq1).
$$

Then every positive-degree coefficient of $fg$ is zero and its constant coefficient is one, so $g=\sum b_nx^n$ is an inverse. This is a formal coefficient calculation and requires no convergence.

Since $A$ is a [local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring), its nonunits are exactly $\mathfrak m$. The nonunits of $B$ are consequently the series with $a_0\in\mathfrak m$, which are exactly $I=\mathfrak mB+xB$: such a series is $a_0+xg$, and conversely an element of $I$ has constant coefficient in $\mathfrak m$. The map $B\to k$ taking the constant coefficient modulo $\mathfrak m$ is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) with [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) $I$, so $I$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal). Every proper [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) consists of nonunits and hence lies in $I$, proving that $I$ is the unique [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal). Thus

$$
\boxed{B\text{ is local},\qquad\operatorname{Max}(B)=\{I\},\qquad B/I\cong k.}
$$

This proves the description of [formal power series over a local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#formal-power-series-over-a-local-ring).

To compute the [cotangent space of a formal power series ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#cotangent-space-of-a-formal-power-series-ring), expand

$$
I^2=\mathfrak m^2B+x\mathfrak mB+x^2B.
$$

Define the $k$-linear map

$$
\Theta:(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\oplus k\longrightarrow I/I^2,\qquad(\bar a,\bar b)\longmapsto a+bx\pmod{I^2}.
$$

Changing a lift $a$ by $\mathfrak m^2$ or a lift $b$ by $\mathfrak m$ changes the image by $I^2$, so the map is well-defined. Every series in $I$ is congruent modulo $x^2B\subseteq I^2$ to its first two coefficients, proving surjectivity. If $a+bx\in I^2$, its constant coefficient lies in $\mathfrak m^2$ and its linear coefficient lies in $\mathfrak m$, so both input classes are zero. This proves injectivity. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I/I^2\cong(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\oplus k,\qquad\dim_k(I/I^2)=d+1.}
$$

Finally, part (a) gives generators $a_1,\ldots,a_d$ of $\mathfrak m$, so $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_d,x)$. Use the [Krull height theorem](../../../commutative-algebra.md#krull-height-theorem) in its lecture form: in a [Noetherian ring](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), a prime minimal over an [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) generated by $r$ elements has height at most $r$. The problem allows Noetherianity of $B$ to be assumed. Apply the theorem to $I$ itself, whose height is the [Krull dimension](../../../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension) of the [local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring) $B$. Hence $\boxed{\dim B=\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq d+1}$.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Since $A$ is a [regular local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#regular-local-ring), $\dim A=d$ is finite. Choose a chain of [prime ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) of length $d$,

$$
\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_d=\mathfrak m.
$$

Such a chain exists because the supremum defining this finite integer [Krull dimension](../../../commutative-algebra.md#krull-dimension) is attained; a maximal-length chain in a [local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#local-ring) ends at its [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal).

For each $i$, the [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $\mathfrak p_i$ is finitely generated because $A$ is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring). This ensures that its extension $\mathfrak p_iB$ consists precisely of series all of whose coefficients lie in $\mathfrak p_i$: if $\mathfrak p_i=(b_1,\ldots,b_r)$, write each coefficient as $\sum_j b_jc_{jn}$ and gather them into the finitely many series $\sum_n c_{jn}x^n$. Thus coefficient reduction gives

$$
B/\mathfrak p_iB\cong(A/\mathfrak p_i)[[x]].
$$

The coefficient ring $A/\mathfrak p_i$ is an [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), and so is its [formal power series ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#formal-power-series): the first nonzero coefficient of a product is the product of the first nonzero coefficients of its factors. Hence each $\mathfrak p_iB$ is prime. Also $\mathfrak p_iB\cap A=\mathfrak p_i$, so the strict inclusions persist upon extension. Finally, $x\notin\mathfrak mB$, since its image in $B/\mathfrak mB\cong k[[x]]$ is nonzero. We obtain the prime chain

$$
\mathfrak p_0B\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_dB=\mathfrak mB\subsetneq\mathfrak mB+(x)=I
$$

of length $d+1$. Therefore $\dim B\geq d+1$. Together with part (b),

$$
\boxed{\dim A[[x]]=d+1.}
$$

Its [embedding dimension](../../../commutative-algebra.md#embedding-dimension) is also $d+1$, by part (b), so $B$ is itself a [regular local ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#regular-local-ring). This proves that [formal power series preserve regular local rings](../../../commutative-algebra.md#formal-power-series-preserve-regular-local-rings).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 7](paper-7.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Let $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$, let $S$ act on $B$ through $\varphi$, and write $\mathfrak pB$ for the [extension of an ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#extension-of-an-ideal) generated by $\varphi(\mathfrak p)$. Since $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=S^{-1}(A/\mathfrak p)$, the [fiber ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#fiber-ring) has the canonical presentations

$$
C=B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong S^{-1}(B/\mathfrak pB)\cong S^{-1}B/\mathfrak pS^{-1}B.
$$

The first [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism) sends $b\otimes(\bar a/s)$ to $\varphi(a)b/s$ modulo $\mathfrak pB$. Its inverse sends the class of $b/s$ to $b\otimes(\bar1/s)$. The tensor balancing relation, reduction modulo $\mathfrak p$ and the fraction relations show that these maps are well-defined and inverse. Thus the description is canonical and does not depend on chosen representatives.

Suppose $\mathfrak q$ is a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) of $B$ with $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p$. Then $\mathfrak pB\subseteq\mathfrak q$ and $\mathfrak q$ is disjoint from $\varphi(S)$. Its [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) $S^{-1}\mathfrak q$ is a proper prime, since the quotient is the [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) of the [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $B/\mathfrak q$. It contains $\mathfrak pS^{-1}B$, and therefore gives a prime

$$
\mathfrak q\longmapsto\mathfrak Q=S^{-1}\mathfrak q/\mathfrak pS^{-1}B\quad\text{in }C.
$$

Conversely, for a prime $\mathfrak Q\subset C$, take its contraction $\mathfrak q$ under the [ring homomorphism](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\psi:B\to C$, $b\mapsto b\otimes1$. Contraction preserves primality. Every $\varphi(a)$ with $a\in\mathfrak p$ maps to zero, while every $\varphi(s)$ with $s\in S$ maps to a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) and hence cannot belong to a proper prime. Thus $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p$.

These constructions are inverse. For a prime disjoint from $\varphi(S)$, the fraction $b/1$ belongs to its localized extension exactly when some $s\in S$ has $\varphi(s)b\in\mathfrak q$; primality then implies $b\in\mathfrak q$. In the other direction, every element of $C$ has a representative $b/s$, and membership in $\mathfrak Q$ is equivalent to membership of $b/1$ because the denominator is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring). Hence $\mathfrak Q$ is recovered by extending its contraction and quotienting. We have proved the canonical [fiber of the map on spectra](../../../commutative-algebra.md#fiber-of-the-map-on-spectra) correspondence:

$$
\boxed{\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}B:\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p\}\ \cong\ \operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)).}
$$

If a denominator maps to zero, the fiber ring is the zero ring and both sets are empty; the same argument includes this case.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

Let $\mathfrak q$ be a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) of $C$. Its [quotient ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#quotient-ring) $D=C/\mathfrak q$ is a nonzero [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) and a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space. For $0\ne a\in D$, multiplication by $a$ is an [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) $K$-linear map $D\to D$, because $D$ has no zero divisors. An [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism) of a [finite-dimensional vector space](../../../vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-vector-space) is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function). In particular $ab=1$ for some $b\in D$. Every nonzero element is therefore invertible, so $D$ is a [field](../../../algebra.md#field) and $\mathfrak q$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal).

To prove finiteness, select any $r$ distinct [maximal ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) $\mathfrak m_1,\ldots,\mathfrak m_r$. Distinct [maximal ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) are comaximal: $\mathfrak m_i+\mathfrak m_j$ strictly contains $\mathfrak m_i$, so must equal $C$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../mathematics.md#chinese-remainder-theorem) gives a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function)

$$
C\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^rC/\mathfrak m_i.
$$

Here is the needed surjectivity explicitly. For each $j\ne i$, choose $a_{ij}\in\mathfrak m_j$ with $a_{ij}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m_i}$, using comaximality. Then $e_i=\prod_{j\ne i}a_{ij}$ is one modulo $\mathfrak m_i$ and zero modulo the other selected [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal). Given any residue tuple represented by $b_i$, the element $\sum_i b_ie_i$ maps to that tuple. Thus the displayed map really is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function).

Each nonzero [field](../../../algebra.md#field) quotient has $K$-dimension at least one, so

$$
r\leq\sum_{i=1}^r\dim_K(C/\mathfrak m_i)\leq\dim_KC.
$$

There cannot be more than $\dim_KC$ [maximal ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal), because otherwise one could select a finite list exceeding this bound. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}C=\operatorname{MaxSpec}C,\qquad |\operatorname{MaxSpec}C|\leq\dim_KC<\infty.}
$$

This proves the description of the [spectrum of a finite-dimensional commutative algebra](../../../algebra.md#spectrum-of-a-finite-dimensional-commutative-algebra). If $C=0$, its spectrum is empty and the same conclusion holds.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

Choose algebra generators $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ for $B$ over $A$. Because the extension is integral, each $b_i$ satisfies a monic equation

$$
b_i^{e_i}+a_{i,e_i-1}b_i^{e_i-1}+\cdots+a_{i,0}=0\qquad(a_{i,j}\in A).
$$

Repeatedly reduce any exponent at least $e_i$ using this equation. Since the generators commute, every polynomial in them becomes an $A$-linear combination of the finitely many monomials

$$
b_1^{\nu_1}\cdots b_n^{\nu_n},\qquad0\leq\nu_i<e_i.
$$

Thus $B$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module) over $A$, with at most $D=\prod_i e_i$ generators. This proves the relevant [subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements](../../../commutative-algebra.md#subalgebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements) assertion rather than merely invoking it.

For a fixed [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak p\subset A$, form the [fiber ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#fiber-ring) $C=B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Tensoring the finite generating list shows $\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}C\leq D$: its [pure tensors](../../../module-theory.md#pure-tensor) with one span the fiber over the [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field) $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Part (b) therefore gives only finitely many primes in $C$. By the canonical bijection in part (a), these are precisely the primes of $B$ contracting to $\mathfrak p$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\#\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}B:A\cap\mathfrak q=\mathfrak p\}\leq\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}(B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p))\leq D<\infty.}
$$

No [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis on $A$ is needed. For an empty algebra-generating list, $B=A$ and the empty product $D=1$ gives the same conclusion.
