<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $p^a$ be the future-directed causal [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) of the fragment captured by the [Kerr black hole](../../../../../../kerr-black-hole.md). In signature $(-+++)$ its conserved [Killing energy](../../../../../../killing-energy.md) and axial [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) are

$$
E=-p_ak^a,\qquad L=p_am^a.
$$

The future-directed null generator of the [Killing horizon](../../../../../../killing-horizon.md) is $\chi^a=k^a+\Omega_Hm^a$, where $\Omega_H$ is the [Kerr horizon angular velocity](../../../../../../kerr-horizon-angular-velocity.md). At a crossing of the future [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md), the scalar product of a future-directed causal vector with a future-directed [null vector](../../../../../../null-vector.md) is nonpositive. For example, in a local orthonormal frame, $\chi=\alpha(1,\mathbf n)$ with $|\mathbf n|=1$ and $\alpha>0$, while $p^0\ge|\mathbf p|$; hence $p\cdot\chi=\alpha(-p^0+\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf n)\le0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E-\Omega_HL=-p\cdot\chi\ge0,\qquad E\ge\Omega_HL.}
$$

This is a local causal restriction, so it applies also to an extremal [Kerr black hole](../../../../../../kerr-black-hole.md). A massive fragment crossing a regular future [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) has a strict inequality; equality is the reversible limiting case, with causal [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) approaching the generator direction.

The [Penrose process](../../../../../../penrose-process.md) exploits the fact that $k^a$ becomes spacelike in the [Kerr ergoregion](../../../../../../kerr-ergoregion.md), allowing future-directed fragments with negative [Killing energy](../../../../../../killing-energy.md). For a positive choice of [Kerr horizon angular velocity](../../../../../../kerr-horizon-angular-velocity.md), a captured negative-[energy](../../../../../../energy.md) fragment must have sufficiently negative [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) to satisfy the inequality. Conservation of [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) in the splitting then gives $E_{\mathrm{escape}}=E_{\mathrm{initial}}-E_{\mathrm{capture}}>E_{\mathrm{initial}}$: the escaping fragment extracts rotational [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) and the captured fragment reduces the [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md)'s mass and [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md).

For the independent thermodynamic argument, take a subextremal [Kerr black hole](../../../../../../kerr-black-hole.md) and a small capture process covered by the [Physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../physical-process-first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md). To first order, $\delta M=E$, $\delta J=L$, so

$$
E-\Omega_HL=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\delta A.
$$

The [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md), under its classical [null energy condition](../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and regularity hypotheses, gives $\delta A\ge0$; since $\kappa>0$, this reproduces $E\ge\Omega_HL$. The ideal reversible limit has $\delta A\to0$. One should not infer a general finite-capture equality for an extremal [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) by simply substituting $\kappa=0$: the linearized nonextremal process proof and its differentiability assumptions must first be controlled. The causal proof already establishes the required inequality there without this issue.
