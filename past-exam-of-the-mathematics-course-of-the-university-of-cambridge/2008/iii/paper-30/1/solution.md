<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A nonempty compact [dynamical system](../../../../../dynamical-system.md) $(X,T)$ is a [minimal dynamical system](../../../../../minimal-dynamical-system.md) if it has no proper nonempty closed forward-invariant subset. Equivalently, every forward [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is dense: the [orbit closure](../../../../../orbit-closure.md) of any point is a nonempty closed forward-invariant set, and conversely a closed invariant set contains the [orbit closure](../../../../../orbit-closure.md) of each of its points.

Consider the collection of nonempty closed forward-invariant subsets of $X$, ordered by reverse inclusion. Every chain has an upper bound, its intersection: the chain has the finite-intersection property, [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) makes the intersection nonempty, and continuity is not even needed for its forward invariance. The [Zorn lemma](../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md) supplies a minimal member $Y$, so $(Y,T|_Y)$ is a [minimal subsystem](../../../../../minimal-subsystem.md). Also $T(Y)$ is compact, nonempty and forward invariant, so minimality gives $T(Y)=Y$. Here the printed word “nontrivial” must mean nonempty; a singleton with its identity map shows that a subsystem with more than one point cannot always be required.

If $y\in Y$, every tail of its forward [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is dense in $Y$, since it is the orbit of $T^Ny\in Y$. Therefore every neighborhood of $y$ contains $T^ny$ for arbitrarily large $n$. Choosing these times successively for balls of radii $1/j$ gives $n_j\to\infty$ with $T^{n_j}y\to y$. This proves the [Birkhoff recurrence theorem](../../../../../birkhoff-recurrence-theorem.md): **every [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) of a nonempty compact metric space has a recurrent point**. In fact every point of the chosen [minimal subsystem](../../../../../minimal-subsystem.md) is recurrent.

Write the continuous circle extension as the [skew product](../../../../../skew-product.md)

$$
\widetilde X=X\times\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,\qquad\widetilde T(x,y)=(Tx,y+\rho(x)).
$$

A sufficient condition, and in this setting also a necessary one, is the [circle skew-product minimality criterion](../../../../../circle-skew-product-minimality-criterion.md): for every integer $m\ne0$ there is no continuous function $f:X\to\mathbb S^1$ such that

$$
\boxed{f(Tx)=e^{2\pi i m\rho(x)}f(x)\quad\hbox{for every }x.}
$$

The [cocycle](../../../../../cocycle.md) $\rho$ is assumed continuous; a merely measurable [cocycle](../../../../../cocycle.md) need not define this topological extension.

To prove sufficiency, choose a [minimal subsystem](../../../../../minimal-subsystem.md) $Z$ of the extension. Its projection is a nonempty closed forward-invariant subset of $X$, hence all of $X$. For $a\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, vertical translation sends $Z$ to another [minimal subsystem](../../../../../minimal-subsystem.md), since it commutes with $\widetilde T$. Two such translates either are disjoint or coincide: a nonempty intersection is closed and forward invariant, so minimality forces equality. Define their common stabilizer

$$
H=\{a:Z+a=Z\}.
$$

This is a closed subgroup of the [circle group](../../../../../circle-group.md). Its closedness follows by taking limits of translations of points in the compact set $Z$, using both a translation and its inverse. Each fiber $Z_x$ is nonempty. If $y,z\in Z_x$, the sets $Z$ and $Z+(z-y)$ intersect at $(x,z)$, so $z-y\in H$. Conversely translating any fiber point by any member of $H$ stays in the fiber. Thus every fiber is exactly one coset of $H$.

If $Z$ is proper, $H$ is proper and therefore finite, say $H=\{0,1/m,\ldots,(m-1)/m\}$ for some positive integer $m$. The character $y\mapsto e^{2\pi imy}$ is constant on each fiber coset, giving a well-defined function $f(x)$ from $Z_x$. This function is continuous: its graph is the continuous image of $Z$ in $X\times\mathbb S^1$, and projection of that compact graph onto the Hausdorff space $X$ is a continuous bijection and hence a homeomorphism. The relation $\widetilde T(Z)=Z$ gives $f(Tx)=e^{2\pi im\rho(x)}f(x)$, contrary to the criterion. Hence $Z=\widetilde X$ and the extension is minimal.

Conversely, a solution of the displayed equation makes $F(x,y)=e^{2\pi imy}/f(x)$ a nonconstant continuous invariant function on the extension. Its nonempty level sets are proper closed invariant subsets, contradicting minimality. This also proves necessity of the [circle skew-product minimality criterion](../../../../../circle-skew-product-minimality-criterion.md).

Apply it with $\alpha=\sqrt2$, $X=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, $Tx=x+\alpha$, and $\rho(x)=2x+\alpha$. The base is a [minimal dynamical system](../../../../../minimal-dynamical-system.md): the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by an irrational angle is the whole [circle group](../../../../../circle-group.md), and its positive forward orbit has the same closure. To see the subgroup assertion, a proper closed subgroup of the circle is finite; if it contained an irrational angle it would be infinite. The elementary classification follows because a closed subgroup with arbitrarily small nonzero elements is dense, while otherwise its least positive representative generates a finite subgroup.

There can be no forbidden $f$ for any $m\ne0$. Indeed, the [winding number of a continuous closed path](../../../../../winding-number-of-a-continuous-closed-path.md) of $x\mapsto f(x+\alpha)$ equals that of $x\mapsto f(x)$, by translation and [homotopy invariance of winding number](../../../../../homotopy-invariance-of-winding-number.md). Consequently their quotient has [winding number](../../../../../winding-number.md) zero. But the proposed quotient $e^{2\pi im(2x+\alpha)}$ has [winding number](../../../../../winding-number.md) $2m\ne0$. Thus the [skew product](../../../../../skew-product.md) is minimal. Starting at $(0,0)$, induction gives

$$
\widetilde T^n(0,0)=(n\alpha,n^2\alpha)\pmod1,
$$

because $n^2\alpha+2n\alpha+\alpha=(n+1)^2\alpha$. Its [orbit](../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) is dense in the two-dimensional [torus](../../../../../torus.md), so its second coordinate is dense in the circle. Removing the initial point does not change density, since every orbit tail in a [minimal dynamical system](../../../../../minimal-dynamical-system.md) is dense. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\{n^2\sqrt2\pmod1:n\geq1\}\text{ is dense in }\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.}
$$
