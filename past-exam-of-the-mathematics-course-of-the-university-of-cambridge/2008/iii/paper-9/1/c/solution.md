↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**False as a whole: the [identity component](../../../../../../identity-component.md) is normal, but need not be open.** Write $G^\circ$ for the [identity component](../../../../../../identity-component.md). The [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $(x,y)\mapsto xy^{-1}$ maps the connected space $G^\circ\times G^\circ$ to a connected set containing $e$, hence into $G^\circ$. Thus $G^\circ$ is a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Conjugation by any $g$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) fixing $e$, so it maps $G^\circ$ onto itself; therefore it is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). It is also closed, since the closure of a connected set is connected and a [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md) is maximal.

For failure of openness, take the additive group $\mathbb Q$ with its usual topology. Any two distinct rational numbers can be separated inside $\mathbb Q$ by an irrational cut between them, so every connected subset is a singleton. Its [identity component](../../../../../../identity-component.md) is consequently $\{0\}$, which is not open because every neighbourhood of zero contains nonzero rational numbers.
