<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**True.** Choose a compact neighbourhood $K$ of the identity in the [locally compact group](../../../../../../locally-compact-group.md) and put $S=K\cup K^{-1}\cup\{e\}$. Inversion is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md), so $S$ is compact; it is symmetric and still a neighbourhood of $e$. Define

$$
\boxed{H=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}S^n.}
$$

The identity belongs to $S$, so these powers are increasing. Symmetry gives $H^{-1}=H$, and $S^mS^n=S^{m+n}$ gives closure under multiplication; hence $H$ is a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Each $S^n$ is compact, being the continuous image of the finite product of compact spaces. Thus $H$ is a [sigma-compact space](../../../../../../sigma-compact-space.md). Finally $S$ contains some open identity neighbourhood $U$, and $H=\bigcup_{h\in H}hU$ is open. This constructs the required [sigma-compact open subgroup of a locally compact group](../../../../../../sigma-compact-open-subgroup-of-a-locally-compact-group.md). The argument also works without Hausdorffness if local compactness means possession of compact neighbourhoods.
