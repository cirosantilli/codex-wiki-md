**False.** Give the two-element group $C_2$ the [indiscrete topology](../../../../../../indiscrete-topology.md) and take $G=\mathbb R\times C_2$ with the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md). The indiscrete factor is a [topological group](../../../../../../topological-group-split.md), since every map into an indiscrete space is continuous, and the product group operations are continuous. Points $(0,e)$ and $(0,a)$, $a\neq e$, have identical neighbourhoods, so $G$ is not a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). However, its continuous projection onto $\mathbb R$ is surjective. If $G$ were compact, its image $\mathbb R$ would be compact, which it is not.
