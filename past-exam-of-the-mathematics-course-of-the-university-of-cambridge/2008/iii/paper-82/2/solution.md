↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [method of multiple scales](../../../../../method-of-multiple-scales.md) applies when a small perturbation acts cumulatively over a long time, as in weakly perturbed [harmonic oscillators](../../../../../simple-harmonic-motion.md) or slowly modulated waves. A regular [asymptotic expansion](../../../../../asymptotic-expansion.md) can contain [secular terms](../../../../../secular-term.md), such as $\epsilon t\sin(\omega t)$, which cease to be small on the time scale of interest. Introduce independent fast and slow times, for example $t$ and $T=\epsilon t$, and allow the leading [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) and phase to depend on $T$. The [solvability condition in the method of multiple scales](../../../../../solvability-condition-in-the-method-of-multiple-scales.md) removes resonant forcing at each order and determines that slow evolution. For the [Mathieu equation](../../../../../mathieu-equation.md), these [amplitude](../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) equations reveal [parametric resonance](../../../../../parametric-resonance.md) and its instability intervals.
