<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Floquet theory](../../../../../../floquet-theory.md) suggests a sequence of instability tongues near

$$
\boxed{\omega=\frac m2,\qquad m=1,2,3,\ldots,\quad
\text{tongue width }O(\epsilon^m).}
$$

At these limiting [frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md) the unperturbed [Floquet multipliers](../../../../../../floquet-multiplier.md) $e^{\pm2\pi i\omega}$ coincide. Each multiplication by $\cos t$ changes a Fourier [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) by one, so $m$ forcing interactions are needed to couple $+m/2$ and $-m/2$. This explains both the sequence of [parametric resonances](../../../../../../parametric-resonance.md) and the successive powers of $\epsilon$ in their widths.

There is no implication that the exact half-integers themselves lie in every finite-$\epsilon$ growing interval. The tongue centers generally shift by $O(\epsilon^2)$, which exceeds the width when $m>2$. It is the narrow intervals near the shifted centers that contain exponentially growing solutions. [Frequencies](../../../../../../frequency.md) staying a fixed distance from every half-integer are stable for sufficiently small $\epsilon$.
