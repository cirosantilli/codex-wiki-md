↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

We construct an [infinite residually finite torsion group](../../../../../infinite-residually-finite-torsion-group.md) using the [Golod-Shafarevich infinitude criterion for pro-p groups](../../../../../golod-shafarevich-infinitude-criterion-for-pro-p-groups.md). Its useful form says that a pro-$p$ presentation on $d$ generators with relators $r_i$ gives an infinite [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md) whenever

$$
1-d\tau+\sum_i\tau^{D(r_i)}<0\qquad\text{for some }0<\tau<1.
$$

Here $D$ is the [Magnus degree of a free pro-p group element](../../../../../magnus-degree-of-a-free-pro-p-group-element.md): map each generator to $1+X_j$ in a noncommutative power-series algebra over $\mathbb F_p$, and take the lowest degree of $w-1$. The criterion permits infinitely many relators with a convergent weight sum. It is a consequence of the Golod-Shafarevich inequality for the completed group algebra; see [Ershov's survey, Theorem 3.1](https://m-ershov.github.io/Research/gssurvey_revised.pdf).

Take $p=2$, $d=2$, and $\tau=3/4$. Let $F$ be the [free pro-p group](../../../../../free-pro-p-group.md) on $a,b$, and enumerate all ordinary finite words $w_1,w_2,\ldots$ in these generators and their inverses. Choose integers $e_i$ sufficiently large that

$$
\tau^{2^{e_i}}<2^{-i-2}.
$$

Form the quotient [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md)

$$
P=F/\overline{\langle\!\langle w_i^{2^{e_i}}:i\ge1\rangle\!\rangle},
$$

where the bar means the closed [normal closure](../../../../../normal-closure.md). If the Magnus image of $w_i$ is $1+v_i$, characteristic two gives

$$
(1+v_i)^{2^{e_i}}-1=v_i^{2^{e_i}},\qquad D(w_i^{2^{e_i}})\ge2^{e_i}D(w_i)\ge2^{e_i}
$$

for nonidentity $w_i$; identity relators contribute zero weight. It follows that

$$
1-2\tau+\sum_i\tau^{D(w_i^{2^{e_i}})}\le1-\frac32+\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-i-2}=-\frac14<0.
$$

Thus the [Golod-Shafarevich infinitude criterion for pro-p groups](../../../../../golod-shafarevich-infinitude-criterion-for-pro-p-groups.md) proves that $P$ is infinite.

Let $G=\langle\bar a,\bar b\rangle$ be the abstract subgroup generated by the two images inside $P$. This subgroup is dense because the two images topologically generate the quotient, and it is infinite: a finite subgroup of a Hausdorff [profinite group](../../../../../profinite-group.md) is closed, so could not be dense in infinite $P$. Every element of $G$ is the image of some ordinary word $w_i$, and the imposed relation makes its order divide $2^{e_i}$. Therefore $G$ is a two-generated [torsion group](../../../../../torsion-group.md). Finally $P$ is an [inverse limit](../../../../../inverse-limit.md) of finite 2-groups. Any nonidentity element of $G$ is nonidentity in $P$ and hence has a nonidentity image in one of those finite groups. Restricting that map to $G$ proves that it is a [residually finite group](../../../../../residually-finite-group.md). We have obtained

$$
\boxed{G\text{ is finitely generated, infinite, residually finite, and every element has finite order}.}
$$

The abstract subgroup $G$ is essential here: the argument establishes finite order for ordinary words, not for every topological limit in the ambient [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md).

There is **no surjective homomorphism from this group to an infinite soluble group**. In fact [finitely generated soluble torsion groups are finite](../../../../../finitely-generated-soluble-torsion-groups-are-finite.md). To prove this, let $S$ be finitely generated, soluble and torsion, and induct on its derived length. In the abelian case, finitely many generators of finite orders generate a finite group. In general the [abelianization](../../../../../abelianization.md) $S/S'$ is a finitely generated abelian torsion group, hence finite. Its kernel $S'$ therefore has finite index and is finitely generated by [Schreier's lemma](../../../../../schreier-s-lemma.md). Explicitly, for a finite coset transversal $T$ containing $1$ and a finite symmetric generating set $X$, the finitely many elements $tx\overline{tx}^{-1}$ generate the finite-index subgroup, where the bar denotes the chosen representative of the coset. Now $S'$ is torsion and has smaller derived length, so induction makes it finite. Hence $S$ is finite as well. Every quotient of $G$ is finitely generated and torsion, so any soluble quotient must be finite.
