<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the [group representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md) generated by the displayed matrices. The coordinate reflections generate $(\mathbb Z_2)^3$, and the cyclic permutation conjugates $\kappa_x$ to $\kappa_y$, then to $\kappa_z$. Thus the group is the [semidirect product](../../../../../../semidirect-product.md) $(\mathbb Z_2)^3\rtimes\mathbb Z_3$, of order $24$. The converted TeX's multiplication sign must not be interpreted as a direct product of commuting factors.

To prove absolute irreducibility, complexify to $\mathbb C^3$ and let $W$ be a nonzero complex [invariant subspace](../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md). It is preserved by the coordinate projections $P_j=(I-\kappa_j)/2$. For a nonzero $w\in W$, at least one coordinate is nonzero, so $P_jw$ is a nonzero multiple of a coordinate vector. Applying the cyclic permutation repeatedly shows that all three coordinate vectors belong to $W$. Hence $W=\mathbb C^3$. Therefore **the real representation is absolutely irreducible**.

Equivalently, a commuting matrix must be diagonal because it commutes with all three reflections; commuting with $\rho$ forces its diagonal entries equal. This also confirms that every equivariant linearization is a scalar multiple of the identity, the condition needed in the branching calculation.
