<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take $B=\mathbb Z$, $N=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, and the [short exact sequence](../../../../../../short-exact-sequence.md)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

Multiplication by two is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md), and the reduction map has image all of $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ and [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $2\mathbb Z$, proving exactness. Any [module homomorphism](../../../../../../module-homomorphism.md) from $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z$ is zero: its generator must map to an integer killed by two. On the other hand, a homomorphism from $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ to itself is determined arbitrarily by the image of its generator, so its Hom group is $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Applying the [Hom functor](../../../../../../hom-functor.md) therefore gives

$$
0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

**This sequence is not exact at $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$:** the incoming image is zero while the outgoing [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is the whole nonzero group. In particular, the identity of $N$ cannot lift through the reduction map to a map $N\to\mathbb Z$.
