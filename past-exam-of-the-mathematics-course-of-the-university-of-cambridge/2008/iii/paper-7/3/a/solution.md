<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose $a_1,\ldots,a_d\in\mathfrak m$ whose classes form a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of the [cotangent space of a local ring](../../../../../../cotangent-space-of-a-local-ring.md) $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, and put $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)$. Spanning gives $\mathfrak m=J+\mathfrak m^2$. Thus the [quotient module](../../../../../../quotient-module.md) $N=\mathfrak m/J$ satisfies $N=\mathfrak mN$. It is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md), since $A$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md).

Here is the needed form of the [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md), with proof. Choose generators $n_1,\ldots,n_r$ of $N$ and write $n_i=\sum_jc_{ij}n_j$ with $c_{ij}\in\mathfrak m$. For the column vector $n$, $(I-C)n=0$. Multiply by the adjugate to obtain $\det(I-C)n=0$. Since $\det(I-C)\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}$, this determinant is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) in the [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) $A$, so every $n_i=0$. Therefore $N=0$, proving $\mathfrak m=J$. Conversely, any generators of $\mathfrak m$ must span its cotangent space, so at least $d$ are needed. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mu(\mathfrak m)=d,}
$$

Here $\mu(M)$ denotes the [minimal number of generators of a module](../../../../../../minimal-number-of-generators-of-a-module.md) $M$.

For the additional minimum, the [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$ is itself $\mathfrak m$-primary, so it supplies an eligible [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) generated by $d$ elements. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\min\{\mu(Q):Q\text{ is }\mathfrak m\text{-primary}\}\leq d.}
$$

The minimum here is over [primary ideals](../../../../../../primary-ideal.md). **A bound $\mu(Q)\leq d$ for every $\mathfrak m$-primary [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) would be false.** Indeed, for $A=k[[u,v]]$ and $\mathfrak m=(u,v)$, $d=2$ but $Q=\mathfrak m^2=(u^2,uv,v^2)$ is primary and needs three generators. To verify primaryness, if $ab\in Q$ and $a\notin Q$, then $b$ cannot be a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), so $b\in\mathfrak m$ and $b^2\in Q$. The three displayed monomials form a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of $Q/\mathfrak mQ=\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^3$, proving the lower bound of three, while they already generate $Q$. This illustrates that [primary ideals can need more generators than the maximal ideal](../../../../../../primary-ideals-can-need-more-generators-than-the-maximal-ideal.md).
