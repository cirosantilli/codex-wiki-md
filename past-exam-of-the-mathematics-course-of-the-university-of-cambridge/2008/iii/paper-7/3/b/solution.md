<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [formal power series](../../../../../../formal-power-series.md) $f=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nx^n$ is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) exactly when its constant coefficient is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) in $A$. If $fg=1$, constant coefficients give $a_0b_0=1$. Conversely, for invertible $a_0$, set

$$
b_0=a_0^{-1},\qquad b_n=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^na_ib_{n-i}\quad(n\geq1).
$$

Then every positive-degree coefficient of $fg$ is zero and its constant coefficient is one, so $g=\sum b_nx^n$ is an inverse. This is a formal coefficient calculation and requires no convergence.

Since $A$ is a [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md), its nonunits are exactly $\mathfrak m$. The nonunits of $B$ are consequently the series with $a_0\in\mathfrak m$, which are exactly $I=\mathfrak mB+xB$: such a series is $a_0+xg$, and conversely an element of $I$ has constant coefficient in $\mathfrak m$. The map $B\to k$ taking the constant coefficient modulo $\mathfrak m$ is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) with [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $I$, so $I$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md). Every proper [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) consists of nonunits and hence lies in $I$, proving that $I$ is the unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{B\text{ is local},\qquad\operatorname{Max}(B)=\{I\},\qquad B/I\cong k.}
$$

This proves the description of [formal power series over a local ring](../../../../../../formal-power-series-over-a-local-ring.md).

To compute the [cotangent space of a formal power series ring](../../../../../../cotangent-space-of-a-formal-power-series-ring.md), expand

$$
I^2=\mathfrak m^2B+x\mathfrak mB+x^2B.
$$

Define the $k$-linear map

$$
\Theta:(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\oplus k\longrightarrow I/I^2,\qquad(\bar a,\bar b)\longmapsto a+bx\pmod{I^2}.
$$

Changing a lift $a$ by $\mathfrak m^2$ or a lift $b$ by $\mathfrak m$ changes the image by $I^2$, so the map is well-defined. Every series in $I$ is congruent modulo $x^2B\subseteq I^2$ to its first two coefficients, proving surjectivity. If $a+bx\in I^2$, its constant coefficient lies in $\mathfrak m^2$ and its linear coefficient lies in $\mathfrak m$, so both input classes are zero. This proves injectivity. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I/I^2\cong(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\oplus k,\qquad\dim_k(I/I^2)=d+1.}
$$

Finally, part (a) gives generators $a_1,\ldots,a_d$ of $\mathfrak m$, so $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_d,x)$. Use the [Krull height theorem](../../../../../../krull-height-theorem.md) in its lecture form: in a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), a prime minimal over an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) generated by $r$ elements has height at most $r$. The problem allows Noetherianity of $B$ to be assumed. Apply the theorem to $I$ itself, whose height is the [Krull dimension](../../../../../../krull-dimension.md) of the [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) $B$. Hence $\boxed{\dim B=\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq d+1}$.
