<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Assume the finite [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is connected, as is necessary for a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md) to exist. For a disconnected [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md), the same procedures operate separately on its [connected components of a graph](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) and produce a minimum spanning [forest](../../../../../forest.md).

Let $T$ be a [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md). If it omitted the unique lightest [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $e$ across the specified [cut of a graph](../../../../../cut-graph-theory.md), adding $e$ would make exactly one [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md): the [graph path](../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) in $T$ between its endpoints, followed by $e$. Since those endpoints are on opposite sides of the [cut of a graph](../../../../../cut-graph-theory.md), that [graph path](../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) contains another crossing [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $f$. Distinct weights give $w_f>w_e$. The [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) $T+e-f$ is still a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md), but has smaller total weight, contradicting minimality. Thus **every minimum spanning tree contains $e$**, the [minimum spanning tree cut property](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree-cut-property.md).

[Prim algorithm](../../../../../prim-s-algorithm.md) starts from one [graph vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md), repeatedly choosing the lightest [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) from the reached [graph vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) to an unreached [graph vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). Connectivity ensures that such an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) exists until all [graph vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) have been reached. Each choice adds one new [graph vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and creates no [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). By the [minimum spanning tree cut property](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree-cut-property.md), every chosen [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) belongs to every [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md). After $|V|-1$ choices the selected [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) form a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md), necessarily a [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md).

For [Kruskal algorithm](../../../../../kruskal-s-algorithm.md), suppose the next accepted [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $e$ joins two different components of the current [forest](../../../../../forest.md), and let $S$ be one of those components. No previously considered lighter [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) crosses $S$: an accepted one already joins [graph vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) within a current component, while a rejected one had its endpoints connected when it was rejected and therefore still has them in a single current component. Consequently $e$ is lightest across the [cut of a graph](../../../../../cut-graph-theory.md) defined by $S$. The [minimum spanning tree cut property](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree-cut-property.md) again places every accepted [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) in every [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md). At termination the accepted [forest](../../../../../forest.md) must be connected, since otherwise a crossing [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of the original connected [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) would have been accepted when considered. It is therefore a [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md).

This reasoning also proves **the minimum spanning tree is unique**. Either procedure produces a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md) all of whose $|V|-1$ [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) belong to every [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md); another [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md) cannot have any different [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md).

For the [minimum spanning tree cycle property](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree-cycle-property.md), suppose a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md) $T$ contained the unique heaviest [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $e$ of a [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md) $C$. Removing $e$ splits $T$ into two components. The remainder of $C$ connects the endpoints of $e$, so it contains an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $f$ across these components. This [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) cannot belong to $T-e$, and $w_f<w_e$. Hence $T-e+f$ is a lighter [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md). Thus **no minimum spanning tree contains $e$**.

Finally consider the [reverse-delete algorithm](../../../../../reverse-delete-algorithm.md), processing [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) from largest to smallest weight. Whenever an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is retained because its deletion would disconnect the current [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md), it is a [bridge in a graph](../../../../../bridge-graph-theory.md); deleting other [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) later cannot create an alternative path around it, so it remains a [bridge in a graph](../../../../../bridge-graph-theory.md). If a currently processed [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $e$ is deletable, it lies on a current [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). None of that [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md)'s other [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) can be an earlier retained heavier [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), since those are [bridges in a graph](../../../../../bridge-graph-theory.md). Thus all other [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) on the [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md) are lighter than $e$. The [minimum spanning tree cycle property](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree-cycle-property.md) says that deleting $e$ removes no [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of any [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md) of the original [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md). Throughout, connectivity and the presence of the unique [minimum spanning tree](../../../../../minimum-spanning-tree.md) are preserved. At termination a surviving [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md) is impossible: any of its [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) was deletable when considered, since that entire surviving [graph cycle](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md) already existed then. The final connected [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is a [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md), and hence is exactly **the minimum spanning tree**.
