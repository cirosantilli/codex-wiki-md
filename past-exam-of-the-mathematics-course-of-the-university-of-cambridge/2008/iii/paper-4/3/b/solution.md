<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For generators $g,h$ of $P$, write $x=g-1$, $y=h-1$. Then

$$
kP\cong k[x,y]/(x^p,y^p).
$$

For each $\lambda\in k$, define a two-dimensional [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M_\lambda$ on basis $v,w$ by $xv=w$, $xw=0$, $yv=\lambda w$, $yw=0$. Every product of two of these operators is zero, so the defining relations hold even when $p=2$.

The action of $x$ is one nonzero nilpotent Jordan block. A decomposition of $M_\lambda$ would decompose its restriction to $k[x]/(x^p)$, which is already indecomposable. Thus $M_\lambda$ is indecomposable. An isomorphism $F:M_\lambda\to M_\mu$ commutes with $x$ and $y$, and consequently

$$
\lambda Fx=Fy=yF=\mu xF=\mu Fx.
$$

Since $F$ is invertible and $x\ne0$, $\lambda=\mu$. The infinite [field](../../../../../../field.md) supplies infinitely many parameters, proving **$kP$ has infinite representation type**. This is the [parameter family for an elementary abelian p-group](../../../../../../parameter-family-for-an-elementary-abelian-p-group.md).
