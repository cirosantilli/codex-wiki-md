<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Projection onto the subring in part (b), along the two-sided [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md), is the [Brauer homomorphism](../../../../../../brauer-morphism.md):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Br}_D:(kG)^D\to kC_G(D),\qquad
\sum_x a_xx\longmapsto\sum_{x\in C_G(D)}a_xx.}
$$

It is a unital [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md) homomorphism because it is the quotient by that [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) followed by the displayed subring identification.

Put $N=N_G(D)$. The projection is $N$-equivariant, since $N$ preserves the [centralizer](../../../../../../centralizer.md). Its restricted kernel is

$$
\boxed{\ker(\operatorname{Br}_D|_{(kG)^N})
=(kG)^N\cap\sum_{E<D}\operatorname{Tr}_E^D((kG)^E).}
$$

Equivalently it is spanned by the sums of the $N$-conjugation orbits on $G$ disjoint from $C_G(D)$. Normality of $D$ in $N$ ensures that an $N$-orbit either lies wholly inside that [centralizer](../../../../../../centralizer.md) or wholly outside it.
