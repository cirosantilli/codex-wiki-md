<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $I$ be the [augmentation ideal](../../../../../../augmentation-ideal.md) of $kN$, generated by the elements $n-1$. First prove its nilpotence. A nontrivial finite $p$-group has a central element $z$ of order $p$. Put $t=z-1$. In characteristic $p$, $t^p=z^p-1=0$, and

$$
kN/tkN\cong k(N/\langle z\rangle).
$$

Induction on $|N|$ says that the image of $I$ in this smaller [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) is nilpotent. Thus $I^m\subseteq tkN$ for some $m$, and $I^{mp}\subseteq t^pkN=0$. The trivial [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) starts the induction. This proves [nilpotence of a p-group augmentation ideal](../../../../../../nilpotence-of-a-p-group-augmentation-ideal.md).

The quotient homomorphism kills exactly the differences of elements in each $N$-coset, so

$$
\ker\tau=I kG=kG I.
$$

Normality of $N$ makes $I$ stable under conjugation by $G$. Moving [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) elements past factors from $I$ therefore gives $(I kG)^r=I^r kG$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\ker\tau\text{ is a nilpotent ideal of }kG.}
$$
