<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use the usual [Krull–Schmidt theorem](../../../../../krull-schmidt-theorem.md) setting, in which indecomposable summands have local [endomorphism rings](../../../../../endomorphism-ring.md); the paper's [rings](../../../../../ring.md) $\mathcal O,k$ satisfy it for the [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) involved. The [relative projective module](../../../../../relative-projective-module.md) condition for $H$ means that $M$ is a [direct summand](../../../../../direct-summand.md) of a [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) induced from $H$, equivalently, $M$ is a summand of $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\operatorname{Res}_H^G M$. A [vertex of an indecomposable module](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) is an inclusion-minimal such [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md). A [source of an indecomposable module](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) at its [module vertex](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) $D$ is an indecomposable $RD$-summand $S$ of $M\downarrow_D$ such that $M\mid S\uparrow^G$.

Existence of a [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) follows by decomposing $M\downarrow_D$ and using [Krull–Schmidt theorem](../../../../../krull-schmidt-theorem.md) on the induced decomposition: the indecomposable summand $M$ must occur in the induction of one indecomposable summand $S$. Such $S$ has [module vertex](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) $D$ as a $D$-module, since relative projectivity of $S$ for a proper [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) would make $M$ relatively projective for that [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md).

In the paper's modular coefficient [rings](../../../../../ring.md), an [indecomposable module](../../../../../indecomposable-module.md) is a [trivial source module](../../../../../trivial-source-module.md) when its [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) is the rank-one trivial [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $R$ of its [module vertex](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md). It is then a summand of $R[G/D]$, a [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md). Conversely suppose $M$ is an indecomposable summand of a [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md). Restriction to a [module vertex](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) $D$ is a sum of [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $R[D/E]$, one for each $D$-orbit of the permutation basis. For a $p$-group $D$, each transitive [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md) is indecomposable: over $k$ it has simple [head of a module](../../../../../head-of-a-module.md) over the [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) $kD$, and over $\mathcal O$ a nontrivial splitting would give a nontrivial splitting after reduction. A [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) $S$ must be one of these summands by [Krull–Schmidt theorem](../../../../../krull-schmidt-theorem.md). Its [module vertex](../../../../../vertex-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) is $D$, whereas $R[D/E]$ is relatively $E$-projective. Thus $E=D$, and $S\cong R$. Therefore **trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) is equivalent to being an indecomposable permutation-module summand**.

The rank-one formulation uses the paper's coefficient [rings](../../../../../ring.md). If the earlier general coefficient-ring wording were applied to a disconnected [ring](../../../../../ring.md), it would need qualification: for $R=k\times k$ and $G=1$, $(k,0)$ is an indecomposable summand of the [permutation module](../../../../../permutation-module.md) $R$, but is not the rank-one trivial [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $R$. One can instead allow indecomposable summands of the trivial coefficient [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) as [module sources](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) in that generality.

For the homomorphism request, take $M_i=\mathcal O[G/H_i]$. [Frobenius reciprocity](../../../../../frobenius-reciprocity.md) and [Mackey decomposition](../../../../../mackey-restriction-formula.md) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal OG}(M_1,M_2)
&\cong (\mathcal O[G/H_2])^{H_1}\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{g\in H_1\backslash G/H_2}\mathcal O.
\end{aligned}
$$

The summand for $g$ is generated by the sum of the cosets in the $H_1$-orbit of $gH_2$. The identical orbit-sum construction over $k$ gives one copy of $k$ per [double coset](../../../../../double-coset.md). These are free bases in both characteristics, since invariance means coefficients are constant on orbits, with no division by orbit size. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal OG}(M_1,M_2)\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(\bar M_1,\bar M_2).}
$$

The same argument applies to [direct sums](../../../../../direct-sum.md) of coset [permutation modules](../../../../../permutation-module.md).

If $M$ is a trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) $kG$-module, choose a permutation lattice $X$ with $M\mid\bar X$. Its projection is an [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) $f\in\operatorname{End}_{kG}(\bar X)$. The endomorphism reduction is surjective by the preceding calculation. Its kernel is $\mathfrak p\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal OG}(X)$, and the endomorphism [algebra](../../../../../algebra-split.md) is complete over $\mathcal O$. By [idempotent refinement theorem](../../../../../idempotent-refinement-theorem.md), $f$ lifts to an [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) $\hat f$. The lattice $\hat fX$ is a permutation-module summand, hence a trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) lattice, and reduces to $M$.

To prove uniqueness within the class of trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) lattices, let $L,L'$ be two such lifts. They are summands of permutation lattices. Lift a modular isomorphism and its inverse between them by the same homomorphism-surjectivity argument, inserting the summand projections on either side. The lifted composites are identity modulo $\mathfrak p$ and therefore invertible over $\mathcal O$, by [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md) or the determinant criterion. The lifted maps are consequently inverse up to an invertible composite and give $L\cong L'$. Thus **each trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) has a unique trivial [module source](../../../../../source-of-an-indecomposable-module.md) lift up to isomorphism**. This is [permutation homomorphisms lift through modular reduction](../../../../../permutation-homomorphisms-lift-through-modular-reduction.md); uniqueness is not claimed among arbitrary lattice lifts.
