<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the following permitted kernel result, stated in the form needed here. For an [Anosov flow](../../../../../../anosov-flow.md) generated by a smooth [surface thermostat](../../../../../../generalized-thermostat-on-a-riemannian-surface.md) on a closed oriented [Riemannian surface](../../../../../../riemannian-surface.md), if a smooth function $u$ on $SM$ satisfies

$$
Fu=h\circ\pi+\eta_x(v),
$$

where $h$ is a smooth base function and $\eta$ is a smooth one-form, then $h=0$ and $\eta$ is an [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md). Conversely these two conditions give a smooth solution by taking a base primitive. This is the [kernel of the thermostat X-ray transform in degrees zero and one](../../../../../../kernel-of-the-thermostat-x-ray-transform-in-degrees-zero-and-one.md). In particular it applies to [Gaussian thermostats](../../../../../../gaussian-thermostat.md) without an additional curvature condition; it is stronger than the magnetic or geodesic special case.

Put $\theta=E^\flat$, so $\theta_x(v)=\langle E(x),v\rangle$. Suppose the thermostat preserves a smooth [volume form](../../../../../../volume-form.md). Write it as $\rho\mu$ with smooth $\rho>0$, choosing its sign separately on each component if necessary. By the [invariant volume criterion for a smooth flow](../../../../../../invariant-volume-criterion-for-a-smooth-flow.md) and the divergence computation in part (b),

$$
0=\operatorname{div}_{\rho\mu}F=-\theta_x(v)+F\log\rho.
$$

Thus $u=\log\rho$ is a smooth solution of $Fu=\theta_x(v)$. Apply the stated kernel theorem with $h=0$ to obtain $\theta=df$ for a smooth base function $f$. The definition of the [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) then gives $E=\operatorname{grad}_g f$.

Conversely, suppose $E=\operatorname{grad}_g f$. Since $d\pi(F)=v$, the vertical part has no effect on a base function and

$$
F(f\circ\pi)=df_x(v)=\langle E(x),v\rangle.
$$

The [divergence of a Gaussian thermostat](../../../../../../divergence-of-a-gaussian-thermostat.md) and the volume-change formula therefore give

$$
\operatorname{div}_{e^{f\circ\pi}\mu}F
=-\langle E,v\rangle+F(f\circ\pi)=0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{E=\operatorname{grad}_g f
\iff\phi_t\text{ preserves a smooth volume form},
\qquad \Omega=e^{f\circ\pi}\mu\text{ is invariant}.}
$$

Only the necessary direction uses the Anosov kernel theorem; the displayed invariant density works for any potential [Gaussian thermostat](../../../../../../gaussian-thermostat.md). In particular its exponent is positive, as required to cancel the negative divergence found in part (b).
