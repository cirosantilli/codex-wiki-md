<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Identify $T^2$ with the product of two unit circles and use the unit sphere $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$. The [surjective degree-zero sphere-to-torus map](../../../../../../surjective-degree-zero-sphere-to-torus-map.md) is

$$
\boxed{F(x,y,z)=(e^{2\pi ix},e^{2\pi iy}).}
$$

Given any torus point, choose its two angle representatives with $x,y\in[-1/2,1/2]$. Then $x^2+y^2\leq1/2$, so $z=\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}$ completes a preimage on the sphere. Thus the map is surjective. The explicit [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) $F_t(x,y,z)=(e^{2\pi itx},e^{2\pi ity})$, $0\leq t\leq1$, contracts it to a constant map. By [homotopy invariance of mapping degree](../../../../../../homotopy-invariance-of-mapping-degree.md), $\boxed{\deg F=0}$, despite surjectivity.
