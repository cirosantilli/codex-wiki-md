<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

With $\hbar=1$, the free propagator is $U_0=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\omega_1t},1,e^{i\omega_2t})$. If $E_j$ are its [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), conjugation multiplies the $jk$ matrix element of $H_1$ by $e^{i(E_j-E_k)t}$. Therefore the [interaction picture](../../../../../../interaction-picture.md) [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) is

$$
\boxed{H_I(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
0&d_1e^{-i\omega_1t}&0\\
d_1e^{i\omega_1t}&0&d_2e^{i\omega_2t}\\
0&d_2e^{-i\omega_2t}&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Opposite off-diagonal phases are complex conjugates, as required by Hermiticity. The upper $2$–$3$ element has the opposite phase sign to the upper $1$–$2$ element because the middle level lies above both outer levels.
