↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $H=\mathbb C^{S_3}$, the [function Hopf algebra of a finite group](../../../../../../function-hopf-algebra-of-a-finite-group.md). Multiplication is pointwise, and

$$
(\Delta f)(g,h)=f(gh),\qquad\varepsilon(f)=f(1),\qquad(Sf)(g)=f(g^{-1}).
$$

The group associativity, identity and inverse laws respectively verify coassociativity, the counit and the [antipode](../../../../../../antipode.md); pointwise products show that $\Delta,\varepsilon$ are algebra homomorphisms. Thus $H$ is a [commutative Hopf algebra](../../../../../../commutative-hopf-algebra.md).

For $g=(12)$ and $h=(23)$, using right-to-left permutation composition, $gh=(123)$ whereas $hg=(132)$. The delta function $f=\delta_{(123)}$ has $(\Delta f)(g,h)=1$ but $(\Delta^{\rm op}f)(g,h)=0$. Therefore **$\boxed{\mathbb C^{S_3}\text{ is commutative but noncocommutative}}$.**
