<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [group algebra](../../../../../../group-algebra.md) $H=\mathbb C[S_3]$ with

$$
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\qquad\varepsilon(g)=1,\qquad S(g)=g^{-1}
$$

on its group basis, extended linearly. Multiplication of group basis elements makes $\Delta,\varepsilon$ algebra homomorphisms, and $g^{-1}g=gg^{-1}=1$ verifies the [antipode](../../../../../../antipode.md) identities. The coproduct is fixed by the tensor flip on every basis element, so this is a [cocommutative Hopf algebra](../../../../../../cocommutative-hopf-algebra.md). But $(12)(23)$ and $(23)(12)$ are distinct basis elements, proving noncommutativity. Thus **$\boxed{\mathbb C[S_3]\text{ is noncommutative but cocommutative}}$.**
