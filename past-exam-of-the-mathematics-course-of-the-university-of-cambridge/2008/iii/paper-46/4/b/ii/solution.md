<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Cohort A alone would give $\widehat F_A(2)=48/100=0.48$. But two-year survival is the product of first-year survival and conditional survival through the second year. Cohort B contributes a full year's information to the first factor even though it has no second-year follow-up. Omitting it discards useful early deaths and survivors. The pooled estimate $0.47$ combines the first-year information from both cohorts with the conditional second-year information from cohort A.

Thus **lack of two-year follow-up is handled by censoring, not by removing the entire short-follow-up cohort**. This is [cohort pooling under administrative censoring](../../../../../../../cohort-pooling-under-administrative-censoring.md). The criticism assumes comparable time-since-entry [survival distributions](../../../../../../../survival-distribution.md): if enrolment cohort or calendar period genuinely changes the [hazard](../../../../../../../hazard-function.md), naive pooling is not justified. One should model or stratify that effect, and an estimand specifically restricted to cohort A may appropriately use its own [survival distribution](../../../../../../../survival-distribution.md).
