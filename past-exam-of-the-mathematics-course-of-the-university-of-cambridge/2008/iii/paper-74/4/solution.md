<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

**The [solar cycle](../../../../../solar-cycle.md) is an oscillation of a magnetic dynamo, with an activity period near eleven years and a magnetic polarity period near twenty-two years.** The observational task is to explain a structured, variable cycle rather than a perfectly periodic reversal of one uniform field. [Sunspots](../../../../../sunspot.md) are cool photospheric concentrations of strong [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md), often occurring as bipolar active regions. Their number rises and falls through successive [solar cycles](../../../../../solar-cycle.md). The [solar butterfly diagram](../../../../../solar-butterfly-diagram.md) records new-cycle spots at middle latitudes and their subsequent equatorward drift; successive cycles overlap near activity minimum. Cycle lengths and amplitudes vary, and the two hemispheres need not evolve in exact synchrony.

Magnetic observations impose stronger constraints than the spot count alone. [Hale's polarity law](../../../../../hale-s-polarity-law.md) gives opposite preferred leading polarities in opposite hemispheres and reverses those assignments in the next activity cycle. [Joy's law](../../../../../joy-s-law.md) gives a statistical tilt with following polarity farther from the equator. The large-scale polar [poloidal magnetic field](../../../../../poloidal-magnetic-field.md) is strongest around activity minimum and reverses near activity maximum, while the spot-producing [toroidal magnetic field](../../../../../toroidal-magnetic-field.md) is prominent during the active phase. Poleward transport of dispersed active-region flux accompanies these polar reversals. The [solar cycle](../../../../../solar-cycle.md) is also visible in coronal activity, flares, ultraviolet emission and the heliospheric magnetic environment. A model must reproduce these migration and phase relations, as well as the cycle's preferred period and predominantly dipolar large-scale [parity](../../../../../parity.md).

[solar grand minima](../../../../../solar-grand-minimum.md) are intervals of unusually weak activity lasting several normal cycles. The [Maunder minimum](../../../../../maunder-minimum.md), approximately 1645–1715, is documented by historical [sunspot](../../../../../sunspot.md) observations. Longer records use cosmogenic carbon-14 in tree rings and beryllium-10 in ice: [cosmic rays](../../../../../cosmic-ray.md) produce these [isotopes](../../../../../isotope.md), and solar magnetic shielding modulates the production. Such records require allowance for terrestrial magnetic shielding and [isotope](../../../../../isotope.md) transport, so they are proxies rather than direct measurements of the solar interior. Very few spots do not prove that the [solar magnetic field](../../../../../solar-magnetic-field.md) or every magnetic oscillation vanished; weak cyclic activity can remain below the threshold for abundant spot emergence.

The energy source for a [solar dynamo](../../../../../solar-dynamo.md) is conducting fluid motion, sustained by [convection](../../../../../convection.md) and rotation. The [resistive induction equation](../../../../../resistive-induction-equation.md) contains stretching, [advection](../../../../../advection.md) and [magnetic diffusion](../../../../../magnetic-diffusion.md); a fossil field merely diffusing away cannot account for the organized recurrent reversals. In an axisymmetric mean-field description, separate a [poloidal magnetic field](../../../../../poloidal-magnetic-field.md) from a [toroidal magnetic field](../../../../../toroidal-magnetic-field.md). [Differential rotation](../../../../../differential-rotation.md) stretches poloidal flux into toroidal flux through the [Omega effect](../../../../../omega-effect.md), with source proportional to $r\sin\theta(\mathbf B_{\rm pol}\cdot\nabla)\Omega$. This alone cannot complete a self-sustaining regeneration loop. Nonaxisymmetric motions or flux emergence must restore poloidal flux, consistent with the [Cowling anti-dynamo theorem](../../../../../cowling-anti-dynamo-theorem.md).

The averaged induction equation is

$$
\partial_t\overline{\mathbf B}=\nabla\times\left(\overline{\mathbf U}\times\overline{\mathbf B}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}-\eta\nabla\times\overline{\mathbf B}\right),\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}=\langle\mathbf u\times\mathbf b\rangle.
$$

A local isotropic illustrative closure is $\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}=\alpha\overline{\mathbf B}-\eta_t\nabla\times\overline{\mathbf B}$; solar rotation and stratification generally require more general tensor coefficients. Helical motions can provide an [alpha effect](../../../../../alpha-effect.md), with the kinematic short-correlation estimate $\alpha\simeq-(\tau/3)\langle\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf u\rangle$. Coupling this poloidal regeneration to shear gives an [alpha-Omega dynamo](../../../../../alpha-omega-dynamo.md). The feedback introduces a phase lag, so an [eigenmode](../../../../../normal-mode.md) can oscillate and reverse sign. [Parker dynamo waves](../../../../../parker-dynamo-wave.md) also migrate, with direction constrained by the signs of the alpha coefficient and shear. Producing equatorward rather than poleward migration is therefore a physical test of the chosen source and rotation profile, not an automatic consequence of any oscillatory solution.

One theoretical scenario is a distributed dynamo in the [convection zone](../../../../../convection-zone.md), where turbulent correlations and shear operate in overlapping regions; near-surface shear may contribute. Another is an [interface solar dynamo](../../../../../interface-solar-dynamo.md), separating a helical regeneration region from a strong-shear reservoir near the [tachocline](../../../../../tachocline.md). The rotational transition and stable layer offer possible toroidal-field amplification and storage, but transporting field between separated regions is essential. Flux-tube or magnetohydrodynamic instabilities can supply alternative regeneration processes. Fully dynamical models solve the momentum and induction equations together and generate [differential rotation](../../../../../differential-rotation.md) and turbulent correlations rather than prescribing all of them, at the cost of difficult solar parameter regimes and nonlinear saturation.

In the [Babcock-Leighton mechanism](../../../../../babcock-leighton-mechanism.md), tilted bipolar regions rise through [magnetic buoyancy](../../../../../magnetic-buoyancy.md) and disperse. Their systematically separated polarities undergo different cancellation and transport: following-polarity flux reaching the poles helps reverse and rebuild the polar field. This makes the observed tilt a source of poloidal regeneration and links surface processes to interior toroidal flux. A [flux-transport solar dynamo](../../../../../flux-transport-solar-dynamo.md) connects these source regions by [meridional circulation in a star](../../../../../meridional-circulation-in-a-star.md), [turbulent diffusion](../../../../../eddy-diffusion.md) and [turbulent magnetic pumping](../../../../../turbulent-magnetic-pumping.md). Poleward surface transport and an assumed equatorward deep return can help explain the opposite migration of polar and spot-bearing fields. The period is sensitive to circulation in an advection-dominated model, whereas [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) dominates the coupling in a different regime. Thus similar observed cycles may arise from different transport assumptions.

A linear growing dynamo must be saturated. The [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) can modify [differential rotation](../../../../../differential-rotation.md) and [convection](../../../../../convection.md), and [dynamo quenching](../../../../../dynamo-quenching.md) can reduce or alter the [alpha effect](../../../../../alpha-effect.md). Magnetic-helicity constraints couple the large-scale and fluctuating fields; transport of helicity can matter for avoiding excessively strong quenching. Random variations of active-region tilts or turbulent coefficients produce amplitude fluctuations. Nonlinear mode competition and deterministic modulation can also produce weak epochs, while stochastic or internally generated intermittency can move a dynamo close to or below its excitation threshold. These are competing explanations for [solar grand minima](../../../../../solar-grand-minimum.md). A successful scenario must explain recovery as well as suppression: a poloidal source that depends entirely on large emerging spots becomes weak precisely when those spots disappear, so recovery may require a residual weak-field regeneration mechanism. Observed hemispheric asymmetry and persistence or loss of cycle phase during a weak epoch offer ways to distinguish the scenarios. The observations establish an organized magnetic cycle; they do not select one unique combination of source location, transport and nonlinear modulation.
