<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $Q_1,Q_2$ are [equivalent martingale measures](../../../../../../risk-neutral-measure.md). Fix a finite maturity $T$ and an event $E\in\mathcal F_T$. Because the market is a [complete market](../../../../../../complete-market.md), replicate the claim $H=B_T\mathbf1_E$, whose discounted payoff is bounded. For a [self-financing strategy](../../../../../../self-financing-portfolio.md), the discounted value satisfies

$$
\widetilde V_{t+1}-\widetilde V_t=h^S_t(\widetilde S_{t+1}-\widetilde S_t),\qquad\widetilde S=S/B.
$$

Under either pricing measure its conditional mean increment is zero, since $h^S_t$ is known at time $t$ and the discounted stock is a [martingale](../../../../../../martingale-split.md). In the standard admissible discrete-time pricing class these finite-horizon values are integrable; a constant lower bound on discounted wealth also gives [integrability](../../../../../../integrability.md) successively from the conditional increment formula. Therefore replication and taking [expectations](../../../../../../expected-value.md) give

$$
\frac{V_0}{B_0}=\mathbb E_{Q_j}[H/B_T]=Q_j(E),\qquad j=1,2.
$$

The replicating [portfolio](../../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) and its initial cost are the same for both measures, so $Q_1(E)=Q_2(E)$. Since $E$ was arbitrary, the measures agree on $\mathcal F_T$, for every finite $T$, and hence on the [sigma-algebra](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) generated by the market [filtration](../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md). Thus $\boxed{\text{the equivalent martingale measure is unique}}$. This is the measure version of [complete markets have unique state-price densities](../../../../../../complete-markets-have-unique-state-price-densities.md).
