<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

After transverse equilibration, a localized release on a sufficiently long aquifer has the [advection-diffusion heat-kernel solution](../../../../../../advection-diffusion-heat-kernel-solution.md)

$$
\boxed{C(x,t)=\frac{\mathcal M}{\sqrt{4\pi D_{\rm eff}t}}
\exp\left[-\frac{(x-x_0-Ut)^2}{4D_{\rm eff}t}\right],}
$$

where $\mathcal M=\int C\,dx$ is conserved. The actual tracer amount per unit source length is $\phi h\mathcal M$ in this normalization. Its center moves at the mean [interstitial velocity](../../../../../../pore-velocity.md) $U$, its variance is $2D_{\rm eff}t$, and its peak decreases as $t^{-1/2}$. A finite initial packet is convolved with this kernel.

Before times of order $h^2/D$, this effective Gaussian description need not hold. The parabolic velocity profile initially shears a packet, and particles initially at different depths acquire different along-flow displacements. Transverse [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) subsequently erases depth memory and produces the long-time [Taylor dispersion](../../../../../../taylor-dispersion.md) law. A short pulse can therefore have an initially asymmetric or strongly distorted longitudinal shape before entering the averaged regime.
