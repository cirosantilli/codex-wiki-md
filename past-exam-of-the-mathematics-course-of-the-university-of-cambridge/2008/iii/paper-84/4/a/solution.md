↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [K-epsilon turbulence model](../../../../../../k-epsilon-turbulence-model.md) evolves [turbulent kinetic energy](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy.md) $k$ and its [turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy-dissipation-rate.md) $\epsilon$, rather than resolving turbulent fluctuations. It closes the [Reynolds stress](../../../../../../reynolds-stress.md) with an isotropic [eddy viscosity](../../../../../../eddy-viscosity.md), $\nu_T=C_\mu k^2/\epsilon$, and models transport of $k$ and $\epsilon$ by gradient diffusion with coefficients $\nu_T/\sigma_k$ and $\nu_T/\sigma_\epsilon$. Production is tied to mean strain; dissipation-equation production and destruction are modeled terms with empirical constants. The characteristic velocity and length are $\sqrt k$ and $k^{3/2}/\epsilon$. These assumptions are primarily for high [Reynolds number](../../../../../../reynolds-number.md), and strongly anisotropic flow, sharp interfaces and molecular-viscosity-dominated regions need additional treatment. The dissipation equation is a closure, not a second exact energy-conservation law.
