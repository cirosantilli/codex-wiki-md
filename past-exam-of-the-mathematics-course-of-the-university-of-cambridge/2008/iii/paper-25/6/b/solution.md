<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Noether–Lefschetz theorem](../../../../../../noether-lefschetz-theorem.md) says that a [very general](../../../../../../very-general-point-of-an-algebraic-parameter-space.md) smooth degree-$d$ surface in $\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}$, for $d\geq4$, has [Picard group](../../../../../../picard-group.md) freely generated by its hyperplane class $H$. Here very general means outside a countable union of proper closed loci in the parameter space of surfaces.

If such a surface contained a [smooth rational curve](../../../../../../smooth-rational-curve.md) $C$, its divisor class would be $mH$ for an integer $m$. Since $H\cdot C=md>0$, one has $m>0$ and therefore $C^2=m^2d>0$. Part (a), however, gives $C^2=-2-(d-4)\deg C\leq-2$, a contradiction. Thus **a [very general surface of degree at least four has no smooth rational curve](../../../../../../very-general-surface-of-degree-at-least-four-has-no-smooth-rational-curve.md)**. The statement concerns smooth embedded curves; a singular curve can still have rational normalization.
