<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use [countability of negative curves](../../../../../../countability-of-negative-curves.md). A [smooth projective surface](../../../../../../smooth-projective-surface.md) over $\mathbb C$ is a [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) [smooth manifold](../../../../../../smooth-manifold.md) homotopy equivalent to a [finite CW complex](../../../../../../finite-cw-complex.md), so its integral [cohomology group](../../../../../../cohomology-group.md) $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$ is finitely generated and therefore countable. Distinct irreducible curves $C,D$ with $C^2<0$ cannot have the same [cohomology class](../../../../../../cohomology-class.md): that would give $C\cdot D=C^2<0$, while distinct effective curves have nonnegative [intersection multiplicities](../../../../../../intersection-multiplicity.md). Assigning a [negative curve](../../../../../../negative-curve-on-a-projective-surface.md) its [cohomology class](../../../../../../cohomology-class.md) is thus [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md), proving **there are at most countably many negative curves**.

For zero [self-intersection](../../../../../../self-intersection-number.md), uncountable families do occur. On the product of two [projective lines](../../../../../../projective-line.md) $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, every fiber $\mathbb P^1\times\{p\}$ has [self-intersection](../../../../../../self-intersection-number.md) zero, since a different fiber is disjoint and has the same [numerical equivalence of divisors](../../../../../../numerical-equivalence-of-divisors.md) class. The parameter $p\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)$ varies over an uncountable set. Thus **yes, there can be uncountably many such curves**.
