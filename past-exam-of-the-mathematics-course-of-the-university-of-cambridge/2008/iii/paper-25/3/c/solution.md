<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $X\subset\mathbb P^3$ have degree $d\geq3$ and hyperplane class $H$. The [adjunction formula](../../../../../../adjunction-formula.md) gives $K_X=(d-4)H$. For a line $L\subset X$, the same formula on the [smooth rational curve](../../../../../../smooth-rational-curve.md) $L$ yields $(K_X+L)\cdot L=-2$, hence

$$
\boxed{L^2=2-d<0.}
$$

Thus all lines are [negative curves](../../../../../../negative-curve-on-a-projective-surface.md), and part (b) makes their set countable. The [Fano scheme of lines](../../../../../../fano-scheme-of-lines.md) is a closed [projective scheme](../../../../../../projective-scheme.md) of $\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)$. A positive-dimensional complex algebraic component would have uncountably many points, so this scheme is zero-dimensional. A [projective scheme](../../../../../../projective-scheme.md) of [finite type](../../../../../../morphism-of-finite-type.md) has only finitely many zero-dimensional points. This proves the [finiteness of lines on a smooth surface of degree at least three](../../../../../../finiteness-of-lines-on-a-smooth-surface-of-degree-at-least-three.md).
