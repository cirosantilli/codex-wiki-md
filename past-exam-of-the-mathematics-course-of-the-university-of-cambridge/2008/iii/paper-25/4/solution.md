<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Choose a [projective embedding](../../../../../projective-embedding.md) of the [abelian surface](../../../../../abelian-surface.md) $X$. By [Bertini's theorem](../../../../../bertini-s-theorem.md), a general [hyperplane section](../../../../../hyperplane-section.md) $C$ is a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) that is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). It is [ample](../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md), so $C^2>0$. Choose $p\in C$ and translate the inclusion so it sends $p$ to the identity of $X$. The [universal property of the Jacobian variety](../../../../../universal-property-of-the-jacobian-variety.md) then gives a homomorphism of [abelian varieties](../../../../../abelian-variety-split.md)

$$
\Phi:J(C)\longrightarrow X
$$

whose composition with the based [Abel map of an algebraic curve](../../../../../abel-map-of-an-algebraic-curve.md) is this translated inclusion.

The image is a closed connected algebraic subgroup, since $J(C)$ is projective, and it contains a translate of $C$. If the image had dimension one, that irreducible curve would be the image subgroup itself. A distinct coset is disjoint from it and has the same numerical divisor class: translation acts trivially on cohomology, being homotopic to the identity on the [complex torus](../../../../../complex-torus.md). Its [self-intersection](../../../../../self-intersection-number.md) would therefore be zero, contradicting $C^2>0$. The image has dimension two and is all of $X$. Thus **$\Phi$ is surjective**, as expressed by the principle that an [ample curve on an abelian surface generates the surface](../../../../../ample-curve-on-an-abelian-surface-generates-the-surface.md).
