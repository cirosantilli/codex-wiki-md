<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Embed $Y$ in [projective space](../../../../../projective-space-split.md) and let $H$ be its hyperplane [Cartier divisor](../../../../../cartier-divisor-split.md). Since $f$ is surjective between integral surfaces, it is [generically finite](../../../../../generically-finite-morphism.md), with degree $r>0$. Its [pullback of a Cartier divisor](../../../../../pullback-of-a-cartier-divisor.md) $L=f^*H$ is a [nef divisor](../../../../../nef-line-bundle.md), and the [projection formula for algebraic cycles](../../../../../projection-formula-for-algebraic-cycles.md) gives $L^2=rH^2>0$. The same formula gives $L\cdot C=H\cdot f_*C=0$, because the curve is contracted to a point.

The [Hodge index theorem for algebraic surfaces](../../../../../hodge-index-theorem-for-algebraic-surfaces.md) makes the [intersection pairing on the Picard group of a surface](../../../../../intersection-pairing-on-the-picard-group-of-a-surface.md) have signature $(1,\rho-1)$ on $N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$. The orthogonal complement of any positive-square vector, including $L$, is [negative definite](../../../../../negative-definite-matrix.md). The numerical class of $C$ is nonzero, since an [ample divisor](../../../../../ample-cartier-divisor.md) on $X$ intersects every nonzero [effective divisor](../../../../../effective-cartier-divisor.md) positively. Thus the [contracted curve has negative self-intersection](../../../../../contracted-curve-has-negative-self-intersection.md) conclusion applies:

$$
\boxed{C^2<0.}
$$
