<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The induced [raising operator](../../../../../../../raising-operator.md) and [lowering operator](../../../../../../../lowering-operator.md) act by

$$
Ev_i=i\,v_{i-1},\qquad Fv_i=(4-i)v_{i+1},\qquad X(u\wedge v)=(Xu)\wedge v+u\wedge(Xv).
$$

Since $Ew_{01}=0$ and $w_{01}$ has weight $6$, it generates the seven-dimensional irreducible [sl2 Lie algebra](../../../../../../../sl2-lie-algebra.md) module $\operatorname{Sym}^6V$. By [complete reducibility of semisimple Lie algebra representations](../../../../../../../weyl-s-theorem-on-complete-reducibility.md), subtracting its weights $6,4,2,0,-2,-4,-6$, each of multiplicity one, from the [weight diagram](../../../../../../../weight-diagram.md) leaves weights $2,0,-2$ once each. Hence

$$
\boxed{U\cong\operatorname{Sym}^6V\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2V.}
$$

These are the only two irreducible isomorphism types occurring as submodules.

For the three-dimensional summand, calculate $Ew_{03}=3w_{02}$ and $Ew_{12}=w_{02}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{v_+=w_{03}-3w_{12}}
$$

is a nonzero [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) of weight $2$. The [classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations](../../../../../../../classification-of-finite-dimensional-sl2-representations.md) shows that the submodule $W$ it generates is the irreducible module $\operatorname{Sym}^2V$, of dimension three.
