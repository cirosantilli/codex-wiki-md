<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose the underlying [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) has a cycle through $k\geq3$ distinct vertices, and let $v_1,\ldots,v_k$ be their unit [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) from admissibility. Adjacent vertices have $(v_i,v_j)\leq-1/2$, and every other pair has nonpositive [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md). Therefore

$$
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k v_i\right\|^2=k+2\sum_{i<j}(v_i,v_j)\leq k+2k\left(-\frac12\right)=0.
$$

But linear independence makes the sum nonzero, whose squared [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) in a [Euclidean space](../../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) must be positive. This contradiction proves that **the underlying [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) of an [admissible Coxeter graph](../../../../../../admissible-crystallographic-coxeter-graph.md) has no cycles**. Double and triple lines record edge multiplicities; they are not cycles of length two. The argument also tolerates chords, which only make the bound smaller.
