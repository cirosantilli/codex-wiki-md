<h1 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Use the $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ [weight of a representation](../../../../../../weight-of-a-representation.md) convention from Question 1: $L_1+L_2+L_3=0$, with [simple roots](../../../../../../simple-root.md) $L_1-L_2$ and $L_2-L_3$. This identification is necessary: the general preceding setup does not by itself specify which [root system](../../../../../../root-system.md) the symbols $L_i$ refer to.

The [Weyl group](../../../../../../weyl-group.md) is $S_3$, acting by permutations of the $L_i$. Let $s_1=(12)$ and $s_2=(23)$, with composition read from right to left. Then

$$
-2L_1+L_2\xrightarrow{s_1}L_1-2L_2\xrightarrow{s_2}L_1-2L_3.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{w=s_2s_1=(1\ 3\ 2),\qquad\mu=L_1-2L_3.}
$$

For direct verification, the diagonal coefficients of $\mu$ are $(1,0,-2)$, so its two [Dynkin labels](../../../../../../dynkin-label.md) are $1-0=1$ and $0-(-2)=2$, both strictly positive. Thus $\mu$ is in the open [Weyl chamber](../../../../../../fundamental-chamber-of-a-root-system.md). The original three coefficients $(-2,1,0)$ are distinct, so its stabilizer is trivial and this permutation is unique.
