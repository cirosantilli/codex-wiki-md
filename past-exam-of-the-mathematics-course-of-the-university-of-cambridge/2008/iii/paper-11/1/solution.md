A proper [filter on a set](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) $X$ is a nonempty family $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$ that excludes $\varnothing$, is closed under finite [intersections](../../../../../set-intersection.md), and is upward closed under inclusion. In particular $X\in\mathcal F$. In a [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md), [convergence of a filter](../../../../../convergence-of-a-filter.md) $\mathcal F\to x$ means that every [neighbourhood](../../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $x$ belongs to $\mathcal F$. An [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) is a proper [filter](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) maximal under inclusion; equivalently, it contains exactly one of $A$ and $X\setminus A$ for every subset $A\subseteq X$.

To justify the equivalence, if $A\notin\mathcal U$ and every member of $\mathcal U$ met $A$, adjoining $A$ would generate a larger proper [filter](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md). Maximality therefore supplies $F\in\mathcal U$ with $F\cap A=\varnothing$, and upward closure gives $X\setminus A\in\mathcal U$. Both complementary sets cannot belong to a proper [filter](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md). Conversely, a [filter](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) with this dichotomy cannot acquire any new set without also containing its complement, which would give an empty [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md).

The [ultrafilter characterization of compact Hausdorff spaces](../../../../../ultrafilter-characterization-of-compact-hausdorff-spaces.md), stated without proof, is: **a [topological space](../../../../../topological-space.md) is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) and [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) exactly when every [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) on it converges to a unique point.**

For a finite [set partition](../../../../../set-partition.md) $X=A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_n$, if no cell belonged to $\mathcal U$, the [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) dichotomy would put all $X\setminus A_j$ in $\mathcal U$. Their finite [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) is empty, a contradiction. Thus some $A_j\in\mathcal U$, and at most one cell can belong because distinct cells are disjoint. The converse is: **a proper [filter on a set](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) meeting every finite partition by containing one cell is an [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md).** Apply the partition hypothesis to $X=A\cup(X\setminus A)$ to obtain the dichotomy, then the maximality argument above. Empty cells can be omitted, and the cases $A=\varnothing$ or $A=X$ are automatic.

For a [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $f:X\to Y$, the [image of a filter](../../../../../pushforward-filter.md) is

$$
f_*\mathcal F=\{B\subseteq Y:f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal F\}.
$$

Since [preimages](../../../../../preimage.md) preserve inclusions and finite [intersections](../../../../../set-intersection.md), this is a proper [filter on a set](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) $Y$. Equivalently, it is the [filter](../../../../../filter-set-theory.md) generated by $f(F)$ for $F\in\mathcal F$: if $f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal F$, then its image is contained in $B$, while $f(F)\subseteq B$ implies $F\subseteq f^{-1}(B)$. For an [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) $\mathcal U$, the identity $f^{-1}(Y\setminus B)=X\setminus f^{-1}(B)$ shows that $f_*\mathcal U$ contains exactly one of $B,Y\setminus B$. Therefore **the [pushforward filter](../../../../../pushforward-filter.md) of an [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) is an [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md)**.

Now let $x\in\ell^\infty$, the [l-infinity sequence space](../../../../../l-infinity-sequence-space.md), and put $a_x(n)=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^nx_j$. All these [Cesaro means](../../../../../cesaro-mean.md) lie in the closed disk $K_x=\{z:|z|\leq\|x\|_\infty\}$, a [compact Hausdorff space](../../../../../compact-hausdorff-space.md). The [pushforward filter](../../../../../pushforward-filter.md) $a_{x*}\mathcal U$ contains $K_x$; restricting it to that disk and applying the [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) theorem gives a unique limit

$$
\boxed{l(x)=\lim_{n\to\mathcal U}a_x(n).}
$$

For real sequences the disk can be replaced by a closed interval. This is the [ultrafilter construction of a Banach limit](../../../../../ultrafilter-construction-of-a-banach-limit.md).

The identities $a_{\alpha x+\beta y}(n)=\alpha a_x(n)+\beta a_y(n)$ give linearity of $l$. Explicitly, the two sets on which $|a_x(n)-l(x)|<\delta$ and $|a_y(n)-l(y)|<\delta$ both belong to $\mathcal U$, hence so does their [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md). On that [intersection](../../../../../set-intersection.md) the difference from $\alpha l(x)+\beta l(y)$ is at most $(|\alpha|+|\beta|)\delta$, proving $l(\alpha x+\beta y)=\alpha l(x)+\beta l(y)$. If $x_n\geq0$, its means lie in $[0,\|x\|_\infty]$, so $l(x)\geq0$. Thus $l$ is a [positive linear functional](../../../../../positive-linear-functional.md). The bound $|l(x)|\leq\|x\|_\infty$ gives $\|l\|\leq1$, while the constant-one sequence satisfies $l(\mathbf1)=1$. Hence $\boxed{\|l\|=1}$.

Because $\mathcal U$ contains no [finite sets](../../../../../finite-set.md), its dichotomy puts every cofinite set in $\mathcal U$. If $x_n\to L$ ordinarily, the [Cesaro theorem for convergent sequences](../../../../../cesaro-theorem-for-convergent-sequences.md) follows directly by splitting the sum: for a fixed $N$ with $|x_j-L|<\varepsilon$ for $j>N$,

$$
|a_x(n)-L|\leq\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^N|x_j-L|+\varepsilon\quad(n>N).
$$

The finite head tends to zero, so $a_x(n)\to L$ ordinarily. Every sufficiently long tail belongs to $\mathcal U$, making its [ultralimit](../../../../../ultralimit.md) also $L$. Thus $\boxed{x_n\to L\Longrightarrow l(x)=L}$.

Finally, for the [left shift on bounded sequences](../../../../../left-shift-on-bounded-sequences.md) $S$,

$$
a_{Sx}(n)-a_x(n)=\frac{x_{n+1}-x_1}{n},\qquad |a_{Sx}(n)-a_x(n)|\leq\frac{2\|x\|_\infty}{n}\to0.
$$

Taking [ultralimits](../../../../../ultralimit.md) along the same cofinite-containing [ultrafilter](../../../../../ultrafilter.md) gives $\boxed{l(Sx)=l(x)}$. This establishes every defining property of a [Banach limit](../../../../../banach-limit.md).
