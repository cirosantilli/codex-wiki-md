<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Take a nonzero complex unital [Banach algebra](../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md), with identity $1$. The [spectrum of an element](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma_A(a)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\lambda1-a\text{ is not invertible in }A\}.}
$$

The standard [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) theorem states that it is a nonempty compact subset of $\mathbb C$, contained in the disk $|\lambda|\leq\|a\|$. Conversely, every nonempty compact set $K\subseteq\mathbb C$ occurs: in the [Banach algebra](../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md) $C(K)$, the coordinate function $a(z)=z$ has [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) exactly $K$, since $\lambda-a$ has a continuous reciprocal precisely when $\lambda\notin K$. Thus **the possible [Banach algebra spectra](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) are exactly the nonempty compact subsets of the complex plane**.

For a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $p$, factor $p(z)-\lambda=c\prod_{j=1}^n(z-z_j)$ over $\mathbb C$. Substitution gives

$$
p(a)-\lambda1=c\prod_{j=1}^n(a-z_j1).
$$

These factors commute. A finite commuting product is invertible exactly when every factor is invertible: if $P=\prod_jx_j$ is invertible, its inverse commutes with each $x_j$, and $x_j^{-1}=(\prod_{k\ne j}x_k)P^{-1}$ is a two-sided inverse. Hence $\lambda$ belongs to the [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) of $p(a)$ exactly when some root $z_j$ belongs to $\sigma_A(a)$, which is equivalent to $\lambda\in p(\sigma_A(a))$. Constant [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) give the same result directly, since $\sigma_A(c1)=\{c\}$. This proves the [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [spectral mapping theorem](../../../../../spectral-mapping-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{\sigma_A(p(a))=p(\sigma_A(a)).}
$$

For a closed unital [Banach subalgebra](../../../../../closed-subalgebra-of-a-banach-algebra.md) $B\subseteq A$, use the common identity $1_A\in B$. An inverse in $B$ is an inverse in $A$, so $\boxed{\sigma_A(b)\subseteq\sigma_B(b)}$. Let $\lambda\in\partial\sigma_B(b)$. Choose $\lambda_n\notin\sigma_B(b)$ tending to $\lambda$. Then $\lambda_n\notin\sigma_A(b)$, and $(\lambda_n1-b)^{-1}\in B$. If $\lambda$ were outside $\sigma_A(b)$, continuity of inversion in $A$ would give

$$
(\lambda_n1-b)^{-1}\longrightarrow(\lambda1-b)^{-1}\in B,
$$

where membership follows from closedness. This would make $\lambda\notin\sigma_B(b)$, contrary to the closed [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) having its boundary inside it. Therefore $\lambda\in\sigma_A(b)$ and is approached by points outside that [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\partial\sigma_B(b)\subseteq\partial\sigma_A(b).}
$$

Continuity of inversion here follows, for example, by writing a nearby element as an invertible element times $1+$ a [norm](../../../../../norm.md)-small perturbation and using the [Neumann series](../../../../../neumann-series.md). This is [spectrum in a closed unital subalgebra](../../../../../spectrum-in-a-closed-unital-subalgebra.md).

To retain the original [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) in a commutative subalgebra, take the [resolvent-generated commutative algebra](../../../../../resolvent-generated-commutative-algebra.md)

$$
C=\overline{\operatorname{alg}\bigl(1,a,(\lambda1-a)^{-1}:\lambda\notin\sigma_A(a)\bigr)}^{\|\cdot\|}.
$$

Every generator commutes with $a$ and with every other generator: commuting invertible elements have commuting inverses, and their inverses commute with all elements commuting with them. [commutativity](../../../../../commutativity.md) persists on the algebra they generate and on its [norm](../../../../../norm.md) closure. For every $\lambda\notin\sigma_A(a)$, the required inverse is in $C$ by construction, so $\sigma_C(a)\subseteq\sigma_A(a)$. The inclusion for unital subalgebras gives the reverse containment. Hence $\boxed{\sigma_C(a)=\sigma_A(a)}$.

Finally, the [Banach subalgebra generated by one element](../../../../../banach-subalgebra-generated-by-one-element.md) is $D=\overline{\{p(a):p\text{ a polynomial}\}}$, and it is commutative. Put $K=\sigma_D(a)$ and suppose its complement has a bounded connected component $U$. The boundary of $U$ lies in $K$. For $\lambda\in U$, the [maximum modulus principle on a bounded domain](../../../../../maximum-modulus-principle-on-a-bounded-domain.md) applied to any [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) gives

$$
|p(\lambda)|\leq\max_{z\in\partial U}|p(z)|\leq\max_{z\in K}|p(z)|=r_D(p(a))\leq\|p(a)\|.
$$

Here $r_D$ is the [spectral radius](../../../../../spectral-radius.md), and the equality uses the already proved [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [spectral mapping theorem](../../../../../spectral-mapping-theorem.md). Define $\chi(p(a))=p(\lambda)$. The displayed bound makes this well defined, even when two [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) represent the same algebra element, and bounded on the dense [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) algebra. Its continuous extension to $D$ remains linear and multiplicative, with $\chi(1)=1$ and $\chi(a)=\lambda$, so it is a [character of an algebra](../../../../../character-of-an-algebra.md). If $\lambda1-a$ were invertible in $D$, applying $\chi$ to its inverse equation would give $0=1$. Thus $\lambda\in K$, contradicting $\lambda\in U$.

There are therefore no bounded complementary components. A compact subset of the plane has exactly one unbounded complementary component: everything outside a sufficiently large disk is connected, and every unbounded component meets it. **Consequently $\boxed{\mathbb C\setminus\sigma_D(a)\text{ is connected}.}$**
