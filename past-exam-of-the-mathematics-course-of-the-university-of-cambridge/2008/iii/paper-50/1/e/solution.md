A [critical exponent](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) describes the leading singular power of an observable in a specified scaling limit. The [order-parameter critical exponent](../../../../../../order-parameter-critical-exponent.md) describes the zero-field ordered branch; the [magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility-critical-exponent.md) describes response to its [conjugate field](../../../../../../field-conjugate-to-an-order-parameter.md); and the critical-isotherm exponent describes response at the critical temperature. They are found in the [Landau approximation](../../../../../../landau-approximation.md) by minimizing the potential and differentiating the resulting equation of state, as calculated in the question's root solution. For a leading $M^{2n}$ stabilizer the same calculation gives $M\asymp(-r)^{1/(2n-2)}$, $\chi\asymp r^{-1}$ and $M\asymp h^{1/(2n-1)}$. Thus $\beta=1/(2n-2)$, $\gamma=1$ and $\delta=2n-1$. Beyond the [Landau approximation](../../../../../../landau-approximation.md), the [renormalization-group relevance](../../../../../../renormalization-group-relevance.md) exponents and [anomalous dimension](../../../../../../anomalous-dimension.md) determine the singular powers; Question 2 derives their relations. The [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) refer to specified paths, and tuning to a higher multicritical point requires eliminating its lower relevant perturbations.
