<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md) exchanges distinct [global minima](../../../../../../global-minimum.md) of the [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md); the [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) jumps and a thermal crossing generically has [latent heat](../../../../../../latent-heat.md). A [continuous phase transition](../../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md) has minima merging continuously and a diverging [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md). A local loss of stability at a [spinodal point](../../../../../../spinodal-point.md) is not the equilibrium coexistence condition.

For the [sextic even Landau potential](../../../../../../sextic-even-landau-potential.md), write $y=M^2$. At a nonzero zero-field [stationary point](../../../../../../stationary-point.md), $r+uy+vy^2=0$. To coexist with $M=0$, also require $ry/2+uy^2/4+vy^3/6=0$. Eliminate $r$ to obtain $y=-3u/(4v)$ and $r=3u^2/(16v)$, which requires $u<0$. On this locus

$$
V(M)=\frac v6M^2\left(M^2+\frac{3u}{4v}\right)^2\geq0,
$$

proving that all three equal minima are globally stable. Their curvatures are $3u^2/(16v)$ at zero and $3u^2/(4v)$ at the nonzero minima, so the [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md) is finite when $u<0$ is fixed. For $u>0$, by contrast, the nonzero minimum approaches zero as $r\uparrow0$, giving the ordinary continuous transition. At fixed $u>0$ and $r<0$, crossing $h=0$ exchanges the two ordered minima discontinuously even though the zero-field thermal transition at $r=0$ is continuous.
