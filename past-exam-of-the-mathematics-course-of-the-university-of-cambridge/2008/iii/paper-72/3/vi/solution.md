<h1 id="3/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

The host mass inside $D$ follows from the same [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md):

$$
M_h(<D)=\frac{2\sigma_h^2D}{G}=\frac{2(3\times10^5)^2(1.5\times10^{21})}{7\times10^{-11}}\,\mathrm{kg}=1.93\times10^{12}M_\odot.
$$

Using the supplied host luminosity gives

$$
\boxed{\Upsilon_h(<50\,\mathrm{kpc})\simeq20M_\odot/L_\odot.}
$$

This treats essentially all the stated optical light as contained in that aperture; a measured luminosity profile would refine that convention. Keeping consistent unrounded quantities,

$$
\frac{\Upsilon_d}{\Upsilon_h}=\frac{m_d}{M_h}\frac{L_h}{L_d}=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\frac{\sigma_d}{\sigma_h}\right)^3\left(\frac{\sigma_h}{\sigma_d}\right)^4=\frac{\sigma_h}{\sqrt2\sigma_d}\simeq21.
$$

Thus **the dwarf ratio is about twenty times the parent ratio**; the rough equal-density tidal convention would give a factor thirty.

There is no logical contradiction in two objects sharing $L\propto\sigma^4$ but having different [mass-to-light ratios](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md). A [virial theorem](../../../../../../virial-theorem.md) scaling gives $M\propto\sigma^2R$, so that $M/L\propto R/\sigma^2$. Equal ratios additionally require homologous relevant radii scaling as $R\propto\sigma^2$. Here the satellite's [tidal radius](../../../../../../tidal-radius.md) scales as $\sigma_d$ in the fixed host field, not as $\sigma_d^2$. Equating the two ratios would require $r_t=D(\sigma_d/\sigma_h)^2\simeq56\,\mathrm{pc}$, far smaller than the derived tidal radius.

The heuristic derivation of the [Faber-Jackson relation](../../../../../../faber-jackson-relation.md) with common [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) and common [surface brightness](../../../../../../surface-brightness.md) made auxiliary structural assumptions; the empirical relation alone cannot enforce them. Furthermore, the [aperture dependence of mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../aperture-dependence-of-mass-to-light-ratio.md) matters: a satellite's whole tidal mass and a host's $50\,\mathrm{kpc}$ halo mass are not homologous half-light-aperture masses. [Dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md) need not follow stellar light, and a [dwarf spheroidal galaxy](../../../../../../dwarf-spheroidal-galaxy.md) is not automatically a scaled giant [elliptical galaxy](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md). The model calculates total gravitating mass: reading its density literally as stars alone would imply an implausibly large purely stellar ratio, exposing the limits of that interpretation.
