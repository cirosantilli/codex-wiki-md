<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md), spherical integration of the [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) gives

$$
M(<R)=\int_0^R4\pi r^2\frac{\sigma^2}{2\pi Gr^2}\,dr=\frac{2\sigma^2R}{G}.
$$

The [spherical shell theorem](../../../../../../spherical-shell-theorem.md) makes the radial attraction $GM(<R)/R^2$. Balancing it with circular centripetal acceleration yields

$$
\boxed{M(<R)=\frac{2\sigma^2R}{G},\qquad V_c(R)=\sqrt{\frac{GM(<R)}R}=\sqrt2\sigma.}
$$

Thus the model has a [flat galaxy rotation curve](../../../../../../flat-galaxy-rotation-curve.md), resembling the outer [galaxy rotation curves](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) of many [spiral galaxies](../../../../../../spiral-galaxy.md). Central rotation curves usually rise, and not all real curves are flat throughout. A pressure-supported [elliptical galaxy](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) does not itself rotate at this test-particle [circular speed](../../../../../../circular-speed.md).

The model has divergent central [mass density](../../../../../../density.md), although finite enclosed mass at each finite radius, and an infinite total mass because $M(<R)\propto R$. Its potential is logarithmic, requiring an outer cutoff for a finite escape energy. A globally constant isotropic [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md), spherical symmetry, and one common profile for all gravitating components are further idealizations. Stellar light commonly has a quite different radial profile from the [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md); real disks are flattened, and halos are neither exact isothermal spheres nor infinite. Tidal truncation also changes the distribution near the satellite boundary.
