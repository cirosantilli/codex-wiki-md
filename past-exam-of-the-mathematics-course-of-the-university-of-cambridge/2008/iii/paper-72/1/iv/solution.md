<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Divide metal enrichment by gas depletion in the [closed-box model of galactic chemical evolution](../../../../../../closed-box-model-of-galactic-chemical-evolution.md):

$$
\frac{dZ}{dM_g}=-\frac{Y}{M_g}.
$$

The [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) appears on integrating from $M_0$ and $Z=0$:

$$
Z=-Y\int_{M_0}^{M_g}\frac{dm}{m}=Y\ln\frac{M_0}{M_g}.
$$

With the [gas fraction of a galaxy](../../../../../../gas-fraction-of-a-galaxy.md) $\mu=M_g/M_0$, this is

$$
\boxed{Z=Y\ln(1/\mu).}
$$

It gives $Z=0$ at $\mu=1$ and increasing [gas-phase metallicity](../../../../../../gas-phase-metallicity.md) as gas is consumed, independently of the time dependence of the [star formation rate](../../../../../../star-formation-rate.md). For this closed initially star-free system, $M_0=M_g+M_s$, so this initial-reservoir fraction equals the present gas-to-total baryonic fraction. That equality fails in an open system.
