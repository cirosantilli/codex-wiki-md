<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $Z$ be the well-mixed [gas-phase metallicity](../../../../../../gas-phase-metallicity.md), measured as a metal mass fraction. Removing gas into [stars](../../../../../../star.md) takes metals at rate $Z\psi$; prompt return restores existing metals at rate $ZF\psi$, together with newly synthesized metals at rate $Y(1-F)\psi$. Thus the [closed-box model of galactic chemical evolution](../../../../../../closed-box-model-of-galactic-chemical-evolution.md) has metal budget

$$
\frac{d(M_gZ)}{dt}=-Z(1-F)\psi+Y(1-F)\psi.
$$

Expand the left side and use $\dot M_g=-(1-F)\psi$. The terms proportional to existing [gas-phase metallicity](../../../../../../gas-phase-metallicity.md) cancel, leaving

$$
M_g\dot Z=Y(1-F)\psi.
$$

Since $\psi=kM_g$, initially pristine gas obeys

$$
\boxed{Z(t)=Y(1-F)kt=Y\lambda t.}
$$

The [stellar yield](../../../../../../stellar-yield.md) here is per permanently locked mass, so inserting another factor $1-F$ into $Y$ would double-count recycling. The approximation treats metals as a trace constituent and ignores finite stellar-return delays. Apply it while $Y\lambda t\ll1$; the formal unlimited linear growth is not a physical prediction for almost entirely metallic gas.
