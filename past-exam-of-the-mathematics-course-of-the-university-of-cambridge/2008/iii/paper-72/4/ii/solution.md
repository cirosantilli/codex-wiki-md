<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Interpret the model comparison at the exam's 2008 epoch. A simulation's failure depends on its treatment of baryons, resolution, and adopted [stellar feedback](../../../../../../stellar-feedback.md); it is not a theorem that the [Lambda-CDM model](../../../../../../lambda-cdm-model.md) cannot produce the observation under any physical implementation. The following were important tensions for then-standard realizations, with different implications for [monolithic galaxy formation](../../../../../../monolithic-galaxy-formation.md).

**Old, alpha-enhanced massive spheroids and downsizing.** The most massive quiescent [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) tend to contain older [stellar populations](../../../../../../stellar-population.md) and elevated [alpha element](../../../../../../alpha-element.md) abundances relative to iron, indicating that much of their [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md) occurred early and rapidly. Prompt core-collapse enrichment followed by shutdown before substantial [delayed iron enrichment](../../../../../../delayed-iron-enrichment.md) accounts for the abundance pattern. Models that spread a massive galaxy's [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md) over many late progenitor-merger episodes can make its stars too young or insufficiently alpha enhanced. A rapid early dissipative collapse naturally gives a short formation interval and is therefore more immediately consistent with this particular observation. However, [downsizing in galaxy formation](../../../../../../downsizing-in-galaxy-formation.md) is about stellar formation, not necessarily final assembly: late [dry galaxy mergers](../../../../../../dry-galaxy-merger.md) can assemble already old alpha-enhanced stars. Early quenching and an appropriate chemical-enrichment model can consequently reconcile hierarchical assembly with the fossil record.

**Thin, high-angular-momentum stellar discs.** The existence of extended [thin galactic disks](../../../../../../thin-galactic-disk.md), including systems with small [galactic bulges](../../../../../../galactic-bulge.md), challenged simulations that produced compact central stellar concentrations or overly thick discs. Premature gas cooling into small progenitors, transfer of [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) to the halo, and repeated dynamical perturbations could cause the [galactic angular-momentum catastrophe](../../../../../../galactic-angular-momentum-catastrophe.md); satellite encounters also increase [galactic disk heating](../../../../../../galactic-disk-heating.md). A comparatively quiet dissipative collapse with approximate conservation of baryonic [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) more readily produces an extended rotating disc, and avoiding subsequent strong perturbations helps retain thinness. Nevertheless, rapid collapse alone does not explain a disc's prolonged [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md) and gas replenishment. Hierarchical histories with quieter late accretion, better feedback, delayed cooling, and dissipative disc regrowth can reduce these tensions; a problem in early implementations is not an unavoidable property of the cosmological framework.

**Cores inferred in low-mass galaxies.** Resolved inner [galaxy rotation curves](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) in a number of low-surface-brightness and dwarf systems favoured shallower central [mass densities](../../../../../../density.md) than the cusps of collisionless cold-dark-matter simulations. For a roughly constant-density [dark-matter core](../../../../../../dark-matter-core.md), $M(<r)\propto r^3$ and $V_c\propto r$; an inner [Navarro--Frenk--White profile](../../../../../../navarro-frenk-white-profile.md) has $\rho\propto r^{-1}$, hence $M(<r)\propto r^2$ and $V_c\propto r^{1/2}$. The differing slopes explain the [cusp--core problem](../../../../../../cusp-core-problem.md). Beam smearing, inclination, and noncircular motions complicate inference, but do not make the issue merely a normalization mismatch. A simple early dissipative collapse does not automatically solve it: baryonic contraction can deepen the central concentration. Potential fluctuations from gas loss and [stellar feedback](../../../../../../stellar-feedback.md) can instead redistribute dark matter and modify a cusp. Thus this observation tests the joint halo-and-baryon model rather than preferentially vindicating the old collapse picture.

**Rapid formation of the stars and hierarchical assembly of the final galaxy are compatible histories.** The comparison must keep those clocks separate, and the historical modelling tensions should not be presented as universal failures of all later simulations.
