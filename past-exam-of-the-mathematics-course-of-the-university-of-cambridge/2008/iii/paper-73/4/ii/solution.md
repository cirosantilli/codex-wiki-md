<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Under the usual interpretation that the extra force simply slows otherwise adiabatic expansion, without changing reaction rates or injecting particles, **the primordial hydrogen mass fraction would be larger**. A smaller [Hubble parameter](../../../../../../hubble-parameter.md) makes cooling slower, delays the end of the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md), and permits more [free-neutron decay after freeze-out](../../../../../../free-neutron-decay-after-freeze-out.md) before nuclear assembly. If the reduced expansion also keeps weak conversions effective longer, the lower eventual [cosmological weak freeze-out](../../../../../../cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md) temperature further reduces the [neutron-to-proton ratio](../../../../../../neutron-to-proton-ratio.md). Both effects lower the [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md) and raise the remaining [hydrogen mass fraction](../../../../../../hydrogen-mass-fraction.md).

For fixed initial neutron fraction $x_{n,1}=n_n/(n_n+n_p)$, the post-freeze-out estimate is $x_n=x_{n,1}e^{-(t_N-t_1)/\tau_n}$, where $t_N$ is the onset of efficient nuclear assembly. Thus $Y_p\simeq2x_n$ decreases when cooling delays $t_N$. The extra time is the time to reach the nuclear-assembly temperature, not an increase in the specified clock interval $300-1$ seconds. An unspecified force that also reheats the plasma, changes weak rates or causes recollapse would require additional information; the simple expansion-only interpretation is essential to this expectation.
