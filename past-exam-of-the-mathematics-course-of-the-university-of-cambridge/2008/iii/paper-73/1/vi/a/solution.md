<h1 id="1/vi/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For [Planck law](../../../../../../../planck-s-law.md) expressed per unit [frequency](../../../../../../../frequency.md), put $x=h\nu/(k_BT)$. At fixed [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md), the frequency dependence is $B_\nu\propto T^3x^3/(e^x-1)$. Differentiation gives

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}
=\frac{x^2[3(e^x-1)-xe^x]}{(e^x-1)^2}.
$$

The positive interior maximum therefore satisfies $3(1-e^{-x_*})=x_*$, with $x_*=2.821439\ldots$ independent of [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md). The zero solution of the rearranged equation is a boundary, not the interior maximum. Hence the [frequency form of Wien's displacement law](../../../../../../../frequency-form-of-wien-s-displacement-law.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\nu_{\max}}{T}=\frac{x_*k_B}{h}
\simeq5.88\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{Hz\,K^{-1}}.}
$$

This frequency peak is not $c$ divided by the peak wavelength of $B_\lambda$: the two spectral densities differ by the frequency-to-wavelength Jacobian.
