<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take a [recursively enumerable set](../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md) $S\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}$ that is not recursive, for example the diagonal halting set from Question 2 shifted by one. To obtain a [finitely generated group](../../../../../../finitely-generated-group.md) with recursive presentation but undecidable [word problem for a group](../../../../../../word-problem-for-groups.md), first define the countable [Right-angled Artin group](../../../../../../right-angled-artin-group.md)

$$
B_S=\langle a_i\ (i\in\mathbb Z)\mid[a_i,a_j]=1\text{ whenever }|i-j|\in S\rangle.
$$

Translation of the indices gives an [automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism.md) $a_i\mapsto a_{i+1}$. The [semidirect product](../../../../../../semidirect-product.md) with its infinite cyclic shift is generated by $a=a_0,t$ and has presentation

$$
G_S=\langle a,t\mid[a,t^nat^{-n}]=1\ (n\in S)\rangle.
$$

To verify this presentation, the semidirect product satisfies its relators, while the reverse map sends $a_i$ to $t^iat^{-i}$; all translated commutation relations follow by conjugation. These maps are inverse on their generators. Thus $B_S$ embeds in $G_S$.

For $n\in S$, the word $[a_0,a_n]$ is trivial. For $n\notin S$, map $a_0,a_n$ to the free generators $x,y$ of $F(x,y)$ and every other $a_i$ to one. Every defining commutator of $B_S$ maps to one, since the only possible relation involving both surviving generators would have separation $n$, which is absent. The resulting [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) sends $[a_0,a_n]$ to the nontrivial reduced word $[x,y]$. Hence

$$
\boxed{[a,t^nat^{-n}]=1\text{ in }G_S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in S.}
$$

This proves the [shift-invariant graph groups encode arbitrary sets](../../../../../../shift-invariant-graph-groups-encode-arbitrary-sets.md) construction rather than supposing that a missing relator automatically means a nontrivial word. A word algorithm for $G_S$ would decide $S$, so none exists. Since $S$ is enumerable, the displayed two-generator presentation is a [recursive presentation of a group](../../../../../../recursive-presentation-of-a-group.md). By the [Higman embedding theorem](../../../../../../higman-s-embedding-theorem.md), $G_S$ embeds in a finitely presented group $G_0$. A word algorithm for $G_0$, composed with substitution of the two fixed generator-image words, would solve the word problem for $G_S$. Therefore **$\boxed{G_0\text{ is finitely presented with insoluble word problem}}$.**
