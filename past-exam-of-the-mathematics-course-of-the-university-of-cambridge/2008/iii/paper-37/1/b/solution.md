<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

As with [quadratic variation](../../../../../../quadratic-variation.md), the increasing process must be normalized by $A_0=0$. Suppose $A,A'$ have this initial value and both $M^2-A$ and $M^2-A'$ are [continuous local martingales](../../../../../../continuous-local-martingale.md). Since $M^2$ and these differences are continuous, both $A$ and $A'$ are continuous. Their difference is a [finite-variation process](../../../../../../finite-variation-process.md), and

$$
A-A'=(M^2-A')-(M^2-A)
$$

is a [continuous local martingale](../../../../../../continuous-local-martingale.md). The result proved in part (a) makes this difference constant. Its initial value is zero, so **$A$ and $A'$ are indistinguishable**. This is the [uniqueness of an increasing square compensator](../../../../../../uniqueness-of-an-increasing-square-compensator.md); it uses no existence argument.

If no initial-value normalization is imposed, the literal uniqueness assertion is false: whenever $A$ works, $A+c$ works for every deterministic constant $c$, since subtracting a constant preserves the [local martingale](../../../../../../local-martingale.md) property. The exact unnormalized conclusion is $A_t-A'_t=A_0-A'_0$ for all $t$, up to [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../../../../indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes.md).
