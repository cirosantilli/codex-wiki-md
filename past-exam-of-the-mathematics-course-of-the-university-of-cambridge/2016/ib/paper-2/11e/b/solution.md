<h1 id="11e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $351=3^3\cdot13$, the number of [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=13$ divides $27$ and is $1$ modulo $13$, hence is $1$ or $27$. In the first case the unique subgroup is a nontrivial proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).

In the second case the $27$ subgroups of order $13$ have pairwise trivial intersection: two such subgroups sharing a nonidentity element would both be generated by it. They account for $27\cdot12=324$ nonidentity elements of order $13$. Exactly $27$ elements, including the identity, remain. Every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=3$ has order $27$, and all its elements lie among these remaining elements, by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md). It must therefore be precisely that set. The [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=3$ is unique and hence [normal](../../../../../../normal-distribution.md). **In either case $G$ is not a [simple group](../../../../../../simple-group.md).**
