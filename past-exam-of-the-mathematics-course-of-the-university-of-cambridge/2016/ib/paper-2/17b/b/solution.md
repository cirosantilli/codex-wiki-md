<h1 id="17b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Part (a) implies $[\hat A,\hat B]=2m\hbar\omega$. Since $\hat H=\hat B\hat A/(2m)+\hbar\omega/2$, it follows that

$$
[\hat H,\hat A]=\frac1{2m}[\hat B\hat A,\hat A]=-\hbar\omega\hat A.
$$

For an [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) $\phi$ of the [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) with [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $E$,

$$
\boxed{\hat H(\hat A\phi)=(E-\hbar\omega)\hat A\phi.}
$$

Thus $\hat A$ is a [ladder operator](../../../../../../ladder-operator.md) lowering the energy by $\hbar\omega$, **provided $\hat A\phi\ne0$**. The zero function is not an [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md); in particular the ground state is annihilated by this operator. This qualification is essential for the later argument.
