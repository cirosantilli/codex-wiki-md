<h1 id="4e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $X$ to be the set $\mathbb Q$, equipped with the [discrete metric](../../../../../../discrete-metric.md)

$$
\boxed{d(p,q)=\begin{cases}0,&p=q,\\1,&p\ne q.\end{cases}\qquad f(p)=p\in\mathbb Q.}
$$

Every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) is eventually constant: its terms must eventually have mutual distance in this [metric](../../../../../../metric.md) less than $1/2$. Consequently $X$ is a [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md). Its topology is the [discrete topology](../../../../../../discrete-space.md), since every singleton is a ball of radius $1/2$ and every subset is a union of such balls.

For the codomain use the usual subspace topology on $\mathbb Q$. The inverse image of any [open set](../../../../../../open-set.md) there is a subset of the discrete $X$, hence is [open](../../../../../../open-set.md). Therefore $f$ is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and plainly [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md). **A continuous image of a complete metric space need not be complete.** The two occurrences of $\mathbb Q$ have different domain and codomain metrics.
