<h1 id="4e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $K$ be a [compact set](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) $X$. For any $x\notin K$ and each $y\in K$, the [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) property gives disjoint [open sets](../../../../../../open-set.md) $U_y\ni y$ and $V_y\ni x$. The sets $U_y$ cover $K$; [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) supplies a finite subcover $U_{y_1},\ldots,U_{y_r}$. The [open set](../../../../../../open-set.md) $V=\bigcap_iV_{y_i}$ contains $x$ and misses every $U_{y_i}$, hence misses $K$. Thus $X\setminus K$ is [open](../../../../../../open-set.md), and **every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed**. The empty-set case is immediate.

Since $\mathbb R$ with its usual topology is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), its [compact sets](../../../../../../compact-space.md) are [closed](../../../../../../closed-set.md). The [open cover](../../../../../../open-cover.md) $\{(-n,n):n\ge1\}$ of $\mathbb R$ has a finite subcover of any [compact set](../../../../../../compact-space.md) $K$. Taking the largest index in that subcover gives $K\subset(-N,N)$, so $K$ is a [bounded set](../../../../../../bounded-set.md). This proves the required direction of the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md) without assuming its converse.
