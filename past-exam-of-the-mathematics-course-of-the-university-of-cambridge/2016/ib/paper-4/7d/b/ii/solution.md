<h1 id="7d/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the oscillatory magnetic field, [Faraday's law](../../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) gives $\mathbf B=(1/\omega)\mathbf k\times\mathbf E$. Indeed its time derivative equals $-\nabla\times\mathbf E$, and the dispersion relation makes it satisfy the remaining [Maxwell equations](../../../../../../../maxwell-equations.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B(z,t)=\frac{kE_0}{\sqrt2\,\omega}(-1,1,0)\cos(kz-\omega t).}
$$

For $\omega=ck$, its amplitude is $E_0/c$. The [magnetic field](../../../../../../../magnetic-field.md), [electric field](../../../../../../../electric-field.md) and direction of propagation are mutually perpendicular. A constant background magnetic field could be added; the expression above is the wave-associated field.
