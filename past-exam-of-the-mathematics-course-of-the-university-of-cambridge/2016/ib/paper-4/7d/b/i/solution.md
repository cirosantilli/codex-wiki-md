↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The phase has [wavevector](../../../../../../../wavevector.md) $\mathbf k=k\mathbf e_z$. The electric field has fixed direction $(\mathbf e_x+\mathbf e_y)/\sqrt2$, so it has [linear polarization](../../../../../../../linear-polarization.md) at $45^\circ$ to the $x$ and $y$ axes, perpendicular to the [wavevector](../../../../../../../wavevector.md). Substitution into the [electromagnetic wave equation](../../../../../../../electromagnetic-wave-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=c^2k^2.}
$$

For the usual positive-frequency convention, $\omega=c|k|$ and propagation is in the direction $\operatorname{sgn}(k)\mathbf e_z$. If $k>0$, this reduces to $\omega=ck$. The following wave formulas assume the nonconstant case $\omega\ne0$.
