<h1 id="14a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $F(p)=\mathcal L[f](p)$. Assume $f$ and $f'$ have right limits at zero and sufficient regularity and exponential-order bounds for the following [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md); take $\operatorname{Re}p$ large enough to kill the boundary terms at infinity. First,

$$
\mathcal L[f'](p)=[e^{-pt}f(t)]_0^\infty+pF(p)=pF(p)-f(0+).
$$

Apply the same calculation to $f'$:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L[f''](p)=p^2F(p)-pf(0+)-f'(0+).}
$$

This is the [Laplace transform of a derivative](../../../../../../laplace-transform-of-a-derivative.md) formula with both initial-data terms retained.
