<h1 id="11e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Work with the usual commutative unital [Noetherian ring](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) convention. First prove that every [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md) of $R^d$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md). For $d=1$, a [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md) is an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md), hence finitely generated by Noetherianity. For the induction step, project a submodule $N\subseteq R^d$ onto its last coordinate. Its image is a finitely generated [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md); choose lifts in $N$ of its generators. The projection kernel lies in $R^{d-1}$ and is finitely generated by induction. The kernel generators together with the chosen lifts generate $N$, since subtracting a combination of the lifts leaves an element of the kernel.

Now choose a surjection $R^d\to M$ from a finite generating set of the [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$. For any [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md) $U\subseteq M$, its inverse image in $R^d$ is finitely generated by the result just proved. The images of those generators generate $U$.

**Every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated.** In particular $M$ is a [Noetherian module](../../../../../../noetherian-module.md): the union of an ascending chain is a submodule, and its finite generators all belong to a single member of the chain, forcing eventual stabilization.
