<h1 id="6b/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of the self-adjoint [angular momentum operator](../../../../../../angular-momentum-operator.md) imply $\langle\psi_1,\psi_2\rangle=0$. The given combination is not normalized: its squared norm is $(\cos^2\omega t+3\sin^2\omega t)/4$, which is always positive. Therefore its properly normalized [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md) is

$$
\langle L_1\rangle_\psi=\frac{\langle\psi,L_1\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}=\hbar\frac{\cos^2\omega t-3\sin^2\omega t}{\cos^2\omega t+3\sin^2\omega t}.
$$

It vanishes when $\tan^2\omega t=1/3$. The first positive solution has $\omega t=\pi/6$, so

$$
\boxed{t_0=\frac{\pi}{6\omega}.}
$$

Normalization changes the value of the [expectation value](../../../../../../expectation-value.md) away from zero, but not its zero times.
