↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [contraction mapping theorem](../../../../../../contraction-mapping-theorem.md) states that a [contraction mapping](../../../../../../contraction-mapping.md) of a **nonempty complete metric space** into itself has a unique [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) $x_*$. For any starting point $x_0$, the iterates $x_{n+1}=f(x_n)$ converge to $x_*$, with the quantitative estimate

$$
\boxed{d(x_n,x_*)\leq\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0).}
$$

Completeness and the self-map condition are essential: the geometric estimate makes the iterates a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), completeness supplies their limit in $X$, and continuity of the [contraction mapping](../../../../../../contraction-mapping.md) makes that limit a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md). If two [fixed points](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) existed, their distance would be at most $q$ times itself, forcing equality of the points.
