<h1 id="17d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a moving closed circuit $C(t)$ bounding an oriented surface $S(t)$, [Faraday's law](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_{\rm em}(t)=\oint_{C(t)}[\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B]\cdot d\mathbf l
=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf n\,dS.}
$$

Here $\mathbf E$ is the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field.md), $\mathbf B$ the [magnetic field](../../../../../../magnetic-field.md), $\mathbf v$ the local velocity of a circuit element, and $\mathcal E_{\rm em}$ the electromagnetic [electromotive force](../../../../../../electromotive-force.md) per unit charge around the circuit. The line orientation and normal $\mathbf n$ obey the right-hand rule. The flux derivative is a total derivative and includes both changing field and changing surface. The term $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ is the [motional electromotive force](../../../../../../motional-electromotive-force.md). A battery contributes an additional non-electromagnetic source electromotive force.
