<h1 id="16b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The agreement is in the **energy levels, not in electron trajectories**. The [Bohr model](../../../../../../bohr-model.md) assumes a particle on a circular orbit, with [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) quantized as $N\hbar$. The [Coulomb bound states](../../../../../../coulomb-bound-state.md) here are spatially distributed [wavefunctions](../../../../../../wave-function.md) with no definite classical orbit. Their [probability density](../../../../../../probability-density.md) is spherically symmetric, and their [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) is zero because $\ell=0$. Their radial distributions also depend on $N$ and can have nodes. A real radial wavefunction times a global stationary phase has zero [probability current](../../../../../../probability-current.md), unlike the circulating point charge in the Bohr picture.
