<h1 id="11e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The multiplicative [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) on this ring is

$$
N(a+b\sqrt{-5})=(a+b\sqrt{-5})(a-b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2.
$$

If an element is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), its positive integer norm has a multiplicative inverse in the positive integers, hence is one. The only integer solutions of $a^2+5b^2=1$ are $(a,b)=(1,0),(-1,0)$. Therefore **the units are exactly $1$ and $-1$**.

The standard failure of [unique factorization](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md) is

$$
6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}).
$$

There is no element of norm two or three: $b\ne0$ gives norm at least five, while $b=0$ gives a square. An alleged nontrivial factorization of $2$ (norm four), $3$ (norm nine), or $1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ (norm six) would require a factor of norm two or three. Thus all four displayed factors are [irreducible elements](../../../../../../irreducible-element.md). No factor in the first factorization is associated to a factor in the second, since associates have the same norm and the norms are respectively four, nine and six. Hence **$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ is not a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md)**.
