<h1 id="16d/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [method of images](../../../../../../method-of-images.md) uses the real [line charge](../../../../../../line-charge.md) at the origin and the opposite [image charge](../../../../../../image-charge.md) at $(2d,0)$. Let

$$
r_1=\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\qquad r_2=\sqrt{(x-2d)^2+y^2}.
$$

Superposing their logarithmic [electrostatic potentials](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) gives, in the physical region $x<d$,

$$
\boxed{\phi(x,y)=\frac{\mu}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\log\frac{r_2}{r_1}.}
$$

At $x=d$, $r_1=r_2$, so $\phi=0$. Its tangential derivatives also vanish on that plane, giving $E_y=E_z=0$, exactly the [electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor](../../../../../../electrostatic-boundary-conditions-at-a-conductor.md). The image lies outside the physical domain, so it creates no extra physical source there. Together with the appropriate behaviour at infinity, [uniqueness of Poisson equation](../../../../../../uniqueness-of-poisson-equation.md) identifies this field with the grounded-conductor solution.
