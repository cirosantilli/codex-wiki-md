<h1 id="16d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

By cylindrical symmetry the [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field.md) of an infinite [line charge](../../../../../../line-charge.md) is radial, independent of $z$ and angle. Use a coaxial cylindrical [Gaussian surface](../../../../../../gaussian-surface.md) of radius $r$ and length $L$. The end caps have zero flux, so [Gauss's law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) gives $2\pi rL E_r=\mu L/\varepsilon_0$. Hence **the field and electrostatic potential are**

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=\frac{\mu}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}\mathbf e_r,\qquad \phi(r)=-\frac{\mu}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\log\frac r{r_0}+C.}
$$

Here $r_0>0$ is an arbitrary reference length, and $\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$. One cannot choose zero [electrostatic potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) at infinity for a single infinite [line charge](../../../../../../line-charge.md), because its logarithmic potential diverges there; the arbitrary reference handles the additive constant.
