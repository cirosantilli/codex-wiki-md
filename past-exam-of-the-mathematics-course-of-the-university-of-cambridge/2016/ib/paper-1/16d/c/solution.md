<h1 id="16d/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the [conductor in electrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../conductor-in-electrostatic-equilibrium.md), the interior [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field.md) is zero and the [electrostatic potential](../../../../../../electric-potential.md) is constant. Earthing fixes that constant to zero. Thus **the conductor boundary conditions** at $x=d$ are

$$
\boxed{\phi(d,y,z)=0,\qquad E_y(d^-,y,z)=E_z(d^-,y,z)=0.}
$$

The normal [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field.md) need not vanish; it is supported by a [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md). The outward normal from this conductor into the exterior is $\mathbf n=-\mathbf e_x$, so the normal jump condition is

$$
\mathbf E(d^-)\cdot\mathbf n=\frac\sigma{\varepsilon_0},\qquad E_x(d^-)=-\frac\sigma{\varepsilon_0}.
$$

This sign convention will determine the sign of the induced [surface charge density](../../../../../../surface-charge-density.md) below.
