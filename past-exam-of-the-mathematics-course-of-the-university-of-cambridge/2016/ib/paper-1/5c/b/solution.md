<h1 id="5c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In cylindrical coordinates, the transverse velocity is $A\mathbf e_\theta/r$, so its contribution to the [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) is $A\,d\theta$. The vertical contribution is $U(z)\,dz$, the differential of an antiderivative of $U$, and therefore has zero integral around any closed curve. If $k$ is the oriented [winding number](../../../../../../winding-number.md) of the curve's projection about the origin, then

$$
\boxed{\oint\mathbf u\cdot d\mathbf r=2\pi A k.}
$$

In particular, **a single positive winding gives $2\pi A$**; reversing orientation reverses the sign. The nonzero [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) is compatible with [irrotational flow](../../../../../../irrotational-flow.md) because the domain is not [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md). For $A\ne0$, an enclosing curve cannot bound a surface contained entirely in the domain to which [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) would apply.
