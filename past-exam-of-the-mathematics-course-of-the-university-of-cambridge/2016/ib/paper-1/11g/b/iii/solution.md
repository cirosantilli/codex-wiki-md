<h1 id="11g/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write the [subsequence](../../../../../../../subsequence.md) as $y_n=x_{k_n}$ with strictly increasing indices $k_n$. If $y_\ell=x_m$, then $d(y_\ell,y_n)=d(x_m,x_{k_n})$. This is a [subsequence](../../../../../../../subsequence.md) of the real sequence converging to $d_m$, so

$$
\boxed{d(y_\ell,y_n)\longrightarrow d_m.}
$$

The conclusion depends on the point $x_m$ itself, even if that point appears at several indices.
