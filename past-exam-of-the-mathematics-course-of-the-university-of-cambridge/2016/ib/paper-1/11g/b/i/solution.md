<h1 id="11g/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For fixed $m$, the [reverse triangle inequality](../../../../../../../reverse-triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
|d(x_m,x_n)-d(x_m,x_k)|\leq d(x_n,x_k).
$$

The right side tends uniformly to zero as $n,k\to\infty$ because $(x_n)$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md). Hence $(d(x_m,x_n))_n$ is a real [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md). By [completeness](../../../../../../../completeness.md) of $\mathbb R$, it has a finite, nonnegative limit:

$$
\boxed{d_m=\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_m,x_n)\geq0.}
$$

This conclusion uses completeness only of the real line, without assuming that $X$ is complete.
