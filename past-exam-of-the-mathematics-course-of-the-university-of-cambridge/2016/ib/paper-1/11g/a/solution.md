<h1 id="11g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in a [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) satisfies: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N$ such that $d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon$ whenever $m,n\geq N$. Suppose a [subsequence](../../../../../../subsequence.md) $x_{n_k}$ converges to $x\in X$. Choose $N$ so that the Cauchy bound is $\varepsilon/2$, and then choose $k$ with $n_k\geq N$ and $d(x_{n_k},x)<\varepsilon/2$. For every $n\geq N$, the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) gives

$$
d(x_n,x)\leq d(x_n,x_{n_k})+d(x_{n_k},x)<\varepsilon.
$$

Thus **the whole Cauchy sequence converges to the subsequential limit**.
