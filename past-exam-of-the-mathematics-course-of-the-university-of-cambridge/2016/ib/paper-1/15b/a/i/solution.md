<h1 id="15b/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the normalized [eigenfunctions](../../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) of the [infinite square well](../../../../../../../infinite-square-well.md),

$$
\phi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2a}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a},\qquad E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2},\quad 0<x<a.
$$

The sine identity expands the initial [wavefunction](../../../../../../../wave-function.md) as

$$
\psi_0=\frac{3\phi_1-\phi_3}{\sqrt{10}}.
$$

The normalization factor $4/\sqrt{5a}$ yields precisely these coefficients. By the [Born rule](../../../../../../../born-rule.md), the [energy measurement](../../../../../../../energy-measurement.md) probabilities are $9/10$ for $E_1$ and $1/10$ for $E_3=9E_1$. Thus **the energy expectation is**

$$
\boxed{\langle E\rangle=\frac9{10}E_1+\frac1{10}E_3=\frac{9\pi^2\hbar^2}{10ma^2}.}
$$

The two probabilities sum to one, also checking the [wavefunction normalization](../../../../../../../wavefunction-normalization.md).
