<h1 id="17c/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In [steady flow](../../../../../../../steady-flow.md), the [Euler equations for an inviscid fluid](../../../../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md) and part (a) give

$$
\nabla H=\mathbf u\times\boldsymbol\omega,\qquad H=\tfrac12|\mathbf u|^2+\frac p\rho+\Phi.
$$

Taking the dot product with $\mathbf u$ yields $\mathbf u\cdot\nabla H=0$, because a [cross product](../../../../../../../cross-product.md) is perpendicular to its first factor. Part (b) therefore gives **Bernoulli's quantity along streamlines**:

$$
\boxed{\tfrac12|\mathbf u|^2+\frac p\rho+\Phi=\text{constant on each streamline}.}
$$

For rotational flow these constants can differ between [streamlines](../../../../../../../streamline.md), since $\mathbf u\times\boldsymbol\omega$ need not vanish and therefore $\nabla H$ need not be zero. Special rotational flows can still have one global constant when that cross product vanishes; the general conclusion is only constancy along individual [streamlines](../../../../../../../streamline.md).
