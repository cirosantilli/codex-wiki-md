<h1 id="14a/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For the weight $w=x$, substitution $x=e^s$ gives

$$
\langle y_m,y_n\rangle_w=\int_1^e\frac{\sin(m\pi\log x)\sin(n\pi\log x)}x\,dx=\int_0^1\sin(m\pi s)\sin(n\pi s)\,ds.
$$

The [product-to-sum formula](../../../../../../../product-to-sum-formula.md) makes this zero when $m\ne n$ and $1/2$ when $m=n$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle y_m,y_n\rangle_w=\tfrac12\delta_{mn}.}
$$

In particular the [eigenfunctions](../../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) are [orthogonal](../../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), and multiplying each by $\sqrt2$ gives an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) for the weighted space.
