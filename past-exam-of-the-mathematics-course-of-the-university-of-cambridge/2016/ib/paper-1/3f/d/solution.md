<h1 id="3f/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) of the [Poincare disc model](../../../../../../poincare-disk-model.md) to move the centre to zero. A [hyperbolic disc](../../../../../../hyperbolic-disc.md) of radius $\rho$ then has Euclidean radius $R=\tanh(\rho/2)$ by the [hyperbolic distance](../../../../../../hyperbolic-distance.md) formula. The [area element of a surface](../../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md) of the [Riemannian metric](../../../../../../riemannian-metric.md) is $4r\,dr\,d\theta/(1-r^2)^2$, so

$$
\operatorname{Area}=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R\frac{4r}{(1-r^2)^2}\,dr\,d\theta=4\pi\left(\frac1{1-R^2}-1\right).
$$

Therefore **the area is independent of the centre**, and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Area}=4\pi\sinh^2(\rho/2)=2\pi(\cosh\rho-1).}
$$
