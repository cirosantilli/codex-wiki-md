<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [firmly nonexpansive mapping](../../../../../../firmly-nonexpansive-mapping.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\|Tz-Tw\|_2^2\leq\langle Tz-Tw,z-w\rangle\quad\text{for all }z,w}.
$$

Writing $d=z-w$ and $a=Tz-Tw$, this is equivalent to

$$
\|a\|_2^2+\|d-a\|_2^2\leq\|d\|_2^2.
$$

It is also equivalent to $\|2a-d\|_2\leq\|d\|_2$, so $R=2T-I$ is a [nonexpansive mapping](../../../../../../nonexpansive-mapping.md). Consequently **firm nonexpansiveness is exactly $1/2$-averagedness**. A [proximal map](../../../../../../proximal-operator.md) of a proper [lower semicontinuous](../../../../../../lower-semicontinuity.md) [convex function](../../../../../../convex-function.md) is a basic example. A $1/2$-[averaged operator](../../../../../../averaged-operator.md) is also $\alpha$-averaged for every $\alpha\in[1/2,1)$, by combining its nonexpansive part with the identity; the equivalence with firm nonexpansiveness specifically uses the parameter $1/2$.
