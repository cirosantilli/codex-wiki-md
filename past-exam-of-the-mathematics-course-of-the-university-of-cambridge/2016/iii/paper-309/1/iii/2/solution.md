<h1 id="1/iii/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [electric part of the Weyl tensor](../../../../../../../electric-part-of-the-weyl-tensor.md) has one normal in each antisymmetric pair of the [Weyl tensor](../../../../../../../weyl-tensor.md). Therefore

$$
n^\alpha E_{\alpha\beta}=n^\alpha n^\rho C_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}n^\sigma=0,
\qquad
n^\beta E_{\alpha\beta}=n^\beta n^\sigma C_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}n^\rho=0.
$$

For the [magnetic part of the Weyl tensor](../../../../../../../magnetic-part-of-the-weyl-tensor.md), contracting the first slot gives $n^\alpha n^\lambda\epsilon_{\alpha\lambda\mu\nu}=0$ by antisymmetry of the [metric volume tensor](../../../../../../../metric-volume-tensor.md); contracting the second gives $n^\beta n^\rho C^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta\rho}=0$ by antisymmetry of the last curvature pair. Consequently **both tensors are entirely spatial**:

$$
\boxed{E_{\alpha\beta}=(\perp E)_{\alpha\beta},\qquad B_{\alpha\beta}=(\perp B)_{\alpha\beta}.}
$$

These [spatial projection tensor](../../../../../../../spatial-projection-tensor.md) identities actually hold for any [Weyl tensor](../../../../../../../weyl-tensor.md); the vacuum assumption is needed subsequently to replace it by the [Riemann curvature tensor](../../../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor.md).
