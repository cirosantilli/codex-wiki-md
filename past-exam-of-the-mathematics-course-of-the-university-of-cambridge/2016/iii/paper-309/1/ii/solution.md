<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the orientation induced by the future-directed [unit normal](../../../../../../unit-normal.md) and positive [lapse function](../../../../../../lapse-function.md) $\alpha$. In coordinates adapted to the [3+1 decomposition of spacetime](../../../../../../3-plus-1-decomposition-of-spacetime.md), any component $\epsilon_{\ell ijk}$ with all four indices spatial vanishes: only three distinct spatial indices are available. The [shift vector](../../../../../../shift-vector.md) therefore contributes nothing, and

$$
\epsilon_{\mu ijk}n^\mu=\alpha^{-1}\epsilon_{0ijk}
=\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{|g|}\,[ijk]
=\sqrt\gamma\,[ijk].
$$

Here $[ijk]$ is the [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md), and the last equality uses the [metric determinant in a 3+1 decomposition](../../../../../../metric-determinant-in-a-3-plus-1-decomposition.md), $|g|=\alpha^2\gamma$. The right side is precisely the spatial [metric volume tensor](../../../../../../metric-volume-tensor.md), with component $\sqrt\gamma$ at $ijk=123$, the appropriate permutation sign, and zero for repeated indices. Hence

$$
\boxed{\tilde\epsilon_{ijk}=n^\mu\epsilon_{\mu ijk}.}
$$

More invariantly, the spatial [volume form](../../../../../../volume-form.md) is the restriction to the [hypersurface](../../../../../../hypersurface.md) of the contraction of the spacetime [volume form](../../../../../../volume-form.md) with $n$.
