<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [formal adjoint](../../../../../../formal-adjoint.md) of $\mathcal L=\Delta-k^2$ is itself. With the bilinear convention used in [Green second identity](../../../../../../green-second-identity.md), take any complex-valued $v$ satisfying $\mathcal Lv=0$. The [product rule for divergence](../../../../../../product-rule-for-divergence.md) gives the family

$$
\boxed{\partial_x(vu_x-uv_x)+\partial_y(vu_y-uv_y)=v\mathcal Lu-u\mathcal Lv=0.}
$$

Equivalently, the [differential one-form](../../../../../../one-form.md)

$$
\omega_v=(vu_x-uv_x)\,dy-(vu_y-uv_y)\,dx
$$

is a [closed differential form](../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md). Arbitrary [linear combinations](../../../../../../linear-combination.md) of adjoint solutions give further members of this family. One can also write it as $\nabla\cdot[v^2\nabla(u/v)]=0$ wherever $v\ne0$, but the first formula has no division and remains valid at zeros of $v$. For a sesquilinear convention replace $v$ by $\overline v$; no such conjugation is built into the bilinear identity used below.
