<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A [Serre fibration](../../../../../serre-fibration.md) $p:E\to B$ has the [homotopy lifting property](../../../../../homotopy-lifting-property.md) for every disc: given $h:D^k\times I\to B$ and $\widetilde h_0:D^k\to E$ with $p\widetilde h_0=h(-,0)$, there is a lift $\widetilde h$ extending $\widetilde h_0$. Equivalently one may test this property on [CW complexes](../../../../../cw-complex.md).

For a map $g:E\to B$, its [mapping-path fibration replacement](../../../../../mapping-path-fibration-replacement.md) is

$$
E_g=\{(e,\gamma):\gamma(0)=g(e)\},\qquad
p(e,\gamma)=\gamma(1).
$$

Path spaces have the compact-open topology, with the usual compactly generated convention. The inclusion $s(e)=(e,\operatorname{const}_{g(e)})$ satisfies $ps=g$. Forgetting the path is a [homotopy inverse](../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) to $s$: shorten $\gamma$ to $u\mapsto\gamma(tu)$, from its constant initial path at $t=0$ to the original path at $t=1$. The equivalence here means a commuting triangle over the same base whose map of total spaces is a [homotopy equivalence](../../../../../homotopy-equivalence.md); it does not assert a fibrewise homotopy inverse over the base. Appending the successive segments of a moving base path gives the [homotopy lifting property](../../../../../homotopy-lifting-property.md) for $p$, even for arbitrary parameter spaces, so $p$ is in particular a [Serre fibration](../../../../../serre-fibration.md). Its ordinary fibre over $b_0$ is the [homotopy fibre](../../../../../homotopy-fiber.md)

$$
\operatorname{hofib}_{b_0}(g)=\{(e,\gamma):\gamma(0)=g(e),\ \gamma(1)=b_0\}.
$$

For a based [Serre fibration](../../../../../serre-fibration.md), with fibre inclusion $j:F\hookrightarrow E$, the [long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration](../../../../../long-exact-sequence-of-homotopy-groups-of-a-fibration.md) is

$$
\cdots\longrightarrow\pi_k(F)\xrightarrow{j_*}\pi_k(E)
\xrightarrow{p_*}\pi_k(B)\xrightarrow{\partial}\pi_{k-1}(F)
\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\pi_1(B)\xrightarrow{\partial}\pi_0(F)
\longrightarrow\pi_0(E)\longrightarrow\pi_0(B).
$$

The maps $j_*,p_*$ compose based sphere representatives with $j,p$; the maps on [path components](../../../../../path-component.md) do the same with points. To define $\partial$, represent a class by $(I^k,\partial I^k)\to(B,b_0)$ and lift the cube starting at $e_0$ on its bottom face, keeping its vertical sides at $e_0$. The opposite face then gives $(I^{k-1},\partial I^{k-1})\to(F,e_0)$. Relative lifting for a [CW pair](../../../../../cw-pair.md) gives this construction; its based homotopy class is independent of choices. At $k=1$ it records the component of the endpoint of the lifted loop. The last terms are pointed sets rather than groups in general.

Now write $W=S^1\vee S^n$ and let $f:W\to S^n$ be the collapse map. Inclusion of the $n$-sphere is a section, so $f_*:\pi_k(W)\to\pi_k(S^n)$ is surjective in every positive degree. Thus all connecting maps in its [homotopy fibre](../../../../../homotopy-fiber.md) sequence vanish. The fibre is [path-connected](../../../../../path-connected-space.md), and

$$
\pi_1(F)\cong\pi_1(W)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\pi_n(F)\cong\ker\bigl(\pi_n(W)\to\pi_n(S^n)\bigr).
$$

The section also gives the first assertion when $n=2$, by killing the connecting map from $\pi_2(S^2)$.

The [universal cover](../../../../../universal-cover.md) of $W$ consists of a line with an $n$-sphere attached at each integer. It is homotopy equivalent to the [wedge sum](../../../../../wedge-sum.md) $\bigvee_{k\in\mathbb Z}S^n$. This cover is $(n-1)$-connected, so the [Hurewicz theorem](../../../../../hurewicz-theorem.md) identifies its $n$th [homotopy group](../../../../../homotopy-group.md) with its $n$th [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md). Choose a generator $t$ of the [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) whose action shifts the sphere indexed by $k$ to that indexed by $k+1$. The resulting [homotopy groups as modules over the fundamental group](../../../../../homotopy-groups-as-modules-over-the-fundamental-group.md) give

$$
\pi_n(W)\cong R=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}].
$$

Every lifted sphere maps to $S^n$ with degree one. Consequently $f_*$ is the augmentation $\varepsilon(\sum a_kt^k)=\sum a_k$, with the trivial $R$-action on $\mathbb Z$. The inclusion of the fibre respects the [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) actions, so

$$
\boxed{\pi_n(F)\cong\ker\varepsilon=(t-1)R\cong R
\quad\text{as a }\mathbb Z[\pi_1(F)]\text{-module}}.
$$

The [augmentation ideal of the infinite cyclic group](../../../../../augmentation-ideal-of-the-infinite-cyclic-group.md) is generated by $t-1$: multiply a Laurent polynomial by a power of $t$ and use divisibility by $t-1$ when its value at one vanishes. Multiplication by $t-1$ is injective, giving the last isomorphism. This is a free module of rank one, whose underlying [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) has countably infinite rank; its $\pi_1$-action is not trivial.
