<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Compare two cooling states of the same [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md), holding its mass, nearly temperature-independent degenerate radius and composition fixed. The [matching of a white-dwarf envelope to a degenerate core](../../../../../../matching-of-a-white-dwarf-envelope-to-a-degenerate-core.md) gives $L\propto T_c^{7/2}$, while the [geometrical thickness of a white-dwarf radiative envelope](../../../../../../geometrical-thickness-of-a-white-dwarf-radiative-envelope.md) gives $s\propto T_c$ at leading thin-layer order. Eliminate $T_c$:

$$
\boxed{\frac{s_1}{s_2}=\frac{T_{c,1}}{T_{c,2}}
=\left(\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right)^{2/7}.}
$$

Thus **a [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) drop makes the envelope thinner**, with $s_2/s_1=(L_2/L_1)^{2/7}$. This is the [white-dwarf envelope thickness-luminosity relation](../../../../../../white-dwarf-envelope-thickness-luminosity-relation.md). Retaining finite thickness in the radiative-zero model gives the correction

$$
\frac{s_1}{s_2}=\left(\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right)^{2/7}
\frac{1+q_2}{1+q_1},\qquad
q_1/q_2=(L_1/L_2)^{2/7},
$$

which reduces to the boxed result for $q_1,q_2\ll1$.
