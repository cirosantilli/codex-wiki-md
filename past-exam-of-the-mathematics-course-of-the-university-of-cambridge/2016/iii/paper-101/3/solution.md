<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Put $u=X_1-1$ and $v=X_2-1$. Evaluation at $(1,1)$ is surjective, and

$$
R/(u,v)\cong\mathbb Z,
$$

so its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is the [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\boxed{P=(u,v)}$. In particular $P/P^2$ is naturally an $R/P=\mathbb Z$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), generated by the classes of $u,v$.

For integers $a,b$, including negative integers, reduction modulo $P^2$ gives

$$
(1+u)^a(1+v)^b\equiv1+au+bv\pmod{P^2}.
$$

For negative powers, this follows from $(1+u)^{-1}\equiv1-u$ and $(1+v)^{-1}\equiv1-v$; the mixed product $uv$ vanishes modulo $P^2$. Therefore a [Laurent polynomial](../../../../../laurent-polynomial.md) $f$ satisfies

$$
f\equiv\theta(f)+D_1(f)u+D_2(f)v\pmod{P^2},\qquad D_i(f)=\left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial X_i}\right|_{(1,1)}\in\mathbb Z.
$$

The [formal derivatives](../../../../../formal-derivative.md) obey the [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md), so $D_i(ab)=\theta(a)D_i(b)+\theta(b)D_i(a)$. If $a,b\in P$, both terms vanish. Hence both $D_i$ vanish on $P^2$ and define a map

$$
P/P^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2,\qquad [f]\longmapsto(D_1(f),D_2(f)).
$$

It sends $[u]$ to $(1,0)$ and $[v]$ to $(0,1)$, and the displayed expansion gives its inverse. Thus the [conormal module](../../../../../conormal-module.md) has the explicit basis

$$
\boxed{P/P^2\cong\mathbb Z^2,\quad [u],[v]\text{ a basis}.}
$$

Let $A=R_P$ be the [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md), with [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m=PR_P$. Every nonzero integer lies outside $P$ and is therefore inverted. The [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) is

$$
\boxed{A/\mathfrak m=\operatorname{Frac}(R/P)=\mathbb Q.}
$$

Indeed there is an explicit identification

$$
A\cong\mathbb Q[u,v]_{(u,v)}:
$$

inverting $1+u$ and $1+v$ has no effect in this [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md), and a polynomial denominator lies outside $(u,v)$ precisely when its constant term is nonzero. Conversely, clearing rational denominators turns every such fraction into a fraction from $R_P$.

For the [Krull dimension](../../../../../krull-dimension.md), the [prime ideal correspondence for localization](../../../../../prime-ideal-correspondence-for-localization.md) preserves the strict chain

$$
(0)\subsetneq(u)\subsetneq(u,v)
$$

of [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) in $R$. They are prime because the successive quotients are the [integral domains](../../../../../integral-domain.md) $R$, $\mathbb Z[X_2,X_2^{-1}]$, and $\mathbb Z$. They remain distinct after [localization at a prime ideal](../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) because they are all contained in $P$. Hence $\dim A\ge2$.

For the upper bound, $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) by the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) and [Localization of a Noetherian ring](../../../../../localization-of-a-noetherian-ring.md). Since $P$ is generated by two elements, the [Krull height theorem](../../../../../krull-height-theorem.md) gives $\operatorname{ht}P\le2$. The [prime ideal correspondence for localization](../../../../../prime-ideal-correspondence-for-localization.md) identifies $\dim R_P$ with $\operatorname{ht}P$, so

$$
\boxed{\dim R_P=2.}
$$

A [Noetherian local ring](../../../../../noetherian-local-ring.md) $(B,\mathfrak n)$ with [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) $\kappa$ is a [regular local ring](../../../../../regular-local-ring.md) if

$$
\boxed{\dim_\kappa(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2)=\dim B.}
$$

By the [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md), the dimension on the left is the minimal number of generators of $\mathfrak n$; this is also called the [embedding dimension](../../../../../embedding-dimension.md).

The [localization of a module](../../../../../localization-of-a-module.md) is exact and commutes with quotients and products of ideals. Applying it to the already computed [conormal module](../../../../../conormal-module.md) gives the [cotangent space of a local ring](../../../../../cotangent-space-of-a-local-ring.md)

$$
\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong(P/P^2)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\cong\mathbb Q^2.
$$

Its [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) is two, equal to $\dim A$. Therefore $\boxed{R_P\text{ is a regular local ring}}$. Notice that $P/P^2$ has a basis over $\mathbb Z$, whereas the localized [cotangent space of a local ring](../../../../../cotangent-space-of-a-local-ring.md) has a basis over the [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) $\mathbb Q$.
