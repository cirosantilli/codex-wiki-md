<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

For a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) $V=\bigoplus_dV_d$ over the [graded ring](../../../../../graded-ring.md) $S=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, with each $X_i$ of degree one, its [Poincare series of a graded module](../../../../../poincare-series-of-a-graded-module.md) is

$$
P_V(t)=\sum_d\dim_k(V_d)t^d.
$$

This is also its [Hilbert series](../../../../../hilbert-series.md). One usually takes $d\ge0$; allowing an integer grading also permits finitely many negative degrees. Homogeneous generators of degrees $a_1,\ldots,a_h$ give a surjection $\bigoplus_j S(-a_j)\to V$, where the [graded shift](../../../../../graded-shift.md) is defined by $V(-a)_d=V_{d-a}$. Consequently every $V_d$ is a finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) and $V_d=0$ for all sufficiently negative $d$.

The [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md) in the standard grading says

$$
\boxed{P_V(t)=\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t)^n},\qquad Q(t)\in\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}].}
$$

For a nonnegatively graded $V$, the numerator is in $\mathbb Z[t]$. Equivalently, the only possible pole of this rational expression is at $t=1$, with order at most $n$.

We prove the [Hilbert-Serre theorem](../../../../../hilbert-serre-theorem.md) by induction on the number of variables. For $n=0$, $S=k$ and $V$ is a finite-dimensional [graded module](../../../../../graded-module.md); thus $P_V(t)$ is a Laurent [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Suppose $n>0$ and put

$$
K=0:_VX_n=\{v\in V:X_nv=0\},\qquad C=V/X_nV.
$$

The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) makes $S$ a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), so $K$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md), as is $C$. Both are [graded modules](../../../../../graded-module.md) killed by $X_n$, hence finitely generated over $S'=k[X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}]$.

The graded [exact sequence](../../../../../exact-sequence.md)

$$
0\longrightarrow K(-1)\longrightarrow V(-1)
\xrightarrow{\ X_n\ }V\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow0
$$

identifies the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and [cokernel](../../../../../cokernel.md) of multiplication by $X_n$. Taking the alternating sum of the finite-dimensional degree components yields

$$
tP_K(t)-tP_V(t)+P_V(t)-P_C(t)=0,
$$

so

$$
(1-t)P_V(t)=P_C(t)-tP_K(t).
$$

By induction the right side has denominator $(1-t)^{n-1}$, which proves the required denominator $(1-t)^n$. Since $(1-t)^nP_V(t)$ has no negative powers when $V$ is nonnegatively graded, its Laurent [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) numerator is an ordinary [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in that case.

For $n\ge1$, this also implies the usual [Hilbert polynomial](../../../../../hilbert-polynomial.md) consequence. Writing $Q(t)=\sum_jq_jt^j$ and using

$$
\frac1{(1-t)^n}=\sum_{d\ge0}\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}t^d
$$

gives, for all sufficiently large $d$,

$$
\dim_kV_d=\sum_jq_j\binom{d-j+n-1}{n-1},
$$

a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in $d$ of degree at most $n-1$. For $n=0$, the graded pieces are eventually zero.

For the final request, a [free resolution](../../../../../free-resolution.md) of $V$ is an [exact sequence](../../../../../exact-sequence.md)

$$
\cdots\longrightarrow F_2\xrightarrow{d_2}F_1\xrightarrow{d_1}F_0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow0
$$

with each $F_i$ a [free module](../../../../../free-module.md). In the graded setting we take finite [direct sums](../../../../../direct-sum.md) of shifts $S(-a)$ and maps preserving degree. The resolution has length at most $n$ if $F_i=0$ for every $i>n$. The [syzygy modules](../../../../../syzygy-module.md) are the successive [kernels](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) which record the relations among generators, then relations among those relations, and so on.

Put $\mathfrak m=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, the homogeneous [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) with $S/\mathfrak m=k$. Choose a homogeneous $k$-basis of $V/\mathfrak mV$ and lift it to $V$. These lifts generate $V$ by the [graded Nakayama lemma](../../../../../graded-nakayama-lemma.md): a bounded-below [graded module](../../../../../graded-module.md) $W$ satisfying $W=\mathfrak mW$ must be zero, since a nonzero homogeneous element of least degree could not be a sum of variables times elements of lower degrees. Apply this to the quotient by the [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) generated by the chosen lifts. This gives a surjection $F_0\to V$ inducing an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) modulo $\mathfrak m$.

Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is finitely generated because $S$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). Repeat the construction for that kernel, then for each subsequent kernel. We obtain a [minimal graded free resolution](../../../../../minimal-graded-free-resolution.md), meaning

$$
d_i(F_i)\subseteq\mathfrak mF_{i-1}\qquad(i\ge1).
$$

Indeed, at each stage the free cover induces an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) modulo $\mathfrak m$, so its kernel lies in $\mathfrak m$ times its source. The sequence is exact by construction, though it may at first appear infinite.

The [Koszul complex](../../../../../koszul-complex.md) on $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ is

$$
K_i=\bigwedge^i S^n\cong S(-i)^{\binom ni},\qquad 0\le i\le n,
$$

with basis vectors $e_j$ given degree one, and differential

$$
\partial(e_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_i})
=\sum_{a=1}^{i}(-1)^{a-1}X_{j_a}
 e_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{e_{j_a}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_i}.
$$

The hat means omission of that factor. Every pair of terms in $\partial^2$ cancels with opposite signs, so this is a [chain complex](../../../../../chain-complex.md). The sequence $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ is a [regular sequence](../../../../../regular-sequence.md): after quotienting by the first $j-1$ variables, the next variable is a [non-zero-divisor](../../../../../non-zero-divisor.md) in the remaining [polynomial ring](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md).

The [Koszul complex](../../../../../koszul-complex.md) is consequently exact in positive degrees and has $H_0=k$, so it is a [Koszul resolution](../../../../../koszul-resolution.md) of $k$ of length $n$. For completeness, one proves this by induction: for one variable it is $0\to S\xrightarrow{X_1}S\to S/(X_1)\to0$. Appending $X_j$ constructs the [mapping cone](../../../../../mapping-cone-homological-algebra.md) of multiplication by $X_j$ on the previous [chain complex](../../../../../chain-complex.md). Its [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) is zero in positive degrees because $X_j$ acts injectively on $S/(X_1,\ldots,X_{j-1})$; its degree-zero [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) is the further quotient. Here a [mapping cone](../../../../../mapping-cone-homological-algebra.md) combines a [chain complex](../../../../../chain-complex.md) with a shifted copy and adds the given multiplication map to the differential, thereby measuring its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and [cokernel](../../../../../cokernel.md) on [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md).

The [Tor functor](../../../../../tor-functor.md) $\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(V,k)$ is defined as the degree-$i$ [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) of $F_\bullet\otimes_S k$ for a [free resolution](../../../../../free-resolution.md) $F_\bullet$ of $V$. It can equally be computed as the [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) of $V\otimes_S K_\bullet$, using the [Koszul resolution](../../../../../koszul-resolution.md) of $k$. To see the equality, form the [double complex](../../../../../double-complex.md) $F_\bullet\otimes_S K_\bullet$. Taking [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) first along $F_\bullet$ leaves $V\otimes_S K_\bullet$, since each $K_i$ is a [free module](../../../../../free-module.md); taking [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) first along $K_\bullet$ leaves $F_\bullet\otimes_S k$, since each $F_i$ is free. The finite sums along each total degree identify both with the [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) of the total complex. This also explains the symmetry used in computing the [Tor functor](../../../../../tor-functor.md).

Since $K_i=0$ for $i>n$, we have $\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(V,k)=0$ for $i>n$. On the other hand, all differentials of the [minimal graded free resolution](../../../../../minimal-graded-free-resolution.md) become zero after tensoring with $k=S/\mathfrak m$, so

$$
\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(V,k)\cong F_i\otimes_S k=F_i/\mathfrak mF_i.
$$

Thus $F_i/\mathfrak mF_i=0$ for $i>n$, and the [graded Nakayama lemma](../../../../../graded-nakayama-lemma.md) implies $F_i=0$ for those $i$. We have proved the required [Hilbert syzygy theorem](../../../../../hilbert-s-syzygy-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{0\longrightarrow F_n\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_0\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow0,}
$$

where some initial $F_i$ may be zero. The argument supplies finitely generated graded [free modules](../../../../../free-module.md), which is stronger than merely giving an ungraded [free resolution](../../../../../free-resolution.md).
