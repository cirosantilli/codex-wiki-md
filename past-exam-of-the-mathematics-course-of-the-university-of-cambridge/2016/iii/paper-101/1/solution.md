<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../laurent-polynomial-ring.md) is a [localization of a ring](../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md):

$$
R=k[X_1,X_2]_{X_1X_2}.
$$

The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) makes $k[X_1,X_2]$ a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), and [Localization of a Noetherian ring](../../../../../localization-of-a-noetherian-ring.md) preserves this property. Thus $\boxed{R\text{ is Noetherian}}$.

For [separating finite Laurent support by an integer weight](../../../../../separating-finite-laurent-support-by-an-integer-weight.md), take a nonzero [Laurent polynomial](../../../../../laurent-polynomial.md) $r$ and let $F\subset\mathbb Z^2$ be its finite set of exponent pairs with nonzero coefficients. Choose an integer $N$ such that

$$
N>\max_{m,m'\in F}|m_1-m'_1|,
$$

where the maximum is zero if $F$ has only one member. Set $(a_1,a_2)=(1,N)$. If $m_1+Nm_2=m'_1+Nm'_2$, then

$$
N|m_2-m'_2|=|m_1-m'_1|<N,
$$

so $m_2=m'_2$ and then $m_1=m'_1$. Consequently all powers of $T$ in $\theta_{(1,N)}(r)$ are distinct. The [linear independence](../../../../../linear-independence.md) of the Laurent [monomials](../../../../../monomial.md) prevents cancellation, giving

$$
\boxed{\theta_{(1,N)}(r)\ne0.}
$$

The choice depends on the finite support of $r$; a single fixed substitution need not detect every nonzero element.

For [Noetherianity of subalgebras of a one-variable Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../noetherianity-of-subalgebras-of-a-one-variable-laurent-polynomial-ring.md), let $A$ be a unital $k$-[subalgebra](../../../../../subalgebra.md) of $k[T,T^{-1}]$. If $A=k$, it is already a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). Otherwise we find a nonconstant $f\in A$ and an ambient [ring](../../../../../ring.md) $B$ containing $A$ such that $B$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) over $k[f]$.

If $A\subseteq k[T]$, choose a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $f=a_dT^d+\cdots+a_0$ in $A$, where $a_d\ne0$. Then $T$ is an [integral element](../../../../../integral-element.md) over $k[f]$, since it satisfies the [monic polynomial](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) equation

$$
T^d+\frac{a_{d-1}}{a_d}T^{d-1}+\cdots+\frac{a_1}{a_d}T+\frac{a_0-f}{a_d}=0.
$$

Thus $B=k[T]$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) over $k[f]$, generated by $1,T,\ldots,T^{d-1}$. The case $A\subseteq k[T^{-1}]$ is identical after replacing $T$ by $T^{-1}$.

In the remaining case $A$ is contained in neither of these two [polynomial rings](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md). There is an element with a positive exponent and an element with a negative exponent. If either already has both, use it as $f$. Otherwise their sum has both, since the positive powers of the first and the negative powers of the second cannot cancel. We obtain

$$
f=\sum_{j=-p}^{q}a_jT^j,\qquad p,q>0,\quad a_{-p}a_q\ne0.
$$

Multiplying this identity by $T^p$ gives a [polynomial equation](../../../../../polynomial-equation.md) for $T$ with leading coefficient $a_q$:

$$
\sum_{j=-p}^{q}a_jT^{j+p}-fT^p=0.
$$

Dividing by $a_q$ makes it monic. Similarly, multiplication by $T^{-q}$ gives a monic equation for $T^{-1}$ after division by $a_{-p}$. Both generators are therefore [integral elements](../../../../../integral-element.md) over $k[f]$, so $B=k[T,T^{-1}]$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) over $k[f]$.

In all three cases $k[f]$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), as a quotient of a one-variable [polynomial ring](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md). The [subalgebra](../../../../../subalgebra.md) $A\subseteq B$ is also a $k[f]$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), so it is a [Noetherian module](../../../../../noetherian-module.md). Every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $A$ is a $k[f]$-[submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of $A$ and is therefore finitely generated over $k[f]$, hence over $A$. This proves

$$
\boxed{\text{Every }k\text{-subalgebra of }k[T,T^{-1}]\text{ is Noetherian}.}
$$

For the final request, take the following [subalgebra](../../../../../subalgebra.md) of the two-variable [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../laurent-polynomial-ring.md):

$$
\boxed{A=k+X_1k[X_1,X_2]=k[X_1,X_1X_2,X_1X_2^2,\ldots].}
$$

Let $J=X_1k[X_1,X_2]$, an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $A$. We have

$$
A/J\cong k,\qquad J^2=X_1^2k[X_1,X_2],\qquad J/J^2\cong X_1k[X_2]
$$

as $k$-[vector spaces](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). In particular, the classes of $X_1X_2^j$, for $j\ge0$, are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md). If $J$ had finitely many generators as an $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), their classes would span $J/J^2$ over $A/J=k$, contradicting its infinite [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md). Hence $J$ is not finitely generated and $A$ is not a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). This illustrates why the preceding result is special to one Laurent variable.
