<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Spec}(n)$, define **$\alpha\sim\beta$** when their [Gelfand–Tsetlin basis](../../../../../../gelfand-tsetlin-basis.md) lines occur in the same [irreducible representation](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md). This is well defined: $GZ_n$ contains the centre of $\mathbb C S_n$, whose [primitive central idempotents](../../../../../../primitive-central-idempotent.md) separate the [irreducible modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md), and part 1(ii) says these central elements are [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in the $X_j$. Consequently the same weight cannot label two different [irreducible modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md).

For [content vectors of standard Young tableaux](../../../../../../content-vector-of-a-standard-young-tableau.md), an [admissible adjacent swap of a standard Young tableau](../../../../../../admissible-adjacent-swap-of-a-standard-young-tableau.md) exchanges coordinates $i,i+1$ whose difference has absolute value greater than $1$. Define **$\alpha\approx\beta$** as the equivalence relation generated by such swaps within $\operatorname{Cont}(n)$.

Equivalently, after reconstructing the tableaux, $\alpha\approx\beta$ means that the two tableaux have the same final [Young diagram](../../../../../../young-diagram.md). Indeed, an admissible swap exchanges consecutive entries in incomparable cells, preserves the diagram and leaves a [standard Young tableau](../../../../../../standard-young-tableau.md). Conversely, tableaux of a fixed shape are [linear extensions of a partially ordered set](../../../../../../linear-extension.md), with the cells ordered by their row and column requirements; any two linear extensions are connected by adjacent swaps of incomparable elements. We explain this connectivity and its spectral consequence in part (v).
