Use the [canonical embedding into the bidual](../../../../canonical-embedding-into-the-bidual.md) $J_Xx(f)=f(x)$. It is an [isometry](../../../../isometry.md) by the [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../../hahn-banach-theorem.md). The [weak-star topology](../../../../weak-star-topology.md) $\sigma(X^{**},X^*)$, restricted to $J_XX$, is exactly the [weak topology](../../../../weak-topology-split.md) $\sigma(X,X^*)$: both are determined by the same scalar evaluations $f(x)$. Therefore if $K\subseteq X$ is a [weakly compact set](../../../../weakly-compact-set.md), $J_XK$ is [compact](../../../../compact-space.md) in the Hausdorff [weak-star topology](../../../../weak-star-topology.md) of $X^{**}$, and hence is closed for the [weak-star topology](../../../../weak-star-topology.md). This explains the first assertion without treating the whole embedded space $J_XX$ as weak-star closed.

For a [bounded linear operator](../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) $T:X\to Y$, its [Banach-space adjoint](../../../../transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator.md) is

$$
T^*:Y^*\to X^*,\qquad T^*y^*=y^*\circ T.
$$

For each $x\in X$, the scalar evaluation $y^*\mapsto(T^*y^*)(x)=y^*(Tx)$ is weak-star continuous on $Y^*$. These are exactly the defining evaluations of the [weak-star topology](../../../../weak-star-topology.md) on $X^*$, so $T^*$ is weak-star-to-weak-star continuous. Equivalently, if $y_\alpha^*\xrightarrow{w^*}y^*$, then $(T^*y_\alpha^*)(x)\to(T^*y^*)(x)$ for every $x$.

The [Goldstine theorem](../../../../goldstine-theorem.md) states that $J_X(B_X)$ is dense in $B_{X^{**}}$ for $\sigma(X^{**},X^*)$, where the balls are closed unit balls. The [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md) states that for any [normed vector space](../../../../normed-vector-space.md) $E$, the closed dual unit ball $B_{E^*}$ is compact for $\sigma(E^*,E)$. Neither assertion claims norm compactness or, in general, sequential compactness.

For the [weakly compact operator](../../../../weakly-compact-operator.md) equivalences below, identify $X$ and $Y$ with their canonical images only when stated. In particular, the range condition means $T^{**}(X^{**})\subseteq J_YY$. We shall prove $(i)\Longleftrightarrow(ii)$, $(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)\Longrightarrow(iv)\Longrightarrow(ii)$. The key identities, obtained by evaluating on $y^*\in Y^*$, are

$$
T^{**}J_X=J_YT,\qquad
(T^{**}F)(y^*)=F(T^*y^*).
$$
