<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For every real $\alpha$, direct substitution gives the [axially equivariant monomial rational map](../../../../../../axially-equivariant-monomial-rational-map.md) identity

$$
\boxed{R(e^{i\alpha}z)=e^{in\alpha}R(z),\qquad R(1/z)=1/R(z).}
$$

The first is a spatial rotation through $\alpha$ about the third axis compensated by a target rotation through $n\alpha$, hence by an [isospin rotation](../../../../../../isorotation.md) of the [Skyrmion](../../../../../../skyrmion.md). The second pairs the spatial half-turn about the first axis with a target half-turn about that axis. For $n>1$, these generate the continuous axial rotation group together with perpendicular half-turns, often written $D_\infty$; it is the group of [rotations preserving an axis](../../../../../../rotations-preserving-an-axis.md) in the three-dimensional [special orthogonal group](../../../../../../special-orthogonal-group.md). Under rotations about the axis, the pure spatial stabilizer of the map itself includes the [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) $C_n$, whereas the larger symmetry just described is combined spatial-target equivariance. Also $R(\overline z)=\overline{R(z)}$ gives a paired reflection symmetry of its angular density.

The [angular Jacobian of a rational map](../../../../../../angular-jacobian-of-a-rational-map.md) is

$$
J_R=n^2\frac{|z|^{2n-2}(1+|z|^2)^2}{(1+|z|^{2n})^2}.
$$

It depends only on latitude. Put $u=\log|z|$; then $J_R=n^2\cosh^2u/\cosh^2(nu)$. Since $\cosh(nu)\geq\cosh u$ for $n\geq1$, the maximum is $n^2$ at $u=0$, the equator. For $n>1$ it vanishes at the two poles, producing an axially symmetric ring of angular [Skyrme baryon density](../../../../../../skyrme-baryon-density.md). For $n=1$, $R$ is the identity: every spatial rotation is compensated by the same target rotation, and the [rational map approximation for Skyrmions](../../../../../../rational-map-approximation-for-skyrmions.md) becomes the fully [spherically symmetric](../../../../../../spherical-symmetry.md) [Skyrmion hedgehog ansatz](../../../../../../skyrmion-hedgehog-ansatz.md). **The charge and uses are**

$$
\boxed{B=n;\quad n=1\text{ gives the spherical hedgehog},\quad n=2\text{ gives a toroidal two-Skyrmion approximation}.}
$$

The degree-one construction captures the symmetry of the unit [Skyrmion](../../../../../../skyrmion.md) exactly; its radial profile still has to be solved. The degree-two map supplies the appropriate [toroidal two-Skyrmion](../../../../../../toroidal-two-skyrmion.md) symmetry and useful initial data. Higher monomials give axial charge-$n$ competitors, but axial symmetry need not minimize the energy: the usual lowest-charge examples already include the [tetrahedral three-Skyrmion](../../../../../../tetrahedral-three-skyrmion.md) and the [cubic four-Skyrmion](../../../../../../cubic-four-skyrmion.md). [Topological charge](../../../../../../topological-charge.md) determines neither shape nor energy optimality by itself.
