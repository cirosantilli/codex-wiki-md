<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The fixed observer is the ray $V=0$. The physically selected zero-[group velocity](../../../../../../../group-velocity.md) saddle defines the [absolute wavenumber](../../../../../../../absolute-wavenumber.md) $k_0$ and [absolute frequency](../../../../../../../absolute-frequency.md) $\omega_0$:

$$
\boxed{\omega'(k_0)=0,\qquad D(k_0,\omega_0)=0,\qquad\omega_0=\omega(k_0).}
$$

For a smooth frequency branch with $D_\omega\ne0$, the saddle condition is equivalently $D_k(k_0,\omega_0)=0$. The [absolute growth rate](../../../../../../../absolute-growth-rate.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sigma(0)=\operatorname{Im}\omega_0.}
$$

Here $k_0$ and $\omega_0$ can be complex. A physical [saddle point](../../../../../../../saddle-point.md) is selected by the localized impulse response and deformation of its [Fourier transform](../../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) contour; an arbitrary algebraic stationary point need not determine [absolute hydrodynamic instability](../../../../../../../absolute-hydrodynamic-instability.md).
