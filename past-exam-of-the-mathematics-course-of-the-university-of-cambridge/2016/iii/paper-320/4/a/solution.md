<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**False.** [Stellar evaporation](../../../../../../stellar-evaporation.md) removes [stars](../../../../../../star.md) above the escape energy after [two-body relaxation](../../../../../../two-body-relaxation.md) has redistributed their energies. The remaining self-gravitating cluster need not become less dense; losing mass is not the same as holding its radius fixed.

For a slowly evolving isolated cluster, apply the [virial theorem](../../../../../../virial-theorem.md) at each approximate equilibrium: $E=T+W=W/2=-T$. [Stars](../../../../../../star.md) evaporating with nearly zero energy at infinity remove little total energy, so the bound cluster has nearly constant negative $E$. With [gravitational radius](../../../../../../gravitational-radius.md) $R_g$ defined by $W=-GM^2/R_g$,

$$
\boxed{R_g=\frac{GM^2}{2|E|}\propto M^2.}
$$

For homologous structure, its characteristic mean density therefore scales as

$$
\boxed{\bar\rho\propto\frac{M}{R_g^3}\propto M^{-5}.}
$$

It increases as mass is lost. Escaping [stars](../../../../../../star.md) carrying positive energy make the bound energy more negative and strengthen the contraction tendency. This is [virial contraction under stellar evaporation](../../../../../../virial-contraction-under-stellar-evaporation.md), related to the negative heat capacity of bound gravitational systems. A tidally limited [globular cluster](../../../../../../globular-cluster.md) has additional radius constraints, so this scaling is not universal, but the claimed inevitable density decrease is false even in the simplest isolated evaporation model.
