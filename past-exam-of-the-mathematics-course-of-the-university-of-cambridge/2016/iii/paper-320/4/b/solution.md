<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**False as a claimed change between isolated steady endpoints.** [Conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) by itself would allow a more negative $W$ and a larger $T$, but both endpoints must also obey the [virial theorem](../../../../../../virial-theorem.md). For a finite isolated equilibrium with no surface term,

$$
2T+W=0,\qquad E=T+W=-T=\frac W2.
$$

If total $E$ is conserved between the two equilibria, the [fixed-energy virial constraint between steady states](../../../../../../fixed-energy-virial-constraint-between-steady-states.md) gives

$$
\boxed{T_{\mathrm{final}}=T_{\mathrm{initial}}=-E,\qquad W_{\mathrm{final}}=W_{\mathrm{initial}}=2E.}
$$

This $T$ includes both ordered rotation and random stellar motion. A [galactic bar](../../../../../../galactic-bar.md) or other instability can redistribute [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) and concentrate a central component, but compensating expansion or rearrangement elsewhere must preserve the global energy constraint. Central concentration alone does not prove that the whole system has a more negative $W$.

A remnant could have different energies if [stars](../../../../../../star.md) escape, or if an external component exchanges energy with it; then the conserved total energy would include that other component. Those cases do not justify treating the bound disc simultaneously as a closed fixed-energy system and as a new equilibrium with increased $T$.
