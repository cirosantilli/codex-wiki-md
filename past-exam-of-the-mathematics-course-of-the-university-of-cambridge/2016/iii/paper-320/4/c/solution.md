<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**False.** The [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md) is infinite in extent and mass. The isolated finite-system [virial theorem](../../../../../../virial-theorem.md) cannot be applied to its global divergent energies without boundary terms.

A direct [Spherical Jeans equation](../../../../../../spherical-jeans-equation.md) counterexample is enough. For isotropic [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) with constant one-dimensional dispersion $\sigma^2$, its density slope gives

$$
\frac d{dr}(\rho\sigma^2)=-\frac{2\rho\sigma^2}{r}=\rho\psi'=-\frac{\rho v_0^2}{r}.
$$

Thus $\sigma^2=v_0^2/2$ and the [circular and isotropic speed comparison in a singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../circular-and-isotropic-speed-comparison-in-a-singular-isothermal-sphere.md) is

$$
\boxed{\langle v^2\rangle_{\mathrm{isotropic}}=\frac32v_0^2,\qquad v_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\frac32}\,v_0.}
$$

This is realized by a positive isotropic distribution $F\propto\exp(E/\sigma^2)$, which gives a [Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md) of [velocities](../../../../../../velocity.md) and $\rho\propto\exp(\psi/\sigma^2)\propto r^{-2}$. [Circular orbits](../../../../../../circular-orbit.md) have instead $\langle v_r^2\rangle=0$ and $\langle v_\theta^2\rangle=\langle v_\phi^2\rangle=v_0^2/2$, so their total mean square speed is $v_0^2$. The isotropic model contains eccentric orbits except for a measure-zero subset.

The [boundary virial term of a singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../boundary-virial-term-of-a-singular-isothermal-sphere.md) explains the different energy budgets of a finite spherical region. At radius $R$, its radial-pressure surface term is $4\pi R^3\rho(R)\sigma_r^2$. It is nonzero in the isotropic model and zero in the circular-orbit model. Equality of the density therefore does not enforce equality of the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) energy through an incorrectly omitted surface term.
