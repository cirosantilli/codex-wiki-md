<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Use a [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t),\mathbb P)$, and put $\mathcal F_{0-}=\mathcal F_0$. The **[predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra)** on $\Omega\times[0,\infty)$ is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P=\sigma\{A\times\{0\},\ A\in\mathcal F_0;\ A\times(s,t],\ A\in\mathcal F_s,\ 0\leq s<t<\infty\}.}
$$

Equivalently it is generated by left-continuous [adapted processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process). A real-valued **[previsible process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process)** is a function $(\omega,t)\mapsto H_t(\omega)$ that is [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) for $\mathcal P$ and the [Borel sigma-algebra](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-sigma-algebra) of $\mathbb R$.

A **[simple predictable process](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process)** has the form

$$
H=h_{-1}\mathbf1_{\{0\}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_i\mathbf1_{(t_i,t_{i+1}]},\qquad 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n<\infty,
$$

where $h_{-1}$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_0$-[measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability), and each $h_i$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_{t_i}$-[measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability). Set $H=0$ after $t_n$; allowing a final infinite interval gives an equivalent dense class. The values may be restricted to finitely many values if desired, by approximating bounded [measurable functions](../../../measure-theory.md#measurable-function) by simple functions. The initial-time term matters for arbitrary [finite measures](../../../measure-theory.md#finite-measure) on $\mathcal P$: a measure can charge time zero, even though the [stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) against a continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md) does not.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

For fixed $u>0$, define the [left-limit sigma-algebra](../../../stochastic-process.md#left-limit-sigma-algebra) by $\mathcal F_{u-}=\sigma(\bigcup_{s<u}\mathcal F_s)$. The section at $u$ of a generating rectangle $A\times(s,t]$ of the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra) is $A$ when $s<u\leq t$, and empty otherwise. In the first case $A\in\mathcal F_s\subseteq\mathcal F_{u-}$. Sections of the time-zero generators are empty for $u>0$.

The collection of subsets of sample-time space whose section at $u$ belongs to $\mathcal F_{u-}$ is a [sigma-algebra](../../../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra): sections commute with complements and countable unions. It therefore contains all of $\mathcal P$. Apply this to $\{(\omega,t):H_t(\omega)\in E\}$ for every [Borel set](../../../measure-theory.md#borel-set) $E$. Thus **[predictable sections at deterministic times](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sections-at-deterministic-times) satisfy**

$$
\boxed{H_u\text{ is }\mathcal F_{u-}\text{-measurable}.}
$$

At $u=0$ the same argument gives $\mathcal F_0$-[measurability](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability), using the convention $\mathcal F_{0-}=\mathcal F_0$. The PDF asks for information strictly before $u$; the TeX aid drops the minus sign.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Let $V$ be the closure of the [simple predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) in the [Hilbert space](../../../hilbert-space.md) $L^2(\mathcal P,\mu)$. Common refinement of deterministic partitions makes these processes a vector space. To prove **[density of simple predictable processes for finite measures](../../../martingale.md#density-of-simple-predictable-processes-for-finite-measures)**, suppose $g\in V^\perp$. Since $\mu$ is a [finite measure](../../../measure-theory.md#finite-measure), the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives $g\in L^1(\mu)$, so

$$
\nu(E)=\int_Eg\,d\mu
$$

defines a finite signed [measure](../../../measure-theory.md#measure). The indicator of each predictable generating rectangle, including each time-zero rectangle, is a [simple predictable process](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process); hence $\nu$ vanishes on these rectangles.

Finite intersections of the rectangles are again rectangles or empty. Add the whole space to this [pi-system](../../../measure-theory.md#pi-system). Its $\nu$-mass is zero because $\mathbf1_{\Omega\times[0,n]}$ is simple and converges to $1$ in $L^2(\mu)$ by the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem). The zero sets of $\nu$ form a [lambda-system](../../../measure-theory.md#dynkin-system) now that its total mass is zero. The [Pi-lambda theorem](../../../probability-theory.md#pi-lambda-theorem) therefore gives $\nu(E)=0$ for every $E\in\mathcal P$. Taking the sets where $g>0$ and $g<0$ gives $g=0$ $\mu$-almost everywhere.

Thus $V^\perp=\{0\}$ and the [orthogonal complement](../../../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-complement) criterion for a closed subspace of a [Hilbert space](../../../hilbert-space.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\overline{\mathcal S}^{\,L^2(\mathcal P,\mu)}=L^2(\mathcal P,\mu).}
$$

Omitting the time-zero term would make this conclusion false when $\mu$ is concentrated at time zero.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

For the [simple predictable process](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) from part (a), define its **[stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral)** by

$$
\boxed{(H\cdot M)_t=\sum_i h_i\bigl(M_{t\wedge t_{i+1}}-M_{t\wedge t_i}\bigr).}
$$

The time-zero value contributes nothing. Write $D_i=M_{t\wedge t_{i+1}}-M_{t\wedge t_i}$. If $i<j$ and $t>t_j$, then $h_iD_i h_j$ is $\mathcal F_{t_j}$-[measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) and $\mathbb E[D_j\mid\mathcal F_{t_j}]=0$. If $t\leq t_j$, then $D_j=0$. Boundedness ensures [integrability](../../../measure-theory.md#integrability), so all off-diagonal terms vanish by [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[(H\cdot M)_t^2]=\sum_i\mathbb E[h_i^2D_i^2].}
$$

The [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) theorem says $M^2-[M]$ is a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). A bounded continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md) is [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function), and localization gives the conditional second-moment identity

$$
\mathbb E[D_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{t_i}]=\mathbb E\bigl([M]_{t\wedge t_{i+1}}-[M]_{t_i}\mid\mathcal F_{t_i}\bigr)\qquad(t\geq t_i).
$$

Multiplication by $h_i^2$ and summation therefore give the **[Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry)**

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E\left|\int_0^t H_s\,dM_s\right|^2=\mathbb E\int_0^t H_s^2\,d[M]_s.}
$$

For [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md), $[M]_s=s$. More generally the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) holds for every [predictable process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) with finite right-hand side, against a continuous [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [martingale](../../../martingale.md). Indeed, on a fixed horizon the [quadratic-variation measure](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation-measure) $\mu_M(E)=\mathbb E\int_0^t\mathbf1_E\,d[M]$ is finite. Part (c) approximates the integrand by [simple predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process); the displayed equality makes the elementary integrals Cauchy in $L^2$ and defines their unique limit. The finite sum is exactly the elementary case of the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry).

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

On the same [measurable space](../../../measure-theory.md#measurable-space), **[absolute continuity of measures](../../../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures)** means

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\mathbb P}\ll\mathbb P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb P(A)=0\implies\widetilde{\mathbb P}(A)=0\text{ for every }A\in\mathcal F.}
$$

The [Radon-Nikodym theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-theorem) then supplies a nonnegative [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) density $Z$ with $\mathbb E_{\mathbb P}Z=1$ and $\widetilde{\mathbb P}(A)=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P}[Z\mathbf1_A]$. The converse follows directly from this formula. Strict positivity of the [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-derivative) gives equivalence of the two [probability measures](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure); [absolute continuity of measures](../../../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures) alone need not give equivalence.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) $M$, its **[stochastic exponential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential)** is

$$
\boxed{Z_t=\mathcal E(M)_t=\exp\{M_t-M_0-\tfrac12[M]_t\}.}
$$

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives $dZ_t=Z_t\,dM_t$ and $Z_0=1$, so $Z$ is a strictly positive [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). The finite-horizon **[Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem)** says: if $Z$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) on $[0,T]$ and $d\widetilde{\mathbb P}=Z_T\,d\mathbb P$ on $\mathcal F_T$, then for every [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) $N$ under $\mathbb P$,

$$
\boxed{\widetilde N_t=N_t-[N,M]_t\text{ is a }\widetilde{\mathbb P}\text{-local martingale},\quad 0\leq t\leq T.}
$$

For an infinite-horizon [change of measure](../../../measure-theory.md#change-of-measure), assume $Z$ is a [uniformly integrable](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) [martingale](../../../martingale.md) and use its terminal density $Z_\infty$. In particular, if $M=\int\theta\,dB$, the process $\widetilde B=B-\int\theta\,ds$ is [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) under the new [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure), by the [Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion).

**The [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) cannot change.** Adding the continuous [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) $-[N,M]$ leaves $[\widetilde N]=[N]$. Also the same pathwise partition sums defining $[N]$ have the same limit under an absolutely continuous [change of measure](../../../measure-theory.md#change-of-measure); convergence in [probability](../../../probability-theory.md#probability) transfers by integrability of the density. Thus the bracket of the unchanged process $N$, now a [semimartingale](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale), is also unchanged. The [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) property itself need not survive. This is [quadratic covariation under an absolutely continuous measure change](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation-under-an-absolutely-continuous-measure-change).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The **[Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) holds on every horizon**, since

$$
\mathbb E\exp\{\tfrac12[M]_T\}\leq e^{C/2}<\infty.
$$

Therefore $Z=\mathcal E(M)$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) and the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) applies through each finite $T$.

The deterministic bound also gives a genuine infinite-horizon density. For any $p>1$, the [stochastic exponential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential) identity is

$$
Z_t^p=\mathcal E(pM)_t\exp\{\tfrac12p(p-1)[M]_t\}.
$$

A nonnegative [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) is a [supermartingale](../../../martingale.md#supermartingale), so

$$
\boxed{\sup_t\mathbb E Z_t^p\leq\exp\{\tfrac12p(p-1)C\}.}
$$

This $L^p$ bound gives [uniform integrability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability). Also $M$ is a [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale), since localization and the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) give $\mathbb E M_t^2=\mathbb E[M]_t\leq C$. The [L2 martingale convergence theorem](../../../martingale.md#l2-martingale-convergence-theorem) gives a finite limit $M_\infty$; hence $Z_\infty=\exp(M_\infty-[M]_\infty/2)>0$ and $\mathbb E Z_\infty=1$. Weighting by $Z_\infty$ defines an equivalent [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) on $\mathcal F_\infty$. This is the [bounded-bracket criterion for a stochastic exponential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#bounded-bracket-criterion-for-a-stochastic-exponential).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

The reference [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) must start at $x$, and both laws must use the same stopped-path rule. Under the reference [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) let $R_s=x+W_s$, let $\tau$ be its exit time from $(a,b)$, and put $T=t\wedge\tau$ and $c=(\delta-1)/2$. The **[Girsanov density for a stopped Bessel process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-density-for-a-stopped-bessel-process)**, as a functional of the stopped reference path, is

$$
\boxed{D(R)=\exp\left\{c\int_0^T\frac{dW_s}{R_s}-\frac{c^2}{2}\int_0^T\frac{ds}{R_s^2}\right\}.}
$$

Use the [predictable process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) $\theta_s=cR_s^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{s\leq\tau\}}$, with any harmless choice at the endpoint. On $[0,t]$, $\int\theta_s^2ds\leq c^2t/a^2$. The [Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) holds; under the density $D$, the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) makes $\widetilde W_s=W_s-\int_0^s\theta_u du$ a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md). Before exit, $dR_s=cR_s^{-1}ds+d\widetilde W_s$, which is the required [stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation). The drift is a [locally Lipschitz function](../../../real-analysis.md#locally-lipschitz-function) on $(0,\infty)$, so [pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness) and [uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law) hold up to this exit time.

For a version involving just the endpoint and an ordinary integral, the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $\log R$ gives

$$
\int_0^T\frac{dW_s}{R_s}=\log\frac{R_T}{x}+\frac12\int_0^T\frac{ds}{R_s^2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{D(R)=\left(\frac{R_T}{x}\right)^{(\delta-1)/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{(\delta-1)(\delta-3)}8\int_0^T\frac{ds}{R_s^2}\right\}.}
$$

The stopped density is [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) from the stopped path, so it is also the [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-derivative) of the stopped-path laws. The stopping times in this formula are evaluated on the reference path, rather than taken from a different realization of $X$. A zero-start reference [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) would have a different deterministic initial value and could not dominate this law.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 202](paper-202.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A **[weak solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation)** consists of a [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space), an [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) continuous process $X$, and a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) $B$ relative to that [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory), with specified initial distribution, such that for every finite $t$ the coefficient integrals are finite and

$$
\boxed{X_t=X_0+\int_0^t\sigma(X_s)\,dB_s+\int_0^t b(X_s)\,ds\quad\text{almost surely for all }t.}
$$

In one dimension the integrability conditions are $\int_0^t\sigma(X_s)^2ds<\infty$ and $\int_0^t|b(X_s)|ds<\infty$. The noise integral is the [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral). A solution on a prescribed stochastic basis with prescribed $B$ is distinguished from this weak construction.

A **[strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation)** uses the prescribed $B$ and initial variable, and is [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) to their augmented [natural filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#natural-filtration); thus no additional randomness is needed. For deterministic $X_0=x$, each $X_t$ is determined by the driving [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) through time $t$. Merely being [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) to a larger [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) is insufficient for this definition.

**[Uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law)** means that any two [weak stochastic solutions](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) with the same initial distribution have the same law of $X$ on continuous path space. **[Pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness)** means that two solutions on one stochastic basis, with the same $B$ and almost surely equal initial values, are [indistinguishable stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes). The latter compares paths under the same noise, rather than just comparing their [probability distributions](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-distribution).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Fix $X_0=x$ and a finite horizon $T$. Begin under [Wiener measure](../../../brownian-motion.md#wiener-measure) $\mathbb P$ with $X_t=x+W_t$, and let $K$ bound $|b|$. The bounded [progressively measurable process](../../../stochastic-process.md#progressive-measurability) $b(X)$ is an admissible [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) integrand. Put

$$
Z_T=\exp\left\{\int_0^T b(X_s)\,dW_s-\frac12\int_0^T b(X_s)^2ds\right\}.
$$

The [Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) follows from $\int_0^T b(X_s)^2ds\leq K^2T$. With $d\mathbb Q=Z_Td\mathbb P$, the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) says that $B=W-\int_0^\cdot b(X_s)ds$ is [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md). Therefore **a [weak stochastic solution](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) exists**, since

$$
\boxed{X_t=x+B_t+\int_0^t b(X_s)ds.}
$$

Consistent finite-horizon densities on canonical continuous path space give a solution for all finite times. No uniform integrability at infinite time is needed for this extension.

For **[uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law)**, take any [weak stochastic solution](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) under a measure $\mathbb Q$ and reverse the [change of measure](../../../measure-theory.md#change-of-measure):

$$
R_T=\exp\left\{-\int_0^T b(X_s)\,dB_s-\frac12\int_0^T b(X_s)^2ds\right\},\qquad d\mathbb P=R_Td\mathbb Q.
$$

Again the [Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) holds. Under $\mathbb P$, $W=B+\int b(X_s)ds=X-x$ is [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md), so the law of $X$ is the fixed shifted [Wiener measure](../../../brownian-motion.md#wiener-measure). Substitution of $dB=dX-b(X)dt$ gives

$$
\boxed{R_T^{-1}=\exp\left\{\int_0^T b(X_s)\,dX_s-\frac12\int_0^T b(X_s)^2ds\right\}.}
$$

Under shifted [Wiener measure](../../../brownian-motion.md#wiener-measure), the [stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) on the right has a fixed [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) version as a functional of the canonical path. It can be defined by [simple predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) converging in the integrand $L^2$ norm and an almost surely convergent subsequence of their integrals. Equivalence of the two finite-horizon measures preserves that version. Thus for every bounded [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) path functional $F$,

$$
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}F(X)=\mathbb E_{\text{shifted Wiener measure}}\left[F(X)\exp\left\{\int_0^T b(X_s)dX_s-\tfrac12\int_0^T b(X_s)^2ds\right\}\right].
$$

The right-hand side depends only on $b,x,T$, not on the chosen solution. This proves [uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law) on every finite horizon, and hence on the whole continuous path space. This is [Weak existence and uniqueness in law for an additive-noise SDE with bounded drift](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-existence-and-uniqueness-in-law-for-an-additive-noise-sde-with-bounded-drift). For merely [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability) $b$, one should not assume that naive deterministic left-endpoint samples of $b(X)$ converge; the predictable $L^2$ approximation is the appropriate definition.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

**The [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) construction proves a [weak stochastic solution](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation), but does not by itself establish a [strong stochastic solution](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation).** It constructs $X=x+W$ first, and then constructs a new driving [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) $B=W-\int b(X_s)ds$. Adaptation of $X$ to the original [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) of $W$ does not by itself prove adaptation to the smaller [natural filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#natural-filtration) of $B$. Inverting this relation requires an additional result; [uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law) alone is insufficient in general.

**For the particular coefficients in part (b), however, the literal claim that a strong solution may fail is incorrect.** The [strong existence theorem for additive-noise SDEs with bounded measurable drift](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-existence-theorem-for-additive-noise-sdes-with-bounded-measurable-drift) gives [strong existence](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-existence) and [pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness) for bounded Borel state-dependent $b$ and unit diffusion. Consequently every weak realization here is determined by its driving noise and initial value. This theorem is deeper than the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) argument and no proof is needed here. Examples with path-dependent drift or with a nonconstant diffusion coefficient do not contradict it.

The valid distinction is therefore: the argument in part (b) has established weak existence and [uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law); strong existence requires an additional theorem, and in this class that theorem is available. A primary reference for the correction is [On strong solutions and explicit formulas for solutions of stochastic integral equations](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=2601&wshow=paper).

A **[continuous martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-martingale)** is an [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) with almost surely continuous paths, $\mathbb E|M_t|<\infty$ for every $t$, and

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s\qquad(s\leq t).}
$$

A **[continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale)** is continuous and [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) and has a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence) of [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) $T_n\uparrow\infty$ almost surely such that every $M^{T_n}_t=M_{t\wedge T_n}$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md). The initial value is normally integrable; the frequently used class $\mathcal M_{c,\mathrm{loc}}$ is normalized to start at zero.

The **[usual conditions for a filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#usual-conditions-for-a-filtration)** are completeness and right continuity. Completeness means that $\mathcal F_0$ contains every subset of each $\mathbb P$-null set in the ambient $\mathcal F$; right continuity means $\mathcal F_t=\bigcap_{u>t}\mathcal F_u$. These conditions concern the [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space), whereas continuity of a [martingale](../../../martingale.md) concerns its sample paths.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

For a continuous vector [semimartingale](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale) $X$ and $f\in C^{1,2}$, the **[Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma)** is

$$
\boxed{f(t,X_t)=f(0,X_0)+\int_0^t\partial_s f(s,X_s)ds+\sum_i\int_0^t\partial_i f(s,X_s)dX_s^i+\frac12\sum_{i,j}\int_0^t\partial_{ij}f(s,X_s)d[X^i,X^j]_s.}
$$

The **[Itô product rule](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule)**, or stochastic [integration by parts](../../../calculus.md#integration-by-parts), is

$$
\boxed{X_tY_t=X_0Y_0+\int_0^tX_s\,dY_s+\int_0^tY_s\,dX_s+[X,Y]_t.}
$$

The additional second-order term is the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation).

For general [semimartingales](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale) with [càdlàg](../../../calculus.md#cadlag) paths, use left limits in the integrands. The **[Itô formula for semimartingales with jumps](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-formula-for-semimartingales-with-jumps)**, for time-independent $f\in C^2$, is

$$
f(X_t)=f(X_0)+\sum_i\int_0^t\partial_i f(X_{s-})dX_s^i+\frac12\sum_{i,j}\int_0^t\partial_{ij}f(X_{s-})d[X^{i,c},X^{j,c}]_s+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left(f(X_s)-f(X_{s-})-\sum_i\partial_i f(X_{s-})\Delta X_s^i\right).
$$

Here $X^c$ denotes the continuous [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) part; the continuous bracket is that of these parts. For time-dependent $f\in C^{1,2}$ add $\int\partial_s f(s,X_{s-})ds$ and insert $s$ in the other evaluations of $f$. The [integration by parts](../../../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) formula becomes

$$
X_tY_t=X_0Y_0+\int_0^tX_{s-}dY_s+\int_0^tY_{s-}dX_s+[X,Y]_t,\qquad [X,Y]_t=[X^c,Y^c]_t+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\Delta X_s\Delta Y_s.
$$

For continuous paths the jump sum vanishes, recovering the first two formulas.

Use two standard results: for every [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) $U$, there is a unique continuous [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) increasing [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) $[U]$, starting at zero, such that $U^2-U_0^2-[U]$ is a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale); and a [continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant](../../../martingale.md#continuous-finite-variation-local-martingale-is-constant).

Define the **[quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation)** by the [polarization identity](../../../linear-algebra.md#polarization-identity)

$$
\boxed{[M,N]=\frac14\bigl([M+N]-[M-N]\bigr).}
$$

This is continuous, [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process), starts at zero, and is of [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function) on compact time intervals. Since $(M+N)^2-(M-N)^2=4MN$, subtraction of the two quadratic-variation identities shows that $MN-M_0N_0-[M,N]$ is a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale). In the zero-start convention this is exactly $MN-[M,N]\in\mathcal M_{c,\mathrm{loc}}$.

If $A$ is another continuous [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) with $A_0=0$ and the same [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) property, then $A-[M,N]$ is both a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) and of [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function). It is constant, and its initial value is zero, so it vanishes. Uniqueness is up to [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes).

**The normalization $[M,N]_0=0$ is essential.** If arbitrary nonzero initial constants are allowed in the [martingale](../../../martingale.md) class and this normalization is omitted, adding a constant to the bracket preserves the claimed property. The usual definition of [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) always includes the zero initial value.

<h4 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#4/d)

The **[Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion)** states that an [adapted](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) continuous real process $M$ with $M_0=0$ is standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) relative to $(\mathcal F_t)$ if and only if it is a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) and $[M]_t=t$. The multidimensional version replaces the bracket identity by $[M^i,M^j]_t=\delta_{ij}t$.

For the converse direction, a standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) is a continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md), and its independent centered increments give [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) $t$. Distinct independent coordinates have zero [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation).

For the substantive direction, fix a vector $\theta\in\mathbb R^d$. The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) applied to

$$
E_t(\theta)=\exp\{i\theta\cdot M_t+\tfrac12|\theta|^2t\}
$$

shows that its drift is zero: the second-order term is $-\tfrac12|\theta|^2E_tdt$ and cancels the time derivative. Thus $E(\theta)$ is a complex [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). On $[0,T]$ its modulus is the deterministic value $e^{|\theta|^2t/2}\leq e^{|\theta|^2T/2}$; localization and the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) make it a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[e^{i\theta\cdot(M_t-M_s)}\mid\mathcal F_s]=e^{-\frac12|\theta|^2(t-s)}.}
$$

This conditional [characteristic function](../../../probability-theory.md#characteristic-function) is the deterministic [characteristic function](../../../probability-theory.md#characteristic-function) of $N(0,(t-s)I)$. For $A\in\mathcal F_s$, multiplying by $\mathbf1_A$ and taking [expectations](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value), then using uniqueness of [characteristic functions](../../../probability-theory.md#characteristic-function), proves that $M_t-M_s$ has this [multivariate normal distribution](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) and is independent of $\mathcal F_s$. Iterated conditioning gives independent increments with [Gaussian distributions](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution). Continuity, the zero initial value and these increment laws are the definition of standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md). The case $d=1$ proves the requested statement; the vector form will also be used below.

Interpret $f$ as a [conformal bijection](../../../complex-analysis.md#biholomorphism) onto $D'$. If it is merely an injective map into a larger target, the correct exit domain is $f(D)$ instead. Let $\zeta$ be the exit time from $D$, write $f=u+iv$, and identify [planar Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-motion) with $B^1+iB^2$. The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../analysis.md#cauchy-riemann-equations) give $u_x=v_y$, $u_y=-v_x$, and both $u,v$ are [harmonic functions](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function). The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma), localized on compact subsets of $D$, yields

$$
du(B_t)=u_x(B_t)dB_t^1+u_y(B_t)dB_t^2,\qquad dv(B_t)=v_x(B_t)dB_t^1+v_y(B_t)dB_t^2.
$$

Thus both coordinates are [continuous local martingales](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale), with

$$
[u(B)]_t=[v(B)]_t=A_t:=\int_0^{t}|f'(B_s)|^2ds,\qquad [u(B),v(B)]_t=\int_0^t(u_xv_x+u_yv_y)(B_s)ds=0\quad(t<\zeta).
$$

Since $f'$ never vanishes, $A$ is strictly increasing before $\zeta$. Let $\eta_s=\inf\{t:A_t>s\}$, defined for $s<A_{\zeta-}$. The [optional time-change theorem](../../../stochastic-process.md#optional-time-change-theorem) gives [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) coordinates for $\beta_s=f(B_{\eta_s})$, with bracket matrix $sI$. The [Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-multidimensional-brownian-motion) makes $\beta$ a standard [planar Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-motion) started at $f(z)$, up to its lifetime. Therefore **[conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#conformal-invariance-of-planar-brownian-motion) takes the form**

$$
\boxed{f(B_t)=\beta_{A_t},\qquad A_t=\int_0^t|f'(B_s)|^2ds,\quad t<\zeta.}
$$

To identify the lifetime, exhaust $D$ by relatively compact open sets $D_n$ with nested closures. The images $f(D_n)$ exhaust $D'$ because $f$ is a homeomorphism onto $D'$. Each stopped transformed path exits $f(D_n)$ at precisely the clock value of its exit from $D_n$. Increasing these exit times gives the exit lifetime of [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) from $D'$, including a possibly infinite lifetime. This identifies $A_{\zeta-}$ with that lifetime and needs no global extension of $f$ to the boundary.

<h4 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h4>

For a [harmonic function](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) $u$ on $\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2$, the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives

$$
u(B_t)=u(B_0)+\int_0^t\nabla u(B_s)\cdot dB_s+\frac12\int_0^t\Delta u(B_s)ds=u(B_0)+\int_0^t\nabla u(B_s)\cdot dB_s.
$$

Stop on leaving increasing discs and at increasing deterministic times. On each such interval the [gradient](../../../calculus.md#gradient) is bounded, so the [stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) is a [martingale](../../../martingale.md). This proves that **$u(B)$ is a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale)**.

If $|u|\leq K$, this is a bounded [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) and hence a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md). Start [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) at an arbitrary $z$. Its [Gaussian heat kernel](../../../diffusion-equation.md#gaussian-heat-kernel) gives

$$
u(z)=\mathbb E_z u(B_t)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}u(v)p_t(v-z)dv,\qquad p_t(v)=\frac1{2\pi t}e^{-|v|^2/(2t)}.
$$

For a unit vector $e$, $\partial_e p_t(v)=-(e\cdot v)p_t(v)/t$, so $\int|\partial_ep_t(v)|dv=\sqrt{2/\pi}/\sqrt t$. Integrating the directional derivative along the segment from $z$ to $w$ yields

$$
|u(z)-u(w)|\leq K\int|p_t(v-z)-p_t(v-w)|dv\leq K\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac{|z-w|}{\sqrt t}.
$$

Letting $t\to\infty$ gives **the [harmonic Liouville theorem](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-liouville-theorem)**:

$$
\boxed{u(z)=u(w)\text{ for all }z,w\in\mathbb C;\quad u\text{ is constant}.}
$$

This [Gaussian heat-kernel proof of the harmonic Liouville theorem](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#gaussian-heat-kernel-proof-of-the-harmonic-liouville-theorem) uses the bounded [martingale](../../../martingale.md) identity established above.

Let the [infinitesimal generator](../../../stochastic-process.md#infinitesimal-generator-stochastic-processes) be the second-order operator

$$
Lg(x)=\frac12\sum_{i,j=1}^d a_{ij}(x)\partial_{ij}g(x)+\sum_{i=1}^d b_i(x)\partial_i g(x),
$$

where $a(x)$ is symmetric positive semidefinite and $b(x)$ is a vector. In the standard bounded-coefficient setting take $a,b$ bounded and [measurable](../../../measure-theory.md#measurability). An **[L-diffusion](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#diffusion-martingale-problem)** is a continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) $X$ for which, for every $g\in C_b^2(\mathbb R^d)$,

$$
\boxed{g(X_t)-g(X_0)-\int_0^t Lg(X_s)ds\text{ is a martingale}.}
$$

Here $C_b^2$ includes boundedness of the function and its derivatives through order two. This is the true-[martingale](../../../martingale.md) version of the [diffusion martingale problem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#diffusion-martingale-problem). The matrix $a$ is the diffusivity, and $b$ is the drift. An [Itô diffusion](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-diffusion) with coefficient $\sigma$ has $a=\sigma\sigma^{\mathsf T}$.

A more general local [martingale problem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) may permit unbounded coefficients and require only a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). That convention is weaker and must not silently replace the true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) property used here and in part (b). The bounded-coefficient convention guarantees all finite-horizon integrability needed below; a sufficient more general replacement is $\mathbb E\int_0^T(\sum_i|b_i(X_s)|+\sum_i a_{ii}(X_s))ds<\infty$ for every $T$.

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

We prove the extension from spatial test functions to **[time-dependent test functions for a diffusion martingale problem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#time-dependent-test-functions-for-a-diffusion-martingale-problem)** directly, without assuming a driving [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md). Fix $0\leq s<t$ and a deterministic partition $s=t_0<\cdots<t_n=t$. For each $j$, $g_j(x)=f(t_j,x)$ lies in $C_b^2$. The defining [martingale problem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#martingale-problem) gives

$$
\mathbb E\left[\mathbf1_A\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(f(t_j,X_{t_{j+1}})-f(t_j,X_{t_j})-\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}Lf(t_j,X_u)du\right)\right]=0\qquad(A\in\mathcal F_s).
$$

Call the sum $S_n$. Telescoping separates the spatial and temporal changes:

$$
S_n=f(t,X_t)-f(s,X_s)-\sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\partial_u f(u,X_{t_{j+1}})du-\sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}Lf(t_j,X_u)du.
$$

The sample path $X$ is uniformly continuous on $[s,t]$ and has compact range. Joint continuity of $\partial_t f$, the spatial first derivatives, and the spatial second derivatives therefore gives, as the mesh tends to zero,

$$
S_n\longrightarrow f(t,X_t)-f(s,X_s)-\int_s^t(\partial_u+L)f(u,X_u)du=M_t^f-M_s^f
$$

almost surely. For bounded $a,b$ and bounded derivatives of $f$, both integrals are uniformly bounded on this finite horizon, as are the two endpoint terms. The [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) permits passage to the limit in the expectation against $\mathbf1_A$. Thus $M_t^f$ is integrable and $\mathbb E[\mathbf1_A(M_t^f-M_s^f)]=0$ for every $A\in\mathcal F_s$, proving

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_t^f\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s^f.}
$$

Adaptation and continuity follow from the formula, so **$M^f$ is a continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md)**. Under the more general integrability condition in part (a), the same proof uses an integrable bound proportional to $1+\int_s^t(\sum_i|b_i(X_u)|+\sum_i a_{ii}(X_u))du$; positive semidefiniteness bounds every off-diagonal entry by the diagonal ones. With only local assumptions this reasoning proves a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), and an integrability hypothesis is needed to upgrade that conclusion.

<h3 id="6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/c)

The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $f(t,x)=e^{x+at}$ gives the **[stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation)**

$$
\boxed{dZ_t=Z_t\,dB_t+(a+\tfrac12)Z_t\,dt,\qquad Z_0=1.}
$$

Write $N_t=\int_0^t Z_u\,dB_u$ for its [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) part. Its clock is the [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation)

$$
A_t=[Z]_t=[N]_t=\int_0^t Z_u^2du.
$$

Because $Z_t>0$, $A$ is strictly increasing. For $s<A_\infty$, its inverse $\tau_s$ satisfies $A_{\tau_s}=s$ and $d\tau_s/ds=Z_{\tau_s}^{-2}$. The [Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem](../../../martingale.md#dambis-dubins-schwarz-theorem), or the [Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion) after the [optional time-change theorem](../../../stochastic-process.md#optional-time-change-theorem), makes $\beta_s=N_{\tau_s}$ a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) for the time-changed [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory). With $Y_s=Z_{\tau_s}$,

$$
Y_s=1+\beta_s+(a+\tfrac12)\int_0^sY_v^{-1}dv,
\qquad
\boxed{dY_s=d\beta_s+\frac{(2+2a)-1}{2Y_s}ds.}
$$

This is the [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) equation with **dimension $\boxed{2+2a}$**, on the positive stochastic interval. This establishes the [Exponential Brownian-to-Bessel time change](../../../brownian-motion.md#exponential-brownian-to-bessel-time-change).

The lifetime convention is important: the inverse is defined only for $s<A_\infty$. If $a<0$, the [strong law for Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#strong-law-for-brownian-motion) gives $B_t/t\to0$, so $Z_t\to0$ exponentially and $A_\infty<\infty$. Then $Y_s\to0$ as $s\uparrow A_\infty$, and this is its first hit of zero after continuous extension. If $a>0$ the clock diverges by the same strong law. If $a=0$ it diverges by [infinite occupation time of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#infinite-occupation-time-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion) on a bounded interval, since $e^{2B_t}$ is bounded below there. Thus the lifetime is infinite when $a\geq0$. Negative dimensions are interpreted by the same positive-state equation killed at zero; no post-zero [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) behavior is asserted.

<h3 id="6/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#6/d)

Work before the first zero of $Z$, with $Z_0=z>0$. For the [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) equation, the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) applied to $x^r$ gives

$$
\boxed{dW_t=rZ_t^{r-1}dB_t+\frac{r(\delta+r-2)}2Z_t^{r-2}dt,\qquad W_0=z^r.}
$$

Its [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) is

$$
C_t=[W]_t=r^2\int_0^tZ_u^{2r-2}du.
$$

This clock is strictly increasing on the positive-state interval. For $s$ before its terminal clock value, let $Y_s=W_{\sigma_s}$ and let $\beta_s=(\int_0^\cdot rZ_u^{r-1}dB_u)_{\sigma_s}$. The [Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem](../../../martingale.md#dambis-dubins-schwarz-theorem) makes $\beta$ a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) there. Since $d\sigma_s=ds/(r^2Z_{\sigma_s}^{2r-2})$, the drift changes to

$$
\frac{r(\delta+r-2)}2\frac{Z_{\sigma_s}^{r-2}}{r^2Z_{\sigma_s}^{2r-2}}=\frac{\delta+r-2}{2rY_s}.
$$

Consequently the **[Power time change of a Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#power-time-change-of-a-bessel-process)** gives

$$
\boxed{dY_s=d\beta_s+\frac{\delta'-1}{2Y_s}ds,\qquad\delta'=2+\frac{\delta-2}{r}.}
$$

The initial value is $z^r$, and this equation identifies a [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) of dimension $\delta'$ until its first zero. Localize both equations on compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$ to justify the inverse and all [stochastic integrals](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-integral) for arbitrary $r>0$.

For completeness, the inverse-clock lifetime really is the positive [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) lifetime: the map $z\mapsto z^r$ is a homeomorphism of $(0,\infty)$, and exits from nested compact intervals correspond under the clock. There is no finite explosion at infinity for a positive [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process), since the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $Y^2$ gives $d(Y^2)=2Y\,d\beta+\delta'ds$; stopping at zero or level $R$ yields a probability of hitting $R$ before a fixed $T$ bounded by $(Y_0^2+\max(\delta',0)T)/R^2$. If the transformed lifetime were finite while $Y$ stayed in a compact positive interval, the inverse clock and the original solution could both be continued. Thus a finite lifetime is approach to zero. For $\delta<2$, also $\delta'<2$ and the positive-state [Bessel process](../../../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process) hits zero; for $\delta\geq2$ the transformed lifetime is infinite. No continuation through zero is needed.

If the initial value is already zero, the interval before its first zero is empty; the positive-state argument concerns starts $z>0$.
