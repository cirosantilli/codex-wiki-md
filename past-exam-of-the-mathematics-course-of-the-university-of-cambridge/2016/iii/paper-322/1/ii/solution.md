<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the [evolved-companion fraction](../../../../../../conditional-evolved-companion-fraction-in-a-coeval-binary-population.md), a [red giant](../../../../../../red-giant.md) has another evolved component precisely for unordered $GG$ or $GW$ pairs. By [conditional independence](../../../../../../conditional-independence.md) at fixed age, their combined slice [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) is $g^2+2gw=9g^2$. Therefore

$$
\Pr(GG\text{ or }GW)=\int_0^1\frac{9y^2}{10000}\,dy=\frac9{30000}.
$$

Divide by the system fraction containing at least one [red giant](../../../../../../red-giant.md) to obtain **the requested conditional fraction**:

$$
\boxed{\Pr(\text{another evolved component}\mid\text{at least one }G)=\frac9{299}\simeq3.01003\%.}
$$

A double-giant system is counted once in this denominator, and the factor two for a giant–[white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) pair represents its two possible component orderings.
