<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Apply condition $(ii)$ with the singleton $S=1$. It says that the [category of elements](../../../../../../category-of-elements.md) $E=(1\downarrow F)$ has all [finite limits](../../../../../../finite-limit.md), including a [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md). The [opposite category](../../../../../../opposite-category.md) $E^{\mathrm{op}}$ therefore has all finite [colimits](../../../../../../colimit.md). In particular it is nonempty; two objects map to their binary [coproduct in a category](../../../../../../coproduct.md); and any parallel arrows are equalized by their [coequalizer](../../../../../../coequalizer.md). These are precisely the conditions for a [filtered category](../../../../../../filtered-category.md). Equivalently, every finite diagram has a cocone, obtained by reversing a [categorical limit](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) in $E$. Thus

$$
\boxed{(ii)\Rightarrow(iii):\quad(1\downarrow F)^{\mathrm{op}}\text{ is filtered}.}
$$
