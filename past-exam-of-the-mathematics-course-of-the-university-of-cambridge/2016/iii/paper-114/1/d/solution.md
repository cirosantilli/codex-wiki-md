<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For any finite-dimensional bounded [cochain complex](../../../../../../cochain-complex.md) $(V^*,d)$ over a [field](../../../../../../field.md), let $r_q=\operatorname{rank}(d:V^q\to V^{q+1})$, with zero ranks outside its degree range. Then

$$
\dim H^q(V,d)=\dim V^q-r_q-r_{q-1}.
$$

Taking the alternating sum cancels the two rank sums. Thus taking [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) preserves the [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) of a finite graded complex. Apply this to $V^q=H^q(M;\mathbb F_p)$ and $d=\beta$, using part (b).

A closed three-dimensional [manifold](../../../../../../topological-manifold.md) has finite-dimensional [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md), vanishing above degree three. Its [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) is zero, even when it is not orientable: [Poincare duality](../../../../../../poincare-duality.md) with $\mathbb F_2$ coefficients gives $b_q=b_{3-q}$, so the alternating sum vanishes. A finite triangulation, or finite [CW complex](../../../../../../cw-complex.md) model, shows that the alternating sum is the same integer with coefficients in any field, since it equals the alternating count of cells. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\chi\bigl(H\beta^*(M;p)\bigr)=\chi(M)=0.}
$$

The primeness assumption makes $\mathbb Z/p$ a field; no orientation assumption on $M$ is needed.
