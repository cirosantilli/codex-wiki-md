<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Put $A=k[t_1,t_2]$. The [punctured affine plane](../../../../../../punctured-affine-plane.md) is covered by the [principal open subschemes](../../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) $D(t_1)$ and $D(t_2)$. Since $A$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md), restriction identifies its [global sections](../../../../../../global-section.md) with

$$
\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=A_{t_1}\cap A_{t_2}\subset k(t_1,t_2).
$$

This intersection is $A$: if $a/t_1^r=b/t_2^s$, then $t_2^sa=t_1^rb$, and [unique factorization](../../../../../../unique-factorization-in-an-integral-domain.md) forces $t_1^r$ to divide $a$. Therefore the inclusion $j:X\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A$ induces the identity on [global regular functions](../../../../../../global-regular-function.md).

If $X$ were an [affine scheme](../../../../../../affine-scheme.md), [affine scheme reconstruction from global sections](../../../../../../affine-scheme-reconstruction-from-global-sections.md) would make $j$ an [isomorphism of schemes](../../../../../../isomorphism-of-schemes.md), because its global-section map is an isomorphism. But its image omits the closed point $(t_1,t_2)$. **The punctured affine plane is therefore not affine.** This is a useful example of global regular functions failing to distinguish a nonaffine open subscheme from its affine ambient space.
