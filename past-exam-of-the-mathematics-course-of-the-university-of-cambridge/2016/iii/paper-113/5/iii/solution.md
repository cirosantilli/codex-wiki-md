<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Eliminate $t_3$ using the linear equation. The homogeneous coordinate ring becomes

$$
B=\mathbb C[t_0,t_1,t_2]/(t_1^3+t_0t_2^2).
$$

The cubic is a nonzero element of a polynomial [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md), hence a non-zero-divisor. It gives the exact sequence of [graded modules](../../../../../../graded-module.md)

$$
0\longrightarrow S(-3)\xrightarrow{\,t_1^3+t_0t_2^2\,}S\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow0,
\qquad S=\mathbb C[t_0,t_1,t_2].
$$

Thus $\dim B_m=\binom{m+2}{2}-\binom{m-1}{2}$ for $m\ge3$. Equivalently, use the [Hilbert series](../../../../../../hilbert-series.md) $(1-z^3)/(1-z)^3$, or the sheafified exact sequence on $\mathbb P^2$. The [Hilbert polynomial](../../../../../../hilbert-polynomial.md) of the [plane cubic](../../../../../../plane-cubic.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Phi_{\mathcal O_X}(m)=3m.}
$$

Its degree is three and its [arithmetic genus](../../../../../../arithmetic-genus.md) is one, although the curve is singular: its affine equation near $[1:0:0]$ is the cusp $t_2^2+t_1^3=0$. The Hilbert polynomial records the arithmetic genus, rather than the genus of the normalization.
