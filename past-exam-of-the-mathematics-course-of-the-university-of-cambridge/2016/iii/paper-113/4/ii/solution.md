<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the closed inclusion $f$, the [direct image sheaf](../../../../../../direct-image-sheaf.md) has stalk $\mathcal F_x$ at $x\in X$ and zero stalk at points outside $X$: outside the closed set there is an open neighbourhood disjoint from $X$. Hence $f_*$ is exact, since [exact sequences of sheaves](../../../../../../exact-sequence-of-sheaves.md) are detected on stalks.

Take a [flasque resolution](../../../../../../flasque-resolution.md) $\mathcal F\to\mathcal I^\bullet$. Direct image preserves flasqueness, because restrictions are the restrictions on inverse-image opens. Exactness makes $f_*\mathcal I^\bullet$ a flasque resolution of $f_*\mathcal F$. Moreover

$$
\Gamma(Y,f_*\mathcal I^\bullet)=\Gamma(X,\mathcal I^\bullet).
$$

The complexes computing [sheaf cohomology](../../../../../../sheaf-cohomology.md) are identical. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H^i(Y,f_*\mathcal F)\cong H^i(X,\mathcal F)\quad(i\geq0).}
$$

This is [sheaf cohomology under a closed inclusion](../../../../../../sheaf-cohomology-under-a-closed-inclusion.md); the closedness hypothesis is used in the exactness of direct image.
