<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Seek a steady downslope solution with $u>0$ constant. The [volume conservation](../../../../../../volume-conservation.md) equation reduces to $h_x=E$, so the [constant-speed entraining gravity current on a slope](../../../../../../constant-speed-entraining-gravity-current-on-a-slope.md) has

$$
\boxed{h=h_0+Ex.}
$$

The conserved [buoyancy flux](../../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md) per unit width is $B=ug'h$, giving

$$
\boxed{g'(x)=\frac{B}{u(h_0+Ex)}.}
$$

Substitute these expressions into the momentum equation. Its advective term is $Eu^2$ and its hydrostatic term is $\tfrac12E(B/u)\cos\theta$. The balance is therefore

$$
Eu^2+\frac{EB}{2u}\cos\theta=\frac Bu\sin\theta-cu^2,
$$

which gives

$$
\boxed{u=\beta_*B^{1/3},\qquad\beta_*^3=\frac{\sin\theta-\tfrac12E\cos\theta}{c+E}.}
$$

The assumed slope inequality makes $\beta_*>0$. The increasing thickness dilutes [reduced gravity](../../../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md) while leaving $g'h$ and the [buoyancy flux](../../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md) constant. The denominator includes both bottom drag and the momentum needed to accelerate newly entrained ambient fluid.
