<h1 id="6/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Every finitely presented [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) $P$ embeds in $P\times C_2$ by $p\mapsto(p,1)$. This [direct product of groups](../../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) is finitely presented: combine finite presentations of the factors and add the finitely many cross-commutation relations between their generators. Projection onto $C_2$ gives a nontrivial [finite quotient of a group](../../../../../../../finite-quotient-of-a-group.md).

Thus no finitely presented $P$ can serve as the forbidden witness $A_-$ for this property. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{Having a nontrivial finite quotient is not a Markov property}.}
$$
