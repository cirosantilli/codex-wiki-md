<h1 id="5/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

The factors $\mathbb Z^2$ and $\mathbb Z$ are [torsion-free groups](../../../../../../torsion-free-group.md), and so is their [free product](../../../../../../free-product.md) $K$. Indeed every element of a [free product](../../../../../../free-product.md) is conjugate either into one factor or to an alternating word whose first and last syllables lie in different factors. A word of the latter kind has nonempty reduced powers of every positive length, so has infinite order. A nonidentity element of either factor also has infinite order.

By the finite-order result in part (b), every finite-order element of the multiple [HNN extension](../../../../../../hnn-extension.md) $K_{\mathcal M}$ is conjugate into the torsion-free base $K$, and hence is the identity. Apply the same result once more to $G_{\mathcal M}$, whose base is $K_{\mathcal M}$. It follows that

$$
\boxed{G_{\mathcal M}\text{ is torsion-free}.}
$$

Thus the failure of a [word problem for a group](../../../../../../word-problem-for-groups.md) to be decidable does not require torsion.
