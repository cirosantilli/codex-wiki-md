<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a [direct product of groups](../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md), $(a,b)^r=(a^r,b^r)$. Thus a finite order for $(a,b)$ makes both coordinate orders finite. Conversely, if their orders are $p$ and $q$, the least positive exponent annihilating both is their [least common multiple](../../../../../../least-common-multiple.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(a,b)=\operatorname{lcm}(p,q)}
$$

when both orders are finite; if either coordinate has infinite order, so does $(a,b)$.
