The [free product](../../../../free-product.md) $G_1*G_2$ is obtained by taking disjoint copies of the elements of the two [groups](../../../../group-split.md) as generators and imposing precisely their internal multiplication relations. Equivalently, if $G_i=\langle X_i\mid R_i\rangle$ with disjoint $X_i$, then

$$
G_1*G_2=\langle X_1\sqcup X_2\mid R_1\sqcup R_2\rangle.
$$

A [nontrivial free product](../../../../nontrivial-free-product.md) has both factors different from the [trivial group](../../../../trivial-group.md).

The [normal form theorem for a free product](../../../../normal-form-theorem-for-a-free-product.md) says that every element has a unique expression

$$
\boxed{g_1g_2\cdots g_r,\quad g_j\in G_{\epsilon_j}\setminus\{1\},\quad \epsilon_j\ne\epsilon_{j+1},}
$$

where the empty expression represents the identity. The $g_j$ are called [syllables in a free product](../../../../syllable-in-a-free-product.md). Thus nonempty alternating products cannot be the identity, and the natural maps of the factors into the [free product](../../../../free-product.md) are injective.

For existence, multiply adjacent syllables from the same factor and delete identity syllables until the word alternates. Each change uses a defining relation and decreases the number of syllables. To establish uniqueness without assuming that these reductions are confluent, let $\mathcal W$ be the set of alternating nonidentity syllable sequences, including the empty sequence. For $h\in G_i$, define a [permutation](../../../../permutation.md) $L_h$ of $\mathcal W$ by prepending $h$, multiplying into the first syllable if it belongs to $G_i$, and deleting it if that product is the identity; for $h=1$, do nothing.

The multiplication law inside $G_i$ gives $L_hL_k=L_{hk}$ for $h,k\in G_i$, and $L_h^{-1}=L_{h^{-1}}$. This can be checked at the first syllable: if a product deletes it, the next syllable belongs to the other factor and is treated as a new first syllable. Hence the defining relations give a [group action](../../../../group-action.md) of $G_1*G_2$ on $\mathcal W$. An alternating word $g_1\cdots g_r$ sends the empty sequence to its own syllable sequence. Two such words representing the same group element induce the same [permutation](../../../../permutation.md), and therefore have identical sequences. This proves the [normal form theorem for a free product](../../../../normal-form-theorem-for-a-free-product.md).

The [universal property of a free product](../../../../universal-property-of-a-free-product.md) states that, for [group homomorphisms](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $f_i:G_i\to H$, there is a unique [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $f:G_1*G_2\to H$ extending both. Explicitly,

$$
\boxed{f(g_1\cdots g_r)=f_{\epsilon_1}(g_1)\cdots f_{\epsilon_r}(g_r).}
$$

The internal multiplication relations are respected because each $f_i$ is a [group homomorphism](../../../../group-homomorphism.md), so this assignment descends from words to the presented [group](../../../../group-split.md). Alternatively, multiplication of two normal forms is concatenation followed by precisely those internal relations, which do not change its value in $H$. Uniqueness holds because the two factors generate the [free product](../../../../free-product.md).
