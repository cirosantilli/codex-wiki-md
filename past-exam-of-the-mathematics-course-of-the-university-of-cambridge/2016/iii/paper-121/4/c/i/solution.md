<h1 id="4/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the [Lévy hierarchy](../../../../../../../levy-hierarchy.md), with [bounded formulas in set theory](../../../../../../../bounded-formula-in-set-theory.md) as the quantifier-free level for this purpose. Let $\operatorname{Ord}(\kappa)$ be the bounded ordinal predicate, and let $\operatorname{Bij}(f,\alpha,\kappa)$ be the bounded predicate saying that $f$ is a graph of a [bijection](../../../../../../../bijection.md) from $\alpha$ onto $\kappa$. Then cardinalhood is expressed by

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Ord}(\kappa)\ \land\
\forall f\,\forall\alpha\in\kappa\ \neg\operatorname{Bij}(f,\alpha,\kappa).}
$$

All quantifiers in $\operatorname{Ord}$ and $\operatorname{Bij}$ are bounded by the displayed sets and the graph. Ordered-pair membership, graph functionality, injectivity and surjectivity have bounded expansions using the usual set coding of [ordered pairs](../../../../../../../ordered-pair.md). The only unbounded quantifier shown is universal over $f$. The formula is therefore $\Pi_1$ and in particular a [Pi-one formula modulo ZF](../../../../../../../pi-one-formula-modulo-zf.md).

In [ZF](../../../../../../../zermelo-fraenkel-set-theory.md), a [cardinal number](../../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) is an [ordinal](../../../../../../../ordinal.md) not in bijection with any smaller ordinal, so this formula has exactly the required meaning. Thus [cardinalhood is Pi-one definable](../../../../../../../cardinalhood-is-pi-one-definable.md); the argument does not use the [axiom of choice](../../../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md).
