↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A uniform example is $\boxed{A=\kappa\setminus\{\omega\}}$. It contains the final segment $[\omega+1,\kappa)$, a [club set](../../../../../../club-set.md). Therefore $A$ belongs to the [club filter](../../../../../../club-filter.md). Every two [club sets](../../../../../../club-set.md) in a regular uncountable cardinal intersect: alternately choose larger points from each and take the countable supremum, which remains below $\kappa$ and lies in both by closure. Hence every member of the [club filter](../../../../../../club-filter.md) is a [stationary set](../../../../../../stationary-set.md), so $A$ is stationary.

However, every finite [ordinal](../../../../../../ordinal.md) belongs to $A$, and their increasing supremum $\omega$ does not. Thus $A$ is not closed and is not a [club set](../../../../../../club-set.md). This is a [stationary nonclosed member of the club filter](../../../../../../stationary-nonclosed-member-of-the-club-filter.md) for every regular uncountable $\kappa$.
