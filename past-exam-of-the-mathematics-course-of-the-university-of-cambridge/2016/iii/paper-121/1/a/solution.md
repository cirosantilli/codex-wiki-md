<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an infinite [cardinal number](../../../../../../cardinal-number.md) $\kappa$, the [gimel function](../../../../../../gimel-function.md) is

$$
\boxed{\gimel(\kappa)=\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}}.
$$

The [singular cardinals hypothesis](../../../../../../singular-cardinals-hypothesis.md) asserts that for every infinite [singular cardinal](../../../../../../singular-cardinal.md) $\kappa$,

$$
\boxed{2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}<\kappa
\ \Longrightarrow\ \gimel(\kappa)=\kappa^+.}
$$

Here $\kappa^+$ is the [successor cardinal](../../../../../../successor-cardinal.md). Equivalently, for every infinite [singular cardinal](../../../../../../singular-cardinal.md),

$$
\kappa^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}
=\max\{\kappa^+,2^{\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)}\}.
$$

To see why the second formulation adds nothing in the other case, put $\theta=\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)$. If $\kappa\leq2^\theta$, then $2^\theta\leq\kappa^\theta\leq(2^\theta)^\theta=2^\theta$ by [infinite cardinal arithmetic](../../../../../../infinite-cardinal-arithmetic.md). Equality $\kappa=2^\theta$ is impossible here: the [König theorem for cardinal numbers](../../../../../../konig-s-theorem-set-theory.md) gives $\operatorname{cf}(2^\theta)>\theta$, whereas $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)=\theta$. Thus in this case $2^\theta\geq\kappa^+$, as required by the maximum formula. For a [strong limit cardinal](../../../../../../strong-limit-cardinal.md) that is singular, the hypothesis also yields $2^\kappa=\kappa^+$: restrictions of a subset of $\kappa$ to a cofinal sequence of smaller cardinals give $2^\kappa\leq\kappa^\theta$, while the reverse inequality is immediate. The quantified implication above is the precise general statement.
