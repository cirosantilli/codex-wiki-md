<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With the condition-first name convention used here, [evaluation of a forcing name](../../../../../../evaluation-of-a-forcing-name.md) is

$$
\eta_G=\{\sigma_G:\exists p\in G\ ((p,\sigma)\in\eta)\}.
$$

Since a [generic filter](../../../../../../generic-filter.md) is nonempty, there is a condition in $G$. In the given [paired forcing name](../../../../../../paired-forcing-name.md), every condition is paired with both $\tau$ and $\tau'$, and there are no other lower-rank names. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\nu(\tau,\tau')_G=\{\tau_G,\tau'_G\}}.
$$

This is the **unordered pair** of the two interpreted sets. If the interpretations coincide, it is a singleton. It is neither an [ordered pair](../../../../../../ordered-pair.md) nor the [set union](../../../../../../set-union.md) of those interpretations. Reversing the two components in the name-pair convention changes only the syntax of the same recursive evaluation.
