↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use [standard notation for forcing](../../../../../../standard-notation-for-forcing.md): $q\leq p$ means that $q$ is stronger than $p$. A [closed forcing](../../../../../../closed-forcing.md) order is $\lambda$-closed when every decreasing sequence $\langle p_\xi:\xi<\delta\rangle$ with $\delta<\lambda$ has a common lower bound:

$$
\boxed{\forall\delta<\lambda\ \forall\text{ decreasing }(p_\xi)_{\xi<\delta}\ \exists q\ \forall\xi<\delta\ (q\leq p_\xi).}
$$

The [chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../chain-condition-for-forcing.md) is

$$
\boxed{\text{every antichain in }\mathbb P\text{ has cardinality strictly below }\lambda.}
$$

An [antichain in a forcing order](../../../../../../antichain-in-a-forcing-order.md) consists of pairwise [incompatible forcing conditions](../../../../../../incompatible-forcing-conditions.md); incompatibility means having no common stronger extension. In particular, the $\aleph_1$-chain condition is the [countable chain condition for forcing](../../../../../../countable-chain-condition-for-forcing.md). These properties and their sequences or antichains are computed inside the ground model $M$.
