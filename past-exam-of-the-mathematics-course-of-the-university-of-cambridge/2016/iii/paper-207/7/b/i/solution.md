In the [counting-process intensity in survival analysis](../../../../../../../counting-process-intensity-in-survival-analysis.md), the [counting process](../../../../../../../counting-process.md) $N_i(t)$ jumps once if an observed event occurs; the [at-risk process](../../../../../../../at-risk-process.md) $Y_i(t)$ is one immediately before $t$ only while individual $i$ is still observed and event-free. Conditional on that information, the chance of an event in a short interval is $h_i(t)\,dt+o(dt)$ for an at-risk individual and zero otherwise. This is the meaning of

$$
\mathbb E(dN_i(t)\mid\mathcal H_{t-})=Y_i(t)h_i(t)\,dt.
$$

The history $\mathcal H_{t-}$ is the [filtration](../../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md) generated by the observed event and censoring histories, entry information, and available [covariates](../../../../../../../covariate.md) strictly before $t$. In particular, it determines the current [risk set](../../../../../../../risk-set.md) but does not reveal future event times. The right-hand side is the infinitesimal [compensator of a counting process](../../../../../../../compensator-of-a-counting-process.md); the equality is interpreted as an intensity statement to first order in $dt$.
