↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A real [Lévy process](../../../../../../levy-process.md) is a [stochastic process](../../../../../../stochastic-process-split.md) $(X_t)_{t\geq0}$ with $X_0=0$ almost surely, [independent increments](../../../../../../independent-increments.md), [stationary increments](../../../../../../stationary-increments.md), and [stochastic continuity](../../../../../../stochastic-continuity.md). The latter means $X_t-X_s\to0$ in probability as $t\to s$. Its usual path convention is [càdlàg](../../../../../../cadlag.md): paths are right-continuous and have left limits. Equivalently one starts with the increment and continuity-in-probability conditions and then chooses the càdlàg version.

The [Lévy characteristic exponent](../../../../../../characteristic-exponent-of-a-levy-process.md) is specified here by

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E e^{i\theta X_t}=e^{-t\psi(\theta)}.}
$$

Some conventions use $e^{t\widetilde\psi(\theta)}$ instead, with $\widetilde\psi=-\psi$. **The sign convention must be fixed before computing the exponent.** The zero process satisfies this definition; excluding it requires an additional nondegeneracy condition.
