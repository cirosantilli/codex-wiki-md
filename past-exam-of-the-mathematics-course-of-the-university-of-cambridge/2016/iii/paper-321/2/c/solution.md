<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take $ck\gg\Omega$, retaining a finite low-frequency branch and a nonzero [wavevector](../../../../../../wavevector.md). In the [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md), neglect $\omega^4$ against $c^2k^2\omega^2$ and neglect $\Omega^2$ against $c^2k^2$ in their coefficient. The [inertial wave](../../../../../../inertial-wave.md) limit is

$$
\boxed{\omega^2\simeq\Omega^2\frac{k_z^2}{k^2}.}
$$

Choose the signed branches $\omega=\epsilon\Omega k_z/k$, $\epsilon=\pm1$. The [phase velocity](../../../../../../phase-velocity.md) normal to the phase planes and the [group velocity](../../../../../../group-velocity.md) are

$$
\mathbf c_p=\frac\omega{k^2}\mathbf k,\qquad \mathbf c_g=\epsilon\Omega\left(\frac{\mathbf e_z}{k}-\frac{k_z\mathbf k}{k^3}\right).
$$

Therefore $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf c_g=\epsilon\Omega(k_z/k-k_zk^2/k^3)=0$, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbf c_p\cdot\mathbf c_g=0.}
$$

The geometric reason is that this limiting [dispersion relation](../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md) is homogeneous of degree zero in $\mathbf k$: its frequency depends on direction, not magnitude. [Euler homogeneous function theorem](../../../../../../euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) gives $\mathbf k\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf k}\omega=0$. Exact orthogonality holds for this [inertial wave](../../../../../../inertial-wave.md) limit; finite compressibility introduces corrections. At a zero-frequency degeneracy the usual propagating-wave interpretation needs a limiting treatment.
