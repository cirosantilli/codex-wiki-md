<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

There are no $\partial_tN$ or $\partial_tN^i$ terms in the displayed [Einstein-Hilbert action](../../../../../../../einstein-hilbert-action.md), so the [lapse function](../../../../../../../lapse-function.md) and [shift vector](../../../../../../../shift-vector.md) have zero [canonical momenta](../../../../../../../canonical-momentum.md). Their equations impose [constraint equations in field theory](../../../../../../../constraint-equation-in-field-theory.md) rather than independent propagation; they are [Lagrange multipliers](../../../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) in the [Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity](../../../../../../../hamiltonian-formulation-of-general-relativity.md).

With the question's positive sign for $K_{ij}=(\dot h_{ij}-D_iN_j-D_jN_i)/(2N)$, vary $\dot h_{ij}$ at fixed $h,N,N^i$. Since $\delta K_{ij}=\delta\dot h_{ij}/(2N)$,

$$
\delta\mathcal L=\sqrt h\,(K^{ij}-Kh^{ij})\delta\dot h_{ij},\qquad \boxed{\pi^{ij}=\sqrt h\,(K^{ij}-Kh^{ij}).}
$$

Thus the [canonical momentum of the spatial metric](../../../../../../../canonical-momentum-of-the-spatial-metric.md) is a weight-one [tensor density](../../../../../../../tensor-density.md). Write $\pi=h_{ij}\pi^{ij}$; in three spatial dimensions $\pi=-2\sqrt hK$, so

$$
K^{ij}=\frac1{\sqrt h}\left(\pi^{ij}-\frac12\pi h^{ij}\right).
$$

The action has suppressed its overall $1/(16\pi G)$, and this normalization is retained here. Reversing the sign convention for [extrinsic curvature](../../../../../../../extrinsic-curvature.md) would reverse the momentum relation; the calculation uses exactly the convention supplied in this question.
