<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) says that the total area of future [event horizon](../../../../../../../event-horizon.md) cross-sections cannot decrease toward the future, assuming the classical [Einstein field equations](../../../../../../../einstein-field-equations.md), the [null energy condition](../../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md), suitable global predictability and regularity, and here the stipulated future completeness of its generators.

A future [event horizon](../../../../../../../event-horizon.md) is an [achronal boundary](../../../../../../../achronal-boundary.md) generated by [null geodesics](../../../../../../../null-geodesic.md). If a smooth generator had $\theta<0$, the [null focusing theorem](../../../../../../../null-focusing-theorem.md) would produce a [conjugate point to a spacelike surface](../../../../../../../conjugate-point-to-a-spacelike-surface.md) within finite [affine parameter](../../../../../../../affine-parameter.md). Future completeness lets the generator reach it, but beyond such a point it could no longer remain on the [achronal boundary](../../../../../../../achronal-boundary.md), a contradiction. Hence $\theta\ge0$ wherever the [null expansion](../../../../../../../null-expansion.md) is defined. A transported area element obeys

$$
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log dA=\theta\ge0.
$$

Every generator present on an early cut continues to a later cut without decreasing its area contribution. New generators can join at past endpoints, adding area; future endpoints in a regular [spacetime](../../../../../../../spacetime.md) are excluded. Therefore

$$
\boxed{A_{\mathrm{later}}\ge A_{\mathrm{earlier}}.}
$$

This argument also covers the sum of initially disconnected [event horizons](../../../../../../../event-horizon.md) of the [black holes](../../../../../../../black-hole.md) in a merger. Nonsmooth crease sets require the standard regularity treatment; they do not provide a classical area-decrease loophole under these hypotheses. Quantum negative-energy flux can violate the classical [null energy condition](../../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md), so this is a classical [black-hole area theorem](../../../../../../../hawking-s-area-theorem.md), not an unconditional law for evaporating [black holes](../../../../../../../black-hole.md).
