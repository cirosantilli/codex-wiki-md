<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**A complete market can replicate every contingent claim in its specified payoff class**: for every terminal $\mathcal F_T$-measurable payoff $X$, there is an admissible [self-financing portfolio](../../../../../../self-financing-portfolio.md) whose terminal wealth equals $X$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). Equivalently, in an arbitrage-free finite-state discrete-time model the [equivalent martingale measure](../../../../../../risk-neutral-measure.md) is unique. In notation,

$$
\boxed{\forall X\text{ in the claim class},\quad\exists\text{ self-financing }\theta\text{ with }V_T^\theta=X\text{ almost surely}.}
$$

[Market completeness](../../../../../../complete-market.md) is a replication property. The following price comparisons also use the usual absence of [arbitrage](../../../../../../arbitrage.md), which the source leaves implicit: without it the initial cost of [claim replication](../../../../../../claim-replication.md) need not be unique. For example, in a deterministic one-period model with cash worth one at both dates and a [stock](../../../../../../stock.md) worth one initially but zero finally, all terminal claims are replicable using cash. Adding any [stock](../../../../../../stock.md) holding changes the initial cost without changing the terminal payoff. This is a complete model with [arbitrage](../../../../../../arbitrage.md), so it has no well-defined unique replication price.
