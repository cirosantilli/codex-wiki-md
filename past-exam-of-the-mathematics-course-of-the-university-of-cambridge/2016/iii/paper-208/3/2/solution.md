↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A [nonstationary process](../../../../../../nonstationary-process.md) has a statistical law that changes with the time origin. In the weak sense, this includes a time-dependent mean or [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md), or a [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md) that depends on the two times separately rather than only on their lag. A [strictly stationary process](../../../../../../strictly-stationary-process.md) requires invariance of every [finite-dimensional distribution](../../../../../../finite-dimensional-distribution.md) under a common time shift.

In the preceding plots, series $1$ suggests a stochastic trend or changing level, and series $4$ suggests a deterministic seasonal mean. These are the intended nonstationary examples. A plot alone cannot prove either conclusion: in particular, a stationary random-phase [sinusoid](../../../../../../sinusoid.md) can have a seasonal-looking trace and an oscillatory ACF. The interpretation of series $4$ as nonstationary concerns a seasonal mean tied to calendar time.

Three standard responses are to remove a fitted deterministic trend or seasonal mean; to use regular or seasonal [differencing](../../../../../../differencing.md) for an appropriate trend or seasonal component; and to stabilize a changing [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) by a transformation or explicit seasonal scale model. For example, logarithms or a [Box–Cox transformation](../../../../../../box-cox-transformation.md) can address level-dependent [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md). The operation should match the source of nonstationarity; [differencing](../../../../../../differencing.md) a varying [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) does not generally make it stationary.
