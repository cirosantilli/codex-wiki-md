<h1 id="6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Multiply the [Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction](../../../../../../dirichlet-laplacian-eigenfunction.md) equation by $u$ and use [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md). The zero [Dirichlet boundary condition](../../../../../../dirichlet-boundary-condition.md) removes the boundary term and gives

$$
\lambda\int_\Omega u^2=\int_\Omega|Du|^2.
$$

The denominator is positive because the [eigenfunction](../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) is nonzero. The numerator is also positive: if it were zero, the given [Sobolev inequality](../../../../../../sobolev-inequality.md) for $u\in W_0^{1,2}(\Omega)$ would force $u=0$. Therefore the [Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue](../../../../../../dirichlet-laplacian-eigenvalue.md) has the positive [Rayleigh quotient](../../../../../../rayleigh-quotient.md)

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{\int_\Omega|Du|^2}{\int_\Omega u^2}>0.}
$$

**Every nonzero Dirichlet eigenfunction in this problem has a positive eigenvalue.**
