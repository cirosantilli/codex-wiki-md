<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the bounded-domain correction, let $\mathcal S$ be the family of [Perron subfunctions for the Poisson equation](../../../../../../perron-subfunction-for-the-poisson-equation.md) $v\in C^0(\overline\Omega)$ with $v\leq\varphi$ on $\partial\Omega$. The subfunction condition is ball comparison: whenever $\overline B\subset\Omega$ and a [classical solution](../../../../../../classical-solution.md) $H$ of $\Delta H=f$ on $B$ satisfies $v\leq H$ on $\partial B$, it also satisfies $v\leq H$ throughout $B$. For a $C^2$ function this is the sign condition $\Delta v\geq f$. A superfunction reverses both inequalities.

**The Perron solution is the pointwise upper envelope of the subfunctions:**

$$
\boxed{u(x)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal S}v(x),\qquad x\in\Omega.}
$$

This is the [Perron method for the Dirichlet problem](../../../../../../perron-method.md) with the [Poisson equation](../../../../../../poisson-equation.md) replacing the homogeneous [Laplace equation](../../../../../../laplace-equation.md). The [power barrier for an exterior cone](../../../../../../power-barrier-for-an-exterior-cone.md) supplies a global member of $\mathcal S$ and a global superfunction, as constructed below. The permitted subfunction-superfunction comparison therefore makes this envelope finite. The standard interior Perron theorem, available in the question, gives $u\in C^2(\Omega)$ and $\Delta u=f$; neither result needs to be proved in this part.
