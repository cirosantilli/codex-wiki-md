<h1 id="3/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume inviscid, unforced evolution, so [shallow-water potential vorticity](../../../../../../../shallow-water-potential-vorticity.md) is conserved. If a column has initial depth $H_1$ and [relative vorticity](../../../../../../../relative-vorticity.md) $\zeta_1$, moving to $H_2>H_1$ at fixed [Coriolis parameter](../../../../../../../coriolis-parameter.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\zeta_2=\frac{H_2}{H_1}(f+\zeta_1)-f,\qquad \zeta_2-\zeta_1=\left(\frac{H_2}{H_1}-1\right)(f+\zeta_1).}
$$

Its [absolute vorticity](../../../../../../../absolute-vorticity.md) is amplified by [vortex stretching](../../../../../../../vortex-stretching.md). A column initially without [relative vorticity](../../../../../../../relative-vorticity.md) develops cyclonic spin: positive in the Northern Hemisphere and negative in the Southern Hemisphere. More generally the increase is in the direction of its original [absolute vorticity](../../../../../../../absolute-vorticity.md), not necessarily the direction of $f$ if the initial spin is unusually strong.
