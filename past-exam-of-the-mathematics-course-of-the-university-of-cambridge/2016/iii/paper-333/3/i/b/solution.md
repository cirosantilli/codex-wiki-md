<h1 id="3/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Conservation of [shallow-water potential vorticity](../../../../../../../shallow-water-potential-vorticity.md) with fixed [relative vorticity](../../../../../../../relative-vorticity.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{H_2}{H_1}=\frac{f_2+\zeta}{f_1+\zeta}.}
$$

For ordinary planetary-vorticity-dominated columns, moving towards the equator decreases the magnitude of [absolute vorticity](../../../../../../../absolute-vorticity.md), so the column must **become shallower and spread horizontally**. This is the compression counterpart of [vortex stretching](../../../../../../../vortex-stretching.md). For $\zeta=0$, the depth ratio is $f_2/f_1$, in either hemisphere before reaching the equator. The displayed general formula is essential if the fixed [relative vorticity](../../../../../../../relative-vorticity.md) is large: the sign of the depth change then depends on the original [absolute vorticity](../../../../../../../absolute-vorticity.md), and conservation cannot continue through zero absolute vorticity with positive finite depth and nonzero conserved potential vorticity.
