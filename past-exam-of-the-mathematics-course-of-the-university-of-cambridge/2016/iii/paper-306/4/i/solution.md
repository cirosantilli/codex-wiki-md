<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The first-order fermionic kinetic term produces second-class [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) [constraints](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md). After eliminating them, the canonical [graded Dirac brackets](../../../../../../graded-dirac-bracket.md) are

$$
\boxed{\{x^m,p_n\}_D=\delta^m{}_n,\qquad\{\psi^m,\psi^n\}_D=-i\eta^{mn},}
$$

with other mixed brackets zero. The bracket of two odd variables is symmetric, consistently with the [graded Poisson bracket](../../../../../../graded-poisson-bracket.md). The [constraint algebra](../../../../../../constraint-algebra.md) is

$$
\boxed{\{\mathcal Q,\mathcal Q\}_D=-ip^2=-2i\mathcal H,\qquad\{\mathcal Q,\mathcal H\}_D=0,\qquad\{\mathcal H,\mathcal H\}_D=0.}
$$

It is the [worldline supersymmetry algebra](../../../../../../worldline-supersymmetry-algebra.md): the square of the fermionic [constraint](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) generates the [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) [constraint](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md).

With $\hbar=1$, quantization converts these to

$$
[\widehat x^m,\widehat p_n]=i\delta^m{}_n,\qquad\{\widehat\psi^m,\widehat\psi^n\}_+=\eta^{mn}.
$$

Represent the fermions by [Gamma matrices](../../../../../../gamma-matrices.md), $\widehat\psi^m=\gamma^m/\sqrt2$, where $\{\gamma^m,\gamma^n\}_+=2\eta^{mn}$, and $\widehat p_m=-i\partial_m$. The wavefunction is a [Dirac spinor](../../../../../../dirac-spinor.md). Its physical-state [constraint](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) is

$$
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal Q}\Psi=-\frac{i}{\sqrt2}\gamma^m\partial_m\Psi=0,}
$$

which is exactly the [massless Dirac equation](../../../../../../massless-dirac-equation.md). Moreover $\widehat{\mathcal Q}^{\,2}=\widehat{\mathcal H}=-\square_D/2$, so this equation implies the remaining mass-shell [constraint](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md). Conversely, a solution of the [massless Dirac equation](../../../../../../massless-dirac-equation.md) satisfies both [constraints](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md). The Klein-Gordon [constraint](../../../../../../constraint-mechanics.md) alone would not impose the spinorial first-order equation.
