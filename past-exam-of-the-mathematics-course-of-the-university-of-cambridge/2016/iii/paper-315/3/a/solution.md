<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The two relevant categories are **strongly irradiated, often transiting [hot Jupiters](../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md), and young [directly imaged exoplanets](../../../../../../exoplanet-direct-imaging.md) with large residual-entropy radii**. The young objects are inflated relative to an old cold mass-radius sequence; a large young radius is not automatically anomalous relative to an age-appropriate formation model.

For a transit, the depth gives $(R_p/R_\star)^2$ after geometric and [limb darkening](../../../../../../limb-darkening.md) corrections; an independently estimated stellar radius sets $R_p$. A measured planet mass then tests its location on the [planetary mass-radius relation](../../../../../../planetary-mass-radius-relation.md). For a directly imaged object, distance and integrated spectral flux give luminosity; a fitted [effective temperature](../../../../../../effective-temperature.md) supplies

$$
\boxed{R_p=\left(\frac{L}{4\pi\sigma_{\rm SB}T_{\rm eff}^4}\right)^{1/2}.}
$$

The imaged radius is model-dependent because temperature, gravity and [exoplanet clouds](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud.md) are inferred from its spectrum; its mass may also depend on age and evolutionary models unless dynamically measured.

Two broad explanatory classes are **slower loss of existing heat and addition of heat to the deep interior**. Specific delayed-cooling proposals are enhanced atmospheric [opacity](../../../../../../opacity.md), which restricts radiative loss, and composition gradients producing [layered convection in a giant planet](../../../../../../layered-convection-in-a-giant-planet.md). Specific heating proposals are [tidal heating](../../../../../../tidal-heating.md) and [Ohmic heating](../../../../../../joule-heating.md) from currents induced by magnetized atmospheric winds, the proposed [Ohmic heating of a giant planet](../../../../../../ohmic-heating-of-a-giant-planet.md). Heating must reach or influence sufficiently deep layers to maintain the interior [specific entropy](../../../../../../specific-entropy.md); merely heating the optically thin upper atmosphere is not equivalent. For young distant objects, retention of formation heat, described by [hot and cold starts of a giant planet](../../../../../../hot-and-cold-starts-of-a-giant-planet.md), itself explains much of the large radius without requiring the hot-Jupiter heating mechanisms.
