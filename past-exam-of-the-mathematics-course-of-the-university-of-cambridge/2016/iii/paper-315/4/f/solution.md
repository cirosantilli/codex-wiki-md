<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Three observable signatures are **a blue optical transmission slope, muted molecular transmission features, and enhanced reflected optical light**.

Small-particle [atmospheric haze](../../../../../../haze.md) can increase the apparent transit radius toward short wavelengths, roughly $0.3$--$1\,\mu\mathrm m$. For [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md)-like extinction, $\sigma\propto\lambda^{-4}$ and an isothermal [atmospheric scale height](../../../../../../atmospheric-scale-height.md) model gives $dR_p/d\ln\lambda\simeq-4H$. Larger particles instead produce a flatter, approximately grey continuum.

An optically thick [exoplanet cloud deck](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud-deck.md) can truncate the slant path through gas and flatten molecular bands in a [exoplanet transmission spectrum](../../../../../../exoplanet-transmission-spectrum.md), for example over $0.4$--$2.5\,\mu\mathrm m$ and the [water](../../../../../../water.md) band around $1.4\,\mu\mathrm m$. The observed quantity is reduced band-to-continuum radius contrast, not necessarily a low molecular abundance.

[exoplanet cloud](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud.md) scattering can produce a high [geometric albedo](../../../../../../geometric-albedo.md) and distinctive optical secondary eclipses/phase curves around $0.4$--$0.9\,\mu\mathrm m$. Longitudinal [exoplanet cloud](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud.md) variation can shift a reflected-light phase maximum. A spectral slope or flat spectrum alone is not a unique [exoplanet cloud](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud.md) identification: particle size, gas scattering, metallicity and stellar contamination must be considered together.
