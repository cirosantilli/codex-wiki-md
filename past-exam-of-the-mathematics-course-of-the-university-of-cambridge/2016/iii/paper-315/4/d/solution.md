↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the 2016 observational context, appropriate choices are as follows.

For atomic species, use optical transit spectra of Na I near **$0.589\,\mu\mathrm m$** and K I near **$0.770\,\mu\mathrm m$**, with [Hubble Space Telescope](../../../../../../hubble-space-telescope.md)/STIS or ground-based high-resolution instruments on [Very Large Telescope](../../../../../../very-large-telescope.md)/[Keck telescope](../../../../../../w-m-keck-observatory.md)-class telescopes. Neutral/ionized ultraviolet metal lines offer additional diagnostics with [Hubble Space Telescope](../../../../../../hubble-space-telescope.md). Resolving a line's shape and distinguishing stellar contamination matters as much as locating its wavelength.

For molecules, use near/mid-IR spectroscopy: [water](../../../../../../water.md) bands near **$1.4$, $1.9$ and $2.7\,\mu\mathrm m$**, CO near **$2.3$ and $4.6\,\mu\mathrm m$**, [methane](../../../../../../methane.md) near **$3.3\,\mu\mathrm m$**, and $\mathrm{CO}_2$ near **$4.3$ or $15\,\mu\mathrm m$**. [Hubble Space Telescope](../../../../../../hubble-space-telescope.md)/WFC3 samples [water](../../../../../../water.md) around $1.1$--$1.7\,\mu\mathrm m$; ground-based [Very Large Telescope](../../../../../../very-large-telescope.md)/CRIRES or [Keck telescope](../../../../../../w-m-keck-observatory.md)/NIRSPEC spectra target accessible near-IR windows such as CO at $2.3\,\mu\mathrm m$. Warm [Spitzer Space Telescope](../../../../../../spitzer-space-telescope.md) provides $3.6$ and $4.5\,\mu\mathrm m$ photometry, while cryogenic [Spitzer Space Telescope](../../../../../../spitzer-space-telescope.md) spectra/longer-wavelength data are archival by 2016. A broadband eclipse point alone does not uniquely identify a molecule.

For [atmospheric escape](../../../../../../atmospheric-escape.md), use ultraviolet transit spectroscopy of H I Ly$\alpha$ at **$121.6\,\mathrm{nm}$**, and suitable C/O/metal lines, with [Hubble Space Telescope](../../../../../../hubble-space-telescope.md)/STIS or COS. Extended, velocity-shifted absorption beyond the optical planet suggests an escaping envelope, although stellar variability, geocoronal emission and interstellar absorption require treatment. Ground-based H$\alpha$ at $656.3\,\mathrm{nm}$ probes excited hydrogen and is a complementary rather than interchangeable measure of neutral-hydrogen escape.
