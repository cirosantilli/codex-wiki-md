Let $x$ denote the generating object, the image of the unique object of the [terminal category](../../../category.md#terminal-category). The [free braided monoidal category on one object](../../../category-theory.md#free-braided-monoidal-category-on-one-object) can be described syntactically. Its objects are all fully parenthesized expressions built from $x$, the [monoidal unit object](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-unit-object) $I$, and a binary [monoidal tensor product](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-tensor-product). Thus $x\otimes(I\otimes x)$ and $(x\otimes I)\otimes x$ are distinct objects, though canonically isomorphic. Its [morphisms](../../../algebra.md#morphism) are generated by the [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator), [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor), [braidings](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) and their inverses, closed under composition and tensor product, subject to the pentagon, triangle, [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) and hexagon axioms. No symmetry relation is imposed on the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding). The embedding of the [terminal category](../../../category.md#terminal-category) selects $x$.

The [braid category](../../../category-theory.md#braid-category) $\mathbf B$ has objects the nonnegative integers, with

$$
\mathbf B(m,n)=\begin{cases}B_n,&m=n,\\\varnothing,&m\ne n,\end{cases}
$$

where $B_n$ is the [braid group](../../../group.md#braid-group) on $n$ strands and $B_0,B_1$ are trivial. Composition is stacking braids, with the first morphism followed by the second. The [monoidal tensor product](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-tensor-product) is addition on objects and side-by-side juxtaposition of braids; its [monoidal unit object](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-unit-object) is $0$. The [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor) are identities, so it is a [strict monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-category).

Choose the positive crossing convention once and for all. Its [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) $c_{m,n}:m+n\to n+m$ is the block braid moving the first $m$ strands over the next $n$ strands while retaining the order within each block. In particular $c_{1,1}=\sigma_1$. The usual [braid group](../../../group.md#braid-group) relations express the [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) and hexagon laws for these block braids. The generating [functor](../../../category.md#functor) $\mathbf1\to\mathbf B$ selects $1$.

**The distinction is parenthesized tensor expressions versus strand counts:** the first construction keeps the structural isomorphisms visible, while $\mathbf B$ has strict tensor arithmetic. These descriptions give the two requested free constructions without needing to establish their [universal properties](../../../category-theory.md#universal-property).

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Define the length of a formal tensor expression recursively by

$$
|I|=0,\qquad |x|=1,\qquad |u\otimes v|=|u|+|v|.
$$

The canonical [strict monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-functor) $Q:\mathcal Fbr\to\mathbf B$ sends $u$ to $|u|$, sends every [associator](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitor](../../../category-theory.md#unitor) to an identity braid, and sends $c_{u,v}$ to the block [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) $c_{|u|,|v|}$. The pentagon and triangle become identity equations; the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) axioms become the corresponding block-braid equations. Thus the assignment respects the defining relations, and

$$
Q(u\otimes v)=Qu+Qv,\qquad QI=0
$$

on the nose. It is a [braided monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#braided-monoidal-functor) with identity comparison maps.

To prove that $Q$ is an [equivalence of categories](../../../category.md#equivalence-of-categories), choose a standard parenthesization $x^{[n]}$ of $n$ copies of $x$, with $x^{[0]}=I$. On $x^{[n]}$, define the image of $\sigma_i$ by canonically exposing the $i$th and $(i+1)$st factors, applying $c_{x,x}$ there, and restoring the chosen parentheses. The [monoidal coherence theorem](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem) makes this independent of the structural rebracketing. Crossings on disjoint pairs commute by the tensor interchange law. The adjacent [braid group relations](../../../group.md#braid-group-relations) follows from the hexagon laws and [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding), as in the Yang–Baxter calculation below. Hence these assignments give [group homomorphisms](../../../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $B_n\to\operatorname{Aut}(x^{[n]})$ and a [functor](../../../category.md#functor) $S:\mathbf B\to\mathcal Fbr$.

Equip $S$ with the canonical rebracketing maps $x^{[m]}\otimes x^{[n]}\to x^{[m+n]}$. Their [monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor) axioms follow from the [monoidal coherence theorem](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem); the block-braiding compatibility follows by iterating the two hexagon laws. Therefore $S$ is a [strong monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strong-monoidal-functor) compatible with the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding).

We have $QS=1_{\mathbf B}$, including its comparison maps. For every formal expression $u$, there is a canonical structural isomorphism

$$
\theta_u:x^{[|u|]}\longrightarrow u
$$

obtained by rebracketing and inserting the units appearing in $u$. These maps form a [natural isomorphism](../../../category.md#natural-isomorphism) $SQ\Rightarrow1_{\mathcal Fbr}$. To check [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality), it suffices to check the generating [morphisms](../../../algebra.md#morphism): for [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor) it is exactly [monoidal coherence](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem); for $c_{u,v}$ it follows from the hexagon expansion into the elementary crossings defining $S$. Composition and tensor product then preserve the equation. The same structural coherence shows that $\theta$ is monoidal.

**Thus $Q$ has a specified quasi-inverse and is the required equivalence:**

$$
\boxed{Q:\mathcal Fbr\simeq\mathbf B\quad\text{is braided and strict monoidal}.}
$$

Only ordinary [monoidal coherence](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem), together with the defining [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) axioms, has been used; a separate braided coherence theorem is unnecessary.

An [opmonoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor) $F:(\mathcal C,\otimes,I)\to(\mathcal D,\otimes,J)$ consists of a [functor](../../../category.md#functor), a [natural transformation](../../../category.md#natural-transformation)

$$
F_2(X,Y):F(X\otimes Y)\longrightarrow FX\otimes FY,
$$

and a [morphism](../../../algebra.md#morphism) $F_0:FI\to J$. These maps need not be invertible. If $\alpha,\lambda,\rho$ are the respective [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor), its axioms are

$$
\alpha^{\mathcal D}(F_2(X,Y)\otimes1)F_2(X\otimes Y,Z)=(1\otimes F_2(Y,Z))F_2(X,Y\otimes Z)F(\alpha^{\mathcal C}),
$$



$$
\lambda^{\mathcal D}(F_0\otimes1)F_2(I,X)=F(\lambda^{\mathcal C}),\qquad\rho^{\mathcal D}(1\otimes F_0)F_2(X,I)=F(\rho^{\mathcal C}).
$$

The first equation has domain $F((X\otimes Y)\otimes Z)$ and codomain $FX\otimes(FY\otimes FZ)$, which fixes the direction of every arrow. This is also called a [colax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor).

An [opmonoidal natural transformation](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-natural-transformation) $\tau:F\Rightarrow G$ between [opmonoidal functors](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor) is a [natural transformation](../../../category.md#natural-transformation) satisfying

$$
\boxed{G_2(X,Y)\tau_{X\otimes Y}=(\tau_X\otimes\tau_Y)F_2(X,Y),\qquad G_0\tau_I=F_0.}
$$

**It respects both the tensor and unit comparison maps.** These are [commutative diagrams](../../../homology.md#commutative-diagram) and a unit [commutative diagram](../../../homology.md#commutative-diagram); the arrow directions are opposite to those for a [lax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor).

An [opmonoidal monad](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-monad) on a [monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-category) is a [monad](../../../category-theory.md#monad) $(T,\eta,\mu)$ whose endofunctor is an [opmonoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor) and whose [unit and multiplication of a monad](../../../category-theory.md#unit-and-multiplication-of-a-monad) are [opmonoidal natural transformations](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-natural-transformation). Suppress only the canonical parentheses. Explicitly,

$$
T_2\eta_{X\otimes Y}=\eta_X\otimes\eta_Y,\qquad T_0\eta_I=1_I,
$$



$$
T_2(X,Y)\mu_{X\otimes Y}=(\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y)T_2(TX,TY)T(T_2(X,Y)),\qquad T_0\mu_I=T_0T(T_0).
$$

The composite [opmonoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor) $T^2$ has tensor comparison $T_2(TX,TY)T(T_2(X,Y))$ and unit comparison $T_0T(T_0)$, explaining the last two equations.

For two [algebras for a monad](../../../category-theory.md#algebra-for-a-monad) $(X,a)$ and $(Y,b)$, define

$$
\boxed{(X,a)\otimes(Y,b)=\bigl(X\otimes Y,(a\otimes b)T_2(X,Y)\bigr),\qquad\mathbf I=(I,T_0).}
$$

Let $c=(a\otimes b)T_2(X,Y)$. The [unit law for a monad algebra](../../../category-theory.md#unit-law-for-a-monad-algebra) follows at once from the opmonoidality of $\eta$:

$$
c\eta_{X\otimes Y}=(a\eta_X)\otimes(b\eta_Y)=1.
$$

For the multiplication law, [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of $T_2$, the algebra laws, and the opmonoidality of $\mu$ give

$$
\begin{aligned}
cT(c)&=(a\otimes b)(Ta\otimes Tb)T_2(TX,TY)T(T_2(X,Y))\\
&=(a\mu_X\otimes b\mu_Y)T_2(TX,TY)T(T_2(X,Y))\\
&=(a\otimes b)T_2(X,Y)\mu_{X\otimes Y}=c\mu_{X\otimes Y}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two unit-comparison equations above similarly make $(I,T_0)$ a [algebra for a monad](../../../category-theory.md#algebra-for-a-monad). If $f,g$ are [morphisms of algebras for a monad](../../../category-theory.md#morphism-of-algebras-for-a-monad), [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of $T_2$ shows that $f\otimes g$ is an algebra morphism.

For a third algebra $(Z,d)$, the base [associator](../../../category-theory.md#associator) is also an algebra morphism: its intertwining equation is precisely the opmonoidal associativity axiom, followed by $a\otimes b\otimes d$. The two base [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor) are algebra morphisms by the opmonoidal unit axioms. Their pentagon and triangle commute because they commute after the faithful [forgetful functor](../../../category.md#forgetful-functor), and the lifted maps have exactly the same underlying [morphisms](../../../algebra.md#morphism).

**The [Eilenberg-Moore category](../../../category-theory.md#eilenberg-moore-category) is therefore monoidal**, with these lifted constraints. Its [forgetful functor](../../../category.md#forgetful-functor) $U:\mathcal C^T\to\mathcal C$ preserves the tensor product, unit object and constraints exactly, so it is a [strict monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-functor).

On the [monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-category) of [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) over the [commutative ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#commutative-ring) $k$, consider the [monad](../../../category-theory.md#monad) $T=H\otimes_k-$ coming from the unit and multiplication of the [bialgebra](../../../commutative-algebra.md#bialgebra) $H$. Its [opmonoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-functor) structure has comparison maps

$$
T_2\bigl(h\otimes(x\otimes y)\bigr)=\sum(h_{(1)}\otimes x)\otimes(h_{(2)}\otimes y),\qquad T_0=\varepsilon:H\otimes_k k\cong H\to k.
$$

Here and below [Sweedler notation](../../../linear-algebra.md#sweedler-notation) abbreviates the [comultiplication](../../../category-theory.md#comultiplication) $\Delta h=\sum h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)}$. The opmonoidal associativity and unit axioms are the coassociativity and counit laws of the [coalgebra](../../../linear-algebra.md#coalgebra). The [unit and multiplication of a monad](../../../category-theory.md#unit-and-multiplication-of-a-monad) are [opmonoidal natural transformations](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-natural-transformation) because the [bialgebra](../../../commutative-algebra.md#bialgebra) axioms say

$$
\Delta(hg)=\sum h_{(1)}g_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)}g_{(2)},\qquad\Delta(1)=1\otimes1,\qquad\varepsilon(hg)=\varepsilon(h)\varepsilon(g),\quad\varepsilon(1)=1.
$$

The [Eilenberg-Moore category](../../../category-theory.md#eilenberg-moore-category) of this [opmonoidal monad](../../../category-theory.md#opmonoidal-monad) is the category of left $H$-[modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics): a monad-algebra map $H\otimes_kM\to M$ is exactly a unital associative action.

Applying the preceding construction gives the [diagonal bialgebra action](../../../commutative-algebra.md#diagonal-bialgebra-action) and the unit action

$$
\boxed{h\cdot(u\otimes v)=\sum(h_{(1)}\cdot u)\otimes(h_{(2)}\cdot v),\qquad h\cdot a=\varepsilon(h)a\quad(a\in k).}
$$

The usual [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor) for the [tensor product of modules](../../../module-theory.md#tensor-product-of-modules) are $H$-linear, and the underlying tensor product is exactly $\otimes_k$. **The forgetful functor into $k$-modules is strict monoidal.** The base need not be a [field](../../../algebra.md#field); the [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) need be neither [flat modules](../../../module-theory.md#flat-module) nor [finitely generated modules](../../../module-theory.md#finitely-generated-module).

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

We use the left internal-hom convention in which $[M,N]$ is right adjoint to $-\otimes_kM$, so its [evaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#evaluation-morphism) has domain $[M,N]\otimes_kM$. For a [Hopf algebra](../../../algebra.md#hopf-algebra) over the [commutative ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#commutative-ring) $k$, take the full $k$-module

$$
[M,N]=\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,N)
$$

and give it the [internal hom for modules over a Hopf algebra](../../../algebra.md#internal-hom-for-modules-over-a-hopf-algebra)

$$
\boxed{(h\cdot f)(u)=\sum h_{(1)}\cdot f(S(h_{(2)})\cdot u).}
$$

The [antipode](../../../algebra.md#antipode) is an antihomomorphism, $S(hg)=S(g)S(h)$, so

$$
(h\cdot(g\cdot f))(u)=\sum h_{(1)}g_{(1)}\cdot f(S(g_{(2)})S(h_{(2)})\cdot u)=((hg)\cdot f)(u).
$$

The unit acts identically. Thus this is a left $H$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics).

The [evaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#evaluation-morphism) is $H$-linear. Using the [diagonal bialgebra action](../../../commutative-algebra.md#diagonal-bialgebra-action) and the antipode identity,

$$
\sum(h_{(1)}\cdot f)(h_{(2)}\cdot u)=\sum h_{(1)}\cdot f(S(h_{(2)})h_{(3)}\cdot u)=h\cdot f(u).
$$

Now let $q:L\otimes_kM\to N$ be $H$-linear and define its ordinary curried map $\widehat q(\ell)(u)=q(\ell\otimes u)$. To see that it too is $H$-linear, compute

$$
\begin{aligned}
(h\cdot\widehat q(\ell))(u)&=\sum h_{(1)}\cdot q(\ell\otimes S(h_{(2)})\cdot u)\\
&=\sum q(h_{(1)}\cdot\ell\otimes h_{(2)}S(h_{(3)})\cdot u)\\
&=q(h\cdot\ell\otimes u)=\widehat q(h\cdot\ell)(u).
\end{aligned}
$$

Conversely, any $H$-linear map $L\to[M,N]$ uncurries to an $H$-linear map by the already established linearity of evaluation. The ordinary tensor–hom [adjunction](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors) therefore restricts to a natural [bijection](../../../function.md#bijection)

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}_H(L\otimes_kM,N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_H(L,[M,N]).}
$$

Postcomposition by an $H$-linear map makes $[M,-]$ a [functor](../../../category.md#functor). This proves that the category of left $H$-[modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is a [left closed monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#left-closed-monoidal-category). **All modules are allowed; no inverse antipode or finite-dimensional dual is required.** Stating the tensor–hom convention explicitly avoids confusing this construction with the closure on the opposite side.

By the [monoidal coherence theorem](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem), calculations may suppress the canonical [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) and [unitors](../../../category-theory.md#unitor), restoring them uniquely afterwards. In this notation the two [Frobenius monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#frobenius-monoidal-functor) identities read

$$
\psi_{A\otimes B,C}\varphi_{A,B\otimes C}=(\varphi_{A,B}\otimes1)(1\otimes\psi_{B,C}),
$$



$$
\psi_{A,B\otimes C}\varphi_{A\otimes B,C}=(1\otimes\varphi_{B,C})(\psi_{A,B}\otimes1).
$$

Write

$$
E=\psi_0F(e)\varphi_{X,Y},\qquad N=\psi_{Y,X}F(n)\varphi_0.
$$

We check both [snake identities](../../../category-theory.md#snake-identity) for this prospective [dual pair in a monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#dual-pair-in-a-monoidal-category). For $FX$, expand and use the first Frobenius identity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(E\otimes1)(1\otimes N)&=(\psi_0\otimes1)(Fe\otimes1)(\varphi_{X,Y}\otimes1)(1\otimes\psi_{Y,X})(1\otimes Fn)(1\otimes\varphi_0)\\
&=(\psi_0\otimes1)(Fe\otimes1)\psi_{X\otimes Y,X}\varphi_{X,Y\otimes X}(1\otimes Fn)(1\otimes\varphi_0)\\
&=(\psi_0\otimes1)\psi_{I,X}F(e\otimes1)F(1\otimes n)\varphi_{X,I}(1\otimes\varphi_0)\\
&=1_{FX}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The third line uses [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of $\psi$ and $\varphi$. The last line uses $(e\otimes1)(1\otimes n)=1_X$, followed by the opmonoidal and monoidal unit axioms.

For $FY$, the second Frobenius identity gives the other calculation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1\otimes E)(N\otimes1)&=(1\otimes\psi_0)(1\otimes Fe)(1\otimes\varphi_{X,Y})(\psi_{Y,X}\otimes1)(Fn\otimes1)(\varphi_0\otimes1)\\
&=(1\otimes\psi_0)(1\otimes Fe)\psi_{Y,X\otimes Y}\varphi_{Y\otimes X,Y}(Fn\otimes1)(\varphi_0\otimes1)\\
&=(1\otimes\psi_0)\psi_{Y,I}F(1\otimes e)F(n\otimes1)\varphi_{I,Y}(\varphi_0\otimes1)\\
&=1_{FY}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus **both triangular identities hold**, and

$$
\boxed{E:FX\otimes FY\to I,\qquad N:I\to FY\otimes FX}
$$

are the [evaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#evaluation-morphism) and [coevaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#coevaluation-morphism) of a [dual pair in a monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#dual-pair-in-a-monoidal-category). Notice that none of the comparison maps was assumed invertible.

Use the specified self-dual-pairing meaning of “coseparable”; categorically this is the [Frobenius monoid](../../../category-theory.md#frobenius-monoid) structure relevant here. The induced [monoid object](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-object) on $FA$ has maps

$$
\boxed{J=F(j)\varphi_0,\qquad M=F(m)\varphi_{A,A},\qquad E_A=\psi_0F(\varepsilon).}
$$

Its associativity follows from associativity of $m$ and the [lax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor) axiom for $\varphi$: both iterated products are $F$ of the corresponding triple product, preceded by the same coherent iterated tensor comparison. The unit laws follow from those of $j,m$ and the two monoidal unit axioms. Thus $(FA,J,M)$ is a [monoid object](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-object).

Its pairing is exactly the evaluation transported in the preceding part:

$$
E_AM=\psi_0F(\varepsilon)F(m)\varphi_{A,A}=\psi_0F(\varepsilon m)\varphi_{A,A}.
$$

If $n:I\to A\otimes A$ is the original [coevaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#coevaluation-morphism), the corresponding new one is $\psi_{A,A}F(n)\varphi_0$. The preceding [Frobenius monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#frobenius-monoidal-functor) calculation proves both [snake identities](../../../category-theory.md#snake-identity). Hence **the pairing of the induced monoid remains self-dual**, giving the claimed canonical structure on $FA$. The terminology in the paper imposes this duality condition; it does not add a separability or semisimplicity condition.

**The algebra must be finite-dimensional, and there is no universal bound on its dimension.** Let $e=\varepsilon m$ and write its [coevaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#coevaluation-morphism) as a finite sum

$$
n(1)=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\otimes b_i.
$$

For any $v\in A$, one [snake identity](../../../category-theory.md#snake-identity) gives

$$
v=\sum_{i=1}^r e(v,a_i)b_i.
$$

Thus the finitely many $b_i$ span $A$, making it a [finite-dimensional vector space](../../../vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-vector-space). The other [snake identity](../../../category-theory.md#snake-identity) shows that $e$ is [nondegenerate](../../../linear-algebra.md#nondegenerate-bilinear-form) in the other variable as well, so the maps to the [dual space](../../../linear-algebra.md#dual-space) determined by this pairing are isomorphisms. In the standard algebraic terminology this is a [Frobenius algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#frobenius-algebra).

Every positive finite [dimension of a vector space](../../../vector-space.md#dimension-vector-space) occurs: take $A=k^d$ with coordinatewise multiplication and $\varepsilon(a_1,\ldots,a_d)=\sum_i a_i$. The coordinate idempotents $e_i$ give $e(e_i,e_j)=\delta_{ij}$ and $n(1)=\sum_i e_i\otimes e_i$. If zero unital algebras are allowed, the zero object gives dimension zero too.

The pairing need not make $A$ a [semisimple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#semisimple-algebra). For example, the [dual numbers](../../../commutative-algebra.md#dual-number) $k[t]/(t^2)$ with $\varepsilon(a+bt)=b$ have pairing matrix

$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

and [coevaluation morphism](../../../category-theory.md#coevaluation-morphism) $1\mapsto1\otimes t+t\otimes1$. This pairing is [nondegenerate](../../../linear-algebra.md#nondegenerate-bilinear-form) over every [field](../../../algebra.md#field), despite the [nilpotent ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#nilpotent-ideal) $(t)$. **Finite-dimensionality is the dimension conclusion; separability is not part of the given definition.**

For an object $X$ of a [braided monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#braided-monoidal-category), take its self-[braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding)

$$
\boxed{y=c_{X,X}:X\otimes X\longrightarrow X\otimes X.}
$$

It is invertible by definition. Suppress canonical [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) using the [monoidal coherence theorem](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-coherence-theorem), and put $R_{12}=y\otimes1$, $R_{23}=1\otimes y$. The hexagon identity gives

$$
c_{X,X\otimes X}=R_{23}R_{12}.
$$

Apply [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of this [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) to the morphism $y:X\otimes X\to X\otimes X$ in its second argument. It says

$$
c_{X,X\otimes X}(1\otimes y)=(y\otimes1)c_{X,X\otimes X}.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
\boxed{R_{23}R_{12}R_{23}=R_{12}R_{23}R_{12}.}
$$

This is the equation for a [Yang–Baxter operator](../../../category-theory.md#yang-baxter-operator). Restoring the uniquely determined [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) gives the non-strict diagram in the paper. **Every object therefore has the canonical Yang–Baxter operator supplied by its self-braiding.**

The [braid group](../../../group.md#braid-group) $B_n$ has generators $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}$ and relations

$$
\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i\ (|i-j|>1),\qquad\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}.
$$

In the [strict monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-category) $\mathcal C$, put $X^{\otimes0}=I$ and send

$$
\boxed{n\longmapsto X^{\otimes n},\qquad\sigma_i\longmapsto1_X^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes y\otimes1_X^{\otimes(n-i-1)}.}
$$

The images are invertible because the [Yang–Baxter operator](../../../category-theory.md#yang-baxter-operator) $y$ is invertible. Distant images commute by the tensor interchange law, and adjacent images satisfy the [braid group relations](../../../group.md#braid-group-relations) by the [Yang–Baxter operator](../../../category-theory.md#yang-baxter-operator) equation tensored with the remaining identities. Thus these assignments define [group homomorphisms](../../../group-theory.md#group-homomorphism) $B_n\to\operatorname{Aut}(X^{\otimes n})$, and hence a [functor](../../../category.md#functor) $\mathbf B\to\mathcal C$.

Juxtaposition of braids puts generators into the corresponding two blocks of tensor factors, so their images are the tensor products of their images. Strictness of $\mathcal C$ gives $X^{\otimes(m+n)}=X^{\otimes m}\otimes X^{\otimes n}$ and $X^{\otimes0}=I$ exactly. **The resulting functor is strict monoidal and sends $1$ to $X$.**

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

We use right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) and write their [coactions](../../../linear-algebra.md#coaction) as $u\mapsto\sum u_{(0)}\otimes u_{(1)}$. A [coquasitriangular structure](../../../category-theory.md#coquasitriangular-structure) gives the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) on these [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule)

$$
b_{M,N}(u\otimes v)=\sum v_{(0)}\otimes u_{(0)}\,\gamma(u_{(1)},v_{(1)}).
$$

In an arbitrary [symmetric monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#symmetric-monoidal-category) this notation abbreviates a composite of the two coactions, the ambient symmetry, and $\gamma$; it does not assume that the objects have elements. The coquasitriangular axioms ensure that this composite is a comodule morphism, is invertible using the convolution inverse of $\gamma$, and satisfies the two hexagon laws. More explicitly, in scalar notation those laws come from

$$
\gamma(ab,c)=\sum\gamma(a,c_{(1)})\gamma(b,c_{(2)}),\qquad\gamma(a,bc)=\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},c)\gamma(a_{(2)},b),
$$

while the comodule-morphism condition is

$$
\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})a_{(2)}b_{(2)}=\sum b_{(1)}a_{(1)}\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)}).
$$

Unit normalizations give the unit constraints. These descriptions are identities of morphisms in the ambient symmetric category, with the displayed reordering carried by its symmetry.

Apply the preceding self-[braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) result to the regular right [comodule](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) $(H,\Delta)$. Its [Yang–Baxter operator](../../../category-theory.md#yang-baxter-operator) is

$$
R(a\otimes b)=\sum b_{(1)}\otimes a_{(1)}\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)}).
$$

Compose its braid equation with the three [counits](../../../category-theory.md#counit). Expanding $R_{23}R_{12}R_{23}$ and cancelling the leading counit factors gives

$$
\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})\gamma(a_{(2)},c_{(1)})\gamma(b_{(2)},c_{(2)}).
$$

Expanding $R_{12}R_{23}R_{12}$ instead gives

$$
\sum\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)})\gamma(b_{(1)},c_{(1)})\gamma(a_{(1)},c_{(2)}).
$$

They are equal by the [Yang–Baxter operator](../../../category-theory.md#yang-baxter-operator) equation. **This is the required scalar identity:**

$$
\boxed{\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})\gamma(a_{(2)},c_{(1)})\gamma(b_{(2)},c_{(2)})=\sum\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)})\gamma(b_{(1)},c_{(1)})\gamma(a_{(1)},c_{(2)}).}
$$

Indeed, after $\Delta^{\otimes3}$ the ordered factors are $a_{(1)},a_{(2)},b_{(1)},b_{(2)},c_{(1)},c_{(2)}$. The two ambient symmetries on the left produce the three pairings $(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})$, $(a_{(2)},c_{(1)})$, $(b_{(2)},c_{(2)})$. On the right, the central symmetry produces $a_{(1)},a_{(2)},b_{(2)},b_{(1)},c_{(1)},c_{(2)}$, yielding exactly the other three pairings. Hence the calculation identifies the actual morphisms requested, also when the category is not a category of vector spaces.

For the given [bimonoids](../../../category-theory.md#bimonoid), use right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule). The [corestriction functor for comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#corestriction-functor-for-comodules) associated to a [comonoid morphism](../../../category-theory.md#comonoid-morphism) $f:H\to K$ keeps underlying objects and [morphisms](../../../algebra.md#morphism), and replaces a [coaction](../../../linear-algebra.md#coaction) $\rho_M$ by $(1\otimes f)\rho_M$. The comonoid-morphism axioms ensure that this is a $K$-coaction.

The tensor [coaction](../../../linear-algebra.md#coaction) for two $H$-[comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) in the ambient [braided monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#braided-monoidal-category) is

$$
\rho_{M\otimes N}=(1_M\otimes1_N\otimes m_H)(1_M\otimes c_{H,N}\otimes1_H)(\rho_M\otimes\rho_N),
$$

and the unit [coaction](../../../linear-algebra.md#coaction) is $j_H:I\to H$. If $f$ is also a [monoid morphism](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-morphism), then $fm_H=m_K(f\otimes f)$ and $fj_H=j_K$. Substituting these identities, and using [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of the ambient [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding), shows that corestriction preserves both tensor and unit coactions exactly. Its structural maps are identities, so it is a [strict monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-functor).

Conversely, suppose this induced [functor](../../../category.md#functor) is strict monoidal. Apply equality of the tensor coactions to the two regular right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) $(H,\Delta_H)$. Then apply $\varepsilon_H\otimes\varepsilon_H$ to their two underlying $H$ factors. The counit laws and [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) of the [braiding](../../../category-theory.md#braiding) remove those factors and leave

$$
fm_H=m_K(f\otimes f).
$$

Equality on the unit [comodule](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) similarly gives $fj_H=j_K$. Thus $f$ is a [monoid morphism](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-morphism). **The criterion is exactly**

$$
\boxed{f_*\text{ is strict monoidal}\iff f\text{ preserves multiplication and unit}.}
$$

The regular-comodule argument uses only the counit laws; it requires no elementwise or finite-dimensional assumption on the ambient category.

A useful [comodule natural transformation formula](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule-natural-transformation-formula) determines $\beta$ from a single [linear functional](../../../linear-algebra.md#linear-functional). Define

$$
\alpha=\varepsilon_H\beta_H:H\to k,
$$

using the regular right [comodule](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) $H$. For any [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) $V$, the cofree right [comodule](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) $V\otimes H$ has coaction $1_V\otimes\Delta$. [Naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) with respect to all maps $h\mapsto v\otimes h$ gives

$$
\beta_{V\otimes H}=1_V\otimes\beta_H.
$$

The coaction $\rho_M:M\to M\otimes H$ is itself a morphism of right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule). Its [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) equation, followed by $1_M\otimes\varepsilon_H$, therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\beta_M(u)=\sum u_{(0)}\alpha(u_{(1)}).}
$$

This derivation works for all [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule), not merely finite-dimensional ones.

The monoidal equation $\beta_{M\otimes N}=\beta_M\otimes\beta_N$, evaluated on the two regular [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) and followed by their [counits](../../../category-theory.md#counit), implies

$$
\alpha(ab)=\alpha(a)\alpha(b).
$$

The unit equation gives $\alpha(1)=1$. Thus $\alpha$ is a unital [algebra homomorphism over a field](../../../algebra.md#algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field) $H\to k$. In addition, the $K$-colinearity of $\beta_H$ gives the useful intertwining relation

$$
\sum g(h_{(1)})\alpha(h_{(2)})=\sum\alpha(h_{(1)})f(h_{(2)}).
$$

This fixes the orientation of $\alpha$ relative to $f,g$.

For the [convolution product for coalgebra maps](../../../linear-algebra.md#convolution-product-for-coalgebra-maps), define $\alpha^{-1}=\alpha S$. Multiplicativity of $\alpha$ and the two [antipode](../../../algebra.md#antipode) identities show

$$
\sum\alpha(h_{(1)})\alpha(S(h_{(2)}))=\varepsilon(h),\qquad\sum\alpha(S(h_{(1)}))\alpha(h_{(2)})=\varepsilon(h).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\beta_M^{-1}(u)=\sum u_{(0)}\alpha(S(u_{(1)})).}
$$

Coassociativity and the two convolution identities verify both composites directly. Since $\beta_M$ was a $K$-comodule morphism, its linear inverse is also a $K$-comodule morphism. Inverting the [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) and monoidal equations shows that the inverses form a monoidal [natural transformation](../../../category.md#natural-transformation) $g_*\Rightarrow f_*$. **Every such monoidal transformation is therefore invertible**, without requiring a bijective antipode.

The induced [internal hom for modules over a Hopf algebra](../../../algebra.md#internal-hom-for-modules-over-a-hopf-algebra) on $\operatorname{Hom}_k(H,H)$ is

$$
(h\cdot T)(x)=\sum h_{(1)}T(S(h_{(2)})x),
$$

where both occurrences of $H$ have their left regular action. If $T$ is a morphism of left $H$-[modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), then

$$
(h\cdot T)(x)=\sum h_{(1)}S(h_{(2)})T(x)=\varepsilon(h)T(x).
$$

So every such morphism is an [invariant vector of a module over a Hopf algebra](../../../algebra.md#invariant-vector-of-a-module-over-a-hopf-algebra).

For the converse, a calculation valid for every $k$-linear $T$ is

$$
\sum(h_{(1)}\cdot T)(h_{(2)}x)=\sum h_{(1)}T(S(h_{(2)})h_{(3)}x)=hT(x).
$$

If $T$ is invariant, its left-hand side is instead $\sum\varepsilon(h_{(1)})T(h_{(2)}x)=T(hx)$. Hence $T(hx)=hT(x)$ for all $h,x$, which is precisely the [R-module homomorphism](../../../module-theory.md#module-homomorphism) condition.

In fact an endomorphism of the left regular [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is determined by $a=T(1)$ and has $T(x)=xa$. Conversely each right multiplication map is left $H$-linear. **Thus the invariant space has the explicit description**

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Hom}_k(H,H)^H=\operatorname{End}_H(H)=\{R_a:x\mapsto xa\mid a\in H\}.}
$$

As a [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) it is isomorphic to $H$, and under composition its algebra is the [opposite algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#opposite-algebra) $H^{\mathrm{op}}$. No dimension or semisimplicity hypothesis is needed.

Because the direction of a [lax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor) compares the target tensor with the image of the source tensor, the given maps $\varphi_{X,Y}:X\bullet Y\to X\diamond Y$ have the correct direction. They need not be invertible unless a [strong monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strong-monoidal-functor) is intended.

Their associativity condition is the following [commutative diagram](../../../homology.md#commutative-diagram), with the [associators](../../../category-theory.md#associator) of the two structures labelled explicitly:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
(X\bullet Y)\bullet Z&\xrightarrow{\varphi_{X,Y}\bullet1}&(X\diamond Y)\bullet Z&\xrightarrow{\varphi_{X\diamond Y,Z}}&(X\diamond Y)\diamond Z\\
\alpha^\bullet\downarrow&&&&\downarrow\alpha^\diamond\\
X\bullet(Y\bullet Z)&\xrightarrow{1\bullet\varphi_{Y,Z}}&X\bullet(Y\diamond Z)&\xrightarrow{\varphi_{X,Y\diamond Z}}&X\diamond(Y\diamond Z).
\end{array}
$$

The two unit conditions, since $\varphi_0=1_I$, are the unit [commutative diagrams](../../../homology.md#commutative-diagram)

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
I\bullet X&\xrightarrow{\varphi_{I,X}}&I\diamond X\\
\lambda^\bullet_X\searrow&&\swarrow\lambda^\diamond_X\\
&X&
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{ccc}
X\bullet I&\xrightarrow{\varphi_{X,I}}&X\diamond I\\
\rho^\bullet_X\searrow&&\swarrow\rho^\diamond_X\\
&X&.
\end{array}
$$

Together with the assumed [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality), these are exactly the axioms for the requested monoidal structure. **There are no further coherence conditions.**

The faithful [strict monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#strict-monoidal-functor) $U$ allows all three diagrams to be checked after applying it. Both tensor structures then have the same underlying tensor and constraints in $\mathcal V$. In particular, the unit conditions are equivalent to

$$
\boxed{U\varphi_{I,X}=1_{UI\otimes UX},\qquad U\varphi_{X,I}=1_{UX\otimes UI}.}
$$

The associativity square becomes the displayed diagram with every tensor replaced by $\otimes$, both associators by $\alpha^{\mathcal V}$, and every $\varphi$ by $U\varphi$. Faithfulness reflects equality of the two resulting composites. One should not identify $\varphi_{I,X}$ itself with an identity in $\mathcal C$: its domain and codomain can be distinct objects with the same image under $U$. Intrinsically it is $(\lambda^\diamond_X)^{-1}\lambda^\bullet_X$, and similarly on the right.

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/b)

Use right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) with [coaction](../../../linear-algebra.md#coaction) $u\mapsto\sum u_{(0)}\otimes u_{(1)}$. Fix the convention that $\diamond$ is induced by $m$ and $\bullet$ by $n$. We classify lax monoidal structures first; the strong case is identified at the end.

Every [natural transformation](../../../category.md#natural-transformation) of the indicated tensor bifunctors is determined by a [linear functional](../../../linear-algebra.md#linear-functional) $\gamma:H\otimes H\to k$, and its components have the [comodule tensor transformation formula](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule-tensor-transformation-formula)

$$
\boxed{\varphi_{M,N}(u\otimes v)=\sum u_{(0)}\otimes v_{(0)}\gamma(u_{(1)},v_{(1)}).}
$$

To prove the assertion, set $\gamma=(\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon)\varphi_{H,H}$. On the cofree [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) $V\otimes H,W\otimes H$, [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) with respect to $h\mapsto v\otimes h$ forces the component to act as $\varphi_{H,H}$ on the two $H$ factors. Apply [naturality](../../../category.md#naturality) to the pair of coactions $M\to M\otimes H$, $N\to N\otimes H$, then apply their [counits](../../../category-theory.md#counit). This yields the formula. Conversely the formula is plainly natural with respect to comodule morphisms, and recovers $\gamma$ from the regular [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule).

It remains to impose exactly three kinds of equations. First, the component must be $H$-colinear from the tensor with product $n$ to the tensor with product $m$. Expanding its two coactions gives **(C)**, the [multiplication compatibility for a comodule tensor transformation](../../../linear-algebra.md#multiplication-compatibility-for-a-comodule-tensor-transformation)

$$
\boxed{\sum m(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)})=\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})n(a_{(2)},b_{(2)}).}
$$

Necessity follows by taking $M=N=H$ and applying the two [counits](../../../category-theory.md#counit); sufficiency follows by substituting the identity into the coaction formula for arbitrary $M,N$.

Second, the two unit triangles in the preceding part are exactly **(U)**:

$$
\boxed{\gamma(1,a)=\varepsilon(a),\qquad\gamma(a,1)=\varepsilon(a).}
$$

Here $1=j(1_k)$ is the common algebra unit. These identities are forced by the regular [comodule](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule) and sufficient by the counit law.

Third, expand the associativity diagram on three right [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule). The two scalar factors, after their [counits](../../../category-theory.md#counit) are removed, are **(A)**:

$$
\boxed{\sum\gamma(m(a_{(1)},b_{(1)}),c)\gamma(a_{(2)},b_{(2)})=\sum\gamma(a,m(b_{(1)},c_{(1)}))\gamma(b_{(2)},c_{(2)}).}
$$

The product in this expression is $m$, because the intermediate arguments $M\diamond N$ and $N\diamond P$ are source-tensor objects. Necessity follows from the three regular [comodules](../../../linear-algebra.md#comodule); sufficiency is the same substitution for arbitrary coactions. Using (C), the same condition can equivalently be written with the target product $n$:

$$
\sum\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})\gamma(n(a_{(2)},b_{(2)}),c)=\sum\gamma(b_{(1)},c_{(1)})\gamma(a,n(b_{(2)},c_{(2)})).
$$

This is the normalized [bialgebra scalar cocycle](../../../commutative-algebra.md#bialgebra-scalar-cocycle) equation for that convention.

**Thus the required structures correspond bijectively to all linear maps $\gamma$ satisfying (C), (U) and (A).** No convolution invertibility has been imposed for a [lax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor) structure. If “monoidal” is required to mean strong, add precisely that $\gamma$ is invertible under the [convolution product for coalgebra maps](../../../linear-algebra.md#convolution-product-for-coalgebra-maps) on $\operatorname{Hom}_k(H\otimes H,k)$. Its convolution inverse $\bar\gamma$ supplies the inverse component by the same displayed formula; conversely natural inverse components recover a convolution inverse by the regular-comodule argument. In that case (C) is the explicit [bialgebra cocycle twist](../../../commutative-algebra.md#bialgebra-cocycle-twist) relation

$$
\boxed{n(a,b)=\sum\bar\gamma(a_{(1)},b_{(1)})m(a_{(2)},b_{(2)})\gamma(a_{(3)},b_{(3)}).}
$$

This also records which multiplication lies on each side of the twist. Interchanging the names of $m,n$ interchanges the two tensor conventions, rather than silently changing the equation.

For a concrete lax example, take the two-dimensional [bialgebra](../../../commutative-algebra.md#bialgebra) with basis $1,e$, product $e^2=e$, and $\Delta(e)=e\otimes e$, $\varepsilon(e)=1$. Set $m=n$ and $\gamma(1,1)=\gamma(1,e)=\gamma(e,1)=1$, $\gamma(e,e)=0$. Normalization and (C) hold, and (A) follows by checking the eight basis triples: triples involving $1$ reduce to normalization, and the triple $(e,e,e)$ has both sides zero. But the $(e,e)$ tensor component is zero, so this [lax monoidal functor](../../../category-theory.md#monoidal-functor) is not strong.
