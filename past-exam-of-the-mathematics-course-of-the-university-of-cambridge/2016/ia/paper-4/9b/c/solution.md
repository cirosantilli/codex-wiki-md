<h1 id="9b/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For fixed finite velocities, the [nonrelativistic limit](../../../../../../nonrelativistic-limit.md) $c\to\infty$ gives $\gamma^2=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1}\to1$. Hence the [relativistic rocket equation](../../../../../../relativistic-rocket-equation.md) reduces to

$$
\boxed{mv'+um'=0},
$$

which is the nonrelativistic [rocket equation](../../../../../../rocket-equation.md).

The conserved quantities called energy in the two calculations include different contributions. The nonrelativistic calculation considered only [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md), which increases as fuel releases [internal energy](../../../../../../internal-energy.md); total energy including that internal reservoir is still conserved. Relativistic energy includes [rest energy](../../../../../../rest-energy.md), and therefore includes the fuel's stored internal energy through the rocket's [rest mass](../../../../../../invariant-mass.md). That rest mass decreases as energy is transferred into the exhaust. Thus [conservation of relativistic energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-relativistic-energy.md) and the nonconservation of kinetic energy express the same physical conversion.
