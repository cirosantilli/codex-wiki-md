<h1 id="5e/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$ and enumerate $B=\{b_1,b_2,\ldots\}$. Each [injective function](../../../../../../../injective-function.md) $f:A\to B$ is determined by the finite tuple $(f(a_1),\ldots,f(a_m))$. Thus these functions form a subset of the finite [Cartesian product](../../../../../../../cartesian-product.md) $B^m$. A [finite Cartesian power of a countable set](../../../../../../../finite-cartesian-power-of-a-countable-set.md) is countable: for example, group the index tuples by the maximum of their coordinates, obtaining a [countable union of countable sets](../../../../../../../countable-union-of-countable-sets.md) consisting of finite groups.

There are also infinitely many such [injective functions](../../../../../../../injective-function.md), since

$$
f_j(a_i)=b_{j+i}\qquad(j\geq0,\ 1\leq i\leq m)
$$

defines pairwise distinct examples. Therefore **$S_1$ is a [countably infinite set](../../../../../../../countably-infinite-set.md)**.
