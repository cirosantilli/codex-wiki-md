<h1 id="5e/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Again let $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$ with $m>1$, and enumerate $B=\{b_1,b_2,\ldots\}$. For each infinite [binary sequence](../../../../../../../bitstream.md) $x=(x_j)_{j\geq1}$, define a function $g_x$ by

$$
g_x(b_i)=a_i\quad(1\leq i\leq m),\qquad
g_x(b_{m+j})=
\begin{cases}
a_1,&x_j=0,\\
a_2,&x_j=1.
\end{cases}
$$

The first $m$ values make every $g_x$ a [surjective function](../../../../../../../surjective-function.md), while the remaining values recover $x$ uniquely. Hence $x\mapsto g_x$ is an [injective function](../../../../../../../injective-function.md) from the uncountable set of [binary sequences](../../../../../../../bitstream.md) into $S_2$. This proves **$S_2$ is an [uncountable set](../../../../../../../uncountable-set.md)**, an instance of [uncountability of surjections onto a nontrivial finite set](../../../../../../../uncountability-of-surjections-onto-a-nontrivial-finite-set.md).
