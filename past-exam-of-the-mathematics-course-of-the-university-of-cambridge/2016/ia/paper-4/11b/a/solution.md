<h1 id="11b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the Earth [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md), the return journey takes $2d/v$. Bob is at rest in this frame, while Alice's [proper time](../../../../../../proper-time.md) is reduced by the [Lorentz factor](../../../../../../lorentz-factor.md) $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. Neglecting the short turnaround interval, **their increases in age** are

$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau_B=\frac{2d}{v},\qquad
\Delta\tau_A=\frac{2d}{v}\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}.
$$

The [twin paradox](../../../../../../twin-paradox.md) is the apparent contradiction from applying reciprocal [time dilation](../../../../../../time-dilation.md) to claim that each twin must be younger than the other. Alice changes inertial frames at the turnaround, so a single comoving [inertial frame](../../../../../../inertial-frame.md) cannot describe her whole journey; Bob uses one inertial frame throughout. The twins follow different [world lines](../../../../../../world-line.md), and integrating [proper time](../../../../../../proper-time.md) along those world lines gives the displayed, consistent ages. The change in Alice's notion of simultaneity at turnaround removes the apparent contradiction. Acceleration distinguishes the paths; its magnitude is not an additional local rule for aging.
