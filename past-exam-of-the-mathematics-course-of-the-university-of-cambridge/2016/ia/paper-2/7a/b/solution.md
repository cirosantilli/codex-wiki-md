<h1 id="7a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For positive integral $m$, the exponent-zero [linear recurrence relation](../../../../../../linear-recurrence-relation.md) fixes $a_n=(-1)^na_0/n!$ for $1\leq n<m$, but at $n=m$ its factor $n-m$ vanishes and leaves $a_m$ free. Choose $a_0=1$ and $a_m=0$. All later coefficients then vanish by the same [linear recurrence relation](../../../../../../linear-recurrence-relation.md).

**A polynomial solution of degree $m-1$ is**

$$
\boxed{P_{m-1}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-1)^nx^n}{n!}.}
$$

For $m=1$ this is the constant solution $1$, in agreement with the [ordinary point](../../../../../../ordinary-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md) exception. The [terminating Frobenius series](../../../../../../terminating-frobenius-series.md) arises because the coefficient equation at the resonant index becomes $0=0$, permitting truncation rather than forcing a logarithm.
