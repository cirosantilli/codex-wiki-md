<h1 id="6a/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the stated order of the two solutions, define their [Wronskian](../../../../../../../wronskian.md) by

$$
W=y_1y_2'-y_1'y_2=\det\begin{pmatrix}y_1&y_2\\y_1'&y_2'\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Taking a [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) cancels the two mixed products: $W'=y_1y_2''-y_1''y_2$. Substituting the [second-order linear differential equation](../../../../../../../second-order-linear-differential-equation.md) for each second [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) cancels the $q$ terms and leaves

$$
\boxed{W'+p(x)W=0,\qquad W(x)=W(x_0)\exp\left(-\int_{x_0}^xp(s)\,ds\right).}
$$

This is the [Abel identity](../../../../../../../abel-s-identity.md). On an interval with continuous coefficients, [linear independence](../../../../../../../linear-independence.md) means $W(x_0)\ne0$; hence the [Wronskian](../../../../../../../wronskian.md) never vanishes anywhere on that interval.
