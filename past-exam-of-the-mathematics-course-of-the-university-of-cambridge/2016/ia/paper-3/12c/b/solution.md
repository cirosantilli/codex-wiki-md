<h1 id="12c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Symmetry of the derivative matrix means $\partial_iF_j=\partial_jF_i$. Contracting this symmetric pair with the antisymmetric [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md) gives

$$
(\nabla\times\mathbf F)_k=\epsilon_{kij}\partial_iF_j=0.
$$

Every circle bounds its planar disk, and the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) is smooth on all of $\mathbb R^3$, including that disk. [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) therefore gives **for either orientation**

$$
\boxed{\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf x=\int_D(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=0.}
$$

The global domain is essential to this argument; the same local symmetry condition on a domain with a hole need not make every closed-curve [line integral](../../../../../../line-integral.md) vanish.
