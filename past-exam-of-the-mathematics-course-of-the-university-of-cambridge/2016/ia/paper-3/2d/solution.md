<h1 id="2d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2D](../2d.md)

[Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that, for a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) $G$ and a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$,

$$
\boxed{|G|=[G:H]|H|.}
$$

To prove it, define an equivalence relation by $x\sim y$ when $x^{-1}y\in H$. Its classes are the left [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) $xH$, which therefore partition $G$. Left multiplication by $x$ gives a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $H\to xH$, so every [coset](../../../../../coset.md) has $|H|$ elements. Counting this partition gives the formula; in particular $|H|$ divides $|G|$. Applying the theorem to the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by an element shows that its [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) divides $|G|$.

Write the given order-two [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) as $N=\{1,z\}$. Conjugation must preserve $N$ and cannot send its nonidentity element to the identity, so $gzg^{-1}=z$ for all $g\in G$. Thus $z$ is in the [center of a group](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md): a [normal subgroup of order two is central](../../../../../normal-subgroup-of-order-two-is-central.md).

Choose $g\notin N$. By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), its [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) is $2$, $p$ or $2p$. It cannot have order two: since $z$ is central, the four distinct elements $1,z,g,zg$ would form a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of order four, whereas $4$ does not divide $2p$ for odd $p$. If $g$ has order $2p$, it already generates $G$. Otherwise $g$ has order $p$, commutes with $z$, and $(gz)^k=1$ implies $g^k=z^{-k}\in\langle g\rangle\cap N=\{1\}$. Hence both $p$ and $2$ divide $k$, so $gz$ has order $2p$. **In either case**

$$
\boxed{G\cong C_{2p}.}
$$
