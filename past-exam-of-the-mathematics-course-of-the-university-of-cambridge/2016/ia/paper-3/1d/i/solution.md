<h1 id="1d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose every [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) belongs to $H$. For $h\in H$ and $g\in G$, the particular [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) $h^{-1}g^{-1}hg$ belongs to $H$. Multiplying on the left by $h$ gives $g^{-1}hg\in H$. Thus $g^{-1}Hg\subseteq H$ for every $g$; applying the same inclusion to $g^{-1}$ gives the reverse inclusion. **Therefore $H$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).**

We can now form the [quotient group](../../../../../../quotient-group.md). Its [group commutators](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) are

$$
(aH)^{-1}(bH)^{-1}(aH)(bH)=(a^{-1}b^{-1}ab)H=H.
$$

A [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) is the [identity element](../../../../../../identity-element.md) exactly when the two elements commute. Hence **$G/H$ is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md)**. Equivalently, the hypothesis says that $H$ contains the [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md), so the quotient factors through the [abelianization](../../../../../../abelianization.md).
