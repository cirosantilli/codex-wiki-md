Conversely, assume $H$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) and the [quotient group](../../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G/H$ is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Commuting the two [cosets](../../../../../../coset.md) $aH$ and $bH$ gives

$$
(a^{-1}b^{-1}ab)H=H,
$$

so every [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ belongs to $H$. This proves the converse and completes the equivalence.

For the [dihedral group](../../../../../../dihedral-group.md) of order ten, choose a rotation $r$ and a reflection $s$ with $r^5=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. Its rotation [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle r\rangle$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), and the quotient by it has order two, hence is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md). Consequently the [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) is contained in $\langle r\rangle$. On the other hand,

$$
[r,s]=r^{-1}s^{-1}rs=r^{-2}.
$$

Since $r^{-2}$ generates the order-five [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) $\langle r\rangle$, the [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) is exactly $\langle r\rangle$. Every [abelian quotient](../../../../../../abelian-quotient.md) must kill this [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). There are only two [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) containing it, since the remaining quotient has prime order. **The complete list, including the trivial quotient, is**

$$
\boxed{D_{10}/\langle r\rangle\cong C_2,\qquad D_{10}/D_{10}\cong\{1\}.}
$$

This is the order-ten instance of the [abelianization of an odd dihedral group](../../../../../../abelianization-of-an-odd-dihedral-group.md).
