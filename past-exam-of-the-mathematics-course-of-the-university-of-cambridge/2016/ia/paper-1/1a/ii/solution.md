<h1 id="1a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write the [complex number](../../../../../../complex-number.md) as $z=x+iy$. Since $iz=-y+ix$, the [modulus of the complex exponential](../../../../../../modulus-of-the-complex-exponential.md) gives

$$
|e^{iz}|=e^{-y}.
$$

This equals one precisely when $y=0$. The [quadratic formula](../../../../../../quadratic-formula.md) shows that the [imaginary part](../../../../../../imaginary-part.md) of either root is $\pm\sqrt{4-b^2}/2$. Consequently the roots are real exactly at the two endpoints:

$$
\boxed{b=-2\ \text{or}\ b=2.}
$$

**Both endpoints satisfy the condition.** The repeated roots are respectively $1$ and $-1$; their multiples by $i$ have exponentials on the [unit circle](../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md).
