<h1 id="1a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Using the exponential definition of the [hyperbolic cosine](../../../../../../hyperbolic-cosine.md) and the [complex exponential function](../../../../../../complex-exponential-function.md),

$$
\cosh(x+iy)=\cosh x\cos y+i\sinh x\sin y.
$$

Thus

$$
\operatorname{Im}(\cosh z)=\sinh x\,\sin y,
\qquad x=-\frac b2,\qquad y=\pm\frac{\sqrt{4-b^2}}2.
$$

For real $x$, the [hyperbolic sine](../../../../../../hyperbolic-sine.md) vanishes only at $x=0$. Also $|y|\le1<\pi$, so the only zero of [sine](../../../../../../sine.md) available to $y$ is $y=0$. The product therefore vanishes exactly when $b=0$ or $4-b^2=0$:

$$
\boxed{b\in\{-2,0,2\}.}
$$

**There are three possible parameter values.** At $b=0$ the roots are $\pm i$ and $\cosh(\pm i)=\cos1$ is real; at $b=\pm2$ the roots themselves are real.
