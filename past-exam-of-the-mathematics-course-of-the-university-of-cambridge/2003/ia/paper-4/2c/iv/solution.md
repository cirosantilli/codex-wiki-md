<h1 id="2c/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**This is not an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).** With the usual convention that zero is a [perfect square](../../../../../../square-number.md), $x^2-x^2=0$ gives [reflexivity](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md), and changing the sign of $x^2-y^2$ shows that the relation is [symmetric](../../../../../../symmetric-relation.md). However $3\sim5$ because $3^2-5^2=-16=-4^2$, and $5\sim13$ because $5^2-13^2=-144=-12^2$, whereas $3^2-13^2=-160$ is neither a positive nor a negative [perfect square](../../../../../../square-number.md): $12^2<160<13^2$. Thus [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) fails. If “[perfect square](../../../../../../square-number.md)” were restricted to positive squares, reflexivity would fail as well, leaving the same negative classification.
