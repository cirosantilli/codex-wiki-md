↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**This is not an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).** For $x=1/2$, $x\overline x=1/4\notin\mathbb Z$, so [reflexivity](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) fails. It is [symmetric](../../../../../../symmetric-relation.md), because $y\overline x$ is the conjugate of $x\overline y$, and an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) is real. It also fails [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md): take $x=z=1/2$ and $y=2$. Then $x\overline y=y\overline z=1$ are [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) but $x\overline z=1/4$ is not. A single failure would suffice, but these examples identify exactly which properties break.
