<h1 id="1d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $|y|>1$, the separated integral is

$$
\frac12\log\left|\frac{1+y}{1-y}\right|=x+C.
$$

Inverting gives

$$
\boxed{y(x)=\coth(x+C).}
$$

The upper branch has $x+C>0$, where $y>1$ decreases towards $1$ as $x\to\infty$. The lower branch has $x+C<0$, where $y<-1$ decreases to $-\infty$ as $x\uparrow-C$. These are separate maximal intervals: the pole is not part of a solution. For the domain $x\ge0$, a lower-branch initial value below $-1$ necessarily reaches that pole in finite positive $x$. The [direction field](../../../../../../direction-field.md) sketch above displays both exterior branches and a central [hyperbolic tangent](../../../../../../hyperbolic-tangent.md) branch.
