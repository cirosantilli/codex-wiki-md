<h1 id="8b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) has only the [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\mu$. If $A$ is [diagonalizable](../../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md), there is an invertible [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $P$ and a diagonal [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $D$ with $A=PDP^{-1}$. Every diagonal entry of $D$ is an [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), hence equals $\mu$. Thus $D=\mu I$ and $A=P(\mu I)P^{-1}=\mu I$. Conversely, $\mu I$ is already diagonal in every [basis](../../../../../../basis.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is diagonalizable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A=\mu I}.
$$

This argument uses the existence of an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) rather than merely the repeated root of the polynomial; repeated [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) alone do not imply diagonalizability.
