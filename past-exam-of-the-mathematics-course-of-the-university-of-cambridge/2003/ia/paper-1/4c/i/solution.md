<h1 id="4c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The absolute ratio of successive terms is $|z|\,n^2/(n+1)^2\to|z|$. The [ratio test](../../../../../../ratio-test.md) gives convergence for $|z|<1$ and divergence for $|z|>1$, so the [radius of convergence](../../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is $\boxed{R=1}$. On the boundary, $|n^{-2}z^n|=n^{-2}$ and

$$
\sum_{n=2}^N\frac1{n^2}\le\sum_{n=2}^N\frac1{n(n-1)}=1-\frac1N.
$$

The positive partial sums are bounded, and thus converge. By the [comparison test for series](../../../../../../comparison-test-for-series.md), the original [power series](../../../../../../power-series.md) **converges absolutely at every point of $|z|=1$**.
