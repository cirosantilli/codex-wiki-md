<h1 id="10f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Compare with the divergent [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) from part (i). The ratio of its new summand to $1/(n\log n)$ is

$$
\frac{1/[n^{1+1/n}\log n]}{1/(n\log n)}=n^{-1/n}=e^{-(\log n)/n}\longrightarrow1.
$$

For completeness, $\log n=\int_1^n x^{-1}\,dx\le\int_1^n x^{-1/2}\,dx=2(\sqrt n-1)$, so $(\log n)/n\to0$. The [limit comparison test](../../../../../../limit-comparison-test.md) now gives **divergence**. Although the exponent $1+1/n$ exceeds one for each summand, it approaches one and does not produce a convergent [p-series](../../../../../../p-series.md) with a fixed exponent greater than one.
