<h1 id="5b/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) identity $A(I-A^T)=A-I$. The product rule for [determinants](../../../../../../../determinant.md), $\det A=1$, and invariance of determinant under [matrix transpose](../../../../../../../transpose.md) give

$$
\det(A-I)=\det A\det(I-A^T)=\det(I-A)=-\det(A-I),
$$

where the last sign comes from dimension three. Hence $\det(A-I)=0$. The real [linear map](../../../../../../../linear-map.md) $A-I$ has a nonzero real vector in its [kernel](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), so **$A$ has an eigenvector with eigenvalue one**. This is the [odd-dimensional special orthogonal transformation has a fixed vector](../../../../../../../odd-dimensional-special-orthogonal-transformation-has-a-fixed-vector.md) property, here proved directly without first classifying the other eigenvalues.
