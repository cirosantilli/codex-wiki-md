↑ **Parent:** [Paper 20](paper-20.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

Work in the [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $A$, writing $a\to x$ for [Heyting implication](../../../mathematical-logic.md#heyting-implication). A [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) is an inflationary, [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) map preserving finite meets. The [open nucleus](../../../category-theory.md#open-nucleus) and [closed nucleus](../../../category-theory.md#closed-nucleus) are

$$
o(a)(x)=a\to x,\qquad c(a)(x)=a\vee x.
$$

Their [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) in the [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) of [nuclei on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) is pointwise. The [distributivity of a Heyting algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#distributivity-of-a-heyting-algebra) and the defining adjunction for [Heyting implication](../../../mathematical-logic.md#heyting-implication) give

$$
(a\to x)\wedge(a\vee x)=x.
$$

If a [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) $l$ bounds both, then $l(a)=1$ and $a\leq l(0)$. Consequently $1=l(a)\leq l(l(0))=l(0)$, so $l$ is the top nucleus. Thus **$o(a)$ and $c(a)$ are complements in $N(A)$**.

For any [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) $j$, put $t_a=o(a)\wedge c(j(a))$. At an arbitrary $x$,

$$
t_a(x)=x\vee\bigl(j(a)\wedge(a\to x)\bigr)\leq j(x),
$$

since $j(a)\wedge(a\to x)\leq j(a)\wedge j(a\to x)=j(a\wedge(a\to x))\leq j(x)$. Moreover $t_a(a)=j(a)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{j=\bigvee_{a\in A}\bigl(o(a)\wedge c(j(a))\bigr).}
$$

Indeed the [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) is at most $j$, and its value at each $a$ is at least $j(a)$. This proves generation by the [open nuclei](../../../category-theory.md#open-nucleus) and [closed nuclei](../../../category-theory.md#closed-nucleus), without assuming that joins of [nuclei on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) are computed pointwise.

For the given [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) $h$, restrict the displayed [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) to $a=h(b)$ and denote the resulting [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) by $r(j)$. The same inequalities show $r(j)\leq j$ and $r(j)(h(b))=j(h(b))$. If $k$ has the same values on $h(B)$, its restricted formula is identical, so $r(j)\leq k$. Hence **$r(j)$ is the least representative of the [equivalence class](../../../set-theory.md#equivalence-class)**. We will also use the consequence

$$
\bigl(\forall b\in B,\ j(hb)\leq k(hb)\bigr)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r(j)\leq r(k).
$$

The least representatives form a [subframe](../../../category-theory.md#subframe). For a family $j_i=r(j_i)$, let $J=\bigvee_i j_i$ in $N(A)$. Restricted monotonicity gives $j_i\leq r(J)$ for every $i$, hence $J=r(J)$. For two least representatives, distribution in $N(A)$ expresses their [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) as the [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set), over $b,d\in B$, of

$$
o(h(b\vee d))\wedge c\bigl(j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\bigr).
$$

Here $o(hb)\wedge o(hd)=o(h(b\vee d))$ and $c(u)\wedge c(v)=c(u\wedge v)$. Since

$$
j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\leq (j\wedge k)(h(b\vee d)),
$$

each summand is below $r(j\wedge k)$. The opposite inequality always holds, proving closure under binary meets. The top is $c(1)$ and is a least representative. Also $r(c(a))=c(a)$, because the summand at $b=0$ is $c(a)$; and $r(o(hd))=o(hd)$, because the summand at $b=d$ is $o(hd)$. It follows from the restricted normal form that **$N_B(A)$ is exactly the [subframe](../../../category-theory.md#subframe) generated by $c(A)$ and $o(h(B))$**.

The map $c:A\to N_B(A)$ preserves finite meets by the formula above. A [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) $l$ bounds every $c(a_i)$ exactly when $a_i\leq l(0)$ for every $i$, equivalently $\bigvee_i a_i\leq l(0)$. The least such nucleus is $c(\bigvee_i a_i)$, so $c$ preserves arbitrary joins too, including $0$. Evaluation at $0$ recovers $a$, so this [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) is injective.

For the [pushout](../../../category.md#pushout), we prove the [universal property](../../../category-theory.md#universal-property) explicitly. Let $u:A\to C$ be a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) for which every $u(hb)$ has a [complement](../../../set.md#complement-of-a-set) $d_b$ in $C$. Define, for $j\in N_B(A)$,

$$
F(j)=\bigvee_{b\in B}d_b\wedge u(j(hb)).
$$

To prove preservation of arbitrary joins, let $u_*$ be the [right adjoint](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors) of $u$ and set $k_z(a)=u_*(z\vee u(a))$ for $z\in C$. This is a [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame): inflationarity follows from the adjunction, finite-meet preservation follows from distribution and [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) preservation by $u_*$, and $u u_*\leq1$ gives $k_z k_z\leq k_z$; inflationarity gives the reverse inequality. Complementation and the adjunction now give

$$
F(j)\leq z\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall b,\ u(j(hb))\leq z\vee u(hb)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall b,\ j(hb)\leq k_z(hb)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ j\leq r(k_z).
$$

The last equivalence uses leastness of $j$. Thus $F$ is a [left adjoint](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors), so preserves arbitrary joins. For binary meets, expand $F(j)\wedge F(k)$. A term indexed by $b,d$ is

$$
d_{b\vee d}\wedge u\bigl(j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\bigr)\leq d_{b\vee d}\wedge u\bigl((j\wedge k)(h(b\vee d))\bigr).
$$

This proves $F(j)\wedge F(k)\leq F(j\wedge k)$; monotonicity proves the reverse inequality. The term at $b=0$ proves $F(1)=1$. Directly,

$$
F(c(a))=u(a),\qquad F(o(hd))=d_d.
$$

For the second identity the term at $b=d$ gives $d_d$, while every term is disjoint from $u(hd)$, because $hd\wedge(hd\to hb)\leq hb$.

Apply this construction first with $A=B$, $h=1_B$, target $N_B(A)$, $u=c_Ah$ and complements $d_b=o_A(hb)$. It gives a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) $v_0:N(B)\to N_B(A)$ satisfying $v_0c_B=c_Ah$ and $v_0o_B=o_Ah$. Given any compatible pair $u:A\to C$, $v:N(B)\to C$, take $d_b=v(o_B(b))$; compatibility supplies the required complements. The constructed $F$ satisfies $Fc_A=u$ and $Fv_0=v$, since the latter equality holds on the open and closed generators of $N(B)$. Uniqueness follows because $c(A)$ and $o(h(B))$ generate $N_B(A)$. Hence **the square with edges $h,c_B,c_A,v_0$ is a [pushout](../../../category.md#pushout) in $\mathbf{Frm}$**.

A [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) has finite meets and joins of nonempty [directed sets](../../../set.md#directed-set), with finite meets distributing over these joins. A [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) homomorphism preserves both operations. Its [preframe tensor product](../../../category-theory.md#preframe-tensor-product) $P\#Q$ has generators $p\#q$ and relations making $(p,q)\mapsto p\#q$ a homomorphism separately in each variable. Equivalently,

$$
\operatorname{PFrm}(P\#Q,R)\cong\{\beta:P\times Q\to R:\beta\text{ preserves finite meets and directed joins separately}\}.
$$

In particular $1\#q=p\#1=1$. The tensor unit is the free [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) on one generator: classically it is $\mathbf2$, and constructively it is the [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $\Omega$ of truth values, with generator $0$.

Regard a [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $L$ as a commutative [monoid object](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-object) in [preframes](../../../category-theory.md#preframe), with multiplication $x*y=x\vee y$ and unit $0$. Binary [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) preserves finite meets and directed joins separately. Conversely take a commutative [monoid object](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-object) $(M,*,e)$ in [preframes](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) and put

$$
R(M)=\{x\in M:e\leq x,\ x*x=x\}.
$$

This set contains $e$ and $1$, since preservation of the empty [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) gives $1*x=1$. If $x,y\in R(M)$, then $x*y\geq x,y$, and

$$
(x\wedge y)*(x\wedge y)=(x*x)\wedge(x*y)\wedge(y*x)\wedge(y*y)=x\wedge y.
$$

Thus finite meets are inherited. If $D\subseteq R(M)$ is nonempty and directed, separate preservation of directed joins gives

$$
\left(\bigvee D\right)*\left(\bigvee D\right)=\bigvee_{d,d'\in D}d*d'=\bigvee D,
$$

because a common upper bound $t\in D$ gives $d*d'\leq t*t=t$. Directed joins are inherited too. Associativity and commutativity show that $x*y$ is [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent); it is the least [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) upper bound of $x,y$. Therefore $e$ is the bottom and $*$ is binary [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) on $R(M)$. Arbitrary joins are directed joins of finite products, including the empty product $e$. Finally

$$
(x\wedge y)*(x\wedge z)=(x*x)\wedge(x*z)\wedge(y*x)\wedge(y*z)=x\wedge(y*z),
$$

so binary distributivity, together with directed distributivity, proves that $R(M)$ is a [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra).

A homomorphism from a [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) monoid to $M$ sends every element to an [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) above $e$ and therefore factors uniquely through $R(M)$. The factor preserves finite joins and directed joins, hence arbitrary joins. Conversely the inclusion $R(M)\to M$ preserves the [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) and [monoid](../../../algebra.md#monoid) operations. Also a [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) monoid homomorphism between [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) is automatically a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism). This proves the full embedding and its [right adjoint](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors): **$\mathbf{Frm}$ is a [coreflective subcategory](../../../category.md#coreflective-subcategory) of $\mathbf{CMon}(\mathbf{PFrm})$**, with coreflector $R$.

The [coproduct](../../../category.md#coproduct) of commutative [monoid objects](../../../category-theory.md#monoid-object) in a [symmetric monoidal category](../../../category-theory.md#symmetric-monoidal-category) is their tensor: the maps are $x\mapsto x\#e_N$, $y\mapsto e_M\#y$, and a pair $f,g$ induces $x\#y\mapsto f(x)*g(y)$. For two [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $L,K$, the induced multiplication on pure generators is

$$
(a\#b)*(a'\#b')=(a\vee a')\#(b\vee b'),\qquad e=0\#0.
$$

Every pure generator is [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) and above $e$. Those properties are preserved by finite meets and directed joins, as just proved, so hold throughout the generated [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe). It is therefore already a [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra), rather than requiring a further coreflection. Consequently

$$
\boxed{L\amalg_{\mathbf{Frm}}K=L\#_{\mathbf{PFrm}}K,\qquad a\#b=i_L(a)\vee i_K(b).}
$$

Rectangles in the localic product are instead $i_L(a)\wedge i_K(b)=(a\#0)\wedge(0\#b)$.

For a [filtered colimit in a category](../../../category.md#filtered-colimit-in-a-category) of [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra), form the colimit $P$ in [preframes](../../../category-theory.md#preframe), with stage maps $i_j$. It can be presented by stage elements, all their finite-meet and directed-join relations, and the transition relations. Since tensoring is a [left adjoint](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors), it preserves colimits. Filteredness makes the diagonal diagram cofinal in the product index diagram, so the stage multiplications induce $P\#P\to P$. The units agree. Each stage element is [idempotent](../../../commutative-algebra.md#idempotent) above the common unit; generation again implies $P=R(P)$. The resulting [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) is the [filtered colimit in a category](../../../category.md#filtered-colimit-in-a-category) in $\mathbf{Frm}$; its binary [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) is induced by computing two representatives at a common later stage. Thus **binary coproducts and filtered colimits of [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) are computed in [preframes](../../../category-theory.md#preframe)**, with the indicated induced [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) operation.

We now prove compactness. For a [compact locale](../../../category-theory.md#compact-locale), its [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $L$ has a top test $q_L:L\to\Omega$, $q_L(a)=[a=1]$, preserving finite meets and directed joins. For compact $L,K$, tensor the tests and use the unit isomorphism $\Omega\#\Omega\cong\Omega$ to obtain a [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) map $q:L\#K\to\Omega$ with

$$
q(a\#b)=[a=1]\vee[b=1].
$$

It reflects top on every pure generator. The property $q(x)=1\Longrightarrow x=1$ is closed under finite meets and directed joins: for a directed [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set), preservation of the [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) says that its truth value is the existential disjunction of the individual truth values, so some term has value $1$. Since pure generators generate the [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe), $q$ reflects top everywhere. Applying $q$ to a directed cover of $1$ therefore shows that a term of that cover is $1$. This proves compactness of the binary coproduct [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra), hence of the binary product [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale).

For a filtered diagram of compact [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra), define on stage $j$

$$
q_j(a)=[\text{there is an arrow }j\to k\text{ for which }a\text{ becomes }1\text{ at stage }k].
$$

These truth-valued maps preserve finite meets: witnesses can be taken to a common later stage, using filteredness. They preserve directed joins: compactness at the witnessing later stage selects a member of the directed family. They are compatible with transitions, again by filteredness, so induce a [preframe](../../../category-theory.md#preframe) map $q:P\to\Omega$. On stage generators, $q(i_j(a))=1$ implies $i_j(a)=1$. The same generation argument proves that $q$ reflects top throughout $P$. Thus the filtered colimit [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) is compact.

Finally the arbitrary [coproduct](../../../category.md#coproduct) of the [frames](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $\mathcal O(X_i)$ is the filtered colimit of their finite coproducts, indexed by finite subsets of the index set; the empty finite coproduct is $\Omega$, itself compact. Finite coproducts are compact by the binary argument, and their filtered colimit is compact by the preceding paragraph. Opposing the categories gives

$$
\boxed{\prod_{i\in I}X_i\text{ is compact whenever every }X_i\text{ is compact}.}
$$

This proves the [localic Tychonoff theorem](../../../category-theory.md#localic-tychonoff-theorem) through [preframes](../../../category-theory.md#preframe), without choosing points or invoking the [axiom of choice](../../../set-theory.md#axiom-of-choice).

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Use the order on [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale) maps in which $f\leq g$ means $f^*(a)\leq g^*(a)$ for every open $a$. A [Hausdorff locale](../../../category-theory.md#hausdorff-locale) has a closed diagonal in its localic square. A [totally unordered locale](../../../category-theory.md#totally-unordered-locale), or $T_U$ [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale), has discretely ordered hom-sets: every comparable pair of maps with any common domain is equal.

In the localic square of $Y$, let

$$
N=\bigvee_{a\wedge b=0}a\times b.
$$

This is the largest open disjoint from the diagonal: inverse image along the diagonal sends the rectangle $a\times b$ to $a\wedge b$. Hence $N$ is the [complement](../../../set.md#complement-of-a-set) of its closure. If $f\leq g$, then

$$
(f,g)^*(a\times b)=f^*(a)\wedge g^*(b)\leq g^*(a)\wedge g^*(b)=0
$$

for every summand of $N$. Thus $(f,g)$ factors through the closure of the diagonal. For a [Hausdorff locale](../../../category-theory.md#hausdorff-locale) that closure is the diagonal itself, so $f=g$. This proof of **Hausdorff implies $T_U$** does not require classical logic.

Here is an explicit classical counterexample to the converse implication from spatial Hausdorffness. Give $\mathbb R$ the [topology](../../../topology.md) generated by the usual opens and by $\mathbb Q$. Every open has the form $U=V\cup(W\cap\mathbb Q)$, where $V,W$ are usual open sets and we may arrange $V\subseteq W$. The [topology](../../../topology.md) is finer than the usual one, so the space is [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space). Put $B=\operatorname{RO}(\mathbb R)$, the [complete Boolean algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#complete-boolean-algebra) of ordinary [regular open elements](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra), and write $\rho(V)=\operatorname{int}\overline V$. Restriction from this space to its rational and irrational subspaces, followed by Booleanization, gives [frame homomorphisms](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism)

$$
f^*(U)=\rho(V),\qquad g^*(U)=\rho(W),\qquad\mathcal O(X)\longrightarrow B.
$$

To justify these formulas, each subspace has its ordinary [subspace topology](../../../topology.md#subspace-topology) and is dense in the ordinary real line. Regular-open algebras of a space and a dense subspace are isomorphic: restrict a regular open and take its interior-closure, with inverse obtained by taking interior-closure in the ambient space. Restriction is a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism), and double negation is a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) into its fixed-element [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra). These observations also show independence of the chosen representation of $U$. Since $V\subseteq W$, we have $f^*\leq g^*$, but $f^*(\mathbb Q)=0$ and $g^*(\mathbb Q)=1$. Thus **this Hausdorff space fails $T_U$ as a [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale)**.

For the remaining arguments about [connected locales](../../../category-theory.md#connected-locale), use the classical-logic option: [positive opens of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#positive-open-of-a-locale) are exactly the nonzero opens. There is a necessary positivity qualification. The empty [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale) has no positive opens, so the printed condition on those opens holds vacuously, although no point $1\to\varnothing$ exists. The point assertions therefore require $X$ to be positive. This qualification is automatic if “totally connected” includes nonemptiness; otherwise it must be added explicitly.

Let $X$ be positive and satisfy the condition on its [positive opens of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#positive-open-of-a-locale). Put $F=\{a\in\mathcal O(X):a\ne0\}$. It contains $1$, excludes $0$, and is upward closed. If $a,b\in F$ had $a\wedge b=0$, their union would be a [positive open of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#positive-open-of-a-locale) with a decomposition into two disjoint nonzero opens, contradicting its being a [connected locale](../../../category-theory.md#connected-locale). Thus $a\wedge b\in F$. If $\bigvee_i a_i\ne0$, not all $a_i$ can be zero; classical logic supplies a nonzero summand. Hence **$F$ is a [completely prime filter](../../../category-theory.md#completely-prime-filter)**, and its characteristic [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) defines a point $p:1\to X$.

The point is dense. Indeed $p^*(a)=0$ implies $a=0$ by the definition of $F$. In nucleus language the image of $p$ has nucleus $p_*p^*$; its value at $0$ is $p_*(0)=0$, so the image is a [dense sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#dense-sublocale).

Conversely suppose an [open locale](../../../category-theory.md#open-locale) has a dense point $p$. Density means $p^*(a)=0$ only if $a=0$, and classically this implies $p^*(a)=1$ for every [positive open of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#positive-open-of-a-locale). If a positive open $u$ split as $a\vee b=u$ with $a\wedge b=0$ and both summands nonzero, then $p^*(a)=p^*(b)=1$, contradicting preservation of their [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set). Thus every [positive open of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#positive-open-of-a-locale) is a [connected locale](../../../category-theory.md#connected-locale), proving the converse under the declared logical convention.

Let $x:X\to1$ be the terminal map. For a positive [totally connected locale](../../../category-theory.md#totally-connected-locale) $X$, the point above satisfies

$$
a\leq x^*p^*(a)\quad(a\in\mathcal O(X)),
$$

because the right side is $1$ when $a\ne0$ and both sides are $0$ otherwise. Hence $1_X\leq px$. If $f:X\to Y$ and $Y$ is a [totally unordered locale](../../../category-theory.md#totally-unordered-locale), composition gives $f\leq fpx$, so $f=fpx$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f=(fp)\circ x,}
$$

which is the required constant factorization. For the empty source the factorization need not exist: the unique map $\varnothing\to\varnothing$ does not factor through $1$. Thus the positivity qualification matters for this assertion too.

Put $A=\mathcal O(X)$ and $\neg a=a\to0$. The closure of the open $a$ is the [closed sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#closed-sublocale) with [closed nucleus](../../../category-theory.md#closed-nucleus) $c(\neg a)$; its interior is the open $\neg\neg a$. The closed [sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#sublocale) complementary to $\neg a$ is also open precisely when $\neg a$ is complemented, in which case its open representative is $\neg\neg a$. Thus the closure is clopen precisely when the [regular open element](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra) $\neg\neg a$ is complemented. Every [regular open element](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra) has this form, so **(i) and (ii) are equivalent**.

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

Suppose every [regular open element](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra) is complemented. In a [Heyting algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#heyting-algebra), a complemented element is regular: if $a\vee b=1$ and $a\wedge b=0$, then $b=\neg a$ and $a=\neg b$. Finite joins of complemented elements are complemented. Hence $\neg\neg a\vee\neg\neg b$ is regular, and it is the least regular upper bound of $a\vee b$. Therefore

$$
\neg\neg(a\vee b)=\neg\neg a\vee\neg\neg b.
$$

This proves **(ii) implies (iii)**. The identification of the [regular open elements](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra) with interiors of closures was established in the preceding part.

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

Conversely assume binary-join preservation by double negation. Since $\neg(a\vee\neg a)=0$, applying the assumption gives

$$
1=\neg\neg(a\vee\neg a)=\neg\neg a\vee\neg a.
$$

Also $\neg\neg a\wedge\neg a=0$. Thus $\neg\neg a$ is complemented, and every [regular open element](../../../mathematical-logic.md#regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra) is clopen. This proves **(iii) implies (ii)**. Double negation already preserves $0$ and all finite meets, so binary-join preservation here is equivalent to preservation of all finite joins.

<h3 id="4/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#4/iv)

The [Booleanization of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#booleanization-of-a-locale) is the [sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#sublocale) with double-negation nucleus. A [flat sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#flat-sublocale) has a nucleus preserving finite joins, equivalently its fixed-element set is closed under ambient finite joins. For completeness, if a nucleus preserves finite joins then its fixed points are closed under them. Conversely, if fixed points are closed under joins, $j(a)\vee j(b)$ is fixed and bounds $a\vee b$, giving $j(a\vee b)\leq j(a)\vee j(b)$; monotonicity gives equality, and preservation of $0$ is precisely $j(0)=0$. Since $\neg\neg0=0$, condition (iv) is exactly (iii). Together the preceding implications prove **all four characterizations of an [extremally disconnected locale](../../../category-theory.md#extremally-disconnected-locale)**.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

For a [Boolean algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#boolean-algebra) $B$, write $\downarrow b$ for its principal [lattice ideal](../../../mathematical-logic.md#lattice-ideal). The [ideal frame of a Boolean algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#ideal-frame-of-a-boolean-algebra) has intersections as meets and ideals generated by unions as joins. The [pseudocomplement](../../../mathematical-logic.md#pseudocomplement) of an ideal $I$ is

$$
I^*=\{b\in B:\ b\wedge i=0\text{ for every }i\in I\}.
$$

In particular $(\downarrow b)^*=\downarrow\neg b$, so every principal ideal is complemented and regular.

If $B$ is complete, put $s=\bigvee_B I$. Then $I^*=\downarrow\neg s$ and $I^{**}=\downarrow s$. Thus all regular ideals are principal and complemented, so the corresponding [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale) is extremally disconnected.

Conversely, if the [ideal frame of a Boolean algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#ideal-frame-of-a-boolean-algebra) is extremally disconnected, each $I^{**}$ is complemented. Any complemented ideal is principal: if $J\vee K=B$ and $J\cap K=\{0\}$, the fact that their generated [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) contains $1$ supplies $b\in J,c\in K$ with $b\vee c=1$. Then $b\wedge c=0$, so $c=\neg b$; every $j\in J$ is disjoint from $c$ and therefore $j\leq b$. Thus $J=\downarrow b$. Write $I^{**}=\downarrow s$. Since $I\subseteq I^{**}$, $s$ bounds $I$. If $t$ is another upper bound, $I\subseteq\downarrow t$ implies $I^{**}\subseteq(\downarrow t)^{**}=\downarrow t$, so $s\leq t$. An arbitrary subset has the same upper bounds as the [lattice ideal](../../../mathematical-logic.md#lattice-ideal) it generates, so every subset of $B$ has a supremum. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Idl}(B)\text{ is extremally disconnected}\iff B\text{ is complete}.}
$$

Now put $A=\mathcal O(X)$ and $B=A_{\neg\neg}$. This is a [complete Boolean algebra](../../../mathematical-logic.md#complete-boolean-algebra), with arbitrary Boolean joins $\neg\neg\bigvee_A b_i$, so the preceding result makes $\gamma X$ extremally disconnected. We construct its map to $X$ using the permitted [Joyal extension lemma](../../../category-theory.md#joyal-extension-lemma).

The map $\sigma:\operatorname{Idl}(B)\to B$, $I\mapsto\bigvee_B I$, is a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism), with [right adjoint](../../../category.md#adjoint-functors) $b\mapsto\downarrow b$. Thus $k(I)=\downarrow\bigvee_B I$ is a [nucleus on a frame](../../../category-theory.md#nucleus-on-a-frame) whose fixed [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) is $B$. It preserves finite joins: both $k(I\vee J)$ and $k(I)\vee k(J)$ equal $\downarrow(\bigvee_B I\vee_B\bigvee_B J)$, and $k(0)=0$. Consequently the [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale) with [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) $B$ is a [flat sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#flat-sublocale) of $\gamma X$. It is also the [Booleanization of a locale](../../../category-theory.md#booleanization-of-a-locale) $X$, so it has its inclusion map to $X$.

The [Joyal extension lemma](../../../category-theory.md#joyal-extension-lemma) says that a map from a [flat sublocale](../../../category-theory.md#flat-sublocale) to a [compact locale](../../../category-theory.md#compact-locale) that is a [regular locale](../../../category-theory.md#regular-locale) extends uniquely to the ambient [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale). Apply it to this inclusion. Its [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) formula gives

$$
\boxed{g^*(a)=\bigvee_{u\prec a}^{\operatorname{Idl}(B)}\downarrow(\neg\neg u),\qquad g:\gamma X\to X,}
$$

where $u\prec a$ is the [well-inside relation](../../../category-theory.md#well-inside-relation) $\neg u\vee a=1$. This also proves directly that the map is surjective. Indeed $u\prec a$ implies $\neg\neg u\leq a$: [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) $\neg u\vee a=1$ with $\neg\neg u$. Every member of the ideal $g^*(a)$ is therefore at most $a$ in $A$, since a finite Boolean [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) of its generators is below $\neg\neg(u_1\vee\cdots\vee u_n)\leq a$; the last inequality follows because $u_1\vee\cdots\vee u_n\prec a$. Regularity gives

$$
a=\bigvee_{u\prec a}u=\bigvee_{u\prec a}\neg\neg u=\bigvee_A\{b\in B:b\in g^*(a)\}.
$$

Thus $a$ can be recovered from $g^*(a)$, so $g^*$ is injective. A [locale](../../../category-theory.md#locale) map with injective inverse-image [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) is a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function). We have obtained **an extremally disconnected $\gamma X$ and a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) $\gamma X\to X$**.

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

A [propositional geometric theory](../../../mathematical-logic.md#propositional-geometric-theory) has primitive propositions and sequents between formulas built with finite conjunction and arbitrary set-indexed disjunction. Start with the free [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) on the primitives, identify conjunction with [meet](../../../set.md#greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set) and disjunction with [join](../../../set.md#least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set), and impose each sequent as an inequality. The resulting quotient [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) is $\mathcal O(X_T)$. In the [sheaf topos](../../../category-theory.md#grothendieck-topos) $\operatorname{Sh}(X_T)$, interpret each primitive as the [subterminal sheaf](../../../category.md#subterminal-sheaf) corresponding to its generating open. Operations on these [subterminal sheaves](../../../category.md#subterminal-sheaf) are precisely the [frame](../../../category-theory.md#complete-heyting-algebra) operations, so every imposed sequent is valid. This constructs the canonical model.

For a [commutative ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#commutative-ring) $R$, denote the generator attached to $r$ by $U_r$. The presentation implies

$$
U_1=1,\qquad U_0=0,\qquad U_r\wedge U_s=U_{rs},\qquad U_{r+s}\leq U_r\vee U_s.
$$

The product equality uses the multiplication implication in one direction and both factor implications in the other; the latter follow by commuting $r,s$. A [ring homomorphism](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring-homomorphism) $\varphi:R\to S$ sends these relations to the corresponding ones in $S$. It therefore gives a [frame homomorphism](../../../category-theory.md#frame-homomorphism) $U_r\mapsto U_{\varphi(r)}$ and hence **a map $X_S\to X_R$ of [locales](../../../category-theory.md#locale)**.

If $ar+bs=1$, then

$$
1=U_{ar+bs}\leq U_{ar}\vee U_{bs}\leq U_r\vee U_s,
$$

so the two opens cover. Conversely let $S=R/(r,s)$ and pull back a putative cover along $X_S\to X_R$. Both generators become $U_0$, so $1=0$ in $\mathcal O(X_S)$. The allowed degeneracy criterion gives $0=1$ in $S$, equivalently $1\in(r,s)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{U_r\vee U_s=1\iff(r,s)=R\iff\exists a,b\in R:\ ar+bs=1.}
$$

This necessity argument never selects a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal).

Finite intersections of the generating opens are again generators, so they form a basis. Assign $R[r^{-1}]$ to $U_r$. The [localization of a ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) carries its ordinary addition and multiplication of fractions; equality of $a/r^n$ and $b/r^m$ means that some power of $r$ annihilates the cross-multiplied difference. On $U_r\cap U_s=U_{rs}$, restriction is further [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring).

We also justify restriction for any inclusion of basic opens. Adding the condition $U_t=1$ to the presentation of $X_R$ gives the open $U_t$ and the presentation of $X_{R[t^{-1}]}$: the generator for $a/t^n$ corresponds to $U_a\wedge U_t$. This is well-defined since equality of fractions, after multiplication by a power of the now-invertible $t$, makes the two membership propositions equivalent. More explicitly, $U_t=1$ implies $U_{t^kz}=U_z$. If $t^kz=0$, this gives $U_z=0$. If $x-y$ is killed by a power of $t$, then $U_{x-y}=U_{y-x}=0$ (since $U_{-1}=1$), and the additive inequalities for $x=y+(x-y)$ and $y=x+(y-x)$ give $U_x=U_y$. A zero element in the localized ring has false membership, multiplication gives intersections, and the additive relation follows by clearing denominators. The reverse assignment on $a\in R$ proves that these maps are inverse. Thus **$X_{R[t^{-1}]}\cong U_t$**. If $U_t\subseteq U_r$, then in this localized theory $U_r=1$. Apply the quotient argument above, with the second generator $0$, to conclude $1\in(r)$ in $R[t^{-1}]$. Therefore $r$ is invertible there, and the [universal property](../../../category-theory.md#universal-property) of [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) gives the restriction $R[r^{-1}]\to R[t^{-1}]$. These restrictions compose uniquely.

It remains to glue, and the allowed basis criterion reduces this to a whole-locale cover $U_r\vee U_s=1$. Let $\alpha\in R[r^{-1}]$ and $\beta\in R[s^{-1}]$ have equal restrictions to $R[(rs)^{-1}]$. Choose a common initial exponent $n$, writing $\alpha=a/r^n$, $\beta=b/s^n$. Equality on the overlap supplies $k\geq0$ with

$$
(rs)^k(s^na-r^nb)=0.
$$

Set $N=n+k$, $A=r^ka$ and $B=s^kb$. Then

$$
\alpha=A/r^N,\qquad\beta=B/s^N,\qquad s^NA=r^NB
$$

with the last equality in $R$ itself. This clearing step is necessary when the [commutative ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#commutative-ring) has [zero divisors](../../../mathematics.md#zero-divisor).

Since $(r,s)=R$, also $(r^N,s^N)=R$. Choose $\lambda r+\mu s=1$. For $N\geq1$, expand $(\lambda r+\mu s)^{2N-1}=1$; each term contains either $r^N$ or $s^N$, producing $C,D\in R$ with $Cr^N+Ds^N=1$. For $N=0$ choose $C=1,D=0$. Define

$$
\boxed{t=CA+DB.}
$$

In $R[r^{-1}]$,

$$
t-A/r^N=D\,(r^NB-s^NA)/r^N=0,
$$

and similarly in $R[s^{-1}]$,

$$
t-B/s^N=C\,(s^NA-r^NB)/s^N=0.
$$

Thus $t$ glues the matching pair. If $t'$ is another gluing, some $r^p$ and $s^q$ annihilate $t-t'$. The same binomial argument gives $(r^p,s^q)=R$, so $t-t'=0$. This proves uniqueness without cancellation or a domain assumption.

The [exactness of the unit-ideal localization Čech complex](../../../ringed-space.md#exactness-of-the-unit-ideal-localization-cech-complex) proves the [sheaf](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#sheaf-mathematics) axiom for the required basic covers, and hence the assignment with its restrictions defines **a [sheaf of rings](../../../ringed-space.md#sheaf-of-rings) $\widetilde R$ with $\widetilde R(U_r)=R[r^{-1}]$**. On the empty basic open, [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) is the [zero ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring), whose underlying set has one section, as required. The construction and proof remain valid for the [zero ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#zero-ring).
