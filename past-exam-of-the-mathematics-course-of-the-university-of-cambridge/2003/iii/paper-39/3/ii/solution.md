<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an [exponential distribution](../../../../../../exponential-distribution.md) with mean $\mu$, the claim [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) is $M_I(r)=(1-\mu r)^{-1}$ for $r<1/\mu$. The calculated [adjustment coefficient](../../../../../../adjustment-coefficient.md) must solve

$$
\frac1{1-\mu R_I}=1+(1+\rho)\mu R_I.
$$

Subtract one, divide by the nonzero factor $\mu R_I$, and obtain $(1-\mu R_I)^{-1}=1+\rho$. Thus

$$
\boxed{R_I=\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)\mu}.}
$$

It belongs to $(0,1/\mu)$ for every $\rho>0$, as required by the transform domain. The Poisson intensity cancels because the premium rate is specified using the same relative loading and mean claim cost.
