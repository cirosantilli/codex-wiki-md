<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Rayleigh number](../../../../../../rayleigh-number.md) compares destabilizing thermal buoyancy with viscous and thermal diffusion:

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Ra}=\frac{g\beta\Delta T\,h^3}{\nu\kappa}.}
$$

The conductive heat flux is $k\Delta T/h$, and the [Nusselt number](../../../../../../nusselt-number.md) is the actual upward heat flux divided by this value. The [four-thirds convective heat-transfer law](../../../../../../four-thirds-convective-heat-transfer-law.md) is the empirical large-Rayleigh closure $\mathrm{Nu}=C\mathrm{Ra}^{1/3}$. It gives

$$
\boxed{q=Ck\left(\frac{g\beta}{\nu\kappa}\right)^{1/3}(\Delta T)^{4/3}.}
$$

The layer depth cancels: thin thermal boundary layers control the resistance while convection mixes the interior. The dimensionless coefficient $C$ depends on the boundary and material-property regime; the two boundary-layer temperature drops can be absorbed into its convention. This is not a universal law for all [Rayleigh-Bénard convection](../../../../../../rayleigh-benard-convection.md). Below convective onset the stable conductive solution instead has $\mathrm{Nu}=1$, and an impulsive initial conductive transient is not described by the steady large-Rayleigh closure.
