<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The mass and heat entrainment equations immediately give

$$
\frac{d}{dz}(F-c_pT_0Q)=0.
$$

Use the specified relation $\rho T=\rho_0T_0=\beta$. Then

$$
F-c_pT_0Q=c_pQ(T-T_0)=c_pT_0Q\left(\frac{\rho_0}{\rho}-1\right).
$$

Meanwhile the kinematic [buoyancy flux](../../../../../../buoyancy-flux.md) is

$$
B=g\frac{\rho_0-\rho}{\rho_0}wb^2
=\frac g{\rho_0}Q\left(\frac{\rho_0}{\rho}-1\right)
=\frac{g}{\rho_0c_pT_0}(F-c_pT_0Q).
$$

Since $T_0$, $\rho_0$ and $c_p$ are constant,

$$
\boxed{dB/dz=0.}
$$

Thus conservation is a consequence of conserved excess heat in this ideal-gas, uniform-ambient model, not merely an assumption that arbitrary non-Boussinesq buoyancy fluxes are conserved in every environment.
