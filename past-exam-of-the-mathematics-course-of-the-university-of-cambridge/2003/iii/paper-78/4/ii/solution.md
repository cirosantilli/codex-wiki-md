<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Complete the crack normal to an orthonormal basis $(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf n)$ with the first two vectors in the crack plane. The [seismic moment tensor](../../../../../../seismic-moment-tensor.md) is diagonal in this basis:

$$
\boxed{M=V\left[\lambda\mathbf e_1\mathbf e_1^{\mathsf T}
+\lambda\mathbf e_2\mathbf e_2^{\mathsf T}
+(\lambda+2\mu)\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}\right]}.
$$

Each term gives an [elastic force dipole](../../../../../../elastic-force-dipole.md), $f_i=-D e_i e_j\partial_j\delta$: two equal and opposite forces in direction $\mathbf e$, separated along $\mathbf e$, with moment $D$. Hence there are two identical in-plane dipoles of strength $\lambda V$ and one normal dipole of strength $(\lambda+2\mu)V$. For positive opening and ordinary positive [Lamé parameters](../../../../../../lame-parameter.md), all are extensional and the normal one is larger by $2\mu V$.

More generally, stable isotropic [linear elasticity](../../../../../../linear-elasticity.md) requires $\mu>0$ and $3\lambda+2\mu>0$. These imply $\lambda+2\mu>|\lambda|$, so the normal dipole has greater magnitude even if an auxetic material has negative $\lambda$ and compressive in-plane dipoles. The point source has zero net force, because it differentiates a delta function, and zero net torque, because its moment tensor is symmetric. Its normal moment distinguishes opening from a pure shear dislocation.
