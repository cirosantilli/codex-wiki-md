**False.** Take the [Bell state](../../../../../../bell-state-split.md)

$$
|\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad\rho=|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|.
$$

The joint state is pure, so $S(\rho)=0$. Taking either [partial trace](../../../../../../partial-trace.md) kills the off-diagonal terms and gives $\rho_1=\rho_2=I_2/2$, whose two [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $1/2$. Thus $S(\rho_1)=S(\rho_2)=1$, contradicting the proposed inequality $1\le0$. [Quantum entanglement](../../../../../../entangled-state.md) allows a pure joint state to have mixed subsystem states.
