<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $\zeta_n$ be a primitive nth [root of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). The [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../../../../cyclotomic-polynomial.md) is

$$
\Phi_n(X)=\prod_{\substack{1\le a\le n\\(a,n)=1}}(X-\zeta_n^a),\qquad X^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(X).
$$

Induction on $n$ shows that $\Phi_n$ is monic with integer coefficients. Here is the essential [irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials](../../../../../../irreducibility-of-cyclotomic-polynomials.md) argument. Let $f$ be the [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) of $\zeta_n$ and let $\ell\nmid n$ be a prime. If $\zeta_n^\ell$ had a different monic [irreducible polynomial](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) $g$, then $f\mid g(X^\ell)$. Reducing modulo $\ell$ gives $\bar f\mid\bar g^\ell$, so $\bar f$ and $\bar g$ have a common factor. As $fg\mid\Phi_n$, this would give a repeated factor of $X^n-1$ modulo $\ell$, impossible because its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $nX^{n-1}$. Thus $\zeta_n^\ell$ is conjugate to $\zeta_n$. Every positive integer prime to $n$ is a product of such primes, so all primitive nth [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) are conjugate. Consequently

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(\zeta_n):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(n),\qquad\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times,\quad \sigma_a(\zeta_n)=\zeta_n^a.}
$$

In particular the [cyclotomic field](../../../../../../cyclotomic-field.md) is an [abelian extension](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md). [Complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) corresponds to $-1$; for $n>2$ its fixed field is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_n+\zeta_n^{-1})$, the maximal real subfield, of degree $\varphi(n)/2$.

The [ring of integers](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathbb Z[\zeta_n]$. One way to see that there is no missing index is to work at each prime $p$. Write $n=p^a m$ with $p\nmid m$. The prime-to-$p$ [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) have separable reduction; their local factors generate [unramified extensions](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) with full integer rings. Over each such factor, adjoining $\zeta_{p^a}$ is generated by the [Eisenstein polynomial](../../../../../../eisenstein-polynomial.md) $\Phi_{p^a}(1+T)$, with [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $\zeta_{p^a}-1$, and hence gives the full integer ring of that totally ramified factor. The coprimality of $p^a$ and $m$ means that $\zeta_n$ generates both sets of [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). Thus $\mathbb Z_p[\zeta_n]$ is maximal at every $p$, proving the integral assertion.

The same local description computes [ramification](../../../../../../ramification-mathematics.md) and splitting. For $p\nmid n$, the [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) is one, the [residue degree](../../../../../../residue-degree.md) is the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $n$, and there are $\varphi(n)/f$ primes above $p$. If $p\mid n$, put $m=n/p^a$; then

$$
e_p=\varphi(p^a),\qquad f_p=\operatorname{ord}_m(p),\qquad g_p=\frac{\varphi(m)}{f_p},
$$

where $\operatorname{ord}_1(p)=1$. Indeed, the prime-to-$p$ part is [unramified](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md), and the $p$-power part is totally ramified by the preceding [Eisenstein polynomial](../../../../../../eisenstein-polynomial.md). Notice the exception $p=2,a=1$: its [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) is one, consistently with $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2m})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$ for odd $m$. Thus finite [ramification](../../../../../../ramification-mathematics.md) occurs only at the indicated conductor primes, with that exception.

We now prove [cyclotomic Artin reciprocity over the rational numbers](../../../../../../cyclotomic-artin-reciprocity-over-the-rational-numbers.md), using arithmetic [Frobenius automorphisms](../../../../../../frobenius-automorphism.md). If $\ell\nmid n$ and $\mathfrak L$ lies over $\ell$, then [Frobenius](../../../../../../frobenius-automorphism.md) sends the reduction of $\zeta_n$ to its $\ell$th power. Distinct nth [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) remain distinct modulo $\mathfrak L$, since $X^n-1$ has separable reduction. Hence the [Artin symbol](../../../../../../artin-symbol.md) is exactly

$$
\operatorname{Art}((\ell))=\sigma_\ell.
$$

Extend multiplicatively to the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $I(n)$ of rational [fractional ideals](../../../../../../fractional-ideal.md) prime to $n$. Such an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) has a unique positive rational generator $q$ whose numerator and denominator are prime to $n$. The formula is

$$
\operatorname{Art}((q))=\sigma_{q\bmod n}.
$$

Every unit residue modulo $n$ has a positive integer representative prime to $n$, so this [Artin map](../../../../../../artin-reciprocity-law.md) is surjective. Its [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is precisely the subgroup $P_{n\infty}$ generated by positive $q\equiv1\pmod n$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I(n)/P_{n\infty}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q).}
$$

This proves the [Artin reciprocity law](../../../../../../artin-reciprocity-law.md) for this extension, including its principal-ray kernel. The infinite component of the [modulus](../../../../../../modulus.md) records positivity and therefore the possible action of [complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md).
