<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Within the positive-amplitude wedge the reduced [periodic orbit](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) equilibrium has amplitude [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $\mu_1$ and positive [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $4r_*^2$. At $\mu_1=0,\mu_2<0$ the transverse pair is imaginary, so the possible secondary event is a [Hopf bifurcation](../../../../../../hopf-bifurcation.md) of the amplitude equations, corresponding to an invariant [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) in the full system.

It is degenerate. Set $\mu_2=-a^2$ and $\mu_1=0$. The [first integral of the quadratic fold-Hopf amplitude flow](../../../../../../first-integral-of-the-quadratic-fold-hopf-amplitude-flow.md) is

$$
F=r(a^2-r^2/3-x^2),\qquad F_r=a^2-r^2-x^2,\qquad F_x=-2rx.
$$

The amplitude equations have the [Hamiltonian system](../../../../../../hamiltonian-system.md) form $\dot r=F_x$, $\dot x=-F_r$, so $\dot F=0$. At $(r,x)=(a,0)$, $F$ has a strict local maximum $2a^3/3$ and negative-definite Hessian. Every nearby regular level is a closed amplitude [periodic orbit](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md), rather than one isolated orbit selected by nonlinear damping. Adding the rotating phase gives an entire family of invariant [tori](../../../../../../torus.md); the full motion is quasiperiodic or periodic according to the phase advance during one amplitude period.

For $\mu_1\ne0$, the amplitude [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) has constant [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) $\mu_1$. The [Bendixson-Dulac theorem](../../../../../../bendixson-dulac-theorem.md) rules out closed amplitude trajectories in the physical simply connected half-plane $r>0$. Hence there is no ordinary branch of isolated invariant [tori](../../../../../../torus.md) on either side in this quadratic system. The secondary event has vanishing radial saturation coefficients; its imaginary linear pair alone does not establish a nondegenerate torus bifurcation.
