<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the [synchronous-to-Newtonian scalar Fourier transformation](../../../../../../synchronous-to-newtonian-scalar-fourier-transformation.md), the coefficient of $\widehat k_i\widehat k_j$ in the transformed spatial [metric perturbation](../../../../../../linearized-gravity.md) must vanish. Thus $h_s+2kL=0$. Vanishing shift then gives $L'=kT$. For $k\ne0$ these conditions fix

$$
\boxed{L=-\frac{h_s}{2k},\qquad T=-\frac{h_s'}{2k^2},\qquad
\xi^\mu=\left(-\frac{h_s'}{2k^2},-\frac{i\widehat k_i h_s}{2k}\right)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.}
$$

The lapse in [conformal Newtonian gauge](../../../../../../newtonian-gauge.md) is $h_{00}^{\rm N}=-2\Phi$, so

$$
\Phi=-(T'+\mathcal HT)=\boxed{\frac{h_s''+\mathcal Hh_s'}{2k^2}}.
$$

The remaining coefficient of $\delta_{ij}$ is

$$
\frac{h-h_s}{3}-2\mathcal HT=-2\Psi,
$$

giving

$$
\boxed{\Psi=-\frac{h-h_s}{6}-\frac{\mathcal Hh_s'}{2k^2}.}
$$

The homogeneous $k=0$ perturbation requires separate background-coordinate choices; the inverse powers of $k$ do not define a transformation for it.
