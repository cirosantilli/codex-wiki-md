<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use units in which the proton [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge.md) is one. The [up quark](../../../../../up-quark.md) and [down quark](../../../../../down-quark.md) both have [spin angular momentum](../../../../../spin.md) $1/2$ and form a flavour [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) doublet:

$$
\boxed{u:\ Q=\tfrac23,\ I=\tfrac12,\ I_3=\tfrac12;\qquad d:\ Q=-\tfrac13,\ I=\tfrac12,\ I_3=-\tfrac12.}
$$

Their [antiquarks](../../../../../antiquark.md) have the opposite charges and third components of [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md), with the same total [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) and spin. This is strong-interaction flavour [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md), not the chiral [weak isospin](../../../../../weak-isospin.md) assignment.

The [Clebsch-Gordan decomposition for SU2](../../../../../clebsch-gordan-decomposition-for-su2.md) says that spins $j_1,j_2$ combine into every $j$ from $|j_1-j_2|$ to $j_1+j_2$ in unit steps. It applies independently to spin and [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md). Thus

$$
\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12=0\oplus1,\qquad \tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12=\tfrac32\oplus\tfrac12\oplus\tfrac12.
$$

Before exchange symmetry is imposed, three light [quarks](../../../../../quark.md) have total spin $S=1/2$ or $3/2$, and total [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) $I=1/2$ or $3/2$; the doublet occurs twice in the uncoupled tensor cube. With zero [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md), $J=S$.

Physical [baryons](../../../../../baryon.md) must also be [colour singlets](../../../../../colour-singlet.md). The [three-quark colour singlet](../../../../../three-quark-colour-singlet.md) is completely antisymmetric, so the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) requires symmetric spatial-spin-flavour symmetry. For a symmetric spatial S-wave state, the spin-flavour factor is symmetric. The $I=3/2$ flavour quartet is symmetric, whereas the $I=1/2$ flavour doublets have mixed permutation symmetry; the spin factors have the analogous symmetries. Symmetric flavour therefore pairs with symmetric spin, while the two mixed factors pair in their unique symmetric combination. This is the [Pauli constraint on three-quark flavour multiplets](../../../../../pauli-constraint-on-three-quark-flavour-multiplets.md); it leaves

$$
\boxed{(I,J)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\quad\text{and}\quad(\tfrac32,\tfrac32).}
$$

Equivalently the symmetric cube of the four-dimensional two-flavour spin space has dimension $\binom63=20=2\cdot2+4\cdot4$. The unmatched spin-isospin pairings in the bare tensor product do not describe the usual symmetric-spatial ground-state [baryons](../../../../../baryon.md).

For three light [quarks](../../../../../quark.md), charge addition gives $Q=I_3+1/2$. The observed [nucleon](../../../../../nucleon.md) doublet and [Delta baryon](../../../../../delta-baryon.md) quartet have the following assignments; the listed contents specify flavour counts, not the complete spin-flavour wavefunction:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{state}&\text{content}&I&I_3&Q\\\hline
p&uud&1/2&1/2&1\\
n&udd&1/2&-1/2&0\\
\Delta^{++}&uuu&3/2&3/2&2\\
\Delta^+&uud&3/2&1/2&1\\
\Delta^0&udd&3/2&-1/2&0\\
\Delta^-&ddd&3/2&-3/2&-1
\end{array}
$$

The [proton](../../../../../proton.md) and [neutron](../../../../../neutron.md) have $J=1/2$; all four [Delta baryons](../../../../../delta-baryon.md) have $J=3/2$. These states have positive [parity](../../../../../parity.md) in the zero-orbital ground-state model.

For a light [quark](../../../../../quark.md)-[antiquark](../../../../../antiquark.md) [meson](../../../../../meson.md), both spin and flavour doublets combine into singlet plus triplet. In a conventional choice of phases the flavour states are

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(I,I_3)&\text{flavour state}&Q\\\hline
(1,1)&u\overline d&1\\
(1,0)&(u\overline u-d\overline d)/\sqrt2&0\\
(1,-1)&d\overline u&-1\\
(0,0)&(u\overline u+d\overline d)/\sqrt2&0
\end{array}
$$

Each row can have $J=0$ or $1$, since the two constituent spins couple to zero or one. At zero [orbital angular momentum](../../../../../orbital-angular-momentum.md) their [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is negative, following the [meson parity and charge conjugation](../../../../../meson-parity-and-charge-conjugation.md) rule $P=(-1)^{L+1}$. The spin-zero isotriplet is the [pion](../../../../../pion.md) multiplet, and the spin-one isotriplet the [rho meson](../../../../../rho-meson.md) multiplet. The nonstrange spin-one isosinglet is the idealized [omega meson](../../../../../omega-meson.md) flavour direction. Its spin-zero counterpart is the nonstrange direction contributing to the [eta and eta prime mesons](../../../../../eta-and-eta-prime-mesons.md); it is not automatically a physical mass eigenstate once other flavours and mixing are included.

Now introduce an [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md)-zero [quark](../../../../../quark.md) $h$ of spin $1/2$ and charge $q_h$. For [baryons with one isospin-singlet quark](../../../../../baryons-with-one-isospin-singlet-quark.md), the two light [quarks](../../../../../quark.md) have either the antisymmetric flavour singlet $[ud]=(ud-du)/\sqrt2$ or the symmetric triplet $uu,\{ud\},dd$. Their colour pair is antisymmetric. Hence [light-diquark spin-isospin correlation](../../../../../light-diquark-spin-isospin-correlation.md) gives $S_{ud}=0$ for $I=0$ and $S_{ud}=1$ for $I=1$. Coupling the $h$ spin produces

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{light pair}&I&J&Q\\\hline
[ud]&0&1/2&q_h+1/3\\
uu&1&1/2\text{ or }3/2&q_h+4/3\\
\{ud\}&1&1/2\text{ or }3/2&q_h+1/3\\
dd&1&1/2\text{ or }3/2&q_h-2/3
\end{array}
$$

The three triplet rows have $I_3=1,0,-1$. For a strange-like charge $q_h=-1/3$, these are the neutral spin-one-half [Lambda baryon](../../../../../lambda-baryon.md), the spin-one-half [Sigma baryon](../../../../../sigma-baryon.md) triplet of charges $1,0,-1$, and its spin-three-half starred partners. For a charm-like charge $q_h=2/3$, they are the spin-one-half [Lambda c baryon](../../../../../lambda-c-baryon.md) of charge $1$, and the [Sigma c baryon](../../../../../sigma-c-baryon.md) triplets of charges $2,1,0$ with spins $1/2$ and $3/2$. Both possible new charge assignments have thus been covered; charge-conjugate [antibaryons](../../../../../antibaryon.md) have opposite charges and unchanged $I,J$.

The [meson doublets with one isospin-singlet quark](../../../../../meson-doublets-with-one-isospin-singlet-quark.md) are $h\overline u,h\overline d$, with

$$
\boxed{I=\tfrac12,\quad I_3=(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12),\quad Q=(q_h-\tfrac23,q_h+\tfrac13),\quad J=0\text{ or }1.}
$$

For $q_h=-1/3$ their charges are $-1,0$, giving $K^-,\overline K^0$-like [kaons](../../../../../kaon.md) and their vector partners; the conjugate doublet has $u\overline h,d\overline h$ and charges $1,0$. For $q_h=2/3$ the charges are $0,1$, giving $D^0,D^+$-like [D mesons](../../../../../d-meson.md) and their vector partners, with charge-conjugate partners as well. Finally a hidden-flavour pair $h\overline h$, if included, is neutral with $I=0$ and $J=0$ or $1$. These are quantum-number classifications; charge and [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) alone do not determine particle masses or mixing angles.
