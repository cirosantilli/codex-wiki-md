The [Markov interlacing lemma](../../../../../../markov-interlacing-lemma.md) says that [differentiation](../../../../../../differentiation.md) preserves [interlacing roots of polynomials](../../../../../../interlacing-roots-of-polynomials.md). More explicitly, if two degree-$d$ real [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) have ordered real roots satisfying $a_1\le b_1\le a_2\le\cdots\le a_d\le b_d$, their $k$th [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) have ordered roots $a_j^{(k)},b_j^{(k)}$ satisfying

$$
a_1^{(k)}\le b_1^{(k)}\le a_2^{(k)}\le\cdots\le a_{d-k}^{(k)}\le b_{d-k}^{(k)},\qquad 0\le k<d.
$$

The analogous alternating order is preserved for [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of consecutive degrees. Common roots are allowed through weak inequalities; strictly interlacing simple roots give strict inequalities. When a derivative is constant, its root list is empty.
