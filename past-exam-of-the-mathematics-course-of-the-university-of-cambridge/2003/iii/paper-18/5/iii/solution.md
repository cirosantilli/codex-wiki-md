<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Given the [natural transformations](../../../../../../natural-transformation.md) and triangle identities, define $\Phi(h)=Gh\eta_A$ and $\Psi(k)=\varepsilon_BFk$. Both assignments are natural because $F,G,\eta,\varepsilon$ are natural. Naturality of the counit at $h$ gives

$$
\Psi\Phi(h)=\varepsilon_BFGhF\eta_A=h\varepsilon_{FA}F\eta_A=h.
$$

Naturality of the unit at $k$ similarly gives

$$
\Phi\Psi(k)=G\varepsilon_BGFk\eta_A=G\varepsilon_B\eta_{GB}k=k.
$$

Thus these are inverse natural Hom-set bijections, defining an [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) $F\dashv G$. Their identity values are the prescribed unit and counit, by their definitions. Finally every [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) with this unit must satisfy the first transpose formula, and every one with this counit the second, so the bijections are forced. **There is exactly one [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) structure with these specified unit and counit.**
