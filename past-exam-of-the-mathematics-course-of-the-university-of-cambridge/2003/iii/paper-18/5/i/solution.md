<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write the [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) bijections as $\Phi_{A,B}:\mathcal D(FA,B)\cong\mathcal C(A,GB)$, natural in $A,B$. The [adjunction unit](../../../../../../unit-of-an-adjunction.md) and [adjunction counit](../../../../../../counit-of-an-adjunction.md) are

$$
\boxed{\eta_A=\Phi_{A,FA}(1_{FA}),\qquad\varepsilon_B=\Phi_{GB,B}^{-1}(1_{GB}).}
$$

Thus $\eta:1_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow GF$ and $\varepsilon:FG\Rightarrow1_{\mathcal D}$ are [natural transformations](../../../../../../natural-transformation.md), by naturality of the bijections. For any $h:FA\to B$ and $k:A\to GB$, their transposes satisfy

$$
\Phi(h)=Gh\,\eta_A,\qquad\Phi^{-1}(k)=\varepsilon_BFk.
$$

The first follows by naturality in the target applied to $1_{FA}$; the second follows by naturality in the source applied to $1_{GB}$. These formulas make the definitions operational for every arrow.
