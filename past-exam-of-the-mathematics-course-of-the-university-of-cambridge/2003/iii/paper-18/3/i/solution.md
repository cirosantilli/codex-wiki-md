<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a small diagram $F:I\to\mathcal D$ in a [locally small category](../../../../../../locally-small-category.md), define its cone [functor](../../../../../../functor.md) on $\mathcal D^{\mathrm{op}}$ by

$$
\operatorname{Cone}_F(A)=\operatorname{Nat}(\Delta A,F)\cong\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal D(A,F(i)),
$$

where $\Delta A$ is the constant diagram. Precomposition acts on cone vertices. A [categorical limit](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md) is a representation of this [functor](../../../../../../functor.md): an object $L$ with natural bijections $\mathcal D(A,L)\cong\operatorname{Cone}_F(A)$. The image of $1_L$ is the universal cone, and the inverse bijection supplies the unique mediating arrow for every cone. Thus the [representable functor](../../../../../../representable-functor.md) formulation and the usual terminal-cone [universal property](../../../../../../universal-property.md) coincide.
