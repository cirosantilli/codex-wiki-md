<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the absolute value around the logarithm in the original PDF. Set $v(z)=\log^+|f(z)|=\max(0,\log|f(z)|)$. This is a nonnegative [subharmonic function](../../../../../../subharmonic-function.md), including at zeros by its continuous extension. Its radial means are nondecreasing: [Poisson integral](../../../../../../poisson-integral.md) comparison on a larger concentric circle bounds the smaller mean by the larger one. The hypothesis gives

$$
0\le T(r)=\int\log^+|f(re^{i\theta})|\,dm
\le\int\big|\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\big|\,dm\longrightarrow0.
$$

Consequently $T(r)=0$ for every $r$, and continuity of $v$ implies $v=0$ throughout the [unit disc](../../../../../../unit-disc.md). Thus **$|f|\le1$ everywhere**, which is stronger than the requested conclusion. In particular $f$ has [bounded characteristic](../../../../../../bounded-characteristic.md), because $\sup_rT(r)<\infty$; it belongs to the [Nevanlinna class](../../../../../../nevanlinna-class.md) and to [H-infinity space](../../../../../../h-infinity-space.md). The hypothesis also excludes the identically zero function, whose absolute logarithmic integral is infinite.
