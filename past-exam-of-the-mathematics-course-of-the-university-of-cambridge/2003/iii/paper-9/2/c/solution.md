<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The zero-free quotient $g$ from [solution](../b/solution.md) has a [holomorphic logarithm](../../../../../../holomorphic-logarithm.md), so $\log|g|$ is [harmonic](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md). Its radial mean is the constant $\log|g(0)|$. Subtract the radial logarithmic means of $f$ and $B$. The first tends to zero by the absolute logarithmic hypothesis; the second tends to zero by [solution](../solution.md). It follows that

$$
\log|g(0)|=\int\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,dm-\int\log|B(re^{i\theta})|\,dm=0.
$$

Since $|g|\le1$ and $|g(0)|=1$, the [maximum modulus principle](../../../../../../maximum-modulus-principle.md) makes $g$ a constant of modulus one. Absorb that constant into the unimodular factor of $B$. **Therefore $f$ itself is a [Blaschke product](../../../../../../blaschke-product.md).** This proves the [vanishing absolute logarithmic mean characterization of Blaschke products](../../../../../../vanishing-absolute-logarithmic-mean-characterization-of-blaschke-products.md); conversely a [Blaschke product](../../../../../../blaschke-product.md) satisfies the absolute version too, since $\log|B|\le0$.
