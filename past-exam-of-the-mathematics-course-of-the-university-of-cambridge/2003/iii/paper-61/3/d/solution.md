<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Eliminate $\mu_2$ from the two connection formulas:

$$
\mu_3=\mu_1e^{i\theta\widehat\sigma_3}(S^{-1}s).
$$

At $(0,T)$, where $\mu_1=I$ and $\theta=-4k^3T$, this is the [half-line mKdV spectral global relation](../../../../../../half-line-mkdv-spectral-global-relation.md)

$$
\boxed{S^{-1}s=e^{4ik^3T\widehat\sigma_3}\mu_3(0,T,k).}
$$

Taking its upper-right entry gives a particularly useful scalar form,

$$
\boxed{A(k)b(k)-B(k)a(k)=e^{8ik^3T}c(k,T),\qquad c=\mu_{3,12}(0,T,k).}
$$

The second column of $\mu_3$ shows that $c$ is analytic in the lower half-plane and has the usual $O(k^{-1})$ normalization. The opposite off-diagonal equation follows from the real-$q$ reduction. The exponential sign here follows from conjugating a $12$ entry, which multiplies it by $e^{8ik^3T}$; reversing it would give the wrong linear [global relation](../../../../../../global-relation-for-a-linear-boundary-value-problem.md) and boundary count. This relation constrains the initial and boundary [scattering data](../../../../../../scattering-data.md); they cannot be chosen independently.
