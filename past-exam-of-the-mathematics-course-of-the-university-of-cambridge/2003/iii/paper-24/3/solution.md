<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The analytic input for these [Mellin transforms](../../../../../mellin-transform.md) is elementary cusp decay. For a level-one weight-$k$ [cusp form](../../../../../cusp-form.md), the [invariant norm of a modular form](../../../../../invariant-norm-of-a-modular-form.md) $y^{k/2}|f(x+iy)|$ is invariant under the [modular group](../../../../../modular-group.md). It is bounded on the standard fundamental region, because the cusp expansion decays exponentially, and hence bounded on all of $\mathbb H$. Fourier inversion gives

$$
c_n=e^{2\pi ny}\int_0^1f(x+iy)e^{-2\pi inx}\,dx.
$$

Taking $y=1/n$ proves the [Fourier coefficient bound for a cusp form](../../../../../fourier-coefficient-bound-for-a-cusp-form.md) $c_n=O(n^{k/2})$. In particular rational translates have polynomially bounded coefficients and exponential decay as $y\to\infty$, uniformly in their real parts. These facts justify the initial Mellin integrations and the later entire continuation.
