<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $T=\dot\gamma$ along an affinely parametrized [geodesic](../../../../../../geodesic.md) and $D_t=\nabla_T$ for the [covariant derivative](../../../../../../covariant-derivative.md) from the [Levi-Civita connection](../../../../../../levi-civita-connection.md). We use the [Riemann curvature tensor](../../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor.md) convention $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$, for which positive [sectional curvature](../../../../../../sectional-curvature.md) means $\langle R(X,Y)Y,X\rangle>0$ on an orthonormal pair. A [Jacobi field](../../../../../../jacobi-field.md) is a smooth [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) along $\gamma$ satisfying

$$
\boxed{D_t^2J+R(J,T)T=0.}
$$

Such a field describes the first-order separation of nearby [geodesics](../../../../../../geodesic.md), as the [geodesic variation](../../../../../../geodesic-variation.md) construction in part (c) shows.

For $t_0>0$, $\gamma(t_0)$ is a [conjugate point](../../../../../../conjugate-point.md) of $\gamma(0)$ along this [geodesic](../../../../../../geodesic.md) if a nonzero [Jacobi field](../../../../../../jacobi-field.md) satisfies $J(0)=J(t_0)=0$. The [multiplicity of a conjugate point](../../../../../../multiplicity-of-a-conjugate-point.md) is

$$
\boxed{\dim\{J:D_t^2J+R(J,T)T=0,\ J(0)=J(t_0)=0\}.}
$$

It is equivalently the dimension of the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of the [differential](../../../../../../differential-of-a-smooth-map.md) of the [Riemannian exponential map](../../../../../../exponential-map-riemannian-geometry.md) at $t_0\dot\gamma(0)$, by [Jacobi fields vanishing at their initial point](../../../../../../jacobi-fields-vanishing-at-their-initial-point.md). Conjugacy concerns the specified [geodesic](../../../../../../geodesic.md) and its two parameter values, even when its endpoints represent the same point of the [Riemannian manifold](../../../../../../riemannian-manifold.md).
