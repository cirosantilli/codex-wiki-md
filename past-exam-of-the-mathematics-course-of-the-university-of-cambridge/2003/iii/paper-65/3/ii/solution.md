<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $s=(r^2+z^2)^{1/2}$. The [Paczyński-Wiita potential](../../../../../../paczynski-wiita-potential.md) is a spherical [Newtonian gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential.md) with $\Phi'(s)=GM/(s-a)^2$ and $\Phi''(s)=-2GM/(s-a)^3$. At $z=0$, $s=r$, so

$$
\Phi_{,r}=\frac{GM}{(r-a)^2},\qquad
\Phi_{,rr}=-\frac{2GM}{(r-a)^3},\qquad
\Phi_{,zz}=\frac{GM}{r(r-a)^2}.
$$

Substituting into the orbital formulas gives

$$
\boxed{\Omega^2=\Omega_z^2=\frac{GM}{r(r-a)^2},\qquad
\kappa^2=\frac{GM(r-3a)}{r(r-a)^3}}.
$$

Choosing positive orbital and vertical frequencies, $\Omega=\Omega_z=\sqrt{GM}/[\sqrt r(r-a)]$. For $r>3a$, $\kappa=\Omega\sqrt{(r-3a)/(r-a)}$. For $a<r<3a$, $\kappa^2<0$ and radial disturbances grow at rate $\sqrt{-\kappa^2}$, even though the vertical motion remains stable. Thus **circular orbits are radially unstable for $a<r<3a$**, and $r=3a$ is the marginal orbit. This recovers the [innermost stable circular orbit](../../../../../../innermost-stable-circular-orbit.md) of the [Paczyński-Wiita circular orbit](../../../../../../paczynski-wiita-circular-orbit.md) model.
