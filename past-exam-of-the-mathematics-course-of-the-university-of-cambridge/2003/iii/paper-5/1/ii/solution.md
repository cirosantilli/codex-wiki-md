<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the classical subgroup-covering construction, assume $X$ is [path-connected](../../../../../../path-connected-space.md), [locally path-connected](../../../../../../locally-path-connected-space.md), and [semilocally simply connected](../../../../../../semilocally-simply-connected-space.md). These are the usual hypotheses underlying the [classification of connected covering spaces](../../../../../../classification-of-connected-covering-spaces.md); an arbitrary space, as literally allowed by the wording, need not admit every [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) covering.

Construct the [path-class construction of a subgroup covering](../../../../../../path-class-construction-of-a-subgroup-covering.md) directly. Consider [paths](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) $\alpha$ starting at $x_0$, and identify $\alpha,\beta$ when they have the same endpoint and the loop class $[\alpha\beta^{-1}]$ belongs to $H$. The [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) properties make this an equivalence relation. Let $Y$ be its set of classes and define $p([\alpha])=\alpha(1)$.

Choose a path-connected open neighbourhood $U$ of $\alpha(1)$ whose loops become [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) in $X$. Such neighbourhoods form a local basis under the stated local hypotheses. Define a sheet

$$
N([\alpha],U)=\{[\alpha\eta]:\eta\text{ is a path in }U\text{ starting at }\alpha(1)\}.
$$

For each point of $U$ the resulting class is independent of the chosen [path](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) $\eta$, since the difference is a loop in $U$, conjugated by $\alpha$, and is trivial in $\pi_1(X,x_0)$. Thus $p$ maps this sheet bijectively onto $U$. Give $Y$ the topology generated by these sheets. Restrictions to smaller such neighbourhoods show that the sheets form a basis and that their endpoint maps are homeomorphisms. Two sheets over $U$ that intersect coincide, by concatenating and reversing [paths](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) within $U$; otherwise they are disjoint. Therefore $p^{-1}(U)$ is a disjoint union of copies of $U$, and $p$ is a [covering map](../../../../../../covering-space.md).

The basepoint is the class of the constant [path](../../../../../../continuous-path.md). The [path](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) $\alpha$ lifts to its successive prefix classes, giving a [path](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) from this basepoint to $[\alpha]$; hence $Y$ is path-connected. A loop $\gamma$ lifts closed exactly when its final prefix class agrees with the constant class, namely when $[\gamma]\in H$. Consequently $p_*(\pi_1(Y))=H$. Part (i) supplies injectivity, so

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(Y)\cong H}.
$$

The missing local hypothesis is substantive. In the [Hawaiian earring](../../../../../../hawaiian-earring.md), every neighbourhood of the common basepoint contains a whole sufficiently small circle. Its loop is nontrivial in the whole space, since retracting onto that circle preserves it. An evenly covered neighbourhood would lift that loop to a closed loop in one sheet, so the image [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of any covering contains a nontrivial element. Therefore the trivial [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) cannot be realized by a simply connected covering of this space. The intended result is valid under the local covering-space hypotheses above, not for every topological space.
