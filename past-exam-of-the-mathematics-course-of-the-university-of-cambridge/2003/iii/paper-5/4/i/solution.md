<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Work with a finite symmetric alphabet $\Sigma=A^{\pm1}$. If $w=a_1\cdots a_m$, its [path](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) $\widehat w$ starts at $1$, visits $a_1\cdots a_j$ at integer time $j$, travels at unit speed along the intervening generator edges, and stays at its endpoint for $t\ge m$.

Two such [paths](../../../../../../continuous-path.md) synchronously $K$-fellow travel if

$$
\boxed{d(\widehat u(t),\widehat v(t))\le K\quad\text{for every }t\ge0}.
$$

An asynchronous version permits increasing time reparametrizations. Checking all integer times suffices after a bounded enlargement of $K$ for the edge interiors.

A [combing of a group](../../../../../../combing-of-a-group.md) chooses a representative word $\sigma(g)$ for every $g$, with $\sigma(1)$ empty. A synchronous [combing](../../../../../../combing-of-a-group.md) has a uniform constant $K$ such that representatives with adjacent endpoints satisfy the [synchronous fellow-traveller property](../../../../../../synchronous-fellow-traveller-property.md). A group admitting this is a synchronous [combable group](../../../../../../combable-group.md). The [formal language](../../../../../../formal-language.md) need not be regular or computable. A [regular language](../../../../../../regular-language.md) of representatives satisfying the synchronous condition instead gives an [automatic group](../../../../../../automatic-group.md). This distinction is relevant to the [finite-state automata](../../../../../../finite-state-machine.md) request in part (iii); the present argument uses the synchronous meaning throughout. The definitions and distinction are also made explicitly in [Combings of Groups and the Grammar of Reparameterization](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/bridson/papers/combings/combings.pdf), Section 1.
