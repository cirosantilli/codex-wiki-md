<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [algorithm](../../../../../../algorithm.md) is a finite effective procedure on finitely encoded inputs; a [Turing machine](../../../../../../turing-machine.md) is one precise model. A decision [algorithm](../../../../../../algorithm.md) must halt on every permitted input and answer correctly. An algorithmic problem specifies the input encoding and the required output; enumerating positive answers alone is not a decision [algorithm](../../../../../../algorithm.md).

For a fixed [finite group presentation](../../../../../../finite-group-presentation.md) $\mathcal P=\langle A\mid R\rangle$, the [word problem for a group](../../../../../../word-problem-for-groups.md) takes a finite word in $A^{\pm1}$ and asks whether it represents the identity. Equality of two represented elements is equivalent to testing $u^{-1}v$. Positive answers are recursively enumerable: enumerate finite products of conjugates of [relators](../../../../../../relator.md) and their inverses, freely reduce them, and compare with the input. Equivalently enumerate finite labelled [disc diagrams](../../../../../../disc-diagram.md). The [van Kampen lemma](../../../../../../van-kampen-lemma.md) ensures that every null word eventually appears. For a non-null word this search continues forever. **Solvability requires a procedure that also terminates with a negative answer.**
