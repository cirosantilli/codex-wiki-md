<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For the [degree-ten partition action of S6](../../../../../degree-ten-partition-action-of-s6.md), take unordered partitions of six letters into two triples. There are $\binom63/2=10$. Fix $A\mid A^c$. Its [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is $(S_3\times S_3)\rtimes S_2=S_3\wr S_2$, permuting each triple and interchanging them. The [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by a 3-cycle in each triple has order nine, so is a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $S_6$. Any [normalizer](../../../../../normalizer.md) preserves its two three-point orbits; conversely all the indicated within-triple [permutations](../../../../../permutation.md) and the swap normalize it. This proves the asserted [normalizer](../../../../../normalizer.md) exactly.

For any different partition $C\mid C^c$, $|A\cap C|$ is one or two after excluding the identical partition. Interchanging $C$ and $C^c$ identifies these two possibilities. The [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of $A\mid A^c$ is transitive on each fixed intersection pattern, hence on all other nine partitions. **The degree-ten action is [two-transitive](../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md).** It is also [faithful](../../../../../faithful-group-action.md): two distinct partitions have a common refinement with two singleton cells and two cells of size two. Every unordered pair of letters occurs as those singleton cells. A [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) fixing every partition therefore fixes every pair and consequently every letter.

For the [subgroup factorization from coset transitivity](../../../../../subgroup-factorization-from-coset-transitivity.md), use [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) $Hg$ (right cosets) and right multiplication by $K$. The orbit of $H$ is precisely $\{Hk:k\in K\}$. This is every [coset](../../../../../coset.md) if and only if every $g$ can be written $hk$, proving

$$
\boxed{G=HK\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\text{ is transitive on }H\backslash G.}
$$

A natural $S_5$ fixing one letter is transitive on the ten partitions: the triple containing the fixed letter is determined by any two of the other five. Taking $H=S_3\wr S_2$, $K=S_5$ gives $S_6=HK$; inversion gives the requested order as well,

$$
\boxed{S_6=S_5(S_3\wr S_2).}
$$

Now let $B$ be the binary [nondegenerate](../../../../../nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) [alternating bilinear form](../../../../../alternating-bilinear-form.md). Its [binary quadratic refinements](../../../../../binary-quadratic-refinement.md) form an [affine space](../../../../../affine-space.md): the difference of two such forms is linear, so for a fixed $Q_0$ every one is uniquely

$$
Q_v(x)=Q_0(x)+B(v,x),\qquad v\in V.
$$

In a [symplectic basis](../../../../../symplectic-basis.md) each refinement is

$$
Q(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^m\bigl(x_iy_i+a_ix_i+b_iy_i\bigr),\qquad
\operatorname{Arf}(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^ma_ib_i\in\mathbb F_2.
$$

A two-dimensional summand with $(a_i,b_i)\ne(1,1)$ admits a [basis](../../../../../basis.md) of singular vectors paired to one and is hyperbolic; the $(1,1)$ summand is anisotropic. Two planes spanned by [anisotropic vectors for a form](../../../../../anisotropic-vector-for-a-form.md) together become two [hyperbolic planes of a quadratic form](../../../../../hyperbolic-plane-quadratic-form.md): with anisotropic [basis](../../../../../basis.md) pairs $(e_1,f_1),(e_2,f_2)$, the singular vectors $e_1+e_2,f_1+e_2$ pair to one; their [orthogonal complement for a sesquilinear form](../../../../../orthogonal-complement-for-a-sesquilinear-form.md) has singular paired [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $e_1+f_1+f_2,e_1+f_1+e_2+f_2$. Consequently every refinement is equivalent to either $m$ [hyperbolic planes of a quadratic form](../../../../../hyperbolic-plane-quadratic-form.md) or $(m-1)$ [hyperbolic planes of a quadratic form](../../../../../hyperbolic-plane-quadratic-form.md) and one anisotropic plane (all three nonzero vectors are [anisotropic vectors for a form](../../../../../anisotropic-vector-for-a-form.md)).

These two types are different. Indeed the character sum of each plane is $+2$ or $-2$, so

$$
\sum_{x\in V}(-1)^{Q(x)}=(-1)^{\operatorname{Arf}(Q)}2^m.
$$

Thus there are exactly two symplectic orbits $Q^+(B),Q^-(B)$, the signs denoting $\operatorname{Arf}=0,1$. Counting the three coefficient pairs with product zero and the one with product one gives

$$
\boxed{|Q^\pm(B)|=\frac{4^m\pm2^m}{2}=2^{m-1}(2^m\pm1).}
$$

The same character sum gives the corresponding numbers of singular vectors, including zero, in a fixed form of either type.

Fix a form $Q$ of type $\epsilon$. Completing the square by $x\mapsto x+v$ shows

$$
\sum_x(-1)^{Q_v(x)}=(-1)^{Q(v)}\sum_x(-1)^{Q(x)},\qquad
\epsilon(Q_v)=\epsilon(Q)(-1)^{Q(v)}.
$$

The [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of $Q$ is exactly $O(V,Q)$. It sends the parameter $v$ by its natural linear action or inverse action according to the chosen left/right convention. Other forms of the same type correspond to nonzero singular vectors $v$, and all forms of opposite type correspond to nonsingular vectors $v$. For two nonzero vectors of the same $Q$ value, the map between their spans is an [isometry of a space with a form](../../../../../isometry-of-a-space-with-a-form.md). [Witt's lemma](../../../../../witt-s-theorem.md) extends it, so the orthogonal [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is transitive on each of these two vector sets. This proves the [two-transitive binary quadratic-form actions](../../../../../two-transitive-binary-quadratic-form-actions.md): the [symplectic group over a finite field](../../../../../symplectic-group-over-a-finite-field.md) is transitive on each type and its point [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) is transitive on all other members of that type.

In particular $O^-_{2m}(2)$ is transitive on the plus orbit. Identify that orbit with the [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) (right cosets) of a plus [stabilizer](../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md), using $Q^g(x)=Q(gx)$ as a right action. The [coset](../../../../../coset.md) criterion then proves

$$
\boxed{Sp_{2m}(2)=O^+_{2m}(2)O^-_{2m}(2).}
$$

Both genuine [two-transitive](../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) actions require $m\ge2$. At $m=1$ the plus orbit has three forms and the minus orbit is a singleton; transitivity and the factorization remain true, while [two-transitive](../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) action on the singleton is only vacuous if one's convention permits degree one. No special simplicity assertion is needed for this argument. At $m=2$ the orbit sizes are ten and six, linking these actions with $Sp_4(2)\cong S_6$ and the partition action above.
