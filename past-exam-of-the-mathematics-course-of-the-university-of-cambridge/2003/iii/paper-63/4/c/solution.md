<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [Type Ia supernova](../../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) is a thermonuclear disruption associated with a [carbon-oxygen white dwarf](../../../../../../carbon-oxygen-white-dwarf.md) in a binary system. [Electron degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../electron-degeneracy-pressure.md) supports the dense star, and initially depends much less on temperature than an [ideal gas](../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) pressure does. Once carbon burning heats degenerate material, the immediate pressure response is insufficient to produce the usual expansion-and-cooling thermostat. The strongly temperature-sensitive [nuclear reactions](../../../../../../nuclear-reaction.md) can therefore undergo a [thermonuclear runaway](../../../../../../thermonuclear-runaway.md).

One channel involves [accretion](../../../../../../accretion.md) toward the [Chandrasekhar limit](../../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md), about $1.4$ solar masses for a typical electron composition. Other proposed channels involve merging [white dwarfs](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) or an initial helium-layer detonation that triggers carbon burning below that mass. Thus the [Chandrasekhar limit](../../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md) motivates an important model, but it is not an assumption that every [Type Ia supernova](../../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) has exactly the same progenitor mass or ignition history. Burning carbon and oxygen into more tightly bound nuclei releases enough [nuclear binding energy](../../../../../../nuclear-binding-energy.md) to overcome the stellar gravitational binding energy. An ordinary successful event ejects the star rather than leaving the compact core characteristic of a [core-collapse supernova](../../../../../../core-collapse-supernova.md).

The ejecta contain iron-group material, including radioactive nickel-56, and intermediate-mass elements from incomplete burning. The [radioactively powered Type Ia supernova light curve](../../../../../../radioactively-powered-type-ia-supernova-light-curve.md) is powered largely by the nickel-56 to cobalt-56 to iron-56 decay chain: its energetic products heat the ejecta, and [radiative diffusion](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) delays the emerging radiation. Near maximum light, ordinary [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) show prominent silicon absorption and lack the hydrogen lines defining Type II events. The spectral classification and the thermonuclear mechanism provide complementary descriptions.

The similar progenitors and radioactive power make [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md) useful [standard candles](../../../../../../standard-candle.md), but their peak [luminosities](../../../../../../luminosity.md) are not identical. The relation between [light curve](../../../../../../light-curve.md) width and peak brightness, together with color corrections, allows standardization for distance measurements. Extinction, composition and explosion diversity still introduce systematic uncertainties. **A Type Ia event is a white-dwarf thermonuclear explosion with a radioactively powered light curve; its corrected luminosity makes it a valuable distance indicator.**
