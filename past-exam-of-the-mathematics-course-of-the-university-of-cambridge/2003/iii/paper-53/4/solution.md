<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

In a massive [four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry](../../../../../four-dimensional-n-1-supersymmetry.md) representation, the two normalized raising [supercharges](../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md) transform as a spin-half doublet. Start with a [Clifford vacuum](../../../../../clifford-vacuum.md) of [superspin](../../../../../superspin.md) $j=1/2$. At occupation levels zero, one and two, the ordinary [spins](../../../../../spin.md) are

$$
\frac12,\qquad \frac12\otimes\frac12=0\oplus1,\qquad \frac12.
$$

The two-creation state has a spin-singlet oscillator factor because the creation operators anticommute. Thus the [massive N=1 vector multiplet](../../../../../massive-n-1-vector-multiplet.md) contains

$$
\boxed{1\ \oplus\ \frac12\ \oplus\ \frac12\ \oplus\ 0.}
$$

There are four bosonic polarizations, $3+1$, and four fermionic polarizations, $2+2$, as required by [boson-fermion degeneracy in a supermultiplet](../../../../../boson-fermion-degeneracy-in-a-supermultiplet.md).

In the massless limit the [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md) multiplicities become $+1,-1$, two copies each of $+1/2,-1/2$, and two zero-helicity states. They separate into the four irreducible [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md) pairs

$$
\boxed{(1,\tfrac12),\quad(\tfrac12,0),\quad(0,-\tfrac12),\quad(-\tfrac12,-1).}
$$

With the lower-helicity convention for [superhelicity](../../../../../superhelicity.md), a pair $(\kappa,\kappa+1/2)$ has labels $\kappa=1/2,0,-1/2,-1$, respectively. A midpoint convention shifts each label by $1/4$; the explicit [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md) pairs remove any naming ambiguity.

Pairing under the [CPT theorem](../../../../../cpt-theorem.md), the first and last pairs form a massless [vector multiplet](../../../../../supersymmetric-vector-multiplet.md), and the middle two form a massless [chiral multiplet](../../../../../chiral-multiplet.md). This is the [massless limit of a massive N=1 vector multiplet](../../../../../massless-limit-of-a-massive-n-1-vector-multiplet.md). The longitudinal vector polarization supplies one of the [chiral multiplet](../../../../../chiral-multiplet.md)'s two real scalar modes; the original spin-zero state supplies the other. Simply discarding the longitudinal polarization would lose a physical degree of freedom and give the wrong decomposition.
