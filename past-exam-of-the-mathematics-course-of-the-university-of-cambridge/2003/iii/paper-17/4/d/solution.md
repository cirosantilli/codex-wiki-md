<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**True for a smooth complex projective surface**, the usual manifold convention in this question. Choose an [ample divisor](../../../../../../ample-cartier-divisor.md) $H$. The auxiliary pencil-existence theorem states that, for all sufficiently large $m$, a general two-dimensional space of sections of $\mathcal O_X(mH)$ defines a [Lefschetz pencil](../../../../../../lefschetz-pencil.md): its base points are transverse and finite, its general fibre is a smooth connected curve, and each singular fibre has exactly one ordinary node, with distinct critical values. High powers separate the required jets, so a generic pencil has precisely these singularities.

Choose such an $m$ divisible by four, and write $D=mH$. The [adjunction formula](../../../../../../adjunction-formula.md) for a smooth connected curve of class $D$ on a smooth projective surface is $2g-2=D\cdot(D+K_X)$. Consequently

$$
g=1+\frac{m^2H^2+mK_X\cdot H}{2}.
$$

Writing $m=4\ell$ gives $g=1+8\ell^2H^2+2\ell K_X\cdot H$, an odd integer. Thus **a sufficiently positive four-divisible polarization gives an odd-genus [Lefschetz pencil](../../../../../../lefschetz-pencil.md)**. The surface is taken to be smooth; a singular projective variety need not support a [Lefschetz pencil](../../../../../../lefschetz-pencil.md) in this smooth-manifold sense.
