<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose a strictly positive pricing density exists. Its weighted payment for any fixed holdings is [integrable](../../../../../../../integrability.md), and

$$
x^\top A_0=\mathbb E[\nu\,x^\top A_1].
$$

If the terminal payment is nonnegative and strictly positive on an [event](../../../../../../../event.md) of positive [probability](../../../../../../../probability.md), positivity of $\nu$ makes this [expected value](../../../../../../../expected-value.md) strictly positive. It cannot therefore have nonpositive initial cost. Thus the first form of [arbitrage](../../../../../../../arbitrage.md) is incompatible with a positive pricing density. The existence argument in part (b) proves that this form occurs whenever separation within the payment span obstructs a density.
