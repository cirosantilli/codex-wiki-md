<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**Only the prime two can ramify.** More precisely, every field $\mathbb Q(P_n)$ is [unramified](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) at every odd [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md); actual ramification at two depends on $P$ and the chosen division point. Use $[2^n]P_n=P$, as printed in the original PDF.

Fix an odd prime $p$ and put $N=2^n$. The [elliptic-curve discriminant](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-discriminant.md) $-2^8$ gives [good reduction](../../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) at $p$, and $N$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z_p$. The [unramified division torsors at good primes](../../../../../../unramified-division-torsors-at-good-primes.md) imply that all [division points of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../division-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) above $P$ are defined over finite [unramified extensions](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) of $\mathbb Q_p$. Here is a direct lifting proof, which does not require $P$ to have integral affine coordinates.

Properness gives a reduction $\widetilde P$. Choose a point $\overline Q$ with $[N]\overline Q=\widetilde P$ over the algebraic closure of the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md), and a finite residue extension containing it. Pass to the corresponding finite [unramified extension](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) $K/\mathbb Q_p$. The [surjectivity of good reduction over a local field](../../../../../../surjectivity-of-good-reduction-over-a-local-field.md) lifts $\overline Q$ to $Q_0\in E(K)$. Its error $[N]Q_0-P$ lies in the [kernel of reduction of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../kernel-of-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md). The [prime-to-residue-characteristic multiplication on a formal group](../../../../../../prime-to-residue-characteristic-multiplication-on-a-formal-group.md) makes $[N]$ bijective on that kernel. Choose the unique kernel point $R$ with $[N]R=[N]Q_0-P$; then $Q=Q_0-R$ satisfies $[N]Q=P$ and has reduction $\overline Q$.

Every algebraic division point has some such reduction and is the unique lift in its division fibre: the difference of two lifts lies in the reduction kernel, which has no $N$-torsion. Thus every chosen $P_n$ lies in an unramified local extension. For every prime of its possibly non-Galois coordinate field above $p$, the [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) is one. Globally,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q(P_n)/\mathbb Q\text{ is unramified outside }\{2\}.}
$$

One cannot universally assert that two actually ramifies: $P=O$ and $P_n=O$ give the field $\mathbb Q$. It can ramify. For example, a half of $T=(-1,0)$ is

$$
Q=\left(-1+\sqrt2,\ 2\sqrt{\sqrt2-1}\right),\qquad [2]Q=T.
$$

The [elliptic curve group law](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-group-law-from-riemann-roch.md) verifies this by doubling. Its coordinate field contains $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, which has [ramification in the quadratic extension generated by the square root of two](../../../../../../ramification-in-the-quadratic-extension-generated-by-the-square-root-of-two.md), so the only possible bad prime does occur for this choice.
