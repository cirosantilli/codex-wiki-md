<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The obstruction is the [local-to-global gap in isogeny descent](../../../../../../local-to-global-gap-in-isogeny-descent.md). A [two-isogeny descent quartic](../../../../../../quartic-covering-in-a-two-isogeny-descent.md) can have points over $\mathbb R$ and every [p-adic field](../../../../../../p-adic-field.md) without a [rational point](../../../../../../rational-point.md). Local tests compute an [isogeny Selmer group](../../../../../../isogeny-selmer-group.md), which can strictly contain the rational-point quotient. For an [isogeny of elliptic curves](../../../../../../isogeny-of-elliptic-curves.md) $\phi:E\to E'$, the comparison is

$$
0\longrightarrow E'(\mathbb Q)/\phi E(\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}^{\phi}(E/\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)[\phi]
\longrightarrow0.
$$

The last group is the kernel of $\phi:\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)\to\operatorname{Sha}(E'/\mathbb Q)$, so it records elements of the [Tate–Shafarevich group](../../../../../../tate-shafarevich-group.md) invisible to all local tests. Searching a locally soluble covering which has no [rational points](../../../../../../rational-point.md) never succeeds. Thus a [Selmer group of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../n-selmer-group.md) gives an upper bound and found independent points give a lower bound, and these need not coincide. Higher descent can improve the bounds, but this particular procedure has no unconditional guarantee of closing every gap.
