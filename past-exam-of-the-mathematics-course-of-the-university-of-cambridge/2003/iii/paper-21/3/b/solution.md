<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The supplied integral equation has [elliptic-curve discriminant](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-discriminant.md) $\Delta=-11$. It therefore has [good reduction](../../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) at every $p\ne11$. At eleven its discriminant has [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) one. Under a change of [Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../weierstrass-equation-of-an-elliptic-curve.md), discriminant valuations differ by a multiple of twelve. A good integral model would have valuation zero, which is impossible. This is the [discriminant valuation obstruction to good reduction](../../../../../../discriminant-valuation-obstruction-to-good-reduction.md); it also shows the displayed model is minimal at eleven. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{The good primes are precisely all primes except }11.}
$$
