Let $\zeta_n$ be a primitive nth [root of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity). The [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-polynomial) is

$$
\Phi_n(X)=\prod_{\substack{1\le a\le n\\(a,n)=1}}(X-\zeta_n^a),\qquad X^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(X).
$$

Induction on $n$ shows that $\Phi_n$ is monic with integer coefficients. Here is the essential [irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials](../../../galois-theory.md#irreducibility-of-cyclotomic-polynomials) argument. Let $f$ be the [minimal polynomial](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) of $\zeta_n$ and let $\ell\nmid n$ be a prime. If $\zeta_n^\ell$ had a different monic [irreducible polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#irreducible-polynomial) $g$, then $f\mid g(X^\ell)$. Reducing modulo $\ell$ gives $\bar f\mid\bar g^\ell$, so $\bar f$ and $\bar g$ have a common factor. As $fg\mid\Phi_n$, this would give a repeated factor of $X^n-1$ modulo $\ell$, impossible because its [derivative](../../../calculus.md#derivative) is $nX^{n-1}$. Thus $\zeta_n^\ell$ is conjugate to $\zeta_n$. Every positive integer prime to $n$ is a product of such primes, so all primitive nth [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity) are conjugate. Consequently

$$
\boxed{[\mathbb Q(\zeta_n):\mathbb Q]=\varphi(n),\qquad\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times,\quad \sigma_a(\zeta_n)=\zeta_n^a.}
$$

In particular the [cyclotomic field](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-field) is an [abelian extension](../../../galois-theory.md#abelian-extension). [Complex conjugation](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugation) corresponds to $-1$; for $n>2$ its fixed field is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_n+\zeta_n^{-1})$, the maximal real subfield, of degree $\varphi(n)/2$.

The [ring of integers](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ring-of-integers) is $\mathbb Z[\zeta_n]$. One way to see that there is no missing index is to work at each prime $p$. Write $n=p^a m$ with $p\nmid m$. The prime-to-$p$ [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity) have separable reduction; their local factors generate [unramified extensions](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) with full integer rings. Over each such factor, adjoining $\zeta_{p^a}$ is generated by the [Eisenstein polynomial](../../../arithmetic.md#eisenstein-polynomial) $\Phi_{p^a}(1+T)$, with [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer) $\zeta_{p^a}-1$, and hence gives the full integer ring of that totally ramified factor. The coprimality of $p^a$ and $m$ means that $\zeta_n$ generates both sets of [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity). Thus $\mathbb Z_p[\zeta_n]$ is maximal at every $p$, proving the integral assertion.

The same local description computes [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics) and splitting. For $p\nmid n$, the [ramification index](../../../arithmetic.md#ramification-index) is one, the [residue degree](../../../arithmetic.md#residue-degree) is the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $n$, and there are $\varphi(n)/f$ primes above $p$. If $p\mid n$, put $m=n/p^a$; then

$$
e_p=\varphi(p^a),\qquad f_p=\operatorname{ord}_m(p),\qquad g_p=\frac{\varphi(m)}{f_p},
$$

where $\operatorname{ord}_1(p)=1$. Indeed, the prime-to-$p$ part is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension), and the $p$-power part is totally ramified by the preceding [Eisenstein polynomial](../../../arithmetic.md#eisenstein-polynomial). Notice the exception $p=2,a=1$: its [ramification index](../../../arithmetic.md#ramification-index) is one, consistently with $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2m})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$ for odd $m$. Thus finite [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics) occurs only at the indicated conductor primes, with that exception.

We now prove [cyclotomic Artin reciprocity over the rational numbers](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-artin-reciprocity-over-the-rational-numbers), using arithmetic [Frobenius automorphisms](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism). If $\ell\nmid n$ and $\mathfrak L$ lies over $\ell$, then [Frobenius](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism) sends the reduction of $\zeta_n$ to its $\ell$th power. Distinct nth [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity) remain distinct modulo $\mathfrak L$, since $X^n-1$ has separable reduction. Hence the [Artin symbol](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-symbol) is exactly

$$
\operatorname{Art}((\ell))=\sigma_\ell.
$$

Extend multiplicatively to the [group](../../../group.md) $I(n)$ of rational [fractional ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#fractional-ideal) prime to $n$. Such an [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) has a unique positive rational generator $q$ whose numerator and denominator are prime to $n$. The formula is

$$
\operatorname{Art}((q))=\sigma_{q\bmod n}.
$$

Every unit residue modulo $n$ has a positive integer representative prime to $n$, so this [Artin map](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-reciprocity-law) is surjective. Its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) is precisely the subgroup $P_{n\infty}$ generated by positive $q\equiv1\pmod n$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{I(n)/P_{n\infty}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q).}
$$

This proves the [Artin reciprocity law](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-reciprocity-law) for this extension, including its principal-ray kernel. The infinite component of the [modulus](../../../complex-analysis.md#modulus) records positivity and therefore the possible action of [complex conjugation](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-conjugation).

The [Kronecker–Weber theorem](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#kronecker-weber-theorem) says that **every finite abelian extension of $\mathbb Q$ is contained in a cyclotomic field**. We prove its quadratic case by identifying the [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics) that a [quadratic number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#quadratic-field) must have.

For an odd prime $\ell$, the cyclic [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)/\mathbb Q$ has exactly one subgroup of index two. Its fixed field $F_\ell$ is quadratic and is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) at every finite prime other than $\ell$. For a squarefree integer $d$, the [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ is $d$ if $d\equiv1\pmod4$, and $4d$ otherwise. A nontrivial quadratic [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) supported on the odd prime $\ell$ must therefore be

$$
\ell^*=(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell.
$$

Indeed $d=\pm1$ cannot give a nontrivial extension unramified at two, whereas exactly one of $\ell,-\ell$ is congruent to one modulo four. Thus

$$
F_\ell=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\ell^*})\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell).
$$

This identifies the [quadratic subfield of a prime cyclotomic field](../../../galois-theory.md#quadratic-subfield-of-a-prime-cyclotomic-field) using [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics), without a Gaussian-sum computation.

Now let $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ with $d$ squarefree. Multiply $d$ by $\prod_{\ell\mid d,\ \ell\text{ odd}}\ell^*$. Every odd prime then occurs with even exponent, so its [square class](../../../galois-theory.md#square-class) is represented by one of $1,-1,2,-2$. Thus adjoining the square roots of the $\ell^*$ removes all possible odd-prime [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics); the remaining quadratic factor is contained in

$$
\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2),\qquad \sqrt2=\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}.
$$

The original $\sqrt d$ is a rational multiple of a product of these square roots. It belongs to the [compositum](../../../algebra.md#field-compositum) of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$ and the prime [cyclotomic fields](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-field) just used. This [compositum](../../../algebra.md#field-compositum) is itself contained in a [cyclotomic field](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-field), proving the theorem for $F$.

The sharper conductor statement follows from the same [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics) calculation. For the fundamental [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) $D$ of $F$, put

$$
D=D_2\prod_{\substack{\ell\mid D\\\ell\text{ odd}}}\ell^*,\qquad D_2\in\{1,-4,8,-8\}.
$$

The possibilities for $D_2$ follow immediately from the quadratic [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) formula. The corresponding fields are trivial, $\mathbb Q(i)$, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})$, lying in the [cyclotomic fields](../../../galois-theory.md#cyclotomic-field) of conductors $1,4,8,8$. Their conductors are coprime to the odd primes in the product. Hence [quadratic Kronecker–Weber theorem by ramification](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#quadratic-kronecker-weber-theorem-by-ramification) gives the explicit conclusion

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{|D|}).}
$$

Write $\mathcal O$ for the [valuation ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#valuation-ring) of $K$, $\pi$ for a [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer), $v(\pi)=1$, and $k=\mathbb F_q$ for the [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field). A useful [strong form of Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#strong-form-of-hensel-lemma) is the following: if $f\in\mathcal O[X]$ and $a_0\in\mathcal O$ satisfy

$$
v(f(a_0))>2v(f'(a_0)),
$$

then there is a root $a\in\mathcal O$ with $v(a-a_0)>v(f'(a_0))$, unique among roots in that ball. If $f(a_0)\ne0$, its distance satisfies $v(a-a_0)=v(f(a_0))-v(f'(a_0))$.

Here is a proof by successive correction. Put $s=v(f'(a_0))$, $d_j=v(f(a_j))$, and

$$
a_{j+1}=a_j-\frac{f(a_j)}{f'(a_j)}.
$$

Stop if a root has already been reached. Otherwise the correction has [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) $d_j-s>s$. Integral Taylor expansions show that $v(f'(a_{j+1}))=s$ and

$$
d_{j+1}\ge2(d_j-s),\qquad d_{j+1}-2s\ge2(d_j-2s).
$$

Thus $d_j-2s$ grows at least geometrically, the corrections tend to zero, and [completeness](../../../topological-analysis.md#completeness) gives a limit $a$ with $f(a)=0$. The first correction has strictly smaller [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) than every later nonzero correction, proving the asserted distance. If $x,y$ are in the ball $v(x-a_0),v(y-a_0)>s$, then

$$
f(x)-f(y)=(x-y)\bigl(f'(a_0)+c\bigr),\qquad v(c)>s.
$$

The second factor cannot vanish. Two roots in the ball must therefore coincide. This proves [Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#hensel-s-lemma); in particular a simple root modulo $\pi$ has a unique lift with that residue.

We use this to establish [existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions](../../../arithmetic.md#existence-and-uniqueness-of-unramified-local-extensions). For each integer $d\ge1$, choose a monic [irreducible polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#irreducible-polynomial) $\bar f\in k[X]$ of degree $d$ and lift it to a monic $f\in\mathcal O[X]$. Reduction and [Gauss lemma](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry) show that $f$ remains irreducible over $K$. Let

$$
A=\mathcal O[X]/(f),\qquad L=K[X]/(f).
$$

The [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) $A$ is finite free and complete over $\mathcal O$, and $A/\pi A\cong\mathbb F_{q^d}$. An element outside $\pi A$ is invertible: lift an inverse from the [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field) and invert the remaining $1+\pi z$ by a convergent geometric series. Every nonzero element of $A$ is $\pi^t$ times such a unit, since $\bigcap_t\pi^t A=0$ in a finite free [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). Thus $A$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#discrete-valuation-ring), its fraction field is $L$, and

$$
[L:K]=d=[k_L:k],\qquad e(L/K)=1.
$$

This constructs an [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) of each degree.

Conversely, let $M/K$ be an [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) of degree $d$. Its [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field) is the unique degree-$d$ [finite field extension](../../../algebra.md#finite-field-extension) $\mathbb F_{q^d}/\mathbb F_q$. Choose a residue root of $\bar f$ in it. The root is simple, so [Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#hensel-s-lemma) lifts it to a root of $f$ in $M$. This embeds $L$ into $M$, and equality of degrees makes it an isomorphism. All $d$ residue conjugates lift in $L$ itself; hence $L/K$ is [Galois](../../../galois-theory.md#finite-galois-extension), and reduction identifies its [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) with the cyclic [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) of the [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field) extension. Its distinguished [Frobenius automorphism](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism) reduces to $x\mapsto x^q$.

**There is exactly one unramified extension $K_d$ of each degree inside a fixed algebraic closure**, and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(K_d/K)\cong C_d,\qquad K_d=K(\mu_{q^d-1}),\qquad K_a\subseteq K_b\iff a\mid b.}
$$

For the middle equality, every nonzero element of $\mathbb F_{q^d}$ lifts uniquely to a $(q^d-1)$st [root of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity), because that exponent is prime to $p$. A primitive such residue generates the [finite field extension](../../../algebra.md#finite-field-extension), so its lift generates $K_d$. Taking the union gives the [maximal unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#maximal-unramified-extension), with [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.

For a [local field](../../../arithmetic.md#local-field) $F$ containing the Nth [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity), fix the [Local Artin map](../../../arithmetic.md#local-artin-map) with arithmetic [Frobenius](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism) convention. The [Hilbert norm residue symbol](../../../arithmetic.md#hilbert-norm-residue-symbol) can be defined by

$$
(a,b)_{F,N}=\frac{\operatorname{rec}_F(b)(a^{1/N})}{a^{1/N}}\in\mu_N.
$$

It is independent of the chosen root, continuous and bimultiplicative, factors through $F^\times/(F^\times)^N$ in each variable, and gives a nondegenerate pairing on that finite [group](../../../group.md). It satisfies $(a,b)_{F,N}=(b,a)_{F,N}^{-1}$ and $(a,1-a)_{F,N}=1$ for $a\ne1$. Its [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) interpretation is

$$
(a,b)_{F,N}=1\iff b\in N_{F(a^{1/N})/F}F(a^{1/N})^\times.
$$

For $N=2$ the [quadratic Hilbert symbol](../../../arithmetic.md#quadratic-hilbert-symbol) is symmetric and sign-valued. It is also characterized by the existence of a nonzero solution of $z^2=ax^2+by^2$. Moreover $(a,-a)_F=1$, and therefore $(a,a)_F=(a,-1)_F$. We now compute both the [square-class groups](../../../galois-theory.md#square-class-group-of-a-field) and the pairing explicitly.

For odd $p$, write $a=p^\alpha u$ with $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. An element is a square exactly when $\alpha$ is even and the residue of $u$ is a square in $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Necessity follows by reduction; sufficiency follows from the simple-root form of [Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#hensel-s-lemma) applied to $X^2-u$, since $2x$ is then a unit. If $\eta$ is any unit with nonsquare residue, this gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q_p^\times/(\mathbb Q_p^\times)^2\cong C_2\times C_2,\qquad\text{representatives }1,\eta,p,\eta p\quad(p\text{ odd}).}
$$

The nonsquare unit defines an [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) quadratic extension. Its [field norms](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) include every unit and have even [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation), as follows from the local norm calculation below. Thus $(\eta,u)_p=1$ for every unit, but $(\eta,p)_p=-1$. Finally $(p,p)_p=(p,-1)_p=(-1/p)$, by symmetry and the preceding unit calculation. Bimultiplicativity now proves [explicit quadratic Hilbert symbols over the p-adic numbers](../../../arithmetic.md#explicit-quadratic-hilbert-symbols-over-the-p-adic-numbers):

$$
\boxed{(p^\alpha u,p^\beta v)_p=(-1)^{\alpha\beta(p-1)/2}\left(\frac up\right)^\beta\left(\frac vp\right)^\alpha\quad(p\text{ odd}).}
$$

Here the factors are [Legendre symbols](../../../number-theory.md#legendre-symbol) of the nonzero residues. The formula works for arbitrary integer [valuations](../../../algebra.md#valuation) $\alpha,\beta$, since only their parity matters.

At $p=2$, the square of every odd integer is one modulo eight. Conversely, for an odd $u\equiv1\pmod8$, apply the [strong form of Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#strong-form-of-hensel-lemma) to $X^2-u$ at $1$: the error has [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) at least three, while the [derivative](../../../calculus.md#derivative) has [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) one. Thus the [unit square classes of the p-adic integers](../../../arithmetic.md#unit-square-classes-of-the-p-adic-integers) are precisely their four residues modulo eight. Combining this with parity of the [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q_2^\times/(\mathbb Q_2^\times)^2\cong C_2^3,\qquad\text{basis }[-1],[2],[5],\quad\text{representatives }\pm1,\pm2,\pm5,\pm10.}
$$

For an odd unit put

$$
\epsilon(u)=\frac{u-1}{2}\pmod2,\qquad\omega(u)=\frac{u^2-1}{8}\pmod2.
$$

Inspection of the residues $1,3,5,7$ gives $[u]=[-1]^{\epsilon(u)}[5]^{\omega(u)}$. It remains to compute six basis pairings. Explicit [field norms](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) give

$$
(-1,2)_2=(-1,5)_2=(2,2)_2=(5,5)_2=1:
$$

indeed $2=1^2+1^2$, $5=1^2+2^2$, $2=2^2-2\cdot1^2$, and $5=5^2-5\cdot2^2$.

In contrast $(-1,-1)_2=-1$. To justify the obstruction even for rational 2-adic coordinates, write $x=2^t x_0$, $y=2^t y_0$ with $x_0,y_0$ integral and at least one odd. Then $v_2(x_0^2+y_0^2)$ is zero if exactly one is odd, and one if both are odd. A sum of squares equal to the unit $-1$ consequently forces $t=0$ and exactly one coordinate odd. Its residue modulo four would then be one, whereas $-1$ is three.

Similarly $(2,5)_2=-1$. If $x^2-2y^2$ is a unit, the [valuations](../../../algebra.md#valuation) of the two terms have opposite parity, so cannot cancel. It follows that $x$ is an odd integral element and $y$ is integral. Modulo eight its [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) is then $1$ or $7$, never $5$. These signs, symmetry, and bimultiplicativity give

$$
\boxed{(2^\alpha u,2^\beta v)_2=(-1)^{\epsilon(u)\epsilon(v)+\alpha\omega(v)+\beta\omega(u)}.}
$$

The two boxed formulas determine the [quadratic Hilbert symbol](../../../arithmetic.md#quadratic-hilbert-symbol) on all nonzero elements, including every rational prime $p$.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

We give the full local-field norm-index argument. Let $G=\langle\sigma\rangle$ be cyclic of order $n$. On an additive $G$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) set $D=\sigma-1$ and $N=1+\sigma+\cdots+\sigma^{n-1}$. The two cyclic [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [groups](../../../group.md) are

$$
\widehat H^0(G,M)=M^G/NM,\qquad\widehat H^{-1}(G,M)=\ker N/DM.
$$

When these are finite, the [Herbrand quotient](../../../group-theory.md#herbrand-quotient) is $h_G(M)=|\widehat H^0(G,M)|/|\widehat H^{-1}(G,M)|$. The periodic complex alternating $D$ and $N$ yields a six-term periodic exact sequence from a short exact sequence of [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). Taking alternating products of orders in that exact sequence proves

$$
h_G(M)=h_G(M')h_G(M'')\qquad(0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0).
$$

For a finite [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), $|NM|=|M|/|\ker N|$ and $|DM|=|M|/|\ker D|$ show that the two [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [groups](../../../group.md) have equal order. Thus the [Herbrand quotient of a finite module](../../../group-theory.md#herbrand-quotient-of-a-finite-module) is one. For the trivial integral [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $\mathbb Z$, the two groups are $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and zero, giving $h_G(\mathbb Z)=n$.

We also need [cyclic cohomology of a regular lattice](../../../group-theory.md#cyclic-cohomology-of-a-regular-lattice). In $R[G]$, fixed coefficient vectors are constant, and every such vector is a norm of a basis vector times its coefficient. The [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) of $N$ consists of vectors whose coefficient sum is zero. For such a vector $(a_i)$, solve $b_{i-1}-b_i=a_i$ successively around the cycle; the final equation holds precisely because $\sum_i a_i=0$. Thus $\ker N=DR[G]$. Both [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [groups](../../../group.md) vanish, so $h_G(R[G])=1$, for $R=\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Z_p$, and likewise for finite direct sums.

Now take a cyclic extension $L/K$ of [p-adic fields](../../../arithmetic.md#p-adic-field) of degree $n$. Write $U_L^r=1+\mathfrak p_L^r$. For $r>v_L(p)/(p-1)$, the convergent [p-adic logarithm](../../../arithmetic.md#p-adic-logarithm) and [p-adic exponential](../../../arithmetic.md#p-adic-exponential-function) give mutually inverse $G$-equivariant [group isomorphisms](../../../algebra.md#group-isomorphism)

$$
U_L^r\cong\mathfrak p_L^r,
$$

where the right side is additive. The convergence assertion follows from the estimates $v_L(x^j/j)\ge jv_L(x)-v_L(p)v_p(j)$ and $v_L(x^j/j!)\ge jv_L(x)-v_L(p)j/(p-1)$; formal inverse identities therefore hold on this ball.

By the [normal basis theorem](../../../galois-theory.md#normal-basis-theorem), choose $\theta$ so that $\{\sigma^i\theta\}$ is a $K$-[basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) of $L$. The lattice $A=\sum_i\mathcal O_K\sigma^i\theta$ is a regular $\mathcal O_K[G]$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). It is a finite direct sum of regular $\mathbb Z_p[G]$-[modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), hence its [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) vanishes. The lattices $A$ and $\mathfrak p_L^r$ are commensurable: their intersection has finite index in both. The exact sequences with these finite quotients establish finiteness of the [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [groups](../../../group.md) and show $h_G(\mathfrak p_L^r)=1$. Thus $h_G(U_L^r)=1$. Since $\mathcal O_L^\times/U_L^r$ is finite, $h_G(\mathcal O_L^\times)=1$ as well.

The $G$-equivariant [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) exact sequence is

$$
1\longrightarrow\mathcal O_L^\times\longrightarrow L^\times\xrightarrow{v_L}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

Its last term has trivial $G$-action. The multiplication rule gives the [Herbrand quotient of the local multiplicative group](../../../group-theory.md#herbrand-quotient-of-the-local-multiplicative-group):

$$
h_G(L^\times)=n.
$$

All the [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [groups](../../../group.md) here are finite by the lattice argument and the exact sequence; finiteness of the norm index has not been assumed.

For completeness we prove the needed [Hilbert theorem 90](../../../galois-theory.md#hilbert-s-theorem-90). If $a\in L^\times$ has [field norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) one, put $A_0=1$ and $A_i=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^j(a)$. The linear map $c\mapsto b=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}A_i\sigma^i(c)$ is not zero by [linear independence of distinct field embeddings](../../../galois-theory.md#linear-independence-of-distinct-field-embeddings). Choose $c$ with $b\ne0$. The relation $A_n=1$ gives $a\sigma(b)=b$, so $a=b/\sigma(b)$. This proves $\widehat H^{-1}(G,L^\times)=0$. The zeroth [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) [group](../../../group.md) is $K^\times/N_{L/K}L^\times$. Consequently the [cyclic local norm index](../../../arithmetic.md#cyclic-local-norm-index) is

$$
\boxed{[K^\times:N_{L/K}L^\times]=[L:K]=n.}
$$

Writing $n=ef$, the [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) of a [field norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) is $v_K(Nx)=f v_L(x)$. The valuation quotient has order $f$, and a norm with [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) zero comes from a unit. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[\mathcal O_K^\times:N_{L/K}\mathcal O_L^\times]=e.}
$$

In particular every unit is a norm in an [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension). These results give the norm assertions used in the [quadratic Hilbert symbol](../../../arithmetic.md#quadratic-hilbert-symbol) computation.

We finish with the [Hasse norm theorem](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#hasse-norm-theorem): **for a cyclic extension of number fields, an element is a global norm if and only if it is a norm at every place**. More precisely the local norm is the norm from the product $L\otimes_K K_v$; in a [Galois extension](../../../galois-theory.md#finite-galois-extension) its factors have the same norm subgroup. The forward implication is immediate. For the converse associate to $a\in K^\times$ the [cyclic algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra) $(L/K,\sigma,a)$. The [splitting criterion for a cyclic algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#splitting-criterion-for-a-cyclic-algebra), proved below, identifies its splitting with $a\in N_{L/K}L^\times$. At a place where the tensor product of fields has several factors, this [algebra](../../../algebra.md) is a matrix algebra over the [cyclic algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra) of one decomposition-field factor, with the generator restricted to its cyclic decomposition subgroup. Thus the local norm assumption makes its [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) zero at every place. Injectivity in the [Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether theorem](../../../associative-algebra.md#albert-brauer-hasse-noether-theorem) makes the global [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) zero, giving the desired global norm. This is the required proof outline; cyclicity is essential to this reduction.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

A [central simple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#central-simple-algebra) over $K$ is a finite-dimensional associative $K$-[algebra](../../../algebra.md) with no nonzero proper two-sided [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) and with center $K$. The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../associative-algebra.md#artin-wedderburn-theorem) writes it uniquely as $M_r(D)$ with $D$ a central [division algebra](../../../algebra.md#division-algebra). After a finite separable [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) extension it becomes $M_d$, so its dimension is $d^2$, and $d$ is its [degree of a central simple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#degree-of-a-central-simple-algebra). Passing from $D$ to a [matrix algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra) over $D$ does not change its [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class).

The [Brauer group](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-group) consists of these classes, with product induced by [tensor product](../../../linear-algebra.md#tensor-product). Extending to a common [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) shows that a [tensor product of central simple algebras](../../../associative-algebra.md#tensor-product-of-central-simple-algebras) is again central simple; its two-sided [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) and center descend from the corresponding full [matrix algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra). The neutral class is $[K]$, and the inverse of $[A]$ is $[A^{\mathrm{op}}]$, because

$$
A\otimes_K A^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\operatorname{End}_K(A),\qquad a\otimes b\mapsto(x\mapsto axb).
$$

The displayed map becomes the standard isomorphism for matrices over a [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field), so it is already an isomorphism over $K$. Thus [Brauer classes](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) measure precisely the obstruction to being a split [matrix algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra).

An explicit family is supplied by [cyclic algebras](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra). Let $L/K$ be cyclic of degree $n$, with generator $\sigma$, and let $a\in K^\times$. Define

$$
A=(L/K,\sigma,a)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}Lu^i,\qquad ux=\sigma(x)u,\qquad u^n=a.
$$

This is an associative [algebra](../../../algebra.md): form the skew polynomial ring with relation $ux=\sigma(x)u$ and quotient by the central relation $u^n-a$. Reduction of powers gives the displayed $n^2$-dimensional $K$-[basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis). An element commuting with every $x\in L$ has no $u^i$ coefficient for $i\ne0$, since $\sigma^i$ is not the identity. Commuting also with $u$ forces the remaining coefficient into $K$. Hence the center is $K$.

Here is an explicit splitting construction. On $L^n$, with [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}$, put

$$
D(x)e_j=\sigma^j(x)e_j,\qquad Ue_j=e_{j-1}\ (j\ge1),\qquad Ue_0=a e_{n-1}.
$$

Then $UD(x)=D(\sigma x)U$ and $U^n=aI$. After extending scalars to $L$, the separable decomposition $L\otimes_KL\cong\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}L$ supplies all diagonal matrices, and their products with powers of $U$ supply all [matrix units](../../../vector-space.md#matrix-unit). Therefore

$$
A\otimes_KL\cong M_n(L).
$$

Any nonzero proper two-sided [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of $A$ would extend to one of $M_n(L)$, which is impossible. Thus this construction really gives a [central simple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#central-simple-algebra), with [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) $L$.

Its central arithmetic feature is the [splitting criterion for a cyclic algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#splitting-criterion-for-a-cyclic-algebra):

$$
\boxed{(L/K,\sigma,a)\text{ splits}\iff a\in N_{L/K}L^\times.}
$$

If $a=Nc$, let $L$ act by multiplication on the $K$-[vector space](../../../vector-space.md) $L$, and let $u$ act as $c\sigma$. Its nth power is multiplication by $Nc=a$, so this gives a representation $A\to\operatorname{End}_K(L)$. It is injective by simplicity and an isomorphism by equal dimension. Conversely, if $A\cong\operatorname{End}_K(V)$ with $\dim_K V=n$, its embedded field $L$ makes $V$ one-dimensional over $L$. The relation for $u$ says that $u$ is $\sigma$-semilinear. In an $L$-[basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) it must have the form $c\sigma$, whose nth power is $Nc$. The relation $u^n=a$ forces $a=Nc$.

More generally the [crossed-product algebra of a Galois extension](../../../associative-algebra.md#crossed-product-algebra-of-a-galois-extension) attaches an [algebra](../../../algebra.md) to a multiplicative two-cocycle. Changing the cocycle by a coboundary rescales its basis and preserves the [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class). Descent of a split [matrix algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#matrix-algebra) identifies

$$
\ker\bigl(\operatorname{Br}(K)\to\operatorname{Br}(L)\bigr)\cong H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),L^\times).
$$

For cyclic $L/K$, cyclic [Tate cohomology](../../../group-theory.md#tate-cohomology-of-a-finite-group) identifies this second [cohomology group](../../../cohomology.md#cohomology-group) with $K^\times/NL^\times$. In a crossed product, rescale successive cyclic basis elements to powers of a single $u$; the remaining datum is $u^n=a$, changed by a [field norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) when $u$ is rescaled. This explains why [cyclic algebras](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra) give every class split by a cyclic extension, and why multiplication of their parameters is addition of their [Brauer classes](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class).

For a [finite field](../../../algebra.md#finite-field) $K=\mathbb F_q$, every finite [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) $L=\mathbb F_{q^n}$ is cyclic over $K$. The [field norm](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#field-norm) is the power map $x\mapsto x^{(q^n-1)/(q-1)}$ on its cyclic multiplicative [group](../../../group.md), and is onto $\mathbb F_q^\times$. Thus every relative [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) is zero, and every [central simple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#central-simple-algebra) has zero class:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb F_q)=0.}
$$

For a non-Archimedean [local field](../../../arithmetic.md#local-field) $K$, the valuation classification of central [division algebras](../../../algebra.md#division-algebra) shows that every [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) splits over a finite [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension). For the degree-$n$ [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) $L/K$ with arithmetic [Frobenius](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism) $\sigma$, units are norms and norm [valuations](../../../algebra.md#valuation) are multiples of $n$. Hence the cyclic relative [Brauer group](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-group) is $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, represented by $a=\pi^r$. The compatible [local Brauer invariant](../../../associative-algebra.md#local-brauer-invariant) is

$$
\operatorname{inv}_K[(L/K,\sigma,a)]=\frac{v_K(a)}n\pmod{\mathbb Z}.
$$

The union over all $n$ gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Br}(K)\cong\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.}
$$

A class with invariant $r/n$ in lowest terms has a central [division algebra](../../../algebra.md#division-algebra) representative of [degree of a central simple algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#degree-of-a-central-simple-algebra) $n$. This is the local valuation classification used above; the explicit [cyclic algebras](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra) exhibit all of its invariants. At the Archimedean places, $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb C)=0$, while $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb R)=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$: its nontrivial class is the [quaternion algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#quaternion-algebra) $(\mathbb C/\mathbb R,\text{conjugation},-1)$, since complex [norms](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) are positive.

For a [number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#number-field), the [Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether theorem](../../../associative-algebra.md#albert-brauer-hasse-noether-theorem) describes the [Brauer group](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-group) by the exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Br}(K)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_v\operatorname{Br}(K_v)\xrightarrow{\ \sum_v\operatorname{inv}_v\ }\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus its classes are exactly finite-support families of local invariants with sum zero; real invariants are $0$ or $1/2$ and complex invariants are zero. Every global [Brauer class](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-class) has a cyclic splitting extension, so [cyclic algebras](../../../associative-algebra.md#cyclic-algebra) also realize all global classes. If $\chi(\sigma)=1/n$, their local invariants are $\chi_v(\operatorname{rec}_{K_v}(a))$, with $\chi_v$ restricted to the local decomposition subgroup. The sum-zero relation for a principal parameter is the invariant form of [Artin reciprocity](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-reciprocity-law). Injectivity supplies the local-to-global splitting test, which yields the [Hasse norm theorem](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#hasse-norm-theorem) for cyclic extensions as explained in the preceding solution. Here the local valuation classification and the global invariant theorem are the structural theorems used to describe the [Brauer groups](../../../associative-algebra.md#brauer-group); the construction and norm-splitting calculation have been proved explicitly.

Fix a prime $\mathfrak q$ of $E$ over $\mathfrak p$, and a prime $\mathfrak r$ of $LE$ over $\mathfrak q$. Let $\mathfrak P$ be its restriction to $L$. Inside the completed compositum, put

$$
F=K_{\mathfrak p},\qquad J=E_{\mathfrak q},\qquad U=L_{\mathfrak P}.
$$

The assumption means $U/F$ is an [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension). Its [residue degree](../../../arithmetic.md#residue-degree) is some $f$; the completed extension $(LE)_{\mathfrak r}/J$ is the corresponding compositum $UJ/J$. It suffices to prove that this is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension), regardless of the [ramification](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ramification-mathematics) of $J/F$.

Here is a proof of [unramified extensions remain unramified under base change](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extensions-remain-unramified-under-base-change) using the classification already established. Choose an integral generator $\alpha$ of $U/F$ whose monic [minimal polynomial](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#minimal-polynomial) $g\in\mathcal O_F[X]$ has irreducible separable reduction of degree $f$. Write $k_F=\mathbb F_q$ and $k_J=\mathbb F_{q^r}$. Let $J_f/J$ be the degree-$f$ [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension). Its [residue field](../../../commutative-algebra.md#residue-field) $\mathbb F_{q^{rf}}$ contains $\mathbb F_{q^f}$, so $\bar g$ has a simple root there. [Hensel lemma](../../../arithmetic.md#hensel-s-lemma) lifts this root to a root of $g$ in $J_f$. This gives an $F$-embedding $U\hookrightarrow J_f$.

An [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) of a [local field](../../../arithmetic.md#local-field) is [Galois](../../../galois-theory.md#finite-galois-extension), so all $F$-embeddings of $U$ into a fixed algebraic closure have the same image. The original $U$ therefore lies in $J_f$, and $UJ$ is an intermediate field of the [unramified extension](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) $J_f/J$. Its [ramification index](../../../arithmetic.md#ramification-index) is one. Since $\mathfrak q$ and $\mathfrak r$ were arbitrary,

$$
\boxed{e(\mathfrak r/\mathfrak q)=1\text{ for every prime above }\mathfrak p.}
$$

This proof also explains why no linear-disjointness assumption on $L$ and $E$, and no assumption that $E/K$ is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension), is needed.

<h4 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#5/ii)

A [modulus of a number field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#modulus-of-a-number-field) is $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\mathfrak m_\infty$, with $\mathfrak m_0$ a nonzero integral [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) and $\mathfrak m_\infty$ a subset of the real places. Let $I_{\mathfrak m}$ be the [group](../../../group.md) of [fractional ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#fractional-ideal) prime to $\mathfrak m_0$, and let $P_{\mathfrak m}$ consist of principal [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $(a)$ with $a\equiv1$ at each prime power in $\mathfrak m_0$ and $a>0$ at each selected real place. The [ray class group](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-group) is

$$
\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)=I_{\mathfrak m}/P_{\mathfrak m}.
$$

The [ray class field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-field) $K_{\mathfrak m}$ is the finite [abelian extension](../../../galois-theory.md#abelian-extension) for which the [Artin map](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-reciprocity-law) has [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) $P_{\mathfrak m}$ and induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak m}/K)$. Equivalently it is the maximal [abelian extension](../../../galois-theory.md#abelian-extension) whose conductor divides $\mathfrak m$.

To compute its order use the [ray class exact sequence](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-exact-sequence), including signatures:

$$
\mathcal O_K^\times\longrightarrow (\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m_0)^\times\times\{\pm1\}^{|\mathfrak m_\infty|}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(K)\longrightarrow1.
$$

The middle map lifts a residue and sign tuple to an element of $K$ by [weak approximation theorem](../../../arithmetic.md#weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values), then takes its principal [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) as a ray class. Different lifts differ by a principal ray factor. Its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) consists exactly of tuples represented by global units. Every ordinary [ideal class](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-class) has a representative prime to the finite modulus, again by [weak approximation theorem](../../../arithmetic.md#weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values); this gives surjectivity of the last map. If $U_{\mathfrak m}$ denotes the [ray units](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-unit), the kernel of the unit map is $U_{\mathfrak m}$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../mathematics.md#chinese-remainder-theorem) and counting units in each prime-power quotient give the [ray class number formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-number-formula)

$$
\boxed{|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)|=\frac{h_K\,2^{|\mathfrak m_\infty|}\,\Phi_K(\mathfrak m_0)}{[\mathcal O_K^\times:U_{\mathfrak m}]},\qquad \Phi_K(\mathfrak m_0)=N\mathfrak m_0\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m_0}\left(1-\frac1{N\mathfrak p}\right).}
$$

First take $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Its [ring of integers](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ring-of-integers) is $\mathbb Z[\omega]$, where $\omega=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2$ and $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$. The [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#minkowski-s-bound) is $(2/\pi)\sqrt3<2$, so every [ideal class](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-class) has a representative of norm one and $h_K=1$. Its units are the six [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity). Modulo seven, the polynomial $X^2+X+1$ has distinct roots $2,4$, and therefore

$$
\mathcal O_K/(7)\cong\mathbb F_7\times\mathbb F_7,\qquad \omega\mapsto(2,4).
$$

The [unit group](../../../algebra.md#unit-group) of this quotient has order $36$. The unit $1+\omega$ maps to $(3,5)$, which has order six; consequently unit reduction is injective and $U_{(7)}=\{1\}$. The [ray class number formula](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-number-formula) gives $36/6=6$.

We also identify the quotient explicitly. Conjugation interchanges the two factors, so the residue [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) is $(x,y)\mapsto xy$. Its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) has order six and equals the image of the six units, since $3\cdot5=1\pmod7$. Hence

$$
\operatorname{Cl}_{(7)}(K)\cong\mathbb F_7^\times\cong C_6.
$$

The field $M=K(\zeta_7)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{21})$ has degree six over $K$, since the degrees over $\mathbb Q$ are twelve and two. It is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) outside seven by the cyclotomic computation and the base-change argument above. At primes over seven, $K$ has completion $\mathbb Q_7$, and $M/K$ is the tame degree-six cyclotomic extension; its conductor exponent is one. Its relative arithmetic [Artin symbol](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-symbol) at an [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $\mathfrak a$ prime to seven acts by

$$
\zeta_7\longmapsto\zeta_7^{N\mathfrak a\bmod7}.
$$

If $\mathfrak a=(a)$ with $a\equiv1\pmod{7\mathcal O_K}$, then $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a)\equiv1\pmod7$. Thus the [Artin map](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#artin-reciprocity-law) kills the principal ray subgroup. It factors through the order-six [ray class group](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-group); the residue [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) quotient computed above shows that it is onto the degree-six [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group), so the fields coincide. This proves the explicit [ray class field of Q of square root minus three modulo seven](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-field-of-q-of-square-root-minus-three-modulo-seven):

$$
\boxed{K_{(7)}=K(\zeta_7)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{21}),\qquad[K_{(7)}:K]=6.}
$$

Now take $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$, and write $s=\sqrt{-6}$. Its [ring of integers](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ring-of-integers) is $\mathbb Z[s]$, its [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) is $-24$, and its units are $\pm1$. The quotient by $(s)$ is

$$
\mathbb Z[s]/(s)\cong\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z,
$$

whose two units are both images of $\pm1$. The [ray class exact sequence](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-exact-sequence) therefore gives $\operatorname{Cl}_{(s)}(K)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(K)$.

For its [class number](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#class-number), the [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#minkowski-s-bound) is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{24}<4$, so each [ideal class](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-class) contains an integral [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of norm at most three. The primes of norms two and three are

$$
\mathfrak p_2=(2,s),\qquad\mathfrak p_3=(3,s),\qquad\mathfrak p_2^2=(2),\quad\mathfrak p_3^2=(3),\quad\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3=(s).
$$

These are the only nontrivial possibilities within the bound, and their [ideal classes](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-class) coincide and have order at most two. The [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $\mathfrak p_2$ is not principal: its being principal would give an integer solution to $x^2+6y^2=2$, which is impossible. Thus $h_K=2$, the [ideal class group](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ideal-class-group) is $C_2$, and the ray field is the [Hilbert class field](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#hilbert-class-field).

We verify its actual identity rather than stopping at the degree. Put

$$
H=K(\sqrt2)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3}),\qquad b=\frac{1+\sqrt{-3}}2.
$$

The two quadratic fields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ are distinct, so $[H:\mathbb Q]=4$ and $[H:K]=2$. The integral elements $1,\sqrt2,b,\sqrt2b$ are a rational [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis). Their trace matrix is the [Kronecker product](../../../vector-space.md#kronecker-product) of the two quadratic trace matrices, with [discriminants](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) $8$ and $-3$. Its [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant) is therefore $8^2(-3)^2=576$. The [discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#discriminant-index-formula-for-an-integral-lattice) shows that the field [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) divides $576$. On the other hand the relative [discriminant ideal](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#discriminant-ideal) formula gives

$$
|D_H|=|D_K|^{[H:K]}N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{H/K})=576\,N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{H/K}).
$$

The norm on the right is a positive integer, so equality is forced. Thus the relative [discriminant ideal](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#discriminant-ideal) is the unit ideal, and $H/K$ is [unramified](../../../arithmetic.md#unramified-extension) at every finite prime. There are no real places of $K$. The extension is quadratic and therefore [Abelian](../../../group.md#abelian-group); its degree equals $h_K$, identifying it with the [Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus six](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#hilbert-class-field-of-q-of-square-root-minus-six). Hence the [ray class field of Q of square root minus six modulo square root minus six](../../../algebraic-number-theory.md#ray-class-field-of-q-of-square-root-minus-six-modulo-square-root-minus-six) is

$$
\boxed{K_{(\sqrt{-6})}=K(\sqrt2)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3}),\qquad[K_{(\sqrt{-6})}:K]=2.}
$$
