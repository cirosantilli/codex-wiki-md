Use the order on [locale](../../../../../locale.md) maps in which $f\leq g$ means $f^*(a)\leq g^*(a)$ for every open $a$. A [Hausdorff locale](../../../../../hausdorff-locale.md) has a closed diagonal in its localic square. A [totally unordered locale](../../../../../totally-unordered-locale.md), or $T_U$ [locale](../../../../../locale.md), has discretely ordered hom-sets: every comparable pair of maps with any common domain is equal.

In the localic square of $Y$, let

$$
N=\bigvee_{a\wedge b=0}a\times b.
$$

This is the largest open disjoint from the diagonal: inverse image along the diagonal sends the rectangle $a\times b$ to $a\wedge b$. Hence $N$ is the [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) of its closure. If $f\leq g$, then

$$
(f,g)^*(a\times b)=f^*(a)\wedge g^*(b)\leq g^*(a)\wedge g^*(b)=0
$$

for every summand of $N$. Thus $(f,g)$ factors through the closure of the diagonal. For a [Hausdorff locale](../../../../../hausdorff-locale.md) that closure is the diagonal itself, so $f=g$. This proof of **Hausdorff implies $T_U$** does not require classical logic.

Here is an explicit classical counterexample to the converse implication from spatial Hausdorffness. Give $\mathbb R$ the [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) generated by the usual opens and by $\mathbb Q$. Every open has the form $U=V\cup(W\cap\mathbb Q)$, where $V,W$ are usual open sets and we may arrange $V\subseteq W$. The [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) is finer than the usual one, so the space is [Hausdorff](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). Put $B=\operatorname{RO}(\mathbb R)$, the [complete Boolean algebra](../../../../../complete-boolean-algebra.md) of ordinary [regular open elements](../../../../../regular-element-of-a-heyting-algebra.md), and write $\rho(V)=\operatorname{int}\overline V$. Restriction from this space to its rational and irrational subspaces, followed by Booleanization, gives [frame homomorphisms](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md)

$$
f^*(U)=\rho(V),\qquad g^*(U)=\rho(W),\qquad\mathcal O(X)\longrightarrow B.
$$

To justify these formulas, each subspace has its ordinary [subspace topology](../../../../../subspace-topology.md) and is dense in the ordinary real line. Regular-open algebras of a space and a dense subspace are isomorphic: restrict a regular open and take its interior-closure, with inverse obtained by taking interior-closure in the ambient space. Restriction is a [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md), and double negation is a [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) into its fixed-element [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md). These observations also show independence of the chosen representation of $U$. Since $V\subseteq W$, we have $f^*\leq g^*$, but $f^*(\mathbb Q)=0$ and $g^*(\mathbb Q)=1$. Thus **this Hausdorff space fails $T_U$ as a [locale](../../../../../locale.md)**.

For the remaining arguments about [connected locales](../../../../../connected-locale.md), use the classical-logic option: [positive opens of a locale](../../../../../positive-open-of-a-locale.md) are exactly the nonzero opens. There is a necessary positivity qualification. The empty [locale](../../../../../locale.md) has no positive opens, so the printed condition on those opens holds vacuously, although no point $1\to\varnothing$ exists. The point assertions therefore require $X$ to be positive. This qualification is automatic if “totally connected” includes nonemptiness; otherwise it must be added explicitly.

Let $X$ be positive and satisfy the condition on its [positive opens of a locale](../../../../../positive-open-of-a-locale.md). Put $F=\{a\in\mathcal O(X):a\ne0\}$. It contains $1$, excludes $0$, and is upward closed. If $a,b\in F$ had $a\wedge b=0$, their union would be a [positive open of a locale](../../../../../positive-open-of-a-locale.md) with a decomposition into two disjoint nonzero opens, contradicting its being a [connected locale](../../../../../connected-locale.md). Thus $a\wedge b\in F$. If $\bigvee_i a_i\ne0$, not all $a_i$ can be zero; classical logic supplies a nonzero summand. Hence **$F$ is a [completely prime filter](../../../../../completely-prime-filter.md)**, and its characteristic [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) defines a point $p:1\to X$.

The point is dense. Indeed $p^*(a)=0$ implies $a=0$ by the definition of $F$. In nucleus language the image of $p$ has nucleus $p_*p^*$; its value at $0$ is $p_*(0)=0$, so the image is a [dense sublocale](../../../../../dense-sublocale.md).

Conversely suppose an [open locale](../../../../../open-locale.md) has a dense point $p$. Density means $p^*(a)=0$ only if $a=0$, and classically this implies $p^*(a)=1$ for every [positive open of a locale](../../../../../positive-open-of-a-locale.md). If a positive open $u$ split as $a\vee b=u$ with $a\wedge b=0$ and both summands nonzero, then $p^*(a)=p^*(b)=1$, contradicting preservation of their [meet](../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Thus every [positive open of a locale](../../../../../positive-open-of-a-locale.md) is a [connected locale](../../../../../connected-locale.md), proving the converse under the declared logical convention.

Let $x:X\to1$ be the terminal map. For a positive [totally connected locale](../../../../../totally-connected-locale.md) $X$, the point above satisfies

$$
a\leq x^*p^*(a)\quad(a\in\mathcal O(X)),
$$

because the right side is $1$ when $a\ne0$ and both sides are $0$ otherwise. Hence $1_X\leq px$. If $f:X\to Y$ and $Y$ is a [totally unordered locale](../../../../../totally-unordered-locale.md), composition gives $f\leq fpx$, so $f=fpx$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f=(fp)\circ x,}
$$

which is the required constant factorization. For the empty source the factorization need not exist: the unique map $\varnothing\to\varnothing$ does not factor through $1$. Thus the positivity qualification matters for this assertion too.
