<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Work in the [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md) $A$, writing $a\to x$ for [Heyting implication](../../../../../heyting-implication.md). A [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) is an inflationary, [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) map preserving finite meets. The [open nucleus](../../../../../open-nucleus.md) and [closed nucleus](../../../../../closed-nucleus.md) are

$$
o(a)(x)=a\to x,\qquad c(a)(x)=a\vee x.
$$

Their [meet](../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) in the [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md) of [nuclei on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) is pointwise. The [distributivity of a Heyting algebra](../../../../../distributivity-of-a-heyting-algebra.md) and the defining adjunction for [Heyting implication](../../../../../heyting-implication.md) give

$$
(a\to x)\wedge(a\vee x)=x.
$$

If a [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) $l$ bounds both, then $l(a)=1$ and $a\leq l(0)$. Consequently $1=l(a)\leq l(l(0))=l(0)$, so $l$ is the top nucleus. Thus **$o(a)$ and $c(a)$ are complements in $N(A)$**.

For any [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) $j$, put $t_a=o(a)\wedge c(j(a))$. At an arbitrary $x$,

$$
t_a(x)=x\vee\bigl(j(a)\wedge(a\to x)\bigr)\leq j(x),
$$

since $j(a)\wedge(a\to x)\leq j(a)\wedge j(a\to x)=j(a\wedge(a\to x))\leq j(x)$. Moreover $t_a(a)=j(a)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{j=\bigvee_{a\in A}\bigl(o(a)\wedge c(j(a))\bigr).}
$$

Indeed the [join](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) is at most $j$, and its value at each $a$ is at least $j(a)$. This proves generation by the [open nuclei](../../../../../open-nucleus.md) and [closed nuclei](../../../../../closed-nucleus.md), without assuming that joins of [nuclei on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) are computed pointwise.

For the given [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) $h$, restrict the displayed [join](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) to $a=h(b)$ and denote the resulting [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) by $r(j)$. The same inequalities show $r(j)\leq j$ and $r(j)(h(b))=j(h(b))$. If $k$ has the same values on $h(B)$, its restricted formula is identical, so $r(j)\leq k$. Hence **$r(j)$ is the least representative of the [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md)**. We will also use the consequence

$$
\bigl(\forall b\in B,\ j(hb)\leq k(hb)\bigr)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r(j)\leq r(k).
$$

The least representatives form a [subframe](../../../../../subframe.md). For a family $j_i=r(j_i)$, let $J=\bigvee_i j_i$ in $N(A)$. Restricted monotonicity gives $j_i\leq r(J)$ for every $i$, hence $J=r(J)$. For two least representatives, distribution in $N(A)$ expresses their [meet](../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) as the [join](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md), over $b,d\in B$, of

$$
o(h(b\vee d))\wedge c\bigl(j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\bigr).
$$

Here $o(hb)\wedge o(hd)=o(h(b\vee d))$ and $c(u)\wedge c(v)=c(u\wedge v)$. Since

$$
j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\leq (j\wedge k)(h(b\vee d)),
$$

each summand is below $r(j\wedge k)$. The opposite inequality always holds, proving closure under binary meets. The top is $c(1)$ and is a least representative. Also $r(c(a))=c(a)$, because the summand at $b=0$ is $c(a)$; and $r(o(hd))=o(hd)$, because the summand at $b=d$ is $o(hd)$. It follows from the restricted normal form that **$N_B(A)$ is exactly the [subframe](../../../../../subframe.md) generated by $c(A)$ and $o(h(B))$**.

The map $c:A\to N_B(A)$ preserves finite meets by the formula above. A [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) $l$ bounds every $c(a_i)$ exactly when $a_i\leq l(0)$ for every $i$, equivalently $\bigvee_i a_i\leq l(0)$. The least such nucleus is $c(\bigvee_i a_i)$, so $c$ preserves arbitrary joins too, including $0$. Evaluation at $0$ recovers $a$, so this [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) is injective.

For the [pushout](../../../../../pushout.md), we prove the [universal property](../../../../../universal-property.md) explicitly. Let $u:A\to C$ be a [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) for which every $u(hb)$ has a [complement](../../../../../complement-of-a-set.md) $d_b$ in $C$. Define, for $j\in N_B(A)$,

$$
F(j)=\bigvee_{b\in B}d_b\wedge u(j(hb)).
$$

To prove preservation of arbitrary joins, let $u_*$ be the [right adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) of $u$ and set $k_z(a)=u_*(z\vee u(a))$ for $z\in C$. This is a [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md): inflationarity follows from the adjunction, finite-meet preservation follows from distribution and [meet](../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) preservation by $u_*$, and $u u_*\leq1$ gives $k_z k_z\leq k_z$; inflationarity gives the reverse inequality. Complementation and the adjunction now give

$$
F(j)\leq z\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall b,\ u(j(hb))\leq z\vee u(hb)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall b,\ j(hb)\leq k_z(hb)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ j\leq r(k_z).
$$

The last equivalence uses leastness of $j$. Thus $F$ is a [left adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md), so preserves arbitrary joins. For binary meets, expand $F(j)\wedge F(k)$. A term indexed by $b,d$ is

$$
d_{b\vee d}\wedge u\bigl(j(hb)\wedge k(hd)\bigr)\leq d_{b\vee d}\wedge u\bigl((j\wedge k)(h(b\vee d))\bigr).
$$

This proves $F(j)\wedge F(k)\leq F(j\wedge k)$; monotonicity proves the reverse inequality. The term at $b=0$ proves $F(1)=1$. Directly,

$$
F(c(a))=u(a),\qquad F(o(hd))=d_d.
$$

For the second identity the term at $b=d$ gives $d_d$, while every term is disjoint from $u(hd)$, because $hd\wedge(hd\to hb)\leq hb$.

Apply this construction first with $A=B$, $h=1_B$, target $N_B(A)$, $u=c_Ah$ and complements $d_b=o_A(hb)$. It gives a [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) $v_0:N(B)\to N_B(A)$ satisfying $v_0c_B=c_Ah$ and $v_0o_B=o_Ah$. Given any compatible pair $u:A\to C$, $v:N(B)\to C$, take $d_b=v(o_B(b))$; compatibility supplies the required complements. The constructed $F$ satisfies $Fc_A=u$ and $Fv_0=v$, since the latter equality holds on the open and closed generators of $N(B)$. Uniqueness follows because $c(A)$ and $o(h(B))$ generate $N_B(A)$. Hence **the square with edges $h,c_B,c_A,v_0$ is a [pushout](../../../../../pushout.md) in $\mathbf{Frm}$**.
