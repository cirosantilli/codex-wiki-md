<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For a [Boolean algebra](../../../../../boolean-algebra.md) $B$, write $\downarrow b$ for its principal [lattice ideal](../../../../../lattice-ideal.md). The [ideal frame of a Boolean algebra](../../../../../ideal-frame-of-a-boolean-algebra.md) has intersections as meets and ideals generated by unions as joins. The [pseudocomplement](../../../../../pseudocomplement.md) of an ideal $I$ is

$$
I^*=\{b\in B:\ b\wedge i=0\text{ for every }i\in I\}.
$$

In particular $(\downarrow b)^*=\downarrow\neg b$, so every principal ideal is complemented and regular.

If $B$ is complete, put $s=\bigvee_B I$. Then $I^*=\downarrow\neg s$ and $I^{**}=\downarrow s$. Thus all regular ideals are principal and complemented, so the corresponding [locale](../../../../../locale.md) is extremally disconnected.

Conversely, if the [ideal frame of a Boolean algebra](../../../../../ideal-frame-of-a-boolean-algebra.md) is extremally disconnected, each $I^{**}$ is complemented. Any complemented ideal is principal: if $J\vee K=B$ and $J\cap K=\{0\}$, the fact that their generated [join](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) contains $1$ supplies $b\in J,c\in K$ with $b\vee c=1$. Then $b\wedge c=0$, so $c=\neg b$; every $j\in J$ is disjoint from $c$ and therefore $j\leq b$. Thus $J=\downarrow b$. Write $I^{**}=\downarrow s$. Since $I\subseteq I^{**}$, $s$ bounds $I$. If $t$ is another upper bound, $I\subseteq\downarrow t$ implies $I^{**}\subseteq(\downarrow t)^{**}=\downarrow t$, so $s\leq t$. An arbitrary subset has the same upper bounds as the [lattice ideal](../../../../../lattice-ideal.md) it generates, so every subset of $B$ has a supremum. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Idl}(B)\text{ is extremally disconnected}\iff B\text{ is complete}.}
$$

Now put $A=\mathcal O(X)$ and $B=A_{\neg\neg}$. This is a [complete Boolean algebra](../../../../../complete-boolean-algebra.md), with arbitrary Boolean joins $\neg\neg\bigvee_A b_i$, so the preceding result makes $\gamma X$ extremally disconnected. We construct its map to $X$ using the permitted [Joyal extension lemma](../../../../../joyal-extension-lemma.md).

The map $\sigma:\operatorname{Idl}(B)\to B$, $I\mapsto\bigvee_B I$, is a [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md), with [right adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) $b\mapsto\downarrow b$. Thus $k(I)=\downarrow\bigvee_B I$ is a [nucleus on a frame](../../../../../nucleus-on-a-frame.md) whose fixed [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md) is $B$. It preserves finite joins: both $k(I\vee J)$ and $k(I)\vee k(J)$ equal $\downarrow(\bigvee_B I\vee_B\bigvee_B J)$, and $k(0)=0$. Consequently the [locale](../../../../../locale.md) with [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md) $B$ is a [flat sublocale](../../../../../flat-sublocale.md) of $\gamma X$. It is also the [Booleanization of a locale](../../../../../booleanization-of-a-locale.md) $X$, so it has its inclusion map to $X$.

The [Joyal extension lemma](../../../../../joyal-extension-lemma.md) says that a map from a [flat sublocale](../../../../../flat-sublocale.md) to a [compact locale](../../../../../compact-locale.md) that is a [regular locale](../../../../../regular-locale.md) extends uniquely to the ambient [locale](../../../../../locale.md). Apply it to this inclusion. Its [frame](../../../../../complete-heyting-algebra.md) formula gives

$$
\boxed{g^*(a)=\bigvee_{u\prec a}^{\operatorname{Idl}(B)}\downarrow(\neg\neg u),\qquad g:\gamma X\to X,}
$$

where $u\prec a$ is the [well-inside relation](../../../../../well-inside-relation.md) $\neg u\vee a=1$. This also proves directly that the map is surjective. Indeed $u\prec a$ implies $\neg\neg u\leq a$: [meet](../../../../../greatest-lower-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) $\neg u\vee a=1$ with $\neg\neg u$. Every member of the ideal $g^*(a)$ is therefore at most $a$ in $A$, since a finite Boolean [join](../../../../../least-upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) of its generators is below $\neg\neg(u_1\vee\cdots\vee u_n)\leq a$; the last inequality follows because $u_1\vee\cdots\vee u_n\prec a$. Regularity gives

$$
a=\bigvee_{u\prec a}u=\bigvee_{u\prec a}\neg\neg u=\bigvee_A\{b\in B:b\in g^*(a)\}.
$$

Thus $a$ can be recovered from $g^*(a)$, so $g^*$ is injective. A [locale](../../../../../locale.md) map with injective inverse-image [frame homomorphism](../../../../../frame-homomorphism.md) is a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md). We have obtained **an extremally disconnected $\gamma X$ and a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) $\gamma X\to X$**.
