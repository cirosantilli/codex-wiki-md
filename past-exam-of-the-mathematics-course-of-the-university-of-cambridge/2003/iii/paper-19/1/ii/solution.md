<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The PDF's displayed inequality uses the relation it purports to define and therefore is not itself a complete definition. The absorption order consistent with that inequality and with the requested result is

$$
\boxed{x\preceq y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x+y=x.}
$$

This is the reverse of the usual natural order of an [incline](../../../../../../incline.md). We specify it explicitly: using instead $x+y=y$ would make the finite-generation conclusion false in general.

[Idempotence](../../../../../../idempotence.md) gives $x+x=x$, proving reflexivity. If $x+y=x$ and $y+z=y$, then [associativity](../../../../../../associative-property.md) gives

$$
x+z=(x+y)+z=x+(y+z)=x+y=x,
$$

so the relation is transitive. If also $y+x=y$, [commutativity](../../../../../../commutativity.md) forces $x=y$. Thus it is even a [partial order](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md). In this order $x+y\preceq x$ follows because $(x+y)+x=x+y$. Absorption says that multiplying a [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) by any additional factors moves it upward in $\preceq$.

Let $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ generate the [incline](../../../../../../incline.md). Distributivity, [commutativity](../../../../../../commutativity.md) and [idempotence](../../../../../../idempotence.md) expand every element into a finite nonempty sum of [monomials](../../../../../../monomial.md) $g^a=g_1^{a_1}\cdots g_r^{a_r}$ with $a\in\mathbb N^r\setminus\{0\}$. A zero exponent means that factor is omitted; no multiplicative identity is assumed. If $a\le b$ componentwise, then $g^a+g^b=g^a$ by absorption, or by [idempotence](../../../../../../idempotence.md) when $a=b$.

We prove the needed [reverse-inclusion well-quasi-ordering of monomial ideals](../../../../../../reverse-inclusion-well-quasi-ordering-of-monomial-ideals.md) directly. Upward-closed [subsets](../../../../../../subset.md) of $\mathbb N^r$ form a [well-quasi-ordering](../../../../../../well-quasi-ordering.md) under reverse inclusion. For $r=1$ these are the tails, plus the [empty set](../../../../../../empty-set.md), ordered like $\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$. For the induction, write an upset $U\subseteq\mathbb N^{r+1}$ as increasing slices $U_0\subseteq U_1\subseteq\cdots$ in $\mathbb N^r$. Their [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) is an upset with a finite minimal basis by [Dickson lemma](../../../../../../dickson-s-lemma.md). To see that finite-basis fact here, $\mathbb N^r$ is a [well-quasi-ordering](../../../../../../well-quasi-ordering.md) by the finite product argument above, so its minimal elements cannot form an infinite [antichain](../../../../../../antichain.md); every element lies above a minimal one because its lower coordinate box is finite. All basis elements of the [union](../../../../../../set-union.md) appear by some finite slice, so the slices eventually stabilize.

Encode $U$ by the finite [word](../../../../../../string.md) of slices before stabilization and its final slice. By the finite-word theorem proved in the other alternative, and finite product closure, these encodings are a [well-quasi-ordering](../../../../../../well-quasi-ordering.md) with slice comparisons given by reverse inclusion. For two comparable encodings, let the earlier pre-stabilization slice $U_k$ match the later slice $V_{f(k)}$, where $f(k)\ge k$. Then

$$
U_k\supseteq V_{f(k)}\supseteq V_k.
$$

Beyond the earlier stabilization index, its final slice contains the later final slice and hence every later slice. Therefore $U\supseteq V$. This proves the induction, including empty slices and the empty upset.

For an [incline](../../../../../../incline.md) element $x$, choose a finite exponent support $F_x$ of a sum representing it and put $\uparrow F_x=\{b:\exists a\in F_x\ (a\le b)\}$. Given an infinite [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) of elements, the just-proved result supplies $i<j$ with $\uparrow F_{x_i}\supseteq\uparrow F_{x_j}$. Every [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) in the chosen sum for $x_j$ is therefore absorbed by a [monomial](../../../../../../monomial.md) in the sum for $x_i$. Adding these absorptions gives $x_i+x_j=x_i$, even if the [incline](../../../../../../incline.md) has further identities between terms. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{every finitely generated incline is well-quasi-ordered by }\preceq.}
$$

The order direction matters. On the positive integers take $x+y=\min(x,y)$ and multiplication to be ordinary addition of integers. This is an [incline](../../../../../../incline.md) generated by $1$. Its usual natural order $x+y=y$ is the reverse of the ordinary numerical order, and the [sequence](../../../../../../sequence.md) $1,2,3,\ldots$ is bad in that order. Its reverse absorption order is the ordinary numerical order, consistent with the theorem above.
