↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The extension must be compared with the actual contour length, **$L=100\,\mathrm m$**. For the illustrative $Q=0.2$ [force](../../../../../../../force.md) and the persistence-length-corrected chain, $z/L\simeq2.15\times10^{-3}$ and $Fb_K/(k_BT)\simeq6.44\times10^{-3}$. Both are small, so the small-[force](../../../../../../../force.md) [Langevin function](../../../../../../../langevin-function.md) expansion and entropic-extension approximation are consistent. Although the mean extension greatly exceeds the unforced millimetre-scale coil size, it remains far below the maximum contour length; that does not violate the model.

The material axial elongation at this [force](../../../../../../../force.md) would be only $F/(EA/L)\simeq5.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm m$, illustrating that most of the predicted coil extension is orientation and straightening rather than stretching the backbone. Thus **the order-of-magnitude entropic response is internally reasonable within the ideal-chain assumptions**, but its numerical optical-[force](../../../../../../../force.md) prediction is not unique. Solvent-dependent [excluded volume](../../../../../../../excluded-volume.md), the unusually large stated contour length, actual trap geometry, and surface interactions all matter to an experimental interpretation.
