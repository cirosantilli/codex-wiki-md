<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Approximate the gap by steady [Couette flow](../../../../../../couette-flow.md), neglecting edge effects and additional losses from membrane deformation. Its [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) gradient is $U/h$, so the [shear stress](../../../../../../shear-stress.md) is $\eta U/h$. The [force](../../../../../../force.md) on the [gliding bacterium](../../../../../../gliding-bacterium.md) and the rate of [viscous dissipation](../../../../../../viscous-dissipation.md) are

$$
F_d=\eta A\frac Uh,\qquad P=F_dU=\eta A\frac{U^2}{h}.
$$

The same power follows by integrating $\eta(U/h)^2$ over gap volume $Ah$. In SI units, $A=10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $h=10^{-7}\,\mathrm m$, and $U=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\dot\gamma=10\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\quad F_d=10^{-12}\,\mathrm N,\quad P=10^{-18}\,\mathrm W.}
$$

An [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) needs a duration or distance: **$E=10^{-18}\Delta t\,\mathrm J$ for $\Delta t$ in seconds**, equivalently $E=F_ds$ for travel distance $s$. Moving one micrometre takes one second and dissipates $10^{-18}\,\mathrm J$. This is the mechanical cost of the sheared film. A numerical biological efficiency would additionally require the motor's input power and the losses associated with its peristaltic actuation.
