<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A slit of height $D$ leaves two unrestricted directions. Its three-dimensional blobs now form a planar [self-avoiding walk](../../../../../../self-avoiding-walk.md), whose size exponent is $3/4$. The [polymer coil in slit confinement](../../../../../../polymer-coil-in-slit-confinement.md) therefore has in-plane size

$$
\boxed{R_\parallel\sim D(N/g)^{3/4}\sim bN^{3/4}(b/D)^{1/4}.}
$$

There is no uniquely preferred axial direction, and the chain is a coil rather than the one-dimensional blob string of a tube. Including a segment [excluded volume](../../../../../../excluded-volume.md) $v$, the [Flory approximation](../../../../../../flory-approximation.md) is $\mathcal F/(k_BT)\sim R_\parallel^2/(Nb^2)+vN^2/(R_\parallel^2D)$. Its minimum satisfies $R_\parallel^4\sim vN^3b^2/D$, agreeing with the displayed scaling when $v\sim b^3$.

If “two-dimensional channel” instead means an intrinsically planar chain confined to a strip of width $D$, a blob is already two-dimensional: $D\sim bg^{3/4}$, and the blobs form a one-dimensional string. That different geometry gives **$X\sim L(b/D)^{1/3}$**. Stating which transverse direction is removed is essential. In either geometry the blob description requires many blobs and a width below the free-coil size.
