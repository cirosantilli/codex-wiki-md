<h1 id="16b/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

By part a the transformed function solves the same equation with $\mu_n$, and by part c its [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) factor has degree $n$. The uniqueness proved in part b therefore gives $q_n=c_np_n$. Comparing their leading [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\widehat{f_n}=c(-i)^nf_n.}
$$

For the unitary convention used above, the Gaussian integral gives $c=1$, so **$c_n=(-i)^n$**. With the unnormalized phase-$e^{-i\lambda x}$ convention, $c=\sqrt{2\pi}$ instead. This proves that [Hermite functions are Fourier eigenfunctions](../../../../../../hermite-functions-are-fourier-eigenfunctions.md), with the normalization-dependent scalar fully specified.
