<h1 id="16b/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Multiplication by $x$ transforms to $i\,d/d\lambda$. Write $p_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^na_jx^j$ and use $\widehat g=cg$ for the Gaussian $g=e^{-x^2/2}$. Then

$$
\widehat{f_n}(\lambda)=c\sum_{j=0}^na_ji^j\frac{d^j}{d\lambda^j}e^{-\lambda^2/2}
=q_n(\lambda)e^{-\lambda^2/2}.
$$

Successive differentiation of the Gaussian gives a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree $j$ with leading term $(-1)^j\lambda^j$ multiplying that same Gaussian. Thus $q_n$ has leading [coefficient](../../../../../../coefficient.md) $ca_n(-i)^n\ne0$. This proves **$q_n$ is a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree exactly $n$**, rather than merely at most $n$.
