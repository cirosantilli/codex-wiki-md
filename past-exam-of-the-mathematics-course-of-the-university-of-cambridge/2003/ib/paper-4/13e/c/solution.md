<h1 id="13e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) [contour](../../../../../../complex-integration-contour.md) has polar representation $2\cos2\theta\,e^{3i\theta}$. It is invariant under complex conjugation and under reflection through the origin. As $\theta$ runs from zero to $2\pi$, the successive real-axis visits are

$$
2,\ 0,\ 1,\ -1,\ 0,\ -2,\ 0,\ -1,\ 1,\ 0,\ 2,
$$

at angles $0,\pi/4,\pi/3,2\pi/3,3\pi/4,\pi,5\pi/4,4\pi/3,5\pi/3,7\pi/4,2\pi$. To specify the crossing direction, differentiate:

$$
\frac d{d\theta}p(e^{i\theta})=i(5p(e^{i\theta})-4e^{i\theta}).
$$

Its imaginary component is $6$ at the point $2$, $-6$ at $-2$, $3$ at either visit to $1$, and $-3$ at either visit to $-1$. At a visit to zero it is $-4\cos\theta$, nonzero for each of the four angles. Therefore all these visits are transverse crossings of the real axis, even though different arcs share the same [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) point.
