<h1 id="15g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [zero-composition subspaces of a linear map](../../../../../../zero-composition-subspaces-of-a-linear-map.md) are subspaces because the defining equations are homogeneous linear equations in $\beta$: they hold for the zero map and are preserved by addition and [scalar multiplication](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md). A map with $\beta\alpha=0$ vanishes on $\operatorname{im}\alpha$, so it factors uniquely through the [quotient vector space](../../../../../../quotient-vector-space.md) $V/\operatorname{im}\alpha$. Conversely every map from that quotient to $U$ gives such a $\beta$. Therefore

$$
M^l(\alpha)\cong L(V/\operatorname{im}\alpha,U),\qquad
\boxed{\dim M^l(\alpha)=(\dim V-\operatorname{rank}\alpha)\dim U}.
$$

The condition $\alpha\beta=0$ says exactly that the [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of $\beta$ lies in $\ker\alpha$. Consequently

$$
M^r(\alpha)\cong L(V,\ker\alpha),\qquad
\boxed{\dim M^r(\alpha)=\dim V\,\dim\ker\alpha}.
$$

Both dimension formulas use the fact that the space of maps between spaces of dimensions $p,q$ has dimension $pq$: [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) units in any chosen [bases](../../../../../../basis.md) supply a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md).
