<h1 id="15g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [dual map](../../../../../../transpose-of-a-linear-map.md) is defined by $\alpha^*(\lambda)=\lambda\circ\alpha$. Suppose $\alpha^*=0$. Then $\lambda(\alpha u)=0$ for every $u\in U$ and every $\lambda\in V^*$. The [dual space](../../../../../../dual-space.md) separates vectors: a nonzero vector can be included in a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) and a coordinate functional takes value one on it. Thus $\alpha u=0$ for every $u$, and $\alpha=0$. This proves [injectivity](../../../../../../injective-function.md) of $\Phi$.

Its source and target have the same finite dimension:

$$
\dim L(U,V)=(\dim U)(\dim V)=\dim L(V^*,U^*).
$$

An injective [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) between equal finite dimensions is [surjective](../../../../../../surjective-function.md) by the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md). Therefore **$\Phi$ is an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md)**. The inverse is also described by taking the dual again and identifying each space canonically with its double dual.
