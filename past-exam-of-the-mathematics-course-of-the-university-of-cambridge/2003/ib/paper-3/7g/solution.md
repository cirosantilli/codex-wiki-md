<h1 id="7g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7G](../7g.md)

For $x\in\ker(\alpha-\lambda I)$, commutation gives

$$
(\alpha-\lambda I)\beta x=\beta(\alpha-\lambda I)x=0.
$$

Thus each [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) of $\alpha$ is an [invariant subspace](../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) for $\beta$.

If $\alpha=\beta^2$, the two [endomorphisms](../../../../../endomorphism.md) commute. The $n$ distinct real [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $\alpha$ have one-dimensional real [eigenspaces](../../../../../eigenspace.md), since their direct sum is the $n$-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). Take a nonzero real [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $x_i$. Its [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md) is invariant under $\beta$, so $\beta x_i=c_ix_i$ for a real scalar $c_i$. Squaring gives

$$
\lambda_i x_i=\alpha x_i=\beta^2x_i=c_i^2x_i,\qquad \boxed{\lambda_i=c_i^2\geq0.}
$$

The simple-spectrum hypothesis is essential: the real $90$-degree [rotation matrix](../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) squares to $-I$ in dimension two, whose negative [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) has a two-dimensional [eigenspace](../../../../../eigenspace.md).
