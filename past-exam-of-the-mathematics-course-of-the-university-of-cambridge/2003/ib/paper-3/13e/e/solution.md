<h1 id="13e/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $f$ have the stated [zeros](../../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) but not be identically zero. The origin is an [isolated singularity](../../../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md). If it were a [removable singularity](../../../../../../removable-singularity.md), the extended [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) would have [zeros](../../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) accumulating at an interior point. The proof in part (c) would force the extension, and then $f$ on its [domain](../../../../../../domain-mathematical-analysis.md), to be identically zero.

If the origin were a [pole](../../../../../../pole.md) of order $m$, then $g(z)=z^mf(z)$ would have a [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) extension with $g(0)\ne0$. [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) would make $g$ nonzero on a small disc, so $f$ would have no [zeros](../../../../../../zero-of-a-function.md) in its punctured disc, again a contradiction. The classification of [isolated singularities](../../../../../../isolated-singularity-split.md) leaves only an **essential singularity at the origin**. This argument applies to every function in part (d), not just the particular sine example.
