↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The complex [Taylor series](../../../../../../taylor-series.md) theorem states that if $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) in $|z-z_0|<R$, then

$$
\boxed{f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(z_0)}{n!}(z-z_0)^n\quad (|z-z_0|<R).}
$$

The [power series](../../../../../../power-series.md) converges absolutely there and uniformly on each smaller closed disc; its coefficients are unique. For $|z-z_0|<r<R$, the [Cauchy integral formula](../../../../../../cauchy-integral-formula.md) also gives $f^{(n)}(z_0)/n!=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{|\zeta-z_0|=r}f(\zeta)(\zeta-z_0)^{-n-1}\,d\zeta$. This is the analytic, rather than finite real-variable, form of [Taylor theorem](../../../../../../taylor-theorem.md) relevant here.
