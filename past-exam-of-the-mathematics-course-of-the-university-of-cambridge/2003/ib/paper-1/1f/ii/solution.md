<h1 id="1f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Assume the subsequence property, and let $f:E\to\mathbb R$ be a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md). If $f$ were unbounded, for each positive integer $j$ we could choose $x_j\in E$ with $|f(x_j)|>j$. There is a subsequence $x_{j_k}\to x$ with $x\in E$. By [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), $f(x_{j_k})\to f(x)$, so that real sequence is bounded. But $|f(x_{j_k})|>j_k\to\infty$, a contradiction. Hence **every continuous real-valued function on $E$ is bounded**. This is the bounded-function consequence of [sequential compactness](../../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md).
