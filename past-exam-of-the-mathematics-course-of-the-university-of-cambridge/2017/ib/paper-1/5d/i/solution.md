<h1 id="5d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) and scalar [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md) are

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=\partial_x(2xy)+\partial_y(x^2+y^2)=4y,\qquad \partial_xu_y-\partial_yu_x=2x-2x=0.
$$

Thus the flow is **irrotational but not incompressible** on the plane. A [velocity potential](../../../../../../velocity-potential.md) satisfying $\mathbf u=\nabla\phi$ is

$$
\boxed{\phi=x^2y+\frac{y^3}{3}+C}.
$$

Indeed its two partial derivatives are the two velocity components. A usual two-dimensional [stream function](../../../../../../stream-function.md) would satisfy $u_x=\psi_y$, $u_y=-\psi_x$ and hence have identically zero [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md). No such [stream function](../../../../../../stream-function.md) exists on an open region where this [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) is not identically zero; the line $y=0$ is not a two-dimensional flow domain.
