<h1 id="10e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For order $48=2^4\cdot3$, the [Sylow theorems](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) give $n_2\mid3$ and $n_2\equiv1\pmod2$, hence $n_2=1$ or $3$. If $n_2=1$, the [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order $16$ is a nontrivial proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). If $G$ were simple in the other case, it would be nonabelian (a finite abelian [simple group](../../../../../../simple-group.md) has prime order), and the preceding result would force $48\mid3!/2=3$, impossible. Thus **every group of order 48 is not simple**.

An example with no normal [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=2$ is

$$
\boxed{G=S_4\times C_2}.
$$

In $S_4$, $P=\langle(1234),(13)\rangle$ is a [dihedral group](../../../../../../dihedral-group.md) of order $8$: conjugation by $(13)$ inverts $(1234)$. Thus $P\times C_2$ has order $16$ and is a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $G$. The only transpositions in $P$ are $(13)$ and $(24)$; conjugating $(13)$ by $(23)$ gives $(12)\notin P$. Therefore $P$ and $P\times C_2$ are not normal. By conjugacy, no other [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=2$ is normal either.
