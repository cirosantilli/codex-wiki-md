↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Sylow theorems](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) state that if a [finite group](../../../../../../finite-group.md) has order $|G|=p^am$ with $p$ prime and $p\nmid m$, it has a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order $p^a$, called a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md). Every [p-group](../../../../../../p-group.md) subgroup is contained in a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md), and all [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for the same prime are [conjugate subgroups](../../../../../../conjugate-subgroup.md). Their number satisfies

$$
\boxed{n_p\equiv1\pmod p,\qquad n_p\mid m}.
$$

More precisely $n_p=[G:N_G(P)]$ for a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P$, where $N_G(P)$ is its [normalizer](../../../../../../normalizer.md).
