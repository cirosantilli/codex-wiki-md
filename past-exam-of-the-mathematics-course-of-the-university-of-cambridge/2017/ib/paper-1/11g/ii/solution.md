↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** If $|f'(x)|\le K$ everywhere, the [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) applied between any two distinct points gives

$$
|f(x)-f(y)|=|f'(\xi)||x-y|\le K|x-y|.
$$

Thus $f$ is [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) and hence [uniformly continuous](../../../../../../uniform-continuity.md). No continuity of $f'$ is needed: differentiability of $f$ gives continuity on each finite closed interval and is exactly the hypothesis needed for the [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) there.
