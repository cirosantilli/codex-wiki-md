<h1 id="13a/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $\operatorname{Im}\lambda<c$, close $C_2$ upwards. The pole at $\lambda$ is outside this contour, while $\alpha_+$ is inside; the pole $\alpha_-$ is also outside. The [residue theorem](../../../../../../../residue-theorem.md) and the minus sign in the definition of $J$ give

$$
\boxed{J(\lambda)=-\frac{r_+}{\alpha_+-\lambda}=\frac{r_+}{\lambda-\alpha_+}}.
$$

This rational function is [holomorphic](../../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) throughout $\operatorname{Im}\lambda<c$, since its only pole lies above $c$. In particular the formula includes $\lambda=\alpha_-$: the original line-integrand has no singularity on $C_2$ there, and no evaluation of $g(\alpha_-)$ is needed. Closing downwards would require a decay hypothesis in the lower half-plane that has not been given; closing upwards avoids that unjustified step.
