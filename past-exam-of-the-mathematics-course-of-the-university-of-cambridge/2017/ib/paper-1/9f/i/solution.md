↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**False.** A [left inverse](../../../../../../left-inverse.md) would force $\alpha$ to be [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md): if $\alpha u=0$, then $u=\beta\alpha u=0$. Take the [surjective linear map](../../../../../../surjective-linear-map.md) $\alpha:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ given by $\alpha(x,y)=x$. Since $(0,1)$ belongs to its [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), every composite $\beta\alpha$ annihilates $(0,1)$ and cannot be the [identity map](../../../../../../identity-function.md) on $\mathbb R^2$.
