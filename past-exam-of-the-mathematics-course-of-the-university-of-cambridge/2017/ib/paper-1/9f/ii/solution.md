<h1 id="9f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** Choose a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ of $V$. By [surjective function](../../../../../../surjective-function.md), choose $u_i\in U$ with $\alpha u_i=v_i$. Define the [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) $\beta$ by $\beta v_i=u_i$ and extend by [linearity](../../../../../../linearity.md). Then $\alpha\beta v_i=v_i$ on every [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) vector, and therefore

$$
\boxed{\alpha\beta=\operatorname{id}_V}.
$$

This constructs a [right inverse](../../../../../../right-inverse.md) and does not require $\alpha$ to be [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). If $V$ is the zero [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md), the unique zero map is the required [right inverse](../../../../../../right-inverse.md).
