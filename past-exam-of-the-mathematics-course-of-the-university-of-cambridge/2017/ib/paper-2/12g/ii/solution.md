<h1 id="12g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The interior of the diamond is $B=\{(x_1,x_2):|x_1|+|x_2|<1\}$. Its [Minkowski functional](../../../../../../minkowski-functional.md), or the uniqueness argument already proved, gives the [L1 norm](../../../../../../l1-norm.md):

$$
\boxed{\|(x_1,x_2)\|_B=|x_1|+|x_2|.}
$$

The final unheaded set $C$ cannot be an [open unit ball](../../../../../../open-unit-ball.md) of a [norm](../../../../../../norm.md), because every norm ball is [convex](../../../../../../convex-function.md) by homogeneity and the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md). Both $p=(9/10,0)$ and $q=(0,9/10)$ lie in $C$: their squared-distance expression is $101/100>1$. Their midpoint has that expression

$$
2(1-9/20)^2=121/200<1,
$$

so $(p+q)/2\notin C$. Thus $\boxed{\text{no norm has }C\text{ as its open unit ball}}$. The strict inequalities in the definition are respected by this explicit counterexample.
