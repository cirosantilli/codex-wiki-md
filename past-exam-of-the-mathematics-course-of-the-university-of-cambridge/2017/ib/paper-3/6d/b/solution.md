<h1 id="6d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $c>0$ denote the stated unit cost of cylindrical wall, and assume $V>0$. The [circular cylinder](../../../../../../circular-cylinder.md) and [hemisphere](../../../../../../hemisphere.md) give the volume constraint and total cost

$$
V=\pi r^2h+\frac23\pi r^3,\qquad C=2\pi crh+4\pi cr^2.
$$

Only the walls and cap have specified costs, so no separate base cost is included. Eliminating $h$ gives

$$
h=\frac{V}{\pi r^2}-\frac23r,\qquad
C(r)=\frac{2cV}{r}+\frac83\pi cr^2,
$$

with $0<r\le(3V/(2\pi))^{1/3}$ from $h\ge0$. Now $C'(r)=-2cV/r^2+(16/3)\pi cr$ vanishes at $r^3=3V/(8\pi)$. Moreover $C''(r)=4cV/r^3+(16/3)\pi c>0$ and the stationary point is feasible, so it is the unique global minimum. The optimal dimensions are

$$
\boxed{r=\left(\frac{3V}{8\pi}\right)^{1/3},\qquad h=2r.}
$$
