<h1 id="2g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The rational sequence $q_n=10^{-n}\lfloor10^n\sqrt2\rfloor$ satisfies $0\le\sqrt2-q_n<10^{-n}$. It is therefore a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in the induced [metric](../../../../../../metric.md) on $\mathbb Q$. Any limit in $\mathbb Q$ would also be its real limit, which is $\sqrt2\notin\mathbb Q$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q\text{ with the Euclidean metric is not complete}.}
$$

Completeness concerns limits lying in the space, so being a [dense subset](../../../../../../dense-set.md) of $\mathbb R$ does not suffice.
