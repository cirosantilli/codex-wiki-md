<h1 id="2g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) is a [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) when every [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) converges to a point of that space: for each $\varepsilon>0$, the condition $d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large $m,n$ must imply existence of $x$ with $d(x_n,x)\to0$.

A [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in $[0,1]$ is also Cauchy in $\mathbb R$, so it converges there by the assumed completeness. Its limit remains between zero and one, since inequalities are preserved under limits. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[0,1]\text{ with the Euclidean metric is complete}.}
$$

This is an instance of the fact that a [closed subset](../../../../../../closed-set.md) of a [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) is complete with its induced [metric](../../../../../../metric.md).
