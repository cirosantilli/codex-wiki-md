<h1 id="3e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $X$ is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), every pair $x\ne y$ has disjoint open neighborhoods $U$ and $V$. The rectangle $U\times V$ is an open neighborhood of $(x,y)$ disjoint from the diagonal. Hence every point of its complement is interior, so the diagonal is a [closed set](../../../../../../closed-set.md).

Conversely, if the diagonal is closed, its complement is open in the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md). For $x\ne y$, the rectangle-basis property gives open $U\ni x$ and $V\ni y$ with $U\times V$ disjoint from the diagonal. If $z\in U\cap V$, then $(z,z)$ lies in that rectangle, a contradiction. Thus $U\cap V=\varnothing$, giving the [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) separation condition. Consequently

$$
\boxed{X\text{ is Hausdorff}\iff\Delta_X\text{ is closed in }X\times X.}
$$

The argument includes spaces with zero or one point, for which both conditions hold.
