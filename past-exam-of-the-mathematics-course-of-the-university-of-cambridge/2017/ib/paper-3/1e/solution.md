<h1 id="1e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1E](../1e.md)

If $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ generate the [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$, define the [R-module homomorphism](../../../../../module-homomorphism.md)

$$
T:R^n\longrightarrow M,\qquad (r_1,\ldots,r_n)\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^nr_jm_j.
$$

This is a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) by the definition of generation. Conversely, an [R-module homomorphism](../../../../../module-homomorphism.md) that is a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) sends the standard [basis](../../../../../basis.md) vectors of the [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) $R^n$ to a generating family, since every vector in $R^n$ is their linear combination. Thus

$$
\boxed{M\text{ is finitely generated}\iff M\text{ is a quotient of some finite free module }R^n.}
$$

This is the [finite generation as a quotient of a finite free module](../../../../../finite-generation-as-a-quotient-of-a-finite-free-module.md) criterion. The zero [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) causes no exception: it is generated by the empty family, or by one zero element.

For the requested example, take $M=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$, with the usual integer scalar action. Reducing both coordinates modulo two gives an [R-module homomorphism](../../../../../module-homomorphism.md) that is a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) $\mathbb Z^2\to M$ with nonzero [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) $2\mathbb Z^2$, so it is not an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Every image of $\mathbb Z$ is a [cyclic module](../../../../../cyclic-module.md), whereas every element of $M$ has additive order at most two and $M$ has four elements. Hence $\boxed{M=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}$ admits no such surjection from $\mathbb Z$.
