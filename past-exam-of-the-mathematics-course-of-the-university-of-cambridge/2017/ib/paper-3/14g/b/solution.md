<h1 id="14g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Assume the parametrization is $C^2$ and regular, so its curvature is defined. Write $\mathbf v=\mathbf a'+y\mathbf b'$ and $W=|\mathbf v\times\mathbf b|>0$. Since $\sigma_x=\mathbf v$ and $\sigma_y=\mathbf b$, its [first fundamental form](../../../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) is

$$
\boxed{I=|\mathbf a'+y\mathbf b'|^2\,dx^2+2(\mathbf a'+y\mathbf b')\cdot\mathbf b\,dx\,dy+|\mathbf b|^2\,dy^2.}
$$

For the unit normal $\mathbf n=(\mathbf v\times\mathbf b)/W$, the mixed coefficient of the [second fundamental form](../../../../../../second-fundamental-form-split.md) is

$$
f=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf b'=\frac{\mathbf a'\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf b')}{W},
$$

where the term containing $\mathbf b'$ twice vanishes. Also $g=\mathbf n\cdot\sigma_{yy}=0$, while $EG-F^2=W^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{K=-\frac{[\mathbf a'\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf b')]^2}{W^4}.}
$$

The [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) vanishes exactly when the displayed scalar triple product is zero. This is the [Gaussian curvature of a ruled surface](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature-of-a-ruled-surface.md) criterion; at a singular point with $W=0$, curvature and the claimed equivalence are not defined.
