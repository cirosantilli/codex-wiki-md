<h1 id="11e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) $R$ with a function $\nu:R\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb N$ such that for each $a\in R$ and $b\ne0$ there are $q,r\in R$ with $a=qb+r$ and either $r=0$ or $\nu(r)<\nu(b)$. Some conventions add a multiplicative monotonicity condition; it is unnecessary for this implication.

Let $I$ be a nonzero [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md). Choose $d\in I\setminus\{0\}$ minimizing $\nu(d)$, possible because the values lie in the nonnegative integers. For $a\in I$, division by $d$ gives $a=qd+r$, with $r=a-qd\in I$. A nonzero remainder would contradict minimality, so $r=0$ and $a\in(d)$. Since $d\in I$, the reverse inclusion also holds. The zero [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is $(0)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{every ideal is principal, so every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain}.}
$$
