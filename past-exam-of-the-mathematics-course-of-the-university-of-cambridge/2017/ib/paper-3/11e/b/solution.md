<h1 id="11e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Reducing coefficients modulo $p$ gives the [ring isomorphism](../../../../../../ring-isomorphism.md)

$$
\mathbb Z[x]/(p,f)\cong\mathbb F_p[x]/(\bar f).
$$

If $d=\deg f>0$, its monic leading coefficient remains one modulo $p$, so $\deg\bar f=d$. [Polynomial division](../../../../../../polynomial-division.md) gives a unique representative of degree less than $d$: existence follows by division, and the difference of two such representatives cannot be a nonzero multiple of a degree-$d$ monic [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). There are $p$ choices for each of the $d$ coefficients, proving

$$
\boxed{|\mathbb Z[x]/(p,f)|=p^d.}
$$

Irreducibility of $\bar f$ is not needed for finiteness; without it the [quotient ring](../../../../../../quotient-ring.md) need not be a field.
