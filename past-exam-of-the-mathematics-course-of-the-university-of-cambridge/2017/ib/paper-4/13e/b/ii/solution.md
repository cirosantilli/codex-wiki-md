<h1 id="13e/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The decisive use of hypothesis (ii) is a neighbourhood construction: for any open $V\subseteq X$ containing a fibre $f^{-1}(\{y\})$, a [closed map](../../../../../../../closed-map.md) gives

$$
\boxed{W=Y\setminus f(X\setminus V)\text{ open},\qquad y\in W,\qquad f^{-1}(W)\subseteq V}.
$$

Indeed, $X\setminus V$ is [closed set](../../../../../../../closed-set.md), its image is [closed set](../../../../../../../closed-set.md), and $y$ cannot be in that image. A point of $f^{-1}(W)$ cannot lie outside $V$. Applying this to the finite union covering each [compact](../../../../../../../compact-space.md) fibre supplies a whole neighbourhood of that fibre's value using the same finitely many cover elements. [Compactness](../../../../../../../compact-space.md) of $K$ selects finitely many such neighbourhoods and hence a finite subcover of $f^{-1}(K)$, proving the desired [compact-preimage theorem for closed maps](../../../../../../../compact-preimage-theorem-for-closed-maps.md).

The [closed map](../../../../../../../closed-map.md) assumption cannot simply be omitted. Give $X=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ the [discrete topology](../../../../../../../discrete-space.md) and $Y=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\geq1\}$ the usual [subspace topology](../../../../../../../subspace-topology.md) from $\mathbb R$. The bijection $f(0)=0$, $f(n)=1/n$ is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md), and all its fibres are [compact](../../../../../../../compact-space.md) singletons. But $Y$ is [compact](../../../../../../../compact-space.md) whereas $f^{-1}(Y)=X$ is an infinite discrete space, which is not [compact](../../../../../../../compact-space.md). The map is not [closed set](../../../../../../../closed-set.md), since the closed subset $\{1,2,\ldots\}$ has image $\{1,1/2,\ldots\}$, missing its limit point $0$.
