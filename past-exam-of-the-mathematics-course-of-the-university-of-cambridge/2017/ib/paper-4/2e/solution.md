<h1 id="2e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2E](../2e.md)

Use the [class equation](../../../../../class-equation.md) for the action of $G$ on itself by [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md):

$$
|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_j[G:C_G(g_j)],
$$

where the sum runs over noncentral [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). By [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), each noncentral class has size a positive power of $p$ greater than one. Thus $|Z(G)|\equiv |G|\equiv0\pmod p$. Since the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md) belongs to the [center of a group](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md),

$$
\boxed{|Z(G)|\geq p>1}.
$$

For $|G|=p^3$, the possible orders of the [center of a group](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) are $p,p^2,p^3$. Order $p^3$ would make $G$ an [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md). Order $p^2$ would make the [quotient group](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G/Z(G)$ have prime order and hence be a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md). But a [cyclic quotient by the center](../../../../../cyclic-quotient-by-the-center.md) forces $G$ to be [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md): if $gZ(G)$ generates the quotient, any two elements are $g^a z$ and $g^b w$ with $z,w\in Z(G)$, and their products commute. Both larger orders contradict the hypothesis. Consequently

$$
\boxed{|Z(G)|=p}.
$$
