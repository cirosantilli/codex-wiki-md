↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Yes, it retracts onto a [graph](../../../../../../../graph-split.md).** On each of the three [spheres](../../../../../../../sphere.md), choose an arc joining the two marked points. A [sphere](../../../../../../../sphere.md) retracts onto this arc: identify it with the vertical meridian parameterized by the height coordinate, and send every point of a given height to the meridian point at that height. This map is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and fixes the meridian. Apply these three [retractions](../../../../../../../retraction.md) and fix the lifted [circles](../../../../../../../circle.md) pointwise; the maps agree at all attachment points. The resulting connected [graph](../../../../../../../graph-split.md) has six vertices and nine edges, so its [fundamental group](../../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) is free of rank $\boxed{9-6+1=4}$.
