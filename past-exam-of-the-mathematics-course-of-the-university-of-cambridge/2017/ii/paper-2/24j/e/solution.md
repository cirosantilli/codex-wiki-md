↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**No, under the absolute-integral convention.** Direct integration gives

$$
\widehat {\mathbf1_{[0,1]}}(\xi)=\frac{1-e^{-i\xi}}{i\xi},\qquad
\left|\widehat {\mathbf1_{[0,1]}}(\xi)\right|=\frac{2|\sin(\xi/2)|}{|\xi|}.
$$

On a fixed positive-length portion of every period, the numerator is bounded below. Summing the resulting lower bounds gives a divergent harmonic series, so the [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) is not in $L^1$. Also there is no [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) almost-everywhere representative: [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) would force it to be $1$ on $(0,1)$ and $0$ on $(-\infty,0)$, giving incompatible [limits](../../../../../../limit-of-a-function.md) at $0$. Symmetrically truncated conditional inversion does hold at interior [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) points and gives half the jump value at the endpoints; this does not contradict the answer.
