<h1 id="4h/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

With the [diagonal halting set](../../../../../../../diagonal-halting-set.md) $H$, define

$$
\boxed{A=\{2e:e\in H\}\cup\{2e+1:e\notin H\}.}
$$

If $A$ were [computably enumerable](../../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md), its odd-index inverse image would make $\mathbb N\setminus H$ [computably enumerable](../../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md). If $\mathbb N\setminus A$ were [computably enumerable](../../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md), its even-index inverse image would do the same. But a set and its complement being [computably enumerable](../../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md) would make the set a [recursive set](../../../../../../../computable-set.md): run the two enumerations in parallel until the input appears in one. This contradicts the undecidability of $H$. Thus **neither $A$ nor its complement is [computably enumerable](../../../../../../../recursively-enumerable-set.md)**.
