<h1 id="20f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Because $Y$ is closed, it is a [nowhere dense set](../../../../../../nowhere-dense-set.md) exactly when its interior is empty. If a ball about $y\in Y$ lay inside $Y$, translating it by $-y$ would put a ball about $0$ inside $Y$. Every vector of $X$ has a small scalar multiple in that ball, and closure under [scalar multiplication](../../../../../../scalar-multiplication.md) would force $Y=X$. This contradiction proves **a proper closed [linear subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) is nowhere dense**.
