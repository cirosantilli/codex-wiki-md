<h1 id="20f/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose $(x_k)_{k\geq1}$ were a countable [Hamel basis](../../../../../../basis.md). Put $E_n=\operatorname{span}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. Each $E_n$ is a finite-dimensional, hence closed, [linear subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $X$. It is proper because $X$ is infinite dimensional, and therefore a [nowhere dense set](../../../../../../nowhere-dense-set.md). A [Hamel basis](../../../../../../basis.md) means that every vector is a finite [linear combination](../../../../../../linear-combination.md), so $X=\bigcup_nE_n$. This contradicts the [Baire category theorem](../../../../../../baire-category-theorem.md) for the [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md) $X$. Hence **an infinite-dimensional Banach space has no countable Hamel basis**. The same proof excludes any countable algebraic spanning set.
