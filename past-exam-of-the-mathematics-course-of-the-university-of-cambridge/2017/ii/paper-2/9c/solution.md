<h1 id="9c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9C](../9c.md)

Differentiation of the [Hubble parameter](../../../../../hubble-parameter.md) gives $\dot H+H^2=\ddot a/a$. The [strong energy condition](../../../../../strong-energy-condition.md) and the acceleration equation therefore imply

$$
\boxed{\dot H+H^2\leq0.}
$$

Assume the present universe is expanding, $H_0>0$, and normalize the [scale factor](../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) by $a(t_0)=1$. Since $\ddot a\leq0$, the past expanding branch has $\dot a(t)\geq\dot a(t_0)>0$. On this branch,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac1H=-\frac{\dot H}{H^2}\geq1.
$$

Integrating from a past time $t$ to $t_0$ gives $H(t)^{-1}\leq H_0^{-1}+t-t_0$. Thus, where the denominator is positive,

$$
\boxed{H(t)\geq\frac1{H_0^{-1}+t-t_0}\quad(t\leq t_0).}
$$

The printed inequality needs this past-time qualification: for future times with $H>0$, the inequality reverses. Independently, a [concave function](../../../../../concave-function.md) lies below its tangent, so

$$
\boxed{a(t)\leq1+H_0(t-t_0).}
$$

Positivity of $a$ prevents the past branch extending before the zero of this tangent. If $t_B$ is the beginning of the expanding universe, its age satisfies $\boxed{t_0-t_B\leq H_0^{-1}}$. Without the normalization $a(t_0)=1$, the tangent bound instead reads $a(t)\leq a(t_0)[1+H_0(t-t_0)]$.
