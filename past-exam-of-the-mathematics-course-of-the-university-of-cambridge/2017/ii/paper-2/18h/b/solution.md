<h1 id="18h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the real quadratic field $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$ with squarefree $d>1$, [Dirichlet unit theorem](../../../../../../dirichlet-s-unit-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_K^\times=\{\pm\epsilon^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}}
$$

for a [fundamental unit](../../../../../../fundamental-unit-number-theory.md) $\epsilon>1$, since the [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) in a real field are $\pm1$.

If $p\mid d$ and $p\equiv3\pmod4$, a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) of norm $-1$ would have the form $(a+b\sqrt d)/2$ with [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) $a,b$ and satisfy $a^2-db^2=-4$. Reducing modulo the odd [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $p$ gives $(a/2)^2\equiv-1\pmod p$, impossible because $-1$ is not a [quadratic residue](../../../../../../quadratic-residue.md) for such a [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md). Hence **every [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) has norm $+1$ in this case**. In contrast, $\boxed{d=2,\ u=1+\sqrt2}$ has norm $-1$.
