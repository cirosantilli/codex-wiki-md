<h1 id="18h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a [number field](../../../../../../number-field.md) with $r$ real embeddings $\sigma_i$ and $s$ conjugate pairs of complex embeddings $\tau_j$, use the [logarithmic embedding of number field units](../../../../../../logarithmic-embedding-of-number-field-units.md)

$$
\ell(u)=(\log|\sigma_1u|,\ldots,\log|\sigma_ru|,2\log|\tau_1u|,\ldots,2\log|\tau_su|).
$$

It is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) from multiplication to addition. A [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) has [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) $\pm1$, so the coordinates sum to zero. Deleting one coordinate identifies this hyperplane with $\mathbb R^{r+s-1}$ and gives the desired map.

If its image coordinates remain in a bounded set, the omitted coordinate is bounded too, and every conjugate of $u$ has bounded absolute value. The coefficients of its monic [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) are [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) and elementary symmetric expressions in at most $[K:\mathbb Q]$ bounded conjugates. They are therefore bounded [integers](../../../../../../integer.md). Only finitely many such [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), and finitely many roots of them, exist. Thus the inverse image of every bounded set is finite. In particular **the kernel is finite and the image is discrete**. This argument includes the zero-dimensional target case; it does not yet assert the full-rank part of [Dirichlet unit theorem](../../../../../../dirichlet-s-unit-theorem.md).
