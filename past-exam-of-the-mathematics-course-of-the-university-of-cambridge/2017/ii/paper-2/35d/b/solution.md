<h1 id="35d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [Einstein static universe](../../../../../../einstein-static-universe.md) with $P=0$ and $\rho_0>0$, the acceleration equation gives $\Lambda=4\pi\rho_0$. The first [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) then gives $k/a_0^2=4\pi\rho_0$, so

$$
\boxed{k=1,\qquad a_0=(4\pi\rho_0)^{-1/2},\qquad\Lambda=4\pi\rho_0.}
$$

Keep $\Lambda,k$ fixed and use the specified perturbation closure $\delta P=\delta\rho/3$. Linearization of the constraint gives $\delta\rho=-3\rho_0\delta a/a_0$. In the acceleration equation the background terms cancel, leaving

$$
\delta\ddot a=-\frac{4\pi}3a_0(\delta\rho+3\delta P)
=8\pi\rho_0\delta a=\frac2{a_0^2}\delta a.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{\delta a=C_+e^{\sqrt{8\pi\rho_0}\,t}+C_-e^{-\sqrt{8\pi\rho_0}\,t}.}
$$

The growing mode establishes **linear instability of the static universe**; a specially chosen decaying perturbation does not remove instability. This uses the pressure-density relation explicitly supplied for the perturbation. If zero density is allowed, there is also the degenerate vacuum static case $k=0$, $\Lambda=0$, which is not the positive-density static universe considered here.
