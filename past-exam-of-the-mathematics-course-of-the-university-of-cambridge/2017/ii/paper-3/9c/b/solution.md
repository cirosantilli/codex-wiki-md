<h1 id="9c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [baryon-to-photon ratio](../../../../../../baryon-to-photon-ratio.md) is extremely small, of order $10^{-9}$. Thus even when the typical [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) is below $I$, the high-[energy](../../../../../../energy.md) tail of the [blackbody radiation](../../../../../../black-body-radiation.md) contains enough [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) above the [ionization energy](../../../../../../ionization-energy.md) to reionize almost every newly formed [hydrogen atom](../../../../../../hydrogen-atom.md). [Cosmological recombination](../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) becomes effective only when this exponentially small tail is also small compared with the [baryon-to-photon ratio](../../../../../../baryon-to-photon-ratio.md). Consequently **recombination occurs at a [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) much lower than $I/k$**, consistent with $kT\simeq0.03I$. The exact freeze-out history also depends on reaction rates and expansion; the equilibrium estimate explains the large suppression of the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md).
