<h1 id="4h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In strict [Chomsky normal form](../../../../../../chomsky-normal-form.md), every production of a [context-free grammar](../../../../../../context-free-grammar.md) is $A\to BC$ or $A\to a$, with $A,B,C$ [nonterminal symbols](../../../../../../nonterminal-symbol.md) and $a$ a [terminal symbol](../../../../../../terminal-symbol.md). Such a derivation cannot produce the [empty word](../../../../../../empty-word.md): it starts with one [nonterminal symbol](../../../../../../nonterminal-symbol.md), and every step either increases the symbol count or replaces one symbol by one symbol. The [context-free language](../../../../../../context-free-language.md) $\{\varepsilon\}$, generated by $S\to\varepsilon$, is therefore **not generated by any strict CNF grammar**.

The convention matters: extended [Chomsky normal form](../../../../../../chomsky-normal-form.md) permits $S\to\varepsilon$ with $S$ excluded from right-hand sides. Under that convention every [context-free language](../../../../../../context-free-language.md) has such a grammar, so the requested example would not exist.
