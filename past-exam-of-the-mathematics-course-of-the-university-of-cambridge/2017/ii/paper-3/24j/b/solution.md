<h1 id="24j/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For conjugate exponents $1<p,q<\infty$, $1/p+1/q=1$, [Hölder's inequality](../../../../../../holder-s-inequality.md) is $\boxed{\mathbb E|XY|\leq\|X\|_p\|Y\|_q}$. If either finite [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) is zero, the corresponding variable is zero [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). Otherwise normalize $u=|X|/\|X\|_p$, $v=|Y|/\|Y\|_q$. [Young inequality](../../../../../../young-s-inequality-for-products.md) $uv\leq u^p/p+v^q/q$ follows by minimizing the right side minus $uv$ in $u\geq0$, whose [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is $u^{p-1}-v$. Integration gives $\mathbb E(uv)\leq1/p+1/q=1$, proving the result. Infinite [norms](../../../../../../norm.md) give the usual extended-value inequality whenever the right side is unambiguous. At the endpoints, $\mathbb E|XY|\leq\|X\|_1\|Y\|_\infty$ follows from the almost-sure bound for $Y$, with the symmetric version for $p=\infty$.
