<h1 id="17g/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Multiplicativity of the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) gives $\delta(gh)=\delta(g)\delta(h)$ and $\delta(1)=1$, so $\delta$ is a [linear character](../../../../../../linear-character.md). Its finite image is a cyclic [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). If it contains $-1$, its order is $2m$ and the [character of a representation](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\delta^m$ maps onto $\{\pm1\}$. The [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is the preimage of its identity, so $\ker(\delta^m)$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of index $2$. The [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $\ker\delta$ itself need not have index $2$.

By [Cauchy theorem for groups](../../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md), a [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $E$ of order $2k$ has an involution $g$. In the [regular representation](../../../../../../regular-representation.md), left multiplication by $g$ is a permutation consisting of $k$ disjoint transpositions, so its [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is $(-1)^k=-1$ when $k$ is odd. The preceding argument gives **a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of index two**.

For a nonabelian [simple group](../../../../../../simple-group.md) of order below $80$, [Burnside's theorem](../../../../../../burnside-s-theorem.md) requires at least three distinct [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) factors. An odd order with three factors is at least $3\cdot5\cdot7=105$. An even order with exactly one factor of $2$ has a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of index $2$. Thus its order must be divisible by $4$ and by at least two odd [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md). Below $80$ the only possibility is $4\cdot3\cdot5=60$, since the next is $4\cdot3\cdot7=84$. Hence $\boxed{|H|=60}$.
