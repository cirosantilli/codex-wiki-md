<h1 id="7e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7E](../7e.md)

After division by $z$, the coefficients are $P=2/z-1$ and $Q=-1/z$. At $0$, $zP$ and $z^2Q$ are [analytic functions](../../../../../space-of-holomorphic-functions.md), so $0$ is a [regular singular point](../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). Its [indicial equation](../../../../../indicial-equation.md) is $r(r-1)+2r=r(r+1)=0$. There are no other finite [singular points of a differential equation](../../../../../singular-point-of-a-differential-equation.md). At infinity the [regular singular point criterion for a second-order equation](../../../../../regular-singular-point-criterion-for-a-second-order-equation.md) would require $P=O(z^{-1})$, but $P\to-1$; hence infinity is an [irregular singular point](../../../../../irregular-singular-point.md).

Substituting $y=f/z$ reduces the equation to $f''-f'=0$, so

$$
\boxed{y(z)=\frac{A+B e^z}{z},\qquad z\ne0.}
$$

A [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md) pair is $1/z$ and $(e^z-1)/z$. Their [Wronskian](../../../../../wronskian.md) is $e^z/z^2\ne0$. The second has a removable singularity at $0$ and value $1$, whereas the first has a simple pole, agreeing with the two [characteristic exponents at a regular singular point](../../../../../characteristic-exponent-at-a-regular-singular-point.md), $0,-1$. The [integer](../../../../../integer.md) difference does not require a [logarithm](../../../../../logarithm.md). At infinity $e^z/z$ has an [essential singularity](../../../../../essential-singularity.md), consistent with the [irregular singular point](../../../../../irregular-singular-point.md); the existence of the simpler solution $1/z$ does not make infinity regular.
