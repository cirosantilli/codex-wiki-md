<h1 id="27j/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a finite-state, finite-horizon discrete-time frictionless market with a strictly positive [numéraire](../../../../../../numeraire.md), the [fundamental theorem of asset pricing](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-asset-pricing.md) states **no arbitrage if and only if there exists an [equivalent martingale measure](../../../../../../risk-neutral-measure.md)** under which all discounted asset prices are [martingales](../../../../../../martingale-split.md). Equivalence means the same null events as the physical measure. In this setting [market completeness](../../../../../../complete-market.md) is equivalent to uniqueness of the [equivalent martingale measure](../../../../../../risk-neutral-measure.md), and a replicable payoff $H$ has price $S_t^0\mathbb E_Q[H/S_T^0\mid\mathcal F_t]$. Without finite-state hypotheses, stronger integrability and closure conditions are needed; this is the standard multi-period course version.
