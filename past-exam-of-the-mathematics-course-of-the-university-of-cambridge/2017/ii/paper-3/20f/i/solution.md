<h1 id="20f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Dominated convergence makes $\widehat f$ [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), and $\|\widehat f\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_1$. Smooth compactly supported functions are dense in $L^1$. Their [Fourier transforms](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) decay at infinity by [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md), so uniform approximation proves the [Riemann-Lebesgue lemma](../../../../../../riemann-lebesgue-lemma.md): $\widehat f\in C_0$.

For density, smooth compactly supported functions are also uniformly dense in $C_0$ by cutoff and mollification. Each such target $g$ is a [Schwartz function](../../../../../../schwartz-function.md); its inverse [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) is another [Schwartz function](../../../../../../schwartz-function.md), hence belongs to $L^1$, and [Fourier inversion](../../../../../../fourier-inversion-theorem.md) recovers $g$. Therefore the [image of a function](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) of $L^1$ is dense in $C_0$.

The $L^1$ [Fourier inversion theorem](../../../../../../fourier-inversion-theorem.md) says that if $f,\widehat f\in L^1$, then $f$ agrees almost everywhere with the inverse [integral](../../../../../../integral.md) of $\widehat f$. If $\widehat f=0$, this applies and gives $f=0$ almost everywhere. Hence **the [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md) as a map of $L^1$ equivalence classes, with dense image in $C_0$**.
