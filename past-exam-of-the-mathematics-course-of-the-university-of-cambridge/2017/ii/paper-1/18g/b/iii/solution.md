<h1 id="18g/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

On the [normal subgroup](../../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N=\langle a,b\rangle$, the first diagonal entry is the [linear character](../../../../../../../linear-character.md) $\chi(a^i b^j)=\varepsilon^i\eta^j$. Its image has order at most three, although $|N|=9$, so its [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) has order at least three. The second entry is the inverse character and has the same [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\rho\text{ is never faithful}}.
$$

Elements of the other coset have off-diagonal image and cannot be the identity. If $(\varepsilon,\eta)\ne(1,1)$, the [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) has order exactly three; otherwise it is all of $N$.

When both roots equal one, only $C$ acts nontrivially and its eigenlines spanned by $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ are invariant, so the representation is reducible. Otherwise one of $A,B$ has two distinct [eigenvalues](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). Any invariant line would be one of its coordinate eigenlines, but $C$ interchanges these two lines. Hence no invariant line exists, and

$$
\boxed{\rho\text{ is irreducible}\iff(\varepsilon,\eta)\ne(1,1)}.
$$
