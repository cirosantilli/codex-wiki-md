<h1 id="21f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

On the one-dimensional [linear subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) $Y=\mathbb R x_0$, define $g(tx_0)=t\|x_0\|$. It is linear and obeys $g(y)\le\|y\|$ even for negative $t$. The [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) on $X$ is a [sublinear functional](../../../../../../sublinear-function.md), so the real [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../../../../hahn-banach-theorem.md) extends $g$ to a [linear functional](../../../../../../linear-functional.md) $f$ with $f(x)\le\|x\|$ for every $x$.

Apply this also to $-x$ to get $-f(x)\le\|x\|$. Hence $|f(x)|\le\|x\|$, proving [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and $\|f\|\le1$. At $x_0\ne0$ the extension satisfies $f(x_0)=\|x_0\|$, forcing the reverse [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) inequality. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f(x_0)=\|x_0\|,\qquad\|f\|=1}.
$$

The exclusion of $x_0=0$ is what ensures that this particular construction attains [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) one.
