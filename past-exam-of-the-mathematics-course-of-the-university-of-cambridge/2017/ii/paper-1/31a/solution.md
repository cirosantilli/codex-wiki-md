<h1 id="31a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [31A](../31a.md)

A [Lie point symmetry](../../../../../lie-point-symmetry.md) is a local one-parameter family of [diffeomorphisms](../../../../../diffeomorphism.md) of $(t,\mathbf x)$ that takes solution graphs of the [differential equation](../../../../../differential-equation-split.md) to solution graphs wherever the transformed graph remains a graph over time. Its infinitesimal generator is $Y=\tau(t,\mathbf x)\partial_t+\sum_i\eta_i(t,\mathbf x)\partial_{x_i}$. Its [first prolongation](../../../../../first-prolongation.md) is

$$
\operatorname{pr}^{(1)}Y=Y+\sum_i\bigl(D_t\eta_i-\dot x_iD_t\tau\bigr)\partial_{\dot x_i},
\qquad D_t=\partial_t+\sum_i\dot x_i\partial_{x_i}.
$$

For a regular first-order equation the infinitesimal criterion is $\operatorname{pr}^{(1)}Y(\Delta)=0$ on $\Delta=0$; it says that the prolonged [vector field](../../../../../vector-field.md) is tangent to the equation submanifold.

With the constant nondegenerate canonical [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) $J$, define the [Poisson bracket](../../../../../poisson-bracket.md) by $\{f,g\}=(\nabla f)^TJ\nabla g$ and the [Hamiltonian vector field](../../../../../hamiltonian-vector-field.md) by $V_f=J\nabla f$. Our convention gives $V_f[h]=\{h,f\}$. Applying the [Jacobi identity](../../../../../jacobi-identity.md) to a test [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $h$,

$$
[V_f,V_g]h=\{\{h,g\},f\}-\{\{h,f\},g\}
=-\{h,\{f,g\}\},
$$

so

$$
\boxed{[V_f,V_g]=-V_{\{f,g\}}}.
$$

A [first integral](../../../../../first-integral.md) $F$ obeys $dF/dt=\{F,H\}=0$. Consequently $[V_F,V_H]=0$, and the local flows commute. The time-independent transformation generated by $V_F$, with $t$ unchanged, therefore maps [integral curves of a vector field](../../../../../integral-curve-of-a-vector-field.md) for $V_H$ to [integral curves of a vector field](../../../../../integral-curve-of-a-vector-field.md): it is a [Lie point symmetry](../../../../../lie-point-symmetry.md).

Conversely, such a time-independent symmetry gives $[V_F,V_H]=0$, hence $V_{\{F,H\}}=0$. Nondegeneracy of $J$ makes $\{F,H\}$ constant on each connected component. At a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) $\mathbf x_0$, $J\nabla H(\mathbf x_0)=0$ implies $\nabla H(\mathbf x_0)=0$, so that constant is zero on its component. Thus **the converse holds on a connected phase space containing a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md)**, or on each component that contains one.

The printed global converse needs this connectedness convention: a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) on one component does not control the constants on other components. For example take one component with $H=(q^2+p^2)/2$, $F=0$, and another with $H=p$, $F=q$. The [Hamiltonian vector fields](../../../../../hamiltonian-vector-field.md) commute everywhere and there is a [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) on the first component, but $F$ is not a [first integral](../../../../../first-integral.md) on the second. Even on a connected space the fixed-point condition matters: $H=p$, $F=q$ commute but $\dot F=1$.
