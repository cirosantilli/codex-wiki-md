<h1 id="19h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

By the preceding argument take a nonzero integer $m\in\mathfrak a$. The [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) property gives $m\mathcal O_L\subseteq\mathfrak a$. If $d=[L:\mathbb Q]$, an [integral basis](../../../../../../integral-basis.md) identifies the additive [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of $\mathcal O_L$ with $\mathbb Z^d$, so

$$
\mathcal O_L/m\mathcal O_L\cong(\mathbb Z/|m|\mathbb Z)^d.
$$

The natural projection onto $\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak a$ is surjective. Thus the latter is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) of order at most $|m|^d$, proving

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak a\text{ is finite abelian}}.
$$

In particular the [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is a full-rank integer lattice; this permits the integral-basis argument in the next part.
