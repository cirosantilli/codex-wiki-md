<h1 id="20i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For loops $\alpha,\beta$ based at $x_0$, the product of their transported loops is represented by

$$
\bar u*\alpha*u*\bar u*\beta*u.
$$

The middle $u*\bar u$ contracts relative to its endpoints, leaving $\bar u*(\alpha*\beta)*u$. Hence $u_\#$ is a [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md). The reverse path induces the inverse: transporting back produces $u*\bar u*\alpha*u*\bar u$, whose outer retraced paths contract. Similarly the other composite contracts at $x_1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(\bar u)_\#\circ u_\#=\operatorname{id},\qquad u_\#\circ(\bar u)_\#=\operatorname{id}}.
$$

This proves the asserted [group isomorphism](../../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) without needing any global path-connectedness hypothesis beyond the supplied path between the two points.
