<h1 id="16h/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a $\Delta$-regular undirected [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md), its [Graph Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian-matrix.md) is $L=\Delta I-A$, and for any complex vector $v$,

$$
v^*Lv=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}|v_i-v_j|^2.
$$

If $Av=\Delta v$, the left side is zero, hence $v_i=v_j$ on every [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md). In a [connected graph](../../../../../../connected-graph.md) all coordinates coincide. Thus the [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is exactly the constant vectors, of dimension one. Since the [adjacency matrix of a graph](../../../../../../adjacency-matrix.md) is symmetric and diagonalizable,

$$
\boxed{\text{the algebraic and geometric multiplicities of }\Delta\text{ are one}.}
$$
