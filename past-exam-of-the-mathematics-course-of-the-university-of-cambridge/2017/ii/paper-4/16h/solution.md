<h1 id="16h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [16H](../16h.md)

For the [Petersen graph](../../../../../petersen-graph.md), each [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) has three neighbours, adjacent vertices have no [common neighbour](../../../../../common-neighbour.md), and distinct nonadjacent vertices have exactly one [common neighbour](../../../../../common-neighbour.md). The entries of $A^2$ count length-two walks, so diagonal entries are three, adjacent entries zero, and other off-diagonal entries one. Hence

$$
\boxed{A^2+A-2I=J.}
$$

The [connected graph](../../../../../connected-graph.md) has the [simple eigenvalue](../../../../../simple-eigenvalue.md) three with [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) $\mathbf1$. On its [orthogonal complement](../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) $J=0$, so the remaining [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) satisfy $\lambda^2+\lambda-2=0$ and are one or minus two. If their multiplicities are $r,s$, then $r+s=9$ and $\operatorname{tr}A=3+r-2s=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{spec}(A)=\{3\text{ (once)},\ 1\text{ (five times)},\ -2\text{ (four times)}\}.}
$$
