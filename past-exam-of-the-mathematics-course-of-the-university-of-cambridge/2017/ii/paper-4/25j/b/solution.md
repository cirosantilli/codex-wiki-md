<h1 id="25j/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If $\theta$ is measure-preserving, equality holds on every measurable set and hence on the specified $\pi$-system. Conversely, let $\mathcal D=\{A\in\mathcal E:\mu_\theta(A)=\mu(A)\}$. Both finite [measures](../../../../../../measure.md) have equal total mass. Therefore $E\in\mathcal D$, complements of members remain in $\mathcal D$ by subtracting from the finite total mass, and disjoint countable [unions](../../../../../../set-union.md) remain in $\mathcal D$ by [countable additivity](../../../../../../countable-additivity.md). Thus $\mathcal D$ is a [Dynkin system](../../../../../../dynkin-system.md).

It contains the generating [pi-system](../../../../../../pi-system.md) $\mathcal A$, so [Dynkin lemma](../../../../../../dynkin-lemma.md) gives $\mathcal E=\sigma(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal D$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mu(\theta^{-1}(A))=\mu(A)\quad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal E.}
$$

This is exactly the measure-preserving property.
