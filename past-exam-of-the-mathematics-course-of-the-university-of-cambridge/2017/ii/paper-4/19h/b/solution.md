<h1 id="19h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Here $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[s]$ with $s=\sqrt{-14}$ and [field discriminant](../../../../../../field-discriminant.md) $-56$. The imaginary-quadratic [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{56}<5$, so each [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md) has an integral representative of [ideal norm](../../../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most four. Representatives therefore use [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) above two and three. Put

$$
\mathfrak p=(2,s),\qquad\mathfrak q=(3,s+1).
$$

The [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) two ramifies, with $\mathfrak p^2=(2)$, and three splits into $\mathfrak q\overline{\mathfrak q}=(3)$. No element has [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) two, because $N(a+bs)=a^2+14b^2$; thus $[\mathfrak p]$ is nontrivial of order two. Norm-four [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) represent $[\mathfrak p]^2=1$ and add no generator, so the class group is generated by $[\mathfrak p],[\mathfrak q]$.

The element $2-s$ has [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) 18. It belongs to $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak q$, but not $\overline{\mathfrak q}$, since reduction there gives $s\equiv1\pmod3$. Its [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) therefore factors as

$$
(2-s)=\mathfrak p\mathfrak q^2.
$$

The exponents follow from the [ideal norm](../../../../../../ideal-norm.md) 18, or from the root $s\equiv2\pmod9$ defining $\mathfrak q^2$. Hence $[\mathfrak q]^2=[\mathfrak p]$, so $[\mathfrak q]$ has order four and already generates all possible classes. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-14}))\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z.}
$$

As a separate check, the reduced forms of [discriminant of a binary quadratic form](../../../../../../discriminant-of-a-binary-quadratic-form.md) $-56$ are $[1,0,14],[2,0,7],[3,2,5],[3,-2,5]$, giving exactly four classes.
