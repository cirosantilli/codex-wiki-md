[Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](../../../../../../dirichlet-s-theorem-on-arithmetic-progressions.md) states that if [positive integer](../../../../../../positive-integer.md) $q$ and [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $a$ satisfy $\gcd(a,q)=1$, then infinitely many [prime numbers](../../../../../../prime-number.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{p\equiv a\pmod q.}
$$

The coprimality hypothesis is essential: if $a$ and $q$ have a common factor greater than one, a [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) in the progression must divide that factor, giving at most finitely many possibilities.
