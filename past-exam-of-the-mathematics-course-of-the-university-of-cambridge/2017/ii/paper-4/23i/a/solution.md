<h1 id="23i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $Q=\varphi(P)$, the [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) of a nonsingular curve at $Q$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md). Choose a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $t$ there. The [ramification index of a morphism of curves](../../../../../../ramification-index-of-a-morphism-of-curves.md) is

$$
\boxed{e_P=\operatorname{ord}_P(\varphi^*t).}
$$

Equivalently, $\varphi^*t=u z^{e_P}$ for a local [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $z$ at $P$ and a [unit in a ring](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $u$. A different choice $t'=vt$ changes its pullback by a [unit in a ring](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), so the index is well-defined. This local definition applies also to inseparable morphisms; conclusions involving separability or [tame ramification](../../../../../../tamely-ramified-extension.md) require those extra hypotheses.
