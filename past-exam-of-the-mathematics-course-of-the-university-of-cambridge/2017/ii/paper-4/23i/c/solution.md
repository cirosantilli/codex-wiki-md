<h1 id="23i/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a nonsingular projective integral curve, apply the [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) to the [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../../divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) $mP$. It gives $\ell(mP)\geq m\deg P+1-g$, where $g$ is the [geometric genus](../../../../../../geometric-genus.md). For sufficiently large $m$, this exceeds the dimension of the space of constant functions; over the usual geometrically integral convention that dimension is one. Thus $L(mP)$ contains a nonconstant [rational function](../../../../../../rational-function.md).

By the definition of $L(mP)$, the [principal divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md) of that function plus $mP$ is effective, so it has no pole away from $P$. Therefore **there is a nonconstant [rational function](../../../../../../rational-function.md) regular on $X\setminus\{P\}$**. If $P$ is not a rational point, using its closed-point degree in the same inequality handles that case. A positive pole order at $P$ is unavoidable, since [regular functions](../../../../../../regular-function.md) on a projective integral curve are constant in its constant field.
