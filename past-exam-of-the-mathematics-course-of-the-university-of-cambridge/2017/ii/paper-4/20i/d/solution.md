<h1 id="20i/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Suppose such an $f$ exists. Orthogonality and unit lengths make

$$
H_s(x)=\cos(\pi s)x+\sin(\pi s)f(x),\qquad0\leq s\leq1,
$$

a [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md) into the [unit sphere](../../../../../../unit-sphere.md), since its squared [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) is one. This is a [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) from the identity to the [antipodal map](../../../../../../antipodal-map.md). Their degrees would coincide, but for even $n\geq2$ they are respectively $1$ and $(-1)^{n+1}=-1$, a contradiction. Thus **no such [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md) exists for even $n$**. At $n=0$ there is not even a [unit vector](../../../../../../unit-vector.md) orthogonal to either point of $S^0\subset\mathbb R$, so nonexistence holds there as well. This is a [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) form of the [Hairy ball theorem](../../../../../../hairy-ball-theorem.md).
