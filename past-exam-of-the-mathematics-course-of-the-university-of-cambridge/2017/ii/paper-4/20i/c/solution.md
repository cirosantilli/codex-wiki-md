<h1 id="20i/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [antipodal map](../../../../../../antipodal-map.md) is the restriction to the boundary of the [unit ball](../../../../../../unit-ball.md) of $-I$ on $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. The induced [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) on the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) is reversed or preserved according to the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) of that ambient [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md), namely $(-1)^{n+1}$. Its degree is therefore $(-1)^{n+1}$. With integral coefficients, for $n\geq1$ the induced top-homology map is

$$
\boxed{a_*:H_n(S^n)\cong\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,\qquad m\mapsto(-1)^{n+1}m.}
$$

At $n=0$, ordinary $H_0(S^0)=\mathbb Z^2$ instead has its two generators exchanged; multiplication by $-1$ is the statement on reduced $H_0(S^0)\cong\mathbb Z$. This distinguishes the small-dimension exception from the usual top-degree formula.
