<h1 id="21f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [bounded linear operator](../../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) $T$ on a complex [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), the [spectrum of a bounded operator](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-bounded-operator.md) consists of $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ for which $T-\lambda I$ does not have a bounded everywhere-defined inverse. A bijective bounded operator has a bounded inverse by the [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md). Using the disjoint spectral convention,

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\sigma_p(T)&=\{\lambda:T-\lambda I\text{ is not injective}\},\\
\sigma_c(T)&=\{\lambda:T-\lambda I\text{ is injective, has dense range, but is not onto}\},\\
\sigma_r(T)&=\{\lambda:T-\lambda I\text{ is injective and has nondense range}\}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

These are respectively the [point spectrum](../../../../../../point-spectrum.md), [continuous spectrum](../../../../../../continuous-spectrum.md) and [residual spectrum](../../../../../../residual-spectrum.md), and partition the [spectrum](../../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md). Some texts let [residual spectrum](../../../../../../residual-spectrum.md) include noninjective operators with nondense range; the displayed convention excludes overlap with [point spectrum](../../../../../../point-spectrum.md).
