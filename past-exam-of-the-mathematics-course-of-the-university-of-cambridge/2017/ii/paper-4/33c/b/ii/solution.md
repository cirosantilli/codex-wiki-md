<h1 id="33c/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A negative-energy odd [bound state](../../../../../../../bound-state.md) has $k=i\kappa$, $\kappa>0$, with $\psi=C\sinh(\kappa x)$ inside and a decaying exponential outside. Its jump condition, equivalently the nontrivial upper-half-plane pole of the preceding S-matrix, gives

$$
\kappa=-\frac{U_0}{2}(1-e^{-2\kappa a}).
$$

Set $w=2\kappa a$ and $g=-aU_0>0$. The condition is $w=g(1-e^{-w})$. The right side is strictly increasing and strictly concave, has slope $g$ at zero and is bounded by $g$. Hence there is exactly one positive intersection iff $g>1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{one odd bound state exists iff }aU_0<-1,\qquad E=-\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m}.}
$$

The formal zero root is not a positive-decay [bound state](../../../../../../../bound-state.md). At $aU_0=-1$ there is a zero-energy threshold solution, not a normalizable [bound state](../../../../../../../bound-state.md).
