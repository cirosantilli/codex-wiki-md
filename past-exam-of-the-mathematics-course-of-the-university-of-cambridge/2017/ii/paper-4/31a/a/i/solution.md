<h1 id="31a/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $b=0,a>0$, the nonzero branches $x=\pm\sqrt{(1-\mu)/a}$ exist for $\mu<1$ and have multiplier $3-2\mu>1$, so both repel. Zero changes from attracting below one to repelling above one: the bifurcation at one is a subcritical [pitchfork bifurcation](../../../../../../../pitchfork-bifurcation-normal-form.md). At minus one its multiplier crosses $-1$, producing a supercritical [period-doubling bifurcation](../../../../../../../period-doubling-bifurcation.md); the newly stable small two-cycle lies on the side $\mu<-1$.
