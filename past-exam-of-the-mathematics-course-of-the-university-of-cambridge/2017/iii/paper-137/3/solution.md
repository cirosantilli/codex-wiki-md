<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Use the [valence formula for the modular group](../../../../../valence-formula-for-the-modular-group.md): for a nonzero [modular form](../../../../../modular-form.md) of weight $k$ on $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$,

$$
v_\infty(f)+\frac12v_i(f)+\frac13v_\rho(f)
+\sum_{z\ne i,\rho}v_z(f)=\frac{k}{12},\qquad\rho=e^{2\pi i/3}.
$$

The sum contains one representative of each other [modular group](../../../../../modular-group.md) orbit of zeros. The orders are nonnegative because $f$ is holomorphic on the [complex upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md) and at infinity. The half and third weights account for the [elliptic stabilizers of the modular group](../../../../../elliptic-stabilizers-of-the-modular-group.md).

In weight two, the transformation under $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ at its fixed point gives $f(i)=i^2f(i)=-f(i)$. Thus any nonzero $f$ would have $v_i(f)\geq1$. The [valence formula for the modular group](../../../../../valence-formula-for-the-modular-group.md) would give a left side at least $1/2$ and a right side $1/6$, which is impossible. Therefore the unheaded request is answered by [vanishing of weight-two level-one modular forms](../../../../../vanishing-of-weight-two-level-one-modular-forms.md):

$$
\boxed{M_2(\Gamma(1))=\{0\}.}
$$
