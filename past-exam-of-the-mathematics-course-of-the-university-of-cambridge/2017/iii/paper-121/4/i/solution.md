↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [forcing atom](../../../../../../forcing-atom.md) is a condition $p\in\mathbb P$ such that any two strengthenings of $p$ are [compatible forcing conditions](../../../../../../compatible-forcing-conditions.md):

$$
p\text{ is an atom}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall q,r\leq p\ \exists s\in\mathbb P\ (s\leq q,r).
$$

An [atomless forcing order](../../../../../../atomless-forcing-order.md) has no such condition; equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\forall p\in\mathbb P\ \exists q,r\leq p\ (q\perp r).}
$$

The meaning of $q\perp r$ is that there is no common stronger condition. For an arbitrary [partial order](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md), an atom need not be a minimal element: the definition concerns compatibility below it. This distinction prevents a minimal-element definition from misclassifying an order with descending but mutually compatible conditions.
