<h1 id="3/iv/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $D\in M$ be a [dense subset of a forcing order](../../../../../../../dense-subset-of-a-forcing-order.md) $\mathbb P$. Then $D\times\mathbb Q\in M$ is dense in the [product forcing](../../../../../../../product-forcing.md) order: below $(p,q)$, choose $p'\leq p$ in $D$ and retain $q$. The [generic filter](../../../../../../../generic-filter.md) $H$ meets it, so its first projection $G_0$ meets $D$. Together with the filter properties proved above, this gives

$$
\boxed{G_0\text{ is }\mathbb P\text{-generic over }M.}
$$

The same argument proves that $G_1$ is $\mathbb Q$-generic over $M$, but genericity over the larger [generic extension](../../../../../../../generic-extension.md) $M[G_0]$ needs the next argument.
