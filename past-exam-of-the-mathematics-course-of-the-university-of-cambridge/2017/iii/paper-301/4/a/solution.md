<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For ordinary bosonic operators at distinct times, [time ordering](../../../../../../time-ordering.md) places the later operator to the left:

$$
\boxed{T\{O_1(t_1)O_2(t_2)\}=\begin{cases}O_1(t_1)O_2(t_2),&t_1>t_2,\\O_2(t_2)O_1(t_1),&t_2>t_1.\end{cases}}
$$

Equivalently, multiply these two products by the corresponding [Heaviside step functions](../../../../../../heaviside-step-function.md). For two odd fermionic operators the second product receives a minus sign; more generally a graded interchange has sign $(-1)^{F_1F_2}$. This sign is the [fermionic sign](../../../../../../fermionic-sign.md) convention for time-ordered field products, not a minus sign for arbitrary bosonic operators. No equal-time convention is needed for the two distinct times in this clause.
