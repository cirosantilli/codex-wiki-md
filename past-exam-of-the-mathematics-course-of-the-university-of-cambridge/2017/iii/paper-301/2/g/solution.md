<h1 id="2/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Insert $\Omega_{jk}=-\epsilon_{jkl}\phi^l$ and the result for $S^{jk}$ into the [Spinor representation of the Lorentz group](../../../../../../spinor-representation-of-the-lorentz-group.md). The [contraction of two Levi-Civita symbols](../../../../../../contraction-of-two-levi-civita-symbols.md) $\sum_{j,k}\epsilon_{jkl}\epsilon^{jkm}=2\delta_l{}^m$ gives

$$
\frac12\Omega_{jk}S^{jk}=\frac i2\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol\phi\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma&0\\0&\boldsymbol\phi\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The [matrix exponential](../../../../../../matrix-exponential.md) of this block diagonal matrix is the stated $O$, so $\psi'(x)=O\psi(\Lambda^{-1}x)$. The plus sign in its exponent follows from both minus signs above; it is tied to the rotation-parameter convention in the PDF.

For a rotation through $2\pi$ about $z$, the [Pauli matrix](../../../../../../pauli-matrices.md) has [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\pm1$, hence $e^{i\pi\sigma^3}=-I_2$. The spacetime rotation is the identity, while

$$
\boxed{\psi'(x)=-\psi(x)\quad\text{for a }2\pi\text{ rotation};\qquad\psi'(x)=\psi(x)\quad\text{for }4\pi.}
$$

This is the [spinor sign under a full spatial rotation](../../../../../../spinor-sign-under-a-full-spatial-rotation.md). A [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) returns to itself already at $2\pi$. The comparison concerns the exponentiated [Lorentz group](../../../../../../lorentz-group.md) representations: a [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) by itself has no distinguished element called a $2\pi$ rotation. The sign records the nontrivial lift of this rotation loop to the [Spin group](../../../../../../spin-group.md).
