<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Specify an initial value or initial [probability distribution](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md). A [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) uses a prescribed [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) on a prescribed [filtered probability space](../../../../../../filtered-probability-space.md). It is continuous, adapted to the completed [filtration](../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md) generated by that [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) and the initial value, and satisfies

$$
X_t=X_0+\int_0^tb(X_s)\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma(X_s)\,dB_s
$$

[almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) for every $t$, with $\int_0^t(|b(X_s)|+\sigma(X_s)^2)ds<\infty$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). The given initial variable is independent of future [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) increments. Some courses allow a larger prescribed [filtration](../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md) in their definition of a [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md); the Brownian-generated convention states explicitly what absence of extra randomness means.

A [weak solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) consists of a [filtered probability space](../../../../../../filtered-probability-space.md), a [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) relative to its [filtration](../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md), and a continuous [adapted process](../../../../../../adapted-process.md) $X$ satisfying the same integrability and integral equation. Here the space and noise are part of the unknown; the [filtration](../../../../../../filtration-probability-theory.md) may contain randomness beyond the initial variable and the driving path.

[Pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md) means that on any common [filtered probability space](../../../../../../filtered-probability-space.md), any two solutions with the same driving [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) and the same initial value are indistinguishable. [Uniqueness in law](../../../../../../uniqueness-in-law.md) means that all [weak solutions](../../../../../../weak-solution.md) with the same initial [probability distribution](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) have the same law as path-valued [random variables](../../../../../../random-variable-split.md), even on different spaces. **Pathwise uniqueness compares shared-noise paths; uniqueness in law compares path distributions.** Neither uniqueness notion by itself asserts existence.
