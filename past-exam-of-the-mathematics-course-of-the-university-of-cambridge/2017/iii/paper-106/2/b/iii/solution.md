<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The coordinate evaluations $e_n:x\mapsto x_n$ belong to the dual [unit ball](../../../../../../../unit-ball.md) of the [l-infinity sequence space](../../../../../../../l-infinity-sequence-space.md) and separate its points. Therefore the [countable separating family metrizes a weakly compact set](../../../../../../../countable-separating-family-metrizes-a-weakly-compact-set.md) result makes any nonempty weakly compact $K\subset\ell_\infty$ a compact [metric space](../../../../../../../metric-space.md) in its weak topology. Choose finite $1/n$-nets and take their countable union $D$; it is weakly dense in $K$.

It follows that $K\subset\overline D^{\,w}\subset\overline{\operatorname{conv}D}^{\,w}$. By [Mazur theorem](../../../../../../../mazur-theorem.md), the last set is $\overline{\operatorname{conv}D}^{\,\|\cdot\|}$. Finite [convex](../../../../../../../convex-function.md) combinations of members of $D$ with nonnegative rational coefficients summing to one form a countable norm-dense subset of $\operatorname{conv}D$: for each fixed finite list, approximate its coefficients in the simplex by rational coefficients, and use the [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) triangle inequality. Thus its [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) closure is a separable [metric space](../../../../../../../metric-space.md) containing $K$.

Every subset of a separable [metric space](../../../../../../../metric-space.md) is separable: balls centred on a countable dense set with positive rational radii form a countable base; intersect this base with the subset and choose one point from each nonempty intersection. Consequently the [weakly compact subsets of l-infinity are norm separable](../../../../../../../weakly-compact-subsets-of-l-infinity-are-norm-separable.md) conclusion is

$$
\boxed{K\text{ is separable for the supremum norm}.}
$$

The empty case is immediate. The convexification matters: the chosen weakly dense set $D$ itself need not be [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) dense in a nonconvex $K$.
