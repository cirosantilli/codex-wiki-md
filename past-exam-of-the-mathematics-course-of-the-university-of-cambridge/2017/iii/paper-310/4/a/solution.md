<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $M_{\rm Pl}$ be the [reduced Planck mass](../../../../../../reduced-planck-mass.md), $M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}=8\pi G$ in units $\hbar=c=1$. Combining that equation with the [Friedmann acceleration equation](../../../../../../friedmann-acceleration-equation.md) gives

$$
\dot H=-\frac{\rho+P}{2M_{\rm Pl}^2}=-\frac{\dot\phi^2}{2M_{\rm Pl}^2}.
$$

Differentiate $3M_{\rm Pl}^2H^2=\rho$ to obtain $\dot\rho=-3H(\rho+P)=-3H\dot\phi^2$. For the homogeneous canonical [scalar field](../../../../../../scalar-field.md), $\dot\rho=\dot\phi\ddot\phi+V_{,\phi}\dot\phi$, so

$$
\dot\phi\bigl(\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V_{,\phi}\bigr)=0.
$$

Where $\dot\phi\ne0$, division gives the [inflaton equation of motion](../../../../../../inflaton-equation-of-motion.md); continuity extends it to isolated turning points:

$$
\boxed{\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi=-V_{,\phi}.}
$$

The [Hubble friction](../../../../../../hubble-friction.md) term describes loss of kinetic energy into expansion.

There is a small logical caveat if the field is frozen on an entire interval: the homogeneous Einstein constraints alone only give the product equation and do not force $V_{,\phi}=0$. The [frozen scalar fields and the Friedmann constraint](../../../../../../frozen-scalar-fields-and-the-friedmann-constraint.md) counterexample makes this explicit. For a canonical scalar, the field equation also follows directly from its [inflaton action in an expanding universe](../../../../../../inflaton-action-in-an-expanding-universe.md), whose homogeneous Lagrangian is $a^3[\dot\phi^2/2-V(\phi)]$. The [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) gives $d(a^3\dot\phi)/dt+a^3V_{,\phi}=0$, including the frozen case, and yields the same boxed equation without division by $\dot\phi$.
