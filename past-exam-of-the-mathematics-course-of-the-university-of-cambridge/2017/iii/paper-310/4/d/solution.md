↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Using the leading predictions just obtained gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
n&n_s&r\\\hline
2&0.96721&0.13115\\
3&0.95902&0.19672\\
4&0.95082&0.26230
\end{array}
$$

The quadratic and cubic [monomial inflation potentials](../../../../../../monomial-inflation-potential.md) satisfy the quoted interval for the [scalar spectral index](../../../../../../scalar-spectral-index.md), whereas the quartic model lies below its lower endpoint. All three exceed the quoted bound on the [tensor-to-scalar ratio](../../../../../../tensor-to-scalar-ratio.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{All three models are excluded by the constraints quoted in the paper.}}
$$

This conclusion uses the exam's numerical bounds and the specified 60-e-fold endpoint convention, not a substitution of later observational constraints. Even a modest endpoint adjustment does not reduce the predicted $r$ by the roughly factor of two or more needed here.
