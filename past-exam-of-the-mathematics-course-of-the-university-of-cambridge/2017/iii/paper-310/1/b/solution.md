<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\Omega=1+\delta$ with $|\delta|\ll1$. Linearizing the [cosmological density parameter flow](../../../../../../cosmological-density-parameter-flow.md) gives $d\delta/d\log a=(1+3\omega)\delta+O(\delta^2)$. For constant [equation-of-state parameter](../../../../../../equation-of-state-parameter.md),

$$
\boxed{\delta(a)=\delta(a_i)\left(\frac a{a_i}\right)^{1+3\omega}\quad\text{to linear order}.}
$$

For variable $\omega$, the correct replacement is $\delta(a)=\delta(a_i)\exp[\int_{a_i}^{a}(1+3\omega(\widetilde a))\,d\log\widetilde a]$. The printed power assumes constant $\omega$.

For [pressureless matter](../../../../../../pressureless-matter.md), the departure from flatness grows as $a$; for [radiation in cosmology](../../../../../../radiation-in-cosmology.md), it grows as $a^2$. Hence in a conventional decelerating expanding history, maintaining $|\Omega-1|\ll1$ at a late epoch requires extremely small departures at early times. This is the [flatness problem](../../../../../../flatness-problem.md), not an assertion that exact flatness is inconsistent: $\Omega=1$ is an exact solution, but it is unstable to small departures in [spatial curvature of an FLRW universe](../../../../../../spatial-curvature-of-an-flrw-universe.md) during these eras. With $\omega<-1/3$ the perturbation instead decays, which supplies the [inflationary solution of the flatness problem](../../../../../../inflationary-solution-of-the-flatness-problem.md).
