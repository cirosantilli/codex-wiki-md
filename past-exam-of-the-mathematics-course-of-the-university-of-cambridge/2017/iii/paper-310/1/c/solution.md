<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The printed $\mathcal H$ is the [conformal Hubble parameter](../../../../../../conformal-hubble-parameter.md), $\mathcal H=aH=\dot a$; $\mathcal H^{-1}$ is the [comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../../comoving-hubble-radius.md), rather than the physical radius $H^{-1}$. Since the derivative requested is with respect to [cosmic time](../../../../../../cosmic-time.md),

$$
\frac d{dt}\mathcal H^{-1}=-\frac{\ddot a}{\dot a^2}
=\frac{4\pi G a\rho}{3\dot a^2}(1+3\omega)
=\frac\Omega{2a}(1+3\omega).
$$

Thus the [cosmic-time derivative of the comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../../cosmic-time-derivative-of-the-comoving-hubble-radius.md) has

$$
\boxed{A=\frac\Omega{2a}>0\quad(a>0,\ \rho>0,\ H\ne0).}
$$

For an expanding universe, the same factor $1+3\omega$ determines whether a small departure from flatness grows and whether the [comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../../comoving-hubble-radius.md) grows. Ordinary matter with $\omega>-1/3$ therefore has both signs associated with the conventional [flatness problem](../../../../../../flatness-problem.md) and [horizon problem](../../../../../../horizon-problem.md). Scales whose physical wavelengths now exceed the [Hubble radius](../../../../../../hubble-radius.md) were even farther outside it, in relative terms, earlier in such an era, rather than being brought inside for causal equilibration. Accelerated expansion with $\omega<-1/3$ reverses both signs.

There is a qualification to the word “always”: an actual [horizon problem](../../../../../../horizon-problem.md) depends on the complete past history, not just this local sign. In a flat, constant-$\omega$ hot [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md) model with $\omega>-1/3$, $a\propto(t-t_B)^p$ with $p=2/[3(1+\omega)]<1$, so the comoving [particle horizon](../../../../../../particle-horizon.md) $\int_{t_B}^t dt'/a(t')$ is finite. The conventional causal problem then accompanies the flatness instability. An earlier accelerated era or a different past boundary can change the causal conclusion even when the present-era [comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../../comoving-hubble-radius.md) is growing. Thus the requested correspondence is valid in that usual expanding hot [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md) setting, not a universal logical equivalence between global horizons and local stability. At $\omega=-1/3$ both local effects are marginal.
