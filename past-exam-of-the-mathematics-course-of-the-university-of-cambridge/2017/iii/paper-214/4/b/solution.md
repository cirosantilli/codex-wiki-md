<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For finite $G$, choose a root $r$ and an ordering of the other [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). Begin with a [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) consisting only of $r$. At each stage take the first [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) not already in the [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md), run an independent [simple random walk](../../../../../../simple-random-walk.md) from it until its first hit of the existing [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md), erase the loops chronologically, and add the resulting [graph path](../../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md). In chronological [loop erasure](../../../../../../loop-erasure.md), revisiting a [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) already in the current list deletes all list entries after that [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md); otherwise append the new [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). The retained final [graph path](../../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) is simple, with new internal [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and exactly one endpoint in the old [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md). Adding it therefore preserves connectedness and creates no [graph cycle](../../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). [Graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) already included are skipped. On a finite [connected graph](../../../../../../connected-graph.md) each walk hits the existing [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md); after finitely many attachments all [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) are present.

The [Wilson algorithm](../../../../../../wilson-s-algorithm.md) theorem states that this [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md) is a [uniform spanning tree](../../../../../../uniform-spanning-tree.md), independently of the chosen root and order. The required hypotheses here are an undirected connected finite [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) and transition [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) proportional to conductance; unit conductances give [simple random walk](../../../../../../simple-random-walk.md) and the uniform law. This is the sampling result being described, not a claim that arbitrary walk transition [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) would produce a uniform [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md).

For an infinite connected locally finite [recurrent graph](../../../../../../recurrent-graph.md), choose a fixed finite root $r$ and enumerate all [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). A connected [locally finite graph](../../../../../../locally-finite-graph.md) is countable, since its finite-radius balls are finite. Run the same algorithm successively. Recurrence and irreducibility imply that a walk from any [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) hits $r$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md), hence hits the current [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md). Every attached [graph path](../../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) is therefore finite. The countable intersection of these probability-one events has [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) one, and the increasing union is an acyclic connected subgraph containing all [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md): each [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) receives a finite route to $r$.

This gives the [uniform spanning tree of a recurrent infinite graph](../../../../../../uniform-spanning-tree-of-a-recurrent-infinite-graph.md). It is not a uniform counting measure on all infinite [spanning trees](../../../../../../spanning-tree.md). Equivalently, its joint [probability distributions](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md) on finite collections of [edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) are the common limits of [uniform spanning trees](../../../../../../uniform-spanning-tree.md) along any [graph exhaustion](../../../../../../graph-exhaustion.md), using the approximations defining the [free uniform spanning forest](../../../../../../free-uniform-spanning-forest.md) and the [wired uniform spanning forest](../../../../../../wired-uniform-spanning-forest.md). To check that equivalence, fix a finite [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) set $F$ and run the chosen enumeration through a finite prefix containing all its endpoints. Once those [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) are in the [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory.md), later attachments cannot add an [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) with both endpoints already included, so membership of $F$ is settled permanently. Only finitely many random walks have been used; each has a finite trajectory [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). All their visited [graph vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and all their [graph neighbours](../../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) lie in a sufficiently large exhaustion set. Couple finite-volume and infinite-volume walks with the same steps there. Whether the exterior is deleted or wired to one boundary [graph vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md), these initial trajectories and their loop erasures then agree. This proves convergence of the [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) of [cylinder sets](../../../../../../cylinder-set.md) determined by those [edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md).

The limiting law is unchanged by the root and ordering because the laws on finite [graphs](../../../../../../graph-split.md) have this invariance. The same coupling shows that changing the exhaustion also leaves the law unchanged. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{recurrent }G:\quad\text{fixed-root Wilson tree}=\text{free limit}=\text{wired limit}.}
$$

The result is a single [spanning tree](../../../../../../spanning-tree.md) because the [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) is recurrent. On a [transient graph](../../../../../../transient-graph.md), infinite-volume spanning-tree limits may instead be [uniform spanning forests](../../../../../../uniform-spanning-forest.md); recurrence cannot be omitted from this conclusion.
