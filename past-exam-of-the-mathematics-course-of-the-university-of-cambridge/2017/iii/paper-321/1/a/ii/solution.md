<h1 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Interpret the outer [Dirac delta function](../../../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) as a boundary source carrying its full mass, or as the limit of a narrow source lying just inside $r_2$. Then $\int_u^{r_2}vS(v)dv=r_2S_0$ for $r_1<u<r_2$, and

$$
\boxed{\Sigma(r)=\frac{2r_2S_0}{3\bar\nu(r)}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r}}\right)=\frac{\dot M}{3\pi\bar\nu(r)}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r}}\right),\qquad \dot M=2\pi r_2S_0.}
$$

Thus the interior is the usual steady [Keplerian accretion disk](../../../../../../../keplerian-accretion-disk.md) with a constant inward [accretion rate](../../../../../../../accretion-rate.md). The product $\bar\nu\Sigma$ vanishes at the stress-free inner edge, and the [surface density of a disk](../../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) does too if the viscosity has a finite nonzero boundary value; far from that edge $\bar\nu\Sigma$ approaches $\dot M/(3\pi)$. Its density need not itself be constant because the viscosity may vary.

The [outer-edge source of a viscous disk](../../../../../../../outer-edge-source-of-a-viscous-disk.md) requires care about the boundary trace. The flux just inside the injecting edge is $\mathcal F(r_2^-)=\dot M$, while the no-flux value outside the source is $\mathcal F(r_2^+)=0$; the source produces the intervening jump. A simultaneously continuous interior condition $\mathcal F(r_2^-)=0$ would contradict nonzero steady injection. A symmetrically regularized delta integrated only up to its center can instead give half its full weight; that would mean a different supplied mass, not a different interior solution for the same $\dot M$. The physically normalized one-sided limiting source removes this convention ambiguity.
