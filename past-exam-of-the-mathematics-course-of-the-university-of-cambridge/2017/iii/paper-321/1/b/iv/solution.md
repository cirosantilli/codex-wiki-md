<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Assume the [vertical dynamical timescale of a disk](../../../../../../../vertical-dynamical-timescale-of-a-disk.md) is shorter than the [thermal timescale of an accretion disk](../../../../../../../thermal-timescale-of-an-accretion-disk.md), which is itself shorter than the [viscous timescale](../../../../../../../viscous-timescale.md). This ordering is controlled in a [thin disk](../../../../../../../thin-disk.md) with $\alpha\ll1$: $t_{\rm dyn}\sim\Omega^{-1}$, $t_{\rm th}\sim(\alpha\Omega)^{-1}$ and $t_\nu\sim(r/H)^2t_{\rm th}$. Therefore compare heating and cooling at fixed [surface density of a disk](../../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md), radius, $\alpha$ and composition. Vertical [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) adjusts on the [vertical dynamical timescale of a disk](../../../../../../../vertical-dynamical-timescale-of-a-disk.md), giving $H\propto T^{1/2}$ and $\rho\propto T^{-1/2}$. Consequently the stipulated [negative hydrogen ion opacity](../../../../../../../negative-hydrogen-ion-opacity.md) varies as $\kappa\propto T^{10-1/6}=T^{59/6}$. The rates per disk face then scale as

$$
\boxed{Q^+\sim\alpha\Sigma\mathcal R\Omega T\propto T,\qquad Q^-\sim\frac{\sigma T^4}{\kappa\Sigma}\propto T^{-35/6}.}
$$

Thus heating increases while cooling decreases after a positive [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) perturbation. At equilibrium $Q^+=Q^-=Q_0$, a small change gives $\delta(Q^+-Q^-)=(41/6)(Q_0/T)\delta T$, which drives the perturbation further from equilibrium for positive effective [heat capacity](../../../../../../../heat-capacity.md). A negative [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) perturbation likewise increases the net cooling. This proves [thermal instability from negative hydrogen ion opacity](../../../../../../../thermal-instability-from-negative-hydrogen-ion-opacity.md) within the local closure; it is the temperature-slope test of [thermal stability of an accretion disk](../../../../../../../thermal-stability-of-an-accretion-disk.md). Holding volume density rather than [surface density of a disk](../../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) fixed would give a different cooling exponent, but still the same qualitative instability; the fixed-column comparison is the relevant thin-disk thermal test.
