<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write the proposed background as $\mathbf U=U(x,z)\mathbf e_y$, $U=-3\Omega x/2+q\Omega z$. It satisfies [incompressibility](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md), and $\mathbf U\cdot\nabla\mathbf U=U\partial_y\mathbf U=0$. A constant [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) has zero [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md). The [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) is $-2\Omega\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf U=2\Omega U\mathbf e_x=-(3x-2qz)\Omega^2\mathbf e_x$, which exactly cancels the prescribed body force. Thus it is a [steady state](../../../../../../steady-state.md).

**The background is ordinary Keplerian radial shear together with vertical shear:** $\partial_xU=-3\Omega/2$ and $\partial_zU=q\Omega$. At a given radius, different heights have different azimuthal velocities. The small $q$ in this question measures vertical shear; it is distinct from the [orbital shear parameter](../../../../../../orbital-shear-parameter.md), whose radial value here remains $3/2$. The [vertical shear instability](../../../../../../vertical-shear-instability.md) draws on this height-dependent differential rotation.
