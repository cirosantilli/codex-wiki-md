<h1 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose the positive-frequency branch and real radial amplitude $u$. The azimuthal amplitude of the [inertial-acoustic wave](../../../../../../../inertial-acoustic-wave.md) is $U_y=-i\Omega u/(2\omega)$, so taking real parts gives

$$
u'_x=u\cos(kx-\omega t),\qquad u'_y=\frac{\Omega u}{2\omega}\sin(kx-\omega t).
$$

For $|k|c_s/\Omega\ll1$, $\omega=\Omega[1+O((kc_s/\Omega)^2)]$. Thus, to leading long-wavelength order,

$$
\boxed{u'_x\simeq u\cos(kx-\Omega t),\qquad u'_y\simeq\frac u2\sin(kx-\Omega t).}
$$

The quarter-cycle phase shift and the factor $1/2$ are the [epicyclic motion](../../../../../../../epicyclic-motion.md) relation in a [Keplerian shearing sheet](../../../../../../../keplerian-shearing-sheet.md). This approximation drops the small frequency detuning; over times $|\omega-\Omega|t\gtrsim1$ that phase approximation is not uniform. The associated fractional [surface density of a disk](../../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) perturbation is $\sigma/\Sigma_0=(ku/\omega)\cos(kx-\omega t)$, so [linearization](../../../../../../../linearization.md) also requires $|ku/\omega|\ll1$. The source's arbitrary real $u$ sets the wave phase/amplitude within that small-perturbation regime.
