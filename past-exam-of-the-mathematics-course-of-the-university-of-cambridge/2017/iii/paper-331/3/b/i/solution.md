<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For this nonlinear ansatz, take $k$ real as well as $U,\mu$ real. Then $|e^{ik(x-Ut)}|=1$, and the advective time [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes. Substitution into the [nonlinear Ginzburg-Landau equation](../../../../../../../nonlinear-ginzburg-landau-equation.md) gives

$$
0=(\mu-k^2)Q-Q^3.
$$

The stipulated positive-amplitude [Ginzburg-Landau plane wave](../../../../../../../ginzburg-landau-plane-wave.md) therefore has

$$
\boxed{Q=\sqrt{\mu-k^2}.}
$$

The strict inequality $\mu>k^2$ guarantees that this is real and positive. The zero solution of the [nonlinear Ginzburg-Landau equation](../../../../../../../nonlinear-ginzburg-landau-equation.md) also exists but is not the required positive wave. Although complex $k$ is useful in the linear impulse analysis, it cannot generally be carried into this constant-amplitude nonlinear ansatz: its spatially varying modulus would make the cubic term carry a different spatial factor.
