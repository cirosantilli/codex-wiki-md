<h1 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At the [absolute wavenumber](../../../../../../../absolute-wavenumber.md), the [group velocity](../../../../../../../group-velocity.md) $d\omega/dk$ is zero. The corresponding accessible saddle therefore governs the disturbance seen at a fixed position. Its exponential [growth rate](../../../../../../../growth-rate.md) is

$$
\operatorname{Im}\omega_0=\mu-\frac{U^2}{4(1+c_d^2)}.
$$

Saddle accessibility can be checked directly here. With $D=1+ic_d$, whose [real part](../../../../../../../real-part.md) is positive, [Fourier inversion](../../../../../../../fourier-inversion-theorem.md) gives the [Green function of the linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation](../../../../../../../green-function-of-the-linear-complex-ginzburg-landau-equation.md),

$$
G(x,t)=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}
\exp\left[\mu t-\frac{(x-Ut)^2}{4Dt}\right],\qquad t>0.
$$

At fixed $x$ its exponential rate is $\operatorname{Im}\omega_0$, while along the packet centre $x=Ut$ it is $\mu$. For real [wavenumbers](../../../../../../../wavenumber.md) of [Fourier modes](../../../../../../../fourier-mode.md), the [temporal growth rate](../../../../../../../growth-rate.md) is $\operatorname{Im}\omega(k)=\mu-k^2$, so the flow is temporally unstable exactly when $\mu>0$.

Consequently the classifications for a localized disturbance in this laboratory frame are

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
0<\mu<\frac{U^2}{4(1+c_d^2)}&:\ \text{convective instability},\\
\mu>\frac{U^2}{4(1+c_d^2)}&:\ \text{absolute instability}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

For [convective hydrodynamic instability](../../../../../../../convective-hydrodynamic-instability.md), a travelling [wave packet](../../../../../../../wave-packet.md) amplifies but the response at each fixed position decays. For [absolute hydrodynamic instability](../../../../../../../absolute-hydrodynamic-instability.md), that fixed-position response amplifies. The equality is the marginal absolute threshold: its exponential rate is zero and the impulse response has a $t^{-1/2}$ prefactor. For $\mu<0$ there is no temporal growth; $\mu=0$ is temporally marginal. When $U=0$ the convective window is empty. These conclusions use the infinite-line impulse problem; a general [dispersion relation](../../../../../../../dispersion-relation.md) requires its own spatial-branch or saddle selection.
