<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For repulsive interactions $g>0$, the [Thomas–Fermi approximation for a condensate](../../../../../../thomas-fermi-approximation-for-a-condensate.md) neglects the density-gradient [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) away from boundaries and vortex cores. The stationary [Gross–Pitaevskii equation](../../../../../../gross-pitaevskii-equation.md) then gives $\mu=V_{\rm ext}(r)+gn(r)$ wherever the condensate is present. At the axis $\mu=gn_0$, hence

$$
\boxed{n(r)=n_0\left(1-\frac{r^2}{R_r^2}\right)_+,\qquad
R_r=\sqrt{\frac{2gn_0}{m\omega_r^2}}.}
$$

Here $(q)_+=\max(q,0)$ and the support is also restricted to the physical bucket $r\leq R_0$. The next parts' explicit outer cutoff $R_r$ assumes $R_r\leq R_0$; if $R_0<R_r$ the wall truncates the parabola and the corresponding [integrals](../../../../../../integral.md) must use $R_0$, not the natural trap radius. A hard-wall boundary layer or the smooth trap-edge healing region is beyond the [Thomas–Fermi approximation for a condensate](../../../../../../thomas-fermi-approximation-for-a-condensate.md). For an untruncated radial profile, the total particle number is $N=\pi H_0n_0R_r^2/2$.
