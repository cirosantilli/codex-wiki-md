<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $x(j)=1$ for every $j\in\mathbb Z$, and let $y$ differ from $x$ only at $j=0$, where $y(0)=2$. Under the [left shift](../../../../../../left-shift.md), the unique defect of $\mathcal L^n y$ lies at coordinate $-n$. Every fixed finite coordinate set eventually avoids the defect, so

$$
\mathcal L^n x=x\longrightarrow x,\qquad \mathcal L^n y\longrightarrow x
$$

in the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md). If a [metric](../../../../../../metric.md) $d$ induces this topology, convergence and the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) imply $d(\mathcal L^n x,\mathcal L^n y)\to0$. But [left shift](../../../../../../left-shift.md) invariance would give

$$
d(\mathcal L^n x,\mathcal L^n y)=d(x,y)>0
$$

for every $n$, a contradiction. Thus **no [compatible metric](../../../../../../compatible-metric.md) invariant under the [left shift](../../../../../../left-shift.md) exists**. The discrete [metric](../../../../../../metric.md) $\mathbf1_{\{x\ne y\}}$ is shift invariant, but it induces the [discrete topology](../../../../../../discrete-space.md) rather than the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md); compatibility is the essential restriction.
