<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Pull the given [finite coloring](../../../../../../finite-coloring.md) $\chi:\mathbb N\to[k]$ back to a [finite coloring](../../../../../../finite-coloring.md) of unordered pairs by putting

$$
\widetilde\chi(\{a,b\})=\chi(ab^2)\qquad(a<b).
$$

The ordering $a<b$ makes this a well-defined [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) on the [edges](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of a [complete graph](../../../../../../complete-graph.md). By [Ramsey's theorem](../../../../../../ramsey-s-theorem.md), some [infinite set](../../../../../../infinite-set.md) $X=\{x_1<x_2<\cdots\}$ has all its pairs of one color $r$ under $\widetilde\chi$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\chi(x_i x_j^2)=r\qquad(i<j).}
$$

Possible coincidences between these products cause no difficulty, since the original [finite coloring](../../../../../../finite-coloring.md) assigns each [positive integer](../../../../../../positive-integer.md) a single color.
