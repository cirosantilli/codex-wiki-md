<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Assume the classical [black-hole area theorem](../../../../../../../hawking-s-area-theorem.md) applies: the [null energy condition](../../../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and the required global regularity or [strong asymptotic predictability](../../../../../../../strong-asymptotic-predictability.md) hypotheses hold. The disconnected initial horizon sections then give $A_f\geq A_1+A_2$. Define

$$
X=\frac{A_1+A_2}{8\pi}
=\sum_{i=1}^2\left(M_i^2+\sqrt{M_i^4-J_i^2}\right),\qquad
J_f=J_1+J_2.
$$

For any regular [Kerr black hole](../../../../../../../kerr-black-hole.md), $|J_i|\leq M_i^2$, so $X\geq|J_1|+|J_2|\geq|J_f|$. The [irreducible mass](../../../../../../../irreducible-mass.md) identity obtained by inverting the Kerr area relation is

$$
M_f^2=\frac12\left(Y+\frac{J_f^2}{Y}\right),\qquad Y=\frac{A_f}{8\pi}\geq|J_f|.
$$

Indeed, $Y=M_f^2+\sqrt{M_f^4-J_f^2}$ implies $(Y-M_f^2)^2=M_f^4-J_f^2$, which gives this expression. At fixed $J_f$, its [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) with respect to $Y$ is $\tfrac12(1-J_f^2/Y^2)\geq0$ on the physical branch. Therefore the [area bound for an axisymmetric Kerr merger](../../../../../../../area-bound-for-an-axisymmetric-kerr-merger.md) is

$$
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{rad}}\leq M_1+M_2-
\sqrt{\frac12\left[X+\frac{(J_1+J_2)^2}{X}\right]}.}
$$

This uses the ideal limit of infinite initial separation, in which the initial total [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) is $M_1+M_2$. At finite separation it is the initial total gravitational [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md), including binding [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md), that replaces this sum. The inequality is an upper limit, not a prediction or a guarantee of saturation; real mergers increase the horizon area.

Changing only a spin sign leaves $X$ unchanged, whereas the square of the final [angular momentum](../../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) changes. For fixed nonzero $|J_1|,|J_2|$, same-sign spins give $(|J_1|+|J_2|)^2$, and opposite signs give $(|J_1|-|J_2|)^2$. The remnant must retain more rotational [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) in the same-sign case, so its minimum allowed [mass](../../../../../../../mass.md) is larger and the radiated-[energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) upper limit is smaller. Cancellation of opposite spins permits more of the initial rotational [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) to be radiated while respecting the same initial total area.

For example, equal extremal initial holes have $M_1=M_2=M$ and $|J_1|=|J_2|=M^2$. Same-sign spins allow at most $(2-\sqrt2)M$, a fraction $1-1/\sqrt2$ of the initial [energy](../../../../../../../energy.md); opposite signs allow at most $M$, a fraction $1/2$. These are geometric bounds under exact [axisymmetry](../../../../../../../axisymmetric-vector-field.md), not achievable-efficiency claims.
