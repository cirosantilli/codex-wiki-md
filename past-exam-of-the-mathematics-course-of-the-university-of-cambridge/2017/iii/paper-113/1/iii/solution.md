<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The rings $A,B$ are nonzero [integral domains](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). Let $\eta$ be the [generic point](../../../../../../generic-point.md) of $Y$, corresponding to $(0)\subseteq A$. Surjectivity provides a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak q\subseteq B$ with $\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=0$. Since $\ker\alpha\subseteq\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, the [ring homomorphism](../../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) $\alpha$ is injective.

Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}A$ and $S=A\setminus\{0\}$. The [scheme-theoretic fibre](../../../../../../scheme-theoretic-fibre.md) at $\eta$ is

$$
\boxed{X_\eta=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}K
=\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_AK)=\operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}B).}
$$

Every element of $\alpha(S)$ is nonzero, so the [localization](../../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) $S^{-1}B$ is a nonzero integral domain. Its zero ideal is prime, ensuring that its spectrum is nonempty; an affine spectrum of an integral domain is an [integral scheme](../../../../../../integral-scheme.md). This proves the [integrality of the generic fibre of an affine dominant morphism](../../../../../../integrality-of-the-generic-fibre-of-an-affine-dominant-morphism.md). In fact the proof only needs injectivity of $\alpha$, rather than surjectivity at every point.
