<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [dual numbers](../../../../../../dual-number.md) $A=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ over any [field](../../../../../../field.md) $k$, let $B=k$, and let $\alpha$ send $\varepsilon$ to zero. Its [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is the nonzero [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $(\varepsilon)$, but both [spectra of rings](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md) have exactly one point: every [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) contains the [nilpotent element](../../../../../../nilpotent.md) $\varepsilon$, and the only prime of $A$ is $(\varepsilon)$. Consequently the induced map is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) for the [Zariski topology](../../../../../../zariski-topology.md), although it is not an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) of [schemes](../../../../../../scheme.md):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}k\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)).}
$$

More generally, quotienting by a [nilpotent ideal](../../../../../../nilpotent-ideal.md) gives a [nilpotent thickening](../../../../../../nilpotent-thickening.md) with unchanged underlying topology. The [structure sheaf](../../../../../../structure-sheaf-of-a-scheme.md) retains information that the topology alone cannot recover.
