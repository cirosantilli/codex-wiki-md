<h1 id="3/iii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Part (b) supplies an [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) $A$ with $D-A$ big. Therefore $D=(D-A)+A$ is big as well, since adding an [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) class to a big class preserves the ample-plus-effective decomposition. The permitted induction hypothesis makes $D$ [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) on every proper positive-dimensional closed subscheme of lower dimension, in particular on every codimension-one integral [subvariety](../../../../../../../closed-subvariety.md). Zero-dimensional restrictions are [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) automatically.

Apply part (ii) to this big $D$ and the [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) $A$ chosen in (b). The result is

$$
\boxed{D-\epsilon A\text{ is nef for }0<\epsilon\ll1.}
$$

For a reducible scheme the same argument is componentwise. If a positive-dimensional irreducible component itself is a strict closed subscheme of $X$, the permitted induction assumption already makes $D$ [ample](../../../../../../../ample-line-bundle.md) on that component; otherwise its codimension-one [subvarieties](../../../../../../../closed-subvariety.md) are lower-dimensional and the same proof applies. Only one finite minimum of bounds is required.
