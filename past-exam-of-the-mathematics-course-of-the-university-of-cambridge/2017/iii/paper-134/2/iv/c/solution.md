<h1 id="2/iv/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [Castelnuovo contraction criterion](../../../../../../../castelnuovo-contraction-criterion.md) says that a [smooth rational curve](../../../../../../../smooth-rational-curve.md) $C$ on a [smooth projective surface](../../../../../../../smooth-projective-surface.md) with $C^2=-1$ can be contracted by a [birational morphism](../../../../../../../birational-morphism.md) to a smooth point of a [smooth projective surface](../../../../../../../smooth-projective-surface.md). The contraction is an isomorphism away from $C$, and its inverse is the [blowup of a smooth algebraic surface](../../../../../../../blowup-of-a-smooth-algebraic-surface.md) at that point. This is the algebraic contraction criterion, valid over the algebraically closed field here; it is not the rationality criterion bearing Castelnuovo's name.

By (b), every $C\in\operatorname{Neg}(K_X)$ satisfies the hypothesis. Therefore the required morphism exists:

$$
\boxed{f_1:X\to X_1,\quad f_1(C)=\{p\},\quad X_1\text{ smooth and projective},\quad X\setminus C\cong X_1\setminus\{p\}.}
$$

The PDF calls $f_1$ a birational map; the conclusion is stronger, since this map is everywhere defined and is a [birational morphism](../../../../../../../birational-morphism.md).
