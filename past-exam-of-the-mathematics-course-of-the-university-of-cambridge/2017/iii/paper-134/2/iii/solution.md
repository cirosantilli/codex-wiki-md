<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Because $C\cong\mathbb P^1$ and $\deg\mathcal O_C(C)=C^2=0$, its normal [line bundle](../../../../../../line-bundle.md) is $\mathcal O_C(C)\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}$. Thus every $\mathcal O_C(mC)$ is trivial and has $H^1=0$. The [divisor restriction exact sequence](../../../../../../divisor-restriction-exact-sequence.md) gives, for every $m\ge1$,

$$
0\to\mathcal O_X((m-1)C)\to\mathcal O_X(mC)\to\mathcal O_C\to0.
$$

The maps $H^1(X,(m-1)C)\to H^1(X,mC)$ are surjective. Their nonnegative finite dimensions eventually stabilize, so for every sufficiently large $m$ restriction $H^0(X,mC)\to H^0(C,\mathcal O_C)=k$ is surjective. A lift of $1$ is nowhere zero along $C$, and the canonical section of $mC$ is nowhere zero outside $C$. Together they generate $\mathcal O_X(mC)$. Therefore $C$ is [semiample](../../../../../../semiample-divisor.md).

For those same large $m$, the exact sequence of [global sections](../../../../../../global-section.md) gives

$$
h^0(X,mC)-h^0(X,(m-1)C)=1.
$$

Thus $h^0(X,mC)=m+O(1)$, and the [Iitaka dimension](../../../../../../iitaka-dimension.md) is exactly one:

$$
\boxed{C\text{ is semiample},\qquad\kappa(C)=1.}
$$

Equivalently, its basepoint-free multiple defines a morphism to a curve: it is nonconstant because its sections grow, and cannot have two-dimensional image because $(mC)^2=0$. This is the [semiampleness of a square-zero rational curve](../../../../../../semiampleness-of-a-square-zero-rational-curve.md); it uses no characteristic-zero vanishing theorem.
