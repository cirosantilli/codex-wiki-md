<h1 id="4/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

One approach uses [exoplanet transmission spectra](../../../../../../exoplanet-transmission-spectrum.md). Three aerosol signatures are:

- Muted molecular-band amplitudes in the near-infrared, for example weak [water](../../../../../../water.md) structure around $1.4\,\mu\mathrm m$: an [optically thick](../../../../../../optically-thick-medium.md) [exoplanet cloud deck](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud-deck.md) raises the continuum and hides part of the gas column.
- A nearly grey transit-radius continuum across optical/near-infrared wavelengths, roughly $0.5$--$2\,\mu\mathrm m$, when particles are large relative to the wavelengths. Suppressed optical atomic-line wings provide a related indication that deep high-[pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) layers are hidden.
- A radius increasing toward blue/ultraviolet wavelengths, roughly $0.3$--$1\,\mu\mathrm m$, for small-particle [scattering of light](../../../../../../scattering-of-light.md). In a simple [Rayleigh scattering](../../../../../../rayleigh-scattering.md) limit, $\sigma\propto\lambda^{-4}$, and the [scattering slope of a transmission spectrum](../../../../../../scattering-slope-of-a-transmission-spectrum.md) is $dR_p/d\log\lambda=-4H$.

A second approach uses the dayside spectrum and brightness obtained with [exoplanet secondary eclipses](../../../../../../exoplanet-secondary-eclipse.md), optionally supported by orbital phase variations. Its three useful aerosol signatures are:

- Enhanced reflected-light [geometric albedo](../../../../../../geometric-albedo.md) or characteristic colour in the visible, roughly $0.4$--$0.9\,\mu\mathrm m$, for a scattering [exoplanet cloud](../../../../../../exoplanet-cloud.md). Absorbing [atmospheric haze](../../../../../../haze.md) can instead lower the reflected flux, so high albedo is not a universal aerosol rule.
- Weak near-/mid-infrared gas features and a cloud-set continuum [brightness temperature](../../../../../../brightness-temperature.md), roughly $1$--$10\,\mu\mathrm m$, when an opaque cloud moves the thermal photosphere to a different [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md). Changes in the [exoplanet thermal phase curve](../../../../../../exoplanet-thermal-phase-curve.md) can support a spatially patchy cloud interpretation, though circulation also affects it.
- Broad condensate-dependent infrared spectral structure, for example a silicate resonance around $10\,\mu\mathrm m$, if particles are sufficiently small and the optical-depth/temperature geometry permits the feature. Its presence and sign depend on particle composition, size and vertical thermal structure.

**Transmission measures aerosol extinction along the limb; eclipse/reflection measurements test dayside scattering and emission.** No single flattened feature proves clouds: small [atmospheric scale height](../../../../../../atmospheric-scale-height.md), low molecular abundances, stellar heterogeneity and thermal-gradient degeneracies must be considered. Joint wavelength coverage and geometries make the inference stronger.
