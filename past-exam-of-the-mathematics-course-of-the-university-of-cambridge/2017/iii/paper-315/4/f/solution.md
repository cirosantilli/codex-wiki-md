<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Three routes away from local [thermochemical equilibrium](../../../../../../thermochemical-equilibrium.md) are:

- Vertical transport and quenching. When the [eddy mixing time](../../../../../../eddy-mixing-time.md) becomes shorter than the [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md), gas retains a deeper composition. In cool, directly imaged giant [exoplanets](../../../../../../exoplanet-split.md), [carbon monoxide–methane quenching](../../../../../../carbon-monoxide-methane-quenching.md) can preserve CO and suppress the methane expected in cool equilibrium layers. In [Jupiter](../../../../../../jupiter.md), CO carried from deeper hot layers, and [phosphine](../../../../../../phosphine.md) maintained against upper-atmosphere chemical loss, are examples of transported disequilibrium species.
- Horizontal transport and quenching. [Atmospheric advection time](../../../../../../atmospheric-advection-time.md) shorter than the local [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md) carries abundances between regions of different [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md). A [hot Jupiter](../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md) can carry CO-rich dayside gas into cooler nightside gas that would otherwise favour [methane](../../../../../../methane.md). In the Solar System, upper-atmosphere CO produced on the illuminated side of [Venus](../../../../../../venus.md) can be redistributed to its dark side by circulation; this is an example of nonlocal production and transport, rather than local dark-side thermochemical equilibrium.
- [Atmospheric photochemistry](../../../../../../atmospheric-photochemistry.md). Ultraviolet photons dissociate or ionize molecules and initiate reaction networks. Models of irradiated hydrogen-rich [exoplanet atmospheres](../../../../../../exoplanet-atmosphere.md) can produce enhanced [hydrogen cyanide](../../../../../../hydrogen-cyanide.md) and hydrocarbon precursors from methane/nitrogen chemistry; whether these products accumulate depends on ultraviolet flux and transport. The [terrestrial atmosphere](../../../../../../terrestrial-atmosphere.md) gives a clear Solar-System example: oxygen photodissociation and subsequent reactions maintain the [ozone layer](../../../../../../ozone-layer.md), which is not a purely thermochemical-equilibrium abundance.

**Vertical mixing, horizontal advection, and photochemistry supply three mechanisms and examples in both exoplanets and Solar-System atmospheres.** A photochemical steady state balances production and loss; it is different from a [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) minimum. These examples describe mechanisms and model expectations rather than asserting unique observational attribution for every planet.
