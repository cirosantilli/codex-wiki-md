<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take an unresolved spherical planet at [Euclidean distance](../../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) $d\gg R$, with uniform surface [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) $T$, stationary isotropic emission, and no intervening extinction. Isothermality does not by itself imply [blackbody](../../../../../../blackbody.md) radiation; first derive the geometric result for a uniform emergent [specific intensity](../../../../../../specific-intensity.md) $I_\lambda$.

The observer sees the projected disc with apparent [solid angle](../../../../../../solid-angle.md) $\Omega_p\simeq\pi R^2/d^2$. Integrating its [specific intensity](../../../../../../specific-intensity.md) over that angle gives the observed spectral [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md)

$$
f_\lambda=\int_{\rm disc}I_\lambda\cos\theta_{\rm obs}\,d\Omega
\simeq\pi I_\lambda\frac{R^2}{d^2},
$$

where $\theta_{\rm obs}$ is the small angle to the detector normal. For a [blackbody](../../../../../../blackbody.md), $I_\lambda=B_\lambda(T)$, and the [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) gives $\int_0^\infty\pi B_\lambda(T)\,d\lambda=\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f_\lambda=\pi B_\lambda(T)(R/d)^2,\qquad
f=\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4(R/d)^2=\frac{L}{4\pi d^2}.}
$$

Equivalently, the surface [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md) is $\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4$ and the [luminosity of a spherical blackbody](../../../../../../luminosity-of-a-spherical-blackbody.md) is $L=4\pi R^2\sigma_{\rm SB}T^4$, leading to the same result by inverse-square dilution. A uniform wavelength-dependent [emissivity](../../../../../../emissivity.md) $\epsilon_\lambda$ multiplies the spectral formula; an atmospheric spectrum can require a full angular [radiative transfer](../../../../../../radiative-transfer.md) integral rather than a single [Planck function](../../../../../../planck-function.md).
