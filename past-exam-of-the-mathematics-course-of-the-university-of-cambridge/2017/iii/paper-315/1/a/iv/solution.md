<h1 id="1/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The functional form is not specific to [exoplanets](../../../../../../../exoplanet-split.md). With positive [pressure](../../../../../../../pressure.md) normalization and physical [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md), a [square-root exponential atmospheric profile](../../../../../../../square-root-exponential-atmospheric-profile.md) can approximate a monotone interval of the [terrestrial atmosphere](../../../../../../../terrestrial-atmosphere.md). An outward-cooling interval, such as part of the [troposphere](../../../../../../../troposphere.md) or [mesosphere](../../../../../../../mesosphere.md), requires $\alpha>0$. An upward-warming interval, such as part of the [stratosphere](../../../../../../../stratosphere.md) heated by ultraviolet absorption or the [thermosphere](../../../../../../../thermosphere.md) heated by high-energy radiation, requires $\alpha<0$.

The local [atmospheric lapse rate](../../../../../../../lapse-rate-atmosphere.md) follows from the [ideal gas](../../../../../../../ideal-gas.md) relation $P=\rho k_BT/(\mu m_H)$ and [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md):

$$
\Gamma\equiv-\frac{dT}{dz}
=\frac{\mu m_Hg}{k_B}\frac{2\sqrt{T-T_0}}{\alpha T}
=\frac{\mu m_Hg}{k_B}\nabla.
$$

For dry terrestrial air, approximately $\gamma=1.4$ and $\mu\simeq29$ give the dry-adiabatic lapse rate $g/c_p\simeq9.8\,\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$. The familiar mean tropospheric value near $6.5\,\mathrm{K\,km^{-1}}$ is less steep; a local fit must satisfy $\nabla\lesssim2/7$ to be dry-convectively stable. Moist [convection](../../../../../../../convection.md) needs the moist parcel thermodynamics instead, and the [terrestrial atmosphere](../../../../../../../terrestrial-atmosphere.md) is not uniformly dry or chemically homogeneous at all heights.

The squared-[logarithm](../../../../../../../logarithm.md) shape cannot reproduce an exactly constant nonzero lapse rate over an arbitrary thick region, all the alternating atmospheric layers, or a finite exactly isothermal region. It is a local parametrization with a fixed sign of the [temperature gradient](../../../../../../../temperature-gradient.md); it has no terrestrial universality.

$$
\boxed{\alpha>0\text{ can fit an outward-cooling interval};\quad
\alpha<0\text{ can fit an upward-warming interval}.}
$$
