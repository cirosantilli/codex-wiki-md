<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $P_0>0$, real $\alpha$, and the real branch $T\ge T_0$, with physical [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md) $T>0$. In [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md) the [pressure](../../../../../../../pressure.md) decreases with height $z$:

$$
\frac{dP}{dz}=-\rho g<0.
$$

Thus an [atmospheric thermal inversion](../../../../../../../inversion-meteorology.md) means $dT/dz>0$, equivalently $dT/dP<0$. For $\alpha\ne0$, the [logarithm](../../../../../../../logarithm.md) of the given relation yields

$$
T(P)=T_0+\frac{\log^2(P/P_0)}{\alpha^2},\qquad
\frac{\log(P/P_0)}{\alpha}=\sqrt{T-T_0}\ge0.
$$

The sign restriction must be retained after squaring. In particular, the inverted branch has $0<P\le P_0$, whereas the non-inverted branch has $P\ge P_0$. By [differentiation](../../../../../../../differentiation.md), on the interior of either branch,

$$
\frac{dT}{d\log P}=\frac{2\log(P/P_0)}{\alpha^2}
=\frac{2\sqrt{T-T_0}}{\alpha},\qquad
\frac{dT}{dz}=-\frac{2\rho g\sqrt{T-T_0}}{\alpha P}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\alpha<0:\ \text{thermal inversion};\qquad
\alpha>0:\ \text{temperature decreases outward}.}
$$

At $T=T_0$, the inverse [temperature gradient](../../../../../../../temperature-gradient.md) vanishes at the branch endpoint, rather than producing a finite isothermal interval. If $\alpha=0$, the relation becomes $P=P_0$ independently of $T$; it is not a nontrivial vertical [atmospheric pressure-temperature profile](../../../../../../../atmospheric-pressure-temperature-profile.md) under [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md). These distinctions prevent the two sides of the squared parabola from being interpreted as one permissible atmosphere.
