<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Convection](../../../../../../../convection.md) mixes material vertically where the [Schwarzschild criterion](../../../../../../../schwarzschild-criterion.md) or [Ledoux criterion](../../../../../../../ledoux-criterion.md) allows it. Its chemical effect is controlled by competition between transport and reaction, rather than by a direct alteration of the equilibrium constant. Parametrize vertical transport by a [vertical eddy diffusion coefficient](../../../../../../../vertical-eddy-diffusion-coefficient.md) $K_{zz}$; the [eddy mixing time](../../../../../../../eddy-mixing-time.md) over a scale height is

$$
\tau_{\rm mix}\sim\frac{H^2}{K_{zz}}.
$$

Let $\tau_{\rm chem}$ be the [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md) of the relevant interconversion. If reactions are faster, the gas follows local [thermochemical equilibrium](../../../../../../../thermochemical-equilibrium.md). If transport becomes faster, composition can be carried upward faster than it re-equilibrates. A [chemical quench level](../../../../../../../chemical-quench-level.md) is approximately where the two times match:

$$
\boxed{\tau_{\rm mix}\sim\tau_{\rm chem}\text{ at quenching};\qquad
\tau_{\rm mix}\ll\tau_{\rm chem}\text{ favours transported abundances}.}
$$

For example, hot deep gas may contain abundant [carbon monoxide](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide.md). Strong mixing can maintain it in cooler layers where equilibrium would favour [methane](../../../../../../../methane.md); this is [carbon monoxide–methane quenching](../../../../../../../carbon-monoxide-methane-quenching.md). Similar reasoning applies to [nitrogen–ammonia quenching](../../../../../../../nitrogen-ammonia-quenching.md). The cooler nightside commonly has slower reaction rates and can preserve such quenched composition more readily, whereas sufficiently rapid dayside chemistry can restore local equilibrium. Neither statement is universal without the actual reaction network and [temperature](../../../../../../../temperature.md).

The observable atmosphere of a strongly irradiated [hot Jupiter](../../../../../../../hot-jupiter.md) may be radiative and stable even if the deeper interior is convective. Deep [convection](../../../../../../../convection.md) influences the photosphere only if some mixing crosses the intervening radiative region, for example through overshoot or other dynamical transport. Moreover, transport from day to night is horizontal circulation, not vertical [convection](../../../../../../../convection.md); its relevant competition is [atmospheric advection time](../../../../../../../atmospheric-advection-time.md) versus [chemical relaxation time](../../../../../../../chemical-relaxation-time.md), giving [horizontal chemical quenching](../../../../../../../horizontal-chemical-quenching.md). **Convection can affect either hemisphere indirectly through connected vertical transport; strong deep convection alone does not guarantee a quenched observable composition.**
