<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On the [unit circle](../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md), $z^{-1}=\overline z$, so

$$
p(z)=2+z+\overline z=(1+z)(1+\overline z)=|1+z|^2.
$$

Thus the [trigonometric polynomial](../../../../../../trigonometric-polynomial.md) is nonnegative, with a zero at $z=-1$, and

$$
\boxed{q(z)=1+z,\qquad p(z)=|q(z)|^2\quad(|z|=1)}.
$$

Equivalently, writing $z=e^{i\theta}$ gives $p(z)=2+2\cos\theta=4\cos^2(\theta/2)$. The factor has [polynomial degree](../../../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) one as required. Multiplication of $q$ by any constant of modulus one leaves the factorization unchanged; uniqueness of $q$ is not claimed.

This is the simplest instance of the [Fejér–Riesz theorem](../../../../../../fejer-riesz-theorem.md), where nonnegativity of a [trigonometric polynomial](../../../../../../trigonometric-polynomial.md) on the [unit circle](../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) admits a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) modulus-square factorization.
