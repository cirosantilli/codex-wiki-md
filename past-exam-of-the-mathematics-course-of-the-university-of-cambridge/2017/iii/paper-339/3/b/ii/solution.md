<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $z\ne0$, the [coefficient](../../../../../../../coefficient.md) symmetry yields

$$
z^{2d}\overline{P(1/\overline z)}
=z^{2d}\overline{(1/\overline z)^dp(1/\overline z)}
=z^dp(z)=P(z).
$$

Thus $P$ equals its reversed-conjugate [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). If $\zeta\ne0$ is a [root of a polynomial](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), with $P(\zeta)=0$, this identity gives $P(1/\overline\zeta)=0$, proving

$$
\boxed{\zeta\text{ a root}\ \Longrightarrow\ 1/\overline\zeta\text{ a root}}.
$$

The [reciprocal-conjugate root pairing](../../../../../../../reciprocal-conjugate-root-pairing.md) also preserves the [multiplicity of a root](../../../../../../../multiplicity-of-a-root.md): reversal and [complex conjugation](../../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) take each factor associated with $\zeta$ to the corresponding factor associated with $1/\overline\zeta$, with the same exponent.

Since $P(0)=p_{-d}\ne0$, no zero [root of a polynomial](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) occurs, so the reciprocal operation is always defined on the [roots of a polynomial](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md). Roots off the [unit circle](../../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) are paired on opposite sides of it; [roots of a polynomial](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) on the [unit circle](../../../../../../../complex-unit-circle.md) are fixed by this operation. Pairing alone does not force even multiplicity at those fixed [roots of a polynomial](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md); that extra fact uses nonnegativity.
