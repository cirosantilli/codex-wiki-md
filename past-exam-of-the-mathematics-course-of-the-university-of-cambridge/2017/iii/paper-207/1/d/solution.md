A [network meta-analysis](../../../../../../network-meta-analysis.md) can offer three distinct benefits, assuming a connected treatment network and credible [transitivity in network meta-analysis](../../../../../../transitivity-in-network-meta-analysis.md).

- **Comparisons without a direct trial.** A path through common comparators permits an [indirect treatment comparison](../../../../../../indirect-treatment-comparison.md) of treatments that have never been randomized against one another.
- **Greater precision.** Combining consistent direct and indirect evidence can reduce the [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) of a treatment-effect estimate and make more complete use of the trials than separate pairwise [meta-analyses](../../../../../../meta-analysis.md).
- **Coherent decisions across many treatments.** A joint [statistical model](../../../../../../statistical-model-split.md) estimates all relative effects on one scale, allowing treatment rankings and their uncertainty to inform a choice among several options rather than presenting disconnected pairs.

These are potential benefits, not guarantees: heterogeneous [effect modifiers](../../../../../../effect-modifier.md), inconsistent evidence, disconnected networks or imprecise rankings can defeat them.
