<h1 id="1/2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

In the flattened coordinates, $\partial_x=\partial_{\tilde x}$ and $\partial_y=2\tilde y\partial_{\tilde x}+\partial_{\tilde y}$. The transformed [formal adjoint](../../../../../../../formal-adjoint.md) consequently has coefficients obtained from finitely many [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) coefficient pullbacks and [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) factors. They have uniform analyticity bounds on a fixed small ball. By the preceding non-characteristic argument, its coefficient of $\partial_{\tilde x}^m$ has modulus bounded away from zero there, uniformly for small $\varepsilon$. Division by it preserves uniform [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) bounds.

The transformed forcing $\tilde g_\varepsilon$ has analogous bounds, with constants that may depend on $g$. Apply the [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) in normal form with zero normal data through order $m-1$ at $\tilde x=0$. The supplied [majorant series](../../../../../../../majorant-series.md) radius result yields a common solution radius $r_g>0$ for all sufficiently small parameters. Tangential differentiation of zero normal data shows that the entire [boundary jet](../../../../../../../boundary-jet.md) through order $m-1$ is zero; changing coordinates preserves this vanishing.

For $(x,y)$ in the closed cap, $|\tilde x|\le\varepsilon$ and $|\tilde y|\le\sqrt\varepsilon$. Choose $\varepsilon$ so small that $\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon}<r_g$, with a strict margin. The whole closed cap then lies in the common [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) solution neighborhood. Pulling the solution back proves

$$
\boxed{P^*v=g\text{ throughout }\omega_\varepsilon,\quad j^{m-1}v|_{\tilde\Sigma_\varepsilon}=0.}
$$

In fact $v$ is [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) on a neighborhood of its closure, which is useful for the boundary integration below.
