<h1 id="1/2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The order-$m$ [principal symbol](../../../../../../../principal-symbol-of-a-partial-differential-equation.md) of the [formal adjoint](../../../../../../../formal-adjoint.md) is $p_m^*=(-1)^mp_m$. Derivatives falling on coefficients contribute only lower-order terms. Thus the original plane remains a [non-characteristic hypersurface](../../../../../../../non-characteristic-hypersurface.md).

For the curved face use the defining function $\rho_\varepsilon=x+y^2-\varepsilon$, whose conormal is $(1,2y)$. On the face with $x>0$, $|y|<\sqrt\varepsilon$ and $0<x\le\varepsilon$. Therefore $(x,y,1,2y)$ tends uniformly to $(0,0,1,0)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Since $p_m^*(0,0;1,0)\ne0$, continuity gives

$$
\boxed{|p_m^*(x,y;1,2y)|\ge c>0\quad\text{on every sufficiently small curved face}.}
$$

The same argument supplies a common neighborhood where the transformed coefficient of the highest [normal derivative](../../../../../../../normal-derivative.md) is nonzero, permitting the [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) normal form used below.
