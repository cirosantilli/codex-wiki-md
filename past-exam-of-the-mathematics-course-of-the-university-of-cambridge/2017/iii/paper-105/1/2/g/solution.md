<h1 id="1/2/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Fix the coefficient normal form and a positive analyticity exponent $\zeta_0$ once and for all. If $g$ is a [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), its pullback $\tilde g_\varepsilon=g(\tilde x-\tilde y^2+\varepsilon,\tilde y)$ is again a [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), with degree bounded independently of $\varepsilon$. On a fixed closed coordinate ball, its finitely many nonzero [derivatives](../../../../../../../derivative.md) are uniformly bounded as the parameter ranges over a fixed compact small interval. Hence there is a finite $A_g\ge1$ such that

$$
\|\partial^\beta(\tilde g_\varepsilon/A_g)\|_\infty\le\beta!\zeta_0^{|\beta|}\quad\text{for every }\beta\text{ and every small }\varepsilon.
$$

All higher [derivatives](../../../../../../../derivative.md) vanish, so arbitrarily high [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) degree causes no failure of this bound; it only changes $A_g$.

The normalized forcing belongs to one common majorant class. The [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) therefore gives a radius $r_*>0$ independent of $g$. The problem is linear with zero data, so multiplying the normalized solution by $A_g$ solves the original forcing without changing its radius. Choose one $\varepsilon$ with the cap inside this radius. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{one }\varepsilon>0\text{ works for every polynomial }g.}
$$

This is the [uniform Cauchy radius for polynomial forcing](../../../../../../../uniform-cauchy-radius-for-polynomial-forcing.md). It asserts a common domain, not a uniform solution-amplitude bound over all [polynomials](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md).
