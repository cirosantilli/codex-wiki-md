<h1 id="1/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Repeated [integration by parts](../../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) against a compactly supported [test function](../../../../../../../test-function.md) gives the bilinear [formal adjoint](../../../../../../../formal-adjoint.md)

$$
\boxed{P^*v=\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial^\alpha(a_\alpha v).}
$$

There is no boundary remainder because $v$ has [compact support](../../../../../../../compact-support.md). The product rule makes this an order-at-most-$m$ linear differential operator with [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) coefficients. The displayed integral pairing has no complex conjugation, so this is the formal transpose; for a sesquilinear pairing the coefficients would be conjugated.

For uniqueness, suppose $Q$ is the difference of two candidates. Then $\int uQv=0$ for every smooth compactly supported $u$ and $v$, so $Qv=0$ for every such $v$. At an interior point, a [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) times a cutoff can prescribe every [derivative](../../../../../../../derivative.md) of $v$ through order $m$ independently. Hence each coefficient of $Q$ is zero. This proves uniqueness as a differential operator, not only uniqueness of its integrated action.
