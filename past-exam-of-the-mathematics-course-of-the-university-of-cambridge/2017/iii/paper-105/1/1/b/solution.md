↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the [wave equation](../../../../../../../wave-equation-split.md), the [principal symbol](../../../../../../../principal-symbol-of-a-partial-differential-equation.md) is $-\tau^2+|\xi|^2$, so its value on the conormal $dt$ is $-1$. Consequently the [Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem](../../../../../../../cauchy-kovalevskaya-theorem.md) applies to [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) displacement and velocity data on this [non-characteristic hypersurface](../../../../../../../non-characteristic-hypersurface.md):

$$
\boxed{\text{the theorem applies to the wave equation at }t=0.}
$$

In addition, this [Cauchy problem for a partial differential equation](../../../../../../../cauchy-problem.md) has [Hadamard well-posedness](../../../../../../../well-posed-problem.md) for nonanalytic data in the usual energy spaces. For example, data in $H^1(\mathbb R^n)\times L^2(\mathbb R^n)$ give a unique solution in $C_tH^1\cap C_t^1L^2$ on bounded time intervals, with continuous dependence; conservation of $\int(|u_t|^2+|\nabla u|^2)$ is its basic estimate. Thus [real analytic](../../../../../../../real-analytic-function.md) existence and energy-space stability are different assertions, and in this example both are available.
