<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Work on a [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space) with the [usual conditions for a filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#usual-conditions-for-a-filtration). A continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md) belongs to the class of [L2-bounded continuous martingales](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale) when

$$
\boxed{\sup_{t\geq0}\mathbb E|M_t|^2<\infty.}
$$

This means a uniform bound over the entire time interval, not merely being [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) at each individual time. The [L2 martingale convergence theorem](../../../martingale.md#l2-martingale-convergence-theorem) gives a terminal variable $M_\infty$ and [almost sure convergence](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) and convergence in the [Lebesgue space](../../../measure-theory.md#lp-space) $L^2$; moreover $M_t=\mathbb E[M_\infty\mid\mathcal F_t]$ by [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation).

The [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra) $\mathcal P$ on $\Omega\times[0,\infty)$ is the smallest [sigma-algebra](../../../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) making every left-continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) measurable. Equivalently, it is generated by

$$
A\times\{0\},\quad A\in\mathcal F_0,
\qquad
A\times(s,t],\quad 0\leq s<t,\ A\in\mathcal F_s,
$$

where the sample coordinate comes first. A [previsible process](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) is precisely a process measurable for this [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra). Values at time zero matter for its definition, although they make no contribution to integration against a continuous [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) starting at zero.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write $N_2=\|M_\infty\|_2$ and $N_*=\|\sup_{t\geq0}|M_t|\|_2$. The [L2 martingale convergence theorem](../../../martingale.md#l2-martingale-convergence-theorem) ensures the existence of $M_\infty$ and yields $|M_\infty|\leq\sup_t|M_t|$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Hence $N_2\leq N_*$.

For a finite horizon $T$, the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality) for the [submartingale](../../../martingale.md#submartingale) $|M|$ gives

$$
\mathbb E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|M_t|^2\leq4\mathbb E|M_T|^2.
$$

The inequality does not require $M_0=0$. Send $T$ to infinity: the left side increases to $N_*^2$ by the [monotone convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem), and the right side tends to $4N_2^2$ by convergence in the [Lebesgue space](../../../measure-theory.md#lp-space) $L^2$. Thus the [equivalent terminal and maximal norms for L2-bounded continuous martingales](../../../martingale.md#equivalent-terminal-and-maximal-norms-for-l2-bounded-continuous-martingales) satisfy

$$
\boxed{\|M_\infty\|_2\leq\left\|\sup_{t\geq0}|M_t|\right\|_2\leq2\|M_\infty\|_2.}
$$

Both are genuine [norms](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) on [L2-bounded continuous martingales](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale) modulo [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes): a zero terminal norm gives $M_t=\mathbb E[M_\infty\mid\mathcal F_t]=0$, first at rational times and then at all times by continuity. This proves that the two [norms](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) are [equivalent norms](../../../functional-analysis.md#equivalent-norms).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

A [simple predictable process](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) has the form $H_s=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}h_j\mathbf1_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}(s)$, where $0=t_0<\cdots<t_m<\infty$ are deterministic and $h_j$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_{t_j}$-measurable. A possible $\mathcal F_0$-measurable value at zero is irrelevant to this [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral). Define

$$
\boxed{(H\mathbin\cdot M)_t=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}h_j\bigl(M_{t\wedge t_{j+1}}-M_{t\wedge t_j}\bigr).}
$$

Each coefficient is known before the corresponding increment. The [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) of each subsequent increment is zero, so the result is a continuous [martingale](../../../martingale.md) starting at zero. It is an [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale) because it is a finite sum of bounded coefficients times stopped increments of an [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale). This definition is independent of the chosen subdivision: splitting an interval simply splits its increment into a telescoping sum. General admissible [predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) are then integrated by completion using the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry); unbounded step coefficients are allowed when the weighted $L^2$ condition in the [quadratic-variation measure](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation-measure) in the next part holds.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

Fix a [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale) $M$. Its [quadratic-variation measure](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation-measure) on the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra) is

$$
\nu_M(A)=\mathbb E\int_0^\infty\mathbf1_A(\omega,s)\,d[M]_s.
$$

It is finite, since $\nu_M(\Omega\times[0,\infty))=\mathbb E[M]_\infty=\mathbb E M_\infty^2-\mathbb E M_0^2$. The source [Hilbert space](../../../hilbert-space.md) is $L^2(M)=L^2(\Omega\times[0,\infty),\mathcal P,\nu_M)$: [predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) with finite $\mathbb E\int H^2d[M]$, identified when equal $\nu_M$-almost everywhere. Its [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) is $\|H\|_{L^2(M)}^2=\mathbb E\int H^2d[M]$.

The target $\mathcal M_0^2$ consists of [L2-bounded continuous martingales](../../../martingale.md#l2-bounded-continuous-martingale) starting at zero, identified up to [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes), with [norm](../../../functional-analysis.md#norm) $\|K\|_{\mathcal M_0^2}=\|K_\infty\|_2$. It is a [Hilbert space](../../../hilbert-space.md): terminal values belong to the closed [linear subspace](../../../vector-space.md#vector-subspace) of $L^2(\mathcal F_\infty)$ whose [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) given $\mathcal F_0$ is zero and whose associated [martingales](../../../martingale.md) have continuous versions. Closure of that continuous-version subspace follows from the [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality) and an almost surely uniformly convergent subsequence.

The [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) says that integration extends uniquely from [simple predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) to a linear [isometry](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#isometry) $L^2(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal M_0^2$, with

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E|(H\mathbin\cdot M)_\infty|^2=\mathbb E\int_0^\infty H_s^2\,d[M]_s.}
$$

For every finite $t$, the same identity holds with the integral restricted to $[0,t]$ and the left side $\mathbb E|(H\mathbin\cdot M)_t|^2$. No claim of surjectivity onto all of $\mathcal M_0^2$ is needed: that would require an additional [Martingale representation theorem](../../../brownian-motion.md#martingale-representation-theorem).

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

Put $S=H\mathbin\cdot M$ and $A_t=\int_0^tH_s^2d[M]_s$. The candidate $A$ is continuous, adapted, nondecreasing, starts at zero and has integrable terminal value. We prove $S^2-A$ is a [martingale](../../../martingale.md), rather than assuming the desired [quadratic variation of a stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation-of-a-stochastic-integral).

The assumed boundedness of $M$ also makes $M^2-[M]$ a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) on every finite horizon. Indeed, apply its local [martingale](../../../martingale.md) identity at a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence); bounded stopped squares and the [Fatou lemma](../../../measure-theory.md#fatou-s-lemma) show that $[M]_t$ is integrable. The stopped expressions are then dominated by $\sup_s|M_s|^2+[M]_t$, so the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) gives the unrestricted conditional identity on that horizon.

First let $H$ be a bounded [simple predictable process](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process). On each interval with coefficient $h$, an increment of $S$ is $h(M_t-M_a)$. The defining [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) identity for $M$, together with the [martingale](../../../martingale.md) property of $M$, makes

$$
(M_t-M_a)^2-([M]_t-[M]_a)
$$

a [martingale](../../../martingale.md) for $t\geq a$. Expanding $S_t^2-S_a^2$ gives a term $2S_ah(M_t-M_a)$ and the square term above; their [conditional expectations](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) yield the desired identity on that interval. Integrability follows because the variables are [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function); concatenating the finitely many intervals gives $S^2-A$ a [martingale](../../../martingale.md).

For general bounded [predictable](../../../martingale.md#predictable-process) $H$, choose bounded [simple predictable processes](../../../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) $H^n\to H$ in $L^2(M)$, which is possible since they generate the [predictable sigma-algebra](../../../martingale.md#predictable-sigma-algebra). Set $S^n=H^n\mathbin\cdot M$ and $A_t^n=\int_0^t(H_s^n)^2d[M]_s$. The [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) and [Doob L2 maximal inequality](../../../martingale.md#doob-l2-maximal-inequality) imply $S_t^n\to S_t$ in $L^2$; the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives $(S_t^n)^2\to S_t^2$ in $L^1$ and

$$
\mathbb E\sup_t|A_t^n-A_t|
\leq\|H^n-H\|_{L^2(M)}\bigl(\|H^n\|_{L^2(M)}+\|H\|_{L^2(M)}\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
$$

Pass the [conditional expectation](../../../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) identity for $(S^n)^2-A^n$ to the $L^1$ limit. Thus $S^2-A$ is a [martingale](../../../martingale.md). Uniqueness in the defining [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) property now gives

$$
\boxed{[H\mathbin\cdot M]_t=\int_0^tH_s^2\,d[M]_s.}
$$

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Use [polarization identity](../../../linear-algebra.md#polarization-identity) of the [quadratic variations](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation):

$$
\boxed{[M,N]=\frac14\bigl([M+N]-[M-N]\bigr).}
$$

This is continuous and adapted, starts at zero, and has [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function) on every compact interval because it is a difference of two nondecreasing processes. The defining [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) property gives that $(M+N)^2-[M+N]$ and $(M-N)^2-[M-N]$ are [continuous local martingales](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale); their difference divided by four is $MN-[M,N]$. This proves existence without assuming a product formula involving an as yet undefined [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation).

If $C$ and $D$ both satisfy the requirements, $C-D$ is a continuous [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) and a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). By [continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant](../../../martingale.md#continuous-finite-variation-local-martingale-is-constant), it is constant; its initial value is zero, so $C=D$ up to [indistinguishability of stochastic processes](../../../stochastic-process.md#indistinguishability-of-stochastic-processes).

There is a minor initial-value convention in this characterization. The displayed uncentred [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) identity is directly valid when $M_0=N_0=0$, or when the initial terms have the requisite integrability. With arbitrary finite initial values, centre the [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) construction and use $MN-M_0N_0-[M,N]$, which starts at zero. Adding back $M_0N_0$ requires it to be integrable under the usual definition of a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale); the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) itself is unaffected by centring.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For [probability measures](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) on $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$, [absolute continuity of measures](../../../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures) means preservation of null sets in one direction:

$$
\boxed{\widetilde{\mathbb P}\ll\mathbb P\iff\bigl(\mathbb P(A)=0\Longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbb P}(A)=0\bigr)\quad\text{for every }A\in\mathcal F.}
$$

Equivalently, by the [Radon-Nikodym theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-theorem), there is an $\mathcal F$-measurable [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-derivative) $Z\geq0$ with $\mathbb E_{\mathbb P}Z=1$ and $\widetilde{\mathbb P}(A)=\mathbb E_{\mathbb P}[Z\mathbf1_A]$. The derivative may vanish, so [absolute continuity of measures](../../../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures) does not require equivalence of the two [probability measures](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure). In particular, the reverse null-set implication is not part of the definition.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The relevant fact is [uniform convergence on compacts in probability under an absolutely continuous measure change](../../../stochastic-process.md#uniform-convergence-on-compacts-in-probability-under-an-absolutely-continuous-measure-change). Write $Z=d\widetilde{\mathbb P}/d\mathbb P$. For any event $E$ and $K>0$,

$$
\widetilde{\mathbb P}(E)\leq K\mathbb P(E)+\mathbb E_{\mathbb P}[Z\mathbf1_{\{Z>K\}}].
$$

The last term tends to zero as $K\to\infty$ because $Z$ is integrable. Consequently, $\mathbb P(E_n)\to0$ implies $\widetilde{\mathbb P}(E_n)\to0$. Apply this to $E_n=\{\sup_{t\leq T}|C_t^n-[M,N]_t|>\varepsilon\}$ for each finite $T$ and $\varepsilon>0$. The given dyadic sums therefore have the same [uniform convergence on compacts in probability](../../../stochastic-process.md#uniform-convergence-on-compacts-in-probability) limit under the new [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure).

This proves [quadratic covariation under an absolutely continuous measure change](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation-under-an-absolutely-continuous-measure-change):

$$
\boxed{[M,N]^{\widetilde{\mathbb P}}=[M,N]^{\mathbb P}\quad\text{up to }\widetilde{\mathbb P}\text{-indistinguishability}.}
$$

Taking $N=M$ proves the corresponding [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) assertion directly; it has not been assumed. The ceiling in the printed sum includes one grid interval extending beyond $t$. This does not affect the limit: on $[0,T]$ the resulting extra product is bounded by the product of the two path oscillations on mesh $2^{-n}$, which tends to zero by [uniform continuity](../../../topological-analysis.md#uniform-continuity) on $[0,T+1]$.

The bracket under the new [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) must be understood as the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) of continuous [semimartingales](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale). [Semimartingale stability under an absolutely continuous measure change](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale-stability-under-an-absolutely-continuous-measure-change) ensures this class is preserved; the [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) property need not be. For example, on a finite horizon, weighting [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) by $\exp(\mu B_T-\mu^2T/2)$ makes $B_t-\mu t$ a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) by the [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem), so $B$ has nonzero drift under the new measure even though its [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) is still $t$. The PDF additionally assumes that $M,N$ remain [local martingales](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) under the new [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure), so its bracket is also defined directly by the [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) characterization.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

For continuous [semimartingales](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale), the [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) adds half the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) to the [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral):

$$
\boxed{S_t=I_t+\frac12[X,Y]_t.}
$$

One can see the factor directly from symmetric endpoint sums. On a [partition of an interval](../../../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval), replacing $X$ at the left endpoint by the average of its two endpoint values adds $\frac12\sum\Delta X\Delta Y$; its limit is $\frac12[X,Y]$. Thus the correction depends only on the continuous [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) parts of the [semimartingales](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#semimartingale). If either integrator or integrand has [finite variation](../../../real-analysis.md#total-variation-of-a-function), the [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) vanishes and these two integrals coincide. Here the printed notation $\partial Y$ denotes [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) integration, equivalently $\circ dY$.

<h3 id="2/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#2/e)

By the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma),

$$
f(X_t)-f(X_0)=\int_0^tf'(X_s)\,dX_s+\frac12\int_0^tf''(X_s)\,d[X]_s.
$$

To compute the [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) correction, apply the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) also to $f'$, which is twice continuously differentiable because $f$ is $C^3$. The continuous [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) part of $f'(X)$ is $\int f''(X_s)dL_s$, where $L$ is the continuous [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) part of $X$. A [finite-variation process](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) has zero [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) with $X$, and the [quadratic variation of a stochastic integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation-of-a-stochastic-integral) together with [polarization identity](../../../linear-algebra.md#polarization-identity) gives

$$
[f'(X),X]_t=\int_0^tf''(X_s)\,d[X]_s.
$$

All these statements can be localized to compact ranges of $X$, so unbounded derivatives create no global integrability requirement. Substitute this identity into the [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) to obtain the [Stratonovich chain rule](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-chain-rule):

$$
\boxed{f(X_t)-f(X_0)=\int_0^tf'(X_s)\,\partial X_s.}
$$

This is the integrated meaning of the requested differential identity; there is no extra second-order term after the [Stratonovich integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stratonovich-integral) correction has been included.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 202](paper-202.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Specify an initial value or initial [probability distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-distribution). A [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) uses a prescribed [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) on a prescribed [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space). It is continuous, adapted to the completed [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) generated by that [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and the initial value, and satisfies

$$
X_t=X_0+\int_0^tb(X_s)\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma(X_s)\,dB_s
$$

[almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) for every $t$, with $\int_0^t(|b(X_s)|+\sigma(X_s)^2)ds<\infty$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The given initial variable is independent of future [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) increments. Some courses allow a larger prescribed [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) in their definition of a [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation); the Brownian-generated convention states explicitly what absence of extra randomness means.

A [weak solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#weak-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) consists of a [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space), a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) relative to its [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory), and a continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) $X$ satisfying the same integrability and integral equation. Here the space and noise are part of the unknown; the [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) may contain randomness beyond the initial variable and the driving path.

[Pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness) means that on any common [filtered probability space](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space), any two solutions with the same driving [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and the same initial value are indistinguishable. [Uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law) means that all [weak solutions](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) with the same initial [probability distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-distribution) have the same law as path-valued [random variables](../../../random-variable.md), even on different spaces. **Pathwise uniqueness compares shared-noise paths; uniqueness in law compares path distributions.** Neither uniqueness notion by itself asserts existence.

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

The [Tanaka equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#tanaka-equation) is an example:

$$
\boxed{dX_t=s(X_t)\,dB_t,\quad X_0=0,\qquad s(x)=\begin{cases}1&x\geq0,\\-1&x<0.\end{cases}}
$$

It has [weak solutions](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) and [uniqueness in law](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#uniqueness-in-law), but lacks [pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness). The value $s(0)=1$ is deliberate: using the [sign function](../../../foundations-of-mathematics.md#sign-function) with value zero at zero would admit the identically zero solution and would change the example.

For a quick verification, take a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) $W$ and put $B=\int s(W)dW$. Its [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) is $t$, so it is a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) by the [Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion), and [associativity of stochastic integration](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#associativity-of-stochastic-integration) gives $W=\int s(W)dB$. Every [weak solution](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#weak-solution) likewise has [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) $t$ and therefore the law of [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md). On this same enlarged [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory), both $W$ and $-W$ solve the equation driven by $B$: $s(-W)=-s(W)$ away from zero, and the [Brownian zero set](../../../brownian-motion.md#brownian-zero-set) has zero time measure, so the discrepancy at zero contributes nothing to the [Itô integral](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral). These solutions differ with positive probability at any positive time. This proves the claimed distinction without relying on a choice of zero convention left unstated.

The [Exponential martingale for Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#exponential-martingale-for-brownian-motion) is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md): independent increments and the [normal distribution](../../../probability-theory.md#normal-distribution) identity $\mathbb E e^{aN}=e^{a^2v/2}$ for $N\sim N(0,v)$ give $\mathbb E[Z_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=Z_s$. Its second moment is

$$
\mathbb E Z_t^2=e^{\mu^2t},\qquad
\boxed{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb E Z_t^2=e^{\mu^2T}<\infty.}
$$

The [uniform integrability from bounded second moments](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability-from-bounded-second-moments) criterion now applies: for $K>0$,

$$
\sup_{t\leq T}\mathbb E[Z_t\mathbf1_{\{Z_t>K\}}]\leq\frac{e^{\mu^2T}}K\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus $\{Z_t:0\leq t\leq T\}$ has [uniform integrability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability). In fact, all values stopped at [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) bounded by $T$ have [uniform integrability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability), since $Z_\tau=\mathbb E[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_\tau]$ and [uniform integrability of conditional expectations](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability-of-conditional-expectations) applies. This is a finite-horizon assertion, not an assertion of [uniform integrability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) over all $t\geq0$.

Define a [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) on the same full measurable space by

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\mathbb P^T}{d\mathbb P}=Z_T=\exp(\mu B_T-\mu^2T/2).}

The [Radon-Nikodym derivative](../../../measure-theory.md#radon-nikodym-derivative) is positive and has [expectation](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value) one, so this is a [probability measure](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-measure) and even equivalent to $\mathbb P$, in particular $\mathbb P^T\ll\mathbb P$. For $t\leq T$ its density on $\mathcal F_t$ is $\mathbb E[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_t]=Z_t$.

The finite-horizon [Girsanov theorem](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#girsanov-theorem) says that if $W$ is a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) and $\mathcal E(\int h\,dW)$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) through $T$ defining the density, then $W_t-\int_0^th_sds$ is a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) under that density for $t\leq T$. Here $h_s=\mu$, and the [stochastic exponential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#doleans-dade-exponential) is exactly $Z$. The previous part verifies the necessary [martingale](../../../martingale.md) property; alternatively the [Novikov condition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#novikov-s-condition) is $e^{\mu^2T/2}<\infty$. Hence $B_t-\mu t$ is a standard [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) on $[0,T]$ relative to the same [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) under $\mathbb P^T$.

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

**No absolutely continuous probability measure can remove this nonzero drift on the whole half-line.** By the [strong law for Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#strong-law-for-brownian-motion), the event

$$
A=\left\{\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{B_t}{t}=0\right\}
$$

has $\mathbb P$-probability one. If $\mathbb P^\infty\ll\mathbb P$, [absolute continuity of measures](../../../measure-theory.md#absolute-continuity-of-measures) gives $\mathbb P^\infty(A)=1$. But if $W_t=B_t-\mu t$ is a [Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md) under $\mathbb P^\infty$, the same [strong law for Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#strong-law-for-brownian-motion) gives $B_t/t=W_t/t+\mu\to\mu$ with $\mathbb P^\infty$-probability one. Since $\mu>0$, these two limiting events are disjoint, a contradiction.

This is [infinite-horizon singularity of Brownian motion with constant drift](../../../brownian-motion.md#infinite-horizon-singularity-of-brownian-motion-with-constant-drift). The finite-horizon densities are consistent on $\mathcal F_t$ but cannot define a globally absolutely continuous measure with the requested property. Indeed,

$$
\boxed{Z_t\longrightarrow0\quad\mathbb P\text{-almost surely},\qquad \mathbb E Z_t=1,}
$$

because $t^{-1}\log Z_t\to-\mu^2/2$. Hence $Z$ lacks [uniform integrability](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) on $[0,\infty)$. A drifted law does exist on canonical path space, but it is mutually singular with [Wiener measure](../../../brownian-motion.md#wiener-measure) on the infinite-horizon path [sigma-algebra](../../../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra).

**The printed assumptions omit the orthogonality needed for the claimed local martingales.** Merely placing two [Brownian motions](../../../brownian-motion.md) on the same [probability space](../../../probability-theory.md#probability-space) does not make them independent. Interpret both as [Brownian motions](../../../brownian-motion.md) relative to a common [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory), and put $C_t=[B,\vartheta]_t$. For $X=e^B\cos\vartheta$ and $Y=e^B\sin\vartheta$, the two-variable [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives

$$
\boxed{dX=X\,dB-Y\,d\vartheta-Y\,dC,\qquad dY=Y\,dB+X\,d\vartheta+X\,dC.}
$$

The pure second derivatives cancel because $[B]_t=[\vartheta]_t=t$, while the mixed [partial derivatives](../../../calculus.md#partial-derivative) are $X_{b\vartheta}=-Y$ and $Y_{b\vartheta}=X$. Thus the mixed [quadratic covariation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) term cannot be omitted.

For a counterexample allowed by the PDF, take $\vartheta=B$. Then $C_t=t$ and $X$ has drift $-Y\,dt$, while $Y$ has drift $X\,dt$. In particular $X_0=1$ makes the latter drift nonzero near zero, so $Y$ is not a [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). Nor is $X$: $Y$ cannot be identically zero, so its accumulated continuous drift is not identically zero. Uniqueness of the [continuous semimartingale decomposition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#continuous-semimartingale-decomposition) excludes cancellation by another [local martingale](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale) term.

The corrected hypothesis is $[B,\vartheta]\equiv0$, for example independent [Brownian motions](../../../brownian-motion.md) in their joint [filtration](../../../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory). Conversely, if both were [local martingales](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale), uniqueness of the [continuous semimartingale decomposition](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#continuous-semimartingale-decomposition) would force $Y\,dC=X\,dC=0$; since $X^2+Y^2=e^{2B}>0$, this forces $dC=0$. Thus the correction is exactly the condition needed for both [local martingales](../../../martingale.md#local-martingale). Under this hypothesis the drift terms vanish and the intended [stochastic differential equations](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) are $dX=X\,dB-Y\,d\vartheta$ and $dY=Y\,dB+X\,d\vartheta$. The continuous integrands are locally bounded, so localization makes these [Itô integrals](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) [continuous local martingales](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale). Subsequent intended conclusions are proved under this explicitly stated correction.

Under the corrected orthogonality hypothesis, the preceding [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) calculation gives $[X]_t=A_t=\int_0^te^{2B_s}ds$. We justify the needed [infinite occupation time of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#infinite-occupation-time-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion), rather than inferring an infinite integral from recurrent visits alone.

Let $T_0=0$, and recursively let $S_n$ be the first exit of $B$ from $(-1,1)$ after $T_n$, and $T_{n+1}$ its first return to zero after $S_n$. [Recurrence of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#recurrence-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion) makes these [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) finite [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). The [Strong Markov property](../../../markov-process.md#strong-markov-property) shows that $D_n=S_n-T_n$ are [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables), each strictly positive. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ with $\mathbb P(D_n\geq\varepsilon)>0$. The [Borel-Cantelli lemma](../../../probability-theory.md#borel-cantelli-lemmas) gives infinitely many such durations and hence $\sum_nD_n=\infty$ [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). These intervals are disjoint, and $|B_s|\leq1$ on each, so

$$
\boxed{[X]_\infty=\int_0^\infty e^{2B_s}\,ds\geq e^{-2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty D_n=\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

This proves the intended conclusion. Without the orthogonality correction, the printed setup gives instead the more general [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) in the previous part; it does not justify identifying that quantity with $A$. The proof here is explicitly for the corrected setup.

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

A [locally defined stochastic process](../../../stochastic-process.md#locally-defined-stochastic-process) is a pair $(X,T)$, where $T$ is a lifetime [stopping time](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) and $X_t(\omega)$ is defined for $0\leq t<T(\omega)$, that is, on a [stochastic interval](../../../stochastic-process.md#stochastic-interval). For continuous local problems one uses [stopping times](../../../martingale.md#stopping-time) $T_n\uparrow T$ with $T_n<T$ on $\{T>0\}$, so the process stopped at each $T_n$ has an ordinary continuous [adapted process](../../../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) version. This is an [announcing sequence for a stopping time](../../../martingale.md#announcing-sequence-for-a-stopping-time); no value at $T$ or after $T$ is implicit in the pair.

A [local solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-process.md#local-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) on an open domain $U$ takes its values in $U$ before $T$ and, on every such stopped interval, satisfies

$$
X_{t\wedge T_n}=X_0+\int_0^{t\wedge T_n}b(X_s)\,ds+\int_0^{t\wedge T_n}\sigma(X_s)\,dB_s,
$$

with the local integrability needed for the ordinary and [Itô integrals](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral). A [maximal local solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#maximal-local-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation) has no extension to a strictly later lifetime that agrees with it before $T$. For locally [Lipschitz continuous](../../../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) coefficients on $U$, take a nested [compact exhaustion](../../../topology.md#compact-exhaustion) $K_n$ of $U$ and the successive exit times: their increasing limit is the maximal lifetime, and on $\{T<\infty\}$ the solution eventually leaves every compact subset of $U$. To obtain the announcing sequence with finite stops even when the path never exits a compact set, cap these exit times by $n$; the capped times still increase to the maximal lifetime. Thus **a finite maximal lifetime means exit from the domain or explosion; it need not mean divergence to infinity**. For $U=(0,\infty)$, reaching the boundary zero terminates the local solution even if an absorbing extension could be defined for a different domain.

Before the lifetime, $X>0$, so apply the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) to $g(x)=\sqrt{x}$ after stopping inside compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$. The derivatives are $g'(x)=1/(2\sqrt{x})$ and $g''(x)=-1/(4x^{3/2})$. Since the [quadratic variation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) of $X$ satisfies $d[X]_t=X_tdt$, we get

$$
\boxed{dY_t=\frac12\,dB_t-\frac1{8Y_t}\,dt,\qquad Y_0=\sqrt{x_0},\quad t<T.}

The negative drift is the second-order correction in the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma). This is a [Lamperti transform](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#lamperti-transform-diffusion) up to a constant scale: the [square root](../../../algebra.md#square-root) transformation makes the noise coefficient constant. Its drift is singular at zero, which is why this [stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) is stated only before the boundary lifetime.

For $0<\alpha<1$, use the power transformation $Y=X^{1-\alpha}$ on the positive [stochastic interval](../../../stochastic-process.md#stochastic-interval). The [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma), with $d[X]=X^{2\alpha}dt$, gives

$$
dY_t=(1-\alpha)\,dB_t-\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2Y_t}\,dt,\qquad Y_0=x_0^{1-\alpha}.
$$

Thus $Y_t\leq x_0^{1-\alpha}+(1-\alpha)B_t$ simultaneously for $t<T$. The right side reaches zero [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) by [recurrence of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#recurrence-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion), and the same positive-path contradiction proves $T$ cannot exceed that [Brownian first-passage time](../../../markov-process.md#brownian-first-passage-time). This establishes the [finite lifetime threshold for a power diffusion](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-lifetime-threshold-for-a-power-diffusion) throughout $0<\alpha<1$.

At $\alpha=1$, the solution is the [geometric Brownian motion](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#geometric-brownian-motion)

$$
X_t=x_0\exp(B_t-t/2).
$$

It is finite and positive at every finite time. On every compact time interval it has a positive minimum, so the hitting times of $1/n$ tend to infinity. Although the [strong law for Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#strong-law-for-brownian-motion) implies $X_t\to0$ as $t\to\infty$, this is not a finite lifetime. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(T<\infty)=\begin{cases}1,&0<\alpha<1,\\0,&\alpha=1.\end{cases}}
$$

The power transformation is a rescaled [Lamperti transform](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#lamperti-transform-diffusion); at $\alpha=1$ the corresponding transformation is the [logarithm](../../../calculus.md#logarithm). No claim about [pathwise uniqueness](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#pathwise-uniqueness) after adjoining the boundary zero is needed: the coefficients are locally [Lipschitz continuous](../../../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) inside the positive domain, which is the domain of the given [maximal local solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#maximal-local-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation).

Set $V(x)=(1+x^2)^{-1}$ and $A_t=\int_0^tV(B_s)ds$. On a fixed horizon $T$, the [Itô formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) and [Itô product rule](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-product-rule) give

$$
d\bigl(e^{A_t}u(T-t,B_t)\bigr)=e^{A_t}\bigl(-u_t+\tfrac12u_{xx}+Vu\bigr)(T-t,B_t)dt+e^{A_t}u_x(T-t,B_t)dB_t.
$$

The drift vanishes by the [partial differential equation](../../../partial-differential-equation.md). This makes $M$ a [continuous local martingale](../../../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale). Since $0\leq A_t\leq T$,

$$
\boxed{|M_t|\leq e^T\sup_{0\leq s\leq T,\,y\in\mathbb R}|u(s,y)|\quad(0\leq t\leq T).}
$$

The right side is a finite deterministic bound under the stated boundedness assumption, so the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) removes a [localizing sequence](../../../martingale.md#localizing-sequence) and $M$ is a true [martingale](../../../martingale.md). If the derivatives are also bounded, the [Itô isometry](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) alternatively proves the stochastic integral is [square-integrable](../../../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) because $\mathbb E\int_0^Te^{2A_s}|u_x(T-s,B_s)|^2ds\leq Te^{2T}\|u_x\|_\infty^2$.

For the [bounded parabolic differentiability class](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#bounded-parabolic-differentiability-class), distinguish global bounds from bounds on each finite time slab. Only the latter are needed here; a single global bound on the [classical solution](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#classical-solution) $u$ would conflict with its requested long-time growth in part (c).

<h3 id="6/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#6/b)

For the true [martingale](../../../martingale.md) of part (a), $M_0=u(T,x)$ and $M_T=f(B_T)e^{A_T}$. Taking [expectations](../../../probability-theory.md#expected-value) gives the [Feynman-Kac formula with a bounded potential](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula-with-a-bounded-potential):

$$
\boxed{u(t,x)=\mathbb E_x\left[f(B_t)\exp\!\left(\int_0^t\frac{ds}{1+B_s^2}\right)\right].}
$$

The right side is finite since its absolute value is at most $e^t\|f\|_\infty$. This also proves uniqueness among [classical solutions](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#classical-solution) bounded on every finite time slab: applying the formula to their difference with zero initial data gives zero. The positive sign of the potential in the [partial differential equation](../../../partial-differential-equation.md) explains the positive exponent; changing it to a negative exponent would solve a different equation.

<h3 id="6/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [6](#6)

<h4 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#6/c)

The [infinite occupation time of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#infinite-occupation-time-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion) implies

$$
A_\infty=\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{1+B_s^2}\geq\frac12\int_0^\infty\mathbf1_{\{|B_s|\leq1\}}ds=\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

For a start $x\ne0$, first wait until $B$ hits zero, which happens in finite time by [recurrence of one-dimensional Brownian motion](../../../brownian-motion.md#recurrence-of-one-dimensional-brownian-motion), and apply the [Strong Markov property](../../../markov-process.md#strong-markov-property). The repeated exit-and-return argument in Question 4(c) then proves the infinite occupation integral. Since $f\geq C>0$, the [Feynman-Kac formula](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#feynman-kac-formula) gives $u(t,x)\geq C\mathbb E_x e^{A_t}$. The random variables $e^{A_t}$ increase to infinity [almost surely](../../../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence); the [monotone convergence theorem](../../../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem) therefore yields the intended [positive-potential growth from Brownian recurrence](../../../stochastic-calculus.md#positive-potential-growth-from-brownian-recurrence):

$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,x)=+\infty\quad\text{for every fixed }x\in\mathbb R.}
$$

**Under global boundedness, no such solution exists when the initial function is bounded below by a positive constant.** The PDF prints $C_b^{1,2}(\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb R)$; if the subscript means bounded over the whole product, its hypotheses with $f\geq C>0$ are incompatible, rather than an example of growth within that class. The precise correction is to require $u$ and its indicated derivatives to be bounded on $[0,T]\times\mathbb R$ for every finite $T$. The derivation above then gives the requested limit. Already $f\equiv1$ satisfies the printed initial-data assumption but cannot have a globally bounded [classical solution](../../../partial-differential-equation.md#classical-solution), by this formula. Also the PDF's $u(x,t)$ in this clause reverses the argument order from $u(t,x)$ used in the equation; the limit above keeps the equation's time-first convention.
