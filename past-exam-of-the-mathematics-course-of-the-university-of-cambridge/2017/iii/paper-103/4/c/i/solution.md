<h1 id="4/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $P$ be the averaging [linear map](../../../../../../../linear-map.md)

$$
P=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\rho(h).
$$

For a [unitary representation](../../../../../../../unitary-representation.md) of a [finite group](../../../../../../../finite-group.md), $P$ is the [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) onto the [invariant subspace](../../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) $V^G$, and $P\rho(h)=\rho(h)P=P$. Applying this to $\rho(g)v=\lambda v$ gives $Pv=\lambda Pv$. Since $\lambda\ne1$, $Pv=0$. The vector $v$ is a [cyclic vector for a group representation](../../../../../../../cyclic-vector-for-a-group-representation.md), so its translates span $V$, and every translate also has projection zero. Thus $P=0$ and

$$
\boxed{V^G=\{0\}.}
$$

This is the [cyclic eigenvector obstruction to invariant vectors](../../../../../../../cyclic-eigenvector-obstruction-to-invariant-vectors.md). Unitarity also gives a direct proof by pairing $v$ with an invariant vector and then pairing every translate; the averaging proof makes the role of cyclicity especially transparent.
