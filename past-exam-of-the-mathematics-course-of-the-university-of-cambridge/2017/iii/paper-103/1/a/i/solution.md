<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Work over the [complex numbers](../../../../../../../complex-number.md). The chain $S_0\subset S_1\subset\cdots\subset S_n$ has [multiplicity-free restriction](../../../../../../../multiplicity-free-restriction.md): each [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) restricts to a [direct sum](../../../../../../../direct-sum.md) of pairwise inequivalent [irreducible representations](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md). This structural fact can be proved before identifying the branching diagram. Indeed, the permitted [Olshanskii centralizer lemma](../../../../../../../olshanskii-centralizer-lemma.md) makes $C_{\mathbb C S_r}(\mathbb C S_{r-1})$ commutative, since it is generated by the center at the previous level and the commuting element $X_r$. In each irreducible block this is the endomorphism algebra of the restricted module; a repeated summand would give a noncommutative [matrix algebra](../../../../../../../matrix-algebra.md) factor. Thus no identification of the branching graph with [Young diagrams](../../../../../../../young-diagram.md) is being assumed here.

Successively restricting an [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) $V$ gives one-dimensional spaces $V_T$ indexed by paths of [irreducible representations](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) from the trivial $S_0$-module to $V$. Choosing one nonzero vector in each $V_T$ gives a [Gelfand–Tsetlin basis](../../../../../../../gelfand-tsetlin-basis.md). Define the [Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra](../../../../../../../gelfand-tsetlin-algebra.md) as the subalgebra of $\mathbb C S_n$ acting diagonally in all these bases. The [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](../../../../../../../artin-wedderburn-theorem.md) identifies

$$
\mathbb C S_n\cong\bigoplus_{V\in\operatorname{Irr}(S_n)}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V).
$$

Under this identification the [Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra](../../../../../../../gelfand-tsetlin-algebra.md) is the direct sum of the full diagonal [matrix algebras](../../../../../../../matrix-algebra.md) in the indicated bases, so it is commutative.

To see that it is actually available inside the [group algebra](../../../../../../../group-algebra.md), let $e_\alpha^{(r)}$ be the central [primitive idempotent](../../../../../../../primitive-idempotent.md) selecting the [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) $\alpha$ of $S_r$. For a path $T=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ the product

$$
P_T=\prod_{r=0}^n e_{\alpha_r}^{(r)}
$$

is the projection onto $V_T$ in its final [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) and is zero in every other final block. These products commute: a center at a higher level commutes with every element at a lower level. The $P_T$ are precisely the diagonal [matrix units](../../../../../../../matrix-unit.md), and therefore span the proposed algebra.

If an element of the [group algebra](../../../../../../../group-algebra.md) commutes with every $P_T$, its matrix preserves every $V_T$ and is diagonal in every block. It already belongs to the [Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra](../../../../../../../gelfand-tsetlin-algebra.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{C_{\mathbb C S_n}(GZ_n)=GZ_n,}
$$

which proves that it is a [maximal commutative subalgebra](../../../../../../../maximal-commutative-subalgebra.md).
