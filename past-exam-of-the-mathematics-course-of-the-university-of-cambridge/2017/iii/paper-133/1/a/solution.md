<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Milnor–Švarc lemma](../../../../../../milnor-svarc-lemma.md) applies to an isometric [properly discontinuous group action](../../../../../../properly-discontinuous-group-action.md) that is also a [cocompact group action](../../../../../../cocompact-group-action.md) on a nonempty [proper metric space](../../../../../../proper-metric-space.md) that is a [geodesic metric space](../../../../../../geodesic-metric-space.md). It concludes that the group is finitely generated and, for a basepoint $x$ and any finite [generating set of a group](../../../../../../generating-set-of-a-group.md) $S$, the [orbit map](../../../../../../orbit-map.md)

$$
\boxed{(G,d_S)\longrightarrow X,\qquad g\longmapsto gx,\text{ is a quasi-isometry}.}
$$

Here $d_S$ is the [word metric](../../../../../../word-metric.md), and a [quasi-isometry](../../../../../../quasi-isometry.md) means a map satisfying two-sided coarse distance bounds with a uniformly coarsely dense image. A free action is not required: finite stabilizers are compatible with proper discontinuity. The properness and cocompactness hypotheses ensure that only finitely many group elements move a chosen basepoint by the bounded amount used to generate the group. Replacing a finite generating set by another does not change the conclusion, by [equivalence of finite word metrics](../../../../../../equivalence-of-finite-word-metrics.md).
