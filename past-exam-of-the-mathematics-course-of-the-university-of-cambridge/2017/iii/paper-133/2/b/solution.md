<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Greendlinger lemma](../../../../../../greendlinger-lemma.md) says that a nonempty freely reduced [null-homotopic](../../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) combinatorial loop in a complex satisfying the [metric small cancellation condition](../../../../../../metric-small-cancellation-condition.md) $C'(1/6)$ contains a consecutive segment of a cell boundary longer than half that boundary. In a reduced [disc diagram](../../../../../../disc-diagram.md) without boundary spurs this is supplied by a boundary [disc diagram shell](../../../../../../disc-diagram-shell.md), or by the single-cell case. The long segment can be replaced by the strictly shorter complementary segment.

The useful precise diagram version also allows degenerate diagrams. A [reduced disc diagram](../../../../../../reduced-disc-diagram.md) is either a single vertex, a single closed cell, a [disc diagram ladder](../../../../../../disc-diagram-ladder.md), or has at least three [disc diagram spurs](../../../../../../disc-diagram-spur.md) or shells whose inner paths consist of at most three pieces. This is the [Greendlinger ladder theorem](../../../../../../greendlinger-ladder-theorem.md). For such a shell, writing the perimeter as $QP$, where $Q$ is the exterior arc and $P$ is the union of its interior pieces, the strict $C'(1/6)$ inequalities give

$$
\boxed{|P|<\tfrac12|\partial R|,\qquad |Q|>\tfrac12|\partial R|.}
$$

A ladder is a chain of cells and possibly connecting edges, with the two boundary paths running along its opposite sides. In a nontrivial ladder with at least two blocks its two ends are shells or spurs. The single-cell case also has the long exterior arc conclusion when viewed against an appropriate boundary decomposition.

A tree diagram with no two-cells should not be described as containing a shell: it has a spur unless it is a point. Retaining the spur and ladder alternatives is essential for the quasiconvexity argument below.
