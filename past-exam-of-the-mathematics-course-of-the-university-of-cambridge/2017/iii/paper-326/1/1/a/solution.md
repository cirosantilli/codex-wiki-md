<h1 id="1/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [forward problem](../../../../../../../forward-problem.md) evaluates a known model at a state: given $u$ and $K$, compute $f=Ku$. The associated [inverse problem](../../../../../../../inverse-problem-split.md) takes the data $f$ and the model $K$ and seeks a state $u$ with $Ku=f$.

In the sense of [Hadamard well-posedness](../../../../../../../well-posed-problem.md), a problem is well posed if a solution exists for every admissible datum, is unique, and depends continuously on the datum in the specified input and output topologies. An [inverse problem](../../../../../../../inverse-problem-split.md) is [ill-posed](../../../../../../../ill-posed-problem.md) if at least one of these requirements fails. Thus **ill-posedness means failure of existence, uniqueness or stability**. The topologies and the admissible data set matter: restricting the data to the range restores existence, but need not restore continuous dependence.
