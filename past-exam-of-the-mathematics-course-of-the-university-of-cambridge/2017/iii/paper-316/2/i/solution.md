<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Treat $a,e$ as instantaneous [osculating orbital elements](../../../../../../osculating-orbital-element.md) of the fixed-central-parameter [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md). The perturbing [work](../../../../../../work.md) changes the [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) according to

$$
\dot\varepsilon=\boldsymbol v\cdot(\bar R\widehat{\boldsymbol r}+\bar T\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta})
 =\bar R\dot r+\bar T\frac hr,\qquad
\dot a=\frac{2a^2}{\mu}\dot\varepsilon.
$$

The [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) and conic equation give

$$
\dot r=\frac{\mu}{h}e\sin f,\qquad
\frac hr=\frac{\mu}{h}(1+e\cos f),\qquad h=\sqrt{\mu a(1-e^2)}.
$$

Substituting proves the [Gauss planetary equation for the semi-major axis](../../../../../../gauss-planetary-equation-for-the-semi-major-axis.md):

$$
\boxed{\dot a=2\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu(1-e^2)}}
[\bar R e\sin f+\bar T(1+e\cos f)]}.
$$

Here $f$ denotes [true anomaly](../../../../../../true-anomaly.md), not the fractional [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) used in Question 1. The equation follows from [work](../../../../../../work.md) alone; a normal perturbing [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) has no instantaneous contribution because the [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) lies in the [orbital plane](../../../../../../orbital-plane.md). For slowly averaged evolution, the perturbation must be weak over one [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md).
