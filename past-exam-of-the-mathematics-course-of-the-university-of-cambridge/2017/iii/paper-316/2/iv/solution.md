<h1 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $\mu_p=GM_p$, $A=\beta GM_\star/c$ and $\kappa=A/a_p^2$. A tightly bound [circumplanetary orbit](../../../../../../circumplanetary-orbit.md) lies well inside the [Hill sphere](../../../../../../hill-sphere.md), so $a/a_p\ll1$ and its [orbital period](../../../../../../orbital-period.md) is short compared with the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md)'s year. Treat the stellar flux and stellar direction $\widehat{\boldsymbol R}$ as constant during one dust [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). With stellar-frame [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $\boldsymbol V_p+\boldsymbol u$, the velocity-dependent [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) is

$$
\boldsymbol a_{\rm PR}=-\kappa[\boldsymbol V_p+\boldsymbol u+
((\boldsymbol V_p+\boldsymbol u)\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol R})\widehat{\boldsymbol R}].
$$

The terms independent of $\boldsymbol u$, including the leading static [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md), do no net [work](../../../../../../work.md) on an unperturbed closed circular [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). Hence

$$
\langle\dot\varepsilon_p\rangle=-\kappa\langle u^2+(\boldsymbol u\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol R})^2\rangle.
$$

For a coplanar circular [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md), $u^2=\mu_p/a$ and the component along the stellar direction has mean square $u^2/2$. Thus $\dot a=2a^2\dot\varepsilon_p/\mu_p$ yields the [Poynting–Robertson decay of a circumplanetary orbit](../../../../../../poynting-robertson-decay-of-a-circumplanetary-orbit.md)

$$
\boxed{\langle\dot a\rangle_{\rm cp}=-3\frac{\beta\mu}{c}\frac a{a_p^2}}.
$$

**The coefficient three assumes coplanarity, which the PDF does not state**. For orbital normal $\widehat{\boldsymbol k}$, the general circular covariance is $\langle u_i u_j\rangle=(u^2/2)(\delta_{ij}-k_i k_j)$, giving

$$
\boxed{\langle\dot a\rangle_{\rm cp}=-\kappa a[3-(\widehat{\boldsymbol k}\cdot\widehat{\boldsymbol R})^2]}.
$$

An [orbital plane](../../../../../../orbital-plane.md) normal to the stellar radial direction has coefficient two during that [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md), a counterexample to an orientation-independent coefficient three. Averaging also over the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md)'s circular stellar [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md), with fixed dust-plane [orbital inclination](../../../../../../orbital-inclination.md) $I$ to it, gives coefficient $3-\tfrac12\sin^2I$. The conservative [forces](../../../../../../force.md) must be weak enough for the assumed approximately circular, planet-bound [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) to persist; the small planet-to-star [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) ratio alone does not ensure this for arbitrary grain $\beta$.
