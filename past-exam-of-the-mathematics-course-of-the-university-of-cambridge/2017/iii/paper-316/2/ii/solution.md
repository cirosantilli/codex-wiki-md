<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [blackbody](../../../../../../blackbody.md) grain absorbs starlight of flux $L_\star/(4\pi r^2)$ and reradiates isotropically in its own rest frame. [Aberration of light](../../../../../../relativistic-aberration.md) makes the incoming beam tilt against transverse motion. In the stellar frame, the reradiated [photons](../../../../../../photon.md) carry [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) proportional to the grain's [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md), producing [Poynting–Robertson drag](../../../../../../poynting-robertson-drag.md); it does not require anisotropic emission in the grain's frame.

Define the [radiation-pressure coefficient](../../../../../../radiation-pressure-coefficient.md)

$$
\boxed{\beta=\frac{L_\star\sigma}{4\pi c\,GM_\star m}}.
$$

For a spherical grain of radius $s$ and [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) $\rho$, $\beta=3L_\star/(16\pi c\,GM_\star\rho s)$, assuming unit [radiation-pressure efficiency](../../../../../../radiation-pressure-efficiency.md). To first order in $v/c$, the [first-order stellar radiation force on a blackbody grain](../../../../../../first-order-stellar-radiation-force-on-a-blackbody-grain.md) is

$$
\boldsymbol F_{\rm rad}=\frac{m\beta\mu}{r^2}
\left[\left(1-\frac{\dot r}{c}\right)\widehat{\boldsymbol r}-\frac{\boldsymbol v}{c}\right].
$$

The incoming absorbed [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) [momentum](../../../../../../momentum.md) contains the relative-flux factor $1-\dot r/c$; reradiation supplies the additional $-\boldsymbol v/c$. Separating the velocity-independent [radiation pressure](../../../../../../radiation-pressure.md) leaves

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol F_{\rm PR}=\frac{m\beta\mu}{r^2}
\left[-\frac{2\dot r}{c}\widehat{\boldsymbol r}
-\frac{r\dot\theta}{c}\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}\right]}.
$$

Thus there are two radial first-order contributions and one tangential contribution. The approximation assumes $v\ll c$, steady absorption/reradiation and constant grain properties; a non-blackbody requires its actual optical efficiencies.
