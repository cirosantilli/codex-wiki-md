<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $r_0$ be the separation of the two centres at release. The [two-body problem](../../../../../../two-body-problem.md) has relative [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) $\varepsilon=v^2/2-G(M_p+M)/r$. Marginal escape has $\varepsilon=0$, so

$$
\boxed{v_{\rm esc}(r_0)=\sqrt{\frac{2G(M_p+M)}{r_0}}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2GM_p}{r_0}}}.
$$

This follows equally by equating the relative [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) $\tfrac12m_{\rm red}v^2$ to the magnitude of the [Newtonian gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-potential-energy.md) $GM_pM/r_0$, where $m_{\rm red}=MM_p/(M+M_p)$.

For a pointlike [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) released at the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md)'s surface, $r_0=R_p$, giving the usual $\boxed{v_{\rm esc}=\sqrt{2GM_p/R_p}}$. If the finite [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) touches that surface, its centre is at $r_0=R_p+R$; the same formula uses this larger separation. The assumption $M_p\gg M$ by itself does not imply $R\ll R_p$. Subsequent formulas written in terms of the planet-only [escape velocity](../../../../../../escape-velocity.md) use the additional small-comet approximation.

This is local escape from an isolated [planet](../../../../../../planet.md). In a stellar [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md), reaching the edge of the [Hill sphere](../../../../../../hill-sphere.md) and escaping the stellar system are different [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) thresholds.
