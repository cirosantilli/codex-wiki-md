<h1 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $\chi=v_{\rm esc}/v_k$, with $v_k=\sqrt{GM_\star/a_p}$. The [Safronov number](../../../../../../safronov-number.md) is $\Theta=\chi^2/2$. In the instantaneous [planet](../../../../../../planet.md) frame let the asymptotic vectors be $\boldsymbol u_{\rm in}$ and $\boldsymbol u_{\rm out}$; their magnitudes agree. In the stellar frame the corresponding [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) change during a short encounter is

$$
\Delta\varepsilon_\star=\boldsymbol V_p\cdot
(\boldsymbol u_{\rm out}-\boldsymbol u_{\rm in}),\qquad
|\Delta\varepsilon_\star|\leq\frac{v_kv_{\rm esc}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The quadratic relative-speed terms cancel. A [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) with [semi-major axis](../../../../../../semi-major-axis.md) comparable to $a_p$ has stellar binding [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) per unit [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) of order $v_k^2/2$. Consequently $\chi$ measures whether a single strong encounter can change a substantial fraction of that binding.

**Large escape-to-orbital-speed ratios favour ejection; small ratios favour collision or [accretion](../../../../../../accretion.md) during repeated encounters**. For $\chi\gg1$, a suitably oriented [gravitational assist](../../../../../../gravitational-assist.md) can eject the [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) before it strikes the [planet](../../../../../../planet.md). For $\chi\ll1$, most individual kicks are too weak; many close passages may be needed, providing repeated chances of collision.

The crossover is of order unity, not a sharp theorem about each [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). The initial binding [energy](../../../../../../energy.md), encounter speed and geometry matter: an already nearly parabolic [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) may be ejected by a low-$\chi$ [planet](../../../../../../planet.md), while an unfavourably directed kick can make a [comet](../../../../../../comet.md) more tightly bound even when $\chi$ is large.
