<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Let $D_c$ be the parent [comet](../../../../../../comet.md)'s diameter, with the same [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) as the fragments, so $m=\pi\rho D_c^3/6$. The required [geometric cross-section](../../../../../../geometric-collision-cross-section.md) is $\sigma_{\rm tot}=4\pi a^2\sqrt{1-e^2}\,f$, using the corrected [phase-mixed orbit](../../../../../../phase-mixed-orbit.md) interpretation.

Substitute this and the spherical [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) into the endpoint-dominated fragment formula. The [parent-body size from a fragment cross-section](../../../../../../parent-body-size-from-a-fragment-cross-section.md) becomes

$$
\boxed{D_c=\left[\frac{16(\alpha-3)}{4-\alpha}\,
f a^2\sqrt{1-e^2}\,D_{\min}^{\alpha-3}D_{\max}^{4-\alpha}\right]^{1/3}}.
$$

The common [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) cancels. The [comet](../../../../../../comet.md)'s radius is $D_c/2$. This expression treats both fragment cutoffs as specified independently and has the same endpoint-approximation restrictions as part (iii).

If a model instead ties the largest fragment to the parent through $D_{\max}=\eta D_c$, with fixed $0<\eta\leq1$, one must solve that dependence too:

$$
\boxed{D_c=\left[\frac{16(\alpha-3)}{4-\alpha}\,
f a^2\sqrt{1-e^2}\,D_{\min}^{\alpha-3}\eta^{4-\alpha}\right]^{1/(\alpha-1)}}.
$$

That extra assumption is not supplied by the PDF; setting $D_{\max}=D_c$ without saying so would change the requested size relation.
