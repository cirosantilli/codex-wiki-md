<h1 id="1/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

Use an [edge-on orbit](../../../../../../edge-on-orbit.md) and an equatorial, effectively central chord. Near the foreground crossing the radial [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) is along the line of sight; the projected transverse speed is $v_\perp=h/r$. In the small stellar-angular-radius limit, $r\gg R_\star$, the crossing time is $2R_\star/v_\perp=2R_\star r/h$.

The [cross-sectional-area current](../../../../../../cross-sectional-area-current.md) therefore places an area

$$
\sigma_{\rm front}=(2fh)\frac{2R_\star r}{h}=4fR_\star r
$$

in front of the stellar disk. For an [optically thin](../../../../../../optically-thin-medium.md) wire and a uniformly bright stellar disk, [transit dimming by an optically thin orbital wire](../../../../../../transit-dimming-by-an-optically-thin-orbital-wire.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{\Delta F}{F_\star}=\frac{\sigma_{\rm front}}{\pi R_\star^2}
=\frac4\pi\frac r{R_\star}f}.
$$

This is a linear occultation estimate, requiring negligible overlap and a fractional dimming much smaller than one. A finite [impact parameter](../../../../../../impact-parameter.md) shortens the chord; [limb darkening](../../../../../../limb-darkening.md), finite wire thickness and variation of $r$ across a large stellar angular extent modify the coefficient. The estimate cannot be extrapolated to dimming greater than unity.
