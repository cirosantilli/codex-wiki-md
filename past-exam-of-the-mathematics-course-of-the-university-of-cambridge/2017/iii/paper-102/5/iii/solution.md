<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose the chain basis $v_0,\ldots,v_6$ from the preceding part, normalized by $v_1=f_1v_0$, $v_2=f_2v_1$, $v_3=f_1v_2$. The associated [sl2 Lie algebra](../../../../../../sl2-lie-algebra.md) strings give $e_1v_1=v_0$, $e_1v_3=2v_2$ and $e_2v_2=v_1$; other raising actions used below vanish because their proposed weights do not occur.

In the [exterior square](../../../../../../exterior-square.md), $v_0\wedge v_1$ is a [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) of weight $2\omega_1-\alpha_1=\omega_2$: $e_1$ produces $v_0\wedge v_0=0$, and $e_2$ kills both factors. Also

$$
w=v_0\wedge v_3-2v_1\wedge v_2
$$

is a nonzero [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) of weight $\omega_1$. Its two $e_1$ terms cancel, and its $e_2$ image is zero. The [Weyl complete reducibility theorem](../../../../../../weyl-complete-reducibility-theorem.md) supplies irreducible summands with those weights; their dimensions $14+7=21$ exhaust $\dim\Lambda^2V$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\Lambda^2V=L(\omega_2)\oplus L(\omega_1).}
$$

For the [symmetric square](../../../../../../symmetric-square.md), $v_0\otimes v_0$ is a [highest-weight vector](../../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) of weight $2\omega_1$, giving a 27-dimensional summand. The [longest Weyl-group element](../../../../../../longest-weyl-group-element.md) of $G_2$ acts as minus the identity, so the [highest weight of a dual representation](../../../../../../highest-weight-of-a-dual-representation.md) is $-w_0\omega_1=\omega_1$: $V$ is self-dual. A nonzero intertwiner $V\to V^*$ gives a [nondegenerate](../../../../../../nondegenerate-bilinear-form.md) [Lie-invariant bilinear form](../../../../../../lie-invariant-bilinear-form.md). It is symmetric, since an [alternating bilinear form](../../../../../../alternating-bilinear-form.md) cannot be nondegenerate in odd [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md). Its inverse is a nonzero invariant vector in $S^2V$, supplying the scalar summand. Since $27+1=28=\dim S^2V$,

$$
\boxed{S^2V=L(2\omega_1)\oplus L(0).}
$$

These are the [exterior and symmetric squares of the seven-dimensional G2 representation](../../../../../../exterior-and-symmetric-squares-of-the-seven-dimensional-g2-representation.md); all highest-weight and dimension claims are specified rather than relying on an unstated cross-product identity.
