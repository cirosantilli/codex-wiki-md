<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In [field characteristic](../../../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) $p$, the identity matrix has [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $p=0$, and hence belongs to $\mathfrak{sl}_p$. Its span is a nonzero proper [central ideal](../../../../../../central-ideal.md), so $\mathfrak{sl}_p$ is not a [simple Lie algebra](../../../../../../simple-lie-algebra.md). In fact its [center of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../center-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is precisely $kI_p$: commuting with every off-diagonal [matrix unit](../../../../../../matrix-unit.md) forces a matrix to be scalar.

For $p>2$, the [matrix-unit extraction lemma for special linear ideals](../../../../../../matrix-unit-extraction-lemma-for-special-linear-ideals.md) still works because two is invertible. Any ideal containing a nonscalar matrix contains every off-diagonal [matrix unit](../../../../../../matrix-unit.md) and every trace-zero diagonal matrix, hence equals $\mathfrak{sl}_p$. Therefore an ideal of the [quotient Lie algebra](../../../../../../quotient-lie-algebra.md) pulls back either to the center or to the whole algebra. The quotient is nonabelian, since $[E_{12},E_{23}]=E_{13}$ remains nonzero modulo the center. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak{sl}_p/kI_p\text{ is simple for }p>2.}
$$

No separation of diagonal [root spaces](../../../../../../root-space.md) is needed, so this proof also handles $p=3$ without assuming their weights are distinct.

For $p=2$, every trace-zero two-by-two matrix has the form $aI+bE_{12}+cE_{21}$. The only nonzero bracket between these basis vectors is $[E_{12},E_{21}]=I$, which is central. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak{sl}_2/kI\text{ is a two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra in characteristic two.}}
$$
