<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use [azimuth](../../../../../../../azimuth.md) measured from north toward west, and positive [hour angle](../../../../../../../hour-angle.md) westward. These conventions are forced by the signs in the coordinate formulae. In the diagram the observer is at $O$, the local vertical meets the [celestial sphere](../../../../../../../celestial-sphere.md) at the [zenith](../../../../../../../zenith.md) $Z$, and the north [celestial pole](../../../../../../../celestial-pole.md) is $P$. The [astronomical horizon](../../../../../../../astronomical-horizon.md) is perpendicular to $OZ$; the [celestial equator](../../../../../../../celestial-equator.md) is perpendicular to $OP$. Their intersections with the [celestial meridian](../../../../../../../celestial-meridian.md) give the north horizon point and the upper equatorial meridian point.

![](../../../../../../../past-exam-of-the-mathematics-course-of-the-university-of-cambridge/2017/iii/paper-338-celestial-sphere.png)

**[Figure 1](#1/a/i/image-the-celestial-sphere-with-horizon-and-equatorial-coordinates). The celestial sphere with horizon and equatorial coordinates**.

The north [celestial pole](../../../../../../../celestial-pole.md) has [altitude](../../../../../../../altitude.md) $\theta$; thus the [angle](../../../../../../../angle.md) between $OP$ and $OZ$ is $90^\circ-\theta$. Project $X$ along its vertical [great circle](../../../../../../../great-circle.md) onto the [astronomical horizon](../../../../../../../astronomical-horizon.md) at $H$: the arc $HX$ is the [altitude](../../../../../../../altitude.md) $\gamma$, and the westward horizon arc from north to $H$ is the [azimuth](../../../../../../../azimuth.md) $\eta$. Project $X$ along its hour circle onto the [celestial equator](../../../../../../../celestial-equator.md) at $Q$: the arc $QX$ is the [declination](../../../../../../../declination.md) $\delta$, and the westward equatorial arc from the upper [celestial meridian](../../../../../../../celestial-meridian.md) to $Q$ is the [hour angle](../../../../../../../hour-angle.md) $h$. Equivalently the triangle on the [celestial sphere](../../../../../../../celestial-sphere.md) $PZX$ has sides $PZ=90^\circ-\theta$, $PX=90^\circ-\delta$ and $ZX=90^\circ-\gamma$. **All five [angles](../../../../../../../angle.md) refer to arcs on their specified reference circles, not arbitrary [angles](../../../../../../../angle.md) in the projected drawing.**
