↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For thin contacting components, [optical powers](../../../../../../../optical-power.md) add. At [wavelength](../../../../../../../wavelength.md) $\lambda$ the total [optical power](../../../../../../../optical-power.md) is $\Phi(\lambda)=\rho_1[n_1(\lambda)-1]+\rho_2[n_2(\lambda)-1]$. An [achromatic lens](../../../../../../../achromatic-lens.md) has $\Phi_F=\Phi_C$, hence

$$
\rho_1(n_{1F}-n_{1C})+\rho_2(n_{2F}-n_{2C})=0,
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}=-\frac{n_{2F}-n_{2C}}{n_{1F}-n_{1C}}.}
$$

For normally dispersive [optical glasses](../../../../../../../optical-glass.md), both index differences are positive, so the two components have opposite curvature factors and opposite [optical powers](../../../../../../../optical-power.md). This uses the thin [Lensmaker's equation](../../../../../../../lensmaker-s-equation.md); finite thickness and separated principal planes require a more detailed design. If the denominator vanishes, use the undivided power-balance equation rather than this ratio. Two-wavelength correction alone does not eliminate the residual [chromatic aberration](../../../../../../../chromatic-aberration.md) at every intermediate [wavelength](../../../../../../../wavelength.md).
