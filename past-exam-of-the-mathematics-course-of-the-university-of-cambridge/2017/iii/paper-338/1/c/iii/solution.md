<h1 id="1/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Set $\Phi_i=1/f_{id}$ and $\Phi=1/f_d$. The [achromatic doublet power balance](../../../../../../../achromatic-doublet-power-balance.md) gives two linear equations:

$$
\Phi_1+\Phi_2=\Phi,\qquad\frac{\Phi_1}{V_{1d}}+\frac{\Phi_2}{V_{2d}}=0.
$$

For distinct nonzero [Abbe numbers](../../../../../../../abbe-number.md), solving yields $\Phi_1=\Phi V_{1d}/(V_{1d}-V_{2d})$ and $\Phi_2=-\Phi V_{2d}/(V_{1d}-V_{2d})$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{f_{1d}=f_d\frac{V_{1d}-V_{2d}}{V_{1d}},\qquad
f_{2d}=f_d\frac{V_{2d}-V_{1d}}{V_{2d}}.}
$$

Equal [Abbe numbers](../../../../../../../abbe-number.md) force the total [optical power](../../../../../../../optical-power.md) to vanish under the achromatic condition; they cannot give a nonzero-power thin [achromatic lens](../../../../../../../achromatic-lens.md). Closely matched [Abbe numbers](../../../../../../../abbe-number.md) require large opposing component powers and are generally unattractive because of the resulting sensitivity and other [optical aberrations](../../../../../../../optical-aberration.md).
