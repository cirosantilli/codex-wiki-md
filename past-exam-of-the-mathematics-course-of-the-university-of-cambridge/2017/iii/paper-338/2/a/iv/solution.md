<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Differentiate the [grating equation](../../../../../../../grating-equation.md) with incidence fixed:

$$
\frac{d\beta}{d\lambda}=\frac{m}{d\cos\beta},\qquad
q=\frac{mf_{\rm cam}}{d\cos\beta}.
$$

The second formula is local to a spectrum centered on the [optical camera](../../../../../../../optical-camera.md) [optical axis](../../../../../../../optical-axis.md), where $dx=f_{\rm cam}\,d\beta$ to first order. Since the illuminated surface width $W$ projects to [optical camera](../../../../../../../optical-camera.md) beam width $A_{\rm cam}=W\cos\beta$,

$$
\boxed{L=\frac{\lambda qA_{\rm cam}}{f_{\rm cam}}=\frac{Wm\lambda}{d}.}
$$

The expression takes positive [diffraction order](../../../../../../../diffraction-order.md) and width magnitudes. Off-axis, a [photodetector](../../../../../../../photodetector.md) mapping $x=f_{\rm cam}\tan(\beta-\beta_0)$ also has a $\sec^2(\beta-\beta_0)$ factor; the exam's compact formula uses the local paraxial mapping.
