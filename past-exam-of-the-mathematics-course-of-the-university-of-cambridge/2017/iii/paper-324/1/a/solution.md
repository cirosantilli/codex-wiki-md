<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the positive-exponent [quantum Fourier transform](../../../../../../quantum-fourier-transform.md) convention $F_N|x\rangle=N^{-1/2}\sum_t e^{2\pi ixt/N}|t\rangle$. The [period of a function](../../../../../../period-of-a-function.md) here is the least positive translation leaving the function unchanged on the [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z_N$. The [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md) promise means $f(x)=f(x')$ exactly when $x\equiv x'\pmod r$. In particular $r\mid N$: the subgroup of period translations is generated by $r$, and wrapping the argument by $N$ preserves its value. This is exact [quantum period finding](../../../../../../quantum-period-finding.md) on a [finite cyclic group](../../../../../../finite-cyclic-group.md), rather than the truncated, nondividing-period version requiring [continued fractions](../../../../../../continued-fraction.md).

Prepare a [uniform superposition state](../../../../../../uniform-superposition-state.md) in the first [quantum register](../../../../../../quantum-register.md) using $F_N|0\rangle$, and perform a [reversible computation](../../../../../../reversible-computation.md) of the function into a second [quantum register](../../../../../../quantum-register.md), cleaning its workspace by [uncomputation](../../../../../../uncomputation.md):

$$
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{x=0}^{N-1}|x\rangle|0\rangle\longmapsto\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{x=0}^{N-1}|x\rangle|f(x)\rangle.
$$

A [quantum measurement in the computational basis](../../../../../../quantum-measurement-in-the-computational-basis.md) of the second [quantum register](../../../../../../quantum-register.md) leaves a [periodic coset state](../../../../../../periodic-coset-state.md) in the first:

$$
|C_a\rangle=\sqrt{\frac rN}\sum_{j=0}^{N/r-1}|a+jr\rangle,\qquad 0\leq a<r.
$$

For the [quantum Fourier transform](../../../../../../quantum-fourier-transform.md) of this [periodic coset state](../../../../../../periodic-coset-state.md), the [finite geometric series](../../../../../../finite-geometric-series.md) vanishes unless the measured label is $t=mN/r$. On those labels its normalized amplitude is $r^{-1/2}e^{2\pi iam/r}$. Thus $m$ has the [uniform distribution on a finite set](../../../../../../discrete-uniform-distribution.md) on $\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$, irrespective of $a$.

Compute the [greatest common divisor](../../../../../../greatest-common-divisor.md) by the [Euclidean algorithm](../../../../../../euclidean-algorithm.md), and set $q=N/\gcd(t,N)$, with $\gcd(0,N)=N$. Since $t=mN/r$,

$$
q=\frac r{\gcd(m,r)}.
$$

The [exact period recovery from a Fourier sample](../../../../../../exact-period-recovery-from-a-fourier-sample.md) is therefore certified by testing $f(q\bmod N)=f(0)$. Every candidate $q$ divides $r$ and lies between $1$ and $r$, so the [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md) promise makes that equality equivalent to $q=r$. This includes $r=N$, where the test uses input $0$, and $r=1$, which always succeeds. The test does not assert that an arbitrary equality of function values certifies an arbitrary period; the Fourier-derived divisor restriction is essential.

The concise result is

$$
\boxed{q=\frac N{\gcd(t,N)},\quad \text{accept iff }f(q\bmod N)=f(0),\quad P_{\mathrm{accept}}=\frac{\varphi(r)}r.}
$$

Here $\varphi$ is the [Euler totient function](../../../../../../euler-totient-function.md), with $\varphi(1)=1$. The [totient lower bound from the Mertens product](../../../../../../totient-lower-bound-from-the-mertens-product.md) gives $\varphi(r)/r=\Omega(1/\max\{1,\log\log N\})$ for $r\leq N$. Each trial uses [polynomial time](../../../../../../polynomial-time.md) [quantum Fourier transforms](../../../../../../quantum-fourier-transform.md), function evaluations and [Euclidean algorithm](../../../../../../euclidean-algorithm.md) arithmetic in $\log N$. Repeating $O(\max\{1,\log\log N\})$ independent trials gives constant success probability; the herald accepts only correct answers.

**The printed asymptotic bound has the wrong direction for this argument.** In [Big O notation](../../../../../../big-o-notation.md), the stated upper bound on the number of [coprime integers](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md) supplies no success lower bound and is itself false uniformly: for prime $N$, $\varphi(N)=N-1$, not $O(N/\log\log N)$. Likewise the single-trial success need not be $O(1/\log\log N)$, since it tends to one for $r=N$ prime. The meaningful corrected guarantee is the lower bound above. If an upper-bounded success rate is literally required, one may deliberately retain an accepted result with an independent probability proportional to $1/\max\{1,\log\log N\}$; this discards useful successes and is unnecessary for efficient recovery.
