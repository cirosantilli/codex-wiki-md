<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Outside a core of radius $r_0$, set $\lambda\simeq\lambda_0$ to leading order. The uniform-state bulk energy cancels on subtraction, while the divergence elastic density is $K\lambda_0^2/(2r^2)$. Its two-dimensional area integral is

$$
\Delta F_{\rm outer}=2\pi\int_{r_0}^L\frac{K\lambda_0^2}{2r^2}r\,dr=\pi K\lambda_0^2\log(L/r_0).
$$

Thus the [logarithmic energy of a planar nematic disclination](../../../../../../logarithmic-energy-of-a-planar-nematic-disclination.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta F=\pi K\lambda_0^2\log(L/r_0)+O(K\lambda_0^2),\qquad L\gg r_0.}
$$

The core suppresses the nematic amplitude to regularize the singular director texture. Balancing lost bulk ordering energy against elasticity gives $r_0$ of order $\sqrt{K/|a|}$. The exact core radius and additive core energy depend on the amplitude profile.

The “local” amplitude disturbance need not be literally compactly supported. The radial [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) following from the PDF density is

$$
a\lambda+b\lambda^3-\frac K4\left(\lambda''+\frac{\lambda'}r-\frac{4\lambda}{r^2}\right)=0.
$$

Its far-field expansion is $\lambda=\lambda_0[1-K/(2|a|r^2)+O(r^{-4})]$. These algebraic tails change only finite contributions, not the logarithm. A regular core has $\lambda=O(r^2)$ for this integer winding.

The large cutoff $L$ is set by the sample size for an isolated defect with corresponding imposed far-field winding, or by the distance to compensating defects or other texture-screening boundaries. An isolated nonzero winding in an infinite ordered plane has a logarithmically divergent excess energy; in a neutral pair, far-field distortions cancel beyond the separation. If $L$ is not much larger than $r_0$, the leading logarithm is not a dominant approximation.
