A [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $A$ is a [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md) if there exists an [injective function](../../../../../../injective-function.md) $A\to\mathbb N$, with $\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. Equivalently, it is a [finite set](../../../../../../finite-set.md) or has a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md) with $\mathbb N$. An infinite [countable set](../../../../../../countable-set.md) is called a [countably infinite set](../../../../../../countably-infinite-set.md).

The empty [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is included: the empty [function](../../../../../../function-split.md) into $\mathbb N$ is injective. This convention distinguishes “countable” from “countably infinite” and will also cover the finite threshold [sets](../../../../../../set-split.md) in the last part.
