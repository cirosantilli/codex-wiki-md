<h1 id="1d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Reduce $z$ using [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md): its [residue class](../../../../../../residue-class.md) modulo $3$ is $0$, $1$, or $-1$. A [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md) therefore has [residue class](../../../../../../residue-class.md) $0$ or $1$, and raising either residue to the positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) power $n$ leaves it unchanged. Consequently

$$
\boxed{z^{2n}\equiv\begin{cases}0\pmod3,&3\mid z,\\1\pmod3,&3\nmid z.\end{cases}}
$$

The exponent in the original PDF is $2n$; the polynomial identities in the converted TeX are a transcription error. The argument uses $n\ge1$; it does not assign $0^0$ a value.
