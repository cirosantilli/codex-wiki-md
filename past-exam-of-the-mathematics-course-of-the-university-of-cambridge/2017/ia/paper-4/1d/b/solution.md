<h1 id="1d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [Pythagorean triple](../../../../../../pythagorean-triple.md), each [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md) is $0$ or $1$ modulo $3$. If neither $x$ nor $y$ were divisible by $3$, [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md) would give $z^2\equiv1+1\equiv2\pmod3$, impossible for a [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md). Thus **at least one of $x,y$ is divisible by $3$**; both can be divisible, as in $(9,12,15)$.

An [odd number](../../../../../../odd-number.md) has [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md) congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, whereas an [even number](../../../../../../even-number.md) has [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md) congruent to $0$. If both $x$ and $y$ were [odd numbers](../../../../../../odd-number.md), their sum of [square numbers](../../../../../../square-number.md) would again be $2$ modulo $4$, which no [square number](../../../../../../square-number.md) can be. Hence **$x$ and $y$ cannot both be odd**. They can both be even; the conclusion does not require the [Pythagorean triple](../../../../../../pythagorean-triple.md) to be primitive.
