A [binary relation](../../../../../../binary-relation.md) on a [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $S$ is a [subset](../../../../../../subset.md) $R\subseteq S\times S$ of its [Cartesian product](../../../../../../cartesian-product.md); $xRy$ means $(x,y)\in R$. It is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) when it is a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md), a [symmetric relation](../../../../../../symmetric-relation.md), and a [transitive relation](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}&xRx&&\text{for every }x\in S,\\&xRy\Longrightarrow yRx&&\text{for every }x,y\in S,\\&xRy\ \text{and}\ yRz\Longrightarrow xRz&&\text{for every }x,y,z\in S.\end{aligned}}
$$

An [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) partitions $S$ into [equivalence classes](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md): two elements belong to the same class exactly when they are related.
