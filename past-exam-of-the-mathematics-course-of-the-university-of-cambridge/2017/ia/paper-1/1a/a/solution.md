<h1 id="1a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

By [Euler's formula](../../../../../../euler-s-formula.md), $z=e^{i\theta}$. Factoring out the half-angle gives

$$
1+z=e^{i\theta/2}\left(e^{-i\theta/2}+e^{i\theta/2}\right)
=2\cos\frac\theta2\,e^{i\theta/2}.
$$

Since $0\leq\theta<\pi$, the cosine is positive. Therefore

$$
\boxed{|1+z|=2\cos\frac\theta2,
\qquad \arg(1+z)=\frac\theta2}.
$$

On the [Argand diagram](../../../../../../complex-plane.md), the points $0,1,z,1+z$ form a rhombus. Its diagonal from $0$ to $1+z$ bisects the angle between the unit vectors $1$ and $z$, so its direction is $\theta/2$; resolving either side along the diagonal gives its length $2\cos(\theta/2)$.
