<h1 id="4e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4E](../4e.md)

Convergence at $z_0$ implies that the terms $a_nz_0^n$ form a [bounded sequence](../../../../../bounded-sequence.md): $|a_nz_0^n|\leq M$ for some $M$. If $|z|<|z_0|$ and $r=|z/z_0|<1$, then

$$
|a_nz^n|=|a_nz_0^n|r^n\leq Mr^n.
$$

Comparison with the convergent [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md) $\sum Mr^n$ proves [absolute convergence](../../../../../absolute-convergence.md). The case $z_0=0$ is vacuous.

The [radius of convergence](../../../../../radius-of-convergence.md) is the number $R\in[0,\infty]$ such that a [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) converges absolutely for $|z|<R$ and diverges for $|z|>R$, with boundary behavior considered separately.

Take

$$
v=-1,\qquad w=1.
$$

Then $\sum v^n/n$ is the convergent [alternating harmonic series](../../../../../alternating-harmonic-series.md), whereas $\sum w^n/n$ is the divergent [harmonic series](../../../../../harmonic-series.md). Thus $\sum z^n/n$ converges at a point of modulus one and diverges at another point of modulus one. It follows that

$$
\boxed{R=1}.
$$
