<h1 id="10d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The function $f$ is [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at $y_0$ when for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $\delta>0$ such that

$$
|x-y_0|<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|f(x)-f(y_0)|<\varepsilon.
$$

Suppose $f$ has a [local minimum](../../../../../../local-minimum.md) at $c$ and is differentiable there. For sufficiently small $h>0$,

$$
\frac{f(c+h)-f(c)}h\geq0,
\qquad
\frac{f(c-h)-f(c)}{-h}\leq0.
$$

Both one-sided limits equal $f'(c)$, so it is both nonnegative and nonpositive. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f'(c)=0}.
$$
