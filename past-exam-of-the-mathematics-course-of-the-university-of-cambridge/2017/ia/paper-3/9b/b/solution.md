<h1 id="9b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a twice [continuously differentiable](../../../../../../continuously-differentiable-function.md) [vector potential](../../../../../../vector-potential.md) $A$, write the [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) with the [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md):

$$
\nabla\cdot B=\partial_i(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_jA_k)
=\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_i\partial_jA_k.
$$

The second derivatives are symmetric in $i,j$, whereas $\epsilon_{ijk}$ is antisymmetric, so the contraction is zero. Equivalently, writing out the three components shows that each mixed derivative cancels its reversed-order partner. Thus

$$
\boxed{\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times A)=0.}
$$

This is [divergence of a curl is zero](../../../../../../divergence-of-a-curl-is-zero.md). The regularity hypothesis is what licenses commutation of the mixed [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md); it must not be omitted for a singular potential without specifying a weaker derivative interpretation.
