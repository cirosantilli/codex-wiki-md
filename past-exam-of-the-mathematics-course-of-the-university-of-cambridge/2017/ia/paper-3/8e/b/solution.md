<h1 id="8e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Write $c=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ n)$, with $n\geq2$. [Conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) relabels a [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), so

$$
c^j(1\ 2)c^{-j}=(1+j\ 2+j),
$$

where labels are read modulo $n$. In particular the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) contains $(1\ 2),(2\ 3),\ldots,(n-1\ n)$. These are the edge [transpositions](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) of a path through all letters; [transpositions on a connected graph generate the symmetric group](../../../../../../transpositions-on-a-connected-graph-generate-the-symmetric-group.md), so the two original generators generate $S_n$.

For the general separation $k$, conjugating $t=(1\ 1+k)$ produces every edge [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) $(i\ i+k)$ modulo $n$. The components of this [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) are precisely the residue classes modulo $d=\gcd(n,k)$: moving along an edge adds or subtracts $k$, and the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ generated by $k$ consists of multiples of $d$. Hence, if $d=1$, the [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) is connected and the same [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) argument gives the whole [symmetric group](../../../../../../symmetric-group.md).

For completeness, the [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) argument can be proved without assuming a generating-set theorem. On a simple path $v_0,\ldots,v_\ell$, put $t_j=(v_{j-1}\ v_j)$. Then

$$
(v_0\ v_\ell)=t_1\cdots t_{\ell-1}t_\ell t_{\ell-1}\cdots t_1.
$$

This is a conjugate of the last edge [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md); connectivity therefore supplies every [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md), and part (a) supplies every [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md).

If $d>1$, there are $d$ residue blocks of size $n/d>1$, since $1\leq k<n$. The cycle $c$ permutes these blocks, and $t$ swaps two letters in the same block. Both preserve this [block system](../../../../../../block-system.md), so their generated [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) does too. The full $S_n$ does not: a [transposition](../../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) exchanging one letter of two different blocks sends a block to a mixture of them. Thus the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) is proper. This proves

$$
\boxed{\langle(1\ 1+k),(1\ 2\ \cdots\ n)\rangle=S_n
\iff \gcd(n,k)=1.}
$$

Thus [a cycle and a transposition generate the symmetric group exactly at coprime separation](../../../../../../a-cycle-and-a-transposition-generate-the-symmetric-group-exactly-at-coprime-separation.md). The obstruction is preservation of a partition, not failure of transitivity: the $n$-cycle already acts transitively even when $d>1$.
