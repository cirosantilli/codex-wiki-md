<h1 id="6e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6E](../6e.md)

[Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that for a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$ of a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $G$, $|G|=[G:H]|H|$. For $x\in G$, two powers among $1,x,\ldots,x^{|G|}$ coincide, giving $x^m=1$ for some positive $m$. The least such exponent $d$ is the [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md), and the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by $x$ consists of the $d$ distinct powers $1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}$. Apply [Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) to obtain $d\mid|G|$.

[Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) states that if a prime $p$ divides the order of a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md), the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) contains an element of order $p$.

The classification of a [group of order eight](../../../../../group-of-order-eight.md) is

$$
\boxed{C_8,\quad C_4\times C_2,\quad C_2\times C_2\times C_2,\quad D_8,\quad Q_8.}
$$

Here $D_8$ denotes the [dihedral group](../../../../../dihedral-group.md) of order eight, not a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of order sixteen. To prove exhaustiveness, every element order is $1,2,4$ or $8$. An element of order eight generates the whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md), giving $C_8$.

If no element has order four or eight, every nonidentity element is an [involution](../../../../../involution.md). Since $(ab)^{-1}=ab$ while $a^{-1}=a$ and $b^{-1}=b$, we have $ab=ba$. Choose an [involution](../../../../../involution.md) $a$, another $b$ outside $\langle a\rangle$, and then $c$ outside the four-element [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle a,b\rangle$. The factors commute and each added factor has trivial intersection with the preceding [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md). The [internal direct product theorem](../../../../../internal-direct-product-theorem.md) gives $G\cong C_2^3$, an [elementary abelian group](../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md).

Otherwise choose $a$ of order four and set $A=\langle a\rangle$. It has index two, so is normal. Choose $b\notin A$. The two [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) show that $a,b$ generate $G$ and that $b^2\in A$. There is no element of order eight, so $b^2$ is either $1$ or $a^2$.

If $G$ is abelian and $b^2=1$, the [internal direct product theorem](../../../../../internal-direct-product-theorem.md) yields $G=A\times\langle b\rangle\cong C_4\times C_2$. If instead $b^2=a^2$, let $c=a^{-1}b$; commutativity gives $c^2=1$ and $c\notin A$, producing the same direct product.

If $G$ is nonabelian, [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $b$ is a nonidentity [group automorphism](../../../../../group-automorphism.md) of $A$: otherwise $a,b$ would commute and hence so would all of $G$. Thus $bab^{-1}=a^{-1}$. With $b^2=1$ these are the relations of $D_8$; with $b^2=a^2$ they are the relations of the [quaternion group](../../../../../quaternion-group.md) $Q_8$. In either presentation every word reduces to $a^ib^j$, $0\leq i<4$, $0\leq j<2$. The standard eight-element model maps onto $G$, and equal orders make that [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) an isomorphism.

Finally [group isomorphisms](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) preserve element orders. The following numbers of elements distinguish all five possibilities:

| [Group](../../../../../group-split.md) | Order 1 | Order 2 | Order 4 | Order 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $C_8$ | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| $C_4\times C_2$ | 1 | 3 | 4 | 0 |
| $C_2^3$ | 1 | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| $D_8$ | 1 | 5 | 2 | 0 |
| $Q_8$ | 1 | 1 | 6 | 0 |

Thus the list is both exhaustive and pairwise non-isomorphic.
