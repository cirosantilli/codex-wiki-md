<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [twist of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../twist-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) $E/\mathbb Q$ is an elliptic curve that becomes isomorphic to $E$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Write

$$
E:y^2=x^3+Ax+B.
$$

For $d\in\mathbb Q^\times$, its [quadratic twist](../../../../../../quadratic-twist-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) may be written

$$
E^d:y^2=x^3+d^2Ax+d^3B.
$$

Over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$, the map $(x,y)\mapsto(d^{-1}x,d^{-3/2}y)$ identifies $E^d$ with $E$.

The hypothesis $j(E)\ne0,1728$ implies

$$
\operatorname{Aut}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(E)=\{\pm1\}.
$$

Consequently twists are classified by

$$
H^1(G_{\mathbb Q},\{\pm1\})
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(G_{\mathbb Q},\{\pm1\})
\simeq\mathbb Q^\times/(\mathbb Q^\times)^2,
$$

where the last identification is [Kummer theory](../../../../../../kummer-theory.md). In the explicit equations, $E^d\simeq_{\mathbb Q}E^e$ exactly when $d/e$ is a rational square. Every nonzero rational square class has a unique square-free integer representative, including its sign, so the twists are parametrized by the nonzero square-free integers.
