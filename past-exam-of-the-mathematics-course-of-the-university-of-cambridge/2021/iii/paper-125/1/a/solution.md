<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../weierstrass-equation-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) over a [field](../../../../../../field.md) $K$ has the general form

$$
y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6,
$$

with nonzero [elliptic-curve discriminant](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-discriminant.md). [Admissible change of Weierstrass coordinates](../../../../../../admissible-change-of-weierstrass-coordinates.md) relates two such equations defining isomorphic pointed curves over $K$:

$$
x=u^2x'+r,
\qquad
y=u^3y'+u^2sx'+t,
$$

where $u\in K^\times$ and $r,s,t\in K$.

If $\operatorname{char}K\ne2,3$, completing the square and translating $x$ simplify every equation. [Short Weierstrass form](../../../../../../short-weierstrass-form.md) is

$$
y^2=x^3+Ax+B,
\qquad
\Delta=-16(4A^3+27B^2)\ne0.
$$

Two short equations are $K$-isomorphic precisely when, for some $u\in K^\times$,

$$
A'=u^4A,
\qquad
B'=u^6B;
$$

the isomorphism from the first curve to the second is $(x,y)\mapsto(u^2x,u^3y)$.
