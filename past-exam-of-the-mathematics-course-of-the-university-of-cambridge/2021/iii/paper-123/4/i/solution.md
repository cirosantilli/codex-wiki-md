<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose that $L/\mathbb Q$ were a [Finite Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md) with [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) $S_3$ and unramified away from $7$. Its quadratic subfield is the fixed field of the alternating subgroup $A_3$. A quadratic field ramified only at $7$ must be $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$: the classification by [fundamental discriminants](../../../../../../fundamental-discriminant.md) shows that $-7$ is the only nontrivial quadratic discriminant supported at $7$. In particular, $7$ ramifies in this quadratic subfield.

Because $7$ does not divide $|S_3|=6$, all ramification at $7$ is [tame ramification](../../../../../../tamely-ramified-extension.md). Its [inertia group](../../../../../../inertia-group.md) is therefore cyclic. Its image in $S_3/A_3\simeq C_2$ is nontrivial because the quadratic subfield is ramified, so the inertia group is generated by a transposition and has order two; it cannot have order six because it is cyclic.

The [discriminant exponent of a tame Galois extension](../../../../../../discriminant-exponent-of-a-tame-galois-extension.md) is therefore

$$
v_7(d_L)=[L:\mathbb Q]\left(1-\frac1e\right)
=6\left(1-\frac12\right)=3.
$$

There is no other finite ramification, so $|d_L|=7^3=343$.

On the other hand, the [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) implies the [Minkowski lower bound for a number-field discriminant](../../../../../../minkowski-lower-bound-for-a-number-field-discriminant.md)

$$
|d_L|\geq
\left(\frac\pi4\right)^{2r_2}
\left(\frac{6^6}{6!}\right)^2.
$$

Since $0\leq r_2\leq3$, the right side is smallest at $r_2=3$ and is greater than $980$. This contradicts $|d_L|=343$, so no such extension exists.
