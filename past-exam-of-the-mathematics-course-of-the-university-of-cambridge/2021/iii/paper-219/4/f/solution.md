<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

It holds generally whenever the posterior is well defined and the expectations exist. Bayes' theorem gives

$$
\log\frac{p(\theta\mid y)}{\pi(\theta)}
=\log L(\theta)-\log Z.
$$

Taking posterior expectations yields

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\{p(\theta\mid y)\Vert\pi\}
=\mathbb E_{\theta\mid y}\log L(\theta)-\log Z,
$$

which rearranges to the claimed evidence decomposition.
