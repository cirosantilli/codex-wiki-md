<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On $\mathbb H\setminus(A\cup C)$, the nonnegative [harmonic function](../../../../../../../harmonic-function.md) $h_A+h_C$ dominates the boundary data $\operatorname{Im}z$ on $A\cup C$: at a point of $A$, for example, $h_A(z)=\operatorname{Im}z$ and $h_C(z)\geq0$. The [maximum principle for harmonic functions](../../../../../../../maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions.md), or equivalently [Brownian motion](../../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) stopped on the union, gives

$$
h_{A\cup C}(z)\leq h_A(z)+h_C(z).
$$

Comparing the coefficients at infinity proves

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{hcap}(A\cup C)
\leq\operatorname{hcap}(A)+\operatorname{hcap}(C)}.
$$
