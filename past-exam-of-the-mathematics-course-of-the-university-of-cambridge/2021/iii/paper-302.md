## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 302](paper-302.md)

<h3 id="1/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/i)

The squared root lengths and inner product are

$$
|\alpha_1|^2=1,\qquad |\alpha_2|^2=3,\qquad
\alpha_1\mathbin\cdot\alpha_2=-\frac32.
$$

With $A_{ij}=2\alpha_i\mathbin\cdot\alpha_j/|\alpha_j|^2$, the [Cartan matrix](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#cartan-matrix) is

$$
\boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-3&2\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

This is the Cartan matrix of the [G2 root system](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#g2-root-system), with $\alpha_1$ short and $\alpha_2$ long.

<h3 id="1/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/ii)

The [root string](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#root-string) through $\alpha_1$ in the $\alpha_2$ direction contains $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, so $\alpha_1$ can be raised once by $\alpha_2$. The string through $\alpha_2$ in the $\alpha_1$ direction contains

$$
\alpha_2,\quad\alpha_2+\alpha_1,\quad
\alpha_2+2\alpha_1,\quad\alpha_2+3\alpha_1,
$$

so $\alpha_2$ can be raised three times by $\alpha_1$.

<h3 id="1/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/iii)

The [Weyl reflection](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-reflection) in the hyperplane perpendicular to $\alpha$ sends a weight $\mu$ to

$$
\boxed{s_\alpha(\mu)=
\mu-\frac{2\mu\mathbin\cdot\alpha}{\alpha\mathbin\cdot\alpha}\alpha}.
$$

<h3 id="1/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/iv)

The positive roots generated by the two simple roots are

$$
\alpha_1,\quad\alpha_2,\quad\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\quad
2\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\quad3\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\quad
3\alpha_1+2\alpha_2.
$$

The [Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra](../../../lie-algebra.md#adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra) has one weight space for each positive and negative root and a rank-two zero-weight space. Its nonzero weights are therefore

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{root}&\text{coordinate}\\ \hline
\alpha_1&(1,0)\\
\alpha_1+\alpha_2&(-\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\\
2\alpha_1+\alpha_2&(\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\\
\alpha_2&(-\tfrac32,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\\
3\alpha_1+\alpha_2&(\tfrac32,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\\
3\alpha_1+2\alpha_2&(0,\sqrt3)
\end{array}
$$

together with their negatives. Thus the diagram consists of a hexagon of six long roots, a hexagon of six short roots, and the origin with multiplicity two. The representation has dimension $12+2=14$.

<h3 id="1/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#1/v)

The [fundamental weights](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#fundamental-weight) satisfy

$$
\frac{2\mu_i\mathbin\cdot\alpha_j}{|\alpha_j|^2}=\delta_{ij}.
$$

Solving these equations gives

$$
\boxed{\mu_1=\left(\frac12,\frac{\sqrt3}2\right)
=2\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\qquad
\mu_2=(0,\sqrt3)=3\alpha_1+2\alpha_2}.
$$

<h3 id="1/vi">vi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/vi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#1/vi)

Starting from the highest weight $\mu_1=2\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, lowering with the simple-root operators and closing under the [Weyl group](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weyl-group) gives

$$
\boxed{0,\quad
\pm\alpha_1,\quad
\pm(\alpha_1+\alpha_2),\quad
\pm(2\alpha_1+\alpha_2)}.
$$

Every weight has multiplicity one. The diagram is the hexagon of short roots with one central weight, so this is the seven-dimensional fundamental irreducible representation of $G_2$.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 302](paper-302.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

The identity and inverse axioms give

$$
\boxed{\varphi^r(x,0)=x^r,\qquad
\varphi^r(0,x)=x^r,\qquad
\varphi^r(\bar x,x)=0}.
$$

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

Setting either argument to zero in the expansion and comparing coefficients gives

$$
\boxed{F^r=0,\qquad A=B=1,\qquad
D^r{}_{st}=E^r{}_{st}=0}.
$$

Thus, through quadratic order,

$$
\boxed{\varphi^r(x,y)=x^r+y^r+C^r{}_{st}x^sy^t+O(3).}
$$

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

Write $\bar x^r=-x^r+q^r{}_{st}x^sx^t+O(x^3)$. Substitution into $\varphi(\bar x,x)=0$ gives

$$
q^r{}_{st}x^sx^t=C^r{}_{st}x^sx^t,
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{\bar x^r=-x^r+C^r{}_{st}x^sx^t+O(x^3)}.
$$

Only the symmetric part of $C^r{}_{st}$ contributes to this expression.

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

Insert the expansions from parts ii and iii successively into  
$g(w)=g(x)^{-1}g(y)^{-1}g(x)g(y)$. All constant and linear terms cancel, leaving

$$
\boxed{w^r=(C^r{}_{st}-C^r{}_{ts})x^sy^t+O(3)}.
$$

The antisymmetric part of the local multiplication law is therefore the infinitesimal [group commutator](../../../group.md#group-commutator).

<h3 id="2/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#2/v)

Since $z+dz=\varphi(z,\theta)$,

$$
dz^r=
\left.\frac{\partial\varphi^r(z,\theta)}{\partial\theta^a}
\right|_{\theta=0}\theta^a+O(\theta^2).
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{dz^r=\mu_a{}^r(z)\theta^a}.
$$

<h3 id="2/vi">vi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/vi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#2/vi)

The infinitesimal change $dy$ can be written

$$
dy^s=\mu_d{}^s(y)\theta^d,
\qquad
\theta^d=\lambda_s{}^d(y)dy^s.
$$

Right multiplication of $g(y)$ by $g(\theta)$ is also right multiplication of $g(z)=g(x)g(y)$ by the same element. Part v therefore gives

$$
dz^r=\mu_d{}^r(z)\lambda_s{}^d(y)dy^s,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial z^r}{\partial y^s}
=\mu_d{}^r(z)\lambda_s{}^d(y)}.
$$

<h3 id="2/vii">vii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/vii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](#2/vii)

Applying $T_a(y)=\mu_a{}^s(y)\partial/\partial y^s$ to a function of $z$ and using part vi,

$$
\mu_a{}^s(y)\frac{\partial z^r}{\partial y^s}
=\mu_a{}^s(y)\lambda_s{}^d(y)\mu_d{}^r(z)
=\mu_a{}^r(z).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{T_a(y)=T_a(z)}.
$$

These are the same [left-invariant vector fields](../../../lie-theory.md#left-invariant-vector-field) in different coordinates.

<h3 id="2/viii">viii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/viii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Viii](#2/viii)

Differentiate the identity in part vi with respect to $y^t$, multiply by $\mu_a{}^s(y)\mu_b{}^t(y)$, and use $\lambda\mu=I$. The product rule gives

$$
\boxed{
\mu_a{}^s\mu_b{}^t\frac{\partial^2z^r}{\partial y^s\partial y^t}
=\mu_a{}^s[T_b\lambda_s{}^c]\mu_c{}^r(z)
+T_b(z)\mu_a{}^r(z)}.
$$

<h3 id="2/ix">ix</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ix/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ix](#2/ix)

Differentiate $\lambda_s{}^c\mu_a{}^s=\delta_a{}^c$ to obtain

$$
\mu_a{}^s(T_b\lambda_s{}^c)
=-(T_b\mu_a{}^r)\lambda_r{}^c.
$$

Multiplying part viii by $\lambda_r{}^c(z)$ consequently gives

$$
\boxed{
\mu_a{}^s\mu_b{}^t
\frac{\partial^2z^r}{\partial y^s\partial y^t}\lambda_r{}^c(z)
=-[T_b(y)\mu_a{}^r(y)]\lambda_r{}^c(y)
+[T_b(z)\mu_a{}^r(z)]\lambda_r{}^c(z)}.
$$

<h3 id="2/x">x</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/x/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [X](#2/x)

Antisymmetrize part ix in $a,b$. The left side vanishes because the second derivative is symmetric in $s,t$. Therefore

$$
f^c{}_{ab}(y)=f^c{}_{ab}(z),
\qquad
f^c{}_{ab}
=[T_a\mu_b{}^r-T_b\mu_a{}^r]\lambda_r{}^c.
$$

Since multiplication by an arbitrary $g(x)$ moves $y$ to any $z$ in the connected coordinate neighborhood, the $f^c{}_{ab}$ are constants. They are the [structure constants](../../../algebra.md#structure-constant).

<h3 id="2/xi">xi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/xi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Xi](#2/xi)

Directly commuting the vector fields gives

$$
[T_a,T_b]
=(\mu_a{}^s\partial_s\mu_b{}^r
-\mu_b{}^s\partial_s\mu_a{}^r)\partial_r.
$$

Using the definition in part x and $\lambda\mu=I$ yields

$$
\boxed{[T_a,T_b]=f^c{}_{ab}T_c}.
$$

<h3 id="2/xii">xii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/xii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Xii](#2/xii)

In exponential coordinates, the [Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula](../../../linear-operator-theory.md#baker-campbell-hausdorff-formula) gives

$$
g(x)g(y)
=\exp\left\{(x^c+y^c)T_c
+\frac12x^ay^b[T_a,T_b]+O(3)\right\}.
$$

Comparison with $\varphi^c(x,y)=x^c+y^c+C^c{}_{ab}x^ay^b+O(3)$ gives

$$
\boxed{f^c{}_{ab}=C^c{}_{ab}-C^c{}_{ba}
=2C^c{}_{[ab]}}.
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 302](paper-302.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

On the branch through the identity,

$$
f(\theta)=\sqrt2\cos(\theta/2),\qquad
\frac{\sin\theta}{2f(\theta)}
=\frac1{\sqrt2}\sin(\theta/2).
$$

Direct calculation gives $M^\dagger M=I$ and $\det M=1$. Thus these matrices lie in

$$
\boxed{SU(2)\subset U(2),\qquad SU(2)\subset SL(2,\mathbb C)},
$$

providing the three requested matrix groups.

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

A vector transforms as $x'^a=R^a{}_bx^b$. The matrix fixes $n$, preserves lengths and orientation, and rotates the perpendicular plane through $\theta$. It is a rotation by angle $\theta$ about the axis $n$, with orientation fixed by the epsilon-term convention.

<h3 id="3/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/iii)

Since

$$
(x\mathbin\cdot\sigma)^2=|x|^2I,\qquad
\det(x\mathbin\cdot\sigma)=-|x|^2,
$$

conjugation by $A\in SU(2)$ preserves Hermiticity, tracelessness, and the determinant. Hence $A(x\mathbin\cdot\sigma)A^\dagger=x'\mathbin\cdot\sigma$ with $|x'|=|x|$. Thus $\boxed{G=SU(2)}$.

<h3 id="3/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/iv)

From $A\sigma_bA^\dagger=R^a{}_b\sigma_a$ and  
$\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_a\sigma_b)=2\delta_{ab}$,

$$
\boxed{R^a{}_b=\frac12\operatorname{tr}
(\sigma_aA\sigma_bA^\dagger)}.
$$

<h3 id="3/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#3/v)

The matrix is

$$
M=\cos\frac\theta2I-i\sin\frac\theta2
\frac{\sigma_2+\sigma_3}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Its image is the rotation through $\theta$ about

$$
\boxed{n=(0,1,1)/\sqrt2}.
$$

<h3 id="3/vi">vi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/vi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#3/vi)

The other element is $\boxed{-M}$. Conjugation by $M$ and $-M$ is identical, so the homomorphism $SU(2)\to SO(3)$ has kernel $\{\pm I\}$ and is the [Spin group](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#spin-group) double cover.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 302](paper-302.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Use the convention

$$
\boxed{[g_1,g_2]=g_1^{-1}g_2^{-1}g_1g_2}
$$

for the [group commutator](../../../group.md#group-commutator).

<h3 id="4/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#4/i)

Invariance for every $x$ requires

$$
\boxed{\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta},
\qquad
\eta_{\rho\sigma}\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\Lambda^\sigma{}_\nu
=\eta_{\mu\nu}.
$$

**Thus $G=O(1,3)$, the [Lorentz group](../../../special-relativity.md#lorentz-group); its identity component is $SO^+(1,3)$.**

<h3 id="4/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#4/ii)

At first order, $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta$ gives  
$\omega^T\eta+\eta\omega=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\omega_{\mu\nu}
=\eta_{\mu\rho}\omega^\rho{}_\nu
=-\omega_{\nu\mu}}.
$$

<h3 id="4/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#4/iii)

Using $\Lambda_a^{-1}=I-\omega_a+\omega_a^2+O(\omega_a^3)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_c
&=\Lambda_2^{-1}\Lambda_1^{-1}\Lambda_2\Lambda_1\\
&=I+\omega_2\omega_1-\omega_1\omega_2
+O(\omega_1^2\omega_2,\omega_1\omega_2^2,\omega_1^3,\omega_2^3).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $(\omega_c)^\mu{}_\nu
=(\omega_2)^\mu{}_\rho(\omega_1)^\rho{}_\nu
-(\omega_1)^\mu{}_\rho(\omega_2)^\rho{}_\nu$.

<h3 id="4/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#4/iv)

Comparison of $U[\Lambda_c]$ with the operator group commutator gives the [Lorentz algebra](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#lorentz-algebra)

$$
\boxed{
[M^{\mu\nu},M^{\rho\sigma}]
=\eta^{\nu\rho}M^{\mu\sigma}
-\eta^{\mu\rho}M^{\nu\sigma}
-\eta^{\nu\sigma}M^{\mu\rho}
+\eta^{\mu\sigma}M^{\nu\rho}}.
$$

There is no factor of $i$ because the generators are anti-Hermitian.

<h3 id="4/v">v</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/v/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#4/v)

Substituting $J_m=\epsilon_{mij}M^{ij}/2$ and $K_i=M^{0i}$ yields

$$
\boxed{
[K_i,K_j]=-\epsilon_{ijk}J_k,\qquad
[J_i,K_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}K_k,\qquad
[J_i,J_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}J_k}.
$$

<h3 id="4/vi">vi</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/vi/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](#4/vi)

For $J_i^\pm=(J_i\pm iK_i)/2$,

$$
\boxed{
[J_i^+,J_j^+]=\epsilon_{ijk}J_k^+,\qquad
[J_i^-,J_j^-]=\epsilon_{ijk}J_k^-,\qquad
[J_i^+,J_j^-]=0}.
$$

<h3 id="4/vii">vii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/vii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](#4/vii)

The two commuting triples each generate a copy of the complexified [SU(2) Lie algebra](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#su-2-lie-algebra). Hence the [chiral decomposition of the complex Lorentz algebra](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#chiral-decomposition-of-the-complex-lorentz-algebra) is

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak{so}(1,3)_\mathbb C
\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)\oplus
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)}.
$$

In physicists' compact notation this is  
$\mathfrak{su}(2)_+\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_-$; the real Lorentz algebra relates the two factors by complex conjugation.
