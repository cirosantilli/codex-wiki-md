<h1 id="1/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The exact initial-mass boundaries depend on metallicity, convective overshooting, rotation, and mass loss, but stars near the upper intermediate-mass range, roughly $6.5$--$9M_\odot$, can become [Super-AGB stars](../../../../../../super-agb-star.md). They ignite carbon but do not immediately ignite neon hydrostatically throughout the core.

Their final fate is set by competition between shell-driven core growth and envelope loss. If a [stellar wind](../../../../../../stellar-wind.md) removes the envelope first, the remnant is an oxygen-neon, or oxygen-neon-magnesium, [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md). If the degenerate core grows toward the [Chandrasekhar mass](../../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md), electron captures on magnesium and neon reduce the electron pressure and can trigger an [electron-capture supernova](../../../../../../electron-capture-supernova.md), leaving a [neutron star](../../../../../../neutron-star.md). Slightly higher-mass stars can ignite further fuels and proceed to ordinary iron-core collapse.
