<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Hydrogen-shell burning deposits helium onto a geometrically thin helium layer. Once helium ignites, the strong temperature dependence of the [Triple-alpha process](../../../../../../triple-alpha-process.md) and the shell's initially weak expansion response produce the [Härm–Schwarzschild instability](../../../../../../harm-schwarzschild-instability.md). The resulting [AGB thermal pulse](../../../../../../thermal-pulse-of-an-asymptotic-giant-branch-star.md) drives a short-lived intershell convection zone, expands the layers above it, and temporarily extinguishes the hydrogen-burning shell before the cycle restarts.

Toward the upper end of the mass range, neutrino cooling keeps the centre cooler than an off-centre shell, so [carbon burning](../../../../../../carbon-burning.md) can ignite off-centre under partial [electron degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../electron-degeneracy-pressure.md). Repeated flashes and an inward-moving carbon flame consume most carbon and leave a degenerate [oxygen-neon-magnesium core](../../../../../../oxygen-neon-magnesium-core.md), surrounded by helium- and hydrogen-burning shells.
