<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For static sources, the spatial Fourier transform of a massive scalar propagator is

$$
\int\frac{d^3\mathbf q}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}}{\mathbf q^2+M_X^2}
=\frac{e^{-M_Xr}}{4\pi r}.
$$

Thus massive-vector exchange likewise produces a [Yukawa potential](../../../../../../yukawa-potential.md), $V(r)\propto e^{-M_Xr}/r$, with range $M_X^{-1}$ instead of the infinite range of the Coulomb potential.

[Quantum chromodynamics](../../../../../../quantum-chromodynamics.md) supplies the requested massless-vector counterexample. Its [gluons](../../../../../../gluon.md) are massless, but [confinement](../../../../../../confinement.md) and the QCD [mass gap](../../../../../../mass-gap.md) prevent a long-range color force between color-singlet asymptotic states. The short range is generated by strong dynamics rather than by a vector-boson mass.
