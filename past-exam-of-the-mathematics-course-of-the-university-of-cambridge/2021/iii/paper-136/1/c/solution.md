<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For odd $p$ there is a decomposition

$$
\mathbb Q_p^\times
\cong p^{\mathbb Z}\times\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb Z_p)
$$

into the valuation factor, the [Teichmuller representative](../../../../../../teichmuller-representative.md) factor, and the group of [principal units](../../../../../../principal-unit.md). If $x$ is a $((p-1)m)$th power for every $m$ coprime to $p$, its valuation is divisible by every such $(p-1)m$, and is therefore zero. Its residue in $\mathbb F_p^\times$ is a $(p-1)$st power, hence is one. Thus $x\in1+p\mathbb Z_p$.

Conversely, exponentiation by any integer $n$ coprime to $p$ is an automorphism of $1+p\mathbb Z_p$. This follows either from the [principal-unit logarithm](../../../../../../principal-unit-logarithm.md), under which it becomes multiplication by $n$, or by applying the Hensel lemma to $Y^n-x$. Since $(p-1)m$ is coprime to $p$, every $x\in1+p\mathbb Z_p$ has a $((p-1)m)$th root for every allowed $m$.
