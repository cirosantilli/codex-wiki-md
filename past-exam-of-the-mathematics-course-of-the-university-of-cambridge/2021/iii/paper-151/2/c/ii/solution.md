<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\pi$ omits a prime $p$, then $A_\pi\subseteq A_{\neg p}\subseteq\mathbb Z_p$ by part i. The reductions

$$
\mathbb Z_p\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z
$$

separate its nonzero elements, so their restrictions separate the elements of $A_\pi$. Thus $A_\pi$ is [residually finite](../../../../../../../residually-finite-group.md). If $\pi$ contains every prime, then $A_\pi=\mathbb Q$, which has no nontrivial finite quotient because it is a [divisible group](../../../../../../../divisible-group.md).
