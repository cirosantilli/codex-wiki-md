<h1 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

If $g=xhx^{-1}$ in $G$, applying each [projection map](../../../../../../../projection-map.md) gives $p_j(g)=p_j(x)p_j(h)p_j(x)^{-1}$ in $G_j$.

Conversely, suppose $p_j(g)$ and $p_j(h)$ are conjugate for every $j$. Define the nonempty finite set

$$
X_j=\{x_j\in G_j:x_jp_j(h)x_j^{-1}=p_j(g)\}.
$$

Every transition map $G_j\to G_i$ carries $X_j$ into $X_i$, so the $X_j$ form an [inverse system](../../../../../../../inverse-system.md). By the [nonemptiness theorem for inverse limits of finite sets](../../../../../../../nonemptiness-theorem-for-inverse-limits-of-finite-sets.md), there is a compatible tuple $(x_j)\in\varprojlim_jX_j\subseteq G$. Coordinatewise equality then gives $xhx^{-1}=g$. This proves the [finite-quotient criterion for conjugacy in a profinite group](../../../../../../../finite-quotient-criterion-for-conjugacy-in-a-profinite-group.md).
