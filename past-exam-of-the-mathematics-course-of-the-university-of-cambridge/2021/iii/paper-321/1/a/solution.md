<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Inside the [innermost stable circular orbit](../../../../../../innermost-stable-circular-orbit.md), nearly circular motion is unstable and gas enters the [plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk](../../../../../../plunging-region-of-a-black-hole-accretion-disk.md). Its inflow time becomes shorter than the time on which internal stress can communicate [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) back to the disk, motivating the [zero-torque inner boundary condition](../../../../../../zero-torque-inner-boundary-condition.md) at $r_{\rm ISCO}$.

In a [steady state](../../../../../../steady-state.md), the given diffusion equation implies

$$
r^{1/2}\frac d{dr}\left(r^{1/2}\bar\nu\Sigma\right)=C.
$$

The constant mass supply fixes $C=\dot M/(6\pi)$, and a second integration gives

$$
\bar\nu\Sigma=\frac{\dot M}{3\pi}
\left[1-C_0r^{-1/2}\right].
$$

Zero torque means $\bar\nu\Sigma=0$ at $r=r_{\rm ISCO}$, so $C_0=r_{\rm ISCO}^{1/2}$. Thus the [Keplerian accretion disk](../../../../../../keplerian-accretion-disk.md) relation is

$$
\boxed{\bar\nu\Sigma=\dot M f(r)},
\qquad
\boxed{f(r)=\frac1{3\pi}
\left[1-\left(\frac{r_{\rm ISCO}}r\right)^{1/2}\right]}.
$$
