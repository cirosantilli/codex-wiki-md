<h1 id="4/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$. Primes of $B_{\mathfrak p}=S^{-1}B$ correspond to primes $\mathfrak q$ of $B$ whose contractions are contained in $\mathfrak p$. By going up, each such $\mathfrak q$ is contained in a prime lying over $\mathfrak p$, and this prime is uniquely $\mathfrak p'$. Hence $\mathfrak p'B_{\mathfrak p}$ is the unique maximal ideal of $B_{\mathfrak p}$.

Localizing this already local ring at its unique maximal ideal changes nothing, so

$$
\boxed{B_{\mathfrak p'}=B_{\mathfrak p}.}
$$

Finally, localization preserves integral extensions; therefore $B_{\mathfrak p'}=B_{\mathfrak p}$ is integral over $A_{\mathfrak p}$.
