<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $x\in A$ is a unit in $B$, then $x^{-1}$ is integral over $A$:

$$
(x^{-1})^n+a_1(x^{-1})^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n=0.
$$

Multiplication by $x^{n-1}$ expresses $x^{-1}$ as an element of $A$, so $x$ is a unit in $A$.

Use the characterization $a\in J(A)$ exactly when $1-ra$ is a unit for every $r\in A$. If $a\in J(B)\cap A$, then $1-ra$ is a unit in $B$ and hence in $A$, proving $a\in J(A)$. Conversely, if $a\in J(A)$, every maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $B$ contracts under the integral extension to a maximal ideal of $A$, which contains $a$. Thus every $\mathfrak n$ contains $a$, so $a\in J(B)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{J(A)=J(B)\cap A.}
$$
