<h1 id="5/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

When every $k_i=1$, cancel common factors to write

$$
P(M,t)=\frac{g(t)}{(1-t)^d},
\qquad g(1)\ne0,
$$

where $d$ is the pole order at $t=1$. Since

$$
(1-t)^{-d}=\sum_{n\geq0}\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}t^n,
$$

the coefficient of $t^n$ in $P(M,t)$ is, for all sufficiently large $n$, a fixed linear combination of shifted binomial polynomials. It is therefore a polynomial in $n$ of degree exactly $d-1$ unless $P=0$, in which case its degree is $-1$.
