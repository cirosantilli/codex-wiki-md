<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The measured [Hubble time](../../../../../../hubble-time.md) would give the Einstein-de Sitter age

$$
t_0=\frac23(14\ {\rm Gyr})\simeq9.3\ {\rm Gyr},
$$

which is less than the measured $12\ {\rm Gyr}$ age of stars that must themselves be younger than the universe. The measurements are therefore incompatible with an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md). For the stated spatially flat [Lambda-CDM model](../../../../../../lambda-cdm-model.md), direct evaluation of the preceding integral gives

$$
H_0t_0=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{0.7}}\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{\frac{0.7}{0.3}}\simeq0.964,
$$

and hence $t_0\simeq13.5\ {\rm Gyr}$. The period of [accelerating expansion](../../../../../../accelerating-expansion-of-the-universe.md) caused by the [cosmological constant](../../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) therefore allows an age consistent with the stellar lower bound.
