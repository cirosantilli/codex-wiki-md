<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The integral of an [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) over the closed curve $S^1$ is zero by [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md), so the map is well defined on [de Rham cohomology](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology.md). It is surjective because the angular form $d\theta$ has integral $2\pi$. If a closed one-form $\alpha$ has zero integral, define

$$
F(e^{i\theta})=\int_0^\theta\alpha.
$$

The zero period makes this definition $2\pi$-periodic, and $dF=\alpha$. Thus the kernel is zero and

$$
H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)\xrightarrow{\int_{S^1}}\mathbb R
$$

is an isomorphism.
