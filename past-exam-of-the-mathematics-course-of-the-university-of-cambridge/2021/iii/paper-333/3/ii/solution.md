<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Linearization about rest replaces the [material derivative](../../../../../../material-derivative.md) by $\partial_t$, so the [quasi-geostrophic potential-vorticity equation](../../../../../../quasi-geostrophic-potential-vorticity-equation.md) becomes

$$
\partial_t\left(
\psi_{xx}+\psi_{yy}+\frac{f_0^2}{N^2}\psi_{zz}
\right)+\beta\psi_x=0.
$$

Insert the [separation of variables](../../../../../../separation-of-variables.md) $\psi=\phi(x,y,t)P(z)$ and impose

$$
P''=-\frac{N^2}{c^2}P.
$$

The horizontal amplitude then satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\partial_t\left(
\nabla_h^2\phi-\frac{f_0^2}{c^2}\phi
\right)+\beta\phi_x=0
}.
$$

The [Linear Rossby-wave equation](../../../../../../linear-rossby-wave-equation.md) therefore governs each vertical normal mode, whose [Rossby deformation radius](../../../../../../rossby-deformation-radius.md) is $c/f_0$.
