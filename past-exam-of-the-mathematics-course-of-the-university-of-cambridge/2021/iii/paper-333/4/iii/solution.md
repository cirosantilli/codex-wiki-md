<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $k>0$, choose the upward-radiating root

$$
m=\sqrt{\frac{N^2}{U^2}-k^2}.
$$

The boundary condition gives a vertical-velocity amplitude of magnitude $Uk h_0$. Incompressibility gives $u'=-(m/k)w'$, so horizontal averaging over one wavelength yields the [wave momentum flux](../../../../../../wave-momentum-flux.md)

$$
\boxed{
\overline{u'w'}
=-\frac12mkU^2h_0^2
=-\frac12kU^2h_0^2
\sqrt{\frac{N^2}{U^2}-k^2}
}.
$$

If the wave is completely absorbed in a remote dissipation layer, this negative flux increases to zero across the layer. The integrated force on the mean flow is therefore

$$
\int_{\mathrm{layer}}-\partial_z\overline{u'w'}\,dz
=\overline{u'w'}_{\mathrm{below}}
=-\frac12mkU^2h_0^2.
$$

It acts opposite to the positive basic flow.
