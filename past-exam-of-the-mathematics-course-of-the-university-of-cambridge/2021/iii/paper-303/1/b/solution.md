<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At the [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md), the central minimum $m=0$ and two nonzero minima $m=\pm m_0$ are degenerate, with barriers between them. Writing $x=m_0^2>0$, stationarity and equal free energies give

$$
a_2+2a_4x+3a_6x^2=0,
\qquad
a_2+a_4x+a_6x^2=0.
$$

Their difference gives $a_4x+2a_6x^2=0$, so

$$
m_0^2=-\frac{a_4}{2a_6},
\qquad
\boxed{a_2=\frac{a_4^2}{4a_6}}.
$$

The magnitude of the [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) therefore jumps from zero to

$$
\boxed{|m_0|=\sqrt{-\frac{a_4}{2a_6}}}.
$$

The two possible signs are related by the model's spin-reversal symmetry.
