<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put

$$
R=k[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw).
$$

Since

$$
R/(x,z)\cong k[y,w]
$$

is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md), $(x,z)$ is a [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md). Moreover $\dim R=3$ and $\dim R/(x,z)=2$, so its [height of an ideal](../../../../../../height-of-an-ideal.md) is one. Therefore $D=V(x,z)$ is a [prime Weil divisor](../../../../../../prime-weil-divisor.md).

Localizing at $x$ eliminates $y$ and gives

$$
R_x\cong k[x,z,w,x^{-1}],
$$

a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md). The [Nagata theorem for divisor class groups](../../../../../../nagata-theorem-for-divisor-class-groups.md) says that $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$ is generated by the height-one primes containing $x$. Since

$$
R/(x)\cong k[y,z,w]/(zw),
$$

these are $D=V(x,z)$ and $E=V(x,w)$. Both occur with multiplicity one in the [principal divisor](../../../../../../principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve.md)

$$
\operatorname{div}(x)=D+E.
$$

The units of $R_x\cong k[z,w][x,x^{-1}]$ are exactly $c x^n$ with $c\in k^*$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$. Consequently the only relation supplied by localization is $[D]+[E]=0$, and

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
\cong\frac{\mathbb Z[D]\oplus\mathbb Z[E]}{\mathbb Z([D]+[E])}
\cong\mathbb Z[D].
$$

This is the [divisor class group of the three-dimensional affine quadric cone](../../../../../../divisor-class-group-of-the-three-dimensional-affine-quadric-cone.md).
