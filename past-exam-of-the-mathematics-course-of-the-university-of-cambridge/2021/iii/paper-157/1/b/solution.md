<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose first that $|E|\geq3$. Complete invariance implies that every iterate maps $\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus E$ into itself. Hence the family $\{f^n\}$ omits the same three points of $E$ on this open set. By [Montel theorem](../../../../../../montel-s-theorem.md),

$$
\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus E\subseteq F(f),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{J(f)\subseteq E}.
$$

If $E$ consists of one or two points, complete invariance makes $f$ permute those points and makes every preimage of them remain in $E$. Some iterate fixes each point and is totally ramified there. In a local coordinate it therefore has the form $w\mapsto aw^m+O(w^{m+1})$ with $m\geq2$, so the point is superattracting for that iterate and belongs to the [Fatou set](../../../../../../fatou-set.md). This proves the [completely invariant closed set of a rational map](../../../../../../completely-invariant-closed-set-of-a-rational-map.md) dichotomy.
