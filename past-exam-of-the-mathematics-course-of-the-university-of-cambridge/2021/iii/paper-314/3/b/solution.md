<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the stated [magnetic vector potential](../../../../../../magnetic-vector-potential.md), part a gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\nabla\beta\times\nabla\phi
+(L\alpha)\nabla\phi}.
$$

Applying the same identity once more and using the [Ampère-Maxwell equation](../../../../../../ampere-s-circuital-law.md) in the magnetostatic limit gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbf J=\frac1{\mu_0}
\left[\nabla(L\alpha)\times\nabla\phi
+(L\beta)\nabla\phi\right]}.
$$

Expanding the [Lorentz force density](../../../../../../lorentz-force-density.md) $\mathbf F_m=\mathbf J\times\mathbf B$, using $\nabla\phi=\mathbf e_\phi/r$ and the vector triple-product identity, separates its poloidal and azimuthal parts:

$$
\boxed{r^2\mu_0\mathbf F_m
=(L\beta)\nabla\beta-(L\alpha)\nabla(L\alpha)
+f\mathbf e_\phi},
$$

where

$$
\boxed{f=[\nabla(L\alpha)\times\nabla\beta]\cdot\mathbf e_\phi}.
$$
