<h1 id="4/2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For every $h\in L^2[0,1]$, the [continuous linear functional](../../../../../../../continuous-linear-functional.md) induced by the [inner product](../../../../../../../inner-product.md) gives

$$
\langle U,h\rangle
=\xi_1\langle\varphi_1,h\rangle
+\xi_2\langle\varphi_2,h\rangle.
$$

This is a [normal random variable](../../../../../../../normal-distribution.md) because it is a [linear combination of independent normal random variables](../../../../../../../linear-combination-of-independent-normal-random-variables.md). Hence the law of $U$ is a [Gaussian measure](../../../../../../../gaussian-measure.md). Its mean is zero, and independence together with $\operatorname{Var}(\xi_i)=1/2$ gives

$$
\mathbb E[\langle U,h\rangle\langle U,g\rangle]
=\frac12\langle\varphi_1,h\rangle\langle\varphi_1,g\rangle
+\frac12\langle\varphi_2,h\rangle\langle\varphi_2,g\rangle.
$$

Thus its [covariance operator of a Gaussian measure](../../../../../../../covariance-operator-of-a-gaussian-measure.md) is

$$
\boxed{
Ch=\frac12\langle\varphi_1,h\rangle\varphi_1
+\frac12\langle\varphi_2,h\rangle\varphi_2}.
$$
