<h1 id="1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A problem is [well posed in the sense of Hadamard](../../../../../../well-posed-problem.md) when a solution exists for every admissible datum, is unique, and depends [continuously](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) on the datum. It is ill posed if any one of these three properties fails.

For the [inverse problem](../../../../../../inverse-problem-split.md) $Au=f$, a [regularization of an inverse problem](../../../../../../regularization-of-an-inverse-problem.md) is a family of bounded maps $R_\alpha:Y\to X$ that approximate the generally unbounded [Moore–Penrose inverse of an operator](../../../../../../moore-penrose-inverse-of-an-operator.md) $A^\dagger$. It is a [convergent regularization of an inverse problem](../../../../../../convergent-regularization-of-an-inverse-problem.md) if there is a parameter rule $\alpha=\alpha(\delta,f^\delta)$ such that

$$
\alpha(\delta,f^\delta)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\sup_{\|f^\delta-f\|\leq\delta}
\|R_{\alpha(\delta,f^\delta)}f^\delta-A^\dagger f\|
\longrightarrow0
$$

as $\delta\downarrow0$ for every $f\in\mathcal D(A^\dagger)$.
