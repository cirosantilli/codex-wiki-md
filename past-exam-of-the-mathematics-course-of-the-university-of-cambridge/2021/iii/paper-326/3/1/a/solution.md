↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [Bayesian inverse problem](../../../../../../../bayesian-inverse-problem.md) consists of a [prior distribution](../../../../../../../prior-probability.md) $\mu_0$ on the unknown $u\in X$, a reference measure on the data space $Y$, and a jointly [measurable](../../../../../../../measurable-function.md) likelihood $L(u;y)$ such that $L(u;\mathord\cdot)$ is a [probability density function](../../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) for $\mu_0$-almost every $u$. For observed data $y$, [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../../../bayes-theorem.md) defines the [posterior distribution](../../../../../../../bayesian-posterior.md) by

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\mu^y}{d\mu_0}(u)
=\frac{L(u;y)}{Z(y)},
\qquad
Z(y)=\int_XL(u;y)\,d\mu_0(u)},
$$

provided $0<Z(y)<\infty$.
