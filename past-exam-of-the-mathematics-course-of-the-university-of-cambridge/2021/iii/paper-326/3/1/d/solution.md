↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The following four assumptions are sufficient, with statements understood for $\mu_0$-almost every $u$ and every $y$:

- $L(u;\mathord\cdot)$ is a strictly positive [probability density function](../../../../../../../probability-density-function.md) on the data space.
- $L(\mathord\cdot;y)\in L^1(X,\mu_0)$.
- There is one $h\in L^1(X,\mu_0)$ such that $L(u;y)\leq h(u)$ for all $y$.
- For each relevant $u$, the map $y\mapsto L(u;y)$ is [continuous](../../../../../../../continuous-function.md).

The first two assumptions give $0<Z(y)<\infty$. The last two allow the [dominated convergence theorem](../../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) to prove $L^1(\mu_0)$ continuity of the normalized posterior density, which is equivalent to continuity in [total variation distance](../../../../../../../total-variation-distance.md).
