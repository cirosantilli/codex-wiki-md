<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At distance $r$, isotropic luminosity $L$ produces radiative acceleration $a_{\rm rad}=\kappa L/(4\pi r^2c)$ in material of [opacity](../../../../../../opacity.md) $\kappa$. In fully ionized hydrogen, one [Electron](../../../../../../electron.md) supplies a [Thomson scattering](../../../../../../thomson-scattering.md) cross-section $\sigma_T$ for approximately one [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) mass of material, so $\kappa=\sigma_T/m_p$. Balancing radiation against the [Newtonian gravitational field](../../../../../../newtonian-gravitational-field.md) of the black hole gives the [Eddington luminosity](../../../../../../eddington-luminosity.md)

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm Edd,H}=\frac{4\pi GM_{\rm BH}m_pc}{\sigma_T}}.
$$

Below this luminosity gravity can confine optically thin ionized hydrogen; above it, electron scattering alone accelerates the gas outward.

An electron--positron pair has mass $2m_e$ and total scattering cross-section $2\sigma_T$, so its opacity is $\sigma_T/m_e$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm Edd,pair}
=\frac{4\pi GM_{\rm BH}m_ec}{\sigma_T}
=\frac{m_e}{m_p}L_{\rm Edd,H}}.
$$

The limit is lower because the radiative force per unit inertial mass is larger in a pair plasma.

For a cloud with arbitrary constant opacity per unit mass,

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm Edd}=\frac{4\pi GM_{\rm BH}c}{\kappa}}.
$$

Suppose the luminosity jumps to a constant $L>L_{\rm Edd}$ while the cloud is at rest at $r_0$. With $\Gamma=L/L_{\rm Edd}$, its radial acceleration is

$$
\ddot r=\frac{GM_{\rm BH}}{r^2}(\Gamma-1).
$$

Multiplication by $\dot r$ and [integration](../../../../../../integral.md) from $r_0$ to [infinity](../../../../../../infinity.md) gives [conservation of energy](../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md)

$$
\frac12v_\infty^2
=\frac{GM_{\rm BH}}{r_0}(\Gamma-1),
\qquad
\boxed{v_\infty=\left[\frac{2GM_{\rm BH}}{r_0}
\left(\frac L{L_{\rm Edd}}-1\right)\right]^{1/2}}.
$$

This result assumes that $L$ and $\kappa$ remain constant and that the cloud stays optically thin.
