<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Assume that both objects emit as [blackbodies](../../../../../../blackbody.md), take $T_*=5778\ {\rm K}$ and $R_p/R_*\simeq R_J/R_\odot=0.100$, and use $T_p=1250\ {\rm K}$ because the line-free window sees the $1$-bar continuum photosphere. Across a narrow bin, the [thermal eclipse depth](../../../../../../thermal-eclipse-depth.md) is

$$
\frac{F_p}{F_*}\simeq
\left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2
\frac{B_\lambda(T_p)}{B_\lambda(T_*)}
=\left(\frac{R_p}{R_*}\right)^2
\frac{e^{hc/(\lambda k_BT_*)}-1}
{e^{hc/(\lambda k_BT_p)}-1}.
$$

At $\lambda=17\,\mu{\rm m}$ this gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{F_p}{F_*}\simeq1.64\times10^{-3}
\simeq1640\ {\rm ppm}}.
$$

Integrating the [Planck law](../../../../../../planck-s-law.md) over the full $1\,\mu{\rm m}$ bin changes this narrow-bin estimate only slightly.
