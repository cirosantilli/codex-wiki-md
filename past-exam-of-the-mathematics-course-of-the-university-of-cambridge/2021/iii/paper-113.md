## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

For every homogeneous $s\in S_+$,

$$
D_+(\varphi(s))
=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}T:\varphi(s)\notin\mathfrak p\}.
$$

Hence

$$
U=\bigcup_{s\in S_+\text{ homogeneous}}D_+(\varphi(s)),
$$

so $U$ is open in the [Zariski topology](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#zariski-topology).

If $\mathfrak p\in U$, then $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)$ is a homogeneous [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) that does not contain the [irrelevant ideal of a graded ring](../../../ringed-space.md#irrelevant-ideal-of-a-graded-ring) $S_+$. Thus

$$
f(\mathfrak p)=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)
$$

defines a map $U\to\operatorname{Proj}S$. On every [standard affine open of Proj](../../../ringed-space.md#standard-affine-open-of-proj) the graded homomorphism induces

$$
(S_s)_0\longrightarrow (T_{\varphi(s)})_0,
$$

and therefore an [affine scheme](../../../ringed-space.md#affine-scheme) morphism

$$
D_+(\varphi(s))\longrightarrow D_+(s).
$$

These morphisms agree after localization on overlaps, so they glue to the required [morphism of schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#morphism-of-schemes) $f:U\to\operatorname{Proj}S$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Let $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}T$. Some homogeneous $t\in T_+$ lies outside $\mathfrak p$. Choose $N$ with $N\deg t\geq d_0$. Since $\varphi_{N\deg t}$ is surjective, $t^N=\varphi(s)$ for some homogeneous $s\in S_+$; primality gives $t^N\notin\mathfrak p$. Thus $\mathfrak p\in U$, and $U=\operatorname{Proj}T$.

Fix a positive-degree homogeneous $s\in S$ and write $q=\varphi(s)$. The map

$$
(S_s)_0\longrightarrow(T_q)_0
$$

is surjective: after multiplying the numerator and denominator of any degree-zero fraction by a sufficiently large power of $q$, its numerator has degree at least $d_0$ and therefore lifts through $\varphi$. It is injective by the same device: if $a/s^n$ maps to zero, then $q^m\varphi(a)=0$ for some $m$, and after increasing $m$ the injectivity of $\varphi$ in degree $(m+n)\deg s$ gives $s^ma=0$. Hence the displayed map is a [ring isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#ring-isomorphism).

The opens $D_+(q)$ obtained in this way cover $\operatorname{Proj}T$, and on every one of them $f$ is an isomorphism onto $D_+(s)$. The inverses agree on overlaps, so $f$ is an [isomorphism of schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#isomorphism-of-schemes). This proves the [Invariance of Proj under an eventual graded isomorphism](../../../ringed-space.md#invariance-of-proj-under-an-eventual-graded-isomorphism).

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Put

$$
R=k[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw).
$$

Since

$$
R/(x,z)\cong k[y,w]
$$

is an [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), $(x,z)$ is a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal). Moreover $\dim R=3$ and $\dim R/(x,z)=2$, so its [height of an ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#height-of-an-ideal) is one. Therefore $D=V(x,z)$ is a [prime Weil divisor](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#prime-weil-divisor).

Localizing at $x$ eliminates $y$ and gives

$$
R_x\cong k[x,z,w,x^{-1}],
$$

a [unique factorization domain](../../../algebra.md#unique-factorization-domain). The [Nagata theorem for divisor class groups](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#nagata-theorem-for-divisor-class-groups) says that $\operatorname{Cl}(R)$ is generated by the height-one primes containing $x$. Since

$$
R/(x)\cong k[y,z,w]/(zw),
$$

these are $D=V(x,z)$ and $E=V(x,w)$. Both occur with multiplicity one in the [principal divisor](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#principal-divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve)

$$
\operatorname{div}(x)=D+E.
$$

The units of $R_x\cong k[z,w][x,x^{-1}]$ are exactly $c x^n$ with $c\in k^*$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$. Consequently the only relation supplied by localization is $[D]+[E]=0$, and

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
\cong\frac{\mathbb Z[D]\oplus\mathbb Z[E]}{\mathbb Z([D]+[E])}
\cong\mathbb Z[D].
$$

This is the [divisor class group of the three-dimensional affine quadric cone](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-class-group-of-the-three-dimensional-affine-quadric-cone).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Cover $Y$ by

$$
U_0=Y\setminus D_Y,
\qquad U_y=D(y),
\qquad U_w=D(w).
$$

These sets cover because a point of $D_Y$ has $(y,w)\ne(0,0)$. On $U_y$, the relation $x=zw/y$ shows that $D_Y$ has local equation $z$; on $U_w$, the relation $z=xy/w$ gives local equation $x$; and on $U_0$ its local equation is $1$. Thus $D_Y$ is a [Cartier divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md).

For the [line bundle associated to a divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md#line-bundle-associated-to-a-divisor) $\mathcal O_Y(D_Y)$, choose local frames

$$
e_0=1,
\qquad e_y=\frac1z,
\qquad e_w=\frac1x.
$$

The transition functions are

$$
e_y=z^{-1}e_0,
\qquad
e_w=x^{-1}e_0,
\qquad
e_y=\frac{x}{z}e_w=\frac wy e_w
$$

on the corresponding overlaps. Every displayed ratio is a [unit in a ring](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) in the corresponding ring of [regular functions](../../../ringed-space.md#regular-function), and the [Čech cocycle condition](../../../ringed-space.md#cech-cocycle-condition) follows immediately.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

The two pairs of regular functions

$$
[x:w]
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
[z:y]
$$

define maps to the [projective line](../../../finite-group-theory.md#projective-line) on the loci where their respective coordinates do not vanish simultaneously. Those loci cover $Y$, since simultaneous failure would force $x=y=z=w=0$. On their overlap the equation $xy=zw$ says that the two projective points are equal. They therefore glue to a morphism

$$
f:Y\longrightarrow\mathbb P_k^1.
$$

Let $[A:B]$ be the homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb P_k^1$. The pullback of the [hyperplane divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md#hyperplane-divisor) $V(A)$ has local equation $x$ on the first chart and $z$ on the second. It is therefore exactly $D_Y=V(x,z)$. Compatibility of the [pullback of a sheaf of modules](../../../ringed-space.md#pullback-of-a-sheaf-of-modules) with the [line bundle associated to a divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md#line-bundle-associated-to-a-divisor) gives

$$
f^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P_k^1}(1)
\cong\mathcal O_Y(D_Y).
$$

This is the [ruling morphism of the punctured three-dimensional affine quadric cone](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#ruling-morphism-of-the-punctured-three-dimensional-affine-quadric-cone) associated with $D_Y$.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

The [direct image sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#direct-image-sheaf) is determined on the basis consisting of [principal open subschemes](../../../ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) $D(b)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}B$. Their inverse images are

$$
f^{-1}D(b)=D(\varphi(b))\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A,
$$

and hence

$$
f_*\widetilde M(D(b))
=\widetilde M(D(\varphi(b)))
=M_{\varphi(b)}.
$$

On the other hand, localization of the restricted-scalar $B$-module gives

$$
(M_B)_b\cong M_{\varphi(b)}.
$$

These isomorphisms commute with restriction to smaller principal opens, so they define the equality of [quasi-coherent sheaves](../../../ringed-space.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf)

$$
\boxed{f_*\widetilde M=\widetilde{M_B}.}
$$

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Because $X$ is a [Noetherian scheme](../../../ringed-space.md#noetherian-scheme), choose a finite affine cover $X=\bigcup_iU_i$. Every intersection $U_i\cap U_j$ is quasi-compact, so choose a finite affine cover $U_i\cap U_j=\bigcup_kU_{ijk}$. If $j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow X$ and $j_{ijk}:U_{ijk}\hookrightarrow X$ are the inclusions, the sheaf axiom gives an exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal F
\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i(j_i)_*(\mathcal F|_{U_i})
\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i,j,k}(j_{ijk})_*(\mathcal F|_{U_{ijk}}),
$$

where the last arrow is the difference of the two restrictions to each overlap chart.

Applying the left-exact [direct image sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#direct-image-sheaf) functor identifies $f_*\mathcal F$ with the kernel of

$$
\bigoplus_i(fj_i)_*(\mathcal F|_{U_i})
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{i,j,k}(fj_{ijk})_*(\mathcal F|_{U_{ijk}}).
$$

Every map $U_i\to Y$ and $U_{ijk}\to Y$ is a morphism between [affine schemes](../../../ringed-space.md#affine-scheme). Part (a) shows that all sheaves in the two finite sums are [quasi-coherent sheaves](../../../ringed-space.md#quasi-coherent-sheaf). Kernels of morphisms of quasi-coherent sheaves on the affine scheme $Y$ are quasi-coherent, because they correspond to kernels of module homomorphisms. Therefore $f_*\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent. This is the [quasi-coherence of direct image under a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism](../../../ringed-space.md#quasi-coherence-of-direct-image-under-a-quasi-compact-quasi-separated-morphism) in the present Noetherian case.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 113](paper-113.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

If the [germ of a sheaf section](../../../ringed-space.md#germ-of-a-sheaf-section) $s_p$ is zero, then by the definition of a [stalk of a sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#stalk-of-a-sheaf) there is a neighbourhood $V$ of $p$ on which $s|_V=0$. Every point of $V$ also has zero germ. Thus the complement of $\operatorname{Supp}s$ is open, so the [support of a sheaf section](../../../ringed-space.md#support-of-a-sheaf-section) is closed.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

The kernel of the restriction map

$$
\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(X\setminus Z,\mathcal F)
$$

consists exactly of sections whose germs vanish outside $Z$, namely $\Gamma_Z(X,\mathcal F)$. This proves exactness. If $\mathcal F$ is a [flasque sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#flasque-sheaf), the restriction map is surjective by definition.

Now let $0\to\mathcal F_1\to\mathcal F_2\to\mathcal F_3\to0$ be exact. The [global section functor](../../../ringed-space.md#global-section-functor) is left exact, so a section of $\Gamma_Z(X,\mathcal F_2)$ mapping to zero lifts uniquely to a global section of $\mathcal F_1$. Its germs outside $Z$ vanish because $\mathcal F_1\to\mathcal F_2$ is injective at each [stalk of a sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#stalk-of-a-sheaf). It therefore lies in $\Gamma_Z(X,\mathcal F_1)$, proving exactness of the supported-section sequence.

Suppose in addition that $\mathcal F_1$ is flasque and take $s_3\in\Gamma_Z(X,\mathcal F_3)$. Surjectivity on global sections gives a lift $s_2\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal F_2)$. On $X\setminus Z$, its restriction comes from some $t_1\in\Gamma(X\setminus Z,\mathcal F_1)$ by left exactness. Extend $t_1$ to $\widetilde t_1\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal F_1)$ using flasqueness. Then $s_2-\widetilde t_1$ maps to $s_3$ and vanishes outside $Z$, proving surjectivity on the right.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

Write $A=k[x,y]$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and $U=X\setminus Z$. Since $A$ is an [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), no nonzero global section is supported only at the origin, so

$$
H_Z^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=0.
$$

The [punctured affine plane](../../../ringed-space.md#punctured-affine-plane) has the affine cover $U=D(x)\cup D(y)$. Its [Čech cochain complex](../../../ringed-space.md#cech-cochain-complex) for the structure sheaf is

$$
0\longrightarrow A_x\oplus A_y
\xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a-b}A_{xy}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Consequently

$$
H^0(U,\mathcal O_U)=A,
\qquad
H^1(U,\mathcal O_U)=\frac{A_{xy}}{A_x+A_y},
\qquad
H^i(U,\mathcal O_U)=0\quad(i\geq2).
$$

Because $X$ is affine, the higher [sheaf cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#sheaf-cohomology) of $\mathcal O_X$ vanishes. The [long exact sequence for local cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#long-exact-sequence-for-local-cohomology) therefore gives

$$
H_Z^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0,
\qquad
H_Z^2(X,\mathcal O_X)
\cong\frac{k[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]}{k[x^{\pm1},y]+k[x,y^{\pm1}]},
$$

and $H_Z^i(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$ for $i\geq3$. The nonzero group has the $k$-basis

$$
\{x^{-a}y^{-b}:a,b\geq1\}.
$$

This computes the [local cohomology of the affine plane supported at the origin](../../../ringed-space.md#local-cohomology-of-the-affine-plane-supported-at-the-origin) in every degree.
