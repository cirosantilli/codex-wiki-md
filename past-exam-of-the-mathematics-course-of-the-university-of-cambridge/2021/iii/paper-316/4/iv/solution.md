<h1 id="4/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The angle

$$
\phi=(p+q)\lambda_2-p\lambda_1-q\Omega_2
$$

obeys the [D'Alembert characteristic](../../../../../../d-alembert-characteristic.md). Because reflection in the reference plane makes the disturbing function even in inclination, the nodal coefficient must be even, and therefore

$$
\boxed{q\ \hbox{is even}}.
$$

At [astronomical conjunction](../../../../../../conjunction-astronomy.md), $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda_c$, so

$$
\boxed{\frac\phi q=\lambda_c-\Omega_2}.
$$

Thus $\phi/q$ is the angular distance from the [ascending node](../../../../../../ascending-node.md) of $M_2$ to the conjunction. When $M_2$ is at that node, $\lambda_2=\Omega_2$, and

$$
\boxed{\frac\phi p=\Omega_2-\lambda_1}.
$$

Up to the chosen sign convention, $\phi/p$ is the angular separation between the inner body and the node when the outer body crosses the reference plane. These two views explain geometrically why libration confines both conjunction and node-crossing phases.
