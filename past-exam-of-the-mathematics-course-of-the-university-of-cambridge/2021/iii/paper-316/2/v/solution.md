<h1 id="2/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

At a [Triangular Lagrange point](../../../../../../triangular-lagrange-point.md), $y\ne0$, so $A=1$. The $x$ equation then gives

$$
\frac{1-\beta_1}{r_1^3}=\frac1{r_2^3}.
$$

Substitution into $A=1$ and $\mu_1+\mu_2=1$ yields

$$
r_2=1,
\qquad
r_1=(1-\beta_1)^{1/3}\equiv s.
$$

Intersecting these two circles gives

$$
\boxed{x=\frac{s^2}{2}-\mu_2,
\qquad
y=\pm s\sqrt{1-\frac{s^2}{4}},
\qquad z=0}.
$$

As $\beta_1$ rises from zero to one, $s$ falls from one to zero. The two points move along the unit circle about $M_2$, from the classical equilateral positions toward $M_1$, where they coalesce when the attraction of $M_1$ is fully cancelled.
