<h1 id="2/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

Put $q_i=1-\beta_i$. Repeating part (v) with radiation from both bodies gives

$$
\boxed{r_1=q_1^{1/3},
\qquad
r_2=q_2^{1/3}}.
$$

The two triangular points are intersections of circles with these radii and centre separation one. If

$$
X=\frac{r_1^2-r_2^2+1}{2},
$$

their coordinates are

$$
\boxed{x=X-\mu_2,
\qquad
y=\pm\sqrt{r_1^2-X^2},
\qquad z=0}.
$$

For ordinary outward radiation pressure, $0\leq\beta_i\leq1$, the non-collinear points exist precisely when the strict [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md)

$$
\boxed{(1-\beta_1)^{1/3}+(1-\beta_2)^{1/3}>1}
$$

holds. Equality merges the two points on the line of centres.
