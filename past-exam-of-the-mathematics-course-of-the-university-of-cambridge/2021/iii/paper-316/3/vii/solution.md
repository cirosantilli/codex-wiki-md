↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

A [cometary dust tail](../../../../../../cometary-dust-tail.md) is a superposition of [synchrones](../../../../../../synchrone.md) and [syndynes](../../../../../../syndyne.md), rather than a material line emitted in one fixed direction. For grains larger than the stellar wavelength, $\beta\propto D^{-1}$: small grains have large $\beta$, are displaced rapidly in the anti-stellar direction, and make a relatively straight broad tail. Large grains have small $\beta$, remain closer to the comet's [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md), and make a more strongly curved dust trail. The observed position angle consequently depends on grain size, release time, orbital phase, and projection onto the [plane of the sky](../../../../../../plane-of-the-sky.md).
