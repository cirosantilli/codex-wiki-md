<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $h=r^2\dot f$ be the [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md). The radial [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) equation has semi-latus rectum $p=h^2/\mu$, so comparison with

$$
r=\frac{\pm a(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos f}
$$

gives $h^2=\mu a(1-e^2)$ for an [elliptic orbit](../../../../../../elliptic-orbit.md) and $h^2=\mu a(e^2-1)$ for a [hyperbolic Kepler orbit](../../../../../../hyperbolic-kepler-orbit.md). At either apsis, $\dot r=0$ and $v=h/r$. Substituting the apsidal radius and angular momentum into part (i), or equivalently using the [vis-viva equation](../../../../../../vis-viva-equation.md), gives

$$
\boxed{C=-\frac{\mu}{2a}\quad\hbox{for a bound orbit},
\qquad
C=+\frac{\mu}{2a}\quad\hbox{for a hyperbolic orbit}}.
$$

**Thus the signs are $C=\mp\mu/(2a)$ in the convention of the question.**
