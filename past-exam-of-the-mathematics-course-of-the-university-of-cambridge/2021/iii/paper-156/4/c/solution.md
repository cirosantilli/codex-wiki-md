<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write the genus-two surface as

$$
S=T_1\cup_\alpha T_2,
$$

where $\alpha$ is separating and each $T_i$ is a one-holed torus. In each $T_i$, choose two disjoint essential proper arcs $p_i,q_i$ from the boundary to itself that form a cut system, so $T_i\setminus(p_i\cup q_i)$ is a disc. Arrange their four endpoints on each copy of $\alpha$ and glue the boundaries so that

$$
p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2
$$

join cyclically into one simple closed curve $\beta$. After smoothing at the four gluing points, $i(\alpha,\beta)=4$. Cutting along $\alpha$ and these four arcs leaves one disc from each $T_i$, so $S\setminus(\alpha\cup\beta)$ consists of two discs. Hence $(\alpha,\beta)$ is the [filling pair on a closed genus-two surface](../../../../../../filling-pair-on-a-closed-genus-two-surface.md).

If their curve-complex distance were at most two, there would be an essential curve $\gamma$ disjoint from both: it would be the intermediate vertex of a length-two path. This contradicts filling. Therefore

$$
\boxed{d_{\mathcal C(S)}([\alpha],[\beta])>2}.
$$
