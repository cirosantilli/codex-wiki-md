<h1 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The boundedness in the [Sobolev space](../../../../../../../sobolev-space-split.md) $H^1(U)$ and the [weak sequential compactness of bounded sequences in a reflexive Banach space](../../../../../../../weak-sequential-compactness-of-bounded-sequences-in-a-reflexive-banach-space.md) give a subsequence converging weakly to some $u\in H^1(U)$. For each integer $m\geq2$, the [Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem](../../../../../../../rellich-kondrachov-theorem.md) makes $H^1(U)\hookrightarrow L^m(U)$ compact because the dimension is two. Repeated extraction followed by the [diagonal argument](../../../../../../../diagonal-argument.md) gives one subsequence $u_{n_k}$ converging strongly to $u$ in every $L^m(U)$ with integral $m\geq2$.

For any finite real $p\geq1$, choose an integer $m\geq\max\{2,p\}$. Since $U$ has finite measure, the [Lp inclusion on a finite measure space](../../../../../../../lp-inclusion-on-a-finite-measure-space.md) gives

$$
\|u_{n_k}-u\|_{L^p(U)}
\leq |U|^{1/p-1/m}\|u_{n_k}-u\|_{L^m(U)}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The same subsequence therefore works for every finite $p$.
