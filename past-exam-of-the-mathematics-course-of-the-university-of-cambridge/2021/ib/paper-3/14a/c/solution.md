<h1 id="14a/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Part (a) makes $\lambda_k<0$ for all sufficiently large $k$, while part (b) would make $\lambda_k\geq0$ whenever $P^{(k)}$ were nonzero. Hence some derivative of $P$ vanishes identically, so $P$ is a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md).

Let its degree be $n$. Then $P^{(n)}$ is a nonzero constant. Substituting $k=n$ into the differentiated equation annihilates both derivative terms and gives

$$
\lambda_nP^{(n)}=0.
$$

Thus $\lambda_n=0$, and

$$
\boxed{\lambda=n(n+1)},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

The nonzero regular solutions are therefore constant multiples of the [Legendre polynomial](../../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md) of degree $n$.
