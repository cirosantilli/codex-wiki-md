<h1 id="12b/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set

$$
g(z)=\frac{f(z)-b}{a}.
$$

Then $g$ is entire and never takes a real value. The continuous function $\operatorname{Im}g$ is nowhere zero; because $\mathbb C$ is connected, it has one sign everywhere. Thus either $\operatorname{Im}g$ or $-\operatorname{Im}g$ is a positive [harmonic function](../../../../../../../harmonic-function.md). Part (i) makes it constant. The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) then force the real part of $g$ to be constant as well. Hence $g$ and therefore $f$ are constant.
