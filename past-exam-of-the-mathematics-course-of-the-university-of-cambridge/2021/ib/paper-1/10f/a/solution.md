<h1 id="10f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

This is always true: it is the [Heine-Cantor theorem](../../../../../../heine-cantor-theorem.md). If uniform continuity failed, there would be an $\varepsilon>0$ and sequences $x_n,y_n\in X$ such that

$$
d_X(x_n,y_n)<\frac1n,
\qquad
d_Y(f(x_n),f(y_n))\geq\varepsilon.
$$

By [sequential compactness of a compact metric space](../../../../../../sequential-compactness-of-a-compact-metric-space.md), some subsequence $x_{n_k}$ converges to $x\in X$. The triangle inequality gives $y_{n_k}\to x$. Continuity then makes both image subsequences converge to $f(x)$, contradicting their separation by $\varepsilon$.
