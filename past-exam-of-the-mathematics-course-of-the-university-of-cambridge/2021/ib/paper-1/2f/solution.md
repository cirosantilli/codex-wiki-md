<h1 id="2f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2F](../2f.md)

The condition $Df_p\ne0$ says that $0$ is a [regular value](../../../../../regular-value.md) of $f$. The [regular level set theorem](../../../../../regular-level-set-theorem.md) therefore makes $f^{-1}(0)$ a smooth submanifold of codimension one in $\mathbb R^3$, hence a [smooth surface](../../../../../smooth-surface.md).

For

$$
F(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2-\cosh(z^2),
$$

the [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is

$$
\nabla F=(2x,2y,-2z\sinh(z^2)).
$$

On $F^{-1}(0)$, $x^2+y^2=\cosh(z^2)\geq1$, so $x$ and $y$ cannot both vanish. Thus $\nabla F\ne0$ there, and the given set is a smooth surface.

Not every smooth surface in $\mathbb R^3$ is a global zero set. Every set $f^{-1}(0)$ is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) because $f$ is continuous, whereas the open unit disk

$$
\{(x,y,0):x^2+y^2<1\}
$$

is a smooth surface but is not closed in $\mathbb R^3$.
