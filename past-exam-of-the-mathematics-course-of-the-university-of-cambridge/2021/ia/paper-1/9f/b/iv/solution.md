This can be false. The function

$$
f(x)=x^3
$$

is a differentiable bijection of $\mathbb R$, but

$$
f^{-1}(y)=\sqrt[3]y
$$

has an unbounded difference quotient at zero and is not differentiable there. The [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md) requires the additional local condition $f'(x)\ne0$.
