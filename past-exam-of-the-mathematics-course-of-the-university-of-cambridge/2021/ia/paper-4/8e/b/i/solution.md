<h1 id="8e/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Suppose all functions $\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ could be listed as $f_1,f_2,\ldots$. Define

$$
g(n)=f_n(n)+1.
$$

Then $g$ differs from $f_n$ at $n$ for every $n$, so it is absent from the list. This [Cantor's diagonal argument](../../../../../../../cantor-s-diagonal-argument.md) proves that $\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}$ is an [uncountable set](../../../../../../../uncountable-set.md).
