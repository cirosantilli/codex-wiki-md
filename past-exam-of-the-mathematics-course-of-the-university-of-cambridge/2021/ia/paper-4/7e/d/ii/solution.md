This is always true for every positive integer $n$. If $\beta^n$ were algebraic, then $\beta$ would be a root of the polynomial $X^n-\beta^n$ over the field of [algebraic numbers](../../../../../../../algebraic-number.md). By [transitivity of algebraic extensions](../../../../../../../transitivity-of-algebraic-extensions.md), $\beta$ would then be algebraic over $\mathbb Q$, contradicting its transcendence.
