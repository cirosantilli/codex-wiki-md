<h1 id="7e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let

$$
\alpha_N=\sum_{n=1}^N2^{-n!}=\frac{p_N}{q_N},
\qquad q_N=2^{N!}.
$$

The tail satisfies

$$
0<\alpha-\alpha_N<2\,2^{-(N+1)!}.
$$

For every fixed positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $d$, this is smaller than $q_N^{-d}$ for all sufficiently large $N$.

The binary expansion has $1$ digits at factorial positions and arbitrarily long blocks of zeros, but it is neither eventually zero nor eventually periodic. Since a [rational number](../../../../../../rational-number.md) has an [eventually periodic binary expansion](../../../../../../eventually-periodic-positional-expansion-of-a-rational-number.md), $\alpha$ is [irrational](../../../../../../irrational-number.md). If it were an [algebraic number](../../../../../../algebraic-number.md) of degree $d$, part (b), or equivalently the [Liouville approximation theorem](../../../../../../liouville-approximation-theorem.md), would give a fixed positive multiple of $q^{-d}$ as a lower bound for all rational approximations. The fractions $p_N/q_N$ violate that bound. Thus $\alpha$ is a [transcendental number](../../../../../../transcendental-number.md).
