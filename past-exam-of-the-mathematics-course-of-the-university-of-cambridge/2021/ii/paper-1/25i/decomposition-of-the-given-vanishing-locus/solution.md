<h1 id="25i/decomposition-of-the-given-vanishing-locus/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Decomposition of the given vanishing locus](../decomposition-of-the-given-vanishing-locus.md)

Subtracting the two defining equations gives

$$
y^2+z^2=(y-iz)(y+iz)=0.
$$

Consequently the variety is

$$
V(y-iz,x^2-z^2-1)
\ \cup\
V(y+iz,x^2-z^2-1).
$$

Each member is irreducible. Indeed, after imposing its linear equation, its [coordinate ring](../../../../../../coordinate-ring.md) is

$$
\mathbb C[x,z]/(x^2-z^2-1).
$$

The invertible linear substitution

$$
u=x-z,\qquad v=x+z
$$

turns this into

$$
\mathbb C[u,v]/(uv-1)\cong\mathbb C[u,u^{-1}],
$$

which is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md). The two curves are distinct and neither contains the other, so they are precisely the [irreducible components of two intersecting complex hyperbolas](../../../../../../irreducible-components-of-two-intersecting-complex-hyperbolas.md).
