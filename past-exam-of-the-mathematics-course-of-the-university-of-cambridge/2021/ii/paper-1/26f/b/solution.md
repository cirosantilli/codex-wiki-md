<h1 id="26f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Since $z>0$, the cone has the parametrization

$$
\phi(r,\theta)
=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,\sqrt a\,r),
\qquad r>0.
$$

Its coordinate derivatives satisfy

$$
\phi_r\cdot\phi_r=1+a,
\qquad
\phi_r\cdot\phi_\theta=0,
\qquad
\phi_\theta\cdot\phi_\theta=r^2,
$$

so its [first fundamental form](../../../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) is

$$
I=(1+a)\,dr^2+r^2\,d\theta^2.
$$

On a sufficiently narrow angular patch, define

$$
R=\sqrt{1+a}\,r,
\qquad
\Theta=\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{1+a}},
$$

and map the cone to the plane point with [polar coordinates](../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) $(R,\Theta)$. The Euclidean metric pulls back as

$$
dR^2+R^2d\Theta^2
=(1+a)\,dr^2+r^2\,d\theta^2=I.
$$

The map is therefore a [local isometry from a circular cone to the plane](../../../../../../local-isometry-from-a-circular-cone-to-the-plane.md), proving that $\Sigma$ is locally isometric to the Euclidean plane.
