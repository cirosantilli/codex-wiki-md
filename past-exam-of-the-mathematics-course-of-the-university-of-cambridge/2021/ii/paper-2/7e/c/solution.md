<h1 id="7e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose there were no singular point in the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../../riemann-sphere.md). Then every finite point would be ordinary, so $p$ and $q$ would be [entire functions](../../../../../../entire-function.md). Ordinary behavior at infinity would force $p(z)=2/z+O(z^{-2})$ and $q(z)=O(z^{-4})$. In particular $p(z)\to0$, so [Liouville theorem](../../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) would give $p\equiv0$, contradicting the required leading term $2/z$. Thus infinity cannot also be ordinary, and every such differential equation has at least one singular point in $\overline{\mathbb C}$.
