The [Riemann-Lebesgue lemma](../../../../../../riemann-lebesgue-lemma.md) states that the [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) of an $L^1$ function is continuous and tends to zero at infinity.

For a [Schwartz function](../../../../../../schwartz-space.md) $f$, differentiation under the integral and [integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) give

$$
ξ^α D^β \widehat f(ξ)=C_{α,β}\widehat{D^α(x^βf)}(ξ).
$$

The [Riemann-Lebesgue lemma](../../../../../../riemann-lebesgue-lemma.md) bounds the right side by an $L^1$ seminorm of $D^α(x^βf)$. Every Schwartz seminorm of $\widehat f$ is therefore bounded by finitely many Schwartz seminorms of $f$. Hence the Fourier transform maps $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ continuously into itself.
