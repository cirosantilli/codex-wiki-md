<h1 id="35d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Below $T_c$ the fugacity is $z=1$ in the continuum ideal-gas approximation. The zero-energy condensate contributes no internal energy, so the same [Bose integral](../../../../../../bose-integral.md) with one additional factor of energy gives

$$
E=B\Gamma(\tfrac{11}2)\zeta(\tfrac{11}2)(k_BT)^{11/2}.
$$

Eliminate $B$ using $N=B\Gamma(9/2)\zeta(9/2)(k_BT_c)^{9/2}$ and $\Gamma(11/2)=(9/2)\Gamma(9/2)$:

$$
\boxed{E=\frac92Nk_BT\frac{\zeta(11/2)}{\zeta(9/2)}\left(\frac T{T_c}\right)^{9/2}\quad(T\le T_c).}
$$

The excited fraction is $(T/T_c)^{9/2}$ and the remaining particles occupy the [Bose-Einstein condensate](../../../../../../bose-einstein-condensate.md). For fixed $B$, the condensed-state energy at fixed $T$ is independent of how many additional particles are placed in the zero-energy ground state.
