<h1 id="12f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The polynomial form of the [Runge approximation theorem](../../../../../../runge-s-theorem.md) says: if $K\subset\mathbb C$ is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md), $\mathbb C\setminus K$ is connected, and $f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) on a neighborhood of $K$, then for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $P$ with $\sup_K|f-P|<\varepsilon$. Choosing errors tending to zero gives a uniformly convergent sequence of [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Equivalently, on an open domain with connected complement in the Riemann sphere, every [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) is the compact-uniform limit of [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). The connected-complement hypothesis excludes enclosed holes containing poles of the function being approximated.
