<h1 id="14e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

When $a=0$, define the [Hamiltonian function](../../../../../../hamiltonian-function.md)

$$
H(x,y)=\frac{x^3}{3}+xy^2-bx.
$$

Then $\dot x=-H_y$ and $\dot y=H_x$, so $\dot H=0$. At $(\sqrt b,0)$ the Hessian of $H$ is $2\sqrt b\,I$, a strict nondegenerate minimum; at $(-\sqrt b,0)$ it is $-2\sqrt b\,I$, a strict maximum. Small regular level curves around either point are closed curves, and the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) is nonzero on them. Motion around each curve therefore returns after a finite period, proving the existence of [periodic orbits](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) and classifying both equilibria as [center equilibria](../../../../../../center-equilibrium.md).

The separatrix level is $H=0$, consisting of $x=0$ and $x^2/3+y^2=b$. The two arcs of this ellipse run from the lower [saddle equilibrium](../../../../../../saddle-equilibrium.md) to the upper one, while the vertical segment runs from the upper saddle to the lower one. They enclose the two families of [periodic orbits](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md), as shown in the last panel of the phase portrait. Outside the loops, regular levels are unbounded.

For $a>0$ the divergence of the original [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) is exactly $-a$. If a [periodic orbit](../../../../../../periodic-orbit.md) existed, uniqueness of trajectories would make it a simple closed curve, tangent everywhere to the [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md). The flux across its boundary would be zero, whereas the [divergence theorem](../../../../../../divergence-theorem.md) would give $-a$ times its positive enclosed area. This contradiction is the [Bendixson-Dulac criterion](../../../../../../bendixson-dulac-theorem.md). Hence $\boxed{\text{periodic orbits exist if and only if }a=0}$.
