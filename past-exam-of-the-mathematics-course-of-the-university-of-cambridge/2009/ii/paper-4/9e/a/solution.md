<h1 id="9e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the canonical [Poisson bracket](../../../../../../poisson-bracket.md) convention $\{H,G\}=\sum_k(H_{q_k}G_{p_k}-H_{p_k}G_{q_k})$. The [Hamiltonian flow](../../../../../../hamiltonian-flow.md) generated by $G$ has $\dot q_k=G_{p_k}$ and $\dot p_k=-G_{q_k}$. The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) therefore gives

$$
\frac{dH}{dt}=\sum_k(H_{q_k}\dot q_k+H_{p_k}\dot p_k)=\{H,G\}.
$$

Thus $H$ is invariant under every such trajectory exactly when $\boxed{\{H,G\}=0}$ identically. Antisymmetry then gives $dG/dt=\{G,H\}=0$ along the flow of $H$, so $G$ is a [constant of motion](../../../../../../conserved-quantity.md).

For $G=\alpha\sum_kp_k$, the generated equations are $\dot q_k=\alpha$, $\dot p_k=0$, hence $q_k(t)=q_k(0)+\alpha t$, $p_k(t)=p_k(0)$. Invariance under this simultaneous translation implies conservation of $\boxed{\sum_kp_k}$ when $\alpha\ne0$; the zero generator alone conveys no additional conservation law.
