<h1 id="22h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a complex [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md), the [spectrum of a bounded operator](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-bounded-operator.md) and [resolvent set](../../../../../../resolvent-set-of-an-operator.md) are

$$
\sigma(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:T-\lambda I\text{ has no bounded two-sided inverse}\},\qquad
\rho(T)=\mathbb C\setminus\sigma(T).
$$

The [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md) says that a bounded bijection between [Banach spaces](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md) automatically has a bounded inverse, so failure of bijectivity suffices here. The [point spectrum](../../../../../../point-spectrum.md) is

$$
\sigma_p(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\ker(T-\lambda I)\ne\{0\}\},
$$

the set of [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md). For a real [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md), these definitions use its complexification.
