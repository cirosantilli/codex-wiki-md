<h1 id="37e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Vertical hydrostatic balance with atmospheric pressure at $y=h(x)$ gives $p(x,y)=p_0+\rho g(h(x)-y)$ to this order. Therefore $p_x=\rho g h'$ and integrating the gradient from part (i), with $h\to0$ on both sides, gives

$$
\boxed{h(x)=-\frac{\mu U\sqrt{2a}}{\rho g\,b^{3/2}}
\frac{s}{(1+s^2)^2},\qquad s=\frac{x}{\sqrt{2ab}}.}
$$

For positive $U$ the upstream surface rises and the downstream surface falls. Thus the displacement scale is $\mu|U|\sqrt a/(\rho g b^{3/2})$, and the flat-geometry consistency condition is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mu |\Omega|a^{3/2}}{\rho g b^{5/2}}\ll1.}
$$

Indeed $\max_s |s/(1+s^2)^2|=9/(16\sqrt3)$, so this scale controls the maximum, not only a typical value. Together with $b/a\ll1$ and $\rho|\Omega|\sqrt a\,b^{3/2}/\mu\ll1$, these conditions ensure a thin, weakly displaced, viscosity-dominated gap. The slope is smaller still, of order $(|h|/b)\sqrt{b/a}$.
