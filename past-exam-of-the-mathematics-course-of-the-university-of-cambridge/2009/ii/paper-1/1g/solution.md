<h1 id="1g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1G](../1g.md)

The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) says that for pairwise coprime positive moduli $m_1,\ldots,m_s$, every choice of residues determines exactly one residue class modulo $M=\prod_jm_j$. Equivalently $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\to\prod_j\mathbb Z/m_j\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism under reduction.

The first two [modular congruences](../../../../../modular-congruence.md) combine to $x=2+15k$. Reducing this expression modulo seven gives $2+k\equiv6$, so $k=4+7j$. Consequently **all integer solutions** are

$$
\boxed{x=62+105j,\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

The moduli are pairwise coprime, so the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) guarantees that this is the entire solution class.
