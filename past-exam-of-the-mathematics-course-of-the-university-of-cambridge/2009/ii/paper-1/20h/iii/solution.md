<h1 id="20h/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Write $f=X^2+bX+c$ and $g=X^2+BX+C$. Since their common square-free [discriminant](../../../../../../discriminant.md) $d$ is congruent to zero or one modulo four, it must be one modulo four; thus $b,B$ are odd. It is not a rational square because a square-free integer square is only zero or one. Both [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) are irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, and their roots generate the same [quadratic extension](../../../../../../quadratic-extension.md) $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$. Their integral roots are $(-b+\sqrt d)/2$ and $(-B+\sqrt d)/2$, respectively. Each generated order is

$$
\mathbb Z\left[\frac{1+\sqrt d}{2}\right]=\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)},
$$

the usual ring of integers for square-free $d\equiv1\pmod4$. This identifies both quotients with the same ring. Explicitly, if $\xi$ denotes the image of $X$ modulo $g$, send the image of $X$ modulo $f$ to $\xi+(B-b)/2$. Equal discriminants give $f(\xi+(B-b)/2)=g(\xi)=0$, and the opposite translation gives its inverse. **The two quotient rings are isomorphic.**
