<h1 id="20h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [basis of a module](../../../../../../basis-of-a-module.md) $m_1,\ldots,m_d$ of the full-rank module $M$, define its [discriminant of elements of a number field](../../../../../../discriminant-of-elements-of-a-number-field.md) by

$$
\operatorname{disc}(M)=\det(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(m_im_j))_{i,j}.
$$

The [field discriminant](../../../../../../field-discriminant.md) is this determinant for an integral basis of $\mathcal O_K$. A change of integral basis with determinant $\pm1$ does not change it. Write $m_j=\sum_i b_{ij}e_i$ in an integral basis and let $B=(b_{ij})$. The two trace matrices satisfy $T_M=B^{\mathsf T}T_{\mathcal O_K}B$. The index of the lattice is $r=|\det B|$, as follows for example from [Smith normal form](../../../../../../smith-normal-form.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{disc}(M)=r^2\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_K).}
$$

Multiplicative closure makes $M$ an order when it contains one, but the determinant argument itself requires only full finite index, so it also covers the stated module hypothesis without that extra condition.
