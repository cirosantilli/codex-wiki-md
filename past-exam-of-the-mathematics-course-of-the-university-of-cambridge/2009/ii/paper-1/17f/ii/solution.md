<h1 id="17f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $A,B$ be the two parts of the [bipartite graph](../../../../../../bipartite-graph.md). Counting edges gives $k|A|=k|B|$, hence $|A|=|B|=n/2$. For $S\subseteq A$, the $k|S|$ edges incident to $S$ all end in $N(S)$, whose vertices have total capacity $k|N(S)|$. Thus $|N(S)|\geq|S|$. [Hall's marriage theorem](../../../../../../hall-s-marriage-theorem.md) gives a [perfect matching](../../../../../../perfect-matching.md), and no [matching in a graph](../../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md) can have more than $n/2$ edges. **The matching number is $\boxed{n/2}$.**
