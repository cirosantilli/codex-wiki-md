<h1 id="25h/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [antipodal map](../../../../../../antipodal-map.md) is the restriction of the smooth linear map $-I$, so is smooth. For $n\geq1$ orient the sphere by the outward-normal convention. Its degree is $(-1)^{n+1}$: the ambient determinant is $(-1)^{n+1}$, and the outward normal is carried to the outward normal at the antipodal point.

Choose a [regular value](../../../../../../regular-value.md) of $f$, available by [Sard theorem](../../../../../../sard-s-theorem.md); if it is outside the image, the degree is zero already. Otherwise its finite preimage is partitioned into distinct antipodal pairs. Differentiating $f\circ h=f$ shows that their local degree signs obey

$$
\varepsilon_f(-x)(-1)^{n+1}=\varepsilon_f(x).
$$

For even $n$, the signs in each pair cancel, so $\deg f=0$. For odd $n$, each pair contributes $2$ or $-2$, so $\deg f$ is even. In neither case can its degree equal the identity's degree one. Since the [degree of a map between oriented manifolds](../../../../../../degree-of-a-map-between-oriented-manifolds.md) is invariant under smooth [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md), **$f$ is not smoothly homotopic to the identity.** For $n=0$, the symmetry makes $f$ constant on the two-point discrete sphere; a continuous homotopy into this discrete space cannot change either point's image, so the conclusion still holds.
