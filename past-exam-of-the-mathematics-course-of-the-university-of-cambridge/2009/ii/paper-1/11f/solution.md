<h1 id="11f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [11F](../11f.md)

A [frieze group](../../../../../frieze-group.md) is a [discrete subgroup](../../../../../discrete-subgroup.md) of [Euclidean isometries](../../../../../euclidean-isometry.md) whose [translation subgroup](../../../../../translation-subgroup.md) is generated by one nonzero vector and has finite index. A [plane crystallographic group](../../../../../wallpaper-group.md) is a [discrete subgroup](../../../../../discrete-subgroup.md) of [Euclidean isometries](../../../../../euclidean-isometry.md) with a rank-two [translation lattice](../../../../../translation-lattice.md) of finite index; equivalently its action has a compact fundamental region.

Here are three [frieze groups](../../../../../frieze-group.md), with explicit generators. The translation $T(x,y)=(x+1,y)$ alone gives $\mathbb Z$. Adding the vertical [reflection in mathematics](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) $S(x,y)=(-x,y)$ gives $\mathbb Z\rtimes C_2$, the [infinite dihedral group](../../../../../infinite-dihedral-group.md), since $STS=T^{-1}$. Instead adding the horizontal [reflection in mathematics](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) $H(x,y)=(x,-y)$ gives $\mathbb Z\times C_2$, since $HT=TH$. Their translation parts are exactly $\langle T\rangle$ and their point parts have respectively one, two and two elements.

For [plane crystallographic groups](../../../../../wallpaper-group.md), let $T_1(x,y)=(x+1,y)$ and $T_2(x,y)=(x,y+1)$. These generate $\mathbb Z^2$. Adding the half-turn $R_2(x,y)=(-x,-y)$ gives $\mathbb Z^2\rtimes C_2$, with inversion on both translation generators. Adding the quarter-turn $R_4(x,y)=(-y,x)$ gives $\mathbb Z^2\rtimes C_4$, with $R_4T_1R_4^{-1}=T_2$ and $R_4T_2R_4^{-1}=T_1^{-1}$. These are the translation, half-turn and square-rotation wallpaper groups. Integer translation vectors and a finite point part make each group a [discrete subgroup](../../../../../discrete-subgroup.md); the unit square meets every orbit, establishing a [cocompact group action](../../../../../cocompact-group-action.md).

For the final claim, suppose a [Euclidean isometry](../../../../../euclidean-isometry.md) $R$ has order five. An orientation-reversing planar [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md) has finite order only when it is a [reflection in mathematics](../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md), so $R$ is a rotation through $\theta=2\pi j/5$, $j=1,2,3,4$, about some centre. Write $A$ for its linear rotation part. If $t_v\in G$ is a nonzero [translation](../../../../../translation-geometry.md), conjugation gives $Rt_vR^{-1}=t_{Av}$. Thus $G$ contains translations by $v$ and by $(A+A^{-1})v=cv$, where $c=2\cos\theta$.

For a primitive fifth root $\zeta$, dividing $1+\zeta+\zeta^2+\zeta^3+\zeta^4=0$ by $\zeta^2$ gives $c^2+c-1=0$. Hence $c$ is irrational. The [pigeonhole principle](../../../../../pigeonhole-principle.md), applied to $N+1$ fractional parts of $0,c,\ldots,Nc$ in $N$ equal intervals, gives integers $m,n$ with $0<|nc-m|\leq1/N$. Consequently $G$ contains nonidentity translations by $(nc-m)v$ tending to the identity. This contradicts the [discrete subgroup](../../../../../discrete-subgroup.md) hypothesis. **There is no element of order five.** This argument needs only the given nonzero translation, not a rank-two lattice.
