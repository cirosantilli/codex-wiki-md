<h1 id="12h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $J_1,J_2,\ldots$ be independent rolls with the specified [probability distribution](../../../../../../probability-distribution.md), and let $F_m$ be the fortune after $m$ games. Its multiplicative evolution gives

$$
\frac1m\log\frac{F_m}{f}=\frac1m\sum_{\nu=1}^m\log\frac{a_{J_\nu}}{u_{J_\nu}}\longrightarrow g(a)=\sum_jp_j\log\frac{a_j}{u_j}
\quad\hbox{almost surely},
$$

by the [strong law of large numbers](../../../../../../strong-law-of-large-numbers.md). The summands have finitely many finite values, so its hypotheses hold. For any $\varepsilon>0$, the probability that $f e^{m(g-\varepsilon)}\leq F_m\leq f e^{m(g+\varepsilon)}$ tends to one. Thus **maximizing $g(a)$ maximizes the typical exponential growth rate**, the objective of [log-optimal multiplicative betting](../../../../../../log-optimal-multiplicative-betting.md). Maximizing expected fortune would be a different objective and would not control the typical repeated-game outcome.
