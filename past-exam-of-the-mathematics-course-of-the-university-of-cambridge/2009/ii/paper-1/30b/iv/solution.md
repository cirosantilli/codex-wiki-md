<h1 id="30b/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the quadratic potential the [characteristic curves](../../../../../../characteristic-curve.md) are harmonic oscillations:

$$
\widehat x=x_0\cos t+v_0\sin t,\qquad \widehat v=v_0\cos t-x_0\sin t.
$$

The inverse is $x_0=x\cos t-v\sin t$, $v_0=v\cos t+x\sin t$. Therefore the [Liouville equation](../../../../../../liouville-equation.md) has solution

$$
\boxed{f(x,v,t)=f_I(x\cos t-v\sin t,\ v\cos t+x\sin t).}
$$

This formula works for smooth data or by transport for locally integrable data, and for measures by pushforward. Although the quadratic $V$ itself is not globally Lipschitz as in the initial sufficient hypotheses, its gradient is globally Lipschitz, which is the property actually needed for this global flow.
