<h1 id="22h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $P_m$ be the [linear subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $m$. It has finite dimension $m+1$, is proper in $P$, and $P=\bigcup_{m\geq0}P_m$. For any norm on $P$, the restricted norm on $P_m$ is equivalent to its coefficient Euclidean norm: on the coefficient unit sphere the continuous norm has a positive minimum and a finite maximum. Thus $P_m$ is complete. A complete subspace of a [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) is closed, since any sequence converging to an ambient limit is Cauchy and has the same limit in the subspace.

Part (b) makes each $P_m$ a closed set with empty interior, hence nowhere dense. If $P$ were a [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md), this countable union would contradict the [Baire category theorem](../../../../../../baire-category-theorem.md). **No norm makes the space of all [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) complete.**
