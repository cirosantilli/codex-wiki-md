<h1 id="28i/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [Lehmann–Scheffé theorem](../../../../../../lehmann-scheffe-theorem.md) states that an unbiased estimator which is a function of a complete sufficient statistic is the unique minimum-variance unbiased estimator; equivalently conditioning any unbiased estimator on such a statistic produces it.

Use the unbiased estimator $I=\mathbf1_{X_1>x}$. Given $T_n=t$, the positive sample vector is uniform on the simplex $\sum_iX_i=t$: the joint exponential density is constant there, independently of $\lambda$. For $t<x$ the conditional probability is zero. For $t\geq x$, translating the first coordinate by $x$ changes the simplex size from $t$ to $t-x$; its $(n-1)$-dimensional volume ratio is $(1-x/t)^{n-1}$. Thus

$$
\mathbb E[I\mid T_n]=\widetilde\phi_n,
$$

including $n=1$, when this is the indicator $\mathbf1_{T_1\geq x}$ up to a null boundary. [Conditional expectation](../../../../../../conditional-expectation.md) preserves unbiasedness, and completeness from part (i) proves **the displayed estimator is the unique minimum-variance unbiased estimator.**

It does not attain the [Cramér-Rao bound](../../../../../../cramer-rao-bound.md). Equality in that bound requires the centred unbiased estimator to be proportional to the [score function](../../../../../../informant-function.md), hence affine in $T_n$ at fixed $\lambda$. This estimator is zero on a positive-probability interval and positive elsewhere, so is not affine; the $n=1$ step function is not affine either.

For the last assertion, the remaining observations are independent of the first $k$. Conditional on $T_k$, the internal proportions of those first $k$ are independent of the remaining sum, so

$$
\mathbb E[I\mid T_k,T_n]=\widetilde\phi_k.
$$

Now the tower property for the nested information $\sigma(T_n)\subseteq\sigma(T_k,T_n)$ gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[\widetilde\phi_k\mid T_n,\lambda]=\mathbb E[I\mid T_n,\lambda]=\widetilde\phi_n.}
$$

Conditioning first on both sums is essential; the sigma-fields generated by $T_k$ and $T_n$ alone are not nested.
