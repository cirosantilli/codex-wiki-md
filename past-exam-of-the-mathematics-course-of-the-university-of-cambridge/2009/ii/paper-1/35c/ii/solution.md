<h1 id="35c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take $\partial_b$ of the [Proca equation](../../../../../../proca-equation.md). Commuting partial derivatives and antisymmetry make $\partial_b\partial_aF^{ab}=0$, while [current conservation](../../../../../../conserved-current.md) gives $\partial_bJ^b=0$. Thus $m^2\partial_bA^b=0$. **For $m\ne0$, $\boxed{\partial_bA^b=0}$.** This [Lorenz constraint in Proca theory](../../../../../../lorenz-constraint-in-proca-theory.md) is a consequence of the massive equations, rather than a gauge choice.

The stated “arbitrary constant” includes a genuine exception at $m=0$. Then the divergence condition is not implied: with $J=0$ and $A_a=\partial_a\chi$, $F=0$ solves the equations for any smooth $\chi$, whereas $\partial_aA^a=\Box\chi$ need not vanish. In the massless theory the Lorenz condition can instead be imposed as a [gauge fixing](../../../../../../gauge-fixing.md).
