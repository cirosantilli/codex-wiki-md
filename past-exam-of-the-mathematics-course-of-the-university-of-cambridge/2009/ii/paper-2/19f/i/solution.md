<h1 id="19f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $\rho$ affords an [irreducible character](../../../../../../irreducible-character.md) $\chi$, entrywise complex conjugation gives a representation with [character](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\overline\chi$. An invariant complex subspace for the conjugate representation conjugates back to one for $\rho$, so irreducibility is preserved. If representations afford $\chi,\psi$, their [tensor product](../../../../../../tensor-product.md) has trace $\chi(g)\psi(g)$, proving that their product is a [character](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md).

With the usual [character inner product](../../../../../../character-inner-product.md),

$$
\langle\chi\psi,1_G\rangle=\frac1{|G|}\sum_g\chi(g)\psi(g)=\langle\chi,\overline\psi\rangle.
$$

The [character orthogonality](../../../../../../character-orthogonality.md) and the first assertion therefore make this equal to one if $\chi=\overline\psi$ and zero otherwise. This also counts the invariant vectors in the tensor-product representation.
