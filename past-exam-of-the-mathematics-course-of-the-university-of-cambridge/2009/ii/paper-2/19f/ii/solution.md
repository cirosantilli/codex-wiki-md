<h1 id="19f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a representation space $V$, let $\chi_S$ and $\chi_A$ be the [characters](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) on $\operatorname{Sym}^2V$ and $\bigwedge^2V$. If the [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $\rho(g)$ are $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d$, the respective traces are $\sum_{i\leq j}\lambda_i\lambda_j$ and $\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i\lambda_j$. Since $\chi(g)=\sum\lambda_i$ and $\chi(g^2)=\sum\lambda_i^2$, this gives

$$
\boxed{\chi_S(g)=\frac{\chi(g)^2+\chi(g^2)}2,\qquad\chi_A(g)=\frac{\chi(g)^2-\chi(g^2)}2.}
$$

Finite-order matrices over $\mathbb C$ are diagonalizable, so this [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) argument applies to every group element. Equivalently the symmetric and antisymmetric projections on $V\otimes V$ are $(I\pm\text{swap})/2$.
