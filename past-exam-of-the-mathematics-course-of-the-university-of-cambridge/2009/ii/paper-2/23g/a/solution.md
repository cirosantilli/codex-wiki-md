<h1 id="23g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [elliptic function](../../../../../../elliptic-function.md) for a [lattice](../../../../../../lattice.md) $\Lambda$ is a [meromorphic function](../../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on $\mathbb C$ satisfying $f(z+\lambda)=f(z)$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$. Its order is the number of poles, counted with multiplicity, in a fundamental parallelogram, equivalently the degree of its map from the [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) to the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../../riemann-sphere.md). Differentiation preserves the periods. If the distinct poles have orders $r_1,\ldots,r_s$, the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) has exactly the same pole locations with orders $r_j+1$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{n=m+s,\qquad m+1\leq n\leq2m.}
$$

The bounds use $1\leq s\leq m$, valid for a nonconstant [elliptic function](../../../../../../elliptic-function.md).

For the [Weierstrass elliptic function](../../../../../../weierstrass-elliptic-function.md) $\wp$, $f=\wp\wp'$ has a single pole of order five, so its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) has order six. For the second example choose $c$ not a critical value of $\wp$ and choose $a$ away from zero and the two points where $\wp=c$. Then

$$
f(z)=\wp(z-a)+\frac{\wp'(z)}{\wp(z)-c}
$$

has a double pole at $a$ and three simple poles: the quotient has residue $-2$ at zero and residue 1 at each of its two other poles. No locations overlap. Hence its order is five and its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) has order $3+2+2+2=9$, as required.
