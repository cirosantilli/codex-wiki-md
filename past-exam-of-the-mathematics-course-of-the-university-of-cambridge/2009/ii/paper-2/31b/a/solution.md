<h1 id="31b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Characteristics beginning at $\xi$ have $x=\xi+t u_I(\xi)$ and carry the constant value $u_I(\xi)$. In the sloping interval, $x=t+(1-t)\xi$. For $0\leq t<1$ the resulting single-valued solution is

$$
\boxed{u(x,t)=\begin{cases}1,&x\leq t,\\(1-x)/(1-t),&t<x<1,\\0,&x\geq1.\end{cases}}
$$

It is continuous and piecewise smooth, locally Lipschitz for $t<1$, but not a classical $C^1$ solution across the two [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) corners. Its weak [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) satisfy the equation, since the function itself has no jumps there. The sloping gradient is $-1/(1-t)$ and blows up at $t=1$, when those characteristics coalesce.

Thus **the continuous characteristic description has maximal interval $0\leq t<1$**. If “[weak solution](../../../../../../weak-solution.md)” is taken literally, it does not cease to exist then: the [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) continuation is a shock with left/right states 1 and 0 and Rankine-Hugoniot speed $1/2$, situated at $x=(t+1)/2$ for $t\geq1$. It is a global [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) [weak solution](../../../../../../weak-solution.md), with the step at $t=1$ as its limiting trace. This distinguishes breakdown of the characteristic parametrization from nonexistence of a weak continuation.
