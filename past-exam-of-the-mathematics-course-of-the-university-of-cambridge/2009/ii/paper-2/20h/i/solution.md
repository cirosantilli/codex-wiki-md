<h1 id="20h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose a nonzero [integral ideal](../../../../../../integral-ideal.md) $I$ in the inverse of any desired [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md). For the nonzero element $x\in I$ supplied by the norm bound, $J=(x)I^{-1}$ is [integral](../../../../../../integral.md), belongs to the desired class and satisfies

$$
N(J)=\frac{|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(x)|}{N(I)}\leq C_K.
$$

There are only finitely many [integral ideals](../../../../../../integral-ideal.md) of bounded norm: identify $\mathcal O_K$ with $\mathbb Z^n$ as an additive group, so ideals of norm $m$ are among its [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of index $m$, of which there are finitely many. Since every [ideal class](../../../../../../ideal-class.md) has one of these bounded-norm representatives, **the [ideal class group](../../../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is finite**.
