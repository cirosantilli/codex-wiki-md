<h1 id="25h/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the positively oriented simple closed boundary, the [turning tangent theorem](../../../../../../turning-tangent-theorem.md) gives $\int k(s)\,ds=2\pi$. If $L$ is its length, continuity gives a point with $k(s_0)\leq2\pi/L$. The [planar isoperimetric inequality](../../../../../../planar-isoperimetric-inequality.md) gives $L^2\geq4\pi A(K)$, hence

$$
\boxed{k(s_0)\leq\frac{2\pi}{L}\leq\sqrt{\frac\pi{A(K)}}.}
$$

For the reversed orientation the [curvature](../../../../../../curvature.md) [integral](../../../../../../integral.md) is $-2\pi$, so a point of nonpositive [curvature](../../../../../../curvature.md) already proves the stated positive upper bound. The region-bounding interpretation is the ordinary Jordan-curve one; self-intersections would require specifying a different notion of enclosed area.
