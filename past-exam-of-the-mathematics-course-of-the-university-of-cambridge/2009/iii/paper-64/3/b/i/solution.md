<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The initial-value problem is a [Riemann problem](../../../../../../../riemann-problem.md). Both the one-dimensional [Euler equations](../../../../../../../euler-equations-for-an-inviscid-fluid.md) and the two constant initial states are unchanged under the simultaneous scaling $(x,t)\mapsto(\lambda x,\lambda t)$ for $\lambda>0$. There is no imposed length or time scale. Rescaling a physically admissible [entropy solution](../../../../../../../entropy-solution.md) therefore gives another solution with exactly the same initial data. Uniqueness of the physical Riemann solution makes the two coincide, so

$$
\boxed{(\rho,u,p)(x,t)=(\rho,u,p)(x/t).}
$$

The smooth pieces are consequently those in part (a), while shocks and contacts lie on rays $x=st$. Across shocks one uses the conservative mass, momentum and total-[energy](../../../../../../../energy.md) equations and the [entropy](../../../../../../../entropy.md) admissibility condition, rather than extending the smooth [entropy](../../../../../../../entropy.md)-advection equation through the discontinuity. Scale invariance by itself, without selecting the unique admissible solution, would not justify the uniqueness step.
