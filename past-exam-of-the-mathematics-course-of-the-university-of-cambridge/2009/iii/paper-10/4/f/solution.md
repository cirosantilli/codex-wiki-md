<h1 id="4/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Part (c) makes $G(A)$ relatively open in $G_B(A)$. It is also relatively closed: if a sequence in $G(A)$ converges to $a\in G_B(A)$, part (e) forbids $a\notin G(A)$. Sequences detect closure here because the topology comes from a norm.

Intersecting this clopen subset with any [connected component](../../../../../../connected-component.md) $C$ of $G_B(A)$ gives a clopen subset of the connected space $C$. It is therefore either empty or all of $C$. Thus **$G(A)$ is the union of those [connected components](../../../../../../connected-component.md) of $G_B(A)$ that it meets**. This is [connected-component permanence of subalgebra invertibility](../../../../../../connected-component-permanence-of-subalgebra-invertibility.md); it does not require [connected components](../../../../../../connected-component.md) to be path connected.
