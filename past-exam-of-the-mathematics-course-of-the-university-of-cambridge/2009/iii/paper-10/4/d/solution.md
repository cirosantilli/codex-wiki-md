<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $c=(1-ab)^{-1}\in A$. From the two inverse identities we have $cab=c-1=abc$. The candidate inverse is

$$
\boxed{(1-ba)^{-1}=1+bca.}
$$

Indeed

$$
(1-ba)(1+bca)=1+b(c-1-abc)a=1,
$$

and

$$
(1+bca)(1-ba)=1+b(c-1-cab)a=1.
$$

Both products have been checked, so this is valid in a noncommutative [Banach algebra](../../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md). It is the identity underlying the [nonzero spectra of products in opposite orders](../../../../../../nonzero-spectra-of-products-in-opposite-orders.md) result.
