<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The kernel and cokernel of an equivariant [Fredholm operator](../../../../../../fredholm-operator.md) are finite-dimensional representations. Define its [equivariant Fredholm index](../../../../../../equivariant-fredholm-index.md) in the [representation ring of a compact group](../../../../../../representation-ring-of-a-compact-group.md) by

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ind}_G S=[\ker S]-[\operatorname{coker}S].}
$$

For $G=SU(2)$ write $H=\widehat\bigoplus_n E_n\otimes\mathbb C^{m_n}$ and $H'=\widehat\bigoplus_n E_n\otimes\mathbb C^{m'_n}$, with finite multiplicities. By [Schur lemma](../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md) an intertwiner has blocks $I_{E_n}\otimes s_n$, where $s_n:\mathbb C^{m_n}\to\mathbb C^{m'_n}$. If its rank is $r_n$, the kernel and cokernel multiplicities are $m_n-r_n$ and $m'_n-r_n$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ind}_G S=\sum_n(m_n-m'_n)[E_n].}
$$

Only finitely many coefficients can be nonzero, because the global kernel and cokernel are finite-dimensional. Closed range is part of the [Fredholm](../../../../../../fredholm-operator.md) hypothesis and supplies the uniform lower bound on the remaining blocks; multiplicity matching alone would not establish that hypothesis. The ordinary index is obtained by taking dimensions, $\operatorname{ind}S=\sum_n(n+1)(m_n-m'_n)$. This calculation applies to any compact group with its finite-dimensional irreducibles in place of the $E_n$.
