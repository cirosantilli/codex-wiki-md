<h1 id="4/4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Take the **[Hopf map](../../../../../../hopf-map.md) $\eta:S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2$**, given by $\eta(z_0,z_1)=[z_0:z_1]$. The domain has positive-dimensional [homology](../../../../../../homology-split.md) only in degree three, while $H_3(S^2;\mathbb Z)=0$. In degree two the source group is zero, and all remaining positive-degree source groups are zero too. Hence the induced map on [homology](../../../../../../homology-split.md) is zero in every positive degree.

For nontriviality, use the [Hopf fibration](../../../../../../hopf-fibration.md) $S^1\to S^3\xrightarrow{\eta}S^2$. It is a [fiber bundle](../../../../../../fiber-bundle-split.md): phase multiplication gives each fibre, and choosing a normalized representative on either standard projective chart gives a local trivialization. The theorem invoked is the [long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration](../../../../../../long-exact-sequence-of-homotopy-groups-of-a-fibration.md). Since the universal cover of $S^1$ is contractible, $\pi_2(S^1)=\pi_3(S^1)=0$. Its exact sequence consequently gives an isomorphism

$$
\eta_*:\pi_3(S^3)\longrightarrow\pi_3(S^2).
$$

The identity class of $S^3$ is nonzero, since an identity map cannot be null-homotopic while inducing the identity on $H_3(S^3;\mathbb Z)$. Its image under $\eta_*$ is the class of $\eta$, so that class is nonzero. **The Hopf map is not homotopic to a constant despite inducing zero on all positive-degree homology.**
