<h1 id="1/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use integral [cellular homology](../../../../../../cellular-chain-complex.md). All three vertices become a single vertex, and all three oriented edges become one oriented [circle](../../../../../../circle.md), with generator $a$. The face goes round that circle three times in the same direction. Thus the quotient has a [CW complex](../../../../../../cw-complex.md) structure with one cell in each of dimensions zero, one and two, and [cellular chain complex](../../../../../../cellular-chain-complex.md)

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\;3\;}\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\;0\;}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

The degree-two boundary is injective and its cokernel is $\mathbb Z/3$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&j=0,\\\mathbb Z/3,&j=1,\\0,&j\geq2.\end{cases}}
$$

The attaching word is $a^3$, so the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) also gives $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z/3$. This is the degree-three circle-attachment model of a [Moore space](../../../../../../moore-space-algebraic-topology.md).
