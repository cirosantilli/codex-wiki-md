<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For the triangle quotient, the [universal coefficient theorem for homology](../../../../../universal-coefficient-theorem-for-homology.md) gives $H_j(X;\mathbb F_2)=0$ for every $j>0$, because its only positive integral [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) is $\mathbb Z/3$. A connected positive-dimensional [closed manifold](../../../../../closed-manifold.md) has a nonzero top mod-two [fundamental class](../../../../../fundamental-class.md), independently of orientability. Thus this quotient cannot be [homotopy equivalent](../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) to such a [manifold](../../../../../topological-manifold.md). A connected zero-dimensional [closed manifold](../../../../../closed-manifold.md) is a point, which its nonzero $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ also excludes.

For the attached-band space, the same [universal coefficient theorem for homology](../../../../../universal-coefficient-theorem-for-homology.md) gives nonzero $H_2(X;\mathbb F_2)$ and zero homology above degree two. A connected [closed manifold](../../../../../closed-manifold.md) homotopy equivalent to it would consequently have dimension two. Its [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) would be one. The [classification theorem for surfaces](../../../../../classification-theorem-for-surfaces.md) says that a closed connected surface of Euler characteristic one is the [real projective plane](../../../../../real-projective-plane.md). But its integral $H_1$ is $\mathbb Z/2$, differing from both possibilities computed in part 2. Thus this space is never [homotopy equivalent](../../../../../homotopy-inverse.md) to a [closed manifold](../../../../../closed-manifold.md).

The antipodal [mapping torus](../../../../../mapping-torus.md) is already a [closed manifold](../../../../../closed-manifold.md): gluing the two boundary components of $S^n\times[0,1]$ by a diffeomorphism gives a smooth $(n+1)$-manifold without boundary. For $n\geq1$, it is orientable exactly when $(-1)^{n+1}=1$, namely when $n$ is odd. In that case $-I$ on $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ is isotopic to the identity through rotations, so the [mapping torus](../../../../../mapping-torus.md) is diffeomorphic to $S^n\times S^1$. The $n=0$ case is a circle as above. **Only the third family can be homotopy equivalent to a closed manifold.**
