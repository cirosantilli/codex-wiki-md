<h1 id="5/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

**False.** Take $M=\mathbb{CP}^2\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $N=S^2\times S^2$, with their standard product and connected-sum [orientations](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md). They are closed [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) four-manifolds. For the [connected sum](../../../../../../connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds.md), removing a four-ball leaves the fundamental group unchanged, and the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) across the separating three-sphere preserves simple connectivity. The [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) computes integral [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z^2,\mathbb Z$ in degrees zero, two and four, and zero in other degrees. The [Künneth theorem](../../../../../../kunneth-theorem.md) gives the same groups for $S^2\times S^2$. Thus their [Betti numbers](../../../../../../betti-number.md) agree and all their integral [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) is torsion-free.

Their [cohomology rings](../../../../../../cohomology-ring.md) nevertheless differ. In $M$, let $u,v$ come from the two projective summands and let $z$ generate degree four. The [cohomology ring of the connected sum of two complex projective planes](../../../../../../cohomology-ring-of-the-connected-sum-of-two-complex-projective-planes.md) has

$$
u^2=v^2=z,\qquad uv=0.
$$

These relations follow by using cycles in the separate summands and the degree-one pinch projections onto them. In $N$, the sphere-factor generators $x,y$ satisfy

$$
x^2=y^2=0,\qquad xy=z.
$$

Consequently every degree-two class of $N$ has square

$$
(ax+by)^2=2abz,
$$

an even multiple of the top generator, whereas $u^2$ in $M$ is a primitive generator. An integral graded-ring isomorphism cannot change this property, since it sends a generator of $H^4$ to its positive or negative. A [homotopy equivalence](../../../../../../homotopy-equivalence.md) induces a graded-ring isomorphism, so none exists. Equivalently, their [intersection forms](../../../../../../intersection-form.md) are $\operatorname{diag}(1,1)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, an odd form and an even form respectively.
