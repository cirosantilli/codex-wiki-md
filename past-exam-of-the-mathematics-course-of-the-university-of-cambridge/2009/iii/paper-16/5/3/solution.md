<h1 id="5/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**True for two positive-dimensional factors.** In fact degree three can be replaced by any nonzero [mapping degree](../../../../../../degree-of-a-continuous-mapping.md). Suppose $M=X^k\times Y^{m-k}$ with $0<k<m$. The factors are closed and connected. They are also orientable: an orientation-reversing loop in either factor would reverse the product [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) while the other factor stays fixed, contradicting the orientation of $M$.

Let $\omega_X,\omega_Y$ be their integral top [orientation classes](../../../../../../fundamental-class.md), and let $\alpha,\beta$ be the corresponding projection pullbacks to $M$. The [Künneth theorem](../../../../../../kunneth-theorem.md) and the product [fundamental class](../../../../../../fundamental-class.md) give

$$
\alpha\smile\beta=\pm\omega_M\in H^m(M;\mathbb Z).
$$

For $f:S^m\to M$, both $f^*\alpha$ and $f^*\beta$ vanish, since a sphere has no [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) in those intermediate positive degrees. Naturality of the [cup product](../../../../../../cup-product.md) therefore gives

$$
f^*\omega_M=\pm f^*\alpha\smile f^*\beta=0.
$$

On the other hand, the definition of degree three gives $f^*\omega_M=3\omega_{S^m}\ne0$. This contradiction proves the [nonzero degree from a sphere obstructs manifold products](../../../../../../nonzero-degree-from-a-sphere-obstructs-manifold-products.md) assertion. **The product with a zero-dimensional point factor is the excluded trivial case.**
