<h1 id="4/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Use [prior elicitation](../../../../../../prior-elicitation.md) to make the qualitative judgments quantitative: ask whether $10\%$ represents a mean, median or mode, and what probability “unlikely above $15\%$” should mean, for example a $5\%$ upper-tail probability. A [Beta distribution](../../../../../../beta-distribution.md) on $(0,1)$ is a convenient family. If $0.1$ is taken as the mean, write its parameters as $a_q=0.1\kappa$, $b_q=0.9\kappa$, then choose $\kappa$ so that the desired tail probability above $0.15$ is obtained. Check its quantiles and [prior predictive distribution](../../../../../../bayesian-model-evidence.md) for plausibility, and assess sensitivity to reasonable alternative elicited values. **The information suggests an informative prior near $0.1$, but does not uniquely determine its parameters.**
