<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Derrick scaling of the Abelian Higgs energy](../../../../../../derrick-scaling-of-the-abelian-higgs-energy.md) must rescale the [gauge potential](../../../../../../gauge-field.md) as well as the [Higgs field](../../../../../../higgs-field.md). Set

$$
\Phi_\lambda(x)=\Phi(\lambda x),\qquad A_{j,\lambda}(x)=\lambda A_j(\lambda x).
$$

Then $B_\lambda(x)=\lambda^2B(\lambda x)$ and

$$
(\partial_j-iA_{j,\lambda})\Phi_\lambda(x)=\lambda(D_j\Phi)(\lambda x).
$$

Let $E_B=\int B^2$, $E_D=\int|D\Phi|^2$ and $E_P=\frac14\int(1-|\Phi|^2)^2$, with all integrals over the plane. Changing variables gives

$$
V(\lambda)=\lambda^2E_B+E_D+\lambda^{-2}E_P.
$$

At a static solution, $V'(1)=2E_B-2E_P=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R^2}B^2\,d^2x=\frac14\int_{\mathbb R^2}(1-|\Phi|^2)^2\,d^2x.}
$$

The covariant-gradient contribution is scale invariant, while the magnetic and potential terms balance. This is why the scalar-only nonexistence conclusion does not extend to the [Abelian Higgs model](../../../../../../abelian-higgs-model.md).
