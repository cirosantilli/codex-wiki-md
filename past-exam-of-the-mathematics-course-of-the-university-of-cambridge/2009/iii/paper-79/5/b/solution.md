<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $y$ from the first wall to the second, with $f>0$. Negligible transverse velocity and cross-channel [geostrophic balance](../../../../../../geostrophic-balance.md) give $fu=-g\partial_y(h+H)=-gh_y$, since the bed elevation $H(x)$ is independent of $y$. Therefore the [volume flux](../../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md) is

$$
Q=\int_0^{b}uh\,dy=-\frac gf\int_0^{b}h h_y\,dy=\boxed{\frac g{2f}(h_1^2-h_2^2).}
$$

For positive downstream [volume flux](../../../../../../volumetric-flow-rate.md), the layer is deeper at the first wall: $h_1>h_2$. This calculation does not require zero [shallow-water potential vorticity](../../../../../../shallow-water-potential-vorticity.md).

Here $h_j$ must denote the wall depth above the local bed. If the surface heights are instead measured from a fixed vertical datum, write them as $\eta_j$ and use $h_j=\eta_j-H(x)$, giving $Q=g[(\eta_1-H)^2-(\eta_2-H)^2]/(2f)$. The compact formula thus uses a bed-relative meaning of the wall heights.
