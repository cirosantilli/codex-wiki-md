<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [Boussinesq approximation](../../../../../../boussinesq-approximation.md) retains small density differences only in the gravitational buoyancy force and uses a constant reference density in inertia and volume conservation. The [entrainment coefficient](../../../../../../entrainment-coefficient.md) $\alpha$ specifies the inward velocity of ambient fluid relative to the thermal boundary, $u_e=\alpha W$. Turbulent interface motions engulf ambient water and mix it into the coherent rising volume.

For a spherical [buoyant thermal](../../../../../../buoyant-thermal.md), $V=4\pi a^3/3$. To leading order in the dilute gas fraction, expansion of the gas does not dominate the total-volume balance, so [fluid entrainment](../../../../../../fluid-entrainment.md) gives

$$
\frac{dV}{dt}=4\pi a^2\alpha W.
$$

But $dV/dt=4\pi a^2 da/dt$ and $dz/dt=W$. Therefore, wherever the thermal is rising,

$$
\boxed{\frac{da}{dz}=\alpha,\qquad a=a_0+\alpha z,\qquad 2a=2a_0+2\alpha z.}
$$

This growth law for a [dilute bubbly thermal](../../../../../../dilute-bubbly-thermal.md) is independent of stratification under the constant-$\alpha$ model. Stratification changes the speed and eventual stopping height; it does not enter this volume-per-distance relation. In real flows the entrainment coefficient itself can vary with stratification and flow structure.
