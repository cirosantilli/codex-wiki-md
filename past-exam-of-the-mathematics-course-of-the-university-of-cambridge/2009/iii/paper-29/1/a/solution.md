The [previsible sigma-algebra](../../../../../../predictable-sigma-algebra.md) $\mathcal P$ on $\Omega\times[0,\infty)$ is generated by $E\times\{0\}$ with $E\in\mathcal F_0$ and by $E\times(s,t]$ with $E\in\mathcal F_s$, $0\leq s<t$. Equivalently it is the smallest sigma-algebra making every left-continuous [adapted process](../../../../../../adapted-process.md) measurable. A [previsible process](../../../../../../predictable-process.md) is a process measurable for $\mathcal P$.

A bounded [simple predictable process](../../../../../../simple-predictable-process.md) has finitely many time intervals and bounded coefficients known at their left endpoints. One may use deterministic endpoints, or the customary more general [elementary predictable process with stopping-time intervals](../../../../../../elementary-predictable-process-with-stopping-time-intervals.md):

$$
H_s=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}h_j\mathbf1_{(T_j,T_{j+1}]}(s),\qquad0=T_0\leq\cdots\leq T_m=\infty,\qquad h_j\in L^\infty(\mathcal F_{T_j}).
$$

A bounded $\mathcal F_0$-measurable value at time zero may be added; it does not affect integration against a [continuous martingale](../../../../../../continuous-martingale.md). A process with finite support is included by adding a zero coefficient on its terminal interval. Both versions of simple processes satisfy the arguments below. The elementary [stochastic integral](../../../../../../stochastic-integral.md) is

$$
\boxed{(H\cdot M)_t=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}h_j\bigl(M_{t\wedge T_{j+1}}-M_{t\wedge T_j}\bigr).}
$$

When a [stopping time](../../../../../../stopping-time.md) is infinite, use the terminal value $M_\infty$, which exists in $L^2$ and almost surely for an [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../../../../l2-bounded-continuous-martingale.md). Refining intervals leaves this definition unchanged.

For a [continuous local martingale](../../../../../../continuous-local-martingale.md) $M$, its [quadratic variation](../../../../../../quadratic-variation.md) $[M]$ is the unique continuous adapted nondecreasing process starting at zero such that $(M_t-M_0)^2-[M]_t$ is a [local martingale](../../../../../../local-martingale.md). It is predictable because it is continuous and adapted. To recover it from the path, use refining deterministic grids, for example $t_k^{(n)}=k2^{-n}$, and form

$$
Q_t^{(n)}=\sum_{k\geq0}\bigl(M_{t\wedge t_{k+1}^{(n)}}-M_{t\wedge t_k^{(n)}}\bigr)^2.
$$

Only finitely many increments are nonzero on a compact time interval. Then

$$
\boxed{Q^{(n)}\longrightarrow[M]\text{ uniformly on compacts in probability}.}
$$

In particular each fixed-time sum converges in probability, and a suitable subsequence converges uniformly on compact intervals almost surely. The partial last grid interval is included in this path approximation.
