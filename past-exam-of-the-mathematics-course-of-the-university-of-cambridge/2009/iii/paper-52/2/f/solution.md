<h1 id="2/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

[Density operator controllability](../../../../../../density-operator-controllability.md) cannot defeat unitary invariants: it grants access to the [unitary orbit of a density operator](../../../../../../unitary-orbit-of-a-density-operator.md), whose members have the same [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md), rather than to every [density operator](../../../../../../density-matrix.md). In the [Hilbert-Schmidt inner product](../../../../../../hilbert-schmidt-inner-product.md) orthonormal normalization of this question,

$$
\|s_0\|^2=\frac{(0.5)^2+(0.5)^2}{2}=\frac14,
\qquad
\|s_1\|^2=\frac12.
$$

[Quantum Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) evolution preserves this norm, so **the target is not reachable from the stated initial state**. Equivalently, their values of [purity of a density operator](../../../../../../purity-of-a-density-operator.md) are $1/2+1/4=3/4$ and $1/2+1/2=1$: the initial state is a [mixed state](../../../../../../mixed-state.md) and the target a [pure state](../../../../../../pure-state.md). Dissipation, an appropriate measurement protocol or an auxiliary system with additional resources would be needed to change the [spectrum](../../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md); freely controlling the closed-system [Quantum Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) alone does not suffice.
