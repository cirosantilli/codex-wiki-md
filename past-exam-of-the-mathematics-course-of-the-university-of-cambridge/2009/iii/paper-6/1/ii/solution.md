<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [derived series of a Lie algebra](../../../../../../derived-series-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is

$$
\mathfrak b^{(0)}=\mathfrak b,\qquad \mathfrak b^{(j+1)}=[\mathfrak b^{(j)},\mathfrak b^{(j)}].
$$

The algebra is a [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md) if $\mathfrak b^{(m)}=0$ for some $m$. Here a bracket of subspaces means the [linear span](../../../../../../linear-span.md) of their [Lie brackets](../../../../../../lie-bracket.md).

The [Lie theorem](../../../../../../lie-s-theorem.md) states that a nonzero finite-dimensional representation of a complex [solvable Lie algebra](../../../../../../solvable-lie-algebra.md) has a common [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md); equivalently, its operators can be simultaneously upper triangularized. Applied to an [Irreducible Lie algebra representation](../../../../../../irreducible-lie-algebra-representation.md), the common [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) gives a nonzero invariant line, which must be the entire module. A scalar action $\rho(x)=\lambda(x)$ respects the [Lie bracket](../../../../../../lie-bracket.md) exactly when $\lambda([x,y])=0$. Thus, in the finite-dimensional setting,

$$
\boxed{\text{irreducibles are }\mathbb C_\lambda,\qquad \lambda\in(\mathfrak b/[\mathfrak b,\mathfrak b])^*.}
$$

Every functional displayed gives a one-dimensional irreducible, and two such modules are isomorphic exactly when their functionals agree.

The finite-dimensional qualification is essential here, although the printed wording does not repeat it. For example, let $[x,y]=y$ and act on $\mathbb C[t]$ by $xf(t)=tf(t)$ and $yf(t)=f(t-1)$. These operators satisfy $[x,y]f=yf$. A nonzero submodule stable under $x$ is an [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $p(t)\mathbb C[t]$. Stability under $y$ forces $p(t)\mid p(t-1)$, so the two [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), having the same degree and leading coefficient, are equal. In characteristic zero a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) invariant under a nonzero shift is constant. The module is therefore an [infinite-dimensional simple module for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra](../../../../../../infinite-dimensional-simple-module-for-the-two-dimensional-affine-lie-algebra.md). It rules out extending the one-dimensional classification to unrestricted representations.
