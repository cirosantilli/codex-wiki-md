<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Microscopically different systems can have the same [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) and appropriately normalized scaling functions. This universality means that they belong to one [universality class](../../../../../../universality-class.md) when coarse-graining brings them to the same [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md). Spatial dimension, [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) symmetry and the range of the interactions affect that classification; changing a lattice spacing or a microscopic coupling generally changes nonuniversal amplitudes and the critical temperature instead.

In the scalar short-range [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md), a positive quartic term and one thermal tuning produce ordinary Ising-type criticality. A generic sextic term does not change that fixed point, but tuning the quartic coefficient to zero exposes a different [tricritical point](../../../../../../tricritical-point.md) with different exponents. The [mean-field approximation](../../../../../../mean-field-approximation.md) itself neglects fluctuations. Its numerical exponents are reliable only when the [Ginzburg criterion](../../../../../../ginzburg-criterion.md) makes those fluctuations subleading; the ordinary scalar [upper critical dimension](../../../../../../upper-critical-dimension.md) is four, while the tricritical one is three. At an upper critical dimension logarithmic corrections can remain.
