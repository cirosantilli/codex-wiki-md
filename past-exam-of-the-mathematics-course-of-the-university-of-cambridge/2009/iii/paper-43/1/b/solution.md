<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the [connected correlation function](../../../../../../connected-correlation-function.md) $G(x)=\langle M(x)M(0)\rangle-\langle M\rangle^2$, since the disconnected constant in an ordered phase does not measure fluctuation decay. Away from criticality its large-distance exponential defines the [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md):

$$
\xi^{-1}=-\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac1r\log|G(r)|,
$$

when that limit exists. Algebraic prefactors do not affect this definition.

Expand the [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md) about a stable homogeneous minimum $M_0$. With $A=V''(M_0)>0$, the quadratic fluctuation energy is $\frac12\int d^Dx[\kappa|\nabla\delta M|^2+A(\delta M)^2]$. The [Gaussian functional integral](../../../../../../gaussian-functional-integral.md) gives

$$
\widetilde G(k)=\frac{k_BT}{\kappa k^2+A},\qquad \boxed{\xi=\sqrt{\kappa/A}.}
$$

This [Ornstein--Zernike correlation function](../../../../../../ornstein-zernike-correlation-function.md) shows why the curvature of the free-energy minimum controls correlations. At a [continuous phase transition](../../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md) that curvature vanishes, allowing long-range fluctuations. In the ordinary quartic theory with $u>0$, $A=r$ above the transition and $A=-2r$ on either ordered branch below, so $\xi\propto|r|^{-1/2}$ on both sides. At an equilibrium first-order transition the competing minima generally retain nonzero curvature, and the [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md) remains finite. The limit of local stability at a [spinodal point](../../../../../../spinodal-point.md) is a distinct mean-field phenomenon.
