<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a ferromagnet the scalar [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) is the spontaneous [spin magnetization](../../../../../../spin-magnetization.md). It distinguishes a symmetry-invariant phase with zero mean from one of two phases with a nonzero mean of opposite signs. At zero field, a finite system with [spin inversion symmetry](../../../../../../spin-inversion-symmetry.md) has exactly zero mean. The spontaneous value is defined by taking the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md) first and then sending the symmetry-breaking [conjugate field](../../../../../../field-conjugate-to-an-order-parameter.md) to zero from one side.

A [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md) promotes the coarse magnetization to a scalar field $M(x)$ and uses

$$
\mathcal F[M]=\int d^Dx\left[\frac\kappa2|\nabla M|^2+\frac r2M^2+\frac u4M^4+\frac v6M^6-hM\right],\qquad \kappa>0,\ v>0.
$$

At $h=0$, [spin inversion symmetry](../../../../../../spin-inversion-symmetry.md) permits only even local powers. The gradient term penalizes spatial variations. The equilibrium statistical weight is $e^{-\mathcal F/(k_BT)}$; the [Landau approximation](../../../../../../landau-approximation.md) chooses a stable minimum of $\mathcal F$, whereas the full functional integral retains fluctuations around it. In a homogeneous phase this gives the [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) $h=rM+uM^3+vM^5$. The field $h$ is conjugate to the [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md), with $M=-\partial f_{\rm eq}/\partial h$.
