<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The first-order coexistence curve approaches the continuous curve as $u\uparrow0$: both meet at $r=u=h=0$. To obtain this [tricritical point](../../../../../../tricritical-point.md), two even coefficients must be tuned to zero, while the sextic coefficient remains positive. A nonzero [conjugate field](../../../../../../field-conjugate-to-an-order-parameter.md) removes the zero-field inversion symmetry and leads to the wing structure described in the root solution.

At $u=h=0$, the [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) becomes $0=rM+vM^5$. For $r<0$,

$$
M^4=-r/v,\qquad V_{\min}=-\frac{|r|^{3/2}}{3\sqrt v},\qquad V''(M)=4|r|.
$$

Above the transition $M=0$ and $V''(0)=r$. These are stable branches of a bounded potential, and the ordered value tends continuously to zero at $r=0$, but with $M\propto(-r)^{1/4}$ rather than the ordinary square-root law. **Tricritical behavior requires $r=u=0$, $h=0$ and $v>0$; a generic path with fixed $u>0$ instead has ordinary critical behavior.**
