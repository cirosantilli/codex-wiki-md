<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use separate [randomization](../../../../../../randomization.md) lists for women with and without a self-reported history of depression. Within each [stratum](../../../../../../stratum.md), the central service could assign intervention and control in a $1:1$ ratio using randomly permuted blocks, preferably with concealed and varying block sizes. This is [stratified randomization](../../../../../../stratified-randomization.md); the internet service can retain [allocation concealment](../../../../../../allocation-concealment.md) because the recruiting staff do not know the next assignment.

History is plausibly prognostic for the outcome. Keeping its distribution similar between arms reduces the risk of a chance imbalance in that important baseline factor and can improve precision. It also allows a comparison within each history [stratum](../../../../../../stratum.md). Stratification does not guarantee balance of every other [covariate](../../../../../../covariate.md), and staff must not be allowed to choose assignments. The [regression model](../../../../../../regression-model.md) can retain history as a [baseline covariate](../../../../../../baseline-covariate.md), respecting the design.
