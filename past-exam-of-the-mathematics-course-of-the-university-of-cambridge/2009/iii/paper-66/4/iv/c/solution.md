<h1 id="4/iv/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A survey depth $R\sim400\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ makes wavelengths substantially larger than its volume poorly measurable. A characteristic inverse depth is $1/R\simeq2.5\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}$; a Fourier wavelength that fits across a dimension $L$ instead requires $k\gtrsim2\pi/L$. For $L$ between $R$ and $2R$, this is roughly $0.008$--$0.016\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}$. A limited sky patch can have smaller transverse dimensions, so its [survey window function](../../../../../../../survey-window-function.md) raises the practical minimum in those directions. An indicative useful band for a broad survey is **around $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}$ and above**, including scales near equality if its volume is large enough.

There is no upper [wavenumber](../../../../../../../wavenumber.md) determined by depth alone. Mean separation of observed galaxies, [galaxy power spectrum shot noise](../../../../../../../galaxy-power-spectrum-shot-noise.md), angular/redshift resolution, and nonlinear clustering set it. Straightforward linear inference is typically restricted to roughly $k\lesssim0.1\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}$ at low redshift, with smaller scales requiring modelling. The magnitude limit does not specify the galaxy [comoving number density](../../../../../../../comoving-number-density.md), so a more precise sampling cutoff cannot be deduced from these data.

The two measurements are complementary. The [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background.md) samples a large volume at an early epoch when relevant perturbations are mostly linear, and is free of [galaxy bias](../../../../../../../galaxy-bias.md); its matter-spectrum inference is indirect and depends on projection, cosmological parameters, foregrounds, and [cosmic variance](../../../../../../../cosmic-variance.md). A [galaxy survey](../../../../../../../galaxy-survey.md) gives three-dimensional late-time information and can reach smaller scales, but observes biased, discrete tracers: [galaxy bias](../../../../../../../galaxy-bias.md), nonlinear gravitational growth, [redshift-space distortions](../../../../../../../redshift-space-distortions.md), selection effects, and [galaxy power spectrum shot noise](../../../../../../../galaxy-power-spectrum-shot-noise.md) must be controlled. Combining both constrains the initial spectrum and its subsequent growth more strongly than either alone, while their overlapping modes test the inferred evolution.
