<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [comoving particle horizon](../../../../../../comoving-particle-horizon.md) is the maximum radial distance a photon could have travelled since the initial [Big Bang](../../../../../../big-bang.md), expressed in [comoving coordinates](../../../../../../comoving-coordinate.md). It describes possible past causal contact, whereas a [cosmological event horizon](../../../../../../cosmological-event-horizon.md) concerns photons that can reach an observer in the future.

For a radial [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) in the [FLRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md), $dr/\sqrt{1-kr^2}=c\,dt/a$. Spatial flatness sets $k=0$, so with $a_0=1$,

$$
r_p(t)=c\int_0^t\frac{dt'}{a(t')}=c\int_0^{a(t)}\frac{da'}{a'^2H(a')}.
$$

The given [Hubble parameter](../../../../../../hubble-parameter.md) then supplies $a^2H=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}+\Omega_{m,0}a+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}a^4}$. Since [cosmological redshift](../../../../../../cosmological-redshift.md) obeys $a=(1+z)^{-1}$,

$$
\boxed{r_p(z)=\frac c{H_0}\int_0^{(1+z)^{-1}}\frac{da}{\sqrt{\Omega_{r,0}+\Omega_{m,0}a+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}a^4}}.}
$$

The corresponding proper horizon at that epoch is $a(z)r_p(z)$.
