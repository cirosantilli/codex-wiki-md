<h1 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The [quasar proximity effect](../../../../../../proximity-effect-astrophysics.md) is weaker [Lyman-alpha forest](../../../../../../lyman-alpha-forest.md) absorption near a [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md)'s emission redshift. Extra photons from the [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) increase the [photoionization rate](../../../../../../photoionization-rate.md) and reduce the neutral fraction without necessarily reducing the total gas density.

For isotropic [specific intensity](../../../../../../specific-intensity.md) $J_\nu$ and a steady, isotropically radiating [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) with [spectral luminosity](../../../../../../spectral-luminosity.md) $L_\nu$, the background and local rates are

$$
\Gamma_{\rm bg}=4\pi\int_{\nu_0}^\infty\frac{J_\nu\sigma_\nu}{h\nu}\,d\nu,\qquad\Gamma_Q(r)=\int_{\nu_0}^\infty\frac{L_\nu\sigma_\nu}{4\pi r^2h\nu}\,d\nu.
$$

Here $r$ is a proper separation small enough that attenuation and expansion across it can initially be neglected, and $\sigma_\nu$ is the hydrogen photoionization cross-section. At fixed density and temperature, [photoionization equilibrium](../../../../../../photoionization-equilibrium.md) makes the neutral [column density](../../../../../../column-density.md), and hence a given line's [optical depth](../../../../../../optical-depth.md), scale as

$$
\boxed{\frac{\tau(r)}{\tau_{\rm field}}\simeq\frac{\Gamma_{\rm bg}}{\Gamma_{\rm bg}+\Gamma_Q(r)}.}
$$

The observed [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) luminosity supplies $\Gamma_Q(r)$; the distance at which the absorption changes significantly estimates where $\Gamma_Q\sim\Gamma_{\rm bg}$. Adopting a background spectral shape then converts the inferred rate to its [specific intensity](../../../../../../specific-intensity.md), often quoted near the hydrogen [ionization energy](../../../../../../ionization-energy.md) threshold.

Important uncertainties include an enhanced gas [density contrast](../../../../../../density-contrast.md) around the host, the unobserved ionizing spectrum, anisotropic emission, source lifetime and variability, the systemic redshift and continuum placement, attenuation between source and absorber, and fluctuations between [lines of sight](../../../../../../line-of-sight.md). Higher gas density enhances recombination and can partially hide the effect, biasing a simple background estimate upward. [Dall'Aglio, Wisotzki and Worseck's 2008 measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.1767) illustrate the observed sightline scatter and the roles of variability and clustering.
