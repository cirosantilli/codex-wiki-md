<h1 id="3/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The [ionizing background](../../../../../../cosmic-ionizing-background.md) is produced mainly by hot stars in [star-forming galaxies](../../../../../../star-forming-galaxy.md) and by accretion-powered [quasars](../../../../../../quasar.md). The stellar contribution depends on [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md) and the [ionizing photon escape fraction](../../../../../../ionizing-photon-escape-fraction.md); the [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) contribution depends on its evolving [luminosity function](../../../../../../luminosity-function-astronomy.md). [Radiative transfer](../../../../../../radiative-transfer.md) through the [intergalactic medium](../../../../../../intergalactic-medium.md), including absorption, recombination emission, and cosmological redshifting, determines the radiation actually available for [photoionization](../../../../../../photoionization.md).

In the picture relevant to this 2009 paper, increasingly rare [quasars](../../../../../../quasar.md) cannot by themselves explain the high-redshift hydrogen ionizing field, so galaxies become especially important above $z\sim3$. Harder [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) radiation is important for doubly ionizing helium around $z\sim3$. Hydrogen [reionization](../../../../../../reionization.md) must be largely accomplished by $z\sim6$. At lower redshift the evolution reflects declining source emissivity together with the increasing propagation distance of ionizing photons; near [reionization](../../../../../../reionization.md), short absorption lengths and spatially uneven ionized regions prevent a simple uniform-background description. The background rate need not follow the [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) population alone: a roughly flat hydrogen [photoionization rate](../../../../../../photoionization-rate.md) over $2\lesssim z\lesssim4$ is compatible with a changing mixture of galaxies and [quasars](../../../../../../quasar.md). [Faucher-Giguère and collaborators' 2009 background calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.4554) and [their preceding opacity analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4177) give this interpretation. These redshifts describe the broad historical picture, rather than precise universal transition times.
