<h1 id="3/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Objects near the derived cutoff are too faint for their absence in ordinary star counts alone to exclude a massive [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md). The decisive test is [gravitational microlensing](../../../../../../gravitational-microlensing.md): compact objects passing in front of background stars produce transient magnification even if they emit no detectable light. For a lens at distance $D_l$ and a source at $D_s$, the physical [Einstein radius](../../../../../../einstein-radius.md) and event timescale are

$$
R_E=\sqrt{\frac{4Gm}{c^2}\frac{D_l(D_s-D_l)}{D_s}},\qquad
 t_E=R_E/v_\perp\propto\sqrt m.
$$

For $D_l=10\,\mathrm{kpc}$, $D_s=50\,\mathrm{kpc}$ and $v_\perp=200\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, a $10^{-3}M_\odot$ lens gives $t_E\simeq2.2$ days, within the regime tested by suitably sampled [gravitational microlensing](../../../../../../gravitational-microlensing.md) surveys. The [microlensing optical depth](../../../../../../microlensing-optical-depth.md) is

$$
\tau=\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\int_0^{D_s}\rho_l(D_l)\frac{D_l(D_s-D_l)}{D_s}\,dD_l,
$$

so at fixed compact-object [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) it is independent of the individual lens [mass](../../../../../../mass.md): reducing individual [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) changes the event durations rather than hiding the total lensing probability.

[EROS observations of the Magellanic Clouds](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0607207) found far fewer candidate events than a halo dominated by compact objects would predict and excluded such objects as the principal Galactic halo component over $6\times10^{-8}<m/M_\odot<15$, under their tested halo models. Their often-quoted eight-percent bound is specifically near $0.4M_\odot$, not a uniform bound at every lens [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). **A halo dominated by the approximately Jupiter-mass objects needed here is incompatible with those microlensing constraints.** Deep optical and [infrared](../../../../../../infrared.md) counts also restrict a large population of luminous low-mass stars and [brown dwarfs](../../../../../../brown-dwarf.md), while the [cosmic baryon fraction](../../../../../../cosmic-baryon-fraction.md) provides a separate constraint on explaining all cosmological [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md) with baryonic objects.
