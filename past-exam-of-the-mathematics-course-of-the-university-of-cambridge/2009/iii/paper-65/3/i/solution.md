<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $x=m/M_\odot$ and absorb units into the [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md) normalization, $dN=A x^{-3}dx$. The specified [mass-luminosity relation](../../../../../../mass-luminosity-relation.md) is $L(x)=L_\odot x^3$, normalized to the Sun. Therefore the total stellar [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) and [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) are

$$
\frac{M_*}{M_\odot}=A\int_{0.1}^1x^{-2}dx=9A,
\qquad
\frac{L_*}{L_\odot}=A\int_{0.1}^1x^0dx=0.9A.
$$

The unknown normalization cancels. **The mass-to-light ratio is**

$$
\boxed{\Upsilon_* = 10\,\frac{M_\odot}{L_\odot}.}
$$

Low-mass [main sequence](../../../../../../main-sequence.md) stars dominate the stellar [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), while equal intervals of $x$ contribute equal [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) under these particular exponents. More generally the [mass-to-light ratio of a power-law stellar population](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio-of-a-power-law-stellar-population.md) with these exponents on $a<x<b$ is $(M_\odot/L_\odot)/(ab)$.
