<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The result is in the broad range expected for an old, relatively faint [stellar population](../../../../../../stellar-population.md), but is high compared with many observed stellar [mass-to-light ratios](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) of a few solar units in optical bands. The comparison must specify the band and whether the measured [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) includes [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md): for example, [early-type galaxy dynamical measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0505042) find an $I$-band normalization near $3.8$ solar units at [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) $200\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, including a contribution from [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md). That is an illustrative observational comparison, not a direct equality with a bolometric stellar calculation.

For an old [stellar population](../../../../../../stellar-population.md), an upper surviving [main sequence](../../../../../../main-sequence.md) mass near $M_\odot$ is reasonable because more massive stars have shorter lifetimes; low-mass [main sequence](../../../../../../main-sequence.md) stars also contain much of the surviving stellar [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). However, [red giants](../../../../../../red-giant.md) and other evolved stars can contribute a large fraction of the light despite their small numbers, so omitting them generally makes this calculated [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) too large. Omitting [stellar remnants](../../../../../../stellar-remnant.md) has the opposite effect on the inferred [mass](../../../../../../mass.md), and a single cubic [mass-luminosity relation](../../../../../../mass-luminosity-relation.md) is only approximate across the stated range. Finally, a single $m^{-3}$ [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md) down to $0.1M_\odot$ is steeper and more low-mass-heavy than commonly adopted distributions, which flatten at low [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). **Ten solar units is a useful toy-model estimate, not a precision stellar-population prediction.**
