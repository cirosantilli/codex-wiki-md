<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A typical [globular cluster](../../../../../../globular-cluster.md) has undergone appreciable [two-body relaxation](../../../../../../two-body-relaxation.md), since its [stellar relaxation time](../../../../../../stellar-relaxation-time.md) is $t_{\rm rel}\sim0.1(N/\ln N)t_{\rm cross}$: for $N\sim10^5$ and [stellar crossing time](../../../../../../stellar-crossing-time.md) $t_{\rm cross}\sim10^6\,\mathrm{yr}$ this is about $10^9\,\mathrm{yr}$, shorter than its age, allowing a relatively relaxed, tidally limited structure. A [galaxy](../../../../../../galaxy-split.md) with $N\sim10^{11}$ and $t_{\rm cross}\sim10^8\,\mathrm{yr}$ instead has $t_{\rm rel}\sim4\times10^{16}\,\mathrm{yr}$ and behaves as a [collisionless stellar system](../../../../../../collisionless-stellar-system.md), retaining differences from formation, [galaxy mergers](../../../../../../galaxy-merger.md), and [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md). **Relaxation erases more memory in clusters than in galaxies**, although real [globular clusters](../../../../../../globular-cluster.md) also differ in concentration and evolutionary state, so structural similarity is approximate.
