<h1 id="4/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

The differential gravitational field of the [Milky Way](../../../../../../milky-way.md) removes loosely bound outer material by [tidal stripping](../../../../../../tidal-stripping.md), giving [globular clusters](../../../../../../globular-cluster.md) and satellite [dwarf galaxies](../../../../../../dwarf-galaxy.md) a characteristic [tidal radius](../../../../../../tidal-radius.md). For a low-mass satellite of [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m$ on a circular orbit of radius $R$ in a spherical host, the [Jacobi tidal radius](../../../../../../jacobi-tidal-radius.md) is $r_t\simeq R\{m/[(3-d\ln M_h/d\ln R)M_h(<R)]\}^{1/3}$; eccentric orbits often make the strongest restriction near pericentre. **Tidal limitation explains the sharp outer falloff**, although escaping stars can form extended [tidal tails](../../../../../../tidal-tail.md), so a fitted luminosity cutoff is not an exact boundary of all associated material.
