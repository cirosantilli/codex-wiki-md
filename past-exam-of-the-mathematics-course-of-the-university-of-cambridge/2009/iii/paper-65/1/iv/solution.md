<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Assume the [stellar yield](../../../../../../stellar-yield.md) is common to the compared [stellar populations](../../../../../../stellar-population.md) and compare their mass-weighted [stellar metallicities](../../../../../../stellar-metallicity.md). Inverting the [metallicity distribution in a leaky box](../../../../../../metallicity-distribution-in-a-leaky-box.md) result gives

$$
\boxed{\nu=\frac{y}{\langle Z\rangle_*}-1.}
$$

For example, with $y=Z_\odot$, a solar-metallicity massive [elliptical galaxy](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) requires $\nu\simeq0$, whereas $\langle Z\rangle_*<0.01Z_\odot$ in a [dwarf galaxy](../../../../../../dwarf-galaxy.md) requires $\nu>99$. A supersolar [stellar metallicity](../../../../../../stellar-metallicity.md) requires a correspondingly supersolar [stellar yield](../../../../../../stellar-yield.md): outflow alone cannot raise $\langle Z\rangle_*$ above $y$. If $y=2Z_\odot$, a massive [elliptical galaxy](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) with $\langle Z\rangle_*$ between $Z_\odot$ and $2Z_\odot$ has $0\le\nu\le1$, while the same low-metallicity [dwarf galaxy](../../../../../../dwarf-galaxy.md) needs $\nu>199$.

**For a yield of order solar, the required mass loading runs from order zero or unity in massive spheroids to order one hundred or more in faint dwarfs.** Without a specified [stellar yield](../../../../../../stellar-yield.md), the robust comparison is

$$
\frac{1+\nu_{\rm dwarf}}{1+\nu_{\rm giant}}
=\frac{\langle Z\rangle_{*,\rm giant}}{\langle Z\rangle_{*,\rm dwarf}}
\gtrsim100.
$$

This explains the sense of the [galaxy mass--metallicity relation](../../../../../../galaxy-mass-metallicity-relation.md); [galactic gas inflow](../../../../../../galactic-gas-inflow.md), incomplete depletion, and changes in the [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md) can also change observed [stellar metallicity](../../../../../../stellar-metallicity.md).
