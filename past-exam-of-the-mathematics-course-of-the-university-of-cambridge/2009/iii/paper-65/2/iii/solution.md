<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A two-body annihilation requires two nearby particles, so the [dark-matter annihilation radiation](../../../../../../dark-matter-annihilation-radiation.md) has emissivity proportional to the square of the [mass density](../../../../../../density.md). With a spatially constant rate coefficient, write

$$
j_a=C_a\rho^2,\qquad
C_a=\frac{E_a\langle\sigma v_{\rm rel}\rangle}{2m_\chi^2}
$$

for identical self-conjugate particles, where $E_a$ is the radiated [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) per annihilation; the factor one-half counts unordered pairs. Particle and antiparticle populations instead require their respective number densities, without that identical-pair factor. For the [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md), the [surface brightness](../../../../../../surface-brightness.md) becomes

$$
I_a(R)=\frac{C_aA^2}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty
\frac{dz}{(R^2+z^2)^2}.
$$

The substitution $z=R\tan\theta$ gives $R^{-3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\pi/(2R^3)$. Hence **annihilation gives the steeper projected profile**

$$
\boxed{I_a(R)=\frac{C_aA^2}{8R^3}
=\frac{C_aV^4}{32\pi^2G^2R^3}\propto R^{-3}.}
$$

For the illustrative cored profile used above, $I_a=C_aA^2/[8(R^2+r_c^2)^{3/2}]$. These expressions assume a smooth [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md), negligible absorption and constant $C_a$; unresolved density clumps or a velocity-dependent annihilation coefficient change the projected shape. The [projection of a spherical power-law emissivity](../../../../../../projection-of-a-spherical-power-law-emissivity.md) summarizes why three-dimensional powers $r^{-2}$ and $r^{-4}$ produce $R^{-1}$ and $R^{-3}$.
