<h1 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Measure the logarithmic [surface brightness](../../../../../../surface-brightness.md) slope in resolved radial annuli after accounting for instrumental blurring and foreground emission. Between a halo core and its outer boundary, [dark-matter decay radiation](../../../../../../dark-matter-decay-radiation.md) predicts $d\ln I/d\ln R=-1$, whereas [dark-matter annihilation radiation](../../../../../../dark-matter-annihilation-radiation.md) predicts $-3$; doubling radius therefore reduces the respective [surface brightness](../../../../../../surface-brightness.md) by factors $2$ and $8$. These are quantitative templates to compare with independently mapped stellar and gaseous components.

An [exponential galactic disk](../../../../../../exponential-galactic-disk.md) has $I_*(R)\propto e^{-R/R_d}$, so $d\ln I_*/d\ln R=-R/R_d$ and $I_*(2R)/I_*(R)=e^{-R/R_d}$. At $R=5R_d$ the latter is about $0.0067$, much faster decline than either halo template, and the projected emission should also be flattened along the [galactic disk](../../../../../../galactic-disk.md). A [galactic bulge](../../../../../../galactic-bulge.md) described by a [Sérsic profile](../../../../../../sersic-profile.md) has $d\ln I_*/d\ln R=-(b_n/n)(R/R_e)^{1/n}$; for $n=4$ and $b_4\simeq7.67$, this is about $-3.4$ at $10R_e$ and continues to steepen, whereas a pure halo power law keeps a fixed slope.

For a [stellar halo](../../../../../../stellar-halo.md) with outer luminosity density proportional to $r^{-p_*}$, the [projection of a spherical power-law emissivity](../../../../../../projection-of-a-spherical-power-law-emissivity.md) gives $I_*\propto R^{1-p_*}$. Illustratively, $p_*=3$--$4$ gives projected slopes $-2$--$-3$: then $I_d/I_*\propto R^{p_*-2}$ rises strongly outward, but annihilation can resemble the steeper stellar template. Gas-related gamma emission must be compared with observed gas columns and the [cosmic ray](../../../../../../cosmic-ray.md) distribution; it is not always proportional to optical light.

**A shallow, extended, approximately spherical $R^{-1}$ component is particularly distinctive; an $R^{-3}$ component needs additional evidence.** Fit the stellar, gas and halo templates together over a broad radial range, including morphology and emission spectra. A measured slope alone cannot uniquely identify [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md), since stellar or gas profiles can coincide with a halo slope over a limited interval.
