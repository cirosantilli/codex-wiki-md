<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**Independence or another ergodicity hypothesis is necessary.** Read literally for an arbitrary translation-invariant [site percolation](../../../../../../site-percolation-split.md) measure, the assertion is false: the mixture assigning [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$ to the all-open configuration and $1/2$ to the all-closed configuration is translation-invariant, but the invariant event of an [infinite percolation cluster](../../../../../../infinite-percolation-cluster.md) has [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$. We prove the zero–one law for the invariant independent law used in part (ii); the proof also works for the stated finite-range independent alternative.

Let $\Gamma$ be the group of translations, or the specified group of [graph automorphisms](../../../../../../graph-automorphism.md), and let $A$ be invariant under it, up to null sets. An event determined by the states in a finite vertex set is a [cylinder set](../../../../../../cylinder-set.md). For every $\varepsilon>0$, approximation in the product [sigma-algebra](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) provides a [cylinder set](../../../../../../cylinder-set.md) $B$, supported on a finite set $F$, with $\mathbb P(A\mathbin\triangle B)<\varepsilon$. Choose $g\in\Gamma$ so that $F$ and $gF$ are disjoint. Then $B$ and $gB$ are [independent events](../../../../../../independent-events.md), have equal [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md), and $\mathbb P(A\mathbin\triangle gB)<\varepsilon$. Hence

$$
\bigl|\mathbb P(A)-\mathbb P(B\cap gB)\bigr|<2\varepsilon,\qquad \mathbb P(B\cap gB)=\mathbb P(B)^2,
$$

and

$$
|\mathbb P(A)-\mathbb P(A)^2|\le 2\varepsilon+2|\mathbb P(B)-\mathbb P(A)|<4\varepsilon.
$$

Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves **the zero–one law** $\boxed{\mathbb P(A)\in\{0,1\}}$. This is [translation ergodicity of Bernoulli percolation](../../../../../../translation-ergodicity-of-bernoulli-percolation.md).

For a [quasi-transitive graph](../../../../../../quasi-transitive-graph.md), the required displacement exists: put $F$ in a ball about a vertex $v$; its orbit is infinite, so an automorphism can move $v$, and therefore that whole ball, beyond any prescribed finite neighborhood. A $k$-independent [site percolation](../../../../../../site-percolation-split.md) law means that the [sigma-algebras](../../../../../../sigma-algebra.md) generated by vertex sets at [graph distance](../../../../../../distance-graph-theory.md) greater than $k$ are independent. Move $F$ far enough that $\operatorname{dist}(F,gF)>k$ and the same proof applies. Thus finite-range independence does give the zero–one law, although it will not give uniqueness.
