<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The two components of a [disjoint union](../../../../../../disjoint-union.md) are open and closed. Restriction therefore gives an isomorphism

$$
\Omega^p(M)\longrightarrow\Omega^p(U)\oplus\Omega^p(V),\qquad \omega\longmapsto(\omega|_U,\omega|_V).
$$

Its inverse assigns the first form on $U$ and the second on $V$; it is smooth because there are no overlaps on which compatibility must be imposed. The [exterior derivative](../../../../../../exterior-derivative.md) is local, so this is an isomorphism of cochain complexes.

A form on $M$ is a [closed differential form](../../../../../../closed-differential-form.md) exactly when both restrictions are closed. It is an [exact differential form](../../../../../../exact-differential-form.md) exactly when both restrictions are exact: primitives on $U$ and $V$ join into a global primitive. Taking the quotient therefore yields the [de Rham cohomology of a finite disjoint union](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology-of-a-finite-disjoint-union.md):

$$
\boxed{H^p_{\mathrm{dR}}(U\sqcup V)\cong H^p_{\mathrm{dR}}(U)\oplus H^p_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\qquad(p\ge0).}
$$

The same argument covers degree zero, with the space of degree-minus-one forms taken to be zero.
