<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Start with a disk in the [zero-handle](../../../../../../zero-handle.md) and attach two untwisted bands passing through the two [one-handles](../../../../../../one-handle.md). The four passages around its boundary occur in the order $x,y,x^{-1},y^{-1}$, giving an embedded once-punctured [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) whose boundary is the attaching circle of the [two-handle](../../../../../../two-handle.md). Push its interior into the existing four-dimensional handlebody, keeping its boundary on the attaching locus, and cap it with the [core disk of a handle](../../../../../../core-disk-of-a-handle.md). The matching product framing makes the union smooth after rounding its corner. This gives an embedded closed [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) $T\subset W$.

The [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) uses the core of the [two-handle](../../../../../../two-handle.md) exactly once. Equivalently, it intersects that handle's relative co-core disk transversely in one point. Its cellular two-chain is therefore the generator of $C_2=\mathbb Z$, which is a cycle and is not a boundary because there are no three-cells. Thus

$$
\boxed{[T]\text{ generates }H_2(W)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad T\cdot T=0.}
$$

This is the zero section in the [commutator handlebody of the torus](../../../../../../commutator-handlebody-of-the-torus.md) $T^2\times D^2$.
