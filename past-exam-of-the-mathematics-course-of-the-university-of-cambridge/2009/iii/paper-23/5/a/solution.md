<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Since the [hom-sets](../../../../../../hom-set.md) in these [categories](../../../../../../category-split.md) are either empty or singleton, an [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) $f\dashv g$ is precisely the condition

$$
\boxed{f(x)\le y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\le g(y)\qquad(x\in X,\ y\in Y).}
$$

The [hom-set](../../../../../../hom-set.md) [bijections](../../../../../../bijection.md) are then the unique possible ones, and [naturality](../../../../../../naturality.md) is automatic. This is a [Galois connection](../../../../../../galois-connection.md) between the two [posets](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md).

Equivalently, the monotone [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md) satisfy the unit and counit inequalities $x\le gf(x)$ and $fg(y)\le y$. The equivalence implies these by taking $y=f(x)$ and $x=g(y)$. Conversely, if the inequalities hold, $f(x)\le y$ implies $x\le gf(x)\le g(y)$, while $x\le g(y)$ implies $f(x)\le fg(y)\le y$.
