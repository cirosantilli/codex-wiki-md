<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Over $\overline{\mathbb F}_3$, take the projective cubic

$$
\boxed{C=V_+(Y^2Z-X^3+XZ^2)\subseteq\mathbb P^2.}
$$

In characteristic three its partial [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) are $F_X=Z^2$, $F_Y=2YZ$ and $F_Z=Y^2+2XZ$. If they vanished simultaneously, $Z=0$ and then $Y=0$. The equation of $C$ would force $X=0$, impossible for a projective point. Thus the [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md) proves smoothness everywhere, including infinity.

It is an irreducible cubic: on the affine chart $Z=1$, the polynomial is $y^2-(x^3-x)$, and $x^3-x$ is not a square in $\overline{\mathbb F}_3(x)$ because its pole at infinity has odd order three. A quadratic in $y$ would factor over that field only if its right side were a square. [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../../../../gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md) then gives affine irreducibility, and homogenization introduces no factor at infinity because $Z$ does not divide $F$. This supplies the [smooth plane cubic in characteristic three](../../../../../../smooth-plane-cubic-in-characteristic-three.md). In particular, choosing a Fermat cubic here would fail, because its cubic partial [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) vanish in characteristic three.
