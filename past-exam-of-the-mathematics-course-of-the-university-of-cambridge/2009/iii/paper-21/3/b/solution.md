<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the [projective hypersurface](../../../../../../projective-hypersurface.md)

$$
\boxed{S=V_+(X_0X_1-X_2^2)\subseteq\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}.}
$$

As a polynomial in $X_0$ over $\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3]$, it is primitive because $X_1$ and $X_2^2$ are coprime. It is linear and irreducible over the fraction field, so [Gauss lemma for polynomials](../../../../../../gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md) proves irreducibility. Thus it is an irreducible surface.

Its gradient is $(X_1,X_0,-2X_2,0)$. The simultaneous zero set on $S$ is exactly

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sing}S=\{[0:0:0:1]\}.}
$$

The [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md) proves every other point is smooth. At that vertex the affine equation in the chart $X_3=1$ has no linear term, so the tangent space has dimension three whereas the surface has dimension two; the vertex is genuinely singular. This is a [projective quadric cone with one singular vertex](../../../../../../projective-quadric-cone-with-one-singular-vertex.md).
