<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the standard convention that a [projective variety](../../../../../../projective-variety.md) is nonempty and irreducible, over an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md). Let $f:X\to\mathbb A^1$ be a [regular function](../../../../../../regular-function.md), and compose with the inclusion into $\mathbb P^1$. Its graph is closed in $X\times\mathbb P^1$: on an affine open of $X$, with homogeneous target coordinates $[T_0:T_1]$, it is given by $T_1-f(x)T_0=0$. This equation has no solution at $T_0=0$, and the local closedness statements glue.

Embed $X$ as a closed subvariety of $\mathbb P^m$. The graph is then closed in $\mathbb P^m\times\mathbb P^1$. Part 1, after swapping factors, makes its projected image $f(X)$ closed in $\mathbb P^1$. Since it avoids infinity, it is a proper closed subset of the projective line and hence finite. A continuous image of an irreducible space is irreducible, and a nonempty finite closed subset over an algebraically closed field is irreducible only if it is a singleton. Hence

$$
\boxed{f\equiv\lambda\text{ for some }\lambda\in k,\qquad H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k.}
$$

This proves [global regular functions on an irreducible projective variety](../../../../../../global-regular-functions-on-an-irreducible-projective-variety.md) are constant, using the closed-projection result rather than assuming it. If one allows disconnected projective algebraic sets instead of irreducible varieties, different components can carry different constants; irreducibility is the relevant convention here.
