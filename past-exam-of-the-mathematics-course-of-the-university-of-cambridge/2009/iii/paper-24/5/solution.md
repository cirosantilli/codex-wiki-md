<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

A [coverage on a category](../../../../../coverage-on-a-category.md) specifies covering families stable under refinement along arrows: if $(f_i:C_i\to C)$ covers and $h:B\to C$ is any arrow, there is a covering family $(g_j:B_j\to B)$ such that every $hg_j$ factors through some $f_i$. Equivalently one works with the associated saturated covering [sieves on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md), satisfying maximality, pullback stability and local transitivity. These determine the same [sheaves on a site](../../../../../sheaf-on-a-site.md). We use this [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) formulation of $J$.

An object is a [J-irreducible object of a site](../../../../../j-irreducible-object-of-a-site.md) if its only covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) is the maximal [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md). The [coverage on a category](../../../../../coverage-on-a-category.md) is a [rigid coverage](../../../../../rigid-coverage.md) if every object is covered by the [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) generated by arrows whose domains are J-irreducible.

Assume $\mathcal C$ has finitely many arrows and every [idempotent morphism](../../../../../idempotent-morphism.md) splits. There are finitely many [sieves on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) on each object $C$, so the intersection of all covering [sieves on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) is a covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) $R_C$: finite intersections of covers are covers by the topology axioms. It is the least cover. Let $I$ be the collection of all arrows $f:B\to C$ lying in $R_C$. This is an [idempotent ideal in a finite category](../../../../../idempotent-ideal-in-a-finite-category.md).

Here are the details. It is closed under precomposition because $R_C$ is a [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md). For $h:C\to D$, the [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) $h^*R_D$ covers $C$; minimality gives $R_C\subseteq h^*R_D$, so $hf\in R_D$. Thus it is also closed under postcomposition. Local transitivity says that the [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md)

$$
S_C=\bigcup_{f\in R_C}fR_{\operatorname{dom}f}
$$

covers $C$. It is contained in $R_C$, while minimality gives the opposite inclusion. Hence $S_C=R_C$, or $I^2=I$.

We prove that such an ideal is generated by the identities it contains. Take $f\in I$. Since $I^2=I$, repeatedly factor its remaining right factor to obtain

$$
f=a_1b_1=a_1a_2b_2=\cdots=a_1\cdots a_nb_n,
$$

with every factor in $I$. Write $p_n=a_1\cdots a_n$. Finiteness gives $p_i=p_j$ for some $i<j$. Their domains coincide, and $t=a_{i+1}\cdots a_j$ is an endomorphism in $I$ satisfying $p_it=p_i$. Some positive power $e=t^m$ is idempotent: the powers are eventually periodic, so choose $m$ beyond the eventual threshold and divisible by its period. Then $p_ie=p_i$. Split $e=ir$ with $i:B\to\operatorname{dom}(p_i)$, $r:\operatorname{dom}(p_i)\to B$ and $ri=1_B$. Since $I$ is a two-sided ideal,

$$
1_B=rei\in I,\qquad f=p_i e b_i=(p_i i)(r b_i).
$$

Thus $f$ factors through an object $B$ whose identity lies in $I$. The latter means $1_B\in R_B$, hence $R_B=yB$; every covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) on $B$ must therefore be maximal. So $B$ is J-irreducible.

Every arrow of $R_C$ consequently factors through a J-irreducible object. The [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) generated by arrows from all such objects contains $R_C$ and is a cover. This proves **every coverage on the finite idempotent-complete category is rigid**. If $R_C$ is empty, the same conclusion holds because the empty [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) already covers $C$.

Let $\mathcal D$ be the full subcategory of J-irreducible objects. For a covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) $S$ on $C$ and any $f:D\to C$ with $D\in\mathcal D$, the pulled-back [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) $f^*S$ covers $D$ and is maximal, so $f\in S$. Therefore $S|_{\mathcal D}=yC|_{\mathcal D}$. For a [categorical presheaf](../../../../../presheaf-category-theory.md) $F$ on $\mathcal D$, define its extension by

$$
H_F(C)=\operatorname{Nat}(yC|_{\mathcal D},F).
$$

This is the right Kan extension along the inclusion. Its adjunction gives

$$
\operatorname{Nat}(S,H_F)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(S|_{\mathcal D},F)=H_F(C),
$$

which is precisely unique amalgamation on $S$. Hence $H_F$ is a sheaf. The [Yoneda lemma](../../../../../yoneda-lemma.md) gives $H_F(D)\cong F(D)$ for $D\in\mathcal D$.

Conversely, if $G$ is a sheaf, its sections on all arrows from $\mathcal D$ into $C$ determine a section on their covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md). A compatible family extends to an arrow factoring through such a $D$ by restriction from its given $D$-section. If an arrow has two factorizations, restrict to the covering [sieve on a category](../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) generated by arrows from $\mathcal D$ into its domain; compatibility makes the two restrictions equal, and the sheaf's uniqueness of amalgamation makes the sections equal. The resulting matching family glues uniquely over $C$. Thus $G(C)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(yC|_{\mathcal D},G|_{\mathcal D})$. Restriction and $F\mapsto H_F$ are inverse up to [natural isomorphism](../../../../../natural-isomorphism.md), proving

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal C,J)\simeq[\mathcal D^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}].}
$$
