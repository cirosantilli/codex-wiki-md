<h1 id="7/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7](../7.md)

Use one ring sort with $0,1,+,-,\cdot$ and the usual unital [commutative ring](../../../../../commutative-ring.md) equations. Write $\operatorname{Inv}(x)$ for the [coherent formula](../../../../../coherent-formula.md) $\exists y\,(xy=1)$. Add the [coherent sequents](../../../../../coherent-sequent.md)

$$
0=1\vdash\bot,\qquad\top\vdash_x\operatorname{Inv}(x)\vee\operatorname{Inv}(1-x).
$$

This presents the [coherent theory of local rings](../../../../../coherent-theory-of-local-rings.md). For an ordinary nonzero ring satisfying these axioms, the nonunits form an ideal: a multiple of a nonunit cannot be a unit; and if nonunits $a,b$ had unit sum, multiplying by $(a+b)^{-1}$ would make two nonunits sum to $1$, contradicting the second axiom. They are also closed under additive inverses. This proper ideal contains every proper ideal, since a proper ideal contains no unit, and its quotient is a field; it is the unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md). Conversely in a [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md), $x$ and $1-x$ cannot both belong to the unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md), so one is a unit. The first sequent excludes the zero ring. In a [topos](../../../../../elementary-topos.md), the disjunction asserts that these unit alternatives hold locally.

Let $\mathcal C$ be a small skeleton of the opposite of the category of finitely presented unital [commutative rings](../../../../../commutative-ring.md); write $C_A$ for the object corresponding to $A$. The [Zariski coverage on finitely presented rings](../../../../../zariski-coverage-on-finitely-presented-rings.md) is generated by

$$
\{C_{A[f_i^{-1}]}\longrightarrow C_A\}_{i=1}^n,\qquad(f_1,\ldots,f_n)=A,
$$

and the empty cover of $C_0$. Localization is finitely presented, and its compatibility with base change proves the covering axioms.

Here is the classifying argument. For an internal commutative ring $M$ and a presentation $A=\mathbb Z[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(p_1,\ldots,p_r)$, define $F_M(C_A)$ as the [subobject](../../../../../subobject.md) of $M^n$ satisfying the polynomial equations $p_i=0$. Ring maps induce maps of these solution objects in the site direction. This is independent of presentation, and it preserves [finite limits](../../../../../finite-limit.md): finite colimits of finitely presented rings correspond to joining finite generators and equations, whose solution objects are [finite limits](../../../../../finite-limit.md). Conversely a left-exact [functor](../../../../../functor.md) $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal E$ reconstructs a commutative ring $F(C_{\mathbb Z[t]})$ using the polynomial maps for addition, multiplication, constants and additive inverse. Presentations by generators and equations then show $F\cong F_M$. Thus flat [functors](../../../../../functor.md) on $\mathcal C$ correspond to internal [commutative rings](../../../../../commutative-ring.md).

Under $F_M$, the localization arrow above identifies its domain with the locus where $f_i$ evaluates to a unit; the inverse witness is unique. The family is jointly epimorphic exactly when these unit loci cover the solution object for $A$. The locality axiom implies this for every unit-ideal family: from a finite sum $\sum f_i g_i=1$, repeatedly apply the two-summand unit alternative, rescaling a unit partial sum when necessary; some $f_i g_i$, hence some $f_i$, is a unit locally. Conversely the cover of $\mathbb Z[t]$ by its localizations at $t$ and $1-t$ gives precisely the locality axiom, while the empty cover of the zero ring gives $0=1\vdash\bot$. Therefore continuous flat [functors](../../../../../functor.md) are exactly models of the displayed [coherent theory](../../../../../coherent-theory.md). The [Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms](../../../../../diaconescu-equivalence-for-geometric-morphisms.md) now identifies

$$
\operatorname{Geom}(\mathcal E,\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal C,Z))\simeq\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{local\ rings}}(\mathcal E),
$$

naturally in $\mathcal E$. This proves the asserted [classifying topos](../../../../../classifying-topos.md) identification.

Since the site is [subcanonical](../../../../../subcanonical-topology.md), the [generic local ring](../../../../../generic-local-ring.md) is the representable sheaf

$$
R=yC_{\mathbb Z[t]},\qquad R(C_A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(\mathbb Z[t],A)=A.
$$

Its ring operations are induced by polynomial substitution. Its infinitesimal [subobject](../../../../../subobject.md) is likewise representable:

$$
D=yC_{\mathbb Z[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)},\qquad D(C_A)=\{a\in A:a^2=0\}.
$$

Products of representables correspond to tensor products of their rings. For every finitely presented $A$, exponential adjunction and the [Yoneda lemma](../../../../../yoneda-lemma.md) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
(R^D)(C_A)&\cong\operatorname{Hom}(yC_A\times D,R)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}(yC_{A[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)},yC_{\mathbb Z[t]})\\
&\cong A[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

Every element of this ring has a unique expression $a+b\varepsilon$, because $1,\varepsilon$ form an $A$-basis. This holds even when $A$ has nilpotents or zero divisors. The expression is natural under every [ring homomorphism](../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) $A\to B$, which sends its two coefficients to their images. The canonical morphism $R\times R\to R^D$, transposed from $(a,b,d)\mapsto a+bd$, is consequently a bijection at every stage $C_A$, with a natural inverse extracting the coefficients. Therefore

$$
\boxed{R\times R\xrightarrow{\sim}R^D,\qquad(a,b)\longmapsto(d\mapsto a+bd).}
$$

This proves the [Kock-Lawvere axiom for the generic local ring](../../../../../kock-lawvere-axiom-for-the-generic-local-ring.md). The uniqueness is an internal sheaf assertion tested at all finitely presented rings, including the ring with its generic square-zero element; restricting the test to fields would lose that element and would not prove uniqueness.
