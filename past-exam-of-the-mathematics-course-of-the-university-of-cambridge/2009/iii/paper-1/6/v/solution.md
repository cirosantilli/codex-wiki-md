<h1 id="6/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

**False.** Use the two-generated [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $G_S$ constructed in part (iii) for a non-recursively-enumerable $S$. Sending the two free generators to $b_0,t$ gives a surjective [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md)

$$
\boxed{F_2\twoheadrightarrow G_S.}
$$

The source is a [finitely presented group](../../../../../../finitely-presented-group.md), with presentation $\langle a,b\mid\ \rangle$. The target is not finitely presented: if it were, its null words could be enumerated from a finite set of relators, and the tested commutators would enumerate $S$, as proved in part (iii). Thus even a quotient of a rank-two [free group](../../../../../../free-group.md) need not admit a finite presentation. A quotient by a finitely normally generated kernel would be finitely presented, but the assertion places no such restriction on its kernel.
