<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

All three generators commute, so the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is the [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) $\mathbb Z^3$ modulo the integer relation lattice generated by $(2,6,0)$ and $(1,2,2)$. Thus $x^my^nz^k=1$ exactly when

$$
(m,n,k)=a(2,6,0)+b(1,2,2)\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb Z).
$$

Eliminating $a,b$ gives $6m-2n-k=0$, and $n=6a+2b$ is even. Conversely, if $n$ is even and $k=6m-2n$, take $a=n/2-m$ and $b=3m-n$; these are integers and reproduce all three coordinates. Therefore

$$
\boxed{x^my^nz^k=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ n\equiv0\pmod2\quad\text{and}\ k+2n-6m=0.}
$$

One can also see the invariant factors directly. The second relation gives $x=y^{-2}z^{-2}$. Substitution into the first gives $y^2z^{-4}=1$. Put $t=yz^{-2}$; then

$$
\boxed{G=\langle t,z\mid t^2=1,\ [t,z]=1\rangle\cong C_2\times\mathbb Z,\qquad
x=z^{-6},\quad y=tz^2.}
$$

The two presentations are equivalent by the explicit substitutions in both directions, so this is not just a quotient guess. An arbitrary input word is first converted, using commutativity, to its exponent totals $m,n,k$. Its coordinates are $(n\bmod2,k+2n-6m)$, giving a terminating [word problem for a group](../../../../../../word-problem-for-groups.md) algorithm.
