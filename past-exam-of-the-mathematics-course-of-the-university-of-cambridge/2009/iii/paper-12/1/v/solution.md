**No: the conclusion need not survive without an additional restriction at infinity.** A counterexample that keeps the [functional](../../../../../../functional.md) finite is $\Omega=\mathbb R^n$ and $F(p)=\tfrac12|p|^2$. The distinct functions $u\equiv0$ and $v\equiv1$ both lie in $C^2(\overline\Omega)$ and have energy zero. Their boundary data agree because $\partial\Omega$ is empty. For any variation with finite [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) energy,

$$
\mathcal F(u+t\varphi)=\mathcal F(v+t\varphi)=\frac{t^2}{2}\int_{\mathbb R^n}|D\varphi|^2,
$$

so both are genuine [critical points](../../../../../../critical-point.md), not merely formal solutions with infinite [functional](../../../../../../functional.md) value.

With nonempty boundary, the classical [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) also illustrates the missing condition: in dimension $n\geq3$, on $\Omega=\{|x|>1\}$, both $0$ and $1-|x|^{2-n}$ are [harmonic functions](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md) with zero boundary values. The latter has finite [Dirichlet energy](../../../../../../dirichlet-energy.md), because its gradient-energy integral is proportional to $\int_1^\infty r^{1-n}dr$, and is stationary under [compactly supported](../../../../../../compact-support.md) variations. It approaches $1$ at infinity while the zero solution approaches $0$.

This is [nonuniqueness of Dirichlet data without control at infinity](../../../../../../nonuniqueness-of-dirichlet-data-without-control-at-infinity.md). For a global variational formulation one must specify the admissible variations: the exterior example is not critical against every finite-energy zero-boundary variation, since varying it in its own direction changes its energy to first order. Prescribing an appropriate limit at infinity, or a variational space in which the difference is an admissible zero-boundary [test function](../../../../../../test-function.md) and constants are excluded, can restore uniqueness.
