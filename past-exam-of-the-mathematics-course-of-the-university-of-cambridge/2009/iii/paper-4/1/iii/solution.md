<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $V$ afford the [irreducible character](../../../../../../irreducible-character.md) $\chi$. The positive [character inner product](../../../../../../character-inner-product.md) $\langle\chi_H,1_H\rangle_H$ is the multiplicity of the [trivial representation](../../../../../../trivial-representation.md) in $V$ restricted to $H$, so there is a nonzero vector $v$ fixed by every element of $H$. Since $N\subseteq\ker\chi$, every element of $N$ acts as the identity on all of $V$. By $G=NH$, every element of $G$ therefore fixes $v$.

The line $\mathbb Cv$ is a nonzero [invariant subspace](../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) of the [irreducible representation](../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) $V$, so it must equal $V$. The action on this line is trivial, proving

$$
\boxed{\chi=1_G.}
$$

The hypothesis on the [kernel of a character](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-character.md) is what makes an $H$-fixed vector into a $G$-fixed vector.
