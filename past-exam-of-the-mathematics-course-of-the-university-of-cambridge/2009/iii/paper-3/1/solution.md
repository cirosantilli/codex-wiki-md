↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Throughout, [rings](../../../../../ring.md) are commutative with identity and [ring homomorphisms](../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) preserve identity. The [spectrum of a commutative ring](../../../../../spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md) is $\operatorname{Spec}A=\{\mathfrak p\subset A:\mathfrak p\text{ is a prime ideal}\}$. For an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $I$, put $V(I)=\{\mathfrak p:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$; these are the closed sets of the [Zariski topology](../../../../../zariski-topology.md). Its axioms follow from

$$
V(0)=\operatorname{Spec}A,\qquad V(A)=\varnothing,\qquad \bigcap_\lambda V(I_\lambda)=V\!\left(\sum_\lambda I_\lambda\right),\qquad V(I)\cup V(J)=V(IJ).
$$

For the last equality, a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) containing $IJ$ must contain $I$ or $J$: if it contained neither, choose $a\in I\setminus\mathfrak p$ and $b\in J\setminus\mathfrak p$, contradicting $ab\in\mathfrak p$. The other equalities follow directly from containment. Thus arbitrary intersections and finite unions of closed sets are closed, proving that this is a [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md).

The [Zorn lemma](../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md) says that a nonempty [partially ordered set](../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) in which every chain has an upper bound has a maximal element. For $A\ne0$, apply it to proper [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) ordered by inclusion. The zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is proper. The union of a nonempty chain is an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) and is proper, because containing one would put one in a member of the chain; the empty chain has the zero [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) as an upper bound. There is therefore a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$. If $a\notin\mathfrak m$, then $\mathfrak m+(a)=A$, so the class of $a$ in $A/\mathfrak m$ is invertible. This [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md) is a [field](../../../../../field.md), making $\mathfrak m$ a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md). Hence **$A\ne0\implies\operatorname{Spec}A\ne\varnothing$**.

For a [ring homomorphism](../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) $f:A\to B$, define $f^*:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ by $f^*(\mathfrak q)=f^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$. The inverse image is proper because $f(1)=1$, and primality follows from $f(ab)=f(a)f(b)$. Moreover

$$
(f^*)^{-1}(V_A(I))=V_B(IB),
$$

where $IB$ is the [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) generated by $f(I)$. This proves [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md). Inverse images also give $(g\circ f)^*=f^*\circ g^*$ and $\mathrm{id}^*=\mathrm{id}$. Thus **$\operatorname{Spec}$ is a contravariant [functor](../../../../../functor.md) from rings to topological spaces**.

For the specified inclusion, the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) gives the points $0$ and $(x-z)$ of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[x]$. Over $\mathbb R$, the nonzero [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) are generated either by $x-r$ with $r\in\mathbb R$, or by $(x-a)^2+b^2$ with $a\in\mathbb R$, $b>0$; all irreducible real [polynomials](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) have degree one or two. Contraction gives the [spectrum map for the complexification of a real polynomial ring](../../../../../spectrum-map-for-the-complexification-of-a-real-polynomial-ring.md):

$$
\boxed{0\mapsto0,\qquad (x-z)\mapsto\begin{cases}(x-z),&z\in\mathbb R,\\((x-\operatorname{Re}z)^2+(\operatorname{Im}z)^2),&z\notin\mathbb R.\end{cases}}
$$

Indeed, a real [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) vanishing at a nonreal $z$ also vanishes at $\bar z$, and is divisible by their quadratic factor; the converse is immediate. This map is surjective. Its fibers contain one point over a real linear factor, two conjugate points over an irreducible quadratic factor, and only the [generic point](../../../../../generic-point.md) over zero. It identifies complex-conjugate pairs. Since each proper [Zariski-closed set](../../../../../zariski-closed-set.md) in either one-variable [polynomial ring](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) is a finite set of closed points, images of closed sets are closed; hence the [Zariski topology](../../../../../zariski-topology.md) on the target is the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) for this identification.
