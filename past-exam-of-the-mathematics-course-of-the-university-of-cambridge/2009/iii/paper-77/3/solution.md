The mechanisms affect surface energy input, heat supply from below, and the transport and thickness of [sea ice](../../../../../sea-ice.md). They reinforce one another: atmospheric warming and ocean heat thin the cover; circulation can export old ice; thinner ice melts earlier, increasing open-water absorption and strengthening [ice-albedo feedback](../../../../../ice-albedo-feedback.md). Heat below the [cold halocline](../../../../../cold-halocline.md) matters only to the extent that mixing delivers it upward. Consequently the seven effects cannot be assigned independent percentages from the information given; their importance varies by region and year.

In a forward-looking answer from the paper's 2009 context, sustained warming would be expected to produce continued [Arctic sea ice decline](../../../../../arctic-sea-ice-decline.md), a longer summer open-water season, less [multi-year sea ice](../../../../../multi-year-sea-ice.md), and potentially nearly ice-free late summers within coming decades. Winter darkness permits new [first-year sea ice](../../../../../first-year-sea-ice.md) to form, so loss of summer ice does not imply immediate year-round disappearance. Winter ice would tend to become thinner, more seasonal, and slower to recover after enhanced ocean heating. A precise date requires a forcing scenario and model, and internal variability causes year-to-year departures. [Wang and Overland's 2009 projection](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2009GL037820/abstract) is a contemporary example of such a conditional summer outlook.
