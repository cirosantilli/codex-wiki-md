<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For $10^4$ such bergs per year, the rate of heat absorption north of the [Antarctic Convergence](../../../../../../antarctic-convergence.md) is

$$
\dot Q=\frac{10^4(1.009575\times10^{17})}{365.25\times86400}\,\mathrm W,
\qquad\boxed{\dot Q\simeq3.20\times10^{13}\,\mathrm W.}
$$

Relative to the specified oceanic polar heat flux,

$$
\boxed{\frac{\dot Q}{2\times10^{15}\,\mathrm W}\simeq0.0160=1.60\%.}
$$

The bergs themselves move equatorward carrying a thermal deficit; their melting removes heat from warmer water. This is equivalent to [poleward heat transport by equatorward iceberg drift](../../../../../../poleward-heat-transport-by-equatorward-iceberg-drift.md), explaining the sign of the inferred poleward contribution. The estimate includes the sensible and latent terms from part (b), and does not include the separate effects of released freshwater on [density stratification](../../../../../../density-stratification.md) or [ocean circulation](../../../../../../ocean-circulation.md).
