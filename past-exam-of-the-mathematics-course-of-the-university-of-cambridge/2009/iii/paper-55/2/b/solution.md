<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the canonical scalar action and the stress-tensor convention in the paper, varying the inverse metric and volume element gives the [stress-energy tensor of a canonical scalar field](../../../../../../stress-energy-tensor-of-a-canonical-scalar-field.md)

$$
T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi-\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}
\left[\frac12\widetilde g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\partial_\beta\phi-V(\phi)\right].
$$

The vacuum [f(R) gravity](../../../../../../f-r-gravity.md) equation, divided by $F$, is

$$
G_{\mu\nu}-\frac1F\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F+\frac1F g_{\mu\nu}\Box F
=-\frac{RF-f}{2F}g_{\mu\nu}.
$$

Insert this in the transformed [Einstein tensor](../../../../../../einstein-tensor.md) from part (a). Since $g_{\mu\nu}=-\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}/F$, its algebraic term becomes $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(RF-f)/(2F^2)$. Also $\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\log(-F)$ gives $\partial_\mu\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\,\partial_\mu F/F$. The remaining derivative tensor is exactly the kinetic part of $T_{\mu\nu}$: the combination $g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla F)^2$ equals $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde\nabla F)^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\widetilde G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu},\qquad V(\phi)=\frac{Rf'(R)-f(R)}{2f'(R)^2}.}
$$

This establishes the [Einstein-frame representation of metric f(R) gravity](../../../../../../einstein-frame-representation-of-metric-f-r-gravity.md) in the specified normalization. To express the potential as a function of $\phi$, choose a branch where $R\mapsto f'(R)$ is locally invertible; the conformal metric already requires $f'<0$.
