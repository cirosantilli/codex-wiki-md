<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [Killing horizon](../../../../../../../killing-horizon.md) must be a null hypersurface whose normal is a [Killing vector field](../../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md). The outer stationary-limit surface instead has $s=r-r_e(\theta)=0$ and, in [Boyer-Lindquist coordinates](../../../../../../../boyer-lindquist-coordinates.md),

$$
g^{ab}\partial_as\,\partial_bs=\frac{\Delta(r_e)+[r_e'(\theta)]^2}{\Sigma}.
$$

On it, $r_e^2-2Mr_e+a^2\cos^2\theta=0$, so $\Delta(r_e)=a^2\sin^2\theta>0$ away from the rotation axis. Its normal is spacelike and the surface timelike. Thus the [Kerr stationary-limit surface is generically timelike](../../../../../../../kerr-stationary-limit-surface-is-generically-timelike.md) and **is not a Killing horizon**, despite $K^2=0$ there. For example its stationary null vector is not normal, since $g(K,\partial_\phi)=-2Mar\sin^2\theta/\Sigma\ne0$ away from the axis. The actual outer [Killing horizon](../../../../../../../killing-horizon.md) is $r=r_+=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$, generated by $K+\Omega_H\Phi$, with [Kerr horizon angular velocity](../../../../../../../kerr-horizon-angular-velocity.md) $\Omega_H=a/(r_+^2+a^2)$. The two surfaces meet on the axis, which does not make the entire stationary-limit surface null.
