<h1 id="1/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

There is only one zero of $f$, at $r=2m$, and the [Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates](../../../../../../kruskal-szekeres-coordinates.md) give four quadrants. Both exterior quadrants have $r>2m$, while the future and past interior quadrants have $0<r<2m$. The [tortoise coordinate](../../../../../../tortoise-coordinate.md) is finite at $r=0$: for $q>0$, $dr_*/dr\sim-\sqrt q\,r^{1/2}/(2m)$, and for $q=0$ it is $-r/(2m)$. Throughout each interior $g^{rr}=f<0$, so constant-$r$ hypersurfaces are spacelike. The assumed [curvature singularity](../../../../../../curvature-singularity.md) at $r=0$ therefore forms a spacelike future boundary in the black-hole region and a spacelike past boundary in the white-hole region. At infinity, $f\to1$ and $R/r\to1$, giving two standard asymptotically flat ends.

The resulting [Penrose diagram](../../../../../../penrose-diagram.md) is the same four-region causal diagram as the maximal [Schwarzschild spacetime](../../../../../../schwarzschild-spacetime.md):

**[Figure 1](#1/g/image-maximal-extension-of-the-charged-single-horizon-geometry-with-two-exteriors-a-black-hole-a-white-hole-and-spacelike-singularities). Maximal extension of the charged single-horizon geometry, with two exteriors, a black hole, a white hole and spacelike singularities**.

Each exterior has its own [past null infinity](../../../../../../past-null-infinity.md), [future null infinity](../../../../../../future-null-infinity.md), spatial infinity and timelike infinities. The future and past [event horizons](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) intersect on the [bifurcation surface](../../../../../../bifurcation-surface.md). The diagram describes the eternal analytic solution; a spacetime formed by collapse need not contain its white-hole region or second exterior.

Unlike a nonextreme [Reissner-Nordstrom black hole](../../../../../../reissner-nordstrom-spacetime.md), this electrically charged solution has **one horizon and spacelike singularities, with no inner Cauchy horizon**. A nonextreme Reissner-Nordstrom solution has two distinct horizons, including an inner [Cauchy horizon](../../../../../../cauchy-horizon.md), and a timelike singularity; its ideal maximal extension contains an infinite sequence of exterior and interior blocks. Charge by itself does not determine this causal structure: the metric and field equations of the gravitational theory matter.
