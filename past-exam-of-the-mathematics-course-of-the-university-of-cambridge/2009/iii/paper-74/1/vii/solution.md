<h1 id="1/vii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vii](../vii.md)

When both [spheres](../../../../../../sphere.md) have the same effective downward weight, the second [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) adds a force-driven disturbance instead of being merely a force-free rigid inclusion. The power now contains both $WV_{1z}$ and $WV_{2z}$, so the one-forced-sphere bound from part (iv) no longer applies.

For $a\ll R$, place the [spheres](../../../../../../sphere.md) in a tandem configuration with $a\ll s\ll R$. Locally the flow around the second [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) is dominated by its downward [Stokeslet](../../../../../../stokeslet.md). Its axial incident [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) at the first [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) is downward, so the first [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) falls faster to leading order. By contrast, the [zero total flux for localized tube Stokes flow](../../../../../../zero-total-flux-for-localized-tube-stokes-flow.md) requires compensating upward flow away from the downward-moving particle core in sections cutting the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md). There are therefore positions in the return flow where the disturbance acts upwards.

One can choose the second [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) in such an upward-flow region generated by the first. The [Lorentz reciprocal theorem](../../../../../../lorentz-reciprocal-theorem-for-stokes-flow.md) makes the axial cross coefficients of the [hydrodynamic mobility matrix](../../../../../../hydrodynamic-mobility-matrix.md) equal, so the downward [force](../../../../../../force.md) on the second [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) then produces an upward contribution to the first [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md)'s [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md). With small well-separated [spheres](../../../../../../sphere.md), this force-driven interaction dominates the higher-order self-resistance change from simply inserting the other [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md). In mobility notation, with downward positive,

$$
V_{1z}\simeq V_0+W\mathcal M_{12}^{zz},
$$

and the cross coefficient can have either sign in the tube. Thus

$$
\boxed{V_{1z}>V_0\text{ in a downward entraining configuration},\qquad
V_{1z}<V_0\text{ in a return-flow configuration}.}
$$

The distinction is the sign of the incident flow, not whether adding a second geometrical obstacle lowers a fixed-velocity dissipation.
