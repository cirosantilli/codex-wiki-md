Here $p$ is odd. For a [finite p-group](../../../../../finite-p-group.md), write $N^p=\langle n^p:n\in N\rangle$. A [powerfully embedded subgroup](../../../../../powerfully-embedded-subgroup.md) satisfies $[N,G]\leq N^p$; this also makes $N$ normal, since every conjugate of an element of $N$ differs from it by an element of $N$. A [powerful p-group](../../../../../powerful-p-group.md) is one with $[G,G]\leq G^p$, namely one powerfully embedded in itself. Use $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and $a^b=b^{-1}ab$ throughout.

The [lower p-series](../../../../../lower-p-series.md) is defined by

$$
G_1=G,\qquad G_{i+1}=G_i^p[G_i,G].
$$

Each quotient $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is an [elementary abelian p-group](../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md), and $[G_i,G]\leq G_{i+1}$. These are generated [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md), without closure bars, because the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is finite.
