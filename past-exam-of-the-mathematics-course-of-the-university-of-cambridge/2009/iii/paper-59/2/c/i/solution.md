<h1 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [alpha-plane](../../../../../../../alpha-plane.md) is a totally null complex two-plane with tangent vectors $u^A\pi^{A'}$ for a fixed primed spinor. A [beta plane](../../../../../../../beta-plane.md) has tangent vectors $\lambda^A u^{A'}$ for a fixed unprimed spinor. The [metric tensor](../../../../../../../metric-tensor.md) pairs two vectors of either family to zero, since the repeated fixed spinor contracts with an [symplectic form](../../../../../../../symplectic-form.md). These are the two chiral families of totally null planes; labels for their self-duality depend on the orientation convention.

Globally in the [twistor correspondence](../../../../../../../twistor-correspondence.md), write

$$
A_\ell=\{W:\ell\subset W\},\qquad B_H=\{W:W\subset H\},
$$

where $\ell$ is a line and $H$ a three-dimensional hyperplane in [twistor space](../../../../../../../twistor-space.md). Both compact families are [Complex projective spaces](../../../../../../../complex-projective-space.md) $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

For distinct $\ell_1,\ell_2$, any $W\in A_{\ell_1}\cap A_{\ell_2}$ must contain their two-dimensional span. Since $W$ itself has dimension two, it must equal that span. Conversely the span belongs to both planes. Thus **two distinct compact alpha-planes meet in exactly one point**:

$$
\boxed{A_{\ell_1}\cap A_{\ell_2}=\{\ell_1+\ell_2\}.}
$$

In affine coordinates, independent $\pi_1,\pi_2$ determine a unique matrix $x$ from the two incidence equations. If the primed spinors are proportional while the projective twistors are distinct, no finite affine solution exists. The compact intersection still exists, at conformal infinity; saying the planes are disjoint without this affine qualification would be wrong for the printed space $M$.
