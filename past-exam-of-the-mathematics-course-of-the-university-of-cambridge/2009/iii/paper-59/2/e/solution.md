<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The meaningful twistor-geometric interpretation of “colinear in $PN$” is that the entire [projective line](../../../../../../projective-line.md) through two distinct projective [null twistors](../../../../../../null-twistor.md) lies in [projective null twistor space](../../../../../../projective-null-twistor-space.md). Mere membership of two points in a [projective line](../../../../../../projective-line.md) would impose no condition, because every pair of distinct points in $\mathbb{CP}^3$ spans one.

Indeed, individual nullness gives

$$
h(aX+bY,aX+bY)=a\overline b\,h(X,Y)+b\overline a\,\overline{h(X,Y)}.
$$

This vanishes for every $a,b\in\mathbb C$ precisely when $h(X,Y)=0$. Thus $W=\operatorname{span}(X,Y)$ is a maximal totally isotropic two-plane, a real point of [Minkowski conformal compactification](../../../../../../minkowski-conformal-compactification.md) under the [Lorentzian reality structure on twistor space](../../../../../../lorentzian-reality-structure-on-twistor-space.md). The two corresponding [alpha-planes](../../../../../../alpha-plane.md) meet at this unique $W$. Hence **their compact intersection is a real spacetime point**, and their real [null geodesics](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) meet there.

There are two qualifications. The point can be at conformal infinity, so a finite affine meeting is not automatic. If $X,Y$ represent the same projective point instead, their [alpha-planes](../../../../../../alpha-plane.md) coincide rather than having a unique intersection. If “colinear” is read only as spanning a line in $\mathbb{CP}^3$, individual nullness alone does not force a real intersection; the additional orthogonality condition above is indispensable.

For a concrete counterexample to the weaker reading, take $X=(0,0,1,0)$ and $Y=(1,0,0,1)$ in $(\omega^0,\omega^1,\pi_{0'},\pi_{1'})$ coordinates. Each is a [null twistor](../../../../../../null-twistor.md), but $h(X,Y)=1$. Their two incidence equations give $x=-i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, which is not Hermitian. Thus their [alpha-planes](../../../../../../alpha-plane.md) meet at a complex point that is not a real Minkowski point.
