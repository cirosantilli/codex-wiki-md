<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $t\geq0$, [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) factors the [survival function](../../../../../../survival-function.md) of the minimum:

$$
\mathbb P(M>t)=\prod_{j=1}^n\mathbb P(X_j>t)
=\prod_{j=1}^ne^{-\lambda t}=e^{-n\lambda t}.
$$

The minimum is nonnegative, and this is exactly the [survival function](../../../../../../survival-function.md) of an [exponential distribution](../../../../../../exponential-distribution.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{M\sim\operatorname{Exp}(n\lambda).}
$$

The parameter here is the rate, rather than the mean. The calculation is the simplest example of [competing exponential clocks](../../../../../../competing-exponential-clocks.md).
