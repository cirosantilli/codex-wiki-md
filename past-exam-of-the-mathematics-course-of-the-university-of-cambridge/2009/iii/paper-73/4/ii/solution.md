<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The fluctuation speed $q$ and the [integral scale of turbulence](../../../../../../integral-scale-of-turbulence.md) $L_u$ give a turnover time $L_u/q$. Dividing the velocity-variance scale $q^2$ by that time gives a [turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy-dissipation-rate.md) proportional to $q^3/L_u$. Likewise [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) variance $\overline{\rho'^2}$ removed over scalar mixing time $L_\rho/q$ gives the [density-variance dissipation rate](../../../../../../density-variance-dissipation-rate.md) proportional to $\overline{\rho'^2}q/L_\rho$. Absorbing dimensionless proportionality coefficients into the specified length scales gives

$$
\boxed{\epsilon=\frac{q^3}{L_u},\qquad\chi=\frac{\overline{\rho'^2}q}{L_\rho}.}
$$

These are integral-scale closure estimates, not exact consequences of dimensional analysis alone. In particular $q^2=2k$ for the turbulent-energy convention in Question 3. The normalization of $\chi$ is the one fixed by the printed scalar balance; if the budget is written for the full variance rather than one half of it, both production and dissipation terms acquire a factor two.
