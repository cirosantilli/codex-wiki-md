<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At the [porous thermal front](../../../../../../thermal-front-in-a-porous-medium.md), the cold polymer solution behind has [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) $\mu$, while the hot solution ahead has [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) $b\mu>\mu$. Thus a more mobile region pushes a less mobile one. A forward protrusion locally reduces the length of high-resistance hot fluid and concentrates [Darcy flux](../../../../../../darcy-velocity.md) into itself; it advances faster, amplifying the protrusion. This is the mechanism of the [Saffman–Taylor instability](../../../../../../saffman-taylor-instability.md).

The leading polymer–oil interface can nevertheless be stable when the hot polymer [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) is at least the oil [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md). Stability of that leading interface does not guarantee stability of the trailing [porous thermal front](../../../../../../thermal-front-in-a-porous-medium.md). Slow [thermal conduction](../../../../../../thermal-conduction.md) makes the latter sharp and offers weak short-wave smoothing; in practice it is the adverse [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) contrast across this front that can invalidate the assumed planar stable-flow calculation.
