Here $k$ is [turbulent kinetic energy](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy.md) per unit mass, $k=\tfrac12\overline{u'^2+v'^2+w'^2}$, rather than the [permeability of a porous medium](../../../../../../permeability-of-a-porous-medium.md) used in the porous-flow questions. The [K-epsilon turbulence model](../../../../../../k-epsilon-turbulence-model.md) represents the high-Reynolds-number transport of this energy and its [turbulent kinetic energy dissipation rate](../../../../../../turbulent-kinetic-energy-dissipation-rate.md) $\epsilon$.

In the $k$ equation, $d[(\nu_T/\sigma_k)k_y]/dy$ is the divergence of modeled [turbulent diffusion](../../../../../../eddy-diffusion.md), $\mathcal P$ is [mean-shear production](../../../../../../mean-shear-production-of-turbulent-kinetic-energy.md), and $-\epsilon$ is molecular conversion of fluctuation energy into heat. Statistical stationarity and fully developed flow have removed storage and streamwise [advection](../../../../../../advection.md) from this reduction.

In the $\epsilon$ equation, $d[(\nu_T/\sigma_\epsilon)\epsilon_y]/dy$ transports dissipation intensity; $C_{\epsilon1}\mathcal P\epsilon/k$ models its generation associated with energy production; and $-C_{\epsilon2}\epsilon^2/k$ models its destruction. The time scale $k/\epsilon$ supplies the source-rate dimension. The $\epsilon$ equation is a closure, not another energy-conservation equation. Finally, $\nu_T=C_\mu k^2/\epsilon$ has [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) dimensions and parametrizes [Reynolds stress](../../../../../../reynolds-stress.md) transport; $\sigma_k,\sigma_\epsilon$ set the relative diffusion strengths, and the remaining constants calibrate the closure.
