<h1 id="9f/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $r=|\sin x|\in[0,1]$. For $r<1$, the absolute summand $r^n/\sqrt n$ is at most $r^n$, so a [geometric series](../../../../../../geometric-series.md) comparison proves [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md). For $r=1$, the [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) is $\sum(-1)^n/\sqrt n$: its positive term sizes decrease to zero, so the [alternating series test](../../../../../../alternating-series-test.md) proves convergence. Its absolute [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) is a divergent [p-series](../../../../../../p-series.md) with exponent $1/2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{ll}x\notin\{\pi/2+k\pi:k\in\mathbb Z\}:&\text{absolute convergence},\\x\in\{\pi/2+k\pi:k\in\mathbb Z\}:&\text{conditional convergence}.\end{array}}
$$

**It converges for every real $x$ and never diverges.**
