↑ **Parent:** [2B](../2b.md)

For a complex [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), its [Hermitian conjugate](../../../../../hermitian-conjugation.md) is obtained by transposing and taking entrywise [complex conjugates](../../../../../complex-conjugate.md):

$$
(A^\dagger)_{ij}=\overline{A_{ji}},\qquad A^\dagger=\overline A^{\,T}.
$$

The defining conditions are

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is Hermitian}\iff A^\dagger=A,\qquad A\text{ is unitary}\iff A^\dagger A=AA^\dagger=I.}
$$

For a square [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), either one of the two inverse identities in the [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) condition implies the other. A [Hermitian matrix](../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) may have complex entries, but its diagonal entries are real.
