<h1 id="10a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The two incoming particles have equal energies and opposite [momenta](../../../../../../momentum.md), so their total [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) in this frame is

$$
P_{\rm in}=\left(\frac{2E}{c},\mathbf0\right).
$$

It is already a [centre-of-momentum frame](../../../../../../center-of-momentum-frame.md). By [four-momentum conservation](../../../../../../four-momentum-conservation.md), the final spatial momenta sum to zero and their energies sum to $2E$. Each product has energy

$$
E_i=\sqrt{\mu_i^2c^4+c^2|\mathbf p_i|^2}\geq\mu_ic^2.
$$

Summing gives the necessary [relativistic collision mass threshold](../../../../../../relativistic-collision-mass-threshold.md)

$$
\boxed{E\geq\frac12\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\mu_i\right)c^2.}
$$

For massive products the equality threshold has all of them at rest in this frame. Above it, multiple products can carry balancing momenta and relative [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md). The displayed condition is necessary, as requested; a single final particle must instead have exactly the incoming invariant mass, and additional physical conservation laws could further restrict a real reaction.
