<h1 id="2e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Eliminate $x$ by twice the first [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md) minus three times the second. This gives

$$
3y\equiv2(4)-3(14)=-34\equiv13\pmod{47}.
$$

The [modular inverse](../../../../../../modular-multiplicative-inverse.md) of $3$ is $16$, because $3\cdot16=48\equiv1$. Hence $y\equiv16\cdot13\equiv20$. Substitution into the second [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md) gives $6x\equiv14-7(20)\equiv15$, and $6^{-1}\equiv8$, so $x\equiv8\cdot15\equiv26$. Thus

$$
\boxed{x=26+47r,\qquad y=20+47s,\qquad r,s\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

In particular, $(x,y)=(26,20)$ works: $9(26)+12(20)=474=10(47)+4$ and $6(26)+7(20)=296=6(47)+14$. The elimination is reversible because $3$ and $6$ have [modular inverses](../../../../../../modular-multiplicative-inverse.md), so the displayed classes contain all solutions.
