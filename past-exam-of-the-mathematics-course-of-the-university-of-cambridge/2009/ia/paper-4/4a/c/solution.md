<h1 id="4a/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With the Sun fixed, its gravitational force on the planet is a [central force](../../../../../../central-force.md). In the fixed orbital plane, the transverse acceleration in [polar coordinates](../../../../../../polar-coordinates.md) therefore vanishes:

$$
r\ddot\theta+2\dot r\dot\theta=0,\qquad \frac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot\theta)=0.
$$

During $dt$, the position vector sweeps an infinitesimal sector of area $dA=\tfrac12r^2d\theta$. Hence the [areal velocity](../../../../../../areal-velocity.md) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{dA}{dt}=\frac12r^2\dot\theta=\frac{L}{2m}=\text{constant}.}
$$

Integrating over any two equal time intervals gives equal swept areas, proving [Kepler's second law](../../../../../../kepler-s-second-law.md). Here $L$ is the signed component of [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) normal to the orbital plane. The argument uses [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md); an inverse-square dependence is not needed for this particular law, only a central force.
