↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [central force](../../../../../../central-force.md) acts along the line joining the particle to a fixed centre. Taking that centre as the origin, its usual position-dependent form is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F(\mathbf r)=f(r)\,\mathbf e_r,\qquad r=|\mathbf r|,\quad\mathbf e_r=\mathbf r/r.}
$$

Positive $f$ gives an outward force and negative $f$ an inward one. For a conventional [central force](../../../../../../central-force.md) the magnitude depends only on distance from the centre, not on direction. The angular-momentum conclusions below require only that the force remain radial about that fixed centre.
