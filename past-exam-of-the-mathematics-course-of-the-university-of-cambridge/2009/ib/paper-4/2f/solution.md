<h1 id="2f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2F](../2f.md)

Let $T$ consist of all [torsion elements of a module](../../../../../torsion-element-of-a-module.md) in $M$. If $rm=0$ and $sn=0$ with $r,s\ne0$, then $rs(m+n)=0$. Since $R$ is an [integral domain](../../../../../integral-domain.md), $rs\ne0$. Also $r(am)=0$ for every scalar $a\in R$. Thus $T$ is the [torsion submodule](../../../../../torsion-submodule.md).

If $r(m+T)=0$ in $M/T$ with $r\ne0$, then $rm\in T$, so some $s\ne0$ satisfies $srm=0$. The domain property again gives $sr\ne0$, hence $m\in T$. Therefore $M/T$ is a [torsion-free module](../../../../../torsion-free-module.md).

Finally, any submodule $N$ with the stated properties satisfies $N\subseteq T$. Conversely, if $m\in T$, its coset in $M/N$ is torsion and therefore zero, so $m\in N$. Hence $\boxed{N=T}$ is the unique choice.
