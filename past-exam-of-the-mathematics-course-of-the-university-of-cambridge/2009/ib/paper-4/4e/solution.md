<h1 id="4e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4E](../4e.md)

[Rouché's theorem](../../../../../rouche-s-theorem.md) states that if $f,g$ are holomorphic on and inside a simple closed contour and $|g|<|f|$ on it, then $f$ and $f+g$ have the same number of zeros inside, counted with multiplicity. On $|z|=1$, the [power series](../../../../../power-series.md) for sine gives

$$
|\sin z|\leq\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac1{(2j+1)!}=\sinh1<2=|2z|.
$$

Apply [Rouché's theorem](../../../../../rouche-s-theorem.md) with $f=2z$ and $g=-\sin z$. There is one zero inside and none on the boundary. Since $z=0$ is a zero and $(2z-\sin z)'|_{z=0}=1$, it is simple. **Exactly one complex number qualifies: $z=0$.**
