<h1 id="1g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1G](../1g.md)

If $\dim V=n$, then $\dim\operatorname{End}(V)=n^2$. The $n^2+1$ [endomorphisms](../../../../../endomorphism.md) $I,T,\ldots,T^{n^2}$ are therefore [linearly dependent](../../../../../linear-dependence.md), so some nonzero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $p$ satisfies $p(T)=0$. The [minimal polynomial](../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) is the [monic polynomial](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) of least degree annihilating $T$. Uniqueness follows because the difference of two such [monic polynomials](../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) would be a nonzero annihilator of smaller degree.

A [Jordan block](../../../../../jordan-block.md) $J_r(\lambda)$ has minimal polynomial $(x-\lambda)^r$, and the minimal polynomial of a direct sum is the [least common multiple](../../../../../least-common-multiple.md) of those of its blocks. Thus an eight-dimensional example is

$$
\boxed{T=J_2(0)\oplus J_3(1)\oplus J_3(1),\qquad m_T(x)=x^2(x-1)^3.}
$$
