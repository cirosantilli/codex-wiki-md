<h1 id="17a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the inner conductor to be at potential $V$ above the outer. A cylindrical [Gauss law](../../../../../../gauss-s-law.md) surface of length $\ell$ encloses charge $\lambda\ell$ and gives $E_r2\pi r\ell=\lambda\ell/\epsilon_0$. The potential difference is

$$
V=\int_a^bE_r\,dr=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\ln\frac ba.
$$

Thus the [coaxial cylindrical capacitor](../../../../../../coaxial-cylindrical-capacitor.md) field and charge are

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{2\pi\epsilon_0V}{\ln(b/a)},\qquad
\mathbf E=\frac{V}{r\ln(b/a)}\mathbf e_r.}
$$

The perfectly conducting surfaces have no tangential [electric field](../../../../../../electric-field.md) in this idealized steady model.
