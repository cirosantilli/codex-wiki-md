↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False.** The [rational numbers](../../../../../../rational-number.md) $\mathbb Q$ with their [subspace topology](../../../../../../subspace-topology.md) from $\mathbb R$ are Hausdorff. If a subset contains two distinct rationals $a<b$, choose an irrational $c$ between them; intersecting the subset with $(-\infty,c)$ and $(c,\infty)$ gives a separation into two nonempty relatively open sets. Thus every nonempty [connected subset](../../../../../../connected-subset.md) of $\mathbb Q$ is a singleton, so it is [totally disconnected](../../../../../../totally-disconnected-space.md). Yet no rational is isolated, because every interval around it contains another rational. Therefore $\mathbb Q$ is not a [discrete space](../../../../../../discrete-space.md). Hausdorffness and total disconnectedness do not imply discreteness.
