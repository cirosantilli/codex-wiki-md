↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**True.** For nonempty [connected spaces](../../../../../../connected-space.md) $X,Y$, choose $x_0\in X$, $y_0\in Y$. For each $x\in X$, the subspace $(X\times\{y_0\})\cup(\{x\}\times Y)$ is [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md): its two [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) pieces meet at $(x,y_0)$. The union of these subspaces over $x$ is all of $X\times Y$, and every piece contains the common [connected subset](../../../../../../connected-subset.md) $X\times\{y_0\}$. Their union is therefore [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md). The assertion is also true if a factor is empty, since the empty product space is [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md). This proves [product of connected spaces](../../../../../../product-of-connected-spaces.md).
