<h1 id="4f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**True.** Fix a point $x_0$ of a nonempty [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md) open set and let $P$ be its [path-connected component](../../../../../../path-component.md). Each point of the open set has a small Euclidean ball contained in it. A ball is [convex](../../../../../../convex-function.md) and [path-connected](../../../../../../path-connected-space.md), so each such ball lies entirely in one [path component](../../../../../../path-component.md). All [path components](../../../../../../path-component.md) are therefore open, and the complement of $P$ is open as well. If the complement were nonempty, these two open sets would disconnect the original set. Thus $P$ is the whole set. In fact the joining paths may be chosen polygonal.
