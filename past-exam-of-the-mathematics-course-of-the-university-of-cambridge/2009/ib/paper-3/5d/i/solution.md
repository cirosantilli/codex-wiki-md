<h1 id="5d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take the circle with the usual positive, counterclockwise orientation. The only singularity is at zero, and the [Laurent series](../../../../../../laurent-series.md) gives

$$
ze^{1/z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^{1-n}}{n!}.
$$

The coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is $1/2$, so the [residue theorem](../../../../../../residue-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\oint_Cze^{1/z}\,dz=2\pi i\cdot\frac12=\pi i.}
$$

The [essential singularity](../../../../../../essential-singularity.md) at zero still has a well-defined [residue](../../../../../../residue.md); the theorem does not require it to be a [pole](../../../../../../pole.md).
