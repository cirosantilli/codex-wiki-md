<h1 id="2f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2F](../2f.md)

For a finite [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of order $p^am$ with $p$ prime and $p\nmid m$, the [Sylow theorems](../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) assert that a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of order $p^a$ exists; every [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) whose order is a power of $p$ is contained in one such [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md); all [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for that prime are conjugate; and their number $n_p$ divides $m$ and is congruent to one modulo $p$. A unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), since conjugation cannot move it to another one.

Here $56=7\cdot8$, so $n_7$ divides eight and is congruent to one modulo seven. Hence $n_7=1$ or $8$. In the first case the [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order seven is normal. In the second case, any two distinct order-seven [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) intersect only at the identity, because a nontrivial intersection would generate both prime-order [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md). Their nonidentity elements therefore account for $8\cdot6=48$ elements.

Exactly eight elements of the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) remain. Every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order eight consists entirely of these remaining elements: a nonidentity element in it has order a power of two and cannot be one of the order-seven elements already counted. Each such [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) must consequently equal the remaining eight-element set. It is unique and hence normal. Thus **a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of order 56 has a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of order seven or of order eight**.
