<h1 id="12g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take

$$
\boxed{M(z)=\frac{3z-1}{3-z}.}
$$

Its defining [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) has nonzero [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) eight, so it is a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md). Direct substitution shows that it fixes $p=1$ and $q=-1$, which satisfy $p\overline q=-1$. However, $M'(1)=8/(3-1)^2=2$. Differentiating the inverse projection formula gives the round spherical line element $ds=2|dz|/(1+|z|^2)$. At a fixed finite point, its local length multiplier under a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) is exactly $|M'(p)|$, since the metric factors in numerator and denominator agree. Here that multiplier is two, so this map is not an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) and cannot be a [rotation in three dimensions](../../../../../../rotation-in-three-dimensions.md) of the sphere. It demonstrates that [antipodal fixed points do not characterize sphere rotations](../../../../../../antipodal-fixed-points-do-not-characterize-sphere-rotations.md).
