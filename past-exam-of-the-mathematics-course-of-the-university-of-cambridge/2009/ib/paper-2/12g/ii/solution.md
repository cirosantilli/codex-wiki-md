<h1 id="12g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Reflection of the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) in the plane $Y=0$ sends $(X,Y,Z)$ to $(X,-Y,Z)$. It preserves [dot products](../../../../../../dot-product.md), hence the [spherical distance](../../../../../../great-circle-distance.md) between every pair of points. Its stereographic expression is

$$
\boxed{z\longmapsto\overline z.}
$$

This is an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) of the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) but is not a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md): at any finite point, its complex difference quotient is one along real increments and minus one along imaginary increments, so it is not [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md). A [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) is [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) away from its pole. The geometric distinction is that this reflection reverses orientation, whereas a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) preserves it.
