<h1 id="1d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $x=3+18k$. The second [modular congruence](../../../../../../modular-congruence.md) becomes $18k\equiv-1\pmod{23}$. The [Bezout identity](../../../../../../bezout-identity.md) above says that $9$ is the inverse of $18$ modulo $23$, so $k\equiv-9\equiv14\pmod{23}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{x=255,\qquad x\equiv255\pmod{414}.}
$$

The checks are $255=18\cdot14+3=23\cdot11+2$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) explains uniqueness modulo $18\cdot23=414$, since the moduli are [coprime integers](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md).
