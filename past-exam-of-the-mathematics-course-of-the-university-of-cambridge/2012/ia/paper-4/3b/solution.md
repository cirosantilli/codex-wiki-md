<h1 id="3b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3B](../3b.md)

By [Newton's third law](../../../../../newton-s-third-law.md), the [force](../../../../../force.md) on the second particle is $-F_{12}$. For $M=m_1+m_2$ and [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) $R=(m_1r_1+m_2r_2)/M$, [Newton's second law](../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) gives $M\ddot R=F_{12}-F_{12}=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{R(t)=R(0)+t\dot R(0).}
$$

The centre moves on a straight line with constant [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md); zero [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) is allowed. For the relative position $r=r_1-r_2$, subtraction of the two equations gives

$$
\ddot r=\left(\frac1{m_1}+\frac1{m_2}\right)F_{12}(r),\qquad
\boxed{\mu\ddot r=F_{12}(r),\quad\mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}.}
$$

Hence the [two-body problem](../../../../../two-body-problem.md) reduces to a single particle of [reduced mass](../../../../../reduced-mass.md) $\mu$, together with the elementary centre motion. Reconstruct the positions as $r_1=R+(m_2/M)r$, $r_2=R-(m_1/M)r$.

**Yes: the two equal [masses](../../../../../mass.md) can follow the same fixed circle in diametrically opposite positions.** Put the stationary [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) at the circle's center and choose tangential [velocities](../../../../../velocity.md) with the same sense of rotation. If the circle has radius $a$, the separation is $2a$ and the mutual gravitational [force](../../../../../force.md) is directed toward that center, with magnitude $Gm^2/(4a^2)$. The [circular orbit](../../../../../circular-orbit.md) condition is $ma\omega^2=Gm^2/(4a^2)$, so an [equal-mass circular binary](../../../../../equal-mass-circular-binary.md) has

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=\frac{Gm}{4a^3}.}
$$

These initial data maintain the opposite positions and give the same constant [angular speed](../../../../../angular-speed.md) for both particles.
