<h1 id="7d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For $P(x)=x^3+4x-7$, the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) $P'(x)=3x^2+4$ is positive everywhere, so the [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) makes $P$ strictly increasing. Since a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is a [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), the [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) and the values $P(1)=-2$, $P(2)=9$ locate its unique real root in $(1,2)$. By the [rational root theorem](../../../../../../rational-root-theorem.md), a rational root of a monic [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) polynomial must be an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md). There is no [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) in $(1,2)$, so **the unique real root is an [irrational number](../../../../../../irrational-number.md)**. The denominator part of that theorem also follows directly: if $a/b$ in lowest terms were a root, $a^3+4ab^2-7b^3=0$ would [force](../../../../../../force.md) $b\mid a^3$, hence $b=1$.
