<h1 id="7d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [exponential function](../../../../../../exponential-function.md) series gives [Euler's number](../../../../../../e-mathematical-constant.md) $e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}1/k!$. Suppose $e=a/b$ with [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) $a,b$ and $b>0$. Choose $n\geq\max(b,2)$. Since $b$ divides $n!$, the number

$$
N=n!\left(e-\sum_{k=0}^n\frac1{k!}\right)
$$

is an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md). But its positive tail satisfies

$$
0<N=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{(n+j)!}
<\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(n+1)^j}=\frac1n<1.
$$

The inequality is strict because the factors after the first exceed $n+1$ when $j\geq2$. No [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) lies strictly between zero and one. This proves the [irrationality of e](../../../../../../irrationality-of-e.md):

$$
\boxed{e
otin\mathbb Q.}
$$
