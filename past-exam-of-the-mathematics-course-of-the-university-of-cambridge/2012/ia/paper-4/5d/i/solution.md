<h1 id="5d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $fg=f\circ g$. **The assertion can fail on an [infinite set](../../../../../../infinite-set.md).** On $X=\mathbb N_0$, [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) $g(n)=n+1$, $f(0)=0$, and $f(n)=n-1$ for $n\geq1$. Then $f(g(n))=n$ for every $n$, but $g(f(0))=1\ne0$. Thus a [right inverse](../../../../../../right-inverse.md) need not be a [left inverse](../../../../../../left-inverse.md).

**On a [finite set](../../../../../../finite-set.md) it is always true.** From $fg=\operatorname{id}_X$, $g$ is an [injection](../../../../../../injective-function.md): equality of two $g$-values gives equality after applying $f$. An injective self-map of a finite [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is a [bijection](../../../../../../bijection.md). Composing $fg=\operatorname{id}_X$ with $g^{-1}$ shows $f=g^{-1}$, and hence $gf=\operatorname{id}_X$. This includes the empty [set](../../../../../../set-split.md).
