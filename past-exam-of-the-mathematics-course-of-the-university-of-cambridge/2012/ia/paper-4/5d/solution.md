<h1 id="5d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5D](../5d.md)

The final property concerns a [pointwise periodic self-map](../../../../../pointwise-periodic-self-map.md). Such a [map](../../../../../function-class.md) is a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md): each point has a predecessor in its finite cycle, giving surjectivity; if $f(x)=f(y)$, choose a common multiple $N$ of periods of $x,y$ and apply $f^{N-1}$ to get $x=y$. Its finite cycles are therefore disjoint.

If $X$ is finite, choose the [least common multiple](../../../../../least-common-multiple.md) of the finitely many cycle lengths. This positive [integer](../../../../../integer.md) $N$ gives $f^N=\operatorname{id}_X$. The empty [set](../../../../../set-split.md) satisfies the property with $N=1$.

Conversely, in the usual [set](../../../../../set-split.md) theory with choice, every infinite $X$ contains a [countably infinite set](../../../../../countably-infinite-set.md). Divide such a subset into disjoint finite blocks $B_j$ of sizes $j+1$, $j\geq1$, make $f$ a cyclic permutation on each block, and fix every other point. Every point returns to itself, but an identity iterate would have to be divisible by every block length $j+1$. No positive [integer](../../../../../integer.md) has that property: take a block longer than that [integer](../../../../../integer.md). Thus the [uniform period criterion for pointwise periodic maps](../../../../../uniform-period-criterion-for-pointwise-periodic-maps.md) gives

$$
\boxed{X\text{ has the property exactly when }X\text{ is finite}.}
$$

The [axiom of choice](../../../../../axiom-of-choice.md) assumption is stated here because extracting a countably infinite subset of an arbitrary infinite [set](../../../../../set-split.md) is not an unrestricted theorem in [set](../../../../../set-split.md) theory without choice.
