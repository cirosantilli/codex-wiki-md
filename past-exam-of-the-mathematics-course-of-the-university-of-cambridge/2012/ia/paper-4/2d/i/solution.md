↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**The intersection is always an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).** Both component relations contain $(x,x)$, so $V$ is a [reflexive relation](../../../../../../reflexive-relation.md). If $xVy$, both relations relate $x$ to $y$, and symmetry in each gives $yVx$. If $xVy$ and $yVz$, [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) in each component gives both $xRz$ and $xSz$, hence $xVz$. This is the [intersection of equivalence relations](../../../../../../intersection-of-equivalence-relations.md); the same argument works for any family of such relations.
