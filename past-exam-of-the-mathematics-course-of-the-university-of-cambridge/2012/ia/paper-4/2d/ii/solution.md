<h1 id="2d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The union need not be an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md)**, because [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) can mix the two relations. Take $X=\{1,2,3\}$. Let $R$ have [equivalence classes](../../../../../../equivalence-class.md) $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3\}$, and $S$ have classes $\{1\}$ and $\{2,3\}$. Then $1W2$ and $2W3$, but neither component relates $1$ to $3$, so $1W3$ fails. The [union of equivalence relations](../../../../../../union-of-equivalence-relations.md) is reflexive and symmetric; it is precisely [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) that may fail.
