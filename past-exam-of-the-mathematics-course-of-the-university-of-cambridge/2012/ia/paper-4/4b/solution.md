<h1 id="4b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4B](../4b.md)

For $0<v<c$, the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) with coincident origins is

$$
\boxed{t'=\gamma\left(t-\frac{vx}{c^2}\right),\qquad x'=\gamma(x-vt),\quad\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

For two simultaneous events in $S$, $\Delta t=0$, so $\Delta t'=-\gamma v\Delta x/c^2$. Distinct spatial positions therefore generally give different times in the boosted frame: this is [relativity of simultaneity](../../../../../relativity-of-simultaneity.md).

Choose the emission events at $(t,x)=(0,0)$ and $(0,d)$. After emission, the [photon](../../../../../photon.md) world lines are $x=ct$ and $x=d+ct$. Applying the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) to either gives

$$
x'-ct'=\gamma(1+v/c)(x-ct).
$$

Thus the two [photons](../../../../../photon.md) move at [speed](../../../../../speed.md) $c$ in $S'$ on parallel lines whose intercepts differ by $\gamma(1+v/c)d$. Measuring their positions at the same $t'$ after both emissions gives the [photon separation under a collinear Lorentz boost](../../../../../photon-separation-under-a-collinear-lorentz-boost.md):

$$
\boxed{\Delta x'=\gamma(1+v/c)d=d\sqrt{\frac{c+v}{c-v}}.}
$$

This separation is constant. It is not the contracted distance between the stationary sources: the two emission events are not simultaneous in $S'$, and one [photon](../../../../../photon.md) has already moved when the other is emitted.
