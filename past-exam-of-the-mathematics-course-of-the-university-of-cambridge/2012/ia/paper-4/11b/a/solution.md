The [parallel axis theorem](../../../../../../parallel-axis-theorem.md) says that for an axis through a body's [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) and a parallel axis at perpendicular distance $d$,

$$
\boxed{I=I_{\rm cm}+Md^2,}
$$

where $M$ is the total [mass](../../../../../../mass.md). The displacement is the distance between the two parallel axes; neither axis may be arbitrary in place of the center-of-mass axis. Expanding the squared distances of [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) elements gives this formula, since the cross term integrates to zero at the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md).
