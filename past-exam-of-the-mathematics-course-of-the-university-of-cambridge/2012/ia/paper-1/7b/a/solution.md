<h1 id="7b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Expansion along the first column gives the [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md)

$$
\chi_M(t)=\det(tI-M)=(t-2)[(t-1)(t-4)+2]=(t-2)^2(t-3).
$$

Thus the [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $2$ has [algebraic multiplicity](../../../../../../algebraic-multiplicity.md) two. Solving $(M-2I)(x,y,z)^T=0$ gives $y=0$ and then $z=0$, leaving $x$ arbitrary. Its [eigenspace](../../../../../../eigenspace.md) is $\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,0)^T\}$ and has [geometric multiplicity](../../../../../../geometric-multiplicity.md) one. A [diagonalisable matrix](../../../../../../diagonalizable-matrix.md) needs an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) [basis](../../../../../../basis.md), so **$M$ is not diagonalisable**.
