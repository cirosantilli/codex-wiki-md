<h1 id="11d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the discontinuous example

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x/2,&0<x\leq1/2,\\(x+1/2)/2,&1/2<x<1.\end{cases}}
$$

In both pieces $0<f(x)<x$. For $x>1/2$, the image stays strictly above $1/2$, and induction gives

$$
f^n(x)=\frac12+2^{-n}\left(x-\frac12\right)\longrightarrow\frac12.
$$

This positive limiting point is not a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md), since $f(1/2)=1/4$. The discontinuity is precisely why the limit cannot be passed through $f$. Therefore **the pointwise zero-limit conclusion can fail without continuity**.
