**No: pointwise convergence need not be [uniform convergence](../../../../../../uniform-convergence.md).** The [continuous function](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f(x)=x^2$ maps $(0,1)$ into itself and is strictly below the identity. Its [iteration of a map](../../../../../../iterated-function.md) gives

$$
\boxed{f^n(x)=x^{2^n}.}
$$

For every finite $n$, these values tend to $1$ as $x\uparrow1$, so $\sup_{0<x<1}f^n(x)=1$. With $\varepsilon=1/2$, choosing $x>2^{-1/2^n}$ gives $f^n(x)>1/2$. Thus no single iterate works for every starting point, although each fixed starting point tends to zero.
