<h1 id="12f/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [series](../../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) $\sum u_n$ with $u_n>0$, suppose $L=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n+1}/u_n$ exists, allowing $L=+\infty$. The [ratio test](../../../../../../../ratio-test.md) says **the series converges if $L<1$ and diverges if $L>1$**. In the first case an eventual ratio bound by some $q<1$ compares the tail to a [geometric series](../../../../../../../geometric-series.md). In the second case an eventual ratio above some $q>1$ prevents the terms tending to zero.

**If $L=1$, the test is inconclusive**: both $\sum1/n$ and $\sum1/n^2$ have limiting ratio one, while the first diverges and the second converges. If the ratio has no limit, this particular limit formulation gives no conclusion.
