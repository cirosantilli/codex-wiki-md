<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Represent the [smooth plane conic](../../../../../../smooth-plane-conic.md) by $v^{\mathsf T}Qv=0$, with $Q$ an invertible symmetric $3\times3$ matrix. The tangent line at $[v]$ has coefficients $\ell=Qv$, because the differential is $2v^{\mathsf T}Q$. Thus its image in the [dual projective space](../../../../../../dual-projective-space.md) satisfies

$$
\ell^{\mathsf T}Q^{-1}\ell=0.
$$

Conversely any nonzero $\ell$ satisfying this equation gives $v=Q^{-1}\ell$, a point of the original [smooth plane conic](../../../../../../smooth-plane-conic.md) whose tangent is $[\ell]$. This correspondence is the restriction of an invertible projective linear transformation. **The [dual conic](../../../../../../dual-conic.md) is therefore the smooth conic**

$$
\boxed{C_1^\vee:\ell^{\mathsf T}Q^{-1}\ell=0.}
$$
