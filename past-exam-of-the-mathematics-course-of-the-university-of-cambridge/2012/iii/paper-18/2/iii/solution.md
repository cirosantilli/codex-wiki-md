<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [Appell–Humbert theorem](../../../../../../appell-humbert-theorem.md) identifies the positive [Hermitian form](../../../../../../hermitian-form.md) $H_1$ with a [holomorphic line bundle](../../../../../../holomorphic-line-bundle.md) whose [First Chern class](../../../../../../first-chern-class.md) is represented, in our linear-first convention, by

$$
\omega=\frac{i}{2}\,dz\wedge d\overline z=dx\wedge dy.
$$

Its integral over the square fundamental domain is $1$, so $\deg L=1$. In particular the oriented Chern pairing is $-E_1(1,i)=1$; using $E_1(1,i)$ without adjusting the Hermitian convention would give the wrong sign.

The [complex torus](../../../../../../complex-torus.md) has a nowhere-vanishing differential $dz$, so its [canonical divisor](../../../../../../canonical-divisor.md) is trivial and its genus is one. A nonzero section of a [line bundle](../../../../../../line-bundle.md) of negative degree would have an [effective divisor](../../../../../../effective-cartier-divisor.md) of negative degree, which is impossible. Thus $H^0(X,L^{-1})=0$. The [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md) and [Serre duality](../../../../../../serre-duality.md) give

$$
h^0(X,L)-h^0(X,L^{-1})=\deg L=1.
$$

**Therefore $\boxed{h^0(X,L)=1}$.** The choice of [semicharacter of a complex lattice](../../../../../../semicharacter-of-a-complex-lattice.md) changes the degree-zero component of the [line bundle](../../../../../../line-bundle.md), but not this calculation.
