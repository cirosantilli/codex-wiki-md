<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $E=\mathbb C/\Lambda$, and let $\Lambda'$ be the inverse image of the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $G$ under the quotient map. Then $\Lambda\subset\Lambda'$ is an inclusion of [Euclidean lattices](../../../../../../euclidean-lattice.md) with $\Lambda'/\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. The [Smith normal form](../../../../../../smith-normal-form.md) supplies a basis $u,v$ of $\Lambda'$ for which

$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z(Nu)+\mathbb Zv.
$$

Choose the orientation of this basis so that $\operatorname{Im}(v/(Nu))>0$. Dividing the coordinate on $\mathbb C$ by $Nu$ gives an isomorphism of [complex tori](../../../../../../complex-torus.md)

$$
E\cong \mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau),\qquad \tau=\frac{v}{Nu}.
$$

Here $u$ maps to $1/N$, and its class generates $\Lambda'/\Lambda$. Thus the isomorphism carries $G$ to the required [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md). **Every pair is represented: $\Phi$ is surjective.** This argument avoids assuming that an arbitrary integer lift of a primitive vector modulo $N$ is itself primitive.
