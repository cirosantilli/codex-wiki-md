<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For nonzero $a$, $\Sigma=0$ requires $r=0$, $\theta=\pi/2$. Oblate Cartesian coordinates satisfy $x^2+y^2=(r^2+a^2)\sin^2\theta$, $z=r\cos\theta$, so this is the [Kerr ring singularity](../../../../../../kerr-ring-singularity.md) of radius $|a|$. Curvature components and generic invariants diverge there. The whole $r=0$ surface is not singular: away from the ring it is a disk through which the extension continues to a sheet with $r<0$. This oblate radial parameter is not a nonnegative Euclidean distance.

In $G=c=1$ units, **$\boxed{J=Ma}$.** Horizon candidates solve $\Delta=0$, giving $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$. For $J^2>M^4$, equivalently $|a|>M$, there are no real roots. **The over-rotating Kerr solution has no [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) hiding its naked ring singularity.** The term [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) in this regime names the Kerr family, not a hidden-singularity [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md).
