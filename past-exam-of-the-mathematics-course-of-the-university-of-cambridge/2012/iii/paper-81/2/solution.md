<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Let $F/K$ be a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md), choose a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak q$ above $\mathfrak p$, and write $k_{\mathfrak q}$ and $k_{\mathfrak p}$ for the [residue fields](../../../../../residue-field.md). The [decomposition group](../../../../../decomposition-group.md) and [inertia group](../../../../../inertia-group.md) are

$$
\boxed{D_{\mathfrak q}=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/K):\sigma\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q\},\qquad
I_{\mathfrak q}=\ker\bigl(D_{\mathfrak q}\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p})\bigr).}
$$

The [Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../frobenius-automorphism.md) on the [finite field](../../../../../finite-field.md) $k_{\mathfrak q}$ is $x\mapsto x^{N\mathfrak p}$. Its inverse image defines a distinguished coset in $D_{\mathfrak q}/I_{\mathfrak q}$; a Frobenius element is a lift of that coset. At an [unramified](../../../../../unramified-extension.md) prime, $I_{\mathfrak q}=1$ and the lift is unique. Changing the prime above $\mathfrak p$ conjugates it, so a [Frobenius conjugacy class](../../../../../frobenius-conjugacy-class.md) is intrinsically attached to $\mathfrak p$. These are arithmetic Frobenius conventions.
