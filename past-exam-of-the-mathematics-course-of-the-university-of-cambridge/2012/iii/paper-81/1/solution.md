<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $L/K$ be an extension of [number fields](../../../../../number-field.md), let $\alpha\in\mathcal O_L$ be a [primitive element of a field extension](../../../../../primitive-element-of-a-field-extension.md), and let $f\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ be its monic [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md). Put $R=\mathcal O_K[\alpha]$ and $S=\mathcal O_L$. The [Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../kummer-dedekind-theorem.md) applies at a nonzero [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathcal O_K$ when $R_{\mathfrak p}=S_{\mathfrak p}$: equivalently, $\mathfrak p$ is outside the support of the finite-index quotient $S/R$. Write the factorization over the [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) $k=\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p$ as

$$
\overline f=\prod_{j=1}^r\overline g_j^{\,m_j},
$$

with distinct monic [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) $\overline g_j$, and choose monic lifts $g_j\in\mathcal O_K[X]$. Then

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak pS=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathfrak P_j^{\,m_j},\qquad
\mathfrak P_j=(\mathfrak p,g_j(\alpha)),\qquad
f(\mathfrak P_j/\mathfrak p)=\deg\overline g_j.}
$$

Here $f(\mathfrak P_j/\mathfrak p)$ denotes the [residue-field degree](../../../../../residue-field-degree.md), and the [ramification index of a prime ideal](../../../../../ramification-index-of-a-prime-ideal.md) is $m_j$.

To prove this, the local index condition gives

$$
S/\mathfrak pS\simeq R/\mathfrak pR
\simeq k[X]/(\overline f)
\simeq\prod_{j=1}^r k[X]/(\overline g_j^{\,m_j}).
$$

The final isomorphism is the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md). Each factor is a local ring with unique maximal ideal generated by $\overline g_j$, so its inverse image in $S$ is exactly $\mathfrak P_j$, and its [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) is $k[X]/(\overline g_j)$. This proves the list of primes and the [residue-field degrees](../../../../../residue-field-degree.md). For the exponents, localize at $\mathfrak P_j$. The [ring of integers of a number field](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is a [Dedekind domain](../../../../../dedekind-domain.md), so $S_{\mathfrak P_j}$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md). If $\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak P_j}=\mathfrak P_j^{e_j}S_{\mathfrak P_j}$, the quotient has $e_j$ successive layers isomorphic to $S/\mathfrak P_j$, and hence $k$-dimension $e_j\deg\overline g_j$. The corresponding polynomial factor has $k$-dimension $m_j\deg\overline g_j$. Thus $e_j=m_j$, completing the proof rather than merely identifying the radicals.

For the calculation, set $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and $t=(\sqrt5-1)/2$. The [ring of integers of a quadratic field](../../../../../ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field.md) gives $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[t]$, with $t^2+t-1=0$. The [algebraic norm](../../../../../algebraic-norm.md) of $4+\sqrt5$ is $11$, so its [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) has [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) $11$. Its quotient ring has prime order and is therefore the [finite field](../../../../../finite-field.md) $\mathbb F_{11}$; consequently **$(4+\sqrt5)$ is a prime ideal**. In that [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md), $\sqrt5=-4=7$ and $t=3$.

Let $L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$. Since $t=\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}$ lies in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$, the permitted identity $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ gives $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_K[\zeta_5]$. Thus the local index condition holds at every prime. The relative [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) is $X^2-tX+1$, whose reduction is

$$
X^2-3X+1=(X-5)(X-9)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{11}[X].
$$

The roots are distinct. Applying the [Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../kummer-dedekind-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{(4+\sqrt5)\mathcal O_L
=(4+\sqrt5,\zeta_5-5)(4+\sqrt5,\zeta_5-9)
=(11,\zeta_5-5)(11,\zeta_5-9).}
$$

For the last equality, evaluation at $5$ or $9$ in $\mathbb F_{11}$ gives $\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}=3$ and therefore $4+\sqrt5=5+2t=0$. Both descriptions are the same kernels of these evaluation maps. Each prime has [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) $11$, [residue-field degree](../../../../../residue-field-degree.md) one and [ramification index of a prime ideal](../../../../../ramification-index-of-a-prime-ideal.md) one.
