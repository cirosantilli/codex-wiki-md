<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use $y$ on the target and $x$ on the source, so the map of coordinate [rings](../../../../../ring.md) sends $y$ to $x^4$. A [closed point](../../../../../closed-point.md) of the real affine line corresponds to a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) generated either by $y-a$, $a\in\mathbb R$, or by a monic irreducible quadratic $q(y)$. The [scheme-theoretic fibre](../../../../../scheme-theoretic-fibre.md) over that point has coordinate algebra

$$
B_p=\mathbb R[x]\otimes_{\mathbb R[y]}\kappa(p)
=\mathbb R[x]/(q_p(x^4)).
$$

Here $\kappa(p)$ is the [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md), and $q_p=y-a$ in the real-point case. The fibre is a scheme, so its nilpotents must be retained.

For $a>0$, put $r=a^{1/4}$. The factors $x-r$, $x+r$, $x^2+r^2$ are pairwise coprime, with the last one irreducible over $\mathbb R$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) identifies $B_p$ with $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb C$, giving three [irreducible components](../../../../../irreducible-component.md).

For $a<0$, put $r=(-a)^{1/4}$. The exact factorization is

$$
x^4+r^4=(x^2+\sqrt2rx+r^2)(x^2-\sqrt2rx+r^2).
$$

Each quadratic has discriminant $-2r^2<0$, and they are distinct and coprime. Thus $B_p\cong\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$, giving two [irreducible components](../../../../../irreducible-component.md).

For $a=0$, $B_p=\mathbb R[x]/(x^4)$. It has one [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md), generated by the class of $x$, so one [irreducible component](../../../../../irreducible-component.md). It is not the reduced point $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$: its class of $x$ is a nonzero [nilpotent element](../../../../../nilpotent.md) with nilpotency index four.

Finally write the irreducible quadratic as $q_p(y)=(y-z)(y-\overline z)$, with $z\notin\mathbb R$. Over $\mathbb C$, $q_p(x^4)$ has four roots above $z$ and four above $\overline z$. They are all simple, because $z\ne0$, and the two sets are disjoint. None is real. Conjugation pairs them into four distinct irreducible real quadratics. Hence $B_p\cong\mathbb C^4$ and this fibre has four [irreducible components](../../../../../irreducible-component.md).

The [real fourth-power fibre classification](../../../../../real-fourth-power-fibre-classification.md) is:

| Target closed-point type | Real coordinate algebra of the fibre | [Irreducible components](../../../../../irreducible-component.md) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $y-a$, $a>0$ | $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb C$ | $3$ |
| $y-a$, $a<0$ | $\mathbb C^2$ | $2$ |
| $y$ | $\mathbb R[x]/(x^4)$ | $1$ |
| Irreducible quadratic | $\mathbb C^4$ | $4$ |

All fibres in each row are isomorphic as affine schemes over $\mathbb R$. The rows are distinct: their numbers of [irreducible components](../../../../../irreducible-component.md) already distinguish them, and the zero fibre is additionally nonreduced. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{exactly four real-scheme isomorphism classes}}.
$$

These are ordinary scheme components, not geometric components counted after complexifying the base. Counting only real roots would miss both the complex-residue factors and the nonreal [closed points](../../../../../closed-point.md).
