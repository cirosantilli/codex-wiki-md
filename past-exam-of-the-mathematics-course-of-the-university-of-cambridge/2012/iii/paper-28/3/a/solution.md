<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose $a\in K^\times$ with $0<|a|_1<1$. For any $x\ne0$, the powers $x^n$ tend to zero precisely when $|x|_1<1$. Equality of the two topologies therefore implies

$$
|x|_1<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |x|_2<1.
$$

In particular $0<|a|_2<1$. For positive integers $n$ and arbitrary integers $m$, apply this equivalence to $x^n a^{-m}$. It says that

$$
\frac{\log|x|_1}{\log|a|_1}>\frac mn\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{\log|x|_2}{\log|a|_2}>\frac mn.
$$

The inequality reverses on division by either negative logarithm. The rational cuts of the two real numbers are equal, so density of $\mathbb Q$ makes the numbers equal. Hence **the [equivalent absolute values](../../../../../../equivalent-absolute-values.md) satisfy**

$$
\boxed{|x|_2=|x|_1^c,\qquad c=\frac{\log|a|_2}{\log|a|_1}>0.}
$$

The formula also holds at zero. Nontriviality is essential to choosing $a$; no Archimedean or non-Archimedean restriction was used in the proof.
