<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take the [formal Laurent series field](../../../../../../../laurent-series-field.md) $K=\mathbb C((T))$, with $v_T$ the least exponent of a nonzero term and $|x|=2^{-v_T(x)}$. Its value group is $\mathbb Z$, so this is a [discrete valuation](../../../../../../../discrete-valuation.md). A [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) has an eventually stable coefficient at every exponent, and its tail has a common lower bound on its exponents. The stabilized coefficients therefore give a [formal Laurent series field](../../../../../../../laurent-series-field.md) element to which the sequence converges. Hence $K$ is complete.

Its [valuation ring](../../../../../../../valuation-ring.md) is $\mathbb C[[T]]$, and its [residue field](../../../../../../../residue-field.md) is the infinite field $\mathbb C$. If $K$ were locally compact, the previous compact-ball argument would make $\mathbb C[[T]]$ compact. Its quotient by the open ideal $T\mathbb C[[T]]$ would then be compact and discrete, hence finite, contradicting its identification with $\mathbb C$. **Thus $\boxed{\mathbb C((T))\text{ is complete and discretely valued but not locally compact}}$.**
