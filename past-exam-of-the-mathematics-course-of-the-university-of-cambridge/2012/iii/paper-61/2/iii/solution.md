<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A pressureless test particle feels tidal gravity, [Coriolis acceleration](../../../../../../coriolis-acceleration.md) and [linear drag](../../../../../../linear-drag.md) toward the prescribed gas velocity. Its planar equations are

$$
\boxed{\ddot x-2\Omega\dot y=2\Omega Sx-\gamma(\dot x-\alpha y),\qquad\ddot y+2\Omega\dot x=-\gamma(\dot y+\beta x).}
$$

There is no gas-pressure force on this test particle. For $(x,y)=(X,Y)e^{\lambda t}$, the coefficient matrix is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^2+\gamma\lambda-2\Omega S&-2\Omega\lambda-\gamma\alpha\\2\Omega\lambda+\gamma\beta&\lambda^2+\gamma\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Nontrivial solutions require a zero determinant. Expansion gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda^4+2\gamma\lambda^3+[2\Omega(2\Omega-S)+\gamma^2]\lambda^2+2\Omega(\alpha+\beta-S)\gamma\lambda+\alpha\beta\gamma^2=0.}
$$

Each root has a nonzero modal vector in the matrix kernel; real motions are obtained from conjugate pairs. Repeated roots may also supply polynomially weighted exponential solutions. Negative real parts make the centre linearly asymptotically attracting for trajectories governed by this interior linear flow.
