<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For constant [opacity](../../../../../../opacity.md), the ratio $T^3/\rho=3c\lambda\mathcal R/(4\sigma)$ cancels the height dependence in the [radiative diffusion](../../../../../../radiative-diffusion.md) coefficient. Since $T_z=-\Omega^2z/[4(1+\lambda)\mathcal R]$, the [radiative flux](../../../../../../radiative-flux.md) is

$$
\boxed{F(z)=\frac c\kappa\frac\lambda{1+\lambda}\Omega^2z.}
$$

Consequently $F_z=c\lambda\Omega^2/[\kappa(1+\lambda)]$. Equating this to the viscous heating rate gives

$$
\boxed{\mu=\frac{4c}{9\kappa}\frac\lambda{1+\lambda},\qquad |z|<H.}
$$

Thus the [dynamic viscosity](../../../../../../dynamic-viscosity.md) is uniform in height even though [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) and [mass density](../../../../../../density.md) decrease. Its units are mass per length per time, since $c/\kappa$ has those units. A pointwise pressure-proportional alpha prescription would be incompatible with this exact profile, which is why the subsequent alpha relation is vertically averaged.
