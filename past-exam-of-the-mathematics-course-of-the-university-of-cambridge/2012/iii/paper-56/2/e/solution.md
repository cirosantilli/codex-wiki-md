<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $\kappa=R/12$, which is constant by the preceding argument. Substitution into [geodesic deviation](../../../../../../geodesic-deviation.md) gives

$$
(D^2S)^a=\kappa\bigl(T^a g(T,S)-S^a g(T,T)\bigr)=\kappa S^a,
$$

using $g(T,T)=-1$ and the established orthogonality. With $V=DS$, [metric compatibility](../../../../../../metric-compatibility.md) yields $\dot f=2g(S,V)$ and

$$
\frac{d}{d\tau}\bigl(g(V,V)-\kappa g(S,S)\bigr)=2g(V,DV)-2\kappa g(S,V)=0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{K=g(DS,DS)-\frac R{12}g(S,S)\text{ is constant}.}
$$

Both $S$ and $DS$ are orthogonal to $T$, so their squared norms are nonnegative. The minus sign in this [conserved deviation energy in constant curvature](../../../../../../conserved-deviation-energy-in-constant-curvature.md) reflects $D^2S=+\kappa S$, rather than an ordinary oscillator when $R>0$.
