↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Take $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$. We first prove the [Ellis–Numakura lemma](../../../../../ellis-numakura-lemma.md) in the one-sided continuity convention needed here. Let $S$ be a nonempty compact [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) with an associative multiplication for which each map $x\mapsto xa$ is continuous. Among its nonempty closed [subsemigroups](../../../../../subsemigroup.md), there is an inclusion-minimal one, $M$: a decreasing chain has nonempty intersection by compactness, and that intersection is a closed [subsemigroup](../../../../../subsemigroup.md), so [Zorn's lemma](../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md) applies.

Fix $a\in M$. The set $Ma$ is nonempty and compact, hence closed in the [Hausdorff space](../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), and is a [subsemigroup](../../../../../subsemigroup.md) because

$$
(xa)(ya)=\big(x(ay)\big)a\in Ma.
$$

Minimality gives $Ma=M$. Thus the set $T=\{x\in M:xa=a\}$ is nonempty. It is closed by the stated continuity, and it is a [subsemigroup](../../../../../subsemigroup.md), since $(xy)a=x(ya)=xa=a$ for $x,y\in T$. Minimality again gives $T=M$, so $a\in T$ and **$\boxed{a^2=a}$**, giving a [semigroup idempotent](../../../../../idempotent-element-of-a-semigroup.md). If one instead uses the opposite one-sided continuity convention, the same proof uses $aM$ and $\{x:ax=a\}$; no joint continuity is required.

Apply this lemma to the [Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers](../../../../../stone-cech-compactification-of-the-natural-numbers.md) with its given addition. In the [addition on the Stone-Čech compactification of the natural numbers](../../../../../addition-on-the-stone-cech-compactification-of-the-natural-numbers.md) convention,

$$
A\in p+q\iff\{n:A-n\in q\}\in p,\qquad A-n=\{t\in\mathbb N:n+t\in A\}.
$$

The continuous variable is $p$ when $q$ is fixed. The granted compactness, Hausdorff property and associativity therefore imply **there exists an [idempotent ultrafilter](../../../../../idempotent-ultrafilter.md) $p$ with $p+p=p$**.

On the positive integers such a $p$ is nonprincipal: a [principal ultrafilter](../../../../../principal-ultrafilter.md) at $n$ adds to itself to give the one at $2n$, which is different. Hence every cofinite set belongs to $p$. If zero is included in one's convention for $\mathbb N$, use the same compact [semigroup](../../../../../semigroup.md) argument on the closed space of [nonprincipal ultrafilters](../../../../../nonprincipal-ultrafilter.md): it is nonempty by compactness of the infinite discrete set's compactification, and the displayed addition sends two free ultrafilters to a free ultrafilter. This avoids the trivial principal idempotent at zero.

To deduce [Hindman's theorem](../../../../../hindman-theorem.md), let $A$ be the unique colour class belonging to $p$. Define

$$
A^*=A\cap\{n:A-n\in p\}.
$$

Idempotence gives $A^*\in p$. The [idempotent-ultrafilter star-set lemma](../../../../../idempotent-ultrafilter-star-set-lemma.md) also gives $A^*-n\in p$ whenever $n\in A^*$. Here is its proof: $A-n\in p$, and applying idempotence to $A-n$ gives

$$
\{t:(A-n)-t\in p\}=\{t:A-(n+t)\in p\}\in p.
$$

Intersecting this set with $A-n$ produces exactly $A^*-n$.

Choose $x_1\in A^*$. Suppose $x_1<\cdots<x_r$ have been chosen with every nonempty finite sum in $A^*$. The finite intersection

$$
A^*\cap\bigcap_{s\in\operatorname{FS}(x_1,\ldots,x_r)}(A^*-s)\cap\{n:n>x_r\}
$$

belongs to $p$, and is therefore nonempty. Choose $x_{r+1}$ from it. The new sums are $x_{r+1}$ and $s+x_{r+1}$, and they all lie in $A^*$. Induction yields

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{FS}(x_1,x_2,\ldots)=\left\{\sum_{i\in F}x_i:\ \varnothing\ne F\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ finite}\right\}\subseteq A.}
$$

This proves **every finite colouring of the positive integers admits a [monochromatic](../../../../../monochromatic-set.md) [finite-sums set](../../../../../finite-sums-set.md) generated by a strictly increasing infinite sequence**, the required [Hindman theorem](../../../../../hindman-theorem.md).
