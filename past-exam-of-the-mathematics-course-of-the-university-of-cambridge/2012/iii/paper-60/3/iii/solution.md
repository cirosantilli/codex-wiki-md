<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) population follows gravitational [hierarchical galaxy formation](../../../../../../hierarchical-galaxy-formation.md): many low-mass [dark-matter haloes](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) and an exponentially rare high-mass tail. The [Press-Schechter halo mass function](../../../../../../press-schechter-halo-mass-function.md) captures this broad form. A constant conversion from [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) mass to luminosity would simply rescale that distribution and predict too much light from both very small and very large systems. The [luminosity function](../../../../../../luminosity-function-astronomy.md) instead has a shallower faint end and a sharp bright-end decline, often described by a [Schechter function](../../../../../../schechter-function.md),

$$
\phi(L)\,dL=\frac{\phi_*}{L_*}\left(\frac L{L_*}\right)^{\alpha_{\rm LF}}e^{-L/L_*}\,dL.
$$

Define the [halo star-formation efficiency](../../../../../../halo-baryon-to-star-conversion-efficiency.md) as $\epsilon_*(M_h)=M_*/(f_bM_h)$, distinguishing this integrated baryon conversion from the [star-formation efficiency per free-fall time](../../../../../../star-formation-efficiency-per-free-fall-time.md). If the stellar [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) is $\Upsilon_*$, a simple one-central-galaxy mapping is $L=\epsilon_*f_bM_h/\Upsilon_*$. For a monotone mapping without scatter,

$$
\frac{dn_{\rm gal}}{d\ln L}=\frac{dn_h}{d\ln M_h}\left|\frac{d\ln M_h}{d\ln L}\right|.
$$

Thus a mass-dependent conversion efficiency and its Jacobian change the shape, not just the horizontal scale, of the [luminosity function](../../../../../../luminosity-function-astronomy.md). Scatter, satellites and the stellar [mass-to-light ratio](../../../../../../mass-to-light-ratio.md) add further differences.

In shallow potential wells, [photoheating](../../../../../../photoionization-heating.md) can prevent gas accretion, while [stellar feedback](../../../../../../stellar-feedback.md) from radiation, stellar winds and [supernovae](../../../../../../supernova.md) heats or ejects gas. These effects lower [halo star-formation efficiency](../../../../../../halo-baryon-to-star-conversion-efficiency.md) toward low mass, helping flatten the faint end. The need for atomic or [molecular line cooling](../../../../../../molecular-line-cooling.md) also affects the smallest systems. Intermediate-mass [dark-matter haloes](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) retain gas and cool efficiently, so the integrated conversion reaches a maximum around galactic [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) masses.

In massive [dark-matter haloes](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md), long [optically thin gas cooling times](../../../../../../optically-thin-gas-cooling-time.md) and stable hot atmospheres reduce fresh cold-gas supply. [Active-galactic-nucleus feedback](../../../../../../active-galactic-nucleus-feedback.md) can replenish the thermal energy lost in cooling and suppress a cooling flow, reducing [halo star-formation efficiency](../../../../../../halo-baryon-to-star-conversion-efficiency.md) and sharpening the bright cutoff. Massive group or cluster [dark-matter haloes](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) contain many distinct galaxies rather than one object with luminosity proportional to their entire mass. [Galaxy mergers](../../../../../../galaxy-merger.md) redistribute stars and can grow already formed bright galaxies, but do not turn all hot [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) gas into new stars. **The [luminosity function](../../../../../../luminosity-function-astronomy.md) is the gravitational [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) population filtered through strongly mass-dependent baryonic physics.** The plotted abundance and efficiency curves are qualitative examples, not observational fits or calibrated mass-to-light relations.
