↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Work over $\mathbb F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$. A [Fréchet space](../../../../../frechet-space.md) is a Hausdorff, complete, metrizable [locally convex space](../../../../../locally-convex-space.md). Its [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) is generated by a countable separating family of [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md) $(p_n)_{n\ge1}$. A neighbourhood base at zero consists of finite intersections

$$
\{x:p_{n_1}(x)<\varepsilon_1,\ldots,p_{n_k}(x)<\varepsilon_k\}.
$$

Replacing $p_n$ by $\max_{j\le n}p_j$ makes the family increasing. One compatible translation-invariant [metric](../../../../../metric.md) is

$$
d(x,y)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}\min\{1,p_n(x-y)\}.
$$

Completeness means that a sequence Cauchy for all these [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md) converges in the space. The separating condition ensures that $d(x,y)=0$ only when $x=y$.

Let $E'$ denote the [continuous dual space](../../../../../continuous-dual-space-split.md) for the original [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) $\tau$. The [weak topology](../../../../../weak-topology-split.md) $\sigma(E,E')$ is the coarsest [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) making all $f\in E'$ continuous. Its basic zero-neighbourhoods are

$$
\{x:|f_1(x)|<\varepsilon_1,\ldots,|f_k(x)|<\varepsilon_k\},
\qquad f_j\in E'.
$$

Equivalently, it is generated by the [seminorms](../../../../../seminorm.md) $x\mapsto|f(x)|$.

To prove the Hausdorff property, fix $z\ne0$. Some continuous [seminorm](../../../../../seminorm.md) $p$ has $p(z)>0$. On the one-dimensional span of $z$, define $f_0(az)=a\,p(z)$. Then $|f_0(az)|=p(az)$. The real or complex [Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../../../hahn-banach-theorem.md) extends this to $f:E\to\mathbb F$ with $|f(x)|\le p(x)$. Thus $f$ is originally continuous and $f(z)\ne0$. This proves [continuous-dual point separation](../../../../../continuous-dual-separation-theorem-for-hausdorff-locally-convex-spaces.md). For distinct $x,y$, inverse images under such an $f$ of disjoint small scalar neighbourhoods of $f(x)$ and $f(y)$ are disjoint weak neighbourhoods. Hence **the [weak topology](../../../../../weak-topology-split.md) is Hausdorff**.

If a [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) is originally continuous, it belongs to $E'$ and is weakly continuous by definition. Conversely, $\sigma(E,E')$ is coarser than $\tau$, so every weakly continuous scalar function is originally continuous. In particular,

$$
\boxed{(E,\sigma(E,E'))'=E'.}
$$

This special case of the [continuous dual of a weak topology](../../../../../continuous-dual-of-a-weak-topology.md) needs no completeness argument.

For the [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) $T:E_1\to E_2$, original continuity implies that $g\circ T\in E_1'$ whenever $g\in E_2'$. Each such composition is weakly continuous on $E_1$, so the defining property of the target [weak topology](../../../../../weak-topology-split.md) makes $T$ weak-to-weak continuous.

Conversely, suppose $T$ is weak-to-weak continuous. Every $g\circ T$, $g\in E_2'$, is then weakly continuous, and hence continuous for $\tau_1$. We show that the [graph of a linear operator](../../../../../graph-of-a-linear-operator.md) $\Gamma(T)$ is closed in the original product [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md). If $x_n\to x$ in $E_1$ and $Tx_n\to y$ in $E_2$, then

$$
g(y)=\lim_n g(Tx_n)=\lim_n(g\circ T)(x_n)=g(Tx)
\qquad(g\in E_2').
$$

By [continuous-dual point separation](../../../../../continuous-dual-separation-theorem-for-hausdorff-locally-convex-spaces.md) on $E_2$, this forces $y=Tx$. The product is metrizable, so sequential closedness is closedness. The [closed graph theorem for Fréchet spaces](../../../../../closed-graph-theorem-for-frechet-spaces.md) now gives original continuity of $T$.

The relevant version of that theorem does use completeness of both spaces: a closed graph is a [Fréchet space](../../../../../frechet-space.md), its first-coordinate projection is a continuous linear bijection onto $E_1$, and the [open mapping theorem for Fréchet spaces](../../../../../open-mapping-theorem-for-frechet-spaces.md) gives a continuous inverse. Composing with the second-coordinate projection gives $T$. Thus

$$
\boxed{T\text{ is originally continuous}\iff T\text{ is weak-to-weak continuous}.}
$$

Equality of continuous scalar functionals does not imply equality of the two [topologies](../../../../../topology-split.md); the [closed graph theorem for Fréchet spaces](../../../../../closed-graph-theorem-for-frechet-spaces.md) is the extra step that makes this conclusion valid.
