<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

A [critical exponent](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) specifies a leading singular power in a response or [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) as the [thermodynamic critical point](../../../../../../thermodynamic-critical-point.md) is approached. Analytic backgrounds and the selected thermodynamic branch must be distinguished from that singular part. Write $r=a t$, $a>0$, and first take $u>0$. The equation of state is $h=rM+uM^3$.

At zero field on the ordered branch, $M=(-r/u)^{1/2}$, giving the [order-parameter critical exponent](../../../../../../order-parameter-critical-exponent.md) $\beta=1/2$. Differentiating the equation of state gives $\chi=(r+3uM^2)^{-1}$: above the transition $\chi=1/r$, below it $\chi=1/(2|r|)$, so the [magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility-critical-exponent.md) is $\gamma=1$. At $r=0$, $M=\operatorname{sgn}(h)(|h|/u)^{1/3}$, giving the [critical-isotherm exponent](../../../../../../critical-isotherm-exponent.md) $\delta=3$.

The minimized singular [free-energy density](../../../../../../free-energy-density.md) is $f_s=0$ above and $f_s=-r^2/(4u)$ below. Two temperature derivatives produce a finite specific-heat jump, corresponding to the [heat-capacity critical exponent](../../../../../../heat-capacity-critical-exponent.md) $\alpha=0$. The inverse quadratic fluctuation kernel is $Kp^2+r+3uM^2$, so $\xi=\sqrt{K/(r+3uM^2)}$ gives $\nu=1/2$; the critical propagator proportional to $p^{-2}$ gives $\eta=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta)=(0,\tfrac12,1,3,\tfrac12,0)\quad\text{in the ordinary Landau approximation}.}
$$

These [mean-field critical exponents](../../../../../../mean-field-critical-exponent.md) follow from minimization and linear response. More generally, linearize the [renormalization group](../../../../../../renormalization-group.md) and differentiate its singular free-energy scaling form to obtain the actual fluctuation-controlled exponents.
