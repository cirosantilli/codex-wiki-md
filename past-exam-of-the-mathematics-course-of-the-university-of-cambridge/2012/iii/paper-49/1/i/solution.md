<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md), a scalar [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) $M$ distinguishes macroscopic phases: for an [Ising model](../../../../../../ising-model.md) it is the [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md) per site, while near a liquid–gas endpoint it can be a shifted density. An ordered [pure thermodynamic phase](../../../../../../pure-thermodynamic-phase.md) has a nonzero value even when the conjugate field is removed. For a symmetry-related pair, take the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md) before $h\to0^\pm$; a finite-volume symmetric ensemble instead has zero average.

Coarse-grain to a slowly varying real field and use a local, symmetry-constrained [free-energy density](../../../../../../free-energy-density.md). A useful convention is

$$
\mathcal F[M]=\int d^Dx\left[\frac K2(\nabla M)^2+\frac r2M^2+\frac u4M^4+\frac v6M^6-hM\right],\qquad K>0.
$$

For [spin inversion symmetry](../../../../../../spin-inversion-symmetry.md), odd powers other than the externally imposed source are absent. Take $v>0$ when the quartic coefficient can be negative; for an ordinary transition $u>0$ already stabilizes the quartic truncation. The [gradient energy](../../../../../../gradient-energy.md) penalizes spatial variations. In the [Landau approximation](../../../../../../landau-approximation.md), minimize this functional, usually first over constant fields. The corresponding [statistical field theory](../../../../../../statistical-field-theory.md) also integrates fluctuations with weight $e^{-\mathcal F/(k_BT)}$. **The [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) identifies the phase; its spatial field describes both equilibrium order and its fluctuations.**
