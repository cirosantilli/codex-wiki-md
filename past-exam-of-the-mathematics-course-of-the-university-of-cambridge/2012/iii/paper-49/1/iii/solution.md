<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [universality class](../../../../../../universality-class.md) groups systems with the same long-distance [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md), despite different microscopic interactions. Spatial dimension, order-parameter symmetry, interaction range and additional soft modes matter; microscopic lattice spacing or a change in a short-range coupling usually does not change the [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md). Irrelevant microscopic distinctions decay under [renormalization-group flow](../../../../../../renormalization-group-flow.md).

For example, a three-dimensional short-range [Ising model](../../../../../../ising-model.md) and a simple-fluid liquid–gas critical endpoint share the scalar Ising [universality class](../../../../../../universality-class.md). The fluid density deviation plays the role of the [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md), with appropriately mixed thermal and field-like control variables. Their critical temperatures and dimensional amplitudes differ, but critical powers and suitable dimensionless amplitude ratios agree. [Landau theory](../../../../../../landau-theory.md) predicts a common set of [mean-field critical exponents](../../../../../../mean-field-critical-exponent.md); fluctuations can replace these values below the [upper critical dimension](../../../../../../upper-critical-dimension.md). **Universality concerns the long-distance singular behavior, not equality of microscopic parameters.**
