<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Direct substitution gives $y^2+y=x^3-x+6$ for both points. The displayed [Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../weierstrass-equation-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) is minimal at every prime by the permitted assumption.

For $P$, neither $364$ nor $11737$ is divisible by three or five. Therefore

$$
v_3(x(P))=v_5(x(P))=-2,\qquad
v_3(y(P))=v_5(y(P))=-3.
$$

The [formal kernel of a minimal Weierstrass equation](../../../../../../formal-kernel-of-a-minimal-weierstrass-equation.md) description gives $P\in E_1(\mathbb Q_3)\cap E_1(\mathbb Q_5)$. Similarly, neither $2059$ nor $98783$ is divisible by three or seven, so $Q\in E_1(\mathbb Q_3)\cap E_1(\mathbb Q_7)$, with the same respective coordinate valuations $-2,-3$.

If a point $T\in E_1(\mathbb Q_p)$ had finite [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $N=p^a n$ with $p\nmid n$, the point $p^aT$ would be killed by $n$. By [prime-to-residue-characteristic multiplication on a formal group](../../../../../../prime-to-residue-characteristic-multiplication-on-a-formal-group.md), multiplication by $n$ is injective on this kernel, so $p^aT=O$. Its order is therefore a power of $p$. Applying this at two distinct primes is the [two-prime formal-kernel test for nontorsion](../../../../../../two-prime-formal-kernel-test-for-nontorsion.md): the order of $P$ would be both a power of three and of five, and the order of $Q$ both a power of three and of seven. Each would have to be the identity, although both are affine points. **Both $P$ and $Q$ have infinite order.**
