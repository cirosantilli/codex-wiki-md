# 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 53](../paper-53-split.md)

The broad turnover near $k\sim10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ marks the [matter-radiation equality scale](../../../../matter-radiation-equality-scale.md). Modes entering the [Hubble radius](../../../../hubble-radius.md) after [matter-radiation equality](../../../../matter-radiation-equality.md) grow efficiently during [matter domination](../../../../matter-domination.md). Modes entering earlier have their cold-matter growth delayed by [radiation domination](../../../../radiation-domination.md): their subhorizon growth is logarithmic rather than proportional to $a$. This is the [Mészáros effect](../../../../meszaros-effect.md). The smaller undulations on the declining side are [baryon acoustic oscillations](../../../../baryon-acoustic-oscillation.md) inherited from the [photon-baryon fluid](../../../../photon-baryon-fluid.md), with an oscillatory wavenumber spacing set approximately by $\pi$ divided by the [sound horizon](../../../../sound-horizon.md). They are damped at sufficiently short scales by photon diffusion and the transfer of the baryon perturbation into the total matter field. The plotted spectrum is a linear prediction; nonlinear evolution changes the actual sufficiently small-scale spectrum at redshift zero.

For an adiabatic growing mode, the [comoving curvature perturbation](../../../../comoving-curvature-perturbation.md) transfers to the matter overdensity as

$$
\Delta_m(k,a)=\frac25\frac{k^2}{\Omega_{m0}H_0^2}
T(k)D(a)\mathcal R(k),
$$

up to an irrelevant overall sign convention for $\mathcal R$. Normalize the [cosmological transfer function](../../../../cosmological-transfer-function.md) by $T\to1$ at small $k$ and the [linear growth factor](../../../../linear-growth-factor.md) by $D\to a$ in matter domination. The relation distinguishes a dimensional [matter power spectrum](../../../../matter-power-spectrum.md) from a [dimensionless cosmological power spectrum](../../../../dimensionless-cosmological-power-spectrum.md): the dimensional curvature spectrum is proportional to $k^{-3}\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}(k)$. Therefore

$$
P_m(k,a)\propto D(a)^2k\,T(k)^2\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}(k).
$$

For locally constant [scalar spectral index](../../../../scalar-spectral-index.md) $n_s$, the [asymptotic cold-dark-matter power spectrum](../../../../asymptotic-cold-dark-matter-power-spectrum.md) is

$$
\boxed{P_m(k)\propto
\begin{cases}
k^{n_s},&k\ll k_{\rm eq},\\
k^{n_s-4}\ln^2(k/k_{\rm eq}),&k\gg k_{\rm eq},
\end{cases}}
$$

because the [cold-dark-matter transfer function](../../../../cold-dark-matter-transfer-function.md) tends to one at small $k$ and scales as $\ln(k/k_{\rm eq})/(k/k_{\rm eq})^2$ at large $k$. The asymptotes refer to the smooth envelope, not the individual baryonic wiggles. The large-$k$ local logarithmic slope is $n_s-4+2/\ln(k/k_{\rm eq})$. For the quadratic potential at $N\simeq60$, derived below, $n_s\simeq0.967$, so the limiting powers are approximately $k^{0.967}$ and $k^{-3.033}\ln^2 k$. Multiplying by $k^3$ to form dimensionless matter power would change both exponents by three; that is not the plotted quantity.
