<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Write $a=t_1$, $b=t_2$, $c=t_3$, and $A=\mathbb C[a,b,c]/(ac-b^2)$. It is the [quadratic cone invariant ring](../../../../../../quadratic-cone-invariant-ring.md)

$$
A\cong\mathbb C[u^2,uv,v^2]=\mathbb C[u,v]^{\{\pm1\}},
$$

where the involution negates both $u$ and $v$. The invariant monomials have even total degree and are generated by $u^2,uv,v^2$; reduction by $ac=b^2$ proves the asserted presentation.

To verify the [normal variety](../../../../../../normal-variety.md) property, suppose $q\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ is integral over $A$. The same monic equation makes $q$ integral over $\mathbb C[u,v]$. This [polynomial ring](../../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) is an [integrally closed domain](../../../../../../integrally-closed-domain.md), so $q\in\mathbb C[u,v]$. Since $q$ belongs to the [fraction field](../../../../../../field-of-fractions.md) of $A$, it is invariant under the involution, and hence belongs to $A$. Thus **$X$ is normal**. The [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) of its equation is $(c,-2b,a)$, so the [Jacobian criterion](../../../../../../jacobian-criterion.md) shows that the origin $o$ is its only [singular point of an algebraic variety](../../../../../../singular-point-of-an-algebraic-variety.md). In particular **$X$ is not smooth**.

Take the [prime Weil divisor](../../../../../../prime-weil-divisor.md) $D=V_X(a,b)$ and $x=o$. We show that $D$ is not a [Cartier divisor](../../../../../../cartier-divisor-split.md) at $o$. In the [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md)

$$
R=A_{(a,b,c)},\qquad\mathfrak m=(a,b,c)R,
$$

its prime [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) is $I=(a,b)R$. The quotient $I/\mathfrak mI$ has [dimension of a vector space](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $2$ over $\mathbb C$: $a,b$ generate it, and they are independent since $\mathfrak mI\subset\mathfrak m^2$ while the relation $ac-b^2$ has no linear term. By [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md), $I$ cannot be generated by one element.

On a [normal variety](../../../../../../normal-variety.md), the ideal of an effective [prime Weil divisor](../../../../../../prime-weil-divisor.md) is the [divisorial ideal](../../../../../../divisorial-ideal.md) of functions with order at least one along that divisor and order at least zero along every other [prime Weil divisor](../../../../../../prime-weil-divisor.md). If $D$ were a [Cartier divisor](../../../../../../cartier-divisor-split.md) at $o$, a local defining [rational function](../../../../../../rational-function.md) $h$ would identify this ideal with $hR$, because an [integrally closed domain](../../../../../../integrally-closed-domain.md) is the intersection of its height-one [localizations at a prime ideal](../../../../../../localization-at-a-prime-ideal.md) inside its [fraction field](../../../../../../field-of-fractions.md). That would make $I$ principal, contradicting the calculation. Therefore $D$ has a nonzero class in the local [divisor class group](../../../../../../divisor-class-group.md).

Now let $D'=D+\operatorname{div}(q)$ be any [linearly equivalent Weil divisor](../../../../../../linear-equivalence-of-weil-divisors.md). If $o$ were outside its [support of a Weil divisor](../../../../../../support-of-a-weil-divisor.md), there would be an [open subset](../../../../../../open-set.md) containing $o$ on which $D'$ is zero. On that neighbourhood $D=-\operatorname{div}(q)$ would be a [principal Weil divisor](../../../../../../principal-weil-divisor.md), hence a [Cartier divisor](../../../../../../cartier-divisor-split.md), which is impossible. Therefore **this point is unavoidable in every representative**:

$$
\boxed{o\in\operatorname{Supp}(D')\quad\text{for every }D'\sim D.}
$$

The argument does not assume that $D'$ is effective. For comparison, $\operatorname{div}(a)=2D$ because on the chart $c\ne0$, $a=b^2/c$; the obstruction has order two, as in the [Divisor class group of an A-type surface singularity](../../../../../../divisor-class-group-of-an-a-type-surface-singularity.md).
