<h1 id="5/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**Among patients still alive at the same time after surgery, chemotherapy has an estimated lung-cancer death rate 3.71 times that of radiotherapy**, assuming proportional [cause-specific hazards](../../../../../../../cause-specific-hazard.md). The estimated 95% [confidence interval](../../../../../../../confidence-interval.md) for this [hazard ratio](../../../../../../../hazard-ratio.md) is $(1.583,8.697)$. Thus the fit suggests a higher instantaneous lung-cancer death rate on chemotherapy, with appreciable uncertainty in its size.

The [null hypothesis](../../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md) of no treatment effect on that hazard is $H_0:\gamma_1=0$ against $\gamma_1\ne0$. Its [Wald test](../../../../../../../wald-test.md) uses $Z=1.3113/0.4346\simeq3.018$, approximately standard normal under the [null hypothesis](../../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md), with two-sided $p=0.00255$. This is evidence against equal lung-cancer death hazards in the fitted model. It is not a statement that five-year lung-cancer death probability is multiplied by 3.71 or that survival duration is divided by 3.71. The [cumulative incidence function](../../../../../../../cumulative-incidence-function.md) depends on both [cause-specific hazards](../../../../../../../cause-specific-hazard.md), and the model needs proportional-hazards and independent observation-censoring assumptions. In a randomised comparison the assigned [treatment contrast](../../../../../../../treatment-contrast.md) is protected against baseline [confounding](../../../../../../../confounding.md), but the instantaneous comparison concerns the groups who have survived to the time in question; it does not identify a treatment effect within a common latent survivor subgroup.
