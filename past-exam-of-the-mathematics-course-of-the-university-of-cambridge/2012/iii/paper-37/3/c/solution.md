<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Compare the species-only model against the additive day-and-species model. The [null hypothesis](../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md) is $H_0:d_2=d_3=0$, with the alternative that at least one is nonzero. The likelihood-ratio statistic is the reduction in [residual deviance](../../../../../../residual-deviance.md),

$$
\boxed{G^2=14.987-14.646=0.341.}
$$

Under the [null hypothesis](../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md), regular [binomial likelihood](../../../../../../binomial-likelihood.md) asymptotics give $G^2\overset{\rm approx}{\sim}\chi^2_2$, because the larger model has two additional parameters. For this [chi-squared distribution](../../../../../../chi-squared-distribution.md) the upper-tail probability is $\exp(-0.341/2)\simeq0.843$. **There is no evidence of a day effect after controlling for species**, and the species-only model is a reasonable simpler choice. The sequential day-only statistic 0.273 from the preceding table answers a different question and must not replace this comparison.
