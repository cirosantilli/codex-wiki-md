<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The relevant no-preference benchmark is $p_A=2/3$, because two of the three equally supplied egg types count as other-species eggs. Its [log odds](../../../../../../log-odds.md) is $\log2\simeq0.6931$, which is below the entire [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md). **Under the fitted binomial model, species A preferentially consumes other-species eggs.** The estimate is $\widehat p_A\simeq0.7134$, and transforming the log-odds [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md) by $e^\theta/(1+e^\theta)$ gives approximately $(0.6810,0.7437)$.

Comparing the interval with zero would test equal numbers of own- and other-species eggs eaten, which is not the no-preference hypothesis for this supply. The conclusion remains model-dependent: eggs are taken from a finite supply without replacement, and depletion or dependence within a vial can affect the binomial approximation. This fitted analysis supports an association with egg type; it does not by itself establish the mechanism of recognition or avoidance.
