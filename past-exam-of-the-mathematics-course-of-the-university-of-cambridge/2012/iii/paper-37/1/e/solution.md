<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The largest positive [regression residual](../../../../../../regression-residual.md) divided by the [residual standard error](../../../../../../residual-standard-error.md) is about $26.6071/5.942=4.48$, $24.7151/5.710=4.33$, and $24.5908/5.737=4.29$ in the three models. In comparison, the most negative [regression residual](../../../../../../regression-residual.md) is only about two [residual standard errors](../../../../../../residual-standard-error.md) below zero. **At least one observation is much heavier than its fitted value, even after adjusting for sex and height.**

A [standardized regression residual](../../../../../../standardized-regression-residual.md) is $e_i/[s\sqrt{1-h_{ii}}]$, so the [regression leverage](../../../../../../regression-leverage.md) adjustment can make these ratios larger. The maximum is selected among 100 correlated [regression residuals](../../../../../../regression-residual.md), however; it is not a prespecified single standard-normal observation. These summaries do not give a formal [regression outlier](../../../../../../regression-outlier.md) test or identify which student is extreme. Ask to check the measurement and data entry, examine residual plots and a normal [quantile-quantile plot](../../../../../../q-q-plot.md), and inspect [regression leverage](../../../../../../regression-leverage.md) and [influential observations](../../../../../../influential-observation.md). A valid observation might reveal a heavier-tailed error distribution, an omitted predictor, or a nonlinear relation. Do not remove it solely because it is unusual, and do not infer from the three summaries alone that all three maxima belong to the same student.
