<h1 id="1/1/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Use generators $a,b,c$ for the three one-handles in the [generalized Heegaard diagram](../../../../../../../generalized-heegaard-diagram.md). Read a downward crossing of the cut from a left-hand hole to its right-hand partner as the corresponding generator. Traverse the attaching curve around the left-hand $A$ hole, starting at its outgoing downward strand, and then traverse the attaching curve around the left-hand $B$ hole in the same way. Their cut-crossing words are

$$
r_1=abca\,b^{-1}a^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1},\qquad
r_2=bcab\,c^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}c^{-1}.
$$

These are the two [relators](../../../../../../../relator.md) imposed by the two [handle attachments](../../../../../../../handle-attachment.md). The remaining boundary is a [torus](../../../../../../../torus.md); no third [relator](../../../../../../../relator.md) should be added. The **fundamental group presentation** is therefore

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(Y)\cong\langle a,b,c\mid r_1,r_2\rangle.}
$$

The exponent-sum vectors are $(1,-1,0)$ and $(0,1,-1)$. Thus the [abelianization](../../../../../../../abelianization.md) is $\mathbb Z$, with $a,b,c$ all mapping to its positive generator $t$.

Apply the [Fox derivatives](../../../../../../../fox-derivative.md) and then this [abelianization](../../../../../../../abelianization.md). The resulting [Alexander matrix](../../../../../../../alexander-matrix.md) is

$$
F(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-t^2+t^3&-1+t-t^3&t^2-t\\
t^2-t&1-t^2+t^3&-1+t-t^3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For example, the three occurrences of $a$ in $r_1$ contribute $1,t^3,-t^2$ to the first entry. Every row sums to zero, as the [Fox calculus](../../../../../../../fox-calculus.md) identity predicts.

The cellular boundary in degree one for the [infinite cyclic cover](../../../../../../../infinite-cyclic-cover-of-a-knot-exterior.md) is $(t-1,t-1,t-1)$. Its kernel is freely generated by $e_a-e_c,e_b-e_c$. Expressing the two relation boundaries in this basis gives the two-by-two matrix obtained by deleting column $c$ of $F(t)$. Its [determinant](../../../../../../../determinant.md) is the order of the [Alexander module of a space](../../../../../../../alexander-module-of-a-space.md). Equivalently, all three maximal [matrix minors](../../../../../../../minor-linear-algebra.md) of $F(t)$ agree up to sign. The **Alexander polynomial** is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_Y(t)\doteq(t^2-t+1)(t^4-t^2+1),}
$$

where $\doteq$ allows multiplication by a [unit](../../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) $\pm t^m$ in the [Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../../../laurent-polynomial-ring.md). Its value at $1$ is $1$.
