<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take the frame dual to the [left-invariant coframe of the translation-dilation group](../../../../../../left-invariant-coframe-of-the-translation-dilation-group.md). It is

$$
\boxed{L_0=\rho\partial_\rho,\qquad L_1=\rho\partial_{x^1},\qquad L_2=\rho\partial_{x^2}.}
$$

Indeed $\lambda^i(L_j)=\delta^i_j$. These are [left-invariant vector fields](../../../../../../left-invariant-vector-field.md), either because they are dual to invariant forms or because $L_j(g)=(dL_g)_eX_j$. For instance, the curve $g\exp(tX_0)$ differentiates to $\rho\partial_\rho$, while $g\exp(tX_1)$ differentiates to $\rho\partial_{x^1}$.

The coordinate [Lie brackets](../../../../../../lie-bracket.md) satisfy

$$
[L_0,L_1]=\rho\partial_{x^1}=L_1,\qquad [L_0,L_2]=L_2,\qquad [L_1,L_2]=0.
$$

For the first identity only the derivative of the coefficient $\rho$ contributes. **The linear map $\boxed{X_j\longmapsto L_j}$ is a [Lie algebra isomorphism](../../../../../../lie-algebra-isomorphism.md)**: it preserves all basis brackets, is injective by independence of the frame, and is onto the three-dimensional space of [left-invariant vector fields](../../../../../../left-invariant-vector-field.md).
