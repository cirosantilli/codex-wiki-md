<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The Weyl fields have odd [Grassmann parity](../../../../../../grassmann-parity.md). With the given epsilon convention, write $\chi_\alpha=(a,b)$ and $\psi_\alpha=(c,d)$, where all four components anticommute. Raising an index gives $\chi^\alpha=(b,-a)$ and $\psi^\alpha=(d,-c)$. Hence

$$
(\chi\psi)=bc-ad,
\qquad(\psi\chi)=da-cb=-ad+bc.
$$

The minus sign from the antisymmetric epsilon tensor is canceled by exchanging the two Grassmann-odd components. Therefore the [Weyl spinor bilinear exchange identity](../../../../../../weyl-spinor-bilinear-exchange-identity.md) is

$$
\boxed{(\chi\psi)=(\psi\chi),\qquad C=1.}
$$

For commuting numerical spinors the exchange sign would instead be negative. The answer here uses fermionic Weyl fields, as appropriate to the supersymmetric model.
