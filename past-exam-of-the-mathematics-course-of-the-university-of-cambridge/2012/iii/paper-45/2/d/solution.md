<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

In the original coordinate, the possible zero-energy [vacuum expectation values](../../../../../../vacuum-expectation-value.md) for $g\ne0$ are

$$
\boxed{v_\pm=\frac{-m\pm\sqrt{m^2-2g\kappa}}{g}.}
$$

They coincide when the [discriminant](../../../../../../discriminant.md) vanishes. With real parameters and a nonnegative [discriminant](../../../../../../discriminant.md) these are real; otherwise the allowed [complex scalar](../../../../../../complex-scalar-field.md) values must be retained.

In the coordinate $u=\varphi-s$ used to remove the linear term, the same vacua are

$$
\boxed{\langle u\rangle=0\quad\text{or}\quad-\frac{2M}{g}.}
$$

The nonzero branch exists in this shifted coordinate when $M\ne0$. Let $w=-2M/g$ be that branch and write $u=w+\widetilde\varphi$. Equivalently, in the original coordinate write $\varphi=v+\widetilde\varphi$ with $v=s+w$. The physical fluctuation field is the same. For $g=0,m\ne0$ there is the unique vacuum $v=-\kappa/m$; for the constant and pure-linear exceptional branches the scalar is a flat direction, with the [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) status already distinguished in part (c).
