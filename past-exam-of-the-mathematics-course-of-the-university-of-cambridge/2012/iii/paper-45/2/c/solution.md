<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [supersymmetric vacuum](../../../../../../supersymmetric-vacuum.md) satisfies $F=0$, equivalently $W'(v)=0$, and has $V(v)=0$. For $g\ne0$, the nonconstant quadratic $W'$ has at least one complex root, including a double root when $\Delta=0$. Since the scalar is complex, these roots are admissible physical vacua whether or not they lie on the real slice. Thus [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) is unbroken at either zero of $W'$. The same is true for $g=0,m\ne0$, where $W'$ is linear and has the root $v=-\kappa/m$.

The exceptional pure-linear model $g=m=0,\kappa\ne0$ has $F^*=-\kappa$ everywhere and $V=|\kappa|^2>0$. It exhibits [flat F-term breaking with a linear superpotential](../../../../../../flat-f-term-breaking-with-a-linear-superpotential.md): all scalar values are flat vacua but no state annihilates every [supercharge](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md). If also $\kappa=0$, the potential is identically zero and [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) is unbroken on a flat moduli space.

Consequently **the generic cubic or massive model does not spontaneously break [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md); only the nonzero purely linear branch does in this single-field canonical model.** The answer is independent of $\alpha$.
