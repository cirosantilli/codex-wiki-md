<h1 id="2/h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [H](../h.md)

For a nonzero common mass,

$$
\boxed{\frac{m_{\rm scalar}}{m_{\rm fermion}}=1.}
$$

This agrees with [supersymmetric mass degeneracy](../../../../../../supersymmetric-mass-degeneracy.md) in an unbroken [chiral superfield](../../../../../../chiral-superfield.md) multiplet. The two real scalar modes have the same mass as the two physical polarizations of the massive [fermion](../../../../../../fermion.md), as the rest-frame multiplet construction predicts.

At $\Delta=0$ both masses vanish; the numerical ratio is $0/0$ and is undefined, but the equality of masses still expresses the unbroken [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md). Equal zero masses in the exceptional flat, pure-linear breaking model do not prove unbroken [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md): there the nonzero auxiliary expectation and positive energy in part (c) are the decisive tests.
