<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take canonical coordinates $q=x_1$ and $p=x_2$. The [Hamiltonian system](../../../../../../hamiltonian-system.md) is

$$
\dot q=-\partial_p\phi(t,q,p),\qquad
\dot p=\partial_q\phi(t,q,p).
$$

In the standard [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) $\dot q=\partial_p H$ and $\dot p=-\partial_qH$, its [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is

$$
\boxed{H(t,q,p)=-\phi(t,q,p).}
$$

An arbitrary function of $t$ can be added without affecting the [characteristic curves](../../../../../../characteristic-curve.md). The [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) generally depends on time through $\omega$ and need not be conserved along a trajectory.
