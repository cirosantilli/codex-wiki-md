<h1 id="3/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

Use the stated Fredholm definition of the [essential spectrum](../../../../../../essential-spectrum-of-a-closed-operator.md), which applies to general bounded operators:

$$
\Sigma_{\mathrm e}(L)=\{z\in\mathbb C:L-zI\text{ is not Fredholm}\}.
$$

For each $z$, part (f) applied to $L-zI$ says that $L-zI$ is [Fredholm](../../../../../../fredholm-operator.md) exactly when $L+K-zI$ is Fredholm. Taking the complement of these equivalent Fredholm conditions gives

$$
\boxed{\Sigma_{\mathrm e}(L+K)=\Sigma_{\mathrm e}(L).}
$$

This is the general [Weyl theorem for bounded compact perturbations](../../../../../../weyl-theorem-for-bounded-compact-perturbations.md). Individual isolated [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) can change; the Fredholm obstruction, and hence this [essential spectrum](../../../../../../essential-spectrum-of-a-closed-operator.md), cannot.
