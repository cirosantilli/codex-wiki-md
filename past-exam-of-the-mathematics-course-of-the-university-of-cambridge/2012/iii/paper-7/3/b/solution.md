<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Work over a complex [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md); for a real space one first complexifies to discuss a complex spectrum. A [bounded linear operator](../../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) $L$ is [self-adjoint](../../../../../../self-adjoint-operator.md) when $L=L^*$, where the [adjoint operator](../../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) is defined by

$$
\langle Lh,k\rangle=\langle h,L^*k\rangle\qquad(h,k\in H).
$$

Equivalently, $\langle Lh,k\rangle=\langle h,Lk\rangle$ for every pair of vectors.

The [spectrum of a bounded operator](../../../../../../spectrum-of-a-bounded-operator.md) is

$$
\boxed{\Sigma(L)=\{z\in\mathbb C:L-zI\text{ has no bounded, everywhere-defined inverse}\}.}
$$

Its complement is the [resolvent set](../../../../../../resolvent-set-of-an-operator.md). On a [Banach space](../../../../../../banach-space-split.md), a bounded bijective operator has a [bounded inverse](../../../../../../bounded-inverse.md) by the [bounded inverse theorem](../../../../../../bounded-inverse-theorem.md), so failure of invertibility is equivalent to failure of bijectivity. An [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is one possible spectral point, but an injective operator that is not onto can also contribute to the [spectrum](../../../../../../spectrum-functional-analysis.md).
