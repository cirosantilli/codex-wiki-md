<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

If $H$ is a finite-dimensional [Hilbert space](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), a choice of [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) identifies its closed [unit ball](../../../../../../unit-ball.md) with a closed bounded ball in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md) makes it compact in the norm topology.

Conversely, if $H$ is infinite-dimensional, choose a unit vector $e_1$ and inductively choose $e_{n+1}$ orthogonal to the span of $e_1,\ldots,e_n$. The finite span is a proper closed subspace, so its [orthogonal complement](../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) contains a nonzero vector that can be normalized. The resulting [orthonormal sequence](../../../../../../orthonormal-sequence.md) lies in the closed [unit ball](../../../../../../unit-ball.md) and satisfies

$$
\|e_n-e_m\|^2=\|e_n\|^2+\|e_m\|^2=2\qquad(n\ne m).
$$

It has no norm-convergent subsequence. [Sequential compactness](../../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md) is equivalent to [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) in a metric space, hence the closed ball is not compact. This proves the [norm-compact unit ball criterion](../../../../../../norm-compact-unit-ball-criterion.md): **a Hilbert space has a norm-compact closed unit ball exactly when it is finite-dimensional.**
