<h1 id="1/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Every Brauer class has a [central division algebra](../../../../../../central-division-algebra.md) representative $D$, and every [central simple algebra](../../../../../../central-simple-algebra.md) is a matrix algebra over such a $D$. Put $d=\deg D$; its [reduced norm](../../../../../../reduced-norm.md) is a homogeneous degree-$d$ polynomial on the $d^2$-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $D$.

If $d>1$, then $d^2>d$, so the $C_1$ property supplies a nonzero $x\in D$ with $\operatorname{Nrd}(x)=0$. But every nonzero element of a [division algebra](../../../../../../division-algebra.md) is invertible, and its [reduced norm](../../../../../../reduced-norm.md) cannot be zero. Thus $d=1$, and a [central division algebra](../../../../../../central-division-algebra.md) of degree one is $K$ itself. Every [central simple algebra](../../../../../../central-simple-algebra.md) over $K$ is therefore a matrix algebra over $K$, giving

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Br}(K)=0}.
$$

This proves the [trivial Brauer group of a C1 field](../../../../../../trivial-brauer-group-of-a-c1-field.md). The essential ingredient is the anisotropy of a [division algebra](../../../../../../division-algebra.md)'s [reduced norm](../../../../../../reduced-norm.md), not the norm polynomial of an arbitrary matrix algebra, which certainly can vanish on nonzero singular matrices.
