<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Choose a [base of a root system](../../../../../fundamental-system-of-a-root-system.md) and its [positive roots](../../../../../positive-root.md), so that the [triangular decomposition of a Lie algebra](../../../../../triangular-decomposition-of-a-lie-algebra.md) is $L=\mathfrak n^-\oplus H\oplus\mathfrak n^+$. A [primitive element of a Lie algebra representation](../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md) of weight $\omega\in H^*$ is a nonzero vector $v$ such that

$$
hv=\omega(h)v\quad(h\in H),\qquad xv=0\quad(x\in\mathfrak n^+).
$$

Equivalently it is a [highest-weight vector](../../../../../highest-weight-vector.md). Being primitive depends on the chosen positive system; no integrality condition is part of this definition.

Construct the [Verma module](../../../../../verma-module.md) for an arbitrary $\omega$. The subalgebra $\mathfrak b=H\oplus\mathfrak n^+$ has a one-dimensional [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $\mathbb C_\omega$ on which $H$ acts by $\omega$ and $\mathfrak n^+$ acts by zero. This is a representation because $[\mathfrak b,\mathfrak b]\subseteq\mathfrak n^+$. Set

$$
M(\omega)=U(L)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak b)}\mathbb C_\omega,
$$

where $U$ denotes the [universal enveloping algebra](../../../../../universal-enveloping-algebra.md). The [Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem](../../../../../poincare-birkhoff-witt-theorem.md), applied with negative-[root vectors](../../../../../root-vector.md) first, then Cartan vectors and positive-[root vectors](../../../../../root-vector.md), gives

$$
M(\omega)\cong U(\mathfrak n^-)
$$

as vector spaces. In particular $v_\omega=1\otimes1$ is nonzero, generates $M(\omega)$, and is primitive of weight $\omega$.

The same ordered monomials show that $M(\omega)$ is a [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) of weight spaces, with weights of the form

$$
\omega-\sum_{i=1}^r n_i\alpha_i,\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
$$

and that its weight-$\omega$ space is exactly $\mathbb Cv_\omega$. Each vector has only finitely many weight components. If a [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) contains a vector with nonzero weight-$\omega$ component, choose $h\in H$ that separates $\omega$ from the finitely many other weights of that vector. [Polynomial interpolation](../../../../../polynomial-interpolation.md) in the action of $h$ extracts a nonzero multiple of $v_\omega$. Such a [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) is all of $M(\omega)$, because $v_\omega$ generates it. Consequently every proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) has zero component in weight $\omega$.

Let $N$ be the sum of all proper [submodules](../../../../../submodule.md). Every finite sum still has zero weight-$\omega$ component, so $N$ is proper; it contains every proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) and is therefore the unique maximal proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md). Its quotient

$$
\boxed{L(\omega)=M(\omega)/N}
$$

is irreducible, and the nonzero image of $v_\omega$ is primitive of weight $\omega$. This proves existence for every $\omega\in H^*$ and gives the [irreducible quotient of a Verma module](../../../../../irreducible-quotient-of-a-verma-module.md). It also proves uniqueness up to isomorphism among irreducible [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) generated by a primitive vector of that weight: the defining relations induce a surjection from $M(\omega)$, whose proper kernel must be $N$.

These [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) are not generally finite-dimensional. For example, in an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ root subalgebra, a primitive vector with $hv=\lambda v$ satisfies

$$
e f^k v=k(\lambda-k+1)f^{k-1}v.
$$

If its irreducible highest-weight [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is finite-dimensional, the weight-lowering sequence eventually stops. For the first $m$ with $f^{m+1}v=0$ and $f^mv\ne0$, the identity forces $\lambda=m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$. In general a finite-dimensional highest-weight [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) therefore requires nonnegative integral values on all [simple coroots](../../../../../simple-coroot.md); arbitrary $\omega$ in the request must allow infinite-dimensional representations. This is consistent with the finite-dimensional qualification made in Question 2.

For [simple coroots](../../../../../simple-coroot.md) $h_1,\ldots,h_r$ forming a basis of $H$, the [fundamental weights](../../../../../fundamental-weight.md) are the [dual basis](../../../../../dual-basis.md) $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_r\in H^*$:

$$
\omega_i(h_j)=\delta_{ij}.
$$

For the [special linear Lie algebra](../../../../../special-linear-lie-algebra.md) $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$, take the diagonal [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md)-zero [Cartan subalgebra](../../../../../cartan-subalgebra.md) and [positive roots](../../../../../positive-root.md) corresponding to upper triangular matrix units. Write $\varepsilon_i(\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3))=t_i$. The [simple roots](../../../../../simple-root.md) are $\alpha_1=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2$, $\alpha_2=\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_3$, and the [simple coroots](../../../../../simple-coroot.md) are

$$
h_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0),\qquad h_2=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,-1).
$$

The [fundamental weights of sl3](../../../../../fundamental-weights-of-sl3.md) are therefore

$$
\boxed{\omega_1(\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3))=t_1,\qquad
\omega_2(\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3))=t_1+t_2=-t_3}.
$$

Checking these on $h_1,h_2$ gives the two coordinate vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. In the Euclidean [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md)-zero plane they can also be written

$$
\omega_1=\frac{2\alpha_1+\alpha_2}{3}=\left(\frac23,-\frac13,-\frac13\right),\qquad
\omega_2=\frac{\alpha_1+2\alpha_2}{3}=\left(\frac13,\frac13,-\frac23\right).
$$

The [Fundamental representations of sl3](../../../../../fundamental-representations-of-sl3.md) make these weights concrete: $\mathbb C^3$ has primitive vector $e_1$ of weight $\omega_1$, and $\Lambda^2\mathbb C^3$ has primitive vector $e_1\wedge e_2$ of weight $\omega_2$. Every finite-dimensional irreducible highest weight is $a\omega_1+b\omega_2$ with nonnegative integers $a,b$.
