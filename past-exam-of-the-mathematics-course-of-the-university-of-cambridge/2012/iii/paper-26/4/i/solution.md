<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $F_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^{n+1}})$ and $F_0=\mathbb Q(\mu_p)$. The [cyclotomic character](../../../../../../cyclotomic-character.md) identifies

$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F_\infty/\mathbb Q)
=\Delta\times\Gamma,\qquad
\Delta\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times,\quad
\Gamma\cong1+p\mathbb Z_p.
$$

Choose $\gamma$ with cyclotomic value $u=1+p$, and put

$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]=\mathbb Z_p[[T]],\qquad T=\gamma-1.
$$

The [Iwasawa algebra](../../../../../../iwasawa-algebra.md) of $G$ is $\Lambda[\Delta]$. Conjugation, independent of lift as in the preceding solution, makes $X$ a [compact Galois module](../../../../../../compact-galois-module.md) over this ring. It is the [p-ramified Iwasawa module](../../../../../../p-ramified-iwasawa-module.md), not the unramified class-group module.

Because $p-1$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z_p$, the [Teichmüller character](../../../../../../teichmuller-character.md) $\omega$ gives orthogonal [character idempotents](../../../../../../character-idempotent.md)

$$
e_\chi=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\chi(\delta)^{-1}\delta,
\qquad \chi=\omega^i,\quad0\leq i\leq p-2.
$$

They sum to one, so $X=\bigoplus_\chi X_\chi$, where $X_\chi=e_\chi X$. Each is a [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over the one-variable ring $\Lambda$. Complex conjugation is the element $-1$ of $\Delta$; characters with $\chi(-1)=1$ are even, and those with $\chi(-1)=-1$ are odd.

The basic **Iwasawa-module rank theorem** in this tower gives

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X_\chi=
\begin{cases}
0,&\chi\text{ even},\\
1,&\chi\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
$$

In particular,

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad
X^+\text{ is torsion},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X^-=\frac{p-1}{2}.}
$$

Thus it would be incorrect to describe all of $X$ as a [torsion module](../../../../../../torsion-module.md).

Here is the [class-field unit sequence](../../../../../../class-field-unit-sequence.md) explaining these ranks. Let $U_\infty$ be the inverse limit, under local norms, of the pro-$p$ completions of the unit groups at the unique prime over $p$ in $F_n$. Let $\overline E_\infty$ be the inverse limit of the closures of the global units in these local unit groups, and let

$$
Y=\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Cl}(F_n)(p)
$$

under ideal norms. The [Artin reciprocity](../../../../../../artin-reciprocity-law.md) maps yield

$$
0\longrightarrow\overline E_\infty
\longrightarrow U_\infty
\longrightarrow X
\longrightarrow Y
\longrightarrow0.
$$

The final module is the [unramified Iwasawa module](../../../../../../unramified-iwasawa-module.md) and is finitely generated torsion, as proved in the other essay. Local-unit theory gives $U_\infty$ [Iwasawa-module rank](../../../../../../iwasawa-module-rank.md) $p-1$, with rank one in each $\Delta$-character. The [Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields](../../../../../../leopoldt-theorem-for-abelian-number-fields.md) gives $\overline E_\infty$ rank $(p-1)/2$, with rank one in each even character and zero in each odd character. In odd characters the possible norm-compatible roots of unity contribute a rank-zero term, not a free summand. Subtracting ranks in the exact sequence gives the displayed result and also finite generation.

For any character, the [Iwasawa module structure theorem](../../../../../../iwasawa-module-structure-theorem.md) describes $X_\chi$ up to [pseudo-isomorphism](../../../../../../pseudo-isomorphism.md):

$$
X_\chi\sim
\Lambda^{r_\chi}
\oplus\bigoplus_j\Lambda/(p^{a_j})
\oplus\bigoplus_k\Lambda/(f_k(T)^{b_k}),
$$

where the $f_k$ are irreducible [distinguished polynomials](../../../../../../distinguished-polynomial.md) and the kernel and cokernel of a [pseudo-isomorphism](../../../../../../pseudo-isomorphism.md) are finite. For a torsion component, its [characteristic ideal](../../../../../../characteristic-ideal.md) is generated by

$$
p^{\sum_j a_j}\prod_k f_k(T)^{b_k}.
$$

The [Iwasawa invariants](../../../../../../iwasawa-invariants.md) are $\mu=\sum a_j$ and $\lambda=\sum b_k\deg f_k$. A characteristic ideal describes the elementary divisors only in aggregate; it is not an assertion that the module is cyclic or that an odd component is actually free.

The trivial character component $X_1$ is zero. Indeed, the maximal abelian pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb Q$ unramified outside $p$ is precisely the rational [cyclotomic Zp-extension](../../../../../../cyclotomic-zp-extension.md), by the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](../../../../../../kronecker-weber-theorem.md). Coprime descent using the [character idempotent](../../../../../../character-idempotent.md) $e_1$, followed by the [abelianization over a Zp-extension](../../../../../../abelianization-over-a-zp-extension.md) formula, identifies $(X_1)_\Gamma$ with the additional abelian pro-$p$ quotient over $\mathbb Q$. There is no such extension beyond the cyclotomic one, so $(X_1)_\Gamma=0$, and the [Compact Nakayama lemma](../../../../../../compact-nakayama-lemma.md) gives $X_1=0$.

The central arithmetic description concerns the nontrivial even characters. Let $g_\chi(T)$ be the integral [Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function](../../../../../../kubota-leopoldt-p-adic-l-function.md) power series with convention

$$
g_\chi(u^{\,1-s}-1)=L_p(\chi,s).
$$

For positive integers $k$ of the matching parity, the interpolation is

$$
L_p(\chi,1-k)
=-\left(1-(\chi\omega^{-k})(p)p^{k-1}\right)
\frac{B_{k,\chi\omega^{-k}}}{k},
$$

where the Dirichlet character in the Euler factor is its primitive associate, and $B_{k,\eta}$ is a [generalized Bernoulli number](../../../../../../generalized-bernoulli-number.md). The **Iwasawa main conjecture**, a theorem here, says

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{char}_\Lambda X_\chi=(g_\chi(T))
\quad(\chi\ne1,\ \chi\text{ even}).}
$$

The trivial character is excluded from this displayed analytic normalization: its $p$-adic zeta function has a pole and must be treated separately.

The proof mechanism starts with norm-compatible [cyclotomic units](../../../../../../cyclotomic-unit.md), for instance ratios

$$
c_n(a)=\frac{1-\zeta_{p^{n+1}}^a}{1-\zeta_{p^{n+1}}}
\quad(p\nmid a),
$$

and their character projections. A [Coleman power series](../../../../../../coleman-power-series.md) encodes a norm-compatible local unit by a single power series. Applying its logarithmic derivative to these cyclotomic units produces the [p-adic L-function](../../../../../../p-adic-l-function.md) above. If $C_\infty$ denotes their closed norm-limit module, this calculation determines

$$
\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(U_{\infty,\chi}/C_{\infty,\chi})=(g_\chi).
$$

The remaining global input is

$$
\operatorname{char}_\Lambda Y_\chi
=\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\overline E_{\infty,\chi}/C_{\infty,\chi})
$$

for nontrivial even $\chi$. In the [class-field unit sequence](../../../../../../class-field-unit-sequence.md) modulo $C_{\infty,\chi}$, multiplicativity of [characteristic ideals](../../../../../../characteristic-ideal.md) cancels these two terms and gives the boxed formula for $X_\chi$.

There are two established routes to this global input. The [Mazur-Wiles theorem](../../../../../../mazur-wiles-theorem.md) constructs suitable abelian extensions from Galois representations associated with [modular forms](../../../../../../modular-form.md) and the [Eisenstein ideal](../../../../../../eisenstein-ideal.md); their sizes supply the missing divisibility. The [cyclotomic Euler system](../../../../../../cyclotomic-euler-system.md) route uses norm relations for cyclotomic units at auxiliary primes and descent to bound the class-group module. The [analytic class number formula](../../../../../../analytic-class-number-formula.md) and the cyclotomic-unit index formula then supply the equality of characteristic ideals. These are substantial arithmetic theorems, rather than consequences of the abstract structure theorem alone.

A useful further result is the **Ferrero-Washington theorem**: $\mu(Y)=0$ for this cyclotomic tower, more generally for cyclotomic towers of abelian [number fields](../../../../../../number-field.md). Its proof rules out an identically zero reduction modulo $p$ of the relevant $p$-adic $L$-series, using distribution of $p$-adic digits. Together with the main conjecture, it gives $\mu(X_\chi)=0$ for each nontrivial even $\chi$. Therefore these torsion components are finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_p$ up to finite error.

Finally, [Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory](../../../../../../kummer-reflection-in-iwasawa-theory.md) relates the even $p$-ramified module to an odd unramified component:

$$
X_\chi\sim Y_{\omega\chi^{-1}}^{\,\iota}(1),
\qquad \iota(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1},
$$

where $(1)$ is the [Tate twist](../../../../../../tate-twist.md) by the cyclotomic character. The corresponding power-series substitution is

$$
T\longmapsto u(1+T)^{-1}-1.
$$

This explains why the $p$-ramified interpolation involves $u^{1-s}$ while the reflected class-group interpolation involves $u^s$. Both the involution and the twist matter. The rank theorem, the class-field sequence, the structure theorem and the explicit even-character characteristic ideals together give the known [compact Galois module](../../../../../../compact-galois-module.md) structure; none justifies replacing every odd component by a free module without further argument.
