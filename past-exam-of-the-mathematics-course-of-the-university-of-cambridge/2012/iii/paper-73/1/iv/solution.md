<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

The high-Reynolds-number balance gives positive mean [vortex stretching](../../../../../../vortex-stretching.md). The [Betchov relation](../../../../../../betchov-relation.md) therefore gives $\langle abc\rangle<0$. To connect this to a fixed longitudinal velocity derivative, additionally assume [isotropic turbulence](../../../../../../isotropic-turbulence.md), or average over the direction of that derivative. For a unit vector $\mathbf n$, the derivative is $d=\mathbf n^TS\mathbf n$. Uniform directional averaging, with $\operatorname{tr}S=0$, gives

$$
\overline{d^2}=\frac2{15}\operatorname{tr}S^2,\qquad
\overline{d^3}=\frac8{105}\operatorname{tr}S^3=\frac8{35}abc.
$$

For example these identities follow by averaging products of direction cosines on a sphere. Hence in [isotropic turbulence](../../../../../../isotropic-turbulence.md)

$$
\langle(\partial_xu_x)^3\rangle=\frac8{35}\langle abc\rangle
=-\frac2{35}\langle\omega_i\omega_jS_{ij}\rangle<0,
$$

and division by the positive derivative [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) to the power $3/2$ gives **negative [longitudinal velocity-gradient skewness](../../../../../../longitudinal-velocity-gradient-skewness.md)**. Homogeneity alone does not fix the orientation statistics and does not establish this sign for every chosen coordinate direction.

Order the [principal strain rates](../../../../../../principal-strain-rates.md) as $a\ge b\ge c$. Except at degeneracies, $a>0$ and $c<0$, so $abc<0$ corresponds to $b>0$: two extensional directions and one compressive direction, or [bi-axial strain](../../../../../../bi-axial-strain.md). The negative average of $abc$ says that such configurations dominate this cubic amplitude-weighted statistic over axial-extension configurations with $b<0$. In the usual physical interpretation, comparably intense strain events are therefore biased toward bi-axial extension.

An unweighted frequency claim needs more information. As a counterexample to deducing it solely from the cubic average, assign strain [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $(1,1,-2)$ to 10% of samples and $(-0.1,-0.1,0.2)$ to 90%, with randomly oriented [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md). Then $\langle abc\rangle=-0.1982<0$, despite bi-axial extension occurring in only 10% of the samples. This example addresses what the [moment](../../../../../../moment.md) inequality can infer, not a construction of a complete turbulent [velocity field](../../../../../../velocity-field.md). A measured frequency preference is a further statistical observation.

The geometric significance is that a fluid element stretches in two directions and contracts in the third, becoming sheet-like while preserving volume. Distributed [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md) is swept into thin layers and its component along an extensional direction is amplified. The associated [shear layers](../../../../../../shear-layer.md) can subsequently roll up and produce [vortex tubes](../../../../../../vortex-tube.md). Thus the weighted strain bias helps explain sheet formation in the [energy cascade](../../../../../../energy-cascade.md) even though the most intense dissipative structures can be tubes.
