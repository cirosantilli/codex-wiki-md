↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On a [measurable space](../../../../../../measurable-space.md) $(E,\mathcal E)$ with a sigma-finite [measure](../../../../../../measure.md) $\mu$, a [Poisson random measure](../../../../../../poisson-random-measure.md) $N$ is a random counting measure such that $N(A)$ has the [Poisson distribution](../../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) with parameter $\mu(A)$ for every measurable $A$ of finite intensity, and the counts on finitely many pairwise disjoint measurable sets are [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md). The sample measures are countably additive; repeated atoms are allowed if the intensity has atoms.

When $\mu(A)=\infty$, exhaustion by finite-intensity subsets gives $N(A)=\infty$ almost surely. Indeed their counts increase, and the [Poisson distribution](../../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) of a count at most any fixed integer tends to zero as the parameter tends to infinity. **[Independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) Poisson counts on disjoint sets, with means prescribed by $\mu$, characterize the [Poisson random measure](../../../../../../poisson-random-measure.md).**
