<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At criticality, [percolation clusters](../../../../../../percolation-cluster.md) occur on every scale. Count their filled outer perimeters, discard microscopic loops through a diameter cutoff, and then pass to a [scaling limit](../../../../../../scaling-limit-of-a-random-curve.md). The cutoff gives a locally finite measure on loops of macroscopic size inside a bounded region; summing over scales gives a [sigma-finite measure](../../../../../../sigma-finite-measure.md) rather than a probability distribution.

The [conformal invariance of planar percolation](../../../../../../conformal-invariance-of-planar-percolation.md) makes these limiting perimeter statistics covariant under [conformal maps](../../../../../../conformal-map.md). Moreover, deciding whether a filled outer perimeter lies in a simply connected subdomain uses only the configuration it encloses: changing the configuration outside that subdomain does not change that perimeter. This is the restriction part of the argument. Conformal [covariance](../../../../../../covariance.md) together with this domain consistency motivates a nonzero [conformal restriction measure on simple loops](../../../../../../conformal-restriction-measure-on-simple-loops.md).

This is an informal construction principle, as requested; it does not replace the tightness, boundary-simplification and uniqueness theorems required to construct the continuum measure rigorously.
