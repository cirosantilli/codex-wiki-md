<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Within the displayed [sequential response selection model](../../../../../../sequential-response-selection-model.md), $\theta=0$ makes every attempt's nonresponse probability independent of $Y$, and hence gives the outcome-independence needed for [complete-case analysis](../../../../../../complete-case-analysis.md). The estimate $\widehat\theta=0.38$ suggests that larger outcomes are associated with greater odds of an unsuccessful attempt. Per one-unit outcome increase, the fitted [odds ratio](../../../../../../odds-ratio.md) is $e^{0.38}\approx1.46$, with the reported interval transformed to $(e^{-0.06},e^{0.82})\approx(0.94,2.27)$.

The [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md) for $\theta$ includes zero, so **there is no rejection of outcome-independent missingness at the five-percent level within this model**. This does not establish that the [missing at random](../../../../../../missing-at-random.md) assumption is true: the interval also allows appreciable outcome dependence, and inference depends on the chosen selection model. The intercepts describe response levels, not independence from the outcome. Nor can the respondents' mean and standard deviation establish the missing outcomes' distribution. There are $300-211=89$ unobserved outcomes, about $29.7\%$, so conclusions about the full trial depend materially on the missingness assumptions.
