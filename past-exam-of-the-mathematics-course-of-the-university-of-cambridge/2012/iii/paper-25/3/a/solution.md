<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [monomorphism](../../../../../../monomorphism.md) $m:A\to B$ satisfies $mu=mv\Rightarrow u=v$ for every parallel pair into $A$. An [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md) $e:X\to Y$ satisfies $ue=ve\Rightarrow u=v$ for every parallel pair out of $Y$. A [strong monomorphism](../../../../../../strong-monomorphism.md) is a [monomorphism](../../../../../../monomorphism.md) with the following lifting property: whenever $e:X\to Y$ is an [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md) and $mu=ve$, with $u:X\to A$ and $v:Y\to B$, there is a unique $d:Y\to A$ with $de=u$ and $md=v$. A [regular monomorphism](../../../../../../regular-monomorphism.md) is a [morphism](../../../../../../morphism.md) occurring as the [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md) of two parallel maps. A [balanced category](../../../../../../balanced-category.md) is one in which every [morphism](../../../../../../morphism.md) that is both a [monomorphism](../../../../../../monomorphism.md) and an [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md) is an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md).

Suppose $m:A\to B$ equalizes $p,q:B\rightrightarrows D$. If $mu=mv$, the common composite has two factorizations through the [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md), so its uniqueness property gives $u=v$. Hence every [regular monomorphism](../../../../../../regular-monomorphism.md) is indeed a [monomorphism](../../../../../../monomorphism.md).

Now take the lifting square just described. The equations $pmu=qmu$ imply $pve=qve$. Cancel the [epimorphism](../../../../../../epimorphism.md) $e$ to obtain $pv=qv$. The [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md) gives a unique $d$ with $md=v$. Since $mde=ve=mu$ and $m$ is a [monomorphism](../../../../../../monomorphism.md), $de=u$. Thus **every [regular monomorphism](../../../../../../regular-monomorphism.md) is a [strong monomorphism](../../../../../../strong-monomorphism.md)**; the existence of the lift uses epic cancellation, and its uniqueness uses the equalizer property.
