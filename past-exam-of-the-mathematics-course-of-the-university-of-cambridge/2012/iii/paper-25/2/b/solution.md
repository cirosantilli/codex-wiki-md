<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

With a [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md) $1$, the [pullback in a category](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md) of $A\to1\leftarrow B$ is the binary [product in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md) $A\times B$. Repetition gives all nonempty finite [products in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md), and $1$ supplies the empty one.

For $f,g:A\rightrightarrows B$, form the diagonal $\Delta_B:B\to B\times B$ and pull it back along $\langle f,g\rangle:A\to B\times B$. Write the resulting maps as $e:E\to A$ and $h:E\to B$. The equation $\langle f,g\rangle e=\Delta_B h$ says $fe=ge=h$. A map $x:X\to A$ with $fx=gx$ pairs with $fx:X\to B$ to give a unique map into this [pullback in a category](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md). Thus $e$ is the [equalizer](../../../../../../equaliser.md) of $f,g$.

We now have the finite [products in a category](../../../../../../product-category-theory.md) and [equalizers](../../../../../../equaliser.md) needed in part (a). **A [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md) and [pullbacks in a category](../../../../../../pullback-category-theory.md) therefore suffice for all [finite limits](../../../../../../finite-limit.md).**
