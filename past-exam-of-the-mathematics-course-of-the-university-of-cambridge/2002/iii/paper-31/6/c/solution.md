<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**There is no density; scaling by two gives a law singular to [Wiener measure](../../../../../../wiener-measure.md).** On $[0,1]$ form the pathwise dyadic sums

$$
Q_n(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{2^n}\bigl(w(k2^{-n})-w((k-1)2^{-n})\bigr)^2.
$$

Under [Wiener measure](../../../../../../wiener-measure.md), [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) normal increments give $\mathbb EQ_n=1$ and $\operatorname{var}(Q_n)=2^{1-n}$. Chebyshev's inequality and the [Borel-Cantelli lemma](../../../../../../borel-cantelli-lemmas.md) show $Q_n\to1$ almost surely, because the variances are summable. For $2B$, every sum is four times its Brownian counterpart and hence tends to four almost surely. The event that the limit equals one separates the two laws. Unlike the linear-drift case, this obstruction already occurs on a finite interval: it is the [Pathwise quadratic variation distinguishes Brownian speeds](../../../../../../pathwise-quadratic-variation-distinguishes-brownian-speeds.md) argument.
