<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For a finite-dimensional integral, $u_i e^{b\cdot u}=\partial_{b_i}e^{b\cdot u}$. Expanding a polynomial interaction $V(u)$ formally and interchanging each finite-order derivative with the regulated integral gives

$$
\int d^nu\,e^{-u^TAu/2+b\cdot u+V(u)}
=e^{V(\partial_b)}I_0(b),
$$

where $I_0$ is the free [Gaussian integral](../../../../../../gaussian-integral.md). This is a perturbation-series identity; it does not assert convergence of the full expansion for an arbitrary potential.

For the Minkowski [functional integral](../../../../../../functional-measure.md), each field insertion is generated by

$$
\phi(x)e^{i\int J\phi}
=\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}e^{i\int J\phi}.
$$

Expand $e^{iS_I[\phi]}$, replace its field factors by these [functional derivatives](../../../../../../functional-derivative.md), and take the derivatives outside the free integral. The result is [polynomial interactions as source differential operators](../../../../../../polynomial-interactions-as-source-differential-operators.md):

$$
\boxed{Z[J]=\exp\left[i\int d^dx\,
\mathcal L_I\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)\right]Z_0[J].}
$$

If the [field interaction term](../../../../../../field-interaction-term.md) contains derivatives of the field, the corresponding spacetime derivatives act on the source-insertion operators as well. For the scalar polynomial interactions the operator in the exponent is

$$
-i\int d^dx\left[
\frac{\lambda_3}{3!}\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^3
+\frac{\lambda_4}{4!}\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^4
+\frac{\lambda_6}{6!}\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\right)^6\right].
$$

The free quadratic source exponential then supplies all [Wick contractions](../../../../../../wick-contraction.md), reproducing the [Feynman rules](../../../../../../feynman-rule.md). For normalized [correlation functions](../../../../../../correlation-function.md), divide the resulting interacting functional by $Z[0]$; the interaction operator changes this denominator too, so it cannot simply be ignored.
