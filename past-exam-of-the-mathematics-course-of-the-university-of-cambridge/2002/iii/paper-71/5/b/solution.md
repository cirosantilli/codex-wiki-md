<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For small [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) fraction the CDM growing mode is $\delta_C\propto a$, and its gravity forces

$$
\delta_B''+\mathcal H\delta_B'+c_s^2k^2\delta_B\simeq4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_C\delta_C.
$$

For an [adiabatic cosmological perturbation](../../../../../../adiabatic-initial-conditions.md) to follow the unsuppressed growing mode, [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) must be below the gravitational forcing frequency: $c_s^2k^2\lesssim4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_C$. With physical wavelength $\lambda=2\pi a/k$, this becomes

$$
\boxed{\lambda\gtrsim\lambda_J=c_s\sqrt{\frac\pi{G\bar\rho_C}}}.
$$

This is [Jeans support versus forced baryon response](../../../../../../jeans-support-versus-forced-baryon-response.md). The wording “only grow” must refer to unsuppressed gravitational structure growth; it is not a theorem that all shorter-wavelength [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) perturbations are absent or constant. In the pressure-dominated regime a forced response $\delta_B\simeq(k_J/k)^2\delta_C$ remains. In particular, a falling [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) can make this small response grow even before it catches up to CDM.

Before [photon decoupling](../../../../../../photon-decoupling.md), the [photon-baryon fluid](../../../../../../photon-baryon-fluid.md) has [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) of order $c/\sqrt3$. Its Jeans scale is roughly a horizon scale, so smaller perturbations oscillate acoustically instead of freely following the growing CDM structures. After decoupling the [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) drops sharply. Above the new Jeans scale, [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) is negligible and subtraction of the CDM equation gives $(\delta_B-\delta_C)''+\mathcal H(\delta_B-\delta_C)'=0$. For $a\propto\tau^2$, the difference is a constant plus a decaying $1/\tau$ term; its ratio to the growing CDM contrast tends to zero. [Baryons](../../../../../../baryon.md) therefore catch up. Under the stipulated post-decoupling $c_s\propto T\propto a^{-1}$, the physical [Jeans length](../../../../../../jeans-length.md) scales as $a^{1/2}$, the comoving scale as $a^{-1/2}$ and the corresponding [Jeans mass](../../../../../../jeans-mass.md) as $a^{-3/2}$.

For the requested instantaneous [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) estimates, define the baryonic [Jeans mass](../../../../../../jeans-mass.md) as the [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) content of a sphere of radius $\lambda_J/2$:

$$
M_{JB}=\frac\pi6\bar\rho_B\lambda_J^3,\qquad\bar\rho_B\simeq0.1\bar\rho_C.
$$

Using the supplied matter-era age gives $t_{\rm dec}=t_0(1+z_{\rm dec})^{-3/2}\simeq9.5\times10^{13}$ s and $\bar\rho_C\simeq1/(6\pi Gt_{\rm dec}^2)\simeq6\times10^{-23}\ {\rm g\,cm^{-3}}$. Then

$$
\boxed{M_{JB}^{\rm before}\sim6\times10^{18}M_\odot\quad(c_s=c/\sqrt3),\qquad M_{JB}^{\rm after}\sim3\times10^4M_\odot\quad(c_s=10^{-5}c)}.
$$

The drop is by $(\sqrt3\times10^{-5})^3$. A definition using the total gravitating [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) instead of [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) content gives values about ten times larger. The pre-decoupling number is an acoustic Jeans order estimate, not an accurate application of the nonrelativistic equations from part a when $c_s\sim c$.
