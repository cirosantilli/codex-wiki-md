<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A system containing a [red giant](../../../../../../red-giant.md) and another evolved component is either a giant–giant pair or an unordered giant–[white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) pair. At common age $y$, these disjoint possibilities have conditional [probabilities](../../../../../../probability.md) $g(y)^2$ and $2g(y)w(y)$. The [conditional evolved-companion fraction in a coeval binary population](../../../../../../conditional-evolved-companion-fraction-in-a-coeval-binary-population.md) therefore has numerator

$$
\Pr(GG\text{ or }GW)=\int_0^1\left(\frac{y^2}{10000}+\frac{2y^2}{2500}\right)dy
=\frac9{30000}.
$$

Divide by the fraction $299/30000$ of systems containing at least one giant:

$$
\boxed{\Pr(\text{another evolved component}\mid\text{at least one giant})=\frac9{299}=0.0301003\ldots.}
$$

Thus **just over $3\%$ of giant-containing binaries have an evolved companion**. The $0.03\%$ numerator is a fraction of all binaries; it is not itself the requested conditional fraction.
