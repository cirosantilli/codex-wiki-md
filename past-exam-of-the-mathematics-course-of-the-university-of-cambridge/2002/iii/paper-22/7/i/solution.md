<h1 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [dense sieve](../../../../../../dense-sieve.md), also called a [persistently nonempty sieve](../../../../../../dense-sieve.md), is one whose every pullback [sieve on a category](../../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) is nonempty: a member factoring through $f$ has the form $fh$, corresponding to a member $h$ of $f^*R$. The maximal [sieve on a category](../../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) clearly has this property. If $R$ is persistently nonempty, then $u^*R$ is too: for any $v$ into the domain of $u$, apply density to $uv$ to find an extension $uvh\in R$, giving $vh\in u^*R$.

For transitivity, suppose $R$ is dense on $n$ and $S$ is a [sieve on a category](../../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) such that $r^*S$ is dense for each $r\in R$. Given $f:m\to n$, choose $h$ with $fh\in R$. Density of $(fh)^*S$, tested on its identity arrow, supplies $k$ with $fhk\in S$. Thus $S$ is dense. These are the maximality, pullback and transitivity axioms of a [Grothendieck topology](../../../../../../grothendieck-topology.md). Therefore **the specified [sieves on a category](../../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) form the coverage $J$**, namely the [double-negation topology](../../../../../../double-negation-topology.md). The empty [sieve on a category](../../../../../../sieve-category-theory.md) is never covering, because its identity pullback is empty.
