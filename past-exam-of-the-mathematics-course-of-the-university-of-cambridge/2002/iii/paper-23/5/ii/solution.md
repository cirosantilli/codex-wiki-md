<h1 id="5/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Assume every $S_n(T)$ is finite. Each of its points is isolated: choose, for every other type, a formula separating it from the given one, and conjoin the finitely many choices. Thus every finite tuple in every model has an isolated [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md). Moreover every [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) is realized in every model: its isolating formula is consistent with the [complete theory](../../../../../../complete-theory.md), so the theory entails that the formula has a realization.

For clarity, here is the one-point extension argument needed for both remaining assertions. If tuples $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ in two models have the same [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) and $c$ is any new element in the first, let $\theta(\mathbf x,y)$ isolate $\operatorname{tp}(\mathbf a,c)$. The formula $\exists y\,\theta(\mathbf x,y)$ belongs to the type of $\mathbf a$, hence also to that of $\mathbf b$. Choose $d$ with $\theta(\mathbf b,d)$. This extends the tuple map to a [partial elementary map](../../../../../../partial-elementary-map.md). If $c$ is different from all earlier entries, the isolating type includes these disequalities, so $d$ is new as well.

Apply this argument alternately to the two enumerations of any two countable models. The resulting back-and-forth map is an isomorphism. Thus (ii) implies (i).

Choose any countable model $A$. The same back-and-forth argument, now within $A$, shows that any two tuples of the same [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md) are related by an [automorphism of a first-order structure](../../../../../../automorphism-of-a-first-order-structure.md). Conversely an automorphism preserves the type. Hence the orbits of $\operatorname{Aut}(A)$ on $A^n$ are exactly its complete $n$-types. There are finitely many of them for every $n$, so the automorphism group is an [oligomorphic permutation group](../../../../../../oligomorphic-permutation-group.md). Therefore **(ii) also implies (iii)**.
