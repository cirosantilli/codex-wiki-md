<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Let $S$ be the set of all [existential sentences](../../../../../../existential-sentence.md) entailed by $T$. Certainly every model of $T$ satisfies $S$. Conversely, take $C\models S$. Part (c) gives $A\models T$ embedded in an [elementary extension](../../../../../../elementary-extension.md) $D$ of $C$. After identifying $A$ with its image, $D$ is a superstructure of a model of $T$. The assumed closure under superstructures implies $D\models T$. Since $C\preccurlyeq D$, it follows that $C\models T$ as well. Hence

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Mod}(T)=\operatorname{Mod}(S),\qquad S\subseteq\exists_1.}
$$

This supplies an existential axiomatization. Conversely, an [existential sentence](../../../../../../existential-sentence.md) is preserved under embeddings because its finite tuple of witnesses and the quantifier-free facts about them persist, explaining why this is the appropriate syntactic class.
