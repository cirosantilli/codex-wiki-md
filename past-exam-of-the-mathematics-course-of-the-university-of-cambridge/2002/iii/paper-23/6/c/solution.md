<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose a [first-order sentence](../../../../../../first-order-sentence.md) $\theta$ defined the [bipartite graphs](../../../../../../bipartite-graph.md), equivalently the 2-colourable graphs. Let $q$ be its [quantifier rank](../../../../../../quantifier-rank.md) and $r=3^q$. Choose an odd integer $N>2r+1$, and compare

$$
A=C_{2N},\qquad B=C_N\sqcup C_N.
$$

Both have $2N$ vertices. Every marked radius-$r$ neighbourhood in either graph is a path with $2r+1$ vertices and its middle vertex marked: the choice of $N$ prevents the ball from wrapping around a cycle. Thus every vertex has exactly the same neighbourhood type in both graphs, and that type occurs $2N$ times in each. Any bijection of their universes meets the [Hanf locality lemma](../../../../../../hanf-locality-lemma.md) hypothesis.

Therefore $A$ and $B$ agree on $\theta$. But $A$ is an even cycle and is bipartite, whereas $B$ contains odd cycles and is not bipartite. This contradiction proves

$$
\boxed{\text{No first-order sentence defines 2-colourability, even on finite graphs.}}
$$

The equal total size is useful here: no large-count threshold or comparison of unequal multiplicities is needed.
