<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [local omission of a partial type](../../../../../../local-omission-of-a-partial-type.md) $\Gamma(\mathbf x)$ by a consistent [first-order theory](../../../../../../first-order-theory.md) $T$ means that, for every formula $\theta(\mathbf x)$ consistent with $T$, there is some $\gamma\in\Gamma$ such that

$$
T\cup\{\exists\mathbf x\,(\theta(\mathbf x)\land\neg\gamma(\mathbf x))\}
$$

is consistent. Equivalently, no formula consistent with $T$ implies every member of $\Gamma$. Thus every possible local condition has a compatible refinement excluding at least one proposed type formula.

The [omitting types theorem](../../../../../../omitting-types-theorem.md) states: in a countable first-order language, if $T$ is consistent and locally omits each of countably many finite-arity families $\Gamma_j$, then it has a countable model omitting them all. Omission means that no tuple satisfies every formula in the family. In the case of a [complete type](../../../../../../complete-type.md), local omission is equivalent to the type being nonisolated. Countability of the language and of the list of omission requirements is essential to this formulation.
