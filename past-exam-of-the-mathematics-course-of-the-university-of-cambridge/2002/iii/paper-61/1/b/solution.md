<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The reproduction hypothesis gives $U1=1$, $U(\cdot)(t)=t$ and $U((\cdot)^2)(t)=t^2$. If “quadratic” were read as degree exactly two, the same identities follow by subtracting pairs of such reproduced [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Hence

$$
U((\cdot-t)^2)(t)=0.
$$

Apply the same positive-barrier argument as in part (a), now with no moment errors. It gives $|Uf(t)-f(t)|\le\varepsilon$ for every $t$ and every $\varepsilon>0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{Uf=f\text{ for every }f\in C[0,1],\qquad U=I.}
$$

The identity is itself a [positive linear operator on continuous functions](../../../../../../positive-linear-operator-on-continuous-functions.md) reproducing all quadratics, so existence as well as uniqueness is established. In particular, reproducing three small test functions is much stronger for a positive operator than merely fixing one finite-dimensional [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) space without positivity.
