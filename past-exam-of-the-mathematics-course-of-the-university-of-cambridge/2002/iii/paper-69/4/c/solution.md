<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

With lattice spacing set to one and a smoothly varying auxiliary field, the discrete action becomes

$$
S[m]=\int_0^{\mathcal L}dx\left[\frac M2\left(\frac{dm}{dx}\right)^2+U(m)\right],\qquad
M=\frac1{J\sinh\kappa}.
$$

This is precisely the [Euclidean path integral](../../../../../../euclidean-path-integral.md) of a quantum particle with $\hbar=1$, mass $M$ and potential $U$. With fixed endpoints it gives the transition amplitude $\langle m_f|e^{-\mathcal L\widehat H_Q}|m_i\rangle$, while periodic classical [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) give its trace. The corresponding operator is

$$
\boxed{\widehat H_Q=-\frac{J\sinh\kappa}{2}\frac{d^2}{dm^2}+U(m),\qquad
\mathcal Z_{\rm periodic}\propto\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\mathcal L\widehat H_Q}.}
$$

This is [classical Ising chain as a quantum particle](../../../../../../classical-ising-chain-as-a-quantum-particle.md). It is the continuum approximation to the exact positive transfer kernel, so its numerical spectrum is accurate when the relevant auxiliary configurations vary slowly. The transfer-kernel reasoning and the domain-wall argument below also support the qualitative conclusion without treating a continuum approximation as an exact finite-lattice identity.

At zero field write $a=\tanh(\kappa/2)/J>0$. Expansion gives

$$
U(m)=-\log2+(a-2)m^2+\frac43m^4+O(m^6).
$$

A double well exists when $a<2$; the stationary points satisfy $am=\tanh(2m)$. For $a\geq2$ there is a single minimum at zero, with the quadratic curvature marginal at equality. Thus a double well is the low-effective-temperature regime of the model, not an unconditional property for every $J$. The exact potential remains confining because $U(m)=am^2-2|m|+O(1)$ at large $|m|$.

Even when two wells exist, a quantum particle with finite mass and a finite barrier tunnels between them. Its one-dimensional confining [Schrödinger operator](../../../../../../schrodinger-operator.md) has a unique, strictly positive ground-state wavefunction. At $h=0$ reflection [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md) makes that state even, so every odd observable has zero expectation. An antisymmetric first excited state is split from it by a positive gap. A spectral expansion of an odd-observable correlation has factors $e^{-x(E_n-E_0)}$; the smallest contributing gap therefore gives a finite classical [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md). The original spin observable, after decoupling, is $\tanh(2m+h)$, which is odd at zero field. Hence

$$
\boxed{\langle\sigma\rangle=0\ \text{at }h=0\text{ for finite }J\text{ and fixed }\kappa>0,\quad
\langle\sigma_0\sigma_r\rangle\to0.}
$$

A deep well can give an exponentially small [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) gap and an extremely long [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md), which can mimic order in a finite sample, but it does not produce true long-range order.

The classical counterpart is the finite cost of a [domain wall](../../../../../../domain-wall.md). Because the pair sum is doubled, a sharp wall has dimensionless energy

$$
E_{\rm wall}=4J\sum_{r\geq1}r e^{-\kappa r}=\frac{4Je^{-\kappa}}{(1-e^{-\kappa})^2}<\infty.
$$

At any finite temperature such walls have nonzero fugacity, divide a long chain into domains and destroy spontaneous magnetization. The range is large for $\kappa\ll1$ but finite. The zero-temperature limit and an infinite-range limit are singular and must not be interchanged casually with the [thermodynamic limit](../../../../../../thermodynamic-limit.md); an infinite-range interaction also requires an extensive normalization before comparing models.

A nonzero $h$ tilts the potential: to first order $U_h(m)=U_0(m)-h\tanh(2m)+O(h^2)$. A positive field favors the positive well and produces positive magnetization. For fixed finite parameters the [ground state](../../../../../../ground-state.md) and classical [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) vary smoothly, so there is no zero-field discontinuity due merely to a double-well bare potential. A discrete broken-symmetry state appears only in an appropriate singular limit, not by ignoring [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md).

More generally a local $d$-dimensional classical statistical model can be sliced along one coordinate and represented by a transfer operator. That coordinate becomes imaginary time; the other $d-1$ coordinates become the spatial coordinates of a quantum system. Infinite extent in the transfer direction corresponds to its ground-state, or zero-quantum-temperature, limit. The classical [Ising model](../../../../../../ising-model.md) is thus related to a quantum Ising system in $d-1$ spatial dimensions, with transverse dynamics generated by the transfer operator. For $d=1$ this is quantum mechanics of finitely many local variables, where [quantum tunnelling](../../../../../../quantum-tunnelling.md) restores [symmetry](../../../../../../symmetry-physics.md). For $d\geq2$ the quantum system has infinitely many spatial degrees of freedom and can have distinct symmetry-broken [ground states](../../../../../../ground-state.md), allowing a classical finite-temperature ordering transition. Locality and anisotropy of the mapping determine the quantum couplings; the dimension correspondence alone does not fix them.
