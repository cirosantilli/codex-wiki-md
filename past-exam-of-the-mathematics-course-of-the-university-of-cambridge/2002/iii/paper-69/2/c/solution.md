<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Keeping both [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) coefficients fixed requires $z^2=Bb^2=Bc^4$. Hence

$$
c=b^{1/2},\qquad B=b^{(d+1)/2},\qquad z=b^{(d+5)/4}.
$$

Equivalently the real-space field normalization is $z_x=z/B=b^{(3-d)/4}$. The transformed thermal and source couplings are

$$
\boxed{t'=b^2t,\qquad h'=b^{(d+5)/4}h,\qquad y_t=2,\quad y_h=(d+5)/4.}
$$

This is the [Gaussian fixed point](../../../../../../gaussian-fixed-point.md) at $t=h=0$, with anisotropic effective volume exponent $D_{\rm eff}=(d+1)/2$. The stiffnesses remain positive parameters labeling the normalization of coordinates and fields. A strictly positive $t$ is a relevant perturbation away from the [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md), not itself a critical [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md).

The two [correlation lengths](../../../../../../correlation-length.md) also follow directly by balancing the kernel: $Kq_\parallel^2\sim t$ gives $\xi_\parallel\sim(K/t)^{1/2}$, while $Lq_\perp^4\sim t$ gives $\xi_\perp\sim(L/t)^{1/4}$. Their different exponents are consistent with the required anisotropic scaling. An omitted scalar quartic interaction has scaling [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $4-D_{\rm eff}=(7-d)/2$, so the quadratic [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) alone does not establish the interacting smectic's critical behavior below physical dimension seven.
