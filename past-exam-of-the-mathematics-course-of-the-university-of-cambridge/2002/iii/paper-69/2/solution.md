The [renormalization group](../../../../../renormalization-group.md) compares descriptions at successively longer length scales. First eliminate short-wavelength degrees of freedom, then rescale coordinates and fields to restore the reference cutoff. This changes the couplings, including interactions and additive free-energy terms generated during elimination. A [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) is a scale-invariant description. Linearized directions growing under coarse graining are relevant, shrinking directions are irrelevant, and zero-eigenvalue directions are marginal and require higher-order analysis.

A diverging [correlation length](../../../../../correlation-length.md) means that many coarse-graining steps can approach a [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) before a relevant perturbation becomes large. Systems with different microscopic details but the same [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) and relevant directions share universal critical behavior. For the quadratic model below, [Fourier modes](../../../../../fourier-mode.md) decouple, so eliminating a high-momentum shell produces a Gaussian [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) but does not couple the remaining modes. The anisotropic [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) kernel requires different length rescalings in the parallel and transverse directions. This is an [anisotropic renormalization group](../../../../../anisotropic-renormalization-group.md), not an isotropic rescaling of all $d$ coordinates.
