**No: there are at most three, including zero and the whole group.** A two-generator ordered [Abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) is [torsion-free](../../../../../../torsion-free-connection.md) and has [rank of an abelian group](../../../../../../rank-of-an-abelian-group.md) at most two. A [convex subgroup of an ordered group](../../../../../../convex-subgroup-of-an-ordered-group.md) is pure: if $ng\in C$ with $n>0$, then $g$ lies between zero and $ng$ or their negatives, so $g\in C$. Therefore a proper inclusion $C<D$ of convex subgroups increases rational rank: an element of $D\setminus C$ cannot have a nonzero integer multiple in $C$. Convex subgroups form a chain, so a rank-two group permits at most two strict inclusions. This is the bound for [convex subgroups of a finite-rank ordered Abelian group](../../../../../../convex-subgroups-of-a-finite-rank-ordered-abelian-group.md). It is sharp: [lexicographically ordered group](../../../../../../lexicographically-ordered-group.md) $\mathbb Z^2$ has exactly $0$, $\{0\}\times\mathbb Z$, and $\mathbb Z^2$.
