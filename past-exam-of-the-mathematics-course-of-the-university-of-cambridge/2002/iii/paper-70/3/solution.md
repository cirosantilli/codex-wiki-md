<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [topological degree](../../../../../topological-degree.md) assigns an integer to a continuous map $f:M\to N$ between closed, connected, oriented manifolds of the same dimension $d$. Their top [homology groups](../../../../../homology-group.md) are infinite cyclic, generated by their [fundamental classes](../../../../../fundamental-class.md). The induced map defines

$$
\boxed{f_*[M]=(\deg f)[N],\qquad \deg f\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

For a smooth map and a [regular value](../../../../../regular-value.md) $y$, its equivalent local description is

$$
\deg f=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\operatorname{sgn}\det(df_x),
$$

where the sign records preservation or reversal of orientation. The preimage is a finite set because the manifolds are compact and $y$ is regular. This is a signed count; opposite-orientation preimages cancel. If $\omega$ is a normalized target [volume form](../../../../../volume-form.md) with $\int_N\omega=1$, the [pullback of a differential form](../../../../../pullback-of-a-differential-form.md) gives the equivalent integral $\deg f=\int_Mf^*\omega$.

The [homotopy invariance of mapping degree](../../../../../homotopy-invariance-of-mapping-degree.md) follows because homotopic maps induce the same homology map. In the smooth description, apply [Stokes theorem](../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) to a homotopy $F:M\times[0,1]\to N$:

$$
\int_Mf_1^*\omega-\int_Mf_0^*\omega
=\int_{M\times[0,1]}d(F^*\omega)=0,
$$

since a top-dimensional form on $N$ is closed. Thus degree cannot change during a smooth deformation preserving the relevant boundary conditions. Degrees multiply under composition, and a nonzero-degree map is surjective: a missed target point would give a regular value with an empty preimage and hence degree zero.

For maps $S^d\to S^d$, the [homotopy group](../../../../../homotopy-group.md) $\pi_d(S^d)=\mathbb Z$ is completely detected by degree. This supplies integer [topological sectors](../../../../../topological-sector.md) for many [classical field-theory solitons](../../../../../classical-field-theory-soliton-split.md). It is important to distinguish two ways spheres arise. In a [nonlinear sigma model](../../../../../nonlinear-sigma-model.md) with the field approaching one fixed target value at infinity, compactify all of $\mathbb R^d$ to $S^d$ and classify the resulting map into the target. For a defect whose vacuum field varies with direction, retain the sphere $S^{d-1}_\infty$ surrounding the core and map it into the [vacuum manifold](../../../../../vacuum-manifold.md). This is the [vacuum-boundary degree as a defect charge](../../../../../vacuum-boundary-degree-as-a-defect-charge.md). An asymptotic limit is a boundary hypothesis; finite energy alone in every possible model need not imply one.

For an [Abelian Higgs vortex](../../../../../nielsen-olesen-vortex.md), the phase at infinity defines $S^1\to U(1)\cong S^1$. Its degree is the [winding number](../../../../../winding-number.md)

$$
n=\frac1{2\pi}\oint d\chi
=\frac e{2\pi}\int B\,d^2x.
$$

Nonzero $n$ forces a zero of the Higgs field inside: a nowhere-vanishing field would extend its normalized phase through the disk and make the boundary loop contractible. This is also the flux classification of the [Bogomolny vortex equations](../../../../../bogomolny-vortex-equation.md).

For a ['t Hooft-Polyakov monopole](../../../../../t-hooft-polyakov-monopole.md), the normalized adjoint [Higgs field](../../../../../higgs-field.md) $\hat\Phi$ gives $S^2_\infty\to SU(2)/U(1)\cong S^2$. With outward orientation its degree is

$$
n=\frac1{8\pi}\int_{S^2_\infty}
\epsilon_{abc}\hat\Phi^a\,d\hat\Phi^b\wedge d\hat\Phi^c.
$$

The integrand is the normalized target area form, so the [hedgehog ansatz for a monopole](../../../../../hedgehog-ansatz-for-a-monopole.md) has degree one. Nonzero degree obstructs extending the normalized Higgs direction through the ball; the full Higgs field must leave the vacuum manifold, normally vanishing in its core. The asymptotic unbroken Abelian [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) measures the same integer with a convention-dependent sign.

For the [Skyrme model](../../../../../skyrme-model.md), a field $U:\mathbb R^3\to SU(2)$ with $U\to1$ at infinity gives $S^3\to S^3$, because [SU(2) as the three-sphere](../../../../../su-2-as-the-three-sphere.md) identifies the target. A choice of orientations gives its [topological baryon number in the Skyrme model](../../../../../topological-baryon-number-in-the-skyrme-model.md) as the winding integral $\pm(24\pi^2)^{-1}\int\operatorname{tr}(U^{-1}dU)^3$; specifying the orientation fixes the sign. Similarly, the transition map at infinity of a four-dimensional [Yang-Mills instanton](../../../../../yang-mills-instanton.md) has degree in $\pi_3(SU(2))$, measured by its Chern charge.

A [scalar-field kink](../../../../../scalar-field-kink.md) illustrates the discrete-vacuum case: its endpoint data lie in $\pi_0(\{\pm1\})$, rather than being a map between connected positive-dimensional spheres. For the double-well field its [topological charge](../../../../../topological-charge.md) is $Q=[\phi(+\infty)-\phi(-\infty)]/2$. A kink has $Q=1$, an antikink $Q=-1$; they cannot individually deform to vacuum while their endpoints are fixed.

Degree classifies topology, rather than every solution. Smooth motion or local perturbations with fixed boundary data preserve it, and a nonzero degree obstructs decay to the vacuum within that class. It does not guarantee existence of a smooth energy minimizer, a fixed size, uniqueness, or dynamical stability. The [degree and energetic stability of a field configuration](../../../../../degree-and-energetic-stability-of-a-field-configuration.md) are distinct: a field can shrink towards a singular limit at fixed degree, as [Derrick's theorem](../../../../../derrick-s-theorem.md) diagnoses in many two-derivative theories. Moreover degree need not classify all homotopy classes for an arbitrary target; other [homotopy groups](../../../../../homotopy-group.md) or invariants may be needed. Within one degree there can be continuous [moduli spaces](../../../../../moduli-space.md) and several stationary solutions.
