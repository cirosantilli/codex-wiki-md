<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

**Dependence on Grassmann variables alone does not specify a superfield transformation law.** A [superfield](../../../../../../superfield.md) is a function on [superspace](../../../../../../superspace.md), with components transforming under the induced [supertranslation](../../../../../../supertranslation.md) and [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) action. Any suitable superfunction can be an unconstrained [superfield](../../../../../../superfield.md) once equipped with that action; a fixed element of a [Grassmann algebra](../../../../../../grassmann-algebra.md) with independently assigned components need not be covariant. A [chiral superfield](../../../../../../chiral-superfield.md), for example,

$$
\Phi(y,\theta)=A(y)+\sqrt2\theta\psi(y)+\theta^2F(y),\qquad y^\mu=x^\mu+i\theta\sigma^\mu\bar\theta,
$$

also obeys $\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi=0$. Keeping only a nonconstant $A(x)$ and forbidding the other components is not a closed [supermultiplet](../../../../../../supermultiplet.md): applying a [supercharge](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md) generates Grassmann-dependent derivative terms. A real unconstrained [superfield](../../../../../../superfield.md) can instead describe a [vector superfield](../../../../../../vector-superfield.md).
