<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The current price of one unit of certain future [consumption](../../../../../../../consumption.md) is the sum of the [Arrow state prices](../../../../../../../arrow-debreu-state-price.md), namely $0.9$. A unit-price [risk-free asset](../../../../../../../risk-free-asset.md) yielding gross return $R$ must satisfy $0.9R=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{10}{9}.}
$$

Normalize the [Arrow state prices](../../../../../../../arrow-debreu-state-price.md) by their sum, or multiply them by $R$, to obtain the [risk-neutral probabilities](../../../../../../../risk-neutral-probability.md):

$$
\boxed{Q=(\tfrac19,\tfrac29,\tfrac13,\tfrac13).}
$$

They are positive and sum to one. Any terminal payoff $Y$ has current price $\sum_sp_sY_s=R^{-1}\mathbb E_QY$. In particular any asset with current price one has $\mathbb E_Q r_i=R$. These pricing probabilities need not equal the physical probabilities governing the investor's [utility](../../../../../../../utility.md).
