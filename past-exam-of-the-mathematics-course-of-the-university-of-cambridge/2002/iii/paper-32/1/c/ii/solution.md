<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $M$ denote the displayed matrix of excess-return coefficients. If it were invertible, choose $v$ with $Mv=(1,0,0)^t$. Hold $v_i$ units of the three risky investments and short $\sum_iv_i$ units of the [risk-free asset](../../../../../../../risk-free-asset.md). Initial net cost is zero. The terminal payoff is

$$
\sum_iv_i(r_i-R)=\sum_iv_i(a_i-R)+\left(\sum_iv_ib_i\right)f_1+
\left(\sum_iv_ic_i\right)f_2=1.
$$

This is a guaranteed [arbitrage](../../../../../../../arbitrage.md), contradicting the assumption. Hence

$$
\boxed{\det M=0.}
$$

The contradiction does not require guessing probabilities for the factors: invertibility would allow simultaneous cancellation of both random exposures while keeping a nonzero constant gain.
