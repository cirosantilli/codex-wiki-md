<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Form a [portfolio](../../../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) with weights $\beta$ in the first factor-exposure asset, $\gamma$ in the second, and $1-\beta-\gamma$ in the [risk-free asset](../../../../../../../risk-free-asset.md). Its total initial investment is one. Its two factor exposures match those of asset $k$, so subtracting its return from $r_k$ leaves a constant. Any nonzero constant difference would allow a zero-cost long-short position with a strictly positive certain payoff after choosing its direction, an [arbitrage](../../../../../../../arbitrage.md). Under absence of [arbitrage](../../../../../../../arbitrage.md), the returns must therefore have the same [expectation](../../../../../../../expected-value.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E r_k=1.05+0.05\beta+0.10\gamma.}
$$

The coefficients are the two factor [risk premiums](../../../../../../../risk-premium.md) relative to the risk-free return, not the expected factor levels themselves. This reasoning permits negative weights, as assumed in the unconstrained [arbitrage pricing theory](../../../../../../../arbitrage-pricing-theory.md) model.
