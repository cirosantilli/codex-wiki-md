<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Using $A$ from part (a), the [portfolio](../../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) is

$$
v=w^t\Sigma w=\lambda^2A=\frac{(\mu-R)^2}{A}.
$$

Thus the minimum-variance correspondence for fixed means has the two branches $\mu=R\pm\sqrt{Av}$. The lower branch is dominated by the upper one with the same [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md), and [portfolios](../../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) with mean below $R$ are also dominated by the risk-free position. Consequently the actual [mean-variance efficient frontier](../../../../../../efficient-frontier.md) is the upper [capital market line](../../../../../../capital-market-line.md):

$$
\boxed{\mu=R+\sqrt{A}\,\sqrt v,\qquad v\ge0.}
$$

Its slope in standard-deviation/mean coordinates is the maximal [Sharpe ratio](../../../../../../sharpe-ratio.md) $\sqrt A$. In [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md)/mean coordinates it is the upper branch of $v=(\mu-R)^2/A$.
