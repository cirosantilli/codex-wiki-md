↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

A [credit derivative](../../../../../credit-derivative.md) separates an exposure to a borrower's credit quality from outright ownership of its loan or bond. In the early-2002 setting, these were a growing, largely dealer-intermediated over-the-counter market. For scale, the contemporaneous BIS comparison reports ISDA member positions of about US$919 billion at end-2001, versus US$632 billion at mid-2001. These are notional amounts, not market values or expected losses, and the survey did not eliminate double-counting; estimates from other surveys cannot simply be combined because their coverage differed. This historical description uses the [BIS Quarterly Review, June 2002, derivatives-market comparison](https://www.bis.org/publ/r_qt0206d.pdf), rather than projecting later market totals back into the paper's date.

The basic unfunded contract is a [credit default swap](../../../../../credit-default-swap.md). The protection buyer pays a periodic spread until maturity or a defined credit event; the seller pays the contracted [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) loss, often notional times one minus recovery. Physical settlement instead delivers eligible defaulted debt in exchange for par. A [total return swap](../../../../../total-return-swap.md) exchanges the reference asset's coupons and price changes against a funding leg: its receiver earns gains, bears price losses and pays funding, obtaining broader market and credit exposure than [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) protection alone. A [credit spread option](../../../../../credit-spread-option.md) pays on widening or narrowing of a quoted [credit spread](../../../../../credit-spread.md), transferring mark-to-market [credit risk](../../../../../credit-risk.md) even without a [default](../../../../../credit-default.md). A [credit-linked note](../../../../../credit-linked-note.md) is funded: the investor supplies principal and accepts a reduction of coupons or principal after a reference credit event, in return for enhanced yield. The investor is economically selling protection and can also face the note issuer's own [credit risk](../../../../../credit-risk.md).

Basket products extend these ideas. A [first-to-default swap](../../../../../first-to-default-swap.md) pays when the first member of a reference basket defaults. A [synthetic CDO](../../../../../synthetic-cdo.md) uses [credit default swaps](../../../../../credit-default-swap.md) to create [portfolio](../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) credit exposure and allocates aggregate losses among tranches. For monetary [portfolio](../../../../../investment-portfolio.md) loss $L$ and attachment/exhaustion amounts $A<D$, the [credit tranche loss function](../../../../../credit-tranche-loss-function.md) is $\min((L-A)^+,D-A)$. These contracts require a model of joint defaults and recoveries, not just a list of individual [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) probabilities. The separation of credit exposure from financing and the role of synthetic structures were already central to the [Federal Reserve's December 2001 discussion of credit derivatives](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/understanding-credit-derivatives-and-their-potential-to-synthesize-riskless-assets.htm).

For risk management, a bank can buy protection on a concentrated borrower exposure while retaining the loan and customer relationship. It can transfer sectors or names it already holds heavily and diversify the remaining [portfolio](../../../../../investment-portfolio.md). Recognition of a hedge for capital purposes depends on the actual transfer and applicable rules, not merely on the instrument's name. Investors can instead sell protection or buy [credit-linked notes](../../../../../credit-linked-note.md) to obtain chosen credit exposure without buying the cash bond, seek a [risk premium](../../../../../risk-premium.md), or take relative-value positions between cash bonds and swaps. Such positions still depend on funding and on the credit-spread basis: contractual events, maturity, currency and recovery terms may fail to match the original exposure. Protection also replaces part of the reference risk with [counterparty credit risk](../../../../../counterparty-credit-risk.md), particularly if the seller may fail when the protected borrower is distressed.

Pricing equates expected discounted contractual cash flows under a [risk-neutral measure](../../../../../risk-neutral-measure.md), calibrated to traded bonds and derivatives rather than substituting historical [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) frequencies. To make this concrete, suppose the risk-neutral [default intensity](../../../../../default-intensity.md) $\lambda(u)$ and [discount factor](../../../../../discount-factor.md) $B(0,u)$ are deterministic, fractional recovery $\mathcal R$ is fixed, and premiums are continuous. Survival is $G(u)=\exp(-\int_0^u\lambda(v)dv)$. A notional-$N$ protection leg pays $N(1-\mathcal R)$ at [default](../../../../../credit-default.md), so its value is $N(1-\mathcal R)\int_0^T B(0,u)\lambda(u)G(u)du$. Premium at annual rate $s$ has value $Ns\int_0^T B(0,u)G(u)du$. A zero-upfront par swap therefore has the [continuous-premium credit-default swap spread](../../../../../continuous-premium-credit-default-swap-spread.md)

$$
\boxed{s=(1-\mathcal R)\frac{\int_0^T B(0,u)\lambda(u)G(u)du}{\int_0^T B(0,u)G(u)du}.}
$$

For constant intensity this reduces exactly to $s=(1-\mathcal R)\lambda$ in this simplified model. Actual discrete premium dates and premium accrued at [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) change the premium leg; stochastic recovery, discounting and counterparty losses change the [expectations](../../../../../expected-value.md). A quoted spread thus cannot universally be read as a physical [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) [probability](../../../../../probability.md). Default-risk premiums and other market effects enter the calibrated pricing measure.

A structural alternative is the [Merton model](../../../../../merton-model.md). If firm assets at debt maturity are $V_T$ and promised debt is $F$, equity pays $(V_T-F)^+$ and debt pays $\min(V_T,F)=F-(F-V_T)^+$. Under the model's diffusion assumptions, debt value is consequently the discounted face value less a put on firm assets. This derives credit prices from asset value, volatility and capital structure, while the simple model allows [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) only at the debt maturity. Intensity models instead calibrate a default-arrival law directly to market credit prices. Neither approach removes model risk in recovery, dependence or calibration, and incomplete credit markets need not select a unique pricing measure. **A useful credit hedge and a fair derivative price both depend on the actual cash-flow and [default](../../../../../credit-default.md) specifications, not the product label alone.**
