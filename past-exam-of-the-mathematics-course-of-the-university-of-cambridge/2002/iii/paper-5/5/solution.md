<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Take the [Jacobi theta functions](../../../../../jacobi-theta-function.md) at zero, with $q=e^{\pi i\tau}$:

$$
\theta_2(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{(n+1/2)^2},\quad
\theta_3(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2},\quad
\theta_4(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^nq^{n^2}.
$$

Define the [modular lambda function](../../../../../modular-lambda-function.md) by

$$
\boxed{\lambda(\tau)=\frac{\theta_2(\tau)^4}{\theta_3(\tau)^4}.}
$$

This is an explicit holomorphic expression on the [complex upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). The product forms

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_2&=2q^{1/4}\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^{2m})(1+q^{2m})^2,\\
\theta_3&=\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^{2m})(1+q^{2m-1})^2,\\
\theta_4&=\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^{2m})(1-q^{2m-1})^2
\end{aligned}
$$

show that all three are nonzero there. The theta identity $\theta_3^4=\theta_2^4+\theta_4^4$ gives $1-\lambda=\theta_4^4/\theta_3^4$, so $\lambda$ omits $0,1$ and infinity on the half-plane.

The [theta-constant inversion and translation laws](../../../../../theta-constant-inversion-and-translation-laws.md), obtained from [Poisson summation](../../../../../poisson-summation-formula.md) and changing indices in the defining sums, give

$$
\lambda(-1/\tau)=1-\lambda(\tau),\qquad
\lambda(\tau+1)=\frac{\lambda(\tau)}{\lambda(\tau)-1}.
$$

These formulas imply invariance under the projective group

$$
\overline{\Gamma(2)}
=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_2(\mathbb Z):
 a,d\text{ odd},\ b,c\text{ even}\right\}/\{\pm I\},
$$

generated by $\tau\mapsto\tau+2$ and $\tau\mapsto\tau/(2\tau+1)$. This is the effective level-two [principal congruence subgroup](../../../../../principal-congruence-subgroup.md).

Its action is properly discontinuous because it is a discrete subgroup of $PSL_2(\mathbb R)$. It is also free. An elliptic integral matrix has trace $0$ or $\pm1$. The odd traces are excluded because $a+d$ is even. Trace zero would give $d=-a$ and $bc=-a^2-1\equiv2\pmod4$, impossible when $b,c$ are even. Thus the only elements acting trivially are the already quotiented central matrices $\pm I$.

A [fundamental domain](../../../../../fundamental-domain.md) is the ideal quadrilateral

$$
-1<\operatorname{Re}\tau<1,\qquad
|\tau-1/2|>1/2,\qquad|\tau+1/2|>1/2,
$$

with vertices $-1,0,1,\infty$. The vertical sides are paired by translation by two, and the semicircular sides by $\tau\mapsto\tau/(2\tau+1)$. Reduction using these pairings gives the domain; equivalently it is a union of six translates of the standard modular fundamental domain, consistent with the six elements of $PSL_2(\mathbb F_2)$. On adjoining the cusp points, there are three vertex classes, two paired edges and one face. The resulting orientable compact surface has Euler characteristic $3-2+1=2$, hence is a sphere. This is the compactification of the [level-two modular curve](../../../../../level-two-modular-curve.md).

All three cusp widths are two, so their local parameters are obtained from $q=e^{\pi i\tau}$ after a modular change of coordinate. The defining products give

$$
\lambda(\tau)=16q-128q^2+O(q^3)
$$

at infinity: there is a simple zero. At the cusp zero, inversion gives $1-\lambda=16q_0+O(q_0^2)$, so the value is $1$. At the cusp one, combine inversion and translation; the value is infinity and the pole is simple. The three cusp classes are precisely the parity classes of primitive rational pairs $(p,q)$: odd-even, even-odd and odd-odd. A Bézout identity, with parity adjusted by adding multiples of $(p,q)$, places any such cusp in the orbit of $\infty$, $0$ or $1$, respectively.

The descended [meromorphic function](../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) on the compactified quotient thus has exactly one simple pole. Its degree is one, since the degree equals its total pole multiplicity; it is therefore a [biholomorphic](../../../../../biholomorphism.md) coordinate on that sphere. Removing the three cusps identifies the quotient with $\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lambda:\mathbb H\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}}
$$

is a [covering map](../../../../../covering-space.md) with deck group $\overline{\Gamma(2)}$. The source half-plane is simply connected, so it is the required [universal covering map](../../../../../universal-cover.md). This proves the [universal covering by the modular lambda function](../../../../../universal-covering-by-the-modular-lambda-function.md), including the absence of interior branching. Three arbitrary distinct punctures are handled by a target [Möbius transformation](../../../../../mobius-transformation.md).
