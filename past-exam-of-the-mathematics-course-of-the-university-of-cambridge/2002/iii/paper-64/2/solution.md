<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Write $\beta_T=(k_BT)^{-1}$ for inverse temperature to distinguish it from the [order-parameter critical exponent](../../../../../order-parameter-critical-exponent.md). A [normalized blocking kernel](../../../../../normalized-blocking-kernel.md) $B_b(\sigma',\sigma)$ is a nonnegative conditional distribution of coarse spins given the fine spins, normalized by

$$
\sum_{\sigma'}B_b(\sigma',\sigma)=1.
$$

For deterministic blocking it can impose a majority rule or block average, with a [Dirac delta](../../../../../dirac-delta-function.md) replacing the sum constraint for continuous coarse variables. Each block occupies $b^D$ original cells, so the new lattice spacing and number of sites are

$$
\boxed{a'=ba,\qquad N'=b^{-D}N.}
$$

The physical volume $Na^D$ is unchanged. After $p$ blockings, $a_p=b^pa$ and $N_p=b^{-pD}N$.

Define the coarse [statistical Hamiltonian](../../../../../statistical-hamiltonian.md) by the exact Boltzmann-weight identity

$$
\sum_\sigma B_b(\sigma',\sigma)e^{-\beta_TH(u,\sigma)}
=e^{-\beta_T[H(Ru,\sigma')+N g(u)]}.
$$

The operator family must include all generated interactions for this [renormalization-group transformation](../../../../../renormalization-group-transformation.md) to be exact; restricting it to a few couplings is an approximation. The scalar $g(u)$ has energy units and is the contribution per original site that is independent of $\sigma'$. Restoring the original constant term $NC$ gives

$$
H(u',\sigma')+N'C'=H(Ru,\sigma')+N[C+g(u)],
$$

hence

$$
\boxed{u'=Ru,\qquad C'=b^D[C+g(u)].}
$$

This convention measures $g$ per original site; defining a constant per new site would move a factor $b^D$ into its definition.

The [identity-operator contribution to renormalization-group free energy](../../../../../identity-operator-contribution-to-renormalization-group-free-energy.md) $C_p$ records the configuration-independent normalization generated by the eliminated spins. It includes their contribution to [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md), including [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) and measure factors, and need not be independent of temperature. It has no effect on normalized probabilities of the retained spins, but is indispensable for the total [partition function](../../../../../canonical-partition-function.md) and its temperature derivatives. Iterating its recursion gives

$$
C_p=b^{pD}\left[C_0+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b^{-jD}g(u_j)\right].
$$

Thus $N_pC_p$ is the original constant $NC_0$ plus the accumulated free-energy contributions of the eliminated degrees of freedom.

To obtain the [additive free-energy recursion under blocking](../../../../../additive-free-energy-recursion-under-blocking.md), use energy-valued [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) per current lattice site,

$$
F(u,C)=-\frac1{\beta_TN}\log Z(u,C,N)=C+f_0(u).
$$

The [blocking kernel](../../../../../blocking-kernel.md) normalization implies

$$
Z(u,C,N)=Z(Ru,C',N/b^D),
\qquad F(u,C)=b^{-D}F(Ru,C').
$$

Substituting the recursion for $C'$ cancels $C$, giving

$$
f_0(u)=b^{-D}f_0(Ru)+g(u).
$$

A second step gives $f_0(u_0)=b^{-2D}f_0(u_2)+g(u_0)+b^{-D}g(u_1)$, and induction proves

$$
\boxed{f_0(u_0)=b^{-pD}f_0(u_p)+
\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}b^{-jD}g(u_j).}
$$

For dimensionless [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) $-\log Z/N$, multiply $F,f_0,g,C$ throughout by $\beta_T$.

The singular [free-energy density](../../../../../free-energy-density.md) must be distinguished from the exact per-site representative $f_0$. If $a(u)$ is a regular background and $f_s=f_0-a$, then

$$
f_s(u)=b^{-D}f_s(Ru)+g_s(u),\qquad
g_s(u)=g(u)-a(u)+b^{-D}a(Ru).
$$

Thus the same displayed inhomogeneous recursion holds with $f_s,g_s$ after subtraction. When a suitable analytic background solves $a(u)=g(u)+b^{-D}a(Ru)$, it leaves a homogeneous singular recursion. [Analytic subtraction of an inhomogeneous renormalization recursion](../../../../../analytic-subtraction-of-an-inhomogeneous-renormalization-recursion.md) explains this separation. A [renormalization-group free-energy resonance](../../../../../renormalization-group-free-energy-resonance.md) can obstruct analytic subtraction and produce a logarithm. The original function $g$ originates in the short-distance spins that were summed out, rather than in an extra external interaction.

Near a [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) $u_*$, linearize the transformation:

$$
R(u_*+\delta u)=u_*+\mathsf A\delta u+O(|\delta u|^2).
$$

An [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $\mathsf A$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $b^{y_i}$ defines a scaling coordinate $t_i$ with $t_i'=b^{y_i}t_i$ to leading order. A [relevant operator](../../../../../relevant-operator.md) has $y_i>0$: its coupling grows towards large distances, so it must be tuned to reach the fixed point. An [irrelevant operator](../../../../../irrelevant-operator.md) has $y_i<0$ and dies away; a [marginal operator](../../../../../marginal-operator.md) has $y_i=0$ and requires higher-order flow analysis. An operator of scaling dimension $x_i$ has $y_i=D-x_i$.

The coarse spins also require a multiplicative normalization. In continuum notation, write

$$
x'=x/b,\qquad \phi'(x')=b^{x_\phi}\phi_<(bx'),
\qquad x_\phi=\frac{D-2+\eta}{2},
$$

where $\phi_<$ is the retained field and $\eta$ is its [anomalous dimension](../../../../../anomalous-dimension.md). This restores the normalization of the critical long-distance [connected correlation function](../../../../../connected-correlation-function.md). Since a uniform [field conjugate to an order parameter](../../../../../field-conjugate-to-an-order-parameter.md) enters through $\int d^Dx\,H\phi$, it has eigenvalue exponent $y_h=D-x_\phi=(D+2-\eta)/2$.

Let $t$ be the thermal relevant coordinate with exponent $y_t>0$. After regular backgrounds have been removed, the leading singular [free-energy density](../../../../../free-energy-density.md) obeys

$$
f_s(t,H)=B^{-D}f_s(B^{y_t}t,B^{y_h}H)
$$

for a large total scale factor $B=b^p$. Choosing $B=|t|^{-1/y_t}$ gives the [scaling hypothesis for critical phenomena](../../../../../scaling-hypothesis-for-critical-phenomena.md)

$$
f_s(t,H)=|t|^{D/y_t}
\Phi_\pm\!\left(H|t|^{-y_h/y_t}\right).
$$

Two temperature derivatives give the [heat-capacity critical exponent](../../../../../heat-capacity-critical-exponent.md); one conjugate-field derivative gives the [order-parameter critical exponent](../../../../../order-parameter-critical-exponent.md); two such derivatives give the [magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent](../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility-critical-exponent.md). Scaling the [correlation length](../../../../../correlation-length.md) gives $\xi(t)=B\xi(B^{y_t}t)$. Consequently,

$$
\alpha=2-\frac D{y_t},\qquad
\beta=\frac{D-y_h}{y_t},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{2y_h-D}{y_t},\qquad
\nu=\frac1{y_t}.
$$

At $t=0$, instead choose $B=|H|^{-1/y_h}$; differentiating gives $\delta=y_h/(D-y_h)$. These [scaling relations for critical exponents](../../../../../scaling-relation-for-critical-exponents.md) assume that irrelevant variables can be set to their fixed-point values without singular consequences. A [dangerously irrelevant coupling](../../../../../dangerously-irrelevant-coupling.md) or logarithmic correction requires keeping the corresponding dependence.

For the [Gaussian field theory](../../../../../gaussian-field-theory.md), integrate out the [Fourier modes](../../../../../fourier-mode.md) in $\Lambda/b<|q|<\Lambda$ and retain $\phi_<$. The modes are independent at zero external field, so this [Gaussian thinning transformation](../../../../../gaussian-momentum-shell-scaling.md) changes the normalization by a Gaussian determinant and leaves a quadratic [statistical Hamiltonian](../../../../../statistical-hamiltonian.md) for the retained modes. Restore the cutoff with

$$
x'=x/b,\qquad
\phi'(x')=b^{(D-2)/2}\phi_<(bx').
$$

In the integrated [statistical Hamiltonian](../../../../../statistical-hamiltonian.md), the volume element contributes $b^D$, each [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) contributes $b^{-1}$, and each field contributes $b^{-(D-2)/2}$. Thus the coefficient $\kappa^{-1}$ of the [gradient energy](../../../../../gradient-energy.md) is unchanged, while

$$
(m^2)'=b^2m^2,\qquad h'=b^{(D+2)/2}h.
$$

In particular $y_t=2$, $y_h=(D+2)/2$ and $\eta=0$. The [Gaussian critical exponents](../../../../../gaussian-critical-exponent.md) and the [Gaussian order-parameter scaling index](../../../../../gaussian-order-parameter-scaling-index.md) therefore give the requested indices

$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{4-D}{2},\qquad
\beta=\frac{D-2}{4}.}
$$

The sign convention $+h\phi$ corresponds to the physical conjugate field $H=-h$ and does not affect these powers.

There are physical qualifications to these Gaussian indices. The strictly quadratic [Gaussian field theory](../../../../../gaussian-field-theory.md) is stable for positive $m^2$ and has no stable ordered phase for negative $m^2$; its purported spontaneous $\beta$ is therefore the formal [Gaussian order-parameter scaling index](../../../../../gaussian-order-parameter-scaling-index.md). A stabilizing quartic term gives an actual ordered phase. Below four dimensions that term is relevant and changes the exponents; above four dimensions it is a [dangerously irrelevant coupling](../../../../../dangerously-irrelevant-coupling.md) and gives the actual mean-field $\beta=1/2$, rather than the Gaussian hyperscaling value.

The [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) power can also be checked directly. Apart from constants, the zero-field Gaussian [free-energy density](../../../../../free-energy-density.md) is

$$
f_0=\frac{k_BT}{2}\int_{|q|<\Lambda}
\frac{d^Dq}{(2\pi)^D}\log(\kappa^{-1}q^2+m^2).
$$

If $m^2\propto t$, its singular second temperature derivative is controlled by $\int d^Dq\,(q^2+\kappa m^2)^{-2}$. For $0<D<4$, rescaling $q=\sqrt{\kappa m^2}\,k$ gives a power $t^{D/2-2}$, as above. At $D=4$ there is a logarithm, and for $D>4$ an analytic background dominates the [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md). The negative formal Gaussian $\alpha$ then describes the subleading singular piece, with logarithmic resonances at appropriate even dimensions. This is the [Gaussian specific-heat infrared threshold](../../../../../gaussian-specific-heat-infrared-threshold.md), and it prevents confusing singular indices with the entire observable.
