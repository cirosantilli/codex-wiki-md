<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $\operatorname{Irr}(G)$ denote the [irreducible characters](../../../../../../irreducible-character.md) of finite-dimensional complex [group representations](../../../../../../group-representation.md) of $G$. A [generalized character](../../../../../../virtual-character.md), or [virtual character](../../../../../../virtual-character.md), is an integral linear combination of these [irreducible characters](../../../../../../irreducible-character.md). A [class function](../../../../../../class-function.md) is a function constant on [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). For [class functions](../../../../../../class-function.md) use the [character inner product](../../../../../../character-inner-product.md)

$$
\langle f,h\rangle_G=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}f(g)\overline{h(g)}.
$$

A [p-elementary group](../../../../../../p-elementary-group.md) is a [direct product of groups](../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) $P\times C$, where $P$ is a [p-group](../../../../../../p-group.md) and $C$ is a [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order coprime to $p$. An [elementary subgroup](../../../../../../elementary-group.md) is a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of this form for some prime $p$. Either factor may be trivial. This notion is different from an [elementary abelian group](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md).

[Brauer's characterization of characters](../../../../../../brauer-s-characterization-of-characters.md) says that a complex [class function](../../../../../../class-function.md) $f$ on $G$ is a [generalized character](../../../../../../virtual-character.md) exactly when its restriction to every [elementary subgroup](../../../../../../elementary-group.md) is a [generalized character](../../../../../../virtual-character.md) there. Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{f\in\mathbb Z\operatorname{Irr}(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\langle f_E,\alpha\rangle_E\in\mathbb Z\ \text{for every elementary }E\leq G,\ \alpha\in\operatorname{Irr}(E)}.
$$

The [character orthogonality](../../../../../../character-orthogonality.md) relations make these inner products the coefficients of the restrictions in their irreducible bases. The theorem detects the integral [character](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) lattice. An ordinary [character of a representation](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) additionally requires every global irreducible coefficient $\langle f,\chi\rangle_G$ to be nonnegative; integrality alone characterizes virtual [characters](../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md), not necessarily actual [representations](../../../../../../group-representation.md).
