<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [C-star algebra](../../../../../c-star-algebra.md) is a complex [Banach algebra](../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md) with a conjugate-linear involution satisfying $(ab)^*=b^*a^*$ and $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$. The inequality $\|a\|^2\le\|a^*\|\|a\|$, and then the same inequality for $a^*$, show that the involution is [isometric](../../../../../isometry.md). In a nonzero [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) [C-star algebra](../../../../../c-star-algebra.md), the identity has [norm](../../../../../norm.md) one.

For a [Hermitian algebra element](../../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-star-algebra.md) $h$, the exponential $e^{ith}$ is unitary for real $t$ and has [norm](../../../../../norm.md) one. If $\lambda\in\sigma(h)$, then $e^{it\lambda}\in\sigma(e^{ith})$: factoring $e^{itz}-e^{it\lambda}=(z-\lambda)q(z)$ with entire $q$ proves this, since the evaluated factors commute. Hence $|e^{it\lambda}|\le1$ for all positive and negative $t$, forcing $\operatorname{Im}\lambda=0$. Thus **[Hermitian algebra elements](../../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-star-algebra.md) have real [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md)**.

For a [normal C-star element](../../../../../normal-element-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) $a$, commutation of $a,a^*$ gives

$$
\|a^2\|^2=\|(a^*)^2a^2\|=\|(a^*a)^2\|=\|a^*a\|^2=\|a\|^4.
$$

The middle equality of [norms](../../../../../norm.md) uses the [C-star identity](../../../../../c-star-identity.md) on the [Hermitian algebra element](../../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-star-algebra.md) $a^*a$. Iterating along powers $2^n$ and applying the [spectral radius formula](../../../../../spectral-radius-formula.md) proves $r(a)=\|a\|$.

Now suppose $A$ is commutative and [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md). Every [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md): if it were dense, it would contain an element within distance less than one of the identity, hence an invertible element by the [Neumann series](../../../../../neumann-series.md), which is impossible for a proper ideal. Its quotient is a complex Banach division algebra and is therefore $\mathbb C$ by the [Gelfand-Mazur theorem](../../../../../gelfand-mazur-theorem.md). [Maximal ideals](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) thus give [characters](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md). Conversely, every [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi(a)\in\sigma_A(a)$, because applying it to an inverse of $a-\varphi(a)1$ would give a contradiction. [Characters](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) are consequently contractive. The converse spectral inclusion follows by placing the proper ideal generated by $a-\lambda1$ in a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md). We have proved

$$
\sigma_A(a)=\{\varphi(a):\varphi\in\Phi_A\}.
$$

The [character space](../../../../../character-space-of-an-algebra.md) $\Phi_A$ is a weak-star [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) subset of the dual unit ball, characterized by $\varphi(1)=1$ and $\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)$. The [Banach-Alaoglu theorem](../../../../../banach-alaoglu-theorem.md) makes it [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) Hausdorff. Define the [Gelfand transform](../../../../../gelfand-representation.md) by $\widehat a(\varphi)=\varphi(a)$. For Hermitian $h$, its values are real; writing $a=h+ik$ with both terms Hermitian shows $\widehat{a^*}=\overline{\widehat a}$. In the commutative algebra every element is normal, so

$$
\|\widehat a\|_\infty=r(a)=\|a\|.
$$

The transform is therefore an [isometric](../../../../../isometry.md) [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) star-homomorphism and has [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) range in $C(\Phi_A)$. Its range separates [characters](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) by their definition and is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) under complex conjugation. The complex [Stone-Weierstrass theorem](../../../../../stone-weierstrass-theorem.md) makes that range dense, hence equal to $C(\Phi_A)$. This proves the [Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem](../../../../../commutative-gelfand-naimark-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{A\cong C(\Phi_A)\quad\text{isometrically as a star algebra}.}
$$

For a nonunital [C-star algebra](../../../../../c-star-algebra.md), its [C-star unitization](../../../../../unitization-of-a-c-star-algebra.md) can be constructed without assuming an operator representation on a [Hilbert space](../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md). Represent $A$ by left multiplication on itself, which is [isometric](../../../../../isometry.md) because testing $L_a$ on $a^*/\|a\|$ gives $\|L_a\|=\|a\|$. Adjoin the identity operator and [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $\|z\|=\|L_z\|$. For $z=a+\lambda1$ and $b\in A$, the [C-star identity](../../../../../c-star-identity.md) in $A$ gives $\|zb\|^2=\|b^*z^*zb\|\le\|b\|\|L_{z^*z}b\|$. Consequently $\|L_z\|^2\le\|L_{z^*z}\|\le\|L_{z^*}\|\|L_z\|$. Applying this also to $z^*$ proves that the new involution is [isometric](../../../../../isometry.md) and that its [C-star identity](../../../../../c-star-identity.md) holds. The extension by the identity is complete, since the [isometric](../../../../../isometry.md) image of $A$ is [closed](../../../../../closed-set.md) and one adjoins just one dimension. Apply the [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) result to a commutative $A^+$; the [character](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) $\infty$ given by $a+\lambda1\mapsto\lambda$ has kernel $A$. Under the isomorphism, this kernel consists of the [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) functions vanishing at $\infty$. Thus **$A\cong C_0(\Phi_A)$** in the nonunital case, with locally [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) [character space](../../../../../character-space-of-an-algebra.md) $\Phi_A=\Phi_{A^+}\setminus\{\infty\}$.

For a Hermitian $h$ in a possibly noncommutative algebra, apply this result to the commutative [unital](../../../../../unital-algebra.md) [C-star subalgebra](../../../../../c-star-subalgebra.md) $C^*(1,h)$ in the [unitization](../../../../../unitization-of-an-algebra.md). Its [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) at $h$ equals the ambient [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md): both [Banach algebra spectra](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) are real, the complement of the ambient [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) real [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md), and the resolvent-component result of Question 1 retains that whole complement in the smaller algebra. [Characters](../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) of this [subalgebra](../../../../../subalgebra.md) are determined by their values at $h$, so its [character space](../../../../../character-space-of-an-algebra.md) is homeomorphic to $\sigma(h)$. We obtain the [continuous functional calculus](../../../../../continuous-functional-calculus.md) $C(\sigma(h))\to C^*(1,h)$.

Interpret $\mathbb R^+$ here as $[0,\infty)$. If $\sigma(h)\subseteq[0,\infty)$, apply this calculus to $t\mapsto\sqrt t$, obtaining a Hermitian $k$ with $k^2=h$. In a nonunital algebra this element remains in $A$ because the defining scalar function vanishes at zero. Conversely, if $h=k^2$ with $k=k^*$, [spectral mapping theorem](../../../../../spectral-mapping-theorem.md) and the real [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) of $k$ give $\sigma(h)=\{t^2:t\in\sigma(k)\}\subseteq[0,\infty)$. Hence **nonnegative [Banach algebra spectrum](../../../../../spectrum-of-an-element.md) is equivalent to being the square of a [Hermitian algebra element](../../../../../hermitian-element-of-a-star-algebra.md)**. A strict-positive interpretation would exclude $h=0$, and would not be equivalent to the assertion about an arbitrary Hermitian square.

Finally, use the two [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) real functions $t\mapsto\sqrt{\max(t,0)}$ and $t\mapsto\sqrt{\max(-t,0)}$ on $\sigma(x)$. Their calculus values $u,v$ are Hermitian, belong to $A$ also in the nonunital case, and satisfy

$$
\boxed{x=u^2-v^2.}
$$
