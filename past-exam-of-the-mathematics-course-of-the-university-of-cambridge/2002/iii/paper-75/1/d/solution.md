<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Thomson scattering](../../../../../../thomson-scattering.md) generates linear [Cosmic microwave background polarization](../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background-polarization.md) when the incident radiation has a [photon quadrupole](../../../../../../photon-quadrupole.md). The cross-section prefers electric fields perpendicular to the scattering direction. Contributions from an isotropic field cancel, and a dipole alone does not supply the required spin-two angular pattern. Schematically the outgoing Stokes combination is proportional to

$$
Q\pm iU\ \propto\ \int d\Omega'\,\sin^2\vartheta\,e^{\pm2i\varphi}\Theta(\widehat n'),
$$

which selects the incident quadrupolar angular dependence. Tight coupling nearly isotropizes the radiation, making the quadrupole small; it develops when the photon mean free path becomes finite near decoupling. At leading tight-coupling order its size scales as $(k/\dot\kappa)v_\gamma$.

The polarization is therefore weaker than the temperature anisotropy: a characteristic amplitude is of order **a few percent to roughly ten percent of the temperature signal**, or several microkelvin compared with temperature fluctuations of several tens of microkelvin on acoustic scales. This is an order-of-magnitude comparison, not a scale-independent ratio of every power-spectrum multipole.

The [photon monopole](../../../../../../photon-monopole.md) follows the acoustic displacement, approximately $\cos(kr_s)$; the velocity sourcing the quadrupole follows $\sin(kr_s)$. Hence polarization maxima occur near displacement zeros, between the main temperature peaks. In the leading coherent-oscillator approximation the shift is half the peak spacing, $\Delta\ell/2$, although projection and gravitational driving modify exact locations. Scalar perturbations generate [E-mode polarization](../../../../../../e-mode-polarization.md) at linear order, not [B-mode polarization](../../../../../../b-mode-polarization.md). Tensor perturbations and lensing can give B modes, and [reionization](../../../../../../reionization.md) generates an additional large-angle polarization feature.
