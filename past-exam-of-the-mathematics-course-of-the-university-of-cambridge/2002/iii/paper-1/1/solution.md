<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

First prove the [primitive three-cycle criterion](../../../../../primitive-three-cycle-criterion.md) directly. The useful generation fact is that adding a [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md) whose support meets a set $S$, with $|S|\geq3$, to the [alternating group](../../../../../alternating-group.md) $A_S$ generates the [alternating group](../../../../../alternating-group.md) on the enlarged set. If the support is $\{a,b,x\}$ with $a,b\in S$ and $x\notin S$, [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $A_S$ and taking inverses produces every [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md) with $x$ and two points of $S$. For $|S|\geq4$ this uses [two-transitivity](../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) of $A_S$; for $|S|=3$, its cyclic action on pairs together with inversion gives both pair orientations. These cycles, together with the cycles supported inside $S$, are all the [three-cycles](../../../../../three-cycle.md) on $S\cup\{x\}$ and generate $A_{S\cup\{x\}}$. Every even [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) is a product of [three-cycles](../../../../../three-cycle.md): pair its transpositions, and use $(a\,b)(b\,c)=(a\,b\,c)$ and $(a\,b)(c\,d)=(a\,c\,b)(a\,c\,d)$, with rightmost factors acting first.

If instead the new support is $\{a,x,y\}$ with just $a\in S$, choose $b\in S\setminus\{a\}$ and conjugate $(a\,x\,y)$ by an element of $A_S$ carrying $a$ to $b$. This gives $(b\,x\,y)$, and

$$
(a\,x\,y)(b\,x\,y)^{-1}=(a\,x\,b).
$$

The two-point-overlap case first adjoins $x$; the original cycle then adjoins $y$. This proves [connected triple supports generate an alternating group](../../../../../connected-triple-supports-generate-an-alternating-group.md) by successively adjoining intersecting supports.

Now take the supports of every conjugate in $G$ of a given [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md), and make these triples the edges of a [hypergraph](../../../../../hypergraph-split.md) on the [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) domain. Its connected components are permuted by $G$, so they form a [block system](../../../../../block-system.md). Since at least one component contains three points, [primitivity](../../../../../primitive-group-action.md) forces the single component to be the entire domain. Start with one triple and adjoin intersecting triples until all points have been reached. The generation argument shows that the [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by the conjugate cycles is $A_n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{A_n\leq G.}
$$

To classify the proper [maximal subgroups](../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) containing a [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md), distinguish the three possible types of action. A [primitive](../../../../../primitive-group-action.md) [maximal subgroup](../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) contains $A_n$, and thus equals $A_n$, since $[S_n:A_n]=2$. An intransitive [maximal subgroup](../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) lies in the full stabilizer $S_k\times S_{n-k}$ of one of its orbits, and maximality makes it that full stabilizer. When $k=n-k$, this stabilizer lies properly inside the [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) that can interchange the two parts, so it is not maximal. Take the smaller part to obtain $1\leq k<n/2$. Such an unequal-part stabilizer is maximal: its transpositions connect all points within each part, and any element outside it carries at least one within-part transposition to a cross-part transposition. The resulting connected graph of transpositions generates $S_n$. To see that last assertion, transpositions along a path generate the transposition of its endpoints by successive conjugations. The stabilizer contains a [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md) exactly when $n-k\geq3$.

A transitive imprimitive [maximal subgroup](../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) preserves a nontrivial partition into $b$ blocks of size $a$, with $n=ab$. It equals the full [permutation wreath product](../../../../../permutation-wreath-product.md) $S_a\wr S_b$. This full block stabilizer is maximal: any [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) not preserving the partition sends some same-block pair to a different-block pair, giving a cross-block transposition by [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md). The block stabilizer is transitive on cross-block pairs, so all cross-block transpositions follow; together with its within-block transpositions they generate $S_n$. A [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md) in this [group](../../../../../group-split.md) must act trivially on the blocks, since any nontrivial block [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) moves at least two entire blocks, and a block three-cycle moves $3a$ points. Thus a single [three-cycle](../../../../../three-cycle.md) exists exactly when $a\geq3$. The full classification, up to [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md), is therefore

$$
\boxed{A_n;\quad S_k\times S_{n-k}\ (1\leq k<n/2,\ n-k\geq3);\quad S_a\wr S_b\ (n=ab,\ a\geq3,\ b\geq2).}
$$

This is the [maximal symmetric-group subgroups containing a three-cycle](../../../../../maximal-symmetric-group-subgroups-containing-a-three-cycle.md) classification. For $n<3$ there are no such [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md).

For the first family of counterexamples to extending the criterion to longer cycles, let $G=PSL_2(p)$ act on the [projective line](../../../../../projective-line.md) $\mathbb F_p\cup\{\infty\}$. The determinant-one [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md)

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

act respectively as $x\mapsto x+1$ and $x\mapsto-1/x$. The first fixes infinity and is one $p$-cycle on the finite points. Its translations make the infinity stabilizer transitive on the other points; the second moves infinity, so the full action is [two-transitive](../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md), hence [primitive](../../../../../primitive-group-action.md). The action is faithful after quotienting by scalar [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md). Counting a nonzero first column and the $p$ second columns with determinant one gives $|SL_2(p)|=p(p^2-1)$, and the scalar [group center](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) has order two. Thus

$$
|PSL_2(p)|=\frac{p(p^2-1)}2<\frac{(p+1)!}2=|A_{p+1}|\qquad(p>3),
$$

since the ratio on the right to the left is $(p-2)!>1$. Consequently **$PSL_2(p)$ is the required example for every prime $p>3$**.

For the final example take $p=59$, and let the [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) $A_5\times A_5$ act on the sixty elements of $A_5$ by

$$
(x,y):t\longmapsto xty^{-1}.
$$

The action is transitive and the stabilizer of the identity is the diagonal [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $\Delta=\{(z,z):z\in A_5\}$. To prove that the diagonal is a [maximal subgroup](../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md), no classification theorem is needed. If $\Delta<K\leq A_5\times A_5$, choose $(x,y)\in K\setminus\Delta$. Multiplying by $(y^{-1},y^{-1})$ gives $(xy^{-1},1)\in K$ with $xy^{-1}\ne1$. The [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $L=\{z:(z,1)\in K\}$ is normal in $A_5$, by diagonal [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md), and is nontrivial. Simplicity of $A_5$ gives $L=A_5$, and then $K=A_5\times A_5$. Thus the [coset](../../../../../coset.md) action is [primitive](../../../../../primitive-group-action.md). It is also faithful: if $xty^{-1}=t$ for every $t$, then $x=y$ by taking $t=1$, and $x$ is central in the centerless [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md) $A_5$, so $x=y=1$. Finally,

$$
\boxed{n=60=59+1,\qquad |G|=60^2=3600,\qquad59\nmid |G|.}
$$

The prime $59$ is odd, and its primality follows by testing the primes $2,3,5,7$, the only ones below its square root. This is a faithful [diagonal primitive permutation group](../../../../../diagonal-primitive-permutation-group.md) whose order is not divisible by the degree minus one.
