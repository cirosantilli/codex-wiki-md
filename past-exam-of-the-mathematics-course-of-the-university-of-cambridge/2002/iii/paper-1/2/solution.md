A [subnormal series](../../../../../subnormal-series.md) is a finite chain $1=G_r\trianglelefteq G_{r-1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G_0=G$, in which each term is normal in the preceding term. Its factors are the [quotient groups](../../../../../quotient-group.md) $G_i/G_{i+1}$. A [normal series of a group](../../../../../normal-series-of-a-group.md) imposes the stronger condition that every term be normal in $G$. These conditions should not be conflated: inserting a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) normal in an intermediate term need not preserve normality in the whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md). A refinement inserts additional terms, and two series are equivalent when their nontrivial factors can be paired by [group isomorphisms](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md).

The basic refinement mechanism is the [Zassenhaus butterfly lemma](../../../../../zassenhaus-butterfly-lemma.md). For $A'\trianglelefteq A$ and $B'\trianglelefteq B$, let $C=A\cap B$ and $D=(A'\cap B)(A\cap B')$. Both factors of $D$ are normal in $C$, so $D\trianglelefteq C$. The map from $C$ onto $A'C/A'(A\cap B')$ has kernel $D$: if an element of $C$ equals $a'b'$ with $a'\in A'$ and $b'\in A\cap B'$, then $a'=cb'^{-1}\in A'\cap B$. Conversely each product in $D$ is in the kernel. The [first isomorphism theorem](../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
\frac{A'(A\cap B)}{A'(A\cap B')}\cong C/D
\cong\frac{B'(A\cap B)}{B'(B\cap A')}.
$$

The denominators are normal in their numerators: [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $C$ preserves them, while [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) by $A'$ or $B'$ changes their elements only by a factor already in that [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md).

Given two [subnormal series](../../../../../subnormal-series.md) $(G_i)$ and $(H_j)$, refine the first by $G_{i+1}(G_i\cap H_j)$, and the second by $H_{j+1}(H_j\cap G_i)$. The endpoints are $G_i,G_{i+1}$ and $H_j,H_{j+1}$ respectively, and the [Zassenhaus butterfly lemma](../../../../../zassenhaus-butterfly-lemma.md) pairs the successive factors indexed by $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$. This proves the [Schreier refinement theorem](../../../../../schreier-refinement-theorem.md).

A [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) is a strict [subnormal series](../../../../../subnormal-series.md) all of whose factors are [simple groups](../../../../../simple-group.md). Every [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) has one: choose a maximal proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), repeat inside it, and use decreasing orders to terminate. A simple factor cannot admit a nontrivial refinement. Applying the preceding refinement theorem to two [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) proves the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../../jordan-holder-theorem.md): their lengths and multisets of simple factors agree, up to [group isomorphism](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md). The factors are the [Jordan–Hölder factors](../../../../../jordan-holder-factor.md), and their number is the [composition length](../../../../../composition-length.md). If $N\trianglelefteq G$, lifting a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) of $G/N$ and adjoining one for $N$ gives

$$
\ell(G)=\ell(N)+\ell(G/N).
$$

These factors do not determine the whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md), because they do not determine how its [group extensions](../../../../../group-extension.md) are assembled. For example, $C_4$ and the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md) both have two factors $C_2$, but one contains an element of order four and the other does not. The [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_3$ has a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) $1<C_3<S_3$, with factors $C_3,C_2$, whereas the [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md) $A_5$ has the single factor $A_5$.

A [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md) is a maximal strict [normal series of a group](../../../../../normal-series-of-a-group.md); each [chief factor](../../../../../chief-factor.md) is a minimal nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) in the appropriate quotient of $G$. Finite [chief factors](../../../../../chief-factor.md) are [characteristically simple](../../../../../characteristically-simple-group.md). To justify their structure, let $N$ be any nontrivial finite characteristically [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md) and choose a minimal nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $M$ of $N$. Its distinct images under automorphisms of $N$ are minimal [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so distinct ones intersect trivially and commute. Choose a maximal family giving an internal [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md). If any other image were not contained in that product, its intersection with the product would be trivial by minimality, and it could be added, a contradiction. Hence this product is characteristic and equals $N$. Each factor $M$ is simple: a proper nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $M$ would be normal in the whole direct product $N$, contrary to the choice of $M$. Thus $N$ is a [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) of isomorphic finite [simple groups](../../../../../simple-group.md). This applies to [chief factors](../../../../../chief-factor.md) by [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) from $G$.

Chief and composition factors need not coincide. In $A_4$, the chain $1<V_4<A_4$ is a [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md), since [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) cycles the three order-two [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) of $V_4$. A [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) instead inserts any one of those [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md), giving factors $C_2,C_2,C_3$. Its inserted order-two [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) is normal in $V_4$ but not in $A_4$. [Chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md) retain the ambient [group](../../../../../group-split.md)'s [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) information that [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) discard.

The [derived series](../../../../../derived-series.md) gives a canonical criterion for a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) to be [soluble](../../../../../solvable-group.md): put $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$. Its terms are characteristic, and each factor is [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md). A [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) is [soluble](../../../../../solvable-group.md) exactly when this series reaches the identity, equivalently when all its composition factors are cyclic of prime order. For one direction, the [derived series](../../../../../derived-series.md) supplies [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md) factors, whose simple refinements are cyclic of prime order. Conversely an extension of two soluble [groups](../../../../../group-split.md) is soluble: if $(G/N)^{(r)}=1$, then $G^{(r)}\subseteq N$; if $N^{(s)}=1$, then $G^{(r+s)}=1$. Induction up a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) with cyclic prime factors proves the other direction. In a finite soluble [group](../../../../../group-split.md), each [minimal normal subgroup](../../../../../minimal-normal-subgroup.md) is elementary [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md): its characteristic [commutator subgroup](../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) must be trivial, its primary components force a single prime, and its characteristic [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of $p$th powers must be trivial. Thus every [chief factor](../../../../../chief-factor.md) is a [vector](../../../../../vector.md) space over some $\mathbb F_p$. In a general [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) the [Jordan–Hölder factors](../../../../../jordan-holder-factor.md) can instead be nonabelian [simple groups](../../../../../simple-group.md).

The [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) demand more than [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md) factors: each factor must be central in the corresponding quotient of the whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md). Define the [upper central series](../../../../../upper-central-series.md) by $Z_0=1$ and $Z_{i+1}/Z_i=Z(G/Z_i)$, and the [lower central series](../../../../../lower-central-series.md) by $\gamma_1=G$ and $\gamma_{i+1}=[\gamma_i,G]$. A [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is [nilpotent](../../../../../nilpotent.md) exactly when the upper series reaches $G$, equivalently when the lower series reaches the identity. Indeed, for any ascending [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) $1=C_0\leq\cdots\leq C_c=G$, induction gives $C_i\subseteq Z_i$ and $\gamma_{c+1-i}\subseteq C_i$; reversing the lower series gives an ascending [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) when it terminates. The least such $c$ is the [nilpotency class](../../../../../nilpotency-class.md). A [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) has [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md) factors, so every [nilpotent group](../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) is [soluble](../../../../../solvable-group.md); the converse fails, as $S_3$ is soluble but has trivial [group center](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md).

For a finite [p-group](../../../../../p-group.md), the [class equation](../../../../../class-equation.md) shows that the [group center](../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) is nontrivial, since every noncentral conjugacy class has size divisible by $p$. Applying the same argument to successive central quotients makes the [upper central series](../../../../../upper-central-series.md) reach the [group](../../../../../group-split.md). More generally a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) is nilpotent exactly when it is the [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) of its [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md). Here is the nontrivial direction. A [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) gives the [normalizer condition for nilpotent groups](../../../../../normalizer-condition-for-nilpotent-groups.md): for a proper [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) $H$, choose the first $Z_i$ not contained in $H$. Then $[Z_i,H]\subseteq Z_{i-1}\subseteq H$, so $Z_i\subseteq N_G(H)$ and $N_G(H)>H$. If a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P$ had proper [normalizer](../../../../../normalizer.md) $N$, then $N_G(N)>N$; but $P$ is the unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $N$, hence characteristic there, and $N_G(N)\subseteq N_G(P)=N$, a contradiction. Thus all [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) are normal, different ones commute because their intersection is trivial, and they form an internal direct product. Conversely each finite [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) is nilpotent, and products of their [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) give a [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) for their direct product.

**[Composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) record simple building blocks, [chief series](../../../../../chief-series.md) record ambient normal structure, [derived series](../../../../../derived-series.md) detect solubility, and [central series](../../../../../central-series.md) detect nilpotence.** Their associated factors and extension data explain why the same [group](../../../../../group-split.md) can have very different useful series.
