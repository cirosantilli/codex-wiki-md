<h1 id="5/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [5](../5.md)

Work in the based CW [homotopy category](../../../../../homotopy-category.md). A homotopy-invariant [functor](../../../../../functor.md) depends on a map only through its based [homotopy class](../../../../../homotopy-class.md). In a sequence of [pointed sets](../../../../../pointed-set.md), exactness at the middle means that the preceding image equals the inverse image of the distinguished point; it does not imply injectivity or surjectivity at the ends. For [groups](../../../../../group-split.md) this is the familiar image-equals-kernel condition. A [half-exact functor](../../../../../topological-half-exact-functor.md) preserves such middle exactness on the appropriate elementary sequence.

For a contravariant [functor](../../../../../functor.md) $F$, the relevant elementary sequence is a [cofibration](../../../../../cofibration.md) $A\hookrightarrow X\to X/A$. Half-exactness is

$$
F(X/A)\longrightarrow F(X)\longrightarrow F(A)
$$

exact at $F(X)$. One should not silently turn this into a short exact sequence. In particular, arbitrary [group](../../../../../group-split.md) actions and [pointed sets](../../../../../pointed-set.md) at low degrees are not interchangeable with [abelian groups](../../../../../abelian-group.md).

The basic contravariant example is $F_Y(X)=[X,Y]_*$, often called a cohomotopy-type [functor](../../../../../functor.md). Suppose a map $f:X\to Y$ becomes [null-homotopic](../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) on $A$. A chosen null-homotopy extends it over the cone attached to $A$, giving a map from the [topological mapping cone](../../../../../mapping-cone-topology.md) to $Y$. Conversely every such extension restricts to a [null-homotopic](../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md) map on $A$. Since the [topological mapping cone](../../../../../mapping-cone-topology.md) of a [subcomplex](../../../../../simplicial-subcomplex.md) inclusion models $X/A$, this proves half-exactness, rather than merely asserting it.

Repeating the [topological mapping cone](../../../../../mapping-cone-topology.md) construction gives the [Puppe sequence](../../../../../puppe-sequence.md)

$$
A\to X\to C_i\to\Sigma A\to\Sigma X\to\cdots.
$$

Contravariant mapping into $Y$ gives

$$
\cdots\to[\Sigma X,Y]_*\to[\Sigma A,Y]_*\to[C_i,Y]_*\to[X,Y]_*\to[A,Y]_*.
$$

This is exact as [pointed sets](../../../../../pointed-set.md). An [H-group](../../../../../h-group.md) structure on $Y$, for example a [loop space](../../../../../loop-space.md) with path concatenation, provides [group](../../../../../group-split.md) structures; repeated looping gives [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) structures in the usual ranges. The [suspension-loop adjunction](../../../../../suspension-loop-adjunction.md) $[\Sigma X,Y]_*\cong[X,\Omega Y]_*$ explains the shifted terms and their compatibility.

The covariant counterpart is $H_T(Y)=[T,Y]_*$, with ordinary [homotopy groups](../../../../../homotopy-group.md) obtained from $T=S^n$. Its natural elementary sequence is a [homotopy fiber sequence](../../../../../homotopy-fiber-sequence.md) $F\to E\to B$, not an arbitrary [cofibration](../../../../../cofibration.md). A map $a:T\to E$ whose composite to $B$ is [null-homotopic](../../../../../null-homotopic-map.md), together with that null-homotopy, determines a map into the [homotopy fibre](../../../../../homotopy-fiber.md). Conversely a fibre map projects to such a null composite. This proves exactness of $[T,F]_*\to[T,E]_*\to[T,B]_*$ at the middle. Adding the loop terms produces the [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) long exact sequence. For a [Serre fibration](../../../../../serre-fibration.md), its ordinary fibre supplies the same [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) information.

This variance distinction matters. Include one [circle](../../../../../circle.md), generated by $a$, in $S^1\vee S^1$, with other generator $b$. The quotient is the second [circle](../../../../../circle.md). The kernel of $\pi_1(S^1\vee S^1)\to\pi_1(S^1)$ is the [normal closure](../../../../../normal-closure.md) of $a$, which contains $bab^{-1}$. The image of the included [circle](../../../../../circle.md) is only the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) generated by $a$, and does not contain that reduced word. Thus covariant $\pi_1$ is not half-exact on this [cofibration](../../../../../cofibration.md). Fibre and cofibre statements cannot be swapped just by reversing notation.

Reduced [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md) gives group-valued contravariant examples. For an abelian coefficient [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $G$, the degree-$q$ [functor](../../../../../functor.md) is represented by an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](../../../../../eilenberg-maclane-space.md):

$$
\widetilde H^q(X;G)\cong[X,K(G,q)]_*.
$$

Its [cofibration](../../../../../cofibration.md) exact sequence continues as $\cdots\to\widetilde H^{q-1}(A)\to\widetilde H^q(X/A)\to\widetilde H^q(X)\to\widetilde H^q(A)\to\cdots$. Ordinary [homotopy groups](../../../../../homotopy-group.md) are instead covariant, with decreasing [group](../../../../../group-split.md) degree across a fibre connecting map. These are the two complementary representable patterns, maps out of a test object and maps into a representing object.

[Brown's representability theorem](../../../../../brown-s-representability-theorem.md) makes the contravariant pattern structural. A reduced contravariant [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) [functor](../../../../../functor.md) on based [CW complexes](../../../../../cw-complex.md) is represented by a [CW complex](../../../../../cw-complex.md) if it converts arbitrary wedges to products and satisfies the Mayer-Vietoris weak-pullback gluing axiom. The latter says that compatible classes on two subcomplexes extend to their union. It contains more information than the single kernel statement of half-exactness. Consequently half-exactness alone is not a representability criterion. In a representing [functor](../../../../../functor.md) these axioms are natural: a map on a wedge is a family of based maps, while compatible maps on an intersection can be adjusted by a [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) and glued across a [homotopy pushout](../../../../../homotopy-pushout.md).

A reduced [generalized cohomology theory](../../../../../generalized-cohomology-theory.md) with the usual wedge axiom supplies these axioms degree by degree. Its representing spaces $E_q$ have $E_q\simeq\Omega E_{q+1}$ by suspension compatibility, assembling an [Omega-spectrum](../../../../../omega-spectrum.md). Thus generalized [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md) can be expressed as $E^q(X)=[\Sigma^\infty X,\Sigma^qE]_{\mathrm{st}}$, while represented [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) is $E_q(X)=\pi_q(E\wedge\Sigma^\infty X)$. The appearance of mapping into a spectrum in one case and smashing with it in the other is another form of the same variance distinction.

There is a literal duality after stabilization and a finiteness restriction. Here a finite based [CW complex](../../../../../cw-complex.md) is identified with its [suspension spectrum](../../../../../suspension-spectrum.md). For a [finite spectrum](../../../../../finite-spectrum.md) $X$, let $DX=F(X,\mathbb S)$ be its [Spanier-Whitehead dual](../../../../../spanier-whitehead-dual.md). Then $D^2X\simeq X$ and $[X,Y]_{\mathrm{st}}\cong[DY,DX]_{\mathrm{st}}$ for [finite spectra](../../../../../finite-spectrum.md). Duality reverses a cofibre triangle into a fibre triangle. In particular,

$$
\widetilde E^q(X)\cong\pi_{-q}(E\wedge DX),\qquad \widetilde E_q(X)\cong[\Sigma^qDX,E]_{\mathrm{st}}.
$$

This turns a [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md) calculation on a finite object into a [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) calculation on its dual, and vice versa. It is [Spanier-Whitehead duality](../../../../../spanier-whitehead-duality.md), not a claim that the full category of ordinary spaces is self-dual.

**Half-exactness is the local image–kernel property; fibres and cofibres, representability, and finite stable duality explain how that property organizes [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) and [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md).** The distinctions between variance, [pointed sets](../../../../../pointed-set.md) and [groups](../../../../../group-split.md), and finite stable objects and arbitrary spaces are essential to using the duality correctly.
