<h1 id="6/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write the [adjunction](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) as a [natural bijection](../../../../../../natural-bijection.md) $\alpha_{C,D}:\mathcal D(FC,D)\to\mathcal C(C,GD)$. The [adjunction unit](../../../../../../unit-of-an-adjunction.md) and [adjunction counit](../../../../../../counit-of-an-adjunction.md) are defined by

$$
\eta_C=\alpha_{C,FC}(1_{FC}),\qquad\varepsilon_D=\alpha^{-1}_{GD,D}(1_{GD}).
$$

[Naturality](../../../../../../naturality.md) of $\alpha$ implies $GF(u)\eta_C=\eta_{C'}u$ for $u:C\to C'$ and $v\varepsilon_D=\varepsilon_{D'}FG(v)$ for $v:D\to D'$, so these are [natural transformations](../../../../../../natural-transformation.md) $1_{\mathcal C}\to GF$ and $FG\to1_{\mathcal D}$. More explicitly, [naturality](../../../../../../naturality.md) at $f:FC\to D$ and $g:C\to GD$ gives the two transpose formulas

$$
\alpha(f)=G(f)\eta_C,\qquad\alpha^{-1}(g)=\varepsilon_DF(g).
$$

These formulas will also prove the [triangle identities for an adjunction](../../../../../../triangle-identities-for-an-adjunction.md).
