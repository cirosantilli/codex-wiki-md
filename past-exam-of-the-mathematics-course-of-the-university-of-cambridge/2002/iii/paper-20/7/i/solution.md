<h1 id="7/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Each [hom-set](../../../../../../hom-set.md) in a [poset](../../../../../../partially-ordered-set.md) is either empty or a singleton. Thus a [natural bijection](../../../../../../natural-bijection.md) $Y(fx,y)\cong X(x,gy)$ is precisely the condition

$$
\boxed{f(x)\leq y\iff x\leq g(y).}
$$

The [naturality](../../../../../../naturality.md) squares commute automatically, because maps between the relevant singleton [hom-sets](../../../../../../hom-set.md) are unique. This is a [Galois connection](../../../../../../galois-connection.md). Equivalently, the [adjunction unit](../../../../../../unit-of-an-adjunction.md) and [adjunction counit](../../../../../../counit-of-an-adjunction.md) are the inequalities $x\leq gf(x)$ and $fg(y)\leq y$. These inequalities imply the displayed equivalence: $f(x)\leq y$ gives $x\leq gf(x)\leq g(y)$, and $x\leq g(y)$ gives $f(x)\leq fg(y)\leq y$.
