<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Let $f\in R[X]$ have degree $n$. A useful reduction prime must preserve its degree and make its reduction separable. Thus exclude the leading coefficient and [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) from $P$; after localizing at those elements one may make $f$ monic. The residue base [field](../../../algebra.md#field) is $k=\operatorname{Frac}(R/P)$, not necessarily the ring $R/P$ itself. Then **the [Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) of the reduced [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) embeds in the generic group, with the same action on the corresponding roots**.

Here is the [unramified reduction of a polynomial Galois group](../../../galois-theory.md#unramified-reduction-of-a-polynomial-galois-group) argument. A UFD is integrally closed. Let $E$ be the [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) over $F$, and use the root algebra $B$ obtained by adjoining all roots after inverting the [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant). It is finite étale over the resulting normal base: successive simple-root adjunctions are unramified, and the distinct generic components separate over a normal ring. Choose a prime $Q$ above $P$. The residue [field](../../../algebra.md#field) $k_Q$ is generated over $k$ by the reduced roots, so it is their [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field). The [decomposition group](../../../arithmetic.md#decomposition-group)

$$
D_Q=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/F):\sigma Q=Q\}
$$

acts on $k_Q$. Its residue action is onto $\operatorname{Gal}(k_Q/k)$; this is the local decomposition-group description of the unramified fiber. Equivalently, the geometric points of that fiber form a free transitive Galois-group set, and the commuting residue-field action identifies the residue group with a stabilizer subgroup of the generic group.

The kernel is the [inertia group](../../../arithmetic.md#inertia-group). An element in that kernel fixes every reduced root. Since the [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant) is a unit, distinct roots have distinct reductions; consequently it fixes every original root and is the identity. Therefore

$$
\boxed{D_Q\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\overline f/k)\leq\operatorname{Gal}(f/F).}
$$

Choosing another prime changes this subgroup by conjugacy, which does not change the cycle types available for computing the generic group.

When $k=\mathbb F_q$, its splitting-field group is generated by [Frobenius](../../../arithmetic.md#frobenius-automorphism). A reduced irreducible factor of degree $d_i$ contributes one $d_i$-cycle. Hence a squarefree factorization with degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ proves that the generic group contains that [cycle type](../../../finite-group-theory.md#cycle-type). Irreducible degree-preserving reduction also proves generic irreducibility by [Gauss's lemma](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry): a nontrivial monic factorization would reduce to one. Several good primes, a discriminant-square test and permutation-group arguments can then identify the group. A ramified or degree-dropping prime does not justify the same cycle test.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Modulo two the [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) becomes $h=X^5+X^2+1$. It has neither zero nor one as a root. The only irreducible quadratic over $\mathbb F_2$ is $X^2+X+1$; modulo it, $X^3=1$ and $h\equiv1$. A reducible quintic has a factor of degree at most two, so $h$ is irreducible. It is also separable: $h'=X^4$, which is coprime to $h$. Thus the rational [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) is irreducible, and its group $G\leq S_5$ is transitive.

Modulo three there is the exact factorization

$$
f=(X^2+1)(X^3+2X+1).
$$

The quadratic has no root. The cubic takes value one at each of $0,1,2$, so it too has no root and is irreducible. The two factors are distinct and coprime. The [Frobenius cycle type](../../../galois-theory.md#frobenius-cycle-type) therefore supplies an element with disjoint cycles of lengths two and three. Its cube is a single [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation).

A transitive action of prime degree is primitive: a block size divides five, so the only sizes are one and five. To finish without classifying all degree-five groups, apply the [primitive group with a transposition](../../../group-theory.md#primitive-group-with-a-transposition) argument. Form the graph of all conjugate [transpositions](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) in $G$. Its connected components are blocks, so it is connected. [Transpositions](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) along the edges of a connected graph generate every [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation): along a path, conjugating adjacent swaps yields the swap of its endpoints. Hence they generate $S_5$, and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f,\mathbb Q)=S_5.}
$$

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Use $R=\mathbb F_2[t]$. At $t=1$ the specialization is $X^5+X^2+1$, already proved irreducible and separable. Because the original [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) is monic, this proves irreducibility over $\mathbb F_2(t)$ and gives a transitive group on five roots.

At $t=0$ the specialization factors as

$$
X^5+X+1=(X^2+X+1)(X^3+X^2+1).
$$

Neither factor has a root in $\mathbb F_2$, so they are irreducible; the factors are coprime and the specialization is separable. For example its derivative is $(X+1)^4$ and its value at one is one. The unramified reduction test gives a $(2)(3)$ [permutation](../../../combinatorics.md#permutation) whose cube is a [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation). The same primitive prime-degree graph argument now gives

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f,\mathbb F_2(t))=S_5.}
$$

No characteristic-zero discriminant-sign argument is being transferred to characteristic two: good specialization and the explicit cycle test are sufficient.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

A [Hilbertian field](../../../algebra.md#hilbertian-field) is a [field](../../../algebra.md#field) over which generic irreducibility can be retained by specializing a parameter. More precisely, for [polynomials](../../../polynomial.md) $f_i(T,X)$ irreducible over $K(T)$ and separable in $X$, and a nonzero exclusion [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) $h(T)$, the [Hilbert subset](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-subset)

$$
H_K(f_1,\ldots,f_r;h)=
\{a\in K:h(a)\ne0,\ f_i(a,X)\text{ irreducible of degree }\deg_Xf_i\text{ for all }i\}
$$

is infinite. Denominators, leading coefficients and discriminants may be incorporated into $h$. The infinite condition permits simultaneous avoidance of any finitely many bad places. Separability is part of the definition, particularly in positive characteristic.

The field-theoretic interpretation is useful in constructive [Galois theory](../../../galois-theory.md). If $L/K(T)$ is finite separable and generated by a root of $f$, specialization at a good $a$ is the residue extension at a place over $T-a$. Irreducibility of $f(a,X)$ means its residue degree is the entire degree $[L:K(T)]$. Thus a Hilbertian [field](../../../algebra.md#field) has infinitely many unramified rational places which are completely inert in each prescribed finite separable extension. For a finite family, one can work in a primitive-element presentation of their separable compositum; preserving its full degree preserves the degrees of all the subextensions.

[Hilbert's irreducibility theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-s-irreducibility-theorem) says that $\mathbb Q$ is Hilbertian, and gives the corresponding assertion for number [fields](../../../algebra.md#field), including infinitely many integral specializations in its classical form. Rational function [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) $k(U)$ over arbitrary [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) $k$ are also Hilbertian. In particular, global function [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) are Hilbertian just as number [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) are. These standard [field](../../../algebra.md#field) classes and the specialization viewpoint are discussed in [Haran and Jarden's introduction to Hilbertian fields](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1991_4_24_6_739_0.pdf). The theorem does not say every parameter is good: $X^2-T$ is generically irreducible, but a square parameter makes it split. For simultaneous irreducibility, one excludes more exceptional parameters, not the entire [field](../../../algebra.md#field).

Hilbertianity survives finite extensions. For the separable case, let $K$ be Hilbertian, $M/K$ finite separable and $f\in M(T)[X]$ irreducible and separable. A primitive element of $M(T)(\alpha)$ over $K(T)$ has degree $[M:K]\deg f$. Apply Hilbertianity to its minimal [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) and exclude the presentation's denominators. The resulting residue [field](../../../algebra.md#field) contains the constant copy of $M$ and has the same total degree over $K$, so its degree over $M$ is $\deg f$. Therefore $f(a,X)$ remains irreducible over $M$, even for infinitely many $a$ chosen in $K$. This argument also handles finitely many [polynomials](../../../polynomial.md) by a compositum. Purely inseparable finite extensions are treated by separating the separable part and transporting its rational places through the unique purely inseparable extension; the finite-extension stability statement remains valid.

The property is not inherited by arbitrary algebraic extensions. An [algebraic closure](../../../algebra.md#algebraic-closure) is not Hilbertian: in [characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero), $X^2-T$ is irreducible over $K(T)$ but every specialization has a root in $K$. In characteristic $p$, use $X^p-X-T$ instead; a hypothetical rational-function root would give its right side a pole of order divisible by $p$, contradicting its simple pole at infinity. All constant specializations split over the algebraically closed [field](../../../algebra.md#field).

Finite [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) also fail. For $K=\mathbb F_q$ of characteristic $p$, consider

$$
f(T,X)=X^p-X-T(T^q-T).
$$

Its right-hand additive datum has a pole of order $q+1$, not divisible by $p$, and cannot be $b^p-b$ for a rational function $b$. The [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) is therefore irreducible over $K(T)$: its roots are $\alpha+c$ for $c\in\mathbb F_p$, so its splitting-field group has prime order unless a root already belongs to the base [field](../../../algebra.md#field). At every $a\in K$, however, $a^q-a=0$ and the specialization is $X^p-X$, which splits. This gives a concrete failure of the required specialization property.

The main constructive application is [Galois-preserving Hilbert specialization](../../../algebra.md#galois-preserving-hilbert-specialization). Suppose $E/K(T)$ is finite Galois with group $G$, and use a primitive element of the entire [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field), not merely a generator of one root [field](../../../algebra.md#field). A good specialization retaining degree $|G|$ has residue group embedded in $G$ by unramified reduction. Since its order is still $|G|$, it is exactly $G$. Preserving only the irreducibility of the original degree-$n$ [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) would prove transitivity, which is weaker than preserving its full group. Resolvents or the primitive generator eliminate that ambiguity.

Consequently a regular realization of $G$ over $K(T)$, with constant [field](../../../algebra.md#field) $K$, yields realizations over the Hilbertian [field](../../../algebra.md#field) $K$. This is the bridge from branch-cycle constructions to the [Inverse Galois problem](../../../galois-theory.md#inverse-galois-problem) over number [fields](../../../algebra.md#field). A realization over $\mathbb C(T)$ alone cannot be specialized to $\mathbb Q$; descent of its coefficients and constants is an additional step. Rational rigidity can supply that descent under its appropriate hypotheses, while ordinary complex rigidity answers only the geometric uniqueness question.

For example, the family $X^n-X-T$ has full symmetric geometric [monodromy](../../../complex-analysis.md#monodromy) in [characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero). It is irreducible because it is linear in $T$. Its derivative has $n-1$ distinct critical roots satisfying $nX^{n-1}=1$, and their distinct critical values are $-(n-1)X/n$. Each simple critical point gives a [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation). These finite branch cycles generate the [monodromy](../../../complex-analysis.md#monodromy), since the infinity loop is the inverse product of the finite loops. Their transitive connected-edge action gives $S_n$. The arithmetic group is also $S_n$, so the constant [field](../../../algebra.md#field) is $\mathbb Q$. Hilbert specialization then produces symmetric [Galois groups](../../../galois-theory.md#galois-group) over $\mathbb Q$. For $n=2$ the same conclusion follows directly from the square-root family.

Finally, the good parameters form a large arithmetic set without being every point or generally a Zariski-open set. Reducibility can occur at infinitely many exceptional points, as the square example shows. The language of thin sets packages those exceptions as images of generically finite separable covers of degree greater than one, together with finitely many points. Hilbertianity asserts that such finitely many exceptional constructions cannot exhaust the rational line. It is an existence principle with powerful constructive consequences, not by itself an algorithm that certifies a particular chosen parameter.

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

For a finite point $p$, use the [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) $v_p$ with local parameter $u=t-p$; at infinity use $u=1/t$. Its extensions $w$ to $E$ are permuted transitively by $G$. The completed residue [field](../../../algebra.md#field) is $F$, because $F$ is algebraically closed, so the local [decomposition group](../../../arithmetic.md#decomposition-group) equals its [inertia group](../../../arithmetic.md#inertia-group).

Write $e=e(w/v_p)$. [Characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero) makes all local ramification tame. One can choose a [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer) $\pi$ in the completion for which

$$
u=\pi^e,\qquad E_w=F((\pi)),\qquad F(t)_{v_p}=F((u)).
$$

Indeed, initially $u$ is $\pi^e$ times a unit; that unit has an $e$th root in $F[[\pi]]$, by its algebraically closed residue [field](../../../algebra.md#field) and the invertibility of $e$. Absorb it into $\pi$. The local automorphisms are then $\pi\mapsto\zeta\pi$, $\zeta\in\mu_e(F)$, and form a cyclic group of order $e$. Define $e_p=e$.

To specify an element rather than a subgroup, **fix a compatible choice of primitive [roots of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity) $\zeta_e\in F$**. Define $\tau_w$ by its [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer) character

$$
\overline{\tau_w(\pi)/\pi}=\zeta_e,
$$

and let $C_p$ be its [conjugacy class](../../../group-theory.md#conjugacy-class) in $G$. Over $\mathbb C$, take $\zeta_e=e^{2\pi i/e}$, corresponding to positive local continuation around $p$. At an unramified point, $e_p=1$ and $C_p=\{1\}$.

Changing the [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer) does not affect this character. If $\pi'=a\pi$ with $a$ a unit, inertia fixes its residue, so

$$
\overline{\tau(\pi')/\pi'}
=\overline{\tau(a)/a}\,\overline{\tau(\pi)/\pi}
=\overline{\tau(\pi)/\pi}.
$$

The character is injective: a nonidentity finite-order automorphism with derivative one would have $\tau(\pi)=\pi+b\pi^r+\cdots$ for $r\geq2$, and its $N$th iterate would have coefficient $Nb$ at that first nonzero displacement. [Characteristic zero](../../../algebra.md#characteristic-zero) contradicts finite order. Thus the chosen primitive character identifies a unique generator.

For another place $w'=\sigma w$, conjugation gives $D_{w'}=\sigma D_w\sigma^{-1}$, preserves its order, and carries $\tau_w$ to $\tau_{w'}$, since $\sigma$ fixes the constants and hence $\zeta_e$. This proves that $e_p$ and the [branch-cycle conjugacy class](../../../arithmetic.md#branch-cycle-conjugacy-class) are independent of the place and [uniformizer](../../../commutative-algebra.md#uniformizer). Without the stated roots-of-unity convention, only the cyclic inertia-subgroup [conjugacy class](../../../group-theory.md#conjugacy-class) is intrinsic: replacing $\zeta_e$ by another primitive root replaces the generator by a coprime power, which need not lie in the same [conjugacy class](../../../group-theory.md#conjugacy-class).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Put $u=t-p$ and write $a(t)=u^r b(u)$, where $b(0)\ne0$. The unit $b$ has a square root in $\mathbb C[[u]]$. Dividing the radical by that square root and by $u^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}$ reduces the completed extension to

$$
\mathbb C((u))(\sqrt{u^{\,r\bmod2}}).
$$

If $r$ is even, it is unramified and locally splits; if $r$ is odd, adjoining $u^{1/2}$ gives [ramification index](../../../arithmetic.md#ramification-index) two. At points where $a(p)\ne0$, $r=0$ and there is no branch. Therefore

$$
\boxed{p\in\mathbb C\text{ branches exactly when it is a zero of odd multiplicity.}}
$$

For infinity set $u=1/t$. If $D=\deg a$,

$$
a(1/u)=u^{-D}b(u),\qquad b(0)\ne0.
$$

The same [quadratic branch parity criterion](../../../arithmetic.md#quadratic-branch-parity-criterion) applies to the negative [valuation](../../../algebra.md#valuation) $-D$, so

$$
\boxed{\infty\text{ branches exactly when }\deg a\text{ is odd}.}
$$

The generic irreducibility excludes $a$ being a square. Local splitting at the even-order places is compatible with that global irreducibility. The number of odd finite orders plus the possible odd infinity order is even, as also follows from the sum of orders of a rational function being zero.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 3](paper-3.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Fix ordered branch positions $P=(p_1,\ldots,p_r)$ and conjugacy classes $C_i$ in $G$. Form the generating product-one set

$$
\mathrm{Ni}(G;\mathbf C)=
\{(g_1,\ldots,g_r):g_i\in C_i,\ g_1\cdots g_r=1,
\ \langle g_1,\ldots,g_r\rangle=G\}.
$$

The [rigid ramification type](../../../galois-theory.md#rigid-ramification-type) condition is that this set is nonempty and consists of one orbit under simultaneous inner conjugation by $G$. Rationality of classes is a further arithmetic condition and is not needed for this complex uniqueness statement.

Let $U=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)\setminus P$. Choose a base point and positively oriented peripheral loops $\gamma_i$ with sole relation $\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_r=1$. A connected Galois cover over $U$ gives a surjective [monodromy](../../../complex-analysis.md#monodromy) map $\pi_1(U)\to G$, and changing the labeling of one fiber conjugates it. Its peripheral images are exactly a tuple in the set above.

For two extensions of the same rigid type, rigidity conjugates their tuples to agree. The loops generate $\pi_1(U)$, so their entire [monodromy](../../../complex-analysis.md#monodromy) maps then agree. This constructs an isomorphism of the unramified covers directly: label fibers at the base point identically, and transport that matching along paths. Independence of path follows from equal [monodromy](../../../complex-analysis.md#monodromy). The resulting map is [holomorphic](../../../complex-analysis.md#complex-differentiability-at-a-point) because the cover maps are local biholomorphisms.

Near each missing point, the cover has local form $u=\pi^{e_i}$. The isomorphism therefore extends over the added points, equivalently by the removable-singularity theorem in these local parameters. Pullback of meromorphic functions gives an isomorphism of the function [fields](../../../algebra.md#field) fixing $\mathbb C(t)$. Hence **there is at most one extension of the prescribed rigid type**. This is uniqueness for fixed branch positions and peripheral conventions, not uniqueness after arbitrarily moving the branch points.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

For $n\geq3$, take $C_1$ to be the class of $n$-cycles, $C_2$ the class of [transpositions](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation), and $C_3$ the class of an $(n-1)$-cycle with one fixed point. With rightmost [permutations](../../../combinatorics.md#permutation) applied first, a witness is

$$
\alpha=(1\,2\,\cdots\,n),\qquad\beta=(1\,2),\qquad
\gamma=(\alpha\beta)^{-1}.
$$

Multiplying an $n$-cycle by a [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) splits it at the two exchanged letters. The resulting cycles have the lengths of the two arcs between those letters in the cyclic ordering. Thus $\alpha\beta$ has type $(n-1,1)$ exactly when the exchanged letters are cyclically adjacent.

Any product-one tuple can first be conjugated so that its first entry is $\alpha$. Its second entry is then one of the $n$ adjacent [transpositions](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation). The [centralizer](../../../group-theory.md#centralizer) $\langle\alpha\rangle$ acts transitively on these choices; the third entry is forced to be the inverse product. Therefore every product-one tuple lies in a single simultaneous-conjugation orbit.

The conjugates $\alpha^j\beta\alpha^{-j}$ exchange all adjacent letters around the cycle. Their connected-edge graph generates $S_n$, so the witness, and every tuple in that orbit, generates $S_n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(C_{(n)},C_{(2,1^{n-2})},C_{(n-1,1)})\text{ is a rigid triple in }S_n.}
$$

For $n=3$ the last two classes coincide, which causes no difficulty: the same three adjacent swaps form one orbit and generate $S_3$.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

The relevant cycle types are $(5,1)$, $(2,2,2)$ and $(3,2,1)$. Conjugate the first entry to $\alpha=(1\,2\,3\,4\,5)$, fixing six. An entry $\beta$ of type $(2,2,2)$ pairs six with one of the five other letters. Powers of $\alpha$ move that partner to one. With the pair $(1\,6)$ fixed, only three matchings remain:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\beta&\text{cycle type of }\alpha\beta\\
(1\,6)(2\,3)(4\,5)&(4,1,1)\\
(1\,6)(2\,4)(3\,5)&(6)\\
(1\,6)(2\,5)(3\,4)&(3,2,1).
\end{array}
$$

Thus there is exactly one [centralizer](../../../group-theory.md#centralizer) orbit of eligible second entries, consisting of five matchings before normalization. The third entry is forced to be $(\alpha\beta)^{-1}$, so there is at most one inner orbit of product-one tuples of these types.

For the actual displayed $g_2$, direct multiplication gives

$$
\alpha g_2=(1\,3)(4\,6\,5),\qquad
(\alpha g_2)^{-1}=(1\,3)(4\,5\,6),
$$

which belongs to the class of the displayed $g_3$. This is a product-one witness. The three displayed [permutations](../../../combinatorics.md#permutation) themselves need not multiply to one; the problem concerns their conjugacy classes.

The group $\langle\alpha,g_2\rangle$ is transitive, because $\alpha$ moves the first five points transitively and $g_2$ connects six to them. Its stabilizer of six contains the five-cycle $\alpha$ and is transitive on the other points. Hence the group is [two-transitive](../../../group-theory.md#two-transitive-group-action) and therefore primitive. Moreover $(\alpha g_2)^3=(1\,3)$ is a [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation). The primitive [transposition](../../../combinatorics.md#transposition-permutation) lemma proves that the group is $S_6$. Consequently all tuples in the sole inner orbit generate $S_6$, and

$$
\boxed{(C_{(5,1)},C_{(2,2,2)},C_{(3,2,1)})\text{ is rigid in }S_6.}
$$

As a count check, there are $6\cdot4!=144$ first entries and five eligible second entries for each, giving $720$ tuples. A generating tuple has simultaneous stabilizer equal to the trivial center of $S_6$, so its inner orbit also has size $720$.

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

The PDF has $q$-power exponents; the [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) is a [linearized polynomial over a finite field](../../../polynomial.md#linearized-polynomial-over-a-finite-field), not the degree-$d$ [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) produced by the converted TeX. Let $V$ be its root set in an [algebraic closure](../../../algebra.md#algebraic-closure). Because $(x+y)^{q^i}=x^{q^i}+y^{q^i}$ and $c^{q^i}=c$ for $c\in\mathbb F_q$,

$$
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y),\qquad f(cx)=c f(x).
$$

Thus $V$ is an $\mathbb F_q$-vector space. Its derivative is $a_0\ne0$, and its degree is $q^d$, so it has exactly $q^d$ distinct roots. A finite [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) of that size has dimension $d$.

Every splitting-field automorphism fixes $\mathbb F_q$ and carries roots to roots. Hence it acts linearly on $V$. The action is faithful because the [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) is generated by the roots. Choosing a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) identifies this injection with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(f,\mathbb F_q(t))\hookrightarrow GL(d,q).}
$$

Different bases conjugate the matrix subgroup. The linear action itself is canonical, while a particular matrix realization is basis-dependent.

<h3 id="5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/b)

Put $N=(q^d-1)/(q-1)$, so $N>1$ and $N\equiv1$ in the characteristic. For a nonzero root $x$, division by $x$ gives

$$
x^{q^d-1}+x^{q-1}+t=0.
$$

The projective point $\mathbb F_qx$ is therefore represented by $s=x^{q-1}$, satisfying

$$
P_t(s)=s^N+s+t=0.
$$

Two nonzero roots give the same $s$ exactly when their ratio lies in $\mathbb F_q^\times$. Thus the $N$ projective points are precisely the roots of $P_t$. The [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) $s^N+s+t$ is irreducible in $\mathbb F_q[s,t]$, since it is linear in $t$ and its quotient ring is $\mathbb F_q[s]$. [Gauss's lemma](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry) makes it irreducible over $\mathbb F_q(t)$, so the action on projective points is transitive.

Fix one such coordinate $s$, so $t=-s^N-s$. The other coordinates satisfy

$$
R(Y,s)=\frac{Y^N-s^N}{Y-s}+1=0.
$$

We prove its irreducibility even over $\overline{\mathbb F}_q(s)$. Make the birational change $z=Y/s$, $h=1/s$. Multiplication by $h^{N-1}$ transforms the equation into

$$
h^{N-1}+B(z)=0,\qquad B(z)=\frac{z^N-1}{z-1}.
$$

Choose a nontrivial $N$th [root of unity](../../../algebra.md#root-of-unity) $\zeta$ over the [algebraic closure](../../../algebra.md#algebraic-closure). Since the characteristic does not divide $N$, $B$ has a simple zero at $\zeta$. Viewed as a [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) in $h$ over $\overline{\mathbb F}_q[z]$, $h^{N-1}+B(z)$ is Eisenstein at the prime $z-\zeta$: the leading coefficient is one, every intermediate coefficient is zero, and the constant term is divisible by that prime exactly once. It is therefore irreducible.

The birational substitution preserves irreducibility after inverting $s$ or $h$. No factor supported solely on $s=0$ is lost, because $R(Y,0)=Y^{N-1}+1$ is nonzero. [Gauss's lemma](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#gauss-s-lemma-riemannian-geometry) then proves that $R(Y,s)$ is irreducible over $\overline{\mathbb F}_q(s)$ and hence over $\mathbb F_q(s)$. Its roots are distinct: a repeated root would have $Y^{N-1}+1=0$ as well as $Y^N+Y+t=0$, forcing $t=0$, impossible generically.

The stabilizer of the chosen projective point is $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_q(s))$, and the irreducibility of $R$ makes it transitive on all the remaining projective points. This proves [projective transitivity of a trinomial linearized polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#projective-transitivity-of-a-trinomial-linearized-polynomial), namely two-transitivity on the one-dimensional subspaces. The supplied group-theoretic result now applies to the linear subgroup from part (a), giving

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(X^{q^d}+X^q+tX,\mathbb F_q(t))\ \supseteq\ SL(d,q).}
$$

The Eisenstein argument remains valid even though $N-1$ is divisible by the characteristic; it concerns irreducibility in $h$, not separability of that projection. Separability of the projective [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) has been checked independently.

<h3 id="5/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/c)

Write $d=2n$ with $n\geq2$, and let $E$ be the [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field). The root set of $X^{q^{2n}}+X^{q^2}+tX$ is an $n$-dimensional [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) over $\mathbb F_{q^2}$, by the argument of part (a) with $q$ replaced by $q^2$.

The constants $\mathbb F_{q^2}$ really occur inside $E$, rather than only in the ambient [algebraic closure](../../../algebra.md#algebraic-closure). Choose a nonzero root $\alpha$. For every $c\in\mathbb F_{q^2}$, $c\alpha$ is also a root, so both elements lie in $E$ and

$$
c=\frac{c\alpha}{\alpha}\in E.
$$

Let $K=\mathbb F_{q^2}(t)$. Since $K/\mathbb F_q(t)$ is Galois of degree two,

$$
H=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\triangleleft G,
\qquad\boxed{[G:H]=2.}
$$

This index statement does not require proving that $E$ has no still larger constant [field](../../../algebra.md#field).

As an extension of $K$, $E$ is the [splitting field](../../../galois-theory.md#splitting-field) of the same [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md). Applying part (b) with base constant size $q^2$ and dimension $n$ gives

$$
SL(n,q^2)\leq H\leq GL(n,q^2).
$$

The [special linear group](../../../group-theory.md#special-linear-group) is the kernel of determinant in the [general linear group](../../../group-theory.md#general-linear-group), so every element of $H$ normalizes it. Therefore **$H$ is the required normal index-two subgroup normalizing $SL(d/2,q^2)$**.

In fact the full group is semilinear over $\mathbb F_{q^2}$: an element outside $H$ acts on constants as $c\mapsto c^q$ and satisfies $g(cv)=c^qg(v)$. [Field](../../../algebra.md#field) automorphisms preserve determinant one, so this also explains the [normalizer](../../../group-theory.md#normalizer) behavior geometrically. This is the [quadratic constant extension of a q-squared linearized splitting field](../../../polynomial.md#quadratic-constant-extension-of-a-q-squared-linearized-splitting-field), not the [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) of part (b) with its $X^q$ term unchanged.
