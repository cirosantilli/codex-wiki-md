<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Three independent motivations are **radiative stability, gauge unification and dark matter**. First, [supersymmetric cancellation of quadratic divergences](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-cancellation-of-quadratic-divergences) protects the Higgs [scalar mass](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-mass) from the [hierarchy problem](../../../standard-model.md#hierarchy-problem); residual corrections are controlled by [soft supersymmetry breaking](../../../supersymmetry.md#soft-supersymmetry-breaking), so avoiding large cancellations favours a breaking scale near the weak scale. Second, [supersymmetric gauge coupling unification](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-gauge-coupling-unification) can reconcile the running of the three [Standard Model](../../../standard-model.md) couplings when [superpartner](../../../supersymmetry.md#sparticle) thresholds lie near that scale. Third, with conserved [R-parity](../../../supersymmetry.md#r-parity), a stable [lightest supersymmetric particle](../../../supersymmetry.md#lightest-supersymmetric-particle) can provide [dark matter](../../../cosmology.md#dark-matter), and weak-scale annihilation can produce an appropriate thermal abundance. These are conditional motivations, not proofs of a TeV threshold. Even without low-scale discovery, [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md) remains important **as a structure in supergravity and superstring theory**, potentially broken at a higher scale, and **as an exact calculational tool** through [superpotential non-renormalization](../../../supersymmetry.md#non-renormalization-theorem), [BPS states](../../../supersymmetry.md#bps-state) and control of strongly coupled [quantum field theories](../../../quantum-field-theory.md).

For physical finite-spin [one-particle states](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#one-particle-state), a positive-energy [Poincaré group](../../../special-relativity.md#poincare-group) representation is labelled by mass and [spin](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#spin) $(m,j)$ when $m>0$, or mass and [helicity](../../../special-relativity.md#helicity) $(0,h)$ when massless. The individual states also carry their [four-momentum](../../../special-relativity.md#four-momentum) and a spin projection. For [four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#four-dimensional-n-1-supersymmetry), the irreducible labels become $(m,j_{
m super})$, with $j_{
m super}$ the [superspin](../../../supersymmetry.md#superspin), or $(0,h_{
m super})$, with a chosen [superhelicity](../../../supersymmetry.md#superhelicity) convention. The constituent representation labels are

$$
\boxed{\text{massive: }(m,j),(m,j),(m,j+\tfrac12),(m,j-\tfrac12),}
$$

with the last entry omitted for $j=0$, and

$$
\boxed{\text{massless: }(0,h),(0,h+\tfrac12).}
$$

A required [CPT completion of a supermultiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#cpt-completion-of-a-supermultiplet) adds the conjugate labels $(0,-h),(0,-h-\tfrac12)$ and conjugates any internal charges.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

**Unbroken extended supersymmetry obstructs the observed chiral matter structure.** In four dimensions, the matter [hypermultiplets](../../../supersymmetry.md#hypermultiplet) of $\mathcal N\ge2$ theories contain conjugate [gauge group representations](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-group-representation), so their [fermions](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) are vectorlike rather than the chiral [Weyl fermions](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#weyl-spinor) of the [Standard Model](../../../standard-model.md). [Extended supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#extended-supersymmetry) also relates interactions more strongly, restricting independent [Yukawa couplings](../../../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) and [superpotentials](../../../supersymmetry.md#superpotential). It can occur at higher energies and break to $\mathcal N=1$ or to no [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md); the objection is to its unbroken realization in an ordinary low-energy chiral particle theory.

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

In the **supersymmetric Higgs mechanism**, an internal [gauge symmetry](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-invariance) is broken by a charged [scalar field](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#scalar-field) expectation value while [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md) can remain unbroken. A massless [vector multiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-vector-multiplet) absorbs a [chiral multiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#chiral-multiplet) to form a [massive N=1 vector multiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#massive-n-1-vector-multiplet): the vector absorbs a scalar [Goldstone boson](../../../critical-phenomenon.md#goldstone-boson), and the [gaugino](../../../supersymmetry.md#gaugino) and matter [fermion](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#fermion) acquire the matching masses. In the **super-Higgs mechanism**, local [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md) itself is spontaneously broken: the [gravitino](../../../supersymmetry.md#gravitino) absorbs the spin-$1/2$ [Goldstino](../../../supersymmetry.md#goldstino) and becomes a massive spin-$3/2$ particle. In a flat vacuum this increases its physical states from two to four.

<h3 id="1/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#1/e)

**The supersymmetry algebra closes on spacetime translations.** Its [supercharges](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) are [Weyl spinors](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#weyl-spinor), and

$$
\{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu.
$$

Thus the composition of two odd [supersymmetry transformations](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-transformation) moves the spacetime argument. The generators also change [spin](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#spin) and connect [bosons](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#boson) with [fermions](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#fermion). They extend the [Poincaré group](../../../special-relativity.md#poincare-group) to the [Super-Poincaré group](../../../supersymmetry.md#super-poincare-group), rather than forming an internal symmetry that commutes with spacetime transformations.

<h3 id="1/f">f</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [F](#1/f)

**Dependence on Grassmann variables alone does not specify a superfield transformation law.** A [superfield](../../../supersymmetry.md#superfield) is a function on [superspace](../../../supersymmetry.md#superspace), with components transforming under the induced [supertranslation](../../../supersymmetry.md#supertranslation) and [Lorentz transformation](../../../special-relativity.md#lorentz-transformation) action. Any suitable superfunction can be an unconstrained [superfield](../../../supersymmetry.md#superfield) once equipped with that action; a fixed element of a [Grassmann algebra](../../../linear-algebra.md#grassmann-algebra) with independently assigned components need not be covariant. A [chiral superfield](../../../supersymmetry.md#chiral-superfield), for example,

$$
\Phi(y,\theta)=A(y)+\sqrt2\theta\psi(y)+\theta^2F(y),\qquad y^\mu=x^\mu+i\theta\sigma^\mu\bar\theta,
$$

also obeys $\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi=0$. Keeping only a nonconstant $A(x)$ and forbidding the other components is not a closed [supermultiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#supermultiplet): applying a [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) generates Grassmann-dependent derivative terms. A real unconstrained [superfield](../../../supersymmetry.md#superfield) can instead describe a [vector superfield](../../../supersymmetry.md#vector-superfield).

<h3 id="1/h">h</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/h/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [H](#1/h)

At two derivatives, specify the [gauge group](../../../relativistic-quantum-field.md#gauge-group) and matter representations, a real [Kähler potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#kahler-potential) $K(\Phi,\bar\Phi)$, a holomorphic [superpotential](../../../supersymmetry.md#superpotential) $W(\Phi)$ and a symmetric holomorphic [gauge kinetic function](../../../supersymmetry.md#gauge-kinetic-function) $f_{ab}(\Phi)$. Gauge couplings and topological angles are contained in $f$; global theories can also have allowed [Fayet–Iliopoulos terms](../../../supersymmetry.md#fayet-iliopoulos-term) for Abelian factors. Local theories require the gravitational coupling and consistent gauging: a constant [Fayet–Iliopoulos term](../../../supersymmetry.md#fayet-iliopoulos-term) is not an independent addition to arbitrary [supergravity](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity) data. A constant in $W$ affects [supergravity](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity) although it is irrelevant in the global theory. **$K$ is not holomorphic or protected; $W$ has no perturbative Wilsonian renormalization; holomorphic $f$ is perturbatively one-loop exact.** Nonperturbative corrections can modify $W$ and $f$, and [wave-function renormalization](../../../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#wave-function-renormalization) makes physical couplings run despite [superpotential non-renormalization](../../../supersymmetry.md#non-renormalization-theorem). Higher-derivative actions require further data.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 68](paper-68.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

Choose the [extended supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#extended-supersymmetry) normalization

$$
\{Q^A_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta B}\}=2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu\delta^A{}_B,\qquad
\{Q^A_\alpha,Q^B_\beta\}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z^{AB},\qquad \epsilon_{12}=1.
$$

The [central charge in supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#central-charge-in-supersymmetry) is antisymmetric in $A,B$, because the full [anticommutator](../../../vector-space.md#anticommutator) is symmetric and $\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}$ is antisymmetric. In a massive [one-particle state](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#one-particle-state) rest frame, $P_\mu=(M,0,0,0)$, and the first [anticommutator](../../../vector-space.md#anticommutator) becomes $2M\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^A{}_B$.

A [unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix](../../../linear-algebra.md#unitary-skew-diagonalization-of-an-antisymmetric-matrix) puts $Z$ into blocks $\begin{pmatrix}0&z_r\\-z_r&0\end{pmatrix}$, with a remaining zero direction if $\mathcal N$ is odd. For one block write $z=|z|e^{i\varphi}$, and label its two supersymmetries $1,2$. The combinations

$$
A_\pm=\frac{Q^1_1\pm e^{i\varphi}(Q^2_2)^\dagger}{\sqrt2},\qquad
B_\pm=\frac{Q^1_2\mp e^{i\varphi}(Q^2_1)^\dagger}{\sqrt2}
$$

have

$$
\{A_\pm,A_\pm^\dagger\}=\{B_\pm,B_\pm^\dagger\}=2M\pm|z|.
$$

For example the mixed contribution to the first expression is $\pm(e^{-i\varphi}z+e^{i\varphi}\bar z)/2=\pm|z|$. This is the phase-aligned version of taking a [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) minus a unitary combination of its adjoints, as suggested by the hint.

For every operator $X$ and normalized state $|v\rangle$,

$$
\langle v|\{X,X^\dagger\}|v\rangle=\|Xv\|^2+\|X^\dagger v\|^2\ge0.
$$

Applying this to $A_-,B_-$ in every block proves **the BPS bound**

$$
\boxed{M\ge\frac12\max_r|z_r|.}
$$

If the algebra is instead written with $2Z$ on its right side, the same result is $M\ge\max_r|Z_r|$; the factor is purely a central-charge convention.

A [BPS state](../../../supersymmetry.md#bps-state) saturates at least one of these inequalities. The corresponding zero-norm combinations of [supercharges](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) and their adjoints annihilate the representation, so part of [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md) is preserved. Its [massive supermultiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#massive-supermultiplet) is shortened: each saturated block removes two complex fermionic oscillators, giving $(2j+1)2^{2\mathcal N-2k}$ states from a spin-$j$ [Clifford vacuum](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#clifford-vacuum) when $k$ blocks saturate, before any necessary [CPT completion of a supermultiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#cpt-completion-of-a-supermultiplet). It preserves $4k$ of the $4\mathcal N$ real [supercharges](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator). Its mass is tied to the [central charges in supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#central-charge-in-supersymmetry) and remains protected while it stays on the same short branch. Stability is not unconditional: a [wall of marginal stability](../../../supersymmetry.md#wall-of-marginal-stability) can permit a charged [BPS state](../../../supersymmetry.md#bps-state) to decay into other [BPS states](../../../supersymmetry.md#bps-state) with the same total charge.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

At fixed nonzero [four-momentum](../../../special-relativity.md#four-momentum), take the finite physical [supermultiplet](../../../supersymmetry.md#supermultiplet) and write $P_F=(-1)^F$ for [fermion parity](../../../topological-quantum-matter.md#fermion-parity). Each [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) is odd, so $P_FQ_\alpha=-Q_\alpha P_F$ and the same holds for its adjoint. By the [cyclic property of the trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#cyclic-property-of-the-trace),

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(P_FQ_\alpha Q_\alpha^\dagger)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\alpha^\dagger P_FQ_\alpha)
=-\operatorname{Tr}(P_FQ_\alpha^\dagger Q_\alpha).
$$

Consequently $\operatorname{Tr}[P_F\{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\}]=0$. Sum the two spinor components of the $\mathcal N=1$ [Super-Poincaré algebra](../../../supersymmetry.md#super-poincare-algebra). Since $\operatorname{Tr}_{2\times2}\sigma^i=0$, their sum is $4H$. On a fixed-energy representation, $H=EI$, yielding

$$
0=4E\operatorname{Tr}P_F=4E(n_B-n_F).
$$

For a nonzero physical one-particle momentum, $E>0$, including the massless case. Hence **the bosonic and fermionic physical state counts agree**:

$$
\boxed{n_B=n_F.}
$$

Equivalently a nonzero normalized [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) and its adjoint define an odd [involution](../../../group-theory.md#involution) pairing the two subspaces. This is [supertrace pairing at positive energy](../../../supersymmetry.md#supertrace-pairing-at-positive-energy); it counts spin polarizations, not merely distinct names of particles.

The phrase “every supermultiplet” requires this positive-energy qualification. A zero-energy [supersymmetric vacuum](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetric-vacuum) with all [supercharges](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) acting as zero is a one-dimensional bosonic representation with no fermionic partner. It is a counterexample to the unrestricted statement, because division by $E$ is then impossible. It does not contradict the requested equality for particle [supermultiplets](../../../supersymmetry.md#supermultiplet).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Sum the diagonal spinor entries of the $\mathcal N=1$ [Super-Poincaré algebra](../../../supersymmetry.md#super-poincare-algebra), obtaining

$$
\boxed{H=\frac14\sum_{\alpha=1}^2\{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\}.}
$$

The expectation in any state is therefore

$$
\langle H\rangle=\frac14\sum_\alpha\bigl(\|Q_\alpha|v\rangle\|^2+\|Q_\alpha^\dagger|v\rangle\|^2\bigr)\ge0.
$$

This proves [energy positivity in global supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md#energy-positivity-in-global-supersymmetry), with the energy origin fixed by the algebra.

If a [ground state](../../../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) is invariant under [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md), all its [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) variations vanish and its energy is zero. Conversely, zero energy makes every nonnegative squared norm above zero, so every [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) annihilates that vacuum. Thus **strictly positive vacuum energy implies supersymmetry breaking**: some [supercharge](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-generator) fails to annihilate the vacuum. A finite-volume regulator can be used before passing to vacuum-energy density. This is a statement about the global [supersymmetry algebra](../../../supersymmetry.md#super-poincare-algebra); it is not a positivity theorem for the general [supergravity F-term potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity-f-term-potential).

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 68](paper-68.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Use Planck units and restrict to the physical [Kähler metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-metric) domain

$$
x=S+\bar S>0,\qquad y=T+\bar T-|C|^2>0.
$$

Put $A=ae^{-\alpha S}$ and $w=A+b$, so that $W=C^3+w$. There are no gauged interactions specified, hence no additional [D-term scalar potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#d-term-scalar-potential). The [supergravity F-term potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity-f-term-potential) is $V=e^K(K^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW}-3|W|^2)$, with $e^K=1/(xy^3)$. Its [Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential](../../../supersymmetry.md#kahler-covariant-derivative-of-a-superpotential) are

$$
D_SW=-\alpha A-\frac W x,\qquad D_TW=-\frac{3W}{y},\qquad D_CW=3C^2+\frac{3\bar C W}{y}.
$$

The nonzero metric entries are

$$
K_{S\bar S}=x^{-2},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}K_{T\bar T}&K_{T\bar C}\\K_{C\bar T}&K_{C\bar C}\end{pmatrix}
=\frac3{y^2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-C\\-\bar C&T+\bar T\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The determinant of this $T,C$ block is $9/y^3>0$, so it is positive definite. With the inverse-index convention $K_{i\bar j}K^{k\bar j}=\delta_i^k$, the raised components are

$$
K^{S\bar S}=x^2,\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}K^{T\bar T}&K^{T\bar C}\\K^{C\bar T}&K^{C\bar C}\end{pmatrix}
=\frac y3\begin{pmatrix}T+\bar T&\bar C\\C&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Keeping these complex-conjugate off-diagonal entries in their correct index positions is essential.

To exhibit the [no-scale supergravity](../../../supersymmetry.md#no-scale-supergravity) cancellation, write the $T,C$ norm as

$$
K^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW}\big|_{T,C}
=\frac y3\left[y|D_TW|^2+|D_CW+\bar C D_TW|^2\right]
=3|W|^2+3y|C|^4.
$$

Here $D_CW+\bar C D_TW=3C^2$. The $3|W|^2$ term cancels the negative term in the [supergravity F-term potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity-f-term-potential). The result is **the nonnegative scalar potential**

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{|C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A|^2}{xy^3}+\frac{3|C|^4}{xy^2}.}
$$

This is the [matter-logarithm no-scale potential](../../../supersymmetry.md#matter-logarithm-no-scale-potential), specialized to the exponential [superpotential](../../../supersymmetry.md#superpotential).

Choose the [supergravity auxiliary field](../../../supersymmetry.md#supergravity-auxiliary-field) convention $F^i=-e^{K/2}K^{i\bar j}\overline{D_jW}$. Substitution into the full inverse [Kähler metric](../../../complex-geometry.md#kahler-metric) gives

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F^S&=e^{K/2}x\,\overline{C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A},\\
F^T&=e^{K/2}y\,\overline{A+b},\\
F^C&=-e^{K/2}y\,\bar C^2.
\end{aligned}}
$$

An overall auxiliary-field phase is conventional. The $C$ dependence of $F^T$ cancels between the two metric terms. If all three [auxiliary fields](../../../supersymmetry.md#auxiliary-field) vanished at a finite physical point, $F^C=0$ would force $C=0$, $F^T=0$ would force $A+b=0$, and $F^S=0$ would then force $\alpha x A=0$. Thus $a=b=0$ is necessary. **For any nontrivial choice of $a,b$, supersymmetry is broken at every finite physical point.** The allowed exception $a=b=0$, $C=0$ is supersymmetric; the statement cannot hold for all arbitrary parameters without this qualification.

The [no-scale volume runaway criterion](../../../supersymmetry.md#no-scale-volume-runaway-criterion) also determines whether the minimum is attained. For fixed $S,C$, differentiation with respect to $y$ gives

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}=-\frac{3|C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A|^2}{xy^4}-\frac{6|C|^4}{xy^3}.
$$

It is negative unless both numerators vanish. Therefore a finite stationary vacuum must satisfy

$$
\boxed{C=0,\qquad b+(1+\alpha x)ae^{-\alpha S}=0.}
$$

Every such point is a global minimum with **$V_{\min}=0$**. For $a,b\ne0$, setting $r=|b/a|$ and $z=\alpha x>0$ reduces existence to

$$
r=(1+z)e^{-z/2},\qquad \arg a-\alpha\operatorname{Im}S=\arg(-b)\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

The derivative of the magnitude function is $(1-z)e^{-z/2}/2$. It rises from $1$ at the excluded boundary $z=0$ to $2e^{-1/2}$ at $z=1$, then falls to zero. Consequently **a finite zero-energy minimum exists exactly when**

$$
\boxed{a,b\ne0,\quad 0<|b/a|\le2e^{-1/2},}
$$

or in the separate trivial family $a=b=0$. There is one positive root for $0<r\le1$, two for $1<r<2e^{-1/2}$, and one double root at equality. If only one of $a,b$ vanishes, or $r$ exceeds this maximum, there is no finite minimum: the infimum is zero as $\operatorname{Re}T\to\infty$. Calling this runaway a finite zero-energy vacuum would be incorrect.

At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum, $W=A+b=-\alpha x A\ne0$, and the auxiliary expectation values reduce to

$$
F^S=F^C=0,\qquad F^T=e^{K/2}y\bar W\ne0.
$$

This explicitly verifies [supersymmetry breaking](../../../supersymmetry.md#supersymmetry-breaking) with zero [vacuum energy](../../../perturbative-quantum-field-theory.md#vacuum-energy) in [no-scale supergravity](../../../supersymmetry.md#no-scale-supergravity). Both real components of $T$ are exact [flat directions of a scalar potential](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#flat-direction-of-a-scalar-potential) on the minimum locus. Away from that locus, $\operatorname{Im}T$ is still absent from the potential, but $\operatorname{Re}T$ is not flat. At a minimum, $C$ is lifted at quartic order: at fixed $S,T$, $V=3|C|^4/[x(T+\bar T)^2]+O(|C|^6)$. A zero quadratic mass does not make $C$ an exact flat direction. The [quartic stabilization at a double no-scale root](../../../supersymmetry.md#quartic-stabilization-at-a-double-no-scale-root) likewise gives a vanishing quadratic mass, but no exact flat direction, for $\operatorname{Re}S$ at $z=1$. For $a=b=0$, the whole $C=0$ family has both $S$ and $T$ flat and [supersymmetry](../../../supersymmetry.md) unbroken.

Finally, the [gravitino mass from a superpotential](../../../supersymmetry.md#gravitino-mass-from-a-superpotential) is

$$
\boxed{m_{3/2}=\frac{|C^3+A+b|}{\sqrt{x}\,y^{3/2}}.}
$$

At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum this becomes

$$
\boxed{m_{3/2}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{x}|A|}{y^{3/2}}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{x}|b|}{(1+\alpha x)y^{3/2}}>0.}
$$

It depends on the unfixed volume modulus, so the model does not predict one numerical mass. For $a=b=0$, $C=0$, the [gravitino](../../../supersymmetry.md#gravitino) is massless.

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Use the printed [hypercharge](../../../standard-model.md#hypercharge) convention; reversing every hypercharge relative to another common convention does not change the gauge invariants. Write $U,D,E,N$ for the singlet [chiral superfields](../../../supersymmetry.md#chiral-superfield) labelled with a superscript $R$ in the paper, and define the weak-doublet contraction $A\cdot B=\epsilon_{ab}A^aB^b$. The most general homogeneous [cubic superpotential with right-handed-neutrino superfields](../../../supersymmetry.md#cubic-superpotential-with-right-handed-neutrino-superfields) separates as $W_3=W_{B,L}+W_{\rm viol}$, where

$$
\boxed{W_{B,L}=(y_u)_{ij}(Q_i\cdot H_2)U_j+(y_d)_{ij}(Q_i\cdot H_1)D_j
+(y_e)_{ij}(L_i\cdot H_1)E_j+(y_\nu)_{ij}(L_i\cdot H_2)N_j,}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
W_{\rm viol}={}&\frac12\lambda_{ijk}(L_i\cdot L_j)E_k+\lambda'_{ijk}(L_i\cdot Q_j)D_k\\
&+\frac12\lambda''_{ijk}\epsilon^{abc}U_{ia}D_{jb}D_{kc}
+\eta_iN_i(H_1\cdot H_2)+\frac16\kappa_{ijk}N_iN_jN_k.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Repeated generation and color indices are summed. Because the [chiral superfields](../../../supersymmetry.md#chiral-superfield) commute while the gauge contractions are antisymmetric,

$$
\lambda_{ijk}=-\lambda_{jik},\qquad \lambda''_{ijk}=-\lambda''_{ikj},\qquad \kappa_{ijk}=\kappa_{(ijk)}.
$$

The factors $1/2$ and $1/6$ remove the corresponding overcounting.

Here is a completeness check, not merely a list of possible terms. A color-singlet cubic can have no colored fields, a fundamental–antifundamental pair, or three fundamentals or three antifundamentals. A colored pair must be $QU$ or $QD$: the remaining weak doublet and [hypercharge](../../../standard-model.md#hypercharge) select respectively $H_2$ or $H_1,L$. Three antifundamentals allow only $UDD$ at zero [hypercharge](../../../standard-model.md#hypercharge); three $Q$ doublets have no weak singlet. With no colored fields, weak invariance requires zero or two doublets. Two positive-hypercharge doublets require $E$, giving $LH_1E$ and $LLE$, while $H_1\cdot H_1=0$. Opposite-hypercharge doublets require $N$, giving $LH_2N$ and $H_1H_2N$. With no doublets, only $NNN$ has zero [hypercharge](../../../standard-model.md#hypercharge). These are exactly the nine structures above.

For [baryon number](../../../standard-model.md#baryon-number) and [lepton number](../../../standard-model.md#lepton-number), assign

$$
B(Q)=\tfrac13,\quad B(U)=B(D)=-\tfrac13,\qquad L(L)=1,\quad L(E)=L(N)=-1,
$$

and zero charges to the Higgs fields. This is the conjugate-singlet convention, so the neutrino [Yukawa coupling](../../../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) conserves [lepton number](../../../standard-model.md#lepton-number). The first four terms conserve both charges. The $LLE$ and $LQD$ terms carry $L=1$, the $UDD$ term carries $B=-1$, the $NH_1H_2$ term carries $L=-1$, and $NNN$ carries $L=-3$. The gauge-singlet neutrino interactions must therefore be included in the violating sector.

If “cubic” instead means a polynomial of degree at most three, there are also the lower-degree gauge invariants

$$
W_{\le2}=W_0+t_iN_i+\frac12M_{ij}N_iN_j+\mu H_1\cdot H_2+\mu_iL_i\cdot H_2,\qquad M_{ij}=M_{ji}.
$$

The Higgs mass term conserves both charges; $t_iN_i$, $M_{ij}N_iN_j$ and $\mu_iL_i\cdot H_2$ violate [lepton number](../../../standard-model.md#lepton-number). These additional terms distinguish the full renormalizable [superpotential](../../../supersymmetry.md#superpotential) from its homogeneous cubic part.

To obtain [squark-mediated proton decay](../../../supersymmetry.md#squark-mediated-proton-decay), combine a [lepton number](../../../standard-model.md#lepton-number)-violating $LQD$ vertex with a [baryon number](../../../standard-model.md#baryon-number)-violating $UDD$ vertex. A nonzero example is $\lambda'_{112}$ with $\lambda''_{112}$, exchanging the singlet [strange quark](../../../standard-model.md#strange-quark) [squark](../../../supersymmetry.md#squark) of mass $M_{\widetilde s}$. The antisymmetry forbids $\lambda''_{111}$, so using three first-generation singlets would not be a valid example. The relevant component vertices contain $e_Lu_L\widetilde s^{\,c}$ and $u^cd^c\widetilde s^{\,c}$, with the required color contraction. Connecting one vertex to the complex conjugate of the other through the massive scalar propagator produces schematically

$$
\mathcal L_{\rm eff}\supset\frac{\lambda'_{112}\lambda''^{*}_{112}}{M_{\widetilde s}^2}
\epsilon_{abc}(u_R^a d_R^b)(e_Lu_L^c)+\mathrm{h.c.}
$$

Parentheses denote Lorentz scalar two-spinor contractions, dotted for the right-handed pair and undotted for the left-handed pair. This dimension-six [proton-decay operator](../../../standard-model.md#proton-decay-operator) contains the three quarks $uud$ and an electron field that can create a [Positron](../../../physics.md#positron). Its hadronic matrix element $\langle\pi^0|\epsilon_{abc}(u_R^ad_R^b)u_L^c|p\rangle$ gives the channel $p\to e^+\pi^0$. The internal strange flavor need not appear in the final state. The two interactions together violate both continuous charges, as required for this channel.

The coefficient of the dimension-six [effective operator](../../../quantum-field-theory.md#effective-operator) has mass dimension $-2$. Thus [dimensional analysis](../../../physics.md#dimensional-analysis) gives

$$
\boxed{\Gamma(p\to e^+\pi^0)\sim c_h\,|\lambda'\lambda''|^2\frac{m_p^5}{M_{\widetilde s}^4},\qquad
\tau_p\sim\frac{M_{\widetilde s}^4}{c_h|\lambda'\lambda''|^2m_p^5},}
$$

where $c_h$ is a dimensionless hadronic and phase-space factor. Its detailed value cannot be fixed by dimensional reasoning. Applying the historical lower bound supplied in the paper gives the [proton-lifetime bound on a product of R-parity-violating couplings](../../../supersymmetry.md#proton-lifetime-bound-on-a-product-of-r-parity-violating-couplings)

$$
\boxed{|\lambda'\lambda''|\lesssim\frac{M_{\widetilde s}^2}{\sqrt{c_h\,(2.4\times10^{64}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1})\,m_p^5}}
\simeq\frac{6.5\times10^{-27}}{\sqrt{c_h}}\left(\frac{M_{\widetilde s}}{1\,\mathrm{TeV}}\right)^2\left(\frac{1\,\mathrm{GeV}}{m_p}\right)^{5/2}.}
$$

The numerical coefficient is only the accuracy of the requested dimensional estimate; the squark mass was not fixed by the question.

Finally, [R-parity](../../../supersymmetry.md#r-parity) is $R_p=(-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$. At the superfield level use [matter parity](../../../supersymmetry.md#matter-parity) $P_M=(-1)^{3(B-L)}$: $Q,U,D,L,E,N$ are odd and $H_1,H_2$ are even. Each conserving [Yukawa coupling](../../../standard-model.md#yukawa-interaction) contains two matter [chiral superfields](../../../supersymmetry.md#chiral-superfield) and one Higgs field, so it is even. Every violating homogeneous cubic above is odd: $LLE,LQD,UDD,NNN$ contain three matter fields, while $NH_1H_2$ contains one. **R-parity forbids all the listed cubic violations and preserves the Yukawa terms that generate fermion masses.** The Higgs bilinear is also even, whereas the lepton bilinear $LH_2$ and the singlet linear term are odd.

The final assertion must not be read as forbidding every possible violation of continuous [baryon number](../../../standard-model.md#baryon-number) or [lepton number](../../../standard-model.md#lepton-number). In particular, [matter parity allows Majorana neutrino masses](../../../supersymmetry.md#matter-parity-allows-majorana-neutrino-masses): $N_iN_j$ is even but carries $L=-2$, a concrete counterexample once quadratic terms are included. Higher-dimensional operators such as $QQQL$ and $(LH_2)^2$ are likewise even and violate the continuous charges. The verified statement here concerns the homogeneous cubic sector, including the neutrino singlet terms.
