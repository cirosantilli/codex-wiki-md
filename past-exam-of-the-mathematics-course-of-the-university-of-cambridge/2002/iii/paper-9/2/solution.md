<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Each [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) of a [bipartite graph](../../../../../bipartite-graph.md) has a strict [preference](../../../../../preference-relation.md) ordering of its [neighbours](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md); every [neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) is preferred to being unmatched. A [stable matching in a bipartite graph](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md) is a [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md) with no blocking [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md): an unused [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) blocks if both endpoints prefer each other to their assigned partners, interpreting an unmatched endpoint as willing to accept any [neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md). Stability does not require a perfect [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md).

For existence, run the [Gale-Shapley algorithm](../../../../../gale-shapley-algorithm.md). Whenever a [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in the first part is unmatched and has an untried [neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md), it proposes to its most preferred untried [neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md). A receiver keeps its preferred proposal and rejects the other, including any previously held proposal. At most $e(G)$ proposals occur, so the procedure stops with a [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md). Suppose an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $bg$ blocked this final [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md). Since $b$ prefers $g$ to its final partner, or is unmatched, $b$ must have proposed to $g$. But a receiver's held proposal only improves, so $g$'s final partner is preferred to $b$ if $b$ was rejected. If $b$ was not rejected, the [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is in the [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md). Both alternatives contradict blocking. **Every finite bipartite preference instance therefore has a [stable matching](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md).**

For the [mutual-worst edge in stable matchings](../../../../../mutual-worst-edge-in-stable-matchings.md), let $M,N$ be [stable matchings](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md) with $bg\in M$ and $bg\notin N$. We give the argument using alternating [graph components](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) including possible unmatched endpoints. If $b$ were unmatched in $N$, then $g$ would have to prefer its $N$-partner to $b$, since otherwise $bg$ would block $N$. Following the $N$-edge from $g$, stability of $M$ forces its other endpoint to have and prefer an $M$-partner. Stability of $N$ then forces the next [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) to have and prefer an $N$-partner. These implications continue along the alternating [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) of $M\mathbin\triangle N$. They cannot stop, because at a missing next [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) the preceding [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) would block the appropriate [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md). They also cannot close into a [cycle in a graph](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md), since the [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) already has endpoint $b$. This contradiction proves that $b$ is matched in $N$. The same reasoning applies to $g$.

Now $b$ prefers its $N$-partner to $g$, because $g$ is its worst choice. Along the alternating [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md), stability of $M$ forces that partner to prefer its $M$-partner; stability of $N$ forces the next [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in $b$'s part to prefer its $N$-partner, and so on. A [graph path](../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) is impossible by the same endpoint argument, so this is a [cycle in a graph](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). Thus throughout this [cycle in a graph](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md) the part containing $b$ prefers $N$ and the part containing $g$ prefers $M$. In particular $g$ must prefer $b$ to its $N$-partner. That contradicts $b$ being $g$'s worst choice. Hence **an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) ranked last by both endpoints, if present in one [stable matching](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md), is present in every [stable matching](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md)**.

For the [list edge coloring](../../../../../list-edge-coloring.md) assertion, let $\Delta$ be the [maximum degree](../../../../../maximum-degree.md). We first prove that the [chromatic index](../../../../../chromatic-index.md) is $\Delta$. Use [induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on the number of [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), with a proper $\Delta$-[edge coloring](../../../../../edge-coloring.md) of $G-uv$. Some color $a$ is missing at $u$ and some color $b$ is missing at $v$. If $a=b$, color $uv$ with it. Otherwise the [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of colors $a,b$ form alternating [graph paths](../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) and even [cycles in a graph](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). The [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) containing $u$ cannot contain $v$: an alternating [graph path](../../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) from $u$, starting with color $b$, would end at $v$ with color $a$, have even length, and place $u,v$ in the same bipartition class. Swap $a,b$ in the [graph component](../../../../../component-graph-theory.md) containing $u$. Now $b$ is missing at both ends of $uv$, so it can be colored $b$. The lower bound $\chi'(G)\ge\Delta$ is immediate at a [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) of [vertex degree](../../../../../degree-graph-theory.md) $\Delta$.

Fix such a proper [coloring](../../../../../colouring-of-a-set.md) $c:E(G)\to\{1,\ldots,\Delta\}$. Orient the [line graph](../../../../../line-graph.md) toward the smaller color at [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in the first part of $G$, and toward the larger color at [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in the second part. A [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) of the [line graph](../../../../../line-graph.md) corresponding to an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of color $j$ has [outdegree](../../../../../outdegree.md) at most

$$
(j-1)+(\Delta-j)=\Delta-1.
$$

Every [induced subgraph](../../../../../induced-subgraph.md) of this orientation has a [digraph kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-directed-graph.md). Indeed, restrict to its [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) [set](../../../../../set-split.md) in the original [bipartite graph](../../../../../bipartite-graph.md), give first-part [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) a preference for smaller colors and second-part [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) a preference for larger colors, and choose a [stable matching](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md). Its [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) form an [independent set](../../../../../independent-set-graph-theory.md) in the [line graph](../../../../../line-graph.md). Every [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) outside the [matching in a graph](../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md) has an endpoint whose partner is preferred to it, by stability; thus it has an outgoing [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) to the [independent set](../../../../../independent-set-graph-theory.md). This proves that the orientation is a [kernel-perfect directed graph](../../../../../kernel-perfect-directed-graph.md).

Here is the [kernel list-coloring lemma](../../../../../kernel-list-coloring-lemma.md) with its proof. If a [kernel-perfect directed graph](../../../../../kernel-perfect-directed-graph.md) $D$ has lists satisfying $|L(v)|\ge d_D^+(v)+1$, choose any color $a$ appearing in a list and a [digraph kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-directed-graph.md) $K$ of the [induced subgraph](../../../../../induced-subgraph.md) on [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) whose lists contain $a$. Color $K$ with $a$, delete $K$, and delete $a$ from the remaining lists. Each remaining [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) that loses a color had an outgoing [neighbour](../../../../../neighbour-of-a-vertex.md) in $K$, so its [outdegree](../../../../../outdegree.md) decreases by at least one; the list inequality persists. [Vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) not losing a color also retain the inequality. [Induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md) on the number of [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) completes a proper list [coloring](../../../../../colouring-of-a-set.md).

Lists of size $\Delta$ satisfy that lemma for our oriented [line graph](../../../../../line-graph.md), so $\chi'_\ell(G)\le\Delta$. Identical lists show $\chi'_\ell(G)\ge\chi'(G)$. Consequently the [Galvin theorem for bipartite list edge coloring](../../../../../galvin-theorem-for-bipartite-list-edge-coloring.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\chi'_\ell(G)=\chi'(G)=\Delta(G).}
$$

The empty [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is immediate; if parallel [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) are allowed, orient their line-graph adjacency in both directions when the two endpoints prescribe opposite directions. The [outdegree](../../../../../outdegree.md) bound still holds, and the same [stable matching](../../../../../stable-matching-in-a-bipartite-graph.md) and [digraph kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-directed-graph.md) proof applies with preferences on incident [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md).
