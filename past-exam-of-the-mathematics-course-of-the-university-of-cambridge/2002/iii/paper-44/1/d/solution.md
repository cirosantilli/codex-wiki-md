<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At rest with all separations initially infinite, both initial kinetic and gravitational energies vanish, so the conserved total energy is zero. During an isolated collapse, $T+W=0$, rather than the equilibrium relation $2T+W=0$. Hence at the stated instantaneous [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md),

$$
\boxed{\langle v_{\rm coll}^2\rangle
=\frac{-2W}{Nm}=\frac{2GNm}{R_g}
=2\langle v_{\rm eq}^2\rangle.}
$$

The rms collapse speed is $\sqrt2$ times the rms equilibrium speed for the same instantaneous $W$ and mass. For a perfectly radial cold infall this mean-square motion includes coherent inward streaming; it need not equal a local random [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) after subtracting that streaming. The quoted comparison follows the question's global mean-square convention.
