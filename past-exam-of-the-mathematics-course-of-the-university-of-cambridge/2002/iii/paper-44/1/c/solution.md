<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Take the [gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) to vanish at infinity. At the position of star $i$, excluding its own point-mass field, the instantaneous test-particle [escape velocity](../../../../../../escape-velocity.md) obeys $v_{{\rm esc},i}^2=-2\Phi_i$. The factor of one half in the total [gravitational potential energy](../../../../../../gravitational-energy.md) is important:

$$
W=\frac12\sum_i m\Phi_i.
$$

Therefore the equal-mass average is

$$
\boxed{\langle v_{\rm esc}^2\rangle
=-\frac2N\sum_i\Phi_i=-\frac{4W}{Nm}
=\frac{4GNm}{R_g}=4\langle v_{\rm eq}^2\rangle.}
$$

Thus the rms escape speed is twice the equilibrium rms speed. This is the [virial mean escape speed](../../../../../../virial-mean-escape-speed.md) relation, using the same three-dimensional averaging convention as the preceding part. It concerns escape in the instantaneous system potential, rather than a subsequent collective rearrangement of the cluster.
