<h1 id="18d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The attractive potential has $\Delta>0$. Its time-independent [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) is

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M}\chi''(x)-\frac{\hbar^2}{2M\Delta}\delta(x)\chi(x)=E\chi(x).
$$

Integrate from $-\varepsilon$ to $\varepsilon$. [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) makes the delta term equal to $-\hbar^2\chi(0)/(2M\Delta)$, while the integral of $E\chi$ tends to zero as the interval shrinks. The second-derivative term integrates to the difference of first derivatives. Hence

$$
\boxed{\chi'(0^+)-\chi'(0^-)=-\frac{\chi(0)}{\Delta}}.
$$

This expresses the point interaction through a derivative jump while the [wavefunction](../../../../../../wave-function.md) remains continuous.
