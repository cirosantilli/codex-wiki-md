<h1 id="14g/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Take $V=\mathbb R^3$, $\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0)$ and $W=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)$. Then $W$ is a proper [invariant subspace](../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md), and the restriction has matrix $\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$. In each case $X(X-1)$ annihilates the operator, while any annihilator must vanish at both zero and one. Consequently

$$
\boxed{m_\alpha(X)=m_\beta(X)=X(X-1),\qquad\deg m_\alpha=2>1}.
$$

This same example works over any scalar field.
