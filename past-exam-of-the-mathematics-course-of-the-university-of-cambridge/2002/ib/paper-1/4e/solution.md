<h1 id="4e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4E](../4e.md)

Let $G$ be the [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) and choose a point $p$. Its orbit $E=\{gp:g\in G\}$ is a nonempty finite set. Every group element permutes $E$ and maps a closed metric disc onto a disc of the same radius. By the permitted uniqueness of the smallest enclosing disc, it fixes that disc and therefore its centre $o$. Thus $G$ lies in the orientation-preserving stabilizer of one point.

In the [Euclidean plane](../../../../../euclidean-plane.md), translate $o$ to zero. Every [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md) fixing zero is an orthogonal [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md): distance preservation gives preservation of inner products by the [polarization identity](../../../../../polarization-identity.md), and its values on an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) then determine the [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md). Orientation preservation gives [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one, so it is a [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md). Consequently $G$ embeds in the circle group of [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) angles.

For the [hyperbolic plane](../../../../../hyperbolic-plane.md), use this precise standard fact about its [isometries](../../../../../isometry.md): after placing a point at the origin of the [Poincare disc model](../../../../../poincare-disk-model.md), every orientation-preserving [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md) fixing it has the form $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$. Equivalently, its point stabilizer is the [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) group, and its action on directions at the point is faithful. This is a general hyperbolic-plane fact allowed by the question. It again identifies $G$ with a finite [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of the circle group.

Finally, a nontrivial finite [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) group has a least positive angle $\theta$ in $[0,2\pi)$. Divide any other angle $\phi$ by $\theta$: the remainder $\phi-k\theta$ is another group angle in $[0,\theta)$, so it must be zero. Similarly the remainder of $2\pi$ is zero, and $\theta=2\pi/m$ for an integer $m$. The [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) through $\theta$ generates the group. The trivial group is cyclic as well. Therefore **both finite orientation-preserving plane [isometry](../../../../../isometry.md) groups are cyclic**. This proves the [finite orientation-preserving plane isometry groups are cyclic](../../../../../finite-orientation-preserving-plane-isometry-groups-are-cyclic.md) result using the same enclosing-disc argument in both geometries.
