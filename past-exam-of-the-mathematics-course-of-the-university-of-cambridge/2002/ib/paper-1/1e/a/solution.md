**No.** Consider the triangular functions $f_n(x)=\max(1-n|x|,0)$. The absolute-value and positive-part maps are [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md), so $|f_n(x)-f_n(y)|\leq n|x-y|$. Each $f_n$ is therefore [uniformly continuous](../../../../../../uniform-continuity.md) on the whole real line. For $x=0$ its value is always one; for every fixed $x\ne0$ it eventually vanishes. Hence its [pointwise limit](../../../../../../pointwise-limit.md) is

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x=0,\\0,&x\ne0.\end{cases}
$$

This limit is not [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at zero: along $x_j=1/j\to0$, $f(x_j)=0\not\to1=f(0)$. Individual [uniform continuity](../../../../../../uniform-continuity.md) does not provide a modulus uniform in $n$.
