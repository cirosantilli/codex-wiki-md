<h1 id="16b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let two regular differentiable paths through $z_0$ have nonzero tangent vectors $v_1,v_2$. The chain rule gives tangent vectors $f'(z_0)v_1$ and $f'(z_0)v_2$ for their images. Multiplication by a nonzero complex number is a positive scaling and [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md): writing $f'(z_0)=re^{i\theta}$ scales lengths by $r>0$ and adds the same angle $\theta$ to both tangents. Equivalently,

$$
\arg\frac{f'(z_0)v_2}{f'(z_0)v_1}=\arg\frac{v_2}{v_1}.
$$

Thus the oriented angle between the paths is preserved, proving **$f$ is conformal at $z_0$**. The nonzero derivative also gives local invertibility; a zero derivative could multiply angles through branching instead.
