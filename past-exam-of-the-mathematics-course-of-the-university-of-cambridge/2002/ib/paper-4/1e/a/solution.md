↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**Neither assertion holds for a general [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md).** Take $X=\{0,1\}$ with $d(0,1)=1$ and center $0$. This is a [discrete topology](../../../../../../discrete-space.md): both singleton sets are open and closed. The [open ball](../../../../../../open-ball.md) is $U=\{0\}$, whereas the [closed ball](../../../../../../closed-ball.md) is $K=X$. Thus the [interior](../../../../../../interior-topology.md) of $K$ is $X$, strictly larger than $U$, and the [closure](../../../../../../closure-topology.md) of $U$ is $\{0\}$, strictly smaller than $K$. In general $U\subseteq\operatorname{int}K$ and $\overline U\subseteq K$, but the inclusions need not be equalities.
