<h1 id="1e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Both assertions hold for a [normed vector space](../../../../../../normed-vector-space.md).** The [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) is continuous, so $U$ is open and $K$ is closed. If $\|x\|=1$, then $x_n=(1+1/n)x$ lies outside $K$ and converges to $x$. Hence no such boundary point belongs to the [interior](../../../../../../interior-topology.md) of $K$, giving $\operatorname{int}K=U$. Conversely, $(1-1/n)x\in U$ converges to every unit vector $x$, so $K\subseteq\overline U$. Closedness gives the reverse inclusion, and $\overline U=K$. Points already in $U$ cause no difficulty. In the zero-dimensional space $U=K=X$, so the conclusions still hold. This is the [interior and closure of norm balls](../../../../../../interior-and-closure-of-norm-balls.md); radial scaling supplies what a general [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) lacks.
