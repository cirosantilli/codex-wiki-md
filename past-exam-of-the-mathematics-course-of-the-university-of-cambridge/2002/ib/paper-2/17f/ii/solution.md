<h1 id="17f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For any complex $\lambda$, the shifted [linear operator](../../../../../../linear-operator.md) $T=\alpha-\lambda I$ is normal: expansion of $T^*T-TT^*$ leaves just $\alpha^*\alpha-\alpha\alpha^*=0$. Apply part (i) to this shifted [normal operator](../../../../../../normal-operator.md):

$$
\ker(\alpha-\lambda I)=\ker(\alpha^*-\bar\lambda I).
$$

Hence a nonzero vector is an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $\alpha$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ if and only if it is an [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) of $\alpha^*$ with [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\bar\lambda$. **The eigenspaces coincide, with conjugate eigenvalues.**
