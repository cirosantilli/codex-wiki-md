<h1 id="10e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [contraction mapping theorem](../../../../../../contraction-mapping-theorem.md) says that a self-map $g$ of a nonempty [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md) satisfying $d(gx,gy)\leq k d(x,y)$ for a constant $0\leq k<1$ has exactly one [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md). Apply it to $g=f^N$ to obtain $p$ with $f^Np=p$.

No continuity of $f$ itself is needed: composition gives

$$
f^N(fp)=f(f^Np)=fp.
$$

Thus $fp$ is also a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) of $f^N$, whose uniqueness forces $fp=p$. Conversely any [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) of $f$ is fixed by $f^N$, so it must equal $p$. **The unique fixed point of the contracting iterate is also the unique fixed point of $f$.** The usual nonempty-space hypothesis is necessary; an empty space has no fixed point.
