<h1 id="10e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $K=\overline{O(x)}$. It is nonempty, closed and bounded in finite-dimensional [Euclidean space](../../../../../../euclidean-norm.md), hence compact by the [Heine-Borel theorem](../../../../../../heine-borel-theorem.md). The strict distance inequality again makes $f$ [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md). If $y\in K$, choose a sequence $y_j\in O(x)$ converging to $y$. Then $fy_j\in O(x)$, and continuity gives $fy_j\to fy$, so $fy\in K$. This proves **$f(K)\subseteq K$**.

The restriction to $K$ satisfies [strict distance decrease on a compact metric space](../../../../../../strict-distance-decrease-on-a-compact-metric-space.md), so part (b) produces a [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) there. If a second [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) existed anywhere in $\mathbb R^n$, the same strict distance inequality applied to the two points would contradict equality of their distance. **Therefore the fixed point is unique in all of $\mathbb R^n$.** This is the [bounded-orbit fixed point for a strictly distance-decreasing Euclidean map](../../../../../../bounded-orbit-fixed-point-for-a-strictly-distance-decreasing-euclidean-map.md).
