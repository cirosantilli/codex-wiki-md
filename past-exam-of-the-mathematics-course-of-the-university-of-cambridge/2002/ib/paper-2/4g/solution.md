<h1 id="4g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4G](../4g.md)

Use the [one-sided product bound for an entire function](../../../../../one-sided-product-bound-for-an-entire-function.md) by considering

$$
F(z)=\exp[-if(z)^2].
$$

This is an [entire function](../../../../../entire-function.md), and $f^2=(u^2-v^2)+2iuv$ gives $|F|=e^{2uv}$. If $uv\leq M$, then $|F|\leq e^{2M}$ everywhere. The [Liouville theorem](../../../../../liouville-theorem.md) therefore makes $F$ constant.

Because an exponential never vanishes, differentiating yields $0=F'=-2iff'F$, and hence $(f^2)'=2ff'=0$. Thus $f^2$ is constant. If that constant is zero, $f=0$; otherwise $f$ takes values in the two square roots of one nonzero constant. Continuity on the [connected space](../../../../../connected-space.md) $\mathbb C$ prevents switching between those two values. **Therefore $f$ is constant.**
