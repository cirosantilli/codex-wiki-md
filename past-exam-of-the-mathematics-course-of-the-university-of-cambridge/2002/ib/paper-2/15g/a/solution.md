<h1 id="15g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

If $U$ is a [unipotent matrix](../../../../../../unipotent-matrix.md), $N=U-I$ is a [nilpotent matrix](../../../../../../nilpotent-matrix.md). If $Uv=\lambda v$ for a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md), then $N^mv=(\lambda-1)^mv=0$ for some $m$, forcing $\lambda=1$.

Conversely, suppose the only [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is $1$. Over $\mathbb C$, the [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) is then $(t-1)^n$. The [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md) gives $(U-I)^n=0$, so $U-I$ is nilpotent. **Thus $U$ is unipotent if and only if its only eigenvalue is one.**
