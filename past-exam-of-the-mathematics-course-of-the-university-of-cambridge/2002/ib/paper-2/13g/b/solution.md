<h1 id="13g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [lexicographic order](../../../../../../lexicographic-order.md) is a [total order](../../../../../../total-order.md). Each point $p=(a,b)$ lies in the order interval between $(a,b-1)$ and $(a,b+1)$, so the order intervals cover $\mathbb R^2$.

If $p$ lies in both $\langle x_1,y_1\rangle$ and $\langle x_2,y_2\rangle$, let $x=\max(x_1,x_2)$ and $y=\min(y_1,y_2)$ in that [total order](../../../../../../total-order.md). Then $x<p<y$ and

$$
\langle x_1,y_1\rangle\cap\langle x_2,y_2\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle.
$$

In particular an order interval contains $p$ and is contained in the intersection. Both conditions of part (a) hold, proving that these intervals form a [basis of a topology](../../../../../../basis-of-a-topology.md). **The resulting topology is the [lexicographic order topology on the real plane](../../../../../../lexicographic-order-topology-on-the-real-plane.md).**
