<h1 id="7f/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**True**. Choose a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ of $U$ and extend it to a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) $u_1,\ldots,u_r,w_1,\ldots,w_s$ of the finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $V$. Put $W=\operatorname{span}(w_1,\ldots,w_s)$. Every [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) vector belongs to $U+W$, so $U+W=V$. [Linear independence](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) of the complete [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) makes a vector belonging to both spans necessarily zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{U\cap W=\{0\},\qquad V=U\oplus W}.
$$

This constructs a [direct-sum complement](../../../../../../direct-sum-complement.md); it need not be unique.
