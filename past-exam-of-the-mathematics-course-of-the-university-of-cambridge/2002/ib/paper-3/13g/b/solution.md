<h1 id="13g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The function is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk. On $|z|=1$, the mapping assumption gives $|g(z)|<1=|z|$. Apply [Rouché's theorem](../../../../../../rouche-s-theorem.md) to $z$ and $-g(z)$. Their sum $z-g(z)$ has the same number of interior zeros as $z$, namely one counting [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{g\text{ has exactly one fixed point in }|z|<1}.
$$

The zero is simple since its total [multiplicity](../../../../../../multiplicity-mathematics.md) is one. No boundary [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) is possible because $g$ maps the boundary strictly inside.
