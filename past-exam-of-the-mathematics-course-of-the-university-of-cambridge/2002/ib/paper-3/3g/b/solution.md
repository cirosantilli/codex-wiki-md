<h1 id="3g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The map $h:X\to X\times Y$, $h(x)=(x,f(x))$, is continuous: the inverse image of a basic open set $U\times V$ is $U\cap f^{-1}(V)$. Its image is exactly the [graph of a function](../../../../../../graph-of-a-function.md) $G_f$. The continuous image of a [compact space](../../../../../../compact-space.md) is compact, so

$$
\boxed{G_f=h(X)\text{ is compact}}.
$$

The [compact graph of a continuous map](../../../../../../compact-graph-of-a-continuous-map.md) conclusion does not need $Y$ to be Hausdorff. In fact projection onto $X$ is a continuous inverse of $h:X\to G_f$, so the graph is homeomorphic to $X$.
