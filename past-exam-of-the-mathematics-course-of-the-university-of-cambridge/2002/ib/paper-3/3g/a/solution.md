<h1 id="3g/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take $(x_0,y_0)$ outside the [graph of a function](../../../../../../graph-of-a-function.md), so $y_0\ne f(x_0)$. Since $Y$ is a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), choose disjoint open neighborhoods $V$ of $y_0$ and $W$ of $f(x_0)$. [Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) makes $U=f^{-1}(W)$ an open neighborhood of $x_0$. The product neighborhood $U\times V$ contains $(x_0,y_0)$ and cannot meet the graph, because every $f(x)$ with $x\in U$ belongs to $W$. The complement is therefore open in the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md), proving

$$
\boxed{G_f\text{ is closed in }X\times Y}.
$$

This is the [closed graph of a map into a Hausdorff space](../../../../../../closed-graph-of-a-map-into-a-hausdorff-space.md) property; no separation assumption on $X$ is required.
