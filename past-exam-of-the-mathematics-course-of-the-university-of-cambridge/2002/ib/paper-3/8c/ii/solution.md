<h1 id="8c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

An enclosing loop has no spanning disk wholly inside the fluid, so initial zero [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md) does not force its [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) to vanish. [Kelvin's circulation theorem](../../../../../../kelvin-s-circulation-theorem.md) says only that the initial [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) is retained:

$$
\boxed{\Gamma(C(t))=\Gamma(C(0)),\quad\text{possibly nonzero}}.
$$

For example $\mathbf u=\Gamma_0\mathbf e_\theta/(2\pi r)$ has zero [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md) outside the cylinder but [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) $\Gamma_0$ around a once-enclosing positively oriented loop. Any two such loops have equal [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md), since their difference bounds an annulus in the fluid and [Stokes theorem](../../../../../../stokes-theorem.md) gives zero over it. More generally a loop with winding number $m$ has [circulation](../../../../../../circulation-physics.md) $m\Gamma_0$. Thus [irrotational flow](../../../../../../irrotational-flow.md) need not have a single-valued velocity potential on the whole exterior domain.
