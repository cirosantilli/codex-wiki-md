<h1 id="12a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In [Cartesian coordinates](../../../../../../cartesian-coordinate-system.md), put $r=(x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2)^{1/2}$. Away from the origin the [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) of $\psi=1/r$ are

$$
\partial_i\psi=-\frac{x_i}{r^3},\qquad
\partial_i^2\psi=-\frac1{r^3}+\frac{3x_i^2}{r^5}.
$$

Summing the three second [partial derivatives](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) yields

$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\psi=-\frac3{r^3}+\frac{3(x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2)}{r^5}=0\qquad(r>0).}
$$

Thus $1/r$ is a [harmonic function](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md) on the punctured space and solves [Laplace equation](../../../../../../laplace-equation.md) there. The origin is excluded: the singularity cannot be treated as an ordinary harmonic point.
