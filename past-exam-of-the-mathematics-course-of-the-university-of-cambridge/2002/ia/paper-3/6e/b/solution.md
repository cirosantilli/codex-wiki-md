<h1 id="6e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Translate one vertex of the [tetrahedron](../../../../../../tetrahedron.md) to the origin and denote the other vertex vectors by $a,b,c$. Translation preserves edge vectors and their [dot products](../../../../../../dot-product.md). We may label the two known perpendicular pairs of [opposite edges of a tetrahedron](../../../../../../opposite-edges-of-a-tetrahedron.md) as the pairs represented by $a,c-b$ and $b,a-c$. Their orthogonality gives

$$
a\cdot c=a\cdot b,\qquad b\cdot a=b\cdot c.
$$

Hence all three pairwise [dot products](../../../../../../dot-product.md) have the same value $s$. The third pair satisfies

$$
c\cdot(b-a)=c\cdot b-c\cdot a=s-s=0,
$$

so **the third pair of opposite edges is also perpendicular**.

Expanding squared lengths using the [dot product](../../../../../../dot-product.md) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
|a|^2+|c-b|^2
&=|b|^2+|a-c|^2\\
&=|c|^2+|b-a|^2\\
&=|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2-2s.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus **the sums of squared opposite-edge lengths are equal**. These two conclusions are the basic identities for [orthogonal opposite edges of a tetrahedron](../../../../../../orthogonal-opposite-edges-of-a-tetrahedron.md).
