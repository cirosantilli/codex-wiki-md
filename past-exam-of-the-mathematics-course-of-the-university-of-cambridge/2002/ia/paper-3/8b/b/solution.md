<h1 id="8b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take the half-turn about the $z$-axis, represented by the [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md)

$$
\boxed{M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

It is an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md) with [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $1$, and it fixes the $z$-axis while reversing both perpendicular coordinates. Direct substitution gives $Ma=b$, $Mb=a$, $Mc=d$ and $Md=c$. A [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) that permutes the four vertices also preserves their [convex hull](../../../../../../convex-hull.md), which is the solid [tetrahedron](../../../../../../tetrahedron.md). Hence this [rotation matrix](../../../../../../rotation-matrix.md) leaves $T$ invariant. Its axis joins the midpoints of the two opposite edges $ab$ and $cd$.
