↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $G$ is a [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) if some element $g\in G$ generates it:

$$
G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}.
$$

For a finite [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md), the [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) of a generator equals the number of elements of the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md).

An example of a finite [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) that is not a [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is the [Klein four-group](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md), realized as

$$
G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2
$$

with componentwise addition modulo two. Addition commutes, so this is an [abelian group](../../../../../../abelian-group.md) of four elements. Each of its three nonzero elements satisfies $g+g=0$, so the [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) it generates has just two elements; the identity generates only itself. **No element generates all four elements.**
