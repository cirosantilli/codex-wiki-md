<h1 id="3a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

To construct a potential for the [exact differential](../../../../../../exact-differential.md), first integrate its $dx$ coefficient with respect to $x$:

$$
F=x^3(y^2+1)+g(y,z).
$$

Matching the $dy$ coefficient gives $g_y=-2z^2$, hence $g=-2yz^2+h(z)$. The remaining [partial derivative](../../../../../../partial-derivative.md) is then

$$
F_z=-4yz+h'(z).
$$

Matching the $dz$ coefficient forces $h'=0$. Therefore a potential, valid throughout $\mathbb R^3$, is

$$
\boxed{F(x,y,z)=x^3(y^2+1)-2yz^2+C.}
$$

Differentiating this expression recovers all three coefficients, proving that the [differential form](../../../../../../differential-form-split.md) really is an [exact differential](../../../../../../exact-differential.md).
