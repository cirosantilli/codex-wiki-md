<h1 id="5b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the same triangle and let $G$ consist of [translations](../../../../../../translation-geometry.md) $x\mapsto x+m$ for $m\in\mathbb Z^2$. All its vertices have [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) coordinates, and one vertex is the origin, so

$$
S=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb Z^2}(m+T)=\mathbb Z^2.
$$

The [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) through a quarter-turn $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$ preserves this [integer lattice](../../../../../../integer-lattice.md), and hence belongs to $H$. It fixes the origin and is not the identity, whereas the only [translation](../../../../../../translation-geometry.md) fixing the origin is the identity. It is therefore outside $G$, proving **$G\subsetneq H$**. This is [symmetry enlargement under group translates](../../../../../../symmetry-enlargement-under-group-translates.md) even though $T$ itself has trivial [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md).
