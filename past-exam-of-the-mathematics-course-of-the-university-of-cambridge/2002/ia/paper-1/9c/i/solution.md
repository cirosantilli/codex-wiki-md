<h1 id="9c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The positive terms $b_n=1/\log(n+1)$ decrease to zero. The [alternating series test](../../../../../../alternating-series-test.md) just proved therefore gives **convergence** of the signed [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md). It is [conditional convergence](../../../../../../conditional-convergence.md): $\log(n+1)\le n$, so $b_n\ge1/n$, and the absolute-value [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) dominates the divergent [harmonic series](../../../../../../harmonic-series.md).

The [comparison test for series](../../../../../../comparison-test-for-series.md) used here says that if $0\le c_n\le d_n$, convergence of $\sum d_n$ implies convergence of $\sum c_n$, while divergence of $\sum c_n$ implies divergence of $\sum d_n$. This follows by comparing increasing [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md). For completeness, the [harmonic series](../../../../../../harmonic-series.md) diverges because every block from $2^k$ to $2^{k+1}-1$ has sum at least $1/2$.
