<h1 id="9c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The absolute-value [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) can be grouped in pairs of nonnegative terms:

$$
\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl(|a_{2n-1}|+|a_{2n}|\bigr)
=\frac54\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}<\infty.
$$

Indeed, $1/n^2\le\int_{n-1}^{n}x^{-2}\,dx$ for $n\ge2$, so these positive [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) are bounded by two. [Completeness of the real numbers](../../../../../../completeness-of-the-real-numbers.md) proves their convergence. The [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) bounds every late signed sum by the corresponding late absolute-value sum, so [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md) implies convergence. Thus **the [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges absolutely**.
