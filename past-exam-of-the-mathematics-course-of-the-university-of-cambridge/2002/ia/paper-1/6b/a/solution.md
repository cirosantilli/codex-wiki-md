<h1 id="6b/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) as $g(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d)$, with $ad-bc\ne0$, on the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../../riemann-sphere.md). If $c\ne0$, infinity is not fixed, and its finite [fixed points](../../../../../../fixed-point.md) solve

$$
cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0.
$$

The pole $-d/c$ cannot solve this equation, since substitution gives $(ad-bc)/c\ne0$. The quadratic has one or two distinct complex roots, so these give exactly one or two [fixed points](../../../../../../fixed-point.md).

If $c=0$, then $a,d\ne0$ and infinity is fixed. If $a\ne d$, there is one additional finite [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md), namely $b/(d-a)$. If $a=d$ and $b\ne0$, the transformation is a nonzero [translation](../../../../../../translation-geometry.md), with no finite [fixed point](../../../../../../fixed-point.md). If $a=d$ and $b=0$, it is the identity and fixes the entire sphere.

Thus **a nonidentity [Möbius transformation](../../../../../../mobius-transformation.md) has one or two [fixed points](../../../../../../fixed-point.md); the identity has infinitely many**. The examples $z\mapsto z+1$, $z\mapsto2z$, and $z\mapsto z$ realize the three possibilities respectively.
