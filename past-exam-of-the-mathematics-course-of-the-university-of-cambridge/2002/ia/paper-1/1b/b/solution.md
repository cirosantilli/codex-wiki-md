<h1 id="1b/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Place the cube vertices at $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$. The four vertices whose coordinate product is $+1$ form $T$; those with product $-1$ form $T'$. Within either [set](../../../../../../set-split.md), two distinct vertices differ in exactly two coordinates, so every edge has length $2\sqrt2$ and each [set](../../../../../../set-split.md) is a [regular tetrahedron](../../../../../../regular-tetrahedron.md).

A member of the [cube rotation group](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry-group-of-a-cube.md) is a [signed permutation matrix](../../../../../../signed-permutation-matrix.md) of [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) one. There are $3!$ permutations of the coordinate axes and four choices of signs for each permutation that give [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) one, so $|G|=24$. Such a [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) maps a coordinate-product class to a coordinate-product class. Moreover, the [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) through a quarter-turn $(x,y,z)\mapsto(-y,x,z)$ interchanges the classes. Thus the [orbit](../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md) of $T$ consists of exactly $T,T'$. By the [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md), the [tetrahedron stabilizer in the cube rotation group](../../../../../../tetrahedron-stabilizer-in-the-cube-rotation-group.md) has order

$$
\boxed{|G_T|=24/2=12.}
$$
