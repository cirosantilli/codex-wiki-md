<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $m=\dim X$ be odd. Use [mod-two Poincare duality](../../../../../../mod-two-poincare-duality.md), which applies even when the [closed manifold](../../../../../../closed-manifold.md) is nonorientable. It gives $b_k^{(2)}=b_{m-k}^{(2)}$, where $b_k^{(2)}=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_k(X;\mathbb F_2)$. The [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) can be computed with any field of coefficients: for a finite cell model, the alternating sum of chain dimensions equals the alternating sum of [homology](../../../../../../homology-split.md) dimensions, and the former counts cells independently of the field.

Consequently $\chi(X)=\sum_{k=0}^m(-1)^k b_k^{(2)}$. Pair each term with index $m-k$. The paired [Betti numbers](../../../../../../betti-number.md) are equal and their signs opposite because $m$ is odd; there is no unpaired middle index. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\chi(X)=0.}
$$

This argument applies componentwise if the manifold is disconnected and does not require an orientation.
