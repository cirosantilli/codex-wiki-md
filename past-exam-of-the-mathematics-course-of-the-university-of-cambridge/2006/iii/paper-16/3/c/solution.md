<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $\dim X=4r+2=2m$, with $m=2r+1$ odd. Rational [Poincare duality](../../../../../../poincare-duality.md) gives $b_k=b_{2m-k}$. Pairing every term outside the middle yields

$$
\chi(X)=2\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^k b_k+(-1)^m b_m.
$$

The [Poincare duality pairing](../../../../../../poincare-duality-pairing.md) on $H^m(X;\mathbb Q)$ is $B(\alpha,\gamma)=\langle\alpha\smile\gamma,[X]\rangle$. It is nondegenerate, and [graded commutativity of the cup product](../../../../../../graded-commutativity-of-the-cup-product.md) gives $B(\alpha,\gamma)=-B(\gamma,\alpha)$ because $m$ is odd. In particular $B(\alpha,\alpha)=0$ over $\mathbb Q$. Part (b) therefore makes $b_m$ even, so

$$
\boxed{\chi(X)\equiv0\pmod2.}
$$

For the failure without orientability, $\mathbb{RP}^2$ is a closed nonorientable surface with one cell in each dimension zero, one and two; hence $\chi=1-1+1=1$. More generally, $\mathbb{RP}^{4r+2}$ is nonorientable and has [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) one. Thus the orientation hypothesis in the [Euler characteristic parity in dimensions congruent to two modulo four](../../../../../../euler-characteristic-parity-in-dimensions-congruent-to-two-modulo-four.md) is essential.
