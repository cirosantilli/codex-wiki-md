<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Let $t=(T-T_c)/T_c$, and suppose $r=r_t t$ with $r_t>0$ while $u>0$ and $\kappa>0$ remain finite at the transition. [Critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) describe the leading singular powers: $M\sim(-t)^\beta$ below the transition, $\chi\sim|t|^{-\gamma}$, $\xi\sim|t|^{-\nu}$, $C_{V,s}\sim|t|^{-\alpha}$, $M(t=0)\sim\operatorname{sgn}(h)|h|^{1/\delta}$, and $G(R,t=0)\sim R^{-(D-2+\eta)}$.

The quartic [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) $h=rM+uM^3$ immediately gives $M=\sqrt{-r/u}$ at zero field below the transition, so $\beta=1/2$. Differentiating with respect to the [conjugate field](../../../../../../field-conjugate-to-an-order-parameter.md) gives $\chi^{-1}=r+3uM^2$, which is $r$ above and $2|r|$ below; therefore $\gamma=1$. At $r=0$, $M=(h/u)^{1/3}$ with its real sign, giving $\delta=3$. The quadratic [correlation function](../../../../../../correlation-function.md) has $\xi\propto|r|^{-1/2}$ and critical [Ornstein--Zernike correlation function](../../../../../../ornstein-zernike-correlation-function.md) proportional to $q^{-2}$, giving $\nu=1/2$ and $\eta=0$.

Finally the minimized potential below the transition is $V_s=-r^2/(4u)$, while it is zero above. Its second [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) is a finite step, so the mean-field [heat-capacity critical exponent](../../../../../../heat-capacity-critical-exponent.md) is $\alpha=0$. In particular,

$$
\boxed{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta)_{\rm MF}=(0,\tfrac12,1,3,\tfrac12,0).}
$$

These are derived [mean-field critical exponents](../../../../../../mean-field-critical-exponent.md). In the fluctuation-dominated [critical region of a phase transition](../../../../../../critical-region-of-a-phase-transition.md), one instead derives exponents from the scaling [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of the [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md), as in the next question. A zero heat-capacity power exponent can also accompany logarithms in other theories, so specifying the power alone does not completely describe that singularity.
