<h1 id="6/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The single bonds give equal simple-root lengths and inner products, after normalization,

$$
(\alpha,\alpha)=(\beta,\beta)=(\gamma,\gamma)=2,\quad
(\alpha,\beta)=(\beta,\gamma)=-1,\quad(\alpha,\gamma)=0.
$$

The [positive roots](../../../../../../positive-root.md) of this [A3 root system](../../../../../../a3-root-system.md) are

$$
\boxed{\alpha,\ \beta,\ \gamma,\ \alpha+\beta,\ \beta+\gamma,\ \alpha+\beta+\gamma.}
$$

The first two sums are [roots](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) by the qualified obtuse-root lemma. The last is a [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) because $(\alpha+\beta,\gamma)=-1$. All have nonnegative simple-root coordinates, hence are positive.

To prove completeness, first note that every [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) is Weyl-conjugate to a [simple root](../../../../../../simple-root.md). For a nonsimple [positive root](../../../../../../positive-root.md) $\delta=\sum c_i\alpha_i$, some $(\delta,\alpha_i)>0$, since $(\delta,\delta)=\sum c_i(\delta,\alpha_i)>0$. Reflection subtracts a positive integer multiple of $\alpha_i$, decreasing its integer height. The reflected [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) stays positive: it leaves the coefficients at all other [simple roots](../../../../../../simple-root.md) unchanged, at least one of which is positive; a [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) has coordinates all of one sign. Repeating this height decrease reaches a [simple root](../../../../../../simple-root.md). Thus every [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) has squared length two in this connected single-bond system.

Write an arbitrary [positive root](../../../../../../positive-root.md) as $a\alpha+b\beta+c\gamma$, with nonnegative integers $a,b,c$. Its squared length condition is

$$
a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc=1,\qquad
2=a^2+c^2+(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2.
$$

Thus $a,c\in\{0,1\}$. For $(a,c)=(0,0)$ the equation gives $b=1$; for $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$ it gives $b=0$ or $1$; for $(1,1)$ it gives $b=1$. These are exactly the six coefficient triples in the displayed list. This proves completeness from the axioms, not merely from recognizing the [Dynkin diagram](../../../../../../dynkin-diagram.md).
