<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

There is an exception to the printed assertion: for $\beta=-\alpha$, the [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product.md) is negative but $\alpha+\beta=0$ is not a [root](../../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md). This already occurs in the rank-one [root system](../../../../../../root-system.md) $R=\{\alpha,-\alpha\}$.

For the intended assertion, assume $\beta\ne-\alpha$. By the preceding list, at least one of $n_{\alpha,\beta},n_{\beta,\alpha}$ equals $-1$. If the first does, the reflection axiom gives

$$
s_\alpha(\beta)=\beta-n_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha=\beta+\alpha\in R.
$$

If the second does, use $s_\beta(\alpha)=\alpha+\beta$ instead. Thus the precise reusable conclusion is

$$
\boxed{(\alpha,\beta)<0,\ \beta\ne-\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha+\beta\in R.}
$$

This proves that [obtuse nonopposite roots have a root sum](../../../../../../obtuse-nonopposite-roots-have-a-root-sum.md) directly from the reflection and integrality axioms, without appealing to the classification of [root systems](../../../../../../root-system.md).
