<h1 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the jump convention $[f]=f_1-f_2$, consistent with region 1 occupying $x>Vt$. The one-dimensional [Lighthill stress tensor](../../../../../../../lighthill-stress-tensor.md) is $T=\rho u^2+p-c_0^2\rho$, so $T=T_2+[T]H(x-Vt)$. Both bulk stresses are constant. The [distributional jump formula for a Heaviside product](../../../../../../../distributional-jump-formula-for-a-heaviside-product.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{Q=[T]=\rho_1u_1^2-\rho_2u_2^2+p_1-p_2-c_0^2(\rho_1-\rho_2).}
$$

The [Rankine-Hugoniot condition](../../../../../../../rankine-hugoniot-conditions.md) for [conservation of mass](../../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) is $[\rho u]=V[\rho]$, while [conservation of momentum](../../../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) gives $[\rho u^2+p]=V[\rho u]$. Hence a physical moving [shock wave](../../../../../../../shock-wave.md) also satisfies

$$
\boxed{Q=(V^2-c_0^2)(\rho_1-\rho_2).}
$$

This sign can be checked directly. Subtracting a constant reference density does not affect derivatives, and $\rho'=\text{constant}+[\rho]H(x-Vt)$. Thus $\rho'_{tt}=V^2[\rho]\delta'(x-Vt)$ and $\rho'_{xx}=[\rho]\delta'(x-Vt)$, exactly reproducing the coefficient above.
