<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Let the tangential [velocity](../../../../../velocity.md) scale be $U$, gap scale $H$ and along-gap length $L\gg H$. The tangential Stokes equation balances $P/L$ with $\mu U/H^2$, while wall shear is $\tau\sim\mu U/H$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P\sim\frac{\mu UL}{H^2},\qquad \frac\tau P\sim\frac HL\ll1.}
$$

This is [lubrication pressure dominates shear stress](../../../../../lubrication-pressure-dominates-shear-stress.md); [pressure](../../../../../pressure.md) [force](../../../../../force.md) projections may nevertheless be small in a particular direction or may give no [torque](../../../../../torque.md) on a spherical surface.

Write the signed centre displacement along the downward axis as $d=\alpha\Delta$. A point $an$ on the inner sphere has distance $|an+d e_z|=a+d\cos\theta+O(\Delta^2/a)$ from the outer centre. The leading normal gap to radius $a+\Delta$ is consequently

$$
\boxed{h(\theta)=\Delta-d\cos\theta=\Delta(1-\alpha\cos\theta).}
$$

The local-curvature corrections are smaller by $\Delta/a$. This approximation also follows by solving the outer-sphere intersection along the ray $n$ from the inner centre and expanding its positive root.
