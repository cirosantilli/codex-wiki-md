<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use units $c=1$ and signature $(+---)$. For a massive [test particle](../../../../../../test-particle.md), write its [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md) as $p^i=mu^i$, where $u^i=dx^i/d\tau$, $\tau$ is Minkowski [proper time](../../../../../../proper-time.md), and $u^iu_i=1$. The stated [flat-background scalar-force theory of gravity](../../../../../../flat-background-scalar-force-theory-of-gravity.md) then gives

$$
\frac{dp^i}{d\tau}=m\left(\partial^i\Phi-u^i u^j\partial_j\Phi\right).
$$

Contract with $p_i=mu_i$. The two terms cancel:

$$
\frac{d(m^2)}{d\tau}=2p_i\frac{dp^i}{d\tau}
=2m^2\left(u^j\partial_j\Phi-u^iu_i u^j\partial_j\Phi\right)=0.
$$

Thus [rest mass](../../../../../../invariant-mass.md) is conserved and the [acceleration](../../../../../../acceleration.md) law is

$$
\boxed{\frac{du^i}{d\tau}=\partial^i\Phi-u^i(u\cdot\partial\Phi).}
$$

There is no mass or composition parameter in this equation. Particles released at the same event with the same [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) consequently have identical trajectories. In a static weak field at small speed, its spatial part becomes $d^2\mathbf x/dt^2=-\boldsymbol\nabla\Phi$, since raising a spatial index introduces a minus sign. The field equation likewise reduces to $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho$ for nonrelativistic matter, because $T^i{}_i\simeq\rho$ and $\Box=-\nabla^2$ in the static limit.

Therefore **yes: the model agrees with the Eötvös test of universal free fall**. In the point-particle/test-body approximation, inertial and passive gravitational masses have the same universal ratio. This is the [weak equivalence principle](../../../../../../weak-equivalence-principle.md) tested by the [Eötvös experiment](../../../../../../eotvos-experiment.md); it is not a claim about strongly self-gravitating bodies or a proof of all aspects of the [Einstein equivalence principle](../../../../../../einstein-equivalence-principle.md).
