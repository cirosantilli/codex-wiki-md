<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $R$ be a nonconstant [rational map](../../../../../rational-map-complex-analysis.md) of the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../riemann-sphere.md), and write $R^n$ for its $n$-fold iterate, with $R^0$ the identity. The appropriate notion of convergence is [uniform convergence](../../../../../uniform-convergence.md) on [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) subsets in the [chordal metric](../../../../../chordal-metric.md), which treats [poles](../../../../../pole.md) as ordinary values at infinity. A family of meromorphic maps is a [normal family](../../../../../normal-family.md) if every sequence in it has a [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) converging locally uniformly in that metric; the limit is meromorphic or constant, possibly the constant infinity.

The [Fatou set](../../../../../fatou-set.md) $F(R)$ consists of points with a neighborhood on which the iterate family is normal. The complementary [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md) is $J(R)=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus F(R)$. Thus **the sphere is the disjoint union of an open stability region $F(R)$ and a closed instability region $J(R)$**. These definitions concern all [subsequences](../../../../../subsequence.md) of iterates, rather than assuming that the iterates themselves converge on every stable region.

[Equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) makes this distinction precise. On a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) subset $K$ of a neighborhood, the family is [equicontinuous](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) if, for every $\epsilon>0$, some $\delta>0$ makes $\chi(R^n(z),R^n(w))<\epsilon$ whenever $z,w\in K$ and $\chi(z,w)<\delta$, uniformly in $n$. The [Arzelà-Ascoli theorem](../../../../../arzela-ascoli-theorem.md) says that [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) of [continuous maps](../../../../../continuous-map.md) from a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) domain to a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) metric target gives relative [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) for [uniform convergence](../../../../../uniform-convergence.md). Apply it to a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) exhaustion and take a diagonal [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) to obtain local normality. Locally uniform spherical limits of meromorphic maps are again meromorphic or constant, by using finite target charts and the locally [uniform limit](../../../../../uniform-limit.md) theorem for [holomorphic functions](../../../../../holomorphic-function.md). Conversely, if [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) failed, there would be maps $f_j$ and points $z_j,w_j\to z$ with $\chi(f_j(z_j),f_j(w_j))$ bounded below by a positive number. A [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) converging uniformly on a [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) neighborhood contradicts this. Hence **local normality and local [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) are equivalent here**.

Both sets are completely invariant:

$$
\boxed{R^{-1}(F)=F=R(F),\qquad R^{-1}(J)=J=R(J).}
$$

For the inverse direction, if the family is normal near $R(z)$, composition with $R$ makes $R^{n+1}$ normal near $z$, and adding the identity does not affect normality. For the forward direction, suppose the iterates are [equicontinuous](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) near $z$. A nonconstant holomorphic sphere map is open. Thus each sufficiently small neighborhood of $z$ maps onto a neighborhood of $R(z)$; every nearby target point $w$ has a preimage $x$ as close to $z$ as desired. Then

$$
\chi(R^n(w),R^n(R(z)))=\chi(R^{n+1}(x),R^{n+1}(z))
$$

is uniformly small. Applying this at each point of the image neighborhood proves [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) there. This argument works at [rational critical points](../../../../../critical-point-of-a-rational-map.md) and requires no single-valued inverse branch. [Surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md) of $R$ supplies the asserted image equalities. Also $F(R^k)=F(R)$ and $J(R^k)=J(R)$ for $k\geq1$: one direction follows by taking a subfamily, and the other by splitting $R^n$ into the finitely many residue classes $R^r\circ(R^k)^m$, $0\leq r<k$.

For degree $d\geq2$, the [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md) is nonempty. Otherwise the family would be [equicontinuous](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) on the whole [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) sphere, giving a [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) $R^{n_j}$ converging uniformly to a continuous sphere map $G$. Maps sufficiently close in chordal distance are [homotopic](../../../../../homotopy.md), using the unique short spherical [geodesic](../../../../../geodesic.md) between their values. They therefore have equal topological degrees. This is impossible because $\deg R^{n_j}=d^{n_j}\to\infty$ whereas $G$ has one fixed degree.

It cannot be a nonempty finite set either. Complete invariance would make $R$ a permutation of that finite set, with just one sphere preimage for each of its points. That preimage would have [local degree of a holomorphic map](../../../../../local-degree-of-a-holomorphic-map.md) $d$. Every [holomorphic cycle](../../../../../cycle-of-a-holomorphic-map.md) in this set would consequently be superattracting for its return map, hence lie in $F(R)$, a contradiction. In particular $J(R)$ contains at least three points.

The [Montel theorem](../../../../../montel-s-theorem.md) in the form used here says that a family of meromorphic maps on a domain, each omitting the same three distinct points of the sphere, is normal for spherical convergence. It also shows that $J(R)$ is perfect. If $a$ were isolated in $J(R)$, choose a disc $U$ with $U\cap J(R)=\{a\}$ and four distinct points of $J(R)$. For each $n$, complete invariance makes $R^n(U)\cap J(R)$ have at most the single point $R^n(a)$. Partition the iterates into finitely many families according to which of the four points, if any, that single value equals. Each family omits at least three [fixed points](../../../../../fixed-point.md), so each is normal by Montel; their finite union is normal. This would put $a$ in $F(R)$, a contradiction. Therefore **the degree-at-least-two [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md) is [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md), infinite and has no [isolated points](../../../../../isolated-point.md)**.

The same omitted-value argument explains its global reach. If an open $U$ meets $J(R)$, then $\bigcup_{n\geq0}R^n(U)$ can omit at most two sphere points, since three omissions would imply normality on $U$. If $J(R)$ had an interior and were not the whole sphere, these image sets would remain in $J(R)$ and their complement would contain the nonempty [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) $F(R)$, impossible. Thus a proper [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md) has empty interior and equals the boundary of $F(R)$. If the [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md) is the whole sphere, the [Fatou set](../../../../../fatou-set.md) is empty instead.

An [attracting periodic point](../../../../../attracting-periodic-point.md) and its [holomorphic cycle](../../../../../cycle-of-a-holomorphic-map.md) has a contracting return map in a small disc, so its basin is in the [Fatou set](../../../../../fatou-set.md). [Repelling periodic points](../../../../../repelling-periodic-point.md) belong to the [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md): return iterates fix the point but have [derivatives](../../../../../derivative.md) equal to unbounded powers of their [periodic-orbit multiplier](../../../../../multiplier-of-a-periodic-orbit-of-an-iteration.md), contradicting [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) bounds for a normal [subsequence](../../../../../subsequence.md) near that fixed value. A standard further global property is that [repelling periodic points](../../../../../repelling-periodic-point.md) are dense in the [Julia set](../../../../../julia-set.md). [Fatou components](../../../../../fatou-component.md) can also support convergence towards a [parabolic cycle](../../../../../parabolic-cycle.md) on the boundary, or rotation behavior in a [Siegel disc](../../../../../siegel-disc.md) or a [Herman ring](../../../../../herman-ring.md); normality need not mean attraction. For example, $R(z)=z^d$ has $J(R)=\{|z|=1\}$, with the interior converging to zero and the exterior to infinity. On the unit circle, arbitrarily close radii have incompatible limiting behavior, preventing normality there.

Degree one has a different picture. A [rational map](../../../../../rational-map-complex-analysis.md) of degree one is a [Möbius transformation](../../../../../mobius-transformation.md). Up to [Möbius conjugacy](../../../../../mobius-conjugacy.md), an elliptic map is $z\mapsto az$ with $|a|=1$; its iterates are rotations and are [equicontinuous](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) on the sphere, so **$F$ is the whole sphere and $J$ is empty** (including the identity). For $z\mapsto az$ with $|a|>1$, all [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) subsets away from zero converge to infinity, while $z_n=a^{-n}\to0$ and $R^n(z_n)=1$ prevent [equicontinuity](../../../../../equicontinuity.md) at zero. Thus **$J=\{0\}$**; for $|a|<1$ the reciprocal coordinate gives **$J=\{\infty\}$**. A nonidentity parabolic map is conjugate to $z\mapsto z+1$. Its iterates converge locally uniformly to infinity on the finite plane, but $z_n=-n\to\infty$ has $R^n(z_n)=0$, so **$J=\{\infty\}$**. [Holomorphic conjugacy](../../../../../holomorphic-conjugacy.md) carries these sets to the corresponding [fixed points](../../../../../fixed-point.md) of the original map. These finite [Julia sets](../../../../../julia-set.md) explain why the degree restriction in many global Julia-set properties is essential.
