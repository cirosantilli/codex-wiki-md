<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [primes](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $2,3,5$ do not divide the [elliptic-curve discriminant](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-discriminant.md) $-91$, so all three reductions are nonsingular. Count affine solutions and add the point at infinity. At $p=2$ the right side takes values $0,1$ at $x=0,1$, while $y^2+y=0$ for both values of $y$. This gives two affine points, namely $(0,0),(0,1)$.

At $p=3$, the right-side values at $x=0,1,2$ are $0,0,2$. The corresponding numbers of solutions to $y^2+y$ are $2,2,1$, giving five affine points. At $p=5$, the right-side values at $x=0,1,2,3,4$ are $0,3,0,2,0$, with respective solution counts $2,0,2,2,2$, giving eight affine points. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\#\widetilde E(\mathbb F_2)=3,\quad\#\widetilde E(\mathbb F_3)=6,\quad\#\widetilde E(\mathbb F_5)=9.}
$$
