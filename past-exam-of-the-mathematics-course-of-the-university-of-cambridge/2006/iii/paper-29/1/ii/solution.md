<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The given relation implies

$$
\pi(1-\phi_1)=(1+\phi_2)\pi.
$$

Take [isogeny degrees](../../../../../../degree-of-an-isogeny.md) and cancel the [positive integer](../../../../../../positive-integer.md) $\deg\pi$. Multiplicativity of [isogeny degree](../../../../../../degree-of-an-isogeny.md) gives

$$
\deg(1-\phi_1)=\deg(1+\phi_2).
$$

As above, $\deg(1-\phi_i)=\#E_i(\mathbb F_q)$ and $\deg\phi_i=q$. The [degree parallelogram law](../../../../../../divisor-proof-of-the-degree-parallelogram-law.md) on $\operatorname{End}(E_2)$ now yields

$$
\deg(1+\phi_2)+\deg(1-\phi_2)=2\deg1+2\deg\phi_2=2(q+1).
$$

Substitution proves

$$
\boxed{\#E_1(\mathbb F_q)+\#E_2(\mathbb F_q)=2(q+1).}
$$

Equivalently, their [Frobenius traces](../../../../../../trace-of-frobenius.md) are negatives of one another. The [isogeny degree](../../../../../../degree-of-an-isogeny.md) argument works over the [algebraic closure](../../../../../../algebraic-closure.md), so it does not wrongly assume that $\pi$ is defined over $\mathbb F_q$; the stated quadratic-extension hypothesis is compatible with the anticommutation relation.
