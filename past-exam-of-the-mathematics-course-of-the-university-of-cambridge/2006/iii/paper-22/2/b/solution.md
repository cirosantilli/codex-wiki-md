<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Start with any [unitary connection](../../../../../../unitary-connection.md) $\nabla_0$. The [Hodge decomposition theorem](../../../../../../hodge-decomposition-theorem.md) gives a unique real [harmonic differential form](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) $\eta$ representing $[iF_{\nabla_0}]$. Since they have the same [de Rham cohomology](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology.md) class, $\eta-iF_{\nabla_0}=d\beta$ for a smooth real one-form $\beta$. Set $\nabla=\nabla_0-i\beta$. The [vector-bundle curvature](../../../../../../curvature-form.md) difference formula gives

$$
\boxed{iF_\nabla=iF_{\nabla_0}+d\beta=\eta.}
$$

This proves existence of a [harmonic-curvature unitary line connection](../../../../../../harmonic-curvature-unitary-line-connection.md) for any metric and any [complex line bundle](../../../../../../complex-line-bundle.md).

Two such [unitary connections](../../../../../../unitary-connection.md) have the same [vector-bundle curvature](../../../../../../curvature-form.md) because harmonic representatives are unique. Their difference is therefore $i\gamma$ with $d\gamma=0$. When $b_1(X)=0$, write $\gamma=d\varphi$ globally. The [bundle gauge transformation](../../../../../../unitary-bundle-gauge-transformation.md) $u=e^{i\varphi}$ changes $\nabla$ to $u^{-1}\nabla u=\nabla+i\,d\varphi$. Thus **the harmonic-curvature connection is unique up to gauge**. On a general base, closed forms modulo the integral-period forms coming from circle-valued gauge transformations leave the torus $H^1(X;\mathbb R)/(2\pi H^1(X;\mathbb Z))$ of possible gauge classes; the hypothesis removes this freedom.
