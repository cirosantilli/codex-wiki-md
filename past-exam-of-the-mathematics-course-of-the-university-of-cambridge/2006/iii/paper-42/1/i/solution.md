<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [standard normal distribution](../../../../../../standard-normal-distribution.md), $m=0$ and $f(0)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, so the [sample median](../../../../../../sample-median.md) has [asymptotic variance](../../../../../../asymptotic-variance.md) $\pi/2$ under scaling by $\sqrt n$. The [central limit theorem](../../../../../../central-limit-theorem.md) gives $\sqrt n\bar X_n\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)$ for the [sample mean](../../../../../../sample-mean.md). Thus **the [sample median](../../../../../../sample-median.md) has $\pi/2$ times the [asymptotic variance](../../../../../../asymptotic-variance.md) of the [sample mean](../../../../../../sample-mean.md)**, and its [asymptotic relative efficiency](../../../../../../asymptotic-relative-efficiency.md) against the [sample mean](../../../../../../sample-mean.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ARE}(\text{median},\text{mean})=\frac2\pi.}
$$
