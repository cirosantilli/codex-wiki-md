<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In established three-dimensional [turbulence](../../../../../../turbulence-split.md), mean [vortex stretching](../../../../../../vortex-stretching.md) is positive: $\langle\omega_i\omega_jS_{ij}\rangle>0$. The [Betchov relation](../../../../../../betchov-relation.md) then gives $\langle abc\rangle<0$. [Bi-axial strain](../../../../../../bi-axial-strain.md) has two positive [principal strain rates](../../../../../../principal-strain-rates.md) and one negative rate, hence $abc<0$; extension along one axis with compression along the other two instead gives $abc>0$.

Thus the cubic strain statistic favors [bi-axial strain](../../../../../../bi-axial-strain.md). Strictly, this is an amplitude-weighted preference: a negative average alone cannot prove that there are more events of one type, since a few strong events could dominate it. Positive mean [vortex stretching](../../../../../../vortex-stretching.md) is additional physical information, not a consequence of homogeneity alone.

[Bi-axial strain](../../../../../../bi-axial-strain.md) spreads a nearly incompressible material volume along two directions while thinning it along the third, favoring sheet-like concentrations of [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md). A sheet can then become unstable and roll up into [vortex tubes](../../../../../../vortex-tube.md). The [Townsend-Betchov cascade cartoon](../../../../../../townsend-betchov-cascade-cartoon.md) uses this sequence to reconcile strain-favored sheets with the intense tubes observed at dissipative scales. Alignment of [vorticity](../../../../../../vorticity.md) with the [principal strain rates](../../../../../../principal-strain-rates.md) also affects the stretching; the determinant statistic does not specify the entire geometry.
