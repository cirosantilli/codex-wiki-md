<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [left adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) is discretization $D$: give the underlying set of $X$ the [discrete topology](../../../../../../discrete-space.md). Write $J$ for indiscretization. Every function $DX\to Y$ is continuous, and every function $X\to JY$ is continuous. The common set of underlying functions supplies the natural bijection

$$
\mathbf{Top}(DX,Y)\cong\mathbf{Top}(X,JY).
$$

The [discrete-indiscrete adjunction](../../../../../../discrete-indiscrete-adjunction.md) has induced [monad](../../../../../../monad.md) $JD$, which assigns the indiscrete topology to the original set. Applying it twice gives the same space, and its multiplication is the identity on that set. Thus **the adjunction is idempotent**. Its dual comonad $DJ$, discretization, is idempotent as well.
