<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [category of ordinals in reverse order](../../../../../../category-of-ordinals-in-reverse-order.md) $\mathbf{Ord}^{\mathrm{op}}$, with a unique arrow $\alpha\to\beta$ exactly when $\alpha\geq\beta$, and the unique [functor](../../../../../../functor.md)

$$
G:\mathbf{Ord}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbf1.
$$

Both categories are [complete categories](../../../../../../complete-category.md). A small limit in the ordinal category is the supremum of the ordinals occurring in the diagram; the empty supremum is $0$, its [terminal object](../../../../../../terminal-object.md). The functor $G$ preserves all these [categorical limits](../../../../../../categorical-limit.md), and both categories are [locally small categories](../../../../../../locally-small-category.md).

A [left adjoint](../../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) would select an [initial object](../../../../../../initial-object.md) of $\mathbf{Ord}^{\mathrm{op}}$, meaning an ordinal at least as large as every ordinal. No such object exists. More specifically, any set of candidate ordinals has an upper bound $\gamma$, and none of its members maps to $\gamma+1$. Thus **the solution-set condition fails**. This example isolates the genuine smallness requirement in the [general adjoint functor theorem](../../../../../../freyd-general-adjoint-functor-theorem.md).
