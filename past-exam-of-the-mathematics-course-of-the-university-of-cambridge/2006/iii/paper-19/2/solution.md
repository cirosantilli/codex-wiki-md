There are two necessary qualifications to the literal formulation. The whole [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $G$ is itself a closed [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), so the claim about containment in the centre must concern **proper closed [normal subgroups](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md)**. Also, the homomorphism conclusion needs **a nontrivial target $H$**: for example, $SU(2)\to\{1\}$ has [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md) differential onto the zero [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md), but its [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is all of $SU(2)$ and no quotient by a finite subgroup is a one-point [group](../../../../../group-split.md). We prove the precise assertions, including these exceptional cases.

Write $\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$. The centre $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)$ is invariant under the [Adjoint representation](../../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra.md), so irreducibility makes it either zero or all of $\mathfrak g$. In the latter case $\mathfrak g$ is [Abelian](../../../../../abelian-group.md). For [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) $G$, the [adjoint action](../../../../../adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-group.md) is then trivial: the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is generated by exponentials, and $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp X}=e^{\operatorname{ad}X}=I$. A nonzero [trivial representation](../../../../../trivial-representation.md) is [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) only in [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) one. A [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) one-dimensional [Lie group](../../../../../lie-group.md) is a circle, so $G\cong U(1)$.

For every other $G$, the [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) of its centre is zero. The centre is a closed [compact](../../../../../compact-space.md) [Lie subgroup](../../../../../lie-subgroup.md); a zero-dimensional [compact Lie group](../../../../../compact-lie-group.md) is finite. Let $N$ be a closed [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). The [closed-subgroup theorem](../../../../../closed-subgroup-theorem.md) identifies $\mathfrak n=\operatorname{Lie}(N)$ as an adjoint-invariant subspace of $\mathfrak g$, hence $\mathfrak n=0$ or $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak g$. If the latter holds, $N$ contains an identity neighbourhood and is open, so connectedness forces $N=G$. If $N$ is proper, it therefore has zero [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) and is finite. For any $a\in N$, the [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) $g\mapsto gag^{-1}$ takes [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) $G$ into the [finite set](../../../../../finite-set.md) $N$, so it is constant and equal to $a$. Thus

$$
\boxed{G\not\cong U(1)\Longrightarrow Z(G)\text{ is finite};\qquad N\lhd G, N\text{ closed and proper}\Longrightarrow N\subseteq Z(G).}
$$

Proper [closed subgroups](../../../../../closed-subgroup.md) of the circle are finite as well, and of course central.

Now let $d\Phi$ be [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md). The [submersion theorem](../../../../../submersion-theorem.md) makes $\Phi(G)$ contain an identity neighbourhood in $H$, so it is an [open subgroup](../../../../../open-subgroup.md). Since $H$ is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md), $\Phi(G)=H$. Its [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) $F$ is closed and normal. If $H$ is nontrivial, $F$ is proper and the preceding argument gives a finite central [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md); this also holds for $G\cong U(1)$, whose proper [closed subgroups](../../../../../closed-subgroup.md) are finite. The Lie-group [first isomorphism theorem](../../../../../first-isomorphism-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{H\cong G/F,\qquad F\subseteq Z(G)\text{ finite},\quad H\ne\{1\}.}
$$

If $H$ is trivial, the correct statement is instead $H=G/G$.

For the [Spin groups](../../../../../spin-group.md), use the real [Clifford algebra](../../../../../clifford-algebra.md) with $v^2=-\|v\|^2$. The [group](../../../../../group-split.md) $\operatorname{Spin}(m)$ consists of products of an [even number](../../../../../even-number.md) of [unit vectors](../../../../../unit-vector.md) inside its invertible even part. [Conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md) on the embedded $\mathbb R^m$ defines the standard double covering $\operatorname{Spin}(m)\to SO(m)$ with [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) $\{\pm1\}$. The Clifford relations show that a unit-vector reflection, with the usual twisted [conjugation](../../../../../conjugation.md), is a hyperplane reflection; the even products give orientation-preserving transformations. The [Cartan–Dieudonné theorem](../../../../../cartan-dieudonne-theorem.md) states that every [orthogonal transformation](../../../../../orthogonal-transformation.md) is a product of reflections, proving [surjectivity](../../../../../surjective-function.md). For $m\geq3$ this [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) covering [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md); $\operatorname{Spin}(2)$ is a circle.

Here is an explicit exterior-square construction of the requested six-dimensional map. Put $E=\mathbb C^4$ with its standard [Hermitian form](../../../../../hermitian-form.md) and [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge e_4$. On $\Lambda^2E$, write $e_{ij}=e_i\wedge e_j$ and define an [antilinear map](../../../../../antilinear-map.md) $J$ by

$$
Je_{12}=e_{34},\quad Je_{13}=-e_{24},\quad Je_{14}=e_{23},\quad
Je_{34}=e_{12},\quad Je_{24}=-e_{13},\quad Je_{23}=e_{14}.
$$

It satisfies $J^2=1$. Intrinsically, if $u\wedge v=B(u,v)\,\mathrm{vol}$ and the [Hermitian inner product](../../../../../hermitian-form.md) $h$ is [linear](../../../../../linearity.md) in its first variable, then $B(u,Jv)=h(u,v)$. Because $SU(4)$ preserves both $B$ and $h$, it commutes with $J$. This is the [real structure of the SU4 exterior square](../../../../../real-structure-of-the-su4-exterior-square.md).

The fixed space $W$ has the following real [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), with all vectors divided by $\sqrt2$:

$$
e_{12}+e_{34},\quad i(e_{12}-e_{34}),\quad
e_{13}-e_{24},\quad i(e_{13}+e_{24}),\quad
e_{14}+e_{23},\quad i(e_{14}-e_{23}).
$$

Thus $W$ is six-dimensional over $\mathbb R$, its complexification is $\Lambda^2E$, and the exterior-square action restricts to an [orthogonal representation](../../../../../orthogonal-representation.md) on $W$. Its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) is one because $SU(4)$ is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md). We obtain $R:SU(4)\to SO(W)\cong SO(6)$.

To compute the [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md), diagonalize $g\in SU(4)$ with [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $z_1,\ldots,z_4$. If $R(g)=I$, its complexified exterior-square action is the identity, hence $z_i z_j=1$ for every $i<j$. Comparing pairs shows that all $z_i$ are equal, and their common value has square one. A [unitary matrix](../../../../../unitary-matrix.md) with that single [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is scalar, so $\ker R=\{\pm I_4\}$. Conversely, those two [scalar matrices](../../../../../scalar-matrix.md) plainly act trivially on the [exterior square](../../../../../exterior-square.md).

The differential of $R$ is [injective](../../../../../injective-function.md) because its [group kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is finite. Both [Lie algebras](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) have [dimension](../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $15$, so the differential is an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Its image is consequently open in [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) $SO(6)$, hence all of $SO(6)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{SU(4)/\{\pm I_4\}\cong SO(6).}
$$

This also realizes $SU(4)$ as $\operatorname{Spin}(6)$, since $SU(4)$ is [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md), by uniqueness of the [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md) [simply connected](../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) covering [group](../../../../../group-split.md).
