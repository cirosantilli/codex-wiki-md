↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False in general.** Take $n=8$, $r=4$, and

$$
\mathcal A=\{A\subseteq[8]:|A|=4,\ |A\cap[4]|\ge3\}.
$$

It is a [Frankl family](../../../../../../complete-intersection-candidate-family.md) with $\binom43\binom41+\binom44=17$ members. Two members each use at least three of the first four points, so they share at least two of those points. Hence it is [t-intersecting](../../../../../../t-intersecting-family.md) with $t=2$, and $r=n/2$.

The first fifteen rank-four sets in [lexicographic order](../../../../../../lexicographic-order.md) are precisely those containing $1,2$. The sixteenth is $1345$, which intersects the earlier set $1278$ only at one. Therefore the lex [initial segment](../../../../../../initial-segment.md) of size seventeen is not two-intersecting. This proves that [lexicographic replacement can destroy two-intersection](../../../../../../lexicographic-replacement-can-destroy-two-intersection.md); an ordinary intersecting bound cannot justify preservation of higher-order intersection.
