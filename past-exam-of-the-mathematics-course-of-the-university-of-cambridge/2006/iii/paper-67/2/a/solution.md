<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [trigonometric polynomial](../../../../../../trigonometric-polynomial.md) $t_n$ of degree at most $n$ has zero [Fourier coefficients](../../../../../../fourier-coefficient.md) outside $[-n,n]$. Its [Fourier partial sum](../../../../../../fourier-partial-sum.md) $s_j(t_n)$ therefore equals $t_n$ for every $j\geq n$. Averaging the sums with $j=n,\ldots,n+m-1$ gives

$$
\boxed{v_{n,m}(t_n)=\frac1m\sum_{j=n}^{n+m-1}t_n=t_n.}
$$

Thus the [de la Vallée Poussin sum](../../../../../../de-la-vallee-poussin-sum.md) reproduces the entire degree-at-most-$n$ approximation space, including when the actual degree is smaller.
