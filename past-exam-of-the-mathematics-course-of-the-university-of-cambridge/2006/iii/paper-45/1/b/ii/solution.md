↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Without [genotyping error](../../../../../../../genotyping-error.md), the observed [genetic marker](../../../../../../../genetic-marker.md) [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md) is a deterministic function $u(g)$ of the underlying [phase-known genotype](../../../../../../../phase-known-genotype.md). In particular $u$ can forget chromosome phase and parental origin. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(X_i=x\mid G_i=g)=\mathbf1_{\{u(g)=x\}}.}
$$

Several different phased [haplotype](../../../../../../../haplotype.md) pairs can receive factor 1 when the observed multilocus [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md) is unphased. With laboratory error, missing [alleles](../../../../../../../allele.md) or uncertain calls, replace the indicator by an error or observation model. A wholly unobserved [genetic marker](../../../../../../../genetic-marker.md) contributes factor 1. Thus a 0-or-1 factor reflects perfect observation of a deterministic [genetic marker](../../../../../../../genetic-marker.md) state, not a general property of every genetic measurement.
