<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a binary disease [phenotype](../../../../../../../phenotype.md), put $p_i(g)=P(X_i=\text{affected}\mid G_i=g)$. The [penetrance](../../../../../../../penetrance.md) function depends on the inheritance model, the disease [alleles](../../../../../../../allele.md) and [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md), and potentially age, sex, environment and other genetic modifiers. Phenocopies allow disease without the putative disease [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md), while incomplete [penetrance](../../../../../../../penetrance.md) allows a [genetic carrier](../../../../../../../genetic-carrier.md) of that [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md) to remain unaffected. Thus the [phenotype](../../../../../../../phenotype.md) factor is $p_i(g)$ for an affected individual and $1-p_i(g)$ for an unaffected one.

Under a fully penetrant model with no phenocopies the [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md) determines disease status, so **the factor is 1 for a compatible [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md) and 0 for an incompatible [genotype](../../../../../../../genotype.md)**. For example, in fully penetrant [recessive inheritance](../../../../../../../recessive-inheritance.md), $aa$ is affected and $AA,Aa$ are unaffected. Missing [phenotype](../../../../../../../phenotype.md) observations contribute the constant factor 1 rather than excluding [genotypes](../../../../../../../genotype.md).
