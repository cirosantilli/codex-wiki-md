<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $E=\langle\nabla u\rangle$ and retain only the independent response of each well-separated small [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md). In the leading [dilute spherical-inclusion conductivity](../../../../../../dilute-spherical-inclusion-conductivity.md) approximation,

$$
P(x)\simeq\beta\sum_{j=1}^N\chi_{B_j}(x)E,\qquad
\beta=\frac{3a_0(a_1-a_0)}{a_1+2a_0}.
$$

Averaging gives $\langle P\rangle\simeq p\beta E$, where $p=\sum_j|B_j|/|\Omega|$. Since $a\nabla u=a_0\nabla u+P$,

$$
a^*E=a_0E+\langle P\rangle.
$$

The spherical response holds for every $E$, so

$$
\boxed{a^*\simeq a_0I+\frac{3pa_0(a_1-a_0)}{a_1+2a_0}I.}
$$

This retains the order-$p$ term and neglects interactions. A precise dilute regime can be obtained by shrinking the [spheres](../../../../../../sphere.md) while holding separated centres fixed. [Volume fraction](../../../../../../volume-fraction.md) alone is not enough if the inclusions instead remain in close clusters whose collective polarization persists at leading order.
