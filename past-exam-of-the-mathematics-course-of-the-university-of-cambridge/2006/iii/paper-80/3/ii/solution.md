<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose the affine trial function $v(x)=\lambda\cdot x$. It has the required trace and constant [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md), so the [Dirichlet principle](../../../../../../dirichlet-principle.md) gives

$$
J(u)\le J(v)=\int_\Omega a(x)\lambda\cdot\lambda\,dx
=|\Omega|(p_1a_1+p_2a_2)|\lambda|^2.
$$

For the macroscopic [isotropic](../../../../../../isotropy.md) effective medium, the corresponding affine-boundary energy is $|\Omega|a^*|\lambda|^2$. Dividing by $|\Omega||\lambda|^2$ for a nonzero imposed [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) yields the [Voigt bound](../../../../../../voigt-bound.md)

$$
\boxed{a^*\le p_1a_1+p_2a_2.}
$$

More generally the same calculation is the [tensor](../../../../../../tensor.md) inequality in the [Loewner order](../../../../../../loewner-order.md) $a^*\preceq\langle a\rangle$. For a finite unresolved specimen the calculation bounds its apparent affine-boundary response; identification with the bulk [effective conductivity](../../../../../../effective-conductivity.md) uses the macroscopic scale separation.
