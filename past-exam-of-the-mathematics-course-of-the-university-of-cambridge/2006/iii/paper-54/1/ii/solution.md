<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In four dimensions, [extended supersymmetry](../../../../../../extended-supersymmetry.md) has $\mathcal N>1$ independent [Weyl spinor](../../../../../../weyl-spinor.md) [supercharges](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md), or $4\mathcal N$ real generators. With the ordinary particle [Super-Poincaré algebra](../../../../../../super-poincare-algebra.md),

$$
\{Q_\alpha^A,\bar Q_{\dot\beta B}\}=2\delta^A_B\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu,
\qquad\{Q_\alpha^A,Q_\beta^B\}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z^{AB},\qquad Z^{AB}=-Z^{BA}.
$$

The [central charges in supersymmetry](../../../../../../central-charge-in-supersymmetry.md) commute with spacetime generators and the [supercharges](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md); an [R-symmetry](../../../../../../r-symmetry.md) can act on their internal indices. Antisymmetry makes a scalar central charge impossible for $\mathcal N=1$.

At positive timelike momentum, the rest-frame [supercharges](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md) act as fermionic oscillators. Without central charges, a spin-$j$ [Clifford vacuum](../../../../../../clifford-vacuum.md) generates $(2j+1)2^{2\mathcal N}$ states. For a [massless supermultiplet](../../../../../../massless-supermultiplet.md), positivity makes half the [supercharges](../../../../../../supersymmetry-generator.md) act trivially. The remaining $\mathcal N$ creation operators generate $2^{\mathcal N}$ states with [helicity](../../../../../../helicity.md) levels $\lambda-k/2$ and multiplicities $\binom{\mathcal N}{k}$. A separate [CPT completion of a supermultiplet](../../../../../../cpt-completion-of-a-supermultiplet.md) may be necessary. Keeping all helicities between $-1$ and $1$ gives $\mathcal N\leq4$ for a theory without gravity; allowing helicity two gives $\mathcal N\leq8$.

A nonzero [central charge in supersymmetry](../../../../../../central-charge-in-supersymmetry.md) changes the oscillator norms. For an $\mathcal N=2$ central-charge block, write $Z^{12}=2z$. Suitable combinations of a charge and the adjoint of the other have [anticommutators](../../../../../../anticommutator.md) $2(M+|z|)$ and $2(M-|z|)$. Their positivity gives

$$
\boxed{M\geq|z|.}
$$

A [BPS state](../../../../../../bps-state.md) saturates this [BPS bound in supersymmetry](../../../../../../bps-bound-in-supersymmetry.md). The combinations with zero anticommutator annihilate it, preserving part of [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) and giving a shortened [BPS supermultiplet](../../../../../../shortened-massive-supermultiplet.md). In this example a scalar-seeded massive multiplet has four states instead of sixteen, before any required CPT completion. More central-charge blocks allow different fractions of preserved supersymmetry. The mass-charge relation is protected as long as the short state exists; this does not guarantee stability against decay everywhere in parameter space.

The main obstruction to unbroken [extended supersymmetry](../../../../../../extended-supersymmetry.md) at low energies is the [chirality constraint on extended supersymmetry](../../../../../../chirality-constraint-on-extended-supersymmetry.md). An $\mathcal N=2$ [vector multiplet](../../../../../../supersymmetric-vector-multiplet.md) contains an additional adjoint complex scalar. A full [hypermultiplet](../../../../../../hypermultiplet.md) contains two $\mathcal N=1$ matter [chiral superfields](../../../../../../chiral-superfield.md) in conjugate gauge representations $R$ and $\overline R$. Its charged fermion spectrum is therefore vectorlike. Half-hypermultiplets in pseudoreal representations do not supply genuinely complex chirality. The $\mathcal N=4$ gauge multiplet is still more restrictive: it has four adjoint [Weyl spinors](../../../../../../weyl-spinor.md) and six adjoint real scalars. These spectra and their constrained interactions cannot directly reproduce the chiral electroweak matter and arbitrary Yukawa couplings of the [Standard Model](../../../../../../standard-model-split.md).

**At most unbroken $\mathcal N=1$ supersymmetry can directly describe the observed chiral four-dimensional gauge spectrum.** Extended supersymmetry can be useful in a higher-energy theory, in an internal sector or before compactification, but it must be reduced or broken to obtain that low-energy spectrum. This is a statement about unbroken symmetry of the relevant sector, not a prohibition on extended supersymmetric ultraviolet descriptions.
