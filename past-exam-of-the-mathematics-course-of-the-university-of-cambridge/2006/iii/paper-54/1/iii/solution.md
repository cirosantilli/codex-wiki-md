<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry](../../../../../../four-dimensional-n-1-supersymmetry.md) is compatible with a chiral gauge spectrum. Each matter [Weyl spinor](../../../../../../weyl-spinor.md) belongs to a [chiral superfield](../../../../../../chiral-superfield.md) with a complex scalar in the same gauge representation, and each [gauge boson](../../../../../../gauge-boson.md) belongs to a [vector multiplet](../../../../../../supersymmetric-vector-multiplet.md) with an adjoint [gaugino](../../../../../../gaugino.md). Thus a supersymmetric extension of the [Standard Model](../../../../../../standard-model-split.md) predicts squarks, sleptons and gauge-fermion partners, together with Higgs-sector partners. Exact unbroken [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) would make partners mass-degenerate, so a phenomenologically useful model needs [supersymmetry breaking](../../../../../../supersymmetry-breaking.md).

The [Minimal supersymmetric Standard Model](../../../../../../minimal-supersymmetric-standard-model.md) uses two Higgs chiral doublets. [Holomorphy](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) of the [superpotential](../../../../../../superpotential.md) prevents use of the conjugate of one doublet as an independent chiral field, so up-type and down-type Yukawa couplings require opposite-hypercharge doublets. Their higgsinos also cancel each other's gauge anomalies. The [superpotential](../../../../../../superpotential.md) has the schematic form

$$
W=y_uQU^cH_u-y_dQD^cH_d-y_eLE^cH_d+\mu H_uH_d,
$$

with gauge contractions understood. Supersymmetry relates the scalar quartic interactions and Yukawa interactions to the same gauge and superpotential couplings, rather than allowing arbitrary independent choices.

A central motivation is the [hierarchy problem](../../../../../../hierarchy-problem.md). A nonsupersymmetric scalar mass can receive corrections proportional to a high ultraviolet cutoff squared. The paired bosonic and fermionic loops in exact [supersymmetry](../../../../../../supersymmetry-split.md) cancel that quadratic sensitivity. [Soft supersymmetry breaking](../../../../../../soft-supersymmetry-breaking.md) introduces scalar squared masses, gaugino masses and appropriate scalar bilinear and trilinear terms without restoring the original quadratic divergence. With a typical soft scale $m_{\rm soft}$, residual corrections have the schematic size

$$
\delta m_H^2\sim\frac{y^2}{16\pi^2}m_{\rm soft}^2\log\frac{\Lambda^2}{m_{\rm soft}^2},
$$

plus finite thresholds. This favors not excessively heavy partners if one wishes to avoid a new tuning, but does not derive their masses or guarantee that they are experimentally accessible. A hidden breaking sector can communicate soft terms through gauge or gravitational interactions.

The altered particle spectrum also changes the [renormalization group](../../../../../../renormalization-group.md) evolution of the [gauge couplings](../../../../../../gauge-coupling.md). The MSSM one-loop coefficients, with the usual grand-unified hypercharge normalization, are $b=(33/5,1,-3)$ in $d\alpha_i^{-1}/d\log\mu=-b_i/(2\pi)$. They allow approximate [supersymmetric gauge coupling unification](../../../../../../supersymmetric-gauge-coupling-unification.md) near $10^{16}$ GeV, subject to threshold corrections. Top-Yukawa effects can drive the up-type Higgs squared mass negative and help produce [radiative electroweak symmetry breaking](../../../../../../radiative-electroweak-symmetry-breaking.md). Neither phenomenon alone specifies a full breaking mechanism.

Without an additional symmetry, gauge invariance allows renormalizable baryon- or lepton-number violating superpotential terms. Conserved [R-parity](../../../../../../r-parity.md), $(-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$, forbids the usual such renormalizable terms, distinguishes ordinary particles from their [superpartners](../../../../../../sparticle.md), and makes the [lightest supersymmetric particle](../../../../../../lightest-supersymmetric-particle.md) stable. A neutral stable lightest partner can be a [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md) candidate; its abundance and interactions require a separate calculation. Pair production followed by cascades to such a particle can give missing-momentum signals. R-parity is an extra model assumption, and does not by itself exclude all higher-dimensional proton-decay operators.

**Possible low-energy consequences are a partner spectrum, controlled scalar-mass sensitivity, a constrained Higgs sector, modified coupling evolution and, with conserved R-parity, a stable lightest partner.** Soft parameters also affect flavor-changing and CP-violating processes. Supersymmetry provides relations and mechanisms, not a unique numerical spectrum or an automatic solution to every flavor or cosmological problem.
