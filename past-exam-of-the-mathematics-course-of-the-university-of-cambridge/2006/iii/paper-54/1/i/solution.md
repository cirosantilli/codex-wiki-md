<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

[Kaluza-Klein theory](../../../../../../kaluza-klein-theory.md) explains apparently four-dimensional physics as the low-energy limit of a higher-dimensional theory with compact spatial directions. In units $\hbar=c=1$, take a circle coordinate $y\sim y+2\pi R$. A periodic scalar has the [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md)

$$
\Phi(x,y)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\phi_n(x)e^{iny/R}.
$$

Substitution into its higher-dimensional [kinetic term](../../../../../../kinetic-term.md), followed by integration over $y$, makes different Fourier modes orthogonal. The internal derivative contributes $n^2/R^2$ to the lower-dimensional squared mass, so

$$
\boxed{m_n^2=M^2+n^2/R^2.}
$$

The quantized internal momentum produces the [Kaluza-Klein tower](../../../../../../kaluza-klein-tower.md). A real higher-dimensional field satisfies $\phi_{-n}=\phi_n^*$; the tower does not introduce independent complex modes for both signs. More general compact spaces replace $n^2/R^2$ by [Laplacian eigenvalues](../../../../../../laplacian-eigenvalue.md), with boundary conditions determining the spectrum.

At energies $E\ll R^{-1}$, nonzero [Kaluza-Klein modes](../../../../../../kaluza-klein-mode.md) cannot be produced and may be integrated out, leaving a four-dimensional [effective field theory](../../../../../../effective-field-theory.md). [Dimensional reduction](../../../../../../dimensional-reduction.md) retains the zero modes at leading order; integrating out the massive modes also generates interactions suppressed by the [Kaluza-Klein compactification scale](../../../../../../kaluza-klein-compactification-scale.md). The higher-dimensional metric decomposes into a four-dimensional metric, vectors from mixed components, and scalar fields from internal components. For a circle, these include an Abelian [gauge field](../../../../../../gauge-field.md) and a [radion](../../../../../../radion.md). Internal coordinate shifts induce the vector's [gauge symmetry](../../../../../../gauge-invariance.md), while the [radion](../../../../../../radion.md) describes the radius. Pure circle reduction alone does not automatically give the chiral matter spectrum of the [Standard Model](../../../../../../standard-model-split.md).

The gravitational scale follows by integrating the [Einstein-Hilbert action](../../../../../../einstein-hilbert-action.md) over an unwarped internal volume $V_n$:

$$
\frac{M_*^{n+2}}2\int d^{4+n}x\sqrt{|G|}\,\mathcal R_{4+n}
\longrightarrow\frac{M_*^{n+2}V_n}2\int d^4x\sqrt{|g|}\,\mathcal R_4,
\qquad\boxed{M_4^2=M_*^{n+2}V_n.}
$$

Here $M_4$ is the [reduced Planck mass](../../../../../../reduced-planck-mass.md). For $n$ equal circle radii, $V_n=(2\pi R)^n$. The [Planck mass from compactification volume](../../../../../../planck-mass-from-compactification-volume.md) relation allows weak four-dimensional gravity even when $M_*$ is much lower. A conventional metric-origin gauge coupling scales as $g_{\rm KK}\sim(M_4R)^{-1}$, so an order-one coupling in that simplest construction tends to place $R$ near the gravitational length scale.

In the [brane-world scenario](../../../../../../brane-world-scenario.md), ordinary matter and its [gauge fields](../../../../../../gauge-field.md) can instead be localized on a three-dimensional spatial [brane](../../../../../../brane.md), while gravity explores the bulk. The absence of ordinary-matter excitations along the extra dimensions then permits larger radii than a model in which all fields propagate there. In [large extra dimensions](../../../../../../large-extra-dimensions.md), gravity changes approximately from a $1/r$ potential for $r\gg R$ to a $1/r^{n+1}$ potential for $M_*^{-1}\ll r\ll R$. For illustration, $M_*$ of order a TeV and $n=2$ give $R$ of order $0.1$ mm; one extra dimension would require an astronomically large radius.

A warped [Randall–Sundrum model](../../../../../../randall-sundrum-model.md) uses

$$
ds^2=e^{-2k|y|}\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu-dy^2,
\qquad m_{\rm IR}=e^{-kL}m_0.
$$

For the usual doubled orbifold interval, $M_4^2=M_5^3(1-e^{-2kL})/k$. The relevant lengths are the bulk curvature radius $k^{-1}$, the proper separation $L$, and the inverse warped excitation scale, of order $(ke^{-kL})^{-1}$. Thus the first mass need not equal $1/L$. **Compact volume dilutes gravity; warping redshifts local masses.** Both mechanisms require consistent field localization and [radius stabilization](../../../../../../radius-stabilization.md); choosing a large radius or separation is not itself a dynamical explanation of it.
