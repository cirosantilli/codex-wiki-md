<h1 id="4/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Interpret the quoted values as the paper's 2006-era rounded cosmological benchmark. The [Hubble constant](../../../../../../../hubble-constant.md) is the present slope of the [Hubble law](../../../../../../../hubble-s-law.md), $v\simeq H_0d$ at sufficiently small [redshift](../../../../../../../redshift.md). A [cosmic distance ladder](../../../../../../../cosmic-distance-ladder.md) begins with geometrical distance anchors, calibrates the [Cepheid period-luminosity relation](../../../../../../../cepheid-period-luminosity-relation.md) of [Cepheid variables](../../../../../../../cepheid-variable.md), and uses those distances to calibrate secondary indicators such as [Type Ia supernovae](../../../../../../../type-ia-supernova.md). Distances to objects far enough away that [peculiar velocities](../../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) are a small fraction of recession velocity then determine the slope. Extinction, metallicity effects, distance-anchor calibration, sample selection and [peculiar velocities](../../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) contribute to its uncertainty.

The [Hubble Space Telescope Key Project final result](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0012376) was $H_0=72\pm8\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, combining random and systematic uncertainty, supporting the rounded value $70$. Independent approaches include distances inferred from gravitational-lens time delays and from cluster X-ray and [Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect](../../../../../../../sunyaev-zeldovich-effect.md) measurements; each brings different model uncertainties. Joint fits to the [cosmic microwave background](../../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background.md) and large-scale structure also constrain $H_0$ within an assumed cosmological model. Thus **the evidence for a value near $70$ combines calibrated distances with independent cross-checks**; the approximate precision in the question should not be read as an identical uncertainty for every method.
