<h1 id="2/ii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the specified flat matter-vacuum model,

$$
F(z)=s-\sqrt{0.3s^3+0.7}.
$$

Its small-[redshift](../../../../../../../redshift.md) behaviour is $F(z)=0.55z+O(z^2)$, since $q_0=\Omega_{m,0}/2-\Omega_{\Lambda,0}=-0.55$. At high [redshift](../../../../../../../redshift.md), matter dominates and $F\sim-\sqrt{0.3}s^{3/2}$, so the initially positive [redshift drift](../../../../../../../redshift-drift.md) must turn negative. To locate the crossing, square $s=\sqrt{0.3s^3+0.7}$; both sides are positive, so squaring introduces no positive-$s$ spurious root. Factorization gives

$$
0.3s^3-s^2+0.7=(s-1)(0.3s^2-0.7s-0.7).
$$

Besides $z=0$, the physical [nonzero redshift-drift root in flat matter-Lambda cosmology](../../../../../../../nonzero-redshift-drift-root-in-flat-matter-lambda-cosmology.md) is

$$
\boxed{z_* =\frac{1+\sqrt{133}}6\simeq2.089.}
$$

The [redshift drift](../../../../../../../redshift-drift.md) is positive for $0<z<z_*$ and negative for $z>z_*$. Its maximum satisfies $1=0.45s^2/\sqrt{0.3s^3+0.7}$ and lies near $z=0.949$, where $F\simeq0.240$. This zero is distinct from the acceleration transition at $z_{\rm acc}=(2\Omega_{\Lambda,0}/\Omega_{m,0})^{1/3}-1\simeq0.671$: [redshift drift](../../../../../../../redshift-drift.md) compares expansion at two epochs, while acceleration is local to one epoch.
