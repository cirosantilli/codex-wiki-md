<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the observed [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md) in $d\lambda/\lambda=dv/c$. Since $\lambda_{\rm obs}=(1+z)\lambda_{\rm rest}$,

$$
\Delta v=c\frac{\Delta z}{1+z}.
$$

For an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md) at $z=3$, $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3/2}=8H_0$, and the [redshift drift](../../../../../../redshift-drift.md) is $\dot z=4H_0-8H_0=-4H_0$. With the rounded numerical values supplied in the paper,

$$
\Delta t\simeq3\times10^8\ {
m s},\qquad
\Delta z\simeq-4(2\times10^{-18})(3\times10^8)=-2.4\times10^{-9}.
$$

Therefore the [spectroscopic velocity drift](../../../../../../spectroscopic-velocity-drift.md) accumulated over ten years is

$$
\boxed{\Delta v\simeq\frac{3\times10^8}{4}(-2.4\times10^{-9})
=-0.18\ {\rm m\,s^{-1}}=-18\ {\rm cm\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The negative sign means a shift toward shorter observed [wavelength](../../../../../../wavelength.md). Omitting the factor $1+z$ would overestimate the apparent velocity shift by four.
