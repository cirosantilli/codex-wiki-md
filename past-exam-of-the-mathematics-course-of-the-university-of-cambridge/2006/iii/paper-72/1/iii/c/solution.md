<h1 id="1/iii/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Put $B=1-2q_0>0$ and introduce $\psi>0$ through

$$
y=\frac{q_0}{B}(\cosh\psi-1).
$$

The expanding equation becomes

$$
\dot y=H_0\sqrt B\coth(\psi/2),\qquad
\frac{dy}{d\psi}=\frac{q_0}{B}\sinh\psi.
$$

Dividing these expressions yields

$$
\frac{dt}{d\psi}=\frac{q_0}{H_0B^{3/2}}(\cosh\psi-1).
$$

Integrating from the [Big Bang](../../../../../../../big-bang.md) at $\psi=0$ proves the [open matter-dominated Friedmann solution](../../../../../../../open-matter-dominated-friedmann-solution.md):

$$
\boxed{H_0t=q_0(1-2q_0)^{-3/2}(\sinh\psi-\psi),\qquad
\frac a{a_0}=\frac{q_0}{1-2q_0}(\cosh\psi-1).}
$$

The [scale factor](../../../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) grows indefinitely. At early times $y\propto t^{2/3}$, as in the matter-dominated flat case, whereas at late times $\dot y\to H_0\sqrt B$ and $y\propto t$ as curvature becomes dominant.
