<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Under the prescribed computational-basis measurement and decision rule, the first state is correctly declared when outcome zero occurs, with probability $\cos^2(\theta/2)$. The second is correctly declared on outcome one, with the same probability. Averaging the two equal priors gives

$$
\boxed{p_{\mathrm{correct}}=\cos^2(\theta/2)=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}.}
$$

One can also verify optimality directly: the two [density matrices](../../../../../../density-matrix.md) differ by $\cos\theta\,\sigma_z$. Since $\cos\theta>0$, choosing the positive eigenspace as the decision for the first state maximizes the [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) contribution to success. This is the [equal-prior discrimination of qubit states](../../../../../../equal-prior-discrimination-of-qubit-states.md) construction along the vertical axis.
