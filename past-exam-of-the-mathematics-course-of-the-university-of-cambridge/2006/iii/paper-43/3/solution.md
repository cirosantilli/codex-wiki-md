<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In the [classical risk model](../../../../../classical-risk-model.md), the insurer's surplus is

$$
U(t)=u+ct-C(t),\qquad C(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}X_j,
$$

where $u\ge0$ is initial capital, $N(t)$ is a [Poisson process](../../../../../poisson-process.md) of rate $\lambda>0$, and the claim sizes are positive [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with [expected value](../../../../../expected-value.md) $\mu$, independent of the claim-arrival process. Premium flows in at constant rate $c$. A positive [relative safety loading](../../../../../relative-safety-loading.md) $\theta$ means

$$
\boxed{c=(1+\theta)\lambda\mu,\qquad\theta>0,}
$$

so premium income exceeds the expected claim cost per unit time. The ruin [stopping time](../../../../../stopping-time.md) is $\tau=\inf\{t\ge0:U(t)<0\}$, with $\tau=\infty$ if ruin never occurs, and the [ultimate ruin probability](../../../../../ultimate-ruin-probability.md) is $\psi(u)=\mathbb P(\tau<\infty)$.

Assume the stated positive [adjustment coefficient](../../../../../adjustment-coefficient.md) $R$ exists and that the claim [moment-generating function](../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) is finite at $R$. The [Lundberg inequality](../../../../../lundberg-inequality.md) is

$$
\boxed{\psi(u)\le e^{-Ru},\qquad u\ge0.}
$$

Here is a proof, including the stopping argument. By conditioning on the [Poisson distribution](../../../../../poisson-distribution.md) of $N(t)$, the [random-sum transform identity](../../../../../random-sum-transform-identity.md) gives

$$
\mathbb E e^{rC(t)}=\exp\big(\lambda t(M(r)-1)\big).
$$

The [adjustment coefficient](../../../../../adjustment-coefficient.md) equation is $\lambda(M(R)-1)=cR$. Consequently the [exponential surplus martingale](../../../../../exponential-surplus-martingale.md)

$$
Z_t=\exp\big(R(C(t)-ct)\big),\qquad Z_0=1,
$$

has [expected value](../../../../../expected-value.md) one at every finite time. More strongly, the claim process has [independent increments](../../../../../independent-increments.md), so for $s\le t$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[Z_t\mid\mathcal F_s]
&=Z_s\,\mathbb E\exp\big(R(C(t)-C(s)-c(t-s))\big)\\
&=Z_s\exp\big((t-s)(\lambda(M(R)-1)-cR)\big)=Z_s.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $Z$ is a nonnegative [continuous-time martingale](../../../../../continuous-time-martingale.md) for the filtration generated by arrivals and claim sizes. For a fixed finite horizon $T$, the [optional stopping theorem](../../../../../optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale.md) at the bounded [stopping time](../../../../../stopping-time.md) $\tau\wedge T$ gives $\mathbb E Z_{\tau\wedge T}=1$. This bounded use is legitimate: the stopped values belong to the closed [martingale](../../../../../martingale-split.md) on $[0,T]$ given by conditional expectations of the integrable variable $Z_T$.

On $\{\tau\le T\}$, the surplus is negative, and therefore $C(\tau)-c\tau=u-U(\tau)>u$. By nonnegativity on the complementary event,

$$
1=\mathbb E Z_{\tau\wedge T}
\ge \mathbb E\big[Z_\tau\mathbf1_{\{\tau\le T\}}\big]
\ge e^{Ru}\mathbb P(\tau\le T).
$$

The ruin events increase to $\{\tau<\infty\}$ as $T$ increases, so continuity of [probability](../../../../../probability.md) gives the claimed [Lundberg inequality](../../../../../lundberg-inequality.md). No expectation identity at the unbounded [stopping time](../../../../../stopping-time.md) $\tau$ is needed. Positive loading by itself does not guarantee a positive [adjustment coefficient](../../../../../adjustment-coefficient.md) for every claim law; the existence assumption is used here.

For the [exponential distribution](../../../../../exponential-distribution.md) of claim sizes, $M(r)=1/(1-\mu r)$ for $r<1/\mu$. Substitution in the [adjustment coefficient](../../../../../adjustment-coefficient.md) equation gives

$$
\frac{\mu r}{1-\mu r}=(1+\theta)\mu r.
$$

Discard the zero root and divide by $\mu r$. The unique positive solution is

$$
\boxed{R=\frac{\theta}{(1+\theta)\mu}},
$$

which lies strictly inside the finite-transform domain.

For deterministic claim sizes, the claim [moment-generating function](../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) is $e^{\mu r}$. Define $h(r)=e^{\mu r}-1-(1+\theta)\mu r$. It satisfies $h(0)=0$, $h'(0)=-\theta\mu<0$, $h''(r)=\mu^2e^{\mu r}>0$, and $h(r)\to\infty$ as $r\to\infty$. Thus its strict [convexity](../../../../../convex-function.md) gives exactly one positive zero $R_\mu$, with $h(r)<0$ for $0<r<R_\mu$.

Put $x=\mu R=\theta/(1+\theta)\in(0,1)$. Since $-\log(1-x)>x$, we have $e^x<1/(1-x)$. Hence

$$
h(R)=e^{\mu R}-1-(1+\theta)\mu R
<\frac1{1-\mu R}-1-(1+\theta)\mu R=0.
$$

It follows that

$$
\boxed{R<R_\mu,\qquad e^{-R_\mu u}<e^{-Ru}\quad(u>0).}
$$

Thus **the deterministic-claim [Lundberg inequality](../../../../../lundberg-inequality.md) provides a smaller upper bound at positive capital**; at zero capital both bounds equal one. This is a special case of [deterministic claims maximize the adjustment coefficient at fixed mean and loading](../../../../../deterministic-claims-maximize-the-adjustment-coefficient-at-fixed-mean-and-loading.md): fixing the mean and loading, variability lowers the exponential decay coefficient relative to deterministic claims. The ordering of these upper bounds alone is not a proof that the actual [ultimate ruin probabilities](../../../../../ultimate-ruin-probability.md) are ordered.
