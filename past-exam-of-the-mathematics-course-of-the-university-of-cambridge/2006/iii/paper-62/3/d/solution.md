<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Use the flat matter-only Einstein-de Sitter approximation for the distance-redshift relation, neglecting radiation corrections at [cosmological recombination](../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md). Let $z_*$ be the [cosmological recombination](../../../../../../recombination-cosmology.md) [redshift](../../../../../../redshift.md). Adiabatic [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) cooling gives $1+z_*=T_*/T_0$. The Hubble rate is $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3/2}$, so the [comoving radial distance](../../../../../../comoving-radial-distance.md) to last scattering is

$$
\chi_* =\int_0^{z_*}\frac{dz}{H(z)}=\frac2{H_0}\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+z_*}}\right).
$$

The [angular diameter distance](../../../../../../angular-diameter-distance.md) is $D_A=\chi_* /(1+z_*)$. A proper [Hubble radius](../../../../../../hubble-radius.md) $H_*^{-1}=H_0^{-1}(1+z_*)^{-3/2}$ therefore subtends the small angle

$$
\boxed{\theta_H=\frac{H_*^{-1}}{D_A}=\frac1{2(\sqrt{1+z_*}-1)}\quad\text{radians}.}
$$

This is the [angular Hubble-radius scale in an Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../angular-hubble-radius-scale-in-an-einstein-de-sitter-universe.md); the present [Hubble constant](../../../../../../hubble-constant.md) cancels.

Taking $T_*=0.3\,\mathrm{eV}$ and the literal printed $T_0=2.35\,\mathrm{meV}$ gives $1+z_*=127.7$ and $\theta_H\simeq0.0486$ radians, or $2.8$ degrees. As noted above, that present [CMB](../../../../../../cosmic-microwave-background.md) [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) is too large by ten. With the corrected $T_0=0.235\,\mathrm{meV}$, $1+z_*=1277$ and

$$
\boxed{\theta_H\simeq0.0144\ \mathrm{rad}\simeq0.83^\circ,}
$$

which gives the familiar order-one-degree scale within the assumed cosmology. Using $0.28\,\mathrm{eV}$ to mark a few-per-cent ionization fraction changes this only modestly. These numbers refer to the Hubble-radius scale, not its diameter. In a matter-only universe the particle-horizon radius is $2H^{-1}$ and would give twice the angle; the acoustic [sound horizon](../../../../../../sound-horizon.md) is a different length again.
