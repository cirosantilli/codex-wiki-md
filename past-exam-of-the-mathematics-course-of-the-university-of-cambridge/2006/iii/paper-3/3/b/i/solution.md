<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The assumed [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) is a [Tarski monster group](../../../../../../../tarski-monster-group.md). For any $g\ne1$, its cyclic [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$ must be proper. Otherwise $G$ would be infinite cyclic, and $\langle g^2\rangle$ would be a proper nontrivial infinite [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md), contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $\langle g\rangle$ has order $p$.

Choose $h\notin\langle g\rangle$, possible because $G$ is infinite. The [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle g,h\rangle$ contains $\langle g\rangle$ strictly. If it were proper, it would have order $p$ and could not strictly contain the order-$p$ [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{G=\langle g,h\rangle.}
$$

In particular, $G$ is a [finitely generated group](../../../../../../../finitely-generated-group.md), generated by two elements.
