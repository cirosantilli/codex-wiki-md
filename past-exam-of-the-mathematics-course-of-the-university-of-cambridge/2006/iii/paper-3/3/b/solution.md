<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the replacement condition, interpret “every element has order $p$” in the usual exponent-$p$ sense: every nonidentity element has order $p$, while the identity has order one. Consider

$$
E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}C_p.
$$

Every nonzero element has order $p$. It is infinite, but no finite collection generates it: the supports of finitely many [group generators](../../../../../../generator-of-a-group.md) cover only finitely many coordinates, so a unit vector outside those coordinates is missing from their generated [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Also $E$ is abelian, and one coordinate [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $C_p$ is a proper nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\text{the exponent-}p\text{ condition implies neither finite generation nor simplicity}.}
$$

The stronger restriction on all proper [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md), rather than just individual element orders, is what forces the conclusions in parts (i) and (ii).
