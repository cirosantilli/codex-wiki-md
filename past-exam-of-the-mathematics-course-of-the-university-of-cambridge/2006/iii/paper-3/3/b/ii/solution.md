<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose $1\ne N\lhd G$ is proper. The defining property makes $N$ cyclic of order $p$. Conjugation gives a [homomorphism](../../../../../../../homomorphism.md)

$$
G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(N),
$$

whose kernel is the [centralizer](../../../../../../../centralizer.md) $C_G(N)$. The [automorphism](../../../../../../../automorphism.md) [group](../../../../../../../group-split.md) has order $p-1$, so the kernel has finite index in $G$. It contains $N$, because $N$ is abelian. If this kernel were proper, it would be an order-$p$ [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md), and its finite index would force $G$ itself to be finite. Therefore $C_G(N)=G$, so $N$ is central.

Choose $h\notin N$. Part (i) shows that $h$ has order $p$. Its cyclic [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) meets $N$ trivially, since two order-$p$ [subgroups](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) with nontrivial intersection are equal. Since $N$ is central,

$$
\langle N,h\rangle=N\times\langle h\rangle\cong C_p\times C_p.
$$

This [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md) has order $p^2$ and is proper because $G$ is infinite, contradicting the assumed order of every proper nontrivial [subgroup](../../../../../../../subgroup.md). Thus no such $N$ exists:

$$
\boxed{G\text{ is simple}.}
$$

Together with part (i), this proves that [Tarski monster groups are two-generated and simple](../../../../../../../tarski-monster-groups-are-two-generated-and-simple.md).
