<h1 id="1b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1B](../1b.md)

The [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) of $x_3^2-x_1^2-x_2^2$ is $2(-x_1,-x_2,x_3)$. On the upper cone, its [Euclidean norm](../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) is $2\sqrt2\,x_3$. Thus the [upward unit normal to an upper circular cone](../../../../../upward-unit-normal-to-an-upper-circular-cone.md) is

$$
\boxed{n=\frac{(-x_1,-x_2,x_3)}{\sqrt2\,x_3},\qquad n_3=\frac1{\sqrt2}.}
$$

Put $x_h=(x_1,x_2)$ and $p_h=(p_1,p_2)$. The hypotheses imply $|x_h|=x_3$ and $|p_h|\le p_3$. The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) therefore gives

$$
p\cdot n=\frac{p_3x_3-p_h\cdot x_h}{\sqrt2\,x_3}
\ge\frac{p_3x_3-|p_h|\,|x_h|}{\sqrt2\,x_3}
=\frac{p_3-|p_h|}{\sqrt2}\ge0.
$$

This is also a direct instance of [self-duality of a second-order cone](../../../../../self-duality-of-a-second-order-cone.md): both $p$ and the chosen unit normal belong to the closed [Lorentz cone](../../../../../second-order-cone.md).
