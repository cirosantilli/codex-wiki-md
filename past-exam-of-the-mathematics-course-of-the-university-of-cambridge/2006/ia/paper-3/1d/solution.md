<h1 id="1d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1D](../1d.md)

Let $c=1/\sqrt2$. A concrete real [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is

$$
\boxed{A_0=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&c&-c\\0&c&c\end{pmatrix}}.
$$

Its first coordinate has [eigenvalue](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-1$. The lower block has [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) $(\lambda-c)^2+c^2$, whose roots are $c\pm ic=(1\pm i)/\sqrt2$. Thus this example has all three required [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md). It is also an [orthogonal matrix](../../../../../orthogonal-matrix.md), but orthogonality is not forced by its spectrum, as the second part shows.
