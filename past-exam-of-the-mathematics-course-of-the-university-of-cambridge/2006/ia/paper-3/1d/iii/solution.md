↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False; in fact no matrix with this spectrum is diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$.** If a real invertible $P$ gave $P^{-1}AP=D$ with $D$ a real [diagonal matrix](../../../../../../diagonal-matrix.md), all [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of $A$ would be the real diagonal entries of $D$. But two of the required [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) have nonzero imaginary parts. This contradiction proves

$$
\boxed{A\text{ is not diagonalizable over }\mathbb R}.
$$

The real block form in the initial example is consistent with this: a two-dimensional rotation block is not a real diagonalization.
