<h1 id="2d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2D](../2d.md)

Both [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) contain the [identity element](../../../../../identity-element.md), so $H\cap K$ is nonempty. If $x,y\in H\cap K$, then $xy^{-1}$ belongs to both $H$ and $K$, hence to their intersection. This gives closure under inverses by setting $x=e$, and closure under products by using the inverse of $y$. Therefore **$H\cap K$ is a subgroup of $G$**, and also of both $H$ and $K$.

[Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md) states that the order of a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) divides the order of its finite ambient [group](../../../../../group-split.md); more precisely $|L|=[L:M]|M|$ for $M\le L$. Applying it twice gives

$$
|H\cap K|\mid p,\qquad |H\cap K|\mid q.
$$

Since $p$ and $q$ are [coprime](../../../../../coprime-integers.md), their only positive common divisor is one. Thus

$$
\boxed{H\cap K=\{e\}}.
$$
