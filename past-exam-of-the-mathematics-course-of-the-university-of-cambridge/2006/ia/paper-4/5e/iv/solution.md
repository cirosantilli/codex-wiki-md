<h1 id="5e/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Condition (iv) says in particular that $T$ is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md), so it is symmetric and transitive. It therefore implies (i) and (ii), closing the chain of implications.

Uniqueness is literal: if two [equivalence relations](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) both contain $R,S$ and are each contained in every such relation, each is contained in the other, so they are equal. Combining the preceding proofs gives

$$
\boxed{(\mathrm i)\Longleftrightarrow(\mathrm{ii})\Longleftrightarrow
(\mathrm{iii})\Longleftrightarrow(\mathrm{iv}).}
$$

Their common relation is the [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) generated by $R\cup S$, and under these conditions it is already obtained by the two-step composite $R\circ S$.
