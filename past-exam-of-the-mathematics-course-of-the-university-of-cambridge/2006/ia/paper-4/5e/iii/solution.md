<h1 id="5e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The inverse argument in part (i) proves that the one-sided inclusion in (iii) already implies equality:

$$
\boxed{S\circ R\subseteq R\circ S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R\circ S=S\circ R.}
$$

Parts (i) and (ii) then make $T$ both symmetric and transitive; it is reflexive from the root solution. Therefore $T$ is an [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) containing $R$ and $S$.

If $U$ is any other [equivalence relation](../../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) containing both, a witness $xRy,\ ySz$ gives $xUy,\ yUz$, and [transitivity](../../../../../../transitive-relation.md) gives $xUz$. Hence $T\subseteq U$. Thus (iii) implies the unique-smallest assertion in (iv).
