↑ **Parent:** [11](../11.md)

An [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) on a [set](../../../../../set-split.md) $A$ is a [binary relation](../../../../../binary-relation.md) that is [reflexive](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md), [symmetric](../../../../../symmetric-relation.md) and [transitive](../../../../../transitive-relation.md): every element is related to itself, related pairs may be reversed, and two consecutive related pairs imply the pair of endpoints is related.

Write $T=R\circ S$, using the printed left-to-right [composition of relations](../../../../../composition-of-relations.md) convention. [Reflexivity](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md) implies $R\subseteq T$ and $S\subseteq T$, and hence $T$ is [reflexive](../../../../../reflexive-relation.md). Reversing a two-step chain gives

$$
T^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ R^{-1}=S\circ R,
$$

since $R,S$ are [symmetric](../../../../../symmetric-relation.md). Also $R\circ R=R$ and $S\circ S=S$: one inclusion is [transitivity](../../../../../transitive-relation.md), and the reverse follows by inserting a reflexive step. These elementary identities supply the equivalences below, without presuming that a composite of [equivalence relations](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md) is automatically an [equivalence relation](../../../../../equivalence-relation.md).
