<h1 id="12f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Parametrize the [logarithmic spiral](../../../../../../logarithmic-spiral.md) by

$$
h:\mathbb R\longrightarrow X,\qquad h(t)=(e^t\cos t,e^t\sin t).
$$

The map is continuous into the ambient plane, hence into $X$ with its [subspace topology](../../../../../../subspace-topology.md), and it is onto by the definition of the spiral. It is injective because its radius $|h(t)|=e^t$ determines $t$ uniquely, even when the angular coordinate winds through repeated turns.

Every point of $X$ is nonzero. The inverse is therefore

$$
\boxed{h^{-1}(x,y)=\log\sqrt{x^2+y^2},}
$$

the restriction of a continuous function on the punctured plane. Hence $h$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) and **the spiral is homeomorphic to the [real line](../../../../../../real-line.md)**. Its accumulation at the origin causes no failure of this argument: the origin is not included in $X$.
