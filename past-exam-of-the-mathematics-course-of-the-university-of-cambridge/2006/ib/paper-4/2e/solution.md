<h1 id="2e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2E](../2e.md)

First quotient by $3$, obtaining the [quotient ring](../../../../../quotient-ring.md)

$$
R\cong\mathbb F_3[X]/(X^2+X+1).
$$

[Polynomial division](../../../../../polynomial-division.md) by this monic quadratic gives a unique representative $a+bX$ with $a,b\in\mathbb F_3$. Uniqueness holds because a nonzero multiple of a monic quadratic cannot have degree less than two. Therefore **$|R|=3^2=9$**.

In characteristic three, $X^2+X+1=(X-1)^2$. The residue $\epsilon=X-1$ is nonzero by the uniqueness of the degree-one representative, but $\epsilon^2=0$. Thus $\epsilon$ is both a [nilpotent element](../../../../../nilpotent.md) and a [zero divisor](../../../../../zero-divisor.md). Consequently **$R$ is not an integral domain**. Equivalently, its multiplication is that of $\mathbb F_3[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, rather than that of a field with nine elements.
