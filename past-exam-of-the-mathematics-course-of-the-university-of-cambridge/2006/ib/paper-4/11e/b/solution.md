<h1 id="11e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A finite [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) is a generating set, so the preceding [determinant trick](../../../../../../determinant-trick.md) applies with $R=k$ and $M=V$. If $p(t)=\det(A-tI)$, the map $k[t]\to k[\phi]$ sending $t$ to $\phi$ is a ring homomorphism. Since a [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is a polynomial expression in its entries, it sends $p(t)$ to $\det(A-\phi I)=0$. Thus

$$
\boxed{p(\phi)=0.}
$$

With the more usual monic [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) $\chi_A(t)=\det(tI-A)=(-1)^{\dim V}p(t)$, this reads $\chi_A(\phi)=0$, the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md). If the matrix convention uses column coordinates instead of the row convention above, transpose $A$; its [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) is unchanged.
