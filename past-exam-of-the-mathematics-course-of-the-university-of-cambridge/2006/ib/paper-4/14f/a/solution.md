<h1 id="14f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $K$ be a compact subset of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md), and take $x\notin K$. For each $y\in K$, the definition of a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) supplies disjoint open neighbourhoods $U_y$ of $y$ and $V_y$ of $x$. Compactness gives finitely many $U_{y_1},\ldots,U_{y_r}$ covering $K$. Their corresponding intersection $V=\bigcap_{j=1}^r V_{y_j}$ is open, contains $x$, and misses $K$. Therefore every point of the complement has an open neighbourhood contained in the complement, so **$K$ is closed**. The empty compact set is closed as well.
