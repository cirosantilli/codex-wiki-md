<h1 id="2e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](../../../../../../structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) applies because every [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md). In invariant-factor form, every finitely generated [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$ over such a domain $R$ has a decomposition

$$
\boxed{M\cong R^r\oplus R/(d_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus R/(d_s),\qquad d_1\mid d_2\mid\cdots\mid d_s,}
$$

where the $d_i$ are nonzero nonunits, $r\geq0$, and either list of summands may be empty. The free [rank](../../../../../../rank-one-quadratic-form.md) $r$ and the invariant factors $d_i$ are unique up to multiplication of each $d_i$ by a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). Equivalently the torsion part is a direct sum of cyclic [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) with prime-power annihilators. The nonunit convention omits zero summands $R/(1)$.
