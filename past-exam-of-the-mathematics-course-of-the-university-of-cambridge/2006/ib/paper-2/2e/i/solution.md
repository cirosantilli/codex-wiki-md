<h1 id="2e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) is a commutative [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) $R$ with a function $\delta:R\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ such that, for $a,b\in R$ with $b\ne0$, there are $q,r\in R$ satisfying $a=qb+r$, where either $r=0$ or $\delta(r)<\delta(b)$. One may also impose the usual monotonicity $\delta(a)\leq\delta(ab)$ for nonzero $a,b$; the example below has it.

**The [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) form a [Euclidean domain](../../../../../../euclidean-domain.md) that is not a [field](../../../../../../field.md).** Use $\delta(a)=|a|$. [Integer](../../../../../../integer.md) division by $|b|$ supplies a remainder with $0\leq r<|b|$, and changing the sign of the quotient handles negative $b$. The [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) are an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md), while $2$ has no multiplicative inverse in them. This verifies both the Euclidean property and the failure to be a [field](../../../../../../field.md).
