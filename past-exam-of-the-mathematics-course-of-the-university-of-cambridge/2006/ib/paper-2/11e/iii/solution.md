<h1 id="11e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Relabel the two primes so that $p<q$. By the [Sylow theorems](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md), the number $n_q$ of Sylow $q$-subgroups divides $p$, so it is either $1$ or $p$. But $n_q\equiv1\pmod q$ and $1<p<q$, ruling out $n_q=p$. Thus there is a unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of order $q$, which is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). It is nontrivial and proper because $1<q<pq$. Consequently **no [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of order $pq$ is simple**. No additional assumption on divisibility of $q-1$ by $p$ is required.
