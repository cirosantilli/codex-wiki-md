<h1 id="1e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take the [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) $R=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$, embedded in $\mathbb C$. It exhibits [failure of unique factorization in the ring of integers generated by square root of minus five](../../../../../../failure-of-unique-factorization-in-the-ring-of-integers-generated-by-square-root-of-minus-five.md):

$$
\boxed{6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}).}
$$

Here is why these are genuinely distinct factorizations into [irreducible elements](../../../../../../irreducible-element.md). The multiplicative [algebraic norm](../../../../../../algebraic-norm.md) $N(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2$ is a nonnegative integer and equals one only for $\pm1$, which are therefore the only [units](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). Neither two nor three is a value of $N$: if $b\ne0$ the value is at least five, and if $b=0$ it is a square. A factorization of $2$ into nonunits would require norms two and two; one of $3$ would require norms three and three; one of $1\pm\sqrt{-5}$ would require norms two and three. All are impossible. Thus all four factors are irreducible. The factors of norms four and nine cannot be [associate elements](../../../../../../associate-element.md) of those of norm six, and $1+\sqrt{-5}$ and $1-\sqrt{-5}$ are not related by a sign. Therefore $R$ is **an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md) which is not a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md)**.
