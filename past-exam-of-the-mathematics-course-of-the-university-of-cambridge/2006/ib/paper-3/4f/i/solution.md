↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**This does not define a [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md) on $\mathbb Z$.** Every singleton $\{2k\}$ is declared open, but their union over $k\in\mathbb Z$ is $2\mathbb Z$, an infinite proper subset. It is therefore not declared open, violating closure under arbitrary unions. This is the example that [finite subsets do not define a topology on an infinite set](../../../../../../finite-subsets-do-not-define-a-topology-on-an-infinite-set.md). The empty set, whole space and finite-intersection requirements do hold, but they do not compensate for the failed union axiom. This is different from the [cofinite topology](../../../../../../cofinite-topology.md), which uses finite complements rather than finite sets.
