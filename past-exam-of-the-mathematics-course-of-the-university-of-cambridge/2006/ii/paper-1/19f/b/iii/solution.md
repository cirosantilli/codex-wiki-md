<h1 id="19f/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

There are **the $p-1$ inflated quotient [characters](../../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md) of dimension one and the single [representation](../../../../../../../group-representation.md) $\rho$ of dimension $p-1$** from part (ii). These are distinct: for $p>2$ their dimensions distinguish $\rho$, and for $p=2$ its nontrivial value on $U$ distinguishes it from the trivial quotient [character](../../../../../../../character-of-a-representation.md). Their dimension squares sum to

$$
(p-1)+(p-1)^2=p(p-1)=|G|.
$$

The sum-of-squares theorem for [irreducible representations](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) leaves no room for any further irreducible. This includes $p=2$, when $G\cong C_2$ and both [representations](../../../../../../../group-representation.md) have dimension one.
