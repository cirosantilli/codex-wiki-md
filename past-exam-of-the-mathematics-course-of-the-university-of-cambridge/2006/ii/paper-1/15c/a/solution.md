<h1 id="15c/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Vary $q_a$ and $p_a$ independently, fixing the endpoint positions. [Integration by parts](../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) gives

$$
\delta S=[p_a\delta q_a]_{t_1}^{t_2}+\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left[(\dot q_a-H_{p_a})\delta p_a+(-\dot p_a-H_{q_a})\delta q_a\right]dt.
$$

The boundary term vanishes, so arbitrary interior variations imply

$$
\boxed{\dot q_a=H_{p_a},\qquad\dot p_a=-H_{q_a}.}
$$

In the Lagrangian principle only $q$ is varied independently, and $\delta\dot q=d(\delta q)/dt$; here positions and momenta are independent paths in [phase space](../../../../../../phase-space.md). The extremality condition is stationarity of the action, not necessarily a minimum. Along [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) motion,

$$
\frac{dH}{dt}=H_{q_a}\dot q_a+H_{p_a}\dot p_a+H_t=H_t,
$$

so **an explicitly time-independent [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) is conserved**.
