<h1 id="15c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The invariant is [phase-space volume](../../../../../../symplectic-volume-form.md), or area $dq\,dp$ for one degree of freedom. It does not require an autonomous [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md). The flow vector $v=(H_p,-H_q)$ has [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) $\partial_qH_p-\partial_pH_q=0$ whenever the mixed [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) agree. If $M(t)$ is the Jacobian of the flow with respect to initial coordinates, then $\dot M=Dv\,M$. Differentiating its [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) gives

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\det M=(\nabla\cdot v)\det M=0,\qquad\det M(0)=1.
$$

Thus the change-of-variables formula shows that any region carried by the flow retains its area. This proves [Liouville theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics](../../../../../../liouville-s-theorem-hamiltonian.md) even when $H=H(q,p,t)$ depends on time.
