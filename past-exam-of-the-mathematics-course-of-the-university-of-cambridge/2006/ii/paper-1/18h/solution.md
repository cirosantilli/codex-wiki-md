<h1 id="18h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [18H](../18h.md)

Let $L$ be the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) of the [separable polynomial](../../../../../separable-polynomial.md). Its [Galois group](../../../../../galois-group.md) is $G=\operatorname{Aut}_K(L)$, acting faithfully on its $n$ distinct roots, hence as a subgroup of $S_n$. An automorphism preserves each root's minimal polynomial. Conversely an isomorphism $K(\alpha)\to K(\beta)$ for roots of the same irreducible factor extends to an embedding of $L$ in an [algebraic closure](../../../../../algebraic-closure.md), and normality of the [splitting field](../../../../../splitting-field.md) makes the image $L$ itself. Thus the root orbits are precisely the irreducible factors' root sets. **The action is transitive exactly when the polynomial is irreducible.**

If $n=p$ is [prime](../../../../../prime-number.md), a transitive action has $p\mid|G|$ by orbit-stabilizer, so [Cauchy theorem for groups](../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) supplies an element of order $p$. Its permutation must be a $p$-cycle. Conversely a $p$-cycle already acts transitively on all $p$ roots. This proves the stated equivalence for [prime](../../../../../prime-number.md) degree.

The factorization theorem modulo a good [prime](../../../../../prime-number.md) says that if an [integer](../../../../../integer.md) polynomial retains its degree and has square-free reduction modulo $p$, and that reduction's irreducible factor degrees are $d_1,\ldots,d_r$, its [Galois group](../../../../../galois-group.md) over $\mathbb Q$ contains a permutation with disjoint cycle lengths $d_1,\ldots,d_r$. Equivalently the finite-field [Galois group](../../../../../galois-group.md), generated by Frobenius on the roots, occurs as an unramified decomposition subgroup. The degree-retention qualification matters for nonmonic polynomials.

For the given quintic, [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at three proves irreducibility. Modulo two it factors as

$$
x^5+x+1=(x^2+x+1)(x^3+x^2+1).
$$

Neither factor has a root in $\mathbb F_2$, so both are irreducible, and they are distinct; the reduction is separable. Thus $G$ contains an element of cycle type $(2)(3)$, whose cube is a [transposition](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md). It is also transitive of degree five.

To prove that this forces the full [symmetric group](../../../../../symmetric-group.md) here, form the [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) on the five roots with an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) for each conjugate in $G$ of that [transposition](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md). The [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is $G$-invariant, and transitivity makes its connected components all the same size. That size divides five; there is an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), so the size cannot be one. Hence the [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is connected. [Transpositions](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) along the [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of a connected [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) generate all [transpositions](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) (move along a path and conjugate successive swaps), and hence generate $S_5$. They all belong to $G$, giving

$$
\boxed{G=S_5.}
$$
