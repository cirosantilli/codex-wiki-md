<h1 id="2g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $x_j=-1+j/4$, $0\le j\le8$. These nine points satisfy $\cos(4\pi x_j)=(-1)^j$. Thus the zero [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has nine alternating error extrema of magnitude one and is minimax among [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree at most seven by the [Chebyshev alternation theorem](../../../../../../equioscillation-theorem.md). Hence **every such [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has uniform error at least one**.

One can also prove the bound directly. If the error were strictly below one everywhere, $g(x_j)$ would have sign $(-1)^j$. The [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) would give a distinct zero in each of the eight intervening intervals, contradicting the degree bound. Since the [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) error attains its maximum on the [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) interval, there is an $x$ with $|f(x)-g(x)|\ge1$.
