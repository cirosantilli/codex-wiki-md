<h1 id="21h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [21H](../21h.md)

The [Simplicial approximation theorem](../../../../../simplicial-approximation-theorem.md) states that a [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) between realizations of finite [simplicial complexes](../../../../../simplicial-complex.md) becomes homotopic to a [simplicial map](../../../../../simplicial-map.md) after sufficiently many [barycentric subdivisions](../../../../../barycentric-subdivision.md) of the domain. More precisely, a vertex assignment is a [simplicial approximation](../../../../../simplicial-approximation.md) when the image of each open star is contained in the open star of its assigned vertex; sufficiently fine subdivision permits such an assignment. The resulting straight-line [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) stays in the appropriate target simplices.

The vertices of a [barycentric subdivision](../../../../../barycentric-subdivision.md) are the barycentres of nonempty faces. An $n$-simplex has $2^{n+1}-1$ such faces. A top-dimensional subdivided simplex corresponds to a full flag of faces of sizes $1,2,\ldots,n+1$, equivalently to an ordering of the original vertices. Thus

$$
\boxed{\#\text{vertices}=2^{n+1}-1,\qquad\#\text{top-dimensional simplices}=(n+1)!.}
$$

Triangulate both copies of the sphere using finite complexes. For each fixed subdivision level there are only finitely many vertex maps to the finite target [vertex set](../../../../../vertex-set.md), hence only finitely many [simplicial maps](../../../../../simplicial-map.md). Every [continuous map](../../../../../continuous-map.md) is homotopic to one of them at some finite level. A [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of [finite sets](../../../../../finite-set.md) is countable, proving **at most countably many [homotopy classes](../../../../../homotopy-class.md)** without needing their classification by degree.
