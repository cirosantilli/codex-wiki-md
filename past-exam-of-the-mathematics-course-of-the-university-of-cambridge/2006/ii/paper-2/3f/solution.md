<h1 id="3f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3F](../3f.md)

For a nonidentity element of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ represented by a determinant-one matrix, the classification is elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic according as the squared [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) is less than, equal to or greater than four. This follows either from the [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) or from the [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) of its fixed-point equation on the boundary of the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md).

The first matrix has [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one and [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) two, and is not scalar. It is therefore **parabolic**. Its fixed-point equation is

$$
z=\frac{5z+8}{-2z-3}\iff 2(z+2)^2=0.
$$

Thus **its unique [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) is $z=-2$ on the real boundary**, of multiplicity two; infinity is not fixed and there is no [fixed point](../../../../../fixed-point.md) in the [upper half-plane](../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). The second matrix has [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) one and [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $-4$, so it is **hyperbolic**. Its two boundary [fixed points](../../../../../fixed-point.md) are $( -1\pm\sqrt3)/2$, consistently with this classification.
