<h1 id="20g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [20G](../20g.md)

Since $26\equiv2\pmod4$, the [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{26}]$ with [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) $104$. Write $P=(2,\epsilon+1)=(2,\sqrt{26})$ and $Q_\pm=(5,\epsilon\pm1)$. Reduction of $X^2-26$ modulo two gives a [double root](../../../../../double-root.md), and modulo five gives the roots $\pm1$. Thus $N(P)=2$, $N(Q_\pm)=5$, and $(5)=Q_+Q_-$. Directly $P^2=(4,2\sqrt{26},26)=(2)$, since $26-6\cdot4=2$. The element $\epsilon+1=6+\sqrt{26}$ belongs to $P$ and $Q_+$ and has [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) ten, so equality of [ideal norms](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) gives

$$
\boxed{(2)=P^2,\quad(5)=Q_+Q_-,\quad(\epsilon+1)=PQ_+.}
$$

The Minkowski bound is $\sqrt{104}/2=\sqrt{26}<6$, so every [ideal class](../../../../../ideal-class.md) contains an [integral ideal](../../../../../integral-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) at most five. Three is inert and gives no [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) three; [ideals](../../../../../ideal.md) of [ideal norm](../../../../../ideal-norm.md) four are $P^2$, principal. Thus the class group is generated by $P,Q_+,Q_-$, and the displayed relations make every class either trivial or $[P]$. The class $[P]$ is nontrivial: a generator would have [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) $\pm2$, but $x^2-26y^2=\pm2$ would give $x^2\equiv2$ or $11\pmod{13}$, neither a [quadratic residue](../../../../../quadratic-residue.md). Hence **the [class number](../../../../../class-number.md) is two**.

The unit $\epsilon=5+\sqrt{26}$ has [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) $-1$. To show it is fundamental, suppose $1<u<\epsilon$ is a positive real embedding of a unit $x+y\sqrt{26}$. Its conjugate is $\pm1/u$, so $y=(u-u')/(2\sqrt{26})$ is a [positive integer](../../../../../positive-integer.md) less than two. Thus $y=1$, and $x^2-26=\pm1$ forces $x=\pm5$. Only $5+\sqrt{26}$ is greater than one, contrary to the strict inequality. Therefore no smaller unit exceeds one. Multiplying any positive unit by a suitable power of $\epsilon^{-1}$ puts it in $[1,\epsilon)$, proving that all units are $\pm\epsilon^n$.

An element of [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) $\pm10$ has [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) $PQ_+$ or $PQ_-$ by [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md). The first is generated by $\epsilon+1$; the second by $\epsilon-1=4+\sqrt{26}$, of [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) $-10$. Two generators of the same [principal ideal](../../../../../principal-ideal.md) differ by a unit. Therefore all, and only, the integer solutions are

$$
\boxed{x+\sqrt{26}y=\pm\epsilon^n(\epsilon\pm1),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

The two displayed signs are independent; multiplication by a norm-minus-one unit exchanges the two [field norm](../../../../../field-norm.md) signs.
