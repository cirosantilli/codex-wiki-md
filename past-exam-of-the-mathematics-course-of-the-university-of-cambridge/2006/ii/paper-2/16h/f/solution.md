<h1 id="16h/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

**True.** Divide a [limit ordinal](../../../../../../limit-ordinal.md) $\lambda$ by $\omega$ using part a: $\lambda=\omega\alpha+n$ with $n<\omega$. If $n>0$, the right side is a successor, contrary to the limit assumption. Hence $n=0$ and $\lambda=\omega\alpha$. For a nonzero limit, $\alpha>0$; if zero is allowed as a limit, it is represented by $\alpha=0$.
