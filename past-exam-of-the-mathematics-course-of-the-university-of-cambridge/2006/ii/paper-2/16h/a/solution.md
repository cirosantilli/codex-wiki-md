↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

**True.** For $\beta>0$, multiplication $\gamma\mapsto\beta\gamma$ is strictly increasing and [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) at [limit ordinals](../../../../../../limit-ordinal.md), and $\beta\gamma\ge\gamma$. Thus there is a greatest $\gamma$ with $\beta\gamma\le\alpha$: the set is bounded, and its supremum still satisfies the inequality by continuity if it is a limit. The remaining ordered interval in $\alpha$ has an [ordinal](../../../../../../ordinal.md) [order type](../../../../../../order-type.md) $\delta$, so $\alpha=\beta\gamma+\delta$. If $\delta\ge\beta$, then $\beta(\gamma+1)\le\alpha$, contradicting maximality. Hence $\delta<\beta$.
