A [measure space](../../../../../../measure-space.md) consists of a set $\Omega$, a sigma-algebra $\mathcal A$ of subsets, and a countably additive map $\mu:\mathcal A\to[0,\infty]$ with $\mu(\varnothing)=0$. A sigma-algebra contains $\Omega$ and is closed under complements and [countable unions](../../../../../../countable-union.md). [Countable additivity](../../../../../../countable-additivity.md) means $\mu(\bigcup_nA_n)=\sum_n\mu(A_n)$ for pairwise disjoint [measurable sets](../../../../../../measurable-set.md). Such sets are called [measurable](../../../../../../measurability.md); a real function is [measurable](../../../../../../measurability.md) when the inverse image of every [Borel set](../../../../../../borel-set.md) belongs to $\mathcal A$.
