<h1 id="25j/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Set $Y_n=\sup_{m\ge n}|f_m|$. It is an extended-real [measurable function](../../../../../../measurable-function.md) and decreases to $Y=\limsup_m|f_m|$. If $f_n\to0$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md), then $Y_n\to0$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). For any $\epsilon>0$, the decreasing sets $\{Y_n>\epsilon\}$ have intersection contained, up to a [null set](../../../../../../null-set.md), in the exceptional set for convergence. Continuity from above of the [probability measure](../../../../../../probability-measure.md) gives $P(Y_n>\epsilon)\to0$.

Conversely, if $Y_n\to0$ in [probability](../../../../../../probability.md), then $P(Y>\epsilon)\le P(Y_n>\epsilon)\to0$ for every positive $\epsilon$. Taking rational $\epsilon>0$ gives $Y=0$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md), so $|f_n|\to0$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). This proves both directions, even when some individual $Y_n$ take the value infinity.
