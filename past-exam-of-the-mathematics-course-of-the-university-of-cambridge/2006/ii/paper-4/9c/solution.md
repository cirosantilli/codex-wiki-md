<h1 id="9c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9C](../9c.md)

For a uniform circular cross-section, averaging gives $\langle x^2\rangle=\langle y^2\rangle=R^2/4$; for a uniform axial coordinate in $[-h,h]$, $\langle z^2\rangle=h^2/3$. Symmetry makes the coordinate axes principal axes, since all products of distinct coordinates integrate to zero. Thus the [principal moments of inertia](../../../../../principal-moment-of-inertia.md) about the centre are

$$
\boxed{I_x=I_y=M\left(\frac{R^2}{4}+\frac{h^2}{3}\right),\qquad I_z=\frac{MR^2}{2}}.
$$

The [inertia tensor](../../../../../inertia-tensor.md) is a scalar multiple of identity exactly when $h^2/3=R^2/4$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\frac{2h}{R}=\sqrt3}
$$

is the height-to-radius ratio for sphere-like rotational inertia; the height-to-diameter ratio is $\sqrt3/2$. The geometric shape remains cylindrical, but its angular momentum is parallel to angular velocity for every axis, just as for a sphere.
