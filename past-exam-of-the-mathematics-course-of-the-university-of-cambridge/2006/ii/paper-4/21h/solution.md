<h1 id="21h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [21H](../21h.md)

Identify the pointed [torus](../../../../../torus.md) with $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ with $p$ at the origin. Its [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) is $\mathbb Z^2$, generated by the two coordinate loops. A [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) fixing $p$ induces an automorphism of this group, giving

$$
\boxed{\varphi(f)=f_*\in GL(2,\mathbb Z)}.
$$

Functoriality makes it a homomorphism. It is nontrivial, indeed onto: every integer invertible matrix defines a pointed [torus](../../../../../torus.md) [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) by $x\mapsto Ax$ modulo the [euclidean lattice](../../../../../euclidean-lattice.md).

If $f_*=I$, lift $f$ to a continuous $F:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ with $F(0)=0$. Its deck-transformation law is $F(x+n)=F(x)+n$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z^2$, because the induced map on coordinate loops is identity. Then

$$
H_t([x])=[(1-t)F(x)+tx]
$$

is well defined: adding $n$ changes the bracketed expression by exactly $n$. It is a continuous pointed [homotopy](../../../../../homotopy.md) from $f$ to identity. Hence **every kernel element is homotopic to the identity**, although the intermediate maps need not be homeomorphisms.
