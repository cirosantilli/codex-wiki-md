<h1 id="24h/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If two simple closed [geodesics](../../../../../../geodesic.md) were disjoint on a sphere-like surface, they would bound an [annulus](../../../../../../annulus-mathematics.md). The [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](../../../../../../gauss-bonnet-theorem.md) applied to it gives $\int_AK\,dA+\int_{\partial A}k_gds=2\pi\chi(A)=0$. Both boundary [geodesic](../../../../../../geodesic.md) curvatures vanish, but positive [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md) makes the area integral strictly positive, a contradiction. Hence **the two [geodesics](../../../../../../geodesic.md) must intersect**.

The assertion fails for nonzero constant signed [geodesic curvature](../../../../../../geodesic-curvature.md). On the unit round sphere a latitude of colatitude $\theta$, oriented in increasing longitude, has $k_g=\cot\theta$. The latitude of colatitude $\pi-\theta$ has the opposite value; reversing its orientation gives the same value as the first. Choosing $0<\theta<\pi/2$ produces two disjoint smooth simple closed curves with the same positive nonzero curvature. Reversing both handles negative values, and $\theta$ can be chosen for any specified nonzero $\lambda$. Thus **constant nonzero [geodesic curvature](../../../../../../geodesic-curvature.md) does not force intersection**.
