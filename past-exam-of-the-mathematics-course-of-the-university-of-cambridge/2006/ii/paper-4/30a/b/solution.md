<h1 id="30a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A smooth $2\pi$-periodic function has coefficients $g_k=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}g(x)e^{-ikx}dx$ decaying faster than any power, by repeated integration by parts. Thus its [Fourier series](../../../../../../fourier-series-split.md) and every differentiated series converge absolutely and uniformly to the corresponding function and derivatives. Solving each spatial [Fourier mode](../../../../../../fourier-mode.md) gives

$$
\boxed{u(t,x)=tg_0+\sum_{k\ne0}g_k\frac{\sin(kt)}k e^{ikx}}.
$$

The zero-frequency term is linear in time, not obtained by division by zero. Uniform convergence on compact time intervals permits termwise differentiation and verifies the initial conditions and the [wave equation](../../../../../../wave-equation-split.md).
