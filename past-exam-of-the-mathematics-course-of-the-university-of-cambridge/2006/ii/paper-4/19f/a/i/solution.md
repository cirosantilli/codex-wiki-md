<h1 id="19f/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose a nonzero vector in an [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md). Its [finite group](../../../../../../../finite-group.md) orbit spans a finite-dimensional [invariant subspace](../../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md), which must be the whole representation. The representing matrices of an [abelian group](../../../../../../../abelian-group.md) commute. Choose an [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) of one matrix; all other matrices preserve it. Repeatedly choosing an [eigenspace](../../../../../../../eigenspace.md) for the restrictions of the remaining matrices gives a common [eigenvector](../../../../../../../eigenvector.md). Its line is invariant under the whole group. Irreducibility therefore makes the representation that line, proving **every irreducible complex representation of a finite [abelian group](../../../../../../../abelian-group.md) is one-dimensional**.
