<h1 id="19f/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Given a [one-dimensional character](../../../../../../../one-dimensional-character.md) $\chi$ of $Z$, form the nonzero space of functions $f:G\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $f(zg)=\chi(z)f(g)$. Right translation $[\rho(h)f](g)=f(gh)$ defines a representation of dimension $[G:Z]$. Centrality gives $\rho(z)f(g)=f(gz)=\chi(z)f(g)$, so $Z$ acts by $\chi$ on the entire space. This representation has an irreducible summand: average any positive [Hermitian inner product](../../../../../../../hermitian-form.md) over the [finite group](../../../../../../../finite-group.md), so invariant subspaces have invariant [orthogonal complements](../../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md), and decompose inductively by dimension. Every irreducible summand has the same central [one-dimensional character](../../../../../../../one-dimensional-character.md) $\chi$. Thus **every [irreducible representation](../../../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) of $Z$ occurs by restriction in the requested way**.
