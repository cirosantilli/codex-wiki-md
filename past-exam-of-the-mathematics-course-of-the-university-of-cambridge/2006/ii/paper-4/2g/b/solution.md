<h1 id="2g/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Suppose the [continuous functions](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) converged pointwise to the rationality indicator. Each $F_N$ is closed, since

$$
F_N=\bigcap_{n\ge N}\left(f_n^{-1}(( -\infty,1/3])\cup f_n^{-1}([2/3,\infty))\right).
$$

[Pointwise convergence](../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md) implies that every point belongs to some $F_N$: its eventual values lie near either zero or one. Thus $\mathbb R=\bigcup_NF_N$. By the [Baire category theorem](../../../../../../baire-category-theorem.md), some $F_N$ contains a nonempty open interval $I$. Choose a rational $r\in I$ and an irrational $s\in I$. For sufficiently large $n\ge N$, convergence gives $f_n(r)>2/3$ and $f_n(s)<1/3$. The [intermediate value theorem](../../../../../../intermediate-value-theorem.md) then forces $f_n$ to take the value $1/2$ between them, at a point in $I\subseteq F_N$. This contradicts the defining condition of $F_N$. Therefore **the rationality indicator is not a pointwise limit of [continuous functions](../../../../../../continuous-function.md)**, the [rationality indicator is not Baire class one](../../../../../../rationality-indicator-is-not-baire-class-one.md) result.
