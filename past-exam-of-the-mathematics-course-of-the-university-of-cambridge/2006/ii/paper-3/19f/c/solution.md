<h1 id="19f/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [dual representation](../../../../../../dual-representation.md) on $V^*$ is $(g\ell)(v)=\ell(g^{-1}v)$. The bilinear form $\varepsilon(z,w)=\det(z,w)$ on $\mathbb C^2$ is nondegenerate and invariant under [SU(2)](../../../../../../su-2-group.md), because every group matrix has determinant one. Thus it gives an equivariant isomorphism $\mathbb C^2\to(\mathbb C^2)^*$. Taking the $n$th [symmetric power](../../../../../../symmetric-power.md) gives an equivariant isomorphism between its $n$th symmetric power and its dual. Since homogeneous polynomials realize $V_n=\operatorname{Sym}^n((\mathbb C^2)^*)$, this proves

$$
\boxed{V_n^*\cong V_n.}
$$
