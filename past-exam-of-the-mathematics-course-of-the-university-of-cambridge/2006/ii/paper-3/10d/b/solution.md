<h1 id="10d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

After its giant phases, the Sun ejects much of its envelope and leaves a carbon–oxygen [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md). The remnant mass lies below the [Chandrasekhar limit](../../../../../../chandrasekhar-limit.md). Its support is chiefly [electron degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../electron-degeneracy-pressure.md): the [Pauli exclusion principle](../../../../../../pauli-exclusion-principle.md) forces electrons into distinct momentum states even at low temperature, giving $P\propto\rho^{5/3}$ when they are nonrelativistic. Consequently pressure support need not cease when nuclear burning ends. The [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) subsequently cools and fades.

The process called inverse beta decay here is [electron capture](../../../../../../electron-capture.md), $e^-+p\to n+\nu_e$, becomes possible for free particles when the electron's total energy exceeds approximately $(m_n-m_p)c^2\simeq2.6m_ec^2$, corresponding to a kinetic energy about $1.6m_ec^2$. Electron capture on nuclei has thresholds modified by nuclear binding energies. In a solar-mass progenitor's ordinary [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md) remnant, the [Fermi energy](../../../../../../fermi-energy.md) is below the relevant capture thresholds, so the electron population providing support is stable. At much higher density, electron capture can reduce the electron density and pressure and promote collapse; this is why stability against [electron capture](../../../../../../electron-capture.md) matters in assessing degeneracy support. **The Sun ends as a cooling white dwarf, rather than a neutron star.**
