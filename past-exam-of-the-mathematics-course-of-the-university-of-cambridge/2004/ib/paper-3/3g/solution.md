<h1 id="3g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3G](../3g.md)

The [Euler formula for a sphere](../../../../../euler-formula-for-a-sphere.md), applied to the surface of a convex polyhedron, is **$V-E+F=2$**. In a regular polyhedron let $q\ge3$ be the common number of edges incident at a vertex. Counting vertex-edge and face-edge incidences, pentagonal faces give $qV=2E$ and $5F=2E$. Hence

$$
2=E\left(\frac2q-1+\frac25\right),
$$

whose right-hand side can be positive only if $q<10/3$. Thus $q=3$. Substitution then yields $2=E/15$, proving

$$
\boxed{E=30,\qquad V=20,\qquad F=12.}
$$

These are the counts of the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md). Here regular polyhedron is understood in the convex, ordinary-face sense used by the stated Euler formula; self-intersecting star constructions do not share these hypotheses.
