<h1 id="14f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The matrix with rows $u,v,e_3$ has determinant

$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&3&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}=3-4=-1.
$$

Its inverse has integer entries by the adjugate formula, so these rows form an integer [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of $L$. The [unimodular basis test for an integer direct summand](../../../../../../unimodular-basis-test-for-an-integer-direct-summand.md) now gives

$$
\boxed{L=N\oplus\mathbb Z(0,0,1).}
$$

Thus **$N$ is a [direct summand](../../../../../../direct-summand.md)**, with the displayed complement. This proves integral splitting, not just real or rational linear independence.
