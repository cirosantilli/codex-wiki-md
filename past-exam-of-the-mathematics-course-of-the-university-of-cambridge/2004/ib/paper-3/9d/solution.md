<h1 id="9d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [9D](../9d.md)

With the proton treated as fixed and electron mass $m$, the classical [energy](../../../../../energy.md) and [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) are

$$
E=\frac12m|\dot{\mathbf r}|^2-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r},\qquad
\mathbf L=m\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}.
$$

For a circular orbit, $L=mrv$ and [Coulomb's law](../../../../../coulomb-s-law.md) determines its [centripetal acceleration](../../../../../centripetal-acceleration.md):

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2},\qquad mrv=n\hbar.
$$

Eliminating $v$ gives $r=4\pi\epsilon_0n^2\hbar^2/(me^2)$. Thus the [Bohr radius](../../../../../bohr-radius.md) and orbit radii are

$$
\boxed{a=\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{me^2},\qquad r_n=n^2a,\quad n=1,2,\ldots.}
$$

The [kinetic energy](../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) is half the magnitude of the attractive [potential energy](../../../../../potential-energy.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{E_n=-\frac{e^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0r_n}
=-\frac{me^4}{2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2n^2}.}
$$

This is the circular-orbit [Bohr model](../../../../../bohr-model.md); including proton recoil replaces $m$ by the reduced mass.
