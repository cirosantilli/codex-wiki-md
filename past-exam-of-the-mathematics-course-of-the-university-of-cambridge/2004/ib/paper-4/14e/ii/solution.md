<h1 id="14e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

On $|z|=3$, the perturbation has modulus $|icz+8|\leq3c+8<11<27=|z^3|$. Thus [Rouché's theorem](../../../../../../rouche-s-theorem.md) gives **three roots inside $|z|<3$, counted with multiplicity**.

For the quadrant information, compare instead with $P_0(z)=z^3+8$, whose roots are $r_1=-2$, $r_2=1+i\sqrt3$, $r_3=1-i\sqrt3$. Their pairwise distances are $2\sqrt3$. On the circle $|z-r_j|=1$,

$$
|P_0(z)|=\prod_{\ell=1}^3|z-r_\ell|\geq(2\sqrt3-1)^2>3,
\qquad |icz|\leq c(|r_j|+1)=3c<3.
$$

The three unit discs are disjoint, and [Rouché localization of polynomial roots](../../../../../../rouche-localization-of-polynomial-roots.md) gives one root in each, counted with multiplicity. The disc around $1+i\sqrt3$ lies strictly in the first quadrant in its interior; that around $1-i\sqrt3$ lies in the fourth quadrant; and that around $-2$ lies in $\operatorname{Re}z<-1$. Consequently **there is exactly one root in each of those two right-hand quadrants, and exactly one root in the left half-plane**. The same count also shows that each root is simple.
