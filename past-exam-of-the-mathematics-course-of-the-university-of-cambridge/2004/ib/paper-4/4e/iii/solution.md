<h1 id="4e/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose an analytic extension $F$ existed on the punctured plane. The [identity theorem](../../../../../../identity-theorem.md) would extend $F^2=z$ from $D$ to that connected domain, so $F$ would never vanish and differentiation would give $2F'/F=1/z$. Integrate around the positively oriented unit circle. The [argument principle](../../../../../../argument-principle.md) says $\int F'/F$ is $2\pi i$ times the integer winding number of the closed curve $F(e^{it})$ about zero, whereas

$$
2\oint\frac{F'}Fdz=\oint\frac{dz}{z}=2\pi i.
$$

This would make that winding number $1/2$, a contradiction. Hence **no such analytic extension exists**. Equivalently, continuation of a square root once around zero reverses its sign, preventing a single-valued holomorphic function on the punctured plane.
