<h1 id="11e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [Hermitian operator](../../../../../../hermitian-operator.md) $A$, every [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) is real. Indeed, if $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\ne0$, self-adjointness gives $\lambda\langle v,v\rangle=\langle Av,v\rangle=\langle v,Av\rangle=\overline\lambda\langle v,v\rangle$. To prove the finite-dimensional [spectral theorem](../../../../../../spectral-theorem.md), proceed by induction on the dimension. The [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) supplies a root of the characteristic polynomial, hence a unit eigenvector $v$. Its [orthogonal complement](../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) is invariant: for $w\perp v$, $\langle Aw,v\rangle=\langle w,Av\rangle=0$. The restriction of $A$ to that complement is again Hermitian. Induction supplies an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of eigenvectors there; adjoining $v$ gives an orthonormal eigenbasis of the whole space. The zero-dimensional case is immediate. Consequently **a Hermitian operator is diagonal in an orthonormal basis, with real diagonal entries**.
