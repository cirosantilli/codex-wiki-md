<h1 id="2f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $P(x)$ denote the polynomial under consideration. The change $x\mapsto x+1$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb Q[x]$ and preserves degrees, so it preserves reducibility. Its transformed polynomial is

$$
P(x+1)=\frac{(x+1)^p-1}{x}
=\sum_{j=1}^p\binom pjx^{j-1}.
$$

It is monic; every other coefficient is divisible by $p$, because $p$ is prime and $1\leq j<p$; and its constant coefficient is $p$, not divisible by $p^2$. The [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) proves that $P(x+1)$ is irreducible, and therefore **$P$ is irreducible**. The argument also includes $p=2$, when the polynomial is linear. This is the prime-index case of an [irreducible polynomial](../../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) arising as a [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../../../../cyclotomic-polynomial.md).
