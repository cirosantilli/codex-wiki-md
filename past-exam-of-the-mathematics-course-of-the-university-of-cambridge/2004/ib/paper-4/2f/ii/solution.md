<h1 id="2f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A cubic over a [field](../../../../../../field.md) is reducible exactly when it has a root in that field: a nontrivial factorization must contain a degree-one factor. By the [rational root theorem](../../../../../../rational-root-theorem.md), a rational root of this monic integer polynomial must be an integer dividing its constant coefficient, hence either $1$ or $-1$. Substitution gives $-2$ and $4$, respectively. Neither is zero. Thus **the cubic is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$**.
