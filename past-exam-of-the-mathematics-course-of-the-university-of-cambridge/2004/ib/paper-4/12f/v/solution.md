<h1 id="12f/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

Use the usual convention that a [ring homomorphism](../../../../../../ring-homomorphism.md) preserves the identity. Such a map from the [quotient ring](../../../../../../quotient-ring.md) to $\mathbb C$ fixes $\mathbb Q$: it fixes integers, and preservation of inverses fixes rational numbers. It is therefore determined by the image $\zeta$ of the class of $x$. The defining relation forces $\zeta^3=1$. Conversely, evaluation at any such $\zeta$ annihilates $(x^3-1)$ and hence factors through the quotient. Thus **there are exactly three unital homomorphisms**, namely

$$
\boxed{[f]\longmapsto f(\zeta),\qquad \zeta\in\{1,e^{2\pi i/3},e^{4\pi i/3}\}.}
$$

If the convention permits nonunital ring homomorphisms, add the zero map. These are the only extra possibilities: the image of $1$ is an idempotent in $\mathbb C$, hence either zero or one, and image zero forces the whole map to vanish.
