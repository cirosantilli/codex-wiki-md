<h1 id="6b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6B](../6b.md)

In three dimensions, the general rank-two [isotropic tensor](../../../../../isotropic-tensor.md) is $a\delta_{ij}$ and the general rank-three [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) invariant under proper rotations is $b\epsilon_{ijk}$. Here $\delta$ is the [Kronecker delta](../../../../../kronecker-delta.md) and $\epsilon$ the [Levi-Civita symbol](../../../../../levi-civita-symbol.md). The latter is orientation-sensitive: an ordinary third-rank [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) invariant under all orthogonal transformations is zero instead. This distinction does not change the stress conclusion.

For an isotropic material, the constitutive [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) must consequently have $A_{ijk}=b\epsilon_{ijk}$ in the proper-rotation convention. Hence $\sigma_{ij}=b\epsilon_{ijk}B_k=-\sigma_{ji}$. Mechanical stress in the model is also symmetric, so $\sigma_{ij}=-\sigma_{ij}$ and every component vanishes. Thus **nonzero stress linear in the field requires anisotropic material coefficients**. This is the [isotropic linear magnetostriction vanishes](../../../../../isotropic-linear-magnetostriction-vanishes.md) restriction, not a claim that all higher-order [magnetostriction](../../../../../magnetostriction.md) is impossible in isotropic materials.
