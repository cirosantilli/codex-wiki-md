<h1 id="5a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The zeros of the denominator satisfy $e^z=\ell\pi$ with nonzero integer $\ell$, since an exponential never vanishes. Equivalently the [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) are

$$
z_{m,k}=\log(m\pi)+ik\pi,\qquad m\geq1,\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
$$

At such a point $e^{z_{m,k}}=(-1)^km\pi$, and the derivative of the denominator is $e^z\cos(e^z)=(-1)^{m+k}m\pi\ne0$. All these [poles](../../../../../../pole.md) are simple, with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_{z_{m,k}}\frac1{\sin(e^z)}=\frac{(-1)^{m+k}}{m\pi}.}
$$
