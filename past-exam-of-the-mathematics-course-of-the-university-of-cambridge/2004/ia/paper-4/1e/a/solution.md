<h1 id="1e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [Euclidean algorithm](../../../../../../euclidean-algorithm.md) gives

$$
100=79+21,\qquad79=3\cdot21+16,\qquad21=16+5,\qquad16=3\cdot5+1.
$$

Back substitution produces the [Bézout's identity](../../../../../../bezout-identity.md)

$$
1=16-3(21-16)=4(79-3\cdot21)-3\cdot21
=19\cdot79-15\cdot100.
$$

Hence a positive [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) solution is $\boxed{m=19,\ n=15}$. More generally, subtracting this identity from any other solution shows $79(m-19)=100(n-15)$. Since $79$ and $100$ are [coprime](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md), the [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) solutions are $m=19+100k$, $n=15+79k$; both are positive precisely when $k\geq0$.
