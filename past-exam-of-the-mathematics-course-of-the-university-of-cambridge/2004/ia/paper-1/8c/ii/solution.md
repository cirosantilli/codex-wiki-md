<h1 id="8c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**No.** For every $n\geq2$, take

$$
a_j=e_j\quad(1\leq j\leq n-1),\qquad
 a_n=e_1+\cdots+e_{n-1}
$$

in $\mathbb R^n$. These form a [minimally dependent vector family](../../../../../../minimally-dependent-vector-family.md): the full family is dependent because $a_n-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_j=0$.

If the omitted [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) is $a_n$, the remaining coordinate [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) are independent. If the omitted [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) is $e_k$, a relation among the retained [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) has an $e_k$ coordinate coming only from $a_n$, so its coefficient must be zero; the coefficients of all retained coordinate [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) must then be zero. Thus every $(n-1)$-element subfamily is [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md), as required for a counterexample. When $n=1$, the empty subfamily is independent while the one-vector family consisting of the [zero vector](../../../../../../zero-vector.md) is not, so the answer remains negative.
