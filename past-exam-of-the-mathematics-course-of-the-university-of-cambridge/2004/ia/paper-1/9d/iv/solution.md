<h1 id="9d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For the final limit, near zero $\cos x>0$, so the real logarithm is well defined. First apply the proved rule to $\tan x/x$ on the right: its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) ratio is $\sec^2x\to1$. Next apply it to $\log(\cos x)/x^2$; the [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) ratio is

$$
\frac{-\tan x}{2x}\longrightarrow-\frac12.
$$

The original quotient is even, so the left limit equals the right limit. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\log\cos x}{x^2}=-\frac12.}
$$
