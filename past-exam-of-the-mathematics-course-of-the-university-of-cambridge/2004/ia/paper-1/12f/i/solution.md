<h1 id="12f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Rearranging the assumed positive-term ratio inequality gives

$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\leq\frac{a_n}{b_n}.
$$

Induction therefore yields $a_n/b_n\leq a_1/b_1$ for every $n\geq1$. In particular

$$
0\leq\sum_{n=1}^N a_n
\leq\frac{a_1}{b_1}\sum_{n=1}^N b_n
\leq\frac{a_1}{b_1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n.
$$

The positive [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) of the first [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) are nondecreasing and bounded above, so the [bounded monotone sequence theorem](../../../../../../bounded-monotone-sequence-theorem.md) proves that they converge. This is the [ratio comparison test](../../../../../../ratio-comparison-test.md), and establishes **convergence of $\sum a_n$**.
