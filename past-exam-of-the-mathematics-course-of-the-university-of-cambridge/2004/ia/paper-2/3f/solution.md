For [random variables](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) with finite second [moments](../../../../../moment.md), their [covariance](../../../../../covariance.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb E\big[(X-\mathbb EX)(Y-\mathbb EY)\big]=\mathbb E[XY]-\mathbb EX\,\mathbb EY.}
$$

The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md) ensures the product is integrable under this hypothesis. More generally the definition works whenever the displayed [expectations](../../../../../expected-value.md) exist. Assertions about arbitrary [random variables](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) below are understood on this domain: without suitable moments a [covariance](../../../../../covariance.md) need not be defined at all.
