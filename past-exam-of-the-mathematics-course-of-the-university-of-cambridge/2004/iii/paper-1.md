## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) says that if $B$ is a commutative [Noetherian ring](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), then $B[t]$ is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring); iteration treats any finite number of variables. Here is a complete leading-coefficient proof. For an [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $I\subseteq B[t]$, let $L_d$ be the set of degree-$d$ coefficients of its polynomials of degree at most $d$, including zero coefficients. These are [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of $B$, and multiplication by $t$ gives $L_d\subseteq L_{d+1}$. The [ascending chain condition](../../../algebra.md#ascending-chain-condition) makes the chain stationary from some $N$ onward. Choose finite generators for $L_0,\ldots,L_N$ and lift them to polynomials $f_{dj}\in I$ of degree at most $d$. If $f\in I$ has degree $m$, set $d=\min(m,N)$ and express its leading coefficient as $\sum_j a_{dj}b_j$, where $a_{dj}$ is the chosen degree-$d$ coefficient. Then $f-\sum_j b_jt^{m-d}f_{dj}$ has smaller degree. Induction proves that the finitely many lifts generate $I$. This proves the theorem, including coefficient [rings](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) with [zero divisors](../../../mathematics.md#zero-divisor).

Put $A=\mathbb CG$ and $L=\mathbb C[X,X^{-1}]$. The [infinite dihedral group](../../../representation-theory.md#infinite-dihedral-group) has normal forms $X^m$ and $X^mY$. The relations move every $Y$ to the right and reduce its exponent modulo two; the action $X:n\mapsto n+1$, $Y:n\mapsto-n$ on $\mathbb Z$ shows that these forms are distinct. Thus

$$
A=L\oplus LY,\qquad Yf(X)=f(X^{-1})Y.
$$

The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) makes $\mathbb C[X]$ [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), and its [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) $L$ is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring): an [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of a [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) is generated by the images of the finite generators of its contraction. Since $A$ is free of rank two over $L$ on the left, it is a [Noetherian module](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-module) over $L$. Every [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) of $A$ is an $L$-[submodule](../../../module-theory.md#submodule), so all its ascending chains stabilize. Therefore **$\mathbb CG$ is left Noetherian**.

For the [center of the infinite dihedral group algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#center-of-the-infinite-dihedral-group-algebra), write an element as $f(X)+g(X)Y$. Commuting it with $X$ gives $g(X)(X^{-1}-X)=0$ in the Laurent [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain), forcing $g=0$. Commuting $f(X)$ with $Y$ then gives $f(X)=f(X^{-1})$. A symmetric [Laurent polynomial](../../../polynomial.md#laurent-polynomial) is a linear combination of $1$ and $X^j+X^{-j}$, $j\ge1$. With $z=X+X^{-1}$, the recursion

$$
S_0=2,\quad S_1=z,\quad S_{j+1}=zS_j-S_{j-1}
$$

expresses $S_j=X^j+X^{-j}$ as a polynomial in $z$. Conversely every polynomial in $z$ is central. The highest Laurent exponent of a nonzero polynomial in $z$ shows that $z$ is transcendental. Hence

$$
\boxed{R=Z(A)=\mathbb C[z],\qquad z=X+X^{-1}.}
$$

The identities $X^{-1}=z-X$ and $X^2-zX+1=0$ give $L=R+RX$. Independence follows by applying $X\mapsto X^{-1}$ to a relation $f(z)+Xg(z)=0$ and subtracting: $(X-X^{-1})g(z)=0$, so $g=f=0$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{A=R\oplus RX\oplus RY\oplus RXY}
$$

is a free $R$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) of rank four.

Let $M$ be a nonzero [simple module](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) on the left. For any $m\ne0$, $M=Am$, so it is finite over $R$. Every central $r\notin P=\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ acts bijectively: its kernel and image are $A$-[submodules](../../../module-theory.md#submodule), and simplicity forces kernel zero and image $M$. To prove that $P$ is maximal, not merely prime, put $B=R/P$; then $M$ is a faithful finite $B$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). For $0\ne b\in B$, $bM=M$. If $m_1,\ldots,m_d$ generate $M$, write $m_i=b\sum_jc_{ij}m_j$. The [adjugate matrix](../../../linear-algebra.md#adjugate-matrix) identity for $I-bC$ shows that $\det(I-bC)$ annihilates every generator and hence $M$. Faithfulness makes this determinant zero in $B$. Its constant term is one, so it has form $1-bq$, proving $bq=1$. Thus $B$ is a [field](../../../algebra.md#field), and **$P$ is a [maximal ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#maximal-ideal) of $R$**. This proves the general principle for [simple modules over an algebra finite over its center](../../../module-theory.md#simple-modules-over-an-algebra-finite-over-its-center) in this instance.

Every maximal [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) of $\mathbb C[z]$ is $(z-\lambda)$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, by factorization of complex polynomials. The [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $M=Am$ is therefore a quotient of the four-dimensional [algebra](../../../algebra.md) $A/(z-\lambda)A$. In particular **$\dim_{\mathbb C}M\le4<\infty$**, as required.

The actual simple dimensions are at most two. If $\lambda\ne\pm2$, choose $u\ne\pm1$ with $u+u^{-1}=\lambda$. The matrices $X=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$ and $Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ give a representation of $A/(z-\lambda)A$ onto $M_2(\mathbb C)$: polynomial functions of $X$ give the two diagonal matrix units, and multiplication by $Y$ gives the off-diagonal ones. Both [algebras](../../../algebra.md) have dimension four, so this is an isomorphism. If $\lambda=\pm2$, the element $X\mp1$ has square zero and is conjugated to its negative by $Y$; its generated two-sided [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) is square zero. It annihilates every [simple module](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module), since its action on a [simple module](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) is either zero or the whole [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), and the latter contradicts its square being zero. The remaining quotient is $\mathbb C[C_2]$, whose two [simple modules](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) have $Y=\pm1$. Thus the generic [simple modules](../../../module-theory.md#irreducible-module) have dimension two, and the four exceptional [one-dimensional characters](../../../representation-theory.md#one-dimensional-character) have $X=\pm1$, $Y=\pm1$.

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

If $M=0$, all its [localizations](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring) are zero. Conversely, take $0\ne m\in M$. Its [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) $I=\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)$ is a proper [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal), so it is contained in a maximal [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $P$, which is prime. The zero criterion for [module localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-module) says that $m/1=0$ in $M_P$ only if some $s\notin P$ satisfies $sm=0$. This would put $s\in I\subseteq P$, a contradiction. Therefore $M_P\ne0$, proving

$$
\boxed{M=0\iff M_P=0\text{ for every prime ideal }P.}
$$

In fact maximal [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) suffice, and neither finite generation nor [Noetherianity](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) is needed for this implication.

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

Set $C=N/\varphi(M)$. The natural map $N_P\to C_P$ is onto. Its kernel is the image of $\varphi_P$: a fraction $n/s$ maps to zero precisely when $tn\in\varphi(M)$ for some $t\notin P$. If $tn=\varphi(m)$, then $n/s=\varphi_P(m/(ts))$. This proves the localized [cokernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#cokernel) identity directly:

$$
\operatorname{coker}(\varphi_P)\cong C_P.
$$

Thus $\varphi_P$ is a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) for every $P$ exactly when $C_P=0$ for every $P$. Part (i) makes this equivalent to $C=0$, or to $\varphi$ being a [surjection](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function). Hence **surjectivity is detected at all prime [localizations](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring)**, and again maximal [ideals](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) alone would suffice.

<h3 id="2/iii">iii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/iii)

Let $S=R\setminus P$. If $Q\subseteq P$ is a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal), then $Q\cap S=\varnothing$. Its extension is

$$
QR_P=\{q/s:q\in Q,\ s\in S\}.
$$

It is proper: $1=q/s$ would give $u(s-q)=0$ for some $u\in S$, and hence $us=uq\in Q$, impossible because $u,s\notin Q$. If a product $(a/s)(b/t)$ lies in $QR_P$, clearing a denominator gives $uab\in Q$ for some $u\in S$. Primeness and $u\notin Q$ imply $a\in Q$ or $b\in Q$. Thus $QR_P$ is prime. Its contraction is $Q$, since $r/1\in QR_P$ implies $ur\in Q$ for some $u\in S$, forcing $r\in Q$.

Conversely, contract a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) $\mathfrak q$ of $R_P$ to $Q=\{r:r/1\in\mathfrak q\}$. This is a [prime ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal) of $R$. It contains no element of $S$, since such an element becomes a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) and cannot belong to the proper [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) $\mathfrak q$. Hence $Q\subseteq P$. Also $r/s\in\mathfrak q$ exactly when $r/1\in\mathfrak q$, because $s/1$ is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring), so $\mathfrak q=QR_P$. These extension and contraction operations are inverse, giving the [prime ideal correspondence for localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#prime-ideal-correspondence-for-localization):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}(R_P)\longleftrightarrow\{Q\in\operatorname{Spec}R:Q\subseteq P\},\qquad Q\mapsto QR_P.}
$$

<h3 id="2/iv">iv</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/iv)

An [associated prime of a module](../../../module-theory.md#associated-prime-of-a-module) is an [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) of a nonzero element. Suppose $Q=\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)$ is prime and $Q\subseteq P$. Then $m/1\ne0$ in $M_P$: a denominator $s\notin P$ killing $m$ would belong to $Q\subseteq P$. Moreover

$$
\operatorname{Ann}_{R_P}(m/1)=QR_P.
$$

Indeed, if $(a/s)(m/1)=0$, some $t\notin P$ has $tam=0$, so $ta\in Q$; primeness and $t\notin Q$ imply $a\in Q$. The reverse containment is immediate. This proves one inclusion in the [localization of associated primes](../../../module-theory.md#localization-of-associated-primes) formula.

For the other, let $\mathfrak q=\operatorname{Ann}_{R_P}(m/s)$ be an [associated prime](../../../module-theory.md#associated-prime-of-a-module) of $M_P$ and contract it to $Q$. By part (iii), $Q$ is prime, $Q\subseteq P$ and $QR_P=\mathfrak q$. Since $s$ is invertible, $\mathfrak q$ is also the [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) of $m/1$. The [Noetherian ring](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) hypothesis makes $Q=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ finitely generated. For each $a_i$, the equality $(a_im)/1=0$ supplies $t_i\notin P$ with $t_ia_im=0$. Put $t=\prod_i t_i$, taking $t=1$ for the zero [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal). Then $Q$ annihilates $tm$, while $tm/1\ne0$ because $t/1$ is a [unit](../../../algebra.md#unit-in-a-ring) and $m/1\ne0$.

If $b(tm)=0$, then $b/1$ annihilates $m/1$, so $b\in Q$. Therefore $\operatorname{Ann}_R(tm)=Q$, and $Q\in\operatorname{Ass}_R(M)$. We conclude

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Ass}_{R_P}(M_P)=\{QR_P:Q\in\operatorname{Ass}_R(M),\ Q\subseteq P\}.}
$$

The single common denominator $t$ is the key point: one must turn a localized [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) into the [annihilator](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) of one actual nonzero element of $M$, not merely assert that [annihilators](../../../module-theory.md#annihilator-ring-theory) commute with [localization](../../../commutative-algebra.md#localization-of-a-ring).

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Use generators $x,D$ with $Dx-xD=1$. The ordered [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) $x^iD^j$ form a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis): construct the [Weyl algebra](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#weyl-algebra) as the differential [Ore extension](../../../associative-algebra.md#ore-extension) of $\mathbb C[x]$ with $Df=fD+f'$, which has unique ordered polynomial expressions. Giving both generators degree one produces the [Bernstein filtration](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#bernstein-filtration), whose [associated graded ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-ring) is $\mathbb C[x,\xi]$. If $a,b\ne0$, their nonzero leading symbols have nonzero product in this polynomial [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain). Thus $ab$ has nonzero leading symbol and cannot vanish. Hence **$A_1(\mathbb C)$ has no nontrivial [zero divisors](../../../mathematics.md#zero-divisor)**.

Here is the left-handed [Ore theorem](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-theorem). Let $S$ be a multiplicative set of [regular elements of a ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#regular-element-of-a-ring), containing $1$ and excluding $0$. There is an injective [Ore localization](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-localization) whose elements are left fractions $s^{-1}a$ if and only if

$$
\boxed{\text{for every }a\in A,s\in S,\text{ there are }t\in S,b\in A\text{ with }ta=bs.}
$$

This is the left [Ore condition](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-condition). For a [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain), taking $S=A\setminus\{0\}$ gives a [division ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring) exactly when this condition holds. With zero-divisor denominators a further left reversibility condition is needed; the regular-denominator statement is the one used here.

Necessity follows by writing $as^{-1}=t^{-1}b$ in the quotient and multiplying by $t$ and $s$, giving $ta=bs$ in the embedded original [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring). For a construction proving sufficiency, use the [left Ore fraction construction](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-ore-fraction-construction): pairs $(s,a)$ represent $s^{-1}a$, and $(s,a)$ and $(t,b)$ are equivalent when

$$
us=vt=w\in S,\qquad ua=vb
$$

for suitable $u,v\in A$. Reflexivity and symmetry are immediate. For transitivity, combine $us=vt=w$ and $u't=v'q=w'$ by a common left multiple $hw=kw'\in S$. Then $(hv-ku')t=0$, so regularity gives $hv=ku'$, which combines the numerator equalities and proves transitivity.

Addition uses the common denominator $w$, with numerator $ua+vb$. To multiply $s^{-1}a$ by $t^{-1}b$, choose $v\in S,c\in A$ with $va=ct$; set

$$
(s^{-1}a)(t^{-1}b)=(vs)^{-1}cb.
$$

The [Ore condition](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-condition) supplies a common refinement whenever a representative or an Ore relation is changed, and regularity cancels the shared denominator, proving independence of choices. The same refinements verify the [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) axioms; the formulas are simply addition and multiplication after clearing left denominators. The map $a\mapsto(1,a)$ is injective, since equivalence to zero would give $ua=0$ with $u\in S$. Each $s\in S$ becomes invertible. A homomorphism inverting $S$ extends uniquely by $(s,a)\mapsto f(s)^{-1}f(a)$, giving the universal property. For all nonzero denominators in a [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain), a nonzero fraction has inverse $(s^{-1}a)^{-1}=a^{-1}s$, so the quotient is a [division ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#division-ring). This is the requested proof sketch, with the fraction equivalence and multiplication made explicit.

It remains to check the left [Ore condition](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#ore-condition) for $A_1(\mathbb C)$. Its graded polynomial [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) is [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) by the [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem). Lifting homogeneous generators of a graded [left ideal](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) and successively cancelling leading symbols proves that the filtered [algebra](../../../algebra.md) is a [left Noetherian ring](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#left-noetherian-ring): each subtraction lowers the nonnegative degree, so it terminates.

In any left Noetherian [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain), nonzero principal [left ideals](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal) intersect. Otherwise $Ab\cap Aa=0$ with $a,b\ne0$ would make

$$
Ab\oplus Aba\oplus Aba^2\oplus\cdots
$$

an infinite direct sum of nonzero [left ideals](../../../associative-algebra.md#left-ideal). To verify directness, reduce a finite relation modulo $Aa$ to make its first summand zero, then cancel $a$ on the right and repeat. Its increasing finite partial sums contradict the [ascending chain condition](../../../algebra.md#ascending-chain-condition). A nonzero element of $Aa\cap As$ therefore has the form $ta=bs\ne0$, with $t\ne0$, proving the required condition. Consequently **$A_1(\mathbb C)$ has a classical [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) of left quotients, and it is a division [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring)**.

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

For $n\ge1$, the [Weyl algebra](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#weyl-algebra) $A=A_n(\mathbb C)$ is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_n,D_1,\ldots,D_n$ with $[D_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$ and the other generator [commutators](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) zero. Iterating the differential [Ore extension](../../../associative-algebra.md#ore-extension) construction gives the ordered [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $x^\alpha D^\beta$. In the [Bernstein filtration](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#bernstein-filtration), both kinds of generator have degree one, and

$$
F_jA=\operatorname{span}\{x^\alpha D^\beta:|\alpha|+|\beta|\le j\},\qquad
\dim F_jA=\binom{j+2n}{2n},\qquad
\operatorname{gr}A\cong\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n].
$$

Leading symbols show that $A$ is a [noncommutative domain](../../../noncommutative-algebra.md#noncommutative-domain). The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) and homogeneous-generator lifting by decreasing degree make it both left and right [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring).

It is also a [simple ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#simple-ring). In a nonzero two-sided [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal), choose a nonzero element and a [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) $x^{\alpha_0}D^{\beta_0}$ of maximal total degree with nonzero coefficient $c$. Applying $(\operatorname{ad}D)^{\alpha_0}(\operatorname{ad}x)^{\beta_0}$ eliminates every lower-degree term and every different term of that maximal degree. The result is the nonzero [scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar) $(-1)^{|\beta_0|}\alpha_0!\beta_0!c$. Thus the [ideal](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ideal) contains $1$. Here the [scalar extraction by iterated Weyl commutators](../../../associative-algebra.md#scalar-extraction-by-iterated-weyl-commutators) uses characteristic zero. Despite simplicity, $A$ has no nonzero finite-dimensional [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics): in such a representation $[D_1,x_1]=I$ would have [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) $0=\dim M$, impossible over $\mathbb C$. Its interesting [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) theory is necessarily infinite-dimensional.

For a finitely generated left [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $M$, choose a finite-dimensional generating subspace $M_0$ and set $M_j=F_jA\,M_0$. This is a [good filtration of a module](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module). Its [associated graded module](../../../commutative-algebra.md#associated-graded-module) is finite over the graded polynomial [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring), so its cumulative Hilbert function is eventually a polynomial,

$$
\dim M_j=\frac{e(M)}{d(M)!}j^{d(M)}+O(j^{d(M)-1})\quad(M\ne0),
$$

with positive integer multiplicity $e(M)$. These are the [Bernstein growth dimension of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#bernstein-growth-dimension-of-a-weyl-algebra-module) and its [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../module-theory.md#multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module). Two good filtrations bound each other after fixed shifts: lift finite homogeneous generators in one filtration, bound their degrees in the other, and use compatibility with $F_jA$; then reverse the roles. The growth degree and leading coefficient therefore do not depend on the [good filtration of a module](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module). The degree is also the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../associative-algebra.md#gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module) and the dimension of the [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module), the support of $\operatorname{gr}M$. Since $M$ is a quotient of a finite sum of copies of $A$, $d(M)\le2n$.

The essential lower bound is the [Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules](../../../associative-algebra.md#bernstein-inequality-for-weyl-algebra-modules):

$$
\boxed{M\ne0\text{ finitely generated}\Longrightarrow d(M)\ge n.}
$$

Here is a full proof. Multiplication gives a linear map $F_jA\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(M_j,M_{2j})$. Prove it injective by induction on $j$. At $j=0$ a [scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar) killing $M_0\ne0$ must vanish. If $a\in F_jA$ kills $M_j$, then for every generator $z$, its [commutator](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) $[a,z]\in F_{j-1}A$ kills $M_{j-1}$: both $azm$ and $zam$ are zero for $m\in M_{j-1}$. Induction gives $[a,z]=0$ for all generators. In the ordered [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis), $[D_i,a]$ differentiates the $x_i$ exponents and $[x_i,a]$ differentiates the $D_i$ exponents with a minus sign. Vanishing of all these [commutators](../../../lie-algebra.md#commutator) in characteristic zero forces $a$ to be a [scalar](../../../vector-space.md#scalar). It then vanishes because it kills $M_0$. This proves the [faithful finite-step action of a Weyl algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#faithful-finite-step-action-of-a-weyl-algebra) and hence

$$
\binom{j+2n}{2n}\le(\dim M_j)(\dim M_{2j})\le(\dim M_{2j})^2.
$$

Comparison of degrees gives $2n\le2d(M)$, proving the inequality. Equally, without invoking eventual polynomiality, this estimate directly bounds the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../associative-algebra.md#gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module) below by $n$. Right [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) satisfy the same inequality through the filtration-preserving isomorphism $A\cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$, $x_i\mapsto x_i$, $D_i\mapsto-D_i$.

A nonzero finitely generated [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) with $d(M)=n$ is a [holonomic Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#holonomic-weyl-algebra-module); the zero [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is conventionally included. This minimal-growth class is stable under [submodules](../../../module-theory.md#submodule), [quotient modules](../../../module-theory.md#quotient-module) and extensions. For $0\to N\to M\to Q\to0$, take the induced and quotient filtrations. Their graded sequence is exact; [Noetherianity](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) of $\operatorname{gr}A$ makes the induced filtrations good. Thus the Hilbert functions add and

$$
d(M)=\max(d(N),d(Q));
$$

if both nonzero terms have the top dimension, their multiplicities add. For a [holonomic Weyl algebra module](../../../associative-algebra.md#holonomic-weyl-algebra-module), every nonzero subquotient has dimension $n$, by the upper bound inherited from $M$ and Bernstein's lower bound. Each nonzero factor in a strict chain therefore consumes positive integral multiplicity. This proves the [multiplicity bounds the length of a holonomic Weyl module](../../../associative-algebra.md#multiplicity-bounds-the-length-of-a-holonomic-weyl-module):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{length}_A(M)\le e(M).}
$$

In particular a holonomic [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) is both [Noetherian](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring) and [Artinian](../../../algebra.md#artinian-ring) and has finite length, although its vector-space dimension is infinite. This distinction separates finite-dimensional representation theory from finite-length Weyl [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) theory.

The polynomial [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, with $x_i$ acting by multiplication and $D_i$ by differentiation, is generated by $1$. Its $M_j$ consists of polynomials of degree at most $j$, with dimension $\binom{j+n}{n}$, so $d=n$ and $e=1$. It is simple: differentiating a maximal-degree [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) of a nonzero polynomial isolates a nonzero constant, and multiplication by polynomials then generates the whole [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics). A second basic example is the delta [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics)

$$
A\Big/\sum_iAx_i.
$$

Using the reverse ordered [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $D^\beta x^\alpha$, its [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) is $\mathbb C[D_1,\ldots,D_n]$; $D_i$ acts by multiplication and $x_i$ by negative differentiation. The same argument proves simplicity and holonomicity. These two examples are exchanged by the [automorphism](../../../algebra.md#automorphism) $x_i\mapsto D_i$, $D_i\mapsto-x_i$. Free [modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics), in contrast, have growth dimension $2n$ and are not holonomic. Thus the central organizing invariants are [good filtration of a module](../../../module-theory.md#good-filtration-of-a-module), growth dimension, characteristic support and multiplicity, with Bernstein's estimate explaining why dimension $n$ is the natural smallest nonzero size.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 1](paper-1.md)

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

Specify a commutative ground [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) $k$ and a unital associative $k$-[algebra](../../../algebra.md) $R$. For an $R$-[bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) $M$, the [Hochschild cochain complex](../../../associative-algebra.md#hochschild-cochain-complex) has

$$
C^q(R,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_k(R^{\otimes_kq},M),\qquad C^0(R,M)=M,
$$

and coboundary

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\delta f)(a_1,\ldots,a_{q+1})={}&a_1f(a_2,\ldots,a_{q+1})\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{q}(-1)^if(a_1,\ldots,a_ia_{i+1},\ldots,a_{q+1})\\
&+(-1)^{q+1}f(a_1,\ldots,a_q)a_{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The [bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) identities and [associativity](../../../group.md#associative-property) cancel the terms of $\delta^2$ in pairs. The [Hochschild cohomology](../../../associative-algebra.md#hochschild-cohomology) is $HH_k^q(R,M)=\ker\delta_q/\operatorname{im}\delta_{q-1}$; the paper's $H^q(R,R)$ uses the regular [bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) as coefficients.

Over a [field](../../../algebra.md#field), the [bar resolution of an associative algebra](../../../algebra.md#bar-resolution-of-an-associative-algebra) explains its homological meaning. Put $R^e=R\otimes_kR^{\mathrm{op}}$. The degree-$q$ bar term is $R^{\otimes_k(q+2)}$, with the outer factors supplying the $R^e$ action, boundary obtained by multiplying adjacent factors with alternating signs, and augmentation multiplication $R\otimes R\to R$. Inserting $1$ at the left gives a $k$-linear contracting homotopy, proving exactness. The terms are free $R^e$-[modules](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) after choosing a $k$-basis for the inner tensor factors. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_{R^e}(-,M)$ gives precisely the displayed cochain complex and therefore

$$
HH_k^q(R,M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R^e}^q(R,M).
$$

This [ground-ring qualification in Hochschild cohomology](../../../associative-algebra.md#ground-ring-qualification-in-hochschild-cohomology) matters for an arbitrary [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring): one can take $k=\mathbb Z$, but the bar terms need not be projective over $R^e$. The ordinary cochain complex then computes relative [Hochschild cohomology](../../../associative-algebra.md#hochschild-cohomology); the absolute Ext identification needs, for example, $R$ projective over $k$. Likewise the extension classification below applies to ground-ring-linearly split extensions. For algebras over a [field](../../../algebra.md#field) these qualifications are automatic.

In degree zero, $(\delta m)(a)=am-ma$, so **$HH^0(R,R)=Z(R)$**. In degree one the cocycle equation is

$$
a\,d(b)-d(ab)+d(a)b=0,
$$

exactly the Leibniz rule for a [derivation of an algebra](../../../associative-algebra.md#derivation-of-an-algebra). The coboundaries are the [inner derivations](../../../associative-algebra.md#inner-derivation) $a\mapsto am-ma$. Thus the [First Hochschild cohomology as outer derivations](../../../associative-algebra.md#first-hochschild-cohomology-as-outer-derivations) is

$$
\boxed{HH^1(R,R)=\operatorname{Der}_k(R)/\operatorname{Inn}(R).}
$$

Its significance is infinitesimal [automorphisms](../../../algebra.md#automorphism) modulo inner ones. Over the [dual numbers](../../../commutative-algebra.md#dual-number) $k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, the map $a\mapsto a+\varepsilon d(a)$ is multiplicative precisely when $d$ satisfies the Leibniz rule. Conjugation by $1+\varepsilon m$ gives $a\mapsto a+\varepsilon(ma-am)$, an inner [derivation](../../../associative-algebra.md#derivation-of-an-algebra) with the opposite sign convention. The quotient therefore measures infinitesimal symmetry not arising from conjugation.

Degree two controls extensions and changes of multiplication. A $k$-linear split [square-zero extension of an algebra](../../../commutative-algebra.md#square-zero-extension-of-an-algebra) with prescribed kernel [bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) $M$ can be written as $E=R\oplus M$. Choose a unital section and let $f(a,b)$ be the difference between its product and its value on $ab$. Then

$$
(a,u)(b,v)=(ab,av+ub+f(a,b)).
$$

Computing both parenthesizations of a triple gives the [associativity](../../../group.md#associative-property) condition

$$
a f(b,c)-f(ab,c)+f(a,bc)-f(a,b)c=0,
$$

namely $\delta f=0$. A unital product has $f(1,a)=f(a,1)=0$, so one uses the [normalized Hochschild cochain complex](../../../associative-algebra.md#normalized-hochschild-cochain-complex). Changing section by $g:R\to M$ gives $f\mapsto f+\delta g$. Conversely the displayed product for any normalized two-cocycle constructs such an extension, and a section change gives the corresponding extension isomorphism. This proves that [second Hochschild cohomology classifies split square-zero extensions](../../../associative-algebra.md#second-hochschild-cohomology-classifies-split-square-zero-extensions):

$$
\boxed{HH^2(R,M)\text{ classifies these extensions, up to equivalence fixing }R,M.}
$$

The zero class is the split algebra extension with no extra product term. With $M=R$, this gives one meaning of the requested $H^2(R,R)$.

For a [first-order associative deformation](../../../associative-algebra.md#first-order-associative-deformation), retain the underlying $k$-[module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) and put $a*b=ab+\varepsilon f(a,b)$. Expanding $(a*b)*c=a*(b*c)$ modulo $\varepsilon^2$ gives exactly $\delta f=0$. A coordinate change $T=1+\varepsilon g$ transports the product to $T^{-1}(T(a)*T(b))$, whose first-order coefficient is $f+\delta g$. Hence

$$
\boxed{HH^2(R,R)\text{ is the space of inequivalent first-order changes of multiplication.}}
$$

This classification is of infinitesimal deformations, not a guarantee that all classes extend to every order. For $a*b=ab+tf(a,b)+t^2\mu_2(a,b)$ modulo $t^3$, the next [associativity](../../../group.md#associative-property) equation is

$$
\delta\mu_2(a,b,c)=f(f(a,b),c)-f(a,f(b,c)).
$$

Substitution of $\delta f=0$ shows the right-hand side is a three-cocycle. Its class in $HH^3(R,R)$ is the [second-order obstruction to an associative deformation](../../../associative-algebra.md#second-order-obstruction-to-an-associative-deformation). It must vanish for a second-order extension to exist. At higher orders the same expansion gives further obstruction cocycles. If $HH^2(R,R)=0$, every first nonzero coefficient of a [formal associative deformation](../../../associative-algebra.md#formal-associative-deformation) is a coboundary and can be removed by a coordinate change. Repeating this order by order gives [formal rigidity from vanishing second Hochschild cohomology](../../../associative-algebra.md#formal-rigidity-from-vanishing-second-hochschild-cohomology), with the transformations converging in the $t$-adic topology. Vanishing of $HH^3$ instead removes these extension obstructions; these are distinct roles.

Examples make the two low degrees concrete. For $R=k[x]$ over a [field](../../../algebra.md#field), [inner derivations](../../../associative-algebra.md#inner-derivation) vanish and every [derivation](../../../associative-algebra.md#derivation-of-an-algebra) is $h(x)\partial_x$, determined by its value on $x$. The [bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) resolution

$$
0\longrightarrow k[x]\otimes k[x]
\xrightarrow{\,x\otimes1-1\otimes x\,}
 k[x]\otimes k[x]\longrightarrow k[x]\longrightarrow0
$$

is exact: the middle tensor [ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#ring) is the polynomial [integral domain](../../../commutative-algebra.md#integral-domain) $k[u,v]$, multiplication identifies its quotient by $u-v$, and multiplication by $u-v$ is injective. Applying [bimodule](../../../module-theory.md#bimodule) Hom gives zero differential. Consequently the [Hochschild cohomology of a polynomial algebra in one variable](../../../associative-algebra.md#hochschild-cohomology-of-a-polynomial-algebra-in-one-variable) is

$$
HH^0(k[x])=k[x],\qquad HH^1(k[x])=k[x]\partial_x,\qquad HH^q(k[x])=0\quad(q\ge2).
$$

This algebra has many infinitesimal [automorphisms](../../../algebra.md#automorphism), while its ordinary associative multiplication is formally rigid.

For $R=k[x,y]$ in characteristic zero, the bilinear map $f(a,b)=(\partial_ya)(\partial_xb)$ is a two-cocycle: substitution in $\delta f$ and the two product rules cancel its four terms. It is not a coboundary, because every $\delta g$ on a commutative [algebra](../../../algebra.md) is symmetric in its two inputs, whereas $f(y,x)-f(x,y)=1$. This class integrates to the [Weyl deformation of a polynomial algebra in two variables](../../../associative-algebra.md#weyl-deformation-of-a-polynomial-algebra-in-two-variables), with $yx-xy=t$. The differential [Ore extension](../../../associative-algebra.md#ore-extension) construction supplies the ordered [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $x^iy^j$ over $k[t]$, so the $t$-adic completion has the fixed underlying [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) $k[x,y][[t]]$. Its first multiplication correction is exactly this $f$. This displays a genuinely noncommutative [formal associative deformation](../../../associative-algebra.md#formal-associative-deformation) detected by $HH^2$.

Finally, the [Hochschild cup product](../../../associative-algebra.md#hochschild-cup-product) $(f\smile g)(a_1,\ldots,a_{p+q})=f(a_1,\ldots,a_p)g(a_{p+1},\ldots,a_{p+q})$ descends to the graded cohomology algebra, and the [Gerstenhaber bracket](../../../associative-algebra.md#gerstenhaber-bracket) organizes the interaction of [derivations](../../../associative-algebra.md#derivation-of-an-algebra) and [formal associative deformation](../../../associative-algebra.md#formal-associative-deformation) cocycles. The calculations above explain why the first two positive degrees are especially useful: $HH^1$ measures outer infinitesimal symmetries, while $HH^2$ measures split extensions and infinitesimal associative structures, with degree three detecting the first obstruction to continuing them.
