<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $X=M\times\mathbb R$ has a complete metric with zero [Ricci curvature](../../../../../../ricci-curvature.md). Since $M$ is closed and [connected](../../../../../../connected-space.md), the complement of $M\times[-A,A]$ has two unbounded components in any complete metric on $X$: their closures are noncompact, while closed bounded sets are [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) by the [Hopf-Rinow theorem](../../../../../../hopf-rinow-theorem.md). Thus $X$ is [disconnected at infinity](../../../../../../disconnected-at-infinity.md), and part (a) supplies a line. The [Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem](../../../../../../cheeger-gromoll-splitting-theorem.md) gives an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md)

$$
X\cong N^3\times\mathbb R.
$$

The [Riemannian product](../../../../../../riemannian-product.md) curvature formula makes $N$ complete and Ricci-flat. A three-dimensional [Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold](../../../../../../ricci-flat-riemannian-manifold.md) is flat: for any orthonormal triple, the three trace equations are $\operatorname{Ric}_{11}=K_{12}+K_{13}$ and their cyclic companions. Solving gives $K_{ij}=\operatorname{Ric}_{ii}+\operatorname{Ric}_{jj}-\operatorname{Scal}/2=0$. Every plane can occur in such a triple, so every [sectional curvature](../../../../../../sectional-curvature.md) vanishes, and hence so does the [Riemann curvature tensor](../../../../../../riemann-curvature-tensor.md).

The complete [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) of $N$ is therefore Euclidean $\mathbb R^3$, and that of $X$ is Euclidean $\mathbb R^4$. But the topological [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) of $X$ is $\widetilde M\times\mathbb R$. Its [deformation retraction](../../../../../../deformation-retraction.md) onto $\widetilde M\times\{0\}$ shows that contractibility of this cover would imply contractibility of $\widetilde M$, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore **no complete Ricci-flat metric exists on $M\times\mathbb R$**. Notice that no product assumption was made about the hypothetical original metric.
