<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Pointwise positive Ricci curvature does not imply [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) or a finite diameter.** The [complete positively curved paraboloid](../../../../../../complete-positively-curved-paraboloid.md) $z=x^2+y^2$ has induced metric

$$
g=(1+4r^2)dr^2+r^2d\theta^2
$$

and [Gaussian curvature](../../../../../../gaussian-curvature.md)

$$
K(r)=\frac{4}{(1+4r^2)^2}>0.
$$

For example, this follows from the graph formula $K=\det D^2z/(1+|Dz|^2)^2$. The metric dominates the Euclidean plane metric, so it is complete by [upward stability of Riemannian completeness](../../../../../../upward-stability-of-riemannian-completeness.md). Its diameter is infinite since its distance dominates Euclidean distance. On a surface the [Ricci tensor](../../../../../../ricci-tensor.md) is $Kg$, so it is strictly positive everywhere, but has no uniform positive lower bound.

**There is no positive-Ricci metric on $S^2\times S^1$.** If one existed, [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of the [unit tangent bundle](../../../../../../unit-tangent-bundle.md) would give $\operatorname{Ric}_{\mathrm{tr}}\geq cg$ for some $c>0$. The lifted metric on the [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) is complete by Q2 and satisfies the same positive lower bound. Part (a) makes this cover [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md). A covering fiber is closed and discrete and therefore finite, implying a finite [fundamental group](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md). But

$$
\pi_1(S^2\times S^1)\cong\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z,
$$

which is infinite. This is the [finite fundamental group from a uniform positive Ricci bound](../../../../../../finite-fundamental-group-from-a-uniform-positive-ricci-bound.md) obstruction; it rules out every possible metric, not just the standard product metric.
