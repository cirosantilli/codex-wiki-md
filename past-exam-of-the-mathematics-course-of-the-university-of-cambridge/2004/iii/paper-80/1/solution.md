<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

At a point $P$ of the [smooth algebraic curve](../../../../../smooth-algebraic-curve.md) $X$, the [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) $\mathcal O_{X,P}$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md). Embed the [projective variety](../../../../../projective-variety.md) $Y$ in $\mathbb P^N$. A [rational map of algebraic varieties](../../../../../rational-map-of-algebraic-varieties.md) to $Y$ has homogeneous coordinates $[f_0:\cdots:f_N]$ in the [function field](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) $k(X)$, not all zero. Let $v_P$ be the local [valuation](../../../../../valuation.md), choose a [uniformizer](../../../../../uniformizer.md) $u$, and set $m=\min_{f_i\ne0}v_P(f_i)$. The coordinates $u^{-m}f_i$ are regular at $P$, and at least one is a unit. On a sufficiently small [open subset](../../../../../open-set.md) containing $P$, all these functions are regular and that coordinate stays nonzero. Their ratios therefore define a [morphism of algebraic varieties](../../../../../morphism-of-algebraic-varieties.md) into the corresponding affine chart of $\mathbb P^N$. Every homogeneous equation defining $Y$ vanishes on the original dense open, hence vanishes identically on this neighborhood. The local extension consequently lands in $Y$.

Two such extensions agree wherever the original [rational map of algebraic varieties](../../../../../rational-map-of-algebraic-varieties.md) is defined. Their equalizer is closed, because $Y$ is a [separated variety](../../../../../separated-variety.md), and contains a dense open. Thus they agree throughout their overlap and glue. **The rational map has a unique extension to a morphism on all of $X$.** Projectivity is needed for the target; the source need not be projective.

For the [function field](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) correspondence, let $K/k$ be a finitely generated [field extension](../../../../../field-extension.md) of [transcendence degree](../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) one. Choose a [transcendence basis](../../../../../transcendence-basis.md) consisting of one element $t$. The extension $K/k(t)$ is finite: finitely many algebraic generators each have finite degree. Take the [integral closures](../../../../../integral-closure.md) of $k[t]$ and $k[t^{-1}]$ in $K$. The finiteness assumption allowed in the paper makes both finite modules over their original coordinate rings. [Integral closure](../../../../../integral-closure.md) commutes with localization, so these two rings agree over $k[t,t^{-1}]$ and glue to the [finite normalization model of a one-variable function field](../../../../../finite-normalization-model-of-a-one-variable-function-field.md) $C_K\to\mathbb P^1$. This is a [finite morphism](../../../../../finite-morphism.md), hence projective, and its [function field](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) is $K$. Its one-dimensional normal local rings are [discrete valuation rings](../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md), so it is regular. Since the [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) $k$ is perfect, regularity here implies smoothness. Thus $C_K$ is a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md).

Conversely the [function field](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) of a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) is finitely generated over $k$ and has [transcendence degree](../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) one. Two such curves with isomorphic [function fields](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) have mutually inverse [birational maps](../../../../../birational-map.md). The extension argument just proved makes both maps morphisms; their composites equal the identity on a dense open, and hence everywhere by [separatedness](../../../../../separated-morphism.md). This proves uniqueness of the smooth projective model. More precisely, pullback gives a contravariant correspondence between nonconstant morphisms of [smooth projective curves](../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) and $k$-embeddings of their [function fields](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md). A field embedding defines the map on affine coordinates near the [generic point](../../../../../generic-point.md), and the extension argument completes it. Constant morphisms are excluded from this correspondence because they do not induce embeddings of [function fields](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md).

Finally let $f:C\to D$ be nonconstant. Its image is dense, and pullback gives a finite extension $k(D)\subset k(C)$: both fields have [transcendence degree](../../../../../transcendence-degree.md) one and the latter is finitely generated. Normalize $D$ in $k(C)$, applying the allowed finite-[integral closure](../../../../../integral-closure.md) result on affine charts. This gives a [finite morphism](../../../../../finite-morphism.md) $C'\to D$ with $C'$ a [smooth projective curve](../../../../../smooth-projective-curve.md) and [function field](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) $k(C)$. The identification of [function fields](../../../../../function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety.md) gives an isomorphism $C\cong C'$ by the preceding paragraph. Its composite with $C'\to D$ equals $f$ at the [generic point](../../../../../generic-point.md), hence equals $f$ everywhere. **Every nonconstant morphism of these curves is finite.**
