<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The angles from part (i) have sines

$$
\sin\theta_P=\frac{\alpha R}{\sqrt{s^2+\alpha^2R^2}},\qquad
\sin\theta_z=\frac{\alpha_fR}{\sqrt{s^2+\alpha^2R^2}}.
$$

By [Snell law for elastic and acoustic waves](../../../../../../snell-law-for-elastic-and-acoustic-waves.md), $\sin\theta_f/\alpha_f=\sin\theta_P/\alpha$. Both angles lie in $[0,\pi/2)$, so **$\theta_f=\theta_z$**. The outgoing fluid ray tilts away from the inward-tilted normal by exactly its tilt from vertical; it therefore travels vertically upwards. This is [hyperbolic interface collimation of P-waves](../../../../../../hyperbolic-interface-collimation-of-p-waves.md).

The source-to-interface distance satisfies

$$
r(R)=\sqrt{R^2+Z(R)^2}=\frac{\alpha D+\alpha_fs}{d^2}.
$$

One can verify this by squaring, using $D^2-s^2=d^2R^2$. Consequently

$$
\frac r\alpha-\frac Z{\alpha_f}=-\frac{s}{\alpha\alpha_f}
=\frac{z_0}{\alpha}-\frac{z_0}{\alpha_f}.
$$

Adding the vertical fluid travel time from the interface to height $z$ gives

$$
\boxed{\tau(R,z)=\frac{r(R)}\alpha+\frac{z-Z(R)}{\alpha_f}
=\frac{z_0}\alpha+\frac{z-z_0}{\alpha_f}.}
$$

Thus surfaces of equal arrival time in the fluid are horizontal planes. Their leading [wave amplitude](../../../../../../wave-amplitude.md) may vary with $R$: a planar [wavefront](../../../../../../wavefront.md) does not imply a spatially uniform plane-wave solution at every order.
