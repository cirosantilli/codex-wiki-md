<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Keep part of the diffusion rather than maximizing all of it. The elementary square inequality gives

$$
Q\sqrt{XJ}\leq\frac\eta2J+\frac{Q^2}{2\eta}X.
$$

Writing $E=\langle B^2\rangle$ and $\lambda=\eta k^2>0$, part (ii), the [Poincaré inequality](../../../../../../poincare-inequality.md) and part (i) imply

$$
E'\leq-\eta J+\frac{Q^2}{\eta}X
\leq-\lambda E+\frac{Q^2A_0^2}{\eta}e^{-2\lambda t}.
$$

Multiply by $e^{\lambda t}$ and integrate from zero:

$$
\boxed{E(t)\leq E(0)e^{-\lambda t}+\frac{Q^2A_0^2}{\eta^2k^2}(e^{-\lambda t}-e^{-2\lambda t})\longrightarrow0.}
$$

Thus stretching by a vertical velocity can transiently generate vertical [magnetic energy](../../../../../../magnetic-energy.md), but cannot maintain it in this zero-mean periodic, $z$-independent setting. For a strictly planar velocity, $Q=0$ and the passive vertical component decays directly. The stated conclusion depends on positive [magnetic diffusivity](../../../../../../magnetic-diffusivity.md) and the spectral gap supplied by zero mean.
