<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

No measured normalization of the [neutral-hydrogen column-density distribution](../../../../../../neutral-hydrogen-column-density-distribution.md), ionizing field or gas [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md) is supplied, so the paper's formulas do not determine numerical [densities](../../../../../../density.md) on their own. With observational data and suitable ionization corrections, the high-redshift [Lyman-alpha forest](../../../../../../lyman-alpha-forest.md) gives a total gas [density](../../../../../../density.md) of order a few $10^{-2}$ relative to today's [critical density](../../../../../../critical-density.md), comparable to the [cosmological baryon density](../../../../../../cosmological-baryon-density.md) $\Omega_b\sim0.04$–$0.05$ for $H_0\sim70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. The conclusion is that a large fraction of baryons at $z\simeq3$ reside in diffuse ionized gas, rather than that every baryon has been individually accounted for. [Schaye's analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0104272) and [Kim and collaborators' survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0101005) support that interpretation under their stated gas and ionizing-background assumptions.

The neutral-gas reservoir traced predominantly by [damped Lyman-alpha systems](../../../../../../damped-lyman-alpha-system.md) is much smaller, of order $10^{-3}$ in the same convention. It is broadly comparable in order of magnitude to today's luminous stellar matter, whose [cosmic stellar mass density](../../../../../../cosmic-stellar-mass-density.md) is a few $10^{-3}$, but exact comparisons depend on cosmology, [helium](../../../../../../helium.md) correction and which stellar components are counted. [Storrie-Lombardi, McMahon and Irwin's neutral-gas census](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9608147) finds especially close comparison with present stellar disks. **Most high-redshift gas is ionized; the smaller neutral reservoir has a mass of roughly the same order as today's visible stellar population.** This comparison does not imply that the measured neutral reservoir alone simply becomes all present-day stars: gas accretion, recycling, ionization changes and outflows continue during [galaxy](../../../../../../galaxy-split.md) evolution.

The [neutral gas density from an absorption distribution](../../../../../../neutral-gas-density-from-an-absorption-distribution.md) is comparatively robust for high columns where the hydrogen is mostly neutral, but saturated lines, dust-obscured quasars, incomplete surveys and small numbers of rare high-column systems affect the integral. The [ionized gas density from an absorption distribution](../../../../../../ionized-gas-density-from-an-absorption-distribution.md) has additional systematic uncertainty in the [cosmic ionizing background](../../../../../../cosmic-ionizing-background.md), [temperature](../../../../../../temperature.md), absorber depth, [helium](../../../../../../helium.md) treatment and small-scale [density](../../../../../../density.md) inhomogeneity. At low columns, line blending and incompleteness matter; at high columns, attenuation makes an optically thin ionization correction invalid. A single [power law](../../../../../../power-law.md) extrapolated outside the measured column range can also produce a misleading mass integral. Hot gas, molecular gas and gas missed by the selected absorption population are not included automatically. These are observational and modelling qualifications to a baryon census, not failures of the column-mass accounting itself.
