<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Use the exact-resource-balance convention for an [exchange economy](../../../../../../exchange-economy.md). If $\omega$ is the integrable initial-endowment profile, a feasible [consumption allocation](../../../../../../consumption-allocation.md) is a measurable integrable function $f:A\to\mathbb R_+^L$ with

$$
\int_Af\,d\mu=\int_A\omega\,d\mu.
$$

It is [Pareto efficient](../../../../../../pareto-efficiency.md) if there is no other feasible [consumption allocation](../../../../../../consumption-allocation.md) $g$ satisfying $g(a)\succeq_a f(a)$ for almost every consumer and $g(a)\succ_a f(a)$ on a set of positive measure. **A Pareto improvement makes nobody worse off, up to a null set, and makes a positive-measure group strictly better off.** Positive measure is essential: reallocating to a single consumer of zero mass does not constitute an aggregate Pareto improvement.
