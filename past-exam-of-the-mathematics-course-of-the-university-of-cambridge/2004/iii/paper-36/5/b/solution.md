<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

There are two equivalent graph-limit requirements. **Both are necessary.** The upper condition is that, for every subsequence $n_j$ and convergent pairs $x_j\to x$, $y_j\to y$,

$$
x_j\succeq_{n_j}y_j\ \text{for every }j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\succeq y.
$$

The lower condition is that every limiting comparison can be approximated: for every $x\succeq y$, there exist $x_n\to x$ and $y_n\to y$ with

$$
x_n\succeq_n y_n\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
$$

The upper condition excludes spurious limiting comparisons; the lower condition prevents genuine limiting comparisons from disappearing. The subsequence quantifier in the upper condition is important: a violation need occur along only some of the indices. On the locally compact separable metric space $X\times X$, these two conditions are exactly [closed convergence of preference relations](../../../../../../closed-convergence-of-preference-relations.md).
