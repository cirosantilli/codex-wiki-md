<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $X=\mathbb R_+^L$ and identify a continuous [preference relation](../../../../../../preference-relation.md) $R$ with its closed graph $G_R=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:xRy\}$. The [closed convergence of preference relations](../../../../../../closed-convergence-of-preference-relations.md) is the topology induced by the [Fell topology](../../../../../../fell-topology.md) on these closed subsets of $X\times X$.

Concretely, a subbasis consists of the sets

$$
\{R:G_R\cap U\ne\varnothing\}\quad(U\subseteq X\times X\text{ open}),\qquad\{R:G_R\cap K=\varnothing\}\quad(K\subseteq X\times X\text{ compact}).
$$

Finite intersections of these requirements form a basis, restricted to the specified complete, transitive, continuous [preference relations](../../../../../../preference-relation.md). Thus a neighborhood may require some comparisons in prescribed open regions and forbid any comparison in prescribed compact regions. The use of compact sets in the avoidance condition is what makes this the [Fell topology](../../../../../../fell-topology.md), rather than a stronger hyperspace topology on an unbounded consumption space.
