<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The hypotheses for [Kummer theory](../../../../../../kummer-theory.md) are that $n\geq1$ is prime to the characteristic of $K$ and that all $n$th [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) lie in $K$. Under these hypotheses, adjoining radicals produces [abelian extensions](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md) of exponent dividing $n$, and conversely every such finite extension is obtained this way. The roots-of-unity hypothesis matters: without it, adjoining one radical need not give a [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md).

We first prove [Hilbert's theorem 90](../../../../../../hilbert-s-theorem-90.md), including its cocycle form. Distinct field automorphisms are linearly independent as functions into the field. Indeed, in a shortest nontrivial relation $\sum_ic_i\tau_i(y)=0$, choose $x$ with $\tau_1(x)\ne\tau_r(x)$. Subtract $\tau_r(x)$ times the relation at $y$ from the relation at $xy$. This eliminates the last term while leaving the first nonzero, contradicting minimality. This is [linear independence of distinct field embeddings](../../../../../../linear-independence-of-distinct-field-embeddings.md).

Let $L/K$ be finite Galois with group $G$, and suppose $a_\sigma\in L^*$ satisfy $a_{\sigma\tau}=a_\sigma\sigma(a_\tau)$. By independence of the automorphisms, choose $c\in L$ for which

$$
b=\sum_{\tau\in G}a_\tau\tau(c)\ne0.
$$

Then

$$
\sigma(b)=\sum_\tau\sigma(a_\tau)\sigma\tau(c)
=a_\sigma^{-1}\sum_\tau a_{\sigma\tau}\sigma\tau(c)=a_\sigma^{-1}b.
$$

Thus every multiplicative cocycle is a coboundary:

$$
\boxed{a_\sigma=b/\sigma(b),\qquad H^1(G,L^*)=0.}
$$

For a [cyclic group](../../../../../../cyclic-group.md) generated by $\sigma$ of order $m$, an element $a$ of norm one defines the cocycle $a_{\sigma^i}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^j(a)$; the condition at $i=m$ is exactly $N_{L/K}(a)=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{N_{L/K}(a)=1\iff a=b/\sigma(b)\text{ for some }b\in L^*.}
$$

The reverse implication follows by telescoping the product of conjugates.

Now let $\Delta$ be a finite subgroup of $K^*/K^{*n}$ and put $L=K(\sqrt[n]{\Delta})$. Finitely many representatives generate it; since $\mu_n\subset K$, the polynomials $X^n-a$ split in $L$ and are separable. Thus $L/K$ is finite Galois. For $\sigma\in G$ and $[a]\in\Delta$, choose $\alpha^n=a$ and define the [Kummer pairing](../../../../../../kummer-pairing.md)

$$
\langle\sigma,[a]\rangle=\sigma(\alpha)/\alpha\in\mu_n.
$$

Changing the root or multiplying $a$ by an $n$th power does not change this value. It is multiplicative in both arguments. Since the radicals generate $L$, it embeds $G$ in $\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta,\mu_n)$. Consequently $G$ is abelian of exponent dividing $n$.

For completeness, establish perfection rather than just this injection. Let $\Delta_L$ consist of all classes in $K^*/K^{*n}$ having an $n$th root in $L$. The same construction gives an injective map $\Delta_L\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mu_n)$: the character is trivial precisely when the root lies in $K$. It is surjective by [Hilbert 90](../../../../../../hilbert-s-theorem-90.md). Given $\chi:G\to\mu_n$, apply the cocycle theorem to $a_\sigma=\chi(\sigma)^{-1}$; it gives $\sigma(b)/b=\chi(\sigma)$, so $b^n\in K$ and its class has the required character. A [finite abelian group](../../../../../../finite-abelian-group.md) of exponent dividing $n$ has as many $\mu_n$-characters as elements, by decomposition into cyclic factors. Therefore

$$
|G|\leq|\Delta|\leq|\Delta_L|=|G|.
$$

It follows that $\Delta=\Delta_L$ and

$$
\boxed{[L:K]=|\Delta|,\qquad G\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta,\mu_n).}
$$

Conversely, for any finite abelian $L/K$ of exponent dividing $n$, [Hilbert 90](../../../../../../hilbert-s-theorem-90.md) supplies eigenvectors for every character. Their $n$th powers lie in $K$, and the intersection of their character kernels is trivial, so they generate $L$. This proves the Kummer classification and the inclusion-reversing duality between subgroups and intermediate extensions.

The simplest case is a cyclic extension of degree $m\mid n$: it has a generator $\alpha$ with $\alpha^m\in K$, and its automorphisms multiply $\alpha$ by $m$th [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). Quadratic extensions in characteristic different from two are the case $n=2$. The cohomological summary is the exact sequence $1\to\mu_n\to K_s^*\xrightarrow{z\mapsto z^n}K_s^*\to1$ together with [Hilbert 90](../../../../../../hilbert-s-theorem-90.md), giving $H^1(G_K,\mu_n)\cong K^*/K^{*n}$.
