<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the absolute-value description, a [place of a number field](../../../../../../place-of-a-number-field.md) is an equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values, where equivalent absolute values give the same topology, or differ by a positive power. More generally this defines the rank-one absolute-value places used in weak approximation for a field. For a [number field](../../../../../../number-field.md), finite places correspond to nonzero [prime ideals](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of its [ring of integers](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md), while infinite places correspond to real embeddings and pairs of conjugate complex embeddings. The completion at $v$ is denoted $K_v$.

A [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) place can also be described by a [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) ring $\mathcal O_v\subset K$ and its residue map into $k_v$, extended by sending elements outside that ring to infinity. The finite places above are the discrete-valuation instances of this description.

The [weak approximation theorem](../../../../../../weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values.md) states that for finitely many pairwise inequivalent nontrivial absolute values $v_1,\ldots,v_r$, the diagonal image of $K$ is dense in $\prod_iK_{v_i}$. Equivalently, for arbitrary $a_i\in K_{v_i}$ and $\epsilon_i>0$, there is $a\in K$ such that

$$
\boxed{|a-a_i|_{v_i}<\epsilon_i\quad(1\leq i\leq r).}
$$

The places must be distinct: two requirements at the same completion need not be compatible. In the number-field setting this permits simultaneous prescribed finite congruences and archimedean approximations.
