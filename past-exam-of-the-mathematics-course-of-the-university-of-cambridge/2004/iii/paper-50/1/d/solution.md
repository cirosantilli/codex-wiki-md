Both [Kaluza-Klein theory](../../../../../../kaluza-klein-theory.md) and the [brane-world scenario](../../../../../../brane-world-scenario.md) use a higher-dimensional bulk. Ordinary Kaluza–Klein matter propagates in the compact directions, producing observable massive towers; absence of weak-scale charged modes requires roughly $R^{-1}\gtrsim\mathrm{TeV}$, or **$R\lesssim2\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm m$**, and traditional constructions often take Planck-size radii. Brane-world matter is localized while gravity explores the bulk, so large flat dimensions can reach the **submillimetre order of magnitude** discussed in the 2004 setting. For $n$ flat dimensions, $M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\sim M_D^{n+2}(2\pi R)^n$. These are model-dependent scales, not universal maxima: warped gravity-localizing models can have a noncompact extra dimension. The historical large-flat-dimension mechanism is described in [https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803315.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803315.)
