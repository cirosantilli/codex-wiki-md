<h1 id="4/i/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

**More than three integrals occur when additional symmetry or degeneracy restricts motion beyond a generic three-frequency invariant torus.** In a time-independent central [gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md), energy and the three components of [specific angular momentum](../../../../../../../specific-angular-momentum.md) are four generically independent [isolating integrals](../../../../../../../isolating-integral.md). They fix the orbital plane as well as the radial-motion parameters. A nonclosed planar rosette typically has two-dimensional orbit closure, rather than a three-dimensional [invariant torus](../../../../../../../invariant-torus.md).

The [Kepler orbit](../../../../../../../kepler-orbit.md) is a stronger example. In addition to $E$ and $\mathbf L$, the conserved [Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector](../../../../../../../laplace-runge-lenz-vector.md)

$$
\mathbf A=\mathbf v\times\mathbf L-\mu\frac{\mathbf r}{r},\qquad \mu=GM,
$$

fixes the apsidal direction. Its constraints $\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf L=0$ and $A^2=\mu^2+2EL^2$ leave five independent integrals among $E,\mathbf L,\mathbf A$. It is a maximally [superintegrable Hamiltonian system](../../../../../../../superintegrable-hamiltonian-system.md), with closed nondegenerate bound orbits.

More than three integrals need not form a set of [Poisson-commuting functions](../../../../../../../poisson-commuting-functions.md); the three-integral limit applies to an independent commuting set, rather than to every conserved orbit constraint. At a nonstationary point in six-dimensional [phase space](../../../../../../../phase-space.md), integral gradients annihilate the nonzero flow vector, so at most five are independent. This explains the [isolating integrals and orbital closure dimension](../../../../../../../isolating-integrals-and-orbital-closure-dimension.md) count. A resonance on one isolated orbit does not by itself prove an additional smooth global integral on a neighborhood.
