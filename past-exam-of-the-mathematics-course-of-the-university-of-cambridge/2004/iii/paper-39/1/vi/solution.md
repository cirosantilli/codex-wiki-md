The [asymptotic normality of the Watterson estimator](../../../../../../asymptotic-normality-of-the-watterson-estimator.md) gives approximate standard error $\sqrt{\theta/\log n}$. Thus **precision at a fixed nonrecombining locus improves only logarithmically with sample size**. To halve the asymptotic standard error, one needs $\log n'\simeq4\log n$, or roughly $n'=n^4$, rather than four times as many sampled chromosomes.

For large samples and positive $\widehat\theta$, a plug-in [normal approximation](../../../../../../normal-approximation.md) gives $\widehat\theta\pm1.96\sqrt{\widehat\theta/\log n}$ as an approximate 95% [confidence interval](../../../../../../confidence-interval.md). At low mutation counts it can perform poorly or give a negative endpoint; the exact count model is then preferable. Multiple independent, comparable loci can provide additional independent information, whereas tightly linked chromosomes still share the random ancestry at one locus. [Genetic recombination](../../../../../../genetic-recombination.md), nonneutral evolution and changing population size require corresponding changes to the model.
