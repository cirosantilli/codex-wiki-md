<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [transmission disequilibrium test](../../../../../../transmission-disequilibrium-test.md) is the paired [McNemar test](../../../../../../mcnemar-s-test.md) statistic $(b-c)^2/(b+c)$. From the raw unambiguous-transmission table,

$$
\boxed{\chi^2_{\rm TDT}=\frac{(11-4)^2}{11+4}=\frac{49}{15}\simeq3.267<3.84.}
$$

The formal one-degree-of-freedom [chi-squared distribution](../../../../../../chi-squared-distribution.md) approximation gives $p\simeq0.071$, so **the calculation does not reject at 5%**. Its ordinary null calibration would require a valid missing-parent inclusion rule; the raw arithmetic alone does not establish one.

Using the complete-trio table gives $\chi^2_{\rm TDT}=36/14=18/7\simeq2.571$, with $p\simeq0.109$. It also does not reject. Homozygous-parent transmissions in $a,d$ supply no discordant contrast for this test.
