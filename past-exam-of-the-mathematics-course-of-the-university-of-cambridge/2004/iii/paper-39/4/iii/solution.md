<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The unmatched [haplotype-relative-risk](../../../../../../haplotype-relative-risk.md) counts satisfy $w=a+b$, $x=c+d$, $y=a+c$ and $z=b+d$. For the raw table this gives

$$
\boxed{(w,x,y,z)=(13,8,6,15),\qquad\widehat{\mathrm{OR}}=\frac{wz}{xy}=\frac{65}{16}=4.0625.}
$$

The [Pearson chi-squared test of independence](../../../../../../pearson-chi-squared-test-of-independence.md) in this two-by-two table has statistic

$$
\chi^2=\frac{(w+x+y+z)(wz-xy)^2}{(w+x)(y+z)(w+y)(x+z)}=\frac{2058}{437}\simeq4.709.
$$

Thus the unmatched calculation gives **nominal evidence at 5%**, with $p\simeq0.030$. It differs from the [transmission disequilibrium test](../../../../../../transmission-disequilibrium-test.md) because it uses an unmatched table, and it also inherits the missing-parent selection issue from the raw counts.

For the ten complete trios, $(w,x,y,z)=(12,8,6,14)$, giving [odds ratio](../../../../../../odds-ratio.md) $7/2$ and the unmatched [Pearson chi-squared test of independence](../../../../../../pearson-chi-squared-test-of-independence.md) statistic $40/11\simeq3.636$, with $p\simeq0.057$. That calculation does not reject at 5%. The nominal significance from the raw table therefore should not be presented as a robust association conclusion without a justified model for incomplete families and the unmatched-table assumptions.
