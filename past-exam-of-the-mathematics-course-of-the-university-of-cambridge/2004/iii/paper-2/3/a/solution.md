<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Since the [two-transitive group action](../../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) is primitive, the orbits of any nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) form only the single block $\Omega$. Thus $N$ is transitive. If $N$ is regular, identify $\Omega$ with $N$ by $x\mapsto x\alpha$. Then $G=NG_\alpha$, $N\cap G_\alpha=1$, and [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) by $G_\alpha$ gives [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) of $N$. The [two-transitive group action](../../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) makes these [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) transitive on $N\setminus\{1\}$. All nonidentity elements therefore have the same order, which must be a prime $p$: a suitable power of an element of composite order would have a smaller nontrivial order. [Cauchy's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../../cauchy-theorem-for-groups.md) now shows $|N|=p^d$.

The [center of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) of prime-power order is nontrivial and is a [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md). The transitive [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) action on nonidentity elements therefore forces $Z(N)=N$. Hence $N$ is an [elementary abelian group](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md), which we identify with the additive [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) $\mathbb F_p^d$. [Conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) by $G_\alpha$ is linear on this [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md). It is faithful: an element fixing $\alpha$ and centralizing $N$ fixes every $x\alpha$, hence is the identity permutation. Consequently

$$
\boxed{n=p^d,\qquad G=N\rtimes G_\alpha\le AGL_d(p).}
$$

Here the [general affine group](../../../../../../general-affine-group.md) acts by translations followed by invertible [linear maps](../../../../../../linear-map.md).
