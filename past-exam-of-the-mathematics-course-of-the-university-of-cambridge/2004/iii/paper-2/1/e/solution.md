<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Put $\Phi=\Phi(G)$ and let $H$ be the full preimage of $F(G/\Phi)$ in $G$. The preceding part gives $\Phi\le F(G)$, so the normal nilpotent [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) $F(G)/\Phi$ lies in $F(G/\Phi)$.

For the reverse inclusion, choose a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P$ of $H$. Its [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) $P\Phi/\Phi$ is the unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of the [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) $H/\Phi$, hence a [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md) there and normal in $G/\Phi$. Thus $P\Phi\trianglelefteq G$. The [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $P$ is also a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $P\Phi$: the p-part of $\Phi$ lies in $P$, because $P\cap\Phi$ is a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of the [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $\Phi$ of $H$. Apply the [Frattini argument](../../../../../../frattini-argument.md) to $P\Phi$:

$$
G=(P\Phi)N_G(P)=\Phi N_G(P).
$$

The [Frattini subgroup](../../../../../../frattini-subgroup.md) property forces $N_G(P)=G$. Hence every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $H$ is normal, so $H$ is a [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) and $H\le F(G)$. Together the inclusions prove

$$
\boxed{F(G/\Phi(G))=F(G)/\Phi(G).}
$$

This is [Frattini lifting of nilpotence](../../../../../../frattini-lifting-of-nilpotence.md); simply calling an extension of [nilpotent groups](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) nilpotent would not establish it.
