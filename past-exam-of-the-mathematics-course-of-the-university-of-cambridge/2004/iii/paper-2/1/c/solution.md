<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Write $F=F(G)$, $C=C_G(F)$ and $D=C\cap F=Z(F)$. Both $C$ and $D$ are normal in $G$. Suppose $C>D$. Choose a [minimal normal subgroup](../../../../../../minimal-normal-subgroup.md) $K/D$ of $G/D$ contained in $C/D$. In a finite [soluble group](../../../../../../solvable-group.md), such a [minimal normal subgroup](../../../../../../minimal-normal-subgroup.md) is [elementary abelian](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md): the last nontrivial term of its [derived series](../../../../../../derived-series.md) is an abelian [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md), so minimality makes the whole [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) abelian; its primary components and [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of elements killed by $p$ then force one prime and exponent $p$.

It follows that $K'\le D$. Moreover $K\le C$ centralizes $D\le F$, so $K'\le Z(K)$. Therefore $K$ is a [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md) of class at most two. Since $K$ is normal in $G$, the definition of the [Fitting subgroup](../../../../../../fitting-subgroup.md) gives $K\le F$. But then $K\le C\cap F=D$, a contradiction. This proves the [Fitting subgroup is self-centralizing in soluble groups](../../../../../../fitting-subgroup-is-self-centralizing-in-soluble-groups.md):

$$
\boxed{C_G(F(G))\le F(G),\qquad C_G(F(G))=Z(F(G)).}
$$
