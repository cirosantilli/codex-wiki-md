<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Put $\Phi=\Phi(G)$ and choose a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P$ of $\Phi$. Since $\Phi$ is normal, the [Frattini argument](../../../../../../frattini-argument.md) gives $G=\Phi N_G(P)$: for $g\in G$, the [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P^g$ and $P$ of $\Phi$ are conjugate by an element of $\Phi$, leaving an element of $N_G(P)$. The non-generator property of the [Frattini subgroup](../../../../../../frattini-subgroup.md) proved above forces $N_G(P)=G$. Thus every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $\Phi$ is normal. Distinct such [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) commute because their [group commutators](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) lie in their trivial intersection. Hence $\Phi$ is a [nilpotent normal subgroup](../../../../../../nilpotent-normal-subgroup.md), and

$$
\boxed{\Phi(G)\le F(G).}
$$
