<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

It suffices to centralize each [minimal normal subgroup](../../../../../../minimal-normal-subgroup.md) $M$. The [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $M\cap F(G)$ is normal in $G$, so it is either $1$ or $M$. In the first case

$$
[M,F(G)]\le M\cap F(G)=1.
$$

In the second case $M$ is a [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md). Its nontrivial [group center](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) is a [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md) of $M$, hence normal in $G$, so minimality makes $M$ abelian. The primary components, which are [characteristic subgroups](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md), and the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) killed by a prime show that $M$ is [elementary abelian](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md) of exponent $p$. Thus $M\le O_p(G)$.

The [finite p-group](../../../../../../finite-p-group.md) $O_p(G)$ acts by [conjugation](../../../../../../conjugation.md) on $M$. Its orbits have prime-power sizes; hence the number of fixed elements is congruent to $|M|=0$ modulo $p$. The identity is fixed, so there is a nonidentity fixed element. Therefore $M\cap Z(O_p(G))\ne1$. This intersection is normal in $G$, and minimality makes it $M$. The other [p-cores](../../../../../../p-core.md) commute with $O_p(G)$, and hence with $M$. Thus every factor of $F(G)$ centralizes $M$. Since the [group socle](../../../../../../socle-of-a-finite-group.md) is generated by these [minimal normal subgroups](../../../../../../minimal-normal-subgroup.md),

$$
\boxed{F(G)\le C_G(\operatorname{Soc}(G)).}
$$
