<h1 id="3/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Subtract the two [density operators](../../../../../../density-matrix.md). The difference is Hermitian with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entry $\alpha\overline\beta(1-\overline c)$. A matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\\overline a&0\end{pmatrix}$ has [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $\pm|a|$, because its square is $|a|^2I$. Its two [singular values](../../../../../../singular-value.md) are therefore both $|a|$. By the definition of the [trace norm](../../../../../../trace-norm.md),

$$
\boxed{d(\rho,\rho')=|\alpha\beta|\,|1-c|.}
$$

For the intended real-overlap [phase-flip channel](../../../../../../phase-flip-channel.md), $1-c=2p$, giving

$$
\boxed{d(\rho,\rho')=2p|\alpha\beta|.}
$$

This vanishes for a [computational basis](../../../../../../computational-basis.md) state, since a phase flip then changes only an unobservable global phase. Its largest value at fixed $p$ is $p$, attained when $|\alpha|=|\beta|=1/\sqrt2$. The general formula remains correct even when the probabilistic premise of part 1 fails.
