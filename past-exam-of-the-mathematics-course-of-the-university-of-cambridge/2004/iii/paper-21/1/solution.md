<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

The [determinant-one cyclic quotient of the affine plane](../../../../../determinant-one-cyclic-quotient-of-the-affine-plane.md) has the closed embedding

$$
\boxed{\mathbb A^2/\mu_n\cong\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb A^3:uv=w^n\},\qquad
\pi(x,y)=(x^n,y^n,xy).}
$$

Here $\mu_n$ denotes the cyclic group generated by the given diagonal matrix. We establish the presentation of the [polynomial invariant ring](../../../../../polynomial-invariant-ring.md) and verify that its fibres really are the [group orbits](../../../../../orbit-of-a-group-action.md).

On the [coordinate ring](../../../../../coordinate-ring.md) $A=\mathbb C[x,y]$, the inverse-pullback action multiplies $x^ay^b$ by $\zeta^{b-a}$. Since these [monomials](../../../../../monomial.md) are a basis, a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is invariant precisely when each of its nonzero [monomials](../../../../../monomial.md) satisfies $a-b\equiv0\pmod n$. If $a\ge b$, such a [monomial](../../../../../monomial.md) is $(x^n)^{(a-b)/n}(xy)^b$; if $b\ge a$, it is $(y^n)^{(b-a)/n}(xy)^a$. Therefore

$$
A^{\mu_n}=\mathbb C[x^n,y^n,xy].
$$

These generators give a [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md) [homomorphism](../../../../../homomorphism.md) $\mathbb C[u,v,w]\to A^{\mu_n}$, and $uv-w^n$ lies in its [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md). To prove there are no further relations, reduce any [monomial](../../../../../monomial.md) using $uv=w^n$. The result is a linear combination of

$$
w^j,\qquad u^iw^j,\qquad v^iw^j\quad(i\ge1,\ j\ge0).
$$

Their images are respectively $x^jy^j$, $x^{ni+j}y^j$ and $x^jy^{ni+j}$. All have distinct exponent pairs, so they are linearly independent in $A$. Any [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) in the [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../../../kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism.md) has zero reduced expression and hence belongs to $(uv-w^n)$. Consequently

$$
A^{\mu_n}\cong\mathbb C[u,v,w]/(uv-w^n).
$$

This proves the claimed [closed subvariety](../../../../../closed-subvariety.md) embedding of the affine quotient.

For surjectivity of $\pi$, if $u\ne0$ choose $x^n=u$ and set $y=w/x$. Then $y^n=w^n/u=v$. If $u=0$, the relation forces $w=0$; choose $x=0$ and any $n$th root of $v$ for $y$. If two points have the same image and $x\ne0$, equality of their $n$th powers gives $x'=\zeta^kx$ and equality of $xy$ gives $y'=\zeta^{-k}y$. If $x=0$, both first coordinates vanish, and the same argument using $y^n$ gives the orbit relation; the case $x=y=0$ has only that one point in its fibre. Thus every fibre is exactly one [group orbit](../../../../../orbit-of-a-group-action.md).

The map is finite: $x$ and $y$ satisfy the monic equations $T^n-u=0$ and $T^n-v=0$, and the finitely many [monomials](../../../../../monomial.md) $x^ay^b$, $0\le a,b<n$, span $A$ as a [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over its [polynomial invariant ring](../../../../../polynomial-invariant-ring.md). A [finite morphism](../../../../../finite-morphism.md) is closed when it is [surjective](../../../../../surjective-function.md) as here and therefore gives the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md). For a [principal open subset](../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) defined by an invariant $h$, its regular functions pull back to $A_h$ and

$$
(A_h)^{\mu_n}=(A^{\mu_n})_h.
$$

Indeed, because $h$ is invariant, an invariant fraction with denominator $h^k$ has invariant numerator. These [principal open subsets](../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) cover the [affine hypersurface](../../../../../affine-hypersurface.md), proving the required local equality of invariant regular functions. Together with the orbit fibres, this verifies the [geometric quotient](../../../../../geometric-quotient.md), not merely a bijection of underlying sets.
