<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

Put $A=\mathbb C[V]=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}A_d$ and $R=A^G$. The linear action preserves [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) degree, so $R=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}R_d$ with $R_0=\mathbb C$. We prove [Hilbert finite generation for reductive invariants](../../../../../hilbert-finite-generation-for-reductive-invariants.md) by combining finite ideal generation in $A$ with a degree-lowering argument in $R$.

Over $\mathbb C$, representations of a [reductive algebraic group](../../../../../reductive-group.md) are completely reducible. On each finite-dimensional $A_d$, project its [isotypic component](../../../../../isotypic-component.md) for the [trivial representation](../../../../../trivial-representation.md) onto itself and its nontrivial summands onto zero. These projections together form a degree-preserving [Reynolds operator](../../../../../reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring.md) $\mathcal R:A\to R$, with $\mathcal R|_R=\operatorname{id}$. The crucial additional property is

$$
\mathcal R(ah)=a\mathcal R(h)\qquad(a\in R,\ h\in A).
$$

Here is why it holds rather than being an arbitrary projection property. For a homogeneous invariant $a$, multiplication by $a$ is a $G$-equivariant linear map $A_d\to A_{d+\deg a}$. It sends invariant vectors to invariant vectors. On a nontrivial irreducible summand, its composition with projection onto the target's trivial summand is zero, since there is no nonzero equivariant map from a nontrivial [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md) to the [trivial representation](../../../../../trivial-representation.md). It therefore commutes with the [Reynolds operators](../../../../../reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring.md). Summing the homogeneous components of $a$ and $h$ proves the displayed [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) property in general.

Let $R_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}R_d$ and form the ideal $I=AR_+$ in the ambient [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) ring. The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) makes $A$ a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), so $I$ is generated by finitely many homogeneous positive-degree invariants $f_1,\ldots,f_s$. To justify choosing the generators from this particular set, start with any finite ideal generating set; each of its members is a finite sum of multiples of homogeneous members of $R_+$, and the union of those finitely many invariants then generates $I$. If $I=0$, there are no positive-degree invariants and the answer is simply $R=\mathbb C$.

Otherwise consider a homogeneous invariant $f$ of degree $d>0$. Since $f\in I$, write

$$
f=\sum_{i=1}^s h_i f_i,\qquad h_i\in A_{d-\deg f_i},
$$

with $h_i=0$ when that degree is negative. Taking the degree-$d$ component of any ideal expression gives exactly such an expression. Apply the [Reynolds operator](../../../../../reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring.md) and its [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) property:

$$
f=\mathcal R(f)=\sum_{i=1}^s\mathcal R(h_i)f_i.
$$

Each coefficient $\mathcal R(h_i)$ is invariant and has degree strictly smaller than $d$, because every $f_i$ has positive degree. Induction on $d$, starting from $R_0=\mathbb C$, now shows that every homogeneous invariant lies in $\mathbb C[f_1,\ldots,f_s]$. An arbitrary invariant is a finite sum of invariant homogeneous components, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C[V]^G=\mathbb C[f_1,\ldots,f_s].}
$$

This proves finite generation as an algebra. No Noetherian property of the [polynomial invariant ring](../../../../../polynomial-invariant-ring.md) was assumed in obtaining the generators.
