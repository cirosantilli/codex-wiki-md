<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Set $S=T=0$ and choose **$\boxed{\bar\nu(r)=\nu_0r}$**, with $\nu_0>0$ constant. Define $y=\sqrt r$ and $g=r^{3/2}\Sigma$. Then $\sqrt r\bar\nu\Sigma=\nu_0g$, and $\partial_r=(2y)^{-1}\partial_y$. Directly transforming the [Keplerian viscous diffusion equation](../../../../../../keplerian-viscous-diffusion-equation.md) gives

$$
\Sigma_t=\frac{3\nu_0}{4r^{3/2}}g_{yy},\qquad g_t=\frac{3\nu_0}{4}g_{yy}.
$$

The [heat-equation transform for linear-radius disk viscosity](../../../../../../heat-equation-transform-for-linear-radius-disk-viscosity.md) is completed by $x=2y/\sqrt{3\nu_0}$:

$$
\boxed{x=2\sqrt{\frac r{3\nu_0}},\qquad g=r^{3/2}\Sigma,\qquad g_t=g_{xx}.}
$$

Equivalently one can use $g=\sqrt r\bar\nu\Sigma$, which differs only by a constant. This is the viscosity law making the transformed diffusivity constant under this square-root-radius reduction. Initial and boundary conditions transform too; the change of variables does not supply unspecified disc-edge data.

The physical diffusion arises from [angular momentum transport](../../../../../../angular-momentum-transport.md). Differential rotation creates a viscous shear stress, which transfers angular momentum between neighboring annuli. Gas that loses angular momentum moves inward, while gas that gains it can move outward. Combining [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) with [mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) turns the stress gradient into a radial mass flux and its divergence into a second radial derivative. Positive [kinematic viscosity](../../../../../../kinematic-viscosity.md) smooths the appropriately weighted profile; matter need not all move in one direction, and an accreting disc can simultaneously spread outward. Viscosity dissipates orbital energy but redistributes angular momentum internally except for boundary losses.
