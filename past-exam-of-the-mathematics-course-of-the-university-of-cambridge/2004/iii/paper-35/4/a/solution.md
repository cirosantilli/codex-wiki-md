<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For an invertible continuous [cumulative distribution function](../../../../../../cumulative-distribution-function.md), monotonicity gives

$$
\mathbb P(F^{-1}(U)\leq x)=\mathbb P(U\leq F(x))=F(x),\qquad U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1).
$$

Thus **each transformed observation has the desired distribution**. More generally use the [quantile function](../../../../../../quantile-function.md) $q(u)=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq u\}$; the same event identity proves [inverse transform sampling](../../../../../../inverse-transform-sampling.md) even when there are atoms. Independent uniform inputs give independent transformed samples; mere uniform marginal distributions do not imply independence between different samples.
