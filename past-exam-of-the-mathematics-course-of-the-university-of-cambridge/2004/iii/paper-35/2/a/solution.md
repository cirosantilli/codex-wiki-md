<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Indifference means equality of the risky [expected utility](../../../../../../expected-utility.md) and the utility of the sure reduced wealth. A second-order [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md), using $\mathbb E\phi=0$, gives

$$
\mathbb E U(W(1+\phi))=U(W)+\frac12W^2U''(W)\mathbb E\phi^2+o(\mathbb E\phi^2).
$$

For the sure alternative,

$$
U(W(1-\psi))=U(W)-WU'(W)\psi+O(\psi^2).
$$

Equating these and retaining the leading terms produces the [small multiplicative risk premium](../../../../../../small-multiplicative-risk-premium.md)

$$
\boxed{\psi\simeq-\frac12\frac{WU''(W)}{U'(W)}\operatorname{Var}(\phi).}
$$

For increasing concave [utility functions](../../../../../../utility-function-split.md) this is positive. The approximation assumes genuinely small shocks with controlled Taylor remainders and positive final wealth; small [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) alone would not justify it for rare large shocks.
