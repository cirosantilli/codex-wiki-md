<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

An [orientation](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) of a topological n-manifold without boundary is a choice of generator $\mu_x$ of each local [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) group $H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, compatible in coordinate neighbourhoods. Equivalently, local [orientations](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) of coordinate discs are chosen so their overlap changes have degree $+1$. This trivializes the [orientation local system](../../../../../orientation-local-system.md).

Here the printed $H^n_{\mathrm{ct}}$ denotes [compactly supported cohomology](../../../../../compactly-supported-cohomology.md), which we write $H_c^n$. We prove the cyclicity assertion without assuming [Poincare duality](../../../../../poincare-duality.md). By the [compactly supported cohomology of Euclidean space](../../../../../compactly-supported-cohomology-of-euclidean-space.md), an open n-disc has one copy of $\mathbb Z$ in degree $n$ and zero in all other degrees. The compact-support [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) for open sets contains

$$
H_c^n(U\cap V)\longrightarrow H_c^n(U)\oplus H_c^n(V)
\longrightarrow H_c^n(U\cup V)\longrightarrow H_c^{n+1}(U\cap V).
$$

Use the finite good cover allowed in the paper. Induction on the number of discs shows that any union of its discs has zero compact-support [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md) above degree $n$: at each step the intersection with the new disc is itself a union of fewer discs with a good cover, so the displayed sequence and its higher-degree terms apply.

The same induction presents $H_c^n(M)$ by a generator $u_i$ for each cover disc, with overlap relations $u_i=\epsilon_{ij}u_j$, where $\epsilon_{ij}=\pm1$ is its [orientation](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) transition sign. Indeed $H_c^n$ of the intersection is generated by its overlap discs; extension by zero sends each generator to the two disc generators with these signs. Passing through the compact-support [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) imposes exactly those relations. This is [top compactly supported cohomology from orientation signs](../../../../../top-compactly-supported-cohomology-from-orientation-signs.md).

Since $M$ is connected, the overlap graph is connected: a disconnection would split the union into two disjoint nonempty open subsets. A spanning tree expresses every generator as $u$ or $-u$, so $H_c^n(M)$ is cyclic. Additional overlap relations can only impose $2u=0$. If $M$ is oriented, use its compatible local [orientations](../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md); every relation becomes $u_i=u_j$, and the presentation is exactly one free generator. Thus

$$
\boxed{H_c^n(M)\text{ is cyclic};\qquad H_c^n(M)\cong\mathbb Z\text{ if }M\text{ is oriented}.}
$$

In fact a nonorientable connected manifold has an orientation-reversing loop in this transition graph and gives $\mathbb Z/2$. In dimension zero the connected manifold is a point and the assertion is immediate.

For a closed oriented n-manifold, [Poincare duality](../../../../../poincare-duality.md) says that its [fundamental class](../../../../../fundamental-class.md) $[M]\in H_n(M;\mathbb Z)$ gives isomorphisms by [cap product](../../../../../cap-product.md)

$$
H^q(M;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\ \cap[M]\ }H_{n-q}(M;\mathbb Z)\qquad\text{for all }q.
$$

For an oriented manifold that is not necessarily compact, the corresponding ordinary-homology statement is $H_c^q(M;\mathbb Z)\cong H_{n-q}(M;\mathbb Z)$, using the locally finite [fundamental class](../../../../../fundamental-class.md). The version without compact supports on [cohomology](../../../../../cohomology-split.md) pairs with locally finite [homology](../../../../../homology-split.md) instead. Orientability and the absence of boundary are essential to these untwisted formulas; for a manifold with boundary one needs [Poincare-Lefschetz duality](../../../../../lefschetz-duality.md), and for a nonorientable manifold one uses the [orientation local system](../../../../../orientation-local-system.md).

For the following cases, $H_0=\mathbb Z$ forces connectedness. The [universal coefficient theorem for cohomology](../../../../../universal-coefficient-theorem-for-cohomology.md) gives

$$
H^1(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(M;\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z),
$$

since the Ext term from $H_0=\mathbb Z$ vanishes. Combining with duality yields the [third homology of a closed oriented four-manifold](../../../../../third-homology-of-a-closed-oriented-four-manifold.md) constraint $H_3\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1,\mathbb Z)$.
