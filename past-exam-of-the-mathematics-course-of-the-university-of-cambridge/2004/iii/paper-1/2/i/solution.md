<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $M=0$, all its [localizations](../../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) are zero. Conversely, take $0\ne m\in M$. Its [annihilator](../../../../../../annihilator-ring-theory.md) $I=\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)$ is a proper [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md), so it is contained in a maximal [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) $P$, which is prime. The zero criterion for [module localization](../../../../../../localization-of-a-module.md) says that $m/1=0$ in $M_P$ only if some $s\notin P$ satisfies $sm=0$. This would put $s\in I\subseteq P$, a contradiction. Therefore $M_P\ne0$, proving

$$
\boxed{M=0\iff M_P=0\text{ for every prime ideal }P.}
$$

In fact maximal [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) suffice, and neither finite generation nor [Noetherianity](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) is needed for this implication.
