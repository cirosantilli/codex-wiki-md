↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

For $n\ge1$, the [Weyl algebra](../../../../../weyl-algebra.md) $A=A_n(\mathbb C)$ is generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_n,D_1,\ldots,D_n$ with $[D_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$ and the other generator [commutators](../../../../../commutator.md) zero. Iterating the differential [Ore extension](../../../../../ore-extension.md) construction gives the ordered [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $x^\alpha D^\beta$. In the [Bernstein filtration](../../../../../bernstein-filtration.md), both kinds of generator have degree one, and

$$
F_jA=\operatorname{span}\{x^\alpha D^\beta:|\alpha|+|\beta|\le j\},\qquad
\dim F_jA=\binom{j+2n}{2n},\qquad
\operatorname{gr}A\cong\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n].
$$

Leading symbols show that $A$ is a [noncommutative domain](../../../../../noncommutative-domain.md). The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../hilbert-basis-theorem.md) and homogeneous-generator lifting by decreasing degree make it both left and right [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md).

It is also a [simple ring](../../../../../simple-ring.md). In a nonzero two-sided [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md), choose a nonzero element and a [monomial](../../../../../monomial.md) $x^{\alpha_0}D^{\beta_0}$ of maximal total degree with nonzero coefficient $c$. Applying $(\operatorname{ad}D)^{\alpha_0}(\operatorname{ad}x)^{\beta_0}$ eliminates every lower-degree term and every different term of that maximal degree. The result is the nonzero [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) $(-1)^{|\beta_0|}\alpha_0!\beta_0!c$. Thus the [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) contains $1$. Here the [scalar extraction by iterated Weyl commutators](../../../../../scalar-extraction-by-iterated-weyl-commutators.md) uses characteristic zero. Despite simplicity, $A$ has no nonzero finite-dimensional [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md): in such a representation $[D_1,x_1]=I$ would have [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $0=\dim M$, impossible over $\mathbb C$. Its interesting [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) theory is necessarily infinite-dimensional.

For a finitely generated left [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$, choose a finite-dimensional generating subspace $M_0$ and set $M_j=F_jA\,M_0$. This is a [good filtration of a module](../../../../../good-filtration-of-a-module.md). Its [associated graded module](../../../../../associated-graded-module.md) is finite over the graded polynomial [ring](../../../../../ring.md), so its cumulative Hilbert function is eventually a polynomial,

$$
\dim M_j=\frac{e(M)}{d(M)!}j^{d(M)}+O(j^{d(M)-1})\quad(M\ne0),
$$

with positive integer multiplicity $e(M)$. These are the [Bernstein growth dimension of a Weyl algebra module](../../../../../bernstein-growth-dimension-of-a-weyl-algebra-module.md) and its [multiplicity of a filtered module](../../../../../multiplicity-of-a-filtered-module.md). Two good filtrations bound each other after fixed shifts: lift finite homogeneous generators in one filtration, bound their degrees in the other, and use compatibility with $F_jA$; then reverse the roles. The growth degree and leading coefficient therefore do not depend on the [good filtration of a module](../../../../../good-filtration-of-a-module.md). The degree is also the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../../../gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module.md) and the dimension of the [characteristic variety of a Weyl algebra module](../../../../../characteristic-variety-of-a-weyl-algebra-module.md), the support of $\operatorname{gr}M$. Since $M$ is a quotient of a finite sum of copies of $A$, $d(M)\le2n$.

The essential lower bound is the [Bernstein inequality for Weyl algebra modules](../../../../../bernstein-inequality-for-weyl-algebra-modules.md):

$$
\boxed{M\ne0\text{ finitely generated}\Longrightarrow d(M)\ge n.}
$$

Here is a full proof. Multiplication gives a linear map $F_jA\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(M_j,M_{2j})$. Prove it injective by induction on $j$. At $j=0$ a [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md) killing $M_0\ne0$ must vanish. If $a\in F_jA$ kills $M_j$, then for every generator $z$, its [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) $[a,z]\in F_{j-1}A$ kills $M_{j-1}$: both $azm$ and $zam$ are zero for $m\in M_{j-1}$. Induction gives $[a,z]=0$ for all generators. In the ordered [basis](../../../../../basis.md), $[D_i,a]$ differentiates the $x_i$ exponents and $[x_i,a]$ differentiates the $D_i$ exponents with a minus sign. Vanishing of all these [commutators](../../../../../commutator.md) in characteristic zero forces $a$ to be a [scalar](../../../../../scalar.md). It then vanishes because it kills $M_0$. This proves the [faithful finite-step action of a Weyl algebra](../../../../../faithful-finite-step-action-of-a-weyl-algebra.md) and hence

$$
\binom{j+2n}{2n}\le(\dim M_j)(\dim M_{2j})\le(\dim M_{2j})^2.
$$

Comparison of degrees gives $2n\le2d(M)$, proving the inequality. Equally, without invoking eventual polynomiality, this estimate directly bounds the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of a module](../../../../../gelfand-kirillov-dimension-of-a-module.md) below by $n$. Right [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) satisfy the same inequality through the filtration-preserving isomorphism $A\cong A^{\mathrm{op}}$, $x_i\mapsto x_i$, $D_i\mapsto-D_i$.

A nonzero finitely generated [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) with $d(M)=n$ is a [holonomic Weyl algebra module](../../../../../holonomic-weyl-algebra-module.md); the zero [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is conventionally included. This minimal-growth class is stable under [submodules](../../../../../submodule.md), [quotient modules](../../../../../quotient-module.md) and extensions. For $0\to N\to M\to Q\to0$, take the induced and quotient filtrations. Their graded sequence is exact; [Noetherianity](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) of $\operatorname{gr}A$ makes the induced filtrations good. Thus the Hilbert functions add and

$$
d(M)=\max(d(N),d(Q));
$$

if both nonzero terms have the top dimension, their multiplicities add. For a [holonomic Weyl algebra module](../../../../../holonomic-weyl-algebra-module.md), every nonzero subquotient has dimension $n$, by the upper bound inherited from $M$ and Bernstein's lower bound. Each nonzero factor in a strict chain therefore consumes positive integral multiplicity. This proves the [multiplicity bounds the length of a holonomic Weyl module](../../../../../multiplicity-bounds-the-length-of-a-holonomic-weyl-module.md):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{length}_A(M)\le e(M).}
$$

In particular a holonomic [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is both [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) and [Artinian](../../../../../artinian-ring.md) and has finite length, although its vector-space dimension is infinite. This distinction separates finite-dimensional representation theory from finite-length Weyl [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) theory.

The polynomial [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, with $x_i$ acting by multiplication and $D_i$ by differentiation, is generated by $1$. Its $M_j$ consists of polynomials of degree at most $j$, with dimension $\binom{j+n}{n}$, so $d=n$ and $e=1$. It is simple: differentiating a maximal-degree [monomial](../../../../../monomial.md) of a nonzero polynomial isolates a nonzero constant, and multiplication by polynomials then generates the whole [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md). A second basic example is the delta [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md)

$$
A\Big/\sum_iAx_i.
$$

Using the reverse ordered [basis](../../../../../basis.md) $D^\beta x^\alpha$, its [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) is $\mathbb C[D_1,\ldots,D_n]$; $D_i$ acts by multiplication and $x_i$ by negative differentiation. The same argument proves simplicity and holonomicity. These two examples are exchanged by the [automorphism](../../../../../automorphism.md) $x_i\mapsto D_i$, $D_i\mapsto-x_i$. Free [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), in contrast, have growth dimension $2n$ and are not holonomic. Thus the central organizing invariants are [good filtration of a module](../../../../../good-filtration-of-a-module.md), growth dimension, characteristic support and multiplicity, with Bernstein's estimate explaining why dimension $n$ is the natural smallest nonzero size.
