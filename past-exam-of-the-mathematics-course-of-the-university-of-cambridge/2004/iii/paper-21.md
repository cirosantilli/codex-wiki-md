## 1

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 21](paper-21.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The [determinant-one cyclic quotient of the affine plane](../../../representation-theory.md#determinant-one-cyclic-quotient-of-the-affine-plane) has the closed embedding

$$
\boxed{\mathbb A^2/\mu_n\cong\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb A^3:uv=w^n\},\qquad
\pi(x,y)=(x^n,y^n,xy).}
$$

Here $\mu_n$ denotes the cyclic group generated by the given diagonal matrix. We establish the presentation of the [polynomial invariant ring](../../../representation-theory.md#polynomial-invariant-ring) and verify that its fibres really are the [group orbits](../../../group-theory.md#orbit-of-a-group-action).

On the [coordinate ring](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#coordinate-ring) $A=\mathbb C[x,y]$, the inverse-pullback action multiplies $x^ay^b$ by $\zeta^{b-a}$. Since these [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) are a basis, a [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) is invariant precisely when each of its nonzero [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) satisfies $a-b\equiv0\pmod n$. If $a\ge b$, such a [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) is $(x^n)^{(a-b)/n}(xy)^b$; if $b\ge a$, it is $(y^n)^{(b-a)/n}(xy)^a$. Therefore

$$
A^{\mu_n}=\mathbb C[x^n,y^n,xy].
$$

These generators give a [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) [homomorphism](../../../algebra.md#homomorphism) $\mathbb C[u,v,w]\to A^{\mu_n}$, and $uv-w^n$ lies in its [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism). To prove there are no further relations, reduce any [monomial](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) using $uv=w^n$. The result is a linear combination of

$$
w^j,\qquad u^iw^j,\qquad v^iw^j\quad(i\ge1,\ j\ge0).
$$

Their images are respectively $x^jy^j$, $x^{ni+j}y^j$ and $x^jy^{ni+j}$. All have distinct exponent pairs, so they are linearly independent in $A$. Any [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) in the [kernel of a ring homomorphism](../../../commutative-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-ring-homomorphism) has zero reduced expression and hence belongs to $(uv-w^n)$. Consequently

$$
A^{\mu_n}\cong\mathbb C[u,v,w]/(uv-w^n).
$$

This proves the claimed [closed subvariety](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#closed-subvariety) embedding of the affine quotient.

For surjectivity of $\pi$, if $u\ne0$ choose $x^n=u$ and set $y=w/x$. Then $y^n=w^n/u=v$. If $u=0$, the relation forces $w=0$; choose $x=0$ and any $n$th root of $v$ for $y$. If two points have the same image and $x\ne0$, equality of their $n$th powers gives $x'=\zeta^kx$ and equality of $xy$ gives $y'=\zeta^{-k}y$. If $x=0$, both first coordinates vanish, and the same argument using $y^n$ gives the orbit relation; the case $x=y=0$ has only that one point in its fibre. Thus every fibre is exactly one [group orbit](../../../group-theory.md#orbit-of-a-group-action).

The map is finite: $x$ and $y$ satisfy the monic equations $T^n-u=0$ and $T^n-v=0$, and the finitely many [monomials](../../../polynomial.md#monomial) $x^ay^b$, $0\le a,b<n$, span $A$ as a [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) over its [polynomial invariant ring](../../../representation-theory.md#polynomial-invariant-ring). A [finite morphism](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#finite-morphism) is closed when it is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) as here and therefore gives the [quotient topology](../../../topology.md#quotient-topology). For a [principal open subset](../../../ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) defined by an invariant $h$, its regular functions pull back to $A_h$ and

$$
(A_h)^{\mu_n}=(A^{\mu_n})_h.
$$

Indeed, because $h$ is invariant, an invariant fraction with denominator $h^k$ has invariant numerator. These [principal open subsets](../../../ringed-space.md#principal-open-subscheme) cover the [affine hypersurface](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#affine-hypersurface), proving the required local equality of invariant regular functions. Together with the orbit fibres, this verifies the [geometric quotient](../../../toric-geometry.md#geometric-quotient), not merely a bijection of underlying sets.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 21](paper-21.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

Using the printed convention that the projective points represent lines of [binary quartics](../../../lie-theory.md#binary-quartic), the locus of [GIT stable points](../../../representation-theory.md#stable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory) is

$$
\boxed{\{[f]:f\text{ has four distinct roots in }\mathbb P^1\}
=\{[f]:\operatorname{Disc}(f)\ne0\}.}
$$

The root at infinity is included. We determine stability by calculating every relevant [algebraic one-parameter subgroup](../../../lie-theory.md#algebraic-one-parameter-subgroup) weight.

Use the natural action $(g\cdot f)(X,Y)=f(g^{-1}(X,Y))$. Every nontrivial [algebraic one-parameter subgroup](../../../lie-theory.md#algebraic-one-parameter-subgroup) of $SL_2$ is conjugate to $\lambda_m(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t^m,t^{-m})$ for some integer $m>0$: its two integral [weights of a representation](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-of-a-representation) sum to zero, and a basis of [weight vectors](../../../semisimple-lie-algebra.md#weight-vector) can be rescaled to have determinant one. Write

$$
f(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^4a_jX^{4-j}Y^j,\qquad
\lambda_m(t)\cdot X^{4-j}Y^j=t^{m(2j-4)}X^{4-j}Y^j.
$$

If $r=\min\{j:a_j\ne0\}$, then $r$ is the [multiplicity of a root](../../../polynomial.md#multiplicity-of-a-root) at the projective point $[1:0]$: in the local coordinate $Y/X$, the [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) begins with a nonzero multiple of $(Y/X)^r$.

The [Hilbert-Mumford criterion for projective stability](../../../representation-theory.md#hilbert-mumford-criterion-for-projective-stability), with $\mu=-\min$(active weights), now gives

$$
\mu([f],\lambda_m)=m(4-2r).
$$

Being a [GIT stable point](../../../representation-theory.md#stable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory) requires this number to be strictly positive for every nontrivial subgroup. Conjugating the subgroup lets $[1:0]$ range over the entire [projective line](../../../finite-group-theory.md#projective-line). Thus the [root-multiplicity criterion for stable binary forms](../../../representation-theory.md#root-multiplicity-criterion-for-stable-binary-forms) becomes $r_p(f)<2$ at every point $p$. A nonzero degree-four form over $\mathbb C$ has four projective roots counted with multiplicity, so this means precisely four distinct roots. This is equivalently nonvanishing of its [discriminant](../../../polynomial.md#discriminant).

The boundary cases show why a weak inequality would give the wrong answer. A triple or quadruple root can be moved to $[1:0]$, leaving only strictly positive weights; its affine representative tends to zero under that subgroup, so it is unstable. If all root multiplicities are at most two, all Hilbert-Mumford values are nonnegative and the point is a [GIT semistable point](../../../representation-theory.md#semistable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory). A double root gives a zero value for a suitable subgroup, so never represents a [GIT stable point](../../../representation-theory.md#stable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory). More concretely, after putting that double root at $[1:0]$ one has

$$
f=Y^2(aX^2+bXY+cY^2),\qquad a\ne0,
$$

and $\lambda_1(t)\cdot f\to aX^2Y^2$. When there are three distinct roots, this limiting form, representing a [GIT semistable point](../../../representation-theory.md#semistable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory) with two distinct roots, is outside the original [group orbit](../../../group-theory.md#orbit-of-a-group-action), so that [group orbit](../../../group-theory.md#orbit-of-a-group-action) is not closed in the semistable locus. When there are two double roots, a change of coordinates gives $X^2Y^2$, which has the positive-dimensional diagonal torus as [stabilizer](../../../group-theory.md#stabilizer-subgroup). Neither case represents a [GIT stable point](../../../representation-theory.md#stable-projective-point-in-geometric-invariant-theory).

For a form with four distinct roots, the projective [stabilizer](../../../group-theory.md#stabilizer-subgroup) is finite: it permutes the four roots, and a [Möbius transformation](../../../group-theory.md#mobius-transformation) fixing three distinct points is the identity. Thus its image in $PGL_2$ injects into the finite [permutation group](../../../finite-group-theory.md#permutation-group) on the roots. The [kernel of a group homomorphism](../../../group-theory.md#kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism) for $SL_2\to PGL_2$ is $\{\pm I\}$, also finite. This agrees with the finite-stabilizer condition in [geometric invariant theory](../../../representation-theory.md#geometric-invariant-theory). The weight calculation already supplies the required strict stability condition for every conjugate subgroup.

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Put $A=\mathbb C[V]=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}A_d$ and $R=A^G$. The linear action preserves [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) degree, so $R=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}R_d$ with $R_0=\mathbb C$. We prove [Hilbert finite generation for reductive invariants](../../../representation-theory.md#hilbert-finite-generation-for-reductive-invariants) by combining finite ideal generation in $A$ with a degree-lowering argument in $R$.

Over $\mathbb C$, representations of a [reductive algebraic group](../../../lie-theory.md#reductive-group) are completely reducible. On each finite-dimensional $A_d$, project its [isotypic component](../../../module-theory.md#isotypic-component) for the [trivial representation](../../../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation) onto itself and its nontrivial summands onto zero. These projections together form a degree-preserving [Reynolds operator](../../../representation-theory.md#reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring) $\mathcal R:A\to R$, with $\mathcal R|_R=\operatorname{id}$. The crucial additional property is

$$
\mathcal R(ah)=a\mathcal R(h)\qquad(a\in R,\ h\in A).
$$

Here is why it holds rather than being an arbitrary projection property. For a homogeneous invariant $a$, multiplication by $a$ is a $G$-equivariant linear map $A_d\to A_{d+\deg a}$. It sends invariant vectors to invariant vectors. On a nontrivial irreducible summand, its composition with projection onto the target's trivial summand is zero, since there is no nonzero equivariant map from a nontrivial [irreducible representation](../../../representation-theory.md#irreducible-representation) to the [trivial representation](../../../representation-theory.md#trivial-representation). It therefore commutes with the [Reynolds operators](../../../representation-theory.md#reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring). Summing the homogeneous components of $a$ and $h$ proves the displayed [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) property in general.

Let $R_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}R_d$ and form the ideal $I=AR_+$ in the ambient [polynomial](../../../polynomial.md) ring. The [Hilbert basis theorem](../../../algebra.md#hilbert-basis-theorem) makes $A$ a [Noetherian ring](../../../algebra.md#noetherian-ring), so $I$ is generated by finitely many homogeneous positive-degree invariants $f_1,\ldots,f_s$. To justify choosing the generators from this particular set, start with any finite ideal generating set; each of its members is a finite sum of multiples of homogeneous members of $R_+$, and the union of those finitely many invariants then generates $I$. If $I=0$, there are no positive-degree invariants and the answer is simply $R=\mathbb C$.

Otherwise consider a homogeneous invariant $f$ of degree $d>0$. Since $f\in I$, write

$$
f=\sum_{i=1}^s h_i f_i,\qquad h_i\in A_{d-\deg f_i},
$$

with $h_i=0$ when that degree is negative. Taking the degree-$d$ component of any ideal expression gives exactly such an expression. Apply the [Reynolds operator](../../../representation-theory.md#reynolds-operator-on-a-polynomial-invariant-ring) and its [module](../../../module-theory.md#module-mathematics) property:

$$
f=\mathcal R(f)=\sum_{i=1}^s\mathcal R(h_i)f_i.
$$

Each coefficient $\mathcal R(h_i)$ is invariant and has degree strictly smaller than $d$, because every $f_i$ has positive degree. Induction on $d$, starting from $R_0=\mathbb C$, now shows that every homogeneous invariant lies in $\mathbb C[f_1,\ldots,f_s]$. An arbitrary invariant is a finite sum of invariant homogeneous components, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbb C[V]^G=\mathbb C[f_1,\ldots,f_s].}
$$

This proves finite generation as an algebra. No Noetherian property of the [polynomial invariant ring](../../../representation-theory.md#polynomial-invariant-ring) was assumed in obtaining the generators.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 21](paper-21.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Let $C$ be the [smooth algebraic curve](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#smooth-algebraic-curve). A nonzero regular section of a [line bundle](../../../ringed-space.md#line-bundle) has an effective zero divisor, so its [degree of a line bundle](../../../ringed-space.md#degree-of-a-line-bundle) is nonnegative. Degrees add under tensor products and change sign on taking the dual. We use genuine [line subbundles](../../../fiber-bundle.md#line-subbundle), so their quotients in $E$ are [locally free](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-free-sheaf). Both bounds can be proved constructively.

For the upper bound, take a rational frame of $E^*$ over the [function field](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#function-field-of-an-algebraic-variety) of $C$. Its two members are [rational sections](../../../ringed-space.md#rational-section-of-a-sheaf-of-modules) with only finitely many poles. Choose an [effective divisor](../../../cartier-divisor.md#effective-cartier-divisor) $D$ large enough to bound the pole orders of both [rational sections](../../../ringed-space.md#rational-section-of-a-sheaf-of-modules). They become [global sections](../../../ringed-space.md#global-section) of $E^*\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$ and define a [sheaf](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#sheaf-mathematics) map

$$
E\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C(D)\oplus\mathcal O_C(D)
$$

which is an isomorphism at the [generic point](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#generic-point). It is therefore [injective](../../../algebra.md#injective-function) as a [sheaf](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#sheaf-mathematics) map: a section in its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) would vanish generically in a [locally free sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-free-sheaf), which is a [torsion-free sheaf](../../../ringed-space.md#torsion-free-sheaf). For any [line subbundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#line-subbundle) $L\subset E$, at least one of its two component maps into $\mathcal O_C(D)$ is nonzero. Such a map is a nonzero [global section](../../../ringed-space.md#global-section) of $\mathcal O_C(D)\otimes L^{-1}$. Its zero [divisor on an algebraic curve](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#divisor-on-an-algebraic-curve) is effective, so

$$
\boxed{\deg L\le\deg D.}
$$

This [upper degree bound for line subbundles on a smooth curve](../../../ringed-space.md#upper-degree-bound-for-line-subbundles-on-a-smooth-curve) depends only on a choice made for $E$, and applies to every one of its [line subbundles](../../../fiber-bundle.md#line-subbundle).

For the lower-bound question we will actually construct subbundles $\mathcal O_C(-mp_0)$ for every sufficiently large integer $m$, where $p_0$ is a fixed point. First prove the [globally generated vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#globally-generated-vector-bundle) property rather than assuming that negative-degree inclusions automatically are subbundles.

Apply the upper-bound argument to the rank-two [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) $F=E^*\otimes K_C$, where $K_C$ is the [canonical bundle](../../../complex-geometry.md#canonical-bundle). Let $B$ be its upper degree bound. Choose $m$ with $m-1>B$. For any point $p\in C$, a nonzero [global section](../../../ringed-space.md#global-section) of $F(-mp_0+p)$ would give a nonzero map

$$
\mathcal O_C(mp_0-p)\longrightarrow F.
$$

Its image can be saturated to a [saturated line subbundle on a smooth curve](../../../ringed-space.md#saturated-line-subbundle-on-a-smooth-curve) $M\subset F$. Locally the [local ring of a smooth algebraic curve](../../../projective-space.md#local-ring-of-a-smooth-algebraic-curve) is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../commutative-algebra.md#discrete-valuation-ring): dividing out the common power of a uniformizer makes the image a primitive rank-one summand, and its quotient is free. Thus the saturation is indeed a subbundle. The original map gives a nonzero [global section](../../../ringed-space.md#global-section) of $M\otimes\mathcal O_C(-mp_0+p)$ and consequently $\deg M\ge m-1$, contradicting the upper bound. Hence $H^0(F(-mp_0+p))=0$ for every $p$. By [Serre duality](../../../ringed-space.md#serre-duality),

$$
H^1(E(mp_0-p))\cong H^0(E^*\otimes K_C(-mp_0+p))^*=0.
$$

The [exact sequence](../../../homology.md#exact-sequence)

$$
0\longrightarrow E(mp_0-p)\longrightarrow E(mp_0)
\longrightarrow E(mp_0)|_p\longrightarrow0
$$

and its [long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology](../../../ringed-space.md#long-exact-sequence-in-sheaf-cohomology) show that evaluation $H^0(E(mp_0))\to E(mp_0)|_p$ is onto for every $p$. This proves the [global generation of positive twists on a smooth curve](../../../fiber-bundle.md#global-generation-of-positive-twists-on-a-smooth-curve) with one bound on $m$ that works at every point.

Write $W=H^0(E(mp_0))$ and $N=\dim W$. The evaluation map $W\otimes\mathcal O_C\to E(mp_0)$ is [surjective](../../../algebra.md#surjective-function) with [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) bundle of rank $N-2$. Consider the incidence subset

$$
Z=\{(p,s)\in C\times W:s(p)=0\}.
$$

It is the total space of this [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) bundle, so $\dim Z=1+(N-2)=N-1$. Its projection into $W$ is closed because $C$ is proper, and its image has dimension at most $N-1<N$. Choose $s$ outside this proper closed subset. It vanishes nowhere. In a local trivialization at any point, one component of $s$ is a unit, so it can be completed to a basis. Therefore $\mathcal O_C\xrightarrow{s}E(mp_0)$ is a subbundle with a [locally free](../../../ringed-space.md#locally-free-sheaf) rank-one quotient. Twisting back gives

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_C(-mp_0)\subset E,\qquad\deg\mathcal O_C(-mp_0)=-m\longrightarrow-\infty.}
$$

This proves the [unbounded negative degrees of line subbundles](../../../ringed-space.md#unbounded-negative-degrees-of-line-subbundles). The nowhere-vanishing construction guarantees a subbundle at every point, including the zeros that might otherwise have occurred in a generic meromorphic inclusion. If the curve has several connected components, apply these constructions on each component and add the degrees; the same upper-bound and no-lower-bound conclusions follow.
