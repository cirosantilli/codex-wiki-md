<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

The [Iwasawa main conjecture](../../../../../main-conjecture-of-iwasawa-theory.md) links two descriptions of cyclotomic arithmetic: Galois [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) built from abelian extensions, and [p-adic L-functions](../../../../../p-adic-l-function.md) built by interpolation of special values. A precise statement must distinguish local [units](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), [global units](../../../../../global-unit-of-a-number-field.md) and the permitted ramification of the extension.

Use global [fields](../../../../../field.md) $F_n=\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)^+$, distinct from the local [fields](../../../../../field.md) $K_n$ in the preceding questions. Put $F_\infty=\bigcup_nF_n$, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F_\infty/\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z_p^\times/\{\pm1\}$, and $\Omega=\mathbb Z_p[[G]]$. Its pro-p subgroup is $\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p$, and choosing a generator identifies its [Iwasawa algebra](../../../../../iwasawa-algebra.md) with $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[S]]$. The prime-to-p finite quotient has order $(p-1)/2$, so its character idempotents decompose $\Omega$ into one-variable power-series [rings](../../../../../ring.md). Characteristic ideals below are understood componentwise in this decomposition.

Let $\mathcal U$ be the norm limit of local [principal units](../../../../../principal-unit.md) in the completions of $F_n$ at $p$, $\mathcal E$ the norm limit of the closures of global [principal units](../../../../../principal-unit.md) there, and $\mathcal C$ the closed [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) generated by compatible [cyclotomic units](../../../../../cyclotomic-unit.md). Thus $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal U$. Let $X$ be the [p-ramified Iwasawa module](../../../../../p-ramified-iwasawa-module.md) of $F_\infty$, and let $Y$ be its [unramified Iwasawa module](../../../../../unramified-iwasawa-module.md), equivalently the norm limit of the p-primary [ideal class groups](../../../../../ideal-class-group.md). In this real tower the relevant character components are finitely generated torsion [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md); the abelian-field [Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields](../../../../../leopoldt-theorem-for-abelian-number-fields.md) supplies the required rank comparison for $X$.

For a torsion one-variable [Iwasawa module](../../../../../iwasawa-module.md), the [Iwasawa module structure theorem](../../../../../iwasawa-module-structure-theorem.md) gives, up to finite kernel and cokernel, elementary factors $\Lambda/(p^{a_i})$ and $\Lambda/(q_j(S)^{b_j})$ with distinguished irreducible $q_j$. Its [characteristic ideal](../../../../../characteristic-ideal.md) is generated by $p^{\sum a_i}\prod_jq_j^{b_j}$; the generator is determined only up to a [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). This records all height-one elementary divisors, not just the order of a finite class group.

Let $I=\ker(\Omega\to\mathbb Z_p)$ be the [augmentation ideal](../../../../../augmentation-ideal.md), and let $\zeta_p$ be the even [p-adic zeta pseudomeasure](../../../../../p-adic-zeta-pseudomeasure.md), normalized by

$$
\int_G x^k\,d\zeta_p=(1-p^{k-1})\zeta(1-k)\qquad(k\geq2\text{ even}).
$$

Even powers are well-defined on $\mathbb Z_p^\times/\{\pm1\}$. The trivial-character pole means that $\zeta_p$ itself need not be integral; $I\zeta_p$ is an integral ideal. In this real, p-ramified convention the main conjecture is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{char}_\Omega X=I\zeta_p.}
$$

On nontrivial even character components this is an equality with the ideal of a [Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function](../../../../../kubota-leopoldt-p-adic-l-function.md) power series. If $\gamma$ corresponds to $u=1+p$ and $S=\gamma-1$, the corresponding interpolation convention is $g_\chi(u^{1-s}-1)=L_p(\chi,s)$. Other formulations use odd components of the unramified [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) of the full complex tower; [Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory](../../../../../kummer-reflection-in-iwasawa-theory.md) introduces the reflected character $\omega\chi^{-1}$ and the accompanying Tate twist/involution. These [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) and power-series variables cannot be exchanged without those corrections. The main conjecture is a theorem, proved by the [Mazur-Wiles theorem](../../../../../mazur-wiles-theorem.md) and also through the cyclotomic Euler-system method.

The [Iwasawa theorem on local cyclotomic units](../../../../../iwasawa-theorem-on-local-cyclotomic-units.md) identifies the purely local quotient

$$
\boxed{\mathcal U/\mathcal C\cong\Omega/(I\zeta_p).}
$$

Its mechanism is visible in Q2. The [Coleman unit-measure exact sequence](../../../../../coleman-unit-measure-exact-sequence.md), on its even part, has no roots-of-unity kernel or first-moment cokernel, since complex conjugation acts by $-1$ on those terms. It identifies $\mathcal U$ with the measure algebra $\Omega$. The measure comes from the [Frobenius-corrected logarithm](../../../../../frobenius-corrected-logarithm.md)

$$
\mathcal L_f(T)=\frac1p\log\frac{f(T)^p}{f((1+T)^p-1)},
$$

whose kth moment is $(1-p^{k-1})\delta_k(f)$. This follows by applying $D^k$ and the chain rule to the two logarithms. For the [units](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) in Q2 and even $k$, use $\zeta(1-k)=-B_k/k$ to obtain

$$
(1-p^{k-1})\delta_k(c(a))
=(p-1)2^k(1-a^k)(1-p^{k-1})\zeta(1-k).
$$

Uniqueness of moments therefore identifies their measures with $(p-1)[2](1-[a])\zeta_p$. The factors $(p-1)[2]$ are [units](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) in $\Omega$, and the differences $1-[a]$ generate $I$. Thus the closed cyclotomic-unit [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) maps exactly to $I\zeta_p$. This local interpolation calculation explains Iwasawa's quotient theorem, including the augmentation correction at the trivial character.

What remains globally is made explicit by [class field theory](../../../../../class-field-theory.md). The [class-field unit sequence](../../../../../class-field-unit-sequence.md) gives

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathcal E/\mathcal C\longrightarrow\mathcal U/\mathcal C
\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow0.
$$

Break this into two [short exact sequences](../../../../../short-exact-sequence.md). Multiplicativity of [characteristic ideals](../../../../../characteristic-ideal.md) yields

$$
\operatorname{char}(\mathcal E/\mathcal C)\operatorname{char}(X)
=\operatorname{char}(\mathcal U/\mathcal C)\operatorname{char}(Y).
$$

Consequently, using the local theorem, the main conjecture is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{char}_\Omega Y=\operatorname{char}_\Omega(\mathcal E/\mathcal C).}
$$

This is the [characteristic-ideal bridge from local units to the main conjecture](../../../../../characteristic-ideal-bridge-from-local-units-to-the-main-conjecture.md). It shows why the local quotient theorem alone is insufficient: it does not measure how many local cyclotomic-unit classes are supplied by [global units](../../../../../global-unit-of-a-number-field.md). The [Euler-system divisibility for real cyclotomic class modules](../../../../../euler-system-divisibility-for-real-cyclotomic-class-modules.md) supplies one containment, while the cyclotomic unit-index and norm-defect comparisons upgrade it to equality. Neither vanishing of class groups nor the Vandiver conjecture is assumed.

There is also the [Iwasawa class-number growth theorem](../../../../../iwasawa-class-number-growth-theorem.md), often called Iwasawa's theorem in discussions of these towers. For sufficiently large $n$,

$$
v_p(h_{F_n})=\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu.
$$

The [Iwasawa invariants](../../../../../iwasawa-invariants.md) $\mu,\lambda$ come from the characteristic series of $Y$; [Ferrero-Washington theorem](../../../../../ferrero-washington-theorem.md) gives $\mu=0$ in this abelian cyclotomic setting. This explains eventual linear growth of the p-adic class-number exponent. It is weaker than the main conjecture: the latter determines the complete [characteristic ideal](../../../../../characteristic-ideal.md), including distinguished polynomial factors, rather than only their total degree and p-power content. The local [unit](../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) theorem and the class-number growth theorem are thus important inputs and consequences in the framework, but neither should be mistaken for the global ideal equality itself.
