<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) satisfies $R_b(u_*)=u_*$. A critical fixed point is scale invariant and has divergent [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md). The [critical surface](../../../../../../critical-surface.md) consists of couplings flowing toward it after relevant variables have been tuned; noncritical flows ultimately leave its neighborhood toward finite-correlation-length phases.

Linearize the [renormalization-group transformation](../../../../../../renormalization-group-transformation.md) near the fixed point and choose scaling coordinates. If the thermal and magnetic coordinates transform as $t'=b^{y_t}t$ and $h'=b^{y_h}h$, the singular [free-energy density](../../../../../../free-energy-density.md) obeys

$$
F_s(t,h)=b^{-D}F_s(b^{y_t}t,b^{y_h}h).
$$

Choose $b=|t|^{-1/y_t}$ to stop at an order-one thermal coordinate. This gives the [scaling hypothesis for critical phenomena](../../../../../../scaling-hypothesis-for-critical-phenomena.md)

$$
F_s(t,h)=|t|^{D/y_t}\Phi_\pm(h/|t|^{y_h/y_t}),\qquad
\xi(t,0)\sim|t|^{-1/y_t}.
$$

Consequently $\nu=1/y_t$ and $\Delta=y_h/y_t$, with $2-\alpha=D/y_t$ when hyperscaling applies. The fixed point determines universal powers, while microscopic couplings set metric factors and amplitudes. Dangerously irrelevant variables and marginal logarithms can qualify the simple homogeneous form.
