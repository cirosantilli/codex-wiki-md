<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Diagonalizing the linearized blocking map gives perturbations $v_i'=\lambda_iv_i=b^{y_i}v_i$. A [relevant operator](../../../../../../relevant-operator.md) has $y_i>0$, equivalently $|\lambda_i|>1$ for a discrete scaling multiplier: its perturbation grows under repeated coarse-graining and must be tuned to reach the critical fixed point. An [irrelevant operator](../../../../../../irrelevant-operator.md) has $y_i<0$ and decays, usually contributing corrections to scaling. A [marginal operator](../../../../../../marginal-operator.md) has $y_i=0$, so linearization alone is insufficient; nonlinear flow can make it marginally relevant or irrelevant, or exactly marginal.

For an ordinary spin-inversion-symmetric critical point the temperature and magnetic field are relevant. Fixing $h=0$ by symmetry leaves the temperature to tune. At a [tricritical point](../../../../../../tricritical-point.md) the quartic coupling is another relevant tuning direction. A [dangerously irrelevant coupling](../../../../../../dangerously-irrelevant-coupling.md) is an exception to simply discarding small variables: setting it to zero can destabilize the ordered potential or make its scaling function singular, even though it flows toward zero.
