<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

A stationary [black hole](../../../../../black-hole.md) behaves mechanically like an equilibrium thermodynamic system. Its horizon area A, asymptotic energy E, [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) J and [electric charge](../../../../../electric-charge.md) Q are linked by the [laws of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../laws-of-black-hole-mechanics.md). Let $\chi^a=t^a+\Omega_H\phi^a$ generate its [Killing horizon](../../../../../killing-horizon.md), with the time translation normalized at infinity; its [surface gravity](../../../../../surface-gravity.md) is defined by $\chi^b\nabla_b\chi^a=\kappa\chi^a$ on that horizon. We use $c=1$ and keep G explicit. The horizon electrostatic potential $\Phi_H$ is measured relative to infinity.

The [Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../zeroth-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) states that kappa is constant on each connected stationary horizon, under the Einstein equations and suitable energy/regularity assumptions. It parallels the [zeroth law of thermodynamics](../../../../../zeroth-law-of-thermodynamics.md), which makes [temperature](../../../../../temperature.md) uniform in equilibrium. Kappa need not be constant in a dynamical geometry. For an asymptotically flat static hole, local observers see gravitationally redshifted temperatures; the equilibrium quantity comparable to kappa is the [temperature](../../../../../temperature.md) referenced to infinity.

The [First law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../first-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) relates nearby stationary solutions:

$$
\boxed{\delta E=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi G}\delta A+\Omega_H\delta J+\Phi_H\delta Q.}
$$

The same leading relation describes a small physical process which perturbs a horizon and subsequently settles to equilibrium, with the appropriate hypotheses on fluxes and final state. The work terms correspond to rotational and electromagnetic work. The structural analogy with the [first law of thermodynamics](../../../../../first-law-of-thermodynamics.md), $dE=T\,dS+\text{work}$, identifies area as an entropy-like variable and [surface gravity](../../../../../surface-gravity.md) as a temperature-like variable. Classically this fixes their product in the first law, rather than their separate normalizations.

The [second law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../second-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md), or [black-hole area theorem](../../../../../hawking-s-area-theorem.md), states that future event-horizon area does not decrease classically. Its essential geometric ingredient is the [Raychaudhuri equation](../../../../../friedmann-acceleration-equation.md) for affinely parametrized null generators. In four dimensions a hypersurface-orthogonal congruence obeys

$$
\frac{d\vartheta}{d\lambda}=-\frac12\vartheta^2-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}-R_{ab}k^ak^b.
$$

The [null energy condition](../../../../../null-energy-condition.md) and Einstein equations make the last term nonpositive. If the expansion became negative, the inequality would focus the generators to a [conjugate point](../../../../../conjugate-point.md) in finite [affine parameter](../../../../../affine-parameter.md). Under future completeness and predictability assumptions, that would conflict with their remaining on an achronal [event horizon](../../../../../event-horizon.md). Thus $\vartheta\geq0$, and the area element, satisfying $d\log dA/d\lambda=\vartheta$, cannot decrease. For a merger the final area is at least the sum of the initial component areas. These assumptions matter: the theorem is a classical statement, not a prohibition of quantum evaporation.

The [third law of black-hole mechanics](../../../../../third-law-of-black-hole-mechanics.md) is an unattainability statement: a nonextremal hole cannot be driven to zero [surface gravity](../../../../../surface-gravity.md) in a finite physical process satisfying the usual bounded-stress and regularity conditions. This parallels the unattainability form of the [Third law of thermodynamics](../../../../../third-law-of-thermodynamics.md). Extremal stationary solutions exist, so the law concerns how an initially nonextremal hole approaches them. The stronger [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) form of Nernst's theorem does not transfer unchanged: extremal holes can have finite area and [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md), depending on their conserved charges.

Quantum field theory turns the mechanical analogy into a physical thermodynamic identification. [Hawking radiation](../../../../../hawking-radiation.md) is thermal at infinity to leading semiclassical order, with

$$
\boxed{T_H=\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi k_B},\qquad S_{\rm BH}=\frac{k_B A}{4G\hbar}.}
$$

These give $T_H\delta S_{\rm BH}=\kappa\delta A/(8\pi G)$ exactly, so the mechanical first law becomes the thermodynamic first law with [Bekenstein-Hawking entropy](../../../../../bekenstein-hawking-entropy.md). Scattering through the exterior adds [greybody factors](../../../../../greybody-factor.md) to the emitted spectrum but leaves this [temperature](../../../../../temperature.md) identification intact. Equilibrium with an external bath and isolated evaporation are different physical states of the field.

During [black-hole evaporation](../../../../../black-hole-evaporation.md), the area can decrease because the semiclassical stress tensor need not satisfy the classical [null energy condition](../../../../../null-energy-condition.md). The proposed [generalized second law](../../../../../generalized-second-law.md) applies instead to $S_{\rm gen}=S_{\rm BH}+S_{\rm outside}$, asserting nondecrease of the combined [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md). A Schwarzschild hole illustrates an unusual thermodynamic property: $T_H\propto E^{-1}$, so its [heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md) is negative. It becomes hotter as it loses energy and is unstable in a simple canonical heat bath, while [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) and energy laws still describe an isolated system. The quantum [temperature](../../../../../temperature.md), area [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) and [generalized second law](../../../../../generalized-second-law.md) supply the correspondence; the mechanical laws alone do not establish a thermal emission spectrum or a microscopic [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) count.
