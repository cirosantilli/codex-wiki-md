<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

In the two-body centre-of-mass frame, [spatial reflection](../../../../../spatial-reflection.md) reverses the relative coordinate of a [quark](../../../../../quark.md)-[antiquark](../../../../../antiquark.md) pair. Its orbital state of angular momentum $L$ has [orbital parity](../../../../../orbital-parity.md) $(-1)^L$, as follows from $Y_{Lm}(-\widehat{\mathbf r})=(-1)^LY_{Lm}(\widehat{\mathbf r})$. Opposite constituent [intrinsic parities](../../../../../intrinsic-parity.md) multiply to minus one. Thus the [meson parity and charge conjugation](../../../../../meson-parity-and-charge-conjugation.md) rules begin with

$$
\boxed{P=\eta_q\eta_{\bar q}(-1)^L=(-1)^{L+1}.}
$$

To obtain [charge conjugation](../../../../../charge-conjugation.md), consider a self-conjugate flavor state and write its [spin](../../../../../spin.md)-orbital wavefunction using ordered [creation operators](../../../../../creation-operator.md) $b_\sigma^\dagger(\mathbf p)d_\tau^\dagger(-\mathbf p)$. [Charge conjugation](../../../../../charge-conjugation.md) interchanges the [quark](../../../../../quark.md) and [antiquark](../../../../../antiquark.md) operators. Reordering them back to [quark](../../../../../quark.md)-first order produces a [fermionic sign](../../../../../fermionic-sign.md) minus one. Interchanging the two [spin](../../../../../spin.md)-$1/2$ labels has eigenvalue $-1$ in the [spin singlet state](../../../../../spin-singlet-state.md) ($S=0$) and $+1$ in the [spin](../../../../../spin.md) triplet ($S=1$), namely $(-1)^{S+1}$. Reversing the relative momentum contributes $(-1)^L$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{C=-(-1)^{S+1}(-1)^L=(-1)^{L+S}.}
$$

Reciprocal phases for the two conjugate [creation operators](../../../../../creation-operator.md) cancel, so the result is independent of that convention. This applies to a state that is mapped to itself by [charge conjugation](../../../../../charge-conjugation.md), not to every electrically neutral flavor state. For example a neutral [meson](../../../../../meson.md) with unequal [quark](../../../../../quark.md) and [antiquark](../../../../../antiquark.md) flavors can be exchanged with a different antimeson. Charged states have no individual $C$ eigenvalue.

Restrict flavor to the $u,d$ [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) doublet. Its product with the conjugate doublet decomposes as $\mathbf2\otimes\overline{\mathbf2}=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1$. Convenient normalized flavor states, with irrelevant overall phase choices, are

$$
|1,+1\rangle=u\bar d,\quad |1,0\rangle=\frac{u\bar u-d\bar d}{\sqrt2},\quad |1,-1\rangle=d\bar u,\quad
|0,0\rangle=\frac{u\bar u+d\bar d}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Orbital excitation normally costs energy, so the simplest low-lying [quark model](../../../../../quark-model.md) states have $L=0$. Coupling the two constituent spins gives $S=0$ or $S=1$, and then $J=S$. The isotriplet with $S=0$ is the [pion](../../../../../pion.md) triplet, while the isotriplet with $S=1$ is the [rho meson](../../../../../rho-meson.md) triplet. The isosinglet with $S=1$ is the nonstrange component of the [omega meson](../../../../../omega-meson.md). Their quantum numbers are

$$
\boxed{\pi^0:\ I=1,\ J^{PC}=0^{-+};\qquad
\rho^0:\ I=1,\ J^{PC}=1^{--};\qquad
\omega:\ I=0,\ J^{PC}=1^{--}.}
$$

The charged [pions](../../../../../pion.md) have $J^P=0^-$ and the charged rho [mesons](../../../../../meson.md) have $J^P=1^-$; [charge conjugation](../../../../../charge-conjugation.md) exchanges the positive and negative members. The same [tensor](../../../../../tensor.md) product also allows an $S=0$, $I=0$ pseudoscalar state, so symmetry alone does not assert that only the listed [mesons](../../../../../meson.md) exist or predict their masses. The physical isosinglet spectrum involves further dynamics and, when strange flavor is included, mixing.

For a vector-[meson](../../../../../meson.md) decay to two spinless [pions](../../../../../pion.md), conservation of [angular momentum](../../../../../angular-momentum.md) forces their relative orbital value to be $L=1$. Their combined [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is $(-1)^2(-1)^L=-1$, and the neutral charge combination has $C=(-1)^L=-1$, matching both $\rho^0$ and $\omega$. Thus $C$ and $P$ alone do not distinguish these two decays. The decisive restriction is [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) together with [Bose statistics](../../../../../bose-einstein-statistics.md).

Two isotriplet [pions](../../../../../pion.md) have $\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf5$, or total $I=0,1,2$. Under exchange the [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md)-$I$ state has sign $(-1)^{2-I}$: the $I=2$ highest state and its lowered partners are symmetric, the $I=1$ highest combination $(|+0\rangle-|0+\rangle)/\sqrt2$ is antisymmetric, and the singlet $(|+-\rangle+|-+\rangle-|00\rangle)/\sqrt3$ is symmetric. The $L=1$ orbital state is antisymmetric. Since [pions](../../../../../pion.md) are [spin](../../../../../spin.md)-zero [bosons](../../../../../boson.md), their total state must be symmetric, requiring the antisymmetric [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) sector $I=1$. This allows $\rho^0\to\pi^+\pi^-$ but excludes an [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md)-conserving $\omega\to\pi^+\pi^-$ amplitude.

There is no analogous obstruction for three [pions](../../../../../pion.md). In a Cartesian [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) basis $a,b,c$, their totally antisymmetric combination $\epsilon_{abc}|\pi^a\pi^b\pi^c\rangle$ is an $I=0$ state. It can multiply a totally antisymmetric momentum amplitude proportional in the rest frame to

$$
\boldsymbol\epsilon_\omega\cdot(\mathbf p_1\times\mathbf p_2).
$$

Momentum conservation $\mathbf p_1+\mathbf p_2+\mathbf p_3=0$ makes this amplitude change sign under every exchange of two [pion](../../../../../pion.md) labels. The product of the flavor and momentum factors therefore obeys [Bose statistics](../../../../../bose-einstein-statistics.md). The cross product supplies $J=1$ and an even [orbital parity](../../../../../orbital-parity.md); multiplying by the three negative [pion](../../../../../pion.md) [intrinsic parities](../../../../../intrinsic-parity.md) gives total $P=-1$. In the charged component, [charge conjugation](../../../../../charge-conjugation.md) exchanges $\pi^+$ and $\pi^-$ and changes the momentum factor's sign, giving $C=-1$. Thus **$\omega\to\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ is compatible with $I=0$ and $J^{PC}=1^{--}$**.

The same distinction is encoded in [G parity](../../../../../g-parity.md): $G=C(-1)^I$ for the neutral member gives $G_\pi=-1$, $G_\rho=+1$, $G_\omega=-1$. An $n$-[pion](../../../../../pion.md) state has $G=(-1)^n$, so the [G-parity selection rule for pion multiplicities](../../../../../g-parity-selection-rule-for-pion-multiplicities.md) allows two [pions](../../../../../pion.md) for the rho and three for the omega. The printed prohibition of the omega two-[pion](../../../../../pion.md) mode is an exact-[isospin](../../../../../isospin.md) strong-interaction selection rule. In the physical theory, [isospin](../../../../../isospin.md)-breaking effects allow a small two-[pion](../../../../../pion.md) mode; it is not an absolute prohibition. The scope of C [parity](../../../../../parity.md) is also stated in the [Particle Data Group quark-model review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/rpp2024-rev-quark-model.pdf), and the physical two-[pion](../../../../../pion.md) mode is recorded in its [omega listing](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/listings/rpp2024-list-omega-782.pdf).
