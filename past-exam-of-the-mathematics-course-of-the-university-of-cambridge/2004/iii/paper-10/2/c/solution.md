<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Define the holomorphic function $e$ on the disconnected open set $U$ to be one on $V$ and zero on $W$. Since $e^2=e$, its [holomorphic functional calculus](../../../../../../holomorphic-functional-calculus.md) value $p=\Theta_x(e)$ is an [idempotent](../../../../../../idempotent.md). The [spectral mapping theorem](../../../../../../spectral-mapping-theorem.md) and the nonempty spectral intersection with $V$ show $1\in\sigma(p)$; thus $p\ne0$. These are the [Riesz projection](../../../../../../riesz-projection.md) values selecting the indicated spectral component.

Part (b) gives $p_n=\Theta_{x_n}(e)\to p$. If $\sigma(x_n)$ avoided $V$, spectral mapping would give $\sigma(p_n)=\{0\}$. A nonzero idempotent always has $1$ in its spectrum: if $1-p_n$ were invertible, $p_n(1-p_n)=0$ would force $p_n=0$. Thus avoiding $V$ forces $p_n=0$. That is impossible eventually, since $p\ne0$ and $p_n\to p$. Therefore **$\sigma(x_n)\cap V\ne\varnothing$ eventually**. This is persistence of a separated spectral component, not a claim that arbitrary individual spectral points vary continuously.
