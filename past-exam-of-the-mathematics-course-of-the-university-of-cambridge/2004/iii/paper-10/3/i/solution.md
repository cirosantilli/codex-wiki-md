<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $K=\bigcap_L L$, where $L$ ranges over all [maximal left ideals](../../../../../../maximal-left-ideal.md). For each such $L$, the action on the [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) $A/L$ has a [primitive ideal](../../../../../../primitive-ideal.md) $P_L$ as its kernel. An element of that kernel annihilates $1+L$, so $P_L\subseteq L$. The intersection $J$ of all primitive ideals is consequently contained in every $L$, hence $J\subseteq K$.

Conversely, take $x\in K$ and any nonzero simple module $E$. For every nonzero $v\in E$, simplicity makes the evaluation map $A\to E$, $a\mapsto av$, surjective. Its kernel is a maximal left ideal, so it contains $x$. Thus $xv=0$ for every nonzero $v$, and also for zero. The element $x$ therefore belongs to the annihilator of every simple module, hence to every primitive ideal. This proves

$$
\boxed{J=\bigcap_{L\text{ maximal left ideal}}L.}
$$

Since the primitive ideals are two-sided ideals, this argument also makes $J$ a two-sided [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md), despite the one-sided ideals in this alternative description.
