<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [primitive ideal](../../../../../primitive-ideal.md) is the kernel of an algebraically irreducible unital representation, equivalently the [annihilator of a module](../../../../../annihilator-of-a-module.md) for a nonzero [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md). Irreducibility here excludes all proper nonzero invariant linear subspaces, not just closed ones. In a [Banach algebra](../../../../../banach-algebra-split.md) the simple modules can be represented as $A/L$ with $L$ a [maximal left ideal](../../../../../maximal-left-ideal.md). Such an ideal is closed: if its closure were all of $A$, it would contain an element close enough to $1$ to be invertible by the [Neumann series](../../../../../neumann-series.md), contradicting properness. Maximality then makes its closure itself. Thus the quotient carries a Banach-space norm and a bounded left action.

Let $P$ be a maximal proper two-sided [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md). The quotient ring $R=A/P$ is nonzero, unital and simple as a ring. By the [Zorn lemma](../../../../../zorn-s-lemma.md), choose a maximal left ideal $L$ of $R$. The [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md) $R/L$ has an annihilator which is a proper two-sided ideal of $R$. Simplicity forces that annihilator to be zero. Its pullback to $A$ is exactly $P$, proving **every maximal proper two-sided ideal is primitive**. If one wants the Banach representation explicitly, realize the same module as the quotient of $A$ by the pullback maximal left ideal, which is closed by the preceding argument.
