<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use $F_{\mu\nu}^a=\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a+g f^{abc}A_\mu^bA_\nu^c$ and $\mathcal L=-F_{\mu\nu}^aF^{a\mu\nu}/4$. Its [cubic and quartic Yang-Mills self-interactions](../../../../../../cubic-and-quartic-yang-mills-self-interactions.md) are

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_3=-\frac g2f^{abc}
(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a)A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu},\qquad
\mathcal L_4=-\frac{g^2}4f^{abc}f^{ade}
A_\mu^bA_\nu^cA^{d\mu}A^{e\nu}.}
$$

Antisymmetry of the [Lie algebra structure constants](../../../../../../structure-constant-of-a-lie-algebra.md) lets the cubic expression also be written as $-g f^{abc}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu}$.

The cubic vertex has three fields and one [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md), corresponding to $n=2,r=1$ in part (b). The quartic vertex has four fields and no [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md), corresponding to $n=4,r=0$. Their dimensions are

$$
[g]=\frac{4-d}{2},\qquad[g^2]=4-d.
$$

With both types present, the same [Feynman diagram](../../../../../../feynman-diagram.md) counting gives

$$
D=d-\frac{d-2}{2}E-V_3[g]-V_4[g^2].
$$

In four dimensions both couplings are dimensionless, and the degree is bounded independently of vertex count. Gauge symmetry, implemented consistently after [gauge fixing](../../../../../../gauge-fixing.md), restricts the ultraviolet [counterterms](../../../../../../counterterm.md) to the allowed renormalizable gauge-invariant and [gauge fixing](../../../../../../gauge-fixing.md)/ghost structures. Thus the counting is consistent with perturbative [renormalizability](../../../../../../renormalizable-quantum-field-theory.md) of ordinary four-dimensional [Yang-Mills theory](../../../../../../yang-mills-theory.md).

In six dimensions $[g]=-1$ and $[g^2]=-2$, giving $D=6-2E+V_3+2V_4$. Increasing the vertex count permits new divergent momentum structures and higher-dimensional operators without a finite closed list of [renormalization](../../../../../../renormalization.md) parameters. **Ordinary Yang-Mills theory is therefore power-counting renormalizable in four dimensions and nonrenormalizable in six dimensions.** It can still be used in six dimensions as an [effective field theory](../../../../../../effective-field-theory.md) with a cutoff and higher-operator expansion; this conclusion concerns the usual perturbative theory.
