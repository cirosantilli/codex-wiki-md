↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

A [topological group](../../../../../topological-group-split.md) is a group with a topology for which $(x,y)\mapsto xy$ and $x\mapsto x^{-1}$ are continuous; we use the Hausdorff convention. Translations and inversion are then [homeomorphisms](../../../../../homeomorphism.md).

For the final generator-extraction assertion, use the [profinite Frattini subgroup](../../../../../profinite-frattini-subgroup.md)

$$
\Phi(G)=\overline{G^p[G,G]}.
$$

For a finitely generated [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md), the [Burnside basis theorem](../../../../../burnside-basis-theorem.md) says that $G/\Phi(G)$ is a finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) over $\mathbb F_p$ and a subset topologically generates $G$ precisely when its image spans that [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md). In particular its dimension is the minimal number $d(G)$ of [topological generators](../../../../../topological-generator.md). A given $d$-element generating set shows $d(G)\leq d$. The image of any other generating set spans the same finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md), so it contains a basis of $d(G)$ elements. Choose their preimages in that generating set and apply the [Burnside basis theorem](../../../../../burnside-basis-theorem.md) again. **Every generating set contains a generating subset of size $d(G)\leq d$.** This argument also applies to an infinite generating set.
