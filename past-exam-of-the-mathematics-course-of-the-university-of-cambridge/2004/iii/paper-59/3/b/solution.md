<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Continue to choose the CDM-comoving synchronous frame. For nonrelativistic baryons, neglect $O(c_s^2)$ corrections to gravitational density weights and [Hubble friction](../../../../../../hubble-friction.md), but retain $c_s^2k^2$, since [gradients](../../../../../../gradient.md) may compensate its small coefficient. Then

$$
\delta_B'=-\vartheta_B-\frac12h',\qquad
\vartheta_B'+\mathcal H\vartheta_B-c_s^2k^2\delta_B=0.
$$

Together with the [metric tensor](../../../../../../metric-tensor.md) equation these give

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\delta_C''+\mathcal H\delta_C'
&=\frac32\mathcal H^2(\Omega_C\delta_C+\Omega_B\delta_B),\\
\delta_B''+\mathcal H\delta_B'+c_s^2k^2\delta_B
&=\frac32\mathcal H^2(\Omega_C\delta_C+\Omega_B\delta_B).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The friction terms contain first [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md), as the original PDF confirms; they are missing in the TeX's transcription of these equations.

When $\Omega_B\ll\Omega_C$, [Cold dark matter](../../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) sets the gravitational forcing. Its matter-era growing mode is $\delta_C\propto a\propto\tau^2$. Compare pressure with the gravitational timescale:

$$
c_s^2k_J^2=\frac32\mathcal H^2\Omega_C
=4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_C.
$$

Converting the comoving wavenumber to a physical wavelength gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda_J=\frac{2\pi a}{k_J}
\simeq c_s\sqrt{\frac\pi{G\bar\rho_C}}.}
$$

For $k\ll k_J$, pressure is negligible and baryons can follow the growing [Cold dark matter](../../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) mode. In that limit subtracting the two equations gives $D''+2D'/\tau=0$ for $D=\delta_B-\delta_C$, so $D=D_0+D_1/\tau$. Relative to $\delta_C\propto\tau^2$, this offset vanishes: baryons catch up.

The phrase “only grow” needs this gravitational-growth interpretation. For a small baryon fraction the baryon equation is forced, not an autonomous Jeans-instability equation. At $k\gg k_J$, its slowly varying response is

$$
\delta_B\simeq\frac{4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho_C}{c_s^2k^2}\delta_C
=\frac{k_J^2}{k^2}\delta_C,
$$

plus acoustic transients. It is suppressed rather than identically absent. For example, with constant [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md), $\mathcal H=2/\tau$ and $\delta_C=A\tau^2$, the equation is $\delta_B''+2\delta_B'/\tau+c_s^2k^2\delta_B=6A$. Its particular solution $6A/(c_s^2k^2)$ is constant, and its oscillatory homogeneous part decays as $1/\tau$. This demonstrates [Jeans support versus forced baryon response](../../../../../../jeans-support-versus-forced-baryon-response.md); the scale above is not a theorem forbidding every forced or transient sub-Jeans change.

Before decoupling, tight photon coupling makes the effective [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) large, about $1/\sqrt3$. The small-$c_s$ approximation just derived is not quantitative in that regime; the coupled photon-baryon fluid must be used. Its large pressure supports acoustic oscillations, and baryon clustering below the corresponding large Jeans/sound scale is inhibited while [Cold dark matter](../../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) wells grow. After decoupling the supplied [sound speed](../../../../../../speed-of-sound.md) is tiny and decreases further as the gas cools. The [Jeans length](../../../../../../jeans-length.md) drops sharply, so many previously supported modes fall into [Cold dark matter](../../../../../../cold-dark-matter.md) wells and approach its growing [density contrast](../../../../../../density-contrast.md). Modes still below the instantaneous pressure-support scale remain suppressed. The subsequent detailed thermal scaling depends on the gas's temperature evolution; the given sound-speed history suffices for this qualitative conclusion.
