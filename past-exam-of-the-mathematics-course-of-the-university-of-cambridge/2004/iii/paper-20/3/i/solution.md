<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An isomorphism of [Lie algebras](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) identifies the local multiplication laws of the identity components. In exponential coordinates the local product is governed by the [Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula](../../../../../../baker-campbell-hausdorff-formula.md),

$$
\log(\exp X\exp Y)=X+Y+\frac12[X,Y]
+\frac1{12}[X,[X,Y]]+\frac1{12}[Y,[Y,X]]+\cdots.
$$

A bracket-preserving linear isomorphism carries this law to the corresponding law in the other [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md). Thus the identity components are locally isomorphic. This statement is local; the exponential coordinates need not cover the whole [group](../../../../../../group-split.md).

The stronger global description uses a [universal covering Lie group](../../../../../../universal-covering-lie-group.md). The [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) of a connected [Lie group](../../../../../../lie-group.md) acquires a [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) law by lifting multiplication with a chosen identity; uniqueness of lifts supplies associativity and inversion. The [covering map](../../../../../../covering-space.md) is a [local diffeomorphism](../../../../../../local-diffeomorphism.md) and a [Lie group homomorphism](../../../../../../lie-group-homomorphism.md), with discrete kernel. That kernel is central: conjugation of one of its elements gives a continuous map from the connected covering [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) into a discrete set, so it is constantly that element.

By integration of the given [Lie algebra isomorphism](../../../../../../lie-algebra-isomorphism.md) and its inverse on [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) covers, these covers are isomorphic. Their two compositions have identity differential and therefore are identity maps by the preceding uniqueness argument. Consequently, for some connected [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) [Lie group](../../../../../../lie-group.md) $\widetilde G$,

$$
\boxed{G_1^\circ\cong\widetilde G/\Gamma_1,\qquad
G_2^\circ\cong\widetilde G/\Gamma_2,}
$$

where $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ are discrete central [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Different quotients need not be isomorphic. The additive [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $\mathbb R$ and the [circle group](../../../../../../circle-group.md) $\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$ have the same one-dimensional [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) but different compactness and [fundamental groups](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md). Another familiar pair is the [SU(2) group](../../../../../../su-2-group.md) and the [SO(3) group](../../../../../../so-3-group.md), with the latter the quotient of the former by $\{I,-I\}$.

Thus **isomorphic [Lie algebras](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) give isomorphic connected [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md), and identify arbitrary identity components up to discrete central quotients**. For disconnected [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) the [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) also omits the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of components and its conjugation and extension data. For example $\mathbb R\times F$, for any finite [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $F$, always has [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathbb R$, while its component [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is $F$.
