<h1 id="10d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [10D](../10d.md)

For a comoving fluid volume, $V\propto a^3$ and $E=\rho c^2V$. The first-law relation $dE=-P\,dV$ gives $c^2(V\,d\rho+\rho\,dV)=-P\,dV$. Since $\dot V/V=3\dot a/a$, the [cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation](../../../../../cosmological-perfect-fluid-continuity-equation.md) is

$$
\boxed{\dot\rho=-3\frac{\dot a}{a}\left(\rho+\frac P{c^2}\right).}
$$

For the Newtonian energy argument, assume negligible [pressure](../../../../../pressure.md), spherical homogeneity, [Newtonian gravity](../../../../../gravitational-acceleration.md) and constant enclosed mass. A test particle at physical radius $r=a\chi$ has constant specific energy

$$
\epsilon=\frac12\dot r^2-\frac{GM}{r},\qquad M=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho r^3.
$$

Divide by $a^2\chi^2/2$ and set $k=-2\epsilon/(c^2\chi^2)$ to obtain the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../friedmann-equations.md)

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G\rho}{3}-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}.}
$$

Homogeneity makes $k$ independent of the chosen comoving shell. In the relativistic interpretation it specifies the spatial-curvature sign: positive, zero or negative $k$ gives closed spherical, flat or open hyperbolic spatial geometry, with a conventional rescaling allowing $k=1,0,-1$. [Newtonian mechanics](../../../../../newtonian-mechanics.md) itself derives the energy constant, not the spatial metric. When [pressure](../../../../../pressure.md) is appreciable, the general relativistic equations still have the displayed Friedmann form, but the constant-mass Newtonian test-particle derivation cannot be applied without that qualification.
