<h1 id="23g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Work geometrically over an [algebraic closure](../../../../../../algebraic-closure.md); the degree and ramification do not change under this base extension. The coordinate squaring map lands in $W$ because its defining equation pulls back to that of $V$. Above a generic target point with all coordinates nonzero there are $2^3$ square-root choices, identified in pairs by simultaneous sign change. Hence

$$
\boxed{\deg\phi=4.}
$$

The effective sign-change [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) is $C_2\times C_2$. At a point with all coordinates nonzero its stabilizer is trivial, and the squaring map is locally étale, since characteristic is not two. Thus $e_p=1$ there. If one coordinate is zero, a sign change of that coordinate fixes the point, giving stabilizer order two. More explicitly, if $X_0=0$, use $t=X_0/X_2$ as a [local parameter](../../../../../../local-parameter-on-a-smooth-algebraic-curve.md); the curve equation has nonzero [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) in the other coordinate, so this is valid, and the corresponding target parameter is $t^2$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{e_p=2\text{ when exactly one coordinate vanishes, and }e_p=1\text{ otherwise}.}
$$

Two coordinates cannot vanish on either curve. For each vanishing coordinate, the other-coordinate ratio solves $z^8=-1$, giving eight distinct points; there are 24 ramification points. As a check, the smooth plane-curve genera are $21$ and $3$, and [Riemann-Hurwitz formula](../../../../../../riemann-hurwitz-formula.md) gives $2\cdot21-2=4(2\cdot3-2)+24$.
