<h1 id="20g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [20G](../20g.md)

Put $w=\sqrt{-14}$. The [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathbb Z[w]$, with [discriminant](../../../../../discriminant.md) $-56$. The [Minkowski bound for ideal classes](../../../../../minkowski-s-bound.md) for an imaginary quadratic field says that every [ideal class](../../../../../ideal-class.md) has an integral representative of norm at most $(2/\pi)\sqrt{56}<5$. Therefore the [ideal class group](../../../../../ideal-class-group.md) is generated by primes above two and three: norm-four representatives contribute only products of primes above two.

The prime two ramifies: $\mathfrak p_2=(2,w)$ has norm two and $\mathfrak p_2^2=(2)$. It is not principal, since an element of norm two would require $a^2+14b^2=2$, which has no integral solution. Thus $[\mathfrak p_2]$ has order two. Since $X^2+14=(X-1)(X+1)$ modulo three, three splits into $\mathfrak p_3=(3,w-1)$ and its conjugate, whose classes are inverses.

The element $2+w$ has norm $18$. It belongs to $\mathfrak p_3$ but not its conjugate, and its norm has exactly one factor two. Unique [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) therefore gives

$$
(2+w)=\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3^2.
$$

Hence $[\mathfrak p_3]^2=[\mathfrak p_2]^{-1}=[\mathfrak p_2]\ne1$. Since all generating classes are powers of $[\mathfrak p_3]$, **$\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-14}))\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z}$.**
