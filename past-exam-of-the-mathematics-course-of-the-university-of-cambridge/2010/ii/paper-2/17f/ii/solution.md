<h1 id="17f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose a nonempty proper $A$ is tight. In every [matching in a graph](../../../../../../matching-graph-theory.md) covering $X$, the vertices of $A$ use every vertex of $\Gamma(A)$. Hence no edge from $X\setminus A$ to $\Gamma(A)$ could occur in such a matching. The assumption that every edge occurs therefore rules out all such edges. By definition there are also no edges from $A$ to $Y\setminus\Gamma(A)$. This separates $A\cup\Gamma(A)$ from the rest, which contains the nonempty set $X\setminus A$, contradicting connectedness. Hall's inequality must therefore be strict on every nonempty proper $A$.
