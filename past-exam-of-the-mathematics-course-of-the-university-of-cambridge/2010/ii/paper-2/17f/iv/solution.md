**No.** In the six-cycle with cyclic vertices $1,2,\ldots,6$, every edge belongs to one of the two alternating [perfect matchings](../../../../../../perfect-matching.md). However, the disjoint edges $12$ and $45$ cannot belong to a common [perfect matching](../../../../../../perfect-matching.md): the remaining vertices $3,6$ are not adjacent. Taking $X=\{1,3,5\}$ and $Y=\{2,4,6\}$ gives the required [bipartite graph](../../../../../../bipartite-graph.md) counterexample.
