<h1 id="23g/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

A [rational function](../../../../../../rational-function.md) with no poles away from $P_\infty$ is regular on the smooth affine curve. Its ring of [regular functions](../../../../../../regular-function.md) is the coordinate ring

$$
k[x,y]/(y^2-\textstyle\prod_j(x-\lambda_j)),
$$

so it has a unique expression $A(x)+yB(x)$ with [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $A,B$. This regular-function assertion uses smoothness, hence normality, of the affine curve, not [Riemann-Roch theorem](../../../../../../riemann-roch-theorem.md).

The nonconstant leading term of $A$ has even pole order $2\deg A$ at infinity, while $yB$ has odd pole order $3+2\deg B$. These different parities prevent cancellation. Thus no nonconstant such function has a pole of order at most one. Therefore

$$
\boxed{L(P_\infty)=k.}
$$
