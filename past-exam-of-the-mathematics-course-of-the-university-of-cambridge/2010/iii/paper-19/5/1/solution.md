<h1 id="5/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

For each fixed $k$, the two quotient [Riemannian metrics](../../../../../../riemannian-metric.md) are not isometric. If an [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) $F:N_1\to N_2$ existed, choose a small open set avoiding the finite preimages of cone points and their inverse images under $F$. Composing $F$ with the local inverse of the first base projection and the second base projection gives a [local isometry](../../../../../../local-isometry.md) of the base [Riemannian metric](../../../../../../riemannian-metric.md) $h_k$. Local rigidity forces this map to be the identity. Hence $p_2F=p_1$ on a dense open set, and by [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) everywhere.

The alleged [isometry](../../../../../../isometry.md) would therefore be an isomorphism of the two [orbifold](../../../../../../orbifold.md) covers over the same base. Covering-space classification makes $K_1,K_2$ conjugate in $\Gamma$, and applying $\rho$ makes $H_1,H_2$ conjugate in $T$, contrary to the supplied hypothesis. Thus $g_{1k},g_{2k}$ are nonisometric.

For $k\ne\ell$, the surface areas are different:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{area}(N,g_{ik})=4\pi(1+2^{-k}).}
$$

Area is preserved by [isometries](../../../../../../isometry.md), so no surface from the $k$th pair is isometric to a surface from the $\ell$th pair. Together these arguments prove the full pairwise nonisometry assertion.
