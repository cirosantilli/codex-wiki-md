<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write $b=g(\rho-\rho_0)/\rho_0>0$ for the [reduced gravity](../../../../../../reduced-gravity-split.md). The [Boussinesq approximation](../../../../../../boussinesq-approximation.md) requires $|\rho-\rho_0|\ll\rho_0$: replace density by $\rho_0$ in inertia and retain its difference in the gravitational force. A [shallow-water approximation](../../../../../../shallow-water-approximation.md) additionally requires a small aspect ratio, negligible leading-order normal acceleration and approximately [hydrostatic pressure](../../../../../../hydrostatic-pressure.md) relative to the ambient fluid:

$$
p-p_{\rm ambient}=\rho_0b(h-z)\cos\theta,\qquad 0<z<h.
$$

[Incompressibility](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) makes the normal velocity smaller than the downslope velocity by the aspect ratio.

Thinness alone does not force the downslope velocity to be uniform across the layer. The additional [top-hat plume model](../../../../../../top-hat-plume-model.md) assumes that [turbulent mixing](../../../../../../turbulent-mixing.md) makes both $u(x,t)$ and $b(x,t)$ approximately uniform inside it, with a sharp transition to the ambient fluid. Then the depth-integrated volume and momentum fluxes are $hu$ and $hu^2$. This is the same integral closure used for a [turbulent plume](../../../../../../turbulent-plume-split.md), applied to a shallow layer; the hydrostatic pressure term is retained because gravity has a component normal to the channel.
