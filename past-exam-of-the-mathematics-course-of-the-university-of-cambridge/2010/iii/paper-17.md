<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Label the six vertices $A,B,C,D,E,F$ clockwise, starting at the upper-left vertex. The arrows identify $AB$ with $ED$, $BC$ with $FE$, and $FA$ with $DC$, preserving the indicated directions. Consequently the [quotient space](../../../topology.md#quotient-topological-space) has exactly two vertices,

$$
P=\{A,C,E\},\qquad Q=\{B,D,F\}.
$$

Orient its three [topological cells](../../../algebraic-topology.md#topological-cell) by $a:A\to B$, $b:B\to C$, and $c:F\to A$. The clockwise boundary of the hexagonal [topological cell](../../../algebraic-topology.md#topological-cell) traverses

$$
abc^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-1}c.
$$

Every [topological cell](../../../algebraic-topology.md#topological-cell) of dimension one therefore occurs once with each sign. The [cellular chain complex](../../../homology.md#cellular-chain-complex), with integral coefficients, is

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\partial_2=0}\mathbb Z^3
\xrightarrow{\partial_1}\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\partial_1=
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&1\\1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Here the rows correspond to $P,Q$. The [image](../../../set-theory.md#image-of-a-function) of $\partial_1$ is the primitive subgroup generated by $Q-P$, and its [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) is freely generated by $a+b$ and $a+c$. Thus the [homology groups](../../../homology.md#homology-group) are

$$
\boxed{H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&j=1,\\
0,&j>2.
\end{cases}}
$$

The two vertex classes are important: identifying all six vertices would give an incorrect [cellular chain complex](../../../homology.md#cellular-chain-complex). The result agrees with the [homology](../../../homology.md) of a [torus](../../../topology.md#torus).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Write a point of the larger [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) as $(u,v)\in\mathbb R^{m+1}\times\mathbb R^{n-m}$. In the complement, $v\ne0$. There is a [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism)

$$
X\longrightarrow B^{m+1}_{\mathrm{open}}\times S^{n-m-1},
\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto\left(u,\frac{v}{\lVert v\rVert}\right),
$$

whose inverse sends $(u,w)$ to $(u,\sqrt{1-\lVert u\rVert^2}\,w)$. In particular the complement has a [deformation retraction](../../../algebraic-topology.md#deformation-retraction) onto its transverse [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Explicitly,

$$
R_t(u,v)=\frac{((1-t)u,v)}{\sqrt{(1-t)^2\lVert u\rVert^2+\lVert v\rVert^2}},
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
$$

The denominator stays positive, the homotopy stays in the complement, and points with $u=0$ remain fixed. This establishes the [homology of an equatorial sphere complement](../../../homology.md#homology-of-an-equatorial-sphere-complement). By [homotopy invariance of homology](../../../homology.md#homotopy-invariance-of-homology), its [homology](../../../homology.md) is therefore the [homology of a sphere](../../../homology.md#homology-of-a-sphere) of dimension $d=n-m-1$:

$$
\boxed{H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&d\ge1\text{ and }j=0,d,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&d=0\text{ and }j=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$

In the codimension-one case, the complement has two contractible components; this is why degree-zero [homology](../../../homology.md) has rank two. Equivalently, in every case its only nonzero [reduced homology](../../../homology.md#reduced-homology) is $\widetilde H_d(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The [torus](../../../topology.md#torus) map acts on $H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z)$ by

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant) is one, so it is an orientation-preserving [homeomorphism](../../../topology.md#homeomorphism) and acts as the identity on both $H_0(T^2)$ and $H_2(T^2)$.

To calculate the [homology](../../../homology.md) of its [mapping torus](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-torus), cover the time [circle](../../../topology.md#circle) by two arcs. Each inverse image has the [homotopy type](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-type) of $T^2$, while their intersection has two components of that [homotopy type](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-type). The map in the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mayer-vietoris-sequence) can be written

$$
H_q(T^2)\oplus H_q(T^2)\longrightarrow H_q(T^2)\oplus H_q(T^2),
\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto(a+f_*b,-a-b).
$$

Adding the output coordinates and using the input coordinate $a+b$ separates an identity map from $f_*-I$. Canceling the identity summands gives the [Wang sequence](../../../algebraic-topology.md#wang-sequence), and hence

$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(f_*-I\mid H_q(T^2))
\longrightarrow H_q(X)
\longrightarrow\ker(f_*-I\mid H_{q-1}(T^2))\longrightarrow0.
$$

In degree one,

$$
A-I=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [image](../../../set-theory.md#image-of-a-function) and [kernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#kernel-of-a-linear-map) are both generated by $(1,-1)$; its [cokernel](../../../linear-algebra.md#cokernel) is $\mathbb Z$ since this image is primitive. Its [Smith normal form](../../../algebra.md#smith-normal-form) is $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, so its [torsion subgroup](../../../group-theory.md#torsion-subgroup) is zero. On the other two nonzero [homology groups](../../../homology.md#homology-group) of the [torus](../../../topology.md#torus), $f_*-I=0$. The displayed [short exact sequences](../../../module-theory.md#short-exact-sequence) split because their right-hand groups are free. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,3,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&j=1,2,\\
0,&j>3.
\end{cases}}
$$

In particular, the [mapping torus](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mapping-torus) contributes an additional degree-one class from the time [circle](../../../topology.md#circle), as well as the invariant degree-one class in the fiber.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

The [chain map](../../../homology.md#chain-map) identity $d_Df=fd_C$ gives, for $(x,y)\in M_i$,

$$
d_f^2(x,y)=\left(d_C^2x,
(-1)^{i-1}f(d_Cx)+(-1)^id_Df(x)+d_D^2y\right)=(0,0).
$$

Thus $M$ is a [chain complex](../../../homology.md#chain-complex); it is a sign convention for the algebraic [mapping cone](../../../homology.md#mapping-cone-homological-algebra).

Put $E_i=C_{i-1}$ with differential $d_E=d_C$. Inclusion into the second summand and projection onto the first give a [short exact sequence of chain complexes](../../../homology.md#short-exact-sequence-of-chain-complexes)

$$
0\longrightarrow D\xrightarrow{\iota}M\xrightarrow{\rho}E\longrightarrow0.
$$

Its [connecting homomorphism](../../../homology.md#connecting-homomorphism) is particularly explicit. If $x\in C_{i-1}$ is a [chain cycle](../../../homology.md#chain-cycle), lift it to $(x,0)\in M_i$ and take its boundary. The second component is $(-1)^if(x)$, so the [long exact sequence in homology](../../../homology.md#long-exact-sequence-in-homology) contains

$$
\cdots\longrightarrow H_i(C)\xrightarrow{(-1)^{i+1}f_*}H_i(D)
\longrightarrow H_i(M)\longrightarrow H_{i-1}(C)
\xrightarrow{(-1)^if_*}H_{i-1}(D)\longrightarrow\cdots.
$$

If every $H_i(M)$ vanishes, the [exact sequence](../../../homology.md#exact-sequence) makes every $f_*$ both injective and surjective. Conversely, if every $f_*$ is an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism), the map $H_i(D)\to H_i(M)$ has zero image, while $H_i(M)\to H_{i-1}(C)$ has zero image and zero kernel; hence $H_i(M)=0$.

One can also see the converse directly on [chain cycles](../../../homology.md#chain-cycle). If $(x,y)\in M_i$ is a [chain cycle](../../../homology.md#chain-cycle), then $d_Cx=0$ and $d_Dy=-(-1)^if(x)$. Injectivity of the [induced map on homology](../../../homology.md#induced-map-on-homology) $f_*$ gives $x=d_Cz$. Subtracting the [chain boundary](../../../homology.md#chain-boundary) $d_f(z,0)$ leaves $(0,t)$ with $d_Dt=0$. Surjectivity of the [induced map on homology](../../../homology.md#induced-map-on-homology) $f_*$ lets us choose a [chain cycle](../../../homology.md#chain-cycle) $w\in C_i$ and $v\in D_{i+1}$ such that

$$
t=(-1)^{i+1}f(w)+d_Dv.
$$

Then $(0,t)=d_f(w,v)$. Thus every [chain cycle](../../../homology.md#chain-cycle) in $M$ is a [chain boundary](../../../homology.md#chain-boundary), proving the [mapping cone acyclicity criterion](../../../homology.md#mapping-cone-acyclicity-criterion):

$$
\boxed{H_*(M)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\text{ is a quasi-isomorphism}.}
$$

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

For each [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) $p$, reduction of the [mapping cone](../../../homology.md#mapping-cone-homological-algebra) is the [mapping cone](../../../homology.md#mapping-cone-homological-algebra) of the reduced [chain map](../../../homology.md#chain-map). Part (a), whose proof works over any coefficient ring, therefore gives

$$
H_i(M\otimes\mathbb F_p)=0\qquad\text{for every }i,p.
$$

Every $M_i$ is a finitely generated [free abelian group](../../../group-theory.md#free-abelian-group). Multiplication by $p$ consequently gives a [short exact sequence of chain complexes](../../../homology.md#short-exact-sequence-of-chain-complexes)

$$
0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{p}M\longrightarrow M\otimes\mathbb F_p\longrightarrow0.
$$

The neighboring terms $H_{i+1}(M\otimes\mathbb F_p)$ and $H_i(M\otimes\mathbb F_p)$ in its [long exact sequence in homology](../../../homology.md#long-exact-sequence-in-homology) both vanish. Thus multiplication by $p$ on $H_i(M)$ is an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism) for every [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) $p$.

The group $H_i(M)$ is a [finitely generated abelian group](../../../group.md#finitely-generated-abelian-group), since it is a quotient of a subgroup of $M_i$. Write it, using the [Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](../../../group.md#fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups), as

$$
H_i(M)\cong\mathbb Z^{b_i}\oplus T_i,
$$

with $T_i$ finite. If $b_i>0$, multiplication by any [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) is not surjective. If $T_i\ne0$, choose a [prime number](../../../number-theory.md#prime-number) dividing its order; multiplication by that prime is not injective. Both contradict the preceding [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism). Hence $H_i(M)=0$ for every $i$.

Applying part (a) integrally now proves the stronger natural conclusion

$$
\boxed{f_*:H_i(C;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H_i(D;\mathbb Z)\quad\text{for every }i.}
$$

This is [prime coefficient detection of quasi-isomorphisms](../../../homology.md#prime-coefficient-detection-of-quasi-isomorphisms). The finite-generation hypothesis is used precisely when excluding nonzero groups on which every prime acts invertibly.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Choose [orientations](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) on the two [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) factors whose product is the specified [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of $X$. Give the four factors of $Y=(S^2)^4$ the corresponding [orientations](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) in the order $1,2,3,4$. The [Künneth theorem](../../../cohomology.md#kunneth-theorem) has no [Tor functor](../../../algebra.md#tor-functor) terms here and gives

$$
\boxed{H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,8,\\
\mathbb Z^4,&j=2,6,\\
\mathbb Z^6,&j=4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$

Let $u_i\in H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)$ be the pullback of the positively normalized [cohomology](../../../cohomology.md) class from factor $i$. The [cohomology ring](../../../cohomology.md#cohomology-ring) is

$$
H^*(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4]/(u_1^2,u_2^2,u_3^2,u_4^2),
\qquad |u_i|=2,
$$

with $\langle u_1u_2u_3u_4,[Y]\rangle=1$. Its generators commute by [graded commutativity of the cup product](../../../cohomology.md#graded-commutativity-of-the-cup-product).

Fix a point in each [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). For each pair $i<j$, let $S_{ij}$ be the four-dimensional [submanifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#submanifold) in which factors $i,j$ vary and the remaining factors equal their fixed points. Orient $S_{ij}$ by the ordered factors $i,j$. The six [homology classes](../../../homology.md#homology-class)

$$
[S_{12}],\ [S_{13}],\ [S_{14}],\ [S_{23}],\ [S_{24}],\ [S_{34}]
$$

give a [coordinate sphere intersection basis](../../../homology.md#coordinate-sphere-intersection-basis) by the [homology cross product](../../../cohomology.md#homology-cross-product) description in the [Künneth theorem](../../../cohomology.md#kunneth-theorem). Moving a fixed point in one of the other two factors produces a disjoint homologous representative of each $S_{ij}$. Hence every basis element has zero [self-intersection number](../../../algebraic-geometry.md#self-intersection-number).

More precisely, the [intersection pairing](../../../homology.md#intersection-pairing) is

$$
[S_{ij}]\mathbin{\cdot}[S_{kl}]=
\begin{cases}
1,&\{i,j\}\sqcup\{k,l\}=\{1,2,3,4\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

For complementary pairs the representatives intersect transversely at one point. The sign is positive because swapping two-dimensional factors contributes $(-1)^{2\cdot2}=1$. For pairs with a common index, a factor fixed in both representatives can be displaced to make them disjoint. In the paired order $(12,34),(13,24),(14,23)$, the [intersection form](../../../homology.md#intersection-form) is three copies of $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

For the [diagonal map](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diagonal-map) $\delta:X\to Y$, write $a,b\in H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$ for the two [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) generators. Then

$$
\delta^*u_1=\delta^*u_3=a,\qquad
\delta^*u_2=\delta^*u_4=b,
\qquad a^2=b^2=0,\quad\langle ab,[X]\rangle=1.
$$

The coefficient of $[S_{ij}]$ in $[\Delta]$ is $\langle u_iu_j,[\Delta]\rangle$. Pullback and the preceding [cup product](../../../cohomology.md#cup-product) relations show that these coefficients are one for $12,14,23,34$ and zero for $13,24$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[\Delta]=[S_{12}]+[S_{14}]+[S_{23}]+[S_{34}].}
$$

There are exactly two complementary pairings among these four terms, each counted twice in the square. Thus

$$
\boxed{[\Delta]\mathbin{\cdot}[\Delta]=2(1+1)=4.}
$$

This computes both the explicit [homology class](../../../homology.md#homology-class) and its [intersection pairing](../../../homology.md#intersection-pairing), including all [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) signs.

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

Work with coefficients $\mathbb F_2$. For $n\ge1$, the [mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mod-two-cohomology-ring-of-real-projective-space) and the [Künneth theorem](../../../cohomology.md#kunneth-theorem) give

$$
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^{2n};\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^{2n+1}),
\qquad
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n};\mathbb F_2)
=\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x^{n+1},y^{n+1}),
$$

where all three generators have degree one. Any [induced map on cohomology](../../../cohomology.md#induced-map-on-cohomology) has $f^*z=\alpha x+\beta y$, with $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb F_2$. By naturality of the [cup product](../../../cohomology.md#cup-product), the only possible term in top degree is

$$
f^*(z^{2n})=(\alpha x+\beta y)^{2n}
=\binom{2n}{n}\alpha^n\beta^n x^ny^n.
$$

All other terms vanish because their exponent of $x$ or $y$ exceeds $n$. But

$$
\binom{2n}{n}=2\binom{2n-1}{n-1}
$$

is even for every $n\ge1$. Thus the displayed top-degree [induced map on cohomology](../../../cohomology.md#induced-map-on-cohomology) is zero for every $f$.

Both top-degree [homology groups](../../../homology.md#homology-group) are one-dimensional over $\mathbb F_2$, and their dual [cohomology groups](../../../cohomology.md#cohomology-group) pair with them nondegenerately. Naturality of this evaluation makes $f_*$ dual to $f^*$, so $f_*$ is also zero and cannot be an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism). This is the [mod-two degree obstruction for equal projective factors](../../../algebraic-topology.md#mod-two-degree-obstruction-for-equal-projective-factors). Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{There is no such map for any positive }n.}
$$

If zero is included among the allowed values, $\mathbb{RP}^0$ is a point and the unique map is an [isomorphism](../../../algebra.md#isomorphism) on $H_0$. In that convention, **the only value is $n=0$**.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

Use the standard [orientations](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) on the three [spheres](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) and the complex [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) on [Complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#complex-projective-space). Let $h\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^3;\mathbb Z)$ satisfy $\langle h^3,[\mathbb{CP}^3]\rangle=1$. The [cohomology ring of complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#cohomology-ring-of-complex-projective-space) is $\mathbb Z[h]/(h^4)$. The [Künneth theorem](../../../cohomology.md#kunneth-theorem) gives

$$
H^*((S^2)^3;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[a,b,c]/(a^2,b^2,c^2),
\qquad |a|=|b|=|c|=2,
\qquad \langle abc,[(S^2)^3]\rangle=1.
$$

Write the [induced map on cohomology](../../../cohomology.md#induced-map-on-cohomology) as $f^*h=pa+qb+rc$ with integers $p,q,r$. Naturality and [graded commutativity of the cup product](../../../cohomology.md#graded-commutativity-of-the-cup-product) give

$$
f^*(h^3)=(pa+qb+rc)^3=6pqr\,abc.
$$

The [mapping degree](../../../homology.md#degree-of-a-continuous-mapping) is therefore $d=6pqr$. This is the [factorial degree obstruction for products of two-spheres](../../../algebraic-topology.md#factorial-degree-obstruction-for-products-of-two-spheres). Every positive value is a positive multiple of six, so **the least possible positive degree is at least $6$**.

To attain it, identify each oriented [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) with $\mathbb{CP}^1$. Send three projective linear forms to the coefficients of their product:

$$
F:(\mathbb{CP}^1)^3\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^3,
\qquad ([a_1:b_1],[a_2:b_2],[a_3:b_3])\longmapsto[c_0:c_1:c_2:c_3],
$$

where

$$
\prod_{i=1}^3(a_i z+b_i w)=c_0z^3+c_1z^2w+c_2zw^2+c_3w^3.
$$

No product of nonzero linear forms is the zero polynomial. Rescaling any factor rescales all coefficients by the same nonzero scalar. The map is therefore well-defined and is a [holomorphic map](../../../complex-analysis.md#holomorphic-map) on the products of projective charts.

Choose a homogeneous cubic with three distinct roots, all in an affine chart. Its preimages are exactly the $3!=6$ orderings of its three projective linear factors. In root coordinates, the derivative of the elementary-symmetric coefficient map has [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant) equal, up to sign, to the [Vandermonde determinant](../../../galois-theory.md#vandermonde-determinant), the product of the three pairwise root differences. This is nonzero for distinct roots, so all six preimages are regular. A nonsingular complex-linear derivative preserves the underlying real [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex), since its real [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant) is the squared absolute value of its complex [determinant](../../../linear-algebra.md#determinant). Each preimage consequently contributes $+1$ to the [mapping degree](../../../homology.md#degree-of-a-continuous-mapping). Hence

$$
\boxed{\deg F=6,\qquad d_{\min}=6.}
$$

This [polynomial multiplication map to complex projective space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#polynomial-multiplication-map-to-complex-projective-space) provides the requested example without assuming that a prescribed [cohomology](../../../cohomology.md) homomorphism is realizable by a continuous map.

## 5

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 17](paper-17.md)

<h3 id="5/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

Let $D^n_+$ and $D^n_-$ be the two closed hemispheres of the [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere). Use $\gamma$ as the [clutching function](../../../fiber-bundle.md#clutching-function) between the trivial bundles on these hemispheres. The [clutching construction](../../../fiber-bundle.md#clutching-construction) defines

$$
V_\gamma=
\bigl((D^n_+\times\mathbb R^k)\sqcup(D^n_-\times\mathbb R^k)\bigr)/\sim,
\qquad (u,v)_+\sim(u,\gamma(u)v)_-,\quad u\in S^{n-1}.
$$

The projection to $S^n$ is locally a [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle). In a collar of the equator, extend the transition function by keeping it constant in the normal coordinate; this gives explicit [vector bundle trivializations](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle-trivialization) across the seam. Since the transition matrices lie in the [special orthogonal group](../../../linear-algebra.md#special-orthogonal-group), the standard fiber [orientations](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) and Euclidean [inner products](../../../linear-algebra.md#inner-product) agree on overlaps. Thus this is an oriented rank-$k$ [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) with a [fiber metric](../../../fiber-bundle.md#fiber-metric).

For $k\ge2$, choose a base point $u_0$ on the equator. A constant change of the negative hemisphere frame replaces $\gamma$ by $\gamma(u_0)^{-1}\gamma$, so we may suppose $\gamma(u_0)=I$. With $e_k$ the last standard unit vector, define the [section obstruction for a clutched vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#section-obstruction-for-a-clutched-vector-bundle)

$$
\boxed{f_\gamma=[u\longmapsto\gamma(u)e_k]\in\pi_{n-1}(S^{k-1});\qquad r=n-1,\ s=k-1.}
$$

The crucial point is that a [nowhere-zero section](../../../fiber-bundle.md#nowhere-zero-section) can be normalized using the [fiber metric](../../../fiber-bundle.md#fiber-metric). It is then represented by continuous maps $\sigma_\pm:D^n_\pm\to S^{k-1}$ satisfying

$$
\sigma_-(u)=\gamma(u)\sigma_+(u)
$$

on the equator.

If $f_\gamma=0$, the [extension-null-homotopy criterion for a sphere](../../../algebraic-topology.md#extension-null-homotopy-criterion-for-a-sphere) extends $u\mapsto\gamma(u)e_k$ to $D^n_-$. Take this extension as $\sigma_-$ and the constant map $e_k$ as $\sigma_+$. They glue to a [nowhere-zero section](../../../fiber-bundle.md#nowhere-zero-section).

Conversely, suppose such a section exists. Restricting a contraction of $D^n_+$ shows that $\sigma_+|_{S^{n-1}}$ is homotopic to a constant. The target [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) is path connected, so the constant can be moved to $e_k$. Multiplying this homotopy pointwise by $\gamma(u)$ gives a [homotopy](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy) from $\gamma\sigma_+$ to $\gamma e_k$. The first of these maps equals $\sigma_-|_{S^{n-1}}$ and extends over $D^n_-$, hence is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map). So $\gamma e_k$ is [null-homotopic](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) too. A freely null-homotopic based sphere map represents zero in its [homotopy group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group); any change of base point sends the zero class to zero. We have proved both directions:

$$
\boxed{f_\gamma=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_\gamma\text{ has a nowhere-zero section}.}
$$

For $n=1$, the same argument reads as the extension criterion for a two-point boundary and uses the trivial set $\pi_0(S^{k-1})$ when $k\ge2$. For $k=1$, the [special orthogonal group](../../../linear-algebra.md#special-orthogonal-group) $SO(1)$ is trivial, so the [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) is already a [trivial vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#trivial-vector-bundle); the corresponding constant map to $S^0$ has zero obstruction. These cases require no higher [homotopy group](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) calculation.

If $k>n$, then $n-1<k-1$ and the permitted vanishing of lower [homotopy groups](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-group) of a [sphere](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#sphere) makes $f_\gamma=0$. Normalize the resulting [nowhere-zero section](../../../fiber-bundle.md#nowhere-zero-section) to a unit section $\sigma$, and set

$$
V'_x=\{v\in(V_\gamma)_x:\langle v,\sigma(x)\rangle=0\}.
$$

Local orthonormal frames show that these [orthogonal complements](../../../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-complement) form a rank-$(k-1)$ [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle). The explicit [vector bundle isomorphism](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle-isomorphism)

$$
V'\oplus(S^n\times\mathbb R)\longrightarrow V_\gamma,
\qquad (x,v,t)\longmapsto v+t\sigma(x)
$$

has inverse $w\mapsto(w-\langle w,\sigma\rangle\sigma,\langle w,\sigma\rangle)$. Orient $V'$ so that a positive frame followed by $\sigma$ is positive in $V_\gamma$. This is [splitting a trivial line from a nowhere-zero section](../../../fiber-bundle.md#splitting-a-trivial-line-from-a-nowhere-zero-section) and proves the oriented splitting

$$
\boxed{V_\gamma\cong V'\oplus\mathbb R\qquad(k>n).}
$$

Finally consider the [special orthogonal sphere fibration](../../../linear-algebra.md#special-orthogonal-sphere-fibration)

$$
SO(s)\longrightarrow SO(s+1)\xrightarrow{p}S^s,
\qquad p(A)=Ae_{s+1}.
$$

The fiber over $e_{s+1}$ consists exactly of $\operatorname{diag}(B,1)$ with $B\in SO(s)$. Near any unit vector, the [Gram-Schmidt process](../../../linear-algebra.md#gram-schmidt-process) continuously completes it to an oriented orthonormal frame, giving local sections of $p$ and local product [fiber bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md) charts. In particular $p$ has the [homotopy lifting property](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-lifting-property).

Let $\alpha:S^r\to SO(s+1)$ be based and suppose $r<s$. The composite $p\alpha$ is a based [null-homotopic map](../../../algebraic-topology.md#null-homotopic-map) by the given vanishing of $\pi_r(S^s)$. Lift a based null-homotopy of $p\alpha$, beginning at $\alpha$, to a homotopy $G:S^r\times[0,1]\to SO(s+1)$. The lift might initially move its base point $u_0$, but $G(u_0,t)$ always fixes $e_{s+1}$. Right multiplication by $G(u_0,t)^{-1}$ corrects this: the homotopy

$$
\widetilde G(u,t)=G(u,t)G(u_0,t)^{-1}
$$

still covers the same sphere homotopy, remains based, and starts at $\alpha$. At its endpoint every matrix fixes $e_{s+1}$, so $\widetilde G(-,1)$ lies in the included $SO(s)$. Thus every [homotopy class](../../../algebraic-topology.md#homotopy-class) in the target is represented by one from $SO(s)$:

$$
\boxed{\pi_r(SO(s))\longrightarrow\pi_r(SO(s+1))\text{ is surjective for }r<s.}
$$

This proves [surjectivity of special orthogonal stabilization below the sphere dimension](../../../linear-algebra.md#surjectivity-of-special-orthogonal-stabilization-below-the-sphere-dimension). For $r=0$, this is the corresponding assertion about path components; the positive-rank [special orthogonal groups](../../../linear-algebra.md#special-orthogonal-group) are connected. The lifting argument also explains the zero term $\pi_r(S^s)$ in the [long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration](../../../algebraic-topology.md#long-exact-sequence-of-homotopy-groups-of-a-fibration).
