<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [adjoint operators](../../../../../../adjoint-operator.md), orthonormal [singular vectors](../../../../../../singular-vector.md) and squared-norm [normal equation for a linear inverse problem](../../../../../../normal-equation-for-a-linear-inverse-problem.md) conditions require [Hilbert spaces](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md). For arbitrary [normed spaces](../../../../../../normed-vector-space.md) the [Banach-space adjoint](../../../../../../transpose-of-a-bounded-linear-operator.md) takes values in the dual spaces and need not produce this formula. Interpret the given [singular value system](../../../../../../singular-system-of-a-compact-operator.md) in [Hilbert spaces](../../../../../../hilbert-space-split.md), with $u_j$ in the unknown space and $v_j$ in the data space, as in the printed identities. Positive [singular values](../../../../../../singular-value.md) tending to zero provide the compact ill-posed case. A general [bounded linear operator](../../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) need not have this property; the [identity operator](../../../../../../identity-operator.md) is a well-conditioned counterexample. Components in the [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) must be fixed by a minimum-norm convention, and the unregularized inverse is defined only for data satisfying the corresponding range condition.
