<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $F=\mathbb F_q$. The [general linear group over a finite field](../../../../../../general-linear-group-over-a-finite-field.md) consists of all invertible [linear maps](../../../../../../linear-map.md) $F^n\to F^n$, and the [special linear group over a finite field](../../../../../../special-linear-group-over-a-finite-field.md) is its determinant-one [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Counting the ordered [bases](../../../../../../basis.md) gives

$$
|GL_n(q)|=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^n-q^i)
=q^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(q^j-1).
$$

The [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) map is onto $F^\times$, since $\operatorname{diag}(t,1,\ldots,1)$ has [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $t$. Its kernel is $SL_n(q)$, so

$$
\boxed{|SL_n(q)|=\frac{|GL_n(q)|}{q-1}.}
$$

For $n>1$, commuting with all the [elementary transvection matrices](../../../../../../elementary-transvection-matrix.md) $I+tE_{ij}$ forces a [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) to be scalar. Therefore the [center of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) of $SL_n(q)$ consists of $\lambda I$ with $\lambda^n=1$. The multiplicative group of a [finite field](../../../../../../finite-field.md) is cyclic, so this [center of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) has size $d=\gcd(n,q-1)$. The [projective special linear group over a finite field](../../../../../../projective-special-linear-group-over-a-finite-field.md) consequently has order

$$
|PSL_n(q)|=\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=2}^n(q^j-1)}{\gcd(n,q-1)}.
$$

Here is a proof of simplicity which isolates all the required ingredients. First we prove the [Iwasawa simplicity lemma](../../../../../../iwasawa-simplicity-lemma.md) in the form needed. Suppose a group $H$ acts faithfully and primitively, is a [perfect group](../../../../../../perfect-group.md), and has an abelian [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $U$ normal in a [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) $H_\alpha$, whose conjugates generate $H$. For a nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N$, its orbits form a [block system](../../../../../../block-system.md). Faithfulness prevents all these orbits from being singletons, so primitivity makes $N$ transitive. Thus $H=NH_\alpha$. Modulo $N$, each conjugate of $U$ has exactly the same image as $U$, because we may write its conjugating element as $nh$ with $n\in N$ and $h\in H_\alpha$. The quotient $H/N$ is generated by this one abelian image and hence is abelian. Since $H$ is a [perfect group](../../../../../../perfect-group.md), $H/N=1$. Thus every nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) is $H$.

Apply this to the action on [projective points](../../../../../../projective-point.md), that is, one-dimensional [vector subspaces](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) of $F^n$. Any ordered pair of distinct lines can be sent to any other by a [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) obtained by extending representatives to [bases](../../../../../../basis.md). Multiplying one target [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) by a suitable nonzero scalar adjusts the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) to one without changing either target line. Thus $SL_n(q)$ is [two-transitive](../../../../../../two-transitive-group-action.md) on these points. A [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) fixing every line is scalar: it is diagonal on a fixed [basis](../../../../../../basis.md), and fixing the lines spanned by $e_i+e_j$ makes all diagonal entries equal. The projective quotient therefore acts faithfully, two-transitively, and primitively.

For the line $\langle e_1\rangle$, use

$$
U=\{I+e_1f:f\in (F^n)^*,\ f(e_1)=0\}.
$$

These [matrices](../../../../../../matrix.md) multiply by addition of $f$, so $U$ is abelian. Conjugating by a [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) preserving $\langle e_1\rangle$ preserves $U$, so it is normal in that [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md). Its conjugates include every [elementary transvection matrix](../../../../../../elementary-transvection-matrix.md) $I+tE_{ij}$.

These [elementary transvection matrices](../../../../../../elementary-transvection-matrix.md) generate $SL_n(q)$ by row elimination. For completeness, row addition is elementary, signed row interchange is elementary, and compensating diagonal scalings are elementary because, in a two-dimensional coordinate block,

$$
w(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-t^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\-t^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
w(t)w(-1)=\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1}).
$$

Elimination using these operations reduces a determinant-one [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) to the identity.

It remains to show that these groups are [perfect groups](../../../../../../perfect-group.md). In the following [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) identities use $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$; the alternative [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) convention generates the same [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md). If $n\ge3$, distinct indices give the [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) identity

$$
[I+aE_{ij},I+bE_{jk}]=I+abE_{ik}.
$$

Every elementary generator is therefore a [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md). For $n=2$ and $q>3$, choose $\lambda\in F^\times$ with $\lambda^2\ne1$, and let $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda^{-1})$. Then

$$
[D,I+tE_{12}]=I+(\lambda^2-1)tE_{12}.
$$

As $t$ varies this supplies all upper [elementary transvection matrices](../../../../../../elementary-transvection-matrix.md), and conjugation supplies the lower ones. Thus $SL_n(q)$, and its projective quotient, are perfect in the asserted cases. The preceding simplicity argument now applies.

The two exceptions are genuine: the projective actions identify $PSL_2(2)$ with $S_3$ and $PSL_2(3)$ with $A_4$, of orders 6 and 12 respectively. The first contains its [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of order 3; the second contains the normal Klein four group. We have proved

$$
\boxed{PSL_n(q)\text{ is simple for }n>1,\quad
(n,q)\notin\{(2,2),(2,3)\}.}
$$
