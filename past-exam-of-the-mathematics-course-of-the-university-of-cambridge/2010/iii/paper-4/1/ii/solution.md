<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a finite group, define the [Frattini subgroup](../../../../../../frattini-subgroup.md) by

$$
\Phi(G)=\bigcap_{M\text{ maximal in }G}M;
$$

the intersection is $G$ when there are no [maximal subgroups](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md), which for a finite group happens only for $G=1$. A second definition is the set of [non-generators](../../../../../../non-generator-of-a-finite-group.md): an element $x$ such that $\langle S,x\rangle=G$ always implies $\langle S\rangle=G$.

If $x\in\Phi(G)$ and $H=\langle S\rangle<G$, put $H$ in a [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) $M$. Then $x\in M$, so $\langle S,x\rangle\le M<G$. Conversely, if $x\notin\Phi(G)$, some [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) $M$ omits it; maximality gives $\langle M,x\rangle=G$, although $M\ne G$. This proves equivalence.

Every [group automorphism](../../../../../../group-automorphism.md) permutes [maximal subgroups](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md), so $\Phi(G)$ is a [characteristic subgroup](../../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md), in particular a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md). Also

$$
H\Phi(G)=G\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H=G:
$$

a proper $H$ would lie in a [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) containing both factors.

Set $F=\Phi(G)$ and take a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) $P$ of $F$. For $g\in G$, normality of $F$ makes $P^g$ a [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $F$. Conjugating inside $F$ therefore gives the [Frattini argument](../../../../../../frattini-argument.md) factorization $G=F N_G(P)$. The preceding generating property forces $N_G(P)=G$. Thus every [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $F$ is normal, and part (i) shows that $F$ is a [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md).

Now suppose $G$ is a [finite p-group](../../../../../../finite-p-group.md). The [normalizer](../../../../../../normalizer.md) condition makes every [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) $M$ normal: $M<N_G(M)$ and maximality force $N_G(M)=G$. The quotient $G/M$ has no proper nontrivial [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md), hence is cyclic of prime order; its p-power order makes that prime $p$. Consequently every such $M$ contains

$$
D=[G,G]G^p,
$$

where the two factors are the [commutator subgroup](../../../../../../commutator-subgroup.md) and the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by pth powers. Thus $D\le\Phi(G)$.

The quotient $G/D$ is an [elementary abelian group](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md), hence a [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md) over $\mathbb F_p$. If $x\notin D$, extend the nonzero [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) $xD$ to a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) and choose a [linear functional](../../../../../../linear-functional.md) nonzero on it. Its kernel pulls back to a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of index $p$, a [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) omitting $x$. Therefore $x\notin\Phi(G)$, proving

$$
\boxed{\Phi(G)\text{ is characteristic and nilpotent},\qquad
\Phi(G)=[G,G]G^p\text{ for finite p-groups}.}
$$

The two factors in the last formula are multiplied; the superscripted expression in the TeX aid is not the formula printed in the PDF.
