<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [Fano plane](../../../../../../fano-plane.md) has as points the seven nonzero [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) of $\mathbb F_2^3$, because every one-dimensional [vector subspace](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md) has just one nonzero [vector](../../../../../../vector.md). Its lines are the seven two-dimensional [vector subspaces](../../../../../../vector-subspace.md). Every line has three points, every point lies on three lines, two points determine the line $\{u,v,u+v\}$, and two lines meet in one point.

The two order-24 classes are therefore [stabilizer subgroups](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of points and of lines. Each is isomorphic to $S_4$: fixing a [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) gives the block group $\mathbb F_2^2\rtimes GL_2(2)$, the full affine group on four points; inverse transpose gives the dual line [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md). Their distinct fixed-point behavior in part (i) distinguishes the two [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md).

For a point $p$ and line $L$, $G_p\cap G_L$ stabilizes the ordered pair $(p,L)$. There are $7\cdot3=21$ incident pairs and $7\cdot4=28$ nonincident pairs. A change of [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) takes any pair to a fixed model of its type, so each type is a single orbit. [Orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) gives the [Fano point-line stabilizer intersection](../../../../../../fano-point-line-stabilizer-intersection.md):

$$
\boxed{|G_p\cap G_L|=
\begin{cases}
8,&p\in L,\\
6,&p\notin L.
\end{cases}}
$$

Both occur: take $L=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle$ and choose $p=\langle e_1\rangle$ or $p=\langle e_3\rangle$.

For the extension, identify [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) and covectors by the standard pairing. The [inverse-transpose automorphism](../../../../../../inverse-transpose-automorphism.md) exchanges a point with its orthogonal plane and exchanges the two order-24 classes. Thus it is outer. The [Fano plane duality group](../../../../../../fano-plane-duality-group.md) $\overline G=G\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle$ has order 336 and acts on points and lines, preserving incidence while allowing their types to be swapped.

The [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) of an unordered point-line pair has twice the corresponding ordered-pair [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md): some element outside $G$ swaps the pair, since duality produces a pair of the same incidence type and $G$ is transitive on such pairs. Hence these [stabilizer subgroups](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) have orders 16 and 12. Also every outside conjugate of a Sylow 7-subgroup can be moved back by an element of $G$, giving

$$
|N_{\overline G}(P)|=2|N_G(P)|=42.
$$

We prove these exhaust the [maximal subgroups](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) other than $G$. Let $K$ be such a [maximal subgroup](../../../../../../maximal-subgroup.md) and $H=K\cap G$. Since $K$ is not contained in $G$, $H$ is normal in $K$ and $|K:H|=2$. If $7\mid|H|$, part (i) gives a unique Sylow 7-subgroup $P$ of $H$. It is characteristic in $H$, hence normalized by $K$. Therefore maximality forces $K=N_{\overline G}(P)$.

Suppose $7\nmid|H|$ and $H\ne1$. The classification argument in part (i) says $H$ fixes a point or a line. Because $H$ is normal in $K$, an element outside $G$ swaps its fixed-point and fixed-line sets, so it fixes at least one of each. Write

$$
d=\dim V^H,\qquad d^*=\dim(V^*)^H.
$$

Fixed lines are kernels of fixed nonzero covectors, since the only nonzero scalar is one. Duality gives $2^d-1=2^{d^*}-1$, hence $d=d^*\in\{1,2\}$.

If $d=1$, there is a unique fixed point and unique fixed line; $K$ preserves their unordered pair. If $d=2$, put $W=V^H$ and $A=((V^*)^H)^\perp$. For every $h\in H$, $\ker(h-I)$ contains the plane $W$ and $\operatorname{im}(h-I)$ lies in the line $A$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ there is only one nonzero [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) $V/W\to A$, so $H$ has order two. Invertibility forces $A\subseteq W$: for $h=I+v f$ one needs $f(v)=0$. Thus $A$ and $W$ are its unique image point and kernel line. These are canonically swapped by duality, so $K$ again preserves their unordered incident pair.

If $H=1$, then $K$ has order two, generated by an involutory duality $u$. For any point $p$, the pair $\{p,u(p)\}$ is preserved by $u$. Hence $K$ lies in an order-12 or order-16 pair [stabilizer subgroup](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) and is not maximal. We have exhausted all cases.

The pair [stabilizer subgroups](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) are themselves maximal: a proper maximal overgroup is one of the just-listed types, and divisibility excludes orders 12 inside 16 or 42, and 16 inside 12 or 42. The order-42 [normalizer](../../../../../../normalizer.md) is maximal by the unique-Sylow argument. Incidence types give one [conjugacy class](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) each; Sylow conjugacy gives one [normalizer](../../../../../../normalizer.md) class. Thus, apart from the normal index-two [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $G$,

$$
\boxed{\overline G:\text{ three maximal-subgroup classes, of orders }12,\ 16,\ 42.}
$$
