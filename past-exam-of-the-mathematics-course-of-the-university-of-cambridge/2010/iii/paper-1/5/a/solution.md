<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Suppose that $H$ is not a [discrete subgroup](../../../../../../discrete-subgroup.md). The identity is then an accumulation point of nonidentity subgroup elements: translate any failure of isolation to the identity. Choose $h_j\in H\setminus\{e\}$ with $h_j\to e$, and use a [local exponential chart](../../../../../../local-exponential-chart.md) to write $h_j=\exp X_j$, where $X_j\ne0$ and $X_j\to0$ in the real [Lie algebra](../../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathfrak g$.

Choose a [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) on this finite-dimensional [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md). By [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of its unit sphere, pass to a subsequence with $X_j/\|X_j\|\to X$, where $\|X\|=1$. For an arbitrary real $t$, choose an [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $m_j$ nearest to $t/\|X_j\|$. Then $m_j\|X_j\|\to t$ and

$$
h_j^{m_j}=\exp(m_jX_j)\longrightarrow\exp(tX).
$$

Every left-hand element belongs to $H$, including for negative $m_j$. Closedness gives $\exp(tX)\in H$. Since $t$ was arbitrary and $X\ne0$, the [limit directions of a closed subgroup](../../../../../../limit-directions-of-a-closed-subgroup.md) provide a nontrivial [one-parameter subgroup](../../../../../../one-parameter-subgroup.md) in $H$. Its derivative at zero is $X$, so it cannot be the constant subgroup.
