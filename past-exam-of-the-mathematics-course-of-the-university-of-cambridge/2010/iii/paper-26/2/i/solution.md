<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Write $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)$ and

$$
D_{\mathfrak q}=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q\}.
$$

Every member of this [decomposition group](../../../../../../decomposition-group.md) preserves $\mathcal O_F$ and $\mathfrak q$, so reduction defines a homomorphism

$$
D_{\mathfrak q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}).
$$

Its kernel is the [inertia group](../../../../../../inertia-group.md). We prove [surjectivity of decomposition group reduction](../../../../../../surjectivity-of-decomposition-group-reduction.md) without presupposing a lift of Frobenius.

If the two [residue fields](../../../../../../residue-field.md) coincide, surjectivity is immediate. Otherwise choose a nonzero $\overline a\in k_{\mathfrak q}$ generating it over $k_{\mathfrak p}$; a generator exists by the [primitive element theorem](../../../../../../primitive-element-theorem.md) for [finite fields](../../../../../../finite-field.md). Use the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) in $\mathcal O_F$ to choose $a$ whose residue is $\overline a$ at $\mathfrak q$ and zero at every other prime above $\mathfrak p$. Consider

$$
P(T)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}(T-\sigma a).
$$

Its coefficients are integral and fixed by $G$, hence lie in $\mathcal O_K$: an element of $K$ integral over $\mathcal O_K$ belongs to this integrally closed [ring of integers](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md).

Reduce at $\mathfrak q$. When $\sigma\notin D_{\mathfrak q}$, the chosen residue of $a$ at $\sigma^{-1}\mathfrak q$ is zero, so $\sigma a$ reduces to zero at $\mathfrak q$. When $\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}$, its residue is $\overline\sigma(\overline a)$, which is nonzero. Therefore

$$
\overline P(T)=T^{|G|-|D_{\mathfrak q}|}
\prod_{\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}}(T-\overline\sigma(\overline a)).
$$

Although this product is written in $k_{\mathfrak q}[T]$, its coefficients belong to $k_{\mathfrak p}$. Put $q=|k_{\mathfrak p}|$. The [finite-field Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../../finite-field-frobenius-automorphism.md) $b\mapsto b^q$ therefore permutes its nonzero roots. In particular, $\overline a^q$ is $\overline\sigma(\overline a)$ for some $\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}$. Since $\overline a$ generates $k_{\mathfrak q}$, that $\overline\sigma$ agrees with Frobenius on the whole field. The [Galois group of a finite field extension](../../../../../../galois-group-of-a-finite-field-extension.md) is cyclic and generated by Frobenius, so its generator lies in the image. Thus

$$
\boxed{D_{\mathfrak q}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}),\qquad
D_{\mathfrak q}/I_{\mathfrak q}\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p})}.
$$
