<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ with $\alpha$ an [algebraic integer](../../../../../algebraic-integer.md), let $m\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be its monic [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md), and write $A=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. The [Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../kummer-dedekind-theorem.md) applies to a rational prime $p$ not dividing the finite index $[\mathcal O_K:A]$. If

$$
\overline m=\prod_{j=1}^r g_j^{e_j}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[X]
$$

with distinct monic [irreducible polynomials](../../../../../irreducible-polynomial.md) $g_j$, choose monic integer lifts $\widetilde g_j$. Then the distinct [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) above $p$ are

$$
\mathfrak p_j=(p,\widetilde g_j(\alpha)),\qquad
\boxed{p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathfrak p_j^{e_j},\quad
[\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p_j:\mathbb F_p]=\deg g_j}.
$$

In particular, $p$ is unramified precisely when $\overline m$ has no repeated irreducible factor. The exclusion of primes dividing the index is essential.

Here is the [quotient-ring proof of the Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../quotient-ring-proof-of-the-kummer-dedekind-theorem.md). The finite group $\mathcal O_K/A$ has order coprime to $p$. Localizing at the integers coprime to $p$ therefore gives $A\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}=\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, and reduction yields

$$
\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\simeq A/pA\simeq\mathbb F_p[X]/(\overline m).
$$

The second isomorphism follows from $A\simeq\mathbb Z[X]/(m)$. The [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md) gives

$$
\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\simeq\prod_j\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j^{e_j}).
$$

Each factor is a [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) with unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) generated by $g_j$ and [residue field](../../../../../residue-field.md) $\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j)$. Pulling its [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) back to $\mathcal O_K$ gives exactly $\mathfrak p_j$; these exhaust the primes above $p$. This proves the asserted [residue degrees](../../../../../residue-degree.md) and their independence of the chosen lifts.

It remains to prove the exponents rather than simply identify the primes. Unique [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) in the [Dedekind domain](../../../../../dedekind-domain.md) $\mathcal O_K$ writes $(p)=\prod_j\mathfrak p_j^{b_j}$. At $\mathfrak p_j$, its [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md) has $p=u\pi^{b_j}$, with $u$ a unit. Thus the [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) of $\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p_j}/p\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p_j}$ has [nilpotence index of an ideal](../../../../../nilpotence-index-of-an-ideal.md) $b_j$: its $b_j$th power is zero and its $(b_j-1)$st is nonzero. In $\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j^{e_j})$ this index is $e_j$, since $g_j^{e_j}=0$ but $g_j^{e_j-1}\ne0$. The displayed product isomorphism identifies these local factors, hence $b_j=e_j$. This completes the theorem's proof. The same local argument works over a base [number field](../../../../../number-field.md) whenever $\mathcal O_K[\alpha]$ equals the full relative [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) after localizing at the chosen base prime.

For the required [cyclotomic field](../../../../../cyclotomic-field.md), the allowed [ring of integers](../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) formula makes the index one. Its [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../../../cyclotomic-polynomial.md) is

$$
\Phi_{80}(X)=\frac{X^{40}+1}{X^8+1}=X^{32}-X^{24}+X^{16}-X^8+1.
$$

For $p\notin\{2,5\}$, the polynomial $X^{80}-1$ has derivative $80X^{79}$ coprime to it modulo $p$. Its divisor $\overline\Phi_{80}$ is therefore squarefree, so the [Kummer-Dedekind theorem](../../../../../kummer-dedekind-theorem.md) gives no ramification.

The [cyclotomic reduction at a conductor prime](../../../../../cyclotomic-reduction-at-a-conductor-prime.md) is explicit at the remaining two primes:

$$
\overline\Phi_{80}=(X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1)^8\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[X],
$$



$$
\overline\Phi_{80}=(X^8+1)^4=(X^4+2)^4(X^4+3)^4\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5[X].
$$

The quartic over $\mathbb F_2$ has no linear factor. Its only possible irreducible quadratic divisor would be $X^2+X+1$, whose roots have order three, whereas its own roots have order five. Hence it is irreducible. The two quartics over $\mathbb F_5$ are also irreducible: their roots have multiplicative order sixteen because their fourth powers are $2$ or $3$, both of order four; the [finite-field Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../finite-field-frobenius-automorphism.md) has orbit length four on an element of order sixteen, since $5^4\equiv1\pmod{16}$ and neither $5$ nor $5^2$ is $1$ modulo sixteen.

Consequently the [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) above $2$ has one prime with ramification index eight and [residue degree](../../../../../residue-degree.md) four. Above $5$ it has two primes, each with ramification index four and [residue degree](../../../../../residue-degree.md) four. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\text{The ramified rational primes are exactly }2\text{ and }5.}
$$
