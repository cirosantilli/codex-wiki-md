<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

[Critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) describe singular behavior near a [continuous phase transition](../../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md). Define $m(t,0)\sim(-t)^\beta$ below $T_c$, susceptibility $\chi(t,0)=\partial m/\partial h\sim|t|^{-\gamma}$, singular [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) $C_s\sim|t|^{-\alpha}$, and critical isotherm $m(0,h)\sim\operatorname{sgn}(h)|h|^{1/\delta}$. The ordinary quartic [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md) gives the equation of state

$$
h=r_t t\,m+um^3.
$$

At zero field its ordered minimum is proportional to $(-t)^{1/2}$. Differentiating the equation of state gives $\chi^{-1}=r_t t+3um^2$, proportional to $|t|$ on either side. At $t=0$, $h=um^3$, and the minimized free energy below the transition is proportional to $-t^2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\beta=\tfrac12,\qquad\gamma=1,\qquad\delta=3,\qquad\alpha=0.}
$$

Here $\alpha=0$ denotes a finite heat-capacity jump, rather than a power divergence. Including gradients gives the [Landau scalar correlation length](../../../../../../landau-scalar-correlation-length.md) $\xi\propto|t|^{-1/2}$ and correlation-length exponent $\nu=1/2$.
