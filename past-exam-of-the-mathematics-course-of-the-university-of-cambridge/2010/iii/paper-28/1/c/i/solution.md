<h1 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The increments have [expected value](../../../../../../../expected-value.md) $p-q<0$. The [strong law of large numbers](../../../../../../../strong-law-of-large-numbers.md) for [independent and identically distributed random variables](../../../../../../../independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables.md) with finite absolute mean gives

$$
\frac{S_n}{n}\longrightarrow p-q<0\qquad\text{almost surely}.
$$

Hence $S_n\to-\infty$. Since $q/p>1$,

$$
\boxed{Z_n=(q/p)^{S_n}\longrightarrow0\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Its [expected value](../../../../../../../expected-value.md) nevertheless stays equal to one. Thus this [martingale](../../../../../../../martingale-split.md) is not [uniformly integrable](../../../../../../../uniform-integrability.md), and it does not converge to zero in $L^1$. This distinction will be avoided in part (ii) by stopping at an upper level, which makes the stopped process bounded.
