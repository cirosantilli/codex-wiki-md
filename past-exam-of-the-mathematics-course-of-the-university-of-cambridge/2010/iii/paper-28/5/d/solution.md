<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Again suppose $P$ is a nonconstant [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) without a complex zero, and put $g=1/P$. As proved in part (c), $g$ is an entire [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) with $|g(z)|\to0$ at infinity. Choose $z_0$ and then a disk large enough that $|g(z)|<|g(z_0)|/2$ outside it. On the closed disk, $|g|$ attains its maximum by the [extreme value theorem](../../../../../../extreme-value-theorem.md), and that maximum is positive and greater than every value outside the disk. It is therefore a global, and in particular local, maximum on $\mathbb C$.

Part (b)(ii) forces $g$ to be constant. A nonzero constant reciprocal makes $P$ constant, a contradiction. Hence $\boxed{P\text{ has a complex zero}}$. Equivalently one can minimize $|P|$ globally and apply the [maximum modulus principle](../../../../../../maximum-modulus-principle.md) to its reciprocal if that minimum is positive. This supplies the second proof of the [fundamental theorem of algebra](../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-algebra.md) using part (b).
