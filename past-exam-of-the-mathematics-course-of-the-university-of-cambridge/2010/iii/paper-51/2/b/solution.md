<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

If the preparation label $i$ is sampled with probability $p_i$ but is not supplied to the experimenter, the [quantum state ensemble](../../../../../../quantum-state-ensemble.md) is represented by

$$
\boxed{\rho=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|,\qquad
p_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i p_i=1.}
$$

For normalized states, this [density operator](../../../../../../density-matrix.md) is Hermitian, positive semidefinite and has trace one. The states in an ensemble need not be orthogonal or distinct. For any [measurement in quantum mechanics](../../../../../../quantum-measurement-split.md) effect $E$, averaging the [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md) over the preparation label gives

$$
P(E)=\sum_i p_i\langle\psi_i|E|\psi_i\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho).
$$

Thus the density operator summarizes all measurements performed on the prepared system alone. For an ensemble of mixed states $\rho_i$, the same reasoning gives $\rho=\sum_i p_i\rho_i$.
