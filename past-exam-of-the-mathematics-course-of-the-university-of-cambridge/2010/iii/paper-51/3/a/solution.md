<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $s=\sin^2\alpha$ and $t=\sin^2\beta$. Then $0<s<t<1/2$, and the ordered squared [Schmidt coefficients](../../../../../../schmidt-coefficient.md) of the source and target are $(1-s,s)$ and $(1-t,t)$.

[Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem](../../../../../../nielsen-s-pure-state-conversion-theorem.md) states that a deterministic exact conversion between bipartite pure states by [LOCC](../../../../../../local-operations-and-classical-communication.md) exists precisely when the source's vector of squared [Schmidt coefficients](../../../../../../schmidt-coefficient.md) is majorized by the target's vector, with zero padding if needed. In the convention $x\prec y$, [majorization](../../../../../../majorization.md) requires $\sum_{j=1}^k x_j^\downarrow\leq\sum_{j=1}^k y_j^\downarrow$ for every $k$, and equality of total sums. Here the first inequality would require $1-s\leq1-t$, but $s<t$ gives the reverse strict inequality. Thus **exact conversion with certainty is impossible**.
