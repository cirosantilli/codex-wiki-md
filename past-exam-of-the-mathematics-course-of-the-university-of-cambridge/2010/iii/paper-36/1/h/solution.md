**No.** A [Bayes factor](../../../../../../bayes-factor.md) favouring the alternative means $B_{01}<1$, but the [posterior odds](../../../../../../posterior-odds.md) are $B_{01}O_{01}$. They are less than one only if $B_{01}<1/O_{01}$. For example, [prior odds](../../../../../../prior-odds.md) of $1000:1$ on the [null hypothesis](../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md), combined with a [Bayes factor](../../../../../../bayes-factor.md) of $100:1$ in favour of the alternative, still give [posterior odds](../../../../../../posterior-odds.md) of $10:1$ on the [null hypothesis](../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md). An unusual claim can require substantial evidence to overcome low initial plausibility.
