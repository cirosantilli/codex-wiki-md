<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Use the orientation that favours the [null hypothesis](../../../../../../null-hypothesis.md):

$$
\boxed{B_{01}(y)=\frac{p(y\mid H_0)}{p(y\mid H_1)}.}
$$

For a composite model the denominator is its [Bayesian model evidence](../../../../../../bayesian-model-evidence.md), obtained by integrating the [likelihood](../../../../../../likelihood-function.md) against the model's [prior distribution](../../../../../../prior-probability.md). This [Bayes factor](../../../../../../bayes-factor.md) exceeds one when the observed data have greater marginal probability under $H_0$. By [Bayes' theorem](../../../../../../bayes-theorem.md),

$$
\frac{\mathbb P(H_0\mid y)}{\mathbb P(H_1\mid y)}
=\frac{p(y\mid H_0)\pi_0/p(y)}{p(y\mid H_1)\pi_1/p(y)}
=\boxed{B_{01}(y)O_{01}}.
$$

Thus the [posterior odds](../../../../../../posterior-odds.md) equal the [prior odds](../../../../../../prior-odds.md) multiplied by the [Bayes factor](../../../../../../bayes-factor.md). The reciprocal orientation instead gives $B_{10}=1/B_{01}$.
