<h1 id="1/g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [G](../g.md)

An alternative that allows small departures from chance, while assigning little probability to near-perfect prediction or near-perfect failure, is more plausible than a uniform [prior distribution](../../../../../../prior-probability.md). For example, a symmetric [Beta distribution](../../../../../../beta-distribution.md) $\operatorname{Beta}(a,a)$ with $a>1$ is centered at $1/2$, with

$$
\mathbb E\theta=\frac12,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\theta)=\frac1{4(2a+1)}.
$$

Choosing $a=50$ gives a prior standard deviation of about $0.05$ and concentrates on modest deviations. Its concentration should represent substantive beliefs specified before examining this experiment, rather than being tuned to the observed count. A continuous [prior distribution](../../../../../../prior-probability.md) centered at $1/2$ still places no point mass at $H_0$; the separate model [prior probability](../../../../../../prior-probability.md) supplies that mass.
