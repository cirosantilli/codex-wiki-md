<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Use $\hbar=c=1$. For a canonical light [scalar field](../../../../../scalar-field.md) with effective mass $m^2\ll H^2$, define the canonical Fourier mode $u_k=a\,\delta\Phi_k$. In the nearly de Sitter approximation it obeys

$$
u_k''+\left(k^2-\frac{a''}{a}\right)u_k=0,\qquad
a\simeq-\frac1{H\eta},\qquad \frac{a''}{a}\simeq\frac2{\eta^2}.
$$

Deep inside the [Hubble radius](../../../../../hubble-radius.md), $k\gg aH$, the mode behaves like a Minkowski oscillator. Choosing the [Bunch-Davies vacuum](../../../../../bunch-davies-vacuum.md) sets $u_k\to e^{-ik\eta}/\sqrt{2k}$. The normalized de Sitter solution is

$$
u_k=\frac{e^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1-\frac{i}{k\eta}\right).
$$

Inflation stretches the physical wavelength until it exits the Hubble radius. For $|k\eta|\ll1$, the growing scalar solution freezes with $|\delta\Phi_k|^2=|u_k/a|^2\to H^2/(2k^3)$. The [dimensionless power spectrum](../../../../../dimensionless-cosmological-power-spectrum.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal P_{\delta\Phi}(k)=\frac{k^3}{2\pi^2}|\delta\Phi_k|^2
\simeq\left(\frac{H_k}{2\pi}\right)^2}.
$$

Here $H_k$ is evaluated when $k=aH$, since slow evolution near horizon exit fixes the frozen amplitude. Nearly constant $H$ and a light field make this spectrum almost scale-invariant; slow background evolution and small mass corrections produce a small tilt. The stretched vacuum modes supply the primordial stochastic fluctuations.
