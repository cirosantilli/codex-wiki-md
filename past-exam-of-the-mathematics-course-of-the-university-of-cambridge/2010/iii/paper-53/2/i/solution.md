<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At temperatures above a few MeV, [weak interactions](../../../../../../weak-interaction.md) such as $n+\nu_e\leftrightarrow p+e^-$ and $n+e^+\leftrightarrow p+\bar\nu_e$ maintain [neutron-proton chemical equilibrium](../../../../../../neutron-proton-chemical-equilibrium.md). Negligible lepton chemical potentials give the [neutron-to-proton ratio](../../../../../../neutron-to-proton-ratio.md) $r_{\rm eq}=n_n/n_p=\exp[-\Delta/(k_BT)]$, where $\Delta=(m_n-m_p)c^2\simeq1.293\,{\rm MeV}$. At high temperature this ratio is nearly one.

During [radiation domination](../../../../../../radiation-domination.md), the interaction time grows as $T^{-5}$, faster than the expansion time $T^{-2}$. Their crossing therefore produces [cosmological weak freeze-out](../../../../../../cosmological-weak-freeze-out.md). If $\Theta=T/(10^{10}\,{\rm K})$, equating the displayed order-of-magnitude times gives $\Theta_f^{-5}\sim g_*^{-1/2}\Theta_f^{-2}$, so

$$
\boxed{T_f\sim10^{10}g_*^{1/6}\ {\rm K},\qquad
T_f\propto(Gg_*)^{1/6}G_{\rm weak}^{-2/3}}.
$$

Using $H^{-1}=2t$ instead of $t$ changes only the order-one coefficient. Before electron-positron annihilation, the usual effective relativistic count is $g_*=2+(7/8)(4+6)=10.75$. The factor $7/8$ weights fermionic relative to bosonic energy density.

After freeze-out, free neutrons decay while protons increase correspondingly. Define the neutron baryon fraction $X_n=n_n/(n_n+n_p)$. In the instantaneous-freeze-out estimate,

$$
X_{n,f}=\frac{r_f}{1+r_f},\qquad r_f=e^{-\Delta/(k_BT_f)},\qquad
X_n(t)=X_{n,f}e^{-(t-t_f)/\tau_n},
$$

where $\tau_n=t_{1/2}/\ln2$ is the neutron mean lifetime. Hence the ratio at the onset of nuclear synthesis is

$$
\boxed{r_N=\frac{r_f e^{-(t_N-t_f)/\tau_n}}
{1+r_f[1-e^{-(t_N-t_f)/\tau_n}]}}.
$$

This expression accounts for the protons produced by neutron decay.

Nuclear burning initially waits at the [deuterium bottleneck](../../../../../../deuterium-bottleneck.md): although deuterium is bound by about $2.2\,{\rm MeV}$, the many photons per baryon can still photodissociate it. Roughly, $\eta_b^{-1}e^{-B_D/(k_BT_N)}\sim1$ sets the bottleneck temperature, with refinements from reaction rates and phase space. Around $k_BT_N\sim0.1\,{\rm MeV}$, deuterium survives, reactions through deuterium, tritium and helium-3 rapidly build tightly bound helium-4, and nearly all surviving neutrons are captured.

Thus $n_{\rm He}\simeq n_n/2$ and the [primordial helium mass fraction](../../../../../../primordial-helium-mass-fraction.md) is

$$
\boxed{Y_{\rm He}\simeq2X_n(t_N)=\frac{2r_N}{1+r_N}}.
$$

For the standard estimate $r_N\simeq1/7$, neutron and proton constituent fractions are $1/8$ and $7/8$, respectively, and $Y_{\rm He}\simeq1/4$. Free neutrons are negligible after capture. The remaining hydrogen mass fraction is about $3/4$, and the helium-to-hydrogen number ratio is about $1/12$.

The printed numerical time coefficients are schematic, not a precision prediction of this quarter fraction: their literal crossing with $g_*=10.75$ gives $k_BT_f\simeq1.28\,{\rm MeV}$. Keeping physical weak-rate coefficients and the gradual freeze-out instead gives an effective freeze-out around $0.7$–$0.8\,{\rm MeV}$, followed by decay over a few minutes. The abundance formulas above explain the standard quarter fraction and its parameter dependence without treating the rounded time formulas as exact nuclear-rate data.
