<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In Questions 3–4, use primes for conformal-time derivatives and $\mathcal H=a'/a$; proper-time derivatives will be stated separately. Let $\xi^\mu=(T,L^i)$. The metric is a [covariant tensor](../../../../../../covariant-tensor.md), so under the passive coordinate change $\widetilde x^\mu=x^\mu+\xi^\mu$,

$$
\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde x)=
\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\widetilde x^\mu}
\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial\widetilde x^\nu}
g_{\alpha\beta}(x).
$$

The inverse Jacobian is $\delta^\alpha_\mu-\partial_\mu\xi^\alpha$ to first order. At the same new coordinate argument, the homogeneous background satisfies $\bar g_{\alpha\beta}(x)=\bar g_{\alpha\beta}(\widetilde x)-T\bar g'_{\alpha\beta}$. Shifting the argument of $\delta g$ or multiplying it by a Jacobian perturbation produces only second-order terms, which we discard. Subtracting the unperturbed metric therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\delta\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}=\delta g_{\mu\nu}
-\bar g_{\alpha\nu}\partial_\mu\xi^\alpha
-\bar g_{\mu\beta}\partial_\nu\xi^\beta
-T\bar g'_{\mu\nu}}.
$$

This is the printed Jacobian expression expanded to linear order, equivalently $\delta\widetilde g=\delta g-\mathcal L_\xi\bar g$. It is a [gauge transformation in cosmological perturbation theory](../../../../../../gauge-transformation-in-cosmological-perturbation-theory.md), not a change of the physical spacetime.
