<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an expanding universe, [cosmic inflation](../../../../../../cosmic-inflation-split.md) requires $\ddot a>0$. Including any vacuum contribution in the total density and pressure, the acceleration equation gives **$\boxed{\rho+3P<0}$**, or $w=P/\rho<-1/3$ for positive density. Equivalently the [comoving Hubble radius](../../../../../../comoving-hubble-radius.md) $(aH)^{-1}=\dot a^{-1}$ decreases. Since $|\Omega_{\rm total}-1|=|k|/(aH)^2$, prolonged acceleration suppresses curvature and addresses the [flatness problem](../../../../../../flatness-problem.md). A sufficiently long inflationary phase also stretches a region that was initially causally connected to encompass the observed region, addressing the [horizon problem](../../../../../../horizon-problem.md). The condition on acceleration must last long enough; an arbitrarily short interval is insufficient.

For a homogeneous canonical [scalar field](../../../../../../scalar-field.md) $\Phi$ with potential $V(\Phi)$,

$$
\rho_\Phi=\tfrac12\dot\Phi^2+V,\qquad P_\Phi=\tfrac12\dot\Phi^2-V,\qquad
\rho_\Phi+3P_\Phi=2(\dot\Phi^2-V).
$$

Thus **$V>\dot\Phi^2$** is sufficient for inflation. A suitably flat positive potential gives [slow-roll inflation](../../../../../../slow-roll-approximation.md): Hubble damping in $\ddot\Phi+3H\dot\Phi+V_{,\Phi}=0$ keeps the kinetic energy small compared with $V$, so $P_\Phi\simeq-\rho_\Phi$.
