<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and $\hbar=c=1$. In the [interaction picture](../../../../../interaction-picture.md), fields evolve with the free [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md), while interactions enter the state evolution through the [Dyson series](../../../../../dyson-series.md). Write the [Klein-Gordon field](../../../../../klein-gordon-field.md) as $\phi=\phi^{(+)}+\phi^{(-)}$, with the [positive-frequency part of a quantum field](../../../../../positive-frequency-part-of-a-quantum-field.md) containing [annihilation operators](../../../../../annihilation-operator.md) and $\phi^{(-)}=(\phi^{(+)})^\dagger$ containing [creation operators](../../../../../creation-operator.md). The mode normalization in the PDF corresponds to $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ and $E_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+m^2}$.

The [time-ordered product](../../../../../time-ordered-product.md) places fields with the latest time on the left. For two bosonic insertions,

$$
T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\}=\theta(x^0-y^0)\phi(x)\phi(y)+\theta(y^0-x^0)\phi(y)\phi(x).
$$

The [normal-ordered product](../../../../../normal-ordered-product.md) $:\!\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)\!:$ instead moves every [creation operator](../../../../../creation-operator.md) to the left of every [annihilation operator](../../../../../annihilation-operator.md), without adding the commutators generated by that rearrangement. Its [vacuum expectation value](../../../../../vacuum-expectation-value.md) is zero whenever a nonempty set of free-field factors remains.

The mode [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) gives the positive-frequency [Wightman function](../../../../../wightman-function.md)

$$
D^+(x-y)=[\phi^{(+)}(x),\phi^{(-)}(y)]
=\langle0|\phi(x)\phi(y)|0\rangle
=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}.
$$

Define the [Feynman propagator](../../../../../feynman-propagator.md) here to be the two-point function itself, rather than that function divided by $i$. Then

$$
\boxed{\Delta_F(x-y)=\langle0|T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\}|0\rangle
=\theta(x^0-y^0)D^+(x-y)+\theta(y^0-x^0)D^+(y-x).}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\Delta_F(x-y)=\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}}{p^2-m^2+i0}.
$$

The positive-energy pole is below and the negative-energy pole above the real axis. Closing the energy [contour integral](../../../../../contour-integral.md) below for positive time separation and above for negative time separation gives the two terms above. Thus this is the [scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription](../../../../../scalar-feynman-propagator-pole-prescription.md), and $(\Box+m^2)\Delta_F=-i\delta^4$. In particular, $T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\}=:\!\phi(x)\phi(y)\!:+\Delta_F(x-y)$: the difference between [time ordering](../../../../../time-ordering.md) and [normal ordering](../../../../../normal-ordering.md) is the [Wick contraction](../../../../../wick-contraction.md).

For $n$ free bosonic fields, the [Wick theorem](../../../../../wick-s-theorem.md) states

$$
\boxed{T\{\phi_1\cdots\phi_n\}
=\sum_{P}\left(\prod_{(i,j)\in P}\Delta_F(x_i-x_j)\right)
:\!\prod_{r\notin V(P)}\phi_r\!:,}
$$

where $P$ runs over all sets of disjoint pairs of indices, including the empty set, and $V(P)$ is the set of paired indices. The empty [normal-ordered product](../../../../../normal-ordered-product.md) equals one. This is an operator identity for free fields, understood with smearing or a regulator when products of [operator-valued distributions](../../../../../operator-valued-distribution.md) require it.

Here is an [inductive proof of the scalar Wick theorem](../../../../../inductive-proof-of-the-scalar-wick-theorem.md). Sort the arguments chronologically so that $x_1^0$ is the latest. For any remaining set $I$, commuting the annihilation part of $\phi_1$ through the creation parts in a [normal-ordered product](../../../../../normal-ordered-product.md) gives

$$
\phi_1:\!\prod_{r\in I}\phi_r\!:
=:\!\phi_1\prod_{r\in I}\phi_r\!:
+\sum_{j\in I}[\phi_1^{(+)},\phi_j^{(-)}]
:\!\prod_{r\in I\setminus\{j\}}\phi_r\!:.
$$

The creation part of $\phi_1$ is already in the correct position. Every commutator on the right is a scalar, so no further operator rearrangement is needed for it. Because $x_1$ is latest, that commutator is $\Delta_F(x_1-x_j)$. Assume the [Wick theorem](../../../../../wick-s-theorem.md) for the other $n-1$ fields and multiply its expansion by $\phi_1$. The first term of the displayed identity gives every pairing in which index 1 is unpaired; the sum gives every pairing in which 1 is paired with exactly one $j$. Each final partial pairing has exactly one such construction. The one-field identity supplies the base case, proving the theorem by [mathematical induction](../../../../../mathematical-induction.md). Applying [time ordering](../../../../../time-ordering.md) restores arbitrary argument order.

Taking the [vacuum expectation value](../../../../../vacuum-expectation-value.md) retains only complete pairings. Odd free correlators vanish; for example the [free scalar four-point function](../../../../../free-scalar-four-point-function.md) is $\Delta_{12}\Delta_{34}+\Delta_{13}\Delta_{24}+\Delta_{14}\Delta_{23}$. These facts explain why propagators are the edges of [Feynman diagrams](../../../../../feynman-diagram.md).

For the interacting [quartic scalar field theory](../../../../../quartic-interaction.md), the [interaction Hamiltonian](../../../../../interaction-hamiltonian.md) density is $\lambda\phi^4/4!$. With a single [time ordering](../../../../../time-ordering.md) acting on external insertions and interaction vertices, the unnormalized numerator expands as

$$
N_n=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i\lambda)^v}{v!(4!)^v}
\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_v\,
\langle0|T\{\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)\phi(z_1)^4\cdots\phi(z_v)^4\}|0\rangle.
$$

Apply the proved [Wick theorem](../../../../../wick-s-theorem.md) term by term. Each complete pairing connects external insertions and four-legged vertices by [Feynman propagators](../../../../../feynman-propagator.md). There are $4!$ assignments of four distinct incident legs at a vertex, canceling its factorial. Permuting identical integrated vertices cancels the corresponding part of $v!$. Permutations still leaving the labeled graph unchanged give its [Feynman-diagram symmetry factor](../../../../../feynman-diagram-symmetry-factor.md). For example,

$$
N_{4,\mathrm{connected}}^{(1)}=-i\lambda\int d^4z\prod_{r=1}^4\Delta_F(x_r-z),
$$

whereas the two-point [tadpole diagram](../../../../../tadpole-diagram.md) has coefficient $-i\lambda/2$, because choosing the two vertex legs contracted with the labeled external fields gives $4\cdot3$ possibilities and the other two contract with each other.

Thus the position-space rules attach $\Delta_F$ to every edge, $-i\lambda\int d^4z$ to every four-legged [interaction vertex](../../../../../interaction-vertex.md), and divide by the graph's [Feynman-diagram symmetry factor](../../../../../feynman-diagram-symmetry-factor.md). Fourier transformation gives the momentum-space [Feynman rules](../../../../../feynman-rule.md):

$$
\boxed{\text{line: }\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
\text{vertex: }-i\lambda,\qquad
\text{loop: }\int\frac{d^4\ell}{(2\pi)^4}.}
$$

Enforce [momentum conservation](../../../../../momentum-conservation.md) at each vertex, retaining the overall four-momentum delta function. External lines of these correlation functions remain unamputated. The unnormalized expression includes [Vacuum Feynman diagrams](../../../../../vacuum-feynman-diagram.md); dividing by $N_0=\langle0|S|0\rangle$ cancels those disconnected vacuum factors. They should not be discarded without that normalization.

There is a time-ordering convention in the printed correlation-function notation worth making explicit. The usual interacting correlator uses the single global [time ordering](../../../../../time-ordering.md) in $N_n$ above. If the displayed $T\{\phi_1\cdots\phi_n\}S$ is read literally with $S$ outside that ordering, the external block and the vertex block are separately time ordered: their cross-contractions are $D^+(x_i-z)$ because each external field stands to the left of every vertex field. The same pairing construction then uses $\Delta_F$ for lines within either block and $D^+$ for lines between blocks; in momentum space the latter is $2\pi\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2-m^2)$. This literal product is different from the conventional globally time-ordered interacting correlator, and does not have the ordinary external [Feynman propagators](../../../../../feynman-propagator.md) derived above.
