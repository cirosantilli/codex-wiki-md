<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [polynya](../../../../../../polynya.md) is a persistent or recurrent area of open water or relatively thin ice within an otherwise ice-covered region. Persistence of the opening does not mean absence of ice formation: a [latent-heat polynya](../../../../../../latent-heat-polynya.md) can continuously make ice and export it. In contrast, a [sensible-heat polynya](../../../../../../sensible-heat-polynya.md) remains open because ocean heat inhibits freezing. Both processes may occur at the same site.

At an Antarctic coast, cold air descending from the elevated continent produces strong [katabatic winds](../../../../../../katabatic-wind.md). Their offshore component drives [sea ice](../../../../../../sea-ice.md) away from a fixed coastline, fast-ice edge or grounded-iceberg barrier. Open water exposed to the cold atmosphere loses heat rapidly; [frazil ice](../../../../../../frazil-ice.md) forms, aggregates into [pancake ice](../../../../../../pancake-ice.md) or consolidated new ice, and is exported offshore. The opening continues as long as export balances production. Headlands, the shelter provided by [fast ice](../../../../../../fast-ice.md) and the direction of the prevailing winds determine the location and size. Terra Nova Bay is a characteristic coastal example. Such [latent-heat polynyas](../../../../../../latent-heat-polynya.md) are ice factories rather than permanently warm holes in the ice pack.

At an [ice shelf](../../../../../../ice-shelf.md) front the fixed boundary is floating shelf ice instead of land. Offshore winds blowing across the shelf can similarly remove newly formed sea ice, so an ice-front [polynya](../../../../../../polynya.md) need not have a different thermodynamic mechanism from a coastal one; the Ross Ice Shelf front provides an important wind-driven example. Shelf-front geometry, ice tongues, grounded icebergs and changes in calving alter the shelter and the supply of drifting pack ice. Some ice-front openings also receive significant sensible heat from currents, tidal mixing or upwelling, and heat carried out of an ice-shelf cavity can affect freezing. A [sensible-heat polynya](../../../../../../sensible-heat-polynya.md) requires a sufficiently warm ocean supply, whereas the latent-heat mechanism requires continuing removal of the ice that does form. Consequently a strict identification of all coastal openings with one mechanism and all ice-shelf openings with the other would be incorrect.

The oceanic roles are substantial. Rapid heat loss and freezing produce [brine rejection](../../../../../../brine-rejection.md), raising shelf-water [salinity](../../../../../../salinity.md) and [mass density](../../../../../../density.md). The resulting convection and circulation can carry dense shelf water into ice-shelf cavities; melting beneath an [ice shelf](../../../../../../ice-shelf.md) modifies it into colder, fresher [ice-shelf water](../../../../../../ice-shelf-water.md). Dense shelf and cavity-modified waters can subsequently descend the continental slope, mix with adjacent waters and contribute to [Antarctic bottom water](../../../../../../antarctic-bottom-water.md). The outcome depends on bathymetry and the density after mixing: not all rejected brine reaches the abyss.

These localized regions can therefore have a disproportionate effect on deep-ocean ventilation, oxygen supply, carbon transport and the large-scale [ocean circulation](../../../../../../ocean-circulation.md). They are also intense sites of ocean-to-atmosphere heat and moisture transfer. Seasonal sunlight arriving in open water supports high biological production where nutrients are available, with consequences for marine food webs and carbon cycling. **Antarctic polynyas connect wind-driven ice export, concentrated ice production and dense-water formation; ice-shelf fronts add a floating boundary and possible cavity-related heat and freshwater exchanges.**
