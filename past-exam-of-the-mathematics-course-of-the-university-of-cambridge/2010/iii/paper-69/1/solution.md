The [solar constant](../../../../../solar-constant.md) measures the incident power per area normal to the sunlight. A spherical [Earth](../../../../../earth.md) of radius $R$ intercepts sunlight over the disc $\pi R^2$, but radiates over its surface $4\pi R^2$. For uniform [surface albedo](../../../../../surface-albedo.md) $a$ in the global energy-budget approximation, absorbed power is $(1-a)S\pi R^2$. Treat the surface as a [blackbody](../../../../../blackbody.md), assume efficient heat redistribution and neglect internal heating. The [Stefan–Boltzmann law](../../../../../stefan-boltzmann-law.md) and the [planetary surface energy balance](../../../../../planetary-surface-energy-balance.md) then give

$$
(1-a)S\pi R^2=4\pi R^2\beta T_E^4,\qquad \boxed{T_E=\left(\frac{(1-a)S}{4\beta}\right)^{1/4}}.
$$

For a nonuniform surface the same power balance instead defines an effective [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md), $T_E^4=\langle T^4\rangle$; its fourth root is not generally the arithmetic mean of the local temperatures. Also, area-averaging the [surface albedo](../../../../../surface-albedo.md) is an approximation to the properly insolation-weighted averaging. These are the simplifications implicit in the naked-planet calculation.

Take $R=6.371\times10^6\,\mathrm m$, so $A_E=4\pi R^2=5.10\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The changed area is $A_i=10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, giving

$$
\Delta a=(0.10-0.80)\frac{A_i}{A_E}=-1.372\times10^{-3},\qquad a_1=0.298628.
$$

The initial [planetary equilibrium temperature](../../../../../planetary-equilibrium-temperature.md) is $T_0=254.8\,\mathrm K$. Thus

$$
\Delta T=T_0\left[\left(\frac{1-a_1}{1-0.3}\right)^{1/4}-1\right]\simeq \boxed{+0.125\,\mathrm K}.
$$

The differential estimate $\Delta T\simeq-T_0\Delta a/[4(1-a_0)]$ gives the same result. This is the global equilibrium response of the specified model, not the immediate local Arctic warming or the full atmospheric response.

The exceptional 2007 [Arctic sea ice decline](../../../../../arctic-sea-ice-decline.md) is best understood as a weather event acting on an already vulnerable ice cover, with several reinforcing pathways. Warmer air increases downward long-wave heating, lengthens the melt season and delays autumn freezing. Changes in snowfall and the development of [melt ponds](../../../../../melt-pond.md) also change the surface energy balance. Air temperature alone, however, does not identify the source of the observed bottom melt: heat must reach the underside through the ocean.

Warmer Atlantic-origin water supplies a larger subsurface [ocean heat content](../../../../../ocean-heat-content.md) after entering through Fram Strait and the Barents Sea. Its influence on [sea ice](../../../../../sea-ice.md) depends on vertical exchange, because a cold, relatively fresh [halocline](../../../../../halocline.md) normally separates it from the ice. Erosion of that [halocline](../../../../../halocline.md), through altered freshwater distributions, wind-driven mixing or changes in circulation, weakens [stable density stratification](../../../../../stable-density-stratification.md) and can increase upward [ocean heat flux](../../../../../ocean-heat-flux.md). A warmer Atlantic layer without this exchange need not produce immediate extra bottom melt. Conversely, warmer Pacific inflow through Bering Strait introduces heat into the shallower western Arctic; advection through the Chukchi Sea provides another route to ice retreat and ocean heat storage.

The [Arctic Oscillation](../../../../../arctic-oscillation.md) changes winds, ice motion and export. Positive phases during earlier years helped remove old, thick ice through Fram Strait and left a younger, thinner pack. Summer 2007 also had a pronounced pressure contrast between the Beaufort region and the Eurasian Arctic, associated with the [Arctic dipole anomaly](../../../../../arctic-dipole-anomaly.md). Its winds promoted ice displacement and warm-air transport. The summer circulation should not be described merely as a particular [Arctic Oscillation](../../../../../arctic-oscillation.md) index: the spatial pattern and the preconditioning from earlier years both matter.

Long-term thinning reduces the [latent heat of fusion](../../../../../latent-heat-of-fusion.md) needed to remove the remaining ice. Replacement of [multi-year sea ice](../../../../../multi-year-sea-ice.md) by [first-year sea ice](../../../../../first-year-sea-ice.md) therefore makes a given summer heat input more effective, while mobile thin ice is more readily redistributed or exported. These dynamical changes can expose open water early enough for substantial solar absorption. Water and [melt ponds](../../../../../melt-pond.md) have lower [surface albedo](../../../../../surface-albedo.md) than snow-covered ice, so absorbed short-wave energy rises, increasing upper-ocean heat storage and bottom melt. This [ice-ocean albedo feedback](../../../../../ice-ocean-albedo-feedback.md) also delays refreezing. In the Beaufort Sea in 2007, enhanced solar heating of the newly exposed upper ocean was an especially important explanation of extreme bottom melt. Thus thinning and wind-driven opening can initiate retreat, while solar heating amplifies it; changes in Atlantic or Pacific heat supply can further reinforce the process where circulation permits.

A further positive [climate feedback](../../../../../climate-feedback.md) involves terrestrial carbon. Loss of the insulating ice cover permits greater autumn and winter transfer of stored ocean heat to the atmosphere. The resulting regional warming can increase thaw of coastal and inland [permafrost](../../../../../permafrost.md). Microbial decomposition of previously frozen organic material then releases [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md), and oxygen-poor wet environments also release [methane](../../../../../methane.md). These [greenhouse gases](../../../../../greenhouse-gas.md) strengthen the [greenhouse effect](../../../../../greenhouse-effect.md), increasing global warming and further thaw. The [permafrost carbon feedback](../../../../../permafrost-carbon-feedback.md) provides the causal chain from summer ice retreat to an additional global influence. An [Arctic methane feedback](../../../../../arctic-methane-feedback.md) involving subsea sources is also possible, but the amount actually reaching the atmosphere depends on oxidation and transport; an abrupt, wholesale release is not a necessary consequence of summer ice loss.

For the bottom-melt estimate, use $\rho_i=917\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and the given $L_f=336000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. Assume the ice at the melting interface is already at its local freezing temperature; there is then no separate sensible-heating term. Per square metre the [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md) requirement is

$$
E=\rho_i L_f h_i=917\times336000\times2=6.16\times10^8\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}}.
$$

July through September has $92$ days, so the mean [ocean heat flux](../../../../../ocean-heat-flux.md) to the ice is

$$
\boxed{\overline Q=\frac{E}{92\times86400}=77.5\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\approx80\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}}.
$$

Using $\rho_i\simeq900\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ instead gives about $76\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, within the intended accuracy. Around the date of this examination, the estimated net anthropogenic [radiative forcing](../../../../../radiative-forcing.md) since preindustrial times was about $1.6\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$; doubling [carbon dioxide](../../../../../carbon-dioxide.md) gives about $3.7\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. The calculated bottom flux is tens of times larger. There is no energy-budget contradiction: it is a local, summer ocean-to-ice heat transfer, supplied by absorbed sunshine, stored heat and transport, whereas [radiative forcing](../../../../../radiative-forcing.md) is a global perturbation to the radiation budget. Redistribution and local absorption can produce much larger regional fluxes than the global forcing.

For an already ice-free $100\,\mathrm m$ water column, take $\rho_w=1025\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and liquid-water [specific heat capacity](../../../../../specific-heat-capacity.md) $c_w\simeq4000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. Assuming all the same energy remains in that uniformly mixed column,

$$
\rho_wc_wH\Delta T_w=E,\qquad \boxed{\Delta T_w\simeq1.50\,\mathrm K,\quad T_{w,\mathrm{end}}=T_{w,\mathrm{start}}+1.50\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm C}.
$$

If the water initially lies near the seawater freezing point, about $-1.8\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm C$, its final temperature is approximately $\boxed{-0.3\,{}^{\circ}\mathrm C}$. An absolute final temperature requires that additional initial condition; the energy calculation itself determines only the temperature increase. The given ice [specific heat capacity](../../../../../specific-heat-capacity.md) is not the appropriate value for heating liquid seawater.
