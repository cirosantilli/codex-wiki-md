<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $h_0=h(0)$ and $S=\pi R^2$. The closed tank has constant mass, so

$$
S\{\rho_u h+\rho_l(H-h)\}
=S\{\rho_u(0)h_0+\rho_l(H-h_0)\}.
$$

Therefore $(\rho_l-\rho_u)h=(\rho_l-\rho_u(0))h_0$. Under the [Boussinesq approximation](../../../../../../boussinesq-approximation.md), define $g'=g(\rho_l-\rho_u)/\rho_l$ and obtain

$$
\boxed{B=g'h=g'_0h_0=\text{constant}.}
$$

The gravitational [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md) is especially easy to evaluate with the stipulated downward depth coordinate: the gravitational potential per unit mass is $-gz$. Hence

$$
E_P=-gS\left\{\rho_u\frac{h^2}2+\rho_l\frac{H^2-h^2}2\right\}
=-\frac{g\rho_lSH^2}2+\frac{\rho_lSBh}2.
$$

This proves the [potential energy of two-layer entrainment](../../../../../../potential-energy-of-two-layer-entrainment.md) relation

$$
\boxed{\dot E_P=\frac12\rho_lSB\dot h.}
$$

The positive sign expresses the lifting and homogenization of dense entrained fluid within the upper layer. The fixed thin interface is neglected in this leading two-layer energy integral.
