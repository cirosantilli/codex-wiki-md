↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Yes, at the weak-field accuracy of the experiment.** The [Pound-Rebka experiment](../../../../../../pound-rebka-experiment.md) measures [gravitational redshift](../../../../../../gravitational-redshift.md), which [Nordstrom theory of gravitation](../../../../../../nordstrom-s-theory-of-gravitation.md) predicts even though it does not predict bending of light.

In a static field, a stationary clock has $d\tau=\phi\,dt$. A [photon](../../../../../../photon.md) has constant [Killing energy](../../../../../../killing-energy.md) $E=-p_t$, and an observer at rest has $U=\phi^{-1}\partial_t$. Its measured frequency is proportional to $-p_iU^i=E/\phi$. Emission at $e$ and reception at $r$ therefore give

$$
\boxed{\frac{\nu_r}{\nu_e}=\frac{\phi_e}{\phi_r}
=1+\Phi_e-\Phi_r+O(\Phi^2).}
$$

An upward ray has $\Phi_r-\Phi_e\simeq gH/c^2$ when ordinary units are restored, so $\Delta\nu/\nu\simeq-gH/c^2$. This is the shift tested using the [Mössbauer effect](../../../../../../mossbauer-effect.md). The [gravitational redshift](../../../../../../gravitational-redshift.md) follows from the physical clock rates; a claim that straight light paths imply no frequency shift would confuse two different observables.
