<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) map acts on $H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z)$ by

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is one, so it is an orientation-preserving [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) and acts as the identity on both $H_0(T^2)$ and $H_2(T^2)$.

To calculate the [homology](../../../../../../homology-split.md) of its [mapping torus](../../../../../../mapping-torus.md), cover the time [circle](../../../../../../circle.md) by two arcs. Each inverse image has the [homotopy type](../../../../../../homotopy-type.md) of $T^2$, while their intersection has two components of that [homotopy type](../../../../../../homotopy-type.md). The map in the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](../../../../../../mayer-vietoris-sequence.md) can be written

$$
H_q(T^2)\oplus H_q(T^2)\longrightarrow H_q(T^2)\oplus H_q(T^2),
\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto(a+f_*b,-a-b).
$$

Adding the output coordinates and using the input coordinate $a+b$ separates an identity map from $f_*-I$. Canceling the identity summands gives the [Wang sequence](../../../../../../wang-sequence.md), and hence

$$
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(f_*-I\mid H_q(T^2))
\longrightarrow H_q(X)
\longrightarrow\ker(f_*-I\mid H_{q-1}(T^2))\longrightarrow0.
$$

In degree one,

$$
A-I=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its [image](../../../../../../image-of-a-function.md) and [kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) are both generated by $(1,-1)$; its [cokernel](../../../../../../cokernel.md) is $\mathbb Z$ since this image is primitive. Its [Smith normal form](../../../../../../smith-normal-form.md) is $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, so its [torsion subgroup](../../../../../../torsion-subgroup.md) is zero. On the other two nonzero [homology groups](../../../../../../homology-group.md) of the [torus](../../../../../../torus.md), $f_*-I=0$. The displayed [short exact sequences](../../../../../../short-exact-sequence.md) split because their right-hand groups are free. Therefore

$$
\boxed{H_j(X;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,3,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&j=1,2,\\
0,&j>3.
\end{cases}}
$$

In particular, the [mapping torus](../../../../../../mapping-torus.md) contributes an additional degree-one class from the time [circle](../../../../../../circle.md), as well as the invariant degree-one class in the fiber.
