<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $L/K$ be a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md) of non-Archimedean [local fields](../../../../../../local-field.md), with [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) $G$, normalized [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) $v_L$, integer ring $\mathcal O_L$, and residue characteristic $p$. The [lower ramification numbering](../../../../../../lower-ramification-numbering.md) is

$$
G_{-1}=G,\qquad
G_i=\{\sigma\in G:v_L(\sigma a-a)\ge i+1
\text{ for every }a\in\mathcal O_L\},\quad i\ge0.
$$

These [higher ramification groups](../../../../../../ramification-group.md) measure successively finer agreement with the identity. The [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $G_0$ is the [inertia group](../../../../../../inertia-group.md), the kernel of the action on the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md). Each $G_i$ is normal in $G$: conjugation permutes integral elements and preserves their [valuations](../../../../../../valuation.md). The [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) eventually become trivial, since each nonidentity automorphism moves some integral element and its displacement has finite [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md).

Let $K_0=L^{G_0}$, the [maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension](../../../../../../maximal-unramified-subextension-of-a-local-field-extension.md). Then $G/G_0$ is the [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) of the residue extension, cyclic and generated by a [Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../../frobenius-automorphism.md). For a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $\pi$ of $L$, the [uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups](../../../../../../uniformizer-criterion-for-lower-ramification-groups.md) gives

$$
G_i=\{\sigma\in G_0:v_L(\sigma\pi-\pi)\ge i+1\}.
$$

To see why one [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) suffices, the extension $L/K_0$ is totally ramified and its integer ring is $\mathcal O_{K_0}[\pi]$. Elements of $G_0$ fix its [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md). Differences of powers of $\pi$ are divisible by $\sigma\pi-\pi$, so the [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) bound for $\pi$ implies it for every integral element. The converse follows by testing $\pi$ itself.

The [tame inertia quotient](../../../../../../tame-inertia-quotient.md) is seen explicitly from

$$
G_0\longrightarrow k_L^\times,\qquad
\sigma\longmapsto\overline{\sigma\pi/\pi}.
$$

It is a homomorphism because inertia acts trivially on the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md), and its kernel is $G_1$. Thus $G_0/G_1$ is cyclic of order not divisible by $p$. For $i\ge1$, the homomorphism

$$
G_i\longrightarrow(k_L,+),\qquad
\sigma\longmapsto
\overline{\frac{\sigma\pi-\pi}{\pi^{i+1}}}
$$

has kernel $G_{i+1}$. Indeed composing two automorphisms adds the leading displacements, since $\sigma\pi/\pi\equiv1$ and inertia fixes residue [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md). Hence each $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is an [elementary abelian p-group](../../../../../../elementary-abelian-group.md). Eventual triviality makes $G_1$ a [p-group](../../../../../../p-group.md); the prime-to-p quotient makes it the unique [Sylow subgroup](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) of $G_0$. This is the [wild inertia group](../../../../../../wild-inertia-group.md). [Tame ramification](../../../../../../tamely-ramified-extension.md) means $G_1=1$.

The filtration also measures the [different exponent](../../../../../../different-exponent.md). For the [totally ramified extension](../../../../../../totally-ramified-extension.md) over $K_0$, the [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) $g$ of $\pi$ has [polynomial roots](../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) $\sigma\pi$, $\sigma\in G_0$. The derivative formula gives

$$
v_L(g'(\pi))=\sum_{\sigma\in G_0\setminus\{1\}}
v_L(\pi-\sigma\pi).
$$

For a fixed $\sigma$, its displacement [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) is the number of indices $i\ge0$ for which it belongs to $G_i$. Interchanging the finite sums therefore gives [different exponent from ramification groups](../../../../../../different-exponent-from-ramification-groups.md):

$$
\boxed{\delta(L/K)=\sum_{i\ge0}(|G_i|-1).}
$$

The unramified part contributes zero. More generally [different in a tower](../../../../../../different-in-a-tower.md) follows by taking products of [bases of a module](../../../../../../basis-of-a-module.md) dual for the [trace pairing](../../../../../../trace-pairing.md), and gives

$$
\delta(L/K)=\delta(L/E)+e(L/E)\delta(E/K).
$$

Thus a tame extension has [different exponent](../../../../../../different-exponent.md) $e-1$, while nontrivial wild [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) contribute additional positive terms.

The lower numbering restricts to [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md): if $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$, its [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) are $H_i=H\cap G_i$, directly from the definition using the same top-field [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md). Quotients need a different indexing. For real $s\ge0$ put $G_s=G_{\lceil s\rceil}$ and define the [Herbrand function](../../../../../../herbrand-function.md)

$$
\varphi(s)=\int_0^s\frac{dt}{[G_0:G_t]},\qquad
\psi=\varphi^{-1},\qquad G^u=G_{\psi(u)}.
$$

This [upper ramification numbering](../../../../../../upper-ramification-numbering.md) stretches or compresses intervals according to the inertia indices. The quotient theorem says that for a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $H$, [upper ramification groups commute with quotients](../../../../../../upper-ramification-groups-commute-with-quotients.md):

$$
(G/H)^u=G^uH/H.
$$

In particular the same upper index has a meaning compatible with passing to a smaller [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md), which lower indices generally lack.

For a tame [totally ramified extension](../../../../../../totally-ramified-extension.md) the picture is simple: $G_0=G$, all $G_i$ with $i\ge1$ are trivial, and the [different exponent](../../../../../../different-exponent.md) is $|G|-1$. The extension $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p)$ for odd $p$ illustrates this, giving exponent $p-2$.

A mixed-characteristic wild family is the [lower ramification filtration of a prime-power cyclotomic extension](../../../../../../lower-ramification-filtration-of-a-prime-power-cyclotomic-extension.md). Take $L=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^r})$ with odd $p$ and $\pi=\zeta_{p^r}-1$. If $\sigma_a(\zeta)=\zeta^a$ and $a\ne1$ modulo $p^r$, then

$$
v_L(\sigma_a\pi-\pi)
=v_L(\zeta^{a-1}-1)=p^{v_p(a-1)}.
$$

The last equality follows because $\zeta^{a-1}$ has order $p^{r-v_p(a-1)}$ and its [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) scales by the [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) in that cyclotomic tower. The lower breaks are $0,p-1,p^2-1,\ldots,p^{r-1}-1$. Integrating the successive indices $(p-1),p(p-1),\ldots$ gives upper breaks $0,1,\ldots,r-1$.

In equal characteristic there is an equally explicit wild example. Start with $K=\mathbb F_q((t))$ and an [Artin–Schreier extension](../../../../../../artin-schreier-extension.md)

$$
y^p-y=t^{-m},\qquad m>0,\quad p\nmid m.
$$

There is no [polynomial root](../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) in $K$: a negative-valuation element has $v(x^p-x)=p\,v(x)$, while the right side has [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) $-m$. Adjoining a [polynomial root](../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) splits the [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), whose [polynomial roots](../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $y+c$ for $c\in\mathbb F_p$, so the extension is cyclic of degree $p$. The [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) equation forces total ramification, $v_L(t)=p$ and $v_L(y)=-m$. Choose $1\le b\le p-1$ with $-bm\equiv1\pmod p$ and set $a=(1+bm)/p$. Then $\pi=t^a y^b$ is a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md). For a nonidentity automorphism $y\mapsto y+c$,

$$
\frac{\sigma\pi}{\pi}=(1+c/y)^b,\qquad
v_L(\sigma\pi-\pi)=1+m;
$$

the first [binomial coefficient](../../../../../../binomial-coefficient.md) $b$ is nonzero modulo $p$, and all subsequent terms have strictly higher [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md). Therefore

$$
G_0=\cdots=G_m=C_p,\qquad G_{m+1}=1,\qquad
\delta=(m+1)(p-1).
$$

This is the [ramification break of an Artin–Schreier pole](../../../../../../ramification-break-of-an-artin-schreier-pole.md). It shows concretely that wild breaks, unlike tame inertia, can occur arbitrarily far out in the filtration.

**The lower [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) measure integral displacements, the upper [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) make quotient comparison possible, and the sizes of the [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) determine the different.**
