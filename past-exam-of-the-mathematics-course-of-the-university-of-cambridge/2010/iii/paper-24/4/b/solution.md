<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For the finite [separable field extension](../../../../../../separable-extension.md) define the [inverse different](../../../../../../inverse-different.md), or [codifferent](../../../../../../inverse-different.md), by

$$
\mathfrak D_{L/K}^{-1}
=\{x\in L:\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x\mathcal O_L)
\subseteq\mathcal O_K\}.
$$

It is a [fractional ideal](../../../../../../fractional-ideal.md) of $\mathcal O_L$. The [different ideal](../../../../../../different-ideal.md) is its inverse [fractional ideal](../../../../../../fractional-ideal.md).

Suppose the integer ring is generated by $\alpha$, with monic [minimal polynomial of an algebraic element](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial-of-an-algebraic-element.md) $g$ of degree $d$. We prove the derivative formula using the [coefficient pairing for a monogenic algebra](../../../../../../coefficient-pairing-for-a-monogenic-algebra.md). If $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d$ are its distinct conjugate [polynomial roots](../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), [Lagrange interpolation](../../../../../../lagrange-polynomial.md) for a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $h$ of degree less than $d$ gives

$$
h(T)=\sum_{i=1}^d h(\alpha_i)
\frac{g(T)}{(T-\alpha_i)g'(\alpha_i)}.
$$

Comparing [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md) of $T^{d-1}$ yields

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}\!\left(\frac{h(\alpha)}{g'(\alpha)}\right)
=[T^{d-1}]h(T).
$$

For any integral [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md), first reduce modulo $g$; the [coefficients](../../../../../../coefficient.md) stay integral because $g$ is monic over $\mathcal O_K$.

It follows that $g'(\alpha)^{-1}\mathcal O_L$ lies in the [codifferent](../../../../../../inverse-different.md). To prove equality, consider the [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md)

$$
A_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}
\left(\frac{\alpha^{i+j}}{g'(\alpha)}\right),
\qquad 0\le i,j\le d-1.
$$

All entries are integral. Entries with $i+j<d-1$ are zero, and those with $i+j=d-1$ are one. Reversing the column order makes the [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) triangular with diagonal ones; thus its [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) is a [unit](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) of $\mathcal O_K$.

Write an arbitrary $x\in L$ as $x=g'(\alpha)^{-1}\sum_j b_j\alpha^j$ with $b_j\in K$. Membership in the [codifferent](../../../../../../inverse-different.md) says $A(b_j)_j\in\mathcal O_K^d$. Since $A$ is invertible over $\mathcal O_K$, this forces every $b_j$ integral. Therefore

$$
\mathfrak D_{L/K}^{-1}=g'(\alpha)^{-1}\mathcal O_L,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathfrak D_{L/K}=(g'(\alpha)).}
$$

Separability ensures that $g'(\alpha)\ne0$; the [trace pairing](../../../../../../trace-pairing.md) calculation also proves that no extra factor has been overlooked.
