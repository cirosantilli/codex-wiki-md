<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Put $f=[k_L:k]$. For an [unramified extension](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) the [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) is one and $[L:K]=f$. The residue $\bar\alpha$ has degree $f$ over $k$. Thus $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{f-1}$ are linearly independent over $K$: a nonzero [linear dependence](../../../../../../linear-dependence.md) can be divided by a [coefficient](../../../../../../coefficient.md) of least [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) and reduced to a nonzero relation among the reduced powers. Hence $[K(\alpha):K]\ge f=[L:K]$, and $\alpha$ generates $L$.

The same argument gives the integral statement, which is stronger than merely generating the [field](../../../../../../field.md). Write

$$
x=\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}c_j\alpha^j,\qquad c_j\in K.
$$

If $m=\min_j v_K(c_j)$, divide by $\pi_K^m$. Since $\pi_K$ remains a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) in the [unramified extension](../../../../../../unramified-extension.md) and the reduced powers form a [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of $k_L/k$, the resulting sum has nonzero residue. Therefore $v_L(x)=m$. In particular $x$ is integral if and only if every [coefficient](../../../../../../coefficient.md) is integral. This proves [unramified residue generator integral basis](../../../../../../unramified-residue-generator-integral-basis.md):

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_L=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{f-1}\mathcal O_K\alpha^j
=\mathcal O_K[\alpha].}
$$
