**False.** Use the [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $X(X^2-p)$, with [splitting field](../../../../../../../splitting-field.md) $K=\mathbb Q_p(\theta)$, $\theta^2=p$, and label the [polynomial roots](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) $0,\theta,-\theta$. Take $\beta=p\theta$. Then

$$
|\beta|=p^{-3/2},\qquad
|\beta-\theta|=|(p-1)\theta|=p^{-1/2},\qquad
|\beta+\theta|=|(p+1)\theta|=p^{-1/2}.
$$

Both $p-1$ and $p+1$ are [p-adic units](../../../../../../../p-adic-unit.md), also when $p=2$. Hence zero is the uniquely closest [polynomial root](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), but $\beta\notin\mathbb Q_p(0)=\mathbb Q_p$, because $\theta\notin\mathbb Q_p$. The [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) need not be irreducible in the given setup. The inclusion established by [Krasner's lemma](../../../../../../../krasner-s-lemma.md) goes in the direction in part (iii), not its converse.
