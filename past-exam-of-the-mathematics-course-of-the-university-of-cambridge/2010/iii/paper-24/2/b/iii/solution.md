<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**True.** Put $H=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q_p(\beta))$. Every $\sigma\in H$ fixes $\beta$, permutes the [polynomial](../../../../../../../polynomial-split.md)'s [polynomial roots](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), and is an isometry by the uniqueness proved in part (a). Therefore

$$
|\beta-\sigma(\alpha_1)|
=|\sigma(\beta-\alpha_1)|
=|\beta-\alpha_1|.
$$

The strict inequalities single out $\alpha_1$ as the unique closest [polynomial root](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md), so $\sigma(\alpha_1)=\alpha_1$ for every $\sigma\in H$. The [Fundamental theorem of Galois theory](../../../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-galois-theory.md) then gives

$$
\boxed{\alpha_1\in K^H=\mathbb Q_p(\beta).}
$$

This [closest-root descent in a Galois extension](../../../../../../../closest-root-descent-in-a-galois-extension.md) is the relevant form of [Krasner's lemma](../../../../../../../krasner-s-lemma.md). If [polynomial roots](../../../../../../../root-of-a-polynomial.md) repeat, the strict hypothesis excludes a second occurrence of $\alpha_1$; otherwise the same argument applies unchanged.
