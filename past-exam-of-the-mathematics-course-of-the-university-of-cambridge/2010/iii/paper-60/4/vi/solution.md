<h1 id="4/vi/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Vi](../vi.md)

In the radial-residual convention $\delta r=a_4\cos4\theta$, **boxy isophotes have $a_4<0$ and disky isophotes have $a_4>0$**, since negative $a_4$ extends the diagonal directions and positive $a_4$ extends the fitted axes. Massive [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) tend to have slowly rotating, anisotropic stellar structures associated with [dry galaxy mergers](../../../../../../dry-galaxy-merger.md), whereas rotating embedded [galactic disks](../../../../../../galactic-disk.md), often associated with dissipation in [gas-rich galaxy mergers](../../../../../../gas-rich-galaxy-merger.md), favour disky structure in lower-mass [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md). These are population trends, as in [Central structure measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9610055), rather than a universal mass rule, and brightness-residual Fourier coefficients must first be converted to this radial convention.
