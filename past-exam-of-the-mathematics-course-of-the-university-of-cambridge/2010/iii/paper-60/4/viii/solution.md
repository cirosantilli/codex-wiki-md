<h1 id="4/viii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Viii](../viii.md)

Many lower-luminosity [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md) have dense central stellar cusps, giving far more photons and kinematic tracers near a central [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) than the shallow depleted cores common in massive [elliptical galaxies](../../../../../../elliptical-galaxy.md), as measured in [Central galaxy profiles](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9610055). Thus **central [surface brightness](../../../../../../surface-brightness.md) can make the smaller black hole easier to measure**, despite its smaller mass, whereas sparse central tracers and uncertain stellar orbital anisotropy weaken measurements in a large core. This is not a universal mass ordering: the [black-hole sphere of influence](../../../../../../black-hole-sphere-of-influence.md) $r_{\rm inf}\simeq GM_\bullet/\sigma^2$ must still be sufficiently resolved in angle, so distance, [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) and [angular resolution](../../../../../../angular-resolution.md) also matter.
