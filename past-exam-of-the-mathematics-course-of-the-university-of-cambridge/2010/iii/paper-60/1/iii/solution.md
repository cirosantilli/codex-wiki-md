<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The thin-sheet field used above requires the gas to sample heights beyond most of the stellar mass, while the stellar [galactic disk](../../../../../../galactic-disk.md) still dominates vertical gravity and is approximately locally plane-parallel. In inner regions a cold gas layer can lie inside the thicker stellar layer, where reflection symmetry gives $K_z\simeq\nu_z^2z$ rather than a constant field. The [vertical Jeans equation](../../../../../../vertical-jeans-equation.md), or gas [hydrostatic equilibrium](../../../../../../hydrostatic-equilibrium.md), then gives

$$
\rho_g(z)=\rho_g(0)\exp\left(-\frac{\nu_z^2z^2}{2\sigma_g^2}\right),\qquad H_g=\frac{\sigma_g}{\nu_z}.
$$

Here $H_g$ is the Gaussian width and $\nu_z$ is the [vertical epicyclic frequency](../../../../../../vertical-epicyclic-frequency.md); in a locally flat [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md), $\nu_z^2\simeq4\pi G\rho_{\rm total}(R,0)$. A stellar layer of approximately fixed thickness then gives $H_g\propto\Sigma_*^{-1/2}$, rather than the inverse [surface density](../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) law.

At large radii, the [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) or the gas's own gravity can exceed the dwindling stellar restoring force, so replacing the total force by $2\pi G\Sigma_*$ fails. A nearly spherical [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) instead gives $\nu_z\simeq v_c/R$ near its plane, and $H_g\simeq\sigma_gR/v_c$ for a flat [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md), much slower than exponential [galactic disk flaring](../../../../../../galactic-disk-flaring.md). Warps, radial gradients across a thick gas layer, non-equilibrium disturbances, and height-dependent [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) also invalidate the locally plane-parallel steady isothermal approximation. **The inverse stellar surface-density law applies only while its force and geometric assumptions apply.**
