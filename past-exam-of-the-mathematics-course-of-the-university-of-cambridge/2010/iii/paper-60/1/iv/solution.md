<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Measure the number-density profile $n(z)$ and vertical [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) $\sigma_z(z)$ of a tracer [stellar population](../../../../../../stellar-population.md). The [vertical Jeans equation](../../../../../../vertical-jeans-equation.md) estimates the restoring field from

$$
K_z=-\frac1n\frac{\partial(n\sigma_z^2)}{\partial z}
-\frac1{Rn}\frac{\partial(Rn\overline{v_Rv_z})}{\partial R}.
$$

The second term accounts for the tilt of the [velocity ellipsoid](../../../../../../velocity-ellipsoid.md); an exponential tracer with constant [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) and negligible tilt gives $K_z=\sigma_z^2/h$ above the plane. The tracer need not supply all the gravitating mass. To infer the [dynamical surface density from vertical stellar motions](../../../../../../dynamical-surface-density-from-vertical-stellar-motions.md), integrate the axisymmetric [Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity](../../../../../../poisson-equation-for-newtonian-gravity.md):

$$
\Sigma_{\rm dyn}(<|z|)=\frac1{2\pi G}\left[K_z(z)+\int_0^z\frac1R\partial_R(R\partial_R\Phi)\,dz'\right],\qquad z>0.
$$

For a nearly flat [galaxy rotation curve](../../../../../../galaxy-rotation-curve.md) with a small radial correction, this reduces to $K_z/(2\pi G)$. Estimate stellar [surface density](../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) independently from counts, the [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md) and faint objects, and add the gas [surface density](../../../../../../surface-density-of-a-disk.md) and [stellar remnants](../../../../../../stellar-remnant.md). The residual

$$
\Sigma_{\rm excess}=\Sigma_{\rm dyn}-\Sigma_{\rm stars}-\Sigma_{\rm gas}
$$

limits the allowed unseen material within the same height interval. A fitted [dark matter halo](../../../../../../dark-matter-halo.md) contributes too: measurements at several heights help distinguish its extended density from a thin dark component in the [galactic disk](../../../../../../galactic-disk.md). Under a specified decomposition, **the disc dark-matter fraction is the inferred dark-disc surface density divided by the total disc surface density**, with uncertainty or an upper limit if the residual is consistent with zero. Selection effects, disequilibrium, the tilt term and uncertain baryonic counts must enter the error budget; an excess alone does not establish that all of it is in the [galactic disk](../../../../../../galactic-disk.md).
