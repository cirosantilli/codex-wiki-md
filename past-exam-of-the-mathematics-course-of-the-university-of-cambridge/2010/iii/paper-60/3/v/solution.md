<h1 id="3/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The assumed central [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) mass is $M_\bullet\sim10^8M_\odot\simeq2\times10^{38}\,\mathrm{kg}$. A mass alone does not fix radiated energy: specify a formation mechanism and its [radiative efficiency of black-hole accretion](../../../../../../radiative-efficiency-of-black-hole-accretion.md). For radiatively efficient accretion, if a fraction $\epsilon$ of the supplied rest-mass energy escapes and the remainder increases the [black hole](../../../../../../black-hole.md) mass, then $M_\bullet=(1-\epsilon)M_{\rm supplied}$ and

$$
\boxed{E_\bullet=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}M_\bullet c^2,qquad
\langle L_\bullet\rangle=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\frac{M_\bullet c^2}{t_{\rm dyn}}}.
$$

Taking the customary order-of-magnitude efficiency $\epsilon\sim0.1$ gives

$$
E_\bullet\simeq2\times10^{54}\,\mathrm J,qquad
\langle L_\bullet\rangle\simeq10^{39}\,\mathrm W\simeq3\times10^{12}L_\odot.
$$

**The coeval accretion phase would be a quasar-scale source, hundreds of times brighter than a typical present $L_*$ galaxy.** Compared directly with the preceding gravitational-collapse estimate,

$$
\frac{E_\bullet}{E_{\rm rad}}\simeq\frac23\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}
\frac{M_\bullet}{M}\left(\frac c\sigma\right)^2\sim10^2.
$$

Despite its small mass fraction, [black-hole accretion](../../../../../../black-hole-accretion.md) extracts a fraction of rest-mass energy, while galactic collapse releases only an energy scale $M\sigma^2\ll Mc^2$. The numerical [luminosity](../../../../../../luminosity.md) is conditional: a [radiatively inefficient accretion flow](../../../../../../radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow.md), direct collapse or assembly by mergers need not radiate this amount over the galactic clock, and the question supplies no unique efficiency.
