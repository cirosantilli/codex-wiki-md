<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Putting the whole assumed gas reservoir into [stars](../../../../../../star.md) during the nominal [galactic dynamical time](../../../../../../galactic-dynamical-time.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\langle\dot M_*\rangle\simeq\frac{10^{11}M_\odot}{6\times10^7\,\mathrm{yr}}\simeq2\times10^3\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}}.
$$

An order-unity shorter collapse clock would raise this to a few thousand [solar masses](../../../../../../solar-mass.md) per year. The [Milky Way](../../../../../../milky-way.md)'s present [star formation rate](../../../../../../star-formation-rate.md) is only of order a few [solar masses](../../../../../../solar-mass.md) per year; for example, [A determination combining Galactic measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1078) finds about $1.7\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ with a specified [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md). **The extreme rapid-collapse rate is therefore roughly three orders of magnitude larger**, resembling an intense short-lived [starburst galaxy](../../../../../../starburst-galaxy.md) phase rather than ongoing ordinary disc [star formation](../../../../../../star-formation.md). This estimate assumes all the quoted mass can form [stars](../../../../../../star.md); it would be smaller if some mass were nonbaryonic [dark matter](../../../../../../dark-matter.md) or if the conversion efficiency were below unity.
