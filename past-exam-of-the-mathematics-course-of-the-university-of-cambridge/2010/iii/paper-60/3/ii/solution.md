↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Using the given constants, the mass is $2\times10^{41}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the [velocity dispersion](../../../../../../velocity-dispersion.md) is $2\times10^5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The nominal [galactic dynamical time](../../../../../../galactic-dynamical-time.md) is

$$
\frac{GM}{\sigma^3}=\frac{(7\times10^{-11})(2\times10^{41})}{(2\times10^5)^3}
=1.75\times10^{15}\,\mathrm s\simeq6\times10^7\,\mathrm{yr}.
$$

Thus **the collapse clock is a few tens of millions of years, of order $10^8$ years**, with $R/\sigma\simeq3\times10^7$ years in the interior [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md) convention. The nominal radius $GM/\sigma^2\simeq3.5\times10^{20}\,\mathrm m\simeq12\,\mathrm{kpc}$ is a plausible galactic scale. A full stellar orbit takes several crossing times, giving order $10^8$ years near a galactic disc radius, whereas the age of the [Milky Way](../../../../../../milky-way.md) is of order $10^{10}$ years. Many orbital passages therefore fit into the lifetime of the [galaxy](../../../../../../galaxy-split.md), as expected; a [galactic dynamical time](../../../../../../galactic-dynamical-time.md) need not equal either its full orbital period or its age.
