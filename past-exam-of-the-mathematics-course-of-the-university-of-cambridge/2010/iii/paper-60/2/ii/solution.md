<h1 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $\nu=1/2$, the [gas-phase metallicity](../../../../../../gas-phase-metallicity.md) relation reduces to $Z=2y(1-M/M_0)$. Elimination of $M$ from [mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) gives

$$
M=M_0\left(1-\frac Z{2y}\right),\qquad
S=2(M_0-M)=\frac{M_0Z}{y}.
$$

With a fixed [initial mass function](../../../../../../initial-mass-function.md), let $\kappa$ be the number of surviving low-mass tracer [stars](../../../../../../star.md) per unit formed stellar mass. Successive [stars](../../../../../../star.md) inherit the monotonically increasing [gas-phase metallicity](../../../../../../gas-phase-metallicity.md), so the cumulative and differential counts are respectively

$$
\boxed{N(<Z)=\frac{\kappa M_0}{y}Z,\qquad \frac{dN}{dZ}=\frac{\kappa M_0}{y}},\qquad 0<Z<Z_f,
$$

where $Z_f=2y(1-M_f/M_0)$ is the abundance at the epoch when star formation stops. Above $Z_f$ the cumulative count is constant, and the differential count vanishes. In the complete gas-exhaustion limit, $Z_f\to2y$ and $N_{\rm total}=2\kappa M_0$, so the normalized [metallicity distribution function](../../../../../../metallicity-distribution-function.md) is $p_Z=1/(2y)$ on $0<Z<2y$; at a finite stopping epoch it is $1/Z_f$. **The distribution is flat in linear metallicity.** A [logarithmic metallicity distribution](../../../../../../logarithmic-metallicity-distribution.md) instead satisfies $dN/d\log_{10}Z=(\ln10)Z\,dN/dZ$ and is not flat.
