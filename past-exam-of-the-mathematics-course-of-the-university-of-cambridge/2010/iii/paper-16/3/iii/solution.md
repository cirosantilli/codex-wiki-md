<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Work on an [affine open subset](../../../../../../affine-open-subscheme.md) $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and write $\mathcal F|_U=\widetilde M$ and $\mathcal E|_U=\widetilde N$ using part (ii). We first show that any section $e\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal E)$ lifts to $\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$.

A surjection of [sheaves](../../../../../../sheaf-mathematics.md) supplies local lifts $g_i$ on a finite cover by [principal open subsets](../../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) $D(s_i)$. Their differences

$$
c_{ij}=g_i-g_j\in M_{s_is_j}
$$

satisfy $c_{ij}+c_{jk}=c_{ik}$ on triple intersections. We spell out why this [Čech cocycle](../../../../../../cech-cocycle-condition.md) is a [coboundary](../../../../../../coboundary.md). Choose a common sufficiently large power $n$ so that $s_j^nc_{ij}$ can be represented in $M_{s_i}$ for every pair. Increasing $n$ also kills the finitely many [localization](../../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) errors in the cocycle identities: after extending $s_k^nc_{ik}$ to $M_{s_i}$ and $s_k^nc_{jk}$ to $M_{s_j}$, their difference in $M_{s_is_j}$ is $s_k^nc_{ij}$. This follows because any error which vanishes after localizing at $s_k$ is killed by a power of $s_k$; there are only finitely many indices.

The $D(s_i^n)$ still cover $U$, so the [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) generated by the $s_i^n$ is $A$. Choose $a_i\in A$ with $\sum_i a_is_i^n=1$ and put

$$
b_i=\sum_k a_ks_k^nc_{ik}\in M_{s_i},
$$

using the extended representatives just chosen. On an overlap, the cocycle identity gives

$$
b_i-b_j=\sum_k a_ks_k^nc_{ij}=c_{ij}.
$$

Thus $g_i-b_i$ agree and glue to a lift of $e$. This is the degree-one argument behind the [exactness of the unit-ideal localization Čech complex](../../../../../../exactness-of-the-unit-ideal-localization-cech-complex.md).

The same reasoning applies to every $D(a)\subseteq U$. Hence we have short exact sequences of [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) of sections on $U$ and on $D(a)$. Localize the first sequence at $a$ and compare them. The maps on the outer [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) are the isomorphisms from part (ii):

$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
0&\to&\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)_a&\to&\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)_a&\to&\Gamma(U,\mathcal E)_a&\to&0\\
&&\downarrow\simeq&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\simeq\\
0&\to&\Gamma(D(a),\mathcal F)&\to&\Gamma(D(a),\mathcal G)&\to&\Gamma(D(a),\mathcal E)&\to&0.
\end{array}
$$

An elementary diagram chase makes the middle map an isomorphism: subtract a lift from the right to reduce surjectivity to the left, and use injectivity on the left to prove injectivity. These isomorphisms identify $\mathcal G|_U$ with the [quasi-coherent sheaf](../../../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md) associated with $\Gamma(U,\mathcal G)$. Doing this on every [affine open subset](../../../../../../affine-open-subscheme.md) proves

$$
\boxed{\mathcal G\text{ is quasi-coherent}.}
$$

The proof even works without the Noetherian assumption.
