<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

As a rough rare-event calculation, divide the observed elective-group event count by the reported adjusted [odds ratio](../../../../../../odds-ratio.md):

$$
\boxed{9/2.7\simeq3.3\text{ events}.}
$$

For a homogeneous illustrative risk, the exact odds conversion starts from $p_1=9/1515$ and gives

$$
p_0=\frac{p_1}{2.7(1-p_1)+p_1},\qquad1515p_0\simeq3.35.
$$

The corresponding baseline risk is about $0.22\%$, compared with $1.62\%$ in the observed reference group, roughly seven times lower. Thus **taking the adjusted result literally implies exceptionally low background risk among the elective patients**.

This is a diagnostic approximation, not an identified mean of [potential outcomes](../../../../../../potential-outcome.md) obtainable from the aggregate table. A conditional [odds ratio](../../../../../../odds-ratio.md) cannot generally be inverted at an aggregate mean: individual background risks are needed for [outcome standardization](../../../../../../outcome-standardization.md). Interpreting the calculation causally also requires [conditional exchangeability](../../../../../../conditional-exchangeability.md), the [positivity assumption](../../../../../../positivity-assumption.md) and a correctly specified outcome model. At very small risks the rare-event approximation makes the estimate of roughly three events useful for checking the plausibility of the adjustment.
