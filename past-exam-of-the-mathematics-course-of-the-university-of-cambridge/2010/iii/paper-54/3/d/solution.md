<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Away from a bifurcation set, the normal to a [Killing horizon](../../../../../../killing-horizon.md) is its nonzero [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) $K$. Any affine tangent to the same generators has the form $k=hK$. In the [optical tensor](../../../../../../optical-tensor.md), derivatives of $h$ contribute a factor of $K_a$ and disappear under the [screen-space projector](../../../../../../screen-space-projector.md). Consequently

$$
B_{(ab)}=h\,q_a{}^cq_b{}^d\nabla_{(d}K_{c)}=0
$$

by the [Killing equation](../../../../../../killing-equation.md). The trace and trace-free symmetric part are therefore zero: $\theta=0$ and $\sigma_{ab}=0$.

For the antisymmetric part, locally the horizon normal is proportional to a gradient. The restriction of its dual one-form to every tangent direction on the horizon is zero. Taking its [exterior derivative](../../../../../../exterior-derivative.md) and evaluating on two screen tangents therefore gives zero as well; equivalently this is the hypersurface-orthogonality consequence of the [Frobenius theorem](../../../../../../frobenius-theorem.md). Since both screen indices are tangential, $B_{[ab]}=0$. Thus the [optical scalars on a Killing horizon](../../../../../../optical-scalars-on-a-killing-horizon.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{\theta=0,\qquad\sigma_{ab}=0,\qquad\omega_{ab}=0.}
$$

No affine normalization of the [Killing vector field](../../../../../../killing-vector-field.md) itself was assumed. At a regular [bifurcation surface](../../../../../../bifurcation-surface.md), $K$ vanishes but the smooth null generators do not; the conclusions for their optical quantities follow by continuity along each horizon branch.
