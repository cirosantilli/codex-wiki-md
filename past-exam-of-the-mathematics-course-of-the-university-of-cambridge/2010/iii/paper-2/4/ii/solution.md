<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Take the [Kronecker quiver](../../../../../../kronecker-quiver.md), with two arrows $\alpha,\beta:1\to2$. There are no paths of length two, so its [path algebra](../../../../../../path-algebra.md) has basis $e_1,e_2,\alpha,\beta$ and dimension four.

For each $\lambda\in k$, take a [quiver representation](../../../../../../representation-of-a-quiver.md) with one-dimensional spaces at both vertices and arrow maps $(1,\lambda)$. An endomorphism consists of scalars $a,b$ with $b=a$ from the first arrow; the second imposes no further condition. Its [endomorphism ring](../../../../../../endomorphism-ring.md) is therefore $k$, which has no nontrivial [idempotent](../../../../../../idempotent.md). Thus the representation is indecomposable. An isomorphism to the representation $(1,\mu)$ again requires $b=a\ne0$, and the second arrow then gives $\lambda=\mu$. Because an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) is infinite, this gives infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic indecomposables. Hence

$$
\boxed{\dim_k kQ_2=4,\qquad kQ_2\text{ is not of finite representation type}.}
$$
