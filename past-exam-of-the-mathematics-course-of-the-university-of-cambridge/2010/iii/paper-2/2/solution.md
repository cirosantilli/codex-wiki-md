An [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) $e$ gives a [projective module](../../../../../projective-module.md) $Ae$, since $A=Ae\oplus A(1-e)$. It is primitive when it cannot be written as a sum of two nonzero orthogonal [idempotents](../../../../../idempotent.md). This is equivalent to indecomposability of $Ae$: a projection onto a [direct summand](../../../../../direct-summand.md) is an [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) of $\operatorname{End}_A(Ae)\cong(eAe)^{\mathrm{op}}$, and conversely such an [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md) splits $Ae$. Starting with $1$, split any nonprimitive summand repeatedly. The process terminates because each splitting increases the number of nonzero vector-space summands in a finite-dimensional algebra. Thus

$$
\boxed{1=e_1+\cdots+e_m,\qquad {}_AA=\bigoplus_{\nu=1}^mAe_\nu,}
$$

with orthogonal [primitive idempotents](../../../../../primitive-idempotent.md) and indecomposable [projective modules](../../../../../projective-module.md). Each summand is generated by its [idempotent](../../../../../idempotent.md).

Let $D=\operatorname{Hom}_k(-,k)$. A [Frobenius algebra](../../../../../frobenius-algebra.md) is a finite-dimensional algebra with $A\cong D(A)$ as left [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over $A$, where $(a\phi)(x)=\phi(xa)$. Equivalently there is a [linear functional](../../../../../linear-functional.md) $\lambda:A\to k$ for which $(x,y)\mapsto\lambda(xy)$ is nondegenerate. A [symmetric algebra](../../../../../symmetric-algebra-frobenius-algebra.md) satisfies the stronger [bimodule](../../../../../bimodule.md) isomorphism $A\cong D(A)$, with $(a\phi b)(x)=\phi(bxa)$; equivalently the functional can be chosen with $\lambda(xy)=\lambda(yx)$. This is the associative-algebra meaning of symmetric, rather than the [symmetric algebra](../../../../../symmetric-algebra.md) of a [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md).

For a finite-dimensional [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md),

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Soc}M=\sum_{S\subseteq M\ \mathrm{simple}}S,\qquad
\operatorname{Rad}M=\bigcap_{N\subsetneq M\ \mathrm{maximal}}N.}
$$

The [socle](../../../../../socle-mathematics.md) is its largest semisimple [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), and the [radical of a module](../../../../../radical-of-a-module.md) gives its largest semisimple quotient. With $J=J(A)$ one has $\operatorname{Rad}M=JM$ and $\operatorname{Soc}M=\{m:Jm=0\}$: $A/J$ is semisimple, and $J$ annihilates every [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md).

Now let $P=Ae$ for a [primitive idempotent](../../../../../primitive-idempotent.md), allowing the [field](../../../../../field.md) $k$ to be nonsplit. Symmetry identifies $Ae$ with $D(eA)$ through the pairing

$$
eA\times Ae\longrightarrow k,\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto\lambda(uv).
$$

It is nondegenerate on these corners. If $v=ve\ne0$, choose $a$ with $\lambda(av)\ne0$; then $\lambda(eav)=\lambda(ave)=\lambda(av)\ne0$. The other variable is treated similarly. The pairing respects the left $A$-action, so

$$
\operatorname{Soc}(Ae)\cong\operatorname{Soc}D(eA)=D(eA/eAJ).
$$

The right-module quotient $eA/eAJ$ is simple. A [primitive idempotent](../../../../../primitive-idempotent.md) remains primitive modulo the nilpotent radical: [idempotents](../../../../../idempotent.md) lift through that radical, so a decomposition in the quotient would lift to one in $eAe$. In the [semisimple algebra](../../../../../semisimple-algebra.md) $A/J$, the left simple $Ae/JAe$ and the dual of the right simple $eA/eAJ$ belong to the same [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) block. There is exactly one left simple isomorphism class in that block, even over a nonalgebraically closed [field](../../../../../field.md). Hence the [head-socle identity for a symmetric algebra](../../../../../head-socle-identity-for-a-symmetric-algebra.md) is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Soc}P\cong P/\operatorname{Rad}P.}
$$

This is an isomorphism of left [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), and need not be canonical.

For the modular examples put $r=(123)$, $s=(12)$, so $r^3=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. The coefficient-of-identity functional on the [group algebra](../../../../../group-algebra.md) is symmetric and nondegenerate, so the identity just proved applies.

In characteristic two, the [central idempotents](../../../../../central-idempotent.md) are $b_0=1+r+r^2$ and $b_1=1-b_0$. The first block has basis $b_0,b_0s$ and is $kC_2\cong k[u]/(u^2)$. For the second, choose a primitive cube root $\omega$ and use

$$
r\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}\omega&0\\0&\omega^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad s\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The two $r$-eigenlines are interchanged by $s$, proving irreducibility. Polynomial interpolation in $r$ gives diagonal [matrix units](../../../../../matrix-unit.md), and multiplying by $s$ gives the off-diagonal units. Thus the four-dimensional block maps onto $M_2(k)$ and is isomorphic to it:

$$
\boxed{kS_3\cong k[u]/(u^2)\oplus M_2(k)\quad(\operatorname{char}k=2).}
$$

There are two [simple modules](../../../../../irreducible-module.md), the trivial one-dimensional [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $T$ and the two-dimensional [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $V$; the sign [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) equals $T$. Their [projective covers](../../../../../projective-cover.md) are $P_T=k[u]/(u^2)$, with layers $T,T$, and $P_V=V$. The regular [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is $P_T\oplus V^{\oplus2}$, and the [Cartan matrix of a group algebra](../../../../../cartan-matrix-of-a-group-algebra.md) is $\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$. Every [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a sum of $T,P_T,V$: the first block is classified by [Jordan chains](../../../../../jordan-chain.md) for a [nilpotent linear map](../../../../../nilpotent-linear-map.md) of lengths one and two, while the [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) block is semisimple. Thus there are exactly three indecomposable isomorphism classes. This describes the [2-modular blocks of S3](../../../../../2-modular-blocks-of-s3.md).

In characteristic three put $u=r-1$. The ideal $I=uA$ satisfies $I^3=0$, and $A/I\cong kC_2\cong k\oplus k$, so $J(A)=I$. The only simples are the trivial and sign [modules](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $S_+,S_-$. Put $e_\pm=(1\pm s)/2$. The projectives $P_\pm=Ae_\pm$ have dimension three. A convenient radical generator is $v=r-r^{-1}$:

$$
v^3=0,\qquad svs=-v,\qquad v=-u-u^2,\qquad v^2=u^2.
$$

The basis $e_\pm,ve_\pm,v^2e_\pm$ gives the radical layers

$$
\boxed{P_+:\ S_+,S_-,S_+;\qquad P_-:\ S_-,S_+,S_-.}
$$

Thus $A=P_+\oplus P_-$ and its [Cartan matrix of a group algebra](../../../../../cartan-matrix-of-a-group-algebra.md) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

The elements $e_-ve_+$ and $e_+ve_-$ give one arrow in each direction between the two simples. All paths of length three vanish, and the six paths of lengths zero, one and two are [linearly independent](../../../../../linear-independence.md). Hence $A$ is the [path algebra](../../../../../path-algebra.md) of that two-vertex oriented cycle modulo the ideal of paths of length three. Its connected [quiver](../../../../../quiver.md) also proves there is a single [block of S3 in characteristic three](../../../../../block-of-s3-in-characteristic-three.md).

Any [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is a $\mathbb Z/2$-graded [vector space](../../../../../vector-space-split.md) given by the $s$-eigenspaces, with $v$ interchanging the grades and $v^3=0$. Constructing [Jordan chains](../../../../../jordan-chain.md) with homogeneous bases in the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) and image filtrations and homogeneous lifts decomposes it into graded chains. Each chain has length $1,2$ or $3$ and is determined by its top sign. It is a [uniserial module](../../../../../uniserial-module.md) and hence indecomposable. Consequently there are exactly six indecomposables, the length-$\ell$ quotients $P_\pm/J^\ell P_\pm$, $\ell=1,2,3$.
