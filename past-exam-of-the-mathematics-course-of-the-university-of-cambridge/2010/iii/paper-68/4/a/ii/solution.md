<h1 id="4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $U_s=U(y_s)$ at an [inflection point](../../../../../../../inflection-point.md) supplied by the preceding identity. Its imaginary part, divided by nonzero $c_i$, gives $\int U''|\psi|^2/|U-c|^2dy=0$. Its real part is

$$
I+\int_{y_1}^{y_2}\frac{U''(U-c_r)}{|U-c|^2}|\psi|^2dy=0.
$$

Adding $(c_r-U_s)$ times the zero [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) replaces $U-c_r$ by $U-U_s$:

$$
\int_{y_1}^{y_2}\frac{U''(U-U_s)}{|U-c|^2}|\psi|^2dy=-I<0.
$$

Therefore the integrand's profile factor must be strictly negative somewhere in the open channel:

$$
\boxed{U''(y)[U(y)-U(y_s)]<0\quad\text{for some }y_1<y<y_2.}
$$

This is the [Fjørtoft theorem](../../../../../../../fjortoft-theorem.md). The positive weight and the strictly negative [integral](../../../../../../../integral.md) establish the strict inequality, not merely a nonpositive value. It is a stronger necessary condition than [Rayleigh's inflection-point theorem](../../../../../../../rayleigh-s-inflection-point-theorem.md), and neither condition alone proves instability.
