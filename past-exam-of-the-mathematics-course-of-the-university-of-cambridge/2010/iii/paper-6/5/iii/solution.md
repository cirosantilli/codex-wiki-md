<h1 id="5/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take the [C-star algebra](../../../../../../c-star-algebra.md) $M_2(\mathbb C)$ and choose

$$
a=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
b=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then $a-1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ is positive, while

$$
b-a=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

is positive, since its [quadratic form](../../../../../../quadratic-form.md) at $(x,y)$ is $|x+y|^2$. Thus $1\leq a\leq b$, as required. However,

$$
\boxed{b^2-a^2=\begin{pmatrix}4&5\\5&6\end{pmatrix}}
$$

has [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $-1$ and [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $5\pm\sqrt{26}$, one of which is negative. Equivalently, its [quadratic form](../../../../../../quadratic-form.md) at the real vector $(5,-4)$ is $-4$. It is therefore not positive. This proves that [squaring is not order preserving in a C-star algebra](../../../../../../squaring-is-not-order-preserving-in-a-c-star-algebra.md), even for invertible elements bounded below by the identity.
