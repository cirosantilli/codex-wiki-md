The [spectrum of a ring](../../../../../spectrum-of-a-commutative-ring.md) $A$ is the set of its proper [prime ideals](../../../../../prime-ideal.md). For an [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) $I$, set $V(I)=\{\mathfrak p:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$. These are the closed sets of the [Zariski topology](../../../../../zariski-topology.md). They satisfy

$$
V(0)=\operatorname{Spec}A,\qquad V(A)=\varnothing,\qquad
\bigcap_\lambda V(I_\lambda)=V\!\left(\sum_\lambda I_\lambda\right),\qquad
V(I)\cup V(J)=V(IJ).
$$

The union identity follows because a [prime ideal](../../../../../prime-ideal.md) containing $IJ$ must contain $I$ or $J$: otherwise choose $a\in I\setminus\mathfrak p$ and $b\in J\setminus\mathfrak p$, contradicting $ab\in\mathfrak p$. These identities verify the closed-set axioms. The [maximal spectrum](../../../../../maximal-spectrum.md) has the subspace topology, whose closed sets are $V(I)\cap\operatorname{MaxSpec}A$.

For a finitely generated $\mathbb C$-algebra, the needed form of the [Hilbert Nullstellensatz](../../../../../hilbert-nullstellensatz.md) is

$$
\boxed{\sqrt I=\bigcap_{\substack{\mathfrak m\supseteq I\\\mathfrak m\ \mathrm{maximal}}}\mathfrak m.}
$$

Here an empty intersection means $A$. We prove this formula rather than assume the desired correspondence.

First establish the residue-field fact. If a [field](../../../../../field.md) $L$ is a [finitely generated algebra](../../../../../finitely-generated-algebra.md) over $\mathbb C$, choose a maximal algebraically independent subset $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ of a finite generating set, with remaining generators $u_1,\ldots,u_s$. Each $u_j$ is algebraic over $\mathbb C(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$. Clearing the finitely many denominators in their monic equations gives a nonzero polynomial $d$ such that each $u_j$ is integral over

$$
R=\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_r,1/d].
$$

Since $L$ is a [field](../../../../../field.md), it contains $1/d$, and $L=R[u_1,\ldots,u_s]$ is an [integral extension](../../../../../integral-extension.md) of $R$. If a [field](../../../../../field.md) is integral over a domain, the domain is itself a [field](../../../../../field.md): for $0\ne a\in R$, an integral equation for $a^{-1}$, multiplied by $a^{n-1}$, expresses $a^{-1}$ in $R$.

But if $r>0$, choose $c\in\mathbb C$ for which $t_1-c$ does not divide $d$. Such a choice exists because a nonzero polynomial has only finitely many distinct factors $t_1-c$. The element $t_1-c$ is a nonunit even after inverting $d$: an inverse would imply that $t_1-c$ divides a power of $d$. Thus $R$ would not be a [field](../../../../../field.md), a contradiction. Hence $r=0$, and $L$ is algebraic over the [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md) $\mathbb C$, forcing $L=\mathbb C$. This proves the [Zariski lemma](../../../../../zariski-s-lemma.md) in the form used here.

Now if $f\notin\sqrt I$, the [localization of a ring](../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) $B=(A/I)[1/f]$ is nonzero: vanishing would mean $f^n\in I$ for some $n$. It is still a [finitely generated algebra](../../../../../finitely-generated-algebra.md) over $\mathbb C$, since one need only adjoin an inverse of $f$. Choose a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak n$ of $B$. By the preceding argument, $B/\mathfrak n=\mathbb C$. The composite $A\to B\to\mathbb C$ is surjective because it contains the constant scalars. Its [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is consequently a [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$ of $A$, contains $I$, and does not contain $f$, whose image is invertible. This proves that the displayed intersection is contained in $\sqrt I$. The reverse inclusion follows because every [maximal ideal](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) is prime and contains the [radical of an ideal](../../../../../radical-of-an-ideal.md) whenever it contains that ideal.

For a closed subset $Z\subseteq\operatorname{MaxSpec}A$, put $I(Z)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak m\in Z}\mathfrak m$. It is a [radical ideal](../../../../../radical-ideal.md). The formulas now show

$$
I\longmapsto V(I)\cap\operatorname{MaxSpec}A,\qquad
Z\longmapsto I(Z)
$$

are inverse maps on [radical ideals](../../../../../radical-ideal.md) and closed sets. Indeed the first followed by the second gives $\sqrt I=I$, while if $Z=V(J)\cap\operatorname{MaxSpec}A$, the second gives $\sqrt J$ and $V(\sqrt J)=V(J)$. Thus **the correspondence is a bijection reversing inclusion**. This is the relation between [radical ideals and closed subsets of the maximal spectrum](../../../../../radical-ideals-and-closed-subsets-of-the-maximal-spectrum.md).

For $A=\mathbb C[x]$, the [maximal ideals](../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) are $\mathfrak m_c=(x-c)$, for $c\in\mathbb C$. Every nonzero proper [radical ideal](../../../../../radical-ideal.md) is generated by a squarefree polynomial, and hence has the form

$$
I_S=\left(\prod_{c\in S}(x-c)\right)
$$

for a finite nonempty subset $S\subset\mathbb C$. The associated closed set is $\{\mathfrak m_c:c\in S\}$. A repeated factor of a polynomial does not change its zero set, but passing to its [radical ideal](../../../../../radical-ideal.md) removes that repeated factor. The two endpoint cases are

$$
\boxed{(0)\longleftrightarrow\mathbb C,\qquad
(1)\longleftrightarrow\varnothing,\qquad
I_S\longleftrightarrow S\text{ for finite }S.}
$$

The empty-product convention makes $I_\varnothing=(1)$. Under $c\mapsto\mathfrak m_c$, the closed sets are exactly the finite subsets and the whole affine line.
