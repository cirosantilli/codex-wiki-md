<h1 id="5/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For an ideal $I$, the canonical multiplication map

$$
\mu_I:I\otimes_AM\longrightarrow M,\qquad i\otimes m\longmapsto im
$$

has image $IM$. Consequently the asserted isomorphism is the natural surjection $I\otimes_AM\twoheadrightarrow IM$ being injective. This is the [flatness criterion using ideals](../../../../../../flatness-criterion-using-ideals.md), whose proof follows from the extension criterion above.

Under the character [Tensor-hom adjunction](../../../../../../tensor-hom-adjunction.md), the dual map $\mu_I^*$ identifies with

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_A(A,M^*)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_A(I,M^*).
$$

Indeed, for $\phi\in M^*$, its restriction corresponds to $i\otimes m\mapsto\phi(im)$. If $M^*$ is [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md), this restriction is surjective, so the cogenerator criterion makes $\mu_I$ injective. Conversely, if every $\mu_I$ is injective, every restriction map in this display is surjective. The proved [Baer criterion](../../../../../../baer-criterion.md) then makes $M^*$ [injective](../../../../../../injective-function.md). Thus

$$
\boxed{(2)\Longleftrightarrow(3).}
$$

In particular, (1) also implies (3) directly by tensoring $I\hookrightarrow A$. No finite-generation condition on $I$, and no Noetherian condition on $A$, has been inserted.
