<h1 id="9e/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [alternating series test](../../../../../../alternating-series-test.md) states that if $b_n\geq0$ decreases to zero, then $\sum(-1)^nb_n$ converges. Indeed, its even and odd [partial sums](../../../../../../partial-sum.md) are monotone in opposite directions and bound each other, while their difference tends to zero. Here $b_n=1/(1+\sqrt n)$ decreases to zero, so the [series](../../../../../../series-mathematics.md) converges.

It fails [absolute convergence](../../../../../../absolute-convergence.md), since for $n\geq1$,

$$
\frac1{1+\sqrt n}\geq\frac1{2\sqrt n}.
$$

The [p-series](../../../../../../p-series.md) $\sum n^{-1/2}$ diverges. This also follows by comparison with the divergent [harmonic series](../../../../../../harmonic-series.md), because $n^{-1/2}\geq n^{-1}$. The [comparison test for series](../../../../../../comparison-test-for-series.md) therefore shows that $\sum b_n$ diverges. Thus

$$
\boxed{\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{(-1)^n}{1+\sqrt n}\text{ converges conditionally}.}
$$
