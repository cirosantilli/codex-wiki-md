<h1 id="2c/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For any [complex number](../../../../../../complex-number.md) $w$, $\operatorname{Re}w\leq |w|$, while the [complex modulus](../../../../../../complex-modulus.md) is multiplicative and unchanged by [complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md). Consequently

$$
|z_1+z_2|^2=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}(z_1\overline{z_2})
\leq |z_1|^2+|z_2|^2+2|z_1||z_2|
=(|z_1|+|z_2|)^2.
$$

Both sides are nonnegative, so taking square roots proves the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md):

$$
\boxed{|z_1+z_2|\leq |z_1|+|z_2|.}
$$

The proposed further inequality fails for perpendicular vectors in the [complex plane](../../../../../../complex-plane.md). Taking $z_1=1$, $z_2=i$ gives

$$
\boxed{|1+i|+|1-i|=2\sqrt2>2=2\max(|1|,|i|).}
$$
