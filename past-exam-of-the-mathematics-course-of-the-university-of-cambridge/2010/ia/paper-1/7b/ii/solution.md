<h1 id="7b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

If $\lambda=0$, then $Av=0$ for a nonzero [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$, contradicting the existence of a [matrix inverse](../../../../../../matrix-inverse.md). Hence $\lambda\ne0$. Multiplying $Av=\lambda v$ by $A^{-1}$ gives $v=\lambda A^{-1}v$, so

$$
\boxed{A^{-1}v=\lambda^{-1}v,\qquad \lambda^{-1}\text{ is an eigenvalue of }A^{-1}.}
$$
