<h1 id="4e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The sequence is at least $1$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$. By the [binomial theorem](../../../../../../binomial-theorem.md), for $n\geq2$,

$$
(1+\varepsilon)^n\geq\binom n2\varepsilon^2
=\frac{n(n-1)}2\varepsilon^2>n
$$

once $(n-1)\varepsilon^2>2$. Taking positive $n$th roots gives $1\leq n^{1/n}<1+\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently large $n$. This proves directly from the definition of a [limit of a sequence](../../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) that

$$
\boxed{n^{1/n}\longrightarrow1.}
$$
