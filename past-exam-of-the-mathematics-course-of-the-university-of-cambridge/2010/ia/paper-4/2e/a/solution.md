<h1 id="2e/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Write the [rational number](../../../../../../rational-number.md) as $r=p/q$ with [coprime integers](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md) $p,q$ and $q>0$. Substituting into the [monic polynomial](../../../../../../monic-polynomial.md) and multiplying by $q^n$ gives

$$
p^n+a_{n-1}p^{n-1}q+\cdots+a_1pq^{n-1}+a_0q^n=0.
$$

All terms except the first are divisible by $q$, so $q\mid p^n$. If $q>1$, it has a [prime factor](../../../../../../prime-factor.md) dividing $p^n$, and the [Euclid lemma](../../../../../../euclid-lemma.md) implies that this prime divides $p$, contradicting [coprimality](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md). Thus $q=1$, proving this special case of the [rational root theorem](../../../../../../rational-root-theorem.md):

$$
\boxed{r\in\mathbb Q\ \Longrightarrow\ r\in\mathbb Z.}
$$
