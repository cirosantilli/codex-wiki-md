<h1 id="5d/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $N\leq G$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) if $gNg^{-1}=N$ for every $g\in G$.

Place the cube with its opposite-face axes along the coordinate axes. Consider

$$
N=\left\{I,\ \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1),\ \operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1),\ \operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1)\right\}.
$$

The three nonidentity elements are the half-turns about those axes. Multiplying two different ones gives the third, and each squares to the identity, so this is a [Klein four-group](../../../../../../klein-four-group.md) of order four.

Every cube rotation permutes the three pairs of opposite faces. The associated [group homomorphism](../../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) $G\to S_3$ has exactly $N$ as its kernel: fixing all three face-axis pairs allows only independent sign changes of the three coordinate directions, with [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) one. Hence the [kernel of the cube face-axis action](../../../../../../kernel-of-the-cube-face-axis-action.md) is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md), and

$$
\boxed{N\trianglelefteq G,\qquad |N|=4.}
$$
