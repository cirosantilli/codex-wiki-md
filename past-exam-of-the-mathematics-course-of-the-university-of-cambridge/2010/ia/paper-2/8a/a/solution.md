<h1 id="8a/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At a [critical point](../../../../../../critical-point.md) $p$ of a twice continuously differentiable function, the linear term in the [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) vanishes:

$$
f(p+h)=f(p)+\frac12h^THh+o(\|h\|^2).
$$

The real symmetric [Hessian matrix](../../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) has an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md). If all its [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are positive, its quadratic term is bounded below by a positive multiple of $\|h\|^2$, giving a **strict [local minimum](../../../../../../local-minimum.md)**. If all are negative, it gives a **strict [local maximum](../../../../../../local-maximum.md)**. If positive and negative [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) both occur, directions along their [eigenvectors](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) give values on both sides of $f(p)$, so the point is a **saddle point of a scalar function**. The non-singularity assumption excludes zero [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md); with them the quadratic test may be inconclusive and higher-order terms are needed.
