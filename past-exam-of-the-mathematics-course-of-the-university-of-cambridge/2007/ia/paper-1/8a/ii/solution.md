<h1 id="8a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The quotient is defined only when $z+\overline u\ne0$. Its unit-modulus condition then gives $|z+u|^2=|z+\overline u|^2$. On expanding with [complex conjugates](../../../../../../complex-conjugate.md),

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=(z+u)(\overline z+\overline u)-(z+\overline u)(\overline z+u)\\
&=(z-\overline z)(\overline u-u)
=4\operatorname{Im}z\,\operatorname{Im}u.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence at least one imaginary part is zero, and **either $z$ or $u$ is real**. Conversely, either reality condition makes the two squared moduli equal; provided the denominator is nonzero, the quotient has modulus one. No conclusion is drawn from an undefined quotient.
