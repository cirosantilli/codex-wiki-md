<h1 id="7c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7C](../7c.md)

For the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md), take $\lambda=(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y)/(\mathbf y\cdot\mathbf y)$ and use positivity of the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product.md):

$$
0\leq|\mathbf x-\lambda\mathbf y|^2
=\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf x-\frac{(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y)^2}{\mathbf y\cdot\mathbf y}.
$$

Multiplication by $\mathbf y\cdot\mathbf y>0$ proves

$$
\boxed{(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf y)^2\leq(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf x)(\mathbf y\cdot\mathbf y).}
$$

Equality holds exactly when $|\mathbf x-\lambda\mathbf y|^2=0$. Positive definiteness of the [inner product](../../../../../inner-product.md) makes this equivalent to $\mathbf x=\lambda\mathbf y$. Conversely, substitution of a scalar multiple gives equality. Thus **equality holds precisely for linearly dependent nonzero vectors**.
