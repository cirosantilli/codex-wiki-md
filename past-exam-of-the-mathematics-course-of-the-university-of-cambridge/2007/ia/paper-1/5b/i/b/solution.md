<h1 id="5b/i/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [vector](../../../../../../../vector.md) $\mathbf r-(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf u)\mathbf u$ is the perpendicular component of $\mathbf r$ relative to the axis $\mathbb R\mathbf u$, so its length is the distance from that axis. Hence **the surface is an infinite [circular cylinder](../../../../../../../circular-cylinder.md) of radius $\beta$ about the line through the origin parallel to $\mathbf u$**. Here $\beta$ is the radius and $\mathbf u$ fixes the axis direction; reversing $\mathbf u$ leaves the same [circular cylinder](../../../../../../../circular-cylinder.md).

If $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2$ are an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) of the [plane](../../../../../../../plane.md) perpendicular to $\mathbf u$, a parameterization is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf r=s\mathbf u+\beta\bigl(\cos\varphi\,\mathbf v_1+\sin\varphi\,\mathbf v_2\bigr),\qquad s\in\mathbb R.}
$$

This verifies that all heights and all angular positions, but no interior points, belong to the surface.
