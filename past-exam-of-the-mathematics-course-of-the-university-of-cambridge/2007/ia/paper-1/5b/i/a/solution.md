<h1 id="5b/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For $\mathbf r\ne0$, the [dot product](../../../../../../../dot-product.md) equation says that the angle $\theta$ between $\mathbf r$ and the oriented [unit vector](../../../../../../../unit-vector.md) $\mathbf u$ satisfies $\cos\theta=\alpha$. Thus $\theta=\arccos\alpha$ is fixed. More explicitly, using the [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) from the introductory solution,

$$
s=\alpha\sqrt{s^2+|\mathbf v|^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|\mathbf v|=|s|\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}{|\alpha|},\qquad \operatorname{sgn}s=\operatorname{sgn}\alpha.
$$

Consequently **the surface is one infinite nappe of a [right circular cone](../../../../../../../right-circular-cone.md), with apex at the origin**, including the apex itself. The cone's axis points along $\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha)\mathbf u$, and its acute semi-angle is $\arccos|\alpha|$. Equivalently, $\alpha$ is the cosine of the angle from the oriented $\mathbf u$ axis. Squaring the original equation without retaining the sign of $s$ would incorrectly add the opposite nappe of a [double circular cone](../../../../../../../double-circular-cone.md). No base disk is included in this infinite lateral surface.
