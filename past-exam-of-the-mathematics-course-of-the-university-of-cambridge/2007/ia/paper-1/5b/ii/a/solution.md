<h1 id="5b/ii/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Set $\mathbf b_0=\mathbf c+h\mathbf n$, where $h=p-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf c$. Then $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf b_0=p$, so $\mathbf b_0$ lies in the [plane](../../../../../../../plane.md). For any position $\mathbf r$ in that [plane](../../../../../../../plane.md), $\mathbf r-\mathbf b_0$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf b_0-\mathbf c=h\mathbf n$. The [Pythagorean theorem](../../../../../../../pythagorean-theorem.md) therefore gives

$$
|\mathbf r-\mathbf c|^2=|\mathbf r-\mathbf b_0|^2+h^2.
$$

On the [sphere](../../../../../../../sphere.md) the left side is $a^2$. Thus the intersection is the [circle](../../../../../../../circle.md) in the [plane](../../../../../../../plane.md) centred at $\mathbf b_0$, with squared radius $a^2-h^2$. Its centre must therefore be $\mathbf b=\mathbf b_0$, and

$$
\boxed{\mathbf b-\mathbf c=\lambda\mathbf n,\qquad
\lambda^2=a^2-\rho^2.}
$$

In terms of the radii, **$\lambda=\pm\sqrt{a^2-\rho^2}$**, with the sign fixed by $\lambda=p-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf c$. In the central-plane case $\rho=a$, both signs give zero. This is the [center and radius of a sphere-plane intersection](../../../../../../../center-and-radius-of-a-sphere-plane-intersection.md) formula with its orientation made explicit.
