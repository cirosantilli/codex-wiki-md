<h1 id="2b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $p=1$, the third coefficient row equals three times the second minus twice the first. The first two rows are independent, so the coefficient [matrix rank](../../../../../../matrix-rank.md) is two. The same dependence must hold for the right-hand side, giving

$$
t^2=3t-2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(t-1)(t-2)=0.
$$

Thus **infinitely many solutions occur exactly for $p=1$ and $t\in\{1,2\}$**. The [null space](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) has dimension one by the [rank-nullity theorem](../../../../../../rank-nullity-theorem.md), and an explicit parameterization, with $s\in\mathbb R$, is

$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{ll}
t=1:&(x,y,z)=(1+2s,-3s,s),\\
t=2:&(x,y,z)=(2s,1-3s,s).
\end{array}}
$$

Substitution verifies all three equations in each family.
