<h1 id="1a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The three spherical [basis vectors](../../../../../../basis-vector.md) form an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md): their displayed Cartesian components have unit lengths and pairwise zero [dot products](../../../../../../dot-product.md). For an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md), a [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) is the sum of its projections onto the [basis vectors](../../../../../../basis-vector.md). Taking the [dot product](../../../../../../dot-product.md) of each Cartesian [unit vector](../../../../../../unit-vector.md) with each spherical [unit vector](../../../../../../unit-vector.md) therefore gives the inverse [change of basis](../../../../../../change-of-basis.md):

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbf i&=\cos\phi\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_r+\cos\phi\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_\theta-\sin\phi\,\mathbf e_\phi,\\
\mathbf j&=\sin\phi\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_r+\sin\phi\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_\theta+\cos\phi\,\mathbf e_\phi,\\
\mathbf k&=\cos\theta\,\mathbf e_r-\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_\theta.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Equivalently, the [change-of-basis matrix](../../../../../../change-of-basis-matrix.md) is [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), so its [matrix inverse](../../../../../../matrix-inverse.md) is its transpose. These formulas use the stated angular convention for [spherical polar coordinates](../../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md); at a pole the tangential [basis vectors](../../../../../../basis-vector.md) still depend on the chosen azimuth.
