<h1 id="10e/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Because $f''$ exists everywhere, $f'$ is differentiable everywhere and therefore continuous everywhere; [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) of $f''$ is not required. For $x<y$, apply the [mean value theorem](../../../../../../mean-value-theorem.md) to $f'$ on $[x,y]$. It gives $\xi\in(x,y)$ with

$$
f'(y)-f'(x)=(y-x)f''(\xi)\geq0.
$$

For $x=y$ equality is immediate. Hence **$f'$ is nondecreasing: $x\leq y$ implies $f'(x)\leq f'(y)$**.
