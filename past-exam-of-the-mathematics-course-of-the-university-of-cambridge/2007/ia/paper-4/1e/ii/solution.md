<h1 id="1e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Factor the expression as $n^3-n=(n-1)n(n+1)$. One of three consecutive [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) is divisible by $3$, so $3\mid n^3-n$. If $n$ is odd, its neighbours $n-1$ and $n+1$ are consecutive even [integers](../../../../../../integer.md). One is divisible by $4$ and the other by $2$, so their product is divisible by $8$. Since $3$ and $8$ are [coprime integers](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md), their product divides the expression:

$$
\boxed{24\mid n^3-n\quad\text{for every odd integer }n.}
$$

The argument also includes negative odd [integers](../../../../../../integer.md) and the zero product at $n=\pm1$.
