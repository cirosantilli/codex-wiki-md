<h1 id="10c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Dot [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) with $\dot{\boldsymbol r}$. The magnetic cross-product term is perpendicular to this [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md), so

$$
m\dot{\boldsymbol r}\cdot\ddot{\boldsymbol r}
=-k\boldsymbol r\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol r}
-e\dot{\boldsymbol r}\cdot(\dot{\boldsymbol r}\times\boldsymbol B)
=-k\boldsymbol r\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol r}.
$$

Thus

$$
\frac d{dt}\left(m|\dot{\boldsymbol r}|^2+k|\boldsymbol r|^2\right)=0,
$$

or

$$
\boxed{\frac12m|\dot{\boldsymbol r}|^2+\frac12k|\boldsymbol r|^2
=\mathrm{constant}.}
$$

The two halves are the [kinetic energy](../../../../../../kinetic-energy.md) and the isotropic spring's [potential energy](../../../../../../potential-energy.md). The expression in the question is twice their sum. The [Lorentz force](../../../../../../lorentz-force.md) changes the direction of motion but supplies no power, because it is perpendicular to [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md); this explains why the magnetic field does not appear in the conserved [mechanical energy](../../../../../../mechanical-energy.md). The sign corresponds to charge $-e$.
