<h1 id="12c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

About a fixed inertial origin, the total [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) is $\boldsymbol L=\sum_i m_i\boldsymbol r_i\times\dot{\boldsymbol r}_i$. Differentiation gives

$$
\dot{\boldsymbol L}=\sum_i\boldsymbol r_i\times m_i\ddot{\boldsymbol r}_i,
$$

because $\dot{\boldsymbol r}_i\times\dot{\boldsymbol r}_i=0$. Separate the external and internal contributions. The combined [torque](../../../../../../torque.md) of an internal pair is

$$
\boldsymbol r_i\times\boldsymbol F_{ij}
+\boldsymbol r_j\times\boldsymbol F_{ji}
=(\boldsymbol r_i-\boldsymbol r_j)\times\boldsymbol F_{ij}=0.
$$

The first equality uses [Newton's third law](../../../../../../newton-s-third-law.md), and the second uses the given central-force condition. All internal torques therefore cancel, leaving

$$
\boxed{\frac{d\boldsymbol L}{dt}=\boldsymbol N,
\qquad\boldsymbol N=\sum_i\boldsymbol r_i\times\boldsymbol F_i^e.}
$$

An equal-and-opposite pair which is not central need not have zero combined [torque](../../../../../../torque.md); this explains why both internal-force hypotheses matter.
