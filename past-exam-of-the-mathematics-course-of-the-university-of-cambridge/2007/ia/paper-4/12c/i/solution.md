<h1 id="12c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For constant particle masses define the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) by $M\boldsymbol X=\sum_i m_i\boldsymbol r_i$, with $M=\sum_i m_i$. Summing [Newton's second law](../../../../../../newton-s-second-law.md) over all particles gives

$$
M\ddot{\boldsymbol X}=\sum_i\boldsymbol F_i^e+
\sum_{i<j}(\boldsymbol F_{ij}+\boldsymbol F_{ji}).
$$

Each pair of internal [forces](../../../../../../force.md) cancels by [Newton's third law](../../../../../../newton-s-third-law.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{M\ddot{\boldsymbol X}=\boldsymbol F^e,\qquad
\boldsymbol F^e=\sum_i\boldsymbol F_i^e.}
$$

Only the action-reaction condition is needed for this translation law; centrality of the internal [forces](../../../../../../force.md) is needed for the [angular momentum](../../../../../../angular-momentum.md) result in part (ii).
