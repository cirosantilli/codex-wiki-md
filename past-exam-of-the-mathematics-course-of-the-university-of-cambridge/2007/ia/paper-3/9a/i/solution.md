<h1 id="9a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [conservative vector field](../../../../../../conservative-vector-field.md) is the [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) of a single-valued differentiable scalar potential. Its [line integral](../../../../../../line-integral.md) is path independent and equals the potential difference. If $\psi\mathbf A=\nabla F$ with a twice continuously differentiable potential, equality of mixed partial derivatives gives

$$
\partial_y(\psi A_1)=\partial_y\partial_xF=\partial_x\partial_yF=\partial_x(\psi A_2).
$$

The [product rule](../../../../../../product-rule.md) then gives $\psi_yA_1+\psi A_{1,y}=\psi_xA_2+\psi A_{2,x}$. Rearranging proves the [integrating-factor equation for a planar vector field](../../../../../../integrating-factor-equation-for-a-planar-vector-field.md):

$$
\boxed{\psi(A_{1,y}-A_{2,x})=A_2\psi_x-A_1\psi_y.}
$$

This argument does not require division by $\psi$, so it remains valid even at its zeros. A nonvanishing [integrating factor](../../../../../../integrating-factor.md) is normally sought when one wants to recover useful potential curves for the original field.
