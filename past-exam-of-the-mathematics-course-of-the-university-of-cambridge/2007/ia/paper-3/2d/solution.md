<h1 id="2d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2D](../2d.md)

A [group isomorphism](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) $f:G\to H$ is a bijection satisfying $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$ for all $x,y\in G$. It sends the identity to the identity and satisfies $f(x^k)=f(x)^k$. Applying its inverse shows that it preserves the [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) exactly.

The [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) $C_8$ has an element of order eight. Every element of the [direct product of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) $C_4\times C_2$ has fourth power equal to the identity, and a generator of the first factor paired with the identity of the second has order four. Every nonidentity element of $C_2\times C_2\times C_2$ has order two. Thus their maximum element orders are respectively $8,4,2$, which an isomorphism would preserve. **The three groups are pairwise nonisomorphic.**

For a fourth example take the [dihedral group](../../../../../dihedral-group.md) of symmetries of a square. It consists of four rotations and four reflections, so has order eight. With $r$ a quarter-turn and $s$ a reflection, $srs=r^{-1}\ne r$, showing $sr\ne rs$. This [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is nonabelian, whereas all three displayed [direct products of groups](../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md) are [abelian groups](../../../../../abelian-group.md). Since a [group isomorphism](../../../../../group-isomorphism.md) preserves commutativity, **the square's dihedral group is isomorphic to none of them**. Its order is eight regardless of whether a notation convention calls it $D_4$ or $D_8$.
