<h1 id="10g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a $2\times2$ [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md), the [characteristic polynomial](../../../../../../characteristic-polynomial.md) is $t^2-\operatorname{tr}(A)t+\det A$. The given [matrix trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) and [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) therefore make it $t^2+1$. By the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](../../../../../../cayley-hamilton-theorem.md), $A^2=-I$, and hence $A^4=I$. Since $A^2\ne I$, the [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $A$ cannot be $1$ or $2$. If $A^3=I$, then $A=A^4(A^3)^{-1}=I$, also impossible. Thus **the order is $4$**.
