<h1 id="11g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The face action gives **$|G|=60$**, as calculated above. Every [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) contains the identity and is a union of whole [conjugacy classes](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md). Thus its possible order is $1$ plus a subset sum of $15,20,12,12$. The distinct resulting numbers are

$$
1,13,16,21,25,28,33,36,40,45,48,60.
$$

By [Lagrange's theorem for finite groups](../../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), a subgroup order divides $60$. Only $1$ and $60$ in this list do so. Consequently **the [normal subgroups](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) are $\{1\}$ and $G$**, and the [rotational symmetry group of a regular icosahedron](../../../../../../rotational-symmetry-group-of-a-regular-icosahedron.md) is a [simple group](../../../../../../simple-group.md).
