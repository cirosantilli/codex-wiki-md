<h1 id="3h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For each fixed $x>0$, [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) of the [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) gives the [pointwise convergence](../../../../../../pointwise-convergence.md) $f_n(x)\to\log x$. But at $x_n=1/n$, which belongs to $(0,1)$ for $n>1$,

$$
f_n(x_n)-\log x_n=\log\frac{2/n}{1/n}=\log2.
$$

Consequently the uniform error cannot tend to zero. In fact $f_n(x)-\log x=\log(1+1/(nx))$ is unbounded as $x\downarrow0$ for every fixed $n$. **The [pointwise limit](../../../../../../pointwise-limit.md) is $\log x$, and convergence is not uniform.**
