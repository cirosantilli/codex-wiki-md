<h1 id="12a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume (iii). Take $Y=X\times X$ and let $f,g$ be the two coordinate projections, [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) by the definition of the [product topology](../../../../../../product-topology.md). Their equality set is the diagonal, so it is closed. For distinct $x_1,x_2$, the point $(x_1,x_2)$ lies in its open complement. Choose a basic rectangle $U_1\times U_2$ containing it and contained in that complement. If $U_1\cap U_2$ were nonempty, a point $u$ in their intersection would give $(u,u)$ in the rectangle, impossible. Thus $U_1,U_2$ are disjoint open neighborhoods of $x_1,x_2$, proving that $X$ is [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md). Hence **(iii) implies (i)**, completing all three equivalences.
