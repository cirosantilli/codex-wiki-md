<h1 id="10g/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Let $G$ be a finite subgroup of $K^\times$. It is abelian since multiplication in the [field](../../../../../../field.md) commutes. The structure theorem therefore writes $G$ as a product of cyclic groups with invariant factors $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_s$, omitting trivial factors. Every element satisfies $g^{d_s}=1$, so $G$ consists of roots of $t^{d_s}-1$. The root bound from part (ii) gives

$$
|G|\leq d_s,
$$

while the decomposition gives $|G|=\prod_jd_j\geq d_s$. Equality forces every factor except the last to be trivial. Hence **$G$ is cyclic**. The trivial subgroup is cyclic as well. This is the [finite multiplicative subgroup of a field is cyclic](../../../../../../finite-multiplicative-subgroup-of-a-field-is-cyclic.md) theorem, proved here using the preceding two parts.

The infinite case is false. In $\mathbb C^\times$, the group of all [roots of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md) is infinite, but every element has finite order. If it were cyclic, its generator would have finite order and the whole group would be finite, a contradiction.
