<h1 id="3h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take $x_n=2\pi n$ and $y_n=2\pi n+1/n$. Then $|y_n-x_n|=1/n\to0$, while

$$
f(x_n)=0,\qquad f(y_n)=\left(2\pi n+\frac1n\right)\sin\frac1n\longrightarrow2\pi.
$$

The [sequential criterion for uniform continuity](../../../../../../sequential-criterion-for-uniform-continuity.md) would force $f(y_n)-f(x_n)\to0$. These pairs contradict it: for example, for all sufficiently large $n$ their output separation is greater than $\pi$, despite arbitrarily small input separation. Thus **$x\sin x$ is not uniformly continuous on $\mathbb R$**.
