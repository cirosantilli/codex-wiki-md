↑ **Parent:** [3H](../3h.md)

A real-valued function $f$ on an interval $I$ is [uniformly continuous](../../../../../uniform-continuity.md) when

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall x,y\in I:\quad |x-y|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ |f(x)-f(y)|<\varepsilon.
$$

The essential point in [uniform continuity](../../../../../uniform-continuity.md) is that $\delta$ is independent of the locations of $x$ and $y$. **Uniform continuity on the whole real line does not imply boundedness**: $f(x)=x$ is [Lipschitz continuous](../../../../../lipschitz-continuity.md) with constant $1$, hence [uniformly continuous](../../../../../uniform-continuity.md), but is unbounded.
