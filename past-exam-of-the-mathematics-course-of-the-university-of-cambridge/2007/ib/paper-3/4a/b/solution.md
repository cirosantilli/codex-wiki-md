<h1 id="4a/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Every nonempty [open set](../../../../../../open-set.md) in the given [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md) contains $1$. Suppose a [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md) $f$ took distinct values $f(m)$ and $f(n)$. Choose disjoint open intervals $U,V\subset\mathbb R$ containing these respective values. By [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md), $f^{-1}(U)$ and $f^{-1}(V)$ are nonempty [open sets](../../../../../../open-set.md) in $\mathbb N$, and therefore both contain $1$. This is impossible because the inverse images of disjoint sets are disjoint. Thus **every such continuous map is constant**. Conversely, constant maps are [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md). This is the [continuous real maps from the initial-segment topology](../../../../../../continuous-real-maps-from-the-initial-segment-topology.md) argument, using the [Hausdorff](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) separation of distinct real points.
