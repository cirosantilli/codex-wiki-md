<h1 id="11g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [symmetric group](../../../../../../symmetric-group.md) $S_5$ has [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) $5!=120=3\cdot40$, so its [Sylow subgroups](../../../../../../sylow-subgroup.md) for $p=3$ have [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) $3$. One example is

$$
\boxed{\langle(123)\rangle=\{e,(123),(132)\}.}
$$

Every nonidentity [permutation](../../../../../../permutation.md) of [order of a group element](../../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) $3$ in $S_5$ is a single [permutation cycle](../../../../../../permutation-cycle.md) of length $3$: two disjoint length-three [permutation cycles](../../../../../../permutation-cycle.md) would require six letters. Choose the three moved letters in $\binom53$ ways, then one of their two length-three [permutation cycles](../../../../../../permutation-cycle.md), giving $20$ such elements. Each subgroup of [cardinality](../../../../../../cardinality.md) $3$ has exactly two nonidentity elements, and distinct such subgroups have no nonidentity element in common. Consequently

$$
\boxed{n_3(S_5)=\frac{\binom53\,2}{2}=10.}
$$

This also satisfies the numerical [Sylow theorem](../../../../../../sylow-theorems.md) restrictions $10\mid40$ and $10\equiv1\pmod3$.
