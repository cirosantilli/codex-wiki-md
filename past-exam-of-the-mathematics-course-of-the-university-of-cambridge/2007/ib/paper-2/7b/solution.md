<h1 id="7b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [7B](../7b.md)

With coincident origins and aligned axes, the [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) is

$$
\boxed{x'=\gamma_v(x-vt),\qquad
t'=\gamma_v\left(t-\frac{vx}{c^2}\right),\qquad
\gamma_v=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.}
$$

Its inverse replaces $v$ by $-v$. Along the worldline of the origin of $S''$, $dx'/dt'=u$. Differentiating the inverse [Lorentz transformation](../../../../../lorentz-transformation.md) gives

$$
dx=\gamma_v(dx'+vdt'),\qquad
dt=\gamma_v(dt'+v,dx'/c^2).
$$

Taking the ratio yields the [relativistic velocity-addition formula](../../../../../velocity-addition-formula.md)

$$
\boxed{V=\frac{u+v}{1+uv/c^2}.}
$$

For $|u|,|v|\ll c$, the denominator tends to one, recovering **$V\simeq u+v$**. Simultaneously $\gamma_v\to1$, so $x'\simeq x-vt$ and $t'\simeq t$: these are the [Galilean transformation](../../../../../galilean-transformation.md) and its Newtonian velocity rule.
