<h1 id="10g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For nonnegative integers $m,n$, [Fourier orthogonality](../../../../../../fourier-orthogonality.md) gives

$$
\langle z^m,z^n\rangle=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i(m-n)\theta}\,d\theta=\delta_{mn}.
$$

Every [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is a finite linear combination of these [monomials](../../../../../../monomial.md), and distinct [monomials](../../../../../../monomial.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{1,z,z^2,z^3,\ldots\quad\text{is an orthonormal basis of }P.}
$$

This is an algebraic [basis](../../../../../../basis.md): no infinite linear combinations are needed in the [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) [vector space](../../../../../../vector-space-split.md).
