↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**False.** The real line with its usual [metric](../../../../../../metric.md) is [complete](../../../../../../completeness.md) and is [homeomorphic](../../../../../../local-homeomorphism.md) to $(0,1)$ with its usual [metric](../../../../../../metric.md), through

$$
h(x)=\frac12+\frac1\pi\arctan x.
$$

Its inverse is $h^{-1}(u)=\tan(\pi(u-1/2))$, so both maps are [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md). However, $(1/n)_{n\ge2}$ is a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) in $(0,1)$ and has no limit there: its only possible real limit is zero. Hence $(0,1)$ is not a [complete metric space](../../../../../../complete-metric-space.md). **Completeness is a property of the [metric](../../../../../../metric.md), not just of its [topology](../../../../../../topology-split.md).**
