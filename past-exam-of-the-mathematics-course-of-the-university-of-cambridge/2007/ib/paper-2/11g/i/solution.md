<h1 id="11g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [field](../../../../../../field.md), for example $\mathbb Q$, is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../../noetherian-ring.md): its only [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md) are $(0)$ and the whole field, both finitely generated.

For a non-Noetherian example, take $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$, the [polynomial ring](../../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) in countably many variables, each individual [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) using only finitely many variables. The ascending chain of [ideals](../../../../../../ideal.md)

$$
(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2,x_3)\subsetneq\cdots
$$

is strictly increasing. Indeed, if $x_{n+1}$ belonged to $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, setting the first $n$ variables to zero would give the impossible equality $x_{n+1}=0$. The chain never stabilizes, contradicting the [ascending chain condition](../../../../../../ascending-chain-condition.md). Thus **$\mathbb Q$ is Noetherian, while $\mathbb Q[x_1,x_2,\ldots]$ is not**.
