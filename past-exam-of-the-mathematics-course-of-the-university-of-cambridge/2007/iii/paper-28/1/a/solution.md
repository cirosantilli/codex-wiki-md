<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [discrete valuation](../../../../../../discrete-valuation.md) identities $v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)$ and $v(x+y)\ge\min(v(x),v(y))$ make $R$ closed under products and sums; it contains $0,1$ and additive inverses. As a [subring](../../../../../../subring.md) of the [field](../../../../../../field.md) $K$, it is an [integral domain](../../../../../../integral-domain.md).

Its [units](../../../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) are exactly the elements of valuation zero: $x^{-1}\in R$ if and only if $v(x)=0$. The remaining elements form the ideal $\mathfrak m=\{x:v(x)>0\}$, and every proper ideal is contained in it, since an ideal containing a unit is the whole ring. Hence $R$ is a [local ring](../../../../../../local-ring.md) with this unique [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md).

For a nonzero ideal $I$, the set of valuations of its nonzero elements is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and has a least value. Choose $x\in I$ attaining it. Every $y\in I$ satisfies $v(y/x)\ge0$, so $y/x\in R$ and $I=(x)$. The [zero ideal](../../../../../../zero-ideal.md) is also [principal](../../../../../../principal-ideal.md). This proves that $R$ is a [discrete valuation ring](../../../../../../discrete-valuation-ring.md), in particular a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md).

Choose a [uniformizer](../../../../../../uniformizer.md) $\pi$, with $v(\pi)=1$. For $z\in K$, some $n\ge0$ makes $\pi^nz\in R$, and $z=(\pi^nz)/\pi^n$ is a quotient of elements of $R$. Thus **$K$ is the [field of fractions](../../../../../../field-of-fractions.md) of $R$**.
