<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [commutant](../../../../../../centralizer.md) is $A'=\{T\in\operatorname{End}V:Ta=aT\text{ for every }a\in A\}$. Clearly $A\subseteq A''$. For the reverse inclusion, first note that $A\xi$ is an [invariant subspace](../../../../../../invariant-subspace.md) for the unital star-algebra $A$, and its [orthogonal complement](../../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) is invariant too. Its projection belongs to $A'$. Thus if $B\in A''$, $B\xi\in A\xi$.

To make the same algebra element work for all [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md), amplify to $V^d$, where $d=\dim V$. The diagonal action $a^{(d)}$ has [commutant](../../../../../../centralizer.md) consisting of block [matrices](../../../../../../matrix.md) whose entries belong to $A'$. Therefore $B^{(d)}$ commutes with this amplified [commutant](../../../../../../centralizer.md). For a basis $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_d$, the subspace $\{(a\xi_1,\ldots,a\xi_d):a\in A\}$ is invariant and reducing for the amplified algebra. Its projection lies in that [commutant](../../../../../../centralizer.md). The preceding argument gives some $a\in A$ with $(B\xi_1,\ldots,B\xi_d)=(a\xi_1,\ldots,a\xi_d)$. Since these [vectors](../../../../../../vector.md) form a basis, $B=a$. Hence

$$
\boxed{A''=A.}
$$

This finite-dimensional [double commutant theorem for star-algebras](../../../../../../double-commutant-theorem-for-star-algebras.md) uses the star hypothesis to make the orbit subspaces reducing.
