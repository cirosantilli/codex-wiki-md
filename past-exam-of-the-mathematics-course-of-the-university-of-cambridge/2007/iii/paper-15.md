<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Regard $T_pM$ as the real [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) of [derivations at a point](../../../differential-geometry.md#derivation-at-a-point) $p$: a vector $v$ acts on germs of [smooth functions](../../../analysis.md#smooth-function) by a linear map satisfying $v(fh)=f(p)v(h)+h(p)v(f)$. In a [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) $x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$, the coordinate derivations $\partial/\partial x^i|_p$ form a [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis), so $v=\sum_i v^i\partial/\partial x^i|_p$, with $v^i=v(x^i)$. To see that these exhaust all derivations, the first-order [Taylor theorem](../../../calculus.md#taylor-theorem) locally writes $f-f(p)=\sum_i(x^i-x^i(p))h_i$ with $h_i(p)=\partial_i f(p)$. The product rule gives $v(f)=\sum_i v(x^i)\partial_i f(p)$; applying this to the coordinate functions also proves independence. Equivalently one can use velocities of smooth curves through $p$.

Set $TM=\coprod_{p\in M}T_pM$, with projection $\pi(p,v)=p$. Over a [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) $x:U\to x(U)$ define

$$
\Phi_x:\pi^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow x(U)\times\mathbb R^n,
\qquad (p,v)\longmapsto(x(p),(v^1,\ldots,v^n)).
$$

Give the disjoint union the topology generated by the inverse images of open sets under these bijections. For overlapping charts $y=f\circ x$, the [chain rule](../../../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives the [tangent coordinate transition](../../../fiber-bundle.md#tangent-coordinate-transition)

$$
\boxed{\Phi_y\Phi_x^{-1}(z,v)=(f(z),D f(z)v).}
$$

It is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse $(w,u)\mapsto(f^{-1}(w),D f^{-1}(w)u)$. Thus the induced charts form a smooth atlas of dimension $2n$. The chain rule gives the cocycle on triple overlaps, and any additional smooth [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) gives compatible induced charts, so the structure is independent of the starting atlas.

The total space is [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space): different base points can be separated downstairs, and two distinct vectors over one point can be separated in a common product chart. A countable coordinate cover, together with countable bases for its products, gives [second countability](../../../topology.md#second-countable-space). Hence this atlas defines a [smooth manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold). In every [local trivialization](../../../fiber-bundle.md#local-trivialization), $\pi$ is projection onto the first factor and the fibre change $v\mapsto D f(z)v$ is an invertible linear map depending smoothly on $z$. Fibrewise addition and scalar multiplication are smooth in these product coordinates. Consequently **$TM\to M$ is naturally a smooth real [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) of rank $n$**.

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

Define the [cotangent space](../../../differential-geometry.md#cotangent-space) $T_p^*M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(T_pM,\mathbb R)$ and the [cotangent bundle](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#cotangent-bundle) $T^*M=\coprod_pT_p^*M$, with projection $(p,\alpha)\mapsto p$. A [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) $x$ gives the dual [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) $dx^1|_p,\ldots,dx^n|_p$. Write $\alpha=\sum_i\xi_i dx^i|_p$ and define the induced [local trivialization](../../../fiber-bundle.md#local-trivialization) by

$$
\Psi_x(p,\alpha)=(x(p),\xi)\in x(U)\times\mathbb R^n.
$$

If $y=f(x)$ and $\alpha=\sum_j\eta_jdy^j$, the [chain rule](../../../calculus.md#chain-rule) gives $\xi=D f(x)^T\eta$. Therefore the [cotangent coordinate transition](../../../symplectic-geometry.md#cotangent-coordinate-transition) is

$$
\boxed{\Psi_y\Psi_x^{-1}(z,\xi)=(f(z),D f(z)^{-T}\xi).}
$$

The inverse [Jacobian matrix](../../../calculus.md#jacobian-matrix) is smooth wherever its determinant is nonzero, so this transition and its inverse are smooth. The cocycle follows either from the chain rule or by dualizing the tangent transitions.

Use these product charts to define the topology and smooth atlas. The [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space) and second-countability arguments are the same as for the [tangent bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#tangent-bundle): separate distinct fibres using the base, separate distinct [covectors](../../../linear-algebra.md#covector) in one product chart, and use a countable coordinate cover. The fibre transitions are smooth linear isomorphisms, so the projection is a smooth [vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) of rank $n$. Using every compatible base [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) shows that this structure is natural and independent of chart choices.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

**Yes, the two real smooth [vector bundles](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle) are isomorphic, although the choice is not canonical.** A standard [smooth manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold) is [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space) and second countable, hence admits a smooth locally finite [partition of unity](../../../differential-geometry.md#partition-of-unity). Choose Euclidean [Riemannian metrics](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-metric) on coordinate neighborhoods and take their partition-of-unity sum. At each point at least one positive coefficient occurs, so the resulting smooth symmetric tensor $g$ is positive definite.

The resulting [musical isomorphism](../../../differential-geometry.md#musical-isomorphism) is

$$
\boxed{\flat_g:TM\longrightarrow T^*M,\qquad v\longmapsto g_p(v,\cdot).}
$$

It covers the identity of $M$ and is linear on every fibre. In coordinates it is $(x,v)\mapsto(x,G(x)v)$, where $G=(g_{ij})$ is a smooth positive-definite [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix). Its inverse uses the smooth inverse [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) $G^{-1}$, giving the map $\sharp_g$. Thus it is a smooth [vector bundle isomorphism](../../../fiber-bundle.md#vector-bundle-isomorphism).

The [Riemannian metric](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-metric) supplies the extra choice: no positive-dimensional real [vector space](../../../vector-space.md) has a linear identification with its dual invariant under all changes of [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis). Indeed a scalar change $tI$ acts by $t$ on vectors and $t^{-1}$ on [covectors](../../../linear-algebra.md#covector), so an equivariant map $L$ would satisfy $tL(v)=t^{-1}L(v)$ for every $t>0$, forcing $L=0$. An isomorphism therefore exists without being a metric-free canonical one.

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For fixed $p$, the [covariant derivative](../../../general-relativity.md#covariant-derivative) is linear in its tangent-vector input, so $L_p:T_pM\to T_pM$, $v\mapsto(\nabla_vX)(p)$, is a well-defined linear map. Its [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) defines the [divergence of a Riemannian vector field](../../../calculus.md#divergence-of-a-riemannian-vector-field). In the given [Riemannian orthonormal frame](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-orthonormal-frame), write $X=\sum_j f_jE_j$. The [Levi-Civita connection](../../../general-relativity.md#levi-civita-connection) product rule gives

$$
\nabla_{E_i}X=\sum_j E_i(f_j)E_j+\sum_j f_j\nabla_{E_i}E_j.
$$

At $p$, the second sum vanishes by the [normal orthonormal frame](../../../differential-geometry.md#normal-orthonormal-frame) hypothesis. Thus the $i$th diagonal coefficient of $L_p$ is $E_i(f_i)(p)$, and

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{div}X(p)=\operatorname{tr}L_p=\sum_{i=1}^nE_i(f_i)(p).}
$$

Equivalently one can take $\sum_i g(\nabla_{E_i}X,E_i)$; the [trace](../../../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) does not depend on the orthonormal [basis](../../../vector-space.md#basis) chosen.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Since the [Riemannian orthonormal frame](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-orthonormal-frame) is positively oriented, its dual [coframe](../../../fiber-bundle.md#coframe) satisfies $\omega_g=\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n$. The form $\nu$ is the [interior product](../../../differential-form.md#interior-product) $\iota_X\omega_g$. The contraction rule for an [exterior product](../../../linear-algebra.md#exterior-product) gives

$$
\iota_{E_i}(\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n)
=(-1)^{i-1}\omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\omega_i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n
=(-1)^{i-1}\theta_i.
$$

One can also obtain the sign by moving $E_i$ from the first argument into slot $i$ of the alternating form, using $i-1$ transpositions. Linearity in $X=\sum_i f_iE_i$ proves the [interior contraction of the Riemannian volume form](../../../differential-geometry.md#interior-contraction-of-the-riemannian-volume-form) formula:

$$
\boxed{\nu=\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^{i+1}f_i\theta_i.}
$$

The exponents $i-1$ and $i+1$ have the same parity.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Fix any point $p$ and use a [normal orthonormal frame](../../../differential-geometry.md#normal-orthonormal-frame) there. Such frames exist: in [geodesic normal coordinates](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#geodesic-normal-coordinates), $g_{ij}(p)=\delta_{ij}$, $\partial_kg_{ij}(p)=0$ and the [Christoffel symbols](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#christoffel-symbol) vanish at $p$. Applying the [Gram-Schmidt process](../../../linear-algebra.md#gram-schmidt-process) smoothly to the coordinate fields gives identity coefficients at $p$ and zero first derivatives there, so it produces the required frame. Reverse one frame vector if necessary to match the [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex).

Because the [Levi-Civita connection](../../../general-relativity.md#levi-civita-connection) is torsion-free, $[E_j,E_k](p)=(\nabla_{E_j}E_k-\nabla_{E_k}E_j)(p)=0$. The supplied formula for the [exterior derivative](../../../differential-form.md#exterior-derivative) of a one-form gives

$$
d\omega_i(E_j,E_k)(p)
=E_j(\delta_{ik})(p)-E_k(\delta_{ij})(p)-\omega_i([E_j,E_k](p))=0.
$$

Thus $d\omega_i(p)=0$, and the graded [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule) implies $d\theta_i(p)=0$. Differentiate the formula in part (b):

$$
d\nu(p)=\sum_i(-1)^{i+1}df_i(p)\wedge\theta_i(p).
$$

Since $df_i=\sum_jE_j(f_i)\omega_j$, every summand with $j\ne i$ vanishes because $\omega_j$ already occurs in $\theta_i$. The remaining term has $\omega_i\wedge\theta_i=(-1)^{i-1}\omega_g$, so both signs cancel. Hence

$$
d\nu(p)=\sum_i E_i(f_i)(p)\omega_g(p)
=(\operatorname{div}X)(p)\omega_g(p).
$$

The point was arbitrary, proving the global [exterior derivative of contracted volume form](../../../differential-geometry.md#exterior-derivative-of-contracted-volume-form) identity

$$
\boxed{d\nu=(\operatorname{div}X)\omega_g.}
$$

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

Use the standard convention that these compact [smooth manifolds](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold) have no boundary. The [interior product](../../../differential-form.md#interior-product) identity for a one-form gives $df\wedge\iota_X\omega_g=df(X)\omega_g=X(f)\omega_g$. For example, in the [coframe](../../../fiber-bundle.md#coframe) from part (b), only the matching index survives in this wedge product, with the same pair of cancelling signs as in part (c). Thus the graded [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule) and part (c) give

$$
d(f\nu)=df\wedge\nu+f\,d\nu
=\bigl(X(f)+f\operatorname{div}X\bigr)\omega_g.
$$

Integrate and apply the [Generalized Stokes theorem](../../../differential-form.md#generalized-stokes-theorem). Since $\partial M=\varnothing$, the integral of $d(f\nu)$ is zero. Rearranging proves [integration by parts for Riemannian divergence](../../../differential-geometry.md#integration-by-parts-for-riemannian-divergence):

$$
\boxed{\int_M X(f)\omega_g=-\int_M f\operatorname{div}X\,\omega_g.}
$$

If a boundary were allowed, the exact general formula would instead have the additional term $\int_{\partial M}f\nu$ on the right; [compactness](../../../topology.md#compact-space) alone does not remove that boundary contribution.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A [connection on a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-vector-bundle) is a linear operator $\nabla:\Gamma(E)\to\Omega^1(M;E)$ satisfying $\nabla(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla s$. Choose coordinates $x^i$ and a [local frame](../../../fiber-bundle.md#frame-of-a-vector-bundle) $e_1,\ldots,e_m$, and define the [connection matrix](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-one-form) by $\nabla e_b=\sum_a A^a{}_b\otimes e_a$, with $A^a{}_b=\sum_iA^a{}_{b i}dx^i$. For a section $s=\sum_a s^ae_a$, the coefficient column satisfies

$$
\boxed{d_As=ds+As,\qquad
(\nabla_i s)^a=\partial_i s^a+\sum_b A^a{}_{b i}s^b.}
$$

The symbol $d$ on the right means the ordinary [exterior derivative](../../../differential-form.md#exterior-derivative) of the coefficient functions.

Extend uniquely to $E$-valued [differential forms](../../../differential-form.md) by the graded [Leibniz rule](../../../calculus.md#leibniz-rule)

$$
d_A(\alpha s)=d\alpha\otimes s+(-1)^r\alpha\wedge\nabla s,
\qquad \alpha\in\Omega^r(M).
$$

In the same [local trivialization](../../../fiber-bundle.md#local-trivialization), an $E$-valued form is a column $\sigma=(\sigma^a)$ and the [covariant exterior derivative](../../../fiber-bundle.md#exterior-covariant-derivative) is

$$
\boxed{(d_A\sigma)^a=d\sigma^a+\sum_b A^a{}_b\wedge\sigma^b,\qquad d_A=d+A\wedge.}
$$

Moving the one-form $A$ past the scalar $r$-form produces exactly the $(-1)^r$ in the graded rule. This explains the sign rather than replacing the rule by an ungraded product formula.

The [endomorphism bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#endomorphism-bundle) has the induced [endomorphism bundle connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#endomorphism-bundle-connection) characterized on sections by $\nabla^{\operatorname{End}}_X(T)s=\nabla_X(Ts)-T\nabla_Xs$. Its coefficient formula is $dT+AT-TA$. On an [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism)-valued $r$-form $T$, extend as a [graded derivation](../../../commutative-algebra.md#graded-derivation) of the [endomorphism-valued exterior product](../../../fiber-bundle.md#endomorphism-valued-exterior-product), giving

$$
\boxed{d_A^{\operatorname{End}}T=dT+A\wedge T-(-1)^rT\wedge A.}
$$

Explicitly its $(a,b)$ entry is $dT^a{}_b+\sum_cA^a{}_c\wedge T^c{}_b-(-1)^r\sum_cT^a{}_c\wedge A^c{}_b$. This version is characterized by

$$
d_A(T\wedge\sigma)=(d_A^{\operatorname{End}}T)\wedge\sigma+(-1)^rT\wedge d_A\sigma.
$$

The [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism)-valued version also satisfies $d_A(T\wedge S)=d_AT\wedge S+(-1)^rT\wedge d_AS$. Products here combine composition of [endomorphisms](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism) with wedge products of coefficient forms; [matrix](../../../vector-space.md#matrix) factors must retain their order.

These local formulas are independent of the [local frame](../../../fiber-bundle.md#frame-of-a-vector-bundle). For $e'=eg$, the [change of frame of a vector-bundle connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#change-of-frame-of-a-vector-bundle-connection) gives $A'=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg$, $\sigma'=g^{-1}\sigma$ and $T'=g^{-1}Tg$. Using $d(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}(dg)g^{-1}$ gives $d_{A'}\sigma'=g^{-1}d_A\sigma$ and $d_{A'}T'=g^{-1}(d_AT)g$. Thus both extended operators are globally defined.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Define the [curvature form of a connection](../../../fiber-bundle.md#curvature-form) by the operator

$$
F(X,Y)s=\nabla_X\nabla_Ys-\nabla_Y\nabla_Xs-\nabla_{[X,Y]}s.
$$

It is alternating in $X,Y$. It is also tensorial. For instance, replacing $s$ by $fs$ makes the coefficient of $s$ from derivatives of $f$ equal to $X(Yf)-Y(Xf)-[X,Y]f=0$; the remaining mixed terms $(Xf)\nabla_Ys$ and $(Yf)\nabla_Xs$ cancel between the two second derivatives. Thus $F(X,Y)(fs)=fF(X,Y)s$. Replacing $X$ by $fX$, the unwanted term $-(Yf)\nabla_Xs$ cancels the opposite term from $[fX,Y]=f[X,Y]-(Yf)X$. Hence $F(fX,Y)=fF(X,Y)$, and the other input follows by alternation.

Consequently $F_p$ depends only on the tangent vectors $X_p,Y_p$ and on $s_p$, giving a smooth global element of $\Omega^2(M;\operatorname{End}E)$. In the coefficient-column convention of part (a), applying $d_A$ twice to an arbitrary $E$-valued $r$-form gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_A^2\sigma
&=d(d\sigma+A\wedge\sigma)+A\wedge(d\sigma+A\wedge\sigma)\\
&=dA\wedge\sigma-A\wedge d\sigma+A\wedge d\sigma+A\wedge A\wedge\sigma\\
&=(dA+A\wedge A)\wedge\sigma.
\end{aligned}
$$

For sections this agrees with the operator definition, so the local [Cartan curvature matrix equation](../../../fiber-bundle.md#cartan-curvature-matrix-equation) is

$$
\boxed{F=dA+A\wedge A,\qquad
F_{ij}=\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i+[A_i,A_j].}
$$

Here $F=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}F_{ij}dx^i\wedge dx^j$, with $F_{ij}=F(\partial_i,\partial_j)$. The same calculation for all degrees proves

$$
\boxed{d_A^2\sigma=F\wedge\sigma.}
$$

Under $e'=eg$, applying the covariance of $d_A$ twice gives $F'=g^{-1}Fg$. This is exactly the transition rule of the [endomorphism bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#endomorphism-bundle), independently confirming that $F$ is a well-defined bundle-valued two-form.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use the [covariant exterior derivative](../../../fiber-bundle.md#exterior-covariant-derivative) on the [endomorphism bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#endomorphism-bundle) and remember that $F$ has degree two. The [Bianchi identity](../../../fiber-bundle.md#bianchi-identity) is $d_AF=dF+A\wedge F-F\wedge A=0$. The local formula from part (b) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
dF&=d(dA+A\wedge A)=dA\wedge A-A\wedge dA,\\
A\wedge F-F\wedge A
&=A\wedge dA+A\wedge A\wedge A-dA\wedge A-A\wedge A\wedge A\\
&=A\wedge dA-dA\wedge A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two lines cancel, establishing

$$
\boxed{d_AF=0.}
$$

Because this is an [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism)-valued equation and the operators transform covariantly under a change of [local frame](../../../fiber-bundle.md#frame-of-a-vector-bundle), this local calculation proves the global identity.

For a [line bundle](../../../ringed-space.md#line-bundle) over $k=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, every fibre [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism) is multiplication by a scalar. Thus $\operatorname{End}E$ is canonically the trivial scalar bundle, even when $E$ itself is nontrivial. In a [local frame](../../../fiber-bundle.md#frame-of-a-vector-bundle) $A$ is a scalar one-form, so $A\wedge A=0$ and $F=dA$ locally. Also $A\wedge F=F\wedge A$, since their degrees are one and two. The [Bianchi identity](../../../fiber-bundle.md#bianchi-identity) therefore reduces to the ordinary closedness condition

$$
\boxed{dF=0.}
$$

This does not assert that the local scalar one-forms $A$ combine into a global one-form.

For two [connections on a vector bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-vector-bundle) $\nabla,\nabla'$, the difference is $C^\infty(M)$-linear in the section: the two $df\otimes s$ terms cancel. It is consequently a global [endomorphism](../../../algebra.md#endomorphism)-valued one-form $a$. In rank one, $a$ is a global ordinary scalar one-form. Locally $A'=A+a$, and the scalar cross terms and $a\wedge a$ vanish, giving

$$
F_{A'}-F_A=d(A+a)-dA=da.
$$

This is a global equality, so the two [closed differential forms](../../../differential-form.md#closed-differential-form) differ by an [exact differential form](../../../differential-form.md#exact-differential-form). It proves the [connection-independent curvature class of a line bundle](../../../fiber-bundle.md#connection-independent-curvature-class-of-a-line-bundle):

$$
\boxed{[F_{A'}]=[F_A]\in H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;k).}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 15](paper-15.md)

<h3 id="4/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#4/a)

For every oriented open set $U$ and nowhere-vanishing [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) form $\omega$, let $\widehat U_\omega$ contain the points $(p,o_p)$ for which $o_p$ is the [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) determined by $\omega_p$. These sets form a [basis of a topology](../../../topology.md#basis-of-a-topology) for the desired topology on the [orientation double cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-double-cover).

They cover the set of all pairs: every point has an oriented [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart), and changing the sign of its [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) form gives the other [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex). To check the basis intersection axiom, consider $\widehat U_\omega$ and $\widehat V_\eta$. On $U\cap V$ there is a unique smooth nonzero function $h$ with $\eta=h\omega$. Their intersection is the sheet over

$$
W=\{p\in U\cap V:h(p)>0\}.
$$

Since $W$ is open, this intersection is $\widehat W_{\omega|_W}$, another basis set. Thus intersections are open without requiring the two local orientations to agree on every component of the overlap.

The restriction $\pi:\widehat U_\omega\to U$ is bijective. Its inverse sends a relatively open set $V\subset U$ to the basis set $\widehat V_{\omega|_V}$, so the inverse is continuous. Conversely the projection of the intersection of $\widehat U_\omega$ with any other basis set is an open agreement set like $W$, so the projection is continuous as well. Hence **each sheet projects homeomorphically onto its oriented open set**. In particular,

$$
\boxed{\pi^{-1}(U)=\widehat U_\omega\sqcup\widehat U_{-\omega},}
$$

with both sheets open. This establishes the two-sheeted [covering space](../../../algebraic-topology.md#covering-space) structure.

<h3 id="4/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#4/b)

For a [coordinate chart](../../../differential-geometry.md#manifold-chart) $x:U\to x(U)$ and either [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) sheet over $U$, take the chart $x\circ\pi$ on that sheet. On a nonempty overlap, its transition to $y\circ\pi$ is exactly $y\circ x^{-1}$, restricted to the open agreement set from part (a). It is smooth with smooth inverse. These charts therefore define a smooth structure for which $\pi$ is locally a [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism).

The topology is [Hausdorff](../../../topology.md#hausdorff-space): points with distinct base points are separated by inverse images of disjoint base neighborhoods; two points over the same base point are separated by the two disjoint sheets over one coordinate neighborhood. A countable coordinate cover of $M$ gives a countable collection of two-sheeted charts, whose Euclidean chart bases show [second countability](../../../topology.md#second-countable-space). Thus the cover is a genuine [smooth manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold), not just a collection of compatible charts. The construction using all base charts makes the smooth structure independent of the chosen atlas. It also makes $\pi:\widehat U_\omega\to U$ a [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism) for every oriented open $U$, since it is a bijective local [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism).

At $\widehat p=(p,o_p)$, the derivative $d\pi_{\widehat p}:T_{\widehat p}\widehat M\to T_pM$ is a linear isomorphism. Define the [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of $T_{\widehat p}\widehat M$ to be the one mapped to $o_p$. On $\widehat U_\omega$ it is represented by $\pi^*\omega$. Where two sheets overlap, their base [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) forms differ by a positive function, so their pullbacks determine the same [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex). This proves the **canonical [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of the cover**:

$$
\boxed{o_{\widehat p}=(d\pi_{\widehat p})^{-1}o_p.}
$$

The deck involution $(p,o_p)\mapsto(p,-o_p)$ reverses this [canonical orientation of the orientation double cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#canonical-orientation-of-the-orientation-double-cover). No global [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of the base was used.

<h3 id="4/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

<h4 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#4/c)

A connected [smooth manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold) is path connected because it is locally path connected. Each connected component $C$ of the [orientation double cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-double-cover) is itself path connected. Choose a point of $C$ over $p$. For any $q\in M$, lift a path from $p$ to $q$ starting at that chosen point, using the [path lifting theorem](../../../algebraic-topology.md#path-lifting-theorem). The lifted path lies in $C$ and ends above $q$. Therefore every component maps onto $M$.

Fix one fibre, containing exactly two points. Every component meets that fibre, and distinct components meet disjoint subsets of it, so **there are at most two connected components**.

If $M$ is orientable, a global continuous choice of tangent-space [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) $o$ gives two disjoint sheets

$$
M_+=\{(p,o_p):p\in M\},\qquad
M_-=\{(p,-o_p):p\in M\}.
$$

They are open: on a sufficiently small oriented coordinate neighborhood the global choice agrees consistently with one of the two local sheets. Each projects homeomorphically to the connected base, so these are precisely two connected components.

Conversely suppose the cover is disconnected. It has exactly two components $C_+,C_-$. Both project onto the base and each fibre contains only two points, so each component meets every fibre exactly once. The restriction $\pi:C_+\to M$ is consequently a bijective local [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism), hence a global [diffeomorphism](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#diffeomorphism). Transporting the canonical [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of $C_+$ through this map or, equivalently, using its smooth inverse section, gives a global [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) of $M$. Thus the [connectedness criterion for the orientation double cover](../../../algebraic-topology.md#connectedness-criterion-for-the-orientation-double-cover) is

$$
\boxed{M\text{ is orientable}\iff\widehat M\text{ has two components}\iff\widehat M\text{ is disconnected}.}
$$

↑ **Parent:** [Paper 15](paper-15.md)

<h3 id="5/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#5/a)

Let $M$ be a compact [Riemannian manifold](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#riemannian-manifold) without boundary. Let $\delta=d^*$ be the formal $L^2$ [adjoint operator](../../../hilbert-space.md#adjoint-operator) of the [exterior derivative](../../../differential-form.md#exterior-derivative), and let $\Delta_H=d\delta+\delta d$ be the [Hodge Laplacian](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-laplacian). Write $\mathcal H^r(M)=\ker\Delta_H\cap\Omega^r(M)$ for the smooth [harmonic differential forms](../../../differential-form.md#harmonic-differential-form). The [Hodge decomposition theorem](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-decomposition-theorem) states that this space is finite-dimensional and

$$
\boxed{\Omega^r(M)=\mathcal H^r(M)\oplus d\Omega^{r-1}(M)\oplus\delta\Omega^{r+1}(M),}
$$

an $L^2$-orthogonal direct sum. Thus every smooth $r$-form is uniquely the sum of a harmonic, an exact and a coexact form; its exact and coexact components are unique, though the primitives used to write those components need not be unique. Also

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H^r(M)\cong H^r_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb R),\qquad
\dim\mathcal H^r(M)=b_r(M).}
$$

The isomorphism sends a [harmonic form](../../../differential-form.md#harmonic-differential-form) to its [de Rham cohomology](../../../differential-form.md#de-rham-cohomology) class. For example, a closed coexact component $\delta\gamma$ must vanish because $\|\delta\gamma\|^2=\langle\gamma,d\delta\gamma\rangle=0$; an exact [harmonic form](../../../differential-form.md#harmonic-differential-form) also vanishes by orthogonality. These facts explain uniqueness of the harmonic representative of a [cohomology class](../../../cohomology.md#cohomology-class).

On an oriented $n$-manifold the [codifferential](../../../differential-form.md#codifferential) is $\delta=(-1)^{n(r+1)+1}*d*$ on $r$-forms. The general formulation above also holds on nonorientable [Riemannian manifolds](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#riemannian-manifold): use the globally defined Riemannian volume density for the $L^2$ inner product and its formal adjoint, rather than require a global ordinary volume form. The closed-manifold hypothesis is essential unless suitable boundary conditions are added.

<h3 id="5/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#5/b)

Use the sign convention in the supplied scalar [Laplace-Beltrami operator](../../../differential-geometry.md#laplace-beltrami-operator) identity, and let $X=\omega^\sharp$ be the metric-dual [vector field](../../../calculus.md#vector-field). Integrate that [Bochner formula for one-forms](../../../differential-form.md#bochner-weitzenbock-formula-for-one-forms) over the closed [Riemannian manifold](../../../riemannian-geometry.md#riemannian-manifold), with its volume density $dV_g$. The integral of a scalar Laplacian is zero: on an oriented manifold this is the [Riemannian divergence theorem](../../../calculus.md#riemannian-divergence-theorem) applied to its gradient, and the same density formula is valid without orientability. Hence

$$
0=\int_M\left(|\nabla\omega|^2+\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)\right)dV_g.
$$

Both terms are nonnegative, because $\operatorname{Ric}\ge0$. The smooth nonnegative integrand must therefore vanish everywhere: a strictly positive value would remain positive on an open neighborhood of positive volume and make its integral positive. In particular $|\nabla\omega|^2=0$ at every point, proving

$$
\boxed{\nabla\omega=0.}
$$

Thus [harmonic one-forms are parallel under nonnegative Ricci curvature](../../../differential-form.md#harmonic-one-forms-are-parallel-under-nonnegative-ricci-curvature). The same argument also gives $\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)=0$ pointwise. No [orientation](../../../algebraic-topology.md#orientation-of-a-simplex) or [connectedness](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) assumption is needed for this step.

<h3 id="5/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [5](#5)

<h4 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#5/c)

By the [Hodge decomposition theorem](../../../differential-form.md#hodge-decomposition-theorem), the first [Betti number](../../../homology.md#betti-number) is $b_1(M)=\dim\mathcal H^1(M)$. Part (b) makes every element of this space a [parallel differential form](../../../differential-geometry.md#parallel-differential-form). For any fixed $p\in M$, consider the linear evaluation map

$$
\operatorname{ev}_p:\mathcal H^1(M)\longrightarrow T_p^*M,\qquad\omega\longmapsto\omega_p.
$$

It is injective. A parallel form with $\omega_p=0$ remains zero under [parallel transport](../../../fiber-bundle.md#parallel-transport) along any path from $p$; [connectedness](../../../geometry-and-topology.md#connected-space) of the [smooth manifold](../../../differential-geometry.md#smooth-manifold) makes every point reachable by such a path. Equivalently, the derivative of the squared norm of a parallel form is zero in every direction, so its norm is constant on the connected manifold. Either argument gives $\omega=0$ globally. Since $\dim T_p^*M=n$, the [dimension bound for harmonic one-forms under nonnegative Ricci curvature](../../../differential-form.md#dimension-bound-for-harmonic-one-forms-under-nonnegative-ricci-curvature) gives

$$
\boxed{b_1(M)\le n.}
$$

For an explicit obstruction in dimension three, take $N=\Sigma_2\times S^1$, where $\Sigma_2$ is a closed orientable surface of genus two. It is a compact connected smooth three-manifold. A cell decomposition of $\Sigma_2$ has one vertex, four one-cells and one two-cell attached by a product of commutators. Its cellular boundary into the one-cells is zero, because each generator has total exponent zero in that attaching word; the boundary of every one-cell is also zero. Therefore $H^1(\Sigma_2;\mathbb R)=\mathbb R^4$. The [Künneth theorem](../../../cohomology.md#kunneth-theorem) in degree one and $H^1(S^1;\mathbb R)=\mathbb R$ give

$$
H^1(N;\mathbb R)\cong H^1(\Sigma_2;\mathbb R)\oplus H^1(S^1;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^5.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\dim N=3,\qquad b_1(N)=5>3,\qquad
N\text{ admits no metric with }\operatorname{Ric}\ge0.}
$$

This is the [Betti-number obstruction to nonnegative Ricci curvature](../../../differential-form.md#betti-number-obstruction-to-nonnegative-ricci-curvature). It rules out every [Riemannian metric](../../../differential-geometry.md#riemannian-metric) on that underlying manifold, not merely a particular product metric.
