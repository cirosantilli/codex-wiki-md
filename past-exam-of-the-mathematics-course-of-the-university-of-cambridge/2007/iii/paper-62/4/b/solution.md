<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At [cosmological horizon exit](../../../../../../cosmological-horizon-exit.md), $k$ is proportional to $aH$; the convention-dependent factor $2\pi$ has zero logarithmic derivative. Define the [first Hubble slow-roll parameter](../../../../../../first-hubble-slow-roll-parameter.md) $\epsilon_H=-\dot H/H^2$. Then

$$
\frac{d\log k}{d\log a}=1-\epsilon_H,\qquad\frac d{d\log k}=\frac1{1-\epsilon_H}\frac d{d\log a}\simeq\frac d{d\log a}.
$$

The [chain rule](../../../../../../chain-rule.md) gives $d/d\log a=(\dot\phi/H)d/d\phi$. In [reduced Planck units](../../../../../../reduced-planck-units.md), the [slow-roll equations](../../../../../../slow-roll-approximation.md) are $3H\dot\phi\simeq-V_{,\phi}$ and $3H^2\simeq V$. Therefore the [horizon-exit logarithmic derivative in slow roll](../../../../../../horizon-exit-logarithmic-derivative-in-slow-roll.md) is

$$
\boxed{k\frac d{dk}\simeq a\frac d{da}=\frac{\dot\phi}H\frac d{d\phi}\simeq-\frac{V_{,\phi}}V\frac d{d\phi}.}
$$
