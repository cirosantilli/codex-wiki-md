<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Both coefficient functions are linear, hence globally Lipschitz. Their norms are bounded by a constant times $|(x,y)|$, so they satisfy the [linear growth condition for an SDE](../../../../../../linear-growth-condition-for-an-sde.md). The [existence and pathwise uniqueness theorem for a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../existence-and-pathwise-uniqueness-theorem-for-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) gives a unique global [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) for every deterministic starting point $(x_0,y_0)\in\mathbb R^2$. Linear growth prevents finite-time explosion.

The theorem gives [pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md), and strong existence with [pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md) gives [uniqueness in law](../../../../../../uniqueness-in-law.md). Thus **every initial value is permitted, and both forms of uniqueness hold**. The coefficients need not be bounded for this theorem; their global Lipschitz and linear-growth properties are sufficient.
