<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [linear operator](../../../../../../linear-operator.md) $M_A$ acts on the first factor, while the partial inner product acts on the second. They therefore commute in the contraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(I_A\otimes\langle\phi_B^*|)(M_A\otimes I_B)|\widetilde\Psi_{AB}\rangle
&=M_A(I_A\otimes\langle\phi_B^*|)|\widetilde\Psi_{AB}\rangle\\
&=\boxed{M_A|\phi_A\rangle}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the operated vector is exactly the [relative state](../../../../../../relative-state-of-a-bipartite-vector.md) obtained from the operated bipartite vector using the same [conjugate index vector](../../../../../../conjugate-index-vector.md). For a physical normalized [pure state](../../../../../../pure-state.md), divide by $\|M_A\phi_A\|$ when that norm is nonzero. If $M_A\phi_A=0$, the vector identity still holds, but there is no normalized output state for that zero-probability branch. An arbitrary operator is not assumed unitary or norm preserving.
