<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $R=\{B_t:0\leq t\leq1\}$ and $h=1/2$. For [planar Brownian motion](../../../../../../planar-brownian-motion.md), the process $h^{-1/2}B_{ht}$ is again standard [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md). Thus its time-$h$ range has law $\sqrt h\,R$. Two-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](../../../../../../lebesgue-measure.md) scales by the square of the spatial factor, giving

$$
\lambda(A_1)\ \stackrel d=\ h\lambda(R)=\tfrac12\lambda(R).
$$

The increments $B_{h+t}-B_h$, $0\leq t\leq h$, form another standard [Brownian motion](../../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md). Its range has the same law as $A_1$, and translation by $B_h$ does not change its area. Hence

$$
\boxed{\lambda(A_1)\ \stackrel d=\ \lambda(A_2)\ \stackrel d=\ \tfrac12\lambda(R).}
$$

The equality concerns the laws of the areas. No [independence](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) between these two original areas is needed here.
