<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In a spatially flat [FLRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md), a radial [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) satisfies $d\chi=c\,dt/a(t)$. Normalize $a_0=1$. The [comoving particle horizon](../../../../../../comoving-particle-horizon.md) at time $t$ is therefore $r_h=c\int_0^t dt'/a(t')$, using the initial singularity as the time origin. The [cosmological redshift](../../../../../../cosmological-redshift.md) relation $1+z=1/a$ gives

$$
\frac{dz}{dt}=-(1+z)H(z),\qquad \frac{dt}{a(t)}=-\frac{dz}{H(z)}.
$$

For matter and a [cosmological constant](../../../../../../cosmological-constant.md) in a spatially flat model, the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) gives $H(z)=H_0[\Omega_{m,0}(1+z)^3+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}]^{1/2}$. The initial singularity corresponds to $z\to\infty$, so reversing the integration limits yields

$$
\boxed{r_h(z)=\frac c{H_0}\int_z^\infty\frac{dz'}{[\Omega_{m,0}(1+z')^3+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}]^{1/2}}.}
$$

This is a [comoving particle horizon](../../../../../../comoving-particle-horizon.md), not a distance to the source currently observed at redshift $z$, and not a future-directed [cosmological event horizon](../../../../../../cosmological-event-horizon.md). The corresponding proper horizon radius at that epoch is $r_h(z)/(1+z)$.
