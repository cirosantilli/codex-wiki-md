<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a radial [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md) in a spatially flat universe, the [comoving radial distance](../../../../../../comoving-radial-distance.md) increment is $d\chi=c\,dz/H(z)$. If the cloud and [quasar](../../../../../../quasar.md) redshifts differ only slightly, take $H$ at their common approximate epoch $z_*$, giving

$$
\Delta\chi\simeq\frac{c\,\delta z}{H(z_*)},\qquad H(z_*)=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{m,0}(1+z_*)^3+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}}.
$$

Multiplying by the [scale factor](../../../../../../scale-factor-cosmology.md) at that epoch gives the local [proper distance in cosmology](../../../../../../proper-distance-in-cosmology.md) relevant to the quasar's radiation flux:

$$
\boxed{d_{cq}\simeq\frac{c\,\delta z}{H_0(1+z_*)\sqrt{\Omega_{m,0}(1+z_*)^3+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}}}.}
$$

If distance is instead expressed in comoving units, it is $(1+z_*)d_{cq}$. Equivalently the small rest-frame velocity difference is $\delta v=c\,\delta z/(1+z_*)$, and $d_{cq}\simeq\delta v/H(z_*)$. This [quasar-cloud redshift separation](../../../../../../quasar-cloud-redshift-separation.md) assumes the redshift difference is due to homogeneous expansion, with negligible [peculiar velocities](../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md). Since the two observations lie on a light cone, the proper separation is understood as the short separation on a nearly common cosmic-time slice; specifying the epoch matters at higher order.
