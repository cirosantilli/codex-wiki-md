<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A complete [Einstein ring](../../../../../../einstein-ring.md) requires an axisymmetric [gravitational lens](../../../../../../gravitational-lens.md) and exact alignment of the source with the lens centre and observer: $\beta=0$. A finite aligned circular source gives a ring of finite width. The background source must lie behind the lens, so the lensing distance factor is positive.

With $\beta=0$ the [thin gravitational lens equation](../../../../../../thin-gravitational-lens-equation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\theta_E^2=\frac{4G}{c^2}\frac{D_{ds}}{D_sD_d}\,M(<D_d\theta_E).}
$$

The [Einstein radius](../../../../../../einstein-radius.md) here is angular, and the mass is projected inside the corresponding aperture. For an extended mass distribution the equation is implicit because the enclosed mass depends on $\theta_E$.

Using the supplied approximations, $D_{ds}/(D_sD_d)=1/(3\times10^{25}\,\mathrm m)$ and $\theta_E=3\times10^{-4}$, the inferred mass is

$$
M=\frac{\theta_E^2c^2}{4G}\frac{D_sD_d}{D_{ds}}
=\frac{(3\times10^{-4})^2(3\times10^8)^2(3\times10^{25})}{4(7\times10^{-11})}\,\mathrm{kg}
\simeq8.7\times10^{44}\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{M(<\theta_E)\simeq4.3\times10^{14}M_\odot.}
$$

This measures the projected aperture mass without requiring the [singular isothermal sphere](../../../../../../singular-isothermal-sphere.md) model used subsequently.
