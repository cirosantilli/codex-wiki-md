<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Adjoin $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$ to the [complex upper half-plane](../../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md). Ordinary points retain their usual neighborhoods. At a rational boundary point $a=\sigma\infty$, a neighborhood basis consists of $a$ together with $\sigma\{z\in\mathbb H:\operatorname{Im}z>Y\}$, with $Y$ arbitrarily large. Different choices of $\sigma\in SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ change it by a translation and sign, so give the same neighborhood system. Give $X(1)$ the [quotient topology](../../../../../../quotient-topology.md). The [modular group](../../../../../../modular-group.md) acts properly discontinuously on $\mathbb H$; its [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) neighborhoods descend to punctured neighborhoods with their [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) point filled in. This quotient is Hausdorff: interior orbits can be separated by proper discontinuity, and a sufficiently high [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) neighborhood avoids any prescribed compact interior neighborhood.

Away from fixed points of the effective group $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$, a small disk is disjoint from its nonidentity translates, so its coordinate gives a [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) quotient coordinate. At an elliptic fixed point $\tau_0$, use

$$
u=\frac{\tau-\tau_0}{\tau-\overline{\tau_0}}.
$$

Its effective [stabilizer](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) rotates $u$ by an $e$th root of unity, where $e=2$ at $i$ and $e=3$ at $\rho$. The quotient coordinate is $w=u^e$, not $u$. All rational boundary points lie in one [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) class. At infinity the [stabilizer](../../../../../../stabilizer-subgroup.md) acts by $\tau\mapsto\tau+1$, and

$$
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
$$

is a punctured-disk coordinate; adjoining $q=0$ fills in the [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md). These charts and their [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) transition maps define the complex structure of the [compactified modular curve](../../../../../../compactified-modular-curve.md).

For [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) use the standard region

$$
\mathcal D=\{\tau\in\mathbb H:|\operatorname{Re}\tau|\leq1/2,\ |\tau|\geq1\}.
$$

Its part with $\operatorname{Im}\tau\leq Y$, $Y>1$, is compact: the imaginary part is at least $\sqrt3/2$. The quotient of its upper tail, with its two vertical sides identified and infinity added, is the closed disk $|q|\leq e^{-2\pi Y}$. These two compact images cover $X(1)$, including the boundary-edge identifications. Thus **$X(1)$ is a [compact Riemann surface](../../../../../../compact-riemann-surface.md)**.

The $j$ function also extends over the [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) with one [simple pole](../../../../../../simple-pole.md), since $j=q^{-1}+O(1)$. Its local expressions at [elliptic points](../../../../../../elliptic-point.md) descend to [holomorphic functions](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) of $u^e$. By Question 1 it maps the remaining quotient bijectively onto $\mathbb C$, so its extension is a degree-one [holomorphic map](../../../../../../holomorphic-map.md) $X(1)\to\mathbb P^1$. A degree-one map of compact Riemann surfaces is [biholomorphic](../../../../../../biholomorphism.md): any local ramification would already give degree at least two. Therefore $X(1)$ is the [Riemann sphere](../../../../../../riemann-sphere.md); this will also identify the base [genus](../../../../../../genus-of-a-surface.md) in Question 5.
