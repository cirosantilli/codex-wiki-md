<h1 id="6/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [cusp form](../../../../../../../cusp-form.md) is [holomorphic](../../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) in the upper half-plane by definition, so it is [meromorphic](../../../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) there. In the reverse direction suppose $f$ is [meromorphic](../../../../../../../meromorphic-function.md) and

$$
|f(\tau)|y^{k/2}\leq B\qquad(\tau\in\mathbb H).
$$

On a small closed disk around an interior point, $y$ is bounded above and away from zero. Hence $|f|$ is bounded there by a fixed constant. A [pole](../../../../../../../pole.md) of positive order would make $|f|$ unbounded along points approaching its center, a contradiction. Every possible [pole](../../../../../../../pole.md) is therefore removable, and **$f$ is [holomorphic](../../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) on $\mathbb H$**. Meromorphicity alone would not establish this conclusion; the bounded invariant [norm](../../../../../../../norm.md) is being used here.
