<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For positive integer $k$, the equivalence is the [bounded invariant norm characterization of cusp forms](../../../../../../bounded-invariant-norm-characterization-of-cusp-forms.md). The three conditions below work together: meromorphicity and the bound remove interior poles; the transformation law produces a periodic local function at every [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md); the same bound makes that function tend to zero at the [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md). Conversely a [cusp form](../../../../../../cusp-form.md) has exponentially decaying [Fourier expansions of a modular form](../../../../../../fourier-expansion-of-a-modular-form.md), and finitely many compact core regions control its invariant [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) everywhere. These implications are proved under the corresponding labelled items.

**Without a positive-weight convention, weight zero is a genuine exception.** The function $f=1$ satisfies all three printed conditions at $k=0$ for every [congruence subgroup](../../../../../../congruence-subgroup.md), but is not a [cusp form](../../../../../../cusp-form.md). Indeed a weight-zero [cusp form](../../../../../../cusp-form.md) would descend to a [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) on the compact curve $X(\Gamma)$ and hence be constant, and vanishing at a [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) would make it zero. Thus the literal unrestricted claim needs the qualification $k>0$.

If negative integer weights are included, both sides contain only the zero function. To check the bounded side, each [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) translate has at most power growth in $y$, hence at most a power of $\log(1/|q_h|)$. For a negative [Laurent coefficient](../../../../../../laurent-coefficient.md) of order $m$, Cauchy's coefficient estimate bounds its absolute value by $C r^m(\log(1/r))^{|k|/2}\to0$, so there is no negative coefficient and every [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) is [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md). If such a nonzero negative-weight form existed, the product of its slash translates over $\Gamma\backslash SL_2(\mathbb Z)$ would be a nonzero [holomorphic](../../../../../../complex-differentiability-at-a-point.md) level-one [modular form](../../../../../../modular-form.md) of negative weight. Right multiplication permutes the translates, and all [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) factors are bounded, so that product really is a level-one form. Its existence contradicts the valence formula. This also excludes negative-weight [modular cusp](../../../../../../cusp-of-a-modular-group.md) forms. The nonzero counterexample is specifically weight zero.
