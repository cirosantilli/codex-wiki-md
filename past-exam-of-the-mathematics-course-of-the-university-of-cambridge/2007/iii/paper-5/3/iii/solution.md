<h1 id="3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose first that $G$ is uniform and $H$ is a powerful [open subgroup](../../../../../../open-subgroup.md). The torsion criterion stated above makes $G$ a [torsion-free group](../../../../../../torsion-free-group.md); so $H$ is a [torsion-free group](../../../../../../torsion-free-group.md) too. It is finitely generated, for example by the [Schreier lemma](../../../../../../reidemeister-schreier-theorem.md), and hence is uniform by the same criterion. Put $d=d(G)$ and $e=d(H)$, and choose $k$ with $P_k(G)\le H$. For every $n\ge0$,

$$
P_{n+k}(G)\le H^{p^n}\le G^{p^n}=P_{n+1}(G).
$$

Uniformity of the two [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) gives

$$
p^{nd}\le [G:H^{p^n}]=[G:H]p^{ne}\le p^{(n+k-1)d}.
$$

Taking logarithms and dividing by $n$, then letting $n\to\infty$, proves $e=d$. This proves (i)$\Rightarrow$(iii) without assuming that $H$ is a power [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md). Conversely every $P_i(G)$ is powerful and open, so (iii) applied to these [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) gives (ii). Therefore **all three conditions are equivalent**.

The remaining nested parts establish the stated properties of the transported operations $+_n$ defined in the root solution.
