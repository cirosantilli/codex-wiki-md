<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For a [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md), the [lower p-series](../../../../../lower-p-series.md) is the descending sequence of closed [characteristic subgroups](../../../../../characteristic-subgroup.md)

$$
\boxed{P_1(G)=G,\qquad P_{i+1}(G)=\overline{P_i(G)^p[P_i(G),G]}.}
$$

The bar means [closure](../../../../../closure-topology.md) of the [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) generated by the displayed powers and [group commutators](../../../../../group-commutator.md). Throughout this question $p$ is odd. For a finitely generated [powerful pro-p group](../../../../../powerful-pro-p-group.md), [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) collection gives $P_i(G)=G^{p^{i-1}}$, each $P_i$ is powerful, and $\Phi(P_i)=P_{i+1}$. All these [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md) are open. A [uniform pro-p group](../../../../../uniform-pro-p-group.md) is such a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) with all [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) indices $[P_i:P_{i+1}]$ equal to $[G:P_2]$. We use the standard powerful-group torsion criterion: a finitely generated [powerful pro-p group](../../../../../powerful-pro-p-group.md) is uniform exactly when it is a [torsion-free group](../../../../../torsion-free-group.md). For a uniform [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of generator number $d$, the indices are $p^d$, and $x\mapsto x^{p^a}$ is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) from $G$ onto $G^{p^a}$.

We record explicitly the collection facts used for the operations below. With $U_b=G^{p^b}=P_{b+1}$, one has

$$
[U_a,U_b]\le U_{a+b+1},\qquad x\equiv y\pmod{U_b}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x^{p^a}\equiv y^{p^a}\pmod{U_{a+b}}.
$$

The second statement is the power-congruence property: the $p$th-power map is an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) on adjacent uniform layers, and collection gives $(yu)^p\equiv y^pu^p\pmod{U_{b+2}}$ for $u\in U_b$. Thus the first nonzero layer of a difference moves down exactly one step under a $p$th power. Iterating gives the asserted equivalence and also justifies taking roots of congruences. These facts follow from powerful-group [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) collection, using divisibility by $p$ of $\binom p2$ and the successive-layer power isomorphisms; they are the uniform-group structure results being used here.

The [transported addition on a uniform pro-p group](../../../../../transported-addition-on-a-uniform-pro-p-group.md) is

$$
\boxed{x+_ny=(x^{p^n}y^{p^n})^{p^{-n}}\qquad(n\ge1).}
$$

The product is in $U_n$ and has a unique $p^n$th root in $G$. For the comparison at $n=1$ define $x+_0y=xy$. The numbered proofs below establish [associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md), approximate [commutativity](../../../../../commutativity.md) and compatibility of these operations.

The intrinsic [Lie algebra of a uniform pro-p group](../../../../../lie-algebra-of-a-uniform-pro-p-group.md) has underlying set $G$, zero equal to the [group](../../../../../group-split.md) identity, additive inverse $x^{-1}$, and operations

$$
\boxed{x+_Ly=\lim_{n\to\infty}(x^{p^n}y^{p^n})^{p^{-n}},\qquad [x,y]_L=\lim_{n\to\infty}[x^{p^n},y^{p^n}]^{p^{-2n}},\qquad \lambda x=x^\lambda\ (\lambda\in\mathbb Z_p).}
$$

Here the [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) is $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. For $\lambda\in\mathbb Z_p$, $x^\lambda$ is the limit of $x^{m_j}$ for integers $m_j\to\lambda$; the pro-$p$ [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) makes it well defined. The addition sequence is [Cauchy](../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) by the compatibility proved below. The [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) estimate puts $[x^{p^n},y^{p^n}]$ in $U_{2n+1}$, so its displayed root exists and lies in $U_1$. Collection of [group commutators](../../../../../group-commutator.md) shows that successive bracket approximants differ in layers tending to the identity, giving the other limit. The [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) identities give additivity of the limiting bracket, and the [Hall-Witt identity](../../../../../hall-witt-identity.md) gives its [Jacobi identity](../../../../../jacobi-identity.md). Approximate [commutativity](../../../../../commutativity.md) makes limiting addition [commutative](../../../../../commutativity.md). The resulting lattice $L_G$ is a [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) over $\mathbb Z_p$ of rank $d(G)$, and $[L_G,L_G]\le pL_G$.

A [powerful Lie algebra over p-adic integers](../../../../../powerful-lie-algebra-over-p-adic-integers.md), in the convention relevant here, is a [Lie algebra](../../../../../lie-algebra-split.md) $L$ whose underlying [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) is finite free over $\mathbb Z_p$ and whose bracket satisfies $[L,L]\le pL$. For $p=2$ the condition is instead $[L,L]\le4L$. For odd $p$, define multiplication on its underlying set by the convergent [Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula](../../../../../baker-campbell-hausdorff-formula.md)

$$
x*y=\operatorname{BCH}(x,y)=x+y+\frac12[x,y]+\frac1{12}\bigl([x,[x,y]]+[y,[y,x]]\bigr)+\cdots.
$$

An iterated bracket of degree $m$ lies in $p^{m-1}L$. The BCH coefficient bound loses at most $\lfloor(m-1)/(p-1)\rfloor$ powers of $p$, so all higher-degree terms are integral, and their valuations tend to infinity. This explains both convergence and why multiplying a lattice with small bracket stays inside the lattice. Formal [associativity](../../../../../associative-property.md) of BCH passes to the convergent series, the identity is $0$, the inverse is $-x$, and every [group](../../../../../group-split.md) power satisfies $x^{*m}=mx$. The quotients by $p^aL$ are [finite p-groups](../../../../../finite-p-group.md). Hence the resulting [compact group](../../../../../compact-group.md) is a finitely generated [torsion-free group](../../../../../torsion-free-group.md) and a [pro-p group](../../../../../pro-p-group.md), and its [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) lies in $pL$. Thus **$(L,*)$ is a [uniform pro-p group](../../../../../uniform-pro-p-group.md)**, with $P_i(L,*)=p^{i-1}L$ and successive index $p^{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_p}L}$.

The standard uniform-group/Lie-lattice correspondence identifies these constructions as mutually inverse: **$L_{(L,*)}\cong L$ and $(L_G,*)\cong G$**. To see why the [inverse limits](../../../../../inverse-limit.md) recover the lattice operations, the addition approximant for a BCH [group](../../../../../group-split.md) is $p^{-n}\operatorname{BCH}(p^nx,p^ny)$: its linear term is $x+y$ and every higher term tends to zero. Similarly its [group commutator](../../../../../group-commutator.md) approximant has limiting term $[x,y]$. In the other direction, a local [logarithm](../../../../../logarithm.md) in the complete normed [group algebra](../../../../../group-algebra.md) identifies the intrinsic operations with algebra addition and [commutator](../../../../../commutator.md) and identifies [group](../../../../../group-split.md) multiplication with BCH; the uniform power maps extend the identification from a sufficiently small power [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) to all of $G$. Continuous [group homomorphisms](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) respect powers, unique roots and limits, while [Lie algebra homomorphisms](../../../../../lie-algebra-homomorphism.md) respect every BCH term, so the equivalence also preserves morphisms.
