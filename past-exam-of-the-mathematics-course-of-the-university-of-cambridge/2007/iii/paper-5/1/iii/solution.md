<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $(L,(\pi_i))$ and $(L',(\pi'_i))$ are [inverse limits](../../../../../../inverse-limit.md) of the same system, in the universal-property sense. The compatible family $(\pi'_i)$ induces a unique continuous [homomorphism](../../../../../../homomorphism.md) $u:L'\to L$ with $\pi_i u=\pi'_i$; similarly there is a unique $v:L\to L'$ with $\pi'_i v=\pi_i$. Then $uv$ and $1_L$ both induce the projections $(\pi_i)$, so uniqueness gives $uv=1_L$. Likewise $vu=1_{L'}$. Thus **the limits are uniquely isomorphic by an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) preserving every projection**. In particular the inverse maps are continuous, so this is an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) of [topological groups](../../../../../../topological-group-split.md), not just abstract [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md).
