<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The first alternative in the supplied analytic-group theorem would give a [faithful representation](../../../../../../faithful-representation.md) of $\mathcal N$ in some $\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z_p)$ or $\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_p[[t]])$. The embedding proved above supplies cyclic [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of order $p^a$ for arbitrarily large $a$. Such orders are impossible in either fixed [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md).

In [characteristic zero](../../../../../../characteristic-zero.md), a [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) $A$ of order $p^a$ is diagonalizable over an algebraic [closure](../../../../../../closure-topology.md), because its [minimal polynomial](../../../../../../minimal-polynomial.md) divides the [separable polynomial](../../../../../../separable-polynomial.md) $X^{p^a}-1$. At least one [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) must be a primitive $p^a$th [root of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md); otherwise $A^{p^{a-1}}=I$. The [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../../../../cyclotomic-polynomial.md) $\Phi_{p^a}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q_p$, since $\Phi_{p^a}(1+X)$ satisfies the [Eisenstein criterion](../../../../../../eisenstein-criterion.md) at $p$. Consequently that [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) has degree $\varphi(p^a)=p^{a-1}(p-1)$ over $\mathbb Q_p$, which must be at most the [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $d$. This bounds $a$.

In characteristic $p$, pass from the power-series ring to its fraction [field](../../../../../../field.md). A [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md) of $p$-power order satisfies $X^{p^a}-1=(X-1)^{p^a}$, so it is unipotent. Writing $A=I+B$, nilpotence gives $B^d=0$. For $p^e\ge d$,

$$
A^{p^e}=I+B^{p^e}=I.
$$

Thus its order is at most $p^{\lceil\log_pd\rceil}$, again a fixed bound. Therefore **the linear alternative is impossible** in both coefficient rings.
