<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $c:\mathbb N\to\{1,\ldots,r\}$ be the given [finite colouring](../../../../../../finite-coloring.md), with $\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$. Give each unordered pair $\{a,b\}$ the colour $c(a+b)$. The infinite [Ramsey theorem for r-sets](../../../../../../ramsey-s-theorem.md), in its two-element case, gives an infinite [homogeneous set for a colouring](../../../../../../homogeneous-set-for-a-colouring.md) $X$. Enumerate it as $x_1<x_2<\cdots$. Every distinct-index sum $x_i+x_j$ has the common pair colour, so

$$
\boxed{\{x_i+x_j:i\ne j\}\text{ is monochromatic}.}
$$

This application of the infinite [Ramsey theorem for r-sets](../../../../../../ramsey-s-theorem.md) uses only unordered pairs.
