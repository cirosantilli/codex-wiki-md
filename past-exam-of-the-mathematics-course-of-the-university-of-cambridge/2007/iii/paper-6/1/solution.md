<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Use unital [rings](../../../../../ring.md), and distinguish left and right [Noetherianity](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md). A useful form of the [noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../noncommutative-hilbert-basis-theorem.md) is the following: if a [ring](../../../../../ring.md) $B$ is generated by a subring $A$ and an element $x$, if $A+xA=A+Ax$, and if $A$ is a [right Noetherian ring](../../../../../right-noetherian-ring.md), then $B$ is a [right Noetherian ring](../../../../../right-noetherian-ring.md). The corresponding left-handed statement holds as well. In particular, a central [polynomial ring](../../../../../polynomial-ring.md) over a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) is [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), and a [skew polynomial ring of an automorphism](../../../../../skew-polynomial-ring-of-an-automorphism.md) preserves either side's [Noetherianity](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md).

Here is a proof of the general right-handed statement. Put $F_{-1}=0$ and

$$
F_n=A+Ax+\cdots+Ax^n=A+xA+\cdots+x^nA.
$$

The equality follows inductively from $A+xA=A+Ax$. These groups form an exhaustive [ascending filtration](../../../../../ascending-filtration.md), stable under multiplication by $A$ on either side, with $F_jF_k\subseteq F_{j+k}$. For any $b\in A$ and $n\geq0$, moving coefficients one step at a time also gives

$$
bx^n=x^nc+v\qquad(c\in A,\ v\in F_{n-1}).
$$

Fix a [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) $I$ of $B$ and define coefficient sets

$$
L_n=\{a\in A:ax^n\in I+F_{n-1}\}.
$$

Each $L_n$ is a [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) of $A$. Additive closure is immediate. If $a\in L_n$ and $b\in A$, use the preceding transport identity to obtain

$$
abx^n=(ax^n)c+av\in I+F_{n-1}.
$$

Here $(ax^n)c$ has this membership because $I$ is a [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) and $F_{n-1}$ is stable under right multiplication by $A$. Also $L_n\subseteq L_{n+1}$, by multiplying $ax^n\in I+F_{n-1}$ on the right by $x$. The [ascending chain condition](../../../../../ascending-chain-condition.md) in $A$ yields $N$ with $L_n=L_N$ for $n\geq N$.

For each $0\leq k\leq N$, choose finite right generators $a_{kj}$ of $L_k$ and lifts $f_{kj}\in I$ with $f_{kj}-a_{kj}x^k\in F_{k-1}$. These finitely many lifts generate $I$ as a [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) of $B$. To prove this, let $f\in I\cap F_d$ and write $f=ax^d+v$, $v\in F_{d-1}$. Then $a\in L_d$. Put $k=\min(d,N)$ and write $a=\sum_ja_{kj}b_j$. Transport each coefficient as $b_jx^d=x^dc_j+v_j$ with $v_j\in F_{d-1}$. Consequently

$$
f-\sum_j f_{kj}x^{d-k}c_j\in I\cap F_{d-1}.
$$

Induction on $d$, with $F_{-1}=0$, expresses $f$ in the right ideal generated by the lifts. Every [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) is therefore finitely generated, proving the theorem. Passing to the [opposite ring](../../../../../opposite-ring.md) proves the left-handed version. This proof does not require unique coefficient normal forms.

A [poly-(cyclic or finite) group](../../../../../poly-cyclic-or-finite-group.md) has a finite [subnormal series](../../../../../subnormal-series.md)

$$
1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_r=G
$$

whose factors $G_i/G_{i-1}$ are cyclic or finite. Normality is required between consecutive terms. We show inductively that each [group ring](../../../../../group-ring.md) $\mathbb Z[G_i]$ is both a [left Noetherian ring](../../../../../left-noetherian-ring.md) and a [right Noetherian ring](../../../../../right-noetherian-ring.md).

The starting [ring](../../../../../ring.md) $\mathbb Z$ is [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), since every [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) is principal. The other module fact we use is that a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md) over a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) on the relevant side is a [Noetherian module](../../../../../noetherian-module.md): it is a quotient of a finite [direct sum](../../../../../direct-sum.md) of regular modules, and the [ascending chain condition](../../../../../ascending-chain-condition.md) passes to finite sums and quotients. For a finite sum this follows by induction, comparing the intersections of a chain with one summand and its images in the quotient by that summand.

If $H\triangleleft K$ has finite index, choosing coset representatives shows that $\mathbb Z[K]$ is finite free as a left and as a right $\mathbb Z[H]$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md). By [finite-module extension preserves Noetherianity](../../../../../finite-module-extension-preserves-noetherianity.md), every one-sided [ideal](../../../../../ideal.md) of $\mathbb Z[K]$ is finitely generated already as a $\mathbb Z[H]$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), and those same generators generate it as a $\mathbb Z[K]$-ideal. Thus either chain condition passes through a finite factor.

If $K/H$ is infinite cyclic, choose $t\in K$ lifting its generator. Every element of $K$ has a unique form $ht^n$, and conjugation by $t$ gives an [automorphism](../../../../../automorphism.md) $\sigma$ of $\mathbb Z[H]$. Hence

$$
\mathbb Z[K]\cong\mathbb Z[H][t,t^{-1};\sigma],\qquad ta=\sigma(a)t.
$$

For the polynomial subring $C=\mathbb Z[H][t;\sigma]$, the condition $A+tA=A+At$ follows from invertibility of $\sigma$, so the proved [noncommutative Hilbert basis theorem](../../../../../noncommutative-hilbert-basis-theorem.md) makes $C$ [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) on both sides. A [right ideal](../../../../../right-ideal.md) $J$ of the [skew Laurent polynomial ring](../../../../../skew-laurent-polynomial-ring.md) is generated by $J\cap C$: for any $f\in J$, choose $m$ large enough that $ft^m\in C$, and recover $f=(ft^m)t^{-m}$. Finite generators of $J\cap C$ over $C$ therefore generate $J$ over the Laurent ring. Left multiplication by a large power of $t$ proves the left-handed statement. These are the Laurent-extension facts used in the induction.

Every factor of the [subnormal series](../../../../../subnormal-series.md) is covered by one of these two cases. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb Z[G]\text{ is left and right Noetherian}.}
$$

The argument proves the more general result that [group rings of poly-(cyclic or finite) groups are Noetherian](../../../../../group-rings-of-poly-cyclic-or-finite-groups-are-noetherian.md) over any coefficient [ring](../../../../../ring.md) satisfying both chain conditions.
