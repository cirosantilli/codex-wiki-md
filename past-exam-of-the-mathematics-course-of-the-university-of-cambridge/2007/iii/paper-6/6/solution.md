<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

Since $M$ is a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md), choose a surjection from a finite [free module](../../../../../free-module.md), $F_0\twoheadrightarrow M$. A finite [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) over a [left Noetherian ring](../../../../../left-noetherian-ring.md) is a [Noetherian module](../../../../../noetherian-module.md), so the kernel $K_1$ is finitely generated. Choose a finite [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) $F_1$ surjecting onto $K_1$, then compose its map with $K_1\hookrightarrow F_0$. Its kernel is again finitely generated. Repeating constructs an exact sequence

$$
\boxed{\cdots\longrightarrow F_2\longrightarrow F_1\longrightarrow F_0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0,}
$$

with all $F_i$ finite [free modules](../../../../../free-module.md), hence [finitely generated projective modules](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md). Thus the requested [projective resolution](../../../../../projective-resolution.md) exists, without asserting that it has finite length.

Assume now that $M\ne0$ and $\operatorname{pd}_RM=n<\infty$. We may arrange a length-$n$ [projective resolution](../../../../../projective-resolution.md) with all terms finite projective. Here is why the preceding construction gives one. Set $K_0=M$, with short exact sequences $0\to K_{j+1}\to F_j\to K_j\to0$. Applying the [Ext functor](../../../../../ext-functor.md), whose positive degrees vanish on a projective first argument, gives by repeated dimension shifting

$$
\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(K_n,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_R(M,N)=0
$$

for every left [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $N$. The final equality follows from the existence of any length-$n$ [projective resolution](../../../../../projective-resolution.md). Vanishing of these degree-one groups means that every short exact sequence ending in $K_n$ splits: its extension class is zero. In particular a free surjection onto $K_n$ splits, so $K_n$ is a [projective module](../../../../../projective-module.md). It is finitely generated by the [Noetherian](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) kernel construction. Use $P_n=K_n$ and $P_j=F_j$ for $j<n$ to obtain

$$
0\longrightarrow P_n\xrightarrow{d_n}P_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0.
$$

For a left [finite projective module](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md) $P$, put $P^*=\operatorname{Hom}_R(P,R)$, a right [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) under $(fr)(p)=f(p)r$. It is a right [finite projective module](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md), and its natural evaluation map

$$
P\longrightarrow P^{**}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(P^*,R),\qquad p\longmapsto(f\longmapsto f(p)),
$$

is an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). These claims hold for a finite [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) by coordinates, and pass to [direct summands](../../../../../direct-summand.md), proving them for every [finite projective module](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md) without any right-Noetherian hypothesis.

Suppose $n\geq1$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M,R)=0$. Computing the [Ext functor](../../../../../ext-functor.md) from this resolution shows that its last dual map

$$
d_n^*:P_{n-1}^*\longrightarrow P_n^*
$$

is surjective. Since $P_n^*$ is projective as a right [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), this map has a right-module section $h:P_n^*\to P_{n-1}^*$, with $d_n^*h=1$. Dualizing gives $h^*d_n^{**}=1$. Under the natural double-dual identifications, $d_n^{**}$ is $d_n$, so this gives a left-module retraction of $d_n$.

Therefore $P_{n-1}=d_n(P_n)\oplus Q$ for a [finite projective module](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md) $Q$. The next differential has kernel $d_n(P_n)$, so restricting it to $Q$ gives an exact resolution with $Q$ replacing $P_{n-1}$ and the initial $P_n$ removed. This has length $n-1$, contradicting the definition of [projective dimension](../../../../../projective-dimension.md). For $n=0$, the nonzero $M$ is a [finite projective module](../../../../../finite-projective-module.md) and a summand of some $R^d$. A nonzero vector in that summand has a nonzero coordinate, so one coordinate projection restricts to a nonzero map $M\to R$. Thus the degree-zero [Hom functor](../../../../../hom-functor.md) is nonzero as well. We have proved

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M,R)\ne0.}
$$

This is [top Ext detects finite projective dimension over a left Noetherian ring](../../../../../top-ext-detects-finite-projective-dimension-over-a-left-noetherian-ring.md). The nonzero-module convention matters when $n=0$: if one assigns projective dimension zero to the zero module, then $\operatorname{Hom}_R(0,R)=0$ is an exception. With the convention $\operatorname{pd}_R0=-\infty$, it is outside the asserted finite nonnegative case.

For an example of infinite [projective dimension](../../../../../projective-dimension.md), take the [dual numbers](../../../../../dual-number.md) $R=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ over a [field](../../../../../field.md) $k$, and $M=k=R/(\varepsilon)$. The [periodic resolution over dual numbers](../../../../../periodic-resolution-over-dual-numbers.md) is

$$
\cdots\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}R\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}R\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}R\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0.
$$

It is exact because both the kernel and image of multiplication by $\varepsilon$ are the ideal $(\varepsilon)$, and that is also the kernel of $R\to k$. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,k)$ gives a copy of $k$ in every degree and zero differentials, since $\varepsilon$ acts as zero on $k$. Consequently

$$
\operatorname{Ext}^j_R(k,k)\cong k\ne0\qquad\text{for every }j\geq0.
$$

A finite [projective resolution](../../../../../projective-resolution.md) would make these groups zero above its length, so

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{pd}_{k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)}k=\infty.}
$$
