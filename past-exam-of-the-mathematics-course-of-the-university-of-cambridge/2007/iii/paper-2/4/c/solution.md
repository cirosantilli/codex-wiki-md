<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The root-of-unity specialization requires $q\ne1$: we take $q$ to be a primitive cube [root of unity](../../../../../../root-of-unity.md). The displayed presentation contains $(q-q^{-1})^{-1}$, so substituting $q=1$ literally is undefined. Its classical $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ limit instead has simple finite-dimensional [modules](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) of arbitrarily large dimension. This is why the primitive-root qualification matters.

First consider [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) occurring in the [quantum plane](../../../../../../quantum-plane.md). Here $[3]=0$, $K^3=1$, and

$$
E^3(x^ry^s)=[s][s-1][s-2]x^{r+3}y^{s-3}=0,
$$

with the analogous formula $F^3=0$. Thus the action factors through the finite-dimensional quotient with $E^3=F^3=0$, $K^3=1$, spanned by $F^iK^jE^l$ with $0\le i,j,l<3$. Any [simple module](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) over this quotient is finite-dimensional, since it is generated by one vector. In a nonzero simple [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md) choose a $K$ [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$ in $\ker E$. This is possible because $E$ is nilpotent and its kernel is $K$-invariant. Then the [highest-weight string for quantum sl2](../../../../../../highest-weight-string-for-quantum-sl2.md) shows that

$$
W=\langle v,Fv,F^2v\rangle
$$

is invariant under $E,K$, and $F^3v=0$ makes it $F$-invariant. Simplicity gives $W=V$, proving **$\dim V\le3$**.

The same bound holds even for arbitrary finite-dimensional [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) over the unrestricted [quantum enveloping algebra of sl2](../../../../../../quantum-enveloping-algebra-of-sl2.md). Indeed $E^3,F^3,K^3$ are central: the string commutation identity has a factor $[3]=0$, and the $K$ commutation factors satisfy $q^6=1$. By [Schur lemma](../../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md), they act as scalars $\alpha,\beta,\kappa$, with $\kappa\ne0$. The element

$$
\Omega=FE+\phi(K),\qquad \phi(K)=\frac{qK+q^{-1}K^{-1}}{(q-q^{-1})^2}
$$

is also central. For example $[E,FE]=(K-K^{-1})E/(q-q^{-1})$, while $[E,\phi(K)]=[\phi(q^{-2}K)-\phi(K)]E=-(K-K^{-1})E/(q-q^{-1})$; the $F$ commutator cancels in the same way. Let $\Omega$ act as $\gamma$.

If $\alpha=0$, choose the highest vector $v$ as above. Its three-vector $F$ string is invariant, now using $F^3v=\beta v$, so again its dimension is at most three. If $\alpha\ne0$, choose a $K$ [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) $v$. The space $W=\langle v,Ev,E^2v\rangle$ is stable under $E,K$ because $E^3=\alpha$, and it is stable under $F$ because

$$
F=(\gamma-\phi(K))E^{-1},\qquad E^{-1}=\alpha^{-1}E^2.
$$

It therefore equals the simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) and has dimension at most three.

Over $\mathbb C$ this also excludes infinite-dimensional [simple modules](../../../../../../irreducible-module.md) of the unrestricted algebra. It is a finite [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) over the central finitely generated commutative [algebra over a field](../../../../../../algebra-over-a-field.md) $Z_0=\mathbb C[E^3,F^3,K^3,K^{-3}]$, using the same 27 ordered spanning elements. A simple [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$, generated by one vector, is consequently finite over $Z_0$. Choose a [maximal ideal](../../../../../../maximal-ideal.md) $\mathfrak m$ in its support. [Nakayama lemma](../../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md) makes $M/\mathfrak mM\ne0$; since $\mathfrak mM$ is a [submodule](../../../../../../submodule.md), simplicity gives $\mathfrak mM=0$. The [Hilbert Nullstellensatz](../../../../../../hilbert-nullstellensatz.md) gives $Z_0/\mathfrak m=\mathbb C$, so $M$ is finite-dimensional and the preceding argument applies. Thus the usual complex, primitive-root interpretation yields

$$
\boxed{\text{every simple }U_q\text{-module has dimension at most }3.}
$$
