<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $M$ be a compact [Riemannian manifold](../../../../../../riemannian-manifold.md) without boundary. Let $\delta=d^*$ be the formal $L^2$ [adjoint operator](../../../../../../adjoint-operator.md) of the [exterior derivative](../../../../../../exterior-derivative.md), and let $\Delta_H=d\delta+\delta d$ be the [Hodge Laplacian](../../../../../../hodge-laplacian.md). Write $\mathcal H^r(M)=\ker\Delta_H\cap\Omega^r(M)$ for the smooth [harmonic differential forms](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md). The [Hodge decomposition theorem](../../../../../../hodge-decomposition-theorem.md) states that this space is finite-dimensional and

$$
\boxed{\Omega^r(M)=\mathcal H^r(M)\oplus d\Omega^{r-1}(M)\oplus\delta\Omega^{r+1}(M),}
$$

an $L^2$-orthogonal direct sum. Thus every smooth $r$-form is uniquely the sum of a harmonic, an exact and a coexact form; its exact and coexact components are unique, though the primitives used to write those components need not be unique. Also

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H^r(M)\cong H^r_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb R),\qquad
\dim\mathcal H^r(M)=b_r(M).}
$$

The isomorphism sends a [harmonic form](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) to its [de Rham cohomology](../../../../../../de-rham-cohomology.md) class. For example, a closed coexact component $\delta\gamma$ must vanish because $\|\delta\gamma\|^2=\langle\gamma,d\delta\gamma\rangle=0$; an exact [harmonic form](../../../../../../harmonic-differential-form.md) also vanishes by orthogonality. These facts explain uniqueness of the harmonic representative of a [cohomology class](../../../../../../cohomology-class.md).

On an oriented $n$-manifold the [codifferential](../../../../../../codifferential.md) is $\delta=(-1)^{n(r+1)+1}*d*$ on $r$-forms. The general formulation above also holds on nonorientable [Riemannian manifolds](../../../../../../riemannian-manifold.md): use the globally defined Riemannian volume density for the $L^2$ inner product and its formal adjoint, rather than require a global ordinary volume form. The closed-manifold hypothesis is essential unless suitable boundary conditions are added.
