<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At fixed $t$, expand the exact [damped harmonic oscillator](../../../../../../damped-harmonic-oscillator.md) solution using $e^{-\epsilon t}=1-\epsilon t+O(\epsilon^2)$, $\omega=1+O(\epsilon^2)$ and $1/\omega=1+O(\epsilon^2)$. Its [Poincaré expansion](../../../../../../asymptotic-expansion.md) through first order is

$$
\boxed{y(\epsilon,t)=(1-\epsilon t)\sin t,\qquad x(t)-y(\epsilon,t)=O(\epsilon^2)\quad(t\text{ bounded}).}
$$

The correction $-\epsilon t\sin t$ is a [secular term](../../../../../../secular-term.md). When $\epsilon t$ is order one it is not small, and the truncated amplitude eventually changes sign and grows in magnitude, instead of reproducing positive damping. This loss of ordering is why a fixed-time [regular perturbation](../../../../../../regular-perturbation.md) is unsuitable on the damping timescale.
