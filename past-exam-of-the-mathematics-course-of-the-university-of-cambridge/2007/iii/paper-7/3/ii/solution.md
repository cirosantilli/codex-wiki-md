<h1 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a nonidentity [transvection](../../../../../../transvection.md) $\tau_{f,d}$, take $e_1=d$ and choose $e_2$ with $f(e_2)=1$. Complete $e_1$ to a basis of $\ker f$ using $e_3,\ldots,e_n$. Then $e_1,e_2,e_3,\ldots,e_n$ is a basis of $V$, and the matrix of the [transvection](../../../../../../transvection.md) is $I+E_{12}$: it sends $e_2$ to $e_2+e_1$ and fixes the other basis vectors. Consequently all nonidentity [transvections](../../../../../../transvection.md) form one [conjugacy class](../../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) in $\operatorname{GL}(V)$. This matrix has [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) one, and conjugation preserves [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md), so

$$
\boxed{T^\#\text{ is a single GL-conjugacy class contained in }\operatorname{SL}(V).}
$$

For $n\ge3$, let $k$ be any GL-conjugator carrying one [transvection](../../../../../../transvection.md) to another. In an adapted basis for the first, the matrix $c=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,s,1,\ldots,1)$ centralizes it and has [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) $s$. Set $s=(\det k)^{-1}$. Then $kc\in\operatorname{SL}(V)$ gives the same conjugation as $k$. This proves the stronger [transvection conjugacy over a finite field](../../../../../../transvection-conjugacy-over-a-finite-field.md) result

$$
\boxed{n\ge3\ \Longrightarrow\ T^\#\text{ is a single SL-conjugacy class}.}
$$

The extra coordinate is essential to this [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) adjustment; no such claim is required in dimension two.
