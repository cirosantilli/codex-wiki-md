<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume $[H,G]\le K$. For $k\in K\le H$ and $g\in G$,

$$
g^{-1}kg=k[k,g]\in K.
$$

Thus $g^{-1}Kg\le K$ for every $g$; applying this also to $g^{-1}$ gives equality. Hence $K$ is a [normal subgroup](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $G$, even though initially it was assumed normal only in $H$. Now the quotient $G/K$ is defined, and for $h\in H$, $g\in G$,

$$
[hK,gK]=[h,g]K=K.
$$

Every element of $H/K$ consequently lies in the [center of a group](../../../../../../center-of-a-group.md) $Z(G/K)$.

Conversely, if $K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ and $H/K\le Z(G/K)$, each displayed quotient [group commutator](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) is trivial, so $[h,g]\in K$. Taking the subgroup generated by these elements gives $[H,G]\le K$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{[H,G]\le K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G\ \text{and}\ H/K\le Z(G/K).}
$$

The forward argument also implies $H\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$, since $g^{-1}hg=h[h,g]\in H$.
