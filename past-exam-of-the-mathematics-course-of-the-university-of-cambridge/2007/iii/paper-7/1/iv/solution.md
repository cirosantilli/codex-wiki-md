<h1 id="1/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

Let $1=A_0\le\cdots\le A_r=A$ and $1=B_0\le\cdots\le B_s=B$ be [central series](../../../../../../central-series.md). Pad the shorter chain by repeating its terminal group, so both have length $t=\max(r,s)$. In the [direct product of groups](../../../../../../direct-product-of-groups.md), [group commutators](../../../../../../group-commutator.md) are coordinatewise, hence

$$
[A_i\times B_i,A\times B]=[A_i,A]\times[B_i,B]\le A_{i-1}\times B_{i-1}.
$$

The equality holds because commutators supported in either coordinate generate the corresponding factor independently. Therefore

$$
\boxed{1=A_0\times B_0\le\cdots\le A_t\times B_t=A\times B}
$$

is a [central series](../../../../../../central-series.md), proving that **a direct product of two nilpotent groups is nilpotent**. More precisely, the coordinatewise recurrence for the [lower central series](../../../../../../lower-central-series.md) gives $\Gamma_j(A\times B)=\Gamma_j(A)\times\Gamma_j(B)$, so the [nilpotency class of a direct product](../../../../../../nilpotency-class-of-a-direct-product.md) is the maximum of the two factor classes.
