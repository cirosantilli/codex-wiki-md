A [central series](../../../../../../central-series.md) is a finite chain of [normal subgroups](../../../../../../normal-subgroup.md)

$$
1=G_0\le G_1\le\cdots\le G_r=G
$$

with $G_i/G_{i-1}\le Z(G/G_{i-1})$ for every $i\ge1$. Equivalently, writing $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ and $[A,B]$ for the subgroup generated by these [group commutators](../../../../../../group-commutator.md), the condition is $[G_i,G]\le G_{i-1}$. Centrality is relative to the whole [quotient group](../../../../../../quotient-group.md), not just to the next subgroup in the chain.

**A group is nilpotent if it admits a finite [central series](../../../../../../central-series.md).** The trivial group admits a series of length zero. For a nontrivial [nilpotent group](../../../../../../nilpotent-group.md), the minimum possible length is its [nilpotency class](../../../../../../nilpotency-class.md).
