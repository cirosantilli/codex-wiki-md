<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $p_j$ of a [density operator](../../../../../../density-matrix.md) are nonnegative and sum to one. Therefore

$$
1-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^2)=\left(\sum_jp_j\right)^2-\sum_jp_j^2=2\sum_{j<k}p_jp_k.
$$

For a non-pure state at least two [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are positive, so the last expression is strictly positive. Combining this with the [Generalized Bloch representation](../../../../../../generalized-bloch-representation.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\|s\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^2)-\frac1N<1-\frac1N.}
$$

Thus every non-pure [quantum state](../../../../../../quantum-state.md) lies strictly inside the radius sphere for $N\geq2$. It need not be an interior point of the entire physical state body: for $N>2$, a rank-deficient mixed [density operator](../../../../../../density-matrix.md) can lie on that body's boundary while still lying inside the Euclidean sphere.
