<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

By [orthonormality](../../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) in the [Generalized Bloch representation](../../../../../../generalized-bloch-representation.md),

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^2)=\frac1N+\sum_{k=1}^{N^2-1}s_k^2.
$$

A [pure state](../../../../../../pure-state.md) has [density operator](../../../../../../density-matrix.md) $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ with $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1$, so $\rho^2=\rho$ and its [purity of a density operator](../../../../../../purity-of-a-density-operator.md) is one. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\|s\|^2=1-\frac1N.}
$$

Every [pure state](../../../../../../pure-state.md) therefore maps to the specified radius-$\sqrt{1-1/N}$ [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) in $\mathbb R^{N^2-1}$, of dimension $N^2-2$. The expansion is unique, so it distinguishes pure-state [density operators](../../../../../../density-matrix.md), while vectors differing only by [global phase](../../../../../../global-phase.md) describe the same point. This proves that pure states lie on the sphere, without asserting that they fill it.
