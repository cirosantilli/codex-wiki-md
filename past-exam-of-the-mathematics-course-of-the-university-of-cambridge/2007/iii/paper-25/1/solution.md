<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Both alternatives can be proved using the same smallness mechanism. We first establish the [initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set](../../../../../initial-object-lemma-for-complete-categories-with-a-weakly-initial-set.md), then obtain the two [adjoint functor](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) existence theorems and their applications.

Let $\mathcal E$ be a [locally small category](../../../../../locally-small-category.md) with all small [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) and a [weakly initial set](../../../../../weakly-initial-set.md) $(W_i)_{i\in I}$. Its [product in a category](../../../../../product-category-theory.md) $W=\prod_iW_i$ is weakly initial: given any object, choose a map from some $W_i$ and compose with the corresponding projection. Take the simultaneous [equalizer](../../../../../equaliser.md) $e:E\to W$ of every endomorphism of $W$ and its identity; local smallness makes this a small [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md). Then $E$ is also weakly initial. If $a,b:E\to X$, form their [equalizer](../../../../../equaliser.md) $j:Y\to E$ and choose $t:W\to Y$. Since $ejt$ is an endomorphism of $W$, the construction of $e$ gives $ejte=e$. Cancelling the [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md) $e$ yields $jte=1_E$. Thus $j$ is both a [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md) and a [split epimorphism](../../../../../split-epimorphism.md), hence invertible, and $a=b$. There is therefore exactly one arrow from $E$ to every object: $E$ is initial.

The [Freyd general adjoint functor theorem](../../../../../freyd-general-adjoint-functor-theorem.md) says that for a [functor](../../../../../functor.md) $U:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ between [locally small categories](../../../../../locally-small-category.md), with $\mathcal A$ complete, a [left adjoint](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) exists exactly when $U$ preserves all small [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) and satisfies the [solution-set condition](../../../../../solution-set-condition.md). That condition requires, for each $B$, a set of arrows $u_i:B\to UA_i$ such that every $u:B\to UA$ factors as $U(f)u_i$ for some $i$ and $f:A_i\to A$.

For sufficiency, the [comma category](../../../../../comma-category.md) $(B\downarrow U)$ is locally small and complete: form a diagram's [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) in $\mathcal A$, and use [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) preservation to obtain its arrow from $B$. The solution set is a [weakly initial set](../../../../../weakly-initial-set.md) in this [comma category](../../../../../comma-category.md). The lemma provides an [initial object](../../../../../initial-object.md) $(LB,\eta_B:B\to ULB)$. Its [universal property](../../../../../universal-property.md) gives

$$
\mathcal A(LB,A)\cong\mathcal B(B,UA),\qquad f\longmapsto U(f)\eta_B.
$$

For $h:B\to B'$, define $Lh$ by $U(Lh)\eta_B=\eta_{B'}h$. Uniqueness proves the identity and composition laws and [naturality](../../../../../naturality.md) of the displayed bijections, hence $L\dashv U$. Conversely, an [adjunction](../../../../../adjoint-functors.md) makes $U$ preserve [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md), since the corresponding [hom-set](../../../../../hom-set.md) [functors](../../../../../functor.md) turn a [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) cone into a [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) of sets. The single arrow $\eta_B$ is a solution set. This proves both directions of the theorem.

Now let $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ have small domain and codomain. The [functor categories](../../../../../functor-category.md) $[\mathcal D,\mathbf{Set}]$ and $[\mathcal C,\mathbf{Set}]$ are locally small, and their [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) are pointwise. Thus the precomposition [functor](../../../../../functor.md) $F^*$ preserves [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) and its domain is complete. To verify its [solution-set condition](../../../../../solution-set-condition.md), fix $X:\mathcal C\to\mathbf{Set}$ and an arrow $x:X\to F^*Y$. Let $Y_0\subseteq Y$ be the subfunctor generated by all elements $x_c(a)$, where $c\in\mathcal C$ and $a\in X(c)$. Explicitly,

$$
Y_0(d)=\{Y(g)(x_c(a)):g:Fc\to d,\ a\in X(c)\}.
$$

These sets are stable under every $Y(h)$. If $\kappa$ is an infinite cardinal bounding $|\operatorname{Mor}\mathcal D|$ and $\left|\coprod_cX(c)\right|$, every $Y_0(d)$ has cardinal at most $\kappa$. Replace each such set by a subset of a fixed set of size $\kappa$. There is only a set of choices of these subsets, of their action maps for the set of arrows of $\mathcal D$, and of maps from $X$ to their restrictions. These coded pairs form a solution set: $x$ factors through $F^*Y_0$ and then the inclusion $Y_0\hookrightarrow Y$. The general theorem now yields

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Lan}_F\dashv F^*.}
$$

Its values may also be written $\operatorname{Lan}_FX(d)=\operatorname{colim}_{(Fc\to d)}X(c)$, the usual [left Kan extension](../../../../../left-kan-extension.md) formula.

For the other alternative, the [categorical limit](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) form of the [Special adjoint functor theorem](../../../../../special-adjoint-functor-theorem.md) says that a limit-preserving [functor](../../../../../functor.md) $U:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ has a left adjoint if $\mathcal A$ is locally small, complete and [well-powered](../../../../../well-powered-category.md), has a [cogenerating set](../../../../../cogenerating-set.md) $(Q_i)_{i\in I}$, and $\mathcal B$ is locally small. A cogenerating set means that maps into its members distinguish any two parallel arrows. The dual theorem says that a colimit-preserving [functor](../../../../../functor.md) from a locally small, cocomplete, [well-copowered category](../../../../../well-copowered-category.md) with a [small generating family](../../../../../generating-set-in-a-category.md) has a right adjoint. These conclusions are also necessary in their preservation clauses.

Here is a proof of the special theorem rather than an invocation of the general theorem without its required solution set. Given $(A,x:B\to UA)$, intersect all subobjects $m:M\hookrightarrow A$ through which $x$ factors after application of $U$. There are only a set of them by well-poweredness. Their intersection is a wide pullback, preserved by $U$, so gives a smallest supporting pair $(A_0,x_0)$. If $p,q:A_0\to Q_i$ satisfy $U(p)x_0=U(q)x_0$, their [equalizer](../../../../../equaliser.md) is a supporting subobject. Minimality makes that [equalizer](../../../../../equaliser.md) invertible, whence $p=q$. Consequently

$$
\mathcal A(A_0,Q_i)\longrightarrow\mathcal B(B,UQ_i),\qquad p\longmapsto U(p)x_0,
$$

is injective. The cogenerating property makes the evaluation arrow from $A_0$ into the [product in a category](../../../../../product-category-theory.md) of the $Q_i$, one factor for each map $A_0\to Q_i$, a [monic arrow](../../../../../monomorphism.md). Its indexing set at each $i$ is identified with a subset of the fixed set $\mathcal B(B,UQ_i)$. Only a set of such [products in a category](../../../../../product-category-theory.md) can occur. Each has only a set of subobjects, and each possible subobject has only a set of arrows from $B$ into its image under $U$. These pairs give a [weakly initial set](../../../../../weakly-initial-set.md) in $(B\downarrow U)$, since every original pair receives the arrow from its minimal supporting pair. The complete-category lemma now gives the adjoint. Reversing all arrows proves the [colimit](../../../../../colimit.md) form of the special theorem.

Apply that dual form to $F^*:[\mathcal D,\mathbf{Set}]\to[\mathcal C,\mathbf{Set}]$. Its domain is cocomplete and locally small, and $F^*$ preserves pointwise [colimits](../../../../../colimit.md). The set of covariant [representable functors](../../../../../representable-functor.md) $\mathcal D(d,-)$ generates the domain: by the [Yoneda lemma](../../../../../yoneda-lemma.md), maps from these representables probe every element at every object and hence distinguish distinct [natural transformations](../../../../../natural-transformation.md). Finally, [epimorphisms](../../../../../epimorphism.md) in this [functor category](../../../../../functor-category.md) are pointwise surjective. Indeed, their pointwise images form subfunctors; if an image is proper, the two inclusions into the pointwise pushout of two copies of the target over that image distinguish the original map, contradicting epicity. Quotients of a fixed [functor](../../../../../functor.md) are therefore specified by compatible equivalence relations on its sets of values, and form a set. Thus the domain is a [well-copowered category](../../../../../well-copowered-category.md). All special-theorem hypotheses hold, giving

$$
\boxed{F^*\dashv\operatorname{Ran}_F.}
$$

Here the [Right Kan extension](../../../../../right-kan-extension.md) can be written $\operatorname{Ran}_FX(d)=\operatorname{Nat}(\mathcal D(d,F-),X)$. In particular both requested adjoints exist, although the two proofs use different smallness hypotheses.
