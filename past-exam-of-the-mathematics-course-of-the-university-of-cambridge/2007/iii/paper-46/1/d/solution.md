<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

This is a family-level [cluster-randomized trial](../../../../../../cluster-randomized-trial.md), but there are only about 1.1 contributing children per family. Most families therefore contribute a single eligible child, leaving little opportunity for within-family [intraclass correlation](../../../../../../intraclass-correlation-coefficient.md) to add much uncertainty. The equal-size exchangeable approximation has [design effect](../../../../../../design-effect.md) $1+(m-1)\rho$, with cluster size $m$ and correlation $\rho$; when $m$ is close to one, this is close to one. A multilevel [Poisson regression](../../../../../../poisson-regression.md) can consequently give nearly the same standard error as the independent-child working analysis.

Agreement is compatible with limited within-family dependence and little change from covariate adjustment; it does not prove all dependence absent. Mean family size alone does not determine the exact [design effect](../../../../../../design-effect.md) for highly unequal cluster sizes.

Larger differences would be expected with **larger families and stronger within-family correlation**, especially with unequal family sizes. Pronounced between-family heterogeneity, recurrent attendances concentrated in particular children, or important imbalanced covariates can also create [overdispersion](../../../../../../overdispersion.md) or change the adjusted comparison.
