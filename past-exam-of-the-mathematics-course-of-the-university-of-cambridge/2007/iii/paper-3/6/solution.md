Choose a maximal [Brauer pair](../../../../../brauer-pair.md) $(D,b_D)$ for $B$, and let $N_G(D,b_D)$ be the subgroup normalizing $D$ and stabilizing the local block $b_D$. The [inertial quotient of a block](../../../../../inertial-quotient-of-a-block.md) and its order, the [inertial index of a block](../../../../../inertial-index-of-a-block.md), are

$$
E(B)=N_G(D,b_D)/(DC_G(D)),\qquad e=|E(B)|.
$$

The [Brauer extended first main theorem](../../../../../brauer-extended-first-main-theorem.md) says that maximal local blocks of defect $D$ are parametrized by the normalizer orbits of defect-$D$ blocks of $k(DC_G(D))$, with stabilizer quotient of order prime to $p$. For abelian $D$, conjugation embeds $E(B)$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(D)$. When $D=C_{p^n}$, its prime-to-$p$ subgroups are cyclic of order dividing $p-1$, so $e\mid p-1$.

We use two precise structural results for a [block with cyclic defect group](../../../../../block-with-cyclic-defect-group.md), with $k$ an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md). First, the [basic algebra](../../../../../basic-algebra.md) is a [Brauer tree algebra](../../../../../brauer-tree-algebra.md) with $e$ edges and exceptional multiplicity $m=(p^n-1)/e$; its simple modules are indexed by its edges. Second, its [stable Auslander–Reiten quiver](../../../../../stable-auslander-reiten-quiver.md) has shape

$$
\mathbb ZA_{p^n-1}/\langle\tau^e\rangle.
$$

The vertices are the isomorphism classes of nonprojective indecomposable modules. These are the cyclic-defect structure and indecomposable-classification results, rather than a claim that all Brauer trees are Morita equivalent to a star. The tube description is also recorded in [Hiss–Lassueur, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07833).

The first result gives $l(B)=e$. In the second, there are $p^n-1$ positions in each slice and $e$ slices modulo the translation. Thus there are $e(p^n-1)$ nonprojective indecomposables. A finite-dimensional split [algebra](../../../../../algebra-split.md) has one [principal indecomposable module](../../../../../principal-indecomposable-module.md) that is a [projective cover](../../../../../projective-cover.md) for each [simple module](../../../../../irreducible-module.md), so there are another $e$ projective indecomposables. Therefore

$$
\boxed{l(B)=e,\qquad\#\operatorname{ind}(B)=e(p^n-1)+e=p^n e.}
$$

The total includes projectives; it is different from the count of ordinary irreducible characters. This is the [indecomposable count for a cyclic defect block](../../../../../indecomposable-count-for-a-cyclic-defect-block.md).

Now let $p=2$ and $D=C_2\times C_2$, the [Klein four-group](../../../../../klein-four-group.md). The extended theorem makes $E(B)$ a subgroup of order prime to $2$ of $\operatorname{Aut}(D)\cong\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\cong S_3$. Its order divides $6$ and is odd, so

$$
\boxed{e=1\text{ or }3.}
$$

For the remaining conclusion we use the central-defect quotient fact: if $Z\le Z(L)$ is a central $p$-subgroup, blocks of $kL$ correspond to blocks of $k(L/Z)$ under the quotient, and their defect groups quotient by $Z$. Central block idempotents lift uniquely here because the kernel is generated by central nilpotent elements; an off-diagonal corner lies in its own product with that nilpotent ideal and is therefore zero. Defect groups quotient by $Z$ by the relative-trace, or equivalently Brauer-pair, characterization. In particular a block with central defect group $D$ has a defect-zero block as its quotient by $J(kD)$.

First suppose $D\le Z(G)$, and write $A=B$. Since $k$ is an [algebraically closed field](../../../../../algebraically-closed-field.md), the defect-zero quotient is $\overline A\cong\operatorname{Mat}_r(k)$ for some $r$. The [algebra](../../../../../algebra-split.md) $A$ is a direct summand of $kG$ as a $kD$-module, hence is projective and therefore free over the [local ring](../../../../../local-ring.md) $kD$. Its rank is $r^2$, since reduction modulo $J(kD)$ gives the $r^2$-dimensional quotient. Lift a primitive idempotent $\bar f$ of $\overline A$ to $f\in A$. The corner $fAf$ is a $kD$-linear direct summand of $A$ and is free over $kD$. Its quotient by $J(kD)$ is $\bar f\overline A\bar f\cong k$, so its rank is one. The central map

$$
kD\longrightarrow fAf,\qquad z\longmapsto zf
$$

is surjective by [Nakayama's lemma](../../../../../nakayama-lemma.md), since $f$ generates its reduction, and is injective because both sides have dimension $|D|$. Thus $fAf\cong kD$ as algebras. Orthogonal primitive idempotents and their equivalences in the matrix quotient lift modulo the nilpotent ideal; lifting the equivalence maps and correcting their products by units lifts the matrix units. Consequently

$$
A\cong\operatorname{Mat}_r(fAf)\cong\operatorname{Mat}_r(kD).
$$

This proves the actual algebra isomorphism of [central defect blocks are matrix algebras](../../../../../central-defect-blocks-are-matrix-algebras.md), as an [algebra](../../../../../algebra-split.md).

Finally suppose $D\triangleleft G$ but is not central. Put $C=C_G(D)$, which is normal in $G$ and contains $D$ since $D$ is abelian. The normal-subgroup argument in Question 5 puts the block idempotent $e_B$ in $kC$. It is therefore the sum of the $G$-conjugates of a block idempotent $c$ of $kC$. The extended first main theorem chooses such a $c$ with defect group $D$; since $D$ is normal, its pair stabilizer is exactly $I=\{g\in G:{}^gc=c\}$. Now

$$
e=[I:DC]=[I:C]=1,
$$

so $I=C$. Within $C$, the defect group $D$ is central, and the first case gives $kCc\cong\operatorname{Mat}_r(kD)$.

We can see the [Fong–Reynolds matrix decomposition](../../../../../fong-reynolds-matrix-decomposition.md) explicitly in this case. Choose representatives $g_1,\ldots,g_t$ for $G/C$, where $t=[G:C]$, and put

$$
u_{ij}=g_i c g_j^{-1}.
$$

Different conjugates of $c$ are orthogonal, so $u_{ij}u_{\ell m}=\delta_{j\ell}u_{im}$ and $\sum_i u_{ii}=e_B$. Moreover $cBc=ckGc=kCc$: a term $ckCgc$ vanishes unless $g$ stabilizes $c$, and that stabilizer is $C$. These matrix units therefore identify $B$ with $\operatorname{Mat}_t(kCc)$, whence

$$
\boxed{B\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{tr}(kD).}
$$

The inertial index condition concerns the stabilizer of the local block; it does not require every element of $G$ to centralize $D$. That distinction is why the second case is necessary.
