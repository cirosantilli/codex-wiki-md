<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let the two tangential [aperture](../../../../../../aperture.md) fields be $F_y(y,z)$ and $F_z(y,z)$, extended by zero off the [aperture](../../../../../../aperture.md). The normal component does not enter $\mathbf n\times\mathbf E_{\rm inc}$, which equals $-F_z\mathbf e_y+F_y\mathbf e_z$. Apply the [Weyl plane-wave representation](../../../../../../weyl-plane-wave-representation.md) and [curl](../../../../../../curl.md) to both components. With the same kernel normalization as part (a),

$$
\boxed{\mathbf E(\mathbf r)=\frac1{2(2\pi)^2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\left[\widehat F_y\left(\mathbf e_y-\frac\nu\kappa\mathbf e_x\right)
+\widehat F_z\left(\mathbf e_z-\frac\eta\kappa\mathbf e_x\right)\right]
e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\,d\nu\,d\eta}.
$$

This is the [electromagnetic aperture angular spectrum](../../../../../../electromagnetic-aperture-angular-spectrum.md). The two [electromagnetic polarization](../../../../../../polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) vectors are linearly independent and both orthogonal, bilinearly, to $\mathbf q$; they need not be orthogonal to each other. Arbitrary tangential [electromagnetic polarization](../../../../../../polarization-of-an-electromagnetic-wave.md) is therefore represented, with its outgoing longitudinal component fixed by $\kappa E_x+\nu E_y+\eta E_z=0$ rather than independently prescribed. Away from grazing components one could equally express the spectrum in a transverse-electric and transverse-magnetic basis. The same limiting outgoing branch handles grazing and [evanescent](../../../../../../evanescent-wave.md) components.

For each component the magnetic amplitude follows from [Faraday's law](../../../../../../faraday-s-law-of-induction.md) as $\mathbf B=\mathbf q\times\mathbf E/\omega$. Together with $\mathbf q^2=k^2$ and [transversality of an electromagnetic plane wave](../../../../../../transversality-of-an-electromagnetic-plane-wave.md), this verifies the source-free [Maxwell equations](../../../../../../maxwell-equations.md). If the doubled-kernel [aperture](../../../../../../aperture.md) convention is used, multiply the entire expression by two, exactly as in part (a).
