<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Write $N=\Gamma'$. As a subgroup of a discrete group, it is a [Kleinian group](../../../../../../kleinian-group.md), even if it is not finitely generated. Since it is infinite, part (b) gives a nonempty closed [Kleinian limit set](../../../../../../limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group.md) $\Lambda(N)$. For $\gamma\in\Gamma$, conjugation and base-point independence give

$$
\gamma\Lambda(N)=\Lambda(\gamma N\gamma^{-1}).
$$

One can see this directly by applying $\gamma$ to $No$: the image is the orbit of $\gamma o$ under the conjugate subgroup, and the choice of interior point does not affect its limit set. Normality makes the conjugate subgroup equal to $N$, so $\Lambda(N)$ is $\Gamma$-invariant. Applying part (c) yields $\Lambda(\Gamma)\subseteq\Lambda(N)$.

Conversely $No\subseteq\Gamma o$, so every boundary accumulation point of the smaller orbit is one of the larger orbit: $\Lambda(N)\subseteq\Lambda(\Gamma)$. Therefore the [limit set of an infinite normal Kleinian subgroup](../../../../../../limit-set-of-an-infinite-normal-kleinian-subgroup.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{\Lambda(\Gamma')=\Lambda(\Gamma).}
$$

Infinitude supplies nonemptiness, normality supplies invariance, and non-elementarity supplies minimality. Properness of the subgroup is not needed, and no finite-generation assumption on it has been inserted.
