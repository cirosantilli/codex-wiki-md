<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Fix an interior point $o\in\mathbb H^3$. The [limit set of a Kleinian group](../../../../../../limit-set-of-a-kleinian-group.md) is the set of boundary accumulation points of its orbit, or

$$
\boxed{\Lambda(\Gamma)=\overline{\Gamma o}^{\,\mathbb H^3\cup S^2_\infty}\cap S^2_\infty.}
$$

The boundary compactification may be realized as the closed unit ball. A discrete isometry group has only finitely many orbit points in each compact interior region: isometries moving $o$ into a compact set form a compact family after accounting for the compact rotation stabilizer, and infinitely many distinct group elements there would contradict discreteness. The stabilizer of $o$ is finite for the same reason. Thus an infinite group has an infinite orbit escaping every compact interior set and a nonempty boundary accumulation set. A finite group has empty limit set. This also shows the displayed intersection does not introduce ordinary interior accumulation points.

For another point $o'$, the corresponding points satisfy $d(\gamma o,\gamma o')=d(o,o')=:C$. In the Poincaré ball model the distance identity is

$$
\sinh\frac{d(u,v)}2=\frac{|u-v|}{\sqrt{(1-|u|^2)(1-|v|^2)}}.
$$

If $\gamma_j o\to\xi\in S^2_\infty$, bounded distance implies

$$
|\gamma_j o-\gamma_j o'|
\le\sinh(C/2)\sqrt{(1-|\gamma_j o|^2)(1-|\gamma_j o'|^2)}\longrightarrow0.
$$

So $\gamma_j o'\to\xi$ as well. Exchanging $o,o'$ proves equality of the two accumulation sets. Hence the definition is **independent of the interior base point**.

It is closed as an intersection of the compact orbit closure with the closed boundary, and it is $\Gamma$-invariant because multiplying an orbit by an element of $\Gamma$ permutes that orbit. Changing ball coordinates or conjugating the geometric realization transports the set by the induced boundary [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md). Different nonconjugate representations of an abstract group are not asserted to have the same subset of one fixed sphere. None of these arguments requires finite generation.
