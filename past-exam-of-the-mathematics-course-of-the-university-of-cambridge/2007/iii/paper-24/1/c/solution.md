<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Use the quotient [lens spaces](../../../../../../lens-space.md) and set $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/7}$. Let $g_j(z_1,z_2)=(\zeta z_1,\zeta^jz_2)$. The map

$$
F:S^3\to S^3,\qquad F(z_1,z_2)=(z_2,\overline{z_1})
$$

is a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md) and satisfies

$$
F g_2(z_1,z_2)=(\zeta^2z_2,\zeta^{-1}\overline{z_1})
=g_3^2F(z_1,z_2),
$$

because $3\cdot2\equiv-1\pmod7$. Since $2$ is invertible modulo $7$, $g_3^2$ generates the entire target deck group. Hence $F$ maps cyclic orbits bijectively to cyclic orbits, as does its inverse, and descends to a [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md):

$$
\boxed{L(7,2)\cong L(7,3).}
$$

This is an explicit map, so no classification theorem for [lens spaces](../../../../../../lens-space.md) is required. It reverses [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md): complex conjugation in one coordinate reverses the ambient real [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md), while swapping the two complex coordinates preserves it.
