<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For the intended standard [Hopf link](../../../../../../hopf-link.md), identify $S^3$ with the unit sphere in $\mathbb C^2$ and its two components with the coordinate circles $z_1=0$ and $z_2=0$. Outside small neighborhoods of those circles, both coordinates are nonzero, and their arguments together with $|z_1|$ give

$$
S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N_L\cong T^2\times I.
$$

On this product the longitude of either component is the meridian of the other. Therefore the two attaching meridians become the two coordinate curves of $T^2$, intersecting exactly once. Adding the product collar does not change a gluing of [solid tori](../../../../../../solid-torus.md). The meridian-intersection-one argument in Question 2(c) identifies the result with the standard genus-one splitting of the sphere. Thus [zero surgery on the Hopf link](../../../../../../zero-surgery-on-the-hopf-link.md) gives

$$
\boxed{M\cong S^3.}
$$

There is a genuine link-type qualification in the wording: merely specifying two linked but individually unknotted components does not determine the [Hopf link](../../../../../../hopf-link.md). For example the two components of the torus link $T(2,4)$ are unknotted but have [linking number](../../../../../../linking-number.md) $2$. Filling their preferred longitudes is zero surgery with linking matrix

$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

so the resulting [first homology](../../../../../../first-homology.md) is its cokernel, $\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/2$, rather than the zero group of $S^3$. The cokernel formula follows because zero longitudes impose twice the other meridian as the homology relations. Thus the sphere answer applies to the standard once-linked Hopf interpretation; the literal unspecified link description alone is insufficient to force a single manifold.
