<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Choose an essential simple closed curve $c$ in the oriented [topological surface](../../../../../../topological-surface.md) and an annular neighborhood $N(c)\cong S^1\times[0,1]$. A positive [Dehn twist](../../../../../../dehn-twist.md) about $c$ is represented on this annulus by

$$
\boxed{\tau_c(e^{2\pi i\theta},s)=(e^{2\pi i(\theta+\rho(s))},s),}
$$

where $\rho$ is zero near $0$, one near $1$, and increases between them. Since a full turn is the identity on the boundary circle, this map is the identity near both boundary components of the annulus and extends by the identity outside it. It preserves [orientation](../../../../../../orientation-of-a-simplex.md) and turns one end relative to the other by one full turn. An inverse twist uses $-\rho$.

More intrinsically, $h$ is a [Dehn twist](../../../../../../dehn-twist.md) if its [mapping class](../../../../../../mapping-class.md) is that of this annulus-supported [homeomorphism](../../../../../../homeomorphism.md), for some curve $c$ and the chosen handedness. Choices of annular coordinates and interpolation give isotopic maps. The genus hypothesis permits essential curves, but the definition also makes sense for boundary-parallel curves on surfaces with boundary.
