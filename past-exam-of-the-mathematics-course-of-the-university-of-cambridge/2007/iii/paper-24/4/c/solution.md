<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each component is an [unknot](../../../../../../unknot.md), so its closed exterior $S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}U_i$ is a [solid torus](../../../../../../solid-torus.md). Choose the usual boundary [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) $(\mu_i,\lambda_i)$, where $\mu_i$ is a meridian of the component and $\lambda_i$ its [Seifert longitude](../../../../../../seifert-longitude.md). In the exterior's [fundamental group](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md), $\mu_i$ generates $\mathbb Z$ and $\lambda_i$ is trivial, since it bounds a meridian disk of that exterior.

Every essential embedded circle on a torus represents a primitive homology class $a\mu_i+b\lambda_i$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$. If it bounds a disk in the exterior, it is null-homotopic there, so $a=0$. Primitivity then forces $b=\pm1$. Essential simple curves of the same primitive unoriented slope on a torus are isotopic: lift them to the plane and straighten their parallel primitive lines, or cut the torus along one curve to obtain an annulus. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\alpha_i\text{ is isotopic to the preferred longitude }\lambda_i.}
$$

This is the [preferred longitude of an unknot](../../../../../../preferred-longitude-of-an-unknot.md). The disk is only required to lie in the complement of its own [solid torus](../../../../../../solid-torus.md); it may meet the other link component, so no additional disjoint-disk assertion is being used.
