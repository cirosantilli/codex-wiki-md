<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For every binary vector $x$, $S_1(x,x)=1$, and hence

$$
\boxed{d_2(x,x)=\frac12\ne0.}
$$

Thus **$d_2$ is not a [metric](../../../../../../../metric.md)**, because it fails identity of indiscernibles. It is nonnegative and symmetric. In fact it also satisfies the [triangle inequality](../../../../../../../triangle-inequality.md) trivially: every value lies in $[1/2,1]$, so $d_2(x,z)\le1\le d_2(x,y)+d_2(y,z)$. That does not repair its nonzero self-distance.
