<h1 id="11f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Compare constant terms in the [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) identity proved above. The constant term of $X^{p-1}-1$ is $-1$, while that of the product is $(-1)^{p-1}(p-1)!$. For odd $p$, this gives $(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod p$. For $p=2$, the same conclusion holds because $1\equiv-1\pmod2$. Thus [Wilson's theorem](../../../../../../wilson-s-theorem.md) follows:

$$
\boxed{(p-1)!+1\equiv0\pmod p.}
$$
