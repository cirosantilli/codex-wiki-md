<h1 id="3g/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3G](../3g.md)

For a [plane crystallographic group](../../../../../wallpaper-group.md), the translation [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) consists of translations by [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) in a rank-two [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) $\Lambda$; the [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) is this additive group of translation [vectors](../../../../../vector.md). The [point group](../../../../../point-group.md) is the image under the linear-part [homomorphism](../../../../../homomorphism.md) $G\to O(2)$, equivalently $G/\Lambda$. Conjugating a translation by an isometry with linear part $A$ gives translation by $Av$, so every point-group element preserves $\Lambda$.

Choose a [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) basis. A point-group rotation is represented in that basis by an integral [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md), because it maps [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) [vectors](../../../../../vector.md) to [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) [vectors](../../../../../vector.md), and its inverse does likewise. The [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) is therefore an integer. In an [orthonormal basis](../../../../../orthonormal-basis.md) the same rotation has [trace](../../../../../matrix-trace.md) $2\cos\theta$, which lies between minus two and two. Hence $2\cos\theta\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. The value two gives the identity, while the other values give angles $\pi$, $\pm2\pi/3$, $\pm\pi/2$, or $\pm\pi/3$. Thus every nonidentity rotation has order

$$
\boxed{2,3,4,\text{ or }6.}
$$

This proves the [crystallographic restriction theorem](../../../../../crystallographic-restriction-theorem.md) from the [lattice](../../../../../lattice.md) action rather than assuming the classification of wallpaper groups.
