<h1 id="14e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [centre manifold theorem](../../../../../../centre-manifold-theorem.md) says that near an [equilibrium](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) of a sufficiently smooth finite-dimensional [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) field, there is a locally invariant [centre manifold](../../../../../../center-manifold.md) tangent to the generalized eigenspace of [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) with zero real part. It can be represented as a graph over that space, with graph value and derivative zero at the [equilibrium](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md); the dynamics on it determine the local centre-direction behaviour. For parameter-dependent systems the parameters may be adjoined as variables with zero derivative, producing an extended [centre manifold](../../../../../../center-manifold.md).

At $r=1$, the linearization has the $x,y$ block $\begin{pmatrix}-\sigma&\sigma\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ and the independent [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $-b$. Its other [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are zero and $-(\sigma+1)$. Hence **the [equilibrium](../../../../../../equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system.md) is nonhyperbolic, the stable subspace has dimension two, and the non-extended centre subspace has dimension one**. A centre [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md) is $(1,1,0)$.
