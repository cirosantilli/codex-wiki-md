<h1 id="8b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the second-order Fuchsian equation represented by the [Papperitz symbol](../../../../../../papperitz-symbol.md), the coefficient of $w'$ is

$$
p(z)=\frac{1-\alpha-\alpha'}z+\frac{1-\gamma-\gamma'}{z-1}.
$$

This follows from the sums of local exponents at its two finite [regular singular points](../../../../../../regular-singular-point.md). Abel's [Wronskian](../../../../../../wronskian.md) identity, obtained by differentiating $w_1w_2'-w_1'w_2$ and using the differential equation, gives $W'=-pW$. Thus, on every branch domain avoiding zero and one,

$$
\frac d{dz}\left[z^{1-\alpha-\alpha'}(1-z)^{1-\gamma-\gamma'}W(z)\right]=0.
$$

The normalized expression is locally constant. Its analytic continuation around either singularity is unchanged: the power factors cancel the [determinant](../../../../../../determinant.md) of the local [monodromy](../../../../../../monodromy.md), equivalently the integral of $-p$ around that singularity. It therefore defines a single-valued [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) on the punctured plane, locally constant near both punctures. Those punctures are removable, so **the normalized expression extends to an entire function**. This argument also covers resonant exponents and avoids assuming two pure power solutions in that case.
