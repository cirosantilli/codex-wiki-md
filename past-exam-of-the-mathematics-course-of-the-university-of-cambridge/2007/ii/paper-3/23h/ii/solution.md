<h1 id="23h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Assume $X$ is a compact smooth $n$-manifold without boundary and $Y$ a connected smooth $n$-manifold without boundary. For a [regular value](../../../../../../regular-value.md) $y$, the inverse function theorem makes $f^{-1}(y)$ discrete, and [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) makes it finite. Define **$\deg_2f=|f^{-1}(y)|\pmod2$**. No orientations are required.

The parity is independent of the [regular value](../../../../../../regular-value.md): join two regular values by a path and perturb the path in its interior to be transverse to $f$. Its inverse image is a compact one-dimensional manifold with boundary consisting of the two endpoint fibres. Every compact one-manifold has an even number of boundary points, so the two counts have the same parity. The same argument proves homotopy invariance. For noncompact $X$ one instead needs a proper map to retain [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of the relevant inverse images.

If $f$ omitted a point of $Y$, that point would be a [regular value](../../../../../../regular-value.md) with empty fibre and hence degree zero. Independence on the connected target gives the contradiction. Therefore **a nonzero degree modulo two forces surjectivity**.
