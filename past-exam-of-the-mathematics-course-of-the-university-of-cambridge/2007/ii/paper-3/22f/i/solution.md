<h1 id="22f/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose coordinates $z$ at $p$ and $w$ at $f(p)$ vanishing at those points. The local expansion is $w\circ f\circ z^{-1}(\zeta)=a\zeta^v+O(\zeta^{v+1})$, $a\ne0$. Define the branching order $v_f(p)=v$. A change of either coordinate has nonzero linear coefficient, so it preserves the leading exponent, proving well-definedness.

Ramification means $v>1$, equivalently vanishing of the derivative in local coordinates. These zeros are isolated unless the local derivative is identically zero, which by analytic continuation would make $f$ constant. An infinite set of [ramification points](../../../../../../ramification-point-of-a-holomorphic-map.md) on a compact surface would have an accumulation point, contradicting isolation. Thus that set is finite.

A nonconstant [holomorphic map](../../../../../../holomorphic-map.md) of compact connected Riemann surfaces is onto, since its image is open by the local form and closed by [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md). It has a finite degree $d\geq1$. The [Riemann-Hurwitz formula](../../../../../../riemann-hurwitz-formula.md) is

$$
2g_R-2=d(2g_S-2)+\sum_{p\in R}(v_f(p)-1).
$$

If both genera equal $g>1$, positivity of $2g-2$ forces $d=1$ and every ramification term zero. The map is bijective and has nonzero derivative everywhere. The [holomorphic inverse function theorem](../../../../../../holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem.md) says that a [holomorphic function](../../../../../../holomorphic-function.md) with nonzero derivative has a local holomorphic inverse; these inverses patch, so **$f$ is biholomorphic**. For $g=1$, multiplication by two on $\mathbb C/\Lambda$ is a degree-four [holomorphic map](../../../../../../holomorphic-map.md) of an [elliptic curve](../../../../../../elliptic-curve.md) to itself and is not biholomorphic.
