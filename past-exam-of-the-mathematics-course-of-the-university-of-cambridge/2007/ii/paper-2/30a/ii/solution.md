<h1 id="30a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $g\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$ define [convolution](../../../../../../convolution.md) by

$$
\boxed{(T*g)(x)=\langle T_y,g(x-y)\rangle.}
$$

This is smooth and has at most [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) growth, hence defines a [tempered distribution](../../../../../../tempered-distribution.md). Its equivalent pairing is $\langle T*g,\phi\rangle=\langle T,\widetilde g*\phi\rangle$ with $\widetilde g(y)=g(-y)$. Fourier duality and the usual Schwartz-function [convolution](../../../../../../convolution.md) calculation give

$$
\boxed{\widehat{T*g}=\widehat T\,\widehat g.}
$$

The product on the right is distributional multiplication by the smooth [Schwartz function](../../../../../../schwartz-function.md) $\widehat g$. With the convention in part (i), no extra factor occurs. One can verify it first for functions by changing $x=y+z$ inside the two integrals, then use the displayed pairing to perform the same calculation for arbitrary $T$.
