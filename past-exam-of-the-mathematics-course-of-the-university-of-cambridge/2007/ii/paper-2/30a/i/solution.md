<h1 id="30a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the Fourier convention $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx$, with inverse $(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat f(\xi)d\xi$. For a [tempered distribution](../../../../../../tempered-distribution.md) $T$, define its [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) by duality:

$$
\boxed{\langle\widehat T,\phi\rangle=\langle T,\widehat\phi\rangle,\qquad\phi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3).}
$$

The [Fourier transform](../../../../../../fourier-transform.md) maps the [Schwartz space](../../../../../../schwartz-space.md) continuously onto itself, making the right side a continuous linear functional there. The use of the same negative exponential in the test-function transform is consistent with bilinear distributional pairing; other unitary or sign conventions change the normalization accordingly.
