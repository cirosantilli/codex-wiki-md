<h1 id="20h/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Since $10\equiv2\pmod4$, the [ring of integers](../../../../../../ring-of-integers.md) is $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{10}]$, with [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) $N(x+y\sqrt{10})=x^2-10y^2$. Norms $\pm2$ and $\pm3$ are impossible: modulo five they would require a square congruent to 2 or 3. An [algebraic integer](../../../../../../algebraic-integer.md) is a unit exactly when its norm is $\pm1$.

If 2 factored into two nonunits, their absolute norms would both be 2, since $N(2)=4$; this is impossible. The same argument for $N(3)=9$ excludes a factorization of 3. Finally $\epsilon+1=4+\sqrt{10}$ has norm 6, so a nonunit factorization would require absolute norms 2 and 3, also impossible. Hence **2, 3 and $\epsilon+1$ are irreducible**. The conjugate $\overline\epsilon+1$ is irreducible by the same argument.

The equality $6=2\cdot3=(4+\sqrt{10})(4-\sqrt{10})$ gives inequivalent factorizations into irreducibles: no norm-six factor is associated to either 2 or 3, whose norms are 4 and 9. A [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md) is a [unique factorization domain](../../../../../../unique-factorization-domain.md). Consequently **$\mathcal O_K$ is not a [principal ideal domain](../../../../../../principal-ideal-domain.md)**.
