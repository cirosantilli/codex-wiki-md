<h1 id="19h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [19H](../19h.md)

For a complex [irreducible representation](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md), every central element acts as a scalar by [Schur lemma](../../../../../schur-s-lemma.md). If the representation is faithful, these scalars embed $Z$ in $\mathbb C^*$. Every finite subgroup of $\mathbb C^*$ is cyclic: if its order is $m$, all its elements satisfy $z^m=1$, and the $m$th [roots of unity](../../../../../root-of-unity.md) form a [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md). Thus **$Z$ is cyclic**.

The trivial group satisfies the conclusion immediately. Otherwise let $G$ be a finite $p$-group with cyclic centre, and decompose a [faithful representation](../../../../../faithful-representation.md) as a direct sum of irreducibles using [Maschke's theorem](../../../../../maschke-s-theorem.md). The nontrivial cyclic $p$-group $Z$ has a unique subgroup $C$ of order $p$. Some component does not kill $C$, since otherwise the whole representation would kill it. If that component had a nontrivial kernel $N$, the kernel would be a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) and the allowed intersection theorem gives $N\cap Z\ne1$. Every nontrivial subgroup of cyclic $Z$ contains $C$, contradicting the choice of component. **That irreducible component is faithful.**

Finally the [regular representation](../../../../../regular-representation.md) is faithful, since left multiplication by a nonidentity element does not fix the basis vector at the identity. A cyclic centre therefore supplies a faithful irreducible component of it. The first argument proves the converse, giving **a finite $p$-group has a faithful irreducible complex representation exactly when its centre is cyclic**.
