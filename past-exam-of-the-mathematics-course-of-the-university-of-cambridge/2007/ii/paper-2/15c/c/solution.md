<h1 id="15c/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A [canonical transformation](../../../../../../canonical-transformation.md) preserves the [symplectic form](../../../../../../symplectic-form.md) and therefore the form of [Hamilton's equations](../../../../../../hamilton-s-equations.md) for arbitrary Hamiltonians. In one degree of freedom,

$$
dQ\wedge dP=(Q_qP_p-Q_pP_q)\,dq\wedge dp=[Q,P],dq\wedge dp.
$$

Thus **the transformation is canonical exactly when $[Q,P]=1$**. Equivalently the chain rule gives $[F,G]_{q,p}=[Q,P]_{q,p}[F,G]_{Q,P}$ for arbitrary transformed functions, so the bracket and [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) dynamics are preserved precisely with this factor one. The condition also makes the Jacobian nonzero and hence supplies local invertibility; a global canonical change of variables additionally requires the stipulated global bijectivity on its domain.
