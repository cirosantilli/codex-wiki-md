<h1 id="2f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2F](../2f.md)

Choose a point of $K$ with both coordinates positive, so its product is positive. The set $K\cap[0,\infty)^2$ is nonempty and compact; the [continuous function](../../../../../continuous-function.md) $xy$ therefore attains its maximum there. A maximizer cannot lie on either axis, where the product is zero. It consequently belongs to $K\cap P^+$ and also maximizes the product on that set.

For uniqueness, the function $\Phi(x,y)=\log x+\log y$ is [strictly concave](../../../../../strictly-concave-function.md) on the positive quadrant: its [Hessian](../../../../../hessian-matrix.md) is $\operatorname{diag}(-1/x^2,-1/y^2)$, [negative definite](../../../../../negative-definite-matrix.md). If two distinct positive points attained the same maximal product, their midpoint would belong to $K\cap P^+$ by [convexity](../../../../../convex-function.md) and [strict concavity](../../../../../strict-concavity.md) would give a larger value of $\Phi$, hence a larger product. This contradiction proves **existence and uniqueness of the positive product maximizer**. This argument is [positive-product maximization on a convex set](../../../../../positive-product-maximization-on-a-convex-set.md); [compactness](../../../../../compact-space.md) should be applied to the closed quadrant intersection, not directly to the potentially noncompact set $K\cap P^+$.
