<h1 id="12f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose a [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $P$ with $\|f-P\|_\infty<\epsilon$, using part (i). For all sufficiently large $n$, its degree is at most $2n-1$, so $I_n(P)=\int_0^1P$. Positive weights summing to one give $|I_n(f-P)|\le\epsilon$, and $|\int_0^1(f-P)|\le\epsilon$. Hence

$$
\left|I_n(f)-\int_0^1f(t)\,dt\right|\le2\epsilon.
$$

Since $\epsilon$ is arbitrary, **the quadratures converge to the integral of every continuous $f$**. The positivity established in part (ii), not just [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) exactness on its own, controls the approximation error uniformly in $n$.
