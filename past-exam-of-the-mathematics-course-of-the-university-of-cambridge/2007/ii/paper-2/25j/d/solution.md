↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

[Almost sure convergence](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) implies [convergence in probability](../../../../../../convergence-in-probability.md), so part (b) identifies the limit as a constant $c$ [almost surely](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). For each $\epsilon>0$, the events $A_n=\{|X_n-c|>\epsilon\}$ are independent. [Almost sure convergence](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) gives $\Pr(A_n\text{ infinitely often})=0$. If their [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) sum diverged, the second [Borel-Cantelli lemma](../../../../../../borel-cantelli-lemmas.md) would instead give [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) one. Hence the sum is finite for every $\epsilon$, proving **[almost sure convergence](../../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md) implies [complete convergence](../../../../../../complete-convergence-of-random-variables.md) for independent sequences**.
