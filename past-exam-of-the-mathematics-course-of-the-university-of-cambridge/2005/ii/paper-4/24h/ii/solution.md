<h1 id="24h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The null condition follows by expansion:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^3\varphi_j^2
=|\phi_u|^2-|\phi_v|^2-2i\,\phi_u\cdot\phi_v
=E-G-2iF.
$$

It vanishes exactly when $E=G$ and $F=0$; the [immersion](../../../../../../immersion.md) hypothesis ensures their common value is positive. Thus it characterizes [isothermal coordinates](../../../../../../isothermal-coordinates.md) for an [immersion](../../../../../../immersion.md).

With complex coordinate $\zeta=u+iv$, mixed [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) commute and

$$
\partial_{\bar\zeta}(x_u-ix_v)
=\tfrac12(x_{uu}+x_{vv}),
$$

and similarly for $y,z$. By the [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md), each $\varphi_j$ is holomorphic exactly when the corresponding coordinate is [harmonic](../../../../../../harmonic-function.md). Combining with the preceding part gives **an isothermal [immersion](../../../../../../immersion.md) is minimal exactly when all three $\varphi_j$ are holomorphic**. Without [immersion](../../../../../../immersion.md) the null condition could also describe a degenerate map, so the nondegeneracy hypothesis matters.
