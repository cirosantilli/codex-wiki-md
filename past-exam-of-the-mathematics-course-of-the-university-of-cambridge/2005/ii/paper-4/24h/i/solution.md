<h1 id="24h/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

An [isothermal parametrization](../../../../../../isothermal-coordinates.md) is an [immersion](../../../../../../immersion.md) with [first fundamental form](../../../../../../first-fundamental-form.md) $E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2=\lambda^2(du^2+dv^2)$, where $\lambda>0$. Thus $E=G=\lambda^2$ and $F=0$.

Write $W=\phi_{uu}+\phi_{vv}$. Differentiating the metric identities gives

$$
W\cdot\phi_u=\tfrac12E_u-\tfrac12G_u=0,\qquad
W\cdot\phi_v=-\tfrac12E_v+\tfrac12G_v=0.
$$

For example $\phi_{vv}\cdot\phi_u=(\phi_v\cdot\phi_u)_v-\phi_v\cdot\phi_{uv}=-G_u/2$; the other identities are analogous. Hence $W$ is normal to the surface. For a unit normal $N$, its normal component is $e+g$. The supplied [mean curvature](../../../../../../mean-curvature.md) formula reduces to $H=(e+g)/(2\lambda^2)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\phi_{uu}+\phi_{vv}=2\lambda^2HN=2\lambda^2\mathbf H}.
$$

A surface is a [minimal surface](../../../../../../minimal-surface.md) when its [mean curvature](../../../../../../mean-curvature.md) [vector](../../../../../../vector.md) vanishes. Since $\lambda^2>0$, the preceding identity proves **minimality is equivalent to $\Delta\phi=0$ in [isothermal coordinates](../../../../../../isothermal-coordinates.md)**.
