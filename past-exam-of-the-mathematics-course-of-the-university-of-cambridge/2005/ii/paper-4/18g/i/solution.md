<h1 id="18g/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $a=\sqrt{2+\sqrt5}>0$ and $b=i/a$, so $b^2=2-\sqrt5$. The roots are $\pm a,\pm b$. The element $2+\sqrt5$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$: its [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) is $-1$, whereas the [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) of a square is a square of a rational number and hence nonnegative. Thus $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)]=2$, and $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q]=4$. This also proves irreducibility of the quartic.

The [splitting field](../../../../../../splitting-field.md) is $K=\mathbb Q(a,i)$, since $b=i/a$ and $i=ab$. Because $\mathbb Q(a)\subset\mathbb R$, adjoining $i$ doubles the degree:

$$
\boxed{[K:\mathbb Q]=8}.
$$

The [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) acts faithfully on the four roots and preserves the partition into the two opposite pairs $\{a,-a\}$ and $\{b,-b\}$, possibly interchanging the pairs. The full permutation group preserving this partition has order eight and is the [dihedral group](../../../../../../dihedral-group.md) of a square. Since the [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) has order eight, it is exactly that group. Equivalently its generators can be chosen as $r:a\mapsto b,\ b\mapsto-a$ and complex conjugation $s:a\mapsto a,\ b\mapsto-b$, with $r^4=s^2=1$ and $srs=r^{-1}$.
