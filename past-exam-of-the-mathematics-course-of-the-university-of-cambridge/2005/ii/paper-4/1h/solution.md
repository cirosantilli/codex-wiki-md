<h1 id="1h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1H](../1h.md)

An odd composite integer $n>1$ coprime to $b$ is a [Fermat pseudoprime](../../../../../fermat-pseudoprime.md) to base $b$ if $b^{n-1}\equiv1\pmod n$. A [Carmichael number](../../../../../carmichael-number.md) is a composite integer satisfying this congruence for every integer $b$ coprime to $n$. The [Korselt criterion](../../../../../korselt-criterion.md) says that a composite $n$ is a [Carmichael number](../../../../../carmichael-number.md) exactly when it is [squarefree](../../../../../squarefree-integer.md) and $p-1$ divides $n-1$ for every [prime](../../../../../prime-number.md) divisor $p$ of $n$.
