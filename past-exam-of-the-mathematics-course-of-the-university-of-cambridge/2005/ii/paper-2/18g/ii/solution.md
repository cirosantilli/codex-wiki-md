<h1 id="18g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For the indicated [cyclic Galois resolvent](../../../../../../cyclic-galois-resolvent.md), reindexing its terms and using $g^m=1$ gives

$$
gR(w)=\zeta R(w).
$$

There exists $w$ with $R(w)\ne0$ because distinct field [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) are [linearly independent](../../../../../../linear-independence.md) as [functions](../../../../../../function-split.md). A short proof is as follows. Take a nonzero relation with the fewest nonzero terms, normalize one coefficient, evaluate it at $uv$ and subtract a scalar multiple of the relation at $v$. Choose $u$ on which two participating [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) differ. This produces a nonzero relation with fewer terms, a contradiction.

Set $\alpha=R(w)\ne0$. Then $g^j\alpha=\zeta^j\alpha$ are $m$ distinct conjugates. Also $g(\alpha^m)=\alpha^m$, so $a=\alpha^m\in K$. The orbit has size $m$, giving $[K(\alpha):K]=m$ and hence $K(\alpha)=M$. All the roots $\zeta^j\alpha$ belong to $M$, so

$$
\boxed{M\text{ is the splitting field of }X^m-a\text{ for }a=R(w)^m\in K.}
$$

This also treats $m=1$ with a nonzero $w\in K$.
