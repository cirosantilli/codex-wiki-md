<h1 id="21h/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [21H](../21h.md)

The [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](../../../../../seifert-van-kampen-theorem.md) states that if a space is covered by path-connected open sets $U,V$ with path-connected intersection containing a basepoint, then its [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) is the [free product](../../../../../free-product.md) $\pi_1(U)*\pi_1(V)$ modulo the [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) generated by identifying the two images of each loop in $U\cap V$. More generally it is the corresponding pushout of groups.

A wedge of two circles has [fundamental group](../../../../../fundamental-group.md) $F(a,b)$, the [free group](../../../../../free-group.md) on their two loops: apply the theorem to thickened circle neighborhoods whose intersection is [contractible](../../../../../contractible-space.md) and use $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb Z$. Represent the [torus](../../../../../torus.md) by a square with opposite edges identified. Remove an interior disk; the remainder deformation retracts onto this wedge. Replacing the disk attaches a two-cell whose boundary follows $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. A disk neighborhood and the punctured [torus](../../../../../torus.md) have annular intersection, and van Kampen kills precisely that boundary loop. Hence

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(T)=\langle a,b\mid aba^{-1}b^{-1}=1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2.}
$$

A [torus](../../../../../torus.md) with a point removed retracts to a [torus](../../../../../torus.md) with a small open disk removed, and then to the same wedge. Thus

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(T\setminus\{p\})\cong F_2.}
$$

For the [connected sum](../../../../../connected-sum-of-oriented-manifolds.md), remove a disk from each [torus](../../../../../torus.md) and glue their boundary circles with reversed orientations. The two pieces have free groups $F(a,b)$ and $F(c,d)$ and their overlap is an annulus. Its boundary generators correspond to $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]^{-1}$, where $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\pi_1(T\#T)=\langle a,b,c,d\mid[a,b][c,d]=1\rangle.}
$$

The boundary relation is essential: this group is neither the [free group](../../../../../free-group.md) on four generators nor the abelian group $\mathbb Z^4$.
