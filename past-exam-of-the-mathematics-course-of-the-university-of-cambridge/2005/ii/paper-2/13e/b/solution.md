<h1 id="13e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

A nonzero spatially constant [steady state](../../../../../../steady-state.md) satisfies $v=u$ and $v=u^2$, hence $(u,v)=(1,1)$. The first derivatives of $u^2/v-u$ there are $1,-1$, while those of $Q(u^2-v)$ are $2Q,-Q$. Thus

$$
\boxed{J=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\2Q&-Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\operatorname{tr}J=1-Q,\quad\det J=Q.}
$$

Since $Q>0$, both [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) have strictly negative real part exactly when **$Q>1$**. For $0<Q<1$ the equilibrium is unstable. At $Q=1$ its linear [eigenvalues](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $\pm i$; this is the marginal boundary and does not give [asymptotic stability](../../../../../../asymptotic-stability.md) from [linearization](../../../../../../linearization.md). The positive-trace criterion in the hint tests positive real parts; stability requires applying it to $-J$.
