<h1 id="11f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

First interpret the decimal expansions as fractional expansions $0.d_1d_2\cdots$, with every $d_j\in\{4,5\}$. The resulting set $X_0$ is compact: a diagonal subsequence argument selects successively stabilized digits from any sequence in $X_0$, and the tail bound $\sum_{j>m}9\cdot10^{-j}=10^{-m}$ proves convergence to the corresponding expansion.

It has empty interior. At level $m$ its points lie in $2^m$ decimal intervals, each of length $10^{-m}$, whose total length is $(1/5)^m$. An interval of positive length could not lie in all these covers. Thus $X_0$ is closed and [nowhere dense](../../../../../../nowhere-dense-set.md). Each translate $X_0+q$ is also [nowhere dense](../../../../../../nowhere-dense-set.md), and the [rational numbers](../../../../../../rational-number.md) are [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md). The [Baire category theorem](../../../../../../baire-category-theorem.md) applied to $\mathbb R$ yields

$$
\boxed{\mathbb R\setminus\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q}(X_0+q)\ne\varnothing.}
$$

Any point in this complement has the required property. If the printed description also permits [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) digits before the decimal point, there are only countably many such [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) parts, and negative expansions merely add reflected translates. This makes $X$ a [countable](../../../../../../countable-set.md) union of translates or reflections of $X_0$. The same countable-union argument therefore still proves that some real $t$ cannot be written as $x+q$.
