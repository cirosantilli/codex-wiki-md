<h1 id="2f/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Set $r=p/q$. If $r\notin[1,2]$, then $|\sqrt2-r|\geq\sqrt2-1>1/4\geq1/(4q^2)$. If $1\leq r\leq2$, [irrationality of the square root of two](../../../../../../irrationality-of-the-square-root-of-two.md) implies that the [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $2q^2-p^2$ is nonzero. Factoring a difference of squares therefore gives

$$
\left|\sqrt2-\frac pq\right|
=\frac{|2q^2-p^2|}{q^2(\sqrt2+p/q)}
\geq\frac1{q^2(\sqrt2+2)}
>\frac1{4q^2}.
$$

Thus the requested **bound holds for every [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $p$ and every nonzero [integer](../../../../../../integer.md) $q$**, without using the [Liouville approximation theorem](../../../../../../liouville-approximation-theorem.md). For [completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md), irrationality follows because $p^2=2q^2$ in lowest terms would make $p$ even and then $q$ even, contradicting [coprimality](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md).
