The [Liouville approximation theorem](../../../../../../liouville-approximation-theorem.md) states that if $\alpha$ is a real irrational [algebraic number](../../../../../../algebraic-number.md) of degree $d$, there is a constant $C(\alpha)>0$ such that, for all integers $p,q$ with $q\ne0$,

$$
\boxed{\left|\alpha-\frac pq\right|\geq\frac{C(\alpha)}{|q|^d}.}
$$

Reduction of the fraction is not necessary: an unreduced denominator only weakens this bound. More generally the same type of bound holds for an [algebraic number](../../../../../../algebraic-number.md) of degree $d$ whenever $p/q\ne\alpha$. The exception is essential for a rational $\alpha$, since an exact rational representation gives zero error. A [rational number](../../../../../../rational-number.md) of degree one obeys the bound directly: if $\alpha=a/b$ in lowest terms and $p/q\ne\alpha$, then $|\alpha-p/q|=|aq-bp|/(|bq|)\geq1/(|bq|)$.
