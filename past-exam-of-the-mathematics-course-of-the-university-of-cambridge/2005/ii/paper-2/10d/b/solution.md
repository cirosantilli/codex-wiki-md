<h1 id="10d/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Within the prescribed uniform-density approximation, $n=3M/(4\pi m_pR^3)$. Treating the given approximate [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) coefficient as one, the [degeneracy pressure](../../../../../../degeneracy-pressure.md) and specified kinetic-energy relation give

$$
E_{\rm kin}=\frac32\frac{h^2}{m_e}n^{5/3}\frac{4\pi R^3}{3}
=\frac{\alpha}{R^2},
\qquad\alpha=\frac32\frac{h^2}{m_e}\left(\frac3{4\pi}\right)^{2/3}
\left(\frac M{m_p}\right)^{5/3}.
$$

For a uniform sphere, $m(r)=M(r/R)^3$, so shell assembly gives

$$
E_{\rm grav}=-\int_0^R\frac{Gm(r)}r\,dm(r)=-\frac{3GM^2}{5R}.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{E_{\rm total}(R)=\frac\alpha{R^2}-\frac\beta R,
\qquad\beta=\frac35GM^2.}
$$

At fixed mass, this [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) has its minimum at $R_*=2\alpha/\beta$, with $E''(R_*)=2\alpha/R_*^4>0$. Expansion beyond this radius raises the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md), while strong compression costs positive [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) growing as $R^{-2}$, faster than the attractive $R^{-1}$ term. This explains radial stability of the model [white dwarf](../../../../../../white-dwarf.md). The numerical coefficient in $\alpha$ inherits the approximate [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) law; a relativistic degeneracy law would change this stability argument.
