<h1 id="15d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the Newtonian dust calculation, use a comoving spherical shell of physical radius $R(t)=a(t)\chi$. Homogeneity gives its enclosed mass $M=4\pi\rho R^3/3$, and the shell theorem removes the gravitational force of exterior shells. Assume pressureless matter, conserved enclosed mass, no [cosmological constant](../../../../../../cosmological-constant.md), and no shell crossing. Its specific conserved mechanical [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) is

$$
\mathcal E=\frac12\dot R^2-\frac{GM}{R}
=\chi^2\left(\frac12\dot a^2-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\rho a^2\right).
$$

Homogeneity permits the [energy](../../../../../../energy.md) per $\chi^2$ to be a constant, written $-kc^2/2$. Dividing by $a^2$ gives

$$
\boxed{H^2+\frac{kc^2}{a^2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho,\qquad H=\dot a/a.}
$$

This is a Newtonian derivation for dust. For radiation the enclosed gravitating mass is not conserved, so this same mechanical-energy argument cannot simply be reused; general relativity supplies the same Friedmann constraint with the total [energy density](../../../../../../energy-density.md) divided by $c^2$, together with pressure-dependent fluid conservation.
