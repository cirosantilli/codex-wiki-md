<h1 id="24h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Let $m=\dim X$, $n=\dim Y$, and $x\in f^{-1}(y)$. In coordinate charts centered at $x$ and $y$, write the map as $F:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$. Surjectivity of its [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) gives an invertible $n\times n$ minor; reorder the domain coordinates so it is the first one. The map

$$
\Phi(u)=(F(u),u_{n+1},\ldots,u_m)
$$

has invertible [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) at the point. By the [inverse function theorem](../../../../../../inverse-function-theorem.md), it is a local coordinate change. In these coordinates the fiber is simply the set whose first $n$ coordinates are zero, so it is locally a coordinate plane of [dimension](../../../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $m-n$. These charts are inherited from $X$, proving

$$
\boxed{f^{-1}(y)\text{ is an embedded submanifold of dimension }\dim X-\dim Y.}
$$

If the fiber is empty, it is the empty [submanifold](../../../../../../submanifold.md) under the usual convention. The no-boundary assumptions avoid any extra [boundary conditions](../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) in this local model.
