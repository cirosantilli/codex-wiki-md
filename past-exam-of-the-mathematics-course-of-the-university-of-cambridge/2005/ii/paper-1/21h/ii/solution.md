<h1 id="21h/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $n\ge2$, the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md) $S^n$ is [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md). The [lifting criterion for a covering space](../../../../../../lifting-criterion-for-a-covering-space.md) says that a based map lifts to a covering whenever the image of its [fundamental group](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) lies in the [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) corresponding to that covering. For the [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) $p:\mathbb R^2\to T$, the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md)'s trivial [fundamental group](../../../../../../fundamental-group.md) meets this condition. Hence $f:S^n\to T$ has a continuous lift $\widetilde f:S^n\to\mathbb R^2$.

Contract the lift linearly to any point $q\in\mathbb R^2$:

$$
H(x,s)=p\bigl((1-s)\widetilde f(x)+sq\bigr),\qquad 0\le s\le1.
$$

This is a continuous [homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) from $f$ to the constant map $p(q)$. Thus **every such sphere-to-torus map is null-homotopic**. The restriction $n\ge2$ is essential; circle maps may carry nonzero fundamental-group classes.
