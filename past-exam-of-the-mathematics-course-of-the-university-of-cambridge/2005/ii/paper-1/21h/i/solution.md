↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The assertion needs **$E$ connected**. Without that assumption, the disjoint union of two tori mapping by the identity on each component is a covering of the [torus](../../../../../../torus.md) and is none of the three listed connected spaces. The correct general statement classifies each connected component; here is the intended connected proof.

The [universal cover](../../../../../../universal-cover.md) is $\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2=T$, and $\pi_1(T)=\mathbb Z^2$. The classification of connected coverings of a connected, locally path-connected, semilocally [simply connected](../../../../../../simply-connected-space.md) space identifies $E$ with $\mathbb R^2/H$ for a [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) $H\le\mathbb Z^2$, acting by translations. A [subgroup](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of $\mathbb Z^2$ is free abelian of rank zero, one or two. For rank zero the quotient is the plane. For rank one, a linear coordinate change sends its generator to a translation in one coordinate, yielding $S^1\times\mathbb R$. For rank two, a linear coordinate change sends a lattice basis to the standard basis, yielding a [torus](../../../../../../torus.md). Thus **a connected covering is a plane, cylinder or [torus](../../../../../../torus.md)**. For arbitrary $E$, its covering components are disjoint unions of these types, rather than necessarily one such space.
