<h1 id="10d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For dilute nonrelativistic nucleons in thermal equilibrium, their [Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution](../../../../../../maxwell-boltzmann-distribution.md) gives $n_j=g_j(m_jkT/2\pi\hbar^2)^{3/2}\exp[(\mu_j-m_jc^2)/kT]$. The [neutron](../../../../../../neutron.md) and [proton](../../../../../../proton.md) spin degeneracies are equal, and their [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) prefactor ratio is nearly one. Thus

$$
\frac{n_n}{n_p}\simeq\exp\left[\frac{\mu_n-\mu_p-Q}{kT}\right].
$$

Weak reaction equilibrium imposes $\mu_n+\mu_{\nu_e}=\mu_p+\mu_e$. Charge neutrality in an electron-positron plasma, together with the small [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) asymmetry, makes $\mu_e/kT$ very small. Assuming negligible neutrino lepton asymmetry also makes $\mu_{\nu_e}/kT$ small. Hence the difference, rather than the absolute [baryon](../../../../../../baryon.md) [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md), is negligible, yielding

$$
\boxed{n_n/n_p\simeq e^{-Q/kT}.}
$$

A large neutrino [chemical potential](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) would invalidate this last approximation; it is an assumption about the lepton asymmetry, not a general rule that baryonic [chemical potentials](../../../../../../chemical-potential.md) vanish.
