<h1 id="2f/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $P(t)=\prod_i(t-x_i)$. For every [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $q$ of degree at most $n-1$, $Pq$ has degree at most $2n-1$ and vanishes at all nodes; thus $\int_{-1}^1Pq=0$. The monic degree-$n$ [orthogonal polynomial](../../../../../../orthogonal-polynomial.md) for this integral is unique: the difference of two such monic [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) has degree below $n$ and is orthogonal to itself, hence is zero. Consequently $P$ is the monic normalization of the degree-$n$ [Legendre polynomial](../../../../../../legendre-polynomial.md), proving **the given nodes are exactly its roots**. Together the three results characterize this [Gaussian quadrature](../../../../../../gaussian-quadrature.md) rule.
