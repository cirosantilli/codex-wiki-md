<h1 id="6e/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $0<q<1$, a finite positive equilibrium exists exactly when $a q^L>b$, and then

$$
\boxed{z_*=(a q^L-b)/[a(1-q^L)].}
$$

The derivative of the ratio's [vector field](../../../../../../vector-field.md) at $z_*$ is $-(aq^L-b)<0$, so this equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable; every positive initial ratio tends to it. The critical real length is $L_c=\log(b/a)/\log q$, and the admissible integer lengths satisfy $L<L_c$. Thus the largest positive integer length, if one exists, is $\boxed{L_{\max}=\lceil L_c\rceil-1}$. At equality the equilibrium is zero, so rounding down without accounting for the strict inequality can be wrong. If $q=1$, mutants are never produced from masters and the ratio grows without a finite positive equilibrium; the finite threshold formula concerns imperfect copying.
