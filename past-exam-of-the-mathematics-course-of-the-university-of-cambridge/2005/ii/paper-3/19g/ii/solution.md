<h1 id="19g/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

In a one-dimensional [representation](../../../../../../group-representation.md), the images commute, so $ba=a^2b$ forces the image of $a$ to be one. With $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ the three possibilities are

$$
\boxed{\rho_j(a)=1,\qquad\rho_j(b)=\omega^j,\qquad j=0,1,2.}
$$

They are inequivalent because they have distinct values at $b$.
