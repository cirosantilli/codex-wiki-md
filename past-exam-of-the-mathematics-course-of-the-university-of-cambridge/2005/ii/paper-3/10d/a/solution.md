<h1 id="10d/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a homogeneous isotropic metric, radial light propagation covers comoving distance $\int c\,dt/a(t)$. The physical [particle horizon](../../../../../../particle-horizon.md) is $d_p(t)=a(t)\int_{t_i}^t c\,dt'/a(t')$, when this is finite. A future [event horizon](../../../../../../event-horizon.md) exists when $d_e(t)=a(t)\int_t^{t_f}c\,dt'/a(t')$ is finite. The [Hubble radius](../../../../../../hubble-radius.md) is the instantaneous scale $c/H$, with $H=\dot a/a$; it is not generally the same as either causal horizon.

For an [Einstein-de Sitter universe](../../../../../../einstein-de-sitter-universe.md), $H=2/(3t)$, so $c/H=3ct/2$, while $d_p=3ct$ from a beginning at zero. The future integral diverges, so there is no event horizon. For an eternal-past [de Sitter spacetime](../../../../../../de-sitter-spacetime.md), $H$ is constant and the particle-horizon integral from $-\infty$ diverges. Its event horizon and Hubble radius both equal $c/H$. Thus **a Hubble radius is not automatically a particle horizon**, even in these simple isotropic models.
