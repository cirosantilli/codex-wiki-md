<h1 id="13e/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

With response rates and elasticity effectively fixed, changing $\lambda_2$ changes $m_2$ and hence intrinsic noise but not the environmental term. Observation (i) therefore indicates **extrinsic noise dominates**, making the mean-dependent intrinsic contribution negligible. For a general feedback law whose elasticity changes substantially with the mean, compensation between terms is another possibility; independence from $\lambda_2$ alone would then not logically identify the dominant term.

Observation (ii) measures the local relaxation rate $\beta_2H$, which is larger under negative feedback. Observation (iii) is not explained by faster adjustment alone. At fixed susceptibility, increasing the response rate increases $\beta_2H/(\beta_1+\beta_2H)$, reducing time averaging and increasing transmitted environmental noise. Feedback lowers [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) primarily by reducing susceptibility and the intrinsic term. Thus **rapid adjustment and low noise share a feedback mechanism but are not a direct causal equivalence**.
