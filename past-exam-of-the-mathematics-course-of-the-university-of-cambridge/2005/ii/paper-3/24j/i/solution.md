<h1 id="24j/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For $m>n$, the $L^1$ difference is $\int_n^m x^{-1}dx=\log(m/n)$. Choosing $m=2n$ makes it $\log2$, so **the sequence is not Cauchy in $L^1$**. Its squared $L^2$ difference is $\int_n^m x^{-2}dx\leq1/n$, which tends uniformly to zero for $m>n$. Hence **it is Cauchy in $L^2$**, with [norm](../../../../../../norm.md) limit $x^{-1}$ on $(1,\infty)$.
