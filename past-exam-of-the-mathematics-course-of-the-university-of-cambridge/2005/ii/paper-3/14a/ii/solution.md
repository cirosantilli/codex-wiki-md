<h1 id="14a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For positive integers $n$, the finite open segment from part (i) works because $k=n/2>0$. For $n\leq0$, take a finite closed contour enclosing both [branch points](../../../../../../branch-point.md) $i,-i$, with the integrand analytic in the exterior of the joining cut. Near infinity choose $(t^2+1)^{n/2-1}=t^{n-2}(1+t^{-2})^{n/2-1}$. Since $n$ is integral, a circuit around both [branch points](../../../../../../branch-point.md) returns to the initial branch, and the endpoint boundary term cancels. Thus this is an admissible finite contour, including $n=0$.

For $n\leq-1$, at $z=0$ that exterior branch is $O(t^{n-2})=O(t^{-3})$. Deforming the contour to a large circle makes its integral tend to zero; equivalently its Laurent series has no $t^{-1}$ coefficient. Hence $\boxed{w(0)=0\quad(n\leq-1)}$. An individual loop around one [branch point](../../../../../../branch-point.md) would not generally return to the same branch when $n$ is odd, which is why both are enclosed.
