A [credit rating](../../../../../../credit-rating.md) is an agency's ordered assessment of an issuer's or particular obligation's capacity and willingness to meet its promised payments. Corporate and sovereign issuers are classified on letter scales, with finer notches, broad investment-grade and speculative-grade groupings, and default categories. Agencies such as Standard & Poor's, Moody's and Fitch also revise ratings as information changes; sovereign assessments involve public finances and institutional or political factors as well as debt-servicing capacity.

A [credit rating](../../../../../../credit-rating.md) summarizes relative creditworthiness rather than specifying an exact, immutable [credit default](../../../../../../credit-default.md) probability. Historical defaults and migrations within rating classes can inform a statistical model. Assuming a homogeneous [Poisson process](../../../../../../poisson-process.md) within one class is a simplifying choice: it posits a constant event intensity and independent increments, neither of which follows merely from a shared rating. The [credit risk](../../../../../../credit-risk.md) model must also distinguish default frequency from [loss given default](../../../../../../loss-given-default.md) severity.
