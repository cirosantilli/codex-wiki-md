<h1 id="3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

A [quasi-coherent sheaf](../../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md) is a [sheaf of modules](../../../../../sheaf-of-modules.md) which locally has a presentation by [free module](../../../../../free-module.md) [sheaves](../../../../../sheaf-mathematics.md):

$$
\mathcal O_U^{(J)}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_U^{(I)}
\longrightarrow\mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow0,
$$

where the index sets may be infinite. Equivalently, on affine neighbourhoods it is an [affine module sheaf](../../../../../affine-module-sheaf.md). A [coherent sheaf](../../../../../coherent-sheaf.md) is locally finitely generated, with the additional requirement that the kernel of every map from a [finite free module](../../../../../finite-free-module.md) [sheaf](../../../../../sheaf-mathematics.md) is locally finitely generated. On a [Noetherian scheme](../../../../../noetherian-scheme.md), these conditions are equivalent to being locally associated with a [finitely generated module](../../../../../finitely-generated-module.md).

For an [affine variety](../../../../../affine-algebraic-set.md) $X$ with [coordinate ring](../../../../../coordinate-ring.md) $A$ and an $A$-[module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) $M$, set

$$
\widetilde M(D(a))=M_a,\qquad
\widetilde M_P=M_{\mathfrak m_P}.
$$

Restriction on [principal open subsets](../../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md) is the natural [module localization](../../../../../localization-of-a-module.md) map. On an arbitrary open subset, sections are families of these stalk values locally represented by a single fraction from a [localization](../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md). This constructs the [affine module sheaf](../../../../../affine-module-sheaf.md); its [global sections](../../../../../global-section.md) are $M$. Choose a free-module presentation $A^{(J)}\to A^{(I)}\to M\to0$. [Exactness of localization](../../../../../exactness-of-localization.md) and its compatibility with direct sums yield

$$
\mathcal O_X^{(J)}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X^{(I)}
\longrightarrow\widetilde M\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus $\widetilde M$ is a [quasi-coherent sheaf](../../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md), without a finite-generation assumption.

We now prove [coherence of an affine module sheaf](../../../../../coherence-of-an-affine-module-sheaf.md). If $M$ is finitely generated, take a [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) $A^r\to M$. Since $A$ is a [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md), its kernel is finitely generated, so $M$ has a [finite presentation of a module](../../../../../finite-presentation-of-a-module.md). More generally, on every principal affine open, the kernel of a map $A_a^s\to M_a$ is a [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of a [finite free module](../../../../../finite-free-module.md) over the [Noetherian ring](../../../../../noetherian-ring.md) $A_a$, and is finitely generated. On sheafifying, this verifies both parts of the definition of a [coherent sheaf](../../../../../coherent-sheaf.md).

Conversely, if $\widetilde M$ is coherent, choose a finite principal-open cover $X=\bigcup_iD(a_i)$ on which it has finitely many generators. Write those local generators as $m_{ij}/a_i^{r_{ij}}$. Their numerators generate a finite [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) $N=\sum_{i,j}Am_{ij}$ of $M$, and $N_{a_i}=M_{a_i}$ for every $i$. To check that $M/N=0$, let $q\in M/N$. Each equality after [localization](../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) gives an exponent $r_i$ such that $a_i^{r_i}q=0$. The ideal generated by the $a_i^{r_i}$ is the [unit ideal](../../../../../unit-ideal.md): otherwise a maximal ideal containing all of them would give a point outside the principal-open cover. A linear combination of these powers equal to $1$ consequently annihilates $q$. Every $q$ is zero, proving

$$
\boxed{\widetilde M\text{ is coherent}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M\text{ is a finitely generated }A\text{-module}.}
$$

Next consider a morphism $\phi:\mathcal G_1\to\mathcal G_2$ between [quasi-coherent sheaves](../../../../../quasi-coherent-sheaf.md). On an affine open $V$ of the target [variety](../../../../../algebraic-variety.md), use the assumed [affine module-sheaf equivalence](../../../../../affine-module-sheaf-equivalence.md) to write $\mathcal G_j|_V=\widetilde{M_j}$, with $M_j=\Gamma(V,\mathcal G_j)$. The [sheaf](../../../../../sheaf-mathematics.md) morphism is the sheafification of the [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md) map $\psi=\Gamma(V,\phi)$. Indeed, its action on a section $m/a^r$ of a principal open is forced by module-linearity and restriction to be $\psi(m)/a^r$. Therefore [exactness of localization](../../../../../exactness-of-localization.md) gives

$$
(\ker\phi)(D(a))=\ker\bigl((M_1)_a\to(M_2)_a\bigr)
=(\ker\psi)_a.
$$

These identities are compatible with restriction, so $(\ker\phi)|_V=\widetilde{\ker\psi}$. Since this holds on an affine cover, **the [kernel sheaf](../../../../../kernel-sheaf.md) is quasi-coherent on every [variety](../../../../../algebraic-variety.md)**. This proof of [kernels of quasi-coherent sheaf morphisms](../../../../../kernels-of-quasi-coherent-sheaf-morphisms.md) does not assert that taking [global sections](../../../../../global-section.md) is right exact.

For [direct image](../../../../../direct-image-sheaf.md) along a morphism of [affine varieties](../../../../../affine-algebraic-set.md), write $Y$ with [coordinate ring](../../../../../coordinate-ring.md) $A$, $X$ with [coordinate ring](../../../../../coordinate-ring.md) $B$, and let $\alpha:A\to B$ be the induced ring homomorphism. If $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$ on $X$, regard $M$ as an $A$-module by [restriction of scalars](../../../../../restriction-of-scalars.md). For $a\in A$,

$$
f^{-1}(D(a))=D(\alpha(a)),\qquad
(f_*\mathcal F)(D(a))=M_{\alpha(a)}=M_a.
$$

The last equality identifies [localization](../../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) with the scalar action of $a$. It identifies the [direct image sheaf](../../../../../direct-image-sheaf.md) with the [affine module sheaf](../../../../../affine-module-sheaf.md) of the restricted [module](../../../../../module-mathematics.md), so $f_*\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent.

Suppose $Y$ is affine and $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^rU_i$ is a finite affine cover. Since a [variety](../../../../../algebraic-variety.md) is separated, every $U_i\cap U_j$ is affine: it is identified with the inverse image of the closed [diagonal morphism](../../../../../diagonal-morphism.md) inside the affine product $U_i\times_kU_j$. Write $g_i=f|_{U_i}$ and $g_{ij}=f|_{U_i\cap U_j}$. Form the morphism of [sheaves of modules](../../../../../sheaf-of-modules.md)

$$
d:\bigoplus_i(g_i)_*(\mathcal F|_{U_i})
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{i,j}(g_{ij})_*(\mathcal F|_{U_i\cap U_j}),
\qquad
(s_i)\longmapsto(s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}-s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}).
$$

Both sums are finite, and every summand is quasi-coherent by the affine calculation. For every open $V\subseteq Y$, the [sheaf gluing axiom](../../../../../sheaf-gluing-axiom.md) on $f^{-1}(V)$ identifies the kernel of this map on sections with $\mathcal F(f^{-1}(V))$. Hence

$$
f_*\mathcal F=\ker d.
$$

The kernel result proved above establishes quasi-coherence.

Finally, for arbitrary $Y$, take an affine neighbourhood $V\subseteq Y$ and apply the preceding argument to $f^{-1}(V)\to V$. The open subset $f^{-1}(V)$ is again a [variety](../../../../../algebraic-variety.md) and has a finite affine cover, because [varieties](../../../../../algebraic-variety.md) have [Noetherian Zariski topology](../../../../../noetherian-zariski-topology.md). The restriction of $f_*\mathcal F$ to $V$ is exactly the direct image for this restricted morphism. Thus

$$
\boxed{f_*\mathcal F\text{ is quasi-coherent for every morphism of varieties}.}
$$

The finiteness and separation conditions are the relevant hypotheses, not affineness of the original source or target. More generally, [quasi-coherence of direct image under a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism](../../../../../quasi-coherence-of-direct-image-under-a-quasi-compact-quasi-separated-morphism.md) is valid. If one permits nonseparated finite-type prevarieties, each overlap in the argument has a finite affine cover; replacing its summand by the sum of direct images from these charts gives the same kernel, since agreement can be tested on a cover. This also proves the assertion in that convention. It does not extend by this argument to arbitrary spaces with an unrestricted infinite cover.
