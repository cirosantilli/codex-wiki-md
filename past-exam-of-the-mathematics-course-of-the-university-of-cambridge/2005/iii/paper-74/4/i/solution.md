<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

In the hydrogen–helium approximation, take helium to be [helium-4](../../../../../../helium-4.md), and assume essentially all neutrons remaining at the onset of efficient nuclear burning enter it. Each [helium-4](../../../../../../helium-4.md) nucleus contains two [neutrons](../../../../../../neutron.md) and two [protons](../../../../../../proton.md), hence

$$
n_{\rm He}=\frac{n_n}{2},\qquad n_H=n_p-n_n,\qquad n_B=n_p+n_n.
$$

Here $n_p,n_n$ count constituent nucleons, not just free particles after burning. Neglecting the small nucleon mass difference and nuclear binding-energy correction, the helium mass is $4m_un_{\rm He}$ and the total baryon mass is $m_un_B$. Thus [neutron-limited helium synthesis](../../../../../../neutron-limited-helium-synthesis.md) gives

$$
\boxed{Y_p=\frac{4n_{\rm He}}{n_B}=\frac{2n_n}{n_p+n_n}=2\left(1+\frac{n_p}{n_n}\right)^{-1}.}
$$

For $n_n/n_p\simeq1/7$, this yields $Y_p\simeq1/4$ and the [hydrogen mass fraction](../../../../../../hydrogen-mass-fraction.md) is $X_p\simeq3/4$. The assumptions require $n_p\ge n_n$ and negligible free neutrons, [deuterium](../../../../../../deuterium.md) and [helium-3](../../../../../../helium-3.md): the phrase hydrogen and helium must here mean predominantly hydrogen-1 and [helium-4](../../../../../../helium-4.md), not an arbitrary mixture of helium isotopes.
