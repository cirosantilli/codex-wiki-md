<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

On the sphere, both [cosmic time](../../../../../../cosmic-time.md) and the [comoving coordinate](../../../../../../comoving-coordinate.md) $r$ are constant. The positive spatial [induced metric](../../../../../../induced-metric.md) obtained from the [FLRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) is therefore $d\ell^2=a^2r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$. Its [area element](../../../../../../area-element-of-a-surface.md) is the square root of its determinant:

$$
dA=a^2r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi.
$$

Integrating over the [spherical coordinates](../../../../../../spherical-coordinate-system.md) gives

$$
\boxed{A=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi a^2r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi=4\pi[a(t)r]^2.}
$$

Thus $ar$ is the [areal radius](../../../../../../areal-radius.md). In a curved spatial slice it need not equal the proper radial distance $a\int_0^r(1-kr'^2)^{-1/2}dr'$; the angular part of the [FLRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md), rather than a Euclidean radial-distance assumption, establishes the [surface area](../../../../../../surface-area.md).
