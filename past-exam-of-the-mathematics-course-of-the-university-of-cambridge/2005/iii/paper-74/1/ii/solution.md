<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For a radial [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md), the [FLRW metric](../../../../../../friedmann-lemaitre-robertson-walker-metric.md) gives

$$
\int_{t_e}^{t_0}\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}=\int_{r_e}^{r_0}\frac{|dr|}{\sqrt{1-kr^2}}.
$$

Two successive wave crests traverse the same comoving path between [comoving observers](../../../../../../comoving-observer.md). Subtracting their path integrals, to first order in their short emission and reception periods, gives $\delta t_0/a(t_0)=\delta t_e/a(t_e)$. Since [cosmic time](../../../../../../cosmic-time.md) is each observer's proper time and the [frequency](../../../../../../frequency.md) is the reciprocal period,

$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\nu_e}{\nu_0}=\frac{a(t_0)}{a(t_e)}.}
$$

This neglects [peculiar velocities](../../../../../../peculiar-velocity.md) and local [gravitational redshift](../../../../../../gravitational-redshift.md) contributions.

For B's light passing through A on its way to Earth, with the comparison event at A being that passage, [cosmological redshift](../../../../../../cosmological-redshift.md) factors multiply:

$$
1+z_B=(1+z_{BA})(1+z_A),\qquad
\boxed{z_{BA}=\frac{4}{2}-1=1.}
$$

This is the intended numerical answer with that reception-event specification. The [reception-event dependence of intergalactic redshift](../../../../../../reception-event-dependence-of-intergalactic-redshift.md) is essential: Earth's two redshifts by themselves neither specify the A–B separation nor identify the emission event at B whose light A receives at a chosen time. For arbitrary directions or a different reception time at A, the general answer is $a(t_{A,\rm rec})/a(t_{B,\rm em})-1$, with the emission time fixed by the A–B [null geodesic](../../../../../../null-geodesic.md); it cannot be deduced from the two supplied numbers alone.
