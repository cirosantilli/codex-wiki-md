<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

With the [Wirtinger derivatives](../../../../../../wirtinger-derivatives.md), $q_{z\bar z}=(q_{xx}+q_{yy})/4$. Put $W=e^{-ikz-\lambda\bar z/(ik)}$, $k\ne0$. The PDF has the factor $\lambda$ in this exponential; omitting it, as the TeX does, would give the wrong [local relation](../../../../../../local-relation.md). Choose

$$
\boxed{A=q_z,\qquad B=-\frac{\lambda}{ik}q.}
$$

Indeed, $W_{\bar z}=-\lambda W/(ik)$ and $W_z=-ikW$, so direct differentiation gives

$$
(WA)_{\bar z}-(WB)_z=W(q_{z\bar z}-\lambda q).
$$

The unwanted $q_z$ terms cancel exactly. Thus the divergence identity is equivalent to the [modified Helmholtz equation](../../../../../../modified-helmholtz-equation.md), including its [Laplace equation](../../../../../../laplace-equation.md) case $\lambda=0$. Equivalently,

$$
\eta=W\left(q_zdz-\frac{\lambda}{ik}q\,d\bar z\right)
$$

is a [closed differential one-form](../../../../../../closed-differential-one-form.md). Its oriented boundary integral supplies the [polygonal modified Helmholtz global relation](../../../../../../polygonal-modified-helmholtz-global-relation.md).
