<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose $\phi_j\in C^r$ in $u$ and $\psi_k\in C^s$ in $v$, including their respective knot joins. The mixed [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md) of the [tensor-product surface basis](../../../../../../tensor-product-surface-basis.md) are

$$
\boxed{\partial_u^p\partial_v^q b_{jk}(u,v)=\phi_j^{(p)}(u)\psi_k^{(q)}(v),\qquad p\le r,\ q\le s.}
$$

These are continuous because each factor is continuous. A finite or locally finite control sum has the same property. In the usual total-order notation the [parametric surface](../../../../../../parametric-surface.md) is therefore at least $C^{\min(r,s)}$, and also has the stronger stated rectangular set of continuous mixed [derivatives](../../../../../../derivative.md). Across a $u$-knot its guaranteed order is $r$, and across a $v$-knot its guaranteed order is $s$. No general improvement over a factor's smoothness is possible: setting $P_{jk}=A_j$ and using $\sum_k\psi_k=1$ recovers the univariate curve $\sum_jA_j\phi_j(u)$ as a [parametric surface](../../../../../../parametric-surface.md) constant in $v$.
