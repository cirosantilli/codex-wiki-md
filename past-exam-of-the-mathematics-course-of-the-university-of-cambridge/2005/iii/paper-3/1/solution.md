<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Let $\lambda=(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots)$ be a [partition of an integer](../../../../../partition-of-an-integer.md) $n$. Its [Young diagram](../../../../../young-diagram.md) consists of cells $(i,j)$ with $1\le j\le\lambda_i$, and its [conjugate partition](../../../../../conjugate-partition.md) has parts $\lambda'_j=\#\{i:\lambda_i\ge j\}$. A numbered [Young tableau](../../../../../young-tableau.md) fills these cells bijectively with $1,\ldots,n$. Its row and column stabilizers $R_t,C_t$ permute entries within each row and each column respectively. A [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) $\{t\}$ remembers the sets in the ordered rows, forgetting their internal order. The [Young subgroup](../../../../../young-subgroup.md) $R_t\cong\prod_i S_{\lambda_i}$ is its stabilizer, so the [Young permutation module](../../../../../young-permutation-module.md) is

$$
M^\lambda=F\{\text{tabloids of shape }\lambda\}
\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{R_t}^{S_n}F.
$$

Here the group acts by permuting numbered entries and extending linearly. A [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) is an invariant linear subspace. Give the [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) basis its orthonormal symmetric [bilinear form](../../../../../bilinear-form.md), denoted $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$. This form is nondegenerate and invariant under the group. Define the [column antisymmetrizer](../../../../../column-antisymmetrizer-of-a-young-tableau.md) and [polytabloid](../../../../../polytabloid.md) by

$$
b_t=\sum_{g\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(g)g\in FS_n,
\qquad e_t=b_t\{t\},
\qquad S^\lambda=\operatorname{span}_F\{e_t\}.
$$

Thus $S^\lambda$ is the [Specht module](../../../../../specht-module.md), generated by any one $e_t$, because the group is transitive on numbered [Young tableaux](../../../../../young-tableau.md). The [orthogonal complement](../../../../../orthogonal-complement.md) $(S^\lambda)^\perp$ consists of vectors pairing to zero with every [polytabloid](../../../../../polytabloid.md).

The key calculation is

$$
\boxed{b_t v=\langle v,e_t\rangle e_t\quad(v\in M^\lambda).}
$$

To prove it, first take $v=\{u\}$. If two entries of one column of $t$ occur in one row of $u$, their [transposition](../../../../../transposition-permutation.md) $s$ fixes $\{u\}$; pairing the terms indexed by $g$ and $gs$ cancels $b_t\{u\}$. This also works in [characteristic two](../../../../../characteristic-two.md), where the paired equal terms have sum zero. Otherwise each row of $u$ contains at most one entry from each column of $t$. The first row has $\lambda_1$ entries and there are $\lambda_1$ columns, so it uses every column once. Removing those entries, the second row must use exactly the columns of remaining positive height, and induction does the same for all rows. Consequently there is a unique $h\in C_t$ with $\{u\}=\{ht\}$. Uniqueness follows from $R_t\cap C_t=1$. In that case

$$
b_t\{u\}=\operatorname{sgn}(h)e_t,
\qquad \langle\{u\},e_t\rangle=\operatorname{sgn}(h).
$$

Linearity proves the identity.

Now let $U\le M^\lambda$ be a [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md). If $U$ is not contained in $(S^\lambda)^\perp$, choose $v\in U$ and $t$ with $\langle v,e_t\rangle\ne0$. The displayed identity puts $e_t$ in $U$, so its group translates put all of $S^\lambda$ in $U$. This proves the [James submodule theorem](../../../../../james-submodule-theorem.md) without a semisimplicity assumption.

Put $R=S^\lambda\cap(S^\lambda)^\perp$. If $R\ne S^\lambda$, every proper [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md) of $S^\lambda$ lies in $R$, by the same theorem. Hence $R$ is its unique maximal [submodule](../../../../../submodule.md), and $D^\lambda=S^\lambda/R$ is [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md). If $R=S^\lambda$, the quotient is zero. This conclusion remains true after every [field extension](../../../../../field-extension.md) $E/F$: the integral [tabloid](../../../../../tabloid.md) construction becomes $M^\lambda_E=E\otimes_FM^\lambda$, and the radical of the restricted form is the kernel of its Gram matrix, so it commutes with extension of scalars. A nonzero Gram entry stays nonzero in $E$. Applying the theorem over $E$ makes $E\otimes_FD^\lambda$ [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md). Thus a nonzero quotient is absolutely [irreducible](../../../../../irreducible-representation.md).

The precise nonvanishing criterion is

$$
\boxed{D^\lambda\ne0\iff p=0\text{ or }\lambda\text{ is a }p\text{-regular partition}.}
$$

A [regular partition](../../../../../regular-partition.md) has fewer than $p$ rows of each positive length. Here is the integral pairing argument behind this condition. Write $m_j$ for the number of rows of length $j$. Expanding two [polytabloids](../../../../../polytabloid.md) into [tabloids](../../../../../tabloid.md), simultaneous [permutations](../../../../../permutation.md) of equal-length rows [partition of an integer](../../../../../partition-of-an-integer.md) all contributions to their pairing into free orbits of size $\prod_jm_j!$; the signs agree within each orbit because the same row [permutation](../../../../../permutation.md) is applied in every relevant column on both sides. Therefore every pairing is divisible by $\prod_jm_j!$. If some $m_j\ge p$, the entire restricted form is zero modulo $p$. Conversely, reverse the order of the entries in each row of a tableau to obtain $t^*$. Counting the matching column [permutations](../../../../../permutation.md) in the two expansions gives, up to the harmless overall sign,

$$
\langle e_t,e_{t^*}\rangle=\pm\prod_j(m_j!)^j.
$$

Indeed the $m_j$ equal rows of length $j$ can be matched independently in each of their $j$ positions; after these choices the decreasing row lengths force every other matching. For a [regular partition](../../../../../regular-partition.md) this integer is nonzero modulo $p$, so the form and its radical quotient are nonzero. In [characteristic zero](../../../../../characteristic-zero.md) it is nonzero for every shape. This supplies the requested justification of the criterion.
