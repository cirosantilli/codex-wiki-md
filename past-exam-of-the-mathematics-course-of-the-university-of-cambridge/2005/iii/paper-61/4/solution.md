<h1 id="4/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

Use [natural units](../../../../../natural-units.md), assume negligible neutrino [chemical potentials](../../../../../chemical-potential.md), and approximate [neutrino decoupling](../../../../../neutrino-decoupling.md) as occurring before [electron-positron annihilation in cosmology](../../../../../electron-positron-annihilation-in-cosmology.md). At high [temperature](../../../../../temperature.md), [weak interactions](../../../../../weak-interaction.md) maintain a common temperature for [neutrinos](../../../../../neutrino.md), [antineutrinos](../../../../../antineutrino.md), [Electrons](../../../../../electron.md), [Positrons](../../../../../positron.md) and [photons](../../../../../photon.md). Since $\Gamma_\nu/H\propto T^3$, the reaction rate eventually loses against expansion. Decoupled relativistic [neutrinos](../../../../../neutrino.md) then free stream; their momenta redshift as $a^{-1}$, so their distribution retains a temperature parameter satisfying $aT_\nu=\mathrm{constant}$.

The still-coupled electromagnetic bath receives the [entropy](../../../../../entropy.md) of annihilating [Electrons](../../../../../electron.md) and [Positrons](../../../../../positron.md). Its [effective number of relativistic entropy degrees of freedom](../../../../../effective-number-of-relativistic-degrees-of-freedom.md) is initially

$$
g_{*s}^{\rm EM}=2+\frac78(2+2)=\frac{11}{2},
$$

where the two [photon](../../../../../photon.md) polarizations count as bosonic states and the four electron-positron spin states have the [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) entropy weight $7/8$. After the pairs disappear, $g_{*s}^{\rm EM}=2$. [Cosmological entropy conservation](../../../../../cosmological-entropy-conservation.md) in this coupled bath gives $g_{*s}^{\rm EM}(aT_\gamma)^3=\mathrm{constant}$. The three already decoupled neutrino species are excluded from this bath's entropy count. At decoupling $T_\nu=T_\gamma$, hence

$$
\boxed{\frac{T_\nu}{T_\gamma}=\left(\frac{2}{11/2}\right)^{1/3}=\left(\frac4{11}\right)^{1/3}\simeq0.714.}
$$

Subsequent expansion preserves this ratio in the idealized history. Residual interactions during annihilation give small corrections to the instantaneous-decoupling approximation. If a relic species later becomes massive, $T_\nu$ here remains the redshifting momentum-distribution parameter; it need not be its nonrelativistic kinetic temperature.

A single ordinary neutrino species includes one populated [neutrino](../../../../../neutrino.md) [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md) and one [antineutrino](../../../../../antineutrino.md) [helicity](../../../../../helicity.md). For negligible [chemical potential](../../../../../chemical-potential.md), integration of the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](../../../../../fermi-dirac-distribution.md) gives

$$
n_\nu+n_{\bar\nu}=\frac{3\zeta(3)}{2\pi^2}T_\nu^3,\qquad n_\gamma=\frac{2\zeta(3)}{\pi^2}T_\gamma^3.
$$

Therefore the [number density of a relativistic neutrino relative to photons](../../../../../number-density-of-a-relativistic-neutrino-relative-to-photons.md), with the antiparticle included, is

$$
\boxed{\frac{n_\nu+n_{\bar\nu}}{n_\gamma}=\frac34\left(\frac{T_\nu}{T_\gamma}\right)^3=\frac3{11}.}
$$

The factor $3/4$ is the fermionic number-density weight, distinct from the energy and entropy weight $7/8$. This relation continues to hold after this species becomes nonrelativistic if its comoving particle number is conserved.

Under the proposed matter-dominated, spatially flat model, the [Friedmann equation](../../../../../friedmann-equations.md) requires the present [relic-neutrino energy density](../../../../../relic-neutrino-energy-density.md) to be approximately the [critical density](../../../../../critical-density.md). Using the unreduced [Planck mass](../../../../../planck-mass.md) $M_P=G^{-1/2}$ gives

$$
\rho_{c0}=\frac{3H_0^2M_P^2}{8\pi}\simeq m_\nu(n_\nu+n_{\bar\nu}).
$$

Thus the [critical-density mass of a thermal relic neutrino](../../../../../critical-density-mass-of-a-thermal-relic-neutrino.md) is

$$
\boxed{m_\nu\simeq\frac{11\pi}{16\zeta(3)}\frac{H_0^2M_P^2}{T_{\gamma0}^3}.}
$$

Taking the paper's rounded values, $T_{\gamma0}=2\times10^{-13}\ {\rm GeV}$, $H_0=h/(4.5\times10^{41}\ {\rm GeV}^{-1})$ and $M_P\sim10^{19}\ {\rm GeV}$, gives

$$
\boxed{m_\nu\sim1.1\times10^{-7}h^2\ {\rm GeV}\sim10^2h^2\ {\rm eV}.}
$$

For $0.5<h<1$, this is of order $30$--$100\ {\rm eV}$ with these approximate constants. More precise constants change the coefficient, not the requested mass scale. This mass is much larger than the present momentum scale $T_{\nu0}\simeq1.4\times10^{-4}\ {\rm eV}$, validating the nonrelativistic [energy density](../../../../../energy-density.md) approximation. The result assumes that one thermal relic species dominates the matter density; it is conditional on that model rather than a measurement of a neutrino mass.
