<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In the [outer solution](../../../../../../outer-expansion.md), derivative terms are small and $\sqrt y\sim\varepsilon k$, so $y_{\rm out}\sim\varepsilon^2k^2$. To resolve its mismatch with the zero left boundary, set $x=\varepsilon\xi$, $y=\varepsilon^2Y$. The original equation becomes exactly $Y''-\sqrt Y+k=0$. The left [inner variable](../../../../../../inner-variable.md) is therefore $\xi=x/\varepsilon$, with $Y(0)=0$ and $Y\to k^2$ as $\xi\to+\infty$.

Multiplying by $Y'$ gives the [first integral of a square-root reaction layer](../../../../../../first-integral-of-a-square-root-reaction-layer.md). Matching $Y=k^2$, $Y'=0$ fixes its constant:

$$
\frac12(Y')^2-\frac23Y^{3/2}+kY=\frac{k^3}{3},\qquad \boxed{(Y')^2=\frac23(\sqrt Y-k)^2(2\sqrt Y+k).}
$$

The positive derivative branch below $k^2$ begins with $Y'(0)=\sqrt{2k^3/3}$ and approaches the plateau exponentially. Thus the left layer has thickness $O(\varepsilon)$, not $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ despite the coefficient on the original second [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md). Its small amplitude is essential in the [dominant balance](../../../../../../dominant-balance.md).
