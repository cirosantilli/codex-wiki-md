<h1 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Put $w=\sqrt Y$. The separated [first integral of a square-root reaction layer](../../../../../../../first-integral-of-a-square-root-reaction-layer.md) gives a primitive $\Phi(Y)$ as defined in [solution](../../solution.md). One can verify it directly: above the plateau,

$$
\frac{d\Phi}{dY}=\left[\frac23(\sqrt Y-k)^2(2\sqrt Y+k)\right]^{-1/2},
$$

while below it the derivative has the opposite sign. Accordingly the increasing left profile uses $\Phi(L)=\Phi(0)-\xi$, and the increasing right profile uses $\Phi(R)=\xi$ after choosing its translation constant.

As $w\to k$, the logarithm dominates:

$$
\Phi(Y)=\sqrt{2k}\log|w-k|+O(1).
$$

Hence the left profile approaches from below with $|\sqrt L-k|\propto e^{-\xi/\sqrt{2k}}$, while the right profile approaches from above with $|\sqrt R-k|\propto e^{\xi/\sqrt{2k}}$ as $\xi\to-\infty$. These are precisely the required two plateau [matching conditions](../../../../../../../asymptotic-matching-condition.md). The absolute value in the logarithm is necessary to use the same real primitive on both sides.
