<h1 id="5/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Multiply the homogeneous [Squire equation](../../../../../../../squire-equation.md) by $\bar\eta$ and integrate over the channel. The zero boundary values remove the [integration by parts](../../../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) boundary term, giving

$$
-i\omega\int_{-1}^{1}|\eta|^2dy+ik\int_{-1}^{1}U|\eta|^2dy+\frac1R\int_{-1}^{1}(|\eta'|^2+q^2|\eta|^2)dy=0.
$$

For real $U$ and $k$, the middle term is purely imaginary. Taking [real parts](../../../../../../../real-part.md) yields the [dissipation identity for a homogeneous Squire mode](../../../../../../../dissipation-identity-for-a-homogeneous-squire-mode.md):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}\omega=-\frac{\int_{-1}^{1}(|\eta'|^2+q^2|\eta|^2)dy}{R\int_{-1}^{1}|\eta|^2dy}<0.}
$$

The strict inequality holds for a nonzero mode with zero endpoint values and positive [Reynolds number](../../../../../../../reynolds-number.md). Hence **all homogeneous Squire modes are temporally stable**. In the complete shear-flow [perturbation](../../../../../../../perturbation.md) system, the Squire variable can also be forced by the wall-normal velocity; this homogeneous-mode result does not rule out instability of that coupled system or its [transient growth](../../../../../../../transient-growth.md).
