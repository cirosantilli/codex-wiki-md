<h1 id="5/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $k>0$, put $q=\sqrt{k^2+l^2}$ and define the [Squire transformation](../../../../../../../squire-transformation.md)

$$
\boxed{k'=q,\qquad l'=0,\qquad R'=\frac{k}{q}R,\qquad c'=c,\qquad \omega'=qc.}
$$

The two-dimensional [Orr-Sommerfeld equation](../../../../../../../orr-sommerfeld-equation.md) then has the same transverse operator because $k'^2=q^2$, and the same viscous coefficient because $k'R'=kR$. Its [boundary conditions](../../../../../../../boundary-condition.md) on $v$ and $v'$ are unchanged. Thus it has the same [eigenfunction](../../../../../../../eigenfunction.md) and the same phase-speed [eigenvalue](../../../../../../../eigenvalue.md).

If the original disturbance grows, $\operatorname{Im}c>0$, so the transformed disturbance also grows. Since $R'\leq R$, every unstable three-dimensional mode maps to an unstable two-dimensional mode at a no larger [Reynolds number](../../../../../../../reynolds-number.md). It follows that **the minimum critical Reynolds number is attained among two-dimensional disturbances**. This is the [Squire theorem](../../../../../../../squire-s-theorem.md) for temporal normal-mode onset, not a statement that three-dimensional transient amplification is impossible. The case $k=0$ is treated separately without dividing by $k$; its streamwise-uniform viscous [normal modes](../../../../../../../normal-mode.md) decay, although coupling can produce [transient growth](../../../../../../../transient-growth.md).
