<h1 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose the axial origin at the [sphere](../../../../../../sphere.md)'s centre. Reflection $x\mapsto-x$ leaves the straight tube, the spherical geometry and the transverse displacement of the centre unchanged. It reverses the prescribed axial [Hagen-Poiseuille flow](../../../../../../hagen-poiseuille-equation.md), while it leaves a hypothetical transverse particle velocity unchanged. On the other hand, [kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow](../../../../../../kinematic-reversibility-of-stokes-flow.md) says that reversing the imposed flow reverses every particle velocity, including that transverse component. The [force-free](../../../../../../force-free.md) and [torque-free](../../../../../../torque-free.md) constraints are preserved by both operations. Uniqueness of the coupled [Stokes flow](../../../../../../stokes-flow-split.md) and rigid-body motion therefore makes the transverse velocity equal to its own negative. It must vanish.

Consequently **$\dot c=0$** as the particle is advected. This is [no lateral migration of a single sphere in a uniform tube in Stokes flow](../../../../../../no-lateral-migration-of-a-single-sphere-in-a-uniform-tube-in-stokes-flow.md). The argument uses the fore-aft symmetry of a rigid sphere and a uniform tube; it does not extend automatically to inertial migration or deformable particles.
