<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

A [preadditive category](../../../../../preadditive-category.md) has an [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) of [morphisms](../../../../../morphism.md) between each pair of objects, and composition is additive in both variables. An [additive category](../../../../../additive-category.md) is a [preadditive category](../../../../../preadditive-category.md) with a [zero object](../../../../../zero-object.md) and finite [products in a category](../../../../../product-category-theory.md); equivalently one may require finite coproducts. The equivalence, and the stronger assertion that these constructions agree canonically, follow from the argument below. An [abelian category](../../../../../abelian-category.md) is an [additive category](../../../../../additive-category.md) with [kernels in a category](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) and [cokernels in a category](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md), in which every [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md) is a [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) and every [epimorphism](../../../../../epimorphism.md) is a [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md). A [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md) with the first property is a [normal monomorphism](../../../../../normal-monomorphism.md); an [epimorphism](../../../../../epimorphism.md) with the second is a [conormal epimorphism](../../../../../conormal-epimorphism.md).

Let $P=\prod_{i=1}^n A_i$, with projections $p_i$. There is a unique $i_j:A_j\to P$ with $p_ii_j=\delta_{ij}1_{A_j}$, interpreting the other components as zero. Since every $p_j$ sends $\sum_i i_ip_i$ to $p_j$, the product's [universal property](../../../../../universal-property.md) gives

$$
p_ii_j=\delta_{ij}1_{A_j},\qquad \sum_i i_ip_i=1_P.
$$

Given arrows $f_i:A_i\to B$, put $f=\sum_i f_ip_i$. Then $fi_j=f_j$. Conversely any arrow with these restrictions equals $f1_P=\sum_i fi_ip_i$, so it is uniquely this sum. Thus $(P,i_i)$ is a [coproduct in a category](../../../../../coproduct.md). The empty product is the [zero object](../../../../../zero-object.md) and also the empty coproduct. The dual argument starts from coproducts and constructs projections; it proves the equivalent definition. Hence **finite products and coproducts are the same biproducts**, with the displayed identities giving the canonical identification.

For the [image factorization in an abelian category](../../../../../image-factorization-in-an-abelian-category.md), let $h:A\to B$, let $c:B\to Q$ be its [cokernel](../../../../../cokernel.md), and let $i:I\to B$ be the [kernel](../../../../../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) of $c$. Since $ch=0$, write $h=ie$. We prove that $e:A\to I$ is epic. Suppose $t:I\to Z$ satisfies $te=0$ and let $l:L\to I$ be its [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md). Then $e=lr$ for some $r:A\to L$. The composite $il$ is monic and hence is a [normal monomorphism](../../../../../normal-monomorphism.md), say $il=\ker z$ for $z:B\to W$. Since $zh=zilr=0$, the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) property makes $z$ factor through $c$, and therefore $zi=0$. The [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) property of $il$ gives $s:I\to L$ with $ils=i$. Cancellation of $i$ yields $ls=1_I$; since $l$ is monic this also gives $sl=1_L$. Thus $l$ is invertible and $t=0$. In a [preadditive category](../../../../../preadditive-category.md), $ue=ve$ now implies $(u-v)e=0$, hence $u=v$. This proves $e$ is an [epimorphism](../../../../../epimorphism.md) and supplies the required factorization.

For uniqueness, an [epimorphism](../../../../../epimorphism.md) that is a [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) is the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of its own [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md). Indeed, if $e=\operatorname{coker}r$ and $k=\ker e$, then $r$ factors through $k$; an arrow killing $k$ therefore kills $r$ and factors uniquely through $e$. Dually, a [normal monomorphism](../../../../../normal-monomorphism.md) is the [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) of its own [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md). If $h=mp$ is any epi–mono factorization, monicity of $m$ gives $\ker p=\ker h$. Both $p$ and $e$ are consequently [categorical cokernels](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of this same [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md). Their [universal properties](../../../../../universal-property.md) give a unique [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md) $\theta$ between the intermediate objects with $\theta p=e$, and cancellation of $p$ gives $i\theta=m$. Therefore **the epi–mono factorization is unique up to the unique compatible isomorphism**.

Here is an [additive category with truncated primary torsion](../../../../../additive-category-with-truncated-primary-torsion.md), with the prime fixed as $2$. Its objects are [finitely generated abelian groups](../../../../../finitely-generated-abelian-group.md) having no element of order $4$, and its morphisms are all [group homomorphisms](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md). Equivalently their $2$-primary torsion [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) has exponent at most $2$. It is a full [subcategory](../../../../../subcategory.md) of [abelian groups](../../../../../abelian-group.md), closed under finite [direct sums](../../../../../direct-sum.md) and [subgroups](../../../../../subgroup.md). Its hom groups and bilinear composition are inherited, and the zero group and finite [direct sums](../../../../../direct-sum.md) belong to it, so it is additive. [Categorical kernels](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) are ordinary [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) [categorical kernels](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md), which are still finitely generated and have no element of order $4$.

To construct [categorical cokernels](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md), for an arbitrary finitely generated [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) $G$ let $t_2(G)$ be its $2$-primary [torsion subgroup](../../../../../torsion-subgroup.md), and define

$$
R(G)=G/(2t_2(G)).
$$

The [Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](../../../../../fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups.md) shows that this quotient lies in the subcategory: it leaves the free and odd-primary parts unchanged and replaces each cyclic $2$-primary summand by either $0$ or $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Every homomorphism $G\to H$ to an allowed group kills $2t_2(G)$, because it sends $2$-primary torsion into a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) killed by $2$. Thus $G\to R(G)$ has the [universal property](../../../../../universal-property.md) of a reflection. For $f:A\to B$, its [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) in the subcategory is

$$
B\longrightarrow R(B/f(A)).
$$

This has exactly the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) [universal property](../../../../../universal-property.md) by the preceding factorization. Finite [categorical limits](../../../../../categorical-limit.md) exist because [equalizers](../../../../../equaliser.md) are [categorical kernels](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) of differences and finite products exist. Finite [colimits](../../../../../colimit.md) exist dually because [coequalizers](../../../../../coequalizer.md) are [categorical cokernels](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of differences and finite coproducts exist.

The [epimorphisms](../../../../../epimorphism.md) in this example are precisely the surjective homomorphisms. A surjection is epic already in [abelian groups](../../../../../abelian-group.md). Conversely, if $f$ is epic in the subcategory, its [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) is zero, so $R(B/f(A))=0$. A nonzero finitely generated [abelian group](../../../../../abelian-group.md) has nonzero reflection: a nonzero free or odd-primary summand survives, and a nonzero finite $2$-primary summand has a nonzero quotient modulo twice itself. Hence $B/f(A)=0$ and $f$ is surjective. Its ordinary [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) belongs to the subcategory, and its quotient by that [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) is its codomain, which also belongs to the subcategory. It is therefore the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of its [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md). This proves that every [epimorphism](../../../../../epimorphism.md) is conormal.

The [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md)

$$
m:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z,\qquad m(n)=4n
$$

is not normal. Its [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) in this category is reduction modulo $2$, since $R(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) of that [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) is the inclusion $2\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z$, whereas the image of $m$ is $4\mathbb Z$. A [normal monomorphism](../../../../../normal-monomorphism.md) is the [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) of its own [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md), as proved above, so $m$ cannot be normal. In fact multiplication by $2$ is itself the [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) of reduction modulo $2$, up to the identification $\mathbb Z\cong2\mathbb Z$. Its composite with itself is multiplication by $4$. Thus this example already exhibits two [normal monomorphisms](../../../../../normal-monomorphism.md) whose composite is not normal.

For the general assertion, let $m:A\to B$ be a nonnormal [monomorphism](../../../../../monomorphism.md) in any [additive category](../../../../../additive-category.md) of the specified kind. Put $c=\operatorname{coker}m$ and $k:K\to B$ be its [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md), and factor $m=kg$. Both $m$ and $k$ are monic, so $g$ is monic. Set $d=\operatorname{coker}g$ and $l:L\to K$ be its [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md). Then $k,l$ are normal, and $g=lr$ for some $r$.

Suppose their composite were normal, say $kl=\ker t$. Since $tm=tklr=0$, $t$ factors through $c$, whence $tk=0$. The [categorical kernel](../../../../../kernel-in-a-category.md) property of $kl$ supplies $s:K\to L$ with $kls=k$; cancellation shows $ls=1_K$, and monicity makes $l$ an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Because $dl=0$, this forces $d=0$. In a [preadditive category](../../../../../preadditive-category.md), vanishing of the [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of $g$ implies that $g$ is epic: any $u,v$ with $ug=vg$ have $u-v$ factor through the zero [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md). By hypothesis $g$ is therefore a [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md), and it is also monic. If $g=\operatorname{coker}a$, monicity and $ga=0$ force $a=0$; a [categorical cokernel](../../../../../cokernel-in-a-category.md) of a zero arrow is an [isomorphism](../../../../../isomorphism.md). Thus $g$ is invertible, making $m=kg$ normal, a contradiction. Consequently **normal monomorphisms cannot be closed under composition in any such category**; the explicit pair $k,l$ witnesses the failure.
