<h1 id="2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

An [immersion](../../../../../immersion.md) $M^4\to\mathbb R^5$ would inject $TM$ into the trivial rank-five bundle. Taking its orthogonal complement gives a normal [real line bundle](../../../../../real-line-bundle.md) $\nu$ with $TM\oplus\nu\cong\varepsilon^5$. The [Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes](../../../../../whitney-product-formula-for-stiefel-whitney-classes.md) requires

$$
w(TM)w(\nu)=1.
$$

The [real line bundle](../../../../../real-line-bundle.md) need not be orientable, but it has no [Stiefel–Whitney classes](../../../../../stiefel-whitney-class.md) above degree one.

Let $a,b$ be the pulled-back degree-one generators from the two real projective planes. Their mod-two [cohomology ring](../../../../../cohomology-ring.md), by the [Künneth theorem](../../../../../kunneth-theorem.md), is $\mathbb F_2[a,b]/(a^3,b^3)$. To compute the tangent classes, at a line $L$ the projective tangent space is $\operatorname{Hom}(L,L^\perp)$. Adding $\operatorname{Hom}(L,L)\cong\mathbb R$ gives $\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbb R^3)\cong3L$, with the real line identified with its dual using a metric. Thus the [stabilized tangent bundle of real projective space](../../../../../stabilized-tangent-bundle-of-real-projective-space.md) gives $T\mathbb{RP}^2\oplus\varepsilon^1\cong3\gamma$ and

$$
w(T\mathbb{RP}^2)=(1+a)^3=1+a+a^2.
$$

The [tangent bundle](../../../../../tangent-bundle.md) of the product is the direct sum of the pulled-back tangent bundles. Since $(1+a+a^2)(1+a)=1$ in this truncated ring, and similarly for $b$,

$$
w(TM)^{-1}=(1+a)(1+b)=1+a+b+ab.
$$

The degree-two class $ab$ is nonzero by the product-ring basis. It cannot be a class of the rank-one bundle $\nu$. Equivalently $w_1(TM)^2+w_2(TM)=ab\ne0$, violating the [codimension-one Stiefel–Whitney immersion obstruction](../../../../../codimension-one-stiefel-whitney-immersion-obstruction.md). Hence **$\mathbb{RP}^2\times\mathbb{RP}^2$ admits no [immersion](../../../../../immersion.md) in $\mathbb R^5$**.
