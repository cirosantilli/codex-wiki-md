<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $\|x_n^{-1}\|\|x-x_n\|<1$, then

$$
x=x_n\bigl(1+x_n^{-1}(x-x_n)\bigr)
$$

is invertible by the [Neumann series](../../../../../../neumann-series.md), a contradiction. Thus every $n$ satisfies

$$
\|x_n^{-1}\|\ge\frac1{\|x-x_n\|}.
$$

Here $x\ne x_n$ because $x_n$ is invertible and $x$ is not. Since $x_n\to x$, this proves the [inverse norm divergence at noninvertible boundary points](../../../../../../inverse-norm-divergence-at-noninvertible-boundary-points.md):

$$
\boxed{\|x_n^{-1}\|\longrightarrow\infty.}
$$
