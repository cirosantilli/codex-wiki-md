<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

If $u$ is invertible, applying the [algebra involution](../../../../../../algebra-involution.md) to $u^{-1}u=uu^{-1}=1$ gives

$$
u^*(u^{-1})^*=(u^{-1})^*u^*=1,
$$

so $u^*$ is invertible and $(u^*)^{-1}=(u^{-1})^*$. The converse follows by applying $*$ again. Since

$$
(x-\lambda1)^*=x^*-\overline\lambda1,
$$

we have $\lambda\notin\sigma_A(x)$ if and only if $\overline\lambda\notin\sigma_A(x^*)$. Taking complements and using that [complex conjugation](../../../../../../complex-conjugation.md) is a bijection gives

$$
\boxed{\sigma_A(x^*)=\{\overline\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma_A(x)\}.}
$$

The conjugation bar in the printed PDF is essential; this is not an assertion that the two spectra always coincide.
