<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The [covariant Landau gauge](../../../../../../landau-gauge-quantum-field-theory.md) is the zero-parameter limit $\xi\to0$ of the covariant gauge family. It imposes the [Lorenz gauge](../../../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md) condition $\partial_\mu A^\mu=0$ sharply. The [Landau gauge photon propagator](../../../../../../landau-gauge-photon-propagator.md) is the transverse part of the inverse from [solution](../c/solution.md):

$$
\boxed{D^{\rm L}_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i}{k^2+i0}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{k^2}\right)}.
$$

Away from the pole, $k^\mu D^{\rm L}_{\mu\nu}=0$, matching the divergence-free condition. This is transversality to the [four-momentum](../../../../../../four-momentum.md), rather than a projector onto only the two physical spatial photon polarizations. The [Lorenz gauge](../../../../../../lorenz-gauge-condition.md) equation alone still admits residual [gauge transformations](../../../../../../gauge-transformation.md) with $\Box\chi=0$; boundary conditions and the pole prescription complete the propagator choice. The parameter limit specifies the Landau propagator without trying to invert the ungauged Maxwell operator on its longitudinal null space.
