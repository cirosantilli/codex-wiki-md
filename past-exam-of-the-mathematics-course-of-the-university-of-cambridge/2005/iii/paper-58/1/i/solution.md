<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Use the symmetric lossless [beam splitter](../../../../../../beam-splitter.md) convention with transmission amplitude $t=1/\sqrt2$ and reflection amplitude $r=i/\sqrt2$. The two mirrors contribute the same fixed [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md), which can be suppressed. At the rightward port $D_1$, the upper-arm route has one reflection at BS1 and one transmission at BS2, while the lower-arm route has one transmission at BS1 and one reflection at BS2. Their [probability amplitudes](../../../../../../probability-amplitude.md) therefore add as

$$
a_1=rt\,e^{i\theta}+tr=\frac{i}{2}(e^{i\theta}+1).
$$

By the [Born rule](../../../../../../born-rule.md),

$$
\boxed{P(D_1)=|a_1|^2=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}=\cos^2\!\left(\frac\theta2\right).}
$$

For the upward port, the two amplitudes are $r^2e^{i\theta}$ and $t^2$, giving $a_0=(1-e^{i\theta})/2$ and $P(D_0)=\sin^2(\theta/2)$. They sum to one, as required for an ideal lossless [Mach-Zehnder interferometer](../../../../../../mach-zehnder-interferometer.md). Thus $D_1$ is bright at zero relative [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) and dark at [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) $\pi$. A fixed unequal reflection [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) or a swapped port labeling would shift the fringe; the stated convention fixes the port and [quantum phase](../../../../../../quantum-phase.md) calibration used below.
