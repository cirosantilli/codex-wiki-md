<h1 id="4/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Take the double cover of $S^2$ branched at $2g+2$ points, with deck [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) $\iota$. One explicit construction cuts the sphere along $g+1$ disjoint arcs pairing the branch points and cross-glues two copies along the cuts. Its [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) is $2\chi(S^2)-(2g+2)=2-2g$, so it is an oriented closed surface of genus $g$. Locally at a branch point the projection is $z\mapsto z^2$ and the [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) is $z\mapsto-z$; thus the [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) is nonidentity and orientation-preserving, also when $g=0$.

The invariant rational [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) of a finite surface action identifies with the [cohomology](../../../../../../cohomology-split.md) of its quotient. For this action, triangulate the quotient with the branch points as vertices and lift the [triangulation](../../../../../../triangulation.md). Quotient [cochains](../../../../../../singular-cochain.md) identify with invariant [cochains](../../../../../../singular-cochain.md), and averaging over the two deck transformations makes taking invariants exact over $\mathbb Q$. Hence the invariant part of $H^1(S;\mathbb Q)$ is $H^1(S^2;\mathbb Q)=0$. Since $\iota^2=1$, its eigenvalues are $\pm1$ and it is therefore $-I$ on the $2g$-dimensional $H^1$. Its actions on $H^0$ and $H^2$ are both the identity. Thus

$$
\boxed{L(\iota)=1-(-2g)+1=2g+2\ne0.}
$$

[Homotopy](../../../../../../homotopy.md) invariance and the result just proved show that **every map homotopic to this [involution](../../../../../../involution.md) has fixed points**, for every genus.
