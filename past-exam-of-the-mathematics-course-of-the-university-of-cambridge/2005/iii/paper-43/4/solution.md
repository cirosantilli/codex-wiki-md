↑ **Parent:** [4](../4.md)

A [credibility estimate](../../../../../credibility-estimate.md) combines a risk's observed experience with collective or [prior](../../../../../prior-probability.md) information, typically as $Z\overline X+(1-Z)m_0$. The [credibility factor](../../../../../credibility-factor.md) $Z\in[0,1]$ is the weight assigned to the individual experience: more reliable or more extensive data generally increases it. [Bayesian credibility](../../../../../bayesian-credibility.md) treats the risk parameter as random with a [prior distribution](../../../../../prior-probability.md) and predicts future claims by averaging the conditional claim mean under its [posterior distribution](../../../../../bayesian-posterior.md). Under [squared-error loss](../../../../../squared-error-loss.md) this [posterior predictive](../../../../../posterior-predictive-distribution.md) mean is optimal. It need not be an [affine combination](../../../../../affine-combination.md) in every Bayesian model; the [Poisson-gamma conjugacy](../../../../../poisson-gamma-conjugacy.md) calculation below yields that credibility form exactly, rather than as a best-linear approximation.
