<h1 id="5/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a [number field](../../../../../../number-field.md) $K$ and a [modulus of a number field](../../../../../../modulus-of-a-number-field.md) $\mathfrak m$, let $I_K(\mathfrak m)$ be the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of [fractional ideals](../../../../../../fractional-ideal.md) prime to its finite part. Let $P_K(\mathfrak m)$ consist of [principal ideals](../../../../../../principal-ideal.md) generated by elements congruent to one modulo its finite part and positive at each real place in its infinite part. The [ray class group](../../../../../../ray-class-group.md) is $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)=I_K(\mathfrak m)/P_K(\mathfrak m)$.

The classification theorem of [class field theory](../../../../../../class-field-theory.md) asserts the existence of a [ray class field](../../../../../../ray-class-field.md) $K_{\mathfrak m}$ and an [Artin map](../../../../../../artin-reciprocity-law.md) [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md)

$$
\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak m}/K).
$$

Its [intermediate fields](../../../../../../intermediate-field.md) correspond contravariantly to [subgroups](../../../../../../subgroup.md) of this [ray class group](../../../../../../ray-class-group.md). Equivalently, for each [congruence subgroup of fractional ideals](../../../../../../congruence-subgroup-of-fractional-ideals.md) $P_K(\mathfrak m)\subseteq H\subseteq I_K(\mathfrak m)$ there is a unique finite [abelian extension](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md) with Artin [group kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) $H$ and [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $I_K(\mathfrak m)/H$. Every finite [abelian extension](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md) occurs for some modulus. For a fixed modulus, these are exactly the extensions whose conductor divides that modulus; the same field may be presented using larger moduli, with the appropriate pulled-back [group kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md).

We quote the conductor facts needed to specialize this classification. The finite conductor exponent is zero exactly at the unramified primes. A real place occurs in the conductor exactly when it becomes complex. Thus conductor one means unramified at every finite prime and split at every real place. With $\mathfrak m=1$ the [ray class group](../../../../../../ray-class-group.md) is the ordinary [ideal class group](../../../../../../ideal-class-group.md), so classification gives an extension $H_K/K$ with

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(H_K/K)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(K),\qquad[H_K:K]=h_K.}
$$

It is unramified at all finite primes, and its real places split. Conversely, every finite [abelian extension](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md) with those properties has conductor one and lies in $K_1=H_K$. This proves the existence and maximality of the ordinary [Hilbert class field](../../../../../../hilbert-class-field.md). Its degree is finite by finiteness of the [ideal class group](../../../../../../ideal-class-group.md). The real-place convention matters: allowing real places to become complex gives the narrow class field instead; for the [imaginary quadratic field](../../../../../../imaginary-quadratic-field.md) in part (ii) this distinction disappears.
