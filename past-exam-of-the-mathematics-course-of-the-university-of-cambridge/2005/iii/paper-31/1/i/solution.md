<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Let $L/K$ be a finite [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md) of [number fields](../../../../../../number-field.md), with [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) $G$, and let $\mathfrak p$ be a nonzero [prime ideal](../../../../../../prime-ideal.md) of $\mathcal O_K$. Write its [prime ideal factorization](../../../../../../prime-ideal-factorization.md) as

$$
\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L=\prod_{j=1}^g\mathfrak P_j^{e_j},\qquad
f_j=[\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak P_j:\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p].
$$

The [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) acts transitively on the primes above $\mathfrak p$. One proof uses the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md). If $\mathfrak Q$ were outside the orbit of $\mathfrak P$, choose an [integral element](../../../../../../integral-element.md) $a$ which is zero modulo $\mathfrak Q$ and one modulo every conjugate of $\mathfrak P$. Its [field norm](../../../../../../field-norm.md) $\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(a)$ belongs to $\mathfrak Q\cap\mathcal O_K=\mathfrak p$, hence to $\mathfrak P$. But each factor is one modulo $\mathfrak P$, a contradiction. Transitivity also makes all $e_j$ equal to a common [ramification index](../../../../../../ramification-index.md) $e$ and all $f_j$ equal to a common [residue degree](../../../../../../residue-degree.md) $f$.

Fix $\mathfrak P$ above $\mathfrak p$. Its [decomposition group](../../../../../../decomposition-group.md) is the stabilizer

$$
D_{\mathfrak P}=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma\mathfrak P=\mathfrak P\}.
$$

The [orbit-stabilizer theorem](../../../../../../orbit-stabilizer-theorem.md) gives $|D_{\mathfrak P}|=|G|/g$. Elements of $D_{\mathfrak P}$ act on the [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md) $\kappa(\mathfrak P)$ over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$; the [group kernel](../../../../../../kernel-of-a-group-homomorphism.md) is the [inertia group](../../../../../../inertia-group.md)

$$
I_{\mathfrak P}=\{\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak P}:\sigma(a)\equiv a\pmod{\mathfrak P}\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal O_L\}.
$$

Thus inertia measures the [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) invisible after reduction, whereas decomposition measures all [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) preserving the chosen prime.

The completion $L_{\mathfrak P}/K_{\mathfrak p}$ is a [Galois extension](../../../../../../finite-galois-extension.md) with [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) $D_{\mathfrak P}$: preservation of $\mathfrak P$ lets an [automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism.md) extend continuously to the completion, and the local embeddings of this normal extension arise in this way. The [degree of a field extension](../../../../../../degree-of-a-field-extension.md) formula is $[L_{\mathfrak P}:K_{\mathfrak p}]=ef$. Reduction gives the [exact sequence](../../../../../../exact-sequence.md)

$$
1\longrightarrow I_{\mathfrak P}\longrightarrow D_{\mathfrak P}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa(\mathfrak P)/\kappa(\mathfrak p))
\longrightarrow1.
$$

For [surjectivity](../../../../../../surjective-function.md), lift a primitive residue-field generator by the [Hensel lemma](../../../../../../hensel-s-lemma.md) to obtain the [maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension](../../../../../../maximal-unramified-subextension-of-a-local-field-extension.md) of $L_{\mathfrak P}/K_{\mathfrak p}$. Its [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md) are the residue-field [automorphisms](../../../../../../automorphism.md). They extend to $L_{\mathfrak P}$ because the latter is normal. Since a finite [residue field](../../../../../../residue-field.md) extension has a cyclic [Galois group](../../../../../../galois-group.md) of order $f$, the [exact sequence](../../../../../../exact-sequence.md) yields

$$
\boxed{|D_{\mathfrak P}|=ef,\qquad |I_{\mathfrak P}|=e,\qquad |D_{\mathfrak P}/I_{\mathfrak P}|=f,\qquad [L:K]=gef.}
$$

The [fixed field](../../../../../../fixed-field.md) of local inertia is the [maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension](../../../../../../maximal-unramified-subextension-of-a-local-field-extension.md), and the extension above it is [totally ramified](../../../../../../totally-ramified-extension.md). Primes in the same global orbit have conjugate [decomposition groups](../../../../../../decomposition-group.md) and [inertia groups](../../../../../../inertia-group.md), so the orders do not depend on the chosen prime.

If $e=1$, inertia is trivial and $D_{\mathfrak P}$ is generated by the unique [Frobenius automorphism](../../../../../../frobenius-automorphism.md) acting on residues by $z\mapsto z^{N\mathfrak p}$. Its order is $f$. In a ramified extension this residue action determines only a coset modulo inertia. A prime splits completely exactly when $e=f=1$, equivalently its [decomposition group](../../../../../../decomposition-group.md) is trivial. A [totally ramified](../../../../../../totally-ramified-extension.md) prime has $g=f=1$ and $I_{\mathfrak P}=D_{\mathfrak P}=G$; an unramified prime has $I_{\mathfrak P}=1$. In an [abelian extension](../../../../../../abelian-extension.md), conjugation does nothing, so the associated [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) and unramified [Artin symbol](../../../../../../artin-symbol.md) are independent of the prime above $\mathfrak p$.
