<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [existence and pathwise uniqueness theorem for a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../existence-and-pathwise-uniqueness-theorem-for-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) states that [locally Lipschitz](../../../../../../locally-lipschitz-function.md) drift $b$ and diffusion $\sigma$ on an open domain give a pathwise unique [strong solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../strong-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md) up to a maximal lifetime $\xi$. The initial value is measurable with respect to the initial sigma-algebra, and the filtration satisfies the [usual conditions for a filtration](../../../../../../usual-conditions-for-a-filtration.md). On $\mathbb R^d$, if $T_n$ are exits from increasing balls, then $T_n\uparrow\xi$; a finite lifetime entails escape from every compact subset. The coefficients need not satisfy a global growth bound for this local result.

One construction truncates the coefficients outside each compact region to [globally Lipschitz](../../../../../../globally-lipschitz-function.md) coefficients, solves the resulting global equations, and patches their solutions until the exits. [Pathwise uniqueness](../../../../../../pathwise-uniqueness.md) makes solutions from larger truncations agree on the smaller stopped interval. Their union is the [maximal local solution of a stochastic differential equation](../../../../../../maximal-local-solution-of-a-stochastic-differential-equation.md), and any extension would contradict the exhaustion of the domain by the exit times.
