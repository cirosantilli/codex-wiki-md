<h1 id="2/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a [stopping time](../../../../../../../stopping-time.md) $T\ge s$, the [optional sampling theorem](../../../../../../../optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale.md) for the [L2-bounded continuous martingale](../../../../../../../l2-bounded-continuous-martingale.md) $M$ gives $\mathbb E[M_T\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s$. Consequently

$$
\mathbb E[M_TN_s]=\mathbb E[M_sN_s].
$$

If $N_s$ is unbounded, first multiply by a bounded truncation and then remove it using $L^2$ bounds and the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../../../../../../../cauchy-schwarz-inequality.md). Thus (ii) implies (iv). Taking $T=s$ proves the converse. For $T=\infty$, the same calculation uses the $L^2$ terminal value.
