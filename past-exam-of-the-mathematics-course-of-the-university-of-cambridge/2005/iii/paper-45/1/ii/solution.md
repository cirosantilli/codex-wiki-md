<h1 id="1/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Label the two paternal ancestral marker copies $P_1,P_2$ and the two maternal copies $M_1,M_2$. Treat these as four distinct ancestral copies in an [outbred pedigree](../../../../../../outbred-pedigree.md); equal observed [alleles](../../../../../../allele.md) need not imply [identity by descent](../../../../../../identity-by-descent.md). By [Mendelian segregation](../../../../../../mendelian-segregation.md), each child independently receives either paternal copy with [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/2$. Thus the indicator $I_P$ that the two [full siblings](../../../../../../full-sibling.md) inherit the same paternal copy satisfies

$$
P(I_P=1)=P(P_1,P_1)+P(P_2,P_2)=\frac14+\frac14=\frac12.
$$

The same argument gives $P(I_M=1)=1/2$ for the maternal copy. Paternal and maternal [meioses](../../../../../../meiosis.md) are independent, so $I_P$ and $I_M$ are independent. Consequently the number $J=I_P+I_M$ of copies shared [identical by descent](../../../../../../identity-by-descent.md) has a [binomial distribution](../../../../../../binomial-distribution.md), giving

$$
\boxed{P(J=0)=\frac14,\qquad P(J=1)=\frac12,\qquad P(J=2)=\frac14.}
$$

This is [Mendelian IBD sharing of full siblings](../../../../../../mendelian-ibd-sharing-of-full-siblings.md). It describes an ordinary, unselected sib pair: conditioning on a shared disease [phenotype](../../../../../../phenotype.md) can change the distribution if there is [genetic linkage](../../../../../../genetic-linkage.md) to its causal locus.
