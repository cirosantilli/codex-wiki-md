<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose a disease-risk [mutation](../../../../../../mutation.md) first occurs on an ancestral [haplotype](../../../../../../haplotype.md). Descendants carrying the risk [allele](../../../../../../allele.md) initially share that surrounding [haplotype](../../../../../../haplotype.md). Historical [genetic recombination](../../../../../../genetic-recombination.md) progressively replaces the flanks while retaining the disease [allele](../../../../../../allele.md), leaving [linkage disequilibrium](../../../../../../linkage-disequilibrium.md) between the causal variant and some nearby [genetic markers](../../../../../../genetic-marker.md). A noncausal marker can therefore have different [allele](../../../../../../allele.md) frequencies in affected individuals and suitably chosen controls. This is the basis of [association mapping](../../../../../../association-mapping.md).

One first tests dense markers or multi-marker [haplotypes](../../../../../../haplotype.md) for [genetic association](../../../../../../genetic-association.md) with the disease [phenotype](../../../../../../phenotype.md). Strongly correlated [single-nucleotide polymorphisms](../../../../../../single-nucleotide-polymorphism.md) can be represented by [tag SNPs](../../../../../../tag-snp.md) to locate an associated region efficiently. Within that region, denser genotyping and examination of disease-associated [haplotypes](../../../../../../haplotype.md) locate historical recombination breakpoints: segments retained across independently recombined disease-bearing [chromosomes](../../../../../../chromosome.md) are stronger candidates for containing the causal [allele](../../../../../../allele.md). Compare affected and unaffected carriers as well, since incomplete [penetrance](../../../../../../penetrance.md) and other risk factors can prevent a perfect haplotype-disease correspondence.

[Association mapping](../../../../../../association-mapping.md) can achieve fine resolution because it uses recombination accumulated over many generations, rather than only the few observed [meioses](../../../../../../meiosis.md) in a present-day linkage [pedigree](../../../../../../pedigree.md). Resolution depends on the local recombination history: a long region of strong LD may identify a disease-associated block while leaving many variants statistically indistinguishable. Additional informative [haplotypes](../../../../../../haplotype.md) or populations with different LD can help separate them. Conversely, very weak LD requires closer marker spacing to avoid missing the causal region.

[Population stratification](../../../../../../population-stratification.md) must be controlled by ancestry-matched comparisons, statistical adjustment or an appropriate family-based association design, since ancestry can affect both disease frequency and marker frequency. A marker association alone establishes neither causality nor identity of the disease gene; nearby correlated variants must be distinguished by further genetic and functional evidence. **Use LD to detect a disease-associated ancestral segment, then use historical recombinants to narrow the causal interval.** The [correlation](../../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) between causal variants and their surrounding [haplotypes](../../../../../../haplotype.md), together with the value of representative markers, is illustrated in [the HapMap study](https://www.genome.gov/Pages/Research/DER/HapMap.pdf).
