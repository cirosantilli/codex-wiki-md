<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let the [first cousins](../../../../../../first-cousin.md) be $X,Y$, with parents $(P,U)$ and $(Q,V)$ respectively, where $P,Q$ are [full siblings](../../../../../../full-sibling.md) and $U,V$ have no ancestral relationship to one another or to that sibling family. To compute $\phi_{XY}$, choose one [allele](../../../../../../allele.md) copy from each cousin. They originate from $P$ and $Q$ with [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/4$. All other parental combinations have zero [kinship coefficient](../../../../../../kinship-coefficient.md) under the otherwise [outbred pedigree](../../../../../../outbred-pedigree.md) assumption. Hence

$$
\phi_{XY}=\frac14\phi_{PQ}=\frac14\cdot\frac14=\frac1{16}.
$$

The two gene copies within a child of $X,Y$ are random transmissions from these parents. They are [identical by descent](../../../../../../identity-by-descent.md) precisely in the event defining parental kinship, so the child's [inbreeding coefficient](../../../../../../inbreeding-coefficient.md) is

$$
\boxed{F_{\mathrm{child}}=\phi_{XY}=\frac1{16}.}
$$

For a direct ancestral-path check, there are two shared noninbred grandparents. Through either grandparent, the two cousin-to-grandparent paths have two [meioses](../../../../../../meiosis.md) each, and matching the ancestral grandparental copy supplies a further factor $1/2$. Each contributes $2^{-2-2-1}=1/32$, giving the same [first-cousin inbreeding coefficient](../../../../../../first-cousin-inbreeding-coefficient.md). Additional ancestral relationships or inbred grandparents would change the result.
