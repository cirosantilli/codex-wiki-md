<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

This question uses proper cosmological time $t$. The future-directed unit normal is

$$
\boxed{n^\mu=(1,0,0,0),\qquad n_\mu=(-1,0,0,0).}
$$

The [non-null hypersurface projection](../../../../../../non-null-hypersurface-projection.md) removes all normal components. The only relevant [Christoffel symbols](../../../../../../christoffel-symbol.md) are $\Gamma^0_{ij}=a\dot a\delta_{ij}$. Since the components of $n_\mu$ are constant,

$$
K_{ij}=\nabla_i n_j=-\Gamma^0_{ij}n_0=a\dot a\delta_{ij}=H g_{ij},\qquad K_{0\mu}=0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{K=3H=3\dot a/a.}
$$

With the printed positive [extrinsic curvature](../../../../../../extrinsic-curvature.md) convention, this is the expansion of the normal comoving congruence. A fixed coordinate cell has proper volume proportional to $a^3$, so $K=d\log(a^3)/dt=\nabla_\mu n^\mu$: the fractional proper-volume expansion rate.
