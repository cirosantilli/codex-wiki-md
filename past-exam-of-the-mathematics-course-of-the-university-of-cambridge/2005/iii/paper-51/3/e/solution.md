<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A finite blocking step integrates degrees of freedom on bounded length scales. Its function $g(u)$ is generally analytic near the critical couplings and contributes to a regular background in the [free-energy density](../../../../../../free-energy-density.md). The nonanalytic critical behavior arises from arbitrarily many blocking steps approaching the critical [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md). After removing the analytic background, the homogeneous recursion determines the leading singular [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md), so the inhomogeneous term usually need not be retained when computing those powers.

There are qualifications. If the singular [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md) has $\alpha<0$, the regular background may dominate the measured total [heat capacity](../../../../../../heat-capacity.md), although the singular exponent remains meaningful. At a [renormalization-group free-energy resonance](../../../../../../renormalization-group-free-energy-resonance.md), repeated accumulation of analytic terms can generate a logarithm. For example, a term proportional to $t^2$ in $g$ has weight $b^{-jD}b^{2jy_t}$; when $D=2y_t$, every step contributes the same multiple of $t^2$. The number of steps before $t$ becomes order one is proportional to $-\ln|t|$, giving a $t^2\ln|t|$ singularity. Thus **the inhomogeneous term can be ignored for generic leading powers, but not indiscriminately for logarithmic corrections**.
