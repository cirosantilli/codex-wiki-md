[Universality of critical phenomena](../../../../../../universality-of-critical-phenomena.md) means that many microscopic models approach the same long-distance [renormalization-group fixed point](../../../../../../renormalization-group-fixed-point.md). After repeated coarse-graining, [irrelevant operators](../../../../../../irrelevant-operator.md) lose influence, whereas a few [relevant operators](../../../../../../relevant-operator.md) describe deviations from criticality. Models with the same spatial dimension, [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) symmetry, interaction range and appropriate relevant controls can therefore have the same [universality class](../../../../../../universality-class.md) despite very different microscopic constituents.

The [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md), appropriately normalized scaling functions and certain amplitude ratios are universal. The transition temperature, individual amplitudes, microscopic length scale and analytic background in the [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) are generally nonuniversal. The numerical value assigned to an [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) also depends on normalization. [Landau-Ginzburg theory](../../../../../../landau-ginzburg-theory.md) expresses this distinction by using a common symmetry-allowed functional form while allowing its coefficients to depend on the material. Computing the true [critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) below the [upper critical dimension](../../../../../../upper-critical-dimension.md) requires retaining fluctuations, rather than merely minimizing that functional.
