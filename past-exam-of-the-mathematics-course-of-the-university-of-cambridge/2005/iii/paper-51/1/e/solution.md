<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

[Critical exponents](../../../../../../critical-exponent.md) describe leading singular powers as the reduced temperature $t$ or conjugate field approaches zero. With $\beta_m$ used for the [order-parameter critical exponent](../../../../../../order-parameter-critical-exponent.md) to avoid confusion with inverse temperature, the definitions include

$$
C_s\sim |t|^{-\alpha},\quad M(t,0)\sim(-t)^{\beta_m},\quad\chi\sim|t|^{-\gamma},\quad\xi\sim|t|^{-\nu},\quad M(0,h)\sim\operatorname{sgn}(h)|h|^{1/\delta}.
$$

At criticality the [connected correlation function](../../../../../../connected-correlation-function.md) behaves as $G(r)\sim r^{-(D-2+\eta)}$. These definitions refer to the singular part and allow additional logarithms at marginal dimensions.

For an ordinary scalar [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md), the [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) is $h=rM+uM^3$. Below the transition $M^2=-r/u$, giving $\beta_m=1/2$. Differentiating the [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) gives $\chi^{-1}=r+3uM^2$: it equals $r$ above and $-2r$ below the transition, so $\gamma=1$. The quadratic fluctuation kernel is $\kappa k^2+r$ in the symmetric phase, giving [correlation length](../../../../../../correlation-length.md) $\xi=\sqrt{\kappa/r}$ and $\nu=1/2$. At zero mass the momentum-space [correlation function](../../../../../../correlation-function.md) is proportional to $k^{-2}$, so $\eta=0$. The minimized [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) and critical [equation of state](../../../../../../equation-of-state.md) give $\alpha=0$ and $\delta=3$, as calculated in the root solution.

At the [tricritical point](../../../../../../tricritical-point.md), $u=0$ and $h=rM+vM^5$. Then $M\sim(-r)^{1/4}$, and $\chi^{-1}=r+5vM^4$ is $r$ above and $-4r$ below the transition. The gradient kernel still gives $\nu=1/2$ and $\eta=0$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{(\alpha,\beta_m,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta)_{\rm ordinary}=(0,1/2,1,3,1/2,0),\quad(\alpha,\beta_m,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta)_{\rm tricritical}=(1/2,1/4,1,5,1/2,0).}
$$

These are [mean-field critical exponents](../../../../../../mean-field-critical-exponent.md). A systematic derivation incorporating fluctuations uses the [renormalization group](../../../../../../renormalization-group.md), extracts the relevant scaling eigenvalues, and differentiates the singular [free-energy density](../../../../../../free-energy-density.md), as in Question 3.
