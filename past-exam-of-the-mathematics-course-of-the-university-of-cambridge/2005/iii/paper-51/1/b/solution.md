<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

In a [first-order phase transition](../../../../../../first-order-phase-transition.md), two distinct [global minima](../../../../../../global-minimum.md) of the [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md) have equal values and exchange stability. The equilibrium [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) jumps, and a first derivative of the equilibrium [free energy](../../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) is discontinuous. Depending on the direction through the [phase diagram](../../../../../../phase-diagram.md), that derivative can be [magnetization](../../../../../../magnetization.md), [entropy](../../../../../../entropy.md) or another conjugate quantity; a first-order transition need not have latent heat along every chosen path.

In a [continuous phase transition](../../../../../../continuous-phase-transition.md), the equilibrium [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) changes continuously while the curvature at the critical minimum vanishes. For $u>0$ and $h=0$, $M=0$ is stable for $r>0$, but for $r<0$ the stable minima have $M\simeq\pm\sqrt{-r/u}$. The [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) therefore goes continuously to zero at $r=0$; the inverse [magnetic susceptibility](../../../../../../magnetic-susceptibility.md) vanishes there.

For $u<0$, the sextic term stabilizes the [Landau free energy](../../../../../../landau-free-energy.md). The nonzero minima can become favorable while $M=0$ still has positive curvature. At $r=3u^2/(16v)>0$ they coexist with $M=0$, so the [order parameter](../../../../../../order-parameter.md) jumps to magnitude $\sqrt{-3u/(4v)}$. Finding a stationary point alone does not locate the transition: **equilibrium requires comparing the values of all competing minima**. Spinodal boundaries describe loss of local stability, not coexistence.
