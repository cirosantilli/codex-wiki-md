<h1 id="4/ii/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Without [surfactant](../../../../../../../surfactant.md), [capillary pressure](../../../../../../../capillary-pressure.md) drives a surface flow towards a growing bulge. [Advection](../../../../../../../advection.md) compresses the [insoluble surfactant](../../../../../../../insoluble-surfactant.md) in converging parts of that motion, increasing concentration and lowering [surface tension](../../../../../../../surface-tension.md). The resulting gradient supplies a tangential [Marangoni stress](../../../../../../../marangoni-effect.md) that opposes the surface motion. This additional resistance slows capillary-driven redistribution of liquid even after the base-tension decrease has already been accounted for.

In the strong-elasticity limit, concentration gradients suppress tangential interface motion. The surface behaves as effectively immobile at leading order. Setting $u_s\approx0$ in the [velocity](../../../../../../../velocity.md) formula gives $\sigma_x\approx hp_x/2$, hence

$$
q\approx-\frac{h^3}{12\mu}p_x,
$$

one-quarter of the clean shear-free mobility $h^3/(3\mu)$. **This mobility reduction explains the quarter-rate limit; it slows the unstable capillary mode without removing the long-wave unstable band.**
