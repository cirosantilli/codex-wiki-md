<h1 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The first equation is [mass conservation](../../../../../../mass-conservation.md) for an [incompressible flow](../../../../../../incompressible-flow.md) whose area may change: local area storage balances the [divergence](../../../../../../divergence.md) of the volume flux $\alpha u$. The two left-hand momentum terms are local acceleration and [convective acceleration](../../../../../../convective-acceleration.md), while $-p_x$ is the driving [pressure gradient](../../../../../../pressure-gradient.md) and $-R(\alpha)u$ models viscous drag per unit mass. The [pressure-area law of a collapsible tube](../../../../../../pressure-area-law-of-a-collapsible-tube.md) expresses transverse wall balance using internal minus prescribed external [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md).

For example, choose a reference area $\mathcal A_*$, length $L$ and [velocity](../../../../../../velocity.md) $U_*$. If dimensional variables are $\mathcal A,s,t_d,v,P$, take

$$
\alpha=\mathcal A/\mathcal A_*,\quad x=s/L,\quad t=U_*t_d/L,\quad
u=v/U_*,\quad p=P/(\rho U_*^2).
$$

The dimensionless external [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) uses the same scale. A dimensional friction rate $r_f(\mathcal A)$ becomes $R(\alpha)=Lr_f(\mathcal A_*\alpha)/U_*$, and the dimensional wall [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md) law is divided by $\rho U_*^2$. These choices make both inertia and the [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md)-gradient coefficient unity; other equivalent reference scales are possible. On the mechanically stable wall branch, increasing area requires increased transmural [pressure](../../../../../../pressure.md), whereas an enlarged tube reduces resistance. Thus **$\boxed{\widetilde P'(\alpha)>0,\qquad R'(\alpha)<0}$**.
