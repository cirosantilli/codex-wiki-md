<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For the contrapositive of $(b)\Rightarrow(c)$, suppose $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$. Consider

$$
M=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1^p,\ldots,x_n^p).
$$

Let $x_i$ act by multiplication and $D_i$ by formal differentiation. Differentiation preserves the defining [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md), since $D_j(x_i^pf)=x_i^pD_jf$ in characteristic $p$. The relations $[D_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$ hold before quotienting and hence hold on $M$. Its [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) consists of the monomials $x^\alpha$ with $0\le\alpha_i<p$, so **$\dim_kM=p^n>0$**. This is the explicit finite-dimensional module for the [Weyl algebra in positive characteristic](../../../../../../weyl-algebra-in-positive-characteristic.md).

Therefore condition (b) fails in positive characteristic and implies characteristic zero. Combining this with the preceding two implications proves **$(a)\Longleftrightarrow(b)\Longleftrightarrow(c)$**. The same representation also has nonzero proper kernel, since the infinite-dimensional [Weyl algebra](../../../../../../weyl-algebra.md) cannot inject into a finite-dimensional endomorphism [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md), confirming its failure of simplicity in positive characteristic.
