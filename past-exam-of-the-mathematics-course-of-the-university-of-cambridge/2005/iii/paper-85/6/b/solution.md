<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

This part proves $(a)\Rightarrow(b)$. If $M\ne0$ were a finite-dimensional unital [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md), its action would give a homomorphism

$$
A_n(k)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_k(M).
$$

The kernel is a two-sided [ideal](../../../../../../ideal.md) and is proper because the identity acts as the identity on $M$. A [simple ring](../../../../../../simple-ring.md) therefore makes the homomorphism injective. This is impossible: $A_n(k)$ has the infinitely many linearly independent ordered [monomials](../../../../../../monomial.md) established in part (a), whereas the endomorphism [algebra](../../../../../../algebra-split.md) has dimension $(\dim_kM)^2$. Thus simplicity excludes every nonzero finite-dimensional [module](../../../../../../module-mathematics.md).

There is also a direct characteristic-zero check: the relation $[D_1,x_1]=1$ acts as a matrix identity. Taking its [trace](../../../../../../matrix-trace.md) gives $0=\dim_kM$ in $k$. In characteristic zero this forces $M=0$. This agrees with the [Weyl algebra has no nonzero finite-dimensional modules](../../../../../../weyl-algebra-has-no-nonzero-finite-dimensional-modules.md) criterion, but the injectivity argument proves the requested implication without presupposing characteristic zero.
