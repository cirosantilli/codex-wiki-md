<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For a complex [K3 surface](../../../../../../k3-surface.md), $L=H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$ is torsion-free and has its integral [cup product](../../../../../../cup-product.md) pairing

$$
q(x,y)=\langle x\smile y,[X]\rangle.
$$

The [K3 intersection lattice](../../../../../../k3-intersection-lattice.md) is

$$
\boxed{L\cong U^{\oplus3}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus2},}
$$

the even unimodular [integral quadratic lattice](../../../../../../integral-quadratic-lattice.md) of signature $(3,19)$. Here $U$ is the [hyperbolic plane](../../../../../../hyperbolic-plane-quadratic-form.md) with Gram matrix

$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

and $E_8(-1)$ is the negative of the positive-definite [E8 lattice](../../../../../../e8-lattice.md). Thus the rank is $3\cdot2+2\cdot8=22$ and the pairing has determinant of absolute value one.

These invariants can be checked from the topology and [Hodge theory](../../../../../../hodge-theory.md). The trivial [canonical bundle](../../../../../../canonical-bundle.md) and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$ give $\chi(\mathcal O_X)=2$. The [Noether formula](../../../../../../noether-formula.md) gives $c_2(X)=24$, which is the topological [Euler characteristic](../../../../../../euler-characteristic.md). Since $X$ is simply connected, $b_1=b_3=0$, so $24=2+b_2$ and $b_2=22$. Integral [Poincare duality](../../../../../../poincare-duality.md) gives unimodularity; the universal coefficient sequence and $H_1(X,\mathbb Z)=0$ give torsion-freeness of $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$.

Its second [Stiefel–Whitney class](../../../../../../stiefel-whitney-class.md) is the reduction modulo two of $c_1(X)$, hence zero. The characteristic-class identity

$$
\langle x^2,[X]\rangle
\equiv\langle x\smile w_2(X),[X]\rangle\pmod2
$$

makes the lattice even. Finally the real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic two-form give two positive directions, a [Kähler class](../../../../../../kahler-class.md) gives a third, and the primitive real $(1,1)$ classes are negative definite by the [Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations](../../../../../../hodge-riemann-bilinear-relations.md). Therefore the signature is $(3,19)$. The classification of indefinite even unimodular lattices then identifies the displayed lattice uniquely. In particular the [Picard group](../../../../../../picard-group.md) is only the integral $(1,1)$ part; it should not be confused with this rank-twenty-two topological lattice.
