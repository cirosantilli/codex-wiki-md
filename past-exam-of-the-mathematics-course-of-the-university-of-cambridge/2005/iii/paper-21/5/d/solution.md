<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

One useful version is the effective [global Torelli theorem for K3 surfaces](../../../../../../global-torelli-theorem-for-k3-surfaces.md). Let $X,Y$ be complex [K3 surfaces](../../../../../../k3-surface.md) and let

$$
\phi:H^2(Y,\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2(X,\mathbb Z)
$$

preserve the integral cup-product pairing and the Hodge decomposition. Suppose that $\phi$ carries some [Kähler class](../../../../../../kahler-class.md) of $Y$ to a [Kähler class](../../../../../../kahler-class.md) of $X$. Then

$$
\boxed{\text{there is a unique isomorphism }f:X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
\text{ such that }f^*=\phi.}
$$

Conversely every [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) has these properties. In particular, for projective [K3 surfaces](../../../../../../k3-surface.md) an integral Hodge isometry carrying an ample polarization to an ample polarization is induced by a unique [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md) of the polarized surfaces. This is the [effective global Torelli statement](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/huybrech/K3Global.pdf).

The Kähler-cone condition is necessary if the prescribed isometry itself is to be induced by an [isomorphism](../../../../../../isomorphism.md). If $\delta$ is the class of an effective smooth rational $(-2)$-curve, the reflection

$$
r_\delta(x)=x+(x,\delta)\delta
$$

is integral, preserves the pairing, and preserves the Hodge decomposition because $\delta$ has type $(1,1)$. It sends $\delta$ to $-\delta$. An [automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism.md) preserves effectivity, whereas $-\delta$ is not effective: it has negative intersection with an ample class. Thus this Hodge isometry is not an [automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism.md) pullback. Requiring an isometry to respect the Kähler chamber removes precisely this kind of obstruction.
