<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Take $D$ nef and big on a complex [K3 surface](../../../../../../k3-surface.md), and put $g=1+D^2/2$. The [K3 linear system dichotomies](../../../../../../k3-linear-system-dichotomies.md) are successive alternatives, so each later statement assumes that the exceptional cases in the earlier ones have been excluded.

The first dichotomy separates [basepoint-free](../../../../../../basepoint-free-divisor.md) systems from [monogonal linear systems on a K3 surface](../../../../../../monogonal-linear-system-on-a-k3-surface.md). In the exceptional case,

$$
D=mE+\Gamma,\quad m\ge2,\quad
E^2=0,\quad E\Gamma=1,\quad\Gamma^2=-2,
\qquad |D|=\Gamma+|mE|.
$$

Otherwise $|D|$ is [basepoint-free](../../../../../../basepoint-free-divisor.md). There are no isolated base points left after excluding the fixed-section case; this is the [first dichotomy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9602006).

For a [basepoint-free](../../../../../../basepoint-free-divisor.md) system, the second dichotomy separates [hyperelliptic linear systems on a K3 surface](../../../../../../hyperelliptic-linear-system-on-a-k3-surface.md) from birational maps to normal images. The degree-two cases are characterized by

$$
D^2=2,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
E^2=0,\ E\text{ an elliptic pencil},\ DE=2,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad D=2B,\ B^2=2,\ |B|\text{ basepoint-free}.
$$

Their images are surfaces of minimal degree: the plane, a [rational normal scroll](../../../../../../rational-normal-scroll.md), or the [Veronese surface](../../../../../../veronese-surface.md). In the birational case, the map is an embedding away from the $(-2)$-curves $\Gamma$ with $D\Gamma=0$, which it contracts to [Du Val singularities](../../../../../../rational-double-point.md). Thus “birational” does not assert an embedding of the original surface. These are the [degree-two and birational projective-model alternatives](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/huybrech/K3Global.pdf).

In the third dichotomy, the birational image is either defined scheme-theoretically by quadrics or has an exceptional cubic requirement. The exceptional threefold containing it is $\mathbb P^3$ for $g=3$, a [quadric hypersurface](../../../../../../quadric-algebraic-geometry.md) in $\mathbb P^4$ for $g=4$, a rational normal threefold [scroll](../../../../../../rational-normal-scroll.md) in the higher-genus trigonal case, or the cone over the [Veronese surface](../../../../../../veronese-surface.md) in the plane-quintic case. For $g\ge5$, trigonal sections come from an elliptic pencil with $DE=3$; the plane-quintic exception has

$$
g=6,\qquad D=2B+\Gamma,\qquad
B^2=2,\ B\Gamma=1,\ \Gamma^2=-2,\quad |B|\text{ basepoint-free}.
$$

All other birational cases have [homogeneous ideal](../../../../../../homogeneous-ideal.md) generated by quadrics. For $g\ge4$, the exceptional ideals are generated by quadrics and cubics. For $g=3$, the image is a quartic hypersurface in $\mathbb P^3$ and its ideal needs its degree-four defining equation; that case must not be included in the cubic-generation assertion. The geometric alternatives, including the low-genus exceptions, are the [plane-quintic exception to the third dichotomy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9602006); the higher-genus trigonal conclusion is also stated in [the projective-model theorem](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~drm/conferences/ITP99/morrison/cortona.pdf).

The point of the final exception is that a plane quintic is not a [trigonal curve](../../../../../../trigonal-curve.md). Its canonical model is the degree-two Veronese embedding of the plane curve, and its quadrics retain an ambient [Veronese surface](../../../../../../veronese-surface.md). Accordingly, one must include the cone-over-Veronese case instead of calling every failure of quadratic generation “trigonal.”
