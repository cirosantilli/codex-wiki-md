<h1 id="4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

At fixed particle numbers, the perturbations satisfy $\int\delta n_i\,d^3x=0$. Expanding the uniform interaction energy, its linear term is $\int(\mu_1\delta n_1+\mu_2\delta n_2)d^3x=0$, and its exact quadratic change in this contact-interaction approximation is

$$
\delta E=\frac12\int\left[U_{11}(\delta n_1)^2+2U_{12}\delta n_1\delta n_2+U_{22}(\delta n_2)^2\right]d^3x.
$$

Completing the square gives

$$
\delta E=\frac12\int\left[U_{11}\left(\delta n_1+\frac{U_{12}}{U_{11}}\delta n_2\right)^2+
\left(U_{22}-\frac{U_{12}^2}{U_{11}}\right)(\delta n_2)^2\right]d^3x.
$$

Hence the [miscibility criterion for a two-component condensate](../../../../../../miscibility-criterion-for-a-two-component-condensate.md) is

$$
\boxed{U_{11}>0,\qquad U_{22}>0,\qquad U_{12}^2<U_{11}U_{22}.}
$$

These are precisely strict [positive definiteness](../../../../../../positive-definiteness.md) of the interaction [matrix](../../../../../../matrix.md), and make every nonzero admissible long-wavelength density perturbation cost energy. With both equilibrium densities positive, a negative [eigenvalue](../../../../../../eigenvalue.md) would instead allow a small sinusoidal perturbation along its [eigenvector](../../../../../../eigenvector.md), with zero mean and negative energy change. The omitted positive [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) term cannot cure arbitrarily long wavelengths in an unbounded system.

If cross-repulsion exceeds the geometric mean of the self-repulsions, the unstable direction favors separation of species; sufficiently strong cross-attraction favors a joint density increase and collapse. Equality gives a zero-cost direction at this order, so it is marginal rather than strictly stable. Constraints near an absent component or finite-system wavelength restrictions require a separate boundary analysis.
