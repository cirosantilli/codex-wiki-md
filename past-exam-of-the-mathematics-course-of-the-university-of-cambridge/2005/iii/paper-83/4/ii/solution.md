<h1 id="4/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For an occupied uniform [two-component Bose-Einstein condensate](../../../../../../two-component-bose-einstein-condensate.md), the [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) and trapping terms vanish. Dividing each stationary equation by its nonzero field gives

$$
\boxed{\mu_1=U_{11}n_1+U_{12}n_2,\qquad\mu_2=U_{12}n_1+U_{22}n_2.}
$$

The same relations follow by differentiating the uniform interaction-energy density $e=U_{11}n_1^2/2+U_{22}n_2^2/2+U_{12}n_1n_2$ with respect to each [number density](../../../../../../number-density.md). When $D=U_{11}U_{22}-U_{12}^2\ne0$, the inverse relations are

$$
n_1=\frac{U_{22}\mu_1-U_{12}\mu_2}{D},\qquad
n_2=\frac{U_{11}\mu_2-U_{12}\mu_1}{D}.
$$

These describe a genuinely overlapping mixture only when both densities are nonnegative. An unoccupied component cannot be divided out of its stationary field equation; it requires the appropriate constrained equilibrium condition instead.
