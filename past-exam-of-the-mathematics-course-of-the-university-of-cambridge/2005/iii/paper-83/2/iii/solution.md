<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use the stationary [quantum vortex](../../../../../../quantum-vortex.md) field

$$
\psi(r,\theta,t)=e^{-i\mu t/\hbar}R(r)e^{i\mathcal N\theta},
$$

with no dependence on the coordinate along the vortex line. The polar [Laplacian](../../../../../../laplacian.md) contributes $R''+R'/r-\mathcal N^2R/r^2$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(R''+\frac{R'}r-\frac{\mathcal N^2R}{r^2}\right)
+\left[sE_R\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi r}d\right)+UR^2\right]R=\mu R.}
$$

A regular vortex with nonzero integer [winding number](../../../../../../winding-number.md) has $R\sim Cr^{|\mathcal N|}$ near the origin. The angular phase gives the [superfluid velocity](../../../../../../superfluid-velocity.md) $\hbar\mathcal N/(mr)$ outside the core. For $s\ne0$, the exterior amplitude responds to the periodically varying potential; it is not generally a constant bulk value. The radial potential here is not a Cartesian translation-invariant [optical lattice](../../../../../../optical-lattice.md), so the Cartesian [Bloch theorem](../../../../../../bloch-s-theorem.md) is not an exact theorem for its full radial equation.
