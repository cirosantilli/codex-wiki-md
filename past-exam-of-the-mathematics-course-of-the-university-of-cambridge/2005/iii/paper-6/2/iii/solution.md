<h1 id="2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take $Y_0=\{0\}$ and $T_0(0)=0$. The hypothesis $p(x)\geq q(x)$ is exactly the required initial domination $0\leq p(x+0)-q(x)$. Applying the extension proved in part (ii) gives

$$
\boxed{q(x)\leq U(x)\leq p(x)\quad\text{for every }x\in X,}
$$

with $U$ linear. There is no reason that $U$ must be nonzero; for instance $p=q=0$ forces $U=0$.

This is the [sandwich form of the Hahn-Banach theorem](../../../../../../sandwich-form-of-the-hahn-banach-theorem.md). Conversely, any [linear map](../../../../../../linear-map.md) between these two functionals satisfies the stronger mixed domination, since $U(y)=U(x+y)-U(x)\leq p(x+y)-q(x)$. Thus the mixed inequality used during extension is the appropriate invariant, not an extra final restriction.
