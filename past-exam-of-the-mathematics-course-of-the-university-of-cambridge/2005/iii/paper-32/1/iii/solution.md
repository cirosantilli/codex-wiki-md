<h1 id="1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Use [reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve](../../../../../../reduction-of-torsion-points-on-an-elliptic-curve.md), which is injective on torsion of order [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) to the residue characteristic. Any two-primary [torsion point of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../torsion-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) must inject into the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of order seven at three, so is zero. Any three-primary [torsion point of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../torsion-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) injects into the [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) of order five at two, so is zero. For a [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) $q\ne2,3$, its primary torsion injects into both reduced [groups](../../../../../../group-split.md) and would have order dividing both five and seven, so is also zero. Decomposing the cyclic [group](../../../../../../group-split.md) generated by any [torsion point of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../torsion-point-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) into its primary parts shows that $E(\mathbb Q)$ has no nonidentity torsion at all. This is the [two-prime bound on rational elliptic torsion](../../../../../../two-prime-bound-on-rational-elliptic-torsion.md), with the residue-characteristic exceptions handled explicitly. Since $P=(0,0)$ is not $O$,

$$
\boxed{P\text{ has infinite order}.}
$$
