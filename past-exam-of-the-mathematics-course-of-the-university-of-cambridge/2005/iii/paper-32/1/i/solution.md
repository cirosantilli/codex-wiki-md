<h1 id="1/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For the [Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../weierstrass-equation-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) in this part, $a_1=a_2=a_6=0$, $a_3=1$, $a_4=-1$. Hence

$$
b_2=0,\qquad b_4=-2,\qquad b_6=1,\qquad c_4=48,\qquad c_6=-216.
$$

The [elliptic-curve discriminant](../../../../../../elliptic-curve-discriminant.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta=\frac{48^3-216^2}{1728}=37.}
$$

Every [prime](../../../../../../prime-number.md) other than $37$ has unit discriminant, so this integral equation gives [good reduction](../../../../../../good-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md). At $37$ the [valuation](../../../../../../valuation.md) is one. An [Admissible change of Weierstrass coordinates](../../../../../../admissible-change-of-weierstrass-coordinates.md) changes discriminant [valuations](../../../../../../valuation.md) by a multiple of twelve; consequently no integral model can lower this value to zero. Thus the equation is minimal there and the reduction is genuinely bad, rather than an artifact of a nonminimal model. Explicitly $(5,18)$ is singular on the reduced affine cubic: both partial derivatives vanish there. The set of [bad reduction](../../../../../../bad-reduction-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) primes is

$$
\boxed{\{37\}.}
$$
