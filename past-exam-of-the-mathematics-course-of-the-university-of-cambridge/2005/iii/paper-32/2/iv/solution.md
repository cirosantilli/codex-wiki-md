<h1 id="2/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For a [Short Weierstrass form](../../../../../../short-weierstrass-form.md) $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$, use $c_4=-48A$ and $\Delta=-16(4A^3+27B^2)$. Both curves have $A=-1$, so $c_4=48=3$ in $\mathbb F_5$. For $E_1$, $B=0$ gives $\Delta_1=64=4$; for $E_2$, $B=1$ gives $\Delta_2=-368=2$. Thus

$$
\boxed{j(E_1)=3^3/4=3,\qquad j(E_2)=3^3/2=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5.}
$$

The [j-invariant of an elliptic curve](../../../../../../j-invariant-of-an-elliptic-curve.md) is preserved by every isomorphism over the algebraic closure. Since these values differ, **the two curves are not geometrically isomorphic**, even though the preceding part gives a degree-two [elliptic isogeny](../../../../../../isogeny-of-elliptic-curves.md).
