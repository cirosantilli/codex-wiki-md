<h1 id="4/a/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

**Zero stationary [isolating integrals](../../../../../../../isolating-integral.md) are possible when time dependence removes [conservation of energy](../../../../../../../conservation-of-energy.md) and no other symmetry or invariant survives.** For instance, a generic time-dependent nonsymmetric [gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) need not have a stationary integral. This is not possible in an ordinary time-independent galactic [gravitational potential](../../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md): $E=\tfrac12v^2+\Phi$ is already an [isolating integral](../../../../../../../isolating-integral.md) on a regular [specific orbital energy](../../../../../../../specific-orbital-energy.md) surface. An explicitly time-dependent constant that encodes an initial condition does not contradict the stationary count.
