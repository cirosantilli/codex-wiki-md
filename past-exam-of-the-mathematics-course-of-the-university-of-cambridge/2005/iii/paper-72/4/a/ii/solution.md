In the distinction relevant here, an [integral of motion](../../../../../../../integral-of-motion.md) is a single-valued function $I(\mathbf x,\mathbf v)$ of instantaneous [phase space](../../../../../../../phase-space.md), with no explicit time dependence, constant along the [orbit](../../../../../../../orbit-dynamical-system.md). In an autonomous [Hamiltonian](../../../../../../../hamiltonian.md) system this is **$\{I,H\}=0$**, where the bracket is the [Poisson bracket](../../../../../../../poisson-bracket.md). An integral must be defined consistently on the [phase space](../../../../../../../phase-space.md) region under discussion; an arbitrary multivalued formula obtained by solving one trajectory does not supply a global integral. Some conventions call explicitly time-dependent conserved functions integrals too; the stationary interpretation is needed for the following counts.
