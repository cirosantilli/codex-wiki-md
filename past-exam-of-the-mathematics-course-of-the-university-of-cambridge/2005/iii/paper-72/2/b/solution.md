<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The stated relation follows for an isolated uniform sphere ending at $r_0$, with the [gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) zero at infinity. Uniform density in a core alone does not fix the [escape velocity](../../../../../../escape-velocity.md): exterior shells add a constant interior [gravitational potential](../../../../../../newtonian-potential-of-a-point-mass.md) while leaving the interior force unchanged.

Under the isolated-sphere assumption, the enclosed [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) and [circular speed](../../../../../../circular-speed.md) are

$$
m(r)=\frac{4\pi}3\rho_0r^3,\qquad V_c^2(r)=\frac{Gm(r)}r=\frac{4\pi G\rho_0}3r^2.
$$

The boundary value is $\Phi(r_0)=-Gm(r_0)/r_0$. Integrating $\Phi'=Gm(r)/r^2$ inwards gives

$$
\Phi(r)=-\frac{2\pi G\rho_0}3(3r_0^2-r^2),\qquad v_e^2(r)=-2\Phi(r).
$$

Thus the [escape and circular speeds inside an isolated uniform sphere](../../../../../../escape-and-circular-speeds-inside-an-isolated-uniform-sphere.md) satisfy

$$
\boxed{v_e^2(r)=V_c^2(r)\left(\frac{3r_0^2}{r^2}-1\right),\qquad 0<r\leq r_0.}
$$

The finite central limit is $v_e^2(0)=4\pi G\rho_0r_0^2$, although $V_c(0)=0$. Adding an exterior spherical shell of [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) $m_s$ at radius $s>r_0$ raises $v_e^2$ by $2Gm_s/s$ and is a direct counterexample to deducing the boxed relation from a uniform core alone.
