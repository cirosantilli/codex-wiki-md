<h1 id="13b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Suppose two distinct minimizers $x_1,x_2$ have the same minimum distance $d$ from $y$. [Convexity](../../../../../../convex-function.md) puts their midpoint $z=(x_1+x_2)/2$ in $A$. Expanding the [Euclidean norm](../../../../../../euclidean-norm.md) gives

$$
\|z-y\|^2=\frac12\|x_1-y\|^2+\frac12\|x_2-y\|^2-\frac14\|x_1-x_2\|^2
=d^2-\frac14\|x_1-x_2\|^2<d^2,
$$

contradicting minimality. Therefore **the nearest point is unique**. Together with the preceding existence proof, this defines the metric projection onto a nonempty closed [convex set](../../../../../../convex-set.md).
