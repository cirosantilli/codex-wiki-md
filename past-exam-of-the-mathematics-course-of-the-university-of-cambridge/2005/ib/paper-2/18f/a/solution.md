<h1 id="18f/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Take $Q_j$ nonzero of exact degree $j$, so $Q_0,\ldots,Q_n$ are an [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) [basis](../../../../../../basis.md) of the [polynomials](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $n$. Their [orthogonal projection](../../../../../../orthogonal-projection.md) is

$$
\boxed{p_n=\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{(f,Q_j)}{(Q_j,Q_j)}Q_j.}
$$

Indeed its residual is [orthogonal](../../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) to every such [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md). For another [polynomial](../../../../../../polynomial-split.md) $q=p_n+r$ of degree at most $n$, the squared error is

$$
\|f-q\|^2=\|f-p_n\|^2+\|r\|^2,
$$

since the cross term vanishes. It is uniquely minimized at $r=0$. The approximation need not have exact degree $n$ when the highest coefficient happens to vanish.
