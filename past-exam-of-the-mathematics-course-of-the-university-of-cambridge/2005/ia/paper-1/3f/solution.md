<h1 id="3f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3F](../3f.md)

A real number $s$ is the [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md) of a nonempty set $A\subset\mathbb R$ if every $a\in A$ satisfies $a\le s$ and no smaller real number is an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md). Equivalently, for every $\eta>0$ there is $a\in A$ with $s-\eta<a\le s$. A [greatest element](../../../../../greatest-element.md), or maximum, additionally belongs to $A$.

Let $s=\sup A$ and suppose $s\notin A$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$. The approximation property gives at least one element of $A$ in $(s-\varepsilon,s)$. If there were only finitely many, they would have a largest value $m<s$. Every other element of $A$ is at most $s-\varepsilon$, so $\max\{m,s-\varepsilon\}<s$ would be an [upper bound](../../../../../upper-bound-in-a-partially-ordered-set.md) for the whole set. This contradicts the definition of the [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md). Therefore **there are infinitely many such elements**, the [accumulation below an unattained supremum](../../../../../accumulation-below-an-unattained-supremum.md).

For a counterexample when the [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md) is attained, take **$A=\{0\}$**. Its [supremum](../../../../../supremum.md) and [greatest element](../../../../../greatest-element.md) are both zero, while every interval $(-\varepsilon,0)$ contains no elements of $A$.
