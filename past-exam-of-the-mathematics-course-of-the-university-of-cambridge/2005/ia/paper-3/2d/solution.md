<h1 id="2d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2D](../2d.md)

A [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) is a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) generated by a single element: there is $g\in G$ such that every element is $g^n$ for some integer $n$. For a [finite group](../../../../../finite-group.md) of prime order $p$, choose $g\ne e$. Its [cyclic subgroup](../../../../../cyclic-subgroup.md) $\langle g\rangle$ has order dividing $p$ by [Lagrange's theorem](../../../../../lagrange-s-theorem.md), and its order is greater than one. Hence $|\langle g\rangle|=p$, and $\langle g\rangle=G$. Thus **every [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of prime order is cyclic**.

A [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md) from $C_{11}=\langle a\rangle$ is determined by $h=\varphi(a)$, since $\varphi(a^j)=h^j$. Its image generator must satisfy $h^{11}=e$, so its [order of a group element](../../../../../order-of-a-group-element.md) divides $11$. As an element of $C_{14}$ its order also divides $14$. These integers are coprime, so the only possible order is one. Thus $h=e$, and

$$
\boxed{\varphi(a^j)=e\quad\text{for every }j.}
$$

This map is indeed a [group homomorphism](../../../../../group-homomorphism.md), so **there is exactly one**, the trivial homomorphism.
