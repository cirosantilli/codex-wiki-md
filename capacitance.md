# Capacitance

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitance)

The [capacitance](capacitance.md) of a linear two-terminal [capacitor](capacitor.md) is the constant relating its separated [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) to its terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md):

$$
Q=CV,\qquad C=\frac QV\quad(V\ne0).
$$

Here $Q$ is the [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) on one [electrical conductor](electrical-conductor.md), the other carries $-Q$, and $V$ is their [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) with the same ordering. Reversing the terminal ordering reverses both $Q$ and $V$ and leaves [capacitance](capacitance.md) unchanged. An ideal passive [capacitor](capacitor.md) has $C\ge0$; an ordinary pair of separated [electrical conductors](electrical-conductor.md) with positive [permittivity](permittivity-split.md) has $C>0$. At $V=0$, use $Q=CV$ rather than the undefined ratio $0/0$.

For example, [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) magnitude $Q=0.8854\,\mathrm{nC}$ at terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $V=10\,\mathrm V$ gives [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C=88.54\,\mathrm{pF}$. At $20\,\mathrm V$, the same linear [capacitor](capacitor.md) has $Q=1.7708\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the same [capacitance](capacitance.md). The plots in [capacitor energy](capacitor-energy.md) show that [capacitance](capacitance.md) is the [slope](slope.md) of the [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) versus terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) graph.

For fixed geometry and linear material response, [capacitance](capacitance.md) depends on dimensions and [permittivity](permittivity-split.md), rather than the source's terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). This is why [capacitance](capacitance.md) is a useful property of a component: once it is known, $Q=CV$ predicts the separated [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) for each operating terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is the [farad](farad.md), $1\,\mathrm F=1\,\mathrm{C/V}$. [OpenStax University Physics 2, §8.1: capacitance and its unit](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/8-1-capacitors-and-capacitance).

For combinations of [capacitors](capacitor.md), the same terminal definition gives an equivalent [capacitance](capacitance.md). Two identical [capacitors](capacitor.md) of [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C$ connected in parallel share the same terminal [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), and their [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) add, giving $C_{\rm eq}=2C$. Connected in series with an initially neutral isolated junction, they carry equal plate [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) magnitudes and their terminal [electric potential differences](electric-potential-difference.md) add, giving $C_{\rm eq}=C/2$. [OpenStax University Physics 2, §8.2: capacitors in series and in parallel](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/8-2-capacitors-in-series-and-in-parallel).
