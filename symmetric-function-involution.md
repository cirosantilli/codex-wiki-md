# Symmetric-function involution

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetric function](symmetric-function.md)

The [Fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials](fundamental-theorem-of-symmetric-polynomials.md) gives freely generated elementary functions, so sending each $e_r$ to $h_r$ defines an [algebra homomorphism](algebra-homomorphism-over-a-field.md). For $E(t)=\sum e_rt^r$ and $H(t)=\sum h_rt^r$, $E(-t)H(t)=1$. Applying the homomorphism shows $\omega(H(t))=E(t)$, hence $\omega^2=1$. Taking logarithms of $H$ gives the displayed action on power sums. It preserves the [Hall inner product of symmetric functions](hall-inner-product-of-symmetric-functions.md) because each power-sum [basis](basis.md) vector acquires only a sign.
