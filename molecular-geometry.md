# Molecular geometry

↑ **Parent:** [Molecule](molecule-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_geometry)

Why do [methane](methane.md), [ammonia](ammonia.md), and [water](water.md) have different shapes when each central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) is surrounded by eight [valence electrons](valence-electron.md)? The answer separates the positions of [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) from the [electron domains](electron-domain.md) used in a [VSEPR model](valence-shell-electron-pair-repulsion-theory.md).

[Molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) describes the three-dimensional arrangement of the [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) in a [molecule](molecule-split.md), including [bond lengths](bond-length.md) and [bond angles](bond-angle.md). For a larger [molecule](molecule-split.md), a shape name often describes just the neighbours of one central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md). A [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md) records connections and electron bookkeeping; its flat layout does not fix [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md).

In the [VSEPR model](valence-shell-electron-pair-repulsion-theory.md), [electron pair geometry](electron-pair-geometry.md) includes both bonding [electron domains](electron-domain.md) and [lone pairs](lone-pair.md), whereas [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) counts only the [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md). In [methane](methane.md), four [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) and no [lone pairs](lone-pair.md) give [tetrahedral molecular geometry](tetrahedral-molecular-geometry.md). In [ammonia](ammonia.md), three [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) and one [lone pair](lone-pair.md) give [trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry](trigonal-pyramidal-molecular-geometry.md). In [water](water.md), two [bond pairs](bond-pair.md) and two [lone pairs](lone-pair.md) give [bent molecular geometry](bent-molecular-geometry.md). All three use the same ideal tetrahedral [electron pair geometry](electron-pair-geometry.md); this does not make their actual [bond angles](bond-angle.md) equal. See [Flowers, Theopold, Langley and Robinson, Chemistry 2e, §7.6: electron-pair and molecular geometries](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-6-molecular-structure-and-polarity).

**[Figure 11](#image-molecular-geometry-domains-and-atoms). Four electron domains, three arrangements of atomic nuclei: methane, ammonia, and water**. Original geometric constructions. The upper row is an ideal [VSEPR model](valence-shell-electron-pair-repulsion-theory.md) counting scaffold: orange LP markers identify [lone pairs](lone-pair.md), not additional [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) or measured [electron density](electron-density.md). The lower row shows only [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md); [ammonia](ammonia.md) and [water](water.md) use the sourced approximate [bond angles](bond-angle.md) discussed below. Sizes and [bond lengths](bond-length.md) are arbitrary. Three-dimensional projections can change the apparent [bond angles](bond-angle.md) on the page. Dotted outlines are geometric guides, not extra [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md).

A static drawing also needs a physical interpretation. The [atomic nuclei](atomic-nucleus-split.md) undergo [oscillations](oscillation.md); an arrangement at a minimum of [potential energy](potential-energy.md) and an experimentally averaged arrangement need not have identical [bond lengths](bond-length.md) or [bond angles](bond-angle.md). This distinction matters when comparing measurements with [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md) calculations; see the [IUPAC Gold Book: experimental bond-length conventions](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/BT07003). [Gas electron diffraction](gas-electron-diffraction.md) gives a concrete route from a laboratory signal to [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) rather than inferring shape from a drawing alone.
