# Winding number

↑ **Parent:** [Complex analysis](complex-analysis-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winding_number)

The winding number records how many net turns a closed [piecewise smooth curve](piecewise-smooth-curve.md) makes around a chosen point $w$. Counterclockwise turns contribute positively, clockwise turns negatively. It measures a traversal, including repeated visits; a drawing of the curve's outline alone can lose that information. For a [closed curve](closed-curve.md) $\gamma$ avoiding $w$,

$$
\operatorname{wind}(\gamma,w)
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-w}.
$$

Equivalently, continuously follow an [argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) of $\gamma(t)-w$ along one journey and divide its total change by $2\pi$. The start and endpoint agree as [complex numbers](complex-number.md), so the change is an [integer](integer.md) number of full turns. The principal [argument](argument-complex-analysis.md) at the endpoints cannot retain those turns; a continuous choice along the parametrized path can.[§11.2, definition of winding number and §11.2.1](topic-11-argument-principle-by-jeremy-orloff.md)

For $\gamma(t)=e^{it}$, $0\leq t\leq2\pi$, the [winding number](winding-number.md) about zero is one. Traversing the same [unit circle](complex-unit-circle.md) twice makes it two; reversing its [orientation of a contour](orientation-of-a-contour.md) makes it negative. The [winding number](winding-number.md) about $w=2$ is zero: the same journey now stays to one side of the point being measured. Joining one counterclockwise loop and one clockwise loop around zero gives net [winding number](winding-number.md) zero even though both loops encircle zero.

The [winding number](winding-number.md) does not change during a [homotopy](homotopy.md) of [closed curves](closed-curve.md) avoiding $w$. This is why it is useful: complicated curves can often be deformed to simple ones whose count is obvious. The [argument principle](argument-principle.md) transfers that robust geometric [integer](integer.md) to a count of [zeros of a function](zero-of-a-function.md) and [poles](pole.md). The example in [boundary sampling can miss winding](boundary-sampling-can-miss-winding.md) shows why finite samples of a curve need control between samples before they can certify the same [winding number](winding-number.md).
