# Infinity-corrected microscope

↑ **Parent:** [Optical microscope](optical-microscope.md)

An [optical microscope](optical-microscope.md) whose [microscope objective](microscope-objective.md), with the specimen at its designed front [focal plane](focal-plane.md), sends out a parallel bundle of [light rays](light-ray.md) from each specimen point. Different specimen points generally give different bundle directions. A [tube lens](tube-lens.md) then forms the finite [intermediate microscope image](intermediate-microscope-image.md); the [microscope eyepiece](microscope-eyepiece.md) views it. This parallel region permits suitable optical accessories to be inserted. Finite-conjugate and infinity-corrected components must be matched to their intended system.[Infinity Optical Systems, Fig. 2(a–b) and following description of objective, tube lens and eyepieces](infinity-optical-systems-by-nikon-instruments-inc.md)

In the ideal [paraxial approximation](paraxial-approximation.md), an object height $h_o$ at objective [focal length](focal-length.md) $f_o$ gives bundle slope $-h_o/f_o$. A [tube lens](tube-lens.md) of [focal length](focal-length.md) $f_t$ maps that slope to $h_i=-f_th_o/f_o$, so

$$
m_o=-\frac{f_t}{f_o}.
$$

This is a two-assembly result. Substituting infinity into the single-objective [transverse magnification](transverse-magnification.md) $-v_o/u_o$ would not describe the finite recorded image. Infinity correction concerns the region after the objective; relaxed-eye viewing concerns the region after the eyepiece. They are distinct conditions.
