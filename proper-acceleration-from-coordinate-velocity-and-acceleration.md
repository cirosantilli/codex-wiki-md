# Proper acceleration from coordinate velocity and acceleration

↑ **Parent:** [Proper acceleration](proper-acceleration.md)

In a Cartesian [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), let $\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf a=d\mathbf v/dt$ be the coordinate [velocity](velocity.md) and [acceleration](acceleration.md) of a massive particle. With $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, differentiating its [four-velocity](four-velocity.md) gives

$$
A^\mu=\left(\frac{\gamma^4\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{c},\ \gamma^2\mathbf a+\frac{\gamma^4(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a)\mathbf v}{c^2}\right).
$$

In the $+---$ [metric signature](metric-signature.md), its invariant [Minkowski norm](minkowski-norm.md) is $A^\mu A_\mu=-\alpha^2$. Expanding that contraction gives the displayed [proper acceleration](proper-acceleration.md). Resolving the coordinate [acceleration](acceleration.md) into components parallel and perpendicular to $\mathbf v$ yields the equivalent formula $\alpha^2=\gamma^6a_\parallel^2+\gamma^4a_\perp^2$. In particular, constant coordinate [acceleration](acceleration.md) and constant [proper acceleration](proper-acceleration.md) are different prescriptions.
