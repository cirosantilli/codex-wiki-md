<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Resolve each [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) by the [orthogonal projections](../../../../orthogonal-projection.md)

$$
a_L=(a\cdot k)k,\qquad a_T=a-(a\cdot k)k.
$$

The [acoustic tensor](../../../../acoustic-tensor.md) acts by $\lambda+2\mu$ on the longitudinal line and by $\mu$ on its perpendicular plane. Thus

$$
\boxed{c_L=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{\rho}},\qquad c_T=\sqrt{\frac\mu\rho}.}
$$

The longitudinal component is a [longitudinal wave](../../../../longitudinal-wave.md), while the two independent transverse polarizations are [transverse waves](../../../../transverse-wave.md). Each projection obeys its own [wave equation](../../../../wave-equation-split.md), and [superposition](../../../../superposition-principle.md) adds the corresponding solutions.

A general planar disturbance is therefore a sum of the two types. For a single sinusoid with a common speed, both projections cannot be nonzero unless the two speeds coincide: projecting the [eigenvalue equation](../../../../eigenvalue-equation.md) gives $(\rho c^2-\lambda-2\mu)a_L=0$ and $(\rho c^2-\mu)a_T=0$. This distinguishes [superposition](../../../../superposition-principle.md) of [waves](../../../../wave-split.md) with different speeds from a mixed polarization traveling rigidly at one speed.
