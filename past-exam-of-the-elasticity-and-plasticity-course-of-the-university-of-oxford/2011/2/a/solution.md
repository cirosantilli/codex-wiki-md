<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Substituting the sinusoidal [plane wave](../../../../plane-wave.md) into the [Navier-Cauchy equation](../../../../navier-cauchy-equation.md) gives $\Delta u=-u$, $u_{tt}=-c^2u$, and $\nabla\operatorname{div}u=-k(k\cdot a)\sin(k\cdot X-ct)$ because $|k|=1$. Equality of the amplitudes is therefore

$$
\boxed{\rho c^2a=\mu a+(\lambda+\mu)k(k\cdot a).}
$$

This is an [eigenvalue problem](../../../../eigenvalue-problem.md) for the [acoustic tensor](../../../../acoustic-tensor.md) $\mu I+(\lambda+\mu)k\otimes k$. Choosing a nonzero [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) parallel to $k$ gives $\rho c^2=\lambda+2\mu$; choosing any nonzero [amplitude](../../../../wave-amplitude.md) perpendicular to $k$ gives $\rho c^2=\mu$. For positive [mass density](../../../../density.md) and stable [Lamé parameters](../../../../lame-parameter.md), both squared speeds are positive. These choices construct nonzero solutions explicitly, proving existence.
