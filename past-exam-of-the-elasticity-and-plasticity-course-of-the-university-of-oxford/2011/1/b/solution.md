<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Choose the outward [unit normal](../../../../unit-normal.md) $n$ to the [boundary](../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) of the chosen current material subbody. The [traction](../../../../traction.md) $t(x,n)$ is the contact [force](../../../../force-split.md) exerted on that subbody per unit current [area](../../../../surface-area.md) at the [boundary](../../../../boundary-of-a-set.md) point. Reversing the subbody side reverses $n$ and $t$.

By [Cauchy's stress theorem](../../../../cauchy-s-stress-theorem.md), the dependence on $n$ is linear. With the usual column-vector convention the [Cauchy stress tensor](../../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md) satisfies

$$
\boxed{t(x,n)=\tau(x)n,\qquad t_i=\tau_{ij}n_j.}
$$

The same point has different [traction](../../../../traction.md) vectors on differently oriented planes, but a single [Cauchy stress tensor](../../../../cauchy-stress-tensor.md) encodes all of them. In the absence of body couples, [conservation of angular momentum](../../../../conservation-of-angular-momentum.md) makes $\tau$ symmetric.
