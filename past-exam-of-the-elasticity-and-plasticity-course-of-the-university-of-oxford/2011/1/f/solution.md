<h1 id="1/f/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [F](../f.md)

Use orthonormal cylindrical bases in the [reference configuration](../../../../reference-configuration.md) and [current configuration](../../../../current-configuration.md). The [deformation gradient](../../../../deformation-gradient.md) maps a radial increment $dR$ to $r'dR$, an azimuthal arc $R\,d\Theta$ to $r\,d\Theta$, and an axial increment $dZ$ to itself. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F=r' e_r\otimes E_R+\frac rR e_\theta\otimes E_\Theta+e_z\otimes E_Z.}
$$

Differentiating the radius relation gives $r'=R/r$. Its three [principal stretches](../../../../principal-stretch.md) are

$$
\boxed{\lambda_r=\frac Rr,\qquad\lambda_\theta=\frac rR,\qquad\lambda_z=1.}
$$

Their product is one, as required by [elastic incompressibility](../../../../elastic-incompressibility.md). The symbols called “strains” in the source are these stretch ratios; engineering [strain](../../../../strain.md) would instead subtract one. The axial stretch is one because this is [plane strain](../../../../plane-strain.md).
