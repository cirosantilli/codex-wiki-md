<h1 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For a surface with unit normal $\mathbf n$, [traction](../../../../../traction.md) is $t_i=\tau_{ij}n_j$. Thus $\tau_{ij}$ is the $i$th force-per-area component on a plane whose positive normal is $\mathbf e_j$. Diagonal entries are normal stresses and off-diagonal entries are shear stresses, with tensile [normal stress](../../../../../normal-stress.md) positive in this convention.

The symmetric [strain](../../../../../strain.md) has six independent components and so does the symmetric [stress](../../../../../stress.md). A general [linear map](../../../../../linear-map.md) between these two six-dimensional spaces therefore has

$$
\boxed{6\times6=36\text{ independent constitutive coefficients}.}
$$

Equivalently choose the minor symmetries $C_{ijkl}=C_{jikl}=C_{ijlk}$. Components antisymmetric in the [strain](../../../../../strain.md) indices have no observable effect and are omitted from this representative.

A further distinction is important: if a [strain energy density](../../../../../strain-energy-density.md) exists, $C_{ijkl}=\partial^2W/(\partial e_{ij}\partial e_{kl})$ has the major symmetry $C_{ijkl}=C_{klij}$. Then the six-by-six map is symmetric and has $6\cdot7/2=21$ independent coefficients. Thus **36 follows from the two stated tensor symmetries alone; 21 additionally uses the energy/reciprocity assumption**, as in the variational setting later in the paper. These are the [minor and major symmetries of linear elasticity](../../../../../minor-and-major-symmetries-of-linear-elasticity.md).
