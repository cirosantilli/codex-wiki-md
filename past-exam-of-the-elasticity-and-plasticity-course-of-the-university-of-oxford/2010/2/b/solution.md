<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Apply the wave ansatz to the oscillatory [displacement](../../../../displacement.md) about a static body-force balance, or equivalently to the zero-body-force homogeneous equation. The parallel and perpendicular polarizations have speeds

$$
\boxed{c_p=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{\rho}},\qquad
c_s=\sqrt{\frac\mu\rho}.}
$$

The first is longitudinal and the second transverse. A wave family is dispersive when its phase speed $\omega/|\mathbf k|$ varies with [wavenumber](../../../../wavenumber.md). In each of these homogeneous bulk families, $\omega=c|\mathbf k|$, so **both are nondispersive** and group and phase speeds coincide.

The positivity assumptions imply $\lambda>-2\mu/3$, hence $\lambda+\mu>\mu/3>0$. Therefore $c_p^2-c_s^2=(\lambda+\mu)/\rho>0$: **[longitudinal waves](../../../../longitudinal-wave.md) travel faster**.
