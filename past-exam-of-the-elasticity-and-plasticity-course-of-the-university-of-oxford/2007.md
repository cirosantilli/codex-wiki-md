## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2007](2007.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

The in-plane [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) equations are $\tau_{xx,x}+\tau_{xy,y}=0$ and $\tau_{xy,x}+\tau_{yy,y}=0$. On a [simply connected](../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space) patch, each [divergence-free](../multivariable-calculus.md#solenoidal-vector-field) row has a [streamfunction](../fluid-mechanics.md#stream-function): write $\tau_{xx}=\phi_y$, $\tau_{xy}=-\phi_x=-\psi_y$, $\tau_{yy}=\psi_x$. Symmetry gives $\phi_x=\psi_y$, so another potential exists with $A_y=\phi$, $A_x=\psi$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\tau_{xx}=A_{yy},\qquad\tau_{yy}=A_{xx},\qquad\tau_{xy}=-A_{xy}.}
$$

If two such [Airy stress functions](../continuum-mechanics.md#airy-stress-function) give the same [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress), their difference has zero [Hessian](../multivariable-calculus.md#hessian-matrix) and is therefore an arbitrary [affine function](../vector-space.md#affine-function) $a+bx+cy$ on a connected domain. This is the full nonuniqueness for a fixed [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). The existence argument is local, or global on a [simply connected](../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space) domain; domains that are [multiply connected regions](../geometry-and-topology.md#multiply-connected-region) require the associated force and moment period conditions for a single-valued global potential. [Equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) alone should not silently erase that topological qualification.

For [plane strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#plane-strain) put $s=e_{xx}+e_{yy}$. The [constitutive equation](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) gives $\tau_{xx}+\tau_{yy}=2(\lambda+\mu)s$. Consequently

$$
e_{xx}=\frac{\tau_{xx}-\lambda s}{2\mu},\quad
e_{yy}=\frac{\tau_{yy}-\lambda s}{2\mu},\quad
e_{xy}=\frac{\tau_{xy}}{2\mu},\quad
s=\frac{\Delta A}{2(\lambda+\mu)}.
$$

The [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) [Saint-Venant compatibility equations](../elasticity-physics.md#saint-venant-strain-compatibility-and-differential-identities) $e_{xx,yy}+e_{yy,xx}-2e_{xy,xy}=0$ then becomes

$$
0=2\mu(e_{xx,yy}+e_{yy,xx}-2e_{xy,xy})
=\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{2(\lambda+\mu)}\Delta^2A.
$$

For stable elastic moduli the coefficient is nonzero, so $\boxed{\Delta^2A=0}$. If $D$ is bounded and smooth and $A=\partial_nA=0$ on its boundary, integrating twice by parts gives $0=\int_DA\Delta^2A\,dV=\int_D(\Delta A)^2\,dV$. Thus $A$ is harmonic with zero boundary value and $\boxed{A=0}$ by uniqueness of the [Dirichlet problem](../analysis.md#dirichlet-problem). The normal-[derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) condition is what removes the extra fourth-order [boundary terms](../calculus.md#boundary-term).

For the pressurized [annulus](../topology.md#annulus-mathematics), substituting the radial [constitutive equations](../continuum-mechanics.md#constitutive-equation) into [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) gives $u''+u'/r-u/r^2=0$, so $u=Cr+D/r$. The stresses are

$$
\tau_{rr}=2(\lambda+\mu)C-\frac{2\mu D}{r^2},\qquad
\tau_{\theta\theta}=2(\lambda+\mu)C+\frac{2\mu D}{r^2}.
$$

Using gauge stresses relative to ambient, the boundaries are $\tau_{rr}(a)=-P$, $\tau_{rr}(b)=0$. Therefore

$$
2(\lambda+\mu)C=\frac{Pa^2}{b^2-a^2},\qquad
2\mu D=\frac{Pa^2b^2}{b^2-a^2},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{|\tau_{rr}-\tau_{\theta\theta}|=\frac{2Pa^2b^2}{(b^2-a^2)r^2},\qquad
\max_{a\leq r\leq b}|\tau_{rr}-\tau_{\theta\theta}|=\frac{2Pb^2}{b^2-a^2}.}
$$

The maximum occurs at the bore. Once the chosen [yield criterion](../continuum-mechanics.md#yield-criterion) is reached, [Hooke's law](../elasticity-physics.md#hooke-s-law) cannot describe the total [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) alone: write total [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) as elastic plus [plastic strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#plastic-strain) and supplement the elastic [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) law with a yield condition, a [plastic flow rule](../continuum-mechanics.md#plastic-flow-rule) and any required [work hardening](../continuum-mechanics.md#work-hardening) law. A plastic region starts near the bore and must be matched to the remaining elastic region. The exact conversion between a principal-[stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) difference and the quoted [yield stress](../rheology.md#yield-stress) depends on whether the material criterion is Tresca or von Mises. This is the [elastic stress concentration in a pressurized thick cylinder](../continuum-mechanics.md#elastic-stress-concentration-in-a-pressurized-thick-cylinder).

↑ **Parent:** [2007](2007.md)

<h3 id="2/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/i)

Decomposing a plane [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) into components parallel and perpendicular to its [wavevector](../elasticity-physics.md#wavevector) gives compressional and shear speeds

$$
\boxed{c_p=\sqrt{(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho},\qquad c_s=\sqrt{\mu/\rho},\qquad
k_p=\omega/c_p,\quad k_s=\omega/c_s.}
$$

The incident [polarization](../wave.md#polarization-waves) is longitudinal. Its reflected [longitudinal wave](../wave.md#longitudinal-wave) reverses the vertical [wavevector](../elasticity-physics.md#wavevector) component while retaining its horizontal component. The converted [shear wave](../elasticity-physics.md#shear-wave) has [wavevector](../elasticity-physics.md#wavevector) $k_s(\sin\beta,-\cos\beta)$ perpendicular to [polarization](../wave.md#polarization-waves) $(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$.

Every term must have the same [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves) along the rigid boundary. Thus

$$
\boxed{k_s\sin\beta=k_p\cos\alpha,\qquad
\sin\beta=\frac{c_s}{c_p}\cos\alpha.}
$$

Choose $\cos\beta>0$ so the reflected [shear wave](../elasticity-physics.md#shear-wave) propagates downward into $y<0$. At $y=0$, both [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) components must vanish for every boundary [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves):

$$
(1+R_1)\cos\alpha+R_2\cos\beta=0,\qquad
(1-R_1)\sin\alpha+R_2\sin\beta=0.
$$

Solving this two-by-two system yields

$$
\boxed{R_1=\frac{\sin(\alpha-\beta)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)},\qquad
R_2=-\frac{\sin2\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}.}
$$

For a stable isotropic material $0<c_s/c_p<1$. An incident wave directed toward the boundary has $0<\alpha<\pi$; choosing $\beta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ by the sine relation gives real reflected waves throughout this range and no zero denominator. In the usual convention restricting incident horizontal propagation to positive $x$, this is $0<\alpha\leq\pi/2$. Grazing endpoints have no incoming normal energy flux and are limiting cases. There is no [critical angle](../electromagnetic-wave.md#critical-angle-for-total-internal-reflection) for incident compressional-to-shear conversion here, because the converted wave is slower; the opposite incident-wave type has different critical-angle behaviour. At [normal incidence](../optics.md#normal-incidence) $\alpha=\pi/2$, $\beta=0$, $R_1=1$ and $R_2=0$, cancelling the incident [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) with the reversed longitudinal [polarization](../wave.md#polarization-waves). This is [compressional-wave reflection from a rigid elastic boundary](../wave-equation.md#compressional-wave-reflection-from-a-rigid-elastic-boundary).

<h3 id="2/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/ii)

The [tension](../force.md#tension) term $Tw_{xx}$ is the transverse restoring force from a nearly straight, tensioned rod. The bending term $-EIw_{xxxx}$ is the force from Euler-Bernoulli flexure, where $E$ is [Young's modulus](../elasticity-physics.md#young-s-modulus) and $I$ the [second moment of area](../elasticity-physics.md#second-moment-of-area). The right side is transverse [inertia](../classical-mechanics.md#inertia). In the displayed one-dimensional equation $\rho$ denotes [linear mass density](../fluid-mechanics.md#linear-mass-density); if it denotes volumetric [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) instead, the inertial coefficient must include cross-sectional area.

For a [Fourier mode](../fourier-analysis.md#fourier-mode), $w_{xx}=-k^2w$, $w_{xxxx}=k^4w$ and $w_{tt}=-\omega^2w$, giving

$$
\boxed{\rho\omega^2=Tk^2+EIk^4.}
$$

For $k>0$ the [phase speed](../dispersion-relation.md#phase-speed) is $c_p=\omega/k=\sqrt{T/\rho+EIk^2/\rho}$, strictly increasing with $k$ when $EI>0$ and $T\geq0$. Thus shorter [wavelengths](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) propagate faster in [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves). The [group velocity](../dispersion-relation.md#group-velocity) $c_g=(T+2EIk^2)/\sqrt{\rho(T+EIk^2)}$ is also increasing, so the statement is consistent with wave-packet transport.

At a simple support the [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) and [bending moment](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-moment) vanish: $w=0$ and $EIw_{xx}=0$ at $x=0,L$. The [eigenmodes](../wave-equation.md#normal-mode) are $\sin(n\pi x/L)$ for $n=1,2,\ldots$. Setting $T=0$ in the [dispersion relation](../dispersion-relation.md) therefore yields

$$
\boxed{\omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{EI}\rho}\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}.}
$$

The integer is positive for a nonzero mode; $n=0$ gives the identically zero sine function, and negative integers repeat the same modes. [Bending stiffness](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-stiffness) makes the frequencies quadratic in mode number, unlike a purely tensioned string.

↑ **Parent:** [2007](2007.md)

<h3 id="3/i">i</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/i/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/i)

For antiplane [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) $u=(0,0,w)$ the only nonzero [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) components are $e_{xz}=w_x/2$ and $e_{yz}=w_y/2$. Commutation of [mixed partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#mixed-partial-derivative) gives the necessary local compatibility condition

$$
\boxed{\partial_ye_{xz}-\partial_xe_{yz}=0.}
$$

The supplied [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) is symmetric, independent of $z$ and trace-free. Its first two [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) equations are automatic; its third gives

$$
\partial_x\left(-\frac{\mu y}{x^2+y^2}\right)+
\partial_y\left(\frac{\mu x}{x^2+y^2}\right)=0
$$

away from the origin. [Antiplane shear](../elasticity-physics.md#antiplane-shear) has zero trace, so isotropic [Hooke's law](../elasticity-physics.md#hooke-s-law) reduces to $\boxed{e=\tau/(2\mu)}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{w_x=-\frac y{x^2+y^2},\qquad w_y=\frac x{x^2+y^2}.}
$$

Locally this integrates to $w=\arg(x+iy)+C$, represented on a suitable branch by $\arctan(y/x)+C$. The [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) condition is satisfied at every point of the [punctured plane](../riemann-surfaces.md#punctured-complex-plane), but the [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) cannot be made globally single-valued there:

$$
\boxed{\oint_{r=R}(w_x\,dx+w_y\,dy)=2\pi.}
$$

A [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) of a globally single-valued smooth [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) would instead integrate to zero around any [closed curve](../topology.md#closed-curve). Local compatibility is therefore not sufficient on this [multiply connected region](../geometry-and-topology.md#multiply-connected-region).

A physical construction is a Volterra [screw dislocation](../physical-chemistry.md#screw-dislocation): cut the cylinder along a radial half-plane ending on its axis, shift one cut face relative to the other parallel to the axis, and rejoin. The axial mismatch is the [Burgers vector](../physical-chemistry.md#burgers-vector). In the given normalization its magnitude is $2\pi$; with a dimensional mismatch $b$ one uses $w=b\arg(x+iy)/(2\pi)$. An intact pristine solid cannot reach this state by a smooth single-valued elastic deformation alone. The axis is a [dislocation core](../physical-chemistry.md#dislocation-core) where the continuum elastic field is singular and must be regularized by microscopic structure or [plastic deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#plastic-deformation). On the punctured domain the differential condition holds locally; globally its nonzero [circulation](../vorticity.md#circulation-physics) detects the defect. In a distributional description, that incompatibility is concentrated on the axis. This is [local strain compatibility versus screw-dislocation circulation](../physical-chemistry.md#local-strain-compatibility-versus-screw-dislocation-circulation).

<h3 id="3/ii">ii</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/ii/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/ii)

Write $e_{xx}=u_{x,x}$, $e_{yy}=u_{y,y}$ and $2e_{xy}=u_{x,y}+u_{y,x}$. Differentiating twice and cancelling the [mixed partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#mixed-partial-derivative) gives

$$
\boxed{e_{xx,yy}-2e_{xy,xy}+e_{yy,xx}=0.}
$$

A convenient set of six three-dimensional [Saint-Venant compatibility equations](../elasticity-physics.md#saint-venant-strain-compatibility-and-differential-identities) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
e_{11,22}+e_{22,11}-2e_{12,12}&=0,\\
e_{22,33}+e_{33,22}-2e_{23,23}&=0,\\
e_{33,11}+e_{11,33}-2e_{13,13}&=0,\\
e_{11,23}+e_{23,11}-e_{12,13}-e_{13,12}&=0,\\
e_{22,13}+e_{13,22}-e_{12,23}-e_{23,12}&=0,\\
e_{33,12}+e_{12,33}-e_{13,23}-e_{23,13}&=0.
\end{aligned}}
$$

They follow by inserting $e_{ij}=(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})/2$ and commuting [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative). They form the six symmetric components of the incompatibility [tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor) $C_{ij}=\epsilon_{ik\ell}\epsilon_{jmn}e_{\ell n,km}$, up to the indicated signs and permutation of components.

They obey the three differential identities $\partial_iC_{ij}=0$, because a symmetric pair of [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) contracts with an antisymmetric [Levi-Civita symbol](../multivariable-calculus.md#levi-civita-symbol). Thus **there are three independent differential constraints**, represented symmetrically by six component equations; they are not six unrelated constraints on the six [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) functions. For example, at a nonzero [wavevector](../elasticity-physics.md#wavevector) directed along the third axis, compatibility imposes $e_{11}=e_{22}=e_{12}=0$ while $e_{13},e_{23},e_{33}$ can arise from the three [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) components. This confirms [rank](../quadratic-form.md#rank-one-quadratic-form) three. One cannot simply discard the same arbitrary three component equations in every direction; their independence is differential, with the stated identities. On a [simply connected](../algebraic-topology.md#simply-connected-space) domain these are the local [Saint-Venant compatibility equations](../elasticity-physics.md#saint-venant-strain-compatibility-and-differential-identities) for [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) to be a symmetric [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient), while domains that are [multiply connected regions](../geometry-and-topology.md#multiply-connected-region) require global closure conditions as in the screw example. This is [Saint-Venant strain compatibility and differential identities](../elasticity-physics.md#saint-venant-strain-compatibility-and-differential-identities).

## 4

↑ **Parent:** [2007](2007.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Take the trace of the constitutive relation at zero [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). It gives $0=(2\mu+3\lambda)e_{kk}-3c\alpha(T-T_0)$. With the observed [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress)-free volume [strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#strain) this requires

$$
\boxed{c=K=\lambda+\frac{2\mu}3.}
$$

This is the [bulk modulus](../elasticity-physics.md#bulk-modulus). A stable solid resists uniform compression, so $\boxed{K>0}$; equivalently the [elastic energy](../elasticity-physics.md#elastic-energy) of a nonzero pure dilation is positive. The [thermal strain](../continuum-mechanics.md#thermal-strain) is isotropic, $e^{\rm th}_{ij}=\alpha(T-T_0)\delta_{ij}/3$, because $\alpha$ here is the volumetric, not linear, [thermal expansion](../thermodynamics.md#thermal-expansion) coefficient.

Let $\beta=K\alpha$. Using $e_{ij}=(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})/2$, [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) divergence is

$$
\partial_j\tau_{ij}=\mu\Delta u_i+(\lambda+\mu)\partial_i(\nabla\cdot u)-\beta\partial_iT.
$$

Zero [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) and [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) therefore give

$$
\boxed{\mu\Delta u+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot u)=\beta\nabla T,\qquad
\beta=\alpha(\lambda+2\mu/3).}
$$

For an applied [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) per unit volume $b$, [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) is $\nabla\cdot\tau+b=0$, so its right-hand side becomes $\beta\nabla T-b$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{b=+\beta\nabla T}
$$

cancels the effective thermal forcing in the [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) equation. Per unit mass the force is $\beta\nabla T/\rho$. This sign follows from the divergence of the [thermal stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#thermal-stress) $-\beta(T-T_0)I$, which is $-\beta\nabla T$. Cancelling that divergence is distinct from removing the [stress tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#cauchy-stress-tensor) itself: boundary tractions and thermal compatibility still matter.

For spatially uniform heating with the entire boundary fixed, $u=0$ is the unique [equilibrium](../dynamical-systems.md#equilibrium-point-of-a-dynamical-system) [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) in a stable elastic solid, and

$$
\boxed{\tau_{ij}=-K\alpha(T-T_0)\delta_{ij}.}
$$

The [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress) is compressive [hydrostatic stress](../pressure.md#hydrostatic-stress) for a temperature rise. The fixed boundary supplies the tractions that prevent [thermal expansion](../thermodynamics.md#thermal-expansion). If $T$ varies spatially and no compensating [body force](../fluid-mechanics.md#body-force) is imposed, fixing the boundary alone does not generally force the interior [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) to vanish: one must solve the displayed forced [Navier-Cauchy equation](../elasticity-physics.md#navier-cauchy-equation) with $u=0$ on the boundary, then recover the [stress](../continuum-mechanics.md#stress). The simple uniform hydrostatic expression should not be asserted for arbitrary temperature fields. This is [uniform thermal stress in a fully constrained solid](../continuum-mechanics.md#uniform-thermal-stress-in-a-fully-constrained-solid).
