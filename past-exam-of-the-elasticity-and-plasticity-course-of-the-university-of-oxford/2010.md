## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

The [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) follows a material point from its reference position to its current position:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf u(\mathbf X,t)=\mathbf x(\mathbf X,t)-\mathbf X.}
$$

The [deformation gradient](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-gradient) is the [Jacobian matrix](../multivariable-calculus.md#jacobian-matrix) $F=\partial\mathbf x/\partial\mathbf X=I+\nabla_X\mathbf u$, and the volume Jacobian is

$$
\boxed{J=\det F,\qquad dV=J\,dV_0.}
$$

For an orientation-preserving physical [deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics), $J>0$ measures the local volume expansion factor. [Incompressibility](../fluid-mechanics.md#incompressible-flow) requires $J=1$; [conservation of mass](../continuum-mechanics.md#mass-conservation) gives $\rho(\mathbf x,t)J=\rho_0(\mathbf X)$. For small [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) [gradients](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient), $J=1+\nabla_X\cdot\mathbf u+O(|\nabla_X\mathbf u|^2)$. If a spatial representation is desired, the same [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) is $\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t)=\mathbf x-\mathbf X(\mathbf x,t)$.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

A Cartesian [Cartesian second-rank tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#cartesian-second-rank-tensor) has components transforming under a constant orthogonal change of axes as

$$
\boxed{T'_{ij}=p_{ia}p_{jb}T_{ab},\qquad T'=PTP^T.}
$$

Here $\mathbf X'=P\mathbf X$ and $\mathbf u'=P\mathbf u$. By the [chain rule](../calculus.md#chain-rule) and orthogonality,

$$
\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial X_j'}
=p_{ia}\frac{\partial u_a}{\partial X_b}\frac{\partial X_b}{\partial X_j'}
=p_{ia}p_{jb}\frac{\partial u_a}{\partial X_b}.
$$

Thus the [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) is a [Cartesian second-rank tensor](../multilinear-algebra.md#cartesian-second-rank-tensor). Its symmetric part transforms in the same way:

$$
e'_{ij}=\frac12p_{ia}p_{jb}
\left(\frac{\partial u_a}{\partial X_b}+\frac{\partial u_b}{\partial X_a}\right)
=p_{ia}p_{jb}e_{ab}.
$$

Hence **the [infinitesimal strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#infinitesimal-strain-tensor) is also second-rank**. This is a passive coordinate rotation, under which both reference and spatial coordinates rotate; it does not assert finite-[deformation](../continuum-mechanics.md#deformation-mechanics) frame indifference of the linearized strain formula.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

The [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) on the oriented solid element is

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\sigma=\tau\mathbf n,\qquad\sigma_i=\tau_{ij}n_j.}
$$

With constant Lamé coefficients and derivatives in the reference variables,

$$
\partial_j\tau_{ij}
=\lambda\partial_i(\partial_ku_k)
+\mu\partial_j(\partial_ju_i+\partial_iu_j)
=(\lambda+\mu)\partial_i(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)+\mu\nabla^2u_i.
$$

Substitution into [balance of linear momentum](../continuum-mechanics.md#balance-of-linear-momentum) gives

$$
\boxed{\rho\mathbf u_{tt}=(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)
+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u+\rho\mathbf g.}
$$

To check the [vector](../vector-space.md#vector) identity componentwise, contract the two alternating [tensors](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor):

$$
[\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf u)]_i
=\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{klm}\partial_j\partial_lu_m
=\partial_i\partial_ju_j-\partial_j\partial_ju_i.
$$

Rearranging proves $\nabla^2\mathbf u=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)-\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf u)$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\rho\mathbf u_{tt}=(\lambda+2\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u)
-\mu\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf u)+\rho\mathbf g.}
$$

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

Assume $\mathbf k\ne0$. Dotting the decomposition with $\mathbf k$ gives

$$
\boxed{A=\frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf k}{|\mathbf k|^2}.}
$$

Crossing it on the left by $\mathbf k$ and using $\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf k=0$ gives $\mathbf k\times\mathbf a=|\mathbf k|^2\mathbf B$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\frac{\mathbf k\times\mathbf a}{|\mathbf k|^2}.}
$$

Indeed $(\mathbf k\times\mathbf a)\times\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|^2=\mathbf a-A\mathbf k$, verifying the decomposition and the imposed transverse condition.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Apply the wave ansatz to the oscillatory [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) about a static body-force balance, or equivalently to the zero-body-force homogeneous equation. The parallel and perpendicular polarizations have speeds

$$
\boxed{c_p=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{\rho}},\qquad
c_s=\sqrt{\frac\mu\rho}.}
$$

The first is longitudinal and the second transverse. A wave family is dispersive when its phase speed $\omega/|\mathbf k|$ varies with [wavenumber](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber). In each of these homogeneous bulk families, $\omega=c|\mathbf k|$, so **both are nondispersive** and group and phase speeds coincide.

The positivity assumptions imply $\lambda>-2\mu/3$, hence $\lambda+\mu>\mu/3>0$. Therefore $c_p^2-c_s^2=(\lambda+\mu)/\rho>0$: **[longitudinal waves](../wave.md#longitudinal-wave) travel faster**.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Substitution of $w_i=f_i(y)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ gives

$$
\boxed{f_i''+\left(\frac{\omega^2}{c_{si}^2}-k^2\right)f_i=0.}
$$

The oscillatory seam solution requires $m^2=\omega^2/c_{s1}^2-k^2$, giving

$$
\boxed{\omega^2=c_{s1}^2(k^2+m^2),\qquad
c_L=\frac\omega{|k|}=c_{s1}\sqrt{1+m^2/k^2}.}
$$

For an exponentially decaying exterior, $f_2''=\ell^2f_2$ with $\ell>0$, so the same differential equation instead requires

$$
\boxed{\ell^2=k^2-\omega^2/c_{s2}^2,\qquad
\omega^2=c_{s2}^2(k^2-\ell^2).}
$$

The printed exterior plus sign is inconsistent with its decaying ansatz and must be a sign error. The guided mode has $c_{s1}<c_L<c_{s2}$, so a slower shear-wave seam than its cladding is necessary.

For an even seam mode $f_1=A\cos(my)$, upper-interface [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) and [traction](../continuum-mechanics.md#traction) continuity give

$$
A\cos(mh)=A_2e^{-\ell h},\qquad
-\mu_1Am\sin(mh)=-\mu_2\ell A_2e^{-\ell h}.
$$

The lower interface gives the same equations when $B_2=A_2$. Eliminating the amplitudes yields

$$
\boxed{\mu_1m\tan(mh)=\mu_2\ell.}
$$

For an odd seam mode $f_1=B\sin(my)$, the equations are

$$
B\sin(mh)=A_2e^{-\ell h},\qquad
\mu_1Bm\cos(mh)=-\mu_2\ell A_2e^{-\ell h}.
$$

With $B_2=-A_2$ the lower condition agrees, and elimination gives

$$
\boxed{\mu_1m\cot(mh)=-\mu_2\ell.}
$$

These interface conditions together with the two corrected dispersion identities determine the guided-wave branches.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

In the formal stiff-cladding limit with a nonvanishing exterior decay rate, the interface [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) tends to zero. The even seam mode then has $\cos(mh)=0$, whereas the odd mode has $\sin(mh)=0$. The positive [wavenumbers](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber) are

$$
\boxed{m_{\mathrm{even},n}=\frac{(n+1/2)\pi}{h},\ n=0,1,\ldots,
\qquad m_{\mathrm{odd},n}=\frac{n\pi}{h},\ n=1,2,\ldots.}
$$

The zero odd index would give an identically zero mode and is excluded. The smallest even transverse [wavenumber](../wave-equation.md#wavenumber) is $\pi/(2h)$, so its lowest branch satisfies

$$
\boxed{\omega^2\geq c_{s1}^2\left(k^2+\frac{\pi^2}{4h^2}\right).}
$$

This is the symmetric-mode cutoff: the actual limiting branches have the discrete listed values of $m$. If the exterior shear speed remains finite, the separate decay requirement $\omega^2<c_{s2}^2k^2$ still applies; the cutoff alone does not replace that guided-wave condition.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2010](2010.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Differentiate the [displacement](../classical-mechanics.md#displacement) components. The shear strains involving $z$ vanish because $u_z=-w_x$ and $v_z=-w_y$. The full [infinitesimal strain tensor](../continuum-mechanics.md#infinitesimal-strain-tensor) is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E=z\begin{pmatrix}
-a&-b&0\\-b&-c&0\\0&0&\dfrac{\nu(a+c)}{1-\nu}
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

In particular $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E=(2\nu-1)(a+c)z/(1-\nu)$. Substituting the Lamé coefficients into the constitutive law gives

$$
\boxed{\tau=-\frac{Ez}{1-\nu^2}
\begin{pmatrix}a+\nu c&(1-\nu)b&0\\(1-\nu)b&c+\nu a&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

For example $\tau_{zz}=\lambda\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E+2\mu e_{zz}=0$: the thickness-direction strain supplies exactly the plane-stress Poisson correction. Also $\tau_{xy}=-Ebz/(1+\nu)$, confirming the off-diagonal factor.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

Contract the [tensors](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor), counting both off-diagonal shear terms:

$$
\mathcal W=\frac12\tau_{ij}e_{ij}
=\frac{Ez^2}{2(1-\nu^2)}
\{a^2+2\nu ac+c^2+2(1-\nu)b^2\}.
$$

Since $\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}z^2\,dz=h^3/12$, integration gives

$$
\boxed{\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}\mathcal W\,dz
=\frac D2\{a^2+2\nu ac+c^2+2(1-\nu)b^2\},\qquad
D=\frac{Eh^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}.}
$$

For the general midplane deflection, expand the energy [density](../fluid-mechanics.md#density) as $w_{xx}^2+2\nu w_{xx}w_{yy}+w_{yy}^2+2(1-\nu)w_{xy}^2$. A variation $w+\varepsilon\eta$ gives

$$
\delta\mathcal U=D\iint_\Omega
\{(w_{xx}+\nu w_{yy})\eta_{xx}
+(w_{yy}+\nu w_{xx})\eta_{yy}+2(1-\nu)w_{xy}\eta_{xy}\}\,dx\,dy.
$$

For clamped variations, $\eta=\partial_n\eta=0$ on the boundary, so integrating twice transfers all derivatives to $w$ without a boundary contribution. The bulk coefficient is

$$
w_{xxxx}+2\nu w_{xxyy}+w_{yyyy}+2(1-\nu)w_{xxyy}
=(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2)^2w.
$$

Stationarity for all compactly supported variations and the fundamental lemma therefore give

$$
\boxed{\nabla^4w=0\quad\text{in the plate interior}.}
$$

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use the same [first variation](../calculus-of-variations.md#first-variation) on the half-plane, with no restrictions on the edge [traces](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) of $\eta$. Write $M=w_{xx}+\nu w_{yy}$. Two $x$ integrations of the $M\eta_{xx}$ term produce the boundary term $-M\eta_x+M_x\eta$ at $x=0$. The mixed term, after one integration in $x$ and one along the edge in $y$, contributes $2(1-\nu)w_{xyy}\eta$. All other boundary terms vanish at infinity. Hence

$$
\delta\mathcal U=D\iint_{x>0}\eta\nabla^4w\,dx\,dy
-D\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\{\eta_x(w_{xx}+\nu w_{yy})-\eta[w_{xxx}+(2-\nu)w_{xyy}]\}_{x=0}\,dy.
$$

The bulk equation makes its area term zero. Stationarity therefore gives exactly

$$
\boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\{\eta_x(w_{xx}+\nu w_{yy})-\eta[w_{xxx}+(2-\nu)w_{xyy}]\}_{x=0}\,dy=0.}
$$

The two edge [traces](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) can be prescribed independently: use $\eta(x,y)=\chi(x)[f(y)+xg(y)]$ with $\chi=1$ near zero and compactly supported smooth $f,g$. The fundamental lemma on the edge then gives

$$
\boxed{w_{xx}+\nu w_{yy}=0,\qquad
w_{xxx}+(2-\nu)w_{xyy}=0\quad(x=0).}
$$

They are zero [bending moment](../elasticity-physics.md#bending-moment) and zero effective transverse shear at a free straight edge. The second includes the edge derivative of the twisting moment; omitting the mixed contribution would give the wrong coefficient.
