# Virial theorem for a homogeneous pair potential

↑ **Parent:** [Virial theorem](virial-theorem.md)

For a differentiable total pair [potential energy](potential-energy.md) homogeneous of degree $q$ in all particle positions, the [Euler theorem for homogeneous functions](euler-theorem-for-homogeneous-functions.md) gives $\sum_i\boldsymbol r_i\cdot\nabla_iW=qW$. Since $\boldsymbol F_i=-\nabla_iW$, the force virial is $-qW$. The bounded-motion time-averaged [virial theorem](virial-theorem.md) consequently gives the displayed equation. For $q\ne0$, total energy is $E=(q+2)\langle K\rangle/q$. A positive kinetic-temperature coefficient makes its [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md) negative exactly when $-2<q<0$. For a pair potential proportional to $r^{p+1}$ this means $-3<p<-1$.
