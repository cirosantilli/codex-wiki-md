# Mutation

↑ **Parent:** [Genetics](genetics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation)

A mutation changes inherited genetic sequence. Under an [infinite sites mutation model](infinite-sites-mutation-model.md), every mutation occurs at a previously unmutated position, so mutations below the sample's common ancestor correspond to [segregating sites](segregating-site.md).

More precisely, a [mutation](mutation.md) changes a genetic [nucleotide sequence](nucleic-acid-sequence.md); in cellular [organisms](organism-split.md) this is a [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) sequence. An altered sequence can be copied by [DNA replication](dna-replication.md) into descendant [biological cells](biological-cell-split.md), changing their [genotype](genotype.md) whether or not an observable [phenotype](phenotype.md) changes. Inheritance along a [cell lineage](cell-lineage.md) does not require transmission to an offspring [organism](organism-split.md). In animals, [germline mutations](germline-mutation.md) can enter offspring through [gametes](gamete.md), whereas [somatic mutations](somatic-mutation.md) remain in body-cell lineages.[Definition and Narration: DNA sequence change, germline transmission and descendants of somatic cells](mutation-by-national-human-genome-research-institute.md)

A [DNA lesion](dna-lesion.md) is damage to the physical [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) molecule, such as a damaged [nucleobase](nucleotide-base.md) or a broken strand. It need not become a lasting [mutation](mutation.md): [DNA repair](dna-repair.md) can restore the original sequence. Conversely, a copied [nucleotide substitution](nucleotide-substitution.md) can leave an ordinary, correctly paired [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) duplex whose sequence differs from the ancestor. The distinction is between a damaged template and a changed inherited message, not simply between harmful and harmless effects.[Chapter 5, DNA Repair, Without DNA Repair, Spontaneous DNA Damage Would Rapidly Change DNA Sequences; Figures 5-46–5-49](molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others.md)

Small [mutations](mutation.md) include [nucleotide substitutions](nucleotide-substitution.md) and [indels](indel.md). Larger changes can remove a [gene](gene.md), duplicate a [chromosome](chromosome.md) region or rearrange its order. [Genetic recombination](genetic-recombination.md) can bring existing [alleles](allele.md) together without creating a new [nucleobase](nucleotide-base.md) at the exchanged positions; the mechanism and the resulting sequence change are different questions. A molecular description of a [mutation](mutation.md), its effect on a [gene](gene.md) product and its effect on a [phenotype](phenotype.md) are likewise three separate statements.

For an original consequence ledger, choose the [coding strand](coding-strand.md) segment $5'\text{-}\mathrm{ATG\ TTT\ GAA\ TAA}\text{-}3'$ with initiation at the first displayed [codon](codon.md). Use the standard [genetic code](genetic-code.md), the same [reading frame](reading-frame.md) and suitable [gene expression](gene-expression.md) context throughout. Its [messenger RNA](messenger-rna.md) has U in place of T, and the predicted [amino acid](amino-acid.md) chain is [methionine](methionine.md)–[phenylalanine](phenylalanine.md)–[glutamic acid residue](glutamic-acid-residue.md), followed by a [stop codon](stop-codon.md). This is a constructed coding segment, not an experimentally measured [gene](gene.md).

<a id="image-original-mutation-ledger-sequence-changes-and-predicted-coding-consequences"></a>
![](mutation-sequence-consequence-ledger.png)

**[Figure 3](#image-original-mutation-ledger-sequence-changes-and-predicted-coding-consequences). Original mutation ledger: sequence changes and predicted coding consequences**. All [DNA](deoxyribonucleic-acid.md) segments are written $5'$ to $3'$ in the same chosen [reading frame](reading-frame.md). Colored letters mark changed or inserted [nucleotides](nucleotide.md); a gap marks a removed [codon](codon.md). Products follow the standard [genetic code](genetic-code.md) under the stated initiation assumption. The ledger predicts [amino acid](amino-acid.md) order or termination, not a measured [phenotype](phenotype.md).

In the second row, position 6 changes from T to C, replacing TTT by TTC. Both corresponding [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) [codons](codon.md) specify [phenylalanine](phenylalanine.md), so the predicted chain is unchanged. Changing position 5 from T to C instead gives TCT, whose [RNA](ribonucleic-acid.md) [codon](codon.md) specifies [serine](serine.md). Changing position 7 from G to T gives TAA and therefore a [stop codon](stop-codon.md) before the original [glutamic acid residue](glutamic-acid-residue.md). Each is one [nucleotide substitution](nucleotide-substitution.md), but their coding consequences differ. Even preservation of the [amino acid](amino-acid.md) chain does not guarantee unchanged [gene expression](gene-expression.md) or [phenotype](phenotype.md).

Insert GGA after ATG to add a [glycine](glycine.md) [codon](codon.md), or remove the complete TTT [codon](codon.md) to remove [phenylalanine](phenylalanine.md). Both [indels](indel.md) preserve the downstream [reading frame](reading-frame.md) because their lengths are three [nucleotides](nucleotide.md). A one-[nucleotide](nucleotide.md) insertion at that location instead produces a [frameshift mutation](frameshift-mutation.md); the positional explanation and a worked regrouping appear under [frameshift mutation](frameshift-mutation.md). The ledger illustrates why interpreting a [genetic variant](genetic-variant.md) requires its position and context, rather than just the label mutation.

[Mutation](mutation.md) supplies new inherited variation on which [natural selection](natural-selection.md) can act. The [Luria-Delbrück experiment](luria-delbruck-experiment.md) makes the distinction unusually visible: [natural selection](natural-selection.md) reveals resistant descendants, while the timing of a prior [mutation](mutation.md) determines how many descendants are available to reveal. This connects an invisible change in one [biological cell](biological-cell-split.md) to a visible pattern across [cell cultures](cell-culture.md).
