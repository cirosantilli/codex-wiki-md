# Logarithmic inversion of a Gaussian divided by its argument

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic expansion](asymptotic-expansion-split.md)

For $\epsilon\downarrow0$, put $L=\log(1/\epsilon)$. Taking a [logarithm](logarithm.md) gives $x^2+\log x=L$, first implying $x\sim\sqrt L$. Substitution of $x=\sqrt L+d$ gives $2\sqrt L\,d+\tfrac12\log L$ at first correction order. Hence $d=-(\log L)/(4\sqrt L)$; substituting back and using the [derivative](derivative.md) of $x^2+\log x$ bounds the remaining root error by $O((\log L)^2/L^{3/2})$.
