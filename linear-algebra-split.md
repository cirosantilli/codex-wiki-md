# Linear algebra

↑ **Parent:** [Algebra](algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_algebra)

[Linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) studies [vector spaces](vector-space-split.md) and [linear maps](linear-map.md): operations that preserve [vector addition](vector-addition.md) and [scalar multiplication](scalar-multiplication.md). For a [linear map](linear-map.md) $T$, the defining rule is $T(au+bv)=aT(u)+bT(v)$, where $u,v$ are [vectors](vector.md) and $a,b$ belong to the underlying [field](field.md). The [vectors](vector.md) might be arrows, lists of measurements, or [polynomials](polynomial-split.md). What matters is that their [linear combinations](linear-combination.md) make sense and that the same rule governs their transformation.

A concrete [linear map](linear-map.md) is $T(x,y)=(x+y,x-y)$. Its two outputs record a sum and a difference. Since $T(1,0)=(1,1)$ and $T(0,1)=(1,-1)$, we immediately obtain $T(2,1)=2(1,1)+(1,-1)=(3,1)$. Knowing the outputs of the [basis vectors](basis-vector.md) determines the output of every [vector](vector.md); a [matrix](matrix.md) stores exactly that information in its columns.

The attraction of [linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md) is that one small collection of ideas answers several different questions. A [system of linear equations](system-of-linear-equations.md) asks which inputs produce a prescribed output. The [image of a linear map](image-of-a-linear-map.md) describes the attainable outputs, while the [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) describes the invisible input changes. A [basis](basis.md) gives every [vector](vector.md) an address, and [change of basis](change-of-basis.md) lets us choose addresses that make a [linear map](linear-map.md) easier to understand. This shared language turns a collection of separate calculations into a picture of what is possible and how much freedom remains. Gilbert Strang's MIT course emphasizes these connections and their usefulness across other disciplines.[Strang, MIT 18.06: Linear Algebra](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/)
