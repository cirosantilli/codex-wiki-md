# Taylor expansion from a periodic derivative equation

↑ **Parent:** [Taylor theorem](taylor-theorem.md)

For a smooth real [function](function-split.md) satisfying the displayed equation with positive [integer](integer.md) $r$, differentiating makes $f^{(j+r)}=f^{(j)}$. On any [compact](compact-space.md) interval, all [derivatives](derivative.md) are therefore bounded by the maximum of the bounds of $f,f',\ldots,f^{(r-1)}$. The [Taylor formula with integral remainder](taylor-formula-with-integral-remainder.md) consequently has error at most $M R^{n+1}/(n+1)!$, which tends to zero uniformly for $|x|\le R$. Hence the [function](function-split.md) equals its globally convergent [Taylor series](taylor-series.md). For $r=3$ and initial [derivatives](derivative.md) $(1,0,0)$, it is $\sum_{k\ge0}x^{3k}/(3k)!$.
