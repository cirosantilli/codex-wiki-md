# Body-diagonal stabilizer in the cube symmetry group

↑ **Parent:** [Symmetry group of a cube](symmetry-group-of-a-cube.md)

For vertices $(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)$, the [symmetry group of a cube](symmetry-group-of-a-cube.md) consists of [signed permutation matrices](signed-permutation-matrix.md). A matrix preserving the unoriented line through $(1,1,1)$ must have all three signs equal, so it has the form $\varepsilon P$, where $P$ is a [permutation matrix](permutation-matrix.md) and $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. Central inversion commutes with every $P$ and is not itself a permutation matrix. Consequently $(P,\varepsilon)\mapsto\varepsilon P$ identifies this twelve-element [stabilizer subgroup](stabilizer-subgroup.md) with the [direct product of groups](direct-product-of-groups.md) $S_3\times C_2$. A body diagonal is different from a face-normal coordinate axis, whose line stabilizer has order sixteen.
