# Molecular ion

↑ **Parent:** [Mass spectrometry](mass-spectrometry.md)

In the [International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry](international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry.md) (IUPAC) [mass spectrometry](mass-spectrometry.md) convention, a [molecular ion](molecular-ion.md) is formed by removing or adding one or more [electrons](electron.md) to a [molecule](molecule-split.md) without fragmentation of its molecular structure.[Gold Book, fifth edition, molecular ion in mass spectrometry: formation by electron removal or addition without fragmentation](iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/molecular-ion-in-mass-spectrometry.md) For example, removing one [electron](electron.md) from intact [water](water.md) gives $\mathrm{H_2O^{\bullet+}}$: two [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) and one [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atom](atom-physics.md), total [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $+e$, and an unpaired [electron](electron.md), hence a [radical cation](radical-cation.md). Atom inventory, net [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md), and electronic state are separate pieces of information. [Hydronium](hydronium.md), $\mathrm{H_3O^+}$, has a different inventory; it is a [polyatomic ion](polyatomic-ion.md), rather than the [molecular ion](molecular-ion.md) obtained by electron removal from [water](water.md).
