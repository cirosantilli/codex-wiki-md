# Oblate-quadrupole epicyclic precession

↑ **Parent:** [Planetary quadrupole coefficient](planetary-quadrupole-coefficient.md)

For $\Phi=-\mu/r+\alpha^2\mu(3z^2-r^2)/r^5$, a nearly circular equatorial orbit has $n^2=\mu/R^3$ and azimuthal frequency $\Omega_c^2=n^2(1+3\alpha^2/R^2)$. The [radial epicyclic frequency](radial-epicyclic-frequency.md) is $\kappa^2=R\,d\Omega_c^2/dR+4\Omega_c^2$, and the [vertical epicyclic frequency](vertical-epicyclic-frequency.md) is $\nu^2=\partial_z^2\Phi|_{z=0}$. Radial stability requires $R^2>3\alpha^2$. The [nodal precession](nodal-precession.md) rate $\Omega_c-\nu$ is negative and the [apsidal precession](apsidal-precession.md) rate $\Omega_c-\kappa$ positive. These sign statements concern small eccentricity and inclination. For an arbitrary inclined orbit, the averaged [argument of periapsis](argument-of-periapsis.md) instead evolves as $(3/2)n\alpha^2[5\cos^2i-1]/[a^2(1-e^2)^2]$ and can regress.
