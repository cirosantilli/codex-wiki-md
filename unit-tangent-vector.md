# Unit tangent vector

↑ **Parent:** [Tangent vector](tangent-vector.md)

For a [regular curve](regular-curve.md) $\mathbf r(t)$ with nonzero [velocity vector](velocity-vector.md), its unit tangent vector is

$$
T(t)=\frac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|}.
$$

The [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) in the denominator removes the traversal [speed](speed.md), leaving a [unit vector](unit-vector.md) that records direction. A positive change of traversal [speed](speed.md) leaves this direction unchanged; reversing the direction of travel reverses $T$. In [arc length](arc-length.md) coordinates, $T=d\mathbf r/ds$.

For the [parabola](parabola.md) $\mathbf r(t)=(t,t^2)$, the [velocity vector](velocity-vector.md) is $(1,2t)$ and the [speed](speed.md) is $\sqrt{1+4t^2}$. At $t=1$, $T=(1,2)^T/\sqrt5$. The [tangent vector](tangent-vector.md) $(1,2)^T$ and the [unit tangent vector](unit-tangent-vector.md) point in the same direction, but only the latter has [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) one. The [principal normal vector](principal-normal-vector.md) is a further choice based on how this direction changes, where the [curvature](curvature.md) is nonzero. [OpenStax Calculus Volume 3, §3.3: unit tangent and principal normal](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-3/pages/3-3-arc-length-and-curvature)
