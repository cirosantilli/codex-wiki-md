<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Fix $A\in\mathcal A_1$ and define

$$
\mathcal D_A=\{B\in\mathcal G_2:\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\}.
$$

This is a [Dynkin system](../measure-theory.md#dynkin-system): it contains $\Omega$; taking [complements](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) preserves the equality because $\mathbb P(A\cap B^c)=\mathbb P(A)-\mathbb P(A\cap B)$; and [countable additivity](../measure.md#countable-additivity) preserves it under disjoint [unions](../set.md#set-union). The hypothesis says that $\mathcal D_A$ contains the [pi-system](../measure-theory.md#pi-system) $\mathcal A_2$. Hence [Dynkin lemma](../measure-theory.md#dynkin-lemma) gives $\mathcal D_A=\mathcal G_2$.

Now fix any $B\in\mathcal G_2$ and put

$$
\mathcal E_B=\{A\in\mathcal G_1:\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\}.
$$

The same [complement](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) and disjoint-[union](../set.md#set-union) calculations make this a [Dynkin system](../measure-theory.md#dynkin-system). The first step shows that it contains $\mathcal A_1$, so [Dynkin lemma](../measure-theory.md#dynkin-lemma) gives $\mathcal E_B=\mathcal G_1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\quad(A\in\mathcal G_1,\ B\in\mathcal G_2).}
$$

This is precisely the definition of [independent sigma-algebras](../probability-theory.md#independent-sigma-algebras), and proves [independence extended from generating pi-systems](../probability-theory.md#independence-extended-from-generating-pi-systems).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

For $m\geq1$, fix signs $x_1,\ldots,x_m$. The constraints $X_j=x_j$ leave exactly two choices for the $(m+1)$ signs $Y_0,\ldots,Y_m$: choose $Y_0$ freely, then set $Y_1=Y_0x_1$ and $Y_j=x_{j-1}x_j$ for $2\leq j\leq m$. Each input tuple has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $2^{-(m+1)}$ by [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables). Consequently

$$
\mathbb P(X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_m=x_m)=2^{-m}
=\prod_{j=1}^m\mathbb P(X_j=x_j).
$$

Every finite subfamily is a [marginal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#marginal-distribution) of one such initial segment, so **the entire sequence is independent**, with fair sign [probability distributions](../probability-distribution.md).

Using the usual everywhere sign-valued representatives, for every $n\geq1$ we have

$$
Y_0=X_n\prod_{j=1}^nY_j.
$$

The right side is [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) with respect to $\sigma(\mathcal Y,\mathcal T_n)$. Therefore $Y_0$ is [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) with respect to their [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) $\mathcal G$.

Write $\mathcal T=\bigcap_n\mathcal T_n$. The [Kolmogorov zero-one law](../probability-theory.md#kolmogorov-s-zero-one-law) applies to the [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) $(X_n)$, so every [event](../probability-space.md#event) $C\in\mathcal T$ has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero or one. Also $Y_0$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal Y$: this follows first for finite [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) of sign [events](../probability-space.md#event) from the given [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), then for their generated [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) by part (a).

The [sets](../set.md) $D\cap C$ with $D\in\mathcal Y$ and $C\in\mathcal T$ form a [pi-system](../measure-theory.md#pi-system) generating $\mathcal H$. If $\mathbb P(C)=0$, the [event](../probability-space.md#event) $D\cap C$ has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero and is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of every [event](../probability-space.md#event) in $\sigma(Y_0)$. If $\mathbb P(C)=1$, it differs from $D$ by a [null set](../measure-theory.md#null-set), so the same [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) follows from that of $Y_0$ and $\mathcal Y$. Another application of part (a) proves

$$
\boxed{Y_0\text{ is }\mathcal G\text{-measurable,}\qquad\sigma(Y_0)\text{ and }\mathcal H\text{ are independent}.}
$$

In particular $\mathcal H$ cannot contain the nontrivial [event](../probability-space.md#event) $\{Y_0=1\}$, whereas $\mathcal G$ does. Thus adjoining a [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) need not commute with a decreasing [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection).

There is a small version qualification if the assumptions specify only the [probability distributions](../probability-distribution.md), allowing exceptional values other than signs. Put $q_n=X_n/(Y_1\cdots Y_n)$ when the denominator is nonzero, and $q_n=0$ otherwise. For $k\geq n$, $q_k$ is [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) with respect to $\sigma(\mathcal Y,\mathcal T_n)$. The [limit superior](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-superior) of $(q_k)$, after [scalar clipping](../topology.md#scalar-clipping) to $[-1,1]$, is therefore $\mathcal G$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) and equals $Y_0$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Literal [measurability](../measure-theory.md#measurability) of an arbitrary version of $Y_0$ need not follow without choosing sign-valued representatives or adjoining the ambient [null sets](../measure-theory.md#null-set). All the [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) conclusions are unaffected.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/c/i)

The first entrance time $\tau_K$ is a [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time), because $\{\tau_K\leq n\}=\bigcup_{j=0}^n\{M_j\leq-K\}\in\mathcal F_n$. Before this time $M_n>-K$; at entrance, the [bounded increments](../stochastic-process.md#bounded-increments) give $M_{\tau_K}>-K-L$. Thus the [stopped martingale](../martingale.md#stopped-martingale)

$$
N_n=M_{n\wedge\tau_K}+K+L
$$

is a [nonnegative martingale](../martingale.md#nonnegative-martingale). Here the [stopped process](../martingale.md#stopped-process) is a [martingale](../martingale.md) since its increment is $\mathbf1_{\{\tau_K>n\}}(M_{n+1}-M_n)$, whose [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) given $\mathcal F_n$ is zero.

The [martingale convergence theorem](../martingale.md#martingale-convergence-theorem) states that a [nonnegative martingale](../martingale.md#nonnegative-martingale) converges [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) to a finite [integrable random variable](../expected-value.md#integrable-random-variable); indeed its [expectations](../expected-value.md) are constant, giving the required bound in the [L1 norm](../normed-vector-space.md#l1-norm). Apply this theorem to $(N_n)$. On $\{\tau_K=\infty\}$ the [stopped process](../martingale.md#stopped-process) equals the original one at every time. Hence

$$
\boxed{M_n\text{ converges to a finite limit almost surely on }\{\tau_K=\infty\}.}
$$

The overshoot bound is why the assumption of [bounded increments](../stochastic-process.md#bounded-increments) matters.

<h4 id="1/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

<h5 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/c/ii)

Apply part (i) for every positive integer $K$ and discard the [countable union](../set.md#countable-union) of its exceptional [null sets](../measure-theory.md#null-set). A [sample path](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) bounded below never reaches $-K$ for some such $K$, and must therefore converge to a finite [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence).

Apply the same argument to the [martingale](../martingale.md) $(-M_n)$. Outside one more [null set](../measure-theory.md#null-set), every [sample path](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) bounded above also converges finitely. Thus a [sample path](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) which does not converge finitely must be unbounded in both directions. Each finite initial segment has finite values, so removing any such segment preserves unboundedness. It follows that the [limit superior](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-superior) is $+\infty$ and the [limit inferior](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-inferior) is $-\infty$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A\cup B)=1.}
$$

This proves the [bounded-increment martingale convergence-or-oscillation dichotomy](../stochastic-process.md#bounded-increment-martingale-convergence-or-oscillation-dichotomy); in particular one-sided divergence to infinity is impossible with positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/d/i)

Choose versions

$$
p_k=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{B_k}\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]\in[0,1],\qquad A_0=0,\qquad A_n=\sum_{k=1}^np_k.
$$

The [stochastic process](../stochastic-process.md) $(A_n)$ is increasing, and its $k$th increment is $\mathcal F_{k-1}$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability), so it is [predictable](../martingale.md#predictable-process). The [Doob decomposition theorem](../martingale.md#doob-decomposition-theorem) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{X_n=M_n+A_n,\qquad M_0=0,\qquad M_n=\sum_{k=1}^n(\mathbf1_{B_k}-p_k).}
$$

To check the [martingale](../martingale.md) property directly, each summand is [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) and has zero [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) given $\mathcal F_{k-1}$. The sums are an [adapted process](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) for the given [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory). Thus $\mathbb E[M_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=M_{n-1}$. Its [bounded increments](../stochastic-process.md#bounded-increments) satisfy $|M_n-M_{n-1}|\leq1$.

<h4 id="1/d/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/d/ii)

Both $X_n$ and $A_n$ are increasing, so each has a [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) in $[0,\infty]$. Apply part (c)(ii) to the [martingale](../martingale.md) $M_n=X_n-A_n$, whose [bounded increments](../stochastic-process.md#bounded-increments) were verified in part (i).

On $\{X_\infty<\infty,\ A_\infty=\infty\}$ we would have $M_n\to-\infty$. On $\{X_\infty=\infty,\ A_\infty<\infty\}$ we would have $M_n\to+\infty$. Both [events](../probability-space.md#event) have [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero by the convergence-or-oscillation conclusion. Hence the two divergence [events](../probability-space.md#event) agree up to a [null set](../measure-theory.md#null-set). Since $X_\infty=\infty$ means precisely that infinitely many $B_n$ occur,

$$
\boxed{\{B_n\text{ infinitely often}\}=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^\infty\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{B_k}\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]=\infty\right\}\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Taking [probabilities](../probability-theory.md#probability) proves the displayed equality in the question. This stronger agreement of [events](../probability-space.md#event) is the [Conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma](../probability-theory.md#conditional-borel-cantelli-lemma).

<h4 id="1/d/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

<h5 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/d/iii)

Suppose the [events](../probability-space.md#event) $(B_n)$ are mutually [independent events](../probability-theory.md#independent-events). Then $B_k$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal F_{k-1}$, first by factorization against finite [intersections](../set.md#set-intersection) of previous [events](../probability-space.md#event) and then by part (a). Therefore

$$
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{B_k}\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]=\mathbb P(B_k)\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

If $\sum_k\mathbb P(B_k)=\infty$, the sum in part (ii) is infinite [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), and consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(B_n\text{ infinitely often})=1.}
$$

This is the [Second Borel-Cantelli lemma](../probability-theory.md#borel-cantelli-second-lemma). In fact part (ii) also yields [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero in the convergent case for these [independent events](../probability-theory.md#independent-events).

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

The [sigma-algebra generated by a random variable](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra-generated-by-a-random-variable) $X$ is the smallest [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) on $\Omega$ making $X$ a [measurable function](../measure-theory.md#measurable-function) into $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$. Equivalently, it is the [intersection](../set.md#set-intersection) of all [sigma-algebras](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) containing the [inverse images](../set-theory.md#preimage) $X^{-1}(C)$ of [Borel sets](../measure-theory.md#borel-set) $C$.

Let $\mathcal D=\{X^{-1}(C):C\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\}$. This collection is already a [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra): $X^{-1}(\mathbb R)=\Omega$,

$$
\bigl(X^{-1}(C)\bigr)^c=X^{-1}(C^c),\qquad
\bigcup_{j=1}^\infty X^{-1}(C_j)=X^{-1}\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^\infty C_j\right).
$$

The right-hand [sets](../set.md) are in $\mathcal D$, since the [Borel sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#borel-sigma-algebra) is closed under the indicated operations. Any [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) making $X$ [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) must contain every member of $\mathcal D$. Conversely $\mathcal D$ makes $X$ [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) by definition. Hence

$$
\boxed{\sigma(X)=\{X^{-1}(C):C\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\}.}
$$

This equality holds exactly as a collection of [sets](../set.md), without completing the [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra).

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

We prove the [Doob-Dynkin lemma](../measure-theory.md#doob-dynkin-lemma) here by [simple function](../measure-theory.md#simple-function) approximation. Choose $C<\infty$ such that $|Z|\leq C$ everywhere, and let

$$
Z_m=2^{-m}\lfloor2^mZ\rfloor.
$$

Each $Z_m$ is a finite-valued $\sigma(X)$-[measurable function](../measure-theory.md#measurable-function) and $Z_m\to Z$ pointwise. Write its level [sets](../set.md) as $A_{m,j}$, with associated values $z_{m,j}$. By part (i), choose [Borel sets](../measure-theory.md#borel-set) $D_{m,j}$ with $A_{m,j}=X^{-1}(D_{m,j})$. Define the [Borel measurable functions](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function)

$$
g_m(x)=\sum_jz_{m,j}\mathbf1_{D_{m,j}}(x).
$$

Although the chosen $D_{m,j}$ need not partition all of $\mathbb R$, their [inverse images](../set-theory.md#preimage) do partition $\Omega$, so $g_m(X)=Z_m$ exactly.

Apply [scalar clipping](../topology.md#scalar-clipping) to each $g_m$ with bounds $[-C-1,C+1]$, preserving this equality, and define $g(x)=\limsup_mg_m(x)$. A bounded [limit superior](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-superior) of [Borel measurable functions](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) is a finite [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function). At every $\omega\in\Omega$ the [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) exists and gives

$$
\boxed{g(X(\omega))=\lim_mZ_m(\omega)=Z(\omega).}
$$

Thus the factorization is exact for a bounded, exactly $\sigma(X)$-[measurable function](../measure-theory.md#measurable-function). If hypotheses are instead given only up to [null sets](../measure-theory.md#null-set), the corresponding conclusion is a factorization holding [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) after choosing a representative.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

For this part and part (c), take $X$ to be $[0,1]$-valued everywhere. The [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) gives $\mathbb P(X=1)=0$, but the paper's expression for $Z_n$ is undefined there because $X_n+2^{-n}>1$. Define $Z_n=0$ on $\{X=1\}$. Keep $X_n=\lfloor2^nX\rfloor/2^n$ exactly as printed, including $X_n=1$ on that [null set](../measure-theory.md#null-set); this preserves the exact [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra) equality in part (ii). Every slope formula below is asserted on $\{X<1\}$, hence [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence).

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

The [floor function](../calculus.md#floor-function) gives the deterministic bound

$$
0\leq X-X_n<2^{-n}
$$

for every outcome, including $X=1$. Thus **all three convergence assertions hold**: there is pointwise, and hence [almost sure convergence](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), as well as [convergence in probability](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-probability) and [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2). In detail, for $\varepsilon>0$ the [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $\mathbb P(|X-X_n|>\varepsilon)$ is zero as soon as $2^{-n}\leq\varepsilon$; also

$$
\mathbb E[|X-X_n|^2]\leq4^{-n}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution) even gives the exact [second moment](../expected-value.md#second-moment) of the error by integrating over the [dyadic partition](../real-analysis.md#dyadic-partition):

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[|X-X_n|^2]=2^n\int_0^{2^{-n}}t^2\,dt=\frac{4^{-n}}3.}
$$

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Every $X_k$ is a [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) of $X$, by the formula involving the [floor function](../calculus.md#floor-function). Thus

$$
\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)\subseteq\sigma(X).
$$

Conversely, part (i) gives the everywhere identity $X=\lim_{k\to\infty}X_k$. For fixed $n$, this is the [pointwise limit](../sequence-and-series.md#pointwise-limit) of [random variables](../random-variable.md) [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) with respect to $\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)$. Such a [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) is [measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability), so $\sigma(X)\subseteq\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)=\sigma(X)\quad(n\geq1).}
$$

These are increasing-precision observations even when the first finitely many are discarded; they do not behave like the [independent random variables](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) whose [tail sigma-algebra](../probability-theory.md#tail-sigma-algebra) appears in Question 1(b).

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

A family $(\xi_i)_{i\in I}$ of [integrable random variables](../expected-value.md#integrable-random-variable) is [uniformly integrable](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) when

$$
\boxed{\lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{i\in I}\mathbb E\bigl[|\xi_i|\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_i|>K\}}\bigr]=0.}
$$

The [Lipschitz condition](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) gives, on $\{X<1\}$,

$$
|Z_n|=2^n|f(X_n+2^{-n})-f(X_n)|\leq2^nL2^{-n}=L.
$$

Our chosen zero value on $\{X=1\}$ satisfies the same bound. Hence the [random variables](../random-variable.md) $(Z_n)$ are [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability), and for $K\geq L$ every tail [expectation](../expected-value.md) in the definition vanishes. Therefore **the family is uniformly integrable**.

<h4 id="2/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#2/b/iv)

For $1\leq j\leq n$, repeated use of the [floor function](../calculus.md#floor-function) identity gives

$$
X_j=2^{-j}\lfloor2^jX_n\rfloor.
$$

Thus the [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) satisfies $\mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_n)$. Each positive-[probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) [atom of a sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#atom-of-a-sigma-algebra) here has the form

$$
A_{n,k}=\{k2^{-n}\leq X<(k+1)2^{-n}\},\qquad 0\leq k<2^n,
$$

with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $2^{-n}$; $\{X=1\}$ is a possible additional [null set](../measure-theory.md#null-set) atom. Averaging over each [dyadic interval](../real-analysis.md#dyadic-interval) gives a version

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[h(X)\mid\mathcal F_n]
=2^n\int_{X_n}^{X_n+2^{-n}}h(u)\,du\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

For a globally defined version of the right side, assign any value, for example zero, on $\{X=1\}$.

Indeed, the displayed [random variable](../random-variable.md) is $\mathcal F_n$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) and bounded by $\sup|h|$. On each $A_{n,k}$ its [integral](../integral.md) is

$$
2^{-n}\,2^n\int_{k2^{-n}}^{(k+1)2^{-n}}h(u)\,du
=\mathbb E[h(X)\mathbf1_{A_{n,k}}],
$$

by the [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution). Every member of $\mathcal F_n$ is a [union](../set.md#set-union) of the displayed [sets](../set.md) and the possible zero-[probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) atom. Summing these identities therefore verifies the defining [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) property on all of $\mathcal F_n$.

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

Each $Z_n$ is a bounded [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) of $X_n$, including the specified value at $X_n=1$. Therefore $(Z_n)$ is an [adapted process](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) for $(\mathcal F_n)$ and consists of [integrable random variables](../expected-value.md#integrable-random-variable).

Fix an [atom of a sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#atom-of-a-sigma-algebra) in $\mathcal F_n$ with positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability), corresponding to the [dyadic interval](../real-analysis.md#dyadic-interval) $[u,u+\delta)$, with $\delta=2^{-n}$. By the [uniform distribution](../continuous-probability-distribution.md#continuous-uniform-distribution), each half has [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) $1/2$. The [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) of $Z_{n+1}$ on that atom is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\bigl[2^{n+1}(f(u+\delta/2)-f(u))
+2^{n+1}(f(u+\delta)-f(u+\delta/2))\bigr]
&=2^n(f(u+\delta)-f(u))\\
&=Z_n.
\end{aligned}
$$

The middle values cancel. On the zero-[probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) atom the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) can be chosen as zero, agreeing with our version of $Z_n$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[Z_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=Z_n\quad\text{almost surely},}
$$

so $(Z_n)$ is a [martingale](../martingale.md). This is the [dyadic slope martingale](../martingale.md#dyadic-slope-martingale) associated with the [Lipschitz function](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) $f$.

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

The [uniformly integrable martingale convergence theorem](../martingale.md#uniformly-integrable-martingale-convergence-theorem) states that a [uniformly integrable](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) [martingale](../martingale.md) $(U_n)$ has an [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) terminal [random variable](../random-variable.md) $U_\infty$ such that $U_n\to U_\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) and in the [L1 norm](../normed-vector-space.md#l1-norm), and

$$
U_n=\mathbb E[U_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n].
$$

The last identity follows by passing to the [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) in $\mathbb E[U_m\mathbf1_A]=\mathbb E[U_n\mathbf1_A]$ for $A\in\mathcal F_n$ and $m\geq n$, using [convergence in L1](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l1).

Part (b)(iii) verifies [uniform integrability](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) for $(Z_n)$, so

$$
\boxed{Z_n\longrightarrow Z_\infty\quad\text{almost surely and in }L^1,\qquad
Z_n=\mathbb E[Z_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n].}
$$

Moreover $|Z_\infty|\leq L$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). In this particular problem the common bound also yields [convergence in Lp](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-lp) for every finite $p\geq1$, including [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2): the [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) applies to $|Z_n-Z_\infty|^p\leq(2L)^p$. General [uniform integrability](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#uniform-integrability) alone does not assert these higher-power conclusions.

<h4 id="2/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/c/iii)

Choose $Z_\infty$ as the pointwise [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) of $(Z_n)$ where it exists, and as zero otherwise. The convergence [event](../probability-space.md#event) and every $Z_n$ are $\sigma(X)$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability), so this is an exactly $\sigma(X)$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) version, bounded by $L$. Part (a)(ii), the [Doob-Dynkin lemma](../measure-theory.md#doob-dynkin-lemma), supplies a [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) $g$ on $[0,1]$ such that $Z_\infty=g(X)$. Applying [scalar clipping](../topology.md#scalar-clipping) to $g$ with bounds $[-L,L]$ preserves that equality and ensures $|g(u)|\leq L$ everywhere.

Part (c)(ii) and the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) formula from part (b)(iv) now give

$$
\boxed{Z_n=\mathbb E[g(X)\mid\mathcal F_n]
=2^n\int_{X_n}^{X_n+2^{-n}}g(u)\,du\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Since the indices $n$ form a [countable](../cardinal-number.md#countable-set) family, these identities may be taken on one common full-[probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) [event](../probability-space.md#event).

For fixed $n,k$, both the slope and the displayed average are constant on the atom $A_{n,k}$, whose [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) is $2^{-n}>0$. An equality holding [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) of these constants forces the deterministic equality

$$
f((k+1)2^{-n})-f(k2^{-n})
=\int_{k2^{-n}}^{(k+1)2^{-n}}g(u)\,du.
$$

Sum over $0\leq k<j$. The [telescoping sum](../arithmetic.md#telescoping-sum) gives

$$
f(j2^{-n})-f(0)=\int_0^{j2^{-n}}g(u)\,du,
\qquad 0\leq j\leq2^n.
$$

Thus the desired identity holds at every dyadic endpoint, including $1$, without evaluating the undefined slope at $X=1$.

Finally, given $x\in[0,1]$, take dyadic endpoints $x_n\to x$. The [Lipschitz condition](../real-analysis.md#lipschitz-continuity) implies $|f(x_n)-f(x)|\leq L|x_n-x|$, and the bound on the [Borel measurable function](../measure-theory.md#borel-measurable-function) $g$ gives

$$
\left|\int_0^{x_n}g(u)\,du-\int_0^xg(u)\,du\right|\leq L|x_n-x|.
$$

Passing to the [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) proves

$$
\boxed{f(x)-f(0)=\int_0^xg(u)\,du\quad\text{for every }x\in[0,1].}
$$

This is a [martingale proof of the integral representation of a Lipschitz function](../martingale.md#martingale-proof-of-the-integral-representation-of-a-lipschitz-function). It constructs a bounded [Lebesgue integrable function](../measure-theory.md#lebesgue-integrable-function) representing the increments of $f$, and in particular proves [absolute continuity](../sobolev-space.md#absolutely-continuous-function) of $f$. No prior assumption that $f$ has a [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is needed.

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

Write $N=a+b+c$ for the conserved total number of tokens. The main case has positive integer initial holdings. If an initial holding is zero, take $\tau=0$; the formulas below still hold when $N>0$. If $N=0$, the [stochastic process](../stochastic-process.md) is constant and $\mathbb E[\tau]=0$.

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

A discrete-time [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time) for a [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) $(\mathcal F_n)$ is a [random variable](../random-variable.md) $\tau$ taking values in $\{0,1,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ such that $\{\tau\leq n\}\in\mathcal F_n$ for every $n$. It means that whether stopping has occurred by time $n$ can be determined from the information available then.

For the first elimination time,

$$
\{\tau\leq n\}=\bigcup_{j=0}^n\{X_jY_jZ_j=0\}.
$$

Each [event](../probability-space.md#event) in this finite [union](../set.md#set-union) belongs to $\mathcal F_j\subseteq\mathcal F_n$, by the definition of the [natural filtration](../stochastic-process.md#natural-filtration). Therefore **$\tau$ is a stopping time**. This verification does not require first knowing whether $\tau$ is finite [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Put $P_n=X_nY_nZ_n$. Before $\tau$, all three players are active. Each unordered pair is selected with [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $1/3$, and each direction of transfer then has [conditional probability](../probability-theory.md#conditional-probability) $1/2$. Given the current holdings $(x,y,z)$, a transfer within the first pair has mean product

$$
\frac{(x+1)(y-1)z+(x-1)(y+1)z}{2}=xyz-z.
$$

For transfers within the other two pairs the analogous values are $xyz-y$ and $xyz-x$. Averaging over pairs gives

$$
\mathbb E[P_{n+1}-P_n\mid\mathcal F_n]=-\frac{x+y+z}{3}=-\frac N3
\quad\text{on }\{n<\tau\}.
$$

At and after elimination the product is zero, since an eliminated player never re-enters. In particular the [stopped process](../martingale.md#stopped-process) has increment

$$
M_{(n+1)\wedge\tau}-M_{n\wedge\tau}
=\mathbf1_{\{n<\tau\}}\left(P_{n+1}-P_n+\frac N3\right).
$$

The [indicator function](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function) is $\mathcal F_n$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability), and the expression in parentheses has zero [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) on its support. The [stopped process](../martingale.md#stopped-process) is [adapted](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) and [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability): at fixed $n$, its absolute value is bounded by $N^3+nN/3$. Hence

$$
\boxed{(M_{n\wedge\tau})_{n\geq0}\text{ is a martingale},\qquad M_0=abc.}
$$

This is the [product martingale for three-player token exchange](../martingale.md#product-martingale-for-three-player-token-exchange).

<h4 id="3/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/a/iii)

After $\tau$, the token product remains zero, whereas the deterministic term $nN/3$ continues to increase. Combining this with the pre-elimination calculation in part (ii),

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_{n+1}-M_n\mid\mathcal F_n]
=\frac N3\mathbf1_{\{\tau\leq n\}}.}
$$

The [stochastic process](../stochastic-process.md) is [adapted](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) and [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) at each fixed time, and the displayed [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) is nonnegative. Thus **$(M_n)$ is a submartingale**.

For $N>0$ it is neither a [martingale](../martingale.md) nor a [supermartingale](../martingale.md#supermartingale). If $a,b,c>0$, there is positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) that the first player transfers a token to the second in each of the next $a$ rounds. Until the last such round all three remain active, so this [event](../probability-space.md#event) has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) $6^{-a}>0$ and entails $\tau\leq a$. At time $n=a$ the displayed [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) is therefore strictly positive on an [event](../probability-space.md#event) of positive [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability), ruling out both the zero drift required of a [martingale](../martingale.md) and the nonpositive drift required of a [supermartingale](../martingale.md#supermartingale). If an initial holding is zero, $\tau=0$ and the same conclusion follows immediately. The degenerate case $N=0$ gives a constant [martingale](../martingale.md).

<h4 id="3/a/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/a/iv)

The bounded-time [optional sampling theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) says that for an [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) discrete-time [martingale](../martingale.md) $(U_n)$ and a [bounded stopping time](../martingale.md#bounded-stopping-time) $S$, $\mathbb E[U_S]=\mathbb E[U_0]$. It requires no assumption about an eventual unbounded [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time).

Apply this theorem with $S=n\wedge\tau$, or simply take the [expectation](../expected-value.md) of the [stopped martingale](../martingale.md#stopped-martingale) in part (ii). For $N>0$ we obtain

$$
\mathbb E[P_{n\wedge\tau}]+\frac N3\mathbb E[n\wedge\tau]=abc.
$$

Since $P_{n\wedge\tau}\geq0$,

$$
\mathbb E[n\wedge\tau]\leq\frac{3abc}{N}.
$$

The [monotone convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem) now yields $\mathbb E[\tau]\leq3abc/N<\infty$. In particular $\tau<\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), which has been established without assuming that [optional stopping](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) is valid at $\tau$.

For each [sample path](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) with finite $\tau$, $P_{n\wedge\tau}$ eventually equals $P_\tau=0$. Also $0\leq P_{n\wedge\tau}\leq N^3$. The [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem) gives $\mathbb E[P_{n\wedge\tau}]\to0$. Taking the [limit of a sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#limit-of-a-sequence) in the earlier identity therefore proves

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[\tau]=\frac{3abc}{a+b+c}.}
$$

For example, initial holdings $(1,1,1)$ give [expectation](../expected-value.md) one, as the first transfer must eliminate its giver. This computes the first elimination time, rather than the later time when one player owns all tokens.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Assume $X$ is [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function). Since $X$ and $Y$ have the same [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), $Y$ is also [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) and $\mathbb E[Y^2]=\mathbb E[X^2]$. The product $XY$ is [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) by the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality).

We use the defining test-function identity for [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation): for bounded $\mathcal G$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) $H$, $\mathbb E[XH]=\mathbb E[YH]$. Take $H=Y\mathbf1_{\{|Y|\leq m\}}$ and let $m\to\infty$. The [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem), with bounds $|XY|$ and $Y^2$, gives

$$
\mathbb E[XY]=\mathbb E[Y^2].
$$

This is the extension of the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) pull-out identity to this [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) multiplier. Consequently

$$
\mathbb E[(X-Y)^2]
=\mathbb E[X^2]-2\mathbb E[XY]+\mathbb E[Y^2]
=\mathbb E[X^2]-\mathbb E[Y^2]=0.
$$

A nonnegative [random variable](../random-variable.md) with zero [expectation](../expected-value.md) is zero [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Hence

$$
\boxed{X=Y\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Geometrically, this is the equality case in the [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) [orthogonal projection](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) interpretation of [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation).

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

The [function](../function.md) $\phi_a(t)=t\wedge a$ is a [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function), and $X\wedge a$ is [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability) since $|X\wedge a|\leq|X|+a$. The concave form of the [conditional Jensen inequality](../measure-theory.md#conditional-jensen-inequality) gives

$$
\mathbb E[X\wedge a\mid\mathcal G]
\leq\bigl(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\bigr)\wedge a
=Y\wedge a\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

The [random variables](../random-variable.md) $X$ and $Y$ have the same [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), so $\mathbb E[X\wedge a]=\mathbb E[Y\wedge a]$. Using the [law of total expectation](../measure-theory.md#law-of-total-expectation), the nonnegative difference between the right and left sides of the inequality has zero [expectation](../expected-value.md). It must vanish [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[X\wedge a\mid\mathcal G]=Y\wedge a\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

The equality comes from the distributional assumption as well as the [conditional Jensen inequality](../measure-theory.md#conditional-jensen-inequality).

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

We first establish the lower truncation identity as well. The pair $(-X,-Y)$ has the same hypotheses as $(X,Y)$, because [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) is linear and negation preserves equality of [probability distributions](../probability-distribution.md). Apply part (ii) to this pair and multiply by $-1$ to get

$$
\mathbb E[X\vee(-a)\mid\mathcal G]=Y\vee(-a).
$$

For every real $t$,

$$
(t\wedge a)\vee(-a)=(t\wedge a)+(t\vee(-a))-t.
$$

Using this identity, part (ii), the lower truncation identity, and linearity of [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[(X\wedge a)\vee(-a)\mid\mathcal G]
=(Y\wedge a)\vee(-a)\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

In particular the argument does not assume that a [function](../function.md) obtained by [scalar clipping](../topology.md#scalar-clipping) is a [convex function](../real-analysis.md#convex-function) or a [concave function](../real-analysis.md#concave-function).

Write $C_a(t)=(t\wedge a)\vee(-a)$. The bounded [random variables](../random-variable.md) $C_a(X)$ and $C_a(Y)$ have the same [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md), and the displayed identity identifies the latter as the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) of the former. They are [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function), so part (i) gives $C_a(X)=C_a(Y)$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Take $a=1,2,\ldots$ and discard the [countable union](../set.md#countable-union) of exceptional [null sets](../measure-theory.md#null-set). At every remaining outcome, let $a\to\infty$ to obtain

$$
\boxed{X=Y\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

Thus [conditional expectation preserving a distribution](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation-preserving-a-distribution) forces equality even when only [integrability](../measure-theory.md#integrability) is assumed.

<h4 id="3/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#3/b/iv)

Let $\mathcal G=T^{-1}(\mathcal F)=\{T^{-1}(A):A\in\mathcal F\}$. [Inverse images](../set-theory.md#preimage) commute with [complements](../set.md#complement-of-a-set) and [countable unions](../set.md#countable-union), so this is a [sub-sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sub-sigma-algebra) of $\mathcal F$. The [random variable](../random-variable.md) $X\circ T$ is $\mathcal G$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability).

The assumption $\mathbb P\circ T^{-1}=\mathbb P$ says that $T$ is a [measure-preserving transformation](../ergodic-theory.md#measure-preserving-transformation). Consequently $X\circ T$ has the same [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) as $X$ and is [integrable](../measure-theory.md#integrability). It also implies $\mathbb E[W\circ T]=\mathbb E[W]$ for every [integrable random variable](../expected-value.md#integrable-random-variable) $W$, first for [indicator functions](../measure-theory.md#indicator-function), then for [simple functions](../measure-theory.md#simple-function), and then by positive and negative approximation.

To identify a [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation), take any $A\in\mathcal F$. The assumed identity with the bounded [random variable](../random-variable.md) $Z=\mathbf1_A$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[X\mathbf1_{T^{-1}(A)}]
&=\mathbb E[X(\mathbf1_A\circ T)]
=\mathbb E[X\mathbf1_A]\\
&=\mathbb E[(X\mathbf1_A)\circ T]
=\mathbb E[(X\circ T)\mathbf1_{T^{-1}(A)}].
\end{aligned}
$$

Every member of $\mathcal G$ has the displayed form $T^{-1}(A)$, so this verifies all defining [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) identities. Therefore

$$
\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]=X\circ T\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

Apply part (iii), since this [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) has the same [probability distribution](../probability-distribution.md) as $X$. We conclude

$$
\boxed{X=X\circ T\quad\mathbb P\text{-almost surely}.}
$$

This is [invariance from integral duality for a measure-preserving transformation](../ergodic-theory.md#invariance-from-integral-duality-for-a-measure-preserving-transformation); neither invertibility of $T$ nor a [second moment](../expected-value.md#second-moment) of $X$ is required.
