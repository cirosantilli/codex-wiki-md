<h1 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For each integer $m\geq1$, let

$$
D_m=\{x\in\mathbb R:\mu(\{x\})\geq1/m\}.
$$

If $D_m$ had $m+1$ distinct members, [finite additivity](../../../../../finite-additivity-of-a-set-function.md) on their disjoint singleton [Borel sets](../../../../../borel-set.md) would give total [measure](../../../../../measure-split.md) at least $(m+1)/m>1$, contradicting $\mu(\mathbb R)=1$. Thus $|D_m|\leq m$.

Every positive singleton [measure](../../../../../measure-split.md) is at least $1/m$ for some sufficiently large $m$. Consequently the set of [point atoms of a measure](../../../../../point-atom-of-a-measure.md) is

$$
\{x:\mu(\{x\})>0\}=\bigcup_{m\geq1}D_m.
$$

A [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of finite [sets](../../../../../set-split.md) is at most [countable](../../../../../countable-set.md). This proves the claim, including the possibility that the set is empty. The same argument gives the [countability of point atoms of a finite measure](../../../../../countability-of-point-atoms-of-a-finite-measure.md), with $|D_m|\leq m\mu(\mathbb R)$ in place of $m$.
