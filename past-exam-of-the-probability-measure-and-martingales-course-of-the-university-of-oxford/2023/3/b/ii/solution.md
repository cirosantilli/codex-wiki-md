<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The [function](../../../../../function-split.md) $\phi_a(t)=t\wedge a$ is a [concave function](../../../../../concave-function.md), and $X\wedge a$ is [integrable](../../../../../integrability.md) since $|X\wedge a|\leq|X|+a$. The concave form of the [conditional Jensen inequality](../../../../../conditional-jensen-inequality.md) gives

$$
\mathbb E[X\wedge a\mid\mathcal G]
\leq\bigl(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\bigr)\wedge a
=Y\wedge a\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

The [random variables](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) $X$ and $Y$ have the same [probability distribution](../../../../../probability-distribution-split.md), so $\mathbb E[X\wedge a]=\mathbb E[Y\wedge a]$. Using the [law of total expectation](../../../../../law-of-total-expectation.md), the nonnegative difference between the right and left sides of the inequality has zero [expectation](../../../../../expected-value-split.md). It must vanish [almost surely](../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[X\wedge a\mid\mathcal G]=Y\wedge a\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

The equality comes from the distributional assumption as well as the [conditional Jensen inequality](../../../../../conditional-jensen-inequality.md).
