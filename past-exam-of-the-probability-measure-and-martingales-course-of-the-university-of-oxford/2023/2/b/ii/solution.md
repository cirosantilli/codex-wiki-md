<h1 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Every $X_k$ is a [Borel measurable function](../../../../../borel-measurable-function.md) of $X$, by the formula involving the [floor function](../../../../../floor-function.md). Thus

$$
\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)\subseteq\sigma(X).
$$

Conversely, part (i) gives the everywhere identity $X=\lim_{k\to\infty}X_k$. For fixed $n$, this is the [pointwise limit](../../../../../pointwise-limit.md) of [random variables](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) [measurable](../../../../../measurability.md) with respect to $\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)$. Such a [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md) is [measurable](../../../../../measurability.md), so $\sigma(X)\subseteq\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\sigma(X_n,X_{n+1},\ldots)=\sigma(X)\quad(n\geq1).}
$$

These are increasing-precision observations even when the first finitely many are discarded; they do not behave like the [independent random variables](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) whose [tail sigma-algebra](../../../../../tail-sigma-algebra.md) appears in Question 1(b).
