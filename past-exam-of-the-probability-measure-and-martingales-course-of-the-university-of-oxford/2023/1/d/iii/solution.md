<h1 id="1/d/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Suppose the [events](../../../../../event.md) $(B_n)$ are mutually [independent events](../../../../../independent-events.md). Then $B_k$ is [independent](../../../../../independent-random-variables.md) of $\mathcal F_{k-1}$, first by factorization against finite [intersections](../../../../../set-intersection.md) of previous [events](../../../../../event.md) and then by part (a). Therefore

$$
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{B_k}\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]=\mathbb P(B_k)\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

If $\sum_k\mathbb P(B_k)=\infty$, the sum in part (ii) is infinite [almost surely](../../../../../almost-sure-convergence.md), and consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(B_n\text{ infinitely often})=1.}
$$

This is the [Second Borel-Cantelli lemma](../../../../../borel-cantelli-second-lemma.md). In fact part (ii) also yields [probability](../../../../../probability.md) zero in the convergent case for these [independent events](../../../../../independent-events.md).
