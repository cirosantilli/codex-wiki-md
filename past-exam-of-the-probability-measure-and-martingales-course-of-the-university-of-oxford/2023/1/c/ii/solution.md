<h1 id="1/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Apply part (i) for every positive integer $K$ and discard the [countable union](../../../../../countable-union.md) of its exceptional [null sets](../../../../../null-set.md). A [sample path](../../../../../sample-path.md) bounded below never reaches $-K$ for some such $K$, and must therefore converge to a finite [limit of a sequence](../../../../../limit-of-a-sequence.md).

Apply the same argument to the [martingale](../../../../../martingale-split.md) $(-M_n)$. Outside one more [null set](../../../../../null-set.md), every [sample path](../../../../../sample-path.md) bounded above also converges finitely. Thus a [sample path](../../../../../sample-path.md) which does not converge finitely must be unbounded in both directions. Each finite initial segment has finite values, so removing any such segment preserves unboundedness. It follows that the [limit superior](../../../../../limit-superior.md) is $+\infty$ and the [limit inferior](../../../../../limit-inferior.md) is $-\infty$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(A\cup B)=1.}
$$

This proves the [bounded-increment martingale convergence-or-oscillation dichotomy](../../../../../bounded-increment-martingale-convergence-or-oscillation-dichotomy.md); in particular one-sided divergence to infinity is impossible with positive [probability](../../../../../probability.md).
