# Tangential launch trajectories across the escape threshold

↑ **Parent:** [Escape velocity](escape-velocity.md)

The same initial distance can lead to strikingly different [orbits](orbit-dynamical-system.md). Let $s=v_0/\sqrt{\mu/r_0}\geq1$ and launch tangentially in one [orbital plane](orbital-plane.md). Then [periapsis](periapsis.md) is the launch point, [specific angular momentum](specific-angular-momentum.md) is $h=r_0v_0$, and

$$
\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu/r_0}=\frac{s^2}{2}-1,\qquad e=s^2-1,\qquad \frac{r}{r_0}=\frac{s^2}{1+(s^2-1)\cos f},
$$

where $f$ is [true anomaly](true-anomaly.md). These follow by combining the initial [specific orbital energy](specific-orbital-energy.md) with the [orbital eccentricity](orbital-eccentricity.md) relation and the [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) equation. With $\mu=8\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$ and $r_0=2\,\mathrm m$, the initial [speeds](speed.md) $2$, $\sqrt6$, $2\sqrt2$, and $2\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ give $e=0,1/2,1,2$: a [circular orbit](circular-orbit.md), an [elliptic orbit](elliptic-orbit.md) with [apoapsis distance](apocentre-distance.md) $6\,\mathrm m$, a [parabolic trajectory](parabolic-trajectory.md), and a [hyperbolic Kepler orbit](hyperbolic-kepler-orbit.md). The two open paths have asymptotic [speeds](speed.md) zero and $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ respectively. Their different directions at large distance are part of the [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md), not an extra scalar escape threshold.
