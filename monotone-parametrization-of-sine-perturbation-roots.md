# Monotone parametrization of sine perturbation roots

↑ **Parent:** [Asymptotic expansion](asymptotic-expansion-split.md)

The real roots of $\sin x=x+\epsilon(1-x^2)$, with $\epsilon>0$, can be counted by solving for the parameter: $\epsilon=E(x)=(x-\sin x)/(x^2-1)$. Positive values occur only on $(-1,0)$ and $(1,\infty)$. Direct [differentiation](differentiation.md) and the half-angle identities give the derivative in the title. It is negative except at isolated points, so $E$ is strictly decreasing on both intervals, with limits $+\infty$ and zero. There are exactly two real roots for each positive parameter. As $\epsilon\to0^+$, [dominant balance](dominant-balance.md) gives $x_-\sim-(6\epsilon)^{1/3}$ and $x_+\sim\epsilon^{-1}$. The exact root count supplements the local [asymptotic expansions](asymptotic-expansion-split.md): a small-root expansion alone would omit the root escaping to infinity.
