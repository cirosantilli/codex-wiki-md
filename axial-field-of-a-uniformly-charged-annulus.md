# Axial field of a uniformly charged annulus

↑ **Parent:** [Electrostatic potential of a uniformly charged disk](electrostatic-potential-of-a-uniformly-charged-disk.md)

For an [annulus](annulus-mathematics.md) of inner radius $a>0$, outer radius $b>a$ and uniform [surface charge density](surface-charge-density.md) $\sigma$, integrate circular rings of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) to get $V(x)=\sigma[\sqrt{x^2+b^2}-\sqrt{x^2+a^2}]/(2\epsilon_0)$ with zero [electric potential](electric-potential.md) at infinity. Differentiation gives the displayed axial [electric field](electric-field-split.md). Its magnitude is greatest at $|x|=(ab)^{2/3}/\sqrt{a^{2/3}+b^{2/3}}$. Near the centre, $E_x=Qx/[2\pi\epsilon_0ab(a+b)]+O(x^3)$, where $Q=\pi\sigma(b^2-a^2)$; an oppositely charged particle constrained to the axis consequently undergoes small-amplitude [simple harmonic motion](simple-harmonic-motion.md).
