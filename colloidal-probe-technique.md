# Colloidal probe technique

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic force microscopy](atomic-force-microscopy.md)

Attach a sphere to an [atomic force microscope](atomic-force-microscope.md) cantilever and approach a planar surface in a controlled fluid. Calibrated cantilever bending measures force as a function of corrected surface separation. The sphere's known curvature allows comparison with planar interaction models through the [Derjaguin approximation](derjaguin-approximation.md). Approach speed, charge, roughness, hydration and jump-to-contact can limit interpretation.

A 1991 demonstration used a $3.5\,\mu\mathrm m$ radius silica sphere against a plane in sodium chloride solutions. The reported double-layer agreement and short-range deviations illustrate both the method and the need to distinguish electrostatic forces from a presumed purely dispersive interaction.[Nature 353, pp. 239–241, institutional abstract: attached silica sphere, planar geometry and NaCl solutions](direct-measurement-of-colloidal-forces-using-an-atomic-force-microscope-by-william-a-ducker-and-others.md)
