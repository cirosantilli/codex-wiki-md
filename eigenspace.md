# Eigenspace

↑ **Parent:** [Eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace)

For an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda$ of a [linear operator](linear-operator.md) $A:V\to V$, its eigenspace is

$$
E_\lambda=\ker(A-\lambda I)=\{v\in V:Av=\lambda v\}.
$$

It contains all [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) for $\lambda$ and the zero [vector](vector.md). Including zero makes this a [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md): if $v,w\in E_\lambda$, then $A(av+bw)=\lambda(av+bw)$ for any [scalars](scalar.md) $a,b$. Every nonzero [vector](vector.md) in this [linear subspace](vector-subspace.md) has the same scaling under $A$. Its [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) is the [geometric multiplicity](geometric-multiplicity.md) of the [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md). If $\lambda$ is not an [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md), the shifted [kernel](kernel-of-a-linear-map.md) is only $\{0\}$.

For the [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ from [eigenvector](eigenvector.md),

$$
E_3=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1)^T\},\qquad E_1=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-1)^T\}.
$$

Each [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) is a line. An [eigenspace](eigenspace.md) can also be a plane or the whole [vector space](vector-space-split.md): for $B=\operatorname{diag}(2,2,5)$, $E_2=\{(x,y,0)^T:x,y\in\mathbb R\}$ is a plane of [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) two, while for $C=2I$ every [vector](vector.md) belongs to $E_2$. A repeated [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) therefore need not prevent [diagonalizability](diagonalizable-matrix.md).

The useful unit of information is the whole [eigenspace](eigenspace.md), rather than a particular choice of [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) inside it. On $E_\lambda$, $A$ is simply multiplication by $\lambda$. Distinct [eigenspaces](eigenspace.md) fit together as a [direct sum](direct-sum.md), and a finite-dimensional [linear operator](linear-operator.md) is [diagonalizable](diagonalizable-matrix.md) exactly when they fill the whole [vector space](vector-space-split.md). For the example above, every [vector](vector.md) splits uniquely as

$$
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}=\frac{x+y}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}+\frac{x-y}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This decomposition makes the independent scalings visible without multiplying out the original [matrix](matrix.md) again.
