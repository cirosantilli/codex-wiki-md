A [projective variety](../../../../projective-variety.md) is a Zariski-closed [subset](../../../../subset.md) of [projective space](../../../../projective-space-split.md) $\mathbb P_k^n$, equivalently the common [zero set](../../../../zero-set.md) of [homogeneous polynomials](../../../../homogeneous-polynomial.md). [Homogeneity](../../../../homogeneity.md) makes their vanishing independent of the nonzero scalar used to represent a projective [point](../../../../point-geometry.md).

A [morphism](../../../../morphism-of-algebraic-varieties.md) is a map locally given by regular coordinate functions in [affine charts](../../../../affine-chart-of-a-variety.md). Equivalently, near each [point](../../../../point-geometry.md) of a projective source embedded in $\mathbb P^n$, a [morphism](../../../../morphism-of-algebraic-varieties.md) to $\mathbb P^m$ can be written

$$
\boxed{[x]\longmapsto[F_0(x):\cdots:F_m(x)]}
$$

using [homogeneous polynomials](../../../../homogeneous-polynomial.md) of a common degree that are not all zero on that [neighbourhood](../../../../neighbourhood-mathematics.md). The expressions must agree on overlaps, and for a specified target [variety](../../../../algebraic-variety-split.md) their values must lie in it. A single global tuple is a sufficient description when available, but is not part of the general definition.
