<h1 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

For any [affine variety](../../../../affine-algebraic-set.md) and [point](../../../../point-geometry.md) $p$, with $\mathfrak m_p=\{f\in A(X):f(p)=0\}$, the standard [localization](../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) description is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_{X,p}\cong A(X)_{\mathfrak m_p}.}
$$

Also $\mathcal O_X(D(g))\cong A(X)_g$ for a [principal open subset](../../../../principal-open-subscheme.md). One way to see the latter is to realize $D(g)$ as the [affine variety](../../../../affine-algebraic-set.md) $\{(x,z):g(x)z=1\}$; its [coordinate ring](../../../../coordinate-ring.md) is $A(X)[z]/(gz-1)=A(X)_g$ and part (d) applies.

For this [cusp](../../../../cusp-algebraic-geometry.md) use $x=t^3,y=t^2$, identifying $A(X)$ with $k[t^2,t^3]$ inside $k(t)$. The parametrization gives exactly the relation $x^2=y^3$; its kernel is generated by this binomial. For example reduce a [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) modulo $x^2-y^3$ to $a(y)+xb(y)$; substitution has even powers from $a(t^2)$ and odd powers at least three from $t^3b(t^2)$, so it can vanish only when both [polynomials](../../../../polynomial-split.md) are zero.

Write $g=x-y=t^2(t-1)$. The [open set](../../../../open-set.md) corresponds to $t\neq0,1$, and

$$
\mathcal O_X(U)=k[t^2,t^3]_{t^2(t-1)}.
$$

To simplify this [ring](../../../../ring.md) without assuming the normalization coordinate is present, observe that in $A(X)$

$$
g^2=(x-y)^2=y(y^2-2x+y).
$$

Thus $y$ becomes a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) after inverting $g$, with inverse $(y^2-2x+y)/g^2$. Consequently $t=x/y$ belongs to the [localization](../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md). Since $g=t^2(t-1)$ is a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md), both $t$ and $t-1$ become [units](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). Conversely every original generator lies in the corresponding two-point [localization](../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) of $k[t]$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_X(U)=k[t,t^{-1},(t-1)^{-1}].}
$$

Finally $p=(1,1)$ corresponds to $t=1$. It lies outside $U$, so compute its [local ring](../../../../local-ring.md) from $X$, at $\mathfrak m=(x-1,y-1)$. The element $y$ is a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md) there, hence $t=x/y$ belongs to $A(X)_{\mathfrak m}$. Every [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) in $t$ nonzero at one is then a [unit](../../../../unit-in-a-ring.md). Conversely a denominator from $A(X)\setminus\mathfrak m$ remains nonzero at $t=1$. These inclusions prove

$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_{X,p}=\bigl(k[x,y]/(x^2-y^3)\bigr)_{(x-1,y-1)}\cong k[t]_{(t-1)}.}
$$

This [smooth](../../../../smooth-function.md) [point](../../../../point-geometry.md) has the ordinary one-variable [local ring](../../../../local-ring.md), even though the global curve has a [cusp](../../../../cusp-algebraic-geometry.md) at its origin.

The [localization](../../../../localization-of-a-ring.md) calculation describes [regular functions on a cusp with two points removed](../../../../regular-functions-on-a-cusp-with-two-points-removed.md).
