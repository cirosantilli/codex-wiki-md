## Julius von Mayer

↑ **Parent:** [Physicist](physicist.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_von_Mayer)

Physician and natural philosopher who contributed to the conservation-of-energy programme in the 1840s, including an argument for the mechanical equivalent of [heat](heat.md).

### James Prescott Joule

↑ **Parent:** [List of physicists](#list-of-physicists)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Prescott_Joule)

James Prescott Joule was an English [physicist](physicist.md) whose private experiments connected [electricity](electromagnetism.md#electricity), [heat](heat.md) and mechanical [work](energy.md#work). His family ran a brewery in Salford; he developed an experimental laboratory while working in the family business.[Biographical account: brewing family, private laboratory and experimental interests](physics-bibliography.md#james-prescott-joule-by-science-museum-group) The Royal Society's archive identifies his occupation as [physicist](physicist.md) and records his life as 1818–1889.[Person record NA7041: occupation and life dates](physics-bibliography.md#james-prescott-joule-by-royal-society)

<a id="image-portrait-of-james-prescott-joule-1882"></a>
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/James_Prescott_Joule_by_John_Collier%2C_1882.jpg" alt="" width="250">

**[Figure 22](#image-portrait-of-james-prescott-joule-1882). Portrait of James Prescott Joule, 1882**. [Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:James\_Prescott\_Joule\_by\_John\_Collier,\_1882.jpg).

His electrical heating experiments placed metal coils in water and measured the [temperature](temperature.md) change. Later, he used the brewery cellar to reduce ambient [temperature](temperature.md) fluctuations during friction experiments and took particular care over [thermometer calibration](temperature.md#thermometer-calibration).[Electrical heating experiments; brewery-cellar friction measurements and precision thermometers](physics-bibliography.md#james-prescott-joule-by-science-museum-group) These practical choices address a central difficulty of [calorimetry](calorimetry.md): a small [temperature](temperature.md) rise can reflect both the intended input of [energy](energy.md) and unwanted [heat transfer](heat.md#heat-transfer) to the surroundings. A quiet baseline and a known [heat capacity](heat.md#heat-capacity) are part of the measurement, not decoration around the formula.

The contrast between [Joule heating](electric-charge.md#joule-heating) and [Joule's paddle-wheel experiment](internal-energy.md#joule-s-paddle-wheel-experiment) makes the connection especially clear. One supplies electrical [work](energy.md#work); the other supplies mechanical [work](energy.md#work). In each case, [calorimetry](calorimetry.md) tracks the resulting change of [internal energy](internal-energy.md). Comparing quantitatively controlled inputs with thermal changes gives an experimental test of [conservation of energy](mathematical-physics.md#conservation-of-energy), rather than merely showing that stirring or an [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current) can warm water. The paddle-wheel account describes the descending weights, vanes, thermal controls and corrections needed for that comparison.

[Joule's free-expansion experiment](thermodynamics.md#joule-s-free-expansion-experiment) asks a different question: how does a gas's [temperature](temperature.md) change during expansion without external [work](energy.md#work)? The [Joule-Thomson effect](enthalpy.md#joule-thomson-effect) instead concerns flow through a restriction and a balance involving [enthalpy](enthalpy.md). Keeping the two experiments distinct prevents a shared historical name from hiding different system boundaries and different conserved quantities.

### Albert Einstein

↑ **Parent:** [List of physicists](#list-of-physicists)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein)

A [physicist](physicist.md) whose work connects [special relativity](special-relativity.md), [general relativity](general-relativity.md), [Brownian motion](brownian-motion.md) and the [photoelectric effect](quantum-mechanics.md#photoelectric-effect). He worked at the Institute for Advanced Study from 1933 until his death in 1955.[Institutional profile: physicist, life dates and Institute appointment](physics-bibliography.md#albert-einstein-institutional-profile-by-institute-for-advanced-study)

In 1905, [Albert Einstein](#albert-einstein) used the [photoelectric effect](quantum-mechanics.md#photoelectric-effect) to explore quantized [light](optics.md#light), related [Brownian motion](brownian-motion.md) to molecular agitation, formulated [special relativity](special-relativity.md), and connected changes in [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) and [energy](energy.md). These problems link visible measurements to otherwise inaccessible structure: emitted [electron](physics.md#electron) energies, suspended-particle displacements and comparisons of clocks.[March, May, June and September 1905: the four papers and their physical questions](physics-bibliography.md#albert-einstein-the-great-works-by-center-for-history-of-physics)

The [Millikan photoelectric experiment](quantum-mechanics.md#millikan-photoelectric-experiment) follows one of those predictions into the laboratory. Its stopping-voltage measurements test a quantitative relation; the experiment's apparatus, surface preparation and electrical corrections matter to that inference. The [Einstein field equations](einstein-field-equations.md) and [general relativity](general-relativity.md) lead to a different set of tests involving [gravity](physics.md#gravity) and [spacetime](special-relativity.md#spacetime).

The [Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921](nobel-prize-in-physics.md#nobel-prize-in-physics-in-1921) recognized his theoretical work, particularly the law of the [photoelectric effect](quantum-mechanics.md#photoelectric-effect); the award was presented in 1922.[Prize motivation and the note that Einstein received the 1921 award in 1922](physics-bibliography.md#albert-einstein-facts-by-nobel-prize-outreach)

### John William Strutt, 3rd Baron Rayleigh

↑ **Parent:** [List of physicists](#list-of-physicists)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Strutt,_3rd_Baron_Rayleigh)

A [physicist](physicist.md) whose research joined [waves](wave.md), [optics](optics.md), [acoustics](physics.md#acoustics) and precise measurement. He maintained a laboratory at Terling and served as Cavendish professor of experimental [physics](physics.md) from 1879 to 1884. At Cambridge he developed laboratory courses; later he donated his Nobel award proceeds toward a Cavendish laboratory extension.[O'Connor and Robertson, biography: Terling laboratory; Cavendish professorship and laboratory courses; donation of Nobel proceeds](physics-bibliography.md#john-william-strutt-lord-rayleigh-by-john-joseph-o-connor-and-edmund-frederick-robertson)

The [Rayleigh-Ramsay argon density experiment](chemical-substance.md#rayleigh-ramsay-argon-density-experiment), pursued with [William Ramsay](chemistry.md#william-ramsay), connects a small discrepancy in gas weighing to chemical separation. Its lesson is practical: agreement within one preparation method does not ensure that a second method produces the same [chemical substance](chemical-substance.md). The [gas density](fluid-mechanics.md#gas-density) model explains how composition and filling conditions both affect the readout.
