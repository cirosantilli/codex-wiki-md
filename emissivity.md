# Emissivity

↑ **Parent:** [Stefan–Boltzmann law](stefan-boltzmann-law.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissivity)

[Emissivity](emissivity.md) compares thermal emission from a surface with emission from an ideal [blackbody](blackbody.md) at the same absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md), using the same [wavelength](wavelength.md) and angular averaging convention. For ordinary passive surfaces in the far field at local [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium-split.md), the ratio lies between zero and one. A [blackbody](blackbody.md) has [emissivity](emissivity.md) one. Unless a spectral or directional qualification is given, the $\epsilon$ in $M=\epsilon\sigma T^4$ is [total hemispherical emissivity](total-hemispherical-emissivity.md): a ratio of emitted [radiant exitances](radiant-exitance.md), not of all outgoing [radiant flux](radiant-flux.md) including reflected radiation.[§19.2, definition of emittance and equilibrium absorption/emission balance](thermodynamics-and-propulsion.md)

[Emissivity](emissivity.md) belongs to a specified surface state, not just a bulk material name. Coating, oxidation and surface finish can change it; [wavelength](wavelength.md), viewing direction and [temperature](temperature-split.md) also matter. A surface that looks white in visible [light](light.md) need not have low [infrared](infrared.md) [emissivity](emissivity.md). This distinction is useful when choosing a radiator coating or interpreting a radiation-based [temperature](temperature-split.md) measurement: a narrow-band detector needs the appropriate [spectral emissivity](spectral-emissivity.md), and its signal can include reflected surrounding radiation. The [grey body](grey-body.md) and [diffuse thermal emitter](diffuse-thermal-emitter.md) assumptions are separate simplifications, addressing [wavelength](wavelength.md) and direction respectively.
