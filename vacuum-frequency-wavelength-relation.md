# Vacuum frequency-wavelength relation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md)

A [monochromatic wave](monochromatic-wave.md) in vacuum has [frequency](frequency.md) $\nu$ and [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$ related by $\nu\lambda=c$, where $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). During one [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) $T=1/\nu$, a constant-[wave phase](phase-waves.md) surface travels one [wavelength](wavelength.md), so $\lambda=cT$. Equivalently, the [plane electromagnetic wave](plane-electromagnetic-wave.md) relation $\omega=ck$ with [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\omega=2\pi\nu$ and [wavenumber](wavenumber.md) $k=2\pi/\lambda$ gives the same result.

As a calculated example, a vacuum [radio wave](radio-wave.md) with $\nu=300\,\mathrm{MHz}=3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{Hz}$ has $\lambda=0.999308\,\mathrm m$ using $c=299792458\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Doubling [frequency](frequency.md) halves [wavelength](wavelength.md), independently of [wave amplitude](wave-amplitude.md) and [wave intensity](intensity-physics.md). The [frequency spectrum](frequency-spectrum.md) of a pulse contains multiple [frequencies](frequency.md); the relation applies to each vacuum component, rather than assigning one exact [wavelength](wavelength.md) to the whole pulse. In a homogeneous medium replace $c$ by the appropriate [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md); the vacuum equation does not assert that all media have the same [wavelength](wavelength.md) at a given [frequency](frequency.md).
