# HCl and DCl harmonic stretch comparison

↑ **Parent:** [Diatomic bond stretch](diatomic-bond-stretch.md)

Keep the same [bond force constant](bond-force-constant.md) $k$ and approximate the nuclear [masses](mass.md) of $^{1}\mathrm H$, $^{2}\mathrm H$, and $^{35}\mathrm{Cl}$ by $1u$, $2u$, and $35u$, respectively. Here $u$ is the [unified atomic mass unit](dalton-unit.md), and $\mathrm D$ denotes [deuterium](deuterium.md). These are chosen [mass number](mass-number.md) approximations, not measured [atomic masses](atomic-mass.md) or natural-abundance averages. With the same [chlorine](chlorine.md) [isotope](isotope.md) in both [molecules](molecule-split.md),

$$
\mu_{\rm HCl}=\frac{35}{36}u,\qquad
\mu_{\rm DCl}=\frac{70}{37}u,\qquad
\frac{\omega_{\rm DCl}}{\omega_{\rm HCl}}
=\frac{\widetilde\nu_{{\rm harm},\rm DCl}}{\widetilde\nu_{{\rm harm},\rm HCl}}
=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\rm HCl}}{\mu_{\rm DCl}}}
=\sqrt{\frac{37}{72}}\approx0.716860.
$$

This is the [isotope scaling of diatomic vibration](isotope-scaling-of-diatomic-vibration.md) prediction. Doubling the light partner's [mass](mass.md) nearly doubles the [reduced mass](reduced-mass.md), lowering the [frequency](frequency.md) by about $28.3\%$. The factor differs from $1/\sqrt2$ because the [chlorine](chlorine.md) partner also moves.

Right: free classical stretches released from the same small [displacement](displacement.md) and zero [velocity](velocity.md), $q_{\rm HCl}/A=\cos\tau$ and $q_{\rm DCl}/A=\cos(\sqrt{37/72}\,\tau)$, with $\tau=\omega_{\rm HCl}t$. This is not a measured time trace or an [infrared](infrared.md) spectrum.

For the same small initial stretch $A$ and zero [velocity](velocity.md), both models start with the same internal [mechanical energy](mechanical-energy.md) $kA^2/2$, but the DCl [oscillation period](period-of-an-oscillation.md) is longer by $\sqrt{72/37}\approx1.394972$. In the [centre of mass](center-of-mass.md) frame, $u_{\rm H}=-35q/36$ and $u_{\rm Cl}=q/36$ for HCl; $u_{\rm D}=-35q/37$ and $u_{\rm Cl}=2q/37$ for DCl. These ratios explain which partner moves and are separate from the change in [frequency](frequency.md).

The common-$k$ assumption belongs to the leading [Born-Oppenheimer approximation](born-oppenheimer-approximation.md): fixed nuclear positions and [electric charges](electric-charge-split.md) determine the electronic [potential energy](potential-energy.md), while nuclear [masses](mass.md) govern motion on that curve. Quantitative comparison requires actual [atomic masses](atomic-mass.md), departures from that approximation, and separation of [anharmonicity](anharmonicity.md) and [molecular rotation](molecular-rotation.md) from the measured band. Even with common $k$, the [quantum harmonic oscillator](quantum-harmonic-oscillator-split.md) predicts a different [zero-point energy](zero-point-energy.md) for each [isotope](isotope.md).[printed pp. 1 and 3, electronic motion; p. 12, Comprehension Question 2: common isotope potential and inferred spring constants](rotation-vibration-spectrum-of-the-hcl-molecule-university-of-florida-laboratory.md)
