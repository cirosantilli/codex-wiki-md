<h1 id="clairaut-s-equation">Clairaut's equation</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ordinary differential equation](ordinary-differential-equation.md)

For a twice differentiable solution, put $p=y'$. Differentiation gives $[x+f'(p)]p'=0$. One family consists of straight lines $y=Cx+f(C)$ with constant slope $C$. The other consists of the [envelope](envelope-mathematics.md) of those lines, given parametrically by $x=-f'(p)$ and $y=f(p)-pf'(p)$ where these equations define a differentiable graph with derivative $p$. An [envelope](envelope-mathematics.md) is a singular solution not obtained by fixing $C$. If only continuous first derivatives are required, a solution may also join a line to its [envelope](envelope-mathematics.md) at their point of tangency, provided the joined function satisfies the original equation. Such joins need not have a continuous second derivative.
