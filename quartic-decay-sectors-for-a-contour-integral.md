# Quartic decay sectors for a contour integral

↑ **Parent:** [Laplace integral](laplace-integral.md)

For a [contour integral](contour-integral.md) with factor $e^{\zeta^4/4+z\zeta}$, the quartic term dominates the linear term on rays at large radius. Writing $\zeta=re^{i\theta}$ gives [real part](real-part.md) $r^4\cos(4\theta)/4$ for the leading exponent. There are four decay sectors, centered at $\theta=\pi/4+j\pi/2$ and each of angular width $\pi/4$. On a closed subsector the integrand and all its $z$ [derivatives](derivative.md) decay faster than any exponential in $r$, locally uniformly in $z$. [Contours](complex-integration-contour.md) joining two such sectors therefore allow [differentiation under the integral sign](differentiation-under-the-integral-sign.md) and have vanishing infinite endpoint terms. Distinct geometric [contours](complex-integration-contour.md) still need an independence proof; their initial moments often give a [Vandermonde matrix](vandermonde-matrix.md) calculation.
