# Octet rule

↑ **Parent:** [Lewis structure](lewis-structure.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_rule)

The [octet rule](octet-rule.md) is a useful empirical guide: many [Lewis structures](lewis-structure.md) of [chemical compounds](chemical-compound.md) containing [carbon](carbon.md), [nitrogen](nitrogen.md), and [oxygen](oxygen.md) give each of those [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) eight [electrons](electron.md) when nonbonding [electrons](electron.md) and all incident bonding [electrons](electron.md) are counted. [Hydrogen](hydrogen.md) instead normally counts two [electrons](electron.md). These are local counts, not independent budgets: a shared [bond pair](bond-pair.md) counts at both endpoints but only once in the [molecule](molecule-split.md)'s [valence electron](valence-electron.md) total.

In [water](water.md), [oxygen](oxygen.md) counts $4+4=8$ [electrons](electron.md); in [ammonia](ammonia.md), [nitrogen](nitrogen.md) counts $2+6=8$; in $\mathrm{O=C=O}$, [carbon](carbon.md) counts $0+8=8$ and each [oxygen](oxygen.md) counts $4+4=8$. The charge-separated [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) drawing also satisfies the [octet rule](octet-rule.md), so the [octet rule](octet-rule.md) alone cannot select the dominant contributor. [Formal charge](formal-charge.md) provides another check.

The [octet rule](octet-rule.md) is a heuristic rather than a theorem about every [chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md). Odd [valence electron](valence-electron.md) totals cannot be partitioned entirely into [electron pairs](electron-pair.md), and some valid [Lewis structures](lewis-structure.md) have fewer or more than eight [electrons](electron.md) around a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md). See [Chemistry 2e, 7.3: exceptions to the octet rule](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-3-lewis-symbols-and-structures). The capacity of the $s$ and $p$ [electron subshells](electron-subshell.md), $2+6=8$, helps motivate the guide for these [chemical elements](chemical-element.md), but does not prove universal compliance. Lewis discussed a stable outer group of eight, together with a two-[electron](electron.md) arrangement for [hydrogen](hydrogen.md), in [The Atom and the Molecule (1916), pp. 768 and 774](https://zenodo.org/records/1429068).
