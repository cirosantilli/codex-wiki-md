# Fortissimo space

↑ **Parent:** [Topological space](topological-space.md)

Choose an uncountable [set](set-split.md) $X$ and a point $a\in X$. Every point other than $a$ is isolated, and a set containing $a$ is [open](open-set.md) precisely when its complement is countable. Equivalently, the [closed sets](closed-set.md) are all subsets containing $a$ together with all countable subsets. Arbitrary intersections and finite unions preserve this latter condition, so it defines a [topology](topology-split.md).

Every convergent [sequence](sequence.md) in this space is eventually equal to its limit. Indeed the set of its values different from its limit is countable and [closed](closed-set.md); its complement is an [open neighbourhood](open-neighbourhood.md) of the limit. Eventual membership in this complement forces eventual equality. Nevertheless $a$ is not isolated, because its singleton has uncountable complement. In particular [sequence convergence](convergence-of-a-sequence-in-a-topological-space.md) alone does not determine this topology.

A real-valued [function](function-split.md) on this space is [continuous](continuous-function.md) exactly when $\{x:f(x)\ne f(a)\}$ is countable. Sufficiency follows by considering the [preimages](preimage.md) of closed subsets of the [real line](real-line.md): each either contains $a$ or is a subset of that countable exceptional set. For necessity, each set $\{x:|f(x)-f(a)|\geq1/n\}$ is closed and excludes $a$, hence is countable; their countable union is the exceptional set.
