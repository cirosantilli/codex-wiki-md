# Radio interferometry

↑ **Parent:** [Aperture synthesis](aperture-synthesis.md)

Radio interferometry measures the [spatial coherence](spatial-optical-coherence.md) of received [radio waves](radio-wave.md). At two [antennas](antenna-radio.md), a receiver preserves the relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md), compensates the geometric delay for a chosen [phase centre](phase-centre.md), and averages a product of one signal with the [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) of the other. In a complex-signal description this correlation is $\langle E_1E_2^*\rangle$. Its real and imaginary parts carry two complementary components of the [complex visibility](complex-visibility.md); measuring only the received powers discards the relative [wave phase](phase-waves.md) needed for general imaging.

A finite frequency band makes delay compensation essential. If the residual delay is $\Delta\tau$, the residual [wave phase](phase-waves.md) varies by $2\pi\Delta\nu\Delta\tau$ across a band of width $\Delta\nu$. Averaging these differently oriented complex contributions reduces the correlation. For an ideal rectangular band the multiplicative factor is $\sin(\pi\Delta\nu\Delta\tau)/(\pi\Delta\nu\Delta\tau)$. A phase correction at just the central [frequency](frequency.md) cannot remove this variation across the band. Long time averages can likewise smear a changing signal.
