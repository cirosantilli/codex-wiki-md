# Reciprocal of a power series

↑ **Parent:** [Power series](power-series.md)

If $f(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nx^n$ has $a_0\ne0$, its reciprocal has a unique [formal power series](formal-power-series.md) $1/f(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}b_nx^n$. Equating coefficients in the [Cauchy product](cauchy-product.md) $f\cdot(1/f)=1$ gives the displayed [recurrence relation](recurrence-relation.md). In particular,

$$
\frac1{f(x)}=\frac1{a_0}-\frac{a_1}{a_0^2}x+\frac{a_1^2-a_0a_2}{a_0^3}x^2+O(x^3).
$$

For a convergent [power series](power-series.md), continuity and $f(0)\ne0$ give a neighborhood without zeros, where the reciprocal is analytic and this series converges. Its radius need not equal the original radius: zeros of $f$ can introduce closer reciprocal singularities.
