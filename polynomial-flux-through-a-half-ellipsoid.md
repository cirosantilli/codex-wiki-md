# Polynomial flux through a half-ellipsoid

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

For the upper curved half of $x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2+z^2/c^2=1$, take the [vector field](vector-field.md) $(xy^2,y^2,C)$ and outward [unit normal](unit-normal.md). Its [divergence](divergence.md) is $y^2+2y$. The odd term integrates to zero over the half-ellipsoid. Scaling a unit half-ball gives $\int y^2dV=2\pi ab^3c/15$. The equatorial closing disk has downward flux $-C\pi ab$. The [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) therefore gives the displayed curved-surface flux. The constant vertical component is not detected by the divergence, but is recovered by accounting for the closing disk.
