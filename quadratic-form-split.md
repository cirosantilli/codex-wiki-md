# Quadratic form

↑ **Parent:** [Linear algebra](linear-algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_form)

A [quadratic form](quadratic-form-split.md) on a [vector space](vector-space-split.md) over a [field](field.md) $F$ is a function $Q:V\to F$ satisfying $Q(av)=a^2Q(v)$ whose [polar bilinear form of a quadratic form](polar-bilinear-form-of-a-quadratic-form.md) $B_Q(v,w)=Q(v+w)-Q(v)-Q(w)$ is a [bilinear form](bilinear-form-split.md). In finite [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md), it is represented in [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) by a homogeneous degree-two [polynomial](polynomial-split.md), or by $Q(v)=v^TAv$ with a suitable [matrix](matrix.md) $A$.

When the [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) is not two, one may choose $A$ to be a [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) and recover it from $B_Q/2$. In [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) two, the symmetric-matrix restriction would lose examples: $Q(x,y)=xy$ has an upper-triangular representing [matrix](matrix.md) $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, while $v^TAv$ for a [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) has no $xy$ cross term. Its polar form is $B_Q((x,y),(u,v))=xv+yu$, an [alternating bilinear form](alternating-bilinear-form.md). The distinction matters when studying [quadratic forms](quadratic-form-split.md) over [finite fields](finite-field.md).[§§6–7: quadratic forms and characteristic two](bilinear-forms-by-keith-conrad.md)
