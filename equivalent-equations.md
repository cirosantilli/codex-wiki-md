# Equivalent equations

↑ **Parent:** [Equation](equation.md)

Two [equations](equation.md) are equivalent on a specified [domain of a function](domain-of-a-function.md) if they have the same [solution set](solution-set.md). Adding the same defined [mathematical expression](expression-mathematics.md) to both sides is reversible. Multiplying or dividing both sides by a [mathematical expression](expression-mathematics.md) is reversible only where that expression is nonzero and invertible in the arithmetic being used. In a [field](field.md), every nonzero scalar is invertible; a general [ring](ring.md) needs an additional check.

For example, $x^2=1$ and $(x-1)(x+1)=0$ are [equivalent equations](equivalent-equations.md) over the [real numbers](real-number.md), by the [distributive property](distributive-property.md). Concluding $x=1$ or $x=-1$ additionally uses the absence of [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md). The modular example in [algebra](algebra-split.md) shows why that extra inference can fail in a [ring](ring.md) with [zero divisors](zero-divisor.md).
