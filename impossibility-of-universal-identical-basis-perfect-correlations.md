# Impossibility of universal identical-basis perfect correlations

↑ **Parent:** [Entangled state](entangled-state.md)

For two systems of equal dimension $d\ge2$, no joint [pure state](pure-state.md) guarantees equal outcome labels whenever both systems undergo the same arbitrary [basic measurement](basic-measurement.md). Equal outcomes in the [computational basis](computational-basis.md) would force $|\Psi\rangle=\sum_jc_j|jj\rangle$. On any two coordinates j,k, changing both bases to the real sum/difference pair makes the different-outcome amplitude $(c_j-c_k)/2$, so all coefficients must agree. Changing both to the imaginary-phase pair makes a different-outcome amplitude $(c_j+c_k)/2$, so those coefficients must also be negatives. They must all vanish, contradicting [normalization](normalization.md). The one-dimensional case is an exception.

The requirement of all _complex_ bases matters. A maximally entangled [vector](vector.md) $d^{-1/2}\sum_j|jj\rangle$ does produce matching labels if one party uses a basis and the other its coordinatewise [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) basis. [Conjugate-state steering from a maximally entangled pair](conjugate-state-steering-from-a-maximally-entangled-pair.md) gives this conjugation explicitly. Identical real bases are a special case, not evidence for identical arbitrary complex bases.
