# Mathematical proof

↑ **Parent:** [Foundations of mathematics](foundations-of-mathematics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof)

A mathematical proof is a finite deductive argument from stated assumptions to a conclusion. It explains why the conclusion follows for every instance covered by its hypotheses. An informal [mathematical proof](mathematical-proof.md) can omit routine detail while making its assumptions and deductions clear; a [formal proof](formal-proof.md) expresses them in a specified formal language with specified inference rules. The distinction between assumptions, [propositions](proposition.md), and deductions is introduced in [Leighton and Rubinfeld, What is a proof?, sections 1–3](https://web.mit.edu/neboat/Public/6.042/proofs.pdf).

Checking examples can suggest a pattern, but need not establish a universally quantified claim. For example, the [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $n^2+n+41$ is a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) for every [integer](integer-split.md) $0\leq n\leq39$, as direct trial division verifies. At $n=40$ it equals $1681=41^2$, a [composite number](composite-number.md). Those first forty successes do not prove the claim for all [natural numbers](natural-number.md); one [counterexample](counterexample.md) disproves it. The point is not to discard computation: it can find a conjecture, detect a mistake, or verify a finite exhaustive case analysis. The question is whether the cases checked cover the statement being claimed.

For a positive example, the sum of two [even integers](even-number.md) is even: writing them as $2a$ and $2b$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ gives $2a+2b=2(a+b)$, and $a+b$ is an [integer](integer-split.md). This direct argument handles every allowed pair at once. A useful [mathematical proof](mathematical-proof.md) therefore does more than certify an answer: it exposes the mechanism and the exact assumptions that make the answer transferable.
