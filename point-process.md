# Point process

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)

A [point process](point-process.md) records random locations by a [random measure](random-measure.md) that counts them. Examples include arrival times of a [queue](queue-queueing-theory.md), positions of [stars](star.md), and the finite [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) configuration of a [random matrix](random-matrix.md). The count in an observation window is a [random variable](random-variable-split.md); its [expected value](expected-value-split.md) is supplied by the [intensity measure of a point process](intensity-measure-of-a-point-process.md), while its fluctuations and relationships with other windows require more information.

For a precise spatial convention, take $E=\mathbb R^d$ with its [Borel sigma-algebra](borel-sigma-algebra.md) and a [probability space](probability-space-split.md) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$. A locally finite [point process](point-process.md) $\Pi$ is a [measurable function](measurable-function.md) from $\Omega$ to the space $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(E)$ of locally finite point [measures](measure-split.md), equipped with the [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) generated by evaluations $\nu\mapsto\nu(A)$ for [Borel sets](borel-set.md) $A$. Each realization has the form

$$
\Pi_\omega=\sum_{j\in J(\omega)}\delta_{X_j(\omega)},\qquad
\Pi_\omega(A)=\sum_{j\in J(\omega)}\mathbf1_{\{X_j(\omega)\in A\}},
$$

where $J(\omega)$ is finite or [countable](countable-set.md) and repeated locations are allowed; $\delta_x$ is a [Dirac measure](dirac-measure.md). Here a point [measure](measure-split.md) is a [counting measure](counting-measure.md) with possible multiplicities, rather than the [counting measure](counting-measure.md) of a [set](set-split.md) of distinct locations.[Section 2.1, Definition 2.1, printed pp. 10-11: point measures and the sigma-algebra generated by evaluations](lectures-on-the-poisson-process-by-gunter-last-and-others.md)

Local finiteness means $\Pi_\omega(B)<\infty$ for every bounded [Borel set](borel-set.md) $B$, on a single [almost sure event](almost-sure-event.md). In $\mathbb R^d$ it suffices to check the [countable](countable-set.md) collection of balls of integer radius centered at the origin, and this is equivalent to finiteness on every [compact set](compact-space.md). On a general [measurable space](measurable-space.md) there is no notion of boundedness without additional structure: state a [metric](metric.md) or an explicit family of observation windows. Another convention on [locally compact spaces](locally-compact-space.md) requires finiteness on [compact sets](compact-space.md); this need not coincide with finiteness on all [bounded sets](bounded-set.md) outside $\mathbb R^d$. Local finiteness does not require finitely many points in all of $E$.[Section 2.4, Definitions 2.11 and 2.13, printed pp. 16-17: bounded-set local finiteness and the countable ball exhaustion](lectures-on-the-poisson-process-by-gunter-last-and-others.md)
