# Potential energy

↑ **Parent:** [Energy](energy-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_energy)

[Potential energy](potential-energy.md) assigns [energy](energy-split.md) to a system's arrangement, so a change of arrangement accounts for the [work](work.md) done by a [conservative force](conservative-force.md). For a time-independent [potential energy](potential-energy.md) $V(\mathbf r)$,

$$
\mathbf F=-\nabla V,
\qquad W_{\mathrm{conservative}}=V_i-V_f=-\Delta V.
$$

The [gradient](gradient.md) points toward increasing [potential energy](potential-energy.md); the [force](force-split.md) points toward decreasing [potential energy](potential-energy.md). Only differences matter: adding a constant to $V$ changes neither the [force](force-split.md) nor its [work](work.md). This endpoint rule follows by substituting $-\nabla V$ into the [line integral](line-integral.md) for [work](work.md). [OpenStax University Physics 1, §8.2: Conservative forces and potential energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/8-2-conservative-and-non-conservative-forces)

Near [Earth](earth.md)'s surface, stipulate constant [gravitational acceleration](gravitational-acceleration.md) $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and measure height $h$ upward from a chosen zero. Then [gravitational potential energy](gravitational-energy.md) is $V=mgh$. Raising a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ [mass](mass.md) by $0.5\,\mathrm m$ increases its [gravitational potential energy](gravitational-energy.md) by $9.81\,\mathrm J$, while [gravity](gravity.md) does $-9.81\,\mathrm J$ of [work](work.md). These are computed model values, not measurements of a particular lift. An ideal [spring](spring.md) obeying [Hooke's law](hooke-s-law.md) instead has $V=kx^2/2$, with [displacement](displacement.md) $x$ from its relaxed state and [spring constant](spring-constant.md) $k$: $k=100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $x=0.2\,\mathrm m$ give $V=2\,\mathrm J$ relative to $x=0$. [OpenStax University Physics 1, §8.1: Gravity and spring examples](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/8-1-potential-energy-of-a-system)
