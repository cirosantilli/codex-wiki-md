# Thomson charge-to-mass experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md)

The [Thomson charge-to-mass experiment](thomson-charge-to-mass-experiment.md) infers the magnitude of an [electron](electron.md)'s [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) by combining electric and magnetic deflection of [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md). The central idea is to determine [speed](speed.md) separately from magnetic curvature: a known [speed](speed.md) turns the observed [gyroradius](gyroradius.md) into a measurement of $e/m$.

In 1897, [J. J. Thomson](joseph-john-thomson.md) used two grounded slits in an evacuated glass tube, then [aluminium](aluminium.md) plates $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide and $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. A [battery](electric-battery.md) supplied their [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). Deflected [cathode rays](cathode-ray.md) produced a [phosphorescence](phosphorescence.md) patch on the glass; an external scale and movable luminous pointer recorded its position in darkness. Greater evacuation reduced gas [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) that screened the [electric field](electric-field-split.md); reversing plate polarity reversed deflection. External coils supplied the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md), calibrated by induction against larger coils. He adjusted equal electric and magnetic deflections. [Cathode Rays (1897), Fig. 2; second m/e method, apparatus and calibration](cathode-rays-by-joseph-john-thomson.md)

His comparison included air, [hydrogen](hydrogen.md), [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md), and [aluminium](aluminium.md) versus [platinum](platinum.md) [cathodes](cathode.md), supporting a common carrier. He explicitly noted uncorrected magnetic bending outside the plates. His tables give $m/e$, the reciprocal of the magnitude used here. The inferred ratio alone could not separate [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) from [mass](mass.md). [Cathode Rays (1897), second m/e method: table, corrections and interpretation](cathode-rays-by-joseph-john-thomson.md)

To connect this readout with the [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md), derive a simple nonrelativistic model in modern [SI](international-system-of-units.md) notation. Let the uniform deflecting region have [length](length.md) $\ell$, followed by a field-free drift of [length](length.md) $L$. Assume a narrow [speed](speed.md) distribution, small deflection [angles](angle.md), negligible collisions, and matching electric and magnetic field profiles. For an [electron](electron.md) with [speed](speed.md) $v$, the electric [acceleration](acceleration.md) has magnitude $eE/m$, the transit [time](time-in-physics.md) is approximately $\ell/v$, and the subsequent drift adds exit transverse [velocity](velocity.md) times $L/v$. Thus the displacement magnitudes measured separately with only [electric field](electric-field-split.md) or only [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) are

$$
|y_E|=\frac{eE}{mv^2}\ell\left(L+\frac{\ell}{2}\right),\qquad
|y_B|\simeq\frac{eB}{mv}\ell\left(L+\frac{\ell}{2}\right).
$$

The electric expression follows from constant transverse [acceleration](acceleration.md); the magnetic expression uses its small-deflection approximation. Equal displacement magnitudes then give

$$
v\simeq\frac{E}{B},\qquad
\frac{e}{m}\simeq\frac{E|y_E|}{B^2\ell(L+\ell/2)}.
$$

This is an ideal derivation of the comparison principle, not a numerical reanalysis of the historical tube. Different exterior [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) profiles invalidate cancellation of the common geometrical factor. The comparison determines $e/m$ through deflection and the known apparatus dimensions; it does not require a downstream semicircle.

For a second, deliberately different teaching geometry, combine a [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md) with a separate region containing only a uniform [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $B_a$ perpendicular to the selected [velocity](velocity.md). Here $B_s$ belongs to the [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md), while $B_a$ belongs to the analyzer: they need not be equal. The magnetic [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md) supplies the [centripetal acceleration](centripetal-acceleration.md), so

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r}=evB_a,\qquad
\frac{e}{m}=\frac{v}{B_a r}=\frac{E}{B_s B_a r}.
$$

If the analyzer turns the beam through a semicircle before it hits a screen in the entry plane, the separation $D$ between entrance and impact is the diameter: $r=D/2$. For a shorter observed arc, the screen displacement is not generally a [radius](radius.md); fit the appropriate [circle](circle.md) geometry. This two-stage [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md) and curvature apparatus is an original ideal teaching model, not a claim that Thomson's Fig. 2 used a separate selector and semicircular analyzer.

Inferring an electron's charge-to-mass ratio from selected speed and magnetic curvature**. Original ideal analyzer with zero [electric field](electric-field-split.md), [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) out of the page, and negative [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) bending upward. The semicircle has screen separation $D=2r$. All numerical inputs are stipulated teaching values, not historical measurements. The [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $B_a$ differs from the upstream [velocity selector](velocity-selector.md) [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) $B_s$.

For a worked example, stipulate $E=2.00\times10^3\,\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$, $B_s=1.00\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm T$, $B_a=2.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm T$, and $D=0.1136\,\mathrm m$. The selected [speed](speed.md) and inferred [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) magnitude are

$$
v=\frac{2.00\times10^3}{1.00\times10^{-3}}=2.00\times10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
r=0.0568\,\mathrm m,
$$



$$
\frac{e}{m}=\frac{2.00\times10^6}{(2.00\times10^{-4})(0.0568)}
\simeq1.76\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}.
$$

The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) units provide an independent check: $(\mathrm{V/m})/\mathrm T=\mathrm{m/s}$ and $(\mathrm{m/s})/(\mathrm{T\,m})=\mathrm{C/kg}$, because $\mathrm T=\mathrm{kg/(C\,s)}$. The [charge-to-mass ratio](charge-to-mass-ratio.md) of the [electron](electron.md) is negative; this calculation gives its magnitude. The bending direction gives the sign of the [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) once the [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) direction is known. Scaling both $e$ and $m$ by the same positive factor preserves every displayed trajectory, so an independent [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) measurement is required to determine [mass](mass.md) separately.

The curvature formula assumes nonrelativistic [momentum](momentum-split.md) $p=mv$. At higher [speeds](speed.md), the [relativistic cyclotron orbit](relativistic-cyclotron-orbit.md) gives $p=\gamma mv=eB_a r$, hence $e/m=\gamma v/(B_a r)$, where $\gamma$ is the [Lorentz factor](lorentz-factor.md); the ideal straight-through selection $v=E/B_s$ still follows from vanishing [Lorentz force](lorentz-force.md). The stipulated [speed](speed.md) is about $0.0067$ times the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md), making $\gamma-1$ about $2.2\times10^{-5}$. For this example the nonrelativistic approximation is adequate, but no massive particle can be selected at $E/B_s\geq c$. Finite slit widths, collisions with residual [gas](gas.md), uncertain [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) calibration, and fields outside the ideal boundaries limit a real inference; repeat at several selected [speeds](speed.md) and analyzer strengths to test consistency of $e/m$ rather than trusting one spot position.
