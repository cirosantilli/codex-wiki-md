# Triple point

↑ **Parent:** [Phase coexistence curve](phase-coexistence-curve.md)

A triple point is a [temperature](temperature-split.md)–[pressure](pressure-split.md) pair at which three distinct bulk [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md) of a one-component substance coexist in [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamic-equilibrium.md). In the familiar [solid](solid.md)–[liquid](liquid.md)–[gas](gas.md) case, their three [phase coexistence curves](phase-coexistence-curve.md) meet. Other combinations, such as two [crystal polymorphs](crystal-polymorph.md) and a [liquid](liquid.md), also qualify: “triple” counts [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md), rather than states-of-matter categories.

For the same substance, the [chemical potential](chemical-potential-split.md) in each [thermodynamic phase](thermodynamic-phase.md) must agree:

$$
\mu_s(T_t,p_t)=\mu_l(T_t,p_t)=\mu_g(T_t,p_t).
$$

There are two independent equality conditions for two controls, [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [pressure](pressure-split.md). Generically they select an isolated pair $(T_t,p_t)$; the third pairwise equality follows from the first two. Equivalently, the [phase rule](phase-rule.md) gives $F=1-3+2=0$. This counts independent intensive controls, not [phase fractions](phase-fraction.md): changing the amounts of the three [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md) need not change the ideal bulk [temperature](temperature-split.md) and [pressure](pressure-split.md), provided all three remain present. Different sets of [crystal polymorphs](crystal-polymorph.md) can give different triple points for the same substance.[Gibbs Phase Rule, three-phase count and pressure–temperature diagram discussion](gibbs-phase-rule-mit-10-213-course-material.md)

At a [liquid-gas critical point](liquid-gas-critical-point.md), the two fluid [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md) instead merge, and their [mass density](density.md) difference and [latent heat](latent-heat.md) vanish. It is the endpoint of the [liquid](liquid.md)–[gas](gas.md) [phase coexistence curve](phase-coexistence-curve.md), rather than a meeting of three distinct [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md). The regular independent-equality count used for a triple point cannot explain this merger. A triple point can retain finite [mass density](density.md) and [entropy](entropy-split.md) differences between all three [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md).[§5.1.2, printed p. 141: vanishing liquid–gas discontinuity at the critical point; Figure 41 separates it from the three-phase junction](statistical-physics-by-david-tong.md)
