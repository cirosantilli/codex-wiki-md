# Stationary middle mass in a three-mass spring chain

↑ **Parent:** [Normal mode](normal-mode.md)

Consider a straight chain with a fixed wall, spring constants $k_1,k_2,k_3>0$, and consecutive [point masses](point-mass.md) $m_1,m_2,m_3>0$, with the third mass freely ending the chain. Displacements are measured from equilibrium. If the middle mass stays at equilibrium, the two adjacent spring forces must cancel: $k_2q_1+k_3q_3=0$. The outer [equations of motion](equation-of-motion.md) then reduce to $m_1\ddot q_1=-(k_1+k_2)q_1$ and $m_3\ddot q_3=-k_3q_3$.

A nonzero oscillatory [normal mode](normal-mode.md) with $q_2=0$ therefore exists exactly when the two outer squared [angular frequencies](angular-frequency.md) agree, as displayed. Its [mode shape](mode-shape.md) is proportional to $(1,0,-k_2/k_3)$ and its frequency does not depend on $m_2$. Appropriate initial displacements and velocities confined to this shape keep the middle mass still. For $k_1=2k$, $k_2=k_3=k$, $m_1=3m$, $m_3=m$, the common frequency is $\sqrt{k/m}$ and the outer displacements are equal and opposite.
