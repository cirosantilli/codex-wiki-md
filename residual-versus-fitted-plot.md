# Residual-versus-fitted plot

↑ **Parent:** [Linear regression](linear-regression-split.md)

A [residual-versus-fitted plot](residual-versus-fitted-plot.md) displays residuals vertically against predicted responses horizontally. Curvature suggests an incorrect conditional-mean model; a fan-shaped spread suggests [heteroscedasticity](heteroscedastic.md), while isolated large residuals may indicate unusual observations. It does not alone measure leverage or influence: [regression leverage](regression-leverage.md) records unusual predictor combinations, and [Cook's distance](cook-s-distance.md) combines leverage and residual size.

The figure in [coefficient of determination](coefficient-of-determination.md) shows why the plot is useful: subtracting the fitted linear trend makes a small curved component easy to see. A visible pattern supplies a reason to investigate the model, while the absence of a pattern in a small sample does not establish that all assumptions hold. Other views address other questions: [regression residuals](regression-residual.md) against observation order can reveal temporal dependence, and a [normal probability plot](normal-q-q-plot.md) examines distributional shape. NIST's statistical handbook develops this use of graphical residual analysis for model validation. [NIST, How can I tell if a model fits my data?](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section4/pmd44.htm)
