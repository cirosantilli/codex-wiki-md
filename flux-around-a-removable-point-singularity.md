# Flux around a removable point singularity

↑ **Parent:** [Divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md)

A [continuous extension](continuous-extension.md) of a [vector field](vector-field.md) across one point need not be [differentiable](differentiable-function.md), so it does not automatically meet the classical hypotheses of the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md). Instead apply the [divergence theorem](divergence-theorem.md) after deleting a ball $B_\varepsilon$ around that point. If $\|\mathbf F\|\leq C\varepsilon^\alpha$ on its boundary in $d$ dimensions, the magnitude of the inner [flux](flux.md) is at most a constant times $\varepsilon^{d-1+\alpha}$. When $d-1+\alpha>0$ this tends to zero. If the [divergence](divergence.md) is also [integrable](integrability.md), passing to the limit recovers the outer [flux](flux.md) from the volume [integral](integral-split.md). For example, a planar [vector field](vector-field.md) with $\|\mathbf F\|\leq r$ has an inner [flux](flux.md) of order $\varepsilon^2$, even when its extension is not [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) at the origin.
