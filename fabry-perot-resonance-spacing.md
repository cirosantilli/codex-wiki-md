<h1 id="fabry-perot-resonance-spacing">Fabry–Pérot resonance spacing</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Fabry–Pérot interferometer](fabry-perot-interferometer.md)

At [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md), with no [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) and zero round-trip reflection [wave phase](phase-waves.md) modulo $2\pi$, the resonance condition in [Fabry–Pérot transmission from multiple reflections](fabry-perot-transmission-from-multiple-reflections.md) is

$$
2nd=m\lambda_m,\qquad
\nu_m=\frac{mc}{2nd},\qquad
\Delta\nu_{\rm FSR}=\nu_{m+1}-\nu_m=\frac{c}{2nd}.
$$

Here $\nu$ is [frequency](frequency.md) and $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md) in [vacuum](vacuum.md). A change of $\nu$ by one [free spectral range](free-spectral-range.md) advances the round-trip [wave phase](phase-waves.md) by $2\pi$. At fixed internal [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) $\theta$, the same nondispersive model gives $c/(2nd\cos\theta)$. Holding the external [angle of incidence](angle-of-incidence.md) fixed in a dispersive medium need not hold the internal $\theta$ fixed, because of [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md).

For stipulated $n=1$ and $d=10\,\mathrm{mm}$, the [free spectral range](free-spectral-range.md) is $14.9896229\,\mathrm{GHz}$ using $c=299792458\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. A [frequency](frequency.md) difference of $0.10\Delta\nu_{\rm FSR}$ is therefore $1.49896229\,\mathrm{GHz}$. Transmission repeats every [free spectral range](free-spectral-range.md): one isolated scan cannot distinguish that separation from $1.10\Delta\nu_{\rm FSR}$. Independent knowledge of the [optical spectrum](optical-spectrum.md), an additional filter, or a changed mirror separation is needed to assign orders.

Equal spacing in [frequency](frequency.md) does not imply equal spacing in [wavelength](wavelength.md). At [normal incidence](normal-incidence.md) the exact adjacent resonance [wavelengths](wavelength.md) obey

$$
\lambda_m-\lambda_{m+1}=\frac{2nd}{m(m+1)}
=\frac{\lambda_m}{m+1}
\simeq\frac{\lambda_m^2}{2nd}\quad(m\gg1).
$$

For example, choosing $2nd=10\lambda_{10}$ makes the exact gap to order 11 equal to $\lambda_{10}/11$, while the high-order estimate gives $\lambda_{10}/10$. The approximation is already ten percent larger at order 10. With [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) or frequency-dependent reflection [wave phase](phase-waves.md), obtain local spacings from the total [phase difference](phase-difference.md) instead: $\Delta\nu\simeq2\pi/(d\delta/d\nu)$ when its [derivative](derivative.md) varies little over one order.
