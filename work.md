# Work

↑ **Parent:** [Energy](energy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_(physics))

[Work](work.md) measures the [energy](energy.md) transferred by a [force](force.md) as its point of application moves. For a [point mass](point-mass.md) following a path $C$, add the contribution of the [force](force.md) along each small [displacement](displacement.md):

$$
W=\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r
=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\mathbf F(t)\cdot\mathbf v(t)\,dt.
$$

This [line integral](line-integral.md) uses the actual path, and the [dot product](dot-product.md) selects the component of [force](force.md) along the motion. A large [force](force.md) perpendicular to every small [displacement](displacement.md) does zero [work](work.md); holding something still also does zero mechanical [work](work.md) on it, even if holding it feels tiring. The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is $\mathrm J=\mathrm{N\,m}$. [OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.1: Work](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-1-work)

The usefulness of [work](work.md) is that it connects a description in terms of [forces](force.md) and distances to one in terms of [energy](energy.md). The [work-energy theorem](work-energy-theorem.md) gives the change in [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) from the total net [work](work.md), often without solving for the entire motion. The [work](work.md) of one selected [force](force.md) equals that change only when the other [forces](force.md) contribute zero total [work](work.md).
