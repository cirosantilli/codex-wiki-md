# Wigner-Eckart theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Reduced matrix element](reduced-matrix-element.md)

The [Wigner-Eckart theorem](wigner-eckart-theorem.md) separates the dependence of a [spherical tensor operator](spherical-tensor-operator.md) on [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) projections from its dynamical content. In one standard convention,

$$
\langle\lambda'j'm'|T_q^{(k)}|\lambda jm\rangle
=\frac{\langle jm;kq|j'm'\rangle}{\sqrt{2j'+1}}
\langle\lambda'j'\Vert T^{(k)}\Vert\lambda j\rangle.
$$

The [reduced matrix element](reduced-matrix-element.md) on the right is independent of $m,m',q$. The [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md) impose $m'=m+q$ and $|j-k|\leq j'\leq j+k$; additional [selection rules](selection-rule.md), including [parity](parity.md), can make the [reduced matrix element](reduced-matrix-element.md) vanish.

To see why the factorization holds, combine the rank-$k$ transformation law of the operator with the spin-$j$ transformation law of the initial state. This gives an [intertwining operator](intertwining-operator.md) from the [tensor product representation](tensor-product-of-group-representations.md) $j\otimes k$ to the final representation $j'$. The [Clebsch-Gordan decomposition](clebsch-gordan-decomposition.md) contains each allowed $j'$ once. Therefore the corresponding [intertwining operator](intertwining-operator.md) is unique up to an overall scalar, which is the [reduced matrix element](reduced-matrix-element.md) after the chosen normalization. Ratios within the same pair of multiplets depend only on [Clebsch-Gordan coefficients](clebsch-gordan-coefficients.md), whenever the common [reduced matrix element](reduced-matrix-element.md) is nonzero.
