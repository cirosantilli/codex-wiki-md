<h1 id="caratheodory-s-theorem-convex-hull">Carathéodory's theorem (convex hull)</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Convex hull](convex-hull.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carathéodory's_theorem_(convex_hull))

Every point in the [convex hull](convex-hull.md) of a [subset](subset.md) of $\mathbb R^d$ is a [convex combination](convex-combination.md) of at most $d+1$ points of that [subset](subset.md). Thus in the plane three ingredients always suffice, however many samples were supplied. The representation of $p=(2,1)$ in [convex hull](convex-hull.md) illustrates this bound. Two ingredients need not suffice: the [centroid](centroid.md) of a [triangle](triangle.md) with only its three [vertices](vertex-graph-theory.md) as input is on none of their three pairwise [line segments](line-segment.md).

A short proof outline removes redundant ingredients. With more than $d+1$ points, the augmented [vectors](vector.md) $(s_i,1)$ are [linearly dependent](linear-dependence.md). A dependence lets the weights vary while preserving both their sum and the represented point. Move along it until one positive weight first becomes zero; all weights stay nonnegative. Repeating leaves at most $d+1$ positive weights.

In his 1911 paper, [Constantin Carathéodory](constantin-caratheodory.md) used [convex geometry](convex-geometry.md) to investigate the possible [Fourier coefficients](fourier-coefficient.md) of positive [harmonic functions](harmonic-function.md). Its introduction interprets convex mixtures through the [center of mass](center-of-mass.md), connecting finite-dimensional geometry to constraints on a function's coefficients.[English translation by Nicholas J. Williams, Introduction §4, original printed p. 195; §I concerns the smallest containing convex domain](uber-den-variabilitatsbereich-der-fourier%E2%80%99schen-konstanten-von-positiven-harmonischen-funktionen-by-constantin-caratheodory.md)
