# Complex inverse cosine via the logarithm

↑ **Parent:** [Arccosine](arccosine.md)

Set $u=e^{iw}$ in $\cos w=z$. The resulting [quadratic equation](quadratic-equation.md) is $u^2-2zu+1=0$, with roots $z\pm i\sqrt{1-z^2}$. Their product is one, so taking a [complex logarithm](complex-logarithm.md) never encounters zero. Solving the exponential equation gives the displayed full inverse relation, with integer $n$. The [principal complex logarithm](principal-complex-logarithm.md) and [principal square root](principal-square-root-of-a-complex-number.md) do not make both signs identical to the principal [inverse cosine](arccosine.md): at $z=0$ they yield $\pm\pi/2$, while principal $\arccos0=\pi/2$. A single analytic branch requires compatible choices of sign and branches.
