# Cherenkov radiation

↑ **Parent:** [Electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherenkov_radiation)

[Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md) is [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md) from a [charged particle](charged-particle.md) exceeding the medium's [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md): $c/n(\omega)<v<c$. Here $n(\omega)$ is the positive [refractive index](refractive-index.md) at [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\omega$. The threshold uses [phase velocity](phase-velocity.md), not [group velocity](group-velocity.md).[§34.7.1, equation (34.41), Fig. 34.26: phase-speed threshold](passage-of-particles-through-matter-by-donald-e-groom-and-spencer-klein.md)

The beauty of [Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md) is that uniform motion can generate a sharply directed [electromagnetic wave](electromagnetic-wave-split.md): the medium's response along successive portions of the track adds with matching [wave phase](phase-waves.md) on a cone. The moving [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) drives the medium's [electric polarization](polarization-density.md); the resulting radiation depends on the collective [refractive index](refractive-index.md). This differs from [luminescence](luminescence.md), where excitation of material is followed by [light](light.md) emission. Both mechanisms can contribute to the same irradiated sample. A visible blue glow alone therefore identifies neither [Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md) nor a particular radioactive source.

The [Cherenkov cone angle](cherenkov-cone-angle.md) turns a measured direction into a constraint on particle [speed](speed.md) when the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) is known. The [Frank-Tamm formula](frank-tamm-formula.md) instead predicts the number of emitted [photons](photon.md) in a specified [wavelength](wavelength.md) interval. These are complementary observables: the [finite-band Cherenkov yield model](finite-band-cherenkov-yield-model.md) below shows why both depend on [optical dispersion](dispersion-optics.md) and on which [wavelengths](wavelength.md) are collected. This makes [Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md) useful for [particle detectors](particle-detector.md), with [photon](photon.md) production, transport and detection treated separately.
