# Fragment antigen-binding

↑ **Parent:** [Antibody](antibody.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragment_antigen-binding)

A [Fab fragment](fragment-antigen-binding.md) contains one [antibody binding site](paratope.md), allowing comparison with the two-site intact [IgG](immunoglobulin-g.md) in the [antibody surface plasmon resonance experiment](antibody-surface-plasmon-resonance-experiment.md).

A [Fab fragment](fragment-antigen-binding.md) combines one complete [immunoglobulin light chain](immunoglobulin-light-chain.md) with one [immunoglobulin heavy chain](immunoglobulin-heavy-chain.md) $V_H,C_{H1}$ portion, including [antibody constant regions](antibody-constant-region.md).[Immunobiology: The Immune System in Health and Disease, 5th edition by Charles A. Janeway, Jr. and others; §3-3: complete light chain paired with heavy-chain VH and CH1 in Fab](immunobiology-the-immune-system-in-health-and-disease-5th-edition-by-charles-a-janeway-jr-and-others/the-structure-of-a-typical-antibody-molecule.md)

The single [paratope](paratope.md) makes a [Fab fragment](fragment-antigen-binding.md) useful for separating [antibody affinity](antibody-affinity.md) from two-arm [avidity](avidity.md). If an intact [IgG](immunoglobulin-g.md) attaches strongly to a surface but its matched [Fab fragments](fragment-antigen-binding.md) detach more readily, loss of the second arm is a candidate explanation even if the individual [paratope](paratope.md) is unchanged. Comparing the formats asks a structural question; it does not by itself establish which binding mechanism dominates.
