# Roots from a completed square

↑ **Parent:** [Vertex form of a quadratic](vertex-form-of-a-quadratic.md)

Setting $a(x-h)^2+k=0$ gives $(x-h)^2=-k/a$. A positive $-k/a$ gives two distinct [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) in the [real numbers](real-number.md); zero gives a repeated [root of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) at $h$; a negative value gives a pair of [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md). For the downward-opening example, the two [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) are $2\pm\sqrt{3/2}$, approximately $0.775255$ and $3.224745$.

The [discriminant](discriminant.md) $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ packages the same calculation, because

$$
k=-\frac{\Delta}{4a},\qquad (x-h)^2=\frac{\Delta}{4a^2}.
$$

Since $4a^2>0$, the sign of $\Delta$ determines which of the three cases occurs. This derives the [quadratic formula](quadratic-formula.md) by a [polynomial identity](polynomial-identity.md), and explains its structure: first shift to $h$, then take a [square root](square-root.md). For the upward-opening example, $(x+3)^2=-4$ gives the [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) [roots of a polynomial](root-of-a-polynomial.md) $-3\pm2i$.
