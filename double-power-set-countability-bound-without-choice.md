# Double-power-set countability bound without choice

↑ **Parent:** [Cantor's theorem](cantor-s-theorem.md)

For each [natural number](natural-number.md) $n$, the family $[A]^n$ of all $n$-element subsets is nonempty when $A$ is not finite. These families are pairwise distinct elements of $\mathcal P(\mathcal P(A))$, giving an explicit [injection](injective-function.md) of the [natural numbers](natural-number.md). No representative from each family is selected. A [power set](power-set.md) cannot be countably infinite in ZF: if $\mathcal P(B)$ were countably infinite, singleton [injection](injective-function.md) would make $B$ an infinite subset of a [countable](countable-set.md) set and hence countably infinite; [Cantor's theorem](cantor-s-theorem.md) would then contradict countability of its [power set](power-set.md). Apply this to $B=\mathcal P(A)$ to make the displayed lower bound strict.
