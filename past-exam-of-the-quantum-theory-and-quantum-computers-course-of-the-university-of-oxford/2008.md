## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="1/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

Let $U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ and write $\psi_S(t)=U(t)\psi_0$ in the [Schrödinger picture](../quantum-system.md#schrodinger-picture), with [observables](../quantum-system.md#observable) $A_S$ constant unless they have an explicit time dependence. In the [Heisenberg picture](../quantum-system.md#heisenberg-picture) the state is $\psi_H=\psi_0$ and

$$
A_H(t)=U(t)^*A_SU(t),\qquad\boxed{\dot A_H=\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,A_H]+U^*(\partial_tA_S)U.}
$$

Differentiate the two [unitary](../vector-bundle.md#unitary-connection) factors to obtain the equation. [Unitarity](../vector-space.md#unitary-operator) gives $\langle\psi_S,A_S\psi_S\rangle=\langle\psi_0,A_H\psi_0\rangle$.

For the [interaction picture](../quantum-system.md#interaction-picture) choose a reference split $H=H_0+V$, put $U_0=e^{-iH_0t/\hbar}$, and define $\psi_I=U_0^*\psi_S$, $A_I=U_0^*A_SU_0$. Then $i\hbar\dot\psi_I=V_I\psi_I$, $V_I=U_0^*VU_0$, while $A_I$ evolves with $H_0$ (and its explicit time dependence). Again

$$
\boxed{\langle\psi_I,A_I\psi_I\rangle=\langle\psi_S,A_S\psi_S\rangle=\langle\psi_0,A_H\psi_0\rangle.}
$$

These are different [unitary](../vector-bundle.md#unitary-connection) allocations of the same dynamics between states and [observables](../quantum-system.md#observable), so measured [expectations](../expected-value.md) agree.

The [canonical commutation relation](../quantum-mechanics.md#canonical-commutation-relation) now gives

$$
\boxed{\dot X_t=P_t/m,\quad\dot P_t=-m\omega^2X_t-\mathcal E,\quad\dot A_t=-i\omega A_t-\mathcal E.}
$$

Multiplication of the last equation by $e^{i\omega t}$ and integration gives

$$
\boxed{A_t=e^{-i\omega t}A_0-\frac{i\mathcal E}{\omega}(e^{-i\omega t}-1).}
$$

Since $A_t^*-A_t=2im\omega X_t$ and $A_0\psi_0=0$, both initial [expectations](../expected-value.md) of $A_0,A_0^*$ vanish. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\langle X_t\rangle=-\frac{\mathcal E}{m\omega^2}(1-\cos\omega t).}
$$

**The printed positive displacement endpoint has the wrong sign** for the [Hamiltonian](../hamiltonian-mechanics.md#hamiltonian)'s positive $\mathcal EX$ term. The two turning values are $0$ and $-2\mathcal E/(m\omega^2)$; their ordering depends on the sign of $\mathcal E$. The result oscillates about the displaced equilibrium $-\mathcal E/(m\omega^2)$, exactly as the force $-\mathcal E$ predicts.

## 2

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="2/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

Choose an [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis) $H_0|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle$ and the [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves) convention $\langle0|\psi'\rangle=0$. Comparing coefficients of the [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) equation at first order yields

$$
(H_0-E_0)\psi'=-(H'-E')|0\rangle.
$$

Projecting onto $|0\rangle$ and the other [eigenvectors](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector) gives

$$
\boxed{E'=\langle0|H'|0\rangle,\qquad\psi'=\sum_{n\ne0}\frac{\langle n|H'|0\rangle}{E_0-E_n}|n\rangle.}
$$

Projection of the next-order equation onto $|0\rangle$ gives

$$
\boxed{E''=\langle0|H'|\psi'\rangle=\sum_{n\ne0}\frac{|\langle n|H'|0\rangle|^2}{E_0-E_n}.}
$$

These are coefficients of the printed series, so $E''$ denotes the quadratic coefficient rather than the second derivative itself. Without a [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves) convention an arbitrary purely imaginary multiple of $\psi_0$ can be added to the first vector correction. Continuous spectrum, if present, requires the corresponding [spectral integral](../hilbert-space.md#spectral-theorem); the oscillator has a discrete [complete](../topological-analysis.md#completeness) [eigenbasis](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenbasis).

Put $K=m\omega^2/2$ and let $g(x)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ denote the ground-state density. Then $H'=K(a^2-x^2)\mathbf1_{[-a,a]}$ and $p=\int_{-a}^ag(x)\,dx$. Because $g'=-xg/\sigma^2$, [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts) yields

$$
I_2:=\int_{-a}^ax^2g\,dx=\sigma^2p-2a\sigma^2g(a),\qquad
I_4:=\int_{-a}^ax^4g\,dx=3\sigma^4p-2a\sigma^2(a^2+3\sigma^2)g(a).
$$

Thus the two first-order shifts are

$$
\boxed{E'_0=K[(a^2-\sigma^2)p+2a\sigma^2g(a)],\qquad
E'_1=K[(a^2-3\sigma^2)p+6a\sigma^2g(a)].}
$$

The first is the supplied ground-state expression; the second follows because the excited-state density is $x^2g/\sigma^2$, so $E'_1=K(a^2I_2-I_4)/\sigma^2$.

Subtracting, and using $K\sigma^2=\hbar\omega/4$, gives

$$
\boxed{E'_1-E'_0=-\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}[p(a)-2ag(a)].}
$$

The bracket is zero at $a=0$, tends to one at infinity, and has derivative $2a^2g(a)/\sigma^2\geq0$. Therefore the first-order predicted level gap is

$$
\boxed{E_1^{\rm app}-E_0^{\rm app}=\hbar\omega\left[1-\frac\lambda2\{p(a)-2ag(a)\}\right].}
$$

For every $\lambda>2$ it becomes negative for all sufficiently large $a$, crossing when $p-2ag=2/\lambda$. It is still positive at $a=0$: the stated conclusion must be read with the large-$a$ regime, rather than for every interval width.

For $\lambda>1$ the inner potential curves downward, placing a central barrier between two symmetric minima at $\pm a$. The [even](../calculus.md#even-function) unperturbed [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) has its greatest density at the central barrier, while the [odd](../calculus.md#odd-function) first [excited state](../energy-eigenvalue.md#excited-state) has a node there and is less penalized. This explains the relative first-order shifts. **A negative predicted gap signals failure of that perturbation approximation**, not inversion of the exact lowest two states: the true one-dimensional [ground state](../quantum-mechanics.md#ground-state) remains [nodeless](../linear-operator-theory.md#nodeless-eigenfunction) and [even](../calculus.md#even-function), with the [odd](../calculus.md#odd-function) state above it. At a high barrier the actual low levels instead form a close double-well pair. The [apparent level crossing from a finite-interval oscillator perturbation](../quantum-mechanics.md#apparent-level-crossing-from-a-finite-interval-oscillator-perturbation) is useful precisely as a diagnostic of the approximation's range.

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="3/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

A [density operator](../density-matrix.md) is a positive self-adjoint [trace-class operator](../compact-operator.md#trace-class-operator) with $\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1}$. A finite [expectation](../expected-value.md) is $\boxed{\langle A\rangle_\rho=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A)}$. It represents a [pure state](../quantum-theory.md#pure-state) exactly when $\rho=|v\rangle\langle v|$ for a unit vector, equivalently $\rho^2=\rho$ or $\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2=1$.

For a [finite-dimensional](../vector-space.md#finite-dimensional-vector-space) [spectral decomposition](../linear-operator-theory.md#spectral-decomposition) $\rho=\sum_jp_j|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|$, introduce an auxiliary space with [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) vectors $f_j$ and use the enlarged space $\mathcal K=\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal E$. Define

$$
\Omega_\rho=\sum_j\sqrt{p_j}\,v_j\otimes f_j,\qquad\widetilde A=A\otimes I.
$$

Then $\|\Omega_\rho\|^2=\sum p_j=1$ and $\langle\Omega_\rho,\widetilde A\Omega_\rho\rangle=\sum p_j\langle v_j,Av_j\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A)$. This [purification](../density-matrix.md#purification-of-a-density-operator) shows that subsystem mixedness can arise from [entanglement](../bell-state.md#entangled-state) with an unobserved environment [even](../calculus.md#even-function) when the total state is pure. Subsystem measurements alone cannot distinguish these two descriptions.

A nonzero pure bipartite vector is entangled when it cannot be written as a [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product) of two vectors; equivalently its [Schmidt rank](../von-neumann-entropy.md#schmidt-rank) exceeds one. For mixed bipartite states, [entanglement](../bell-state.md#entangled-state) means absence of a convex decomposition into product [density operators](../density-matrix.md).

For the nonorthogonal expansion in the question, the unnormalized reduced operator is

$$
\boxed{S_1=\sum_{j,k}\langle\eta_k,\eta_j\rangle\,|\xi_j\rangle\langle\xi_k|.}
$$

Indeed $\langle\Psi,(A\otimes I)\Psi\rangle=\sum_{j,k}\langle\xi_k,A\xi_j\rangle\langle\eta_k,\eta_j\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(AS_1)$. Positivity follows because $\langle v,S_1v\rangle=\|\sum_j\langle v,\xi_j\rangle\eta_j\|^2\geq0$, and $\operatorname{Tr}S_1=\|\Psi\|^2=:Z$. Thus $\boxed{\rho_1=S_1/Z}$ is a [density operator](../density-matrix.md); if $\Psi$ is already [normalized](../mathematics.md#normalization), $Z=1$. The Gram factors cannot be dropped merely because the vectors are called bases. For unnormalized $\Psi$, physical [expectations](../expected-value.md) likewise divide its raw quadratic form by $Z$.

For the [Schmidt decomposition](../von-neumann-entropy.md#schmidt-decomposition) diagonalize $S_1$ in an [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) $\xi_j$ with its positive [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) $c_j^2$. Expand $\Psi=\sum_j\xi_j\otimes w_j$ with $w_j=(\langle\xi_j|\otimes I)\Psi$. The reduced [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) gives $\langle w_k,w_j\rangle=c_j^2\delta_{jk}$. Therefore $\eta_j=w_j/c_j$ are [orthonormal](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) and

$$
\boxed{\Psi=\sum_{j:c_j>0}c_j\xi_j\otimes\eta_j.}
$$

Zero-[eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) components vanish because their squared [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) are zero. This proves the decomposition for an arbitrary nonzero vector; the zero vector uses the empty sum. For a unit vector $\sum c_j^2=1$.

In the numerical example the coefficient [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) in the two product bases is $C=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\0&1&2\end{pmatrix}$. Taking the first-subsystem [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) yields

$$
\boxed{S_2=C^{\mathsf T}C=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&5&8\\3&8&13\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

The vector $(1,-2,1)^{\mathsf T}$ is in its [nullspace](../kernel-of-a-linear-map.md), so [rank](../quadratic-form.md#rank-one-quadratic-form) is at most two; its leading $2\times2$ minor has [determinant](../multilinear-algebra.md#determinant) one, so $\boxed{\operatorname{rank}S_2=2}$. This is the [rank](../quadratic-form.md#rank-one-quadratic-form) of $C$ and the [Schmidt rank](../von-neumann-entropy.md#schmidt-rank), proving [entanglement](../bell-state.md#entangled-state). **The displayed source ket has [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) squared $19$**, and the displayed reduced [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) has [trace](../linear-algebra.md#matrix-trace) $19$. The physical [density operator](../density-matrix.md) is $\boxed{\rho_2=S_2/19}$; its nonzero [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are $(19\pm\sqrt{337})/38$, since $CC^{\mathsf T}=\begin{pmatrix}14&8\\8&5\end{pmatrix}$. The raw [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) is correct for the unnormalized quadratic form actually printed.

↑ **Parent:** [2008](2008.md)

<h3 id="4/solution">Solution</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [4](#4)

Put $d=2^N$ and $\lambda=e^{2\pi i/d}$, with nonnegative integer $N$ so the register exists. The [geometric sum](../sequence-and-series.md#geometric-series) satisfies

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\lambda^{j(k-l)}=\begin{cases}d,&k=l\pmod d,\\0,&k\ne l\pmod d.\end{cases}
$$

Consequently the [matrix](../vector-space.md#matrix) $F_+$ with entries $d^{-1/2}\lambda^{jk}$ has $F_+^*F_+=I$. This proves [unitarity](../vector-space.md#unitary-operator) and

$$
\boxed{f(j)=d^{-1/2}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\lambda^{-jk}\widehat f(k).}
$$

For a binary output string $j=\sum_{r=1}^Nj_r2^{N-r}$, expansion of the printed [tensor product](../multilinear-algebra.md#tensor-product) gives coefficient

$$
d^{-1/2}\prod_{r=1}^N\lambda^{2^{N-r}kj_r}=d^{-1/2}\lambda^{kj}.
$$

Thus the printed product is exactly $F_+|k\rangle$. It is a correct [product decomposition of a Fourier phase state](../quantum-fourier-transform.md#product-decomposition-of-a-fourier-phase-state), but the printed recovery identity has inconsistent signs:

$$
\boxed{\sum_k\widehat f(k)F_+|k\rangle=\sum_jf(-j\bmod d)|j\rangle,}
$$

by the [square of the quantum Fourier transform](../quantum-fourier-transform.md#square-of-the-quantum-fourier-transform). To recover $f(j)$ instead, use $F_-=F_+^*$, replacing every product [phase](../wave.md#phase-waves) by its complex conjugate. Equivalently one may reverse the sign in the definition of $\widehat f$. The discrepancy is visible for $d\geq4$ by taking $f$ concentrated at $1$: the printed expression returns the [basis](../vector-space.md#basis) vector at $d-1$. The recovery display also uses $k$ as the summation variable on its right while its summand uses $j$; that dummy variable should be $j$.

For factorization first check $\gcd(3,91)=1$. Successive residues are

$$
3^0,3^1,\ldots,3^6\equiv1,3,9,27,81,61,1\pmod{91}.
$$

The first return is $\boxed{r=6}$. Its half power is $3^3=27$, which is neither $1$ nor $-1$ modulo $91$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\gcd(27-1,91)=13,\qquad\gcd(27+1,91)=7,\qquad91=7\cdot13.}
$$

In [Shor's algorithm](../quantum-theory.md#shor-s-algorithm) prepare a uniform exponent register of size $Q=2^n$, conveniently $91^2\leq Q<2\cdot91^2$, and reversibly compute $3^x\bmod91$ into a second register. Measuring that second register, or tracing it out, leaves periodic exponent correlations of period $r$. Conditional on one measured residue, the first register is a superposition over an arithmetic progression of exponents separated by $r$. The [quantum Fourier transform](../quantum-fourier-transform.md) converts this periodic structure into peaks near $k/Q=s/r$. [Continued fractions](../diophantine-approximation.md#continued-fraction) recover $s/r$; when $s$ is coprime to $r$, its denominator is $r$. Verify by [modular exponentiation](../number-theory.md#modular-exponentiation) and repeat when the reduced denominator is insufficient. For this example $Q=16384$ is suitable, and useful peaks include those near $Q/6$ and $5Q/6$. Either transform sign gives the same period information, so the source's inverse-sign error does not invalidate the order-finding mechanism.

The Part C inverse-transform display also prints $j$ as its summation index while the summand uses $k$; that sum must be over $k$, as in the corrected inverse above.
