# Stationary distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution)

A stationary distribution is a [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) $\pi$ preserved by a [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md)'s evolution. For a [Discrete-time Markov chain](discrete-time-markov-chain.md) in the row convention,

$$
\pi P=\pi,\qquad \pi_i\geq0,\qquad\sum_i\pi_i=1.
$$

Thus $\pi$ is a normalized nonnegative [left eigenvector](left-eigenvector.md) of the [transition matrix](stochastic-matrix.md) for [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) one. Starting with $X_0$ distributed as $\pi$ makes every $X_n$ have that same [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md). For a time-homogeneous [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md), the path [probabilities](probability.md) are also unchanged by shifting all observation times together: the process has [strict stationarity](strictly-stationary-process.md). This does not mean its individual paths are constant. See [Hao Wu's MIT lecture 2, stationary distributions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-445-introduction-to-stochastic-processes-spring-2015/2edaa8542469ce692226813a8bd1ec88_MIT18_445S15_lecture2.pdf).

For the sunny/rainy [transition matrix](stochastic-matrix.md), write $\pi=(a,1-a)$. The sunny component of $\pi P=\pi$ gives $a=0.8a+0.4(1-a)$, so $0.6a=0.4$ and

$$
\pi=\left(\frac23,\frac13\right),\qquad
\pi P=\left(\frac{8}{15}+\frac{2}{15},\frac{2}{15}+\frac{3}{15}\right)=\pi.
$$

Moreover,

$$
\pi_SP_{SR}=\frac23\frac15=\frac{2}{15}=\frac13\frac25=\pi_RP_{RS}.
$$

These equal stationary flows verify [detailed balance](detailed-balance.md) and show that the [Markov chain](markov-chain-split.md) is [reversible](reversible-markov-chain.md). The diagonal [detailed balance equations](detailed-balance.md) hold automatically. Only $1/3$ of the stationary days are rainy, but $0.6$ of the days following a rainy day are rainy: [stationarity](stationary-process.md) permits dependence.

A [finite Markov chain](finite-markov-chain.md) that is an [irreducible Markov chain](irreducible-markov-chain.md) has a unique [stationary distribution](stationary-distribution.md). If it is also an [aperiodic Markov chain](aperiodic-markov-chain.md), $\mu_0P^n\to\pi$ for every initial [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md). The sunny/rainy example satisfies both conditions: both directions are possible and each state has a positive [holding probability of a Markov chain](holding-probability-of-a-markov-chain.md). See [Hao Wu's MIT lecture 4, convergence theorem](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-445-introduction-to-stochastic-processes-spring-2015/5bd4774f613aa28bd111c36f3a3beeed_MIT18_445S15_lecture4.pdf). For an infinite [state space](state-space.md), the additional [positive recurrence](positive-recurrent-markov-chain.md) hypothesis in the [countable-state Markov chain convergence theorem](countable-state-markov-chain-convergence-theorem.md) matters.

Existence of a [stationary distribution](stationary-distribution.md) alone does not give this convergence. Consider the deterministic two-cycle

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\pi=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right).
$$

Here $\pi C=\pi$, but starting in the first state gives alternating [probability distributions](probability-distribution-split.md) $(1,0),(0,1),(1,0),\ldots$. Its [period of a state in a Markov chain](period-of-a-state-in-a-markov-chain.md) is two. The running fraction of visits to either state nevertheless tends to $1/2$, illustrating the [Markov chain ergodic theorem](markov-chain-ergodic-theorem.md). Starting with $\pi$ instead keeps the one-time [probability distributions](probability-distribution-split.md) stationary even though every path alternates.
