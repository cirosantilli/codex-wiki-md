# Endpoint obstruction to an algebraic inverse approximation theorem

↑ **Parent:** [Inverse theorem for trigonometric approximation](inverse-theorem-for-trigonometric-approximation.md)

The [first Jackson theorem for periodic approximation](first-jackson-theorem-for-periodic-approximation.md) applied to $|\sin\theta|$ gives algebraic best error $O(n^{-1})$ for $\sqrt{1-x^2}$. Its ordinary interval [modulus of continuity](modulus-of-continuity.md) at $1/n$ is at least $\sqrt{2/n-1/n^2}$. Substituting that error rate into the unmodified [inverse theorem for trigonometric approximation](inverse-theorem-for-trigonometric-approximation.md) would instead bound the modulus by $O(\log(n+1)/n)$, a contradiction. Algebraic inverse estimates must incorporate the endpoint compression of [cosine substitution for polynomial approximation](cosine-substitution-for-polynomial-approximation.md).
