<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The [compact phase sectors](../../../../../compact-phase-sectors.md) arise because the angle is compact, $\theta\equiv\theta+2\pi$. A circuit avoiding a defect core can have integer winding

$$
\boxed{q=\frac1{2\pi}\oint\nabla\theta\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb Z.}
$$

Smooth single-valued small fluctuations remain in the zero-winding sector. Vortices with $q\ne0$ cannot be unwound without a core or a boundary crossing; the smooth [Gaussian](../../../../../normal-distribution.md) theory misses those sectors. For $\theta=q\arg(x+iy)$ outside a core of [radius](../../../../../radius.md) $a$,

$$
\beta E_q=\frac{\bar K}{2}\int_a^L2\pi r\,dr\,\frac{q^2}{r^2}+\beta E_c
=\pi\bar Kq^2\log(L/a)+\beta E_c.
$$

The positional [entropy](../../../../../entropy-split.md) of a freely placed defect is approximately $S/k_B=2\log(L/a)$. For unit charge the corresponding heuristic free-energy cost is

$$
\beta F_1\simeq(\pi\bar K-2)\log(L/a)+\beta E_c.
$$

Actual bulk configurations obey net-charge constraints, with vortex-antivortex pairs attracted logarithmically. At low [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) they remain bound; they renormalize the [phase stiffness](../../../../../phase-stiffness.md) but do not screen away the algebraic [correlations](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md). Heating can unbind the pairs into a screening [vortex](../../../../../phase-vortex.md) plasma with short-range [correlations](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md): this is the [Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition](../../../../../berezinskii-kosterlitz-thouless-transition.md).

**[Figure 2](#2/c/image-compact-phase-with-opposite-winding). Compact phase with opposite winding**. Original constructed XY orientation field theta=arg(z+1)-arg(z-1), with unit positive and negative winding about the two excluded cores. Arrows show magnetization orientation, not fluid velocity; a circuit around both cores has zero net winding.

The sharp criterion uses the long-distance renormalized stiffness $K_R$, not a bare coefficient: $K_R(T_{\rm BKT}^-)=2/\pi$, giving algebraic exponent $\eta_c=1/4$. Both [phases](../../../../../phase-waves.md) have zero conventional spontaneous [magnetization](../../../../../magnetization.md) at positive [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md); they differ in [vortex](../../../../../phase-vortex.md) binding, [correlations](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) and stiffness.[§4.4 and §4.4.1: compact angle, vortex energy/entropy and the two-dimensional transition](../../../../../statistical-field-theory-by-david-tong.md)
