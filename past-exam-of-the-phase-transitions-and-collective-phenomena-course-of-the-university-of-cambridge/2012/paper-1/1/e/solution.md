<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The susceptibility is the integrated [connected correlation function](../../../../../connected-correlation-function.md), up to a nonsingular field-normalization factor:

$$
\chi\propto\int d^dx\,G_c(\mathbf x),\qquad
G_c(\mathbf x)=\langle m(\mathbf x)m(0)\rangle-\langle m\rangle^2.
$$

Above the transition at zero field the subtraction vanishes. In an ordered [thermodynamic phase](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md) it is essential; integrating the disconnected constant would incorrectly give a [volume](../../../../../volume.md) divergence unrelated to the desired response.

With a microscopic cutoff $a$ and $r=|\mathbf x|$, radial integration and $u=r/\xi$ give

$$
\chi_s\propto S_d\int_a^\infty r^{1-\eta}g(r/\xi)\,dr
=S_d\xi^{2-\eta}\int_{a/\xi}^\infty u^{1-\eta}g(u)\,du.
$$

If $g(0)$ is finite, its large-distance tail decays sufficiently rapidly and $\eta<2$, the dimensionless integral approaches a finite nonzero constant. Thus

$$
\boxed{\chi_s\sim\xi^{2-\eta}\sim s^{-(2-\eta)\nu},\qquad\gamma=(2-\eta)\nu.}
$$

Short-distance contributions give a regular background. This is the [Fisher scaling relation](../../../../../fisher-scaling-relation.md); it concerns the fluctuating connected part of the source [correlation](../../../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) ansatz.
