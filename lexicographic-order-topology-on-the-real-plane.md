# Lexicographic order topology on the real plane

↑ **Parent:** [Order topology](order-topology.md)

Give $\mathbb R^2$ the [lexicographic order](lexicographic-order.md) and the [order topology](order-topology.md). Every point $(a,b)$ has an interval neighbourhood between $(a,b-\varepsilon)$ and $(a,b+\varepsilon)$, lying entirely in the vertical line $\{a\}\times\mathbb R$. These intervals give each such line its usual real [topology](topology-split.md), and every line is open. Thus this space is a disjoint union of uncountably many open copies of the real line and is not a [second-countable space](second-countable-space.md): any [basis of a topology](basis-of-a-topology.md) must supply a different member inside each of these disjoint nonempty open sets.
