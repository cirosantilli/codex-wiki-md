# Relative volatility

↑ **Parent:** [Distillation](distillation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_volatility)

For a two-component [mixture](mixture-split.md), let $x$ be component A's [mole fraction](mole-fraction.md) in the [liquid](liquid.md) and $y$ its [mole fraction](mole-fraction.md) in the coexisting [vapor](vapor.md). At interior compositions, define the relative volatility of A with respect to B by the ratio of composition odds:

$$
\alpha=\frac{y/(1-y)}{x/(1-x)}.
$$

Thus $\alpha>1$ means A is enriched in the [vapor](vapor.md), while $\alpha=1$ means $y=x$. Solving this definition gives

$$
\alpha x(1-y)=y(1-x),\qquad
 y=\frac{\alpha x}{1+(\alpha-1)x}.
$$

Here a constant [relative volatility](relative-volatility.md) is a stipulated model of [vapor-liquid equilibrium](vapor-liquid-equilibrium.md), not a measured property of a named [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md). Real [relative volatility](relative-volatility.md) can vary with composition, [temperature](temperature-split.md), and [pressure](pressure-split.md). The relation specifies no absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md) scale: even fixing $\alpha=2$ does not determine a [boiling](boiling.md)-[temperature](temperature-split.md) curve or a [heat](heat-split.md) requirement.
