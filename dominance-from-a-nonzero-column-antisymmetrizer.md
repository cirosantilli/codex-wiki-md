# Dominance from a nonzero column antisymmetrizer

↑ **Parent:** [Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau](column-antisymmetrizer-of-a-young-tableau.md)

Let $t$ be a [Young tableau](young-tableau.md) of shape $\lambda$, and let $u$ be a [Young tableau](young-tableau.md) of shape $\mu$, both on $n$ entries. If the [Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau](column-antisymmetrizer-of-a-young-tableau.md) $b_t$ satisfies $b_t\{u\}\ne0$ over any [field](field.md), then $\lambda$ dominates $\mu$ in the [dominance order on partitions](dominance-order-on-partitions.md).

If two entries from one column of $t$ lie in the same row of $u$, their transposition fixes the [tabloid](tabloid.md) $\{u\}$. Pairing terms of $b_t$ by this transposition gives $b_t\{u\}=0$, including in [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md) two. Otherwise the first $r$ rows of $u$ contain at most $\min(r,\lambda'_j)$ entries from column $j$ of $t$. Thus

$$
\sum_{i=1}^r\mu_i\leq\sum_j\min(r,\lambda'_j)=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i
$$

for every $r$, proving dominance.
