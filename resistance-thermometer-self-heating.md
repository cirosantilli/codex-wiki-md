# Resistance thermometer self-heating

↑ **Parent:** [Resistance thermometer](resistance-thermometer.md)

Measuring [electric current](electric-current.md) creates [Joule heating](joule-heating.md), raising the sensing [temperature](temperature-split.md). At fixed bath [temperature](temperature-split.md), compare settled readings at $I$ and $\sqrt2I$ and extrapolate the approximately linear $I^2$ dependence toward zero current.[§4.1, printed pp. 24–26, equation (4.1.1): self-heating, two-current extrapolation, and medium dependence](guide-on-secondary-thermometry-industrial-platinum-resistance-thermometers-by-jonathan-pearce-and-others.md)

Under the local model $R(I)=R_{\rm zero}+kI^2$,

$$
R_{\rm zero}=2R(I)-R(\sqrt2I).
$$

For synthetic readings $109.629\,\Omega$ at $1\,\mathrm{mA}$ and $109.633\,\Omega$ at $\sqrt2\,\mathrm{mA}$, the extrapolated value is $109.625\,\Omega$. The lower-current excess $0.004\,\Omega$ corresponds to $0.01039\ldots\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in the [Pt100 local linear model](pt100-local-linear-model.md). At $109.625\,\Omega$, $1\,\mathrm{mA}$ dissipates $109.625\,\mathrm{\mu W}$; power alone does not specify a [temperature](temperature-split.md) rise without a thermal coupling model. These stipulated values are not measurements.

Repeat the comparison after settling and test the $I^2$ approximation if necessary. Changing from liquid to air changes [heat transfer](heat-transfer.md); a self-heating correction need not transfer unchanged. The [saturation-pressure equilibrium measurement](saturation-pressure-equilibrium-measurement.md) provides an existing example of controlling this disturbance in resistance thermometry.
