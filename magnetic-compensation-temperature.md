# Magnetic compensation temperature

↑ **Parent:** [Ferrimagnetism](ferrimagnetism.md)

At a [magnetic compensation temperature](magnetic-compensation-temperature.md), $M_A=M_B\ne0$: an ordered [ferrimagnet](ferrimagnetism.md) has zero net [magnetization](magnetization.md). Such cancellation need not occur in every material.[§8, Fig.7; §§11–12, pp.335–338: cancellation within an ordered phase](magnetism-and-the-local-molecular-field-by-louis-neel.md)

For an original stipulated example, let $t=T/T_C$, with $0\le t\le1$, and prescribe sublattice magnitudes per common [volume](volume.md) by

$$
M_A(t)=M_0(1-t),\qquad M_B(t)=\frac32M_0(1-t)^2,\qquad M_0>0.
$$

Take A along $+z$ and B along $-z$, retaining a fixed sublattice orientation convention. These are toy functions, not measured curves or a self-consistent [mean-field approximation](mean-field-approximation.md). Their difference factors exactly:

$$
M_z(t)=M_A-M_B=\frac{M_0}{2}(1-t)(3t-1).
$$

At $t=0$, $M_z=-M_0/2$. At $t=1/3$, both magnitudes equal $2M_0/3$ and $M_z=0$: this is compensation with finite order. At $t=1/2$, $M_z=M_0/8>0$. At $t=1$, both magnitudes vanish: that endpoint represents the stipulated [Curie temperature](curie-temperature.md), not another compensation point. For $t>1$ stipulate both ordered magnitudes zero; do not extrapolate the polynomials into negative sublattice magnitudes.

The sign change refers to the net component relative to the fixed A direction. A freely reorienting specimen in an applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) need not display this signed branch directly. The example isolates the cancellation mechanism; it predicts neither a material's transition [temperature](temperature-split.md) nor its response to [magnetic domains](magnetic-domain.md) or an applied [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md).
