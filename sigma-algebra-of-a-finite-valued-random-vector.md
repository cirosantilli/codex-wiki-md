# Sigma-algebra of a finite-valued random vector

↑ **Parent:** [Sigma-algebra generated by a random variable](sigma-algebra-generated-by-a-random-variable.md)

Let a [random vector](random-vector.md) $V$ have a finite actual range of $q$ values. Its nonempty fibres partition the [sample space](sample-space.md), and every [event](event.md) in $\sigma(V)$ is a [union](set-union.md) of those fibres. Thus each fibre is an [atom of a sigma-algebra](atom-of-a-sigma-algebra.md) and $\sigma(V)$ has exactly $2^q$ elements. This assertion concerns the actual range; additional values attained only on [null sets](null-set.md) must still be counted when working with an uncompleted [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) literally.

To verify the claim, the fibres belong to $\sigma(V)$ as preimages of singleton [Borel sets](borel-set.md). Their [unions](set-union.md) form a finite [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) making $V$ [measurable](measurability.md), so minimality of the generated [sigma-algebra](sigma-algebra.md) gives the reverse inclusion. Distinct subcollections have distinct [unions](set-union.md) because the fibres are nonempty and disjoint.

Consequently a finite history of finite-valued observations generates a finite [natural filtration](natural-filtration.md) at each fixed time. It may be strictly smaller than the [filtration](filtration-probability-theory.md) of its driving continuous observations: a single [uniform random variable](uniform-random-variable.md) already generates infinitely many distinct [events](event.md) $\{U\leq t\}$ with different [probabilities](probability.md).
