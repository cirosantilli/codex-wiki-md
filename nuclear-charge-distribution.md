# Nuclear charge distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md)

A spatial [charge density](charge-density.md) describing the [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) inside an [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md). In a static description centered on the [nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), normalize it by

$$
\int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf r)\,d^3r=Ze,
$$

where $Z$ is the [atomic number](atomic-number.md) and $e>0$ the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). A [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md) is an average over a nuclear [quantum state](quantum-state.md), rather than a photograph of immobile [protons](proton.md). Even when the average has [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md), individual [nucleons](nucleon.md) need not sit on spherical shells. The [proton charge radius](proton-charge-radius.md) and the internal [charge density](charge-density.md) of the [neutron](neutron-split.md) also contribute: a distribution of pointlike proton centers is an approximation to a [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md), not its definition.

The experimental attraction is that an [electron](electron.md) probes [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) through the [electromagnetic interaction](electromagnetic-interaction.md), without itself feeling the [nuclear force](nuclear-force-split.md). Varying its [momentum transfer](momentum-transfer.md) changes the spatial scale tested. Long wavelengths mainly constrain a [nuclear charge radius](nuclear-charge-radius.md); shorter wavelengths can distinguish a diffuse edge from a sharper one through [diffraction](diffraction.md). Recovering the entire [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md) from a finite range of noisy scattering data remains an [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md).
