# Isotropic harmonic oscillator

↑ **Parent:** [Simple harmonic motion](simple-harmonic-motion.md)

The classical [isotropic harmonic oscillator](isotropic-harmonic-oscillator.md) has the same [angular frequency](angular-frequency.md) $\Omega>0$ in every Cartesian direction. Its [equation of motion](equation-of-motion.md) is $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-\Omega^2\mathbf r$, with solution $\mathbf r=\mathbf A\cos\Omega t+\mathbf B\sin\Omega t$. The trajectory lies in the span of $\mathbf A,\mathbf B$ and is an [ellipse](ellipse.md) centred on the force centre, degenerating to a line for dependent vectors. For nonzero [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md), $r^2$ oscillates at $2\Omega$ unless the trajectory is exactly circular. This distinguishes radial and Cartesian [frequencies](frequency.md).
