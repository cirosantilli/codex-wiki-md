# RLC circuit

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RLC_circuit)

An [RLC circuit](rlc-circuit.md) combines [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) $R$, [inductance](inductance.md) $L$, and [capacitance](capacitance.md) $C$. The [capacitor](capacitor.md) stores [energy](energy.md) in its [electric field](electric-field.md); the [inductor](inductor.md) stores [energy](energy.md) in its [magnetic field](magnetic-field.md). Their exchange can produce an [oscillation](oscillation.md), while [Joule heating](joule-heating.md) removes [energy](energy.md). A [series RLC circuit](series-rlc-circuit.md) gives a particularly clear example: changing $R$ turns ringing into a direct return to equilibrium, and changing $L$ or $C$ tunes a [resonance](resonance.md).

These circuits connect two useful viewpoints. In time, they describe how stored [energy](energy.md) is released after switching. In [frequency](frequency.md), they select a range of signals: the [resonant series RLC response](resonant-series-rlc-response.md) explains radio tuning and simple filters. The same mathematics describes a [damped harmonic oscillator](damped-harmonic-oscillator.md), so insight about mechanical ringing transfers directly to an [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md). [Feynman lectures, I.23.3: electrical and mechanical resonance](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_23.html#Ch23-S3); [Feynman lectures, II.22.7: frequency filters](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_22.html#Ch22-S7).
