**[Auditory verbal hallucinations](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) are associated with psychotic disorders but are neither sufficient for [schizophrenia](../../../schizophrenia.md) nor confined to people with a [mental disorder](../../../mental-disorder.md). Their clinical significance depends on the wider pattern of experience, distress, functioning, context and risk.** The distinction between an association and a diagnostic implication is essential: a high [probability](../../../probability.md) of [voice hearing](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) among people with a diagnosis does not establish the same [probability](../../../probability.md) of that diagnosis among all people who hear voices.

An [auditory verbal hallucination](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) is an experience of a voice without an ordinary corresponding external speaker. That phenomenological description does not specify its cause, the person's interpretation or a [need for care](../../../need-for-care.md). [Psychosis](../../../psychosis.md) is a broader clinical description involving disturbances such as hallucinations, delusional conviction or disorganization; [schizophrenia](../../../schizophrenia.md) requires a pattern assessed over time and in context. A single perceptual experience should therefore not be treated as a miniature diagnosis. Equally, locating an experience outside a diagnostic category does not mean that distress or support needs are unreal.

Population research illustrates the importance of context and definition. [Maurice M. Ohayon](../../../maurice-m-ohayon.md)'s surveys of 13,057 people in three European countries distinguished experiences around falling asleep or waking from daytime hallucinations. [Sleep](../../../sleep.md)-transition experiences were often not associated with a specific pathology, whereas daytime auditory and visual hallucinations showed stronger associations with psychiatric conditions. Thus a broad prevalence of unusual perception cannot be quoted as the prevalence of persistent daytime [voice hearing](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md), and the existence of nonpathological experiences cannot justify ignoring the risk associations of other presentations.[2000; representative telephone surveys, timing and pathological associations](../../../prevalence-of-hallucinations-and-their-pathological-associations-in-the-general-population-by-maurice-m-ohayon.md)

More directly, [Kirstin Daalman](../../../kirstin-daalman.md) and colleagues compared 118 clinical and 111 otherwise healthy people who reported recurrent [auditory verbal hallucinations](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md). Perceived location, number, loudness and personification did not reliably separate the groups, whereas [emotional valence](../../../emotional-valence.md) of the content, frequency and perceived control differed more strongly. The comparison is important because nonclinical participants had recurrent experiences, not only a one-off event around [sleep](../../../sleep.md). It undermines a simple rule that an externally located or vivid voice establishes psychotic disorder.[2011; recurrent voice-hearer groups and phenomenological dimensions](../../../the-same-or-different-a-phenomenological-comparison-of-auditory-verbal-hallucinations-in-healthy-and-psychotic-individuals-by-kirstin-daalman-and-others.md)

The findings do not mean that the groups are identical. Negative content, distress and reduced control can be clinically important even if loudness is shared. Selection also matters: volunteers willing to identify as healthy voice-hearers may have unusual [coping](../../../coping.md) resources or interpretations. A cross-sectional contrast cannot show that every currently nonclinical participant will remain well, nor that one dimension has the same predictive value in an unselected population. The in-sample classification performance of a feature should not be treated as a validated universal diagnostic test.

[Emmanuelle Peters](../../../emmanuelle-peters.md) and colleagues compared people with persistent [psychotic experiences](../../../psychotic-experience.md) without a [need for care](../../../need-for-care.md), clinical participants and controls. The nonclinical group could report marked unusual experiences while differing from the clinical group in functioning, emotional problems, appraisals and social circumstances. Childhood trauma occurred in both experience groups, so its presence did not uniquely identify clinical status. This supports examining protective factors and the consequences of an experience alongside its occurrence. It does not establish that one protective factor causally prevents illness.[2016; clinical, nonclinical persistent-experience and control-group characterization](../../../clinical-socio-demographic-and-psychological-characteristics-in-individuals-with-persistent-psychotic-experiences-with-and-without-a-need-for-care-by-emmanuelle-peters-and-others.md)

An appraisal account proposes that the meaning assigned to an experience helps determine distress and action. A voice interpreted as an omnipotent persecutor may have different consequences from an equally vivid experience understood as manageable. That difference should not be reduced to whether an explanation is simply “internal” or “external”: personal threat, perceived power, control and context may be more informative. Nor should culturally meaningful explanations automatically be classed as [delusions](../../../delusion.md) merely because they differ from a clinician's expectations.

[Emmanuelle Peters](../../../emmanuelle-peters.md) and colleagues extended the comparison with experimental anomalous-experience tasks. These included computer-based apparent thought interference and manipulated headphone sounds, followed by appraisal measures. Clinical participants made more threatening interpretations than the nonclinical experience group, which was closer to controls on these tasks. Presenting comparable events strengthens the argument that interpretation contributes beyond naturally different experiences. However, the groups were not randomly assigned clinical status and the tasks were analogues; the study does not prove that appraisal alone caused later illness.[2017; three induced-experience tasks, interviews and appraisal comparisons](../../../clinical-relevance-of-appraisals-of-persistent-psychotic-experiences-in-people-with-and-without-a-need-for-care-an-experimental-study-by-emmanuelle-peters-and-others.md)

Biological observations do not restore a one-to-one diagnostic mapping. [Kelly M. J. Diederen](../../../kelly-m-j-diederen.md) and colleagues used [symptom](../../../symptom.md)-capture [functional magnetic resonance imaging](../../../functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging.md) while clinical and nonclinical participants indicated voice episodes by squeezing and releasing a balloon. The study found substantial similarities in hallucination-associated activity. A process can therefore have a neural correlate without being specific to a disorder. The result also does not prove identical [neural codes](../../../neural-code.md) or show that every causal pathway to [voice hearing](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) is the same; imaging resolution, sample size and comparison conditions constrain the conclusion.[2012; symptom-capture imaging, balloon-squeeze/release episode indication and group comparison](../../../auditory-hallucinations-elicit-similar-brain-activation-in-psychotic-and-nonpsychotic-individuals-by-kelly-m-j-diederen-and-others.md)

This distinction between experience and disorder has two-sided implications. Defining all [voice hearing](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) as disease risks pathologizing people who function well and do not want clinical treatment, while defining it as always harmless could obscure severe distress or other problems. [Need for care](../../../need-for-care.md) should be evaluated through the person's difficulties, circumstances and preferences, with appropriate [attention](../../../attention.md) to risk and other causes. Actual service contact is an imperfect measure because access, [discrimination](../../../discrimination.md) and willingness to seek help affect who receives a diagnosis.

A stronger evidence base would follow representative voice-hearers over time, measure multiple dimensions separately and record changes in functioning as well as diagnostic outcomes. It would test whether appraisal predicts later distress beyond baseline severity and whether changing a proposed process improves meaningful outcomes. Cultural comparison also requires equivalent questions rather than assuming that the same word for a voice has the same interpretation everywhere. These designs could distinguish a broad continuum of experience from the more specific processes that lead to disabling difficulties.

**[Voice hearing](../../../auditory-verbal-hallucination.md) is transdiagnostic and can be nonclinical; its association with [schizophrenia](../../../schizophrenia.md) is a conditional relationship, not an identity.** The appropriate scientific task is to explain which dimensions, contexts and interacting processes make an experience distressing or disabling. This retains the value of diagnostic assessment while avoiding the inference that one unusual experience determines a person's mental health or humanity.
