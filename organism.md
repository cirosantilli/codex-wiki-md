# Organism

↑ **Parent:** [Biology](biology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism)

An [organism](organism.md) is an individual living system, made of one [biological cell](biological-cell.md) or an integrated assembly of [biological cells](biological-cell.md). An [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) cell is an entire [organism](organism.md); a [neuron](neuron.md) is normally a specialized part of an [animal](animal.md). [Metabolism](metabolism.md), regulated exchange through [cell membranes](cell-membrane.md), and inherited information connect these very different scales. See [OpenStax Biology 2e, 1.2: living systems and levels of organization](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology).

Keep two questions separate: how many [biological cells](biological-cell.md) make the [organism](organism.md), and how those [biological cells](biological-cell.md) are organized. A [yeast](yeast.md) and an [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) are both [unicellular organisms](unicellular-organism.md), but the [yeast](yeast.md) is a [eukaryote](eukaryote.md) and the [Escherichia coli](escherichia-coli.md) a [prokaryote](prokaryote.md). A [plant](plant.md) and an [animal](animal.md) are [multicellular organisms](multicellular-organism.md) with [eukaryotic](eukaryote.md) cellular organization. Neither distinction ranks [organisms](organism.md) as better or worse: [natural selection](natural-selection.md) acts on reproduction in particular environments.
