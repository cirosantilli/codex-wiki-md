# Kinetic friction

↑ **Parent:** [Friction](friction.md)

[Kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) is [friction](friction.md) during nonzero [contact slip](contact-slip.md). For one-dimensional motion along a surface, choose right as positive and write $v$ for the body's contact-point [velocity](velocity.md) and $U$ for the surface's contact-point [velocity](velocity.md), both measured in the same [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). The signed [relative velocity](relative-velocity.md) is $w=v-U$. The ideal Coulomb law for the tangential [force](force.md) on the body is

$$
f_k=-\mu_kN\operatorname{sgn}(v-U),\qquad v-U\ne0.
$$

Here $N\ge0$ is the magnitude of the [normal force](normal-force.md), $\mu_k\ge0$ is the [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md), and $\operatorname{sgn}$ is the [sign function](sign-function.md). Thus [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) opposes [contact slip](contact-slip.md), which can differ from the body's direction of motion. In several tangential directions the same law reads $\mathbf f_k=-\mu_kN\mathbf w/|\mathbf w|$ for a nonzero tangential [relative velocity](relative-velocity.md) $\mathbf w$. The magnitude law is documented in [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §6.2: static and kinetic friction](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/6-2-friction).

For a stipulated toy model, take a nonrotating box with [mass](mass.md) $m=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, [normal force](normal-force.md) $N=10\,\mathrm N$, [coefficient of static friction](coefficient-of-static-friction.md) $\mu_s=0.5$, and [coefficient of kinetic friction](coefficient-of-kinetic-friction.md) $\mu_k=0.3$. These are assumed model parameters, rather than measured properties of any particular pair of materials; the prescribed [normal force](normal-force.md) is consistent with horizontal support under $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no other vertical [forces](force.md). On stationary ground, a box already sliding right has $f_k=-3\,\mathrm N$. With an applied horizontal [force](force.md) $F=6\,\mathrm N$ to the right, [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) gives $a=(F+f_k)/m=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, for as long as rightward [contact slip](contact-slip.md) persists.

Direction is clearer by comparing two instantaneous states of this same model. If the box has $v=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on stationary ground, $U=0$ and $w=+1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) acts left with magnitude $3\,\mathrm N$. If the box still has $v=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ but a belt beneath it has $U=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, then $w=-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$: [kinetic friction](kinetic-friction.md) acts right with magnitude $3\,\mathrm N$, even while the box moves right.

In the belt case, the [power](power.md) delivered to the box is $P_{\mathrm{box}}=f_kv=+3\,\mathrm W$. With no other horizontal [force](force.md), its [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) therefore increases by the [work-energy theorem](work-energy-theorem.md). By [Newton's third law](newton-s-third-law.md), the box exerts $-f_k$ on the belt, so the contact [power](power.md) delivered to the belt is $P_{\mathrm{belt}}=-f_kU=-6\,\mathrm W$. Their sum is $-3\,\mathrm W$: [frictional heating](frictional-heating.md) produces thermal [internal energy](internal-energy.md) at $3\,\mathrm W$. An ideal motor maintaining the prescribed belt [velocity](velocity.md) supplies $+6\,\mathrm W$ against this contact load, neglecting other losses, so its input divides into $3\,\mathrm W$ of box [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and $3\,\mathrm W$ of [frictional heating](frictional-heating.md). These are instantaneous rates, since the box's [velocity](velocity.md) subsequently changes. This balance follows directly from [power](power.md) as [force](force.md) times [velocity](velocity.md) and [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md); the power law is given in [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.4: power](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/7-4-power).

When [contact slip](contact-slip.md) vanishes, including a transition to [rolling without slipping](rolling-without-slipping.md), the nonzero-slip law no longer applies. Determine the required [static friction](static-friction.md) from the no-slip condition and the [equations of motion](equation-of-motion.md), then test its bound. Substituting $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$ into the sliding law would incorrectly exclude a nonzero [static friction](static-friction.md).
