# Rutherford scattering

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic physics](atomic-physics-split.md)  
🏷️ **Tags:** [Atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_scattering)

Rutherford scattering is [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) by a [Coulomb potential](coulomb-potential-energy.md). In the repulsive classical case, a positively charged projectile bends around a positively charged [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md). A close approach can turn it backwards without a contact collision. The calculation connects an invisible [impact parameter](impact-parameter.md) with a measurable outgoing [angle](angle.md), then with a [differential scattering cross-section](differential-scattering-cross-section.md) and a detector count.

Assume a projectile of [mass](mass.md) $m$ and [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $ze$, a stationary point [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) of [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) $Ze$, and positive $z,Z$. Here $e$ is the [elementary charge](elementary-charge.md). Neglect [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md), [electron](electron.md) [screening effect](screening-effect.md), [energy](energy-split.md) loss, and the [nuclear force](nuclear-force-split.md). With [vacuum permittivity](vacuum-permittivity.md) $\varepsilon_0$, define

$$
V(r)=\frac{\kappa}{r},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{zZe^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}>0,\qquad
E=\frac12mv^2,\qquad L=mvb.
$$

Here $E$ and $v$ are the incident [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [speed](speed.md) far from the [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md), $b\geq0$ is the [impact parameter](impact-parameter.md), and $L$ is the magnitude of [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md). The [nonrelativistic limit](nonrelativistic-limit.md) requires $v\ll c$, with $c$ the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). The fixed-target approximation requires the target [mass](mass.md) to be much greater than $m$. In [SI](international-system-of-units.md), $\kappa$ has units $\mathrm{J\,m}$, $E$ has units $\mathrm J$, and $b$ has units $\mathrm m$.
