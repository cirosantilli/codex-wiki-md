# Prime ideals below a principal prime

↑ **Parent:** [Prime ideal](prime-ideal.md)

Let $R$ be a [unique factorization domain](unique-factorization-domain.md) and let $p$ be a [prime element](prime-element.md). The only [prime ideals](prime-ideal.md) contained in the [principal ideal](principal-ideal.md) $(p)$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$. To prove this, suppose $0\ne P\subsetneq(p)$. Then $p\notin P$. For $0\ne f\in P$, [unique factorization](unique-factorization-in-an-integral-domain.md) allows us to write $f=p^kg$ with $g\notin(p)$. Since $P$ is a [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) and $p\notin P$, repeated primality gives $g\in P\subseteq(p)$, a contradiction.

In the particular [polynomial ring](polynomial-ring.md) $\mathbb Z[t]$, when $p$ is a [prime number](prime-number-split.md), the same proof uses only the integer coefficients: factor out the largest common power of $p$ from the coefficients of a nonzero [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $f$. This gives $f=p^kg$ with $g\notin(p)$ directly, without requiring the general [unique factorization domain](unique-factorization-domain.md) theorem for [polynomial rings](polynomial-ring.md).
