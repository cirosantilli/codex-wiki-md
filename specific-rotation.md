# Specific rotation

↑ **Parent:** [Optical rotation](optical-rotation.md)

For a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) in a validated linear regime,

$$
\alpha=[\alpha]_{\lambda}^{T}\,l\,c,
$$

where $l$ is path length in $\mathrm{dm}$ and $c$ is [solute](solute.md) [mass](mass.md) per final solution [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) in $\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}$. These conventions give [specific rotation](specific-rotation.md) units $\mathrm{deg\,mL\,g^{-1}\,dm^{-1}}$. Specify [solvent](solvent.md), [temperature](temperature-split.md) $T$ and [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$: [specific rotation](specific-rotation.md) is conditional on the sample environment.[McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §5.3: observed rotation divided by path length and mass concentration](organic-chemistry-by-john-mcmurry/5-3-optical-activity.md)

For an original model, stipulate an unnamed [enantiomer](enantiomer.md) pair in the same [achiral](achirality.md) [solvent](solvent.md) at $T=20^\circ\mathrm C$ and [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda=589\,\mathrm{nm}$. Assign the positive member [specific rotation](specific-rotation.md) $+40\,\mathrm{deg\,mL\,g^{-1}\,dm^{-1}}$, assumed constant over the modeled [concentrations](concentration.md). With $l=1\,\mathrm{dm}$ and $c=0.200\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}$,

$$
\alpha=(+40)(1)(0.200)^\circ=+8^\circ.
$$

These are chosen model parameters, not measured properties of a named material. Doubling only $l$ or only $c$ doubles $\alpha$ within this stipulated linear model; doubling both multiplies it by four. A real [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) must be checked for [concentration](concentration.md) dependence rather than assuming that $0.200\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}$ is dilute enough.

For an additive mixture containing only this [enantiomer](enantiomer.md) pair as solutes, put $k=[\alpha]_+$ and write $c_+,c_-$ for their mass [concentrations](concentration.md). Then

$$
\alpha=kl(c_+-c_-),\qquad c=c_++c_-.
$$

Because [enantiomers](enantiomer.md) have the same [molar mass](molar-mass.md), their relative [mole fractions](mole-fraction.md) within the pair also equal their relative mass fractions: $c_+=x_+c$, $c_-=x_-c$. Thus $\alpha=klc(x_+-x_-)$, which connects [optical rotation](optical-rotation.md) to [enantiomeric excess](enantiomeric-excess.md).
