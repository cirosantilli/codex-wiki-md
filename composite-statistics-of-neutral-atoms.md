# Composite statistics of neutral atoms

↑ **Parent:** [Spin-statistics theorem](spin-statistics-theorem.md)

Exchanging two identical bound atoms exchanges their constituent [fermions](fermion.md). An atom with mass number $A$ and atomic number $Z$ contains $A$ nucleons and $Z$ electrons, so its exchange sign is $(-1)^{A+Z}$ when its internal state is preserved. An odd count gives a composite [fermion](fermion.md); an even count gives a composite [boson](boson.md). This is equivalently the integer-versus-half-integer total-spin distinction in the [Spin-statistics theorem](spin-statistics-theorem.md). For example, neutral lithium-6 has nine fermionic constituents and is a fermion, while neutral lithium-7 has ten and is a boson. Counting nucleons alone instead classifies the nucleus, not the neutral atom. This effective composite-particle description requires energies and separations where the bound atom remains intact.
