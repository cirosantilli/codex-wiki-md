# Frobenius case classification

↑ **Parent:** [Frobenius method](frobenius-method.md)

For a real pair of [indicial roots](indicial-root.md) $r_1\geq r_2$ at a [regular singular point](regular-singular-point.md) of a [second-order linear differential equation](second-order-linear-differential-equation.md), the second local solution is classified by the gap $\Delta$ and by the [recurrence relation](recurrence-relation.md) for the smaller [indicial root](indicial-root.md). Case I has $\Delta\notin\mathbb Z$ and two pure [Frobenius solutions](frobenius-solution.md). Case II has $\Delta=0$ and an independent [Logarithmic Frobenius solution](logarithmic-solution-from-a-repeated-frobenius-exponent.md). With a positive integer gap, Case IIIa means the exceptional coefficient equation is inconsistent, so a nonzero multiple of the first [Frobenius solution](frobenius-solution.md) times a [logarithm](logarithm.md) is necessary. Case IIIb means that equation is consistent and leaves a free coefficient, so there are two pure [Frobenius solutions](frobenius-solution.md). This IIIa/IIIb convention is the one used in the Oxford course notes.[Oxford A6 Differential Equations II lecture notes, draft 3 January 2023, §5.2.5, printed p. 5–7: III(a) has a nonzero right-hand side at the exceptional index; III(b) has a zero right-hand side](https://www.cjhb.site/Files.php/Books/Differential%20Equation/02_ODE/Differential%20Equations%20II.pdf#page=55)

The free coefficient in Case IIIb adds a multiple of the first [Frobenius solution](frobenius-solution.md), as explained by the [undetermined coefficient at Frobenius resonance](undetermined-coefficient-at-frobenius-resonance.md). Stating whether the [logarithm](logarithm.md) occurs is useful alongside the case name because other courses may choose different labels.
