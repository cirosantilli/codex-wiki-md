# Solute

↑ **Parent:** [Solution (chemistry)](solution-chemistry.md)

A [solute](solute.md) is a component of a [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) distinguished from its [solvent](solvent.md), typically present in a smaller proportion. There can be several [solutes](solute.md). A [concentration](concentration.md) must name both what is counted and the denominator: [molar concentration](molar-concentration.md) uses [amount of substance](amount-of-substance.md) per final [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md); a solute-to-solvent [mass](mass.md) ratio has another denominator.[Chemistry 2e, §3.3, Solutions: solute convention and molarity denominator](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/3-3-molarity.md)

“Added [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md)” and “dissolved [sodium](sodium.md) [cations](cation.md)” describe different levels of the same inventory. [Dissociation of an ionic compound](dissociation-of-an-ionic-compound.md) changes the grouping of the particles; it preserves their [chemical elements](chemical-element.md). A dissolved [ion](ion.md) remains an [ion](ion.md), and dissolved [sucrose](sucrose.md) remains molecular unless a separate [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction-split.md) occurs. Thus the name of the weighed [solute](solute.md) need not be the name of every dissolved [chemical species](chemical-species.md).
