# Preparation uncertainty

↑ **Parent:** [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md)

[Preparation uncertainty](preparation-uncertainty.md) describes the outcome spread intrinsic to a specified [quantum state preparation](quantum-state-preparation.md). Operationally, prepare many copies of the same [quantum state](quantum-state.md), measure [position](position.md) on one group and [momentum](momentum-split.md) on another, and estimate the two [variances](variance-split.md). Ideal measurements still have the spreads predicted by the [Born rule](born-rule.md). No earlier [position](position.md) measurement need occur in the [momentum](momentum-split.md) group. The [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) constrains these two distributions belonging to the same preparation.

[Measurement backaction](measurement-backaction.md) concerns how a particular [measurement in quantum mechanics](quantum-measurement-split.md) changes a [quantum state](quantum-state.md). Instrument resolution concerns how its reported outcomes relate to the intended [observable](observable.md). Those questions require a model of the instrument and its interaction with the system. Substituting an instrument error and an induced change for $\Delta x$ and $\Delta p$ in the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md) does not follow from its proof. The practical value of [preparation uncertainty](preparation-uncertainty.md) is that it bounds achievable input spreads before any such instrument model is chosen.
