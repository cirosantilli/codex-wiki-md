# Nuclear deformation

↑ **Parent:** [Atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md)

Departure of an intrinsic nuclear shape from [spherical symmetry](spherical-symmetry.md). Here intrinsic means referred to axes attached to a model [nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md). A nuclear [quantum state](quantum-state.md) with definite [spin](spin.md) need not have the same laboratory [charge density](charge-density.md) as that intrinsic distribution. In particular, the [ground state](ground-state.md) of a deformed [even-even nucleus](even-even-nucleus.md) can have $I=0$, a spherical laboratory [expectation value](expectation-value.md), and a nonzero [intrinsic nuclear quadrupole moment](intrinsic-nuclear-quadrupole-moment.md).

For an illustrative uniform [charge density](charge-density.md) inside a [spheroid](spheroid.md), let the semiaxes be $(a,a,c)$ and keep its [volume](volume.md) equal to that of a sphere of radius $R$:

$$
a^2c=R^3,\qquad a=R\eta^{-1/3},\qquad c=R\eta^{2/3},\qquad\eta=\frac ca>0.
$$

Thus $\eta>1$ gives a [prolate spheroid](prolate-spheroid.md), $\eta=1$ a sphere, and $\eta<1$ an [oblate spheroid](oblate-spheroid.md). These are three-dimensional equal-[volume](volume.md) shapes: equal areas of their drawn cross-sections would be a different constraint. Real [nuclear charge distributions](nuclear-charge-distribution.md) have diffuse surfaces and need not be uniform, axially symmetric or confined to one shape.
