# Cosmic microwave background

↑ **Parent:** [Cosmology](cosmology-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background)

The [cosmic microwave background](cosmic-microwave-background-split.md) is nearly isotropic thermal [electromagnetic radiation](electromagnetic-radiation-split.md). Its spectrum connects [radio receiver](radio-receiver.md) measurements with the universe's thermal history: [redshift preservation of a thermal photon spectrum](redshift-preservation-of-a-thermal-photon-spectrum.md) cools a [blackbody](blackbody.md) spectrum as space expands. The 1965 Princeton analysis described this relation.[pp.415–416: expansion cools radiation while preserving a thermal spectrum](cosmic-black-body-radiation-by-r-h-dicke-and-others.md)

A [radiometer](radiometer.md) cannot label a received [photon](photon.md) as cosmic, atmospheric or instrumental. Its measured [power](power.md) combines the incoming radiation with the receiver response. The useful question is which comparisons and controls can isolate a sky contribution. This makes the [Penzias-Wilson microwave background experiment](penzias-wilson-microwave-background-experiment.md) an example of both [observational cosmology](observational-cosmology.md) and [measurement calibration](measurement-calibration.md).
