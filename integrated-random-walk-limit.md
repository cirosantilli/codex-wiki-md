# Integrated random-walk limit

↑ **Parent:** [Donsker's theorem](donsker-s-theorem.md)

For a [random walk](random-walk.md) with independent identically distributed steps of mean zero and [variance](variance-split.md) one, the [Donsker invariance principle](donsker-s-theorem.md) and the [continuous mapping theorem](continuous-mapping-theorem.md) for integration give this limit. The integral of the polygonal interpolation differs from the right-endpoint sum by $S_n/(2n^{3/2})$, whose squared [expectation](expected-value.md) is $1/(4n^2)$. The [Slutsky theorem](slutsky-theorem.md) removes that error. The limiting variable is the time-one value of [integrated Brownian motion](integrated-brownian-motion.md), with law $N(0,1/3)$.
