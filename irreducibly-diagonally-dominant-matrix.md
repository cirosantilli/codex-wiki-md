# Irreducibly diagonally dominant matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Diagonal dominance](diagonal-dominance.md)

A complex square [matrix](matrix.md) is irreducibly diagonally dominant when every row satisfies $|a_{ii}|\geq\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|$, at least one row is strict, and its nonzero off-diagonal entries define a [strongly connected directed graph](strong-connectivity.md). Such a [matrix](matrix.md) is nonsingular. For if $Az=0$, choose a component of maximum modulus. The row inequality forces every out-neighbour to have that same modulus, unless the maximum is zero. Connectivity propagates this modulus to a strict row, where the row inequality is impossible. Thus the maximum is zero and $z=0$. This extends nonsingularity of a [strictly diagonally dominant matrix](strictly-diagonally-dominant-matrix.md) to many finite difference matrices with strict dominance only near a boundary.
