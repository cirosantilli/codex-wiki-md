# Electrical resistance

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical resistance and conductance](electrical-resistance-and-conductance.md)

[Electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) measures the [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) needed to drive a given [electric current](electric-current.md) through a component. For an [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md) under fixed material conditions, $R=V/I$ is constant and [Ohm's law](ohm-s-law.md) gives $V=IR$. The [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is the [ohm](ohm.md), $1\,\Omega=1\,\mathrm{V/A}$. At a fixed positive [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md), increasing [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) reduces [electric current](electric-current.md). [Electrical conductance](electrical-conductance.md) $G=1/R$ expresses the same response reciprocally. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §9.3: resistance and its unit](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-3-resistivity-and-resistance).

For a straight uniform wire at rest, with constant [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) $\sigma>0$, [length](length.md) $L$ and [wire cross-sectional area](wire-cross-sectional-area.md) $A$, the uniform [Ohmic conductor](ohmic-conductor.md) model gives

$$
R=\frac{L}{\sigma A}=\rho_{\mathrm{el}}\frac LA,\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac1\sigma.
$$

The [electrical resistivity](electrical-resistivity.md) $\rho_{\mathrm{el}}$ characterizes the material; [electrical resistance](electrical-resistance.md) also includes geometry. Doubling [length](length.md) doubles $R$, while doubling [wire cross-sectional area](wire-cross-sectional-area.md) halves $R$, with material and [temperature](temperature.md) fixed. The uniform-field assumption includes insulated sides and equipotential end faces, as explained in [uniform current in a straight homogeneous wire](uniform-current-in-a-straight-homogeneous-wire.md). [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, §9.3: wire geometry and resistivity](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/9-3-resistivity-and-resistance).

As a defined wire example, take $L=1\,\mathrm m$, $A=1\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ and $\sigma=5\times10^5\,(\Omega\,\mathrm m)^{-1}$. Then $R=2\,\Omega$; applying [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) $5\,\mathrm V$ drives [electric current](electric-current.md) $2.5\,\mathrm A$. With the same [length](length.md) and material, a second wire of [wire cross-sectional area](wire-cross-sectional-area.md) $2\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ has $R=1\,\Omega$ and carries $5\,\mathrm A$ at that same [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md). Fixing [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) here differs from fixing [electric current](electric-current.md) in the [current density](current-density.md) comparison: stating what is held fixed is essential.
