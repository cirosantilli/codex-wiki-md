<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Choose the polar axis along $\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2$. Then

$$
\int e^{-ik\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot(\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2)}d\Omega
=2\pi\int_{-1}^1e^{-ikdu}du=4\pi\frac{\sin(kd)}{kd}.
$$

Using the [angular interference integral for two point scatterers](../../../../../angular-interference-integral-for-two-point-scatterers.md), integrating the two self terms and the [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) terms gives

$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm tot}=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\left[|c_1|^2+|c_2|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(c_1c_2^*)\frac{\sin(kd)}{kd}\right]}.
$$

For $kd\ll1$, the amplitudes add coherently and $\sigma\to4\pi|c_1+c_2|^2/k^2$; when $kd\gg1$ the angle-integrated [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) is suppressed. These limits hold for fixed incoming direction; no incoming-orientation average has been silently imposed.
