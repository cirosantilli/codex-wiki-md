<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Set $q=e^{-\beta\hbar\omega}$. The oscillator populations are $(1-q)q^n$, and differentiating the [geometric series](../../../../../geometric-series.md) gives $\bar n=q/(1-q)$. The [canonical oscillator coordinate variance](../../../../../canonical-oscillator-coordinate-variance.md) follows from $2\bar n+1=(1+q)/(1-q)=\coth(\beta\hbar\omega/2)$, and

$$
\boxed{\langle x^2\rangle_T=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\coth\left(\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_BT}\right)}.
$$

It approaches $\hbar/(2m\omega)$ at zero [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and $k_BT/(m\omega^2)$ at high [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), agreeing respectively with ground-state fluctuations and classical equipartition.
