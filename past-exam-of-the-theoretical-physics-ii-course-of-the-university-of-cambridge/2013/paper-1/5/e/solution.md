<h1 id="5/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**The literal printed difference is zero**, because the same spin-index product appears twice, and zero is invariant under every transformation. The intended [SU(2)-invariant antisymmetric spinor contraction](../../../../../su-2-invariant-antisymmetric-spinor-contraction.md) uses opposite indices in the second term. With $\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, define $I=\phi^T\varepsilon\chi$. For any two-by-two matrix,

$$
U^T\varepsilon U=(\det U)\varepsilon.
$$

Thus for $U\in SU(2)$,

$$
\boxed{I'=(U\phi)^T\varepsilon(U\chi)=I}.
$$

Expanding the [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) identity proves the result without complex conjugating either [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md). This is the invariant alternating tensor behind the [spin singlet state](../../../../../spin-singlet-state.md). If the two [spinors](../../../../../spinor.md) are identical, the contraction vanishes. An antisymmetric internal two-boson state also needs an antisymmetric additional degree of freedom for overall bosonic symmetry; it does not lie in the fully symmetric fixed-two-mode sector discussed above.
