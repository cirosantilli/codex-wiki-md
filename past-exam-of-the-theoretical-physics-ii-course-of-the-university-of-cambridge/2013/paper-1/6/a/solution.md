<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A constant electrostatic [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) offset is removed locally by the [quantum phase](../../../../../quantum-phase.md) substitution $\psi=e^{-iV_0t/\hbar}\psi_0$, since $(i\hbar\partial_t-V_0)\psi=e^{-iV_0t/\hbar}i\hbar\partial_t\psi_0$. Therefore the two [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) branches become

$$
\boxed{\psi=\sum_k\frac1{\sqrt{2\omega_k}}\left[
a_ke^{ikx-i(\omega_k+V_0/\hbar)t}
+b_k^\dagger e^{-ikx+i(\omega_k-V_0/\hbar)t}\right]}.
$$

The normalization uses the kinetic [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) $\omega_k$, while the canonical [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are $E=V_0\pm\hbar\omega_k$. The opposite-frequency branch is not obtained by replacing $\omega_k$ by the same shifted positive [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) in both terms. A globally constant offset produces no [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), whereas different offsets on opposite sides of a step cannot be removed by a single smooth [global phase](../../../../../global-phase.md).
