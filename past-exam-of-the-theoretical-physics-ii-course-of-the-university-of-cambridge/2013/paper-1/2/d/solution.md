<h1 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Measure intermediate time $u$ from $t'$. The [classical path](../../../../../classical-path.md) with the fixed endpoints is

$$
x_c(u)=x'+\frac{X}{T}u+\frac{\alpha}{2m}u(T-u),\qquad 0\leq u\leq T.
$$

It satisfies $m\ddot x_c=-\alpha$. Direct integration of $m\dot x_c^2/2-\alpha x_c$ gives

$$
\boxed{S_c=\frac{mX^2}{2T}-\frac{\alpha T(x+x')}{2}-\frac{\alpha^2T^3}{24m}}.
$$

For an arbitrary path write $x=x_c+\eta$ with $\eta(0)=\eta(T)=0$. Integrating the cross term by parts gives $-\int(m\ddot x_c+\alpha)\eta\,du=0$. Thus $S[x]=S_c+\int m\dot\eta^2du/2$ exactly. The fluctuation [path integral](../../../../../path-integral.md) is the free endpoint-fixed one, so its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) supplies $\sqrt{m/(2\pi i\hbar T)}$. Multiplication by $\theta(T)e^{iS_c/\hbar}$ reproduces the previous kernel. This exact [classical-path factorization of a quadratic path integral](../../../../../classical-path-factorization-of-a-quadratic-path-integral.md) is possible because a linear potential introduces no additional quadratic fluctuation term.
