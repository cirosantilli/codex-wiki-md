<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At coincident points the Gaussian expression has $g_{ij}=n$. Its six contributions give

$$
C^{(3)}(\mathbf r,\mathbf r,\mathbf r)
=(1+3\epsilon+2)n^3.
$$

When all mutual separations become large and the density stays the same nonzero $n$, only $n_1n_2n_3=n^3$ remains. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\text{bosons: }6,\qquad\text{fermions: }0}.
$$

The bosonic value is $3!$ from Gaussian exchange pairings; the fermionic [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) vanishes when its rows coincide. Density-normalized correlations avoid the additional homogeneity assumption. For a specified bosonic number product, part (c)'s occupation sum must replace the six-pairing formula, so neither the universal factor six nor a universal printed ratio follows from that specification alone.
