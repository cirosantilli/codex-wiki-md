<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Define $\Omega=\sqrt{\Delta^2+\Omega_R^2}$. Squaring the two-by-two matrix gives $H_R^2=\hbar^2\Omega^2 I/4$, so

$$
\boxed{E_\pm=\pm\frac{\hbar\Omega}{2},\qquad
|\Psi_\pm(t)\rangle=e^{\mp i\Omega t/2}e^{-i\omega tS_z/\hbar}|\pm\rangle_R}.
$$

Here $|\pm\rangle_R$ is the corresponding normalized [eigenstate](../../../../../eigenstate.md) at $t=0$. In the rotating frame only its [quantum phase](../../../../../quantum-phase.md) changes. In the laboratory frame its [spin](../../../../../spin.md) direction also rotates about $z$ at [angular velocity](../../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\omega$: $\langle\mathbf S\rangle_\pm=\pm\hbar(\Omega_R\cos\omega t,\Omega_R\sin\omega t,\Delta)/(2\Omega)$. An arbitrary initial state is the same fixed linear combination of these two solutions. If $\Omega=0$, the rotating-frame [Hamiltonian operator](../../../../../hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) vanishes and the entire doublet is degenerate; evolution is just $R(t)$.
