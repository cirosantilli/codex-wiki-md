<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A Klein-Gordon solution is second order in time: both $\Psi$ and $\partial_t\Psi$ are needed as [initial data](../../../../../initial-data-in-general-relativity.md). Its free modes have both signs of [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md),

$$
E=\pm\sqrt{c^2p^2+m^2c^4}.
$$

This differs from the first-order Schrödinger evolution of a fixed one-particle [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md).

Moreover, $|\Psi|^2$ does not generally obey the required [probability](../../../../../probability.md) [continuity equation](../../../../../continuity-equation.md). The conserved Klein-Gordon density is proportional to

$$
\rho_{\rm KG}=i\left(\Psi^*\partial_t\Psi-(\partial_t\Psi^*)\Psi\right),
$$

up to a positive conventional constant. A pure positive-frequency mode gives $+2\omega|\Psi|^2$ and a negative-frequency mode gives $-2\omega|\Psi|^2$. **This density is not positive definite and cannot be a universal position [probability density](../../../../../probability-density.md).**

For a free field, one can choose a positive-frequency subspace with a positive integrated inner product. Time-dependent backgrounds generally mix it with the negative-frequency subspace, so simply deleting the latter is not dynamically consistent. In [quantum field theory](../../../../../quantum-field-theory-split.md) the complex [Klein-Gordon field](../../../../../klein-gordon-field.md) instead has [particle](../../../../../particle-physics.md) and [antiparticle](../../../../../antiparticle.md) sectors; its [conserved current](../../../../../conserved-current.md) is interpreted as charge current, and creation or annihilation of pairs need not fit a fixed-particle-number [wavefunction](../../../../../wave-function-split.md). These are limitations of the naive single-particle interpretation, not a failure of the relativistic field equation.
