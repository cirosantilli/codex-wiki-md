<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Work in [temporal gauge](../../../../../temporal-gauge.md), with zero scalar potential, and use the charge sign exactly as in the given covariant spatial derivative. A spatially uniform [vector potential](../../../../../vector-potential.md) acts on a Fourier mode as

$$
\left(\nabla-\frac{ie\mathbf A(t)}\hbar\right)
\{\psi_{\mathbf k}(t)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}\}
=i\left(\mathbf k-\frac{e\mathbf A(t)}\hbar\right)
\psi_{\mathbf k}(t)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}.
$$

There are no spatial derivatives of $\mathbf A$. Substitution into the field equation and multiplication by $c^2$ therefore gives

$$
\boxed{\ddot\psi_{\mathbf k}
+\omega_{\mathbf k-e\mathbf A(t)/\hbar}^2\psi_{\mathbf k}=0,
\qquad
\omega_{\mathbf q}=\sqrt{c^2|\mathbf q|^2+\frac{m^2c^4}{\hbar^2}}.}
$$

The canonical [wavevector](../../../../../wavevector.md) $\mathbf k$ is conserved by spatial translation symmetry; the [kinetic momentum](../../../../../kinetic-momentum.md) is $\hbar\mathbf k-e\mathbf A(t)$. The absence of a term involving $\dot{\mathbf A}$ in this mode equation follows from [temporal gauge](../../../../../temporal-gauge.md) and the spatial minimal-coupling operator. The [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) still affects evolution through the changing oscillator [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md).
