<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For a [unitary transformation](../../../../../unitary-operator.md) $e^S$, take $S^\dagger=-S$ and $S=O(t)$. [Operator conjugation by an exponential](../../../../../operator-conjugation-by-an-exponential.md) gives

$$
H'=H+[S,H]+\frac12[S,[S,H]]+\cdots.
$$

Separate the unperturbed energy $\mathcal E(\Delta)$ from $H_t=O(t)$. To first order,

$$
H'=\mathcal E(\Delta)+H_t+[S,\mathcal E(\Delta)]+O(t^2).
$$

Therefore **eliminating the first-order hopping requires**

$$
\boxed{[S,\mathcal E(\Delta)]=-H_t.}
$$

This is a [Schrieffer-Wolff transformation](../../../../../schrieffer-wolff-transformation.md): virtual transitions are removed from the leading [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) and reappear as higher-order effects within a retained occupation sector. The same canonical-elimination strategy relates localized-moment and effective-exchange descriptions in the original construction.[Abstract, canonical transformation in the small-mixing limit](../../../../../relation-between-the-anderson-and-kondo-hamiltonians-by-john-robert-schrieffer-and-p-a-wolff.md)

Such a choice requires the hopping to connect separated unperturbed energies. If it connects exactly degenerate states, those states must remain in the retained degenerate block; a singular perturbative generator is not an acceptable replacement.
