<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

For any two distinct normalized [Majorana fermion operators](../../../../majorana-fermion-operator.md) $\eta,\chi$, let $f=(\eta+i\chi)/2$. Then

$$
\{f,f^\dagger\}=\frac14\big(\{\eta,\eta\}+\{\chi,\chi\}\big)=1,
$$

and

$$
\{f,f\}=\frac14\big(\{\eta,\eta\}-\{\chi,\chi\}+2i\{\eta,\chi\}\big)=0.
$$

The same applies to $f^\dagger$. Both specified pairs have this form. Moreover, the Majorana operators entering $f_1$ are all distinct from those entering $f_2$, so every cross [anticommutator](../../../../anticommutator.md) is zero. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\{f_i,f_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij},\qquad
\{f_i,f_j\}=\{f_i^\dagger,f_j^\dagger\}=0}.
$$

Thus $f_1,f_2$ are ordinary [fermionic annihilation operators](../../../../fermionic-annihilation-operator.md), constructed by a different pairing of the four Majorana operators.
