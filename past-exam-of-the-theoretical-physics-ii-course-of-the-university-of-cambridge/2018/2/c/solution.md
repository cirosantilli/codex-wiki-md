<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Away from the shell, the reduced radial equation is free. Regularity at the origin and the definition of the S-wave [phase shift](../../../../phase-shift.md) give

$$
u_0(r)=\begin{cases}A\sin kr,&r<R,\\B\sin(kr+\delta_0),&r>R.\end{cases}
$$

The reduced radial [wave function](../../../../wave-function-split.md) is continuous at $R$; otherwise its second derivative would contain a delta derivative not present in the equation. Integrating the equation across the shell gives the jump

$$
u_0'(R^+)-u_0'(R^-)=\frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}u_0(R).
$$

Divide by the common shell value and substitute the two free solutions. This yields

$$
\boxed{k\cot(kR+\delta_0)-k\cot(kR)=\frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}}.
$$

If the shell value vanishes, use the undivided matching equations or a continuous limit rather than this logarithmic-derivative division. For an explicit [phase shift of a delta-shell potential](../../../../phase-shift-of-a-delta-shell-potential.md), write $x=kR$ and $g=2mV_0R/\hbar^2$. The cotangent addition formula gives

$$
\boxed{\tan\delta_0(k)=-\frac{g\sin^2x}{x+g\sin x\cos x}}.
$$

The phase is defined modulo $\pi$; choose its branch continuously as energy or coupling varies. At zero coupling the physical [phase shift](../../../../phase-shift.md) is zero.
