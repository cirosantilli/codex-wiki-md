<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

Set $C(\beta)=[A,e^{-\beta B}]$. The [product rule](../../../../../product-rule.md) gives

$$
C'+BC=[B,A]e^{-\beta B},\qquad C(0)=0.
$$

Multiplying by the [integrating factor](../../../../../integrating-factor.md) $e^{\beta B}$ and integrating yields the [commutator of an operator with an exponential](../../../../../commutator-of-an-operator-with-an-exponential.md):

$$
\boxed{[A,e^{-\beta B}]
=e^{-\beta B}\int_0^\beta
e^{\lambda B}[B,A]e^{-\lambda B}\,d\lambda.}
$$

Equivalently, differentiate $e^{\beta B}Ae^{-\beta B}-A$ directly; its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) is the integrand and it vanishes at zero. The argument is exact for finite [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md), and for [bounded operators](../../../../../continuous-linear-operator.md) with their norm-convergent exponentials; unbounded [linear operators](../../../../../linear-operator.md) require an appropriate common domain.
