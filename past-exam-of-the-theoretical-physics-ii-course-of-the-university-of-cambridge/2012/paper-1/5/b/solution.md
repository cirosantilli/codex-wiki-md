<h1 id="5/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The exponent is $iS_N/\hbar$, where

$$
S_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\epsilon\,\frac m2
\left(\frac{x_{j+1}-x_j}{\epsilon}\right)^2
$$

is the classical [action](../../../../../action.md) of the piecewise-linear interpolating path. For smooth paths its limit is $S=\int_0^T m\dot x^2\,dt/2$. The discretization defines the quantum [integral](../../../../../integral-split.md); it does not assert that typical quantum paths are differentiable. Weighting histories by their [action](../../../../../action.md) phase is the central formulation of the original path-integral paper.[1948, abstract: amplitudes from histories weighted by the exponential of classical action in units of reduced Planck's constant](../../../../../space-time-approach-to-non-relativistic-quantum-mechanics-by-richard-feynman.md)

When [action](../../../../../action.md) differences on the experimental scales are large compared with $\hbar$, nearby nonstationary phases cancel. [stationary phase](../../../../../stationary-phase-method.md) selects $\delta S=0$ at fixed endpoints, hence $m\ddot x=0$. The surviving free classical trajectory is $x_{\rm cl}(t)=x_0+(x_T-x_0)t/T$, with [action](../../../../../action.md) $m(x_T-x_0)^2/(2T)$. This is the semiclassical mechanism; it does not mean each individual path becomes a classical path.
