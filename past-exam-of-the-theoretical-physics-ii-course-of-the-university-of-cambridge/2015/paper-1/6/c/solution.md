<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For nonzero $\chi$, the outer product $X=\chi\chi^\dagger$ is rank-one Hermitian positive semidefinite, so $\det X=0$ and $ct=\chi^\dagger\chi/2>0$. It therefore represents a future-directed [null vector](../../../../../null-vector.md). Conversely any nonzero future-null Hermitian $X$ has [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) $2ct$ and zero, so its positive [eigenvector](../../../../../eigenvector.md) multiplied by $\sqrt{2ct}$ supplies $\chi$. The phase $\chi\mapsto e^{i\zeta}\chi$ leaves $X$ unchanged. A past-directed [null vector](../../../../../null-vector.md) needs $X=-\chi\chi^\dagger$, rather than the positive outer product alone.

For the printed normalized [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md), direct multiplication yields

$$
X=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}
1+\cos\theta&\sin\theta\,e^{-i\phi}\\
\sin\theta\,e^{i\phi}&1-\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Comparing with the printed $x+iy$ convention gives

$$
\boxed{(ct,x,y,z)=\frac12
(1,\ \sin\theta\cos\phi,\ -\sin\theta\sin\phi,\ \cos\theta).}
$$

**Its usual spatial [azimuth](../../../../../azimuth.md) is $-\phi$, not $\phi$.** The source's asserted usual-angle identification has a sign inconsistency. Either conjugating the two [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) phases or replacing the upper-right [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) entry by $x-iy$ restores the usual [azimuth](../../../../../azimuth.md) $\phi$. Neither alteration is made silently here. A further positive scale factor sets the magnitude of an arbitrary future-null [vector](../../../../../vector.md); the displayed normalized [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) fixes $ct=1/2$ in the chosen coordinate units.
