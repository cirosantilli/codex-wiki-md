<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The association between four real coordinates and Hermitian two-by-two [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) is a real-linear bijection. Congruence by $S$ preserves Hermiticity, so $X'=SXS^\dagger$ defines another real [four-vector](../../../../../four-vector.md) depending linearly on the original. Its [determinant](../../../../../determinant.md) is

$$
\det X=(ct)^2-x^2-y^2-z^2,\qquad
\det X'=\det S\,\det X\,\det S^\dagger=\det X.
$$

Thus the induced invertible linear map preserves the Minkowski quadratic form:

$$
\boxed{\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda=\eta,\qquad
\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).}
$$

The maps obtained continuously from the identity are proper and orthochronous. For example, a future timelike [vector](../../../../../vector.md) gives a positive-definite $X$, which remains positive definite under invertible congruence; its [time](../../../../../time-in-physics.md) direction is preserved. The [matrices](../../../../../matrix.md) $S$ and $-S$ induce the same [four-vector](../../../../../four-vector.md) transformation, illustrating the [Lorentz spinor double cover](../../../../../lorentz-spinor-double-cover.md).

The source uses $x+iy$ in the upper-right entry, corresponding to $X=ctI+x\sigma_x-y\sigma_y+z\sigma_z$ for the standard [Pauli matrices](../../../../../pauli-matrices.md). This is a permissible basis convention, but its minus sign in the $y$ basis must also be retained in the [spinor](../../../../../spinor.md) identification.
