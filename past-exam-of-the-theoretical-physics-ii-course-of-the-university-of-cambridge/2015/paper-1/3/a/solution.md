<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Outside a finite-range [scattering potential](../../../../../scattering-potential.md), the stationary [Schrödinger equation](../../../../../schrodinger-equation.md) becomes the free [Helmholtz equation](../../../../../helmholtz-equation.md). The specified incident state is a [plane wave](../../../../../plane-wave.md) along $+z$. The scattered disturbance must be outgoing: the scattering experiment has an incoming incident beam but no independently imposed spherical wave arriving from infinity. At large radius its angular pattern is encoded by a [scattering amplitude](../../../../../scattering-amplitude.md) $f_k(\theta,\phi)$:

$$
\boxed{\psi_k(\mathbf r)\sim e^{ikz}+f_k(\theta,\phi)\frac{e^{ikr}}r.}
$$

An outgoing three-dimensional wave has amplitude proportional to $1/r$ because its [probability current](../../../../../probability-current.md) spreads over area proportional to $r^2$. Indeed its radial current is $(\hbar k/m)|f_k|^2/r^2$ to leading order, while the incident current density is $\hbar k/m$. Therefore $d\sigma/d\Omega=|f_k|^2$. The sign $e^{+ikr}$ assumes the stationary [time](../../../../../time-in-physics.md) factor $e^{-iE_kt/\hbar}$.
