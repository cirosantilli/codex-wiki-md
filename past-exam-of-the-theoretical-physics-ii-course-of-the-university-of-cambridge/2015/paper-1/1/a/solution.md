<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [spin](../../../../../spin.md) operators here are [dimensionless](../../../../../dimensionless-quantity.md): $S^j=\sigma_j/2$, so $\Omega$ has units of inverse [time](../../../../../time-in-physics.md) and $\Phi$ is a [dimensionless](../../../../../dimensionless-quantity.md) pulse area. Integrating the Schrödinger evolution across an infinitesimally short pulse gives $U_\Phi=e^{-i\Phi S^x}$. The finite free [Hamiltonian](../../../../../hamiltonian.md) contributes a vanishing integrated phase within that pulse. Between pulses the free evolution is $U_\Omega=e^{-i\Omega TS^z}$.

In the $S^z$ eigenbasis, $S^z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)/2$ and $(\sigma_x)^2=I$. Consequently

$$
U_\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\Omega T/2},e^{i\Omega T/2}),\qquad
U_\Phi=I\cos(\Phi/2)-i\sigma_x\sin(\Phi/2).
$$

Starting just before a pulse, the kick acts first and the free interval second. Operators act on states right to left, hence

$$
\boxed{U(NT-0^+,-0^+)=(U_\Omega U_\Phi)^N.}
$$

There are exactly $N$ kicks, at [times](../../../../../time-in-physics.md) $0,T,\ldots,(N-1)T$; the pulse at $NT$ is excluded. Sampling just after kicks would change the displayed product order. This endpoint convention specifies the [delta-kicked spin Floquet operator](../../../../../delta-kicked-spin-floquet-operator.md).
