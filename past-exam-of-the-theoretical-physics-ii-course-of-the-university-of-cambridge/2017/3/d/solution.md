<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At the origin, the normalized [bound state in a delta potential](../../../../bound-state-in-a-delta-potential.md) contributes $|\varphi_0(0)|^2=\kappa$, and the odd [scattering eigenfunctions](../../../../scattering-wavefunction.md) contribute zero. The even continuum functions from the preceding part have

$$
|\varphi_{k,e}(0)|^2=\frac1\pi\cos^2\delta_e=\frac1\pi\frac{k^2}{k^2+\kappa^2}.
$$

Their [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) is $E_k=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$. Inserting these weights into the [spectral resolution of a quantum propagator](../../../../spectral-resolution-of-a-quantum-propagator.md), and using evenness to extend the integral to the whole real axis, yields for $T>0$

$$
\boxed{K(0,T|0,0)=\kappa e^{-iE_0T/\hbar}
+\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{k^2}{\kappa^2+k^2}
e^{-i\hbar k^2T/(2m)}.}
$$

This is the [quantum propagator of an attractive delta potential](../../../../quantum-propagator-of-an-attractive-delta-potential.md). The printed continuum exponent has an extra factor of $\hbar$: its time phase must be $-iE_kT/\hbar=-i\hbar k^2T/(2m)$. This correction also makes the exponent [dimensionless](../../../../dimensionless-quantity.md). Oscillatory integrals are understood with an infinitesimal positive convergence factor before taking its limit.
