<h1 id="4/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The opposite $x$-axis [pure states](../../../../pure-state.md) have projectors $(I\pm\sigma_x)/2$. Averaging them gives

$$
\boxed{\rho_C=\frac12\frac{I+\sigma_x}{2}+\frac12\frac{I-\sigma_x}{2}=\frac I2=\rho_B.}
$$

Therefore **C and B cannot be distinguished by any measurement on the atoms alone**. Every [POVM](../../../../positive-operator-valued-measure.md) outcome has the same [probability](../../../../probability.md) $\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)$ for the two preparations; this is the operational equivalence of different [quantum state ensembles](../../../../quantum-state-ensemble.md). Accessible preparation labels or additional correlations would be extra information. C can be distinguished from A by the $x$-axis [spin measurement](../../../../spin-measurement.md) in part (c).
