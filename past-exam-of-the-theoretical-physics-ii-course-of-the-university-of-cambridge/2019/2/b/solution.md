<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Integrate the squared [partial-wave expansion of a scattering amplitude](../../../../partial-wave-expansion-of-a-scattering-amplitude.md) over the solid angle. [Legendre polynomial](../../../../legendre-polynomial.md) orthogonality eliminates terms with unequal angular-momentum indices, giving

$$
\sigma_{\rm tot}=2\pi\int_{-1}^1|f(\theta)|^2\,d(\cos\theta)
=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell.
$$

At zero [scattering angle](../../../../scattering-angle.md), $P_\ell(1)=1$ and $\operatorname{Im}(e^{i\delta_\ell}\sin\delta_\ell)=\sin^2\delta_\ell$. Consequently

$$
\operatorname{Im}f(0)=\frac1k\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell,
\qquad\boxed{\sigma_{\rm tot}=\frac{4\pi}{k}\operatorname{Im}f(0).}
$$

This is the [optical theorem](../../../../optical-theorem.md). In the present elastic problem the total and elastic [cross sections](../../../../cross-section-physics.md) coincide. For an absorptive interaction the inclusive total still obeys the [optical theorem](../../../../optical-theorem.md), but need not equal the integral of the elastic differential [cross section](../../../../cross-section-physics.md).
