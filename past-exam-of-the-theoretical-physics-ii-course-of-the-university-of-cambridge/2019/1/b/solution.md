Here the dimensionless [spin one-half](../../../../spin-one-half.md) operator is $S_x=\sigma_x/2$. Since the [Pauli matrix](../../../../pauli-matrices.md) obeys $\sigma_x^2=I$, the even and odd terms of the [matrix exponential](../../../../matrix-exponential.md) give

$$
e^{-i\theta S_x}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m(\theta/2)^{2m}}{(2m)!}I
-i\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m(\theta/2)^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}\sigma_x
=I\cos\frac\theta2-i\sigma_x\sin\frac\theta2.
$$

This is the [exponential of a Pauli vector](../../../../exponential-of-a-pauli-vector.md) for an $x$-axis [rotation](../../../../rotation-mathematics.md). With a physical [spin angular momentum](../../../../spin.md) $S_x^{\rm phys}=\hbar\sigma_x/2$, the exponent is $-i\theta S_x^{\rm phys}/\hbar$.
