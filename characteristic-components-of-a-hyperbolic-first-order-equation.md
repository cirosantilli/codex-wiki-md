# Characteristic components of a hyperbolic first-order equation

↑ **Parent:** [Method of characteristics](method-of-characteristics.md)

The characteristic [ordinary differential equations](ordinary-differential-equation.md) $\dot x=y$, $\dot y=x$ preserve $u=x^2-y^2$. Away from the origin, $u=x^2-y^2$, $v=2xy$ have nonzero [Jacobian determinant](jacobian-determinant.md) $4(x^2+y^2)$, and the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) reduces the displayed equation to $2(x^2+y^2)f_v=0$. Hence locally $f=F(u)$.

Globally, a [level set](level-set.md) of $u$ can have disconnected characteristic components, permitting different profiles on its different branches. A smooth counterexample on the whole plane is $f=\exp[-1/(x^2-y^2)^2]$ where $x>0$ and $x^2>y^2$, and $f=0$ elsewhere. The exponential is flat on the wedge boundary, so this extension is smooth and satisfies the equation. Nevertheless the points $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$ have equal $u$ and different function values. One global profile $F(u)$ therefore needs an additional branch-identification condition, rather than following from the local change of variables alone.
