# Finitely many upper and lower peaks prevent convergence

↑ **Parent:** [Peak of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md)

Suppose both $(a_n)$ and $(-a_n)$ have only finitely many [peaks of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md). Beyond some index $N$, every term has a strictly larger later term and a strictly smaller later term. Starting from $a_N$, construct one strictly increasing and one strictly decreasing [monotone subsequence](monotone-subsequence.md).

If the original [sequence](sequence.md) had [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) $L$, both selected [subsequences](subsequence.md) would tend to $L$. The increasing one has a second term strictly above $a_N$, so $L>a_N$; the decreasing one similarly forces $L<a_N$. This contradiction proves nonconvergence. For example, $a_n=(-1)^nn$ has no [peaks of a sequence](peak-of-a-sequence.md) in either sign and diverges.
