# Differentiability restored by a quadratic zero

↑ **Parent:** [Product rule](product-rule.md)

For a [continuous function](continuous-function.md) $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$, $g(t)=t^2f(t)$ is [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) at zero with $g'(0)=0$, because $[g(h)-g(0)]/h=hf(h)\to0$. At any nonzero point $x$, $g$ is [differentiable](differentiable-function.md) exactly when $f$ is: one direction uses the [product rule](product-rule.md), and the converse applies it to $f(t)=t^{-2}g(t)$ near $x$.
