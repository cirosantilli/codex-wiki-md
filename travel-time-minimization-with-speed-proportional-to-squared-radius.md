# Travel-time minimization with speed proportional to squared radius

↑ **Parent:** [Calculus of variations](calculus-of-variations-split.md)

For speed $v=kr^2$ in the punctured plane, the travel-time element is $ds/(kr^2)$. Under the [inversion in a circle](inversion-in-a-circle.md) with radial coordinate $u=1/r$, this becomes $k^{-1}\sqrt{du^2+u^2d\theta^2}$, the [arc length](arc-length.md) in [Euclidean space](euclidean-norm.md) divided by $k$. Thus a shortest travel-time route corresponds to a straight [line segment](line-segment.md) in the inverted plane, whenever that segment avoids the origin and lies in the allowed region. Between $(a,0)$ and $(0,a)$ the inverted segment has equation $u(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)=1/a$. The route is $r=a(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)$, and its minimum travel time is $\sqrt2/(ka)$. The [triangle inequality](triangle-inequality.md) for [arc length](arc-length.md) proves global minimality.
