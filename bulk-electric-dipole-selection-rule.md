# Bulk electric-dipole selection rule

↑ **Parent:** [Nonlinear optical susceptibility](nonlinear-optical-susceptibility.md)

Suppose a homogeneous bulk material is unchanged when all spatial coordinates are reversed. Both [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) and [electric field](electric-field-split.md) reverse under this inversion, so a local [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) response must obey $P(-E)=-P(E)$. Substituting the [nonlinear optical susceptibility](nonlinear-optical-susceptibility.md) expansion gives

$$
P(-E)+P(E)=2\epsilon_0\left(\chi^{(2)}E^2+\chi^{(4)}E^4+\cdots\right)=0.
$$

For every sufficiently small $E$, all even coefficients vanish. In the full [tensor](tensor.md) relation, two reversed input [electric fields](electric-field-split.md) leave their product unchanged while the output [electric polarization](polarization-density.md) changes sign, forcing every bulk electric-dipole component of $\chi^{(2)}$ to vanish.

The qualification matters: the argument assumes inversion [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md), a local bulk [electric dipole](electric-dipole.md) response, and no applied bias that breaks that [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md). A surface breaks bulk inversion [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md); spatially varying or magnetic responses also lie outside this local electric-dipole model. Thus detecting [second-harmonic generation](second-harmonic-generation.md) at a surface or through a weaker magnetic contribution does not contradict the bulk selection rule. Conversely, absence of inversion [symmetry](symmetry-physics.md) permits some quadratic components but does not guarantee a nonzero component in every beam geometry.
