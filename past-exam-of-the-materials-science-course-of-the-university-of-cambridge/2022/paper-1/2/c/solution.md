<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Each missing oxide ion leaves two units of excess positive effective charge, compensated by reducing two Ce$^{4+}$ ions to Ce$^{3+}$. For $0.2$ [oxygen vacancies](../../../../../oxygen-vacancy.md) per conventional [unit cell](../../../../../unit-cell.md), $0.4$ of its four cerium ions must be trivalent: **10% Ce$^{3+}$**. This assumes the same number of cation sites per unit [volume](../../../../../volume.md) as in the doped calculation and that oxygen loss is compensated entirely by this cerium-valence change. If reduction changes the cell [volume](../../../../../volume.md) appreciably, matching the absolute vacancy density requires correcting the percentage for that change.
