<h1 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose an objective aperture around a precipitate [superlattice reflection](../../../../../superlattice-reflection.md), such as $100$, which is absent from the disordered aluminium matrix. The selected beam then makes precipitates bright in [dark-field transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../dark-field-transmission-electron-microscopy.md), while the matrix gives little intensity in that beam. Tilt the grain to place the chosen reflection close to its [Bragg diffraction](../../../../../bragg-diffraction.md) condition and preferably approach a two-beam geometry. This reduces excitation error and can increase precipitate brightness; the exact maximum also depends on foil thickness, [strain](../../../../../strain.md) and dynamical scattering. Selecting a shared fundamental reflection would give much poorer chemical-order selectivity.
