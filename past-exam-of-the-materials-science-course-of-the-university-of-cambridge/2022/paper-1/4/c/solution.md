<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The lamellae are **[ferrite](../../../../../ferrite.md) and [cementite](../../../../../cementite.md)**, together forming [pearlite](../../../../../pearlite.md). The displayed quantity is surface height in an [atomic force microscopy](../../../../../atomic-force-microscopy.md) image. [Nital](../../../../../nital.md) preferentially attacks the [ferrite](../../../../../ferrite.md), leaving it lower and therefore darker; the narrow raised [cementite](../../../../../cementite.md) lamellae appear bright. This is not the same contrast mechanism as brightness in an optical micrograph. Faster cooling that still permits [pearlite](../../../../../pearlite.md) formation causes transformation at greater undercooling and generally gives **finer lamellar spacing**. Cooling sufficiently rapidly to bypass diffusional transformation instead produces other products, potentially [martensite](../../../../../martensite.md); it does not merely keep making [pearlite](../../../../../pearlite.md) indefinitely finer.
