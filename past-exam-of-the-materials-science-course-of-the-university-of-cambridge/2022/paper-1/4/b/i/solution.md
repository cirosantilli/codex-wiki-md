<h1 id="4/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

At $700^\circ\mathrm C$ the undercooling below the eutectoid [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) is small, so the free-energy driving [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) for forming [pearlite](../../../../../../pearlite.md) is weak and [nucleation](../../../../../../nucleation.md) is difficult. At $300^\circ\mathrm C$ the driving [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) is larger, but [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) [diffusion](../../../../../../diffusion.md) is slow, limiting the coupled growth of [ferrite](../../../../../../ferrite.md) and [cementite](../../../../../../cementite.md). Their competition produces the nose of the [isothermal transformation diagram](../../../../../../isothermal-transformation-diagram.md) near $550^\circ\mathrm C$ and $1\,\mathrm s$. A roughly linear quench from $740$–$800^\circ\mathrm C$ must pass left of this nose, requiring a rate of order **$2\times10^2$–$3\times10^2\,\mathrm{K\,s^{-1}}$**, then continue below the martensite-start region near $240^\circ\mathrm C$. The tangent to the full start curve, rather than just the nose, refines this estimate; a true continuous-cooling diagram would be preferable. [Martensite](../../../../../../martensite.md) forms by a coordinated lattice-shape change without long-range [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) partitioning, so its formation is a [displacive transformation](../../../../../../displacive-transformation.md). The tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation of [zirconia](../../../../../../zirconia.md) is a nonmetallic example.
