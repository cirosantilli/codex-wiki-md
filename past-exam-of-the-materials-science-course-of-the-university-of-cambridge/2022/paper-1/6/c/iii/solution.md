<h1 id="6/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

On a logarithmic strength–density [materials selection chart](../../../../../../materials-selection-chart.md), constant $M_A$ gives $\sigma_f=M_A\rho$, a guideline of slope one; constant $M_B$ gives $\sigma_f=M_B^2\rho^2$, of slope two. Moving each guideline upwards selects better candidates. **A suitably qualified longitudinal carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer is a promising candidate for both designs.** Composite families occupy favourable high-strength/low-density regions in published course selection charts, but a beam must meet its compressive as well as tensile allowable.[Unit 5, strength–density charts and composite selection examples; family comparisons are screening evidence, not universal longitudinal compression data](../../../../../../examples-of-materials-selection-by-wright-state-university.md)

For scale, an explicitly illustrative candidate with $\sigma_{\rm lim}=1\,\mathrm{GPa}$ in both required loading senses and $\rho=1600\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ would have $M_A=6.25\times10^5\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-2}}$ and $M_B=19.76\,\mathrm{Pa^{1/2}m^3kg^{-1}}$, exceeding the best supplied materials. These are assumed screening values, not measured properties of a specified product.
