<h1 id="6/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Both outer faces of the [cantilever](../../../../../../cantilever.md) are loaded, so use the pine's lower longitudinal [compressive strength](../../../../../../compressive-strength.md), $40\,\mathrm{MPa}$, rather than its $85\,\mathrm{MPa}$ [tensile strength](../../../../../../tensile-strength.md). Consistent SI units give $M_A$ values $(2.564,2.143,0.7273)\times10^5\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-2}}$ for steel, aluminium and pine respectively. Their $M_B$ values are $5.734,8.748,11.499\,\mathrm{Pa^{1/2}m^3kg^{-1}}$. Hence **Design A: steel best, pine worst; Design B: pine best, steel worst.** Aluminium is intermediate in both cases. These rankings concern the stated strength constraint; a [toughness](../../../../../../toughness.md) or deflection constraint could change feasibility.
