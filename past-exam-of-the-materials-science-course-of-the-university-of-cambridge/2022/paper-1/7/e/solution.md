<h1 id="7/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

The [coordination number](../../../../../coordination-number.md) rises from six in the [rock salt structure](../../../../../rock-salt-structure.md) to eight in the [CsCl structure](../../../../../caesium-chloride-structure.md). In the elementary hard-sphere [ionic bonding](../../../../../ionic-bonding-split.md) model, accommodating eight opposite neighbours favours a larger cation/anion radius ratio: the geometrical limiting ratios are $\sqrt2-1$ for sixfold and $\sqrt3-1$ for eightfold coordination. The high-pressure change is therefore consistent with $r_K/r_{Cl}$ increasing under compression. Since

$$
\frac{d}{dP}\ln\frac{r_K}{r_{Cl}}=\kappa_{r,Cl}-\kappa_{r,K},\qquad
\kappa_r=-\frac{d\ln r}{dP},
$$

**the chloride ion is inferred to be more compressible than the potassium ion** in this radius-ratio interpretation. This is a qualitative structural argument; real ionic radii depend on coordination and electronic structure, and the transition does not uniquely determine separate microscopic compressibilities.
