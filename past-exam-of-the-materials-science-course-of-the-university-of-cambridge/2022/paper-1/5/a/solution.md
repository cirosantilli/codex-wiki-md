<h1 id="5/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The exponential in the [Peierls stress](../../../../../peierls-stress.md) makes a large core-width-to-[Burgers vector](../../../../../burgers-vector.md) ratio $w/b$ favourable for easy [dislocation glide](../../../../../dislocation-glide.md). Densely packed slip planes often have wider cores because the shear distortion can spread over more atomic rows, reducing the lattice-periodic barrier. Close-packed directions provide short lattice translations and hence small allowed Burgers vectors; their line energies, approximately proportional to $Gb^2$, are also relatively low. These effects favour dense planes and directions, but the relation between packing and core width is a physical argument rather than a consequence of substituting arbitrary independent $w,b$ into the formula. Body-centred cubic metals have densely packed directions without a truly close-packed plane, and their screw-core behaviour can require additional qualifications.
