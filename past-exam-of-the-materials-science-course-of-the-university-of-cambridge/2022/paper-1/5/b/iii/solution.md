<h1 id="5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

[Burgers vector](../../../../../../burgers-vector.md) conservation gives two candidate reactions, depending on the relative signs of the approaching segments:

$$
\mathbf b_1+\mathbf b_2=a[001],\qquad
\mathbf b_1-\mathbf b_2=a[1\bar10].
$$

Initially $b_1^2+b_2^2=3a^2/2$. The sum product has $b^2=a^2$, while the difference product has $b^2=2a^2$. Thus **the $a[001]$ product is favoured by [Frank's rule](../../../../../../frank-s-rule.md); the $a[1\bar10]$ product is disfavoured**. The result is not annihilation, because the two supplied vectors are not opposite. The same ordering survives a simple character correction: with $\nu=0.30$, line energy proportional to $Gb^2[\cos^2\chi+\sin^2\chi/(1-\nu)]$ gives the two parent segments a combined coefficient $1.786a^2$, whereas any character of the sum product has coefficient between $a^2$ and $1.429a^2$ and any character of the difference product at least $2a^2$, comparing equal lengths and common logarithmic factors. A favourable [dislocation junction](../../../../../../dislocation-junction.md) can obstruct further glide and harden the crystal. Its actual mobility requires the junction line and allowed glide plane: the second screw can occupy several $\{110\}$ planes containing $[\bar111]$, so geometry is not unique. For instance, its $(101)$ plane intersects $(10\bar1)$ along $[010]$; an $a[001]$ junction on that line would glide geometrically on $(100)$, not either original plane. [Dislocation cross-slip](../../../../../../dislocation-cross-slip.md), junction unzipping or bypass can compete under load. An energy inequality alone is not a universal proof of a permanently sessile product.
