<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Interpret the supplied real quantity as the [storage modulus](../../../../../storage-modulus.md) $E'$, with $k$ the [spring](../../../../../spring.md) modulus in this continuum representation and $\eta$ the corresponding [viscosity](../../../../../dynamic-viscosity-split.md). Let $\tau=t_R=\eta/k$. Then

$$
E'=\frac{k\omega^2\tau^2}{1+\omega^2\tau^2}.
$$

**At high frequency, $\omega\tau\gg1$, the response approaches the [spring](../../../../../spring.md) stiffness; at low frequency it becomes much softer**:

$$
\boxed{E'\simeq k\quad(\omega\tau\gg1),\qquad
E'\simeq k\omega^2\tau^2=\frac{\eta^2\omega^2}{k}
\quad(\omega\tau\ll1).}
$$

In the series [Maxwell model](../../../../../linear-maxwell-fluid.md), the [spring](../../../../../spring.md) and [dashpot](../../../../../dashpot.md) carry the same [stress](../../../../../stress.md) while their strains add. A rapidly changing load allows little viscous flow, so the [spring](../../../../../spring.md) dominates the [strain](../../../../../strain.md) response. Over a long cycle the [dashpot](../../../../../dashpot.md) has time to deform, giving a small [storage modulus](../../../../../storage-modulus.md) and a fluid-like low-frequency response. This is generic viscoelastic mechanics, independent of the biological parts deferred above.
