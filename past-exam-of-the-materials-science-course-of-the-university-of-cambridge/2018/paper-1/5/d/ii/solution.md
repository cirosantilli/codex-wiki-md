↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For Ti–6Al–4V, use its own [fracture toughness](../../../../../../fracture-toughness.md) and yield [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) in

$$
\boxed{t_{\max,\rm Ti}=\frac1\pi
\left(\frac{K_{IC,\rm Ti}}{Y\sigma_{y,\rm Ti}}\right)^2,
\qquad t_{\rm yield,\rm Ti}\simeq\frac{7\,\mathrm{MPa\,m}}{\sigma_{y,\rm Ti}}.}
$$

The same unit-conversion formulas from part(i) apply. A feasible yield-limited screening interval requires $t_{\rm yield}<t_{\max}$, using consistent crack geometry and low-temperature properties.

High specific strength and corrosion resistance can be attractive, but cost, fabrication, joining and toughness must also be assessed. The label HCP is not a sufficient rule that every [titanium](../../../../../../titanium.md) alloy has the same sharp [ductile-to-brittle transition](../../../../../../ductile-to-brittle-transition.md) as ferritic [steel](../../../../../../steel.md); actual low-temperature alloy and microstructural data are needed.
