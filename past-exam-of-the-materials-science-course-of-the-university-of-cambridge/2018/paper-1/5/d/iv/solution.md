<h1 id="5/d/iv/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](../iv.md)

For high-strength [steel](../../../../../../steel.md), use

$$
\boxed{t_{\max,\rm high}=\frac1\pi
\left(\frac{K_{IC,\rm high}}{Y\sigma_{y,\rm high}}\right)^2,
\qquad t_{\rm yield,\rm high}\simeq\frac{7\,\mathrm{MPa\,m}}{\sigma_{y,\rm high}}.}
$$

At fixed toughness, raising yield [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) reduces $t_{\max}$ quadratically but reduces the required pressure thickness only linearly. Thus a higher-strength grade can have a worse leak-before-break screening margin. The relevant screening combination is $K_{IC}^2/\sigma_y$, since simultaneous satisfaction requires

$$
\boxed{\frac{K_{IC}^2}{\sigma_y}>\pi Y^2Pr
\simeq22.0Y^2\,\mathrm{MPa\,m}.}
$$

Use measured low-temperature toughness and appropriate joining/environmental performance; strength alone does not establish suitability. The comparison also has to remain within the thin-wall approximation.
