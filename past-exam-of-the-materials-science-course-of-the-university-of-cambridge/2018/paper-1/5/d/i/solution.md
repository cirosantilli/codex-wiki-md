For the [aluminium](../../../../../../aluminium.md) alloy, write $K_{IC,\rm Al}$ and $\sigma_{y,\rm Al}$ for the appropriate data-book properties at the relevant material state and [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). The yield-limited screening bounds from part(c), with $r\simeq0.70\,\mathrm m$, are

$$
\boxed{t_{\max,\rm Al}=\frac1\pi
\left(\frac{K_{IC,\rm Al}}{Y\sigma_{y,\rm Al}}\right)^2,
\qquad t_{\rm yield,\rm Al}\simeq\frac{7\,\mathrm{MPa\,m}}{\sigma_{y,\rm Al}}.}
$$

For $K$ in $\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m}$ and $\sigma_y$ in $\mathrm{MPa}$, the numerical substitutions are $t_{\max}(\mathrm{mm})=318.31[K/(Y\sigma_y)]^2$ and $t_{\rm yield}(\mathrm{mm})=7000/\sigma_y$.

A high-strength alloy's large yield [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) can require a small allowable leak-before-break thickness even when its minimum pressure thickness is modest. FCC structure generally favors low-temperature plasticity, but does not by itself establish the alloy's [fracture toughness](../../../../../../fracture-toughness.md), weld performance, corrosion behavior or fatigue resistance.
