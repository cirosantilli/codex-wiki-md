For mild [steel](../../../../../../steel.md), the corresponding bounds are

$$
\boxed{t_{\max,\rm mild}=\frac1\pi
\left(\frac{K_{IC,\rm mild}}{Y\sigma_{y,\rm mild}}\right)^2,
\qquad t_{\rm yield,\rm mild}\simeq\frac{7\,\mathrm{MPa\,m}}{\sigma_{y,\rm mild}}.}
$$

A relatively low yield [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) increases the thickness needed to carry pressure, but a favorable toughness-to-yield-strength ratio can give a much larger leak-before-break upper bound. This tradeoff often makes a tough structural [steel](../../../../../../steel.md) more attractive than choosing the highest strength alone.

For Arctic service, the [ductile-to-brittle transition](../../../../../../ductile-to-brittle-transition.md) is crucial. A room-temperature toughness cannot be assumed to persist below the transition, and welding, constraint and loading rate affect the comparison. A suitably qualified low-temperature [steel](../../../../../../steel.md) can be practical; the word mild alone does not establish that qualification.
