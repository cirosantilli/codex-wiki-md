A [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is a set of equivalent points generated by integer combinations of three independent translation vectors. A [crystal motif](../../../../../crystal-motif.md) is the arrangement of atoms attached identically to every [lattice point](../../../../../lattice-point.md). Together they specify the periodic [crystal structure](../../../../../crystal-structure.md): lattice translations repeat the motif without changing its internal arrangement.

A [unit cell](../../../../../unit-cell.md) is a region whose translations tile the structure. A [primitive unit cell](../../../../../primitive-unit-cell.md) contains one [lattice point](../../../../../lattice-point.md) after sharing boundary points with neighboring cells. A conventional cell can contain more than one [lattice point](../../../../../lattice-point.md) to display the crystal's symmetry with simple axes and angles; for example, a conventional face-centered cube has four lattice points but a less visually symmetric primitive cell has one.

**Rotational symmetry compatible with a periodic three-dimensional lattice has order $1,2,3,4$ or $6$**, as expressed by the [crystallographic restriction theorem](../../../../../crystallographic-restriction-theorem.md). A crystallographic fivefold axis is excluded from an ordinary periodic crystal. This statement concerns exact lattice symmetry; it does not exclude local fivefold atomic arrangements or nonperiodic ordered structures.
