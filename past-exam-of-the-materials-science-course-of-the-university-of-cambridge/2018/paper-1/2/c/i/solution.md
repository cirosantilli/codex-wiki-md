<h1 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

**If removal restores the original random-domain magnetic state, the [magnetostrictive strain](../../../../../../magnetostrictive-strain.md) and induced [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) return toward their original values.** Elastic [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) transfer then relaxes the [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) on the central layer.

A ferromagnetic [nickel](../../../../../../nickel.md) layer can retain [remanent magnetization](../../../../../../remanent-magnetization.md) and an altered domain population, so removing the external field does not guarantee removal of all [magnetostriction](../../../../../../magnetostriction.md). The electrical time response also depends on leakage and the input impedance of the voltmeter: an open-circuit strain-induced [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) can decay through a finite resistance even while some [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) remains. The final observed [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) is therefore history- and circuit-dependent.
