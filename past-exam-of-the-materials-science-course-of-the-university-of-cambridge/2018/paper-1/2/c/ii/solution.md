<h1 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**Reversing a saturating longitudinal field gives approximately the same [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) and the same [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md) polarity relative to the original unmagnetized reference.** In the isotropic orientational model,

$$
\varepsilon^{\rm mag}_{ij}=\frac{3\Lambda}{2}
\left(m_im_j-\frac{\delta_{ij}}3\right).
$$

It is unchanged under $\mathbf m\to-\mathbf m$. Both positive and negative longitudinal saturation therefore contract the negative-magnetostriction [nickel](../../../../../../nickel.md) in the same axial direction. The resulting [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) on the piezoelectric layer has the same sign.

During field reversal there can be a hysteretic transient as domains reorganize, and finite circuit leakage can alter the measured waveform. Those effects do not turn the saturated [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) law into a response odd in the field.
