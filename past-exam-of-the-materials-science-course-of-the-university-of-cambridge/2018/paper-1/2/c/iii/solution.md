<h1 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Assume that the transverse field is large enough to saturate through the thickness despite demagnetizing effects, and retain the thin-layer plane-stress and open-circuit assumptions from part(a). The two imposed in-plane strains now become

$$
\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_y=-\Lambda/2=2\times10^{-5}.
$$

Their sum is $-\Lambda$, whereas it was $\Lambda/2$ for longitudinal saturation. The in-plane [stress](../../../../../../stress.md) sum, and hence the induced open-circuit [voltage](../../../../../../voltage.md), is multiplied by $-2$:

$$
\boxed{V_{\rm thickness}\simeq-2V_{\rm longitudinal}.}
$$

Thus **the magnitude is about $24\,\mathrm{mV}$ with the opposite polarity**, under this model. The larger demagnetizing field for an out-of-plane direction means the same external field magnitude might not produce the same degree of saturation. Changes in thickness constraint or [strain](../../../../../../strain.md) transfer would also modify the estimate.
