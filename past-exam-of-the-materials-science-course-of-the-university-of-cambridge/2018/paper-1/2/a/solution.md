<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) is a mechanically mediated effect: [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) [magnetostriction](../../../../../magnetostriction.md) strains the bonded central layer, and the direct [piezoelectric effect](../../../../../direct-piezoelectric-effect.md) converts [stress](../../../../../stress.md) into electrical displacement. **An estimate of $|d_{31}|$ requires a strain-transfer and electrical-boundary assumption.**

Let $x$ run along the applied field and $z$ through the layers. Assume saturated, approximately volume-preserving isotropic [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) [magnetostriction](../../../../../magnetostriction.md), perfect bonding, mechanically dominant [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) faces and a high-impedance [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) measurement. Relative to the random-domain state,

$$
\varepsilon_x=\Lambda=-4\times10^{-5},\qquad
\varepsilon_y=-\Lambda/2=2\times10^{-5}.
$$

Treat the thin central layer as in [plane stress](../../../../../plane-stress.md), with these imposed in-plane strains, negligible converse-effect backreaction and isotropic elastic constants. Then

$$
\sigma_x=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\varepsilon_x+\nu\varepsilon_y),
\quad
\sigma_y=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\varepsilon_y+\nu\varepsilon_x),
$$

so

$$
\sigma_x+\sigma_y=\frac{E(\varepsilon_x+\varepsilon_y)}{1-\nu}
=\frac{E\Lambda}{2(1-\nu)}=-1.80\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$

For a transversely polarized layer,

$$
D_3=d_{31}(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)+\epsilon_0\kappa E_3.
$$

The open-circuit increment has $\Delta D_3\simeq0$, and $|E_3|=|V|/t=800\,\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$. Hence

$$
\boxed{|d_{31}|\simeq
\frac{\epsilon_0\kappa|V|}{t|\sigma_x+\sigma_y|}
=6.69\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C\,N^{-1}}
=6.69\,\mathrm{pC\,N^{-1}}.}
$$

The observed [voltage](../../../../../voltage.md) magnitude does not specify the sign without electrode polarity and poling direction. A one-dimensional approximation retaining only axial [stress](../../../../../stress.md) $E\Lambda$ instead gives $4.78\,\mathrm{pC\,N^{-1}}$. That is a different mechanical model. [Nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) stiffness and thickness, interface compliance, central-layer tensor coefficients and electrical loading are not specified, so **the experiment does not determine a unique exact intrinsic coefficient without these assumptions**.
