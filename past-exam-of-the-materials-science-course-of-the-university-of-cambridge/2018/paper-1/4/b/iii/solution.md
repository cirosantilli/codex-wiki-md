↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

**The profile estimate and equilibrium lever-rule estimate need not agree exactly.** The height trace contains only four major ridges, making it a small sample of the microstructure. Pixel size, ridge-threshold choice, tip geometry, contamination, polishing damage and unequal etching change apparent widths. A finite-radius AFM tip can broaden the narrow raised [cementite](../../../../../../cementite.md) regions, biasing their inferred fraction upward.

The equilibrium calculation assumes eutectoid [pearlite](../../../../../../pearlite.md) with the chosen carbon composition and tie-line values. Off-eutectoid [steel](../../../../../../steel.md) can include proeutectoid [ferrite](../../../../../../ferrite.md) or [cementite](../../../../../../cementite.md) outside the [pearlite](../../../../../../pearlite.md); alloying, carbon segregation and non-equilibrium cooling alter the simple Fe–C estimate. Carbon solubility also varies with [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). The mass-to-volume conversion requires phase densities.

The observed difference between roughly $17\%$ ridge fraction and $11\%$ equilibrium phase volume is therefore a reason to examine sampling and the etched-surface measurement, not to [force](../../../../../../force-split.md) the image widths to match the [lever rule](../../../../../../lever-rule.md). Multiple colonies, repeated calibrated traces and a phase-sensitive measurement would improve the comparison.
