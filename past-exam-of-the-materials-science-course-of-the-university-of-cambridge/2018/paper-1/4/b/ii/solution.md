<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For eutectoid [pearlite](../../../../../../pearlite.md), apply the [lever rule](../../../../../../lever-rule.md) between [ferrite](../../../../../../ferrite.md) and [cementite](../../../../../../cementite.md) at the transformation [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md). Using standard approximate Fe–C values $C_e=0.76$, $C_\alpha=0.022$ and $C_{\rm cem}=6.67$ in weight percent carbon,

$$
\boxed{w_{\rm cem}=\frac{C_e-C_\alpha}{C_{\rm cem}-C_\alpha}
=\frac{0.76-0.022}{6.67-0.022}\simeq0.111,
\qquad w_\alpha\simeq0.889.}
$$

These are **mass fractions**. To compare with an unbiased geometric line fraction, convert using the phase densities:

$$
f_{\rm cem}=\frac{w_{\rm cem}/\rho_{\rm cem}}
{w_{\rm cem}/\rho_{\rm cem}+(1-w_{\rm cem})/\rho_\alpha}.
$$

With representative densities $\rho_{\rm cem}\simeq7.69$ and $\rho_\alpha\simeq7.87\,\mathrm{Mg\,m^{-3}}$, this gives about **$11.3\,\mathrm{vol\%}$ [cementite](../../../../../../cementite.md) and $88.7\,\mathrm{vol\%}$ [ferrite](../../../../../../ferrite.md)**. Because the densities are similar, the mass and volume fractions differ only modestly. The phase-diagram values here are conventional approximations, not a claim that the exact 2018 Data Book has been recovered.
