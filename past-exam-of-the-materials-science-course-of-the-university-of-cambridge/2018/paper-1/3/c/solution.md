↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**Sample B should show two FCC peak families**, one from a copper-rich phase and one from a silver-rich phase. Their lattice parameters are set by the compositions of the coexisting phases, not by one [lattice parameter](../../../../../lattice-constant.md) at the overall alloy composition.

For corresponding [Miller indices](../../../../../miller-index.md) the silver-rich phase has a larger [lattice parameter](../../../../../lattice-constant.md) and hence a larger interplanar spacing, so its reflection occurs at smaller $2\theta$. Some reflections may overlap or be weak. Relative intensities depend on phase fractions, atomic scattering factors, absorption and texture; a raw peak-height ratio is not directly a [mass fraction](../../../../../mass-fraction.md).

At room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) the mutual solubilities in the supplied Cu–Ag diagram are small, so the two families would be near the elemental copper and silver positions. Slow cooling is important: a metastable supersaturated single phase would be a different interpretation. Quantitative phase or composition analysis would require calibrated [diffraction](../../../../../diffraction.md) and an appropriate refinement, not merely counting the visible peaks.
