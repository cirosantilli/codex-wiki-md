↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**SEM with [energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy](../../../../../energy-dispersive-x-ray-spectroscopy.md)** can identify [gold](../../../../../gold.md) versus silver through their characteristic X-ray energies. Acquire spectra and maps from representative regions: two compositionally distinct regions would support the two-phase interpretation of B. With standards or validated matrix corrections it can estimate local compositions and, with representative sampling, an overall composition. Surface topography and the interaction volume limit spatial interpretation.

**[X-ray fluorescence](../../../../../x-ray-fluorescence.md)** provides a complementary more bulk-averaged elemental measurement. [Gold](../../../../../gold.md) and silver produce different characteristic lines; calibrated intensities can yield their mass fractions, with corrections for absorption, excitation and specimen geometry. It does not by itself determine whether an element occupies one or two crystallographic phases.

Both methods supply elemental information missing from a lattice-parameter-only identification. A purely morphological micrograph could reveal phase separation but would not alone give a reliable numerical chemical composition.
