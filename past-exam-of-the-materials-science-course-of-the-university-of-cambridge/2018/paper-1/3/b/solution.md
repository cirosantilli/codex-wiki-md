<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

**Sample A is the Cu–Au alloy.** At the indicated copper-rich composition, the supplied Cu–Au diagram permits one copper-rich solid-solution phase. The Cu–Ag diagram instead gives two equilibrium solid phases at room [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). A single consistent set of FCC peaks therefore identifies A under the stated equilibrium assumptions; similar pure-metal lattice parameters alone would not distinguish [gold](../../../../../gold.md) from silver.

Use [Vegard's law](../../../../../vegard-s-law.md) as an approximate atomic-fraction interpolation:

$$
a\simeq(1-x_{\rm Au})a_{\rm Cu}+x_{\rm Au}a_{\rm Au}.
$$

With the higher-angle estimate from part(a),

$$
x_{\rm Au}=\frac{0.363857-0.3615}{0.4078-0.3615}\simeq0.0509.
$$

This is about $5.1$ atomic percent, not weight percent. Using rounded molar masses $M_{\rm Au}\simeq197$ and $M_{\rm Cu}\simeq63.55\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$,

$$
\boxed{w_{\rm Au}=\frac{x_{\rm Au}M_{\rm Au}}
{x_{\rm Au}M_{\rm Au}+(1-x_{\rm Au})M_{\rm Cu}}
\simeq0.143,\quad\text{about }14\,\mathrm{wt\%\ Au}.}
$$

Using the first peak alone instead gives roughly $12.6\,\mathrm{wt\%}$. The justified conclusion is therefore about **$13$–$14\,\mathrm{wt\%}$ [gold](../../../../../gold.md)**, with uncertainty from angle rounding, calibration and the approximate linear lattice-composition relation.
