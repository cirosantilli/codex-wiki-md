<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

[Orowan bypass](../../../../../orowan-bypass.md) bows a [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) around an unshearable obstacle and leaves a loop. A [dislocation line-tension estimate](../../../../../dislocation-line-tension-estimate.md) for a segment with tension $T_\ell$ gives the limiting shear-stress scale

$$
\Delta\tau\simeq\frac{2T_\ell}{b\ell},
$$

where $\ell$ is a free obstacle gap on the slip plane. For an FCC perfect [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md),

$$
b=\frac a{\sqrt2}
=\frac{0.36}{\sqrt2}\ {\rm nm}
\simeq0.2546\ {\rm nm}.
$$

Using $T_\ell\simeq Gb^2/2$ and the simple gap estimate $\ell\simeq L-d\simeq0.34\ {\rm \mu m}$ gives

$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau\simeq\frac{Gb}{\ell}
\simeq5.5\times10^7\ {\rm Pa}.}
$$

Converting to tensile [stress](../../../../../stress.md) requires an orientation factor. With an explicitly assumed polycrystalline FCC [Taylor factor](../../../../../taylor-factor.md) $M\simeq3$,

$$
\boxed{\Delta\sigma_y\simeq M\Delta\tau
\sim(1.6\text{--}1.7)\times10^8\ {\rm Pa}\approx160\text{--}170\ {\rm MPa}.}
$$

The graph and spacing approximations do not justify a precise last digit. A favorably oriented single crystal with Schmid factor1/2 would instead give $\Delta\sigma_y\simeq2\Delta\tau$, around110MPa.

This is a line-tension estimate. A true slip-plane obstacle distribution, line character, logarithmic elastic corrections and [precipitate](../../../../../precipitate.md) size distribution are not specified. If the quoted pitch is used directly as an effective free spacing, neglecting diameter, the same simplified calculation gives $\Delta\tau\simeq19\ {\rm MPa}$ and $M\Delta\tau\simeq57\ {\rm MPa}$. The larger gap-corrected estimate explicitly uses the size inferred above; the tensile factor and spacing convention must not be silently treated as provided measurements.
