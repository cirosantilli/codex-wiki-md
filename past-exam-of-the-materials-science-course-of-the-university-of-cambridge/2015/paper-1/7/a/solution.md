<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

At two-phase equilibrium, the endpoints of a common tangent to the two molar free-energy curves give the coexisting compositions. They are not the separate curve minima. Reading the graph gives approximately $x_{B,\alpha}\simeq0.02$ and $x_{B,m}\simeq0.64$, with a few atomic-percent graphical uncertainty.

Let $f_\alpha$ be the fraction of atoms in the [precipitate](../../../../../precipitate.md) [thermodynamic phase](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md). Conservation of the overall B fraction gives

$$
0.55=f_\alpha x_{B,\alpha}+
(1-f_\alpha)x_{B,m},
$$

and hence

$$
\boxed{f_\alpha=\frac{x_{B,m}-0.55}
{x_{B,m}-x_{B,\alpha}}
\simeq\frac{0.64-0.55}{0.64-0.02}
\simeq0.145\approx15\%.}
$$

About85percent remains in the B-richer matrix. Smooth readings of the original curves put the result near14–15percent; the graph does not justify many significant figures. The result is an atomic or molar [thermodynamic phase](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md) fraction. Its identification with a [volume fraction](../../../../../volume-fraction.md) in the next estimate requires comparable atomic volumes of the two [thermodynamic phases](../../../../../thermodynamic-phase.md).
