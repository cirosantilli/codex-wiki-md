<h1 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the supplied Arrhenius law as a diffusion-scale calculation. The stated temperature and duration become $T=573.15\ {\rm K}$ and $t=10800\ {\rm s}$. With $R=8.31446\ {\rm J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$,

$$
D=2\times10^{-6}
\exp\left[-\frac{84138}{(8.31446)(573.15)}\right]
\simeq4.30\times10^{-14}\ {\rm m^2\,s^{-1}}.
$$

With the standard [diffusion-distance convention](../../../../../../diffusion-distance-convention.md), the characteristic scale is

$$
\boxed{\ell_D=\sqrt{Dt}\simeq2.15\times10^{-5}\ {\rm m}
\simeq22\ {\rm \mu m}.}
$$

The one-dimensional rms displacement $\sqrt{2Dt}$ is about30micrometres, and the three-dimensional rms displacement $\sqrt{6Dt}$ about53micrometres. These different prefactors follow from the [diffusion-distance convention](../../../../../../diffusion-distance-convention.md); the [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md) is unchanged.

The scale is much larger than atomic spacing and can permit local [carbon](../../../../../../carbon.md) redistribution and carbide formation over microstructural distances. It is not evidence that the entire macroscopic specimen becomes compositionally homogeneous. Trapping, concentration gradients, evolving carbides and the use of a [ferrite](../../../../../../ferrite.md) diffusivity for an initially defect-rich transformation product limit a literal tracer-distance interpretation.
