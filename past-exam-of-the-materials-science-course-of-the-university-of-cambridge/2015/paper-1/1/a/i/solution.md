<h1 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [vertical mirrors of the diamond cubic structure](../../../../../../vertical-mirrors-of-the-diamond-cubic-structure.md) have the required orientation: their normals lie in the $(001)$ plane. In the chosen atomic-origin convention, their traces satisfy

$$
\boxed{x-y=\frac n2,\qquad x+y=\frac n2,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,}
$$

with $x,y$ expressed as fractions of $a$. These are the two orientations of vertical $\{110\}$ mirrors and their translated copies. For example, the plane $x=y$ interchanges the first two coordinates and leaves every [diamond](../../../../../../diamond.md) site in an equivalent site. [Reflection](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md) in $x+y=1/2$ sends $(x,y,z)$ to $(1/2-y,1/2-x,z)$, which also preserves the full three-dimensional motif.

The projected traces alone do not test a mirror: the atom heights must also agree under [reflection](../../../../../../reflection-mathematics.md). In particular, a vertical $\{100\}$ trace can appear plausible in an unlabelled projection but does not preserve all heights as a pure mirror; glide operations are different symmetry elements.
