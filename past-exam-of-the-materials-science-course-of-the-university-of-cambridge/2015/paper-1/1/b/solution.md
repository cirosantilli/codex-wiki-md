<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The nearest-neighbor displacement is $a[111]/4$, of length $a\sqrt3/4$. Touching [hard spheres](../../../../../hard-sphere.md) therefore obey

$$
2r_{\rm atom}=\frac{\sqrt3a}{4},\qquad
r_{\rm atom}=\frac{\sqrt3a}{8}.
$$

There are eight atoms per conventional cubic cell, giving

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm diamond}=
\frac{8(4\pi r_{\rm atom}^3/3)}{a^3}
=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{16}\simeq0.3401.}
$$

For monatomic FCC close packing, four atoms per cell touch along a face diagonal, so $r_{\rm atom}=a/(2\sqrt2)$ and

$$
\boxed{f_{\rm ccp}=\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2}\simeq0.7405.}
$$

Thus the [diamond](../../../../../diamond.md) [packing fraction](../../../../../packing-fraction.md) is much smaller. The tetrahedral network leaves substantial open volume. Although its [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is FCC, the two-atom motif and the shorter hard-sphere contact distance prevent it from inheriting the FCC close-packing fraction.
