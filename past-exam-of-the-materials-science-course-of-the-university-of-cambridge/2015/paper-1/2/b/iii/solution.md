<h1 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The [quartz compensator](../../../../../../quartz-compensator.md) is a [birefringent optical compensator](../../../../../../birefringent-optical-compensator.md): a retarder of known principal directions. With its [principal axes](../../../../../../principal-axis.md) at45degrees to the [polarizer](../../../../../../polarizer.md) and analyser, the previously dark isotropic background acquires the compensator's own [interference colour](../../../../../../interference-colour.md). The crystals are now viewed through two retarders.

When a specimen's slow axis is parallel to the compensator's slow axis, the retardations add. A90degree relative principal-axis rotation pairs the specimen's slow axis with the compensator's fast axis, and the signed retardations subtract:

$$
\Gamma_{\rm total}=\Gamma_q+\Gamma_s
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
\Gamma_{\rm total}=\Gamma_q-\Gamma_s.
$$

Thus different crystallites can shift to higher or lower [interference](../../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) orders even if their original hue was similar. In the subtracting orientation, equal magnitudes can compensate and yield extinction. These shifts identify fast and slow specimen directions.

At a general relative angle the full [polarization](../../../../../../polarization-waves.md) transformation is required; simple scalar addition is only valid for aligned [principal axes](../../../../../../principal-axis.md). The source does not specify the compensator retardation, so no unique list of resulting named colours can be assigned. The robust predictions are a changed background, orientation-dependent addition or subtraction, and possible compensation.
