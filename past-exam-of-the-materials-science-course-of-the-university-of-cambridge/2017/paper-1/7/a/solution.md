<h1 id="7/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [heat engine](../../../../../heat-engine.md) must reject some absorbed heat to a colder reservoir. For heat addition and rejection at absolute [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md) $T_h$ and $T_c$, the Carnot bound is

$$
\eta\le1-\frac{T_c}{T_h}.
$$

At fixed $T_c$, increasing $T_h$ increases this upper limit and can increase useful specific work in an appropriately designed gas-turbine cycle. Thus **a higher hot-side [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) permits more efficient conversion of heat to work**, rather than merely heating the exhaust. Real combustion occurs over a [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) range, and actual efficiency also depends on pressure ratio, losses and cycle design: peak [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) alone does not determine it. The materials incentive is to sustain higher turbine inlet [temperatures](../../../../../temperature-split.md) while maintaining strength, [oxidation](../../../../../oxidation.md) resistance and acceptable [creep](../../../../../creep-deformation.md) life.
