<h1 id="7/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The nickel-rich $\gamma$ matrix is a disordered substitutional [solid solution](../../../../../solid-solution.md) on a [face-centered cubic lattice](../../../../../face-centered-cubic-lattice.md). The ordered $\gamma^{\prime}$ phase has the [L12 structure](../../../../../l12-structure.md), approximately $\mathrm{Ni_3Al}$: [aluminium](../../../../../aluminium.md) occupies [cube](../../../../../cube.md) corners and [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) occupies face centres. Its occupied-site geometry is often called ordered FCC, but **its chemically labelled [Bravais lattice](../../../../../bravais-lattice.md) is primitive cubic**, with a four-atom basis. A face-centre [translation](../../../../../translation-geometry.md) exchanges chemical sublattices and is not a symmetry of the ordered crystal.

Each Al atom has twelve nearest-neighbour Ni atoms at [Euclidean distance](../../../../../euclidean-distance.md) $a/\sqrt2$. Each Ni atom has twelve nearest neighbours too, but these are **four Al and eight Ni**. The chemical distinction matters for slip. In disordered $\gamma$, the shortest perfect [Burgers vector](../../../../../burgers-vector.md) is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf b_\gamma=\frac a2\langle110\rangle,\qquad |\mathbf b_\gamma|=a/\sqrt2.}
$$

For the ordered primitive cubic [translation](../../../../../translation-geometry.md) lattice, the shortest order-preserving perfect vector is

$$
\boxed{\mathbf b_{\gamma^{\prime},\min}=a\langle100\rangle,\qquad |\mathbf b|=a.}
$$

This does not imply that $\langle100\rangle$ slip must dominate. A vector must lie in a favourable glide plane and have an accessible core structure and activation [stress](../../../../../stress.md). Matrix [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) commonly glide on $\{111\}\langle110\rangle$ systems. A single $a\langle110\rangle/2$ passage through $\gamma^{\prime}$ leaves an [antiphase boundary](../../../../../antiphase-boundary.md); a second matching passage restores order, giving total vector $a\langle110\rangle$. Such a [superdislocation](../../../../../superdislocation.md) has a longer total vector than $a\langle100\rangle$, yet its constituent [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) can move on the favourable close-packed planes and interact with the matrix. Paired motion balances line and antiphase-boundary energies, so the shortest abstract lattice [translation](../../../../../translation-geometry.md) alone does not select the active [slip system](../../../../../slip-system.md).[explanation of matrix translations, ordered-phase antiphase boundaries and paired dislocations](../../../../../nickel-based-superalloy-dislocation-structure-by-h-k-d-h-bhadeshia.md)
