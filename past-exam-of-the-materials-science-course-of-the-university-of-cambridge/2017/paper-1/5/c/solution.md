<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Let $V_c$ be the carbon-fibre [volume fraction](../../../../../volume-fraction.md) of the whole composite. Then $V_m=0.4$ and $V_g=0.6-V_c$. Under the same [longitudinal rule of mixtures](../../../../../longitudinal-rule-of-mixtures.md),

$$
E=3(0.4)+75(0.6-V_c)+220V_c=46.2+145V_c\quad\mathrm{GPa}.
$$

Setting $E=71.94\,\mathrm{GPa}$ gives

$$
\boxed{V_c=0.1775,\qquad V_g=0.4225,\qquad V_m=0.4000.}
$$

Thus carbon occupies **17.8% of the complete composite**, or about $29.6\%$ of its total fibre [volume](../../../../../volume.md). The resulting $60\%$ total fibre fraction is much more plausible than the glass-only estimate. This remains an ideal stiffness calculation requiring aligned, effectively loaded fibres; strength and fatigue checks are separate.
