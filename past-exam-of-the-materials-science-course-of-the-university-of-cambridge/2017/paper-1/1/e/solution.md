<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A larger [barium](../../../../../barium.md) [cation](../../../../../cation.md) expands the [fluorite structure](../../../../../fluorite-structure.md) and can widen the constrictions through which [fluoride](../../../../../fluoride.md) [ions](../../../../../ion.md) move. A plausible explanation is therefore a smaller [ionic migration barrier](../../../../../ionic-migration-barrier.md) and greater [fluoride](../../../../../fluoride.md) mobility. In a simple dilute-carrier description, $\sigma_{\rm ion}=n|q|\mu$, or $nq^2D/(k_BT)$ when an uncorrelated [Nernst-Einstein relation](../../../../../nernst-einstein-relation.md) applies. [fluoride](../../../../../fluoride.md) [vacancies](../../../../../vacancy.md) and [interstitial defects](../../../../../interstitial-defect.md), including those generated as [Frenkel pairs](../../../../../frenkel-pair.md), provide possible carriers.

**More open migration pathways can raise the conductivity**, but [ion](../../../../../ion.md) size by itself is not a proof: defect formation [free energies](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) set $n$, migration [free energies](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md) set mobility, and correlated hopping can alter the simple relation. A controlled comparison would use crystals with comparable impurity content, thermal history and [electrode](../../../../../electrode.md) conditions, and measure conductivity over [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) to separate plausible activation behaviour from extrinsic defect effects.
