<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

The actual [ionic radii](../../../../../ionic-radius.md) need not equal the limiting ratio, and electronic charge distributions are not rigid [sphere](../../../../../sphere.md) boundaries. In the same unlike-contact [hard-sphere model](../../../../../hard-sphere-model.md), put $q=r_a/r_c$ and use $a=4(r_a+r_c)/\sqrt3$ to get

$$
\eta(q)=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{4}\frac{1+2q^3}{(1+q)^3}.
$$

A [cation](../../../../../cation.md) somewhat larger relative to the [anion](../../../../../anion.md) than the limiting size opens gaps between the [anions](../../../../../anion.md). Starting from $q=1.366$ and reducing $q$ modestly reduces this [packing fraction](../../../../../packing-fraction.md); thus **a lower value is expected for that departure from the ideal contact construction**. The formula is not monotonic for all imaginable ratios, so no universal inequality should be inferred without specifying [radii](../../../../../radius.md). Coordination-dependent [ionic radii](../../../../../ionic-radius.md), [polarizability](../../../../../electric-polarizability.md), nonionic contributions to bonding, [thermal expansion](../../../../../thermal-expansion.md) and [point defects](../../../../../point-defect.md) also limit the model. No numerical real-crystal [packing fraction](../../../../../packing-fraction.md) is determined by the supplied data alone.
