<h1 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A heated [cathode](../../../../../cathode.md) emits [electrons](../../../../../electron.md), accelerated through a potential difference onto a metal target. Deceleration produces a [bremsstrahlung](../../../../../bremsstrahlung.md) continuum with $\lambda_{\min}=hc/(eV_{\rm acc})$; inner-shell ionization followed by electronic relaxation produces characteristic lines. The pictured instrument is consistent with the copper-target TEL-X-Ometer used for teaching diffraction. Its [copper](../../../../../copper.md) $K_\alpha$ radiation has wavelength about $0.154\,\mathrm{nm}$, comparable with atomic plane spacings. A [nickel](../../../../../nickel.md) filter preferentially suppresses [copper](../../../../../copper.md) $K_\beta$, while slits collimate the beam; filtering does not remove every continuum [photon](../../../../../photon.md).

A [Geiger-Müller counter](../../../../../geiger-counter.md) converts photon-induced gas ionization into countable electrical pulses. A scintillator with [photomultiplier](../../../../../photomultiplier.md) is another detector option. A detector-arm scan records count rate against scattering angle; coupled crystal rotation preserves the usual reflection geometry. These instrument features are documented by its manufacturer.[Sections 3.5–3.6 and 7.1: crystal/detector geometry and detection; section 4.6: copper characteristic wavelengths](../../../../../tel-x-ometer-x-ray-system-manual-by-joseph-dohm.md)

For the numerical calculations below use **$\lambda=0.154\,\mathrm{nm}$**, explicitly identifying the characteristic line assumed. The photograph alone does not give a measured wavelength or angular travel limit; conclusions depending on these parameters are stated conditionally. A different selected line changes the angles but not a crystallographic extinction.
