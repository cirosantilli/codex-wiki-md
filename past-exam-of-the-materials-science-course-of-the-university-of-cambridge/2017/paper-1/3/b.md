For a cubic crystal, $d_{hkl}=a/\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}$ and first-order [bragg diffraction](../../../../bragg-diffraction.md) requires $2d_{hkl}\sin\theta=\lambda$. The face-centred lattice sum is

$$
L_{hkl}=1+e^{i\pi(k+l)}+e^{i\pi(h+l)}+e^{i\pi(h+k)}.
$$

It is four if $h,k,l$ are all even or all odd, and zero for mixed parity. With the NaCl basis displaced by $(\tfrac12,0,0)$, the allowed amplitudes are $F_{hkl}=4[f_{\rm Cl}+(-1)^{h+k+l}f_{\rm Na}]$, up to the arbitrary choice of origin/species. Both allowed parity classes survive because the two ionic scattering factors are not identical. Intensities depend on $|F|^2$, geometry, absorption and scattering vector.

There are three separate tests: **nonzero [structure factor](../../../../structure-factor.md), accessible [Bragg angle](../../../../bragg-angle.md), and accessible plane orientation**. In a symmetric reflection scan the scattering vector is normal to the specimen face, selecting the surface-parallel family and its allowed orders. Other families need a different mounting, independent orientation control or transmission/another facet. The original diagrams distinguish these geometric requirements; angles in the reciprocal-space panel are between plane normals, not [Bragg angles](../../../../bragg-angle.md).
