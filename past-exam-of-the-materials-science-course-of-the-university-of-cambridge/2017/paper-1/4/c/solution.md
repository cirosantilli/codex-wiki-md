<h1 id="4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

Choose the orientation relation $(0001)_{\rm hcp}\parallel(111)_{\rm ccp}$ with aligned close-packed rows, for example $[11\bar20]_{\rm hcp}\parallel[1\bar10]_{\rm ccp}$. The two phases then share matching triangular layers at the broad faces. The stacking changes from $ABCABC\ldots$ to $ABAB\ldots$ across the phase region, so broad [coherent crystal interfaces](../../../../../coherent-crystal-interface.md) can be made by a local change of stacking, with small mismatch if the in-plane atomic spacings are similar.

At the cylindrical edge, different layer sequences terminate against one another. Registry cannot generally be maintained everywhere, so ledges, stacking defects and possibly [misfit dislocations](../../../../../misfit-dislocation.md) are needed; this interface is less coherent and often more costly. Thus **a low-energy basal/$(111)$ face and a higher-energy edge favour a thin plate**. An ideal same-element stacking change need not imply zero edge energy or exactly zero face energy.
