<h1 id="4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

At fixed transformed [volume](../../../../../volume.md), an equilibrium [solid-state precipitate](../../../../../solid-state-precipitate.md) seeks a low total [free energy](../../../../../thermodynamic-free-energy.md), including the orientation-dependent [interfacial energy](../../../../../interfacial-energy.md) and elastic [strain](../../../../../strain.md) energy associated with misfit and applied [stress](../../../../../stress.md). Low-energy interface orientations occupy large faces. The Wulff construction captures surface-energy [anisotropy](../../../../../anisotropy.md) when [strain](../../../../../strain.md) and other contributions can be neglected; a [solid-state precipitate](../../../../../solid-state-precipitate.md) generally also pays [coherency strain](../../../../../coherency-strain.md) energy, and its optimum shape can change with size.

During formation, growth kinetics and [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) may prevent the equilibrium shape being reached. Coherent, semicoherent and incoherent interfaces have different bonding and defect structures, so their energies and attachment rates differ. **The disc shape is consistent with much cheaper broad faces than edges**, but morphology alone does not uniquely measure the energies or prove equilibrium.
