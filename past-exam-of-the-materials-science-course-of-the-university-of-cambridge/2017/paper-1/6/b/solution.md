<h1 id="6/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The maximum recoverable [elastic energy](../../../../../elastic-energy.md) per initial [volume](../../../../../volume.md) is the [area](../../../../../surface-area.md) under the linear [stress](../../../../../stress.md)–[strain](../../../../../strain.md) curve up to failure:

$$
u_f=\int_0^{\varepsilon_f}E\varepsilon\,d\varepsilon=\frac12E\varepsilon_f^2=\boxed{\frac{\sigma_f^2}{2E}}.
$$

Using $\sigma_f=C\sqrt{EG_c}$ gives $u_f=C^2G_c/2$, so **maximize $G_c$** for the fixed geometry. $G_c$ has units of energy per crack [area](../../../../../surface-area.md), while $u_f$ has energy per specimen [volume](../../../../../volume.md); the geometric factor supplies the necessary inverse length. The cancellation of $E$ is why the strain-maximizing index from (a) is not appropriate here.
