<h1 id="2/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

**[ferroelectric poling](../../../../../ferroelectric-poling.md) is essential.** An initially unpoled ceramic has differently oriented [crystal grains](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) and domains whose charge responses largely cancel. An applied [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) favours suitably oriented domains and leaves a remanent macroscopic polarization after removal; it does not rotate all [crystal grains](../../../../../grain-in-a-polycrystal.md) into a [single crystal](../../../../../single-crystal.md).

In the linear coefficient model the added source polarization is

$$
\Delta P_{\rm source}=d\Delta\sigma=(80\times10^{-12})\frac{10(9.81)}{10^{-4}}=\boxed{7.85\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{C\,m^{-2}}.}
$$

This is the stress-generated contribution at fixed [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), equivalent to the short-circuit charge per face [area](../../../../../surface-area.md). Under open-circuit conditions the induced field supplies an opposing [dielectric](../../../../../dielectric.md) contribution, so $\Delta D=0$; the source quantity must not be confused with a change of total free [electrode](../../../../../electrode.md) charge. For scale, room-temperature single-domain [barium titanate](../../../../../barium-titanate.md) has [spontaneous polarization](../../../../../spontaneous-polarization.md) of order $0.26\,\mathrm{C\,m^{-2}}$: the calculated increment is about $3.0\times10^{-4}$ of that value, a small response rather than wholesale reversal of the [spontaneous polarization](../../../../../spontaneous-polarization.md).[pp. 1799–1800, spontaneous-polarization measurements and temperature dependence](../../../../../dielectric-and-optical-properties-of-melt-grown-batio3-by-s-h-wemple-and-others.md)

For the requested displacement estimate, assign the whole additional dipole moment of one cell to movement of its single $\mathrm{Ti^{4+}}$ [ion](../../../../../ion.md) relative to a fixed background. Then $\Delta P=(4e)\Delta x/(a^2c)$, giving

$$
\Delta x=\frac{(7.848\times10^{-5})(0.3994\times10^{-9})^2(0.4033\times10^{-9})}{4(1.6022\times10^{-19})}=\boxed{7.88\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm m.}
$$

This formal-charge estimate is only an equivalent displacement: real polarization changes involve [oxygen](../../../../../oxygen.md) and [barium](../../../../../barium.md) displacements, electronic polarization and [Born effective charges](../../../../../born-effective-charge.md), as well as possible domain-wall motion. The coefficient supplied for the ceramic cannot uniquely determine an actual microscopic [titanium](../../../../../titanium.md) trajectory.
