<h1 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

For [quartz](../../../../../quartz.md), $A=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $t=5.0\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, the load and [stress](../../../../../stress.md) are $F=4.905\,\mathrm N$ and $\Delta\sigma=4.905\times10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Therefore

$$
|\Delta V|=\frac{(2.3\times10^{-12})(5.0\times10^{-4})(4.905\times10^4)}{(8.854\times10^{-12})(3.8)}=\boxed{1.68\,\mathrm V.}
$$

As a check, $Q=dF=11.28\,\mathrm{pC}$ and $C=6.73\,\mathrm{pF}$ give the same result. This is the ideal maximum open-circuit change on applying the load. Input [capacitance](../../../../../capacitance.md) reduces it to $Q/(C+C_{\rm in})$, while finite leakage makes it decay: it is not a guaranteed permanent DC output.
