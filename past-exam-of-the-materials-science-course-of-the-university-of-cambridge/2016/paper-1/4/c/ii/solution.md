<h1 id="4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The room-temperature endpoints are pure Ag and pure Cu. Conservation of the total [copper](../../../../../../copper.md) [mass](../../../../../../mass.md) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{w_\alpha=1-0.28=0.72,\qquad w_\beta=0.28.}
$$

The answer is **72% [silver](../../../../../../silver.md) phase and 28% [copper](../../../../../../copper.md) phase by [mass](../../../../../../mass.md)**. The change from the high-temperature fractions is caused by decreasing solid solubility: the phases reject the opposite component during equilibrium cooling. Simply retaining the eutectic-temperature lever-rule fractions would violate the stipulated room-temperature equilibrium compositions.
