**[Eutectic solidification](../../../../../eutectic-solidification.md) is the coupled transformation of a liquid into two distinct solid phases at the [eutectic temperature](../../../../../eutectic-temperature.md) and composition**, $L\rightarrow\alpha+\beta$. At fixed [pressure](../../../../../pressure-split.md) in a binary system the three-phase equilibrium is invariant. An exactly eutectic liquid can therefore finish freezing at one [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md), instead of over a broad liquid-plus-solid interval.

[Eutectic compositions](../../../../../eutectic-composition.md) are useful in joining alloys, for example silver-copper [brazing](../../../../../brazing.md) filler: the comparatively low melting [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and narrow freezing interval help liquid flow into a joint and then solidify. Alternating growth of the two phases often produces a fine lamellar [eutectic microstructure](../../../../../eutectic-microstructure.md). The eutectic is a two-phase aggregate, not a new single solid phase.
