<h1 id="5/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

A second [slip system](../../../../../slip-system.md) begins to operate as the tensile-axis rotation makes its [resolved shear stress](../../../../../resolved-shear-stress.md) comparable to that on the primary system. [Dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) on intersecting planes interact much more strongly, giving the transition from easy single slip to rapid [work hardening](../../../../../work-hardening.md). The standard geometric estimate neglects differences between the evolving critical [stresses](../../../../../stress.md) of the two systems.

The primary direction is $\mathbf s\parallel[101]$. Single-slip shear carries the line joining the specimen ends, expressed in crystal coordinates, along

$$
\mathbf r(t)=(1,2,4)+t(1,0,1)=(1+t,2,4+t).
$$

The initial fundamental sector has $x<y<z$. Its first reached boundary is $x=y$, at $t=1$, giving $\mathbf r=[225]$. At that orientation $(\overline111)[101]$ and $(1\overline11)[011]$ have equal [Schmid factors](../../../../../schmid-factor.md). Since the component perpendicular to the primary slip direction is unchanged by that shear, the length ratio follows directly:

$$
\frac{L}{L_0}=\frac{|(2,2,5)|}{|(1,2,4)|}
=\sqrt{\frac{33}{21}}=\sqrt{\frac{11}7}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_{\rm eng}=\sqrt{11/7}-1=0.2536\simeq25.4\%.}
$$

The corresponding logarithmic [strain](../../../../../strain.md) is $\ln(L/L_0)=0.2260$. This is an **ideal equal-critical-stress geometric onset estimate**. Real secondary slip can start earlier or later because of [latent hardening](../../../../../latent-hardening.md), nonuniform deformation and grip constraints; the problem supplies no hardening law that would determine that shift.[§3.12.2, points10–12: tensile-axis rotation, secondary slip and stages of hardening](../../../../../applied-mechanics-of-solids-by-a-f-bower.md)
