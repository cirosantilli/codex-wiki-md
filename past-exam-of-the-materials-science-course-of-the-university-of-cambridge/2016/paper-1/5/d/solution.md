<h1 id="5/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

**The printed numerical ratios are about $35$, not about $10$**:

$$
\frac{27.5}{0.78}=35.3,\qquad\frac{27.5}{0.8}=34.4.
$$

The qualitative contrast is nevertheless meaningful. FCC [aluminium](../../../../../aluminium.md) has close-packed [glide planes](../../../../../glide-plane.md) and relatively mobile [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md). [Magnesium](../../../../../magnesium.md) also has easy close-packed basal glide, although it lacks enough equivalently easy [slip systems](../../../../../slip-system.md) for arbitrary room-temperature polycrystal deformation.

BCC [iron](../../../../../iron.md) has no close-packed plane. Its screw-dislocation cores and lattice-friction barrier give much stronger resistance to glide; thermally activated kink motion makes the critical [stress](../../../../../stress.md) particularly temperature- and rate-sensitive. This is the relevant Peierls-stress explanation. A high polycrystal constraint factor cannot explain the supplied contrast, because the data are already [critical resolved shear stresses](../../../../../critical-resolved-shear-stress.md). Neither the precise factor35 nor a universal BCC/FCC ratio follows from crystal packing alone: purity, [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) structure, [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and testing conditions also matter.
