<h1 id="5/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take ordinary [basal slip](../../../../../../basal-slip.md) in [magnesium](../../../../../../magnesium.md), $(0001)\langle11\overline20\rangle$. In three-index hexagonal notation, $[101]$ denotes a vector proportional to $\mathbf a_1+\mathbf c$, not a cubic Cartesian $(1,0,1)$ with equal axis lengths. The [basal slip](../../../../../../basal-slip.md) direction closest to the tensile axis is along $\mathbf a_1$; its four-index representative is $[2\overline1\overline10]$.

For $r=c/a$,

$$
\cos\phi=\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^2}},\qquad
\cos\lambda=\frac1{\sqrt{1+r^2}},\qquad
m=\frac{r}{1+r^2}.
$$

Thus the algebraic answer is

$$
\boxed{\sigma_y=0.8\frac{1+r^2}{r}\,\mathrm{MPa}.}
$$

An external crystallographic reference gives $a=0.320927\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $c=0.521033\,\mathrm{nm}$, hence $r\simeq1.624$ and **$\sigma_y\simeq1.79\,\mathrm{MPa}$**. This is a conditional external-data estimate, not a fabricated value attributed to the supplied Data Book.[Volume1, magnesium entry reproduced in COD9008506](../../../../../../crystal-structures-by-r-w-g-wyckoff.md)

The tensile axis rotates towards the selected [basal slip](../../../../../../basal-slip.md) direction during ideal single slip. As the axis approaches the basal plane, the resolved basal shear eventually becomes small; other modes, hardening and instability can interrupt the single-slip description. The boxed expression also permits direct substitution of any lattice ratio used in the examination Data Book; the numerical estimate above is explicitly tied to the cited crystallographic data. Taking a $45^\circ$ angle solely from the indices would incorrectly use a cubic metric.
