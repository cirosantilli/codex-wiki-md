<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume one temperature-independent [activation energy](../../../../../activation-energy.md) over this interval and an unchanged dopant-controlled carrier population. Write the [Arrhenius equation](../../../../../arrhenius-equation.md) as $D=D_0\exp[-Q/(k_BT)]$. The two measurements give

$$
\frac Q{k_B}=\frac{\ln 10}{1/1000-1/1180}=1.5095\times10^4\,\mathrm K,
\qquad Q=1.301\,\mathrm{eV}=125.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Using $D(T)=10^{-13}\exp[(Q/k_B)(1/1000-1/T)]$ yields

$$
\boxed{D(1250)=2.047\times10^{-12},\qquad
D(1110)=4.463\times10^{-13}\quad\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}.}
$$

The [Nernst-Einstein relation](../../../../../nernst-einstein-relation.md) includes the factor $1/T$: $\sigma=nq^2D/(k_BT)$. For fixed $n$ and a fixed correlation correction,

$$
\sigma(1110)=0.08\frac{D(1110)}{D(1250)}\frac{1250}{1110}
=\boxed{1.964\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}}.
$$

Using conductivity proportional to $D$ alone would miss the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) factor. Carrier association, a changing vacancy population or a changing [diffusion](../../../../../diffusion.md) correlation factor would invalidate this simple extrapolation.
