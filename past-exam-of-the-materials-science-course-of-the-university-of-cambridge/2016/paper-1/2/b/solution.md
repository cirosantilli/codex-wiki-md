<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For an oxide [ion](../../../../../ion.md), or an [oxygen vacancy](../../../../../oxygen-vacancy.md) measured relative to the ideal lattice, the charge magnitude is $|q|=2e$. The independent-carrier Nernst-Einstein inversion at $1250\,\mathrm K$ gives

$$
\boxed{n_{\rm eff}=\frac{\sigma k_BT}{(2e)^2D}
=6.569\times10^{27}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}.}
$$

Here $D$ and $n_{\rm eff}$ must describe the same carrier convention. If the quoted diffusivity is a mobile-vacancy diffusivity, this is the vacancy [density](../../../../../density.md). If it is literally an oxygen-ion tracer diffusivity, it is an effective oxygen-ion [density](../../../../../density.md) under an uncorrelated model. Vacancy [density](../../../../../density.md) cannot be substituted for oxygen-ion [density](../../../../../density.md) while retaining the same diffusivity. This distinction is consequential in the last part.
