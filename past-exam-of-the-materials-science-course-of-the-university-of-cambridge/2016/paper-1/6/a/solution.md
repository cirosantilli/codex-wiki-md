<h1 id="6/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a spherical homogeneous [nucleus](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md), the capillarity approximation gives

$$
\Delta G(r)=4\pi r^2\gamma+\frac{4\pi r^3}{3}\Delta g_V.
$$

The first term penalizes creating an interface; below the melting point the bulk free-energy change per [volume](../../../../../volume.md) $\Delta g_V$ is negative and favors growth. Near $T_m$, neglect the [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) dependence of the [latent heat](../../../../../latent-heat.md). Since the free-energy difference vanishes at $T_m$,

$$
\Delta g_V\simeq\Delta H_V\frac{\Delta T}{T_m},
\qquad\Delta T=T_m-T>0,
\qquad\Delta H_V<0\quad\text{on freezing}.
$$

Set the [radius](../../../../../radius.md) derivative to zero:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\Delta G}{\mathrm dr}=8\pi r\gamma+4\pi r^2\Delta g_V=0,
\qquad
\boxed{r^*=-\frac{2\gamma}{\Delta g_V}
=-\frac{2\gamma T_m}{\Delta H_V\Delta T}.}
$$

The nonzero stationary point is a maximum, since $\Delta G''(r^*)=-8\pi\gamma<0$. Subcritical [nuclei](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) lower free energy by shrinking; supercritical [nuclei](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md) lower it by growing. The positive [radius](../../../../../radius.md) and its inverse-supercooling dependence follow from the negative freezing [enthalpy](../../../../../enthalpy-split.md). This derivation assumes an isotropic, size-independent interfacial energy and a bulk-like spherical [nucleus](../../../../../atomic-nucleus-split.md); it is not a molecular-scale kinetic theory.
