<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

**The [solid](../../../../../solid.md) trace is wurtzite; the dashed trace is [zinc](../../../../../zinc.md) blende.** The isolated strong dashed peak is the cubic $111$ reflection. The [solid](../../../../../solid.md) trace has hexagonal $100$, $002$ and $101$ reflections in this angular interval; its central $002$ peak is the one at $28.65^\circ$, while cubic $111$ occurs at $28.53^\circ$. These assignments are consistent with the absence of lower-angle reflections.

In the ideal close-packed model, the cubic $111$ and hexagonal $002$ reflections probe the same local alternating layer geometry. Their scattering strength per suitably normalized amount of material and their angular factors are therefore very similar, but [powder diffraction multiplicity](../../../../../powder-diffraction-multiplicity.md) differs: the cubic family has eight directed normals, while hexagonal $002$ has two. This explains the approximately **fourfold greater intensity** of the dashed peak. Comparing unnormalized conventional-cell [structure factors](../../../../../structure-factor.md) alone would be misleading because the cells contain different numbers of formula units. Peak heights additionally depend on line widths; the multiplicity argument applies most directly to integrated intensities.
