<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Taking Cu K$\alpha$ [wavelength](../../../../../wavelength.md) $\lambda=0.15418\,\mathrm{nm}$, [Bragg's law](../../../../../bragg-s-law.md) gives $d=\lambda/(2\sin\theta)$. For cubic $111$, $a=\sqrt3d_{111}$ and the nearest Zn–S separation is $\sqrt3a/4=3d_{111}/4$. For ideal [wurtzite structure](../../../../../wurtzite.md), $c=2d_{002}$ and the nearest separation is $3c/8=3d_{002}/4$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{r_{\rm cubic}=0.2346\,\mathrm{nm},\qquad r_{\rm hexagonal}\simeq0.2337\,\mathrm{nm}.}
$$

Both have tetrahedral nearest-neighbour [coordination number](../../../../../coordination-number.md) four, but different further-neighbour stacking and lattice relaxation can shift their equilibrium bond lengths. The hexagonal value assumes ideal $c/a=\sqrt{8/3}$ and $u=3/8$; a real distorted [wurtzite structure](../../../../../wurtzite.md) can have unequal axial and inclined bonds. One measured $002$ spacing alone does not determine every bond length without that structural assumption.
