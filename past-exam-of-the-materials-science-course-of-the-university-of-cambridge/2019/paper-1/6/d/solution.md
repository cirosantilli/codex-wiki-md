<h1 id="6/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Irradiation both creates [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) loops and supplies mobile point defects that allow existing [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) to climb, rearrange and annihilate. At low initial [dislocation density](../../../../../dislocation-density.md), net production can dominate; at high initial density, sink absorption and recovery can dominate. A simple illustrative rate equation is

$$
\frac{d\rho}{dD}=A-B\rho,\qquad
\rho(D)=\frac AB+\left(\rho_0-\frac AB\right)e^{-BD}.
$$

Thus **both initial states approach $\rho_{\rm ss}=A/B$** in this fixed-condition model. Here $D$ is irradiation dose, not a [diffusion coefficient](../../../../../diffusion-coefficient.md). The asymptote reflects a dynamic balance, not the cessation of damage. It need not be the same for different alloys, temperatures, dose rates or applied stresses.
