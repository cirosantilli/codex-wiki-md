<h1 id="6/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The annealed sample initially has relatively few obstacles to [dislocation glide](../../../../../dislocation-glide.md). After its elastic response it yields, undergoes appreciable plastic deformation and develops [strain hardening](../../../../../work-hardening.md). Irradiation produces [dislocation loops](../../../../../dislocation-loop.md) and defect clusters that obstruct gliding [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md), usually raising the [yield strength](../../../../../yield-strength.md). In a simple dispersed-obstacle model the strengthening scales as $\Delta\sigma\sim M\alpha Gb\sqrt{Nd}$, with obstacle density $N$ and diameter $d$.

Irradiated material often has less uniform plastic elongation and weaker subsequent hardening because slip localizes into channels that have cleared irradiation obstacles. Earlier [necking](../../../../../necking-engineering.md) or fracture can then reduce ductility. The elastic slope is much less affected than the yield [stress](../../../../../stress.md) in this schematic comparison; the exact strength and elongation depend on dose and [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md).
