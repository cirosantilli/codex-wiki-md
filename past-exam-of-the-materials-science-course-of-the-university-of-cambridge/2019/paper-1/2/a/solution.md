<h1 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Features only a few tens of nanometres wide lie below the ordinary visible-light [diffraction limit](../../../../../diffraction-limit.md), approximately $\lambda/(2\mathrm{NA})$. The microscope cannot resolve individual rods, and unpolarized illumination does not directly reveal the orientation-dependent optical response. **[Scanning electron microscopy](../../../../../scanning-electron-microscope.md) and [atomic force microscopy](../../../../../atomic-force-microscopy.md)** can resolve the surface pattern. [Transmission electron microscopy](../../../../../transmission-electron-microscopy.md) is another possible choice if a suitably thin specimen can be prepared.
