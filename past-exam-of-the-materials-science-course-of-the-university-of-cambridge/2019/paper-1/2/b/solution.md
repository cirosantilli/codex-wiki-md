<h1 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

An optically [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md) film changes the phase of both transverse polarization components equally, so crossed [polarizers](../../../../../polarizer.md) still extinguish the transmitted light. The etched rod-and-gap composite has **[form birefringence](../../../../../form-birefringence.md)**: its effective [permittivity](../../../../../permittivity-split.md) depends on the direction of the [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md), even when the constituent material is intrinsically [isotropic](../../../../../isotropy.md). In a simple subwavelength lamellar model,

$$
\epsilon_\parallel=f\epsilon_r+(1-f)\epsilon_g,\qquad
\epsilon_\perp=\left(\frac f{\epsilon_r}+\frac{1-f}{\epsilon_g}\right)^{-1}.
$$

The precise effective-medium formula changes with rod geometry, but the [anisotropy](../../../../../anisotropy.md) remains. Resolving incident light along the two principal directions produces a [phase retardation](../../../../../phase-retardation.md). An ideal lossless specimen between crossed [polarizers](../../../../../polarizer.md) transmits

$$
I=I_P\sin^2(2\alpha)\sin^2(\pi\Gamma/\lambda),\qquad\Gamma=(n_{\rm slow}-n_{\rm fast})t,
$$

where $I_P$ is the intensity after the first polarizer. Extinction occurs when a principal direction follows either polarizer; transmission is strongest near $45^\circ$.
