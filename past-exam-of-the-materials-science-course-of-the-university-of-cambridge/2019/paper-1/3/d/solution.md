<h1 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

At a common test [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md) and otherwise comparable microstructure, chemical order generally raises the [yield strength](../../../../../yield-strength.md). An ordinary FCC [dislocation](../../../../../dislocation.md) displaced by $a\langle110\rangle/2$ can interchange Au and Cu sublattices, creating an [antiphase boundary](../../../../../antiphase-boundary.md) behind it. This additional energy resists slip; paired [dislocations](../../../../../dislocation.md) can restore the order after the second member passes. The disordered phase does not pay the same ordering penalty. Comparing specimens actually tested at their different phase-stability temperatures also includes temperature-dependent mobility and recovery, so the ordering argument alone does not predict a unique measured strength ratio.
