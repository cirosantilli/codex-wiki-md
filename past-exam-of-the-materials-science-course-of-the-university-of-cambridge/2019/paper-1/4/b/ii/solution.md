<h1 id="4/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At $138\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, one line is tangent to all three [Gibbs free energy](../../../../../../gibbs-free-energy.md) curves at approximately $x_{\rm Sn}=0.03$ for (Bi), $0.61$ for [liquid](../../../../../../liquid.md), and $0.93$ for $(\beta\mathrm{Sn})$. These three phases can coexist at the [eutectic point](../../../../../../eutectic-point.md). Between the two [solid](../../../../../../solid.md) compositions, the equilibrium phase fractions depend on how much [liquid](../../../../../../liquid.md) has already transformed; exactly at the invariant [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md), the overall composition alone does not fix all three fractions. At the eutectic overall composition, cooling just below the [temperature](../../../../../../temperature-split.md) eliminates the [liquid](../../../../../../liquid.md) and leaves the two solids.
