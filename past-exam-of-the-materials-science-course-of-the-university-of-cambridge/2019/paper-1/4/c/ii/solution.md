<h1 id="4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

At $125\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, primary Bi-rich crystals are surrounded by a transformed two-phase [eutectic microstructure](../../../../../../eutectic-microstructure.md). Reading approximately $x_B=0.026$ and $x_S=0.94$ gives phase compositions $1.49$ and $89.90\,\mathrm{wt\%}$ Sn. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{f_{\beta\rm Sn}\simeq\frac{0.1958-0.0149}{0.8990-0.0149}=0.205,\qquad f_{\rm Bi}\simeq0.795.}
$$

These are total **phase mass fractions**, including both primary and eutectic material. They differ from the [microconstituent](../../../../../../microconstituent.md) fractions: just before the eutectic reaction, roughly $39\%$ by mass is [liquid](../../../../../../liquid.md) and subsequently becomes the eutectic constituent, with roughly $61\%$ primary Bi-rich material. Further slow cooling changes the phase compositions and allows secondary precipitation. Faster cooling usually refines the primary crystals and eutectic spacing and increases retained segregation; the equilibrium diagram does not specify the resulting nonequilibrium fractions uniquely.
