<h1 id="4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

(Bi) and $(\beta\mathrm{Sn})$ are Bi-rich and Sn-rich [terminal solid solutions](../../../../../terminal-solid-solution.md), not intermetallic compounds. At the eutectic composition, cooling through $138\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ gives

$$
\boxed{L\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Bi})+(\beta\mathrm{Sn}).}
$$

The two solids grow together as a fine [eutectic microstructure](../../../../../eutectic-microstructure.md), commonly lamellar or rod-like. Below about $13\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, the tin-rich component has an equilibrium tendency to transform from metallic tetragonal [white tin](../../../../../white-tin.md) to diamond-cubic [grey tin](../../../../../grey-tin.md), with a substantial volume increase. This is [tin pest](../../../../../tin-pest.md), which can disrupt the microstructure mechanically. The dashed transition does not transform the Bi-rich phase into [tin](../../../../../tin.md). Actual transformation can be slow and depend strongly on impurities and [nucleation](../../../../../nucleation.md); crossing the line does not guarantee immediate disintegration.
