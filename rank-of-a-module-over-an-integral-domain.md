# Rank of a module over an integral domain

↑ **Parent:** [Module (mathematics)](module-mathematics.md)

For a [module](module-mathematics.md) $M$ over an [integral domain](integral-domain.md) $R$ with [field of fractions](field-of-fractions.md) $K$, its rank is the [dimension](dimension-vector-space.md) of $K\otimes_R M$. The [tensor product of modules](tensor-product-of-modules.md) here extends scalars to a [field](field.md), turning the question into a dimension calculation. For a [free module](free-module.md) $R^r$, the result is $r$.

For a [finitely generated module](finitely-generated-module.md) over a [principal ideal domain](principal-ideal-domain.md), the [structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain](structure-theorem-for-finitely-generated-modules-over-a-principal-ideal-domain.md) gives $M\cong R^r\oplus\bigoplus_iR/(d_i)$, with $d_i$ nonzero nonunits. Each [torsion element of a module](torsion-element-of-a-module.md) vanishes after extension of scalars, because its annihilating nonzero scalar becomes invertible in $K$. Thus $K\otimes_R M\cong K^r$, proving that the rank is the number of free summands. The rank does not record the [torsion submodule](torsion-submodule.md): for example, $R\oplus R/(d)$ and $R$ have the same rank.
