# Tetrahedral molecular geometry

↑ **Parent:** [Molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_molecular_geometry)

In [tetrahedral molecular geometry](tetrahedral-molecular-geometry.md), four neighbouring [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) surround a central [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) in the directions of the vertices of a [tetrahedron](tetrahedron.md). In the regular idealization, the central [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus-split.md) lies at the centre of a [regular tetrahedron](regular-tetrahedron.md), and every pair of [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md) makes the same [bond angle](bond-angle.md). [Methane](methane.md), $\mathrm{CH}_4$, provides the standard example: its [carbon](carbon.md) has four bonding [electron domains](electron-domain.md) and no [lone pair](lone-pair.md). See [Chemistry 2e, §7.6: tetrahedral methane](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-6-molecular-structure-and-polarity).

The exact ideal [bond angle](bond-angle.md) follows from four directions proportional to $(1,1,1)$, $(1,-1,-1)$, $(-1,1,-1)$, and $(-1,-1,1)$. Each has squared [Euclidean norm](euclidean-norm.md) $3$, and each distinct pair has [dot product](dot-product.md) $-1$. Hence

$$
\theta=\arccos(-1/3)\approx109.4712^\circ.
$$

This is a statement about a [regular tetrahedron](regular-tetrahedron.md), rather than a universal exact value for every [molecule](molecule-split.md) labelled tetrahedral. It also explains why a cross-shaped flat drawing with $90^\circ$ gaps is misleading: four equal directions can spread out more evenly in three dimensions.

The same [vectors](vector.md) sum to zero. Consequently, four equal bond-dipole contributions along those directions cancel. The symmetric [methane](methane.md) [molecule](molecule-split.md) is [nonpolar](nonpolar-molecule.md), but a tetrahedral shape alone does not imply a [nonpolar molecule](nonpolar-molecule.md): replacing one neighbour can make the electronic distribution and bond contributions unequal. [Chemical polarity](chemical-polarity.md) depends on both [molecular geometry](molecular-geometry.md) and [electron density](electron-density.md). See [McMurry, Organic Chemistry, §2.2: molecular dipoles and methane](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-2-polar-covalent-bonds-and-dipole-moments).
