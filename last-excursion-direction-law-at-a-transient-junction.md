# Last-excursion direction law at a transient junction

↑ **Parent:** [Markov chain](markov-chain.md)

At a junction, let $a_j$ be the probability that one excursion leaves through branch $j$ and never returns. If $a=\sum a_j>0$, repeated returns restart the same probabilities by the [Strong Markov property](strong-markov-property.md). Thus the probability of $m$ returns is $(1-a)^m$, and eventual escape through $j$ is $\sum_{m\geq0}(1-a)^m a_j=a_j/a$. When the branches have outward random-walk drift, the final infinite excursion tends to the corresponding end almost surely.
