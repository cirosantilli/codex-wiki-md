# Feynman integral

↑ **Parent:** [Feynman diagram](feynman-diagram.md)

A [Feynman integral](feynman-integral.md) is the momentum-space integral associated with a [Feynman diagram](feynman-diagram.md): each independent [loop momentum](loop-momentum.md) is integrated with measure $d^dk/(2\pi)^d$, and [propagators](propagator.md), vertex numerators and momentum conservation determine the integrand. Its definition also requires a pole prescription and, when divergent, a [regularization in quantum field theory](regularization-in-quantum-field-theory.md). [Feynman parameters](feynman-parameter.md) combine propagator denominators and permit shifts of loop momenta. A massless two-propagator example is $\int d^dk/[(2\pi)^d k^2(p-k)^2]$, with its causal prescriptions understood. Subtracting local [counterterms](counterterm.md) turns a regulated diagram into a finite contribution to a renormalized amplitude.
