# Induced dipole moment and quadratic energy shift

↑ **Parent:** [Electric polarizability](electric-polarizability.md)

For a nondegenerate parity-symmetric ground state in an external [electric field](electric-field-split.md), write the perturbation as $V=-E\hat d$. The first-order state correction gives

$$
\langle\hat d\rangle=2E\sum_{k\ne0}\frac{|\langle k|\hat d|0\rangle|^2}{E_k^{(0)}-E_0^{(0)}}+O(E^3)=\alpha E+O(E^3).
$$

The positive coefficient $\alpha$ is the [electric polarizability](electric-polarizability.md) along the applied direction. Meanwhile [second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory](second-order-nondegenerate-perturbation-theory.md) gives $\delta E_0^{(2)}=-\alpha E^2/2$. Thus the induced [electric dipole moment](electric-dipole-moment.md) satisfies $d=-2\delta E_0^{(2)}/E$, not $-\delta E_0^{(2)}/E$. The [Hellmann–Feynman theorem](hellmann-feynman-theorem.md) independently gives $d=-\partial E_0/\partial E$.
