# Ideal gas

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_gas)

An ideal gas is a model of a dilute [gas](gas.md) whose particle centres move according to [classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md) and occupy negligible [volume](volume-thermodynamics.md) compared with their container. Treating the particles as [point masses](point-mass.md) removes the molecular-size correction; neglecting interactions between separated particles removes the intermolecular contribution to [potential energy](potential-energy.md). The usual [kinetic theory](kinetic-theory.md) model retains brief [elastic collisions](elastic-collision.md) with the walls and, optionally, with other particles: these transfer [momentum](momentum.md) while conserving the total [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) of the colliding bodies. There is no appreciable interaction [energy](energy.md) stored between [collisions](collision.md). These assumptions lead to the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md), $PV=Nk_BT$. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, section 2.1: molecular model and ideal gas law](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-1-molecular-model-of-an-ideal-gas) [OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, section 2.2: kinetic-model assumptions](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-2-pressure-temperature-and-rms-speed)

The model's attraction is its universality: at the same [temperature](temperature.md) and [number density](number-density.md), different classical [ideal gases](ideal-gas.md) have the same [pressure](pressure.md), even when their particle [masses](mass.md) differ. In [ideal gas pressure from wall collisions](ideal-gas-pressure-from-wall-collisions.md), heavier particles move more slowly at a given [temperature](temperature.md), and the two effects cancel. For example, one [mole](mole-unit.md) of any [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) at $300\,\mathrm K$ in $0.025\,\mathrm{m^3}$ has [pressure](pressure.md) approximately $99.774\,\mathrm{kPa}$, as computed under the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md).

The [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md) describes the translational contribution to [pressure](pressure.md); it does not specify all of the [internal energy](internal-energy.md) or the [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md). A [monatomic gas](monatomic-gas.md) has three classical translational [degrees of freedom](degree-of-freedom.md), whereas a [gas](gas.md) of [molecules](molecule.md) can also store [rotational kinetic energy](rotational-kinetic-energy.md) and [energy](energy.md) in vibrational [normal modes](normal-mode.md). An [equation of state](equation-of-state.md) alone therefore does not determine how much [heat](heat.md) a chosen [temperature](temperature.md) increase requires. It also does not select a process: [isothermal expansion](isothermal-expansion.md) and [adiabatic expansion](adiabatic-expansion.md) need different additional constraints. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, section 2.3: internal degrees of freedom and heat capacity](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-3-heat-capacity-and-equipartition-of-energy)

A [real gas](real-gas.md) departs from this approximation when particle size or interactions become important, especially at high [number density](number-density.md) or low [temperature](temperature.md) near a [phase transition](phase-transition.md). The [Van der Waals equation](van-der-waals-equation.md) adds such corrections. Independently, at sufficiently low [temperature](temperature.md) or high [number density](number-density.md), [quantum statistics](quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md) can become essential: even particles with negligible interactions need not obey the classical [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md). The classical statistical assumption is the [nondegenerate gas](nondegenerate-gas.md) regime, which is distinct from neglecting interactions. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 2, section 2.1: departures from ideal-gas behavior](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-1-molecular-model-of-an-ideal-gas) [3 Quantum Gases](statistical-physics-by-david-tong/3-quantum-gases.md)
