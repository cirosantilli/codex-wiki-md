# Transverse relativistic interception

↑ **Parent:** [Lorentz transformation](lorentz-transformation.md)

In a station frame, a target begins at $(0,a)$ with [velocity](velocity.md) $(v,0)$, and an interceptor launches from the origin at [speed](speed.md) u greater than v. Straight-line interception requires its [velocity](velocity.md) components to be $(v,\sqrt{u^2-v^2})$, and takes station time $t=a/\sqrt{u^2-v^2}$. The target-frame coordinate interval is $\gamma_v(t-v\Delta x/c^2)=t/\gamma_v$, since $\Delta x=vt$. This differs from the interceptor's [proper time](proper-time.md) $t/\gamma_u$. A symmetric return to the stationary launch point instead has target-frame round-trip coordinate duration $2\gamma_v t$, because its endpoint spatial separation in the station frame is zero.
