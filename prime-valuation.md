# Prime valuation

↑ **Parent:** [Number theory](number-theory-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_valuation)

For a [prime number](prime-number-split.md) $p$ and a [positive integer](positive-integer.md) $n$, the prime valuation is the exponent of $p$ in the [prime factorization](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) of $n$, equivalently

$$
v_p(n)=\max\{e\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:p^e\mid n\}.
$$

Thus $v_2(360)=3$ because $8$ divides $360$ but $16$ does not; $v_3(360)=2$, $v_5(360)=1$ and $v_7(360)=0$. In particular $v_p(1)=0$. [Multiplication](multiplication.md) adds [prime valuations](prime-valuation.md), $v_p(ab)=v_p(a)+v_p(b)$, and the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md) makes comparison of [prime valuations](prime-valuation.md) an exact test for [divisibility](divisibility-split.md) between [positive integers](positive-integer.md). This definition extends to a nonzero [integer](integer-split.md) by taking its [absolute value](absolute-value.md); for zero one commonly sets $v_p(0)=+\infty$.
