# Elastic collision

↑ **Parent:** [Collision mechanics](collision-mechanics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_collision)

An [elastic collision](elastic-collision.md) preserves total [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) between the incoming and outgoing states. For isolated colliding bodies, [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md) also holds. The [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) condition supplies information beyond [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md) alone. [OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, section 9.4: Types of Collisions](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-4-types-of-collisions)

Consider a stipulated one-dimensional example with rightward positive: $m_1=2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $m_2=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, incoming [velocities](velocity.md) $u_1=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $u_2=0$. The total [momentum](momentum.md) is $P=6\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$ and total [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) is $K=9\,\mathrm J$. In [SI](international-system-of-units.md) numerical values, outgoing [velocities](velocity.md) $v_1,v_2$ satisfy

$$
2v_1+v_2=6,\qquad v_1^2+\frac12v_2^2=9.
$$

Substituting $v_2=6-2v_1$ gives $3(v_1-1)(v_1-3)=0$. The root $v_1=3$, $v_2=0$ repeats the incoming state; the outgoing separating state is $v_1=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $v_2=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its [momenta](momentum.md) add to $2+4=6\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$ and its [kinetic energies](kinetic-energy.md) add to $1+8=9\,\mathrm J$. The incoming and outgoing [relative velocities](relative-velocity.md) both have magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, consistent with [coefficient of restitution](coefficient-of-restitution.md) $e=1$.

These are ideal numerical examples, not experimental measurements.
