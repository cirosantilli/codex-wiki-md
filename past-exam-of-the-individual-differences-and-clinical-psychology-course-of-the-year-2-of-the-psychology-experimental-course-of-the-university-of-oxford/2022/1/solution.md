**Three useful maintaining mechanisms are catastrophic [threat appraisal](../../../threat-appraisal.md), avoidance with [safety behaviors](../../../safety-behavior.md), and [self-focused attention](../../../self-focused-attention.md).** They are separable hypotheses about why [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) continues after an immediate threat has passed, not three diagnoses or a claim that every [anxiety disorder](../../../anxiety-disorder.md) has identical causes. A maintaining factor should predict persistence and, ideally, manipulating it should change [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) or [learning](../../../learning.md). [Correlation](../../../pearson-correlation-coefficient.md) between a symptom and [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) alone is insufficient, because [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) can itself produce that symptom.

The first mechanism is [catastrophic misinterpretation](../../../catastrophic-misinterpretation.md). In [David M. Clark](../../../david-m-clark.md)'s cognitive model of [panic disorder](../../../panic-disorder.md), a bodily sensation is interpreted as evidence of imminent disaster. Perceived danger increases [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) and bodily arousal; the resulting sensations appear to confirm the interpretation. A racing heart can thereby become evidence _for_ an emergency precisely because the person has become frightened. The model explains a feedback loop and recurrent fear of sensations, rather than requiring a new external danger for every attack. It is a theoretical account with testable predictions, not itself a randomized demonstration that all panic has one cause.[1986, cognitive model and interpretation of bodily sensations](../../../a-cognitive-approach-to-panic-by-david-m-clark.md)

Treatment addresses the appraisal through collaborative [cognitive behavioral therapy](../../../cognitive-behavioral-therapy.md). Therapist and patient identify a specific feared prediction, consider alternatives, and design a [behavioral experiment](../../../behavioral-experiment.md) that distinguishes them. For example, a medically appropriate, supervised [interoceptive exposure](../../../interoceptive-exposure.md) exercise can test whether a familiar benign sensation necessarily leads to the feared loss of control. The aim is evidence-based revision of a prediction, not repeated reassurance or the assertion that every physical symptom is harmless. Assessment determines what can safely be tested. Changes in conviction, avoidance and functioning matter as well as immediate [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md).

Clinical evidence supports the broader approach but requires careful interpretation. In a 1994 comparison involving 64 patients, [David M. Clark](../../../david-m-clark.md) and colleagues compared [cognitive therapy](../../../cognitive-therapy.md) with applied relaxation, [imipramine](../../../imipramine.md) and an initial waiting condition. All active treatments helped; [cognitive therapy](../../../cognitive-therapy.md) had advantages at several assessments, including after medication withdrawal. However, all treatments included self-exposure homework, and treatment contact differed. Thus the trial supports a treatment package while leaving open how much benefit arose specifically through appraisal change, exposure, expectancy or their interaction.[1994, treatment conditions, assessments and follow-up](../../../a-comparison-of-cognitive-therapy-applied-relaxation-and-imipramine-in-the-treatment-of-panic-disorder-by-david-m-clark-and-others.md)

The second mechanism is [avoidance learning](../../../avoidance-learning.md) supported by [negative reinforcement](../../../negative-reinforcement.md). Leaving a feared situation reduces distress immediately, increasing the likelihood of leaving again. Subtler [safety behaviors](../../../safety-behavior.md), such as excessive preparation, monitoring or seeking reassurance, can have the same maintaining effect. They also prevent [expectancy violation](../../../expectancy-violation.md): if catastrophe fails to occur, the person attributes safety to the precaution rather than revising the danger belief. Some precautions can increase the very symptom being monitored, making the original belief appear even more credible.

[Paul Salkovskis](../../../paul-salkovskis.md) and colleagues tested this idea in patients with [panic disorder](../../../panic-disorder.md) and [agoraphobia](../../../agoraphobia.md). During a brief exposure session, participants either maintained or dropped their within-situation [safety behaviors](../../../safety-behavior.md). Those dropping them showed greater subsequent reductions in catastrophic beliefs and [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md). Because exposure occurred in both conditions, this provides more specific evidence about how exposure is conducted than a comparison with no treatment. Nevertheless, it was a brief experimental intervention, not a demonstration that every behavior called “safety seeking” is harmful in every context.[1999, 15-minute exposure manipulation and repeated behavioral test](../../../an-experimental-investigation-of-the-role-of-safety-seeking-behaviours-in-the-maintenance-of-panic-disorder-with-agoraphobia-by-paul-salkovskis-and-others.md)

The corresponding intervention is planned [exposure therapy](../../../exposure-therapy.md) with gradual removal of the behaviors that block [learning](../../../learning.md). A prediction is specified before exposure and the observed outcome compared with it afterward. Repetition across contexts tests whether [learning](../../../learning.md) transfers. Treatment should distinguish unnecessary defensive rituals from objectively useful precautions; indiscriminate risk-taking is not the therapeutic mechanism. Nor is success simply enduring maximum distress until it falls: an informative experience may change expectations even without complete within-session [habituation](../../../habituation.md).

The third mechanism is [self-focused attention](../../../self-focused-attention.md), especially prominent in [social anxiety disorder](../../../social-anxiety-disorder.md). Monitoring one's voice, posture or apparent blushing consumes [attention](../../../attention.md) that could register the actual interaction. An internal image based on [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) sensations is then mistaken for how an observer sees the person. Monitoring and rehearsal can disrupt conversation, reduce access to disconfirming social information and increase the subjective impression of poor performance. This explains how private [anxiety](../../../anxiety.md) can become apparently public evidence of failure.

[Freda McManus](../../../freda-mcmanus.md) and colleagues first documented greater reported [safety behavior](../../../safety-behavior.md) use among highly socially anxious participants, then experimentally manipulated [safety behaviors](../../../safety-behavior.md) and self-focus. Their results supported adverse effects of these processes. A later clinical component study evaluated a [self-focused attention](../../../self-focused-attention.md)/[safety behavior](../../../safety-behavior.md) experiment and [video feedback](../../../video-feedback.md), supporting their intended effects on negative self-impressions and symptoms. The combined manipulation does not uniquely isolate [attention](../../../attention.md) from every associated behavior, and a single-session component study is not a substitute for a full treatment trial.[2008, interview study and experimental manipulation](../../../why-social-anxiety-persists-by-freda-mcmanus-and-others.md)[2009, self-focus/safety-behavior and video-feedback procedures](../../../a-demonstration-of-the-efficacy-of-two-of-the-components-of-cognitive-therapy-for-social-phobia-by-freda-mcmanus-and-others.md)

Treatment can compare inward monitoring with attending to the conversation and can use carefully prepared [video feedback](../../../video-feedback.md) to test a concrete prediction about visible performance. The recording should be viewed as evidence, rather than scanned selectively for flaws; otherwise the same bias can operate on the video. Across the three mechanisms, the common principle is to identify a feedback loop, manipulate a hypothesized link and measure what changes. **A good formulation selects the mechanisms actually operating in the person and uses cognitive revision, informative exposure and [attention](../../../attention.md) change to interrupt them.** These mechanisms concern persistence; they need not explain why vulnerability first developed.
