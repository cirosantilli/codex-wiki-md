# Compression ratio of a perpendicular magnetohydrodynamic shock

↑ **Parent:** [Perpendicular magnetohydrodynamic shock](perpendicular-magnetohydrodynamic-shock.md)

Put $c_1^2=\gamma p_1/\rho_1$ and $v_{A1}^2=B_1^2/(\mu_0\rho_1)$. The mass, [momentum](momentum.md) and energy jump conditions for a [perpendicular magnetohydrodynamic shock](perpendicular-magnetohydrodynamic-shock.md) factor into the unchanged state $x=1$ and

$$
(2-\gamma)v_{A1}^2x^2+[(\gamma-1)u_1^2+2c_1^2+\gamma v_{A1}^2]x-(\gamma+1)u_1^2=0.
$$

For the compressive physical branch $x>1$, positive downstream [pressure](pressure.md) implies $x<(\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)$ when $\gamma>1$ and the upstream [sound speed](speed-of-sound.md) or [Alfvén speed](alfven-speed.md) is nonzero. Indeed the polynomial implies

$$
\frac{p_2}{\rho_1}=
\frac{c_1^2[(\gamma+1)x-(\gamma-1)]/\gamma
+(\gamma-1)v_{A1}^2(x-1)^3/2}
{(\gamma+1)-(\gamma-1)x}.
$$

It then gives $u_1^2>c_1^2+v_{A1}^2$: the upstream flow exceeds the perpendicular fast [magnetosonic wave](magnetosonic-wave.md) speed. The exactly cold unmagnetized strong-shock limit reaches the upper compression bound.
