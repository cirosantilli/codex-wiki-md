# Even reflection across a homogeneous Neumann boundary

↑ **Parent:** [Neumann boundary condition](neumann-boundary-condition.md)

If a [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) on one side of a straight boundary has zero [normal derivative](normal-derivative.md) there, even [reflection](reflection-mathematics.md) extends it harmonically across the boundary. Locally take the boundary to be $x=0$ and assume $u$ and its first [derivatives](derivative.md) extend continuously to its open segment. Define $\widetilde u(x,y)=u(|x|,y)$. The zero value of $u_x$ makes the first [derivatives](derivative.md) match across the join. Applying [Green's first identity](green-s-first-identity.md) on the two sides against a smooth compactly supported test function leaves no interface term, so the reflected function is weakly harmonic and hence harmonic by [Weyl lemma](weyl-lemma.md).

Reflecting across both edges of a right-angle corner gives a [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) on a punctured neighbourhood when both [normal derivatives](normal-derivative.md) vanish. If the original function is bounded at the corner, the [removable singularity for a bounded harmonic function](removable-singularity-for-a-bounded-harmonic-function.md) makes the extension harmonic there too. Thus a maximum on such an edge or corner can be tested using the interior [strong maximum principle for harmonic functions](strong-maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions.md).
