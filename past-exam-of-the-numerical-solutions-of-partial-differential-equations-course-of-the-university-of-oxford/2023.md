## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

Write $w=u-v$ and $r=f-g$. Subtraction of the two [boundary value problems](../boundary-value-problem.md) gives $-w''+w+u^3-v^3=r$, with $w(0)=w(1)=0$. Multiply by $w$ and apply [integration by parts](../calculus.md#integration-by-parts):

$$
\int_0^1(-w'')w\,dx=-[w'w]_0^1+\int_0^1(w')^2\,dx=\int_0^1(w')^2\,dx.
$$

Consequently the required [energy identity](../numerical-analysis.md#energy-identity) is

$$
\int_0^1\left((w')^2+w^2+(u^3-v^3)w\right)dx=\int_0^1rw\,dx.
$$

The nonlinear term is nonnegative pointwise, since

$$
(u^3-v^3)(u-v)=(u-v)^2(u^2+uv+v^2),\qquad u^2+uv+v^2=(u+v/2)^2+3v^2/4\geq0.
$$

This is the [monotonicity energy estimate for a semilinear Dirichlet problem](../numerical-analysis.md#monotonicity-energy-estimate-for-a-semilinear-dirichlet-problem) for the [monotone function](../calculus.md#monotonic-function) $s\mapsto s^3$. Apply [Young's inequality for products](../nonlinear-analysis.md#young-s-inequality-for-products), $rw\leq(r^2+w^2)/2$, inside the last [integral](../integral.md) and discard the nonnegative nonlinear term. Moving $\tfrac12\int_0^1w^2dx$ to the left gives

$$
\int_0^1(u'-v')^2dx+\frac12\int_0^1(u-v)^2dx\leq\frac12\int_0^1(f-g)^2dx.
$$

In particular, equal forcing gives $w=0$, proving [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness) and a quantitative [continuous dependence on forcing](../boundary-value-problem.md#continuous-dependence-on-forcing) estimate for this [boundary value problem](../boundary-value-problem.md).

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

Let $f_i=f(ih)$ and $g_i=g(ih)$, and define the scaled [forward difference operator](../finite-difference.md#forward-difference-operator), [backward difference operator](../finite-difference.md#backward-difference-operator) and [second-order central difference](../finite-difference.md#second-order-central-difference) by

$$
D_x^+W_i=\frac{W_{i+1}-W_i}{h},\qquad D_x^-W_i=\frac{W_i-W_{i-1}}h,\qquad
D_x^+D_x^-W_i=\frac{W_{i+1}-2W_i+W_{i-1}}{h^2}.
$$

The two [finite difference methods](../finite-difference.md#finite-difference-method) are the nonlinear systems

$$
\boxed{-D_x^+D_x^-U_i+U_i+U_i^3=f_i,\qquad -D_x^+D_x^-V_i+V_i+V_i^3=g_i,\quad 1\leq i\leq N-1,}
$$

with the prescribed [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) $U_0=V_0=A$ and $U_N=V_N=B$. These equations determine the interior nodal values.

For clarity distinguish the interior and edge [inner products](../inner-product.md) and their [norms](../normed-vector-space.md#norm):

$$
(P,Q)_h=h\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}P_iQ_i,\quad
(P,Q]_{h}=h\sum_{i=1}^NP_iQ_i,\quad
\|P\|_h^2=(P,P)_h,\quad
\|P]\!\|_h^2=(P,P]_{h}.
$$

The bracketed [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) includes the last edge $i=N$. Let $W=U-V$, so $W_0=W_N=0$, and put $q_i=D_x^-W_i$ for $1\leq i\leq N$. Direct telescoping gives [discrete summation by parts](../finite-difference.md#discrete-summation-by-parts):

$$
(-D_x^+D_x^-W,W)_h
=-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(q_{i+1}-q_i)W_i
=\sum_{i=1}^Nq_i(W_i-W_{i-1})
=h\sum_{i=1}^Nq_i^2.
$$

Subtract the two nonlinear systems, multiply each equation by $hW_i$ and sum. The resulting discrete [energy identity](../numerical-analysis.md#energy-identity) is

$$
\|D_x^-W]\!\|_h^2+\|W\|_h^2+h\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(U_i^3-V_i^3)W_i=(f-g,W)_h.
$$

The nonlinear summands are nonnegative by the factorization in (a). Using [Young's inequality for products](../nonlinear-analysis.md#young-s-inequality-for-products) at every node gives

$$
\boxed{\|D_x^-U-D_x^-V]\!\|_h^2+\frac12\|U-V\|_h^2\leq\frac12\|f-g\|_h^2.}
$$

This also proves [uniqueness](../mathematical-logic.md#uniqueness) of each discrete solution. Existence can be seen by minimizing, with the endpoints fixed, the [strictly convex](../real-analysis.md#strictly-convex-function) [energy functional](../calculus-of-variations.md#energy-functional)

$$
J_f(W)=\frac h2\sum_{i=1}^N(D_x^-W_i)^2+h\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{W_i^2}2+\frac{W_i^4}4-f_iW_i\right).
$$

It is a [coercive function](../real-analysis.md#coercive-function) of its finitely many interior values, so it attains a minimum. Its first [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative) are $h(-D_x^+D_x^-W_i+W_i+W_i^3-f_i)$; thus its [minimizer](../analysis.md#global-minimizer) solves the scheme. The same argument applies to $g$.

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

Use $u_i=u(x_i)$ for the sampled exact solution, and let $e_i=U_i-u_i$. The [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) imply $e_0=e_N=0$. The definition of the [truncation error](../numerical-analysis.md#truncation-error) $\varphi$ gives the exact discrete equation

$$
-D_x^+D_x^-u_i+u_i+u_i^3=f_i-\varphi_i.
$$

Subtracting it from the numerical equation therefore gives

$$
-D_x^+D_x^-e_i+e_i+(U_i^3-u_i^3)=\varphi_i.
$$

The sign on the right is positive because $\varphi_i$ was defined as the difference approximation minus the exact [second derivative](../calculus.md#second-derivative). Multiply by $he_i$, sum over the interior nodes and apply [discrete summation by parts](../finite-difference.md#discrete-summation-by-parts):

$$
\|D_x^-e]\!\|_h^2+\|e\|_h^2+h\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(U_i^3-u_i^3)e_i=(\varphi,e)_h.
$$

Again $(U_i^3-u_i^3)e_i=e_i^2(U_i^2+U_iu_i+u_i^2)\geq0$. By [Young's inequality for products](../nonlinear-analysis.md#young-s-inequality-for-products), $(\varphi,e)_h\leq\tfrac12\|\varphi\|_h^2+\tfrac12\|e\|_h^2$. Hence

$$
\boxed{\|D_x^-U-D_x^-u]\!\|_h^2+\frac12\|U-u\|_h^2\leq\frac12\|\varphi\|_h^2.}
$$

No fourth [derivative](../calculus.md#derivative) is needed for this step: the assumed twice continuous differentiability suffices to define $\varphi$ and obtain the discrete [energy estimate](../partial-differential-equation.md#energy-estimate).

<h3 id="1/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#1/d)

On the [vector space](../vector-space.md) of nodal values with zero endpoints define the [discrete Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#discrete-sobolev-norm)

$$
\boxed{\|W\|_{1,h}=\left(\|W\|_h^2+\|D_x^-W]\!\|_h^2\right)^{1/2}
=\left(h\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}W_i^2+h\sum_{i=1}^N\left(\frac{W_i-W_{i-1}}h\right)^2\right)^{1/2}.}
$$

It is a [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) because it is the [Euclidean norm](../normed-vector-space.md#euclidean-norm) of the scaled list of nodal values and first [finite differences](../finite-difference.md), and vanishes only for the zero list. It is the discrete counterpart of the [Sobolev space](../sobolev-space.md) $H^1$ [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). Here $W=U-u$ has zero endpoints, so this definition applies directly.

Set $M_4=\max_{0\leq x\leq1}|u^{(4)}(x)|$, finite by the assumed [continuity](../calculus.md#continuous-function). [Taylor's theorem](../calculus.md#taylor-s-theorem) with fourth-order remainders gives, for suitable $\xi_{i,+}\in(x_i,x_i+h)$ and $\xi_{i,-}\in(x_i-h,x_i)$,

$$
u(x_i\pm h)=u_i\pm hu'_i+\frac{h^2}2u''_i\pm\frac{h^3}6u'''_i+\frac{h^4}{24}u^{(4)}(\xi_{i,\pm}).
$$

Adding the two expansions cancels the odd [derivatives](../calculus.md#derivative), so

$$
\varphi_i=\frac{h^2}{24}\left(u^{(4)}(\xi_{i,+})+u^{(4)}(\xi_{i,-})\right),\qquad
|\varphi_i|\leq\frac{M_4h^2}{12}.
$$

Consequently $\|\varphi\|_h^2\leq h(N-1)M_4^2h^4/144\leq M_4^2h^4/144$. Doubling the bound in (c) gives $2\|D_x^-e]\!\|_h^2+\|e\|_h^2\leq\|\varphi\|_h^2$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{\|U-u\|_{1,h}\leq\|\varphi\|_h\leq\frac{M_4}{12}h^2.}
$$

Thus $M_4/12$ is a valid nonnegative constant. To satisfy the request for a strictly positive constant even when $u^{(4)}\equiv0$, take **$C=\max\{1,M_4\}/12$**, independent of $h$. This proves second-order [convergence of a numerical method](../numerical-analysis.md#convergence-of-a-numerical-method) in a [discrete Sobolev norm](../sobolev-space.md#discrete-sobolev-norm), including control of the [backward difference operator](../finite-difference.md#backward-difference-operator) applied to the error.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For $u^{(m,n)}(x,y)=\sin(m\pi x)\sin(n\pi y)$, direct differentiation yields

$$
\partial_{xx}u^{(m,n)}=-(m\pi)^2u^{(m,n)},\qquad
\partial_{yy}u^{(m,n)}=-(n\pi)^2u^{(m,n)}.
$$

Since the planar [Laplacian](../multivariable-calculus.md#laplacian) is $\Delta=\partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy}$, the required [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) of $-\Delta+I$ is

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{m,n}=1+\pi^2(m^2+n^2)>0.}
$$

On the sides $x=0,1$ the first [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) factor vanishes, and on $y=0,1$ the second [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) factor vanishes. Thus the homogeneous [Dirichlet boundary condition](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) holds on the whole boundary. The [eigenfunction](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) is nonzero, for instance at $(x,y)=(1/(2m),1/(2n))$, where its value is one.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

The [five-point Laplacian](../finite-difference.md#five-point-laplacian) on the square grid is

$$
\boxed{\Delta_hW_{i,j}=\frac{W_{i+1,j}+W_{i-1,j}+W_{i,j+1}+W_{i,j-1}-4W_{i,j}}{h^2},\qquad1\leq i,j\leq N-1.}
$$

It adds the [second-order central differences](../finite-difference.md#second-order-central-difference) in the two coordinate directions and uses the given zero [Dirichlet boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#dirichlet-boundary-condition) when a neighbouring node lies on the boundary.

The [sine addition formula](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine-addition-formula) implies

$$
\sin((i+1)\alpha)+\sin((i-1)\alpha)-2\sin(i\alpha)
=2(\cos\alpha-1)\sin(i\alpha)
=-4\sin^2(\alpha/2)\sin(i\alpha).
$$

Apply this identity with $\alpha=m\pi h$ in the $x$ direction and with $\alpha=n\pi h$ in the $y$ direction. For $U_{i,j}^{(m,n)}=\sin(m\pi ih)\sin(n\pi jh)$, it gives

$$
-\Delta_hU_{i,j}^{(m,n)}+U_{i,j}^{(m,n)}
=\left[1+\frac4{h^2}\left(\sin^2\frac{m\pi h}2+\sin^2\frac{n\pi h}2\right)\right]U_{i,j}^{(m,n)}.
$$

Hence the discrete [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) are

$$
\boxed{\Lambda_{m,n}=1+\frac4{h^2}\left(\sin^2\frac{m\pi h}2+\sin^2\frac{n\pi h}2\right)>0,\qquad1\leq m,n\leq N-1.}
$$

Both [sine](../geometry-and-topology.md#sine) factors vanish on their respective boundary sides. Also $U_{1,1}^{(m,n)}>0$, since $m\pi/N$ and $n\pi/N$ lie strictly between zero and $\pi$. Thus each array is a nonzero discrete [eigenvector](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvector), rather than merely the zero solution of the homogeneous system. The separated structure is also described by the [five-point Dirichlet Laplacian as a Kronecker sum](../finite-difference.md#five-point-dirichlet-laplacian-as-a-kronecker-sum).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Set $z=\pi h/2$, so $0<z\leq\pi/4$. The formulas in (a) and (b) give

$$
\lambda_{1,1}-\Lambda_{1,1}=\frac8{h^2}(z^2-\sin^2z).
$$

For $0\leq t\leq\pi/4$, $0\leq\sin t\leq t$ and $1-\cos t=2\sin^2(t/2)\leq t^2/2$. Therefore

$$
0\leq z-\sin z=\int_0^z(1-\cos t)dt\leq\frac{z^3}6.
$$

These [trigonometric inequalities](../geometry-and-topology.md#trigonometric-inequality) imply

$$
0\leq z^2-\sin^2z=(z-\sin z)(z+\sin z)\leq\frac{z^3}6(2z)=\frac{z^4}3.
$$

Substitution yields the explicit [eigenvalue](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue) error bound

$$
\boxed{|\lambda_{1,1}-\Lambda_{1,1}|\leq\frac{\pi^4}{6}h^2,\qquad C=\frac{\pi^4}{6}.}
$$

This constant is asymptotically sharp: [Taylor's theorem](../calculus.md#taylor-s-theorem) gives $\sin^2z=z^2-z^4/3+O(z^6)$, so $(\lambda_{1,1}-\Lambda_{1,1})/h^2\to\pi^4/6$. Here $O$ denotes [Big O notation](../real-analysis.md#big-o-notation).

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

For every admissible $m,n$, the [trigonometric inequality](../geometry-and-topology.md#trigonometric-inequality) $\sin z\leq z$ for $z\geq0$ gives

$$
\frac4{h^2}\sin^2\frac{m\pi h}2\leq m^2\pi^2,\qquad
\frac4{h^2}\sin^2\frac{n\pi h}2\leq n^2\pi^2.
$$

Thus $\Lambda_{m,n}\leq\lambda_{m,n}$, in particular for $m=n=N-1$ and every $N\geq3$.

For these highest-index [eigenvalues](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenvalue), use $h=1/N$ and $\sin((N-1)\pi/(2N))=\cos(\pi/(2N))$ to obtain

$$
\lambda_{N-1,N-1}-\Lambda_{N-1,N-1}
=2\pi^2(N-1)^2-8N^2\cos^2\frac\pi{2N}.
$$

Divide by $N^2$ and take the [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function):

$$
\boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{N-1,N-1}-\Lambda_{N-1,N-1}}{N^2}=2\pi^2-8>0.}
$$

For example, eventually the normalized difference exceeds $(2\pi^2-8)/2$, so the unnormalized difference is at least $(\pi^2-4)N^2$ and tends to $+\infty$. This does not contradict (c): there the indices stay fixed as the grid is refined; here the [eigenfunctions](../linear-operator-theory.md#eigenfunction) oscillate increasingly quickly, with [wavelength](../wave-equation.md#wavelength) comparable to the mesh spacing. It illustrates the [high-frequency eigenvalue error of the discrete Laplacian](../finite-difference.md#high-frequency-eigenvalue-error-of-the-discrete-laplacian).

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

Use the [Backward Euler method](../numerical-analysis.md#backward-euler-method) in time and the prescribed [central finite differences](../finite-difference.md#central-finite-difference) in space. With $j\in\mathbb Z$ and $m\geq0$, the implicit [finite difference method](../finite-difference.md#finite-difference-method) for the [advection-diffusion equation](../diffusion-equation.md#advection-diffusion-equation) is

$$
\boxed{\frac{U_j^{m+1}-U_j^m}{\Delta t}+\frac{U_{j+1}^{m+1}-U_{j-1}^{m+1}}{2\Delta x}
=a\frac{U_{j+1}^{m+1}-2U_j^{m+1}+U_{j-1}^{m+1}}{(\Delta x)^2},\qquad U_j^0=u_0(j\Delta x).}
$$

Equivalently, for $\nu=\Delta t/\Delta x$ and $\mu=\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2$, the new time level solves

$$
(\nu/2-a\mu)U_{j+1}^{m+1}+(1+2a\mu)U_j^{m+1}+(-\nu/2-a\mu)U_{j-1}^{m+1}=U_j^m.
$$

The spatial terms are evaluated at the new time level, making this an implicit scheme. On the whole line there are no finite-endpoint [boundary conditions](../differential-equation.md#boundary-condition); in the next part its solution is chosen in the [l2 sequence space](../banach-space.md#l2-sequence-space).

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

For a grid sequence define its [semidiscrete Fourier transform](../fourier-analysis.md#semidiscrete-fourier-transform) in the dimensionless frequency variable $\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]$ by

$$
\widehat W(\theta)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}W_je^{-ij\theta},\qquad
W_j=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\widehat W(\theta)e^{ij\theta}d\theta.
$$

For general square-summable data the transform is interpreted as an limit in the [L2 norm](../real-analysis.md#l2-norm), rather than an [absolutely convergent](../sequence-and-series.md#absolute-convergence) pointwise sum. The [Parseval identity](../fourier-analysis.md#parseval-identity) in this normalization is

$$
\|W\|_{\ell_2}^2=\Delta x\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}|W_j|^2
=\frac{\Delta x}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\widehat W(\theta)|^2d\theta.
$$

The [Fourier symbols](../finite-difference.md#fourier-symbol-of-a-difference-operator) of $D_x^0$ and $D_x^+D_x^-$ are $i\sin\theta/\Delta x$ and $-4\sin^2(\theta/2)/(\Delta x)^2$. Transforming the implicit equation gives

$$
\left(1+4a\mu\sin^2(\theta/2)+i\nu\sin\theta\right)\widehat U^{m+1}=\widehat U^m.
$$

Its [amplification factor](../finite-difference.md#amplification-factor) is consequently

$$
G_I(\theta)=\frac1{1+4a\mu\sin^2(\theta/2)+i\nu\sin\theta}.
$$

The denominator has real part at least one because $a\geq0$. It never vanishes, so multiplication by $G_I$ defines a unique next value in the [l2 sequence space](../banach-space.md#l2-sequence-space), and

$$
|G_I(\theta)|^2=\frac1{(1+4a\mu\sin^2(\theta/2))^2+\nu^2\sin^2\theta}\leq1.
$$

The [Parseval identity](../fourier-analysis.md#parseval-identity) now gives

$$
\boxed{\|U^{m+1}\|_{\ell_2}\leq\|U^m\|_{\ell_2}\leq\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}\quad\text{for every }m\geq0,\ \Delta x,\Delta t>0.}
$$

This is unconditional [practical stability of a finite difference scheme](../numerical-analysis.md#practical-stability-of-a-finite-difference-scheme): in the course's terminology the numerical evolution does not increase this [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). Linearity gives the same estimate for perturbations of the initial data. This contraction convention is stronger than a finite-time mesh-uniform bound allowing physical exponential growth.[Archived Oxford NSDE I Lecture 12, PDF page 2, definition of practical stability as non-growth of the discrete norm](https://www.numerical.rl.ac.uk/media/people/tyrone-rees/nsde/notes/Lecture12.pdf)

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Use the [forward Euler method](../numerical-analysis.md#euler-method) in time and evaluate both spatial [finite difference operators](../finite-difference.md#finite-difference-operator) at the old time level:

$$
\boxed{\frac{U_j^{m+1}-U_j^m}{\Delta t}+\frac{U_{j+1}^{m}-U_{j-1}^{m}}{2\Delta x}
=a\frac{U_{j+1}^{m}-2U_j^{m}+U_{j-1}^{m}}{(\Delta x)^2},\qquad U_j^0=u_0(j\Delta x).}
$$

With $\nu=\Delta t/\Delta x$ and $\mu=\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2$, the explicit update is

$$
\boxed{U_j^{m+1}=(a\mu+\nu/2)U_{j-1}^m+(1-2a\mu)U_j^m+(a\mu-\nu/2)U_{j+1}^m.}
$$

Every value on the new time level is computed directly from three old values. The sum of the stencil weights is one, as required to preserve constant solutions of the [advection-diffusion equation](../diffusion-equation.md#advection-diffusion-equation).

<h3 id="3/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

Using the [semidiscrete Fourier transform](../fourier-analysis.md#semidiscrete-fourier-transform) from (b), the explicit scheme has [amplification factor](../finite-difference.md#amplification-factor)

$$
G_E(\theta)=1-4a\mu\sin^2(\theta/2)-i\nu\sin\theta.
$$

Set $r=a\mu$ and $s=\sin^2(\theta/2)\in[0,1]$, so $\sin^2\theta=4s(1-s)$. Direct expansion gives

$$
|G_E|^2=(1-4rs)^2+4\nu^2s(1-s),\qquad
|G_E|^2-1=4s\left[(\nu^2-2r)+(4r^2-\nu^2)s\right].
$$

The expression in square brackets is an [affine function](../vector-space.md#affine-function) of $s$. At the two endpoints its values are

$$
\nu^2-2r\leq0,\qquad 4r^2-2r=2r(2r-1)\leq0,
$$

using precisely $\nu^2\leq2r\leq1$. Every intermediate value is a [convex combination](../convex-set.md#convex-combination) of these endpoints, so $|G_E|^2\leq1$ for every frequency. The [Parseval identity](../fourier-analysis.md#parseval-identity) proves

$$
\boxed{\|U^m\|_{\ell_2}\leq\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}\quad(m\geq0),\qquad\nu^2\leq2a\mu\leq1.}
$$

Thus the scheme is [practically stable](../numerical-analysis.md#practical-stability-of-a-finite-difference-scheme) under the given condition. For $a>0$ it is equivalent to $\Delta t\leq2a$ and $\Delta t\leq(\Delta x)^2/(2a)$. This is the [Forward Euler stability for centered advection-diffusion](../finite-difference.md#forward-euler-stability-for-centered-advection-diffusion) calculation with the diffusion coefficient retained. The proof does not require all three stencil weights to be nonnegative.

<h3 id="3/e">e</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/e/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [E](#3/e)

When $a=0$, the explicit [amplification factor](../finite-difference.md#amplification-factor) becomes

$$
G_E(\theta)=1-i\nu\sin\theta,\qquad |G_E(\theta)|^2=1+\nu^2\sin^2\theta.
$$

A single [Fourier mode](../fourier-analysis.md#fourier-mode) has constant modulus in space and is not in the [l2 sequence space](../banach-space.md#l2-sequence-space). Instead choose a whole interval of frequencies on which there is definite growth. Let

$$
E=[\pi/4,3\pi/4]\cup[-3\pi/4,-\pi/4],\qquad\widehat U^0(\theta)=\mathbf1_E(\theta).
$$

The [inverse Fourier transform](../fourier-transform.md#inverse-fourier-transform) here gives the real [sequence](../sequence-and-series.md#sequence)

$$
U_j^0=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sin(3\pi j/4)-\sin(\pi j/4)}{\pi j},&j\ne0,\\
\dfrac12,&j=0.
\end{cases}
$$

By the [Parseval identity](../fourier-analysis.md#parseval-identity), its squared [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) is $\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}^2=\Delta x\,|E|/(2\pi)=\Delta x/2$, finite and strictly positive. These data are also compatible with sampling a bounded [continuous function](../calculus.md#continuous-function): use

$$
u_0(x)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sin(3\pi x/(4\Delta x))-\sin(\pi x/(4\Delta x))}{\pi x/\Delta x},&x\ne0,\\
\dfrac12,&x=0.
\end{cases}
$$

The value at zero is the [limit](../calculus.md#limit-of-a-function) of the displayed quotient, and $u_0(j\Delta x)=U_j^0$.

On $E$ we have $\sin^2\theta\geq1/2$. Therefore the transformed solution and the [Parseval identity](../fourier-analysis.md#parseval-identity) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|U^m\|_{\ell_2}^2
&=\frac{\Delta x}{2\pi}\int_E(1+\nu^2\sin^2\theta)^m\,d\theta\\
&\geq(1+\nu^2/2)^m\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

For each fixed pair $\Delta x,\Delta t>0$, take

$$
\boxed{c(\Delta x,\Delta t)=\sqrt{1+\frac12\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\right)^2}-1>0.}
$$

Taking square roots proves $\|U^m\|_{\ell_2}\geq(1+c)^m\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}$ for every $m\geq1$. In particular the [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm) already increases at the first step, so **no positive mesh pair gives practical stability** in the contraction sense of (b).

The native PDF places the existence of $c>0$ before the universal quantifier over mesh pairs. If this means a single positive $c$ independent of $\Delta x,\Delta t$, that assertion is false for nonzero data. Indeed, for every datum the same [Fourier symbol](../finite-difference.md#fourier-symbol-of-a-difference-operator) gives

$$
\|U^1\|_{\ell_2}\leq\sqrt{1+\nu^2}\,\|U^0\|_{\ell_2}.
$$

For any proposed fixed $c>0$, choose $0<\nu<\sqrt{(1+c)^2-1}$. The upper bound then contradicts the requested lower bound at $m=1$. The correct quantifiers are $\forall\Delta x,\Delta t>0\ \exists c=c(\Delta x,\Delta t)>0$, with nonzero square-summable data as constructed above.

Finally, failure of this stronger [practical stability of a finite difference scheme](../numerical-analysis.md#practical-stability-of-a-finite-difference-scheme) should not be confused with every possible refinement notion of [numerical stability](../numerical-analysis.md#stability-of-a-numerical-method). If $\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2\leq K$, then $m\Delta t\leq T$ gives

$$
|G_E(\theta)|^m\leq\exp(m\nu^2/2)\leq\exp(KT/2).
$$

Thus a finite-time mesh-uniform bound can hold under this severe refinement even though contraction fails on every positive mesh. This is the distinction recorded in [centered-advection refinement-dependent stability](../finite-difference.md#centered-advection-refinement-dependent-stability).
