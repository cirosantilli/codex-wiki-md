# Heat

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamics](thermodynamics-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat)

Heat is [energy](energy-split.md) transferred across a [thermodynamic system](thermodynamic-system.md)'s boundary because of a [temperature](temperature-split.md) difference. It is measured in [joules](joule.md), like [work](work.md) and [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md). A hot cup transfers [heat](heat-split.md) to cooler surroundings; its [temperature](temperature-split.md) describes its state, while the transferred [heat](heat-split.md) describes a process. The cup stores [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md), rather than a substance called [heat](heat-split.md). [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, OpenStax, University Physics Volume 2, section 1.4: heat and internal energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/1-4-heat-transfer-specific-heat-and-calorimetry)

For a [closed system](closed-system.md) with unchanged bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](potential-energy.md), choose $Q>0$ for [heat](heat-split.md) entering and $W>0$ for [work](work.md) done by the system. The [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) gives

$$
\Delta U=Q-W,\qquad dU=\delta Q-\delta W.
$$

Here $U$ is the stored [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md). For example, a sealed piston cylinder receiving $20\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [heat](heat-split.md) and delivering $5\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [work](work.md) gains $15\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md). A [closed system](closed-system.md) retains its matter while exchanging [energy](energy-split.md); it need not be an [isolated system](isolated-system.md). [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, OpenStax, First law of thermodynamics and sign conventions](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/3-3-first-law-of-thermodynamics)

![](heat-work-internal-energy-balance.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-heat-input-and-work-output-determine-the-internal-energy-increase). Heat input and work output determine the internal energy increase**. The dashed boundary encloses a [closed system](closed-system.md) in a sealed piston cylinder. The incoming arrow carries $Q=20\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [heat](heat-split.md); the outgoing arrow carries $W=5\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of [work](work.md) done by the system. With unchanged bulk [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) and [potential energy](potential-energy.md), $\Delta U=15\,\mathrm{kJ}$. No matter crosses the boundary.

Unlike [internal energy](internal-energy-split.md), [heat](heat-split.md) is not a [state function](state-function.md): different paths between the same equilibrium states can transfer different $Q$, with compensating differences in [work](work.md). The symbol $\delta Q$ records this dependence on the path; there is no stored quantity whose difference is always $Q$. The two paths in [first law of thermodynamics](first-law-of-thermodynamics-split.md) illustrate this directly. Supplied [heat](heat-split.md) can raise [temperature](temperature-split.md) as [sensible heat](sensible-heat.md), change a [phase fraction](phase-fraction.md) through [latent heat](latent-heat.md), or help supply expansion [work](work.md). Measuring [temperature](temperature-split.md) alone therefore does not determine transferred [heat](heat-split.md).

For a [reversible thermodynamic process](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md), $\delta Q_{\rm rev}=T\,dS$, where $T$ is absolute [temperature](temperature-split.md) and $S$ is [entropy](entropy-split.md). This identification requires reversibility: an irreversible [adiabatic process](adiabatic-process.md) has $Q=0$ and can nevertheless increase [entropy](entropy-split.md). Likewise $Q=\Delta H$, with [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md) $H$, requires the particular fixed-[pressure](pressure-split.md) conditions in [enthalpy](enthalpy-split.md). These distinctions make [heat](heat-split.md) useful as a precise boundary-transfer quantity in [heat engines](heat-engine.md), [refrigerators](refrigerator.md) and heating calculations.

Historically, James Prescott Joule's paddle experiments showed that stirring [water](water.md) with input [work](work.md) could raise its [temperature](temperature-split.md), helping establish [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md). The lesson is still useful: a [temperature](temperature-split.md) rise can result from [work](work.md) even without incoming [heat](heat-split.md). [Ling, Moebs and Sanny, OpenStax, section 1.4: Joule's paddle experiment](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/1-4-heat-transfer-specific-heat-and-calorimetry)
