# Rank-one elliptic curve with roots zero two and ten

↑ **Parent:** [Two-isogeny descent](two-isogeny-descent.md)

This [elliptic curve](elliptic-curve.md) has [torsion subgroup](torsion-subgroup.md) $\{O,(0,0),(2,0),(10,0)\}\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ and the infinite-order point $(1,3)$. Good-reduction point counts at three and eleven are four and sixteen, which bound the torsion order by four using [reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve](reduction-of-torsion-points-on-an-elliptic-curve.md). Doubling $(1,3)$ gives $(361/36,-323/216)$; after the shift $X=x-4$ to $y^2=X^3-28X-48$, its nonintegral coordinate proves nontorsion by the [Nagell–Lutz theorem](nagell-lutz-theorem.md). The [two-isogeny formula](two-isogeny-formula.md) gives the partner $Y^2=X^3+24X^2+64X$. The first [two-torsion square-class homomorphism](two-torsion-square-class-homomorphism.md) has image $\{1,2,5,10\}$, while the second has image $\{1,-1\}$ by the [signed divisor-two obstruction for an isogeny covering](signed-divisor-two-obstruction-for-an-isogeny-covering.md). The [two-isogeny rank formula](square-class-index-formula-for-two-isogeny-descent.md) therefore gives $2^r=4\cdot2/4=2$, so the rank is one. The argument does not determine the index of the subgroup generated by $(1,3)$.
