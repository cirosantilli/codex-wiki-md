# Wavefunction matching at a finite potential step

↑ **Parent:** [Time-independent Schrödinger equation](time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md)

For a finite jump in a one-dimensional [potential energy](potential-energy.md), with constant [mass](mass.md) and no [delta potential](delta-potential.md), both the [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md) and its first spatial [derivative](derivative.md) are continuous. Integrating the [time-independent Schrödinger equation](time-independent-schrodinger-equation.md) across a vanishing interval makes the jump in $\psi'$ vanish, since the integral of $(V-E)\psi$ vanishes. A jump in $\psi$ itself would produce a derivative of a [Dirac delta function](dirac-delta-function.md) in $\psi''$, with no matching term in the equation. The second [derivative](derivative.md) can jump. This matching allows transmission and [quantum tunnelling](quantum-tunnelling.md); a finite step is not a hard wall.
