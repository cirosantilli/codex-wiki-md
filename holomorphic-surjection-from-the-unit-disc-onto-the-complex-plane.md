# Holomorphic surjection from the unit disc onto the complex plane

↑ **Parent:** [Holomorphic function](holomorphic-function-split.md)

The [rational function](rational-function.md) $F(z)=z^2/(1-z)^2$ is [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) on the [unit disc](unit-disc.md) and maps it onto the entire [complex plane](complex-plane.md). For any $w\in\mathbb C$, choose a square root $r$ with $\operatorname{Re}r\geq0$ and put $z=r/(1+r)$. The identity $|1+r|^2-|r|^2=1+2\operatorname{Re}r>0$ gives $|z|<1$, while $F(z)=r^2=w$. It is not injective: the two choices $r=\pm i$ give distinct points in the disc with image $-1$.

A [holomorphic](holomorphic-function-split.md) bijection in this direction is impossible: its inverse would be a bounded [entire function](entire-function.md), contradicting the [Liouville theorem](liouville-theorem.md). Thus a surjection onto the [complex plane](complex-plane.md) can exist even when a [conformal equivalence](conformal-equivalence.md) cannot.
