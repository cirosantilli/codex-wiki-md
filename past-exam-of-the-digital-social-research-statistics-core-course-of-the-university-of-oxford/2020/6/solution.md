<h1 id="6/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [6](../6.md)

**Read conditionally, the fitted slope says that a one-point higher skill score is associated with about 0.209 more household devices in the fitted [conditional expectation](../../../conditional-expectation.md). The printed summary also claims a significant slope and about 28.93% explained sample variation. Those claims cannot be assembled into one valid fitted model: several regression identities fail, so a single verified coefficient-and-uncertainty report is unavailable.**

The response is a device count, while the predictor is a skill score. A [simple linear regression](../../../simple-linear-regression.md) models $\mathbb E(Y\mid x)=\beta_0+\beta_1x$, where $Y$ is the device count and $x$ the skill score. [Ordinary least squares](../../../ordinary-least-squares.md) chooses the [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) and [regression slope](../../../regression-slope.md) to minimize the [residual sum of squares](../../../residual-sum-of-squares.md). The [regression slope](../../../regression-slope.md) describes a conditional association, not the [causal effect](../../../causal-effect.md) of training or acquiring a device.

The [descriptive statistics](../../../descriptive-statistics.md) put the device count between $0$ and $8$, with [sample mean](../../../sample-mean.md) about $4.494$ and [sample standard deviation](../../../sample-standard-deviation.md) about $2.128$ among $2053$ records. Skill scores range from $0$ to $24$, with [sample mean](../../../sample-mean.md) about $13.546$ and [sample standard deviation](../../../sample-standard-deviation.md) about $5.989$ among $2051$ records. Two missing skill values account for the stated two deleted records; because device counts have no missing values, the regression complete-case sample has $2051$ observations. Estimating two [regression coefficients](../../../regression-coefficient.md) leaves $2051-2=2049$ residual [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md), matching that part of the output. The units of the two [standard deviations](../../../standard-deviation.md) differ, so comparing $5.989$ with $2.128$ does not establish that skill is more variable in a substantive sense.

If the coefficient estimates are trusted, the [fitted values](../../../fitted-values.md) follow $\widehat y=0.392122+0.209240x$. The [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) predicts about $0.392$ devices at a skill score of zero; zero is within the stated score range, but precision and the number of observations near zero are not shown. A five-point increase in score corresponds to about $1.0462$ additional fitted devices and a ten-point increase to about $2.0924$. At a score of ten the fitted value is about $2.4845$, and at 24 it is about $5.4139$. [Expected counts](../../../expected-frequency.md) may be fractional even though individual counts are integers. These are conditional interpretations of the coefficient fields, not reconstructed raw observations or validated individual predictions.

A coefficient's [standard error](../../../standard-error.md) estimates its sampling uncertainty, whereas the [residual standard error](../../../residual-standard-error.md) describes unexplained response variation. For the [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md), $0.392122/0.114570\approx3.42255$, agreeing with the printed $t=3.423$ and rounded $p=0.000633$. Conditional on the model assumptions, this would reject a zero [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) at conventional significance levels. The [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) test is not a test of whether skills predict devices. For the slope, the printed $p=0.013672$ would reject $H_0:\beta_1=0$ at 5% but not 1%; it does not assign a [probability](../../../probability.md) to the [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md) or measure the usefulness of prediction. The significance stars simply encode thresholds and add no [independent](../../../independent-random-variables.md) evidence.[Value: coefficient, standard-error, two-sided t and p columns; sigma, residual degrees of freedom and F statistic](../../../summarizing-linear-model-fits-in-the-r-documentation.md)

The slope row fails its own arithmetic check:

$$
\frac{0.209240}{0.079377}\approx2.63603\ne2.882.
$$

At $2049$ [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md) the ratio gives a two-sided [p-value](../../../p-value.md) about $0.00845$, while the printed $t=2.882$ gives about $0.003993$; neither is $0.013672$. Thus the choice of which source field is trusted changes the apparent strength of evidence, including the 1% decision. A 95% [confidence interval](../../../confidence-interval.md) built conditionally from the printed estimate and [standard error](../../../standard-error.md) would be about $[0.0536,0.3649]$, but it is not a verified [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) from a coherent fitted model. In fact, using the printed [residual standard error](../../../residual-standard-error.md) $1.793$, the complete predictor [sample size](../../../sample-size.md) and predictor [standard deviation](../../../standard-deviation.md) gives a slope [standard error](../../../standard-error.md) near $0.006612$, not $0.079377$.

The printed [coefficient of determination](../../../coefficient-of-determination.md) $R^2=0.2893$ would mean that the fitted line explains $28.93\%$ of the complete-case sample's centred device-count [sum of squares](../../../sum-of-squares.md) relative to an intercept-only fit; $71.07\%$ remains unexplained by that comparison. It is not the [percentage](../../../percentage.md) of devices caused by skill, the fraction of correctly predicted households, or a measure of future predictive accuracy. The [adjusted coefficient of determination](../../../adjusted-coefficient-of-determination.md) $0.289$ is consistent with that $R^2$ and the given [sample size](../../../sample-size.md), allowing rounding. The small adjustment reflects fitting one slope among many cases; it does not validate the model.

For a one-predictor [ordinary least squares](../../../ordinary-least-squares.md) fit with an [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) and the usual common-variance [standard errors](../../../standard-error.md), the [simple-regression output identities](../../../simple-regression-output-identities.md) require

$$
F=t_{\mathrm{slope}}^2=\frac{(n-2)R^2}{1-R^2}.
$$

Here the printed slope t gives $F\approx8.305924$, the printed F field says $83.2$, and the printed $R^2$ instead implies $F\approx834.073$. These cannot all hold. Moreover, $F=83.2$ on $1$ and $2049$ [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md) has [p-value](../../../p-value.md) about $1.72\times10^{-19}$, not $0.001978$. Reading the printed F [p-value](../../../p-value.md) alone would give a 1% significant test of the zero-slope model, but its reported calibration is incompatible with its statistic. No significance label resolves these identities.

There is also a contradiction that does not depend on tail [probabilities](../../../probability.md). [Ordinary least squares](../../../ordinary-least-squares.md) with an [intercept](../../../regression-intercept.md) makes the [arithmetic mean](../../../arithmetic-mean.md) of [fitted values](../../../fitted-values.md) equal the [arithmetic mean](../../../arithmetic-mean.md) of observed responses on the analysed records. The printed coefficients and predictor mean give $0.392122+0.209240(13.546)\approx3.22649$. Deleting two device counts anywhere in the printed range $[0,8]$, and allowing the reported overall mean to be rounded, would leave the complete-case device mean between approximately $4.49008$ and $4.49888$. The [mean bounds after deleting bounded observations](../../../mean-bounds-after-deleting-bounded-observations.md) therefore rule out reconciling $3.22649$ with the response summary merely by citing those two missing predictor values. This is not the mistaken assumption that full-sample and complete-case [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md) must always be identical.

If its own field were trusted, [residual standard error](../../../residual-standard-error.md) $1.793$ estimates the unexplained response [standard deviation](../../../standard-deviation.md), in device-count units. It is neither the [standard error](../../../standard-error.md) of the slope nor a guarantee that every prediction is accurate to $1.793$ devices. The residual five-number summary has extremes near $-5.206$ and $5.086$ and [median](../../../median.md) near $-0.060$, with central [quartiles](../../../quartile.md) near $-1.372$ and $1.252$. The roughly central [median](../../../median.md) does not establish normally distributed errors, [homoscedasticity](../../../homoscedasticity.md), [independence](../../../independent-random-variables.md) or a linear [conditional expectation](../../../conditional-expectation.md). A [residual-versus-fitted plot](../../../residual-versus-fitted-plot.md) could check curvature and spread; a [normal probability plot](../../../normal-q-q-plot.md) could assess an error-shape approximation. Neither diagnostic can be recovered from five residual quantiles alone. Individual [prediction intervals](../../../prediction-interval.md) include response variation as well as coefficient uncertainty and are wider than [confidence intervals](../../../confidence-interval.md) for the [conditional expectation](../../../conditional-expectation.md).

For conventional finite-sample t and F calibration, a [normal linear model](../../../normal-linear-model-split.md) assumes [independent](../../../independent-random-variables.md) mean-zero normal errors of common [variance](../../../variance-split.md) with an appropriate predictor specification. Mean-zero errors conditional on skill and adequate sampling remain essential even when large-sample approximations relax normality. A bounded count response can have nonconstant conditional [variance](../../../variance-split.md), so residual diagnostics and a scientifically suitable response model matter; count data do not become normally distributed just because the sample is large. [Poisson regression](../../../poisson-regression.md) is one possible alternative to investigate, but its own conditional mean–[variance](../../../variance-split.md) assumptions and the bounded device range require assessment, not automatic substitution. Complete-case selection can also bias the fitted association if it depends on the response in a relevant way. **The only safe summary is conditional: the printed slope is positive, but the numerical effect, its [standard error](../../../standard-error.md), t/F evidence and explanatory fit require a corrected, coherent output before a single definitive regression claim is made.**
