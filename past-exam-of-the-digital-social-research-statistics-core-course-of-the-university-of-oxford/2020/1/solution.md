<h1 id="1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

**Taken at face value, the printed two-sided [p-value](../../../p-value.md) rejects equality of average [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) at the 5% level, with the non-employed group scoring higher. The effect magnitude and strength of evidence cannot be reported as one coherent result because the estimates, [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md), [test statistic](../../../test-statistic.md) and [p-value](../../../p-value.md) contradict one another.**

Let $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ be the expected [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) scores in the two employment groups in the [target population](../../../target-population.md). The [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md) is $H_0:\mu_0-\mu_1=0$ and the two-sided [alternative hypothesis](../../../alternative-hypothesis.md) is $H_1:\mu_0-\mu_1\ne0$. A [Welch's t-test](../../../welch-s-t-test.md) compares the difference of [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md) with its [standard error](../../../standard-error.md), estimating the two group [variances](../../../variance-split.md) separately:

$$
t=\frac{\bar y_0-\bar y_1}{\sqrt{s_0^2/n_0+s_1^2/n_1}}.
$$

The reference [Student t-distribution](../../../student-s-t-distribution.md) has approximate, generally noninteger [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md); it does not require equality of the two [variances](../../../variance-split.md). Observations should be [independent](../../../independent-random-variables.md), with finite group [variances](../../../variance-split.md); [normal distributions](../../../normal-distribution.md) give the usual small-sample rationale, while large samples permit an approximate normal [sampling distribution](../../../sampling-distribution.md) under suitable conditions. A large sample cannot correct a biased sampling procedure or dependence between observations.[§1.3.5.3, unequal-variance statistic and Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom](../../../two-sample-t-test-for-equal-means-by-national-institute-of-standards-and-technology.md)

A [p-value](../../../p-value.md) is a [probability](../../../probability.md) calculated _under the [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md) and the sampling assumptions_: for this [two-sided test](../../../two-sided-hypothesis-test.md) it measures the [probability](../../../probability.md) of an absolute [test statistic](../../../test-statistic.md) at least as large as the observed one. It is not the [probability](../../../probability.md) that the [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md) is true, the [probability](../../../probability.md) that chance caused the observed difference, or a measure of the [effect size](../../../effect-size.md). If the [significance level](../../../significance-level.md) $\alpha=0.05$ was chosen in advance, the printed $p=0.049521$ falls just below it. This would reject $H_0$ at 5%, but not at 1%. The [significance level](../../../significance-level.md) describes the long-run [probability](../../../probability.md) of a [type I error](../../../type-i-and-type-ii-errors.md), rejecting a true [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md); a [type II error](../../../type-i-and-type-ii-errors.md) instead fails to reject a false [null hypothesis](../../../null-hypothesis.md). [Statistical power](../../../statistical-power.md) is the rejection [probability](../../../probability.md) under a specified [alternative hypothesis](../../../alternative-hypothesis.md). None of these errors for this particular realised study can be read as a [posterior probability](../../../posterior-probability.md) from $p$.

Both the printed [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md) and the positive [confidence interval](../../../confidence-interval.md) point toward greater [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) in group 0, the non-employed group. Their magnitudes disagree. Subtracting the printed [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md) gives $8.656650-6.322812=2.333838$ scale units. Yet the printed 95% [confidence interval](../../../confidence-interval.md) has midpoint $(0.2822355+0.6743412)/2=0.47828835$, not $2.333838$. A two-sided Welch [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) is centred on the difference of [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md). This violates the [consistency identities for a two-sided t-test](../../../consistency-identities-for-a-two-sided-t-test.md); the [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) and estimates cannot describe the same reported comparison. If the [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) alone were trusted, it would exclude zero and indicate an increase between about $0.28$ and $0.67$ units, but that is explicitly a conditional interpretation. A 95% [confidence interval](../../../confidence-interval.md) refers to repeated-sample coverage of a fixed [statistical parameter](../../../statistical-parameter.md), not a 95% [posterior probability](../../../posterior-probability.md) for that realised [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md).[Arguments and Value: two-sided default, separate group variances, estimate, statistic, p.value and conf.int](../../../student-s-t-test-in-the-r-documentation.md)

There are additional arithmetic warnings. The printed $t=6.1616$ at $2022.4$ [statistical degrees of freedom](../../../statistical-degrees-of-freedom.md) gives a two-sided [p-value](../../../p-value.md) about $8.67\times10^{-10}$, not $0.049521$. Thus taking the [test statistic](../../../test-statistic.md) as authoritative would imply much stronger evidence, not merely a marginal 5% result. The employment counts sum to $2311$, but their [percentages](../../../percentage.md) are $905/2311\approx39.16\%$ and $1406/2311\approx60.84\%$, not $39.27\%$ and $60.73\%$. If those counts also apply to the two [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) [sample means](../../../sample-mean.md), their [weighted arithmetic mean](../../../weighted-arithmetic-mean.md) would be about $7.237$, not the reported overall $8.348$; separate complete-case group sizes are not supplied, so that last cross-output comparison is conditional on a common sample. No uniquely corrected difference, [standard error](../../../standard-error.md) or [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) follows from these contradictory fields.

The [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) summary describes a bounded observed scale from $0$ to $16$, with [median](../../../median.md) $9$ and middle [quartiles](../../../quartile.md) $7$ and $12$. The [interquartile range](../../../interquartile-range.md) is $5$; the overall [sample mean](../../../sample-mean.md) $8.348$ being below the [median](../../../median.md) does not, by itself, establish a particular distributional shape. These [descriptive statistics](../../../descriptive-statistics.md) cannot supply the missing coherent group [variances](../../../variance-split.md) or a standardized [effect size](../../../effect-size.md). Even a corrected small [p-value](../../../p-value.md) would establish an association under a [statistical model](../../../statistical-model-split.md), rather than a [causal effect](../../../causal-effect.md) of employment. Age, other background [covariates](../../../covariate.md), [selection bias](../../../selection-bias.md) and the quality of the [political efficacy](../../../political-efficacy.md) measurement remain relevant. **A defensible report gives the conditional 5% decision and direction, identifies the contradictions, and withholds a single numerical effect-and-uncertainty claim until the output is checked.**
