↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

**Employment is observed for all 2053 listed cases. [Social media](../../../social-media.md) use is observed for 1424 cases, with 629 missing. The age [percentages](../../../percentage.md) describe the five visible rows, whose counts total 1730; they cannot be treated as a complete age distribution for the stated 2053 cases.**

A [frequency table](../../../frequency-table.md) describes category counts and their [relative frequencies](../../../relative-frequency.md). The full-sample [percentage](../../../percentage.md) uses every listed case as denominator; a [valid percentage](../../../valid-percentage.md) excludes [missing data](../../../missing-data.md) for that variable. A [cumulative percentage](../../../cumulative-percentage.md) adds the [valid percentages](../../../valid-percentage.md) up to an ordered category. The denominator matters as much as the number: a statement about respondents with recorded use need not describe every respondent.

Employment is a [binary categorical variable](../../../binary-categorical-variable.md): 1216 cases are employed and 837 are not, or about $59.23\%$ and $40.77\%$. Neither employment category is missing in this output. Employment labels have no intrinsic numerical spacing; codes zero and one identify the two categories under a chosen ordering convention. [Cumulative percentages](../../../cumulative-percentage.md) for a [nominal categorical variable](../../../nominal-categorical-variable.md) depend on the arbitrary display order and add little to this two-category description.

Age groups are an [ordinal categorical variable](../../../ordinal-categorical-variable.md) because their [intervals](../../../interval-mathematics.md) have a natural ordering, although the grouped representation loses exact ages. Among the five _visible_ age rows, ages 25–44 form the largest group, with $562/1730\approx32.49\%$, and ages 14–24 the smallest, with $182/1730\approx10.52\%$. The other visible groups account for $20.98\%$, $16.42\%$ and $19.60\%$ on that denominator. About $43.01\%$ of the visible five-row count is below 45. These [percentages](../../../percentage.md) should not be silently applied to the stated total $2053$: for example, the same $562$ cases are only $27.37\%$ of $2053$.

The age variable key advertises six categories, whereas only five appear. Their counts sum to $1730$, leaving a difference of $323$ against the printed total; the table nevertheless prints zero missing values. This is an internal source discrepancy, not permission to label those $323$ cases as a particular unseen age group or as recorded [missing data](../../../missing-data.md). The oldest row is labelled 65+ here but 65–74 in Figure 2. Even the age [percentage](../../../percentage.md) total has a small display discrepancy: the five rounded [percentages](../../../percentage.md) sum to $100.01\%$, while the printed valid total and last [cumulative percentage](../../../cumulative-percentage.md) say $99.99\%$. The much larger count inconsistency remains after allowing ordinary rounding.

[Social media](../../../social-media.md) use is also an [ordinal categorical variable](../../../ordinal-categorical-variable.md). Its six ordered categories express increasing frequency, not equally spaced measurements: averaging category labels or claiming a one-unit change has a constant time meaning would require an additional scoring convention. Among the $1424$ observed responses, the [mode](../../../mode-statistics.md) is never, at $459/1424\approx32.23\%$, followed by daily, at $28.37\%$. Weekly use accounts for $18.19\%$, use more than once a day for $11.38\%$, less-than-monthly use for $6.39\%$, and monthly use for $3.44\%$. The first three categories together comprise about $42.06\%$ of valid responses. Consequently the middle ordered response lies in the weekly category: cumulative frequency first passes one half there.

Combining the two daily categories gives $566/1424\approx39.75\%$ of valid responses using [social media](../../../social-media.md) at least daily. The same recorded responses are $566/2053\approx27.57\%$ of all listed cases. The second [percentage](../../../percentage.md) is the fraction _recorded_ as daily users, not an estimate that classifies all missing responses as nonusers. Missing [social media](../../../social-media.md) responses make up $629/2053\approx30.64\%$. The directly calculated valid fraction is $69.36\%$; summing the individually rounded full-sample [percentages](../../../percentage.md) produces the printed $69.37\%$, a minor rounding difference. With no account of the [missing-data mechanism](../../../missing-data-mechanism.md), the observed valid distribution should not automatically be generalized to those with missing use. Employment being fully observed does not make [social media](../../../social-media.md) missingness ignorable. **The substantive distributional summary must retain both the valid-response denominator and the missing-data qualification.**
