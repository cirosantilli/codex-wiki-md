<h1 id="newton-s-third-law">Newton's third law</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Newton's laws of motion](newton-s-laws-of-motion.md)

In the pair-interaction model of [Newtonian mechanics](newtonian-mechanics-split.md), two bodies exert equal and opposite [forces](force-split.md) on each other:

$$
\mathbf F_{A\mathrm{\ on\ }B}=-\mathbf F_{B\mathrm{\ on\ }A}.
$$

Both [vectors](vector.md) are expressed in the same [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) at the same [time](time-in-physics.md). The subscripts name the agent and the recipient: $A$ acts on $B$, and $B$ acts on $A$. These are [forces](force-split.md) on different bodies, so they cannot cancel in the net [force](force-split.md) on either body. [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) still uses all the [forces](force-split.md) on the particular body whose [acceleration](acceleration.md) is sought. Identifying this [third-law interaction pair](third-law-interaction-pair.md) does not require the bodies to be isolated: additional [external forces](external-force.md) can act at the same [time](time-in-physics.md).

The distinction makes [Newton's third law](newton-s-third-law.md) useful both for drawing a [free-body diagram](free-body-diagram.md) and for deriving [momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md). A body-by-body calculation retains one member of each [third-law interaction pair](third-law-interaction-pair.md); a calculation for a collection containing both bodies cancels that pair in the total [momentum](momentum-split.md) balance. Equal interaction [forces](force-split.md) consequently coexist with nonzero, generally unequal [accelerations](acceleration.md).

[Isaac Newton](isaac-newton.md) stated the equality and opposition of mutual actions in Law III of his 1687 _Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica_. The explanation distinguishes equal opposite changes of [momentum](momentum-split.md) from changes of [velocity](velocity.md), which are inversely proportional to the bodies' [masses](mass.md) when other influences are absent. Corollary III then explains why mutual interactions preserve the signed total [momentum](momentum-split.md). The equations here are modern notation for that distinction. [Isaac Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica (1687), Axiomata sive leges motus, printed p. 13, Lex III and its explanation; pp. 16–17, Corollarium III](philosophiae-naturalis-principia-mathematica-by-isaac-newton/axiomata-sive-leges-motus.md)

The instantaneous body-pair equation has a scope: use it for the mechanical interactions assumed by this [Newtonian mechanics](newtonian-mechanics-split.md) model. In [electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md), bodies can exchange [momentum](momentum-split.md) with an [electromagnetic field](electromagnetic-field.md); their instantaneous mutual [forces](force-split.md) need not sum to zero. [Momentum conservation](momentum-conservation.md) then accounts for [electromagnetic field momentum](electromagnetic-field-momentum.md) as well as matter, and for [momentum](momentum-split.md) crossing the chosen boundary. This complication occurs in classical [electromagnetism](electromagnetism-split.md), without invoking [quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md).[The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume II, §27–6, equation (27.21) and final two examples: momentum of matter, field momentum, and failure of particle-only action and reaction](the-feynman-lectures-on-physics-volume-ii-by-richard-feynman-and-others/chapter-27-field-energy-and-field-momentum.md)
