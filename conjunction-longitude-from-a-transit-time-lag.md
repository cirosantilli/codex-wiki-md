# Conjunction longitude from a transit time lag

↑ **Parent:** [Conjunction (astronomy)](conjunction-astronomy.md)

For two uniform angular motions with $P_b<P_c$, an inner [exoplanet transit](exoplanet-transit.md) at zero and next outer [exoplanet transit](exoplanet-transit.md) at $\tau$, the next [conjunction](conjunction-astronomy.md) solves $(n_b-n_c)t+n_c\tau=2\pi$. Hence its longitude modulo $2\pi$ is

$$
\Lambda=2\pi\frac{1-\tau/P_b}{P_c/P_b-1}.
$$

For an eccentric outer body this is only an approximation. Let $f_L,M_L$ be its line-of-sight anomalies and $\Delta_e(t)=f(t)-M(t)-(f_L-M_L)$. Exact true alignment instead requires $(n_b-n_c)t+n_c\tau-\Delta_e(t)=2\pi$. The correction follows directly from uniform [mean anomaly](mean-anomaly.md) and nonuniform [true anomaly](true-anomaly.md).
