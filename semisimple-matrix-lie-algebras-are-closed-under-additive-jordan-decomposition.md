# Semisimple matrix Lie algebras are closed under additive Jordan decomposition

↑ **Parent:** [Adjoint compatibility of additive Jordan decomposition](adjoint-compatibility-of-additive-jordan-decomposition.md)

For a complex [semisimple Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra-split.md) $\mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{End}(V)$ and $X\in\mathfrak g$, both parts of its [Additive Jordan decomposition](jordan-chevalley-decomposition.md) lie in $\mathfrak g$. The polynomial semisimple part of $\operatorname{ad}X$ shows $X_s$ normalizes $\mathfrak g$. Split $\operatorname{End}(V)=\mathfrak g\oplus M$ by the [Weyl complete reducibility theorem](weyl-complete-reducibility-theorem.md); the $M$-component of $X_s$ centralizes $\mathfrak g$. On each irreducible summand of $V$ it is scalar by the [Schur lemma](schur-s-lemma.md). Its trace is zero because $\mathfrak g$ is a [perfect Lie algebra](perfect-lie-algebra.md) and the nilpotent part has zero trace, so those scalars vanish. Nonsemisimple subalgebras may fail this property: $\mathbb C(I+N)$ with $N\ne0$ nilpotent contains neither Jordan part of $I+N$.
