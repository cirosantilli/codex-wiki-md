# Brayton cycle

↑ **Parent:** [Thermodynamic cycle](thermodynamic-cycle.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brayton_cycle)

The ideal Brayton cycle consists of [adiabatic compression](adiabatic-compression.md), constant-pressure [heat](heat-split.md) addition, [adiabatic expansion](adiabatic-expansion.md), and constant-pressure heat rejection. Its ideal working substance is an [ideal gas](ideal-gas-split.md) with constant [heat capacities](heat-capacity.md), and the insulated stages are [reversible thermodynamic processes](reversible-thermodynamic-process.md).

For pressure ratio $r_p=p_h/p_l$ and [heat capacity ratio](heat-capacity-ratio.md) $\gamma=C_p/C_V$, a [reversible ideal-gas adiabat](reversible-ideal-gas-adiabat.md) has $T\propto p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$. Thus $T_2/T_1=T_3/T_4=k=r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$, and the constant-pressure heat transfers have $Q_{\rm out}/Q_{\rm in}=(T_4-T_1)/(T_3-T_2)=1/k$. The [thermal efficiency](thermal-efficiency.md) is

$$
\boxed{\eta=1-r_p^{-(\gamma-1)/\gamma}.}
$$

This formula requires constant [heat capacities](heat-capacity.md) and internally reversible insulated stages. An arbitrary dissipative [adiabatic process](adiabatic-process.md) does not satisfy the same pressure-temperature relation.
