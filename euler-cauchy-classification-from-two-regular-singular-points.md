# Euler-Cauchy classification from two regular singular points

↑ **Parent:** [Cauchy-Euler equation](cauchy-euler-equation.md)

Normalize the second-order equation as $y''+P(z)y'+Q(z)y=0$. If every point other than zero and infinity is ordinary and both exceptions are at worst [regular singular points](regular-singular-point.md), then $zP$ and $z^2Q$ extend holomorphically across zero and stay bounded at infinity. [Liouville's theorem](liouville-theorem.md) makes them constants, yielding the displayed [Euler-Cauchy equation](euler-cauchy-equation.md). If both exceptions must be genuinely singular, exclude $(A,B)=(0,0)$ and $(2,0)$, which make zero and infinity respectively ordinary.
