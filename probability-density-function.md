# Probability density function

↑ **Parent:** [Absolutely continuous probability distribution](absolutely-continuous-probability-distribution.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_density_function)

A [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) represents probabilities as areas under a nonnegative [measurable function](measurable-function.md). For a real [random variable](random-variable-split.md) with an [absolutely continuous probability distribution](absolutely-continuous-probability-distribution.md), it satisfies

$$
\mathbb P(X\in A)=\int_A f_X(x)\,dx,\qquad
f_X(x)\geq0,\qquad \int_{\mathbb R}f_X(x)\,dx=1
$$

for every [Borel set](borel-set.md) $A$. Its height is probability per unit of the outcome variable, not the probability of that exact outcome. Thus a [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) may exceed one, while the probability of a [singleton](singleton-mathematics.md) is zero. NIST describes the interval-probability interpretation through integration. [NIST: probability density functions and interval probabilities](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda362.htm).

For the example $f_Y(y)=2y$ on $[0,1]$, $f_Y(0.8)=1.6$, but $\mathbb P(Y=0.8)=0$ and $\mathbb P(0.7<Y<0.9)=0.9^2-0.7^2=0.32$. The [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) is $F_Y(y)=y^2$ within that [interval](interval-mathematics.md), connecting density height to the local accumulation rate.

Changing the scale changes the density height. If $Z=100Y$, then $f_Z(z)=f_Y(z/100)/100=z/5000$ on $[0,100]$. At the corresponding value $z=80$, its height is $0.016$, while $\mathbb P(70<Z<90)=0.32$ is unchanged. The factor $1/100$ compensates for the wider integration variable. This is a [change of variables](change-of-variables-formula.md), not a change in the underlying probabilities.

A [probability density function](probability-density-function.md) is determined only [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md): changing its values on a [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) zero set changes no probabilities. The [Cantor distribution](cantor-distribution.md) has no such representation, and a [mixed probability distribution](mixed-probability-distribution.md) can have both a point mass and a density component, so differentiating its [cumulative distribution function](cumulative-distribution-function.md) does not recover all its mass.
