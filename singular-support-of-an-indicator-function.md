# Singular support of an indicator function

↑ **Parent:** [Singular support](singular-support.md)

For any [Lebesgue measurable set](lebesgue-measurable-set.md) $A\subseteq\Omega$, the [singular support](singular-support.md) of its [indicator function](indicator-function.md), viewed as a [regular distribution](regular-distribution.md), is the intersection of the two [essential closures](essential-closure.md) in the displayed formula. Outside this intersection the [indicator function](indicator-function.md) is constant [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) on a ball and hence represents a [smooth function](smooth-function.md). At a point in the intersection, each sufficiently small ball has positive [Lebesgue measure](lebesgue-measure.md) in both sets. Any continuous representative on that ball must take only the values zero and one everywhere: every other value would persist in a smaller ball and violate equality [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md). By connectedness of the ball it would then be constant, a contradiction.

If $A$ is open, its [essential closure](essential-closure.md) is its relative closure. Its [singular support](singular-support.md) can still be smaller than its topological [boundary](boundary-of-a-set.md): $A=\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$ represents the constant one and has empty [singular support](singular-support.md).
