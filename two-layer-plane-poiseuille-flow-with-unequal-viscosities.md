# Two-layer plane Poiseuille flow with unequal viscosities

↑ **Parent:** [Plane Poiseuille flow](plane-poiseuille-flow.md)

Two viscous layers occupy $-a<y<0$ and $0<y<a$, with viscosities $\mu_-$ and $\mu_+$. A uniform driving [pressure gradient](pressure-gradient.md) $G=-p_x>0$, a stationary flat interface and no-slip outer walls give the displayed [plane Poiseuille flow](plane-poiseuille-flow.md), where $U_0=Ga^2/(\mu_-+\mu_+)$ and $C=Ga(\mu_--\mu_+)/[2(\mu_-+\mu_+)]$. These constants follow from [continuity](continuous-function.md) of velocity and shear stress at the interface and zero wall velocities. The upper-to-lower volume-flux ratio is $(1+7r)/[r(7+r)]$, with $r=\mu_+/\mu_-$. As $r\to\infty$, the interface becomes effectively stationary, the upper flux vanishes, and the lower layer approaches flow between two stationary plates a distance $a$ apart. The upper wall stress remains finite despite its vanishing velocity gradient.
