# Regular functions on a cusp with two points removed

↑ **Parent:** [Cuspidal cubic](cuspidal-cubic.md)

Parametrize the affine [cuspidal cubic](cuspidal-cubic.md) $x^2=y^3$ by $x=t^3,y=t^2$. Removing the [zero set](zero-set.md) of $g=x-y$ removes exactly the parameter values zero and one. Its regular-function [ring](ring.md) is the [localization](localization-of-a-ring.md) $k[t^2,t^3]_g$. To recover the normalization coordinate inside this [ring](ring.md), use $g^2=y(y^2-2x+y)$: inverting $g$ makes $y$ a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md), so $t=x/y$ lies in the [localization](localization-of-a-ring.md). As $g=t^2(t-1)$ is a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md), so are $t$ and $t-1$, proving the displayed equality. This calculation is valid in every [characteristic](characteristic-of-a-field.md). At the [smooth](smooth-function.md) [point](point-geometry.md) $(1,1)$ of the original curve the [local ring](local-ring.md) is instead $k[t]_{(t-1)}$: $y$ is a [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md) there, and every [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) in $t$ nonzero at one is invertible. That [point](point-geometry.md) is absent from the displayed [open subset](open-set.md), so its [local ring](local-ring.md) is computed in the original curve.
