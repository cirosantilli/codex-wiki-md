<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/a/i)

On a [probability space](../probability-space.md) $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, a standard one-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) is a real-valued [stochastic process](../stochastic-process.md) $B=(B_t)_{t\geq0}$ with $B_0=0$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), continuous [sample paths](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence), and [independent increments](../stochastic-process.md#independent-increments), such that

$$
\boxed{B_t-B_s\sim N(0,t-s)\qquad(0\leq s<t).}
$$

Here the second parameter is the [variance](../variance.md). Thus its [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) have the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution) with [mean](../expected-value.md) zero and [variance](../variance.md) equal to elapsed time. Equivalently, relative to a specified [filtration](../stochastic-process.md#filtration-probability-theory) $(\mathcal F_t)$, require $B$ to be an [adapted process](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) and $B_t-B_s$ to be [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal F_s$ with this [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution). Taking the [natural Brownian filtration](../brownian-motion.md#natural-brownian-filtration) recovers the first definition.

<h4 id="1/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

<h5 id="1/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/a/ii)

In the [Lévy construction of Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md#levy-construction-of-brownian-motion), start with $B_0=0$ and $B_1=Z_0$, where $Z_0$ has the [standard normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-distribution). Suppose values are defined at the endpoints $u=k2^{-n}$ and $v=(k+1)2^{-n}$. Set

$$
B_{(u+v)/2}=\frac{B_u+B_v}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{v-u}}2Z_{n,k},
$$

using fresh [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) [standard normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-random-variable) $Z_{n,k}$, also [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of everything previously used. Join the values by straight segments to obtain a continuous piecewise linear process $B^{(n)}$ on the dyadic grid.

To verify the [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment), write $D=B_v-B_u$ and $\ell=v-u$. The two new increments are $D/2+\sqrt\ell Z_{n,k}/2$ and $D/2-\sqrt\ell Z_{n,k}/2$. If $D\sim N(0,\ell)$, their [variances](../variance.md) are $\ell/2$ and their [covariance](../variance.md#covariance) is zero. They are [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution), so they are [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables). Induction proves the required [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) on every dyadic grid.

For convergence, successive interpolants differ by tents of disjoint interiors, so

$$
\|B^{(n+1)}-B^{(n)}\|_\infty
=2^{-n/2-1}\max_{0\leq k<2^n}|Z_{n,k}|.
$$

The [Gaussian tail bound](../probability-and-statistics.md#gaussian-tail-bound) and the [union bound](../probability-inequality.md#boole-s-inequality) give

$$
\mathbb P\!\left(\max_{k<2^n}|Z_{n,k}|>n\right)
\leq2^{n+1}e^{-n^2/2}.
$$

These [probabilities](../probability-theory.md#probability) are summable. The [First Borel-Cantelli lemma](../probability-theory.md#borel-cantelli-first-lemma) and $\sum_n n2^{-n/2}<\infty$ therefore prove [uniform convergence](../sequence-and-series.md#uniform-convergence) of the interpolants [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Their limit has continuous [sample paths](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path). Approximating any finite set of times by dyadic times passes the [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) increment laws to the limit, giving a standard [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) on $[0,1]$.

Equivalently, with $\psi_{n,k}(t)=\int_0^tH_{n,k}(s)\,ds$, these tents give the [Lévy-Ciesielski expansion](../brownian-motion.md#levy-ciesielski-expansion)

$$
\boxed{B_t=Z_0t+\sum_{n=0}^\infty\sum_{k=0}^{2^n-1}Z_{n,k}\psi_{n,k}(t),
\quad\text{uniformly almost surely on }[0,1].}
$$

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Let $S_N=\sum_{n=0}^N\sum_{k=0}^{2^n-1}a_{n,k}H_{n,k}$. The assumed [orthonormality](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-set) of the [Haar wavelets](../fourier-analysis.md#haar-wavelet) gives

$$
\|S_N\|_{\mathcal H}^2=\sum_{n=0}^N\sum_{k=0}^{2^n-1}a_{n,k}^2.
$$

Enumerate the coefficients level by level as $A_1,A_2,\ldots$. They are [independent and identically distributed](../random-variable.md#independent-and-identically-distributed-random-variables) [standard normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-random-variable), so the squares have [mean](../expected-value.md) one. The [strong law of large numbers](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#strong-law-of-large-numbers) implies

$$
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mA_j^2\longrightarrow1\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\|S_N\|_{\mathcal H}^2\longrightarrow\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.}
$$

A convergent sequence in a [Hilbert space](../hilbert-space.md) must be bounded in [norm](../normed-vector-space.md#norm). Hence this random [Haar wavelet](../fourier-analysis.md#haar-wavelet) series diverges in $\mathcal H$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence).

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

Enumerate the [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) consisting of $H_0$ and the [Haar wavelets](../fourier-analysis.md#haar-wavelet) $H_{n,k}$ as $(e_j)_{j\geq1}$, and enumerate the corresponding [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) [standard normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-random-variable) as $(A_j)_{j\geq1}$. Set $c_j=\langle h,e_j\rangle_{\mathcal H}$. The [Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis](../hilbert-space.md#parseval-identity-for-a-hilbertian-basis) gives

$$
\sum_{j=1}^\infty c_j^2=\|h\|_{\mathcal H}^2<\infty.
$$

The partial sums $T_m=\sum_{j=1}^mc_jA_j$ form a [martingale](../martingale.md) for $\sigma(A_1,\ldots,A_m)$, since the next summand is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the past and has [mean](../expected-value.md) zero. Moreover,

$$
\mathbb ET_m^2=\sum_{j=1}^mc_j^2\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
$$

The [martingale convergence theorem](../martingale.md#martingale-convergence-theorem) for a [martingale](../martingale.md) bounded in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) proves convergence [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) and [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2). Equivalently one may apply the [Kolmogorov convergence theorem](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#kolmogorov-convergence-theorem) to the summable [variances](../variance.md) $c_j^2$.

A finite [linear combination of independent normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#linear-combination-of-independent-normal-random-variables) is normal. Its [characteristic function](../characteristic-function.md) is $\mathbb E e^{iuT_m}=\exp(-u^2\sum_{j\leq m}c_j^2/2)$. Passing to the limit identifies

$$
\boxed{Wh=\lim_{m\to\infty}T_m\quad\text{almost surely and in }L^2(\mathbb P),
\qquad Wh\sim N(0,\|h\|_{\mathcal H}^2).}
$$

For $h=0$ this is the constant zero [random variable](../random-variable.md). Each fixed $h$ has its own permissible null set; simultaneous pathwise convergence for all $h\in\mathcal H$ is unnecessary.

<h4 id="1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/b/iii)

For any $h_1,\ldots,h_m\in\mathcal H$ and real $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, linearity of the finite partial sums and [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2) give

$$
\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell Wh_\ell
=W\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell h_\ell\right)
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb P).
$$

By part (ii), this [random variable](../random-variable.md) has the [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution)

$$
\boxed{\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell Wh_\ell
\sim N\!\left(0,\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell h_\ell\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2\right).}
$$

A finite family is [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) precisely when every real linear combination has a possibly degenerate [normal distribution](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution). Thus every finite subfamily of $(Wh)_{h\in\mathcal H}$ is [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution), which is the meaning of joint Gaussianity for this collection.

<h4 id="1/b/iv">iv</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iv/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iv](#1/b/iv)

Use the same finite truncation $T_m(h)=\sum_{j\leq m}\langle h,e_j\rangle A_j$ for $f$ and $g$. Since $\mathbb E[A_iA_j]=\delta_{ij}$ by [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), zero [means](../expected-value.md) and unit [variances](../variance.md),

$$
\mathbb E[T_m(f)T_m(g)]
=\sum_{j\leq m}\langle f,e_j\rangle\langle g,e_j\rangle.
$$

Here $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](../multilinear-algebra.md#kronecker-delta). The [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) and [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2) justify passing to the limit in the product's [expectation](../expected-value.md). The [Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis](../hilbert-space.md#parseval-identity-for-a-hilbertian-basis) now gives

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[(Wf)(Wg)]=\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal H}
=\int_0^1f(t)g(t)\,dt.}
$$

Because each $Wh$ has [mean](../expected-value.md) zero, this is also its [covariance](../variance.md#covariance) identity. Thus $W$ is an [isonormal Gaussian process](../gaussian-process.md#isonormal-gaussian-process) over $\mathcal H$.

<h4 id="1/b/v">v</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/v/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [V](#1/b/v)

Write $e_t=\mathbf1_{[0,t]}$ as an element of [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space). Parts (iii) and (iv) show that $B_t=We_t$ is a centered [Gaussian process](../gaussian-process.md) with [covariance function](../stochastic-process.md#covariance-function)

$$
\mathbb E[B_sB_t]=\langle e_s,e_t\rangle=\min(s,t).
$$

In particular $B_t-B_s\sim N(0,|t-s|)$. The fourth [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) of a centered [normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) of [variance](../variance.md) $v$ is $3v^2$, so

$$
\mathbb E|B_t-B_s|^4=3|t-s|^2.
$$

The [Kolmogorov continuity theorem](../stochastic-process.md#kolmogorov-continuity-theorem), with moment exponent $4$ and time exponent $2>1$, provides a [continuous modification](../stochastic-process.md#continuous-modification) $\widehat B$. At each finite set of times this [modification of a stochastic process](../stochastic-process.md#modification-of-a-stochastic-process) has the same joint law as $B$.

For $0\leq t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m\leq1$, the successive increments are [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution). The [covariance](../variance.md#covariance) of two increments on disjoint intervals equals the [inner product](../inner-product.md) of their disjoint interval indicators and is zero. Zero cross-[covariances](../variance.md#covariance) of [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) variables imply [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), and each increment has [variance](../variance.md) $t_j-t_{j-1}$. Finally $e_0=0$ in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space), so $\widehat B_0=0$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). Therefore

$$
\boxed{\widehat B\text{ is standard Brownian motion on }[0,1].}
$$

Independence from the whole past in its [natural Brownian filtration](../brownian-motion.md#natural-brownian-filtration) follows by extending finite-dimensional [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) to the generated [sigma-algebra](../measure-theory.md#sigma-algebra).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For any real [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis) $(G_i)$, the argument of (b)(ii) gives [almost sure convergence](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) and [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2) of $\widetilde Wh$. Finite linear combinations have [normal distributions](../probability-distribution.md#normal-distribution), and the [Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis](../hilbert-space.md#parseval-identity-for-a-hilbertian-basis) gives

$$
\mathbb E[(\widetilde Wf)(\widetilde Wg)]=\sum_i\langle f,G_i\rangle\langle g,G_i\rangle
=\langle f,g\rangle.
$$

Thus $W$ and $\widetilde W$ are centered [Gaussian processes](../gaussian-process.md) with the same [covariance function](../stochastic-process.md#covariance-function). More explicitly, their finite-dimensional [characteristic functions](../characteristic-function.md) are both

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E\exp\!\left(i\sum_{j=1}^mu_jWh_j\right)
=\exp\!\left(-\frac12\left\|\sum_{j=1}^mu_jh_j\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2\right).}
$$

This proves the [basis invariance of an isonormal Gaussian process](../gaussian-process.md#basis-invariance-of-an-isonormal-gaussian-process): equality of all [finite-dimensional distributions](../stochastic-process.md#finite-dimensional-distribution), and hence equality in law of the indexed collections.

For the specified cosine [orthonormal basis](../inner-product.md#orthonormal-basis), put $\lambda_i=\pi(i-\tfrac12)$. Its coefficient against an interval indicator is

$$
\langle\mathbf1_{[0,t]},G_i\rangle
=\sqrt2\int_0^t\cos(\lambda_i s)\,ds
=\frac{\sqrt2\sin(\lambda_i t)}{\lambda_i}.
$$

Consequently the [Brownian half-integer sine expansion](../statistical-modelling.md#brownian-half-integer-sine-expansion), a [Karhunen–Loève expansion](../statistical-modelling.md#karhunen-loeve-expansion), is

$$
\boxed{\widetilde B_t
=\sqrt2\sum_{i=1}^\infty b_i\frac{\sin(\pi(i-\tfrac12)t)}{\pi(i-\tfrac12)}.}
$$

For every fixed $t$, the sum converges [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence) and in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) because the coefficient squares sum to $\|\mathbf1_{[0,t]}\|^2=t$. Its [continuous modification](../stochastic-process.md#continuous-modification) is a standard [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) by (b)(v). The expansion also holds in $L^2(\Omega\times[0,1])$: the [expectation](../expected-value.md) of the squared tail is the integral of the coefficient-square tail, which tends to zero by the [dominated convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#dominated-convergence-theorem), with bound $t$.

To realize this expansion for the original $B$, choose $b_i=WG_i$. These are [Jointly Gaussian](../probability-and-statistics.md#multivariate-normal-distribution) with [covariance matrix](../variance.md#covariance-matrix) equal to the identity, hence are [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) [standard normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#standard-normal-random-variable). The finite [orthogonal projections](../hilbert-space.md#orthogonal-projection) of $\mathbf1_{[0,t]}$ onto $G_1,\ldots,G_N$ converge in $\mathcal H$, and the identity $\mathbb E|Wh|^2=\|h\|^2$ transfers that convergence to the displayed expansion for $B_t$. With an independently chosen sequence $(b_i)$ the resulting [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) has the same law; a pathwise identification uses the coupling just given.

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/a/i)

Let $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m<\infty$ and let $\xi_j$ be bounded $\mathcal F_{t_j}$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) [random variables](../random-variable.md). For the bounded [simple predictable process](../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process)

$$
H_s=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\xi_j\mathbf1_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}(s),
$$

the [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) against a [continuous martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-martingale) $M\in\mathcal H^{2,c}$ is defined by the increment sum

$$
\boxed{(H\mathbin\cdot M)_t=\int_0^tH_s\,dM_s
:=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\xi_j\bigl(M_{t\wedge t_{j+1}}-M_{t\wedge t_j}\bigr).}
$$

The same definition applies to [elementary predictable processes with stopping-time intervals](../martingale.md#elementary-predictable-process-with-stopping-time-intervals), replacing $t_j$ by ordered [stopping times](../martingale.md#stopping-time) $T_j$ and taking bounded $\mathcal F_{T_j}$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability) coefficients. Refinement of the intervals leaves the increment sum unchanged. The initial value of the [integral](../integral.md) is zero, and its [martingale](../martingale.md) property follows from the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) identity for subsequent increments of $M$.

<h4 id="2/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/a/ii)

For a bounded [simple predictable process](../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) $H$ and a [continuous martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-martingale) $M\in\mathcal H^{2,c}$, the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) states

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E\left|\int_0^tH_s\,dM_s\right|^2
=\mathbb E\int_0^tH_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s.}
$$

Here $\langle M\rangle$ is the [predictable quadratic variation](../stochastic-calculus.md#predictable-quadratic-variation), equal to $[M]$ for a [continuous martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-martingale). On the whole half-line, the terminal form is

$$
\mathbb E|(H\mathbin\cdot M)_\infty|^2
=\mathbb E\int_0^\infty H_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s.
$$

The [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) starts at zero even when $M_0\ne0$. For [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md), $d\langle B\rangle_s=ds$.

For completeness, cross terms of the finite increment sum have [expectation](../expected-value.md) zero by conditioning on the earlier endpoint of the later increment. Also $M^2-\langle M\rangle$ is a [martingale](../martingale.md), so the remaining terms satisfy

$$
\mathbb E[\xi_j^2(M_v-M_u)^2]
=\mathbb E[\xi_j^2(\langle M\rangle_v-\langle M\rangle_u)]
\quad(u=t_j<t,\ v=t_{j+1}\wedge t).
$$

This proves the displayed identity without a change-of-variables formula. Completing bounded [simple predictable processes](../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) in the norm $(\mathbb E\int H^2d\langle M\rangle)^{1/2}$ defines the [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) of every [square-integrable stochastic integrand](../stochastic-calculus.md#square-integrable-stochastic-integrand). This completion will justify the unbounded Brownian integrands below.

<h4 id="2/a/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

<h5 id="2/a/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/a/iii)

Fix $t>0$, a deterministic [partition of an interval](../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval) $\pi$, and write $\eta(s)=t_j$ on $[t_j,t_{j+1})$. Its mesh is $|\pi|=\max_j(t_{j+1}-t_j)$. The [Brownian increment](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) [variance](../variance.md) gives

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^t|B_s-B_{\eta(s)}|^2ds
=\frac12\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^2\leq\frac{t|\pi|}{2}.
$$

Thus the left-endpoint sums $\sum_jB_{t_j}\Delta B_j$ converge in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) to the [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral). Although these step coefficients are unbounded, clipping them at finite levels first makes them bounded [simple predictable processes](../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process); the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) and their finite second [moments](../expected-value.md#moment) remove the clipping.

The elementary identity $B_{t_{j+1}}^2-B_{t_j}^2=2B_{t_j}\Delta B_j+(\Delta B_j)^2$ gives

$$
2\sum_jB_{t_j}\Delta B_j=B_t^2-\sum_j(\Delta B_j)^2.
$$

The squared [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) are [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables), with [means](../expected-value.md) $\Delta t_j$ and [variances](../variance.md) $2(\Delta t_j)^2$, since a centered [normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) of [variance](../variance.md) $v$ has fourth [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) $3v^2$. Therefore

$$
\mathbb E\left|\sum_j(\Delta B_j)^2-t\right|^2
=2\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^2\leq2t|\pi|\longrightarrow0.
$$

Passing to the [convergence in L2](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#convergence-in-l2) limits yields

$$
\boxed{\int_0^tB_s\,dB_s=\frac12(B_t^2-t).}
$$

This holds for each fixed $t$; continuous [sample paths](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) of both sides extend equality from rational times to all $t$ on one event of [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) one.

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/b/i)

To establish the [Brownian weighted quadratic variation in L2](../stochastic-calculus.md#brownian-weighted-quadratic-variation-in-l2) for the deterministic [partition of an interval](../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval), decompose

$$
\sum_jB_j(\Delta B_j)^2-\int_0^tB_s\,ds
=U_\pi+V_\pi,
$$

where $U_\pi=\sum_jB_j((\Delta B_j)^2-\Delta t_j)$ and $V_\pi=\sum_jB_j\Delta t_j-\int_0^tB_sds$.

The summands in $U_\pi$ are [martingale differences](../martingale.md#martingale-difference): $B_j$ is $\mathcal F_{t_j}$-[measurable](../measure-theory.md#measurability), and the following [Brownian increment](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal F_{t_j}$. Their conditional [means](../expected-value.md) are zero, so distinct summands are [orthogonal](../inner-product.md#orthogonal-vectors) in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space). Since $\mathbb EB_j^2=t_j$ and $\mathbb E[((\Delta B_j)^2-\Delta t_j)^2]=2(\Delta t_j)^2$,

$$
\mathbb EU_\pi^2=2\sum_jt_j(\Delta t_j)^2
\leq2t^2|\pi|\longrightarrow0.
$$

For the [Riemann sum](../real-analysis.md#riemann-sum) error, the [Cauchy-Schwarz inequality](../probability-and-statistics.md#cauchy-schwarz-inequality) gives

$$
\mathbb EV_\pi^2
\leq t\sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\mathbb E|B_j-B_s|^2ds
=\frac t2\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^2
\leq\frac{t^2|\pi|}{2}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus the triangle inequality in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) proves

$$
\boxed{\sum_jB_j(\Delta B_j)^2
\longrightarrow\int_0^tB_s\,ds\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb P).}
$$

The time integration variable is $s$.

<h4 id="2/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/b/ii)

Apply the exact discrete product identity

$$
t_{j+1}B_{j+1}-t_jB_j=t_j\Delta B_j+B_{j+1}\Delta t_j
$$

and sum over the deterministic [partition of an interval](../real-analysis.md#partition-of-an-interval). Since $B_0=0$,

$$
\sum_jt_j\Delta B_j=tB_t-\sum_jB_{j+1}\Delta t_j.
$$

The left side converges in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) to $\int_0^ts\,dB_s$ by the definition and [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry), since

$$
\int_0^t|s-\eta(s)|^2ds
=\frac13\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^3\leq\frac{t|\pi|^2}{3}.
$$

The right-endpoint [Riemann sum](../real-analysis.md#riemann-sum) converges in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) to $\int_0^tB_sds$: its squared error is at most

$$
t\sum_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}\mathbb E|B_{j+1}-B_s|^2ds
=\frac t2\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\int_0^ts\,dB_s=tB_t-\int_0^tB_s\,ds.}
$$

As in (a)(iii), continuity extends fixed-time equality to equality of the continuous processes.

<h4 id="2/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

<h5 id="2/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/b/iii)

Summing the exact cubic increment identity gives

$$
B_t^3=\sum_j(\Delta B_j)^3
+3\sum_jB_j(\Delta B_j)^2
+3\sum_jB_j^2\Delta B_j.
$$

The first sum tends to zero in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space). Indeed, odd [moments](../expected-value.md#moment) of centered [normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) vanish, a centered [normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) of [variance](../variance.md) $v$ has sixth [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) $15v^3$, and [independence](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of the [Brownian increments](../brownian-motion.md#brownian-increment) gives

$$
\mathbb E\left|\sum_j(\Delta B_j)^3\right|^2
=15\sum_j(\Delta t_j)^3\leq15t|\pi|^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

The second sum converges in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) to $3\int_0^tB_sds$ by (i).

For the last sum, first note that $\mathbb E\int_0^tB_s^4ds=\int_0^t3s^2ds=t^3$, so $B_s^2$ is a [square-integrable stochastic integrand](../stochastic-calculus.md#square-integrable-stochastic-integrand). If $s=t_j+u$ and $D=B_s-B_j$, then $D$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $B_j$, with $D\sim N(0,u)$. Hence

$$
\mathbb E|B_s^2-B_j^2|^2
=\mathbb E|2B_jD+D^2|^2=4t_ju+3u^2.
$$

It follows that

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^t|B_s^2-B_{\eta(s)}^2|^2ds
\leq2t^2|\pi|+t|\pi|^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

The [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) therefore makes $\sum_jB_j^2\Delta B_j$ converge in [L2 space](../measure-theory.md#l2-space-is-a-hilbert-space) to $\int_0^tB_s^2dB_s$, with bounded truncations supplying the initial [simple predictable processes](../martingale.md#simple-predictable-process) if needed. Taking limits in the cubic identity proves

$$
\boxed{\int_0^tB_s^2\,dB_s=\frac13B_t^3-\int_0^tB_s\,ds.}
$$

The proof uses only increment algebra, [moments](../expected-value.md#moment) of [normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) and the definition of the [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#2/c/i)

Put $M_t=B_t^3-3tB_t$. It is an [adapted process](../stochastic-process.md#adapted-process) and is integrable at every finite time, because [normal random variables](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) have finite absolute [moments](../expected-value.md#moment) of every order. Fix $0\leq s<t$ and let $D=B_t-B_s$. Then $D$ is [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) of $\mathcal F_s$, has [mean](../expected-value.md) zero, [variance](../variance.md) $t-s$, and third [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) zero. Expanding the cube inside the [conditional expectation](../measure-theory.md#conditional-expectation) gives

$$
\mathbb E[B_t^3\mid\mathcal F_s]
=B_s^3+3B_s\mathbb ED^2
=B_s^3+3(t-s)B_s.
$$

Also $\mathbb E[B_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=B_s$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]
=B_s^3+3(t-s)B_s-3tB_s
=B_s^3-3sB_s=M_s.}
$$

These are exactly the defining conditions for a [martingale](../martingale.md).

<h4 id="2/c/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#2/c/ii)

Interpret the ordered [Iterated Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#iterated-ito-integral) recursively as $I_0(t)=1$ and

$$
I_n(t)=\int_0^t I_{n-1}(s)\,dB_s.
$$

The diagonal boundary of the ordered region does not affect this recursive definition. For $n=1$, $I_1(t)=B_t$. For $n=2$, part (a)(iii) gives $I_2(t)=\tfrac12(B_t^2-t)$. For $n=3$, use parts (b)(ii) and (b)(iii):

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3(t)
&=\frac12\int_0^t(B_s^2-s)\,dB_s\\
&=\frac12\left(\frac13B_t^3-\int_0^tB_s\,ds
-tB_t+\int_0^tB_s\,ds\right)
=\frac16(B_t^3-3tB_t).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the three [Probabilists' Hermite polynomials](../orthogonal-polynomial.md#probabilists-hermite-polynomial) give

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
1!I_1(t)&=B_t=t^{1/2}h_1(B_t/\sqrt t),\\
2!I_2(t)&=B_t^2-t=t\,h_2(B_t/\sqrt t),\\
3!I_3(t)&=B_t^3-3tB_t=t^{3/2}h_3(B_t/\sqrt t).
\end{aligned}}
$$

The right sides are interpreted as their polynomial extensions at $t=0$. These [space-time Hermite polynomials](../orthogonal-polynomial.md#space-time-hermite-polynomial) arise here directly from ordered [Iterated Itô integrals](../stochastic-calculus.md#iterated-ito-integral).

<h4 id="2/c/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

<h5 id="2/c/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#2/c/iii)

Part (ii) represents the process as an [Iterated Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#iterated-ito-integral):

$$
B_t^3-3tB_t=6I_3(t)=3\int_0^t(B_s^2-s)\,dB_s.
$$

For a centered [normal random variable](../probability-distribution.md#gaussian-random-variable) $B_s$ of [variance](../variance.md) $s$,

$$
\mathbb E(B_s^2-s)^2=3s^2-2s^2+s^2=2s^2.
$$

Consequently, for every finite $T$,

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^T(B_s^2-s)^2ds=\frac{2T^3}{3}<\infty.
$$

The [Itô integral](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) of this [square-integrable stochastic integrand](../stochastic-calculus.md#square-integrable-stochastic-integrand) is a true [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [martingale](../martingale.md) on $[0,T]$. Since $T$ is arbitrary,

$$
\boxed{B_t^3-3tB_t\text{ is a martingale},\qquad
\mathbb E(B_t^3-3tB_t)^2=6t^3.}
$$

The last identity is the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry).

## 3

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/a/i)

The [optional stopping theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) for a right-continuous [martingale](../martingale.md) $M$ on a [filtered probability space](../stochastic-process.md#filtered-probability-space) satisfying the [usual conditions for a filtration](../stochastic-process.md#usual-conditions-for-a-filtration) states that, for bounded [stopping times](../martingale.md#stopping-time) $\sigma\leq\tau$,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_\tau\mid\mathcal F_\sigma]=M_\sigma,
\qquad \mathbb EM_\tau=\mathbb EM_\sigma.}
$$

For a [submartingale](../martingale.md#submartingale) the conditional equality becomes $\mathbb E[M_\tau\mid\mathcal F_\sigma]\geq M_\sigma$, and for a [supermartingale](../martingale.md#supermartingale) it becomes the reverse inequality.

For a [uniformly integrable martingale](../martingale.md#uniformly-integrable-martingale), boundedness of the [stopping times](../martingale.md#stopping-time) can be removed, with the terminal limit used if a [stopping time](../martingale.md#stopping-time) is infinite. A useful application is to a bounded [stopped martingale](../martingale.md#stopped-martingale) $M_{t\wedge\tau}$ with $\tau<\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence): apply the bounded-time theorem first and then the [bounded convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#bounded-convergence-theorem) to obtain $\mathbb EM_\tau=\mathbb EM_0$. Finiteness of $\tau$ alone is not a sufficient hypothesis for an arbitrary [martingale](../martingale.md).

<h4 id="3/a/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

<h5 id="3/a/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/a/ii)

Let $X=(X^1,\ldots,X^d)$ be a [continuous semimartingale](../stochastic-calculus.md#continuous-semimartingale), with $X^j=X_0^j+M^j+A^j$, where $M^j$ is a zero-starting [continuous local martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) and $A^j$ is a continuous [finite-variation process](../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) starting at zero. For $f\in C^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^d)$, the [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) is

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
f(t,X_t)=f(0,X_0)
&+\int_0^t\partial_sf(s,X_s)\,ds
+\sum_{j=1}^d\int_0^t\partial_jf(s,X_s)\,dX_s^j\\
&+\frac12\sum_{j,k=1}^d\int_0^t\partial_{jk}f(s,X_s)\,d[M^j,M^k]_s.
\end{aligned}}
$$

Here $[M^j,M^k]$ is the [quadratic covariation](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation); the [finite-variation process](../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) parts contribute no such term. The integrals against the [finite-variation processes](../stochastic-calculus.md#finite-variation-process) are understood pathwise, and those against the [continuous local martingales](../martingale.md#continuous-local-martingale) are [Itô integrals](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-integral) after localization.

For $d$-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) with [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) standard coordinates, $d[B^j,B^k]_s=\delta_{jk}ds$. In particular, if $f\in C^2$ is independent of time,

$$
f(B_t)=f(B_0)+\sum_j\int_0^t\partial_jf(B_s)\,dB_s^j
+\frac12\int_0^t\Delta f(B_s)\,ds,
$$

where $\Delta$ is the [Laplace operator](../partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-operator). The same formulas hold up to exit from an open domain on which $f$ is smooth.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/b/i)

Let $\rho=|z_0|$ and $f(x,y)=\log\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. On the [annulus](../topology.md#annulus-mathematics) $r<\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<R$, its [gradient](../multivariable-calculus.md#gradient) and [Laplace operator](../partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-operator) are

$$
f_x=\frac{x}{x^2+y^2},\qquad f_y=\frac{y}{x^2+y^2},
\qquad \Delta f=0.
$$

Thus $f$ is a [harmonic function](../partial-differential-equation.md#harmonic-function) there. The stopped [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) gives

$$
\log|B_{t\wedge\tau}|=\log\rho
+\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\frac{B_1(s)}{|B_s|^2}\,dB_1(s)
+\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\frac{B_2(s)}{|B_s|^2}\,dB_2(s).
$$

The sum of the squared coefficients is $|B_s|^{-2}\leq r^{-2}$ before $\tau$. The [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) therefore makes the stochastic term a true [square-integrable](../measure-theory.md#square-integrable-function) [martingale](../martingale.md) on every finite interval. Continuity prevents overshoot at exit, so $\log r\leq\log|B_{t\wedge\tau}|\leq\log R$. This proves the required bounded [martingale](../martingale.md) property.

Before taking the terminal [expectation](../expected-value.md), prove that $\tau$ is finite. Set $\theta_R=\inf\{t\geq0:|B_t|=R\}$. The [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) for $|B|^2$ shows that

$$
|B_{t\wedge\theta_R}|^2-\rho^2-2(t\wedge\theta_R)
$$

is a [martingale](../martingale.md), since its stopped stochastic integrands are bounded. Taking [expectations](../expected-value.md) yields

$$
2\mathbb E(t\wedge\theta_R)
=\mathbb E|B_{t\wedge\theta_R}|^2-\rho^2\leq R^2-\rho^2.
$$

The [monotone convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#monotone-convergence-theorem) gives $\mathbb E\theta_R\leq(R^2-\rho^2)/2<\infty$. Hence $\tau\leq\theta_R<\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence).

The [bounded convergence theorem](../measure-theory.md#bounded-convergence-theorem), equivalently the [optional stopping theorem](../martingale.md#optional-sampling-theorem-for-a-supermartingale) applied to this bounded [martingale](../martingale.md), gives $\mathbb E\log|B_\tau|=\log\rho$. The two exit events partition the sample space up to a null set. Writing $p=\mathbb P(\tau_r<\tau_R)$,

$$
\log\rho=p\log r+(1-p)\log R.
$$

Solving gives the [planar Brownian annulus hitting probability](../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-annulus-hitting-probability)

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(|B_\tau|=r)=\mathbb P(\tau_r<\tau_R)
=\frac{\log(R/\rho)}{\log(R/r)}.}
$$

<h4 id="3/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#3/b/ii)

Fix an outer radius $R>\rho=|z_0|$. By continuity, reaching zero before radius $R$ requires first reaching every radius $\varepsilon\in(0,\rho)$. The [planar Brownian annulus hitting probability](../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-annulus-hitting-probability) from (i) therefore implies

$$
0\leq\mathbb P(\tau_0<\tau_R)
\leq\mathbb P(\tau_\varepsilon<\tau_R)
=\frac{\log(R/\rho)}{\log(R/\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow0
\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
$$

Thus $\mathbb P(\tau_0<\tau_R)=0$ for every fixed $R>\rho$.

On any continuous [sample path](../stochastic-process.md#sample-path) that reaches zero in finite time, the radius has a finite maximum before that time. There is therefore some integer $m>\rho$ for which zero is reached before radius $m$. Consequently

$$
\{\tau_0<\infty\}=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb N,\,m>\rho}\{\tau_0<\tau_m\}.
$$

Every event on the right has [probability](../probability-theory.md#probability) zero, so

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\tau_0=\infty)=1.}
$$

This proves that [planar Brownian motion avoids a fixed point](../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-motion-avoids-a-fixed-point) even though it can reach arbitrarily small positive radii.

<h4 id="3/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

<h5 id="3/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#3/b/iii)

First suppose $0<r<\rho=|z_0|$. For every $R>\rho$, the [planar Brownian annulus hitting probability](../brownian-motion.md#planar-brownian-annulus-hitting-probability) gives

$$
\mathbb P(\tau_r<\infty)
\geq\mathbb P(\tau_r<\tau_R)
=\frac{\log(R/\rho)}{\log(R/r)}\longrightarrow1
\quad(R\to\infty).
$$

Hence $\tau_r<\infty$ [almost surely](../convergence-of-random-variables.md#almost-sure-convergence). This uses increasing outer radii; letting the inner radius tend to zero instead proves the different claim in part (ii).

For $r>\rho$, the disk-exit estimate in (i), with outer radius $r$, directly yields $\mathbb E\tau_r\leq(r^2-\rho^2)/2<\infty$.

For $r=\rho$, retain the definition $\tau_r=\inf\{t>0:|B_t|=r\}$. The first case shows that radius $\rho/2$ is reached in finite time. Starting there, the [Strong Markov property](../markov-chain.md#strong-markov-property) of [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md) and the disk-exit argument show that radius $\rho$ is subsequently reached in finite time. Thus the infimum over positive hitting times is finite in this case as well. Altogether,

$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\tau_r<\infty)=1\qquad\text{for every }r>0.}
$$

The conclusion can also be made simultaneous in all $r>0$: intersect the probability-one events for positive rational radii, then use continuity to cross each intermediate radius.

<h3 id="3/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

Let $f(y)=|y|$ away from zero. Its first and second [partial derivatives](../multivariable-calculus.md#partial-derivative) are

$$
\partial_kf(y)=\frac{y_k}{|y|},\qquad
\partial_{jk}f(y)=\frac{\delta_{jk}}{|y|}-\frac{y_jy_k}{|y|^3}.
$$

Thus its [Laplace operator](../partial-differential-equation.md#laplace-operator) is $\Delta f(y)=(n-1)/|y|$. The [quadratic covariations](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-covariation) of the [independent](../random-variable.md#independent-random-variables) Brownian coordinates satisfy $d[B_j,B_k]_s=\delta_{jk}ds$. The stopped [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{X_{t\wedge\tau}=|x|+\frac{n-1}{2}\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\frac{ds}{X_s}
+\sum_{k=1}^n\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\frac{B_k(s)}{X_s}\,dB_k(s).}
$$

For $t<\tau$, this is the required integral representation.

To identify the driving [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md), the stopped stochastic term alone is insufficient: its [quadratic variation](../stochastic-calculus.md#quadratic-variation) is $t\wedge\tau$. Extend its unit direction after exit. Define a vector [predictable process](../martingale.md#predictable-process) $u(s)$ by

$$
u(s)=\begin{cases}B(s)/X_s,&s\leq\tau,\\(1,0,\ldots,0),&s>\tau,\end{cases}
\qquad
W_t=\sum_{k=1}^n\int_0^tu_k(s)\,dB_k(s).
$$

Predictability follows from the continuous adapted direction before stopping and the left-continuous adapted indicator $\mathbf1_{\{s\leq\tau\}}$. This $W$ is a zero-starting [continuous martingale](../martingale.md#continuous-martingale), and

$$
[W]_t=\int_0^t\sum_{k=1}^nu_k(s)^2ds=t.
$$

The [Lévy characterization of Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md#levy-characterization-of-brownian-motion) identifies $W$ as a one-dimensional [Brownian motion](../brownian-motion.md). Before $\tau$, the preceding representation becomes the [stochastic differential equation](../stochastic-calculus.md#stochastic-differential-equation) of the dimension-$n$ [Bessel process](../brownian-motion.md#bessel-process):

$$
\boxed{dX_t=\frac{n-1}{2X_t}\,dt+dW_t\qquad(t<\tau).}
$$

The fully stopped equation has both integrals stopped at $\tau$.

For $n=2$, apply the one-dimensional [Itô formula](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-s-lemma) to the [logarithm](../calculus.md#logarithm). Before $\tau$, $d[X]_t=dt$, so the two drift terms cancel:

$$
d\log X_t=\frac1{X_t}\left(\frac1{2X_t}\,dt+dW_t\right)
-\frac1{2X_t^2}\,dt=\frac1{X_t}\,dW_t.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\log X_{t\wedge\tau}=\log|x|+\int_0^{t\wedge\tau}\frac{dW_s}{X_s}
\quad\text{is a bounded martingale}.}
$$

Indeed $X_s\geq r$ before stopping, so the [Itô isometry](../stochastic-calculus.md#ito-isometry) bounds the stochastic term's second [moment](../expected-value.md#moment) by $t/r^2$, and $\log X_{t\wedge\tau}\in[\log r,\log R]$. This is the [martingale](../martingale.md) assertion under the paper's standing stopping assumption. Without stopping, the same calculation establishes only a [local martingale](../martingale.md#local-martingale); the stopped qualification is essential for the stated argument.
