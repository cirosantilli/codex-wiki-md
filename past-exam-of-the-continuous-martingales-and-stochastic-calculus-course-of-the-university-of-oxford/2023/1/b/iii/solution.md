<h1 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For any $h_1,\ldots,h_m\in\mathcal H$ and real $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, linearity of the finite partial sums and [convergence in L2](../../../../../convergence-in-l2.md) give

$$
\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell Wh_\ell
=W\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell h_\ell\right)
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb P).
$$

By part (ii), this [random variable](../../../../../random-variable-split.md) has the [normal distribution](../../../../../normal-distribution.md)

$$
\boxed{\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell Wh_\ell
\sim N\!\left(0,\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^mu_\ell h_\ell\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2\right).}
$$

A finite family is [Jointly Gaussian](../../../../../multivariate-normal-distribution.md) precisely when every real linear combination has a possibly degenerate [normal distribution](../../../../../normal-distribution.md). Thus every finite subfamily of $(Wh)_{h\in\mathcal H}$ is [Jointly Gaussian](../../../../../multivariate-normal-distribution.md), which is the meaning of joint Gaussianity for this collection.
