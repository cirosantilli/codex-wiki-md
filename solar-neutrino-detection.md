# Solar neutrino detection

↑ **Parent:** [Solar neutrino](solar-neutrino.md)

[Solar neutrino detection](solar-neutrino-detection.md) asks how an invisible [electron neutrino](electron-neutrino.md) from the [Sun](sun.md) becomes a measurable laboratory record. The [Homestake experiment](homestake-experiment.md) stores a reaction product for later counting; [Kamiokande](kamiokande.md) records light promptly. The former measures an exposure-integrated rate, whereas the latter can retain event times and statistical source-direction information. These are complementary ways to connect [stellar nuclear fusion](stellar-nuclear-fusion.md) with [neutrino astronomy](neutrino-astronomy.md).

A detector samples a weighted part of a [neutrino flux](neutrino-flux.md), rather than counting every passing [neutrino](neutrino.md). For a target population $N_T$, an ideal reaction rate is

$$
R=N_T\int_{E_{\min}}^\infty\phi_e(E)\sigma_e(E)\,dE,
$$

where $\phi_e(E)$ is the differential [electron neutrino](electron-neutrino.md) [neutrino flux](neutrino-flux.md) and $\sigma_e(E)$ is the relevant [cross section](cross-section-physics.md). A [detection efficiency](detection-efficiency.md) belongs inside the [integral](integral-split.md) if it depends on [energy](energy-split.md). Two detectors can therefore disagree in raw count rate while observing the same source. Interpreting a deficit requires both a source prediction and a detector response.
