<h1 id="failure-of-the-converse-to-lagrange-s-theorem">Failure of the converse to Lagrange's theorem</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md)

Divisibility of a proposed subgroup order is necessary by [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md) but is not sufficient in arbitrary [finite groups](finite-group.md). The [alternating group](alternating-group.md) $A_4$ has order $12$ and no [subgroup](subgroup.md) of order $6$. Such a subgroup would have index two, hence be a [normal subgroup](normal-subgroup.md). The quotient would have order two, so every element of order three would map to its identity. But $A_4$ has eight distinct three-cycles, which together with the identity cannot fit in a subgroup of order six. This obstruction contrasts with [cyclic groups](cyclic-group.md), which have a subgroup of every divisor order.
