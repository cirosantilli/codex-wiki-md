# Optical rays in a reciprocal-height refractive index

↑ **Parent:** [Geometrical optics](geometrical-optics-split.md)

For the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) $\mu=1/y$ on the upper half-plane, [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md) and the [Beltrami identity](beltrami-identity.md) give $1/[y\sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2}]=1/R$. The nonvertical rays are arcs of [circles](circle.md) centered on $y=0$; vertical lines are the remaining rays. These are also [geodesics](geodesic.md) of the [Poincaré half-plane model](poincare-half-plane-model.md). A ray entering the strip $0<y\leq1$ toward decreasing $y$ at angle $\theta$ to the vertical has limiting horizontal displacement $\tan(\theta/2)$, at most one. The line $y=0$ is approached only after infinite [optical path length](optical-path-length.md) because the [refractive index](refractive-index.md) diverges there.
