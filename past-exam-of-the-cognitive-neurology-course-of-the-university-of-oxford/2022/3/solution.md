**Right [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) damage can produce disturbances of [attention](../../../attention.md), spatial representation, action, construction, body awareness and awareness of deficits. They cannot all be reduced to inaccurate visual localization.** Some syndromes contain a localization component, but others concern competition, internally represented space or knowledge of one's own impairment. [Brain lesion](../../../brain-lesion.md) extent and disconnection also matter: not every syndrome occurs after every injury to the right [parietal lobe](../../../parietal-lobe.md), and a clinical right-hemisphere [stroke](../../../stroke.md) is not necessarily an isolated [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) experiment.

A prominent syndrome is [hemispatial neglect](../../../hemispatial-neglect.md). Patients may fail to orient to or report contralesional events, omit items during cancellation, neglect one side of a drawing or attend disproportionately to one side of their body. Neglect differs from [hemianopia](../../../hemianopia.md): a primary visual-field defect concerns sensory availability, whereas neglect concerns the selection and use of information. The two can coexist, so visual-field testing and [attention](../../../attention.md) tests should be compared rather than allowing one diagnosis to explain every omission.

[Visual extinction](../../../visual-extinction.md) makes the competitive component especially clear. A contralesional event may be detected when presented alone but missed when an ipsilesional event appears simultaneously. A simple inability to assign a location to every contralesional stimulus is insufficient when single-stimulus performance is much better. Altered competition, salience and allocation of [attention](../../../attention.md) become relevant. Task control is essential, however, because stimulus strength, response demands and unequal sensory deficits can influence apparent extinction.

Neglect can also involve represented rather than currently viewed space. [Edoardo Bisiach](../../../edoardo-bisiach.md) and colleagues asked two patients to describe familiar surroundings from imagined viewpoints. Left-sided details were omitted, while changing the imagined viewpoint changed which details were omitted. The Piazza del Duomo experiment is important because the relevant landmarks were not being viewed at that moment: a retinal localization deficit cannot by itself explain selective omission within an internally generated scene.[1978, familiar-scene imagery from different viewpoints](../../../unilateral-neglect-of-representational-space-by-edoardo-bisiach-and-others.md)

The result does not uniquely settle whether a spatial representation is absent or [attention](../../../attention.md) fails to access part of an available representation. The ability to mention a previously omitted landmark from another viewpoint is evidence that information can remain available. Reference frames also matter: a bias can relate to the body, current gaze, an object's own structure or the arrangement imagined in a task. A theory calling all these effects “localization” needs to specify its representations and selection operations; otherwise the label becomes too broad to explain the differences.

Right [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) damage can impair spatial construction and dressing. A person may misorganize components in copying or assembly, or struggle to relate clothing to the body. These problems can involve spatial relations, [body representation](../../../body-representation.md), planning and neglect rather than merely estimating the coordinates of an isolated target. Weakness, sensory loss and failure to understand instructions must be separated from [constructional apraxia](../../../constructional-apraxia.md) or [dressing apraxia](../../../dressing-apraxia.md). An inaccurate drawing alone does not distinguish these possible causes.

A related but distinct syndrome is [optic ataxia](../../../optic-ataxia.md): inaccurate visually guided reaching not adequately explained by elementary weakness or visual loss. [Marie-Thérèse Perenin](../../../marie-therese-perenin.md) and colleagues studied arm movements in ten patients with unilateral [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) [brain lesions](../../../brain-lesion.md), three of them right-sided, using filmed reaching behavior and comparisons across conditions. Their work characterized a disturbance of [visuomotor transformation](../../../visuomotor-transformation.md) mechanisms. The relationship between target location, hand and gaze can matter, supporting a transformation from visual information into action rather than one general map of consciously perceived position.[1988, unilateral-lesion series and visually directed arm movements](../../../optic-ataxia-a-specific-disruption-in-visuomotor-mechanisms-by-marie-therese-perenin-and-others.md)

This distinction is not absolute proof of entirely separate [vision](../../../vision.md)-for-[perception](../../../perception.md) and [vision](../../../vision.md)-for-action systems: reaching also requires [attention](../../../attention.md), calibration and adequate [perception](../../../perception.md). It does show why simply asking whether a patient can say where a target is cannot characterize every visuomotor deficit. Moreover, the severe combination of restricted visual awareness, reaching difficulty and impaired voluntary gaze classically associated with bilateral parieto-occipital damage should not be indiscriminately attributed to a single right [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) [brain lesion](../../../brain-lesion.md).

Awareness and body-related syndromes provide a still stronger challenge to a purely visual account. Patients can show [anosognosia](../../../anosognosia.md) for [hemiplegia](../../../hemiplegia.md), fail to acknowledge part of their body ([asomatognosia](../../../asomatognosia.md)), or misattribute limb ownership ([somatoparaphrenia](../../../somatoparaphrenia.md)). These phenomena differ from failing to attend to the left side of a page. In a series of 97 right-[brain](../../../brain.md)-damaged patients, [Edoardo Bisiach](../../../edoardo-bisiach.md) and colleagues found dissociations between unawareness of motor or visual-field defects, elementary deficits and personal or extrapersonal neglect. Thus motor incapacity, spatial inattention and awareness of incapacity cannot simply be equated.[1986, anosognosia for hemiplegia/hemianopia and dissociations from neglect](../../../unawareness-of-disease-following-lesions-of-the-right-hemisphere-by-edoardo-bisiach-and-others.md)

Clinical interpretation should therefore compare cancellation and search, single versus double simultaneous stimulation, field testing, body awareness, copying and visually guided action. Auditory or tactile manifestations also challenge a visual-only account. A patient's report and behavior should be assessed separately, since an inability to perform an action does not imply unawareness of that inability, and a verbal denial can have several cognitive causes.

**The syndromes point to partly dissociable operations within distributed right-hemisphere networks: selecting events, representing space and the body, converting information into action and monitoring one's condition.** Visual localization explains some component demands, but extending it to every symptom would erase the informative dissociations. [Brain lesion](../../../brain-lesion.md) studies support the necessity of distinguishing those computations while leaving open exactly how [parietal](../../../parietal-lobe.md) tissue and its connected systems implement them.
