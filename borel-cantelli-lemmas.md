# Borel-Cantelli lemmas

↑ **Parent:** [Probability theory](probability-theory-split.md)

The [Borel-Cantelli lemmas](borel-cantelli-lemmas.md) turn a [series](series-mathematics.md) of [event](event.md) [probabilities](probability.md) into a statement about an entire infinite sequence of observations. A [summable sequence](summable-sequence.md) of [probabilities](probability.md) forces only finitely many occurrences [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), regardless of [independence](independent-random-variables.md). A divergent [series](series-mathematics.md) forces infinitely many occurrences when the [events](event.md) are mutually [independent events](independent-events.md). The precise [event](event.md) being measured is the [limsup of events](limsup-of-events.md).

Imagine alarms with [probabilities](probability.md) $p_n=1/(n+1)^2$ or $p_n=1/(n+1)$, starting at $n=1$. Both individual [probabilities](probability.md) tend to zero. Nevertheless, the first alarm sequence eventually stops [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md), whereas the second keeps returning [almost surely](almost-sure-convergence.md) if its [events](event.md) are mutually [independent events](independent-events.md). Reusing one [uniform random variable](uniform-random-variable.md) for every alarm instead produces the [shared-uniform counterexample to the second Borel-Cantelli lemma](shared-uniform-counterexample-to-the-second-borel-cantelli-lemma.md). Thus the individual [probabilities](probability.md) alone do not determine long-run recurrence.
