# Korselt criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Carmichael number](carmichael-number.md)

A composite [integer](integer-split.md) $N>1$ is a [Carmichael number](carmichael-number.md) if and only if it is a [squarefree integer](square-free-integer.md) and

$$
p-1\mid N-1\qquad\text{for every prime }p\mid N.
$$

The criterion is named for [Alwin Korselt](alwin-korselt.md). The two requirements are different: the first excludes repeated [prime factors](prime-factor.md), while the second makes the exponent $N-1$ a common multiple of the prime-field return exponents.[Theorem 2 (Korselt), printed p. 2](carmichael-numbers-and-korselt-s-criterion-by-keith-conrad.md)

For $1105=5\cdot13\cdot17$, the numbers $4,12,16$ divide $1104$ with quotients $276,92,69$. Thus **1105 is another [Carmichael number](carmichael-number.md)**. By contrast $341=11\cdot31$ is squarefree, but $30\nmid340$; a single failed divisor condition excludes it. This illustrates the appeal of the criterion: a statement about every [unit](unit-in-a-ring.md) becomes a small calculation using a known [prime factorization](fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic.md).

The composite condition must be retained. A [prime number](prime-number-split.md) is squarefree and satisfies the divisibility condition, but is excluded from the definition of a [Carmichael number](carmichael-number.md). The resulting certificate also depends on the supplied factors really being [prime numbers](prime-number-split.md); merely finding a product decomposition is insufficient.
