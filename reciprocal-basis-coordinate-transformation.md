# Reciprocal-basis coordinate transformation

↑ **Parent:** [Reciprocal basis](reciprocal-basis.md)

In a real finite-dimensional [inner product space](inner-product-space.md), let two [bases](basis.md) satisfy $\langle u_i,w_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}$. Taking [inner products](inner-product-split.md) with $u_i$ in $v=\sum_jb_jw_j$ gives $b_i=\langle u_i,v\rangle$. If also $v=\sum_ja_ju_j$, then $b_i=\sum_j\langle u_i,u_j\rangle a_j$. Thus the [Gram matrix](gram-matrix.md) $G_U$ converts the original [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) into [reciprocal basis](reciprocal-basis.md) coordinates, and $G_W=G_U^{-1}$ converts back. With Euclidean Cartesian column [basis matrices](basis-matrix.md) $U,W$, reciprocity is $U^TW=I$, hence $W=U^{-T}$ and $W^{-1}U=U^TU=G_U$. This is why [dot products](dot-product.md) with nonorthogonal [basis vectors](basis-vector.md) are coordinates in the reciprocal [basis](basis.md), rather than coordinates in the original [basis](basis.md).
