# Haar measure

↑ **Parent:** [Measure](measure-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haar_measure)

A left [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) on a [locally compact group](locally-compact-group.md) that is a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md) is a nonzero regular [Borel measure](borel-measure.md), finite on [compact sets](compact-space.md), satisfying $\mu(gA)=\mu(A)$ for every [Borel set](borel-set.md) $A$. Such a [measure](measure-split.md) exists and is unique up to a positive scalar. Right [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) instead satisfies $\mu(Ag)=\mu(A)$; the two need not coincide. On a [compact group](compact-group.md), normalization gives total mass one and invariance on both sides. See [Tornier, Haar Measures, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10956v1#S2).

For a [finite group](finite-group.md), normalized [Haar measure](haar-measure.md) is uniform counting; for the [circle group](circle-group.md), it is [normalized angle integration on the circle](normalized-angle-integration-on-the-circle.md). These examples turn symmetry into a rule for averaging.
