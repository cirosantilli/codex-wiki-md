In weak-coupling conventional [superconductivity](../../../../../superconductivity-split.md), a useful guide is $k_BT_c\sim\hbar\omega_{\rm ph}\exp[-1/\lambda_{\rm eff}]$: an appreciable phonon scale and an effective attraction after Coulomb effects are favorable. A large [density of states](../../../../../density-of-states.md) at the [Fermi level](../../../../../fermi-level.md) can strengthen pairing, while light constituent masses can raise relevant phonon frequencies. Increasing [electron](../../../../../electron.md)–phonon coupling without limit is not a general strategy: lattice instabilities, competing order and changes to the phonon spectrum constrain it, and stronger coupling requires treatment beyond the simple exponential expression.

For unconventional high-temperature superconductivity, strong electronic interactions and magnetic fluctuations can support sign-changing pairing, as in [d-wave superconductivity](../../../../../d-wave-superconductivity.md). Layered structures and proximity to magnetic order may enhance useful correlations, but can also promote competing insulating or density-wave states. Sufficient mobile-carrier [phase stiffness](../../../../../phase-stiffness.md) is needed to turn pairing into coherent superconductivity; strong pairing alone does not guarantee a large transition [temperature](../../../../../temperature-split.md). Disorder that is relatively harmless to an isotropic conventional pair state can be pair-breaking for sign-changing gaps. There is no universal recipe established by these qualitative conditions: the pairing interaction, symmetry, carrier density, competing phases and phase coherence must all be considered.
