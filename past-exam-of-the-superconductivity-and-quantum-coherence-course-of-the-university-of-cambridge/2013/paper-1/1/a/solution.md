<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A neutral [superfluid](../../../../../superfluid.md) has a complex [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) whose phase describes coherent particle transport. For a Bose condensate it is represented by a field amplitude; for a paired Fermi superfluid it is a pair amplitude. In a symmetry-breaking description, selecting its phase breaks the global particle-number $U(1)$ symmetry. A common [phase shift](../../../../../phase-shift.md) leaves the [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) unchanged, while a phase gradient costs [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) and produces [superfluid velocity](../../../../../superfluid-velocity.md). Long-wavelength phase oscillations give a gapless [Goldstone mode](../../../../../goldstone-boson.md), generally a sound wave when density fluctuations provide the conjugate dynamics. A finite isolated state with exact particle number need not have a nonzero field expectation: the phase-selected language describes the ordered limit and relative-phase coherence.

In a [superconductor](../../../../../superconductor.md), the paired [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) carries charge of magnitude $2e$ and couples to the [electromagnetic field](../../../../../electromagnetic-field.md). The meaningful spatial phase combination is $\hbar\nabla\theta-q\mathbf A$, with signed pair charge $q$. [Gauge symmetry](../../../../../gauge-invariance.md) is a redundancy, so “breaking local gauge symmetry” is not a literal breaking of a measurable physical symmetry. The gauge-invariant electromagnetic response includes the [Meissner effect](../../../../../meissner-effect.md) and a finite [London penetration depth](../../../../../london-penetration-depth.md). Long-range Coulomb [forces](../../../../../force-split.md) move the would-be neutral phase mode to a plasma scale through the [Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor](../../../../../anderson-higgs-mechanism-in-a-superconductor.md).

Additional symmetries can be broken by particular pairing states. Conventional isotropic spin-singlet pairing need not break spatial rotations or time reversal. A [d-wave superconductor](../../../../../d-wave-superconductor.md) has a gap transforming nontrivially under crystal symmetries, and some triplet or multicomponent states can also break spin-rotation or time-reversal symmetries. These additional choices are not required by superconductivity itself. A nonzero pairing amplitude, phase coherence and electromagnetic screening describe related but distinct aspects of the ordered state.
