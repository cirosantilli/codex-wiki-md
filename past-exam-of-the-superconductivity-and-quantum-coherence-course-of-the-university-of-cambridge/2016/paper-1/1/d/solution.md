A [Josephson junction](../../../../../josephson-junction.md) weakly couples two superconducting condensates. In a consistent orientation convention, its [Gauge-invariant Josephson phase](../../../../../gauge-invariant-josephson-phase.md) $\delta$ obeys

$$
I=I_c\sin\delta,\qquad \dot\delta=\frac{2eV}{\hbar},\qquad
E_J=\frac{\hbar I_c}{2e}.
$$

The [DC Josephson effect](../../../../../dc-josephson-effect.md) permits a current at zero voltage; the [AC Josephson effect](../../../../../ac-josephson-effect.md) gives oscillations at frequency $2eV/h$. The junction's energy is $-E_J\cos\delta$. Adding a capacitance gives a charging scale $E_C=e^2/(2C)$ and, in the simplest isolated-island model, Hamiltonian $4E_C(n-n_g)^2-E_J\cos\delta$. Competition between phase locking and charge fluctuations permits quantized circuit dynamics, rather than an ordinary linear resistor.

Two junctions in a loop form a [DC SQUID](../../../../../dc-squid.md). When junctions are equal and loop self-inductance is negligible, their phase difference is fixed by external flux and their currents interfere:

$$
\boxed{I_c(\Phi)=2I_0\left|\cos\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right|.}
$$

This supplies sensitive magnetometry and flux-tunable circuit elements. Unequal junctions leave nonzero minima; finite inductance and screening change the simple pattern. The phase-sensitive gap test in the preceding alternative adds an internal pairing-phase offset to this same interference principle.

[Matter-wave interference](../../../../../matter-wave-interference.md) also compares coherent complex amplitudes. For overlapping condensates with local densities $n_1,n_2$,

$$
n(\mathbf r)=n_1+n_2+2\sqrt{n_1n_2}\cos\big(\Delta\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r+\Delta\theta\big).
$$

An atomic interferometer can split a trapped condensate, evolve its two components, release them and image the overlapping density by absorption of a probe beam. For two initially separated sources a distance $d$ apart, free expansion for time $t$ gives an approximate far-field fringe spacing $ht/(md)$. The measured fringe phase encodes relative phase and accumulated dynamical phase; population imbalance lowers visibility. Interference between expanding condensates was an important demonstration of atomic coherence.[Condensate interference and absorption images of overlapping expanding clouds](../../../../../nobel-lecture-when-atoms-behave-as-waves-bose-einstein-condensation-and-the-atom-laser-by-wolfgang-ketterle.md)

Josephson oscillation requires an ongoing weak link that exchanges particles; interference after release need not have such a link. Independent condensates can show fringes with a different relative phase in each realization, so a single image is not evidence of a reproducible phase reference over many runs. In both settings the useful object is relative phase, but superconducting circuits also couple electrically to magnetic flux and charge, while neutral-atom devices accumulate matter-wave phase from motion and external potentials.
