↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [superconducting gap function](../../../../../superconducting-gap-function.md) is a momentum-dependent pairing amplitude $\Delta(\mathbf k)$, including a phase as well as a magnitude. A fully gapped isotropic state and a state with symmetry-required nodes can have different low-temperature responses. [Heat capacity](../../../../../heat-capacity.md), thermal transport, [London penetration depth](../../../../../london-penetration-depth.md) and tunnelling spectra probe the low-energy [quasiparticle](../../../../../quasiparticle.md) spectrum. Activated behavior suggests a nonzero minimum gap; power laws can indicate nodes. For example, clean line nodes give a penetration-depth correction linear in temperature, whereas disorder can change the apparent power.

Such magnitude-sensitive probes require care: impurities, several bands, anisotropic full gaps and surface states can imitate or modify simple expectations. A node does not by itself establish a particular sign pattern, and the absence of a coherence peak is not a complete symmetry determination. The strongest identification combines momentum-sensitive spectra with [phase-sensitive superconducting gap experiments](../../../../../phase-sensitive-superconducting-gap-experiment.md).

For an illustrative $d_{x^2-y^2}$ state, $\Delta(\varphi)\propto\cos2\varphi$ changes sign under a $90^\circ$ rotation. A [Josephson junction](../../../../../josephson-junction.md) to a conventional s-wave reference can turn that sign into an intrinsic phase shift. In a corner [DC SQUID](../../../../../dc-squid.md) with junctions on orthogonal crystal faces, one branch can acquire an extra $\pi$ relative to the other. For equal junctions and negligible loop inductance,

$$
I_c(\Phi)=2I_0\left|\cos\left(\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}+\frac{\delta_{\rm int}}2\right)\right|.
$$

Changing $\delta_{\rm int}$ from zero to $\pi$ shifts the interference pattern by half a flux period. The 1993 YBCO–Pb experiment compared phase-coherent junction geometries and found evidence for that sign reversal, while explicitly discussing SQUID asymmetry and trapped vortices. Those controls matter: an arbitrary shifted trace is not by itself a proof of pairing symmetry.[1993, phase coherence of YBCO–Pb dc SQUIDs; abstract identifies asymmetry and vortex-trapping qualifications](../../../../../experimental-determination-of-the-superconducting-pairing-state-in-ybco-by-d-a-wollman-and-others.md)

A [tricrystal half-flux experiment](../../../../../tricrystal-half-flux-experiment.md) arranges grain-boundary junctions so that a sign-changing order parameter produces an odd total $\pi$ shift around a ring. Fluxoid consistency then favors a half-integer flux state, with the detailed spontaneous flux depending on loop inductance and junction strengths. Choosing comparison geometries without that frustration and controlling trapped external flux separates the pairing-phase effect from ordinary flux trapping.[1994 primary tricrystal-ring pairing-symmetry test](../../../../../pairing-symmetry-and-flux-quantization-in-a-tricrystal-superconducting-ring-by-c-c-tsuei-and-others.md)

The conclusion is strongest when the same candidate symmetry explains nodes, crystal-axis dependence and these phase-sensitive interference results. The phase-sensitive observation distinguishes a sign-changing gap from a merely anisotropic positive gap; no single temperature power law supplies that distinction.
