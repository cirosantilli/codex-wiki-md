<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

An [Abrikosov vortex](../../../../../abrikosov-vortex.md) combines a core of suppressed superconducting [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md), circulating [electric current](../../../../../electric-current.md) and a $2\pi$ winding of the [quantum phase](../../../../../quantum-phase.md). In the bulk limit a singly wound vortex carries the [superconducting flux quantum](../../../../../superconducting-flux-quantum.md) $\Phi_0=h/(2e)$. More precisely it is the [fluxoid](../../../../../fluxoid.md) that is quantized; appreciable current on the integration contour prevents identifying enclosed flux alone with the integer.

For a [type-II superconductor](../../../../../type-ii-superconductor.md), the [Ginzburg-Landau parameter](../../../../../ginzburg-landau-parameter.md) is $\kappa=\lambda/\xi>1/\sqrt2$. The [superconducting mixed state](../../../../../superconducting-mixed-state.md) allows vortices between the [lower critical magnetic field](../../../../../lower-critical-magnetic-field.md) and [upper critical magnetic field](../../../../../upper-critical-magnetic-field.md). A short [superconducting coherence length](../../../../../superconducting-coherence-length.md) permits a high orbital upper field,

$$
\boxed{B_{c2}=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi\xi^2}.}
$$

Thus surviving a large applied field generally requires accommodating many vortices, not excluding flux everywhere. The [Abrikosov vortex lattice](../../../../../abrikosov-vortex-lattice.md) provides their equilibrium arrangement in the simplest isotropic model. Material anisotropy, disorder and thermal fluctuations can change that arrangement and its stability. Spin-polarization limits can also constrain an actual upper field, beyond this orbital estimate.

Carrying a large transport [current density](../../../../../current-density.md) is a separate problem. For a straight vortex with tangent $\widehat{\mathbf t}$, the driving [Lorentz force](../../../../../lorentz-force.md) per unit length is $\mathbf f_L=\mathbf J\times\Phi_0\widehat{\mathbf t}$. Vortex motion produces a mean [electric field](../../../../../electric-field-split.md) $\mathbf E=\mathbf B\times\mathbf v_L$ and hence dissipative power density $\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E$. A large $B_{c2}$ alone therefore does not imply a large usable [superconducting critical current](../../../../../superconducting-critical-current.md).

[Flux pinning](../../../../../flux-pinning.md) holds vortices at favorable defects: suppressing the order parameter costs less where superconductivity is already weakened. Precipitates, dislocations, grain boundaries and deliberately introduced damage can provide pinning, subject to material-specific trade-offs. A defect scale comparable with a core size can be effective; a defect that destroys connected superconducting paths is harmful. In a coarse force balance, a maximum pinning force density $F_p$ gives $J_cB\simeq F_p$ for transverse transport. [Vortex depinning](../../../../../vortex-depinning.md) and thermally activated [flux creep](../../../../../flux-creep.md) can produce a voltage below an ideal static threshold.

High-field magnet conductors consequently need both a large upper field and strong, persistent pinning while retaining mechanical integrity and electrical connectivity. [Niobium-titanium](../../../../../niobium-titanium.md) and [niobium-tin](../../../../../niobium-tin.md) conductors illustrate why superconducting composition and microstructural processing are developed together. A critical-current measurement should specify field, temperature and voltage criterion; it is not simply a measurement of the temperature at which resistance first vanishes.
