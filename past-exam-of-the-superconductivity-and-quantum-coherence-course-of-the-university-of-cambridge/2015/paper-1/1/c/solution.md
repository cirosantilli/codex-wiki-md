<h1 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The superconducting order is a coherent pair amplitude, schematically $\Delta\propto\langle c_\downarrow c_\uparrow\rangle=|\Delta|e^{i\phi}$. Under a global [fermion](../../../../../fermion.md) phase change $c\mapsto e^{i\alpha}c$, it acquires phase $2\alpha$. A symmetry-broken thermodynamic description selects a pair phase. This must be distinguished from electromagnetic gauge freedom: a local gauge transformation changes the phase and potentials together and is a redundancy of the description, not an observable symmetry that the material literally violates.

A conventional centrosymmetric [s-wave superconductor](../../../../../s-wave-superconductor.md) usually has an even-parity orbital pair state and a [spin singlet state](../../../../../spin-singlet-state.md), consistent with the antisymmetry of two [fermions](../../../../../fermion.md). Its ordinary zero-field equilibrium state preserves time reversal. The electromagnetic coupling makes phase dynamics and screening part of the [Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor](../../../../../anderson-higgs-mechanism-in-a-superconductor.md); the [Meissner effect](../../../../../meissner-effect.md) is a measurable consequence. The condensate phase description does not imply that electric charge is no longer conserved.

In an [unconventional superconductor](../../../../../unconventional-superconductor.md), the gap can transform nontrivially under the crystal point group or have other nonstandard pairing structure. A common example is [d-wave superconductivity](../../../../../d-wave-superconductivity.md), with sign changes and possible nodes in the gap. This does not automatically imply an additional observable fourfold-rotation breaking: a rotation that reverses the gap's sign can be accompanied by a gauge phase change. A nematic superconducting state instead breaks an actual crystal rotation symmetry in its gauge-invariant responses.

A chiral combination such as $p_x+ip_y$ can break time reversal, while a spin-triplet [order parameter](../../../../../order-parameter.md) can also distinguish spin directions when the applicable spin-rotation and spin-orbit constraints permit it. [Helium-3 superfluidity](../../../../../helium-3-superfluidity.md) illustrates paired-fermion order with richer spin and orbital structure. These possibilities must be tested rather than equated: **unconventional pairing does not by itself mean spin-triplet pairing, time-reversal breaking, or nematicity**. Gap angular dependence, phase-sensitive [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md), spin response and spontaneous magnetic signatures probe different symmetry questions. The [phase-sensitive test of d-wave pairing](../../../../../phase-sensitive-test-of-d-wave-pairing.md) is more specific than inferring a gap sign merely from its magnitude.
