↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

[Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity](../../../../ginzburg-landau-theory-of-superconductivity.md) describes the superconducting phase by a complex [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) and a gauge-invariant free-energy functional. A common normalization is

$$
F=\int d^3x\left[f_n+\alpha|\Psi|^2+\frac\beta2|\Psi|^4
+\frac1{2m_*}|(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf A)\Psi|^2+\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\right],
$$

with $\beta>0$, pair charge $q=-2e$ and $\alpha\simeq a(T-T_c)$. In zero field the uniform minimum has $|\Psi|^2=-\alpha/\beta$ below $T_c$. Variation gives the order-parameter equation and supercurrent. The characteristic lengths are

$$
\xi=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_*|\alpha|}},\qquad
\lambda_L=\sqrt{\frac{m_*}{\mu_0q^2|\Psi|^2}},\qquad
\kappa_{\rm GL}=\lambda_L/\xi.
$$

The Meissner response screens static [magnetic fields](../../../../magnetic-field-split.md) over $\lambda_L$. In the simple isotropic theory, $\kappa_{\rm GL}<1/\sqrt2$ gives type-I behavior; $\kappa_{\rm GL}>1/\sqrt2$ allows type-II vortices and a mixed state between lower and upper critical fields. Flux quantization, [vortex](../../../../phase-vortex.md) structure and currents follow from phase stiffness and electromagnetic coupling. This is a phenomenological expansion most directly controlled near $T_c$; static coefficients alone do not determine every dynamical mode or dissipation rate.

[BCS theory](../../../../bcs-theory.md) supplies a microscopic pairing description. An attractive channel near the [Fermi surface](../../../../fermi-surface.md) gives the [Cooper instability](../../../../cooper-instability.md), and its many-pair [coherent state](../../../../coherent-state.md) leads to the self-consistent gap and fermionic Bogoliubov [quasiparticles](../../../../quasiparticle.md) described above. For an isotropic weak-coupling model with constant single-spin [density of states](../../../../density-of-states.md) $N_s(0)$, attraction $V>0$ and cutoff $\hbar\omega_D$,

$$
\Delta(0)\simeq2\hbar\omega_D e^{-1/[N_s(0)V]},\qquad
k_BT_c\simeq1.14\hbar\omega_D e^{-1/[N_s(0)V]}.
$$

It predicts $2\Delta(0)/(k_BT_c)\simeq3.53$ in that specified model. A full gap suppresses low-temperature [quasiparticles](../../../../quasiparticle.md) exponentially, whereas nodes permit power laws such as the $T^2$ [heat capacity](../../../../heat-capacity.md) derived above. The weak-coupling ratio is not a universal value for all superconductors. Expanding the microscopic theory near its transition gives Ginzburg-Landau coefficients, connecting the descriptions; a phenomenological [order parameter](../../../../order-parameter.md) alone does not determine the microscopic pairing interaction.

The most direct useful transport consequence is the absence of steady bulk resistive loss below the relevant current, [temperature](../../../../temperature-split.md) and field limits. [Superconducting magnets](../../../../superconducting-magnet.md) therefore support persistent high currents in [nuclear magnetic resonance](../../../../nuclear-magnetic-resonance-split.md) instruments and [particle accelerators](../../../../particle-accelerator-split.md). Their stored magnetic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) remains finite, and a [superconducting quench](../../../../superconducting-quench.md) requires protection; refrigeration, current leads, mechanical stress and [vortex](../../../../phase-vortex.md) motion remain engineering costs. Zero dc resistance does not eliminate all ac loss or justify unlimited current.

[SQUIDs](../../../../squid.md) exploit phase-sensitive [interference](../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) for sensitive flux measurements, while Josephson circuits provide voltage-frequency conversion and nonlinear elements for superconducting quantum devices. These applications use coherence, not just low resistance. Proposed or specialized power uses include low-loss cables, fault-current limiters and magnetic [energy](../../../../energy-split.md) storage, whose usefulness must include cooling and installation costs. Higher transition [temperatures](../../../../temperature-split.md) can relax refrigeration requirements but do not remove grain-boundary, current-density and field constraints. Neither BCS nor Ginzburg-Landau theory alone supplies a complete device design or guarantees that a particular material is economical.
