<h1 id="1/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [electron-phonon interaction](../../../../../electron-phonon-interaction.md) can produce a retarded attraction. A carrier distorts the ionic lattice; a second carrier can lower its [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) in that distortion. Integrating out a [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) gives a frequency-dependent interaction with an attractive low-frequency region. A schematic Matsubara-frequency contribution is $V_{\rm ph}(\mathbf q,i\omega_n)\propto-|g_{\mathbf q}|^2\omega_{\mathbf q}/(\omega_n^2+\omega_{\mathbf q}^2)$. It must be combined with the screened Coulomb repulsion, not treated as a statement that the bare electron-electron [force](../../../../../force-split.md) is attractive.

Retardation separates the electronic and [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) scales and reduces the repulsive contribution in the pairing channel to a Coulomb pseudopotential. In the simplest weak-coupling approximation, a positive net dimensionless pairing strength gives

$$
k_BT_c\sim1.14\hbar\omega_D\exp[-1/(\lambda_{\rm ep}-\mu^*)].
$$

The formula assumes a [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) cutoff and simplified coupling; strong-coupling calculations need the spectral interaction rather than this one-number replacement. The [phonon](../../../../../phonon.md) [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md)'s dependence on ionic [mass](../../../../../mass.md) motivates an [isotope effect](../../../../../isotope-effect.md), whose actual exponent can be modified by other physics.

An interaction need not be attractive in every [momentum](../../../../../momentum-split.md) channel to support pairing. Spin fluctuations or other electronic correlations can favor a sign-changing gap: in the gap equation, repulsive scattering between two regions can contribute constructively when their gap signs differ. Such mechanisms are candidates for [unconventional superconductivity](../../../../../unconventional-superconductivity.md). Observing a d-wave gap identifies symmetry information but does not, by itself, establish a unique microscopic pairing mechanism in a cuprate.
