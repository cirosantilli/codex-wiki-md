<h1 id="1/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

A [Josephson junction](../../../../../josephson-junction.md) weakly couples two superconducting condensates. Its coupling [energy](../../../../../energy-split.md) is $-E_J\cos\varphi$, with a gauge-invariant phase difference across the junction. Choose current and voltage orientations consistently so that

$$
\boxed{I=I_c\sin\varphi,\qquad E_J=\frac{\hbar I_c}{2e},\qquad
\dot\varphi=\frac{2eV}{\hbar}.}
$$

The [DC Josephson effect](../../../../../dc-josephson-effect.md) permits a stationary zero-voltage supercurrent up to $I_c$. A constant nonzero voltage produces the [AC Josephson effect](../../../../../ac-josephson-effect.md), with [frequency](../../../../../frequency.md) $f=2e|V|/h$. Resistive and capacitive currents are additional circuit contributions, not part of the ideal sinusoidal supercurrent alone.

In a symmetric [DC SQUID](../../../../../dc-squid.md) with negligible loop [self-inductance](../../../../../self-inductance.md), the two junction phases differ by $2\pi\Phi/\Phi_0$. Adding their currents and maximizing over the common phase gives

$$
\boxed{I_{c,\rm SQUID}(\Phi)=2I_c\left|\cos\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right|.}
$$

Flux changes therefore modulate the critical current or a suitably biased output voltage. This converts phase [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) into sensitive flux measurement. Finite loop [inductance](../../../../../inductance.md), unequal junctions, noise and readout conditions alter the simple modulation; the loop flux then includes screening currents. A [SQUID](../../../../../squid.md) is useful in magnetometry, but achieving sensitivity requires both a suitable circuit and controlled environmental noise.
