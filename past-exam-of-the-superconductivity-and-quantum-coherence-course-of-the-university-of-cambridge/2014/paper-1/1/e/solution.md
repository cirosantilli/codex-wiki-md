<h1 id="1/e/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [E](../e.md)

A [SQUID](../../../../../squid.md) converts [magnetic flux](../../../../../magnetic-flux.md) into a periodic [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) response of superconducting weak links. A [DC SQUID](../../../../../dc-squid.md) has two [Josephson junctions](../../../../../josephson-junction.md) in a loop. For equal junction critical currents $I_0$ and negligible loop self-inductance, their [Gauge-invariant Josephson phases](../../../../../gauge-invariant-josephson-phase.md) differ by $2\pi\Phi/\Phi_0$, with $\Phi_0=h/(2e)$. Adding $I_0\sin\delta_1+I_0\sin\delta_2$ and maximizing over the common phase gives

$$
\boxed{I_c(\Phi)=2I_0\left|\cos\frac{\pi\Phi}{\Phi_0}\right|.}
$$

Thus the critical current repeats for every added flux quantum. A suitable current bias and dissipative junction dynamics convert the changing critical current into an observable voltage modulation. Junction asymmetry prevents exact zero minima, and appreciable self-inductance makes the flux include the circulating current's contribution; these require a modified response model. The [Jaklevic Josephson interference experiment](../../../../../jaklevic-josephson-interference-experiment.md) supplies an early experimental realization of the [interference](../../../../../interference-wave-propagation.md) principle.

An [RF SQUID](../../../../../rf-squid.md) instead uses one junction in a loop inductively coupled to a radio-frequency resonator. In either design, a pickup coil and flux transformer can couple a small external field into the sensor, and flux feedback can keep it near a sensitive operating point over a larger measurement range. Applications include weak-field magnetometry, measurements of magnetic susceptibility and persistent currents, geophysical surveys and precision low-current detection. Gradiometers suppress common external fields, while thermal, junction and environmental noise set practical sensitivity. The instrument measures a flux-dependent electrical response; sensitivity is not established solely by the small size of $\Phi_0$.
