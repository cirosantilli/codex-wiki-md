# Minkowski spacetime

↑ **Parent:** [Special relativity](special-relativity-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_spacetime)

[Minkowski spacetime](minkowski-spacetime.md) is the flat four-dimensional [spacetime](spacetime.md) of [special relativity](special-relativity-split.md): one time coordinate and three spatial [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md), equipped with a [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md). More precisely, it is an [affine space](affine-space.md), so no [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) is a preferred origin; differences between [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) are [four-vectors](four-vector.md). [Inertial frames](inertial-frame.md) give different [coordinates](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) to the same [spacetime events](event-relativity.md), but agree on their [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md).

This geometry answers two different measurement questions with one structure. Which [spacetime events](event-relativity.md) can be connected by a light signal or a massive observer? The [light cone](light-cone.md) classifies them. How much time does a moving clock record? Its [world line](world-line.md) and the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) determine its [proper time](proper-time.md). The minus sign in the [Minkowski metric](minkowski-metric.md) is what allows these answers to differ from ordinary [Euclidean distance](euclidean-distance.md).

For a concrete one-space-dimensional slice, start at the origin $O$ and measure $t$ in seconds and $x$ in [light-seconds](light-second.md). The [spacetime event](event-relativity.md) $A$ at $(t,x)=(4\,\mathrm s,3\,\mathrm{ls})$ can be reached by an observer moving at $0.75c$, whereas $B=(2\,\mathrm s,3\,\mathrm{ls})$ would require $1.5c$ to be reached directly from $O$. The [Minkowski interval](minkowski-interval.md) distinguishes them even though both are three [light-seconds](light-second.md) away in that [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md).

Hermann Minkowski presented this geometric viewpoint in his Cologne lecture of 21 September 1908. His account treats particle histories as [world lines](world-line.md), changes of [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md) as choices of time axis, and clock readings as [proper time](proper-time.md). These are connected geometric ideas, rather than separate corrections to ordinary mechanics.[Minkowski, Space and Time, §§I–III; Saha's translation in the 1920 collection](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66944/old/66944-h/66944-h.htm)
