# Two successive hydraulic controls

↑ **Parent:** [Hydraulic flow in an inverted channel](hydraulic-flow-in-an-inverted-channel.md)

For two same-height throats carrying the same light-layer [volume flux](volumetric-flow-rate.md) and [reduced gravity](reduced-gravity-split.md), the narrower throat has the larger critical minimum [energy](energy.md). A narrower downstream throat can back up and submerge an upstream throat. If the first throat is narrower, it can control discharge and feed a supercritical current through the second; a [hydraulic jump](hydraulic-jump.md) followed by a second control is also possible when the available energy drop and intermediate geometry permit it. Equal throat widths are a marginal lossless case. Relative width alone does not fix jump position or mixing in the intervening reservoir.
