# Computer science

↑ **Parent:** [Codex Wiki](split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science)

Computer science studies how information can be represented and transformed by [algorithms](algorithm-split.md), and what those transformations can achieve. Its central questions connect [mathematics](mathematics-split.md) to practical design: what answer should a procedure produce, why is it correct, and how much work does it require as its [input](input-computer-science.md) grows? [Computability theory](computability-theory-split.md) asks which tasks can be solved at all; [computational complexity theory](computational-complexity-theory.md) asks what resources a solution needs.

The beauty is that choosing a representation can change what is feasible. Finding a name among a million entries sounds like a million checks. Store the entries in a [sorted array](sorted-array.md), and [binary search](binary-search.md) needs at most twenty middle-entry comparisons. The order makes each unsuccessful comparison eliminate a whole region of possibilities. A [data structure](data-structure-split.md) supplies useful organization; an [algorithm](algorithm-split.md) exploits it; [time complexity](time-complexity.md) explains the resulting savings.

[Algorithm correctness](algorithm-correctness.md) covers every permitted [input](input-computer-science.md), including absent names and empty collections. [Time complexity](time-complexity.md) predicts scaling; [statistical program profiling](statistical-program-profiling.md) investigates a particular implementation.
