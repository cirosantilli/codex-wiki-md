# Convergence from fixed-length series blocks

↑ **Parent:** [Convergent series](convergent-series.md)

For a fixed positive [integer](integer.md) $m$, if $a_n\to0$ and the [series](series-mathematics.md) of the displayed block sums converges, then the original [series](series-mathematics.md) converges to the same [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md). Its [partial sum](partial-sum.md) at a block endpoint is exactly a [partial sum](partial-sum.md) of $\sum b_n$; the difference at an intermediate endpoint has at most $m-1$ terms, all tending to zero. This justification is essential when block cancellation proves convergence without [absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md). It does not apply without further control to blocks whose lengths grow.
