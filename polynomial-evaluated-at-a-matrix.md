# Polynomial evaluated at a matrix

↑ **Parent:** [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](cayley-hamilton-theorem-split.md)

For a [polynomial](polynomial-split.md) $p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^d a_jt^j$, substitute a fixed [square matrix](square-matrix.md) $A$ for $t$, taking $A^0=I$. In particular, $p(t)=t^2-3t+1$ becomes $p(A)=A^2-3A+I$, not $A^2-3A$ with the scalar $1$ added separately to each entry. [Polynomials](polynomial-split.md) in the same [matrix](matrix.md) commute with each other, even though arbitrary [matrix multiplication](matrix-multiplication.md) is [noncommutative](noncommutativity.md).
