# Confidence interval

↑ **Parent:** [Statistical inference](statistical-inference-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval)

A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.md) is a random interval constructed from a [statistical sample](statistical-sample.md) to cover a fixed unknown [statistical parameter](statistical-parameter.md) with a prescribed [coverage probability](coverage-probability.md). For a scalar target $\theta$, a procedure with nominal confidence $1-\alpha$ satisfies

$$
\mathbb P_\theta\{L(X)\leq\theta\leq U(X)\}\geq1-\alpha
\quad\text{for every admissible }\theta.
$$

The subscript records which [probability distribution](probability-distribution-split.md) generates the repeated samples. The endpoints are [statistics](statistics.md), so they vary from sample to sample; the target is fixed. Equality gives exact nominal [coverage probability](coverage-probability.md), while the inequality also permits conservative procedures. For a general [estimand](estimand.md) $\psi(\theta)$, replace the target inside the interval by $\psi(\theta)$.

A realized [confidence interval](confidence-interval.md) communicates the precision of an estimate in the target's own units. Reporting $1.2$ alone conceals the difference between an estimate with [standard error](standard-error.md) $0.5$ and one with [standard error](standard-error.md) $0.05$. A narrow [confidence interval](confidence-interval.md) indicates greater precision for the stated [statistical model](statistical-model-split.md), rather than a guarantee that its assumptions describe the experiment correctly.

Once $x$ is observed, $C(x)$ either contains the fixed target or does not. The 95% statement concerns the random procedure $C(X)$, not a [posterior probability](posterior-probability.md) of 0.95 that this particular interval contains $\theta$. A [credible interval](credible-interval.md) instead assigns specified [posterior probability](posterior-probability.md) to a region under [Bayesian inference](bayesian-statistics-split.md). These interpretations can differ even when the numerical endpoints agree. The repeated-sampling interpretation is explained in [NIST's confidence-limits teaching reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda352.htm).

Jerzy Neyman's 1937 paper formulates the fixed-target, random-endpoint construction in Section II, especially equation (20) on page 348. Its confidence coefficient must hold across the allowed [statistical parameters](statistical-parameter.md), rather than only at the value suggested by one sample. See [Neyman, Outline of a Theory of Statistical Estimation Based on the Classical Theory of Probability](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1937.0005) and the [original paper scan](https://wwwusers.ts.infn.it/~milotti/Didattica/StatisticaAvanzata/Neyman_1937.pdf).
