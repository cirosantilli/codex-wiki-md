# Harmonic expansion on a disk

↑ **Parent:** [Laplace equation in polar coordinates](laplace-equation-in-polar-coordinates.md)

A real [harmonic function](harmonic-function.md) on the unit [disk](disk-mathematics.md) has the displayed expansion, convergent locally uniformly with its [derivatives](derivative.md) in the interior. If its continuous boundary data have [Fourier coefficients](fourier-coefficient.md) $A_0,A_n,B_n$ in the convention $f(\theta)=A_0/2+\sum_{n\geq1}(A_n\cos(n\theta)+B_n\sin(n\theta))$, its [harmonic extension](harmonic-extension.md) has $a_0=A_0/2$, $a_n=A_n$, $b_n=B_n$.

To obtain the radial factors, [separation of variables](separation-of-variables.md) in the [Laplace equation in polar coordinates](laplace-equation-in-polar-coordinates.md) gives $r^2R''+rR'-\lambda R=0$ and $\Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0$. Angular [periodicity](periodicity.md) restricts nonconstant modes to $\lambda=n^2$ for positive integers $n$. The radial factors are $r^n,r^{-n}$, with $1,\log r$ for the constant angular mode. Boundedness at the origin removes $r^{-n}$ and $\log r$. Each surviving factor $r^n\cos(n\theta)$ or $r^n\sin(n\theta)$ is a [harmonic polynomial](harmonic-polynomial.md), the real or imaginary part of $(x+iy)^n$. The expansion can also be derived from the [Poisson integral on the unit disk](poisson-integral-on-the-unit-disk.md) for the [disk](disk-mathematics.md).
