# Fourier cosine coefficients of a sine mode

↑ **Parent:** [Half-range Fourier cosine series](half-range-fourier-cosine-series.md)

For a positive integer $m$, take $f(x)=\sin(mx)$ on $0<x<\pi$ and form its [half-range Fourier cosine series](half-range-fourier-cosine-series.md). The product-to-sum identity gives

$$
a_n=\frac1\pi\left[\frac{1-(-1)^{m+n}}{m+n}+\frac{1-(-1)^{m-n}}{m-n}\right],\qquad n\geq0,\ n\ne m.
$$

Thus $a_n=4m/[\pi(m^2-n^2)]$ for opposite parity and zero for the same parity. The exceptional integral $a_m=(2/\pi)\int_0^\pi\sin(mx)\cos(mx)dx$ is zero. The constant term is $a_0/2$, not $a_0$. These coefficients represent the even periodic extension, not the original sine wave on negative arguments. Their quadratic decay gives [absolute convergence](absolute-convergence.md).
