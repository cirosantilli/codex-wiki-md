<h1 id="a/4/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

The order-$\epsilon^2$ equation is

$$
(\partial_t^2+1)y_2=-2y_{0,tT}-a_2y_0-\cos t\,y_1.
$$

Using $\cos t\cos2t=(\cos3t+\cos t)/2$ and $\cos t\sin2t=(\sin3t+\sin t)/2$, its [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md) coefficients are

$$
\cos t:\ -2B_T+(5/12-a_2)A,\qquad
\sin t:\ 2A_T-(a_2+1/12)B.
$$

[secular term](../../../../../secular-term.md) are removed by setting these to zero:

$$
A_T=\tfrac12(a_2+1/12)B,\qquad
B_T=\tfrac12(5/12-a_2)A.
$$

The [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) of this two-by-two system satisfy

$$
\lambda^2=\frac14(a_2+1/12)(5/12-a_2).
$$

They are real with opposite signs for $-1/12<a_2<5/12$. Generic initial amplitudes then contain an exponentially growing slow-time component. At either leading endpoint the [matrix](../../../../../matrix.md) is nonzero [nilpotent matrix](../../../../../nilpotent-matrix.md), so suitable initial amplitudes give linear slow-time growth; higher-order detuning decides the precise corrected boundary. Outside this interval the leading [eigenvalues](../../../../../eigenvalue.md) are purely imaginary and the slow amplitudes remain oscillatory.

Substitution into $a=1+a_2\epsilon^2+\cdots$ gives the requested leading instability interval

$$
\boxed{1-\frac{\epsilon^2}{12}+O(\epsilon^3)
\leq a\leq1+\frac{5\epsilon^2}{12}+O(\epsilon^3).}
$$

“Can become unbounded” permits special initial amplitudes without growth. The calculation identifies the [second instability tongue of a weak Mathieu oscillator](../../../../../second-instability-tongue-of-a-weak-mathieu-oscillator.md) and its [slow amplitudes for the second Mathieu instability tongue](../../../../../slow-amplitudes-for-the-second-mathieu-instability-tongue.md); it is not an assertion that every solution grows throughout the closed interval.
