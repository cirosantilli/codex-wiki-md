<h1 id="a/3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The only [nondegenerate stationary point of a phase](../../../../../nondegenerate-stationary-point-of-a-phase.md) of the phase $t^2$ is the endpoint zero. Its usual leading [stationary phase method](../../../../../stationary-phase-method.md) term is proportional to the amplitude there, but $\tan^2(0)=0$. Thus the nominal $x^{-1/2}$ stationary contribution vanishes.

Expand one order further in the local amplitude: $\tan^2t=t^2+O(t^4)$. The rescaling $t=u/\sqrt x$ then gives a first nonzero stationary contribution of order $x^{-3/2}$, with coefficient $-\sqrt{2\pi}/8$ in the real integral, as in part (i). Meanwhile the upper endpoint is nonstationary and has nonzero amplitude; its [integration by parts](../../../../../integration-by-parts.md) contribution is $2\sin(\pi^2x/16)/(\pi x)$, of order $x^{-1}$.

Consequently **the nonstationary endpoint dominates the first nonzero saddle contribution**. A [stationary phase method](../../../../../stationary-phase-method.md) formula retaining only the usual leading saddle term would miss the answer. The full [stationary phase method](../../../../../stationary-phase-method.md), with the vanishing-amplitude correction and the endpoint term, agrees with [method of steepest descent](../../../../../method-of-steepest-descent.md). This is [quadratic stationary endpoint with quadratically vanishing amplitude](../../../../../quadratic-stationary-endpoint-with-quadratically-vanishing-amplitude.md).
