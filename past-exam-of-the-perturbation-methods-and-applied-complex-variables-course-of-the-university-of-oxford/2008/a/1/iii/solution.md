<h1 id="a/1/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Take $g(x)=x$ and $f(x)=x+1$. Then $f/g=1+1/x\to1$, so $f\sim g$. However,

$$
\frac{e^{f(x)}}{e^{g(x)}}=e
$$

for every $x$, and this ratio does not approach one. Thus **[asymptotic equivalence](../../../../../asymptotic-equivalence.md) need not survive exponentiation**. A small relative error between large exponents can leave a nonvanishing additive error. This is the distinction in [asymptotic comparison of exponential functions](../../../../../asymptotic-comparison-of-exponential-functions.md).
