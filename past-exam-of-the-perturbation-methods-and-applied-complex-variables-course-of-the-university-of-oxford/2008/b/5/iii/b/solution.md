<h1 id="b/5/iii/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

To supply nonzero channel flux while letting the speed decay in the large upper region, take a logarithmic [velocity potential](../../../../../../velocity-potential.md):

$$
\boxed{w(z)=\frac2\pi\log Z(z).}
$$

Use the [logarithm](../../../../../../logarithm.md) analytic on the [upper half-plane](../../../../../../upper-half-plane-complex-analysis.md), with argument between zero and $\pi$. Its [complex velocity](../../../../../../complex-velocity.md) is

$$
\frac{dw}{dz}=\frac1R.
$$

At upper infinity it tends to zero. At the channel end $Z\to0$, it tends to $1/i=-i$, which means $(u,v)=(0,1)$, as required. The [streamfunction](../../../../../../stream-function.md) is constant on each real boundary interval, and differs by two between the two channel walls. That flux equals the channel width two times the unit upward speed, providing an independent normalization check.
