<h1 id="a/3/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Use the fast time $T_0=t$ and slow time $T=\epsilon^2t$. A possible intermediate scale $\epsilon t$ has no leading modulation: the absence of first-order [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md) imposes independence of that scale. Write

$$
U_0=A(T)e^{iT_0}+\overline A(T)e^{-iT_0}.
$$

At first order choose the nonresonant particular solution

$$
U_1=2|A|^2-\tfrac13A^2e^{2iT_0}-\tfrac13\overline A^{,2}e^{-2iT_0},
$$

with any needed fundamental homogeneous correction assigned consistently when matching the initial data. At second order,

$$
(D_0^2+1)U_2=2U_0U_1-2D_0D_TU_0.
$$

The coefficient of $e^{iT_0}$ on the right is $(10/3)|A|^2A-2iA_T$. A bounded oscillatory correction requires this [resonance](../../../../../resonance.md) coefficient, and its conjugate, to vanish. The [quadratic oscillator phase-modulation condition](../../../../../quadratic-oscillator-phase-modulation-condition.md) is therefore

$$
\boxed{A_T=-\frac{5i}{3}|A|^2A.}
$$

It resums the secular phase accumulation into a slow modulation instead of leaving it as a growing term in the correction.
