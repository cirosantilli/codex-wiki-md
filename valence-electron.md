# Valence electron

↑ **Parent:** [Chemical bond](chemical-bond-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_electron)

A [valence electron](valence-electron.md) belongs to the electronic arrangement used to describe a [chemical atom](atom-physics.md)'s [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md) and ordinary [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction-split.md). For the neutral [hydrogen](hydrogen.md), [carbon](carbon.md), [nitrogen](nitrogen.md), and [oxygen](oxygen.md) [chemical atoms](atom-physics.md) used here, the [valence electrons](valence-electron.md) occupy the outermost occupied [electron shell](electron-shell.md). Their respective [electron configurations](electron-configuration.md) are $1s^1$, $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^2$, $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3$, and $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$, giving one, four, five, and six [valence electrons](valence-electron.md). See [Chemistry 2e, 6.4: electron configurations and valence electrons](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/6-4-electronic-structure-of-atoms-electron-configurations).

[Lewis structures](lewis-structure.md) show these [valence electrons](valence-electron.md) while omitting the inner [electrons](electron.md). Consequently, eight drawn [valence electrons](valence-electron.md) in [water](water.md) do not mean eight total [electrons](electron.md): neutral [water](water.md) has ten, with two inner [oxygen](oxygen.md) [electrons](electron.md) omitted. Neutral [ammonia](ammonia.md) likewise has ten total [electrons](electron.md) but eight [valence electrons](valence-electron.md); neutral [carbon dioxide](carbon-dioxide.md) has twenty-two total [electrons](electron.md) but sixteen [valence electrons](valence-electron.md).

This distinction makes the small counts useful: connections and possible [lone pairs](lone-pair.md) can be organized without representing every [electron](electron.md) or solving the entire many-[electron](electron.md) [wavefunction](wave-function-split.md). The outermost-[electron shell](electron-shell.md) shortcut applies to the examples above, rather than serving as a universal definition for all [chemical elements](chemical-element.md); the electronic arrangement relevant to [chemical bonds](chemical-bond-split.md) can also involve other [electron subshells](electron-subshell.md).
