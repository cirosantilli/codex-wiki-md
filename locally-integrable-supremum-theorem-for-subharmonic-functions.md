# Locally integrable supremum theorem for subharmonic functions

↑ **Parent:** [Subharmonic function](subharmonic-function.md)

For a countable family of [subharmonic functions](subharmonic-function.md), a [locally integrable](locally-integrable-function.md) pointwise supremum is subharmonic in the [distributional derivative](distributional-derivative.md) sense. Finite maxima preserve subharmonicity: approximate the maximum by the smooth convex coordinatewise increasing function $(s+t+\sqrt{(s-t)^2+\delta^2})/2$, apply the [chain rule](chain-rule.md) after mollification, then pass to a local [L1 norm](l1-norm.md) convergence limit. The maxima $g_N=\max(f_1,\ldots,f_N)$ increase to $g$ and are bounded in absolute value by $|f_1|+|g|$ on compact sets, so the [dominated convergence theorem](dominated-convergence-theorem.md) completes the proof. The hypothesis cannot be omitted: $f_j=j$ has infinite supremum. To obtain a pointwise [subharmonic function](subharmonic-function.md), take the [canonical representative of a subharmonic distribution](canonical-representative-of-a-subharmonic-distribution.md).
