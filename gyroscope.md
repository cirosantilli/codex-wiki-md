# Gyroscope

↑ **Parent:** [Angular momentum](angular-momentum.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroscope)

A [gyroscope](gyroscope.md) is a rotating instrument whose rapidly spinning, nearly symmetric rotor provides a direction reference through its [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md). In an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md), negligible external [torque](torque.md) approximately preserves that direction by [conservation of angular momentum](conservation-of-angular-momentum.md). A transverse [torque](torque.md) turns the [angular momentum](angular-momentum.md) instead: the surprising sideways motion of a supported spinning wheel is [precession](precession.md). An ideal freely falling test gyroscope in [general relativity](general-relativity-split.md) has its spin undergo [parallel transport](parallel-transport.md) along its [timelike geodesic](timelike-geodesic.md). Comparing that direction with distant reference axes measures [Lense-Thirring precession](lense-thirring-precession.md); the direction need not remain fixed in arbitrary rotating coordinates.

A [gyroscope direction reference](gyroscope-direction-reference.md) is useful precisely because its direction can persist while its housing turns. A [spinning-wheel precession experiment](spinning-wheel-precession-experiment.md) deliberately supplies gravitational [torque](torque.md) to reveal how that persistence fails. The [gyroscope vector geometry](gyroscope-vector-geometry.md) explains the direction, and [slow steady gyroscope precession](slow-steady-gyroscope-precession.md) gives a numerical prediction with a check on its assumptions.
