# Bragg crystal reflection experiment of 1913

↑ **Parent:** [Bragg's law](bragg-s-law.md)

[William Lawrence Bragg](william-lawrence-bragg.md) introduced the plane interpretation; his father [William Henry Bragg](william-henry-bragg.md) built the [X-ray](x-ray.md) spectrometer. Their joint 1913 paper, [The Reflection of X-rays by Crystals](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1913.0040), reports the experiment. [The Bragg law: Lawrence's 1912 interpretation and the angle convention](the-bragg-law-university-of-leeds-library-special-collections.md) [Description: William Henry Bragg designed and made the spectrometer](william-bragg-s-spectrometer-royal-institution-collection.md)

An [X-ray tube](x-ray-tube.md) supplied radiation through lead slits to a crystal mounted with its cleavage face through the rotation axis. A rotating [ionization chamber](ionization-chamber.md) replaced a spectrometer telescope; [electric current](electric-current.md) measured the scattered signal, and a circular scale supplied angular readings. They first scanned the chamber at fixed crystal orientation, then swept crystal and chamber together to follow the reflection. [Bragg and Bragg (1913), pp. 428–430: preparation, source, slits, chamber, angular scale and scanning](the-reflection-of-x-rays-by-crystals-by-william-henry-bragg-and-william-lawrence-bragg.md)

Changing chamber gas checked detector dependence; readings on opposite sides checked face-setting errors. Repeated peaks with [sines](sine.md) of half-deflections in integer ratios, together with matching [absorption coefficients](absorption-coefficient.md), distinguished repeated [diffraction orders](diffraction-order.md) of the same [wavelength](wavelength.md) from different radiation components. Comparing crystal faces constrained [interplanar spacings](interplanar-spacing.md). Their limits included slit broadening, face alignment and incomplete interception at glancing incidence; assigning absolute [wavelengths](wavelength.md) still required a structural choice for $d$. [pp. 428–430: gas and alignment controls; pp. 435–437 and Fig. 3: order comparison, absorption test, finite-face limitation and spacing ambiguity](the-reflection-of-x-rays-by-crystals-by-william-henry-bragg-and-william-lawrence-bragg.md)
