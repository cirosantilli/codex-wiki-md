# Differentiation along refining metric partitions

↑ **Parent:** [First-crossing maximal inequality for refining partitions](first-crossing-maximal-inequality-for-refining-partitions.md)

Assume in addition that the [probability space](probability-space-split.md) is a [metric space](metric-space.md), continuous integrable functions are dense in $L^1$, and the diameters of its partition cells tend to zero at each point. For a continuous $g$, the oscillation average tends to zero because every point in $M_k(x)$ is within its [diameter](diameter.md) of $x$. For $h=f-g$, the upper limit of the oscillation of $f$ is bounded by the partition maximal function of $h$ plus $|h(x)|$. The [first-crossing maximal inequality for refining partitions](first-crossing-maximal-inequality-for-refining-partitions.md) and [Chebyshev's inequality](chebyshev-s-inequality.md) bound the [measure](measure-split.md) where this exceeds $2\varepsilon$ by $2\|h\|_1/\varepsilon$. Approximation proves the displayed [almost everywhere](almost-everywhere.md) assertion. Refinement supplies the maximal estimate; shrinking [diameter](diameter.md) supplies the approximation argument. Without shrinking [diameter](diameter.md), a constant partition generally converges only to its cell averages.
