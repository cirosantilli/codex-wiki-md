<h1 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The [vertex separator](../../../../vertex-separator.md) version of [Menger's theorem](../../../../menger-theorem.md) says that, for distinct nonadjacent [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) $x,y$ of a finite [graph](../../../../graph-split.md), the maximum number of [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../../../../internally-vertex-disjoint-paths.md) from $x$ to $y$ equals the minimum size of an $x$–$y$ [vertex separator](../../../../vertex-separator.md).

Use the following version of the [max-flow min-cut theorem](../../../../max-flow-min-cut-theorem.md): in a finite directed [flow network](../../../../flow-network-split.md) with nonnegative [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) [flow network edge capacities](../../../../flow-network-edge-capacity.md), the maximum [flow value](../../../../strength-of-a-flow.md) equals the minimum [cut capacity](../../../../cut-capacity.md), and a [maximum flow](../../../../maximum-flow-problem.md) can be chosen to have [integer](../../../../integer-split.md) values on every directed [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md). The last clause is the [integral max-flow theorem](../../../../integral-max-flow-theorem.md).

Here is the reduction, including the correspondence with [vertex separators](../../../../vertex-separator.md). Put $N=|V(G)|$. Perform [vertex splitting](../../../../vertex-splitting.md) on each [vertex](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) $v\ne x,y$, replacing it by $v_{\rm in},v_{\rm out}$ with a directed [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $v_{\rm in}\to v_{\rm out}$ of [flow network edge capacity](../../../../flow-network-edge-capacity.md) one. Keep $x$ as the source and $y$ as the sink. For each original undirected [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $uv$, put directed [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) from the output of $u$ to the input of $v$ and from the output of $v$ to the input of $u$, each of [flow network edge capacity](../../../../flow-network-edge-capacity.md) $N$; an unsplit endpoint uses $x$ or $y$ itself.

A family of $p$ [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../../../../internally-vertex-disjoint-paths.md) gives a feasible integral [flow](../../../../flow.md) of value $p$, by sending one unit down each [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md). Conversely, decompose an integral [flow](../../../../flow.md) into unit source-to-sink [graph paths](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) and [directed cycles](../../../../directed-cycle.md), discarding the [directed cycles](../../../../directed-cycle.md). This decomposition can be obtained by following positive-[flow](../../../../flow.md) directed [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) and subtracting one unit along a source-to-sink [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) or a [directed cycle](../../../../directed-cycle.md), repeating until the [flow value](../../../../strength-of-a-flow.md) is exhausted. A capacity-one split [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) can belong to at most one unit [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md). The projected [graph paths](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) in $G$ are therefore [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../../../../internally-vertex-disjoint-paths.md). Thus the largest number of such [graph paths](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) equals the maximum [flow value](../../../../strength-of-a-flow.md).

Deleting a [vertex separator](../../../../vertex-separator.md) $S$ corresponds to removing its split [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), of total [flow network edge capacity](../../../../flow-network-edge-capacity.md) $|S|$. This destroys every source-to-sink [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md); taking the source-reachable [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) after removal gives a [cut of a flow network](../../../../cut-of-a-flow-network.md) of [cut capacity](../../../../cut-capacity.md) at most $|S|$. Thus the minimum [cut capacity](../../../../cut-capacity.md) is no greater than the minimum [vertex separator](../../../../vertex-separator.md) size.

For the reverse inequality, deleting all $N-2$ internal [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) separates $x,y$, because they are nonadjacent. Hence a [minimum cut](../../../../minimum-cut.md) has [cut capacity](../../../../cut-capacity.md) at most $N-2<N$ and cannot contain a directed [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) of [flow network edge capacity](../../../../flow-network-edge-capacity.md) $N$. Its outgoing [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) are therefore split [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md). Let $S$ be the original [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) associated with them. Any $x$–$y$ [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) avoiding $S$ would lift to a source-to-sink [graph path](../../../../path-in-a-graph.md) crossing this [cut of a flow network](../../../../cut-of-a-flow-network.md) without using an outgoing [edge](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), an impossibility. Hence $S$ is a [vertex separator](../../../../vertex-separator.md), with size exactly the [cut capacity](../../../../cut-capacity.md). The two minima agree, and the [max-flow min-cut theorem](../../../../max-flow-min-cut-theorem.md) proves [Menger's theorem](../../../../menger-theorem.md).
