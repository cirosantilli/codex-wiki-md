<h1 id="2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Choose a [spanning tree](../../../../../spanning-tree.md) of the [connected](../../../../../connectivity-graph-theory.md) [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) and [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) it at $x$. List [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in nonincreasing order of their [distance](../../../../../distance.md) from $x$ in this [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md), breaking ties arbitrarily. The [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md) has [distance](../../../../../distance.md) zero and is last, so $v_n=x$.

Every other [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) has a parent one step closer to the [root](../../../../../root-of-a-root-system.md). That parent is adjacent to it and appears later in the ordering. Hence

$$
\boxed{\forall i<n\ \exists j>i:\ v_iv_j\in E(H).}
$$

For a [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) of one [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) the condition is vacuous and the same ordering works.
