## 1

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="1/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#1/a)

A [proper edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-edge-coloring) assigns colours to the [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of a [graph](../graph.md) so that distinct [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) sharing a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) have different colours. The [edge chromatic number](../graph-coloring.md#edge-chromatic-number) $\chi'(G)$ is the least number of colours in such an assignment. Equivalently, it is the least number of [matchings in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) whose disjoint union is the entire collection of [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph).

[Vizing's theorem](../graph-coloring.md#vizing-s-theorem) states that a finite [simple graph](../graph.md#simple-graph) of [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) $\Delta$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\Delta\leq\chi'(G)\leq\Delta+1.}
$$

The lower bound holds because all [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) incident with a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ require different colours.

[Hall's marriage theorem](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-s-marriage-theorem) states that a finite [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md) with [vertex sets](../graph.md#vertex-set) $A,B$ has a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) saturating $A$ if and only if

$$
\boxed{|N(S)|\geq|S|\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq A,}
$$

where $N(S)\subseteq B$ is the [graph neighbourhood](../graph-theory.md#graph-neighbourhood) of $S$. Here [matching saturation](../matching-graph-theory.md#matching-saturation) means that each [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $A$ is incident with an [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of the [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md); it does not require every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $B$ to be met.

<h3 id="1/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/b/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#1/b/i)

Let $A_\Delta=\{a\in A:d_G(a)=\Delta\}$. Apply [Hall's marriage theorem](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-s-marriage-theorem) to the [induced subgraph](../graph-theory.md#induced-subgraph) on $A_\Delta\cup B$, which is a [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md). For any [subset](../set.md#subset) $S\subseteq A_\Delta$, [double counting](../combinatorics.md#double-counting-proof-technique) the [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) from $S$ to its [graph neighbourhood](../graph-theory.md#graph-neighbourhood) gives

$$
\Delta|S|=\sum_{a\in S}d_G(a)=e_G(S,N(S))
\leq\sum_{b\in N(S)}d_G(b)\leq\Delta|N(S)|.
$$

The equality uses the fact that every [neighbour](../graph-theory.md#neighbour-of-a-vertex) of $S$ lies in $B$, so all [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) incident with $S$ have been counted. The final inequality uses the [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) bound on $B$. Since $\Delta>0$, division gives the [Hall condition](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-condition) $|S|\leq|N(S)|$.

Consequently there is a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) $M_1$ saturating $A_\Delta$. If $A_\Delta$ is empty, use the empty [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md). This proves the required [matching covering maximum-degree vertices in a bipartite graph](../matching-graph-theory.md#matching-covering-maximum-degree-vertices-in-a-bipartite-graph) on the specified side; other [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) need not be met.

<h4 id="1/b/ii">ii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](#1/b/ii)

By part (i), choose a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) $M_1$ meeting all [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ in $A$. Interchanging the two [vertex sets](../graph.md#vertex-set) gives a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) $M_2$ meeting all such [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) in $B$. Consider the spanning [subgraph](../graph-theory.md#subgraph) $H$ with [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $M_1\cup M_2$.

Every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) has [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) at most two in $H$. An [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) belonging to both [matchings in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) forms a whole two-[vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory), since neither endpoint can meet another [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of either [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md). On every other nontrivial [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory), the [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) alternate between $M_1$ and $M_2$, so the [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) is a [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) or an even [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph). We select one of the two [matchings in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) separately on each [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory).

On a [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph), either choice covers every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). On a [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) with an odd number of [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), the first and last [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) belong to the same [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md); choose it, and it covers every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of the [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). For a common [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), choose that [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph).

It remains to treat a [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) with a positive even number of [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). Its endpoints lie in the same side of the [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md). Every internal [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) meets both [matchings in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md). If both endpoints lie in $A$, choose $M_1$: every endpoint of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ must already be met by $M_1$, by its defining property. If both endpoints lie in $B$, choose $M_2$ for the same reason. Thus the choice meets all [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ in that [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory).

Finally, an isolated [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $H$ cannot have [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ in $G$, since it would have been met by $M_1$ or $M_2$. The union of the selected [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) over the [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) is a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md), because different [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) have disjoint [vertex sets](../graph.md#vertex-set). It meets every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$ in $G$. The useful mechanism is the [union of two matchings](../matching-graph-theory.md#union-of-two-matchings) and its alternating [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory).

<h4 id="1/b/iii">iii</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#1/b)

<h5 id="1/b/iii/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](#1/b/iii)

Use [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on the [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) $\Delta$, including $\Delta=0$ as the base case. A [graph](../graph.md) of [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) zero has no [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), so zero colours suffice.

For $\Delta>0$, part (ii) supplies a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) $M$ meeting every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $\Delta$. Delete its [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), retaining all [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). Each [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) formerly of [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) loses one incident [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph); every other [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) already had [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) at most $\Delta-1$. Hence

$$
\Delta(G-M)\leq\Delta-1.
$$

The resulting [graph](../graph.md) is still a [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md). By [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction), it has a [proper edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-edge-coloring) using at most $\Delta-1$ colours. Give every [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of $M$ one new colour. Since $M$ is a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md), this produces a [proper edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-edge-coloring) of $G$ with at most $\Delta$ colours. The [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) at a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of [maximum degree](../graph-theory.md#maximum-degree) require at least $\Delta$ colours, so

$$
\boxed{\chi'(G)=\Delta.}
$$

This is [Kőnig's line colouring theorem](../graph-coloring.md#konig-s-line-colouring-theorem).

<h3 id="1/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [1](#1)

<h4 id="1/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#1/c)

For even $r\geq2$, take the [complete graph](../graph-theory.md#complete-graph) $K_{r+1}$. It is an $r$-[regular graph](../graph-theory.md#regular-graph) on an odd number of [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). Every [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) has at most $r/2$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), so $r$ colours in a [proper edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-edge-coloring) could cover at most $r^2/2$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). But

$$
|E(K_{r+1})|=\frac{r(r+1)}2>\frac{r^2}2.
$$

Therefore $\chi'(K_{r+1})>r$; [Vizing's theorem](../graph-coloring.md#vizing-s-theorem) gives **$\chi'(K_{r+1})=r+1$**. This includes the [graph triangle](../graph.md#triangle-in-a-graph) when $r=2$.

For odd $r\geq3$, first construct a [graph](../graph.md) $H_r$ on $r+2$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). Start with the [complete graph](../graph-theory.md#complete-graph) on

$$
\{v,a,b,c_1,\ldots,c_{r-1}\},
$$

and delete the [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $va$, $vb$, and $c_1c_2,c_3c_4,\ldots,c_{r-2}c_{r-1}$. The pairing is possible because $r-1$ is even. In the initial [complete graph](../graph-theory.md#complete-graph), every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) has [vertex degree](../graph-theory.md#degree-graph-theory) $r+1$. The distinguished [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $v$ loses two [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) and every other [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) loses exactly one, giving

$$
d_{H_r}(v)=r-1,\qquad d_{H_r}(x)=r\quad(x\ne v).
$$

Take two disjoint copies of $H_r$, with distinguished [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $v,v'$, and add the [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $vv'$. The resulting [simple graph](../graph.md#simple-graph) $G_r$ is an $r$-[regular graph](../graph-theory.md#regular-graph). The added [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) is the only [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) between the two copies; each copy has the odd number $r+2$ of [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory).

Suppose an $r$-colour [proper edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-edge-coloring) existed. At each [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), the $r$ incident [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) would use all $r$ colours. Thus each colour would form a [perfect matching](../matching-graph-theory.md#perfect-matching). Any [perfect matching](../matching-graph-theory.md#perfect-matching) must use $vv'$: without it, each odd-sized copy would have to be covered by internal pairs, which is impossible by [parity](../quantum-mechanics.md#parity). Therefore all $r$ colour classes would have to contain the same [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $vv'$, a contradiction. Hence [Vizing's theorem](../graph-coloring.md#vizing-s-theorem) again gives

$$
\boxed{\chi'(G_r)=r+1>r.}
$$

These constructions give [class two regular graphs](../graph-theory.md#class-two-regular-graph) for every $r\geq2$. The odd-$r$ construction exhibits the [bridge obstruction to regular edge colouring](../graph-coloring.md#bridge-obstruction-to-regular-edge-colouring); $H_r$ is a [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph) because every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) other than $a,b,v$ is adjacent to $v$, while $a,b$ are adjacent to those [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory).

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="2/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#2/a)

For a finite [graph](../graph.md) $G$, its [chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial) $p_G(x)$ is the [polynomial](../polynomial.md) whose value at each positive [integer](../integer.md) $q$ counts [proper vertex colourings](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) of $G$ from a fixed palette of $q$ labelled colours. Adjacent [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) must receive different colours, and unused colours are allowed.

For an [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $e=uv$, the [deletion-contraction recurrence for the chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#deletion-contraction-recurrence-for-the-chromatic-polynomial) is

$$
\boxed{p_G(x)=p_{G-e}(x)-p_{G/e}(x).}
$$

Here $G-e$ deletes only the [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $e$, while $G/e$ performs [edge contraction](../graph-minor.md#edge-contraction), identifying $u,v$ and removing $e$. Any parallel [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) produced by [edge contraction](../graph-minor.md#edge-contraction) can be replaced by a single [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), since their constraints on [proper vertex colourings](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) are identical.

Indeed, the [proper vertex colourings](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) of $G-e$ in which $u,v$ differ are precisely those of $G$, while those in which $u,v$ agree correspond bijectively to [proper vertex colourings](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) of $G/e$. This proves the equality for every positive [integer](../integer.md) $q$, hence as a [polynomial identity](../polynomial.md#polynomial-identity). Together with $p_G(x)=x^{|V(G)|}$ for a [graph](../graph.md) without [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), the recurrence also proves by [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) that the counting function is a [polynomial](../polynomial.md).

<h3 id="2/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#2/b)

Let the nonempty [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) of $G$ be $G_1,\ldots,G_t$. A [proper vertex colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) can be chosen separately on each [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory), since no [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) joins different [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory). The [multiplication principle](../combinatorics.md#rule-of-product) gives, first at positive [integers](../integer.md) and then as a [polynomial identity](../polynomial.md#polynomial-identity),

$$
p_G(x)=\prod_{j=1}^t p_{G_j}(x).
$$

It remains to justify that every factor has zero constant term. More generally, for every nonempty [simple graph](../graph.md#simple-graph) $H$, prove $p_H(0)=0$ by [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) on the number of [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). If there are no [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), then $p_H(x)=x^{|V(H)|}$ with $|V(H)|\geq1$. Otherwise choose an [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $e$. Both $H-e$ and the simplified [edge contraction](../graph-minor.md#edge-contraction) $H/e$ are nonempty [graphs](../graph.md) with fewer [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). The [deletion-contraction recurrence for the chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#deletion-contraction-recurrence-for-the-chromatic-polynomial) and [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction) give

$$
p_H(0)=p_{H-e}(0)-p_{H/e}(0)=0.
$$

Thus $p_{G_j}(x)=xq_j(x)$ for a [polynomial](../polynomial.md) $q_j$, and

$$
\boxed{p_G(x)=x^t\prod_{j=1}^t q_j(x),\qquad x^t\mid p_G(x).}
$$

For the empty [graph](../graph.md), $t=0$ and $p_G(x)=1$, so the conclusion also holds. This is the [chromatic polynomial product over components](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial-product-over-components).

<h3 id="2/c">c</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/c/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#2/c)

Write $V=V(G)$ and $E=E(G)$. For $F\subseteq E$, let $c(F)$ denote the number of [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) of the spanning [subgraph](../graph-theory.md#subgraph) $(V,F)$, including isolated [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). The [inclusion-exclusion principle](../combinatorics.md#inclusion-exclusion-principle), applied to the unwanted events that the two endpoints of an [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) receive the same colour, gives the [chromatic polynomial inclusion-exclusion formula](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial-inclusion-exclusion-formula)

$$
p_G(x)=\sum_{F\subseteq E}(-1)^{|F|}x^{c(F)}.
$$

To justify the exponent, imposing equal colours across all [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) in $F$ forces a single colour on each [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) of $(V,F)$, with independent choices between its [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory). Thus the corresponding count at $q$ colours is $q^{c(F)}$. Equality for all positive [integers](../integer.md) proves the [polynomial identity](../polynomial.md#polynomial-identity).

Fix an index $i$ in the stated range. A [subset](../set.md#subset) $F$ contributes to the coefficient of $x^{n-i}$ exactly when $n-c(F)=i$. If $(V,F)$ contains a [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) of length $s$, the hypothesis gives $s\geq\ell$. All its $s$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) lie in one [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory). For any [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) $D$, its contribution to $n-c(F)$ is $|V(D)|-1$, so

$$
n-c(F)=\sum_D(|V(D)|-1)\geq s-1\geq\ell-1.
$$

This contradicts $i\leq\ell-2$. Therefore every contributing $(V,F)$ is a [forest](../tree-graph-theory.md#forest). The [forest](../tree-graph-theory.md#forest) identity $|F|=n-c(F)$ then gives $|F|=i$.

Conversely, every $i$-[edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) [subset](../set.md#subset) is a [forest](../tree-graph-theory.md#forest), since a [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) in it would have at most $i<\ell$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). It therefore has $c(F)=n-i$ and contributes exactly $(-1)^i$. There are $\binom mi$ such [subsets](../set.md#subset), by the definition of a [binomial coefficient](../binomial-coefficient.md). Consequently

$$
[x^{n-i}]p_G(x)=(-1)^i\binom mi,\qquad
\boxed{a_i=\binom mi\quad(0\leq i\leq\ell-2).}
$$

Here the coefficient notation is understood within the defined indices $0\leq i\leq n$; if the displayed range is extended past $n$, the coefficients beyond the constant term can be set to zero. For a [forest](../tree-graph-theory.md#forest), $m\leq n-1$, so the [binomial coefficients](../binomial-coefficient.md) there are also zero. The key reason [girth determines initial chromatic coefficients](../graph-coloring.md#girth-determines-initial-chromatic-coefficients) is that a [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) can affect only exponents whose loss of [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) is at least $\ell-1$.

<h3 id="2/d">d</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [2](#2)

<h4 id="2/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#2/d)

First reduce the [unicyclic graph](../graph-theory.md#unicyclic-graph) to its unique [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) $C$. Delete the [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of $C$ but retain every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). The remaining [graph](../graph.md) is a [forest](../tree-graph-theory.md#forest), since any surviving [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) would be a second one in $G$. Each of its [connected components of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) contains a [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $C$: follow a [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) to $C$ in the original [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph), stopping at the first [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $C$; this initial segment uses no [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of $C$.

No such [connected component of a graph](../graph.md#component-graph-theory) contains two [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $C$. Otherwise, on the [tree](../tree-graph-theory.md) [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) between them, choose two successive visits to $C$. The segment between these visits has no internal [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) on $C$ and uses no [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of $C$. Combining it with either connecting arc of $C$ gives a second [graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph), a contradiction. Thus the remaining [graph](../graph.md) consists of [trees](../tree-graph-theory.md), each rooted at exactly one [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $C$.

After choosing a [proper vertex colouring](../graph-coloring.md#proper-vertex-coloring) of $C$ from $q$ colours, colour these [rooted trees](../tree-graph-theory.md#rooted-tree) away from their roots. Each of the $n-\ell$ off-[graph cycle](../graph-theory.md#cycle-in-a-graph) [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) has one already coloured [parent in a rooted tree](../tree-graph-theory.md#parent-in-a-rooted-tree), leaving exactly $q-1$ choices. The [multiplication principle](../combinatorics.md#rule-of-product) therefore gives

$$
p_G(x)=(x-1)^{n-\ell}p_{C_\ell}(x).
$$

Equivalently, this repeatedly applies the [chromatic polynomial after attaching a leaf](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial-after-attaching-a-leaf) formula.

To compute the [chromatic polynomial of a cycle](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial-of-a-cycle), the base case is the [graph triangle](../graph.md#triangle-in-a-graph):

$$
p_{C_3}(x)=x(x-1)(x-2)=(x-1)^3-(x-1).
$$

For $s\geq4$, deleting a [cycle graph](../graph-theory.md#cycle-graph) [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) gives a [path graph](../graph-theory.md#path-graph) on $s$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), while [edge contraction](../graph-minor.md#edge-contraction) gives $C_{s-1}$. The [chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial) of that [path graph](../graph-theory.md#path-graph) is $x(x-1)^{s-1}$, by choosing the colour of its first [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) and then each successive [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). Hence the [deletion-contraction recurrence for the chromatic polynomial](../graph-coloring.md#deletion-contraction-recurrence-for-the-chromatic-polynomial) gives

$$
p_{C_s}(x)=x(x-1)^{s-1}-p_{C_{s-1}}(x).
$$

Using [mathematical induction](../foundations-of-mathematics.md#mathematical-induction), substitute $p_{C_{s-1}}(x)=(x-1)^{s-1}+(-1)^{s-1}(x-1)$ to obtain

$$
p_{C_s}(x)=(x-1)^s+(-1)^s(x-1).
$$

Multiplying by the factor for the attached [trees](../tree-graph-theory.md) proves the [chromatic polynomial of a unicyclic graph](../graph-coloring.md#chromatic-polynomial-of-a-unicyclic-graph) formula

$$
\boxed{p_G(x)=(x-1)^n+(-1)^\ell(x-1)^{n-\ell+1}.}
$$

## 3

↑ **Parent:** [2023](2023.md)

<h3 id="3/a">a</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/a/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [A](#3/a)

A [graph](../graph.md) is a [k-connected graph](../graph-theory.md#k-connected-graph) if it has at least $k+1$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) and deleting any [set](../set.md) of fewer than $k$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), together with their incident [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), leaves a [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph).

The [vertex connectivity](../graph-theory.md#vertex-connectivity) $\kappa(G)$ is the largest such $k$. Equivalently, for a [graph](../graph.md) with at least two [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), it is the smallest number of [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) whose deletion either disconnects the [graph](../graph.md) or leaves a single [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory). The latter convention gives $\kappa(K_n)=n-1$ for a [complete graph](../graph-theory.md#complete-graph). A disconnected [graph](../graph.md) has [vertex connectivity](../graph-theory.md#vertex-connectivity) zero; one usually also sets $\kappa(K_1)=0$.

For distinct nonadjacent [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $x,y$, the [local connectivity](../graph-theory.md#local-connectivity) is

$$
\boxed{\kappa_G(x,y)=\min\{|S|:S\subseteq V(G)\setminus\{x,y\},\ \text{there is no }x\text{--}y\text{ path in }G-S\}.}
$$

Such a [set](../set.md) $S$ is an $x$–$y$ [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator). By [Menger's theorem](../graph-theory.md#menger-theorem), [local connectivity](../graph-theory.md#local-connectivity) also equals the maximum number of [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#internally-vertex-disjoint-paths) from $x$ to $y$. If no [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) exists already, the empty [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator) gives zero.

<h3 id="3/b">b</h3>

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/b/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [B](#3/b)

The [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator) version of [Menger's theorem](../graph-theory.md#menger-theorem) says that, for distinct nonadjacent [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $x,y$ of a finite [graph](../graph.md), the maximum number of [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#internally-vertex-disjoint-paths) from $x$ to $y$ equals the minimum size of an $x$–$y$ [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator).

Use the following version of the [max-flow min-cut theorem](../flow-network.md#max-flow-min-cut-theorem): in a finite directed [flow network](../flow-network.md) with nonnegative [integer](../integer.md) [flow network edge capacities](../flow-network.md#flow-network-edge-capacity), the maximum [flow value](../flow-network.md#strength-of-a-flow) equals the minimum [cut capacity](../flow-network.md#cut-capacity), and a [maximum flow](../flow-network.md#maximum-flow-problem) can be chosen to have [integer](../integer.md) values on every directed [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). The last clause is the [integral max-flow theorem](../flow-network.md#integral-max-flow-theorem).

Here is the reduction, including the correspondence with [vertex separators](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator). Put $N=|V(G)|$. Perform [vertex splitting](../flow-network.md#vertex-splitting) on each [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $v\ne x,y$, replacing it by $v_{\rm in},v_{\rm out}$ with a directed [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $v_{\rm in}\to v_{\rm out}$ of [flow network edge capacity](../flow-network.md#flow-network-edge-capacity) one. Keep $x$ as the source and $y$ as the sink. For each original undirected [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $uv$, put directed [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) from the output of $u$ to the input of $v$ and from the output of $v$ to the input of $u$, each of [flow network edge capacity](../flow-network.md#flow-network-edge-capacity) $N$; an unsplit endpoint uses $x$ or $y$ itself.

A family of $p$ [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#internally-vertex-disjoint-paths) gives a feasible integral [flow](../flow-network.md#flow) of value $p$, by sending one unit down each [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). Conversely, decompose an integral [flow](../flow-network.md#flow) into unit source-to-sink [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) and [directed cycles](../graph-theory.md#directed-cycle), discarding the [directed cycles](../graph-theory.md#directed-cycle). This decomposition can be obtained by following positive-[flow](../flow-network.md#flow) directed [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) and subtracting one unit along a source-to-sink [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) or a [directed cycle](../graph-theory.md#directed-cycle), repeating until the [flow value](../flow-network.md#strength-of-a-flow) is exhausted. A capacity-one split [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) can belong to at most one unit [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). The projected [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) in $G$ are therefore [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#internally-vertex-disjoint-paths). Thus the largest number of such [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) equals the maximum [flow value](../flow-network.md#strength-of-a-flow).

Deleting a [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator) $S$ corresponds to removing its split [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), of total [flow network edge capacity](../flow-network.md#flow-network-edge-capacity) $|S|$. This destroys every source-to-sink [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph); taking the source-reachable [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) after removal gives a [cut of a flow network](../flow-network.md#cut-of-a-flow-network) of [cut capacity](../flow-network.md#cut-capacity) at most $|S|$. Thus the minimum [cut capacity](../flow-network.md#cut-capacity) is no greater than the minimum [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator) size.

For the reverse inequality, deleting all $N-2$ internal [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) separates $x,y$, because they are nonadjacent. Hence a [minimum cut](../flow-network.md#minimum-cut) has [cut capacity](../flow-network.md#cut-capacity) at most $N-2<N$ and cannot contain a directed [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) of [flow network edge capacity](../flow-network.md#flow-network-edge-capacity) $N$. Its outgoing [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) are therefore split [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). Let $S$ be the original [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) associated with them. Any $x$–$y$ [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) avoiding $S$ would lift to a source-to-sink [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) crossing this [cut of a flow network](../flow-network.md#cut-of-a-flow-network) without using an outgoing [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph), an impossibility. Hence $S$ is a [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator), with size exactly the [cut capacity](../flow-network.md#cut-capacity). The two minima agree, and the [max-flow min-cut theorem](../flow-network.md#max-flow-min-cut-theorem) proves [Menger's theorem](../graph-theory.md#menger-theorem).

<h4 id="3/c/i">i</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [C](#3/c)

<h5 id="3/c/i/solution">Solution</h5>

↑ **Parent:** [I](#3/c/i)

Add two new [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $s,t$ to $G$, joining $s$ to every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $U$ and $t$ to every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $W$, with no other new [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). The [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $s,t$ are nonadjacent. Suppose $S\subseteq V(G)$ has $|S|<k$. Since $G$ is a [k-connected graph](../graph-theory.md#k-connected-graph), $G-S$ is a [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph). Both $U\setminus S$ and $W\setminus S$ are nonempty because each original [set](../set.md) has [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $k$. Choose $u\in U\setminus S$ and $w\in W\setminus S$; a [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) between them in $G-S$ extends to an $s$–$t$ [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) avoiding $S$.

Consequently no fewer than $k$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) can form an $s$–$t$ [vertex separator](../graph-theory.md#vertex-separator). By [Menger's theorem](../graph-theory.md#menger-theorem), there are at least $k$ [internally vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#internally-vertex-disjoint-paths) from $s$ to $t$. There cannot be more than $k$, since $s$ has only $k$ [neighbours](../graph-theory.md#neighbour-of-a-vertex).

Remove $s,t$ from these $k$ [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). The resulting [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) in $G$ start at distinct [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $U$, end at distinct [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $W$, and are pairwise [vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#vertex-disjoint-paths): any shared [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) would have been an internal [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) common to two of the original [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). Because there are exactly $k$ [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph), they use every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $U$ and $W$ as an endpoint. None can pass through another terminal internally, since that terminal already occurs as an endpoint of one of the [vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#vertex-disjoint-paths). Thus there are $k$ [vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#vertex-disjoint-paths) from $U$ to $W$, with some pairing of the endpoints. This is also an immediate application of the [Set version of Menger theorem](../graph-theory.md#set-version-of-menger-theorem).

<h5 id="3/c/ii/solution">Solution</h5>

**No.** A counterexample works for every $k\geq2$. Take the [complete bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md#complete-bipartite-graph) $G=K_{k,k}$ with [vertex sets](../graph.md#vertex-set)

$$
A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\},\qquad B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_k\}.
$$

It is a [k-connected graph](../graph-theory.md#k-connected-graph): deleting fewer than $k$ [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) leaves at least one [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) in each side, and the remaining [complete bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md#complete-bipartite-graph) is a [connected graph](../graph.md#connected-graph).

Choose

$$
U=\{b_1,a_1,\ldots,a_{k-1}\},\qquad
W=\{b_2,\ldots,b_k,a_k\},
$$

and order these [sets](../set.md) so that $u_1=b_1$ and $w_1=b_2$. Order the other members arbitrarily. These are disjoint [sets](../set.md) of [cardinality](../cardinal-number.md#cardinality) $k$, and every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $G$ is one of the specified endpoints.

If the prescribed $k$ [vertex-disjoint paths](../graph-theory.md#vertex-disjoint-paths) existed, no one of them could have an internal [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory), because every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) must serve as the endpoint of one of the [graph paths](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph). Thus each [graph path](../graph-theory.md#path-in-a-graph) would have to be a single [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph). But $b_1,b_2$ lie in the same side of the [bipartite graph](../bipartite-graph.md), so there is no [edge](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) $b_1b_2$. This contradicts the requirement on $P_1$.

For $k=2$ this is the four-[vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) [cycle graph](../graph-theory.md#cycle-graph), with opposite [vertices](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) paired. The stronger property that every prescribed pairing can be realized is the [linked graph](../graph-theory.md#linked-graph) property; [vertex connectivity](../graph-theory.md#vertex-connectivity) alone does not guarantee it.

↑ **Parent:** [3](#3)

<h4 id="3/d/solution">Solution</h4>

↑ **Parent:** [D](#3/d)

We verify the [Hall condition](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-condition) for $U$. Let $S\subseteq U$. If $S$ is empty, then $|N(S)|=0=|S|$. Suppose $0<|S|\leq n/2$. Choose $u\in S$. Its [graph neighbourhood](../graph-theory.md#graph-neighbourhood) is contained in $N(S)$, so the [minimum degree](../graph-theory.md#minimum-degree-of-a-graph) hypothesis gives

$$
|N(S)|\geq d_G(u)\geq n/2\geq|S|.
$$

If $|S|>n/2$, suppose for a contradiction that some [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) $w\in W\setminus N(S)$ exists. All [neighbours](../graph-theory.md#neighbour-of-a-vertex) of $w$ lie in $U\setminus S$, so

$$
d_G(w)\leq n-|S|<n/2,
$$

contradicting the [minimum degree](../graph-theory.md#minimum-degree-of-a-graph) hypothesis. Thus $N(S)=W$ and $|N(S)|=n\geq|S|$.

Every [subset](../set.md#subset) $S\subseteq U$ satisfies the [Hall condition](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-condition). By [Hall's marriage theorem](../matching-graph-theory.md#hall-s-marriage-theorem), there is a [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) saturating $U$. Since $|U|=|W|=n$, its $n$ [edges](../graph-theory.md#edge-of-a-graph) also cover every [vertex](../graph.md#vertex-graph-theory) of $W$. It is therefore a **complete matching**, meaning a [perfect matching](../matching-graph-theory.md#perfect-matching). For $n=0$, the empty [matching in a graph](../matching-graph-theory.md) already has this property.
