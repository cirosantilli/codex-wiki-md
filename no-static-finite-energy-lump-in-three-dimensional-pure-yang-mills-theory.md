# No static finite-energy lump in three-dimensional pure Yang-Mills theory

↑ **Parent:** [Derrick scaling](derrick-scaling.md)

For a smooth [gauge field](gauge-field.md) on $\mathbb R^3$ with finite positive magnetic [energy](energy.md), dilate its [connection one-form](connection-one-form.md) by $A_i^{(s)}(x)=sA_i(sx)$. Its [gauge curvature](gauge-field-strength.md) scales as $F_{ij}^{(s)}(x)=s^2F_{ij}(sx)$, so the energy scales as $s^4s^{-3}E=sE$. A static stationary configuration must have zero derivative at $s=1$, forcing $E=0$ and hence $F=0$. This [Derrick scaling](derrick-scaling.md) argument assumes the dilation preserves the boundary conditions and that boundary terms vanish. It does not exclude four-dimensional [Yang-Mills instantons](yang-mills-instanton.md), whose Euclidean action is scale invariant.
