# Hofstadter nuclear electron scattering experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Nuclear charge distribution](nuclear-charge-distribution.md)

[Robert Hofstadter](robert-hofstadter.md)'s Stanford installation selected [electrons](electron.md) up to $190\,\mathrm{MeV}$. [Magnetic spectrometer](magnetic-spectrometer.md) scans counted shielded Lucite [Cherenkov radiation](cherenkov-radiation.md) pulses per monitor charge. [Carbon-12](carbon-12.md) at $187\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $80^\circ$ resolved $185.1\,\mathrm{MeV}$ [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) and $180.7\,\mathrm{MeV}$ [inelastic scattering](inelastic-scattering.md) ($4.43\,\mathrm{MeV}$ excitation). Calibration used forward-beam energy, [hydrogen](hydrogen.md) [nuclear recoil](nuclear-recoil.md) and [carbon-12](carbon-12.md) [energy levels](energy-level.md). Closed entrance slits checked background.[§III(b)(1), pp. 222–223, Fig. 11; §IV(a), pp. 227–231, Figs. 14–17](electron-scattering-and-nuclear-structure-by-robert-hofstadter.md)

The key distinction is between an [electron](electron.md) counter and an [energy](energy-split.md) measurement. Here the counter registers particles admitted by the [magnetic spectrometer](magnetic-spectrometer.md); changing its [magnetic field](magnetic-field-split.md) selects another outgoing [momentum](momentum-split.md). A peak is therefore reconstructed from a scan, rather than read as one pulse whose height equals the scattered [electron](electron.md) [energy](energy-split.md). Dividing counts by monitored incident [electric charge](electric-charge-split.md) compensates for changes in beam intensity. A peak's height alone is insufficient: its area and the instrument's accepted [momentum](momentum-split.md) interval determine the selected yield.

An [inelastic scattering](inelastic-scattering.md) peak corresponds to a different final nuclear [quantum state](quantum-state.md), so including it in the [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) yield would answer a different structural question. Conversely, finite [energy](energy-split.md) resolution can hide sufficiently nearby excitations beneath an apparent [elastic scattering](elastic-scattering.md) peak. Background subtraction, target-loss corrections, angular acceptance and counter efficiency all enter before counts become a [differential scattering cross-section](differential-scattering-cross-section.md). A dip in uncorrected counts is therefore not automatically a zero of a [nuclear charge form factor](nuclear-charge-form-factor.md).
