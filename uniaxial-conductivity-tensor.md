# Uniaxial conductivity tensor

↑ **Parent:** [Electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md)

A uniaxial [electrical conductivity](electrical-conductivity.md) has one distinguished direction $n$ and equal response in its orthogonal plane. Its eigenvalues are $\alpha$ in $n^\perp$ and $\alpha+\gamma$ along $n$, so it is invertible precisely when $\alpha(\alpha+\gamma)\ne0$. For an [electric field](electric-field.md) perpendicular to $n$, the [electric current density](current-density.md) is $J=\alpha E$. Positive dissipation requires both eigenvalues to be nonnegative, a physical constraint separate from algebraic invertibility. For $D_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}n_k$, one has $D^2=nn^{\mathsf T}-I$, and $\sigma D^2=-\sigma$ is equivalent to $\gamma=-\alpha$; the conductivity then vanishes along $n$.
