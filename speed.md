# Speed

↑ **Parent:** [Velocity](velocity.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed)

[Speed](speed.md) is the nonnegative magnitude of [velocity](velocity.md), a [scalar](scalar.md) rather than a [vector](vector.md). In orthonormal [Cartesian coordinates](cartesian-coordinate-system.md),

$$
v(t)=|\mathbf v(t)|=\sqrt{v_x(t)^2+v_y(t)^2+v_z(t)^2}.
$$

Its [SI](international-system-of-units.md) unit is $\mathrm{m/s}$. In the reversal example under [velocity](velocity.md), the endpoint [velocities](velocity.md) $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $+2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ both have [speed](speed.md) $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, but the [speed](speed.md) falls to zero between them. A [speed](speed.md) reading alone therefore cannot tell which way the body is moving.

For a smooth path, the total distance traveled is its [arc length](arc-length.md), $L=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}v(t)\,dt$. Unlike the [integral](integral.md) of a signed [velocity](velocity.md) component, this [integral](integral.md) cannot cancel outward and return motion. The reversal example travels $2\,\mathrm m$ in $2\,\mathrm s$, giving an average [speed](speed.md) of $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while its average [velocity](velocity.md) is zero. This makes [speed](speed.md) useful for estimating travel duration or fuel use, while [velocity](velocity.md) also tracks progress toward a destination. [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, section 3.2: distance traveled versus displacement](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/3-2-instantaneous-velocity-and-speed)
