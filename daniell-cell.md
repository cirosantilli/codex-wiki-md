# Daniell cell

↑ **Parent:** [Galvanic cell](galvanic-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniell_cell)

A [Daniell cell](daniell-cell.md) uses the [zinc](zinc.md)/$\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ and $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$/[copper](copper.md) pairs. In the separated-compartment teaching version, a [zinc](zinc.md) [electrode](electrode.md) contacts an aqueous [chemical solution](solution-chemistry.md) containing $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$, and a [copper](copper.md) [electrode](electrode.md) contacts one containing $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$. A [salt bridge](salt-bridge.md) supplies an ionic connection; a wire and load supply an electronic connection. During discharge,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{anode, oxidation:}\quad&\mathrm{Zn}(s)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}(aq)+2e^-,\\
\text{cathode, reduction:}\quad&\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}(aq)+2e^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}(s),\\
\text{net:}\quad&\mathrm{Zn}(s)+\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}(aq)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}(aq)+\mathrm{Cu}(s).
\end{aligned}
$$

The [zinc](zinc.md) [electrode](electrode.md) loses material and [copper](copper.md) is deposited at the [cathode](cathode.md). Each [half-reaction](half-reaction.md) conserves [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) counts and [electric charge](electric-charge.md): the first has net charge zero on each side, the second $+2e-2e=0$ on the left and zero on the right. The net reaction has $+2e$ on both sides, with no [Electrons](electron.md) left in its [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md). The Zn/Cu teaching cell and these [half-reactions](half-reaction.md) are described in [Florida State University, Redox Reactions in Voltaic Cells: Construction of a Potential Series, lab background](https://www.chem.fsu.edu/chemlab/chm1lab/redox/background.html).

Creating one [mole](mole-unit.md) of $\mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$ adds $2F$ of positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) to the [anode](anode.md) compartment's ionic inventory; consuming one [mole](mole-unit.md) of $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ removes $2F$ of positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) from the [cathode](cathode.md) compartment. Here $F$ is the [Faraday constant](faraday-constant.md). In an ideal bookkeeping example using a [potassium](potassium.md) [nitrate](nitrate.md) [salt bridge](salt-bridge.md), two [moles](mole-unit.md) of [nitrate](nitrate.md) [anions](anion.md) entering the [anode](anode.md) compartment compensate the first change, and two [moles](mole-unit.md) of [potassium](potassium.md) [cations](cation.md) entering the [cathode](cathode.md) compartment compensate the second. Other [ions](ion.md) in the cell can share the actual transport. The two compensating inventory changes do not mean that $4F$ flowed through the load: the same series [electric current](electric-current.md) transports $2F$ through each cross-section of the circuit per [mole](mole-unit.md) of net reaction. See [salt bridge](salt-bridge.md).

Under standard-state conditions near $298\,\mathrm K$, tabulated reduction potentials give a cell [electromotive force](electromotive-force.md) of approximately $1.10\,\mathrm V$. This is a reversible reference value, not a promised loaded reading; actual composition matters through [thermodynamic activity](thermodynamic-activity.md). The tabulated [copper](copper.md) and [zinc](zinc.md) values and their subtraction are given in [Purdue University, Electrochemical Reactions: standard-state reduction potentials](https://chemed.chem.purdue.edu/genchem/topicreview/bp/ch20/electro.php). For the net [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) as written, the molar [Gibbs free energy](gibbs-free-energy.md) change obeys

$$
\Delta_r G=-2F\mathcal E.
$$

Thus $\mathcal E=1.10\,\mathrm V$ corresponds to about $-212\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ of net reaction. At fixed [temperature](temperature.md) and [pressure](pressure.md), $-\Delta_r G$ is the maximum reversible electrical [work](work.md) per [mole](mole-unit.md) for this cell reaction. It is not automatically all the [heat](heat.md) released by a finite-current cell. See [17.4 Potential, Free Energy, and Equilibrium](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/17-4-potential-free-energy-and-equilibrium.md) (§17.4: cell potential and free energy).
