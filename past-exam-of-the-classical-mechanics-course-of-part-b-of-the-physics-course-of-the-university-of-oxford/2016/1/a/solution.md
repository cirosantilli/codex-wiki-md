<h1 id="1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

In a frame rotating with [angular velocity](../../../../angular-velocity.md) $\boldsymbol\Omega$, a particle of relative [velocity](../../../../velocity.md) $\mathbf v$ experiences the [Coriolis force](../../../../coriolis-force.md)

$$
\boxed{\mathbf F_C=-2m\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v.}
$$

For nearly horizontal atmospheric motion, the relevant vertical [rotation](../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) component is $\Omega\sin\ell$, with latitude $\ell$. In the northern hemisphere the horizontal deflection is to the right of the motion. A low-pressure center produces an inward pressure-gradient force; an anticlockwise circulation has an outward [Coriolis force](../../../../coriolis-force.md) to oppose it. A high-pressure center instead supports clockwise circulation, with inward [Coriolis force](../../../../coriolis-force.md) opposing the outward pressure-gradient force.

The sense reverses in the southern hemisphere. There, anticlockwise circulation is anticyclonic and corresponds to high [atmospheric pressure](../../../../atmospheric-pressure.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{Earth northern hemisphere: low anticlockwise, high clockwise; Jupiter's Great Red Spot: high pressure.}}
$$

This is the large-scale rotating-fluid interpretation; [friction](../../../../friction.md), centrifugal [acceleration](../../../../acceleration.md) and departures from ideal balance modify actual wind profiles. NASA's description independently identifies the southern, counterclockwise [Great Red Spot](../../../../great-red-spot.md) as a high-pressure system.[Description comparing the southern-hemisphere counterclockwise Great Red Spot with low-pressure terrestrial hurricanes](../../../../the-great-red-spot-an-ancient-storm-in-jupiter-s-atmosphere-by-national-aeronautics-and-space-administration.md)
