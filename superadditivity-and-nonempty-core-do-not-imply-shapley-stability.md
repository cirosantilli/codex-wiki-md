# Superadditivity and nonempty core do not imply Shapley stability

↑ **Parent:** [Superadditive coalitional game](superadditive-coalitional-game.md)

On three players, let $v(S)=1$ exactly when $S$ contains player 1 and at least one of players 2 and 3, and zero otherwise. This [simple cooperative game](simple-cooperative-game.md) is superadditive: disjoint [coalitions](coalition-game-theory.md) cannot both contain player 1, so at most one can have value one. Its [core of a cooperative game](core-game-theory.md) is exactly $\{(1,0,0)\}$, since singleton nonnegativity and the two winning-pair constraints force the other two coordinates to zero. In a random player ordering, player 1 is pivotal unless it comes first, whereas each other player is pivotal in just one of the six orders. Thus its [Shapley value](shapley-value.md) is $(2/3,1/6,1/6)$, outside the [core](core-game-theory.md): coalition $\{1,2\}$ receives only $5/6<1$. [Convex cooperative games](convex-cooperative-game.md) have the stronger property that their [Shapley value belongs to the core of a convex game](shapley-value-belongs-to-the-core-of-a-convex-game.md).
