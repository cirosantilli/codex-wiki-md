# Electric potential difference

↑ **Parent:** [Electric potential](electric-potential.md)

An [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) compares [electric potentials](electric-potential.md) at two points. For a move from $a$ to $b$, define $\Delta\Phi=\Phi(b)-\Phi(a)$; the terminal convention $V_{AB}=\Phi(A)-\Phi(B)$ instead means the potential at $A$ relative to $B$, so the order must always be stated. In [electrostatics](electrostatics.md), the [line integral](line-integral.md) of the [electric field](electric-field.md) gives

$$
\Delta\Phi=-\int_a^b\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l,\qquad
\Delta U=q\Delta\Phi,\qquad
W_{\mathrm{field}}=q\int_a^b\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=q\bigl(\Phi(a)-\Phi(b)\bigr)=-\Delta U.
$$

Here $\Delta U$ is the change in interaction [potential energy](potential-energy.md) of the test [electric charge](electric-charge.md) $q$ with fixed sources, while $W_{\mathrm{field}}$ is [work](work.md) done by the [electric field](electric-field.md). For an external agent moving the charge slowly without a change in [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), its [work](work.md) is $\Delta U$. A positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) gains [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) when accelerated toward lower [electric potential](electric-potential.md); a negative [electric charge](electric-charge.md) gains [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) when accelerated toward higher [electric potential](electric-potential.md). Both motions lower $q\Phi$.[§7.2: potential difference, work, and energy](university-physics-volume-2-by-samuel-j-ling-and-others/7-2-electric-potential-and-potential-difference.md)

For example, fix $Q=+2\,\mathrm{nC}$ at the origin and compare distances $r_a=0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $r_b=0.10\,\mathrm m$. With the stipulated approximation $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and zero at infinity,

$$
\Phi(a)=\frac{kQ}{r_a}=89.9\,\mathrm V,\qquad
\Phi(b)=\frac{kQ}{r_b}=179.8\,\mathrm V,\qquad
\Delta\Phi=89.9\,\mathrm V.
$$

Moving $q=-3\,\mathrm{nC}$ inward therefore gives

$$
U_a=-2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J,\qquad
U_b=-5.394\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J,\qquad
\Delta U=-2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J,\qquad
W_{\mathrm{field}}=+2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J.
$$

If only the [electric force](electric-force.md) does [work](work.md), [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) makes the gain in [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md) $2.697\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$. Direct integration confirms the signs: $\int_{r_a}^{r_b}kQ\,dr/r^2=kQ(1/r_a-1/r_b)=-89.9\,\mathrm V$, which multiplied by the negative test [electric charge](electric-charge.md) gives positive [work](work.md). The [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) is unchanged by choosing a different [electrical reference potential](electrical-reference-potential.md).

In an [electrical circuit](electrical-circuit.md), an [electric potential difference](electric-potential-difference.md) is the voltage across a component's terminals. In an [electrical network](electrical-network.md), a vertex [voltage](voltage.md) is specified relative to a reference vertex; its differences across edges drive [current flow](current-flow.md). For a uniform [electric field](electric-field.md), the integral reduces to $\Delta\Phi=-\mathbf E\cdot(\mathbf r_b-\mathbf r_a)$. A field $\mathbf E=300\,\mathrm{V/m}\,\widehat{\mathbf x}$ across a displacement $0.04\,\mathrm m\,\widehat{\mathbf x}$ therefore gives $\Delta\Phi=-12\,\mathrm V$, as in the interior approximation for a [parallel-plate capacitor](parallel-plate-capacitor.md). The [volt](volt.md), measured in [joules](joule.md) per [coulomb](coulomb.md), expresses [energy](energy.md) transfer per unit [electric charge](electric-charge.md), which makes it useful for comparing components without specifying the amount of charge transported.
