# Kapitza thermal boundary experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Kapitza resistance](kapitza-resistance.md)

[Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitza](pyotr-leonidovich-kapitza.md)'s 1941 study suspended cleaned, annealed [copper](copper.md) blocks in liquid [helium](helium.md). Sealed, gas-filled cavities held a heater and a [bronze](bronze.md) [resistance thermometer](resistance-thermometer.md). Electrical readout tracked the heating-induced [temperature](temperature-split.md) rise; the earlier readout method assumed a stable bath without a separate bath [thermometer](thermometer.md). A nearby unheated block warmed much less, localizing the drop near the heated surface. Gold plating and coatings tested surface effects; tight cavities prevented liquid ingress. These were solid-body readings, not probes resolving the microscopic interfacial layer. [The Study of Heat Transfer in Helium II, chapter 38, §2 pp. 584–585; §6 pp. 616–621, Figs. 25–29](the-study-of-heat-transfer-in-helium-ii-by-pyotr-leonidovich-kapitza.md)

Original functional schematic and invented [temperature](temperature-split.md) profile. $T_s-T_l$ denotes the interface drop after bulk corrections; no apparatus geometry, microscopic probe placement or measured trace is reconstructed. The liquid-side reference in this operational diagram is distinct from a claim that Kapitza installed a probe immediately next to the surface.

The measurement logic suggests a modern control sequence: calibrate both [temperature](temperature-split.md) readouts, establish their zero-load difference, increase heater [power](power.md), wait for a [steady state](steady-state.md), and repeat while monitoring the bath. Determine lead losses and the bulk [temperature gradients](temperature-gradient.md) before using $r_B=(T_s-T_l)/q$. These are proposed controls derived from the [energy](energy-split.md) balance, rather than additional reported steps in the 1941 apparatus. A nearby unheated solid also has its own interface: its reading need not equal the liquid's local [temperature](temperature-split.md) exactly. The experiment supports a drop concentrated near the surface, while a microscopic thickness and a universal interfacial law require further evidence.
