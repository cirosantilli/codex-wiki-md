# Cell membrane

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane)

The [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), also called the [plasma membrane](cell-membrane.md), is the selective boundary separating a [biological cell](biological-cell.md)'s [cytoplasm](cytoplasm.md) from its surroundings. Both [prokaryotes](prokaryote.md) and [eukaryotes](eukaryote.md) have a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). Where a [cell wall](cell-wall.md) is present, it is a distinct structure outside the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md); the two names are not synonyms. This separation lets a [biological cell](biological-cell.md) maintain internal conditions while exchanging material through [membrane transport](membrane-transport.md). See [NHGRI: cell membrane](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Cell-Membrane-Plasma-Membrane).

The familiar architecture is a [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md) containing [membrane proteins](membrane-protein.md). In a [phospholipid](phospholipid.md)-based [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md), two [membrane leaflets](membrane-leaflet.md) face opposite ways: [hydrophilic](hydrophilicity.md) heads face the [water](water.md) on each side, while [hydrophobic](hydrophobicity.md) tails face one another in the interior. The heads are therefore exposed both outside and inside the [biological cell](biological-cell.md). This arrangement combines a water-facing surface with a barrier to many water-soluble [solutes](solute.md); the schematic represents organization rather than an atomistic structure. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell: the lipid bilayer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26871/).

Original [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) schematic showing [phospholipids](phospholipid.md), [hydrophilic](hydrophilicity.md) heads, [hydrophobic](hydrophobicity.md) tails, an [ion channel](ion-channel.md), and a [carrier protein](carrier-protein.md). Arrows illustrate possible passage, not measured rates. Shapes, sizes, spacing and numbers of [molecules](molecule.md) are illustrative.

[Selective permeability](selective-permeability.md) means that different substances cross at different rates under comparable conditions. Small [nonpolar](nonpolar-molecule.md) [molecules](molecule.md) can pass through a [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md) by [diffusion](diffusion.md), while [ions](ion.md) encounter a strong barrier without a suitable route. An [ion channel](ion-channel.md) supplies a selective water-filled pathway when open; a [carrier protein](carrier-protein.md) binds a [solute](solute.md) and changes shape to expose it to the other side. These [membrane proteins](membrane-protein.md) therefore add specific routes to the barrier. An open [ion channel](ion-channel.md) permits [passive transport](passive-transport.md), whereas a [carrier protein](carrier-protein.md) can mediate either [passive transport](passive-transport.md) or [active transport](active-transport.md). See [Alberts et al.: principles of membrane transport](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26815/).

The useful combination is retention plus controlled exchange: a completely impermeable boundary would prevent uptake, whereas unrestricted mixing would erase differences in [concentration](concentration.md) and [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md). The [fluid mosaic model](fluid-mosaic-model.md) connects these functions to an organized but mobile [lipid bilayer](lipid-bilayer.md) containing [proteins](protein.md).
