# Cell membrane

↑ **Parent:** [Biological cell](biological-cell.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane)

The [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), also called the [plasma membrane](cell-membrane.md), is the selective boundary separating a [biological cell](biological-cell.md)'s [cytoplasm](biological-cell.md#cytoplasm) from its surroundings. Both [prokaryotes](organism.md#prokaryote) and [eukaryotes](organism.md#eukaryote) have a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). Where a [cell wall](biological-cell.md#cell-wall) is present, it is a distinct structure outside the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md); the two names are not synonyms. This separation lets a [biological cell](biological-cell.md) maintain internal conditions while exchanging material through [membrane transport](#membrane-transport). See [NHGRI: cell membrane](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Cell-Membrane-Plasma-Membrane).

The familiar architecture is a [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) containing [membrane proteins](#membrane-protein). In a [phospholipid](chemical-substance.md#phospholipid)-based [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer), two [membrane leaflets](mathematical-biology.md#membrane-leaflet) face opposite ways: [hydrophilic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophilicity) heads face the [water](chemical-substance.md#water) on each side, while [hydrophobic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobicity) tails face one another in the interior. The heads are therefore exposed both outside and inside the [biological cell](biological-cell.md). This arrangement combines a water-facing surface with a barrier to many water-soluble [solutes](mixture.md#solute); the schematic represents organization rather than an atomistic structure. See [Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell: the lipid bilayer](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26871/).

![](cell-membrane-selective-barrier.png)

**[Figure 1](#image-two-membrane-leaflets-and-selective-routes-through-a-cell-membrane). Two membrane leaflets and selective routes through a cell membrane**. Original [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) schematic showing [phospholipids](chemical-substance.md#phospholipid), [hydrophilic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophilicity) heads, [hydrophobic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobicity) tails, an [ion channel](#ion-channel), and a [carrier protein](#carrier-protein). Arrows illustrate possible passage, not measured rates. Shapes, sizes, spacing and numbers of [molecules](molecule.md) are illustrative.

[Selective permeability](#selective-permeability) means that different substances cross at different rates under comparable conditions. Small [nonpolar](physical-chemistry.md#nonpolar-molecule) [molecules](molecule.md) can pass through a [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) by [diffusion](thermodynamics.md#diffusion), while [ions](chemistry.md#ion) encounter a strong barrier without a suitable route. An [ion channel](#ion-channel) supplies a selective water-filled pathway when open; a [carrier protein](#carrier-protein) binds a [solute](mixture.md#solute) and changes shape to expose it to the other side. These [membrane proteins](#membrane-protein) therefore add specific routes to the barrier. An open [ion channel](#ion-channel) permits [passive transport](#passive-transport), whereas a [carrier protein](#carrier-protein) can mediate either [passive transport](#passive-transport) or [active transport](#active-transport). See [Alberts et al.: principles of membrane transport](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26815/).

The useful combination is retention plus controlled exchange: a completely impermeable boundary would prevent uptake, whereas unrestricted mixing would erase differences in [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) and [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md). The [fluid mosaic model](#fluid-mosaic-model) connects these functions to an organized but mobile [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) containing [proteins](protein.md).

## GLUT1

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

[GLUT1](#glut1) is a facilitative glucose transporter expressed in many tissues. Human beta cells can rely predominantly on GLUT1 rather than sharing the high GLUT2 abundance of rodent beta cells.

## GLUT2

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

[GLUT2](#glut2) is a facilitative glucose transporter with important roles in hepatocytes and in the classical rodent beta-cell model. It supports transport down a concentration gradient and should not be assumed to be the predominant human beta-cell glucose transporter.

## Lipid divide

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

The [lipid divide](#lipid-divide) is the contrast between characteristic archaeal membrane lipids and characteristic bacterial/eukaryotic membrane lipids, including glycerol stereochemistry and ether versus ester linkages. It constrains hypotheses for eukaryotic origins without uniquely determining the sequence of ancestral membrane changes.

## Membrane fission

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

[Membrane fission](#membrane-fission) separates a connected membrane into distinct components, for example by severing a vesicle neck. Bending a membrane into a bud without severing that neck is not yet fission.

## Membrane fusion

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_fusion)

[Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion) joins initially separate lipid bilayers, changing connections between membrane compartments. It requires controlled rearrangement of both leaflets while avoiding uncontrolled leakage.

### Vesicle docking

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion)

### Content mixing assay

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion)

A measurement of exchange between previously separate enclosed aqueous compartments. Leakage into the surrounding medium is a different event and needs a separate control.

### Lipid mixing assay

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion)

A readout of lipid redistribution between membrane populations, for example through [fluorescence dequenching](fluorescence.md#fluorescence-dequenching). It must be distinguished from aqueous [content mixing](#content-mixing-assay) and leakage.

### Hemifusion

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion)

[Membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion) intermediate in which contacting membrane leaflets have merged while the aqueous compartments remain separate. A [lipid mixing assay](#lipid-mixing-assay) therefore need not establish full [membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion).

## Membrane curvature

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

Membrane shape affects the energetic cost and geometry of a bud, neck or protrusion. Proteins, lipid composition and mechanical forces can change curvature; curvature alone does not perform the topological reconnection required for [membrane fusion](#membrane-fusion) or [membrane fission](#membrane-fission).

## Homeoviscous adaptation

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

[homeoviscous adaptation](#homeoviscous-adaptation) changes membrane composition in response to conditions such as growth [temperature](temperature.md), helping preserve useful membrane dynamics. The original [Escherichia coli](organism.md#escherichia-coli) study measured temperature-dependent fatty-acid changes and membrane-lipid viscosity by spin labeling.[pp. 522–525, temperature-dependent composition and viscosity measurements](biology-bibliography.md#homeoviscous-adaptation-a-homeostatic-process-that-regulates-the-viscosity-of-membrane-lipids-in-escherichia-coli-by-michael-sinensky)

## Membrane nanodomain

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

A [membrane nanodomain](#membrane-nanodomain) is a small region with locally differing composition or molecular organization. Its size, lifetime and physical origin require measurement; detergent resistance alone does not demonstrate a pre-existing domain in a living membrane.

## Selective permeability

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)

[Selective permeability](#selective-permeability) means that a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) permits different substances to pass at different rates under comparable conditions. Both the [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) and the available [membrane proteins](#membrane-protein) contribute to this selection.

Artificial boundaries can also have [selective permeability](#selective-permeability). For [osmosis](#osmosis), specify which [solutes](mixture.md#solute) cross and over what observation interval: a boundary that retains one [solute](mixture.md#solute) need not retain another. A [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane) is the ideal solvent-only boundary used in the [osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure) calculation.

### Membrane permeability

↑ **Parent:** [Selective permeability](#selective-permeability)

[membrane permeability](#membrane-permeability) describes passage of a specified substance through a membrane under stated conditions. It is distinct from lateral [diffusion](thermodynamics.md#diffusion) within the membrane and from the mechanical force needed to rupture the membrane.

### Semipermeable membrane

↑ **Parent:** [Selective permeability](#selective-permeability)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semipermeable_membrane)

A [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane) ideally passes the [solvent](mixture.md#solvent) while excluding the specified [solutes](mixture.md#solute). In the aqueous [osmosis](#osmosis) model below, [water](chemical-substance.md#water) can cross but the counted dissolved particles cannot. Real [selective permeability](#selective-permeability) is often a matter of different rates, so this idealization needs checking over the measurement interval. Neither an impermeable sheet nor a freely mixing opening realizes this model.[Gold Book, osmotic pressure: definition of the solvent-only membrane](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/osmotic-pressure)

## Membrane protein

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_protein)

A [membrane protein](#membrane-protein) is a [protein](protein.md) associated with a biological membrane. Its association, orientation and mobility are separate properties: being embedded does not imply spanning, and spanning does not imply moving freely. [Membrane proteins](#membrane-protein) supply routes for [membrane transport](#membrane-transport) and recognition for [signal transduction](signal-transduction.md).[Cooper, The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition, Cell Membranes, opening paragraph and Membrane Proteins](biology-bibliography.md#the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/cell-membranes)

An [ion channel](#ion-channel) supplies a pathway for [ions](chemistry.md#ion); a [carrier protein](#carrier-protein) changes which side can reach a bound [solute](mixture.md#solute); a [cell-surface receptor](protein.md#cell-surface-receptor) connects an external [ligand](protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) to intracellular [signal transduction](signal-transduction.md). These examples distinguish passage from communication: a signal can cross the boundary as a change of [protein](protein.md) activity without the [ligand](protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) itself crossing.[Alberts and others, Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition, Membrane Proteins Can Be Associated with the Lipid Bilayer in Various Ways: association reflects function](biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/membrane-proteins)

[Membrane topology](#membrane-topology) tells us which parts can meet a given [ligand](protein.md#ligand-biochemistry); [lateral mobility of membrane proteins](#lateral-mobility-of-membrane-proteins) tells us whether two parts can approach each other along the boundary. ### Ferroportin

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

An iron-export [protein](protein.md). IRE-containing transcripts can be regulated at [biological translation](molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology), while protein-level and systemic regulation provide additional controls.

### Transferrin receptor 1

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

A receptor supporting uptake of transferrin-bound [iron](chemical-substance.md#iron). [RNA](polymer.md#ribonucleic-acid) regulation involving its 3′ region can increase transcript stability during [iron](chemical-substance.md#iron) shortage.

### OmpX

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

A bacterial outer-membrane [protein](protein.md) studied by [NMR](nuclear-magnetic-resonance.md) in optimized lipid [nanodiscs](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer-nanodisc).[Nanodisc structure determination](biology-bibliography.md#optimized-phospholipid-bilayer-nanodiscs-facilitate-high-resolution-structure-determination-of-membrane-proteins-by-franz-hagn-and-others)

### Membrane mimic

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

An experimental environment designed to shield hydrophobic [protein](protein.md) surfaces while retaining relevant [membrane](cell-membrane.md) properties. Detergent [micelles](physical-chemistry.md#micelle), [bicelles](mathematical-biology.md#bicelle) and [lipid bilayer nanodiscs](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer-nanodisc) have different limitations.

### Nephrin

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

A cell-surface [protein](protein.md) whose phosphorylated intracellular region can recruit signalling adaptors.

### Cargo receptor

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

A [cargo receptor](#cargo-receptor) links a transported component to sorting machinery. An ER export receptor can bind a soluble lumenal cargo while exposing a cytoplasmic recognition site to the coat.

### Integrin

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrin)

An [integrin](#integrin) is a transmembrane adhesion receptor linking extracellular ligands to intracellular signalling and [cytoskeleton](biological-cell.md#cytoskeleton) interactions. Integrin-dependent contacts transmit force in many forms of [cell migration](biological-cell.md#cell-migration); their necessity depends on the physical environment.

### Hydrophobic mismatch

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

[hydrophobic mismatch](#hydrophobic-mismatch) is an energetic incompatibility between a membrane protein's hydrophobic span and the hydrophobic thickness of its surrounding [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). Local deformation, altered conformation or changed association can partly accommodate it.

### Integral membrane protein

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_membrane_protein)

An [integral membrane protein](#integral-membrane-protein) is firmly incorporated into a membrane, commonly through contact with the [hydrophobic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobicity) interior of the [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). Extraction behavior is an operational distinction: it resists treatments that remove many [peripheral membrane proteins](#peripheral-membrane-protein). [Transmembrane proteins](#transmembrane-protein) are one class; integral does not mean spanning.[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Membrane Proteins Can Be Associated with the Lipid Bilayer in Various Ways, Figure 10-17 and extraction-based distinction](biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/membrane-proteins)

#### Transmembrane protein

↑ **Parent:** [Integral membrane protein](#integral-membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmembrane_protein)

A [transmembrane protein](#transmembrane-protein) has at least one [polypeptide chain](protein.md#polypeptide) crossing the complete [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). One crossing is single-pass; several crossings in one chain are multipass. [Hydrophobic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobicity) regions face the membrane interior, while [hydrophilic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophilicity) regions can face [water](chemical-substance.md#water).[The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition by Geoffrey M. Cooper; Cell Membranes, Membrane Proteins: spanning regions, single and multiple crossings](biology-bibliography.md#the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/cell-membranes)

##### Tail-anchored protein

↑ **Parent:** [Transmembrane protein](#transmembrane-protein)

A [tail-anchored protein](#tail-anchored-protein) has a membrane-spanning segment near its [C-terminus](protein.md#c-terminus); many reach the [endoplasmic reticulum](biological-cell.md#endoplasmic-reticulum) after [biological translation](molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology).

### Peripheral membrane protein

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_membrane_protein)

A [peripheral membrane protein](#peripheral-membrane-protein) associates with a membrane face through noncovalent interactions, without spanning its [hydrophobic](physical-chemistry.md#hydrophobicity) interior. Association with another [membrane protein](#membrane-protein) can suffice; peripheral describes attachment, not an unimportant function.[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Membrane Proteins Can Be Associated with the Lipid Bilayer in Various Ways: noncovalent attachment and peripheral proteins](biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/membrane-proteins)

### Lipid-anchored protein

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid-anchored_protein)

A [lipid-anchored protein](#lipid-anchored-protein) attaches through a covalently linked [lipid](chemical-substance.md#lipid). The anchor inserts into the [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer); the [polypeptide chain](protein.md#polypeptide) need not cross it. This is a description of attachment rather than a synonym for [transmembrane protein](#transmembrane-protein).[The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition by Geoffrey M. Cooper; Cell Membranes, Membrane Proteins: covalently attached lipid anchors on either membrane face](biology-bibliography.md#the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/cell-membranes)

### Membrane topology

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_topology)

[Membrane topology](#membrane-topology) specifies the sidedness of a membrane-associated [polypeptide chain](protein.md#polypeptide): where its [N-terminus](protein.md#n-terminus), [C-terminus](protein.md#c-terminus) and intervening [protein domains](protein.md#protein-domain) lie relative to the [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). Side-specific placement is essential to interpreting a [cell-surface receptor](protein.md#cell-surface-receptor).[Molecular Biology of the Cell, 4th edition by Bruce Alberts and others; Membrane Proteins Can Be Associated with the Lipid Bilayer in Various Ways: aqueous domains on either side and receptor orientation](biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/membrane-proteins)

For an original worked example, stipulate a continuous [polypeptide chain](protein.md#polypeptide) with its [N-terminus](protein.md#n-terminus) outside a [biological cell](biological-cell.md), exactly three complete crossings of the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), and no additional excursions into the [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer). Its segments lie, in order, outside, inside, outside, inside. A [protein domain](protein.md#protein-domain) between the first and second crossings faces the [cytosol](biological-cell.md#cytosol); one between the second and third faces the exterior; the [C-terminus](protein.md#c-terminus) faces the [cytosol](biological-cell.md#cytosol). Counting crossings determines these placements without specifying an atomic [protein structure](protein.md#protein-structure).

The proof is a side-switching argument. Assign exterior the sign $+1$ and interior $-1$. Each complete crossing multiplies the sign by $-1$, so after $m$ crossings,

$$
s_m=(-1)^m s_0.
$$

An odd number puts the termini on opposite sides, an even number on the same side. This assumes complete crossings of one boundary; a chain that enters and returns to the same side without crossing must not be counted as a complete crossing. Nor does the rule determine which side the [N-terminus](protein.md#n-terminus) starts on.

As a constructed accessibility test, put an [epitope](protein.md#epitope) on the outside segment between crossings two and three, and another on the inside segment between crossings one and two. Stipulate an intact [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) that excludes the test [antibody](protein.md#antibody) and accessible [epitopes](protein.md#epitope) that bind it. Applying the [antibody](protein.md#antibody) outside can label only the first [epitope](protein.md#epitope). If both become accessible after opening the boundary, the difference tests sidedness under those assumptions. Failure to label an [epitope](protein.md#epitope) alone is insufficient: it could also be inaccessible because of [protein folding](protein.md#protein-folding) or poor [antibody affinity](protein.md#antibody-affinity). This is a proposed reasoning exercise, separate from the historical mixing experiment below.

### Lateral mobility of membrane proteins

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane protein](#membrane-protein)

[Lateral mobility of membrane proteins](#lateral-mobility-of-membrane-proteins) is movement along a membrane surface. It differs from [membrane transport](#membrane-transport) through the boundary and from reversing [membrane topology](#membrane-topology). Attachment, crowding and barriers can restrict [diffusion](thermodynamics.md#diffusion); the [fluid mosaic model](#fluid-mosaic-model) does not require every [membrane protein](#membrane-protein) to wander freely.[Cooper, The Cell: A Molecular Approach, 2nd edition, Structure of the Plasma Membrane, Mobility of Membrane Proteins: lateral movement and restrictions](biology-bibliography.md#the-cell-a-molecular-approach-2nd-edition-by-geoffrey-m-cooper/structure-of-the-plasma-membrane)

An original local model treats a small, approximately flat patch as an unbounded plane with uniform [diffusion coefficient](brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient) $D$, no drift and no trapping. Represent the two displacement coordinates by independent [Brownian motions](brownian-motion.md), each with zero [expected value](expected-value.md) and [variance](variance.md) $2Dt$. Since $r^2=x^2+y^2$, addition of the two coordinate expectations gives

$$
\langle r^2\rangle=4Dt,\qquad r_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{4Dt}.
$$

This is a root-mean-square displacement, not distance traveled along a jagged trajectory or the displacement of every [protein](protein.md). For a stipulated $D=0.08\,\mu\mathrm{m}^2\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$, $\langle r^2\rangle=1.6\,\mu\mathrm{m}^2$ and $r_{\mathrm{rms}}\approx1.265\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. Reaching a stipulated root-mean-square scale $L=2\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ gives $t=L^2/(4D)=12.5\,\mathrm{s}$. These model values are not measured [diffusion coefficients](brownian-motion.md#diffusion-coefficient) or mixing times from the historical experiment.

Multiplying the target displacement scale by two multiplies the model time by four. This quadratic scaling is a useful way to see why local encounters and redistribution over an entire [biological cell](biological-cell.md) need not happen on the same timescale. A curved, bounded [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), restricted paths and different mobile populations require more than this flat-patch model; disappearance of an initial pattern alone does not determine $D$.

#### Mouse-human heterokaryon membrane-mixing experiment

↑ **Parent:** [Lateral mobility of membrane proteins](#lateral-mobility-of-membrane-proteins)

In 1970 [Larry Frye](biology.md#larry-frye) and [Michael Edidin](biology.md#michael-edidin) fused mouse C11D and human VA-2 [cell lines](biological-cell.md#cell-line) with [Sendai virus](biology.md#sendai-virus), forming [heterokaryons](biological-cell.md#heterokaryon). Suspensions were maintained in a shaking water bath at $37\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Removed aliquots were stained by indirect [immunofluorescence](fluorescence.md#immunofluorescence): [antibodies](protein.md#antibody) against parental surface [antigens](protein.md#antigen), then differently fluorescent secondary [antibodies](protein.md#antibody). A Leitz Ortholux [fluorescence microscope](fluorescence.md#fluorescence-microscope) used a mercury lamp and color-selecting filters. Green mouse and red human surface patterns became intermixed.[Frye and Edidin, The rapid intermixing of cell surface antigens after formation of mouse-human heterokaryons, pp. 319–325: Summary, Cell fusion, Fluorescent staining, Fluorescence microscopy, Table 1, time-course results](biology-bibliography.md#the-rapid-intermixing-of-cell-surface-antigens-after-formation-of-mouse-human-heterokaryons-by-larry-frye-and-michael-edidin)

Same-species fusions tested [antibody specificity](protein.md#antibody-specificity); stable hybrid lines supplied a two-color control. Inhibitors of [biological translation](molecular-genetics.md#translation-biology) did not prevent mixing; lowering [temperature](temperature.md) inhibited it. These observations supported lateral redistribution over rapid synthesis. The authors could not conclusively exclude passage through [cytoplasm](biological-cell.md#cytoplasm) and return. The readout compared populations, not trajectories of a continuously observed individual [biological cell](biological-cell.md), and did not establish unrestricted motion of every [membrane protein](#membrane-protein).[pp. 324–331: specificity controls, inhibitor studies, temperature dependence and Discussion of mechanisms iii and iv](biology-bibliography.md#the-rapid-intermixing-of-cell-surface-antigens-after-formation-of-mouse-human-heterokaryons-by-larry-frye-and-michael-edidin)

## Ion channel

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel)

An [ion channel](#ion-channel) is a [membrane protein](#membrane-protein) that provides a selective pathway for [ions](chemistry.md#ion) across a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). An open pore permits [passive transport](#passive-transport) down the relevant [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient); it does not supply the [free energy](thermodynamic-potential.md#thermodynamic-free-energy) for uphill transport as a pump involved in [active transport](#active-transport) does. Two things are needed for a net [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current): an available pathway and an [electrochemical potential](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-potential) difference. Opening a pore alone need not produce a net [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current). See [Ion Channels and the Electrical Properties of Membranes](biology-bibliography.md#molecular-biology-of-the-cell-4th-edition-by-bruce-alberts-and-others/ion-channels-and-the-electrical-properties-of-membranes).

The [selective permeability](#selective-permeability) of an [ion channel](#ion-channel) depends on interactions between its pore and the passing [ions](chemistry.md#ion), not simply on a hole's diameter. Some [ion channels](#ion-channel) strongly favor [potassium](chemical-substance.md#potassium) over [sodium](chemical-substance.md#sodium); others admit several kinds of positively charged [ions](chemistry.md#ion). Thus selective does not necessarily mean exclusive to one [ion](chemistry.md#ion) species. See [Purves et al., Neuroscience, 2nd edition (2001): molecular structure of ion channels](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11123/) and the mixed-channel example in [Purves et al.: principles derived from studies of the neuromuscular junction](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11013/).

<a id="image-an-open-ion-channel-supplies-a-selective-passive-pathway"></a>
![](ion-channel-selective-passage.png)

**[Figure 4](#image-an-open-ion-channel-supplies-a-selective-passive-pathway). An open ion channel supplies a selective passive pathway**. Original model schematic: selected positive [ions](chemistry.md#ion) can traverse a pore in a [membrane protein](#membrane-protein), while the differently shaped particles are excluded from this modeled pore. The arrow shows one possible net direction when the corresponding [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) favors entry; the pore does not enforce that direction. Shapes, particle counts and dimensions are illustrative, not physical microscopic data or a universal selectivity rule.

Gating is the opening and closing of an [ion channel](#ion-channel)'s pathway. A [voltage-gated ion channel](membrane-potential.md#voltage-gated-ion-channel) responds to [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md); an [ionotropic receptor](protein.md#ionotropic-receptor) can respond to a bound [ligand](protein.md#ligand-biochemistry). Gating changes the available [electrical conductance](electric-charge.md#electrical-conductance), while the [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) supplies the drive for [passive transport](#passive-transport). For a fixed [reversal potential](membrane-potential.md#reversal-potential) in the simple model below, opening more identical [ion channels](#ion-channel) increases the magnitude of the [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current) at a fixed nonzero drive, without choosing its sign independently of [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md). The [two-state ion-channel gating](membrane-potential.md#two-state-ion-channel-gating) model describes how the open fraction can change. See the Alberts reference above.

For a worked approximation, hold an open population's [electrical conductance](electric-charge.md#electrical-conductance) at $g=10\,\mathrm{nS}$ and its [reversal potential](membrane-potential.md#reversal-potential) at $E_{\mathrm{rev}}=-80\,\mathrm{mV}$. Use $V=V_{\mathrm{inside}}-V_{\mathrm{outside}}$ for [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) and **inward-positive [conventional current](electric-charge.md#conventional-current)**:

$$
I_{\mathrm{in}}=g(E_{\mathrm{rev}}-V),\qquad g\geq0.
$$

At $V=-100,-80,-60\,\mathrm{mV}$, respectively, the [electric currents](electric-charge.md#electric-current) are $+200,0,-200\,\mathrm{pA}$, since $1\,\mathrm{nS}\times1\,\mathrm{mV}=1\,\mathrm{pA}$. Positive [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current) means net positive [electric charge](electric-charge.md) entering, or net negative [electric charge](electric-charge.md) leaving; it does not mean that every [ion](chemistry.md#ion) moves inward. For a pore passing only positive [ions](chemistry.md#ion), the three results mean net inward passage, no net passage, and net outward passage. These stipulated values describe an illustrative Ohmic approximation, not measured properties of a particular [ion channel](#ion-channel); see [reversal potential](membrane-potential.md#reversal-potential) for the current plot and limitations.

The useful connection is between a molecular gate and a controllable [electric current](electric-charge.md#electric-current). A [ligand](protein.md#ligand-biochemistry) can change [electrical conductance](electric-charge.md#electrical-conductance) through an [ionotropic receptor](protein.md#ionotropic-receptor), or [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) can change it through a [voltage-gated ion channel](membrane-potential.md#voltage-gated-ion-channel), connecting recognition and electrical signaling. In a historical landmark, Erwin Neher and Bert Sakmann reported currents from individual [ion channels](#ion-channel) in frog muscle membranes in 1976. See [Neher and Sakmann, Single-channel currents recorded from membrane of denervated frog muscle fibres, Nature 260, 799–802 (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1038/260799a0).

## Membrane transport

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_transport)

[Membrane transport](#membrane-transport) is the passage of substances across a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). For the small [solutes](mixture.md#solute) considered here, distinguish the available route from its driving force: a [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer), [ion channel](#ion-channel), or [carrier protein](#carrier-protein) determines how passage can occur, while differences in [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) and [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) determine which direction is energetically downhill. A large driving force cannot make an unavailable route conduct. See [Alberts et al.: principles of membrane transport](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26815/).

For an ideal dilute [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) at uniform [temperature](temperature.md) and [pressure](pressure.md), with the same reference molar [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) on both sides, the molar [electrochemical potential](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-potential) change for transferring a [solute](mixture.md#solute) from compartment A to B is

$$
\Delta\widetilde\mu_{\mathrm{A}\to\mathrm{B}}=RT\ln\!\left(\frac{c_{\mathrm B}}{c_{\mathrm A}}\right)+zF(V_{\mathrm B}-V_{\mathrm A}).
$$

Here $c_{\mathrm A},c_{\mathrm B}$ are [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration), $T$ is absolute [temperature](temperature.md), $R$ is the [molar gas constant](ideal-gas.md#molar-gas-constant), $z$ is signed [ion](chemistry.md#ion) charge number, $F$ is the [Faraday constant](physics.md#faraday-constant), and $V_{\mathrm B}-V_{\mathrm A}$ is the [electric potential](electric-field.md#electric-potential) difference. The [natural logarithm](calculus.md#natural-logarithm) contains a dimensionless ratio. The electrical term vanishes for an uncharged [solute](mixture.md#solute). More generally, use [thermodynamic activities](chemical-potential.md#thermodynamic-activity) in place of the ideal [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) ratio. This combines the ideal-solution [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) difference with electrical [energy](energy.md) per mole; see [IUPAC Gold Book: electrochemical potential](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/E01945). For a [biological cell](biological-cell.md), taking B inside and A outside makes the voltage term use the existing [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) convention.

### Passive transport

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane transport](#membrane-transport)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_transport)

[Passive transport](#passive-transport) is net movement down the transported substance's [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) without coupling that movement to another source of [free energy](thermodynamic-potential.md#thermodynamic-free-energy). [Facilitated diffusion](#facilitated-diffusion) is [passive transport](#passive-transport) through an [ion channel](#ion-channel) or a passive [carrier protein](#carrier-protein). Providing such a route can speed passage, but does not turn an uphill transfer into a downhill one. For an uncharged [solute](mixture.md#solute) under the ideal conditions above, net [passive transport](#passive-transport) runs from higher to lower [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration); for an [ion](chemistry.md#ion), the [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) also matters. See [Cooper, The Cell: transport of small molecules](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9847/).

In the figure, stipulate eight neutral [solute](mixture.md#solute) tracers in A and two in B, with equal fixed [volumes](geometry-and-topology.md#volume), identical surroundings, and no imposed [electric potential](electric-field.md#electric-potential) difference. The [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) ratio is $c_{\mathrm A}/c_{\mathrm B}=8/2=4$, so A-to-B transfer has $\Delta\widetilde\mu=-RT\ln4\simeq-1.38629RT$, or $-3.458\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ at the assumed [temperature](temperature.md) $T=300\,\mathrm K$. If this [solute](mixture.md#solute) can cross, [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium) has equal expected [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration): five tracers per side on average. Individual crossings occur in both directions; an observed ten-particle snapshot need not contain exactly five on each side. These stipulated counts illustrate a driving force, not a measured flow rate or a prediction of the time to equilibrate.

For a separate illustrative [ion](chemistry.md#ion) calculation, keep $c_{\mathrm A}/c_{\mathrm B}=4$, but assign $z=+1$ and $V_{\mathrm A}-V_{\mathrm B}=-60\,\mathrm{mV}$ at $300\,\mathrm K$. Using $R\simeq8.31446\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $F\simeq96485.33\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}$, B-to-A transfer gives $RT\ln4-0.060F\simeq3.458-5.789=-2.331\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Thus an appropriate open [ion channel](#ion-channel) can permit [passive transport](#passive-transport) toward the higher [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration): the electrical contribution outweighs the opposing [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) contribution. This stipulated [membrane potential](membrane-potential.md) is not inferred from the tracer counts.

#### Osmosis

↑ **Parent:** [Passive transport](#passive-transport)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmosis)

[Osmosis](#osmosis) is net movement of [water](chemical-substance.md#water) across a boundary whose permeability differs for [water](chemical-substance.md#water) and relevant [solutes](mixture.md#solute). The direction depends on the [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) of [water](chemical-substance.md#water), including both dissolved material and [pressure](pressure.md). A [cell wall](biological-cell.md#cell-wall) can let [water](chemical-substance.md#water) reach the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), yet oppose expansion through [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure). Zero net movement does not mean individual [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecules](molecule.md) have stopped crossing.

Imagine pure [water](chemical-substance.md#water) on side A and a [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) on side B of a [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane). At equal [temperature](temperature.md) and [pressure](pressure.md), dissolved particles lower the [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) of [water](chemical-substance.md#water) in B, and net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) transfer initially favors A to B. Retaining the [solutes](mixture.md#solute) lets [water](chemical-substance.md#water) redistribute without simply mixing everything. Increasing B's [pressure](pressure.md) opposes entry; enough [pressure](pressure.md) gives [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium) with unequal [solute](mixture.md#solute) [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration). This is why “[water](chemical-substance.md#water) moves toward more [solute](mixture.md#solute)” requires a pressure condition.[§5.2, Osmosis and Mechanism: selective passage and pressure opposing entry](biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/5-2-passive-transport)

At [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium), the [chemical potentials](chemical-potential.md) of exchangeable [water](chemical-substance.md#water) are equal. Individual [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecules](molecule.md) still move and cross in both directions, with equal mean opposing transfer rates and zero net [flux](mathematical-physics.md#flux). A stationary liquid level alone does not prove this: a blocked route can also give no visible transfer. [Selective permeability](#selective-permeability) provides a route; [water potential](#water-potential) supplies the direction, and neither alone determines the speed. The [cell wall](biological-cell.md#cell-wall), [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure) and [plasmolysis](biological-cell.md#plasmolysis) articles show why a living boundary's mechanical support and its [selective permeability](#selective-permeability) must be distinguished.

##### Water potential

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_potential)

[Water potential](#water-potential) expresses the [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) of [water](chemical-substance.md#water) relative to a stated pure-[water](chemical-substance.md#water) reference in units of [pressure](pressure.md). For a nearly incompressible liquid at fixed [temperature](temperature.md), use a constant reference [molar volume](thermodynamics.md#molar-volume) $\bar V_w$ and write

$$
\Psi=\frac{\mu_w-\mu_w^{\mathrm{ref}}}{\bar V_w}.
$$

The reference is pure [water](chemical-substance.md#water) at the same [temperature](temperature.md) and a chosen [pressure](pressure.md) $P_0$. Both sides must use the same reference. Neglecting height differences and interactions with solid surfaces, the approximation is

$$
\mu_w\simeq\mu_w^{\mathrm{ref}}+RT\ln a_w+\bar V_w(P-P_0),\qquad
\Psi\simeq(P-P_0)+\frac{RT}{\bar V_w}\ln a_w=\Psi_p+\Psi_s.
$$

Here $a_w$ is the dimensionless [thermodynamic activity](chemical-potential.md#thermodynamic-activity) of [water](chemical-substance.md#water), $R$ the [molar gas constant](ideal-gas.md#molar-gas-constant), and $T$ the absolute [temperature](temperature.md). The [natural logarithm](calculus.md#natural-logarithm) is therefore applied to a dimensionless quantity. The [pressure](pressure.md) contribution $\Psi_p=P-P_0$ increases with [pressure](pressure.md); the [solute potential](#solute-potential) $\Psi_s$ is negative when $a_w<1$. Across an available passive water route, net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) transfer favors higher to lower [water potential](#water-potential). The familiar [plant](organism.md#plant) description adds effects of height and interactions with solid surfaces when they matter; they are omitted in the two liquid compartments here.[Biology 2e, §30.5, Water Potential, Solute Potential, Pressure Potential and Figure 30.32: component balance and direction of transfer](biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants)

Adding [solute](mixture.md#solute) and raising [pressure](pressure.md) can thus cancel in [water potential](#water-potential) while each contribution remains nonzero. Equality of [water potential](#water-potential) does not imply equality of [pressure](pressure.md) or of every [solute](mixture.md#solute) [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration). In a supported [plant](organism.md#plant) cell, the [cell wall](biological-cell.md#cell-wall) permits a positive [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure) to balance a negative [solute potential](#solute-potential); [plasmolysis](biological-cell.md#plasmolysis) illustrates the opposite loss of support after [water](chemical-substance.md#water) leaves.

###### Solute potential

↑ **Parent:** [Water potential](#water-potential)

[Solute potential](#solute-potential) is the contribution to [water potential](#water-potential) from dissolved material, with pure [water](chemical-substance.md#water) assigned zero. In the nearly incompressible description, $\Psi_s=(RT/\bar V_w)\ln a_w$. In a dilute ideal [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry), $\Psi_s\simeq-RTc_{\mathrm{osm}}$, where $c_{\mathrm{osm}}$ is the [osmotic concentration](#osmotic-concentration) of dissolved particles. Thus [solute potential](#solute-potential) is negative while the corresponding [osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure) is positive: $\Psi_s\simeq-\Pi$. This sign difference distinguishes a contribution to [water potential](#water-potential) from the compensating mechanical [pressure](pressure.md).[Biology 2e, §30.5, Solute Potential: negative concentration contribution](biology-bibliography.md#biology-2e-by-mary-ann-clark-and-others/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants)

##### Osmotic pressure

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmotic_pressure)

[Osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure) is the excess [pressure](pressure.md) needed on a [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) to maintain osmotic [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium) with pure [solvent](mixture.md#solvent) across a [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane). For a nearly incompressible [solvent](mixture.md#solvent), equality of [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) gives $\Pi\simeq-(RT/\bar V_w)\ln a_w$. It is an equilibrium property; an observed initial swelling rate is a different measurement.[Gold Book, osmotic pressure: equilibrium definition, solvent activity formula and dilute ideal limit](chemistry-bibliography.md#iupac-compendium-of-chemical-terminology-fifth-edition-gold-book-by-international-union-of-pure-and-applied-chemistry/osmotic-pressure)

For a dilute ideal [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) of retained particles,

$$
\Pi=\frac{nRT}{V}=RTc_{\mathrm{osm}}.
$$

Here $n$ is the [amount of substance](physics.md#amount-of-substance) of independently dissolved [solute](mixture.md#solute) particles, measured in moles; $V$ is the final [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry), not the starting [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of pure [solvent](mixture.md#solvent). Count the resulting [ions](chemistry.md#ion) separately if a [solute](mixture.md#solute) dissociates; do not multiply twice if $n$ already counts them. With $c_{\mathrm{osm}}$ in $\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}$ and $R$ in $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, $RTc_{\mathrm{osm}}$ has units $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}=\mathrm{Pa}$. This resembles the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas.md#ideal-gas-law), although the particles here are dissolved in a liquid.

The assumptions are uniform [temperature](temperature.md), a dilute ideal [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry), a nearly incompressible [solvent](mixture.md#solvent), and a [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane) that retains the counted [solutes](mixture.md#solute) while passing [solvent](mixture.md#solvent). Association, incomplete dissociation and interactions change the relation between added material and independent particles; nonideal [thermodynamic activities](chemical-potential.md#thermodynamic-activity) invalidate simple particle counting. The formula does not describe a concentrated brine merely because the apparatus contains a membrane.

A short derivation explains why particles are counted. In an ideal mixture, $a_w=x_w=n_w/(n_w+n)$, with $n_w$ the [amount of substance](physics.md#amount-of-substance) of [water](chemical-substance.md#water). If $n\ll n_w$, then $-\ln x_w=\ln(1+n/n_w)\simeq n/n_w$ and $V\simeq n_w\bar V_w$. Substitution gives $\Pi\simeq RTn/V$. This is a dilute-limit approximation, not a claim that [water](chemical-substance.md#water) [molecules](molecule.md) stop moving or that dissolved particles are a gas.

###### Osmometry

↑ **Parent:** [Osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure)

Measure the [pressure](pressure.md) difference across a solvent-permeable barrier that retains the solute, at known [temperature](temperature.md). For dilute [polymers](polymer.md), the zero-concentration intercept of reduced [pressure](pressure.md) determines the [number-average molar mass](chemistry.md#number-average-molar-mass). Leakage, aggregation and ionic contributions can change the inferred molecular population.

###### Reduced osmotic-pressure intercept

↑ **Parent:** [Osmometry](#osmometry)

For [polymer](polymer.md) [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass) [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) $c$, the leading ideal chain [pressure](pressure.md) is $RTc/M_n$. Plotting $\Pi/c$ against $c$ and extrapolating removes the leading virial correction. The intercept has units of energy per [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass); without the measurement [temperature](temperature.md) it determines $M_n/T$, not an absolute molar [mass](classical-mechanics.md#mass).

###### Osmosis concentration-pressure example

↑ **Parent:** [Osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure)

Take two reservoirs separated by a [semipermeable membrane](#semipermeable-membrane) that passes [water](chemical-substance.md#water) and retains one neutral [solute](mixture.md#solute). Stipulate $T=300\,\mathrm K$, $c_A=10\,\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}$ and $c_B=30\,\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}$, equivalent to $10$ and $30\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$. Treat both [chemical solutions](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) as dilute and ideal. These are invented model conditions, not measurements or recommended biological conditions.

For [pressures](pressure.md) measured at the same height beside the membrane, define $\Delta P=P_B-P_A$. The model predicts

$$
\Psi_A-\Psi_B=RT(c_B-c_A)-\Delta P,\qquad
RT(c_B-c_A)\simeq49.89\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

At $\Delta P=0$, net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) transfer favors A to B. At $\Delta P\simeq49.89\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the opposing [pressure](pressure.md) balances the [osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure) difference, giving zero net transfer with unequal [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration). At $\Delta P=75\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the sign reverses and net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) transfer favors B to A, toward the more dilute reservoir. Raising [pressure](pressure.md) changes the total [water potential](#water-potential), rather than changing the membrane into a one-way gate.

In a closed [membrane osmometer](#membrane-osmometer), entering [water](chemical-substance.md#water) causes [dilution](mixture.md#dilution) of the receiving [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) and changes its [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume); the final balance must use final [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration). ##### Membrane osmometer

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_osmometer)

A [membrane osmometer](#membrane-osmometer) connects [osmosis](#osmosis) to a visible liquid level or a measured balancing [pressure](pressure.md). In a teaching version, a [solute](mixture.md#solute)-containing pouch is joined to a narrow vertical tube and submerged in external [water](chemical-substance.md#water). Entry increases the enclosed [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) and raises the meniscus. Both the column and any stretched pouch can contribute an opposing [pressure](pressure.md).

The University of Toronto's [Osmosis (Brine) by University of Toronto Department of Physics](biology-bibliography.md#osmosis-brine-by-university-of-toronto-department-of-physics) demonstration (activity Me-012b, Description, steps 1–14) specifies soaking a dialysis membrane tube, tying one end closed and fastening the other around a thistle tube's narrow end. A stand holds the tube. Colored brine is cooled to room [temperature](temperature.md) and injected using a syringe to reduce bubbles; the pouch is completely immersed in a $1$ or $2\,\mathrm L$ beaker of distilled [water](chemical-substance.md#water). A taped marker records the initial level; subsequent meniscus height is the readout. The protocol proposes another pouch containing distilled [water](chemical-substance.md#water) as a comparison, and its older instructions explicitly check seals for leaks.[Osmosis (Brine), Description steps 1–14, water-filled comparison and Old Description A.2: apparatus, readout and leak check](biology-bibliography.md#osmosis-brine-by-university-of-toronto-department-of-physics)

A rise is consistent with net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) entry. To infer [osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure) quantitatively, use a retained [solute](mixture.md#solute) and establish a stable equilibrium after accounting for [dilution](mixture.md#dilution). If both free surfaces are at the same atmospheric [pressure](pressure.md), the liquids have approximately equal [mass densities](fluid-mechanics.md#density) $\rho$, and the pouch adds negligible mechanical load, the [hydrostatic pressure](fluid-mechanics.md#hydrostatic-pressure) difference at membrane height is approximately $\Delta P=\rho gh$, where $h$ is the meniscus height above the external surface and $g$ is [gravitational acceleration](newton-s-law-of-universal-gravitation.md#gravitational-acceleration). This is the liquid-column inference, not a pressure calibration supplied by the Toronto demonstration. For unequal [mass densities](fluid-mechanics.md#density), compare the two column pressures separately.

Useful additional controls are matched initial levels and geometry, constant [temperature](temperature.md), replicate runs, and monitoring external [solute](mixture.md#solute) to test retention. These are proposed checks, not reported Toronto results. Leaks, trapped bubbles, [surface tension](surface-tension.md) in the narrow tube, [evaporation](critical-phenomenon.md#evaporation) and membrane stretching can alter the level. In particular, a dialysis membrane may pass dissolved salt: the brine setup illustrates transient movement but cannot be assumed to retain every dissolved [ion](chemistry.md#ion). Color improves visibility without proving salt retention. A stationary level can reflect weakening [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) differences or leakage rather than osmotic [thermodynamic equilibrium](thermodynamics.md#thermodynamic-equilibrium); a rising level alone supplies neither $n$ nor a verified value of $nRT/V$.

##### Osmotic concentration

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmotic_concentration)

In the dilute ideal particle-count description, [osmolarity](#osmotic-concentration) is the total [amount of substance](physics.md#amount-of-substance) of independently dissolved [solute](mixture.md#solute) particles per [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry): $c_{\mathrm{osm}}=\sum_j n_j/V$. It includes both membrane-permeating and retained [solutes](mixture.md#solute); specifying a particular membrane is unnecessary to count particles. A neutral undissociated [solute](mixture.md#solute) contributes one particle per [molecule](molecule.md), whereas separated [ions](chemistry.md#ion) are counted individually. Common units are $\mathrm{osmol\,L^{-1}}$ or $\mathrm{mOsm\,L^{-1}}$, with one osmole denoting one mole of the counted particles. [Osmolality](physiology.md#osmolality) uses mass of [solvent](mixture.md#solvent) instead of [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) of [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry); the two numerical values should not be identified without the appropriate mass-to-volume conversion.[Cell Membranes—Structure and Transport, Osmosis: paragraphs beginning Earlier this semester and Osmolarity is a useful term; total particle counting](biology-bibliography.md#osmosis-cell-membranes-structure-and-transport-byu-idaho)

This ideal counting definition states its scope: interactions can require a [thermodynamic activity](chemical-potential.md#thermodynamic-activity) description of [osmotic pressure](#osmotic-pressure). [Tonicity](#tonicity) asks a different question, namely how that surrounding [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) changes a specified [biological cell](biological-cell.md)'s [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) through its particular [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md).

##### Tonicity

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonicity)

[Tonicity](#tonicity) describes how a surrounding [chemical solution](mixture.md#solution-chemistry) changes cellular [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) through [osmosis](#osmosis). It depends on which [solutes](mixture.md#solute) remain effectively excluded on the timescale of interest, not simply on the amount of dissolved material. The following comparisons take a functional [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md), an initially unpressurized [biological cell](biological-cell.md), and effectively excluded [solutes](mixture.md#solute) as the reference; rising [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure) can oppose entry in a walled [biological cell](biological-cell.md).

Operationally, [hypotonic solutions](#hypotonic-solution) cause sustained swelling, [hypertonic solutions](#hypertonic-solution) sustained shrinking, and [isotonic solutions](#isotonic-solution) no sustained [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) change in the specified passive comparison. State the reference [biological cell](biological-cell.md), membrane properties and observation interval. A [permeating solute](#permeating-solute) can transiently change [water potential](#water-potential) while crossing; after its [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) equilibrates, its initial unequal [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) cannot be treated as a permanently retained osmotic load. Thus equal [osmolarities](#osmotic-concentration) need not imply [isotonic solutions](#isotonic-solution). Conversely, a stationary walled [biological cell](biological-cell.md) may have unequal [osmolarities](#osmotic-concentration) balanced by [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure).[Cell Membranes—Structure and Transport, Osmosis: paragraphs beginning Here is another way and final Note; permeating solutes and transient effects](biology-bibliography.md#osmosis-cell-membranes-structure-and-transport-byu-idaho)

###### Permeating solute

↑ **Parent:** [Tonicity](#tonicity)

A [permeating solute](#permeating-solute) can cross the specified membrane appreciably during the observation interval. This is a property of a [solute](mixture.md#solute)-membrane-time comparison, not an immutable label attached to a [molecule](molecule.md). Passage can reduce a [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) difference through [passive transport](#passive-transport) while [water](chemical-substance.md#water) is also moving; it does not require [water](chemical-substance.md#water) to wait.

For an original dilute ideal example, stipulate a flexible model [biological cell](biological-cell.md) with $20\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ retained particles and an external reservoir with $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ retained particles plus $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ freely permeating neutral [solute](mixture.md#solute). Initially the total [osmolarities](#osmotic-concentration) are equal. Suppose the permeating [solute](mixture.md#solute) eventually has equal internal and external [concentrations](mathematical-physics.md#concentration), there is negligible [pressure](pressure.md) difference, no [active transport](#active-transport), and the internal retained particle amount is conserved. The permeating contributions cancel from the final osmotic balance; the internal retained [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) must fall from $20$ to $10\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$. The required [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) is twice its initial value. This illustrates an initially equal-[osmolarity](#osmotic-concentration) but [hypotonic solution](#hypotonic-solution); it assumes the boundary can survive that expansion and predicts no universal time course.

###### Hypotonic solution

↑ **Parent:** [Tonicity](#tonicity)

A [hypotonic solution](#hypotonic-solution) favors initial net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) entry under the reference conditions above. A susceptible [biological cell](biological-cell.md) may undergo [osmotic lysis](#cytolysis); a sufficiently strong [cell wall](biological-cell.md#cell-wall) can instead support [turgor pressure](biological-cell.md#turgor-pressure).

###### Hypertonic solution

↑ **Parent:** [Tonicity](#tonicity)

A [hypertonic solution](#hypertonic-solution) favors initial net [water](chemical-substance.md#water) loss under the reference conditions above. In a walled [plant](organism.md#plant) cell, enough loss can produce [plasmolysis](biological-cell.md#plasmolysis).

###### Isotonic solution

↑ **Parent:** [Tonicity](#tonicity)

An [isotonic solution](#isotonic-solution) produces no sustained change in cellular [volume](geometry-and-topology.md#volume) under the stated comparison conditions. [Water](chemical-substance.md#water) can still exchange in both directions.

##### Cytolysis

↑ **Parent:** [Osmosis](#osmosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytolysis)

[Osmotic lysis](#cytolysis) is rupture after excessive [water](chemical-substance.md#water) entry and swelling. It is an outcome for susceptible [biological cells](biological-cell.md), not an inevitable response of every [biological cell](biological-cell.md) to a [hypotonic solution](#hypotonic-solution). [Cell walls](biological-cell.md#cell-wall) can bear the resulting load, and cellular mechanisms can regulate [water](chemical-substance.md#water) balance. A [protoplast](biological-cell.md#protoplast) challenge tests a particular preparation under particular conditions.

### Active transport

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane transport](#membrane-transport)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_transport)

[Active transport](#active-transport) moves a substance uphill in its [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) by coupling the transfer to a downhill process. Some [carrier proteins](#carrier-protein) use [ATP](molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) [hydrolysis](chemical-reaction.md#hydrolysis); others couple uphill transfer of one [solute](mixture.md#solute) to downhill transfer of another [solute](mixture.md#solute), often an [ion](chemistry.md#ion). The latter mechanism uses a stored [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) and need not consume [ATP](molecule.md#adenosine-triphosphate) directly at that [carrier protein](#carrier-protein). Maintaining the driving [electrochemical gradient](chemical-potential.md#electrochemical-gradient) still requires a source of [energy](energy.md). See [Alberts et al.: carrier proteins and active membrane transport](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26896/).

For the neutral 8:2 example, B-to-A transfer costs $RT\ln4\simeq1.38629RT$ per mole. As an explicitly assumed bookkeeping example, suppose a tightly coupled cycle supplies a downhill [free energy](thermodynamic-potential.md#thermodynamic-free-energy) change of $-2RT$ per mole of transferred [solute](mixture.md#solute). The total is then $\Delta G_{\mathrm{cycle}}=RT\ln4-2RT\simeq-0.61371RT$. This makes forward cycling energetically possible under those assumptions; it specifies neither a molecular mechanism nor a rate. Merely adding a [carrier protein](#carrier-protein) cannot supply the missing [free energy](thermodynamic-potential.md#thermodynamic-free-energy). Conversely, as the transported [concentration](mathematical-physics.md#concentration) ratio rises, the required uphill cost rises, so a fixed driving process has a finite thermodynamic limit.

#### Proton pump

↑ **Parent:** [Active transport](#active-transport)

A membrane transport system that uses an energy source to move [protons](physics.md#proton) against their electrochemical gradient.

##### Vacuolar proton ATPase

↑ **Parent:** [Proton pump](#proton-pump)

An ATP-driven proton pump that acidifies many eukaryotic endomembrane compartments.

#### Secondary active transport

↑ **Parent:** [Active transport](#active-transport)

Transport of a substrate against its electrochemical tendency using downhill movement of another solute, rather than direct ATP hydrolysis by that transporter.

#### Sodium-potassium ATPase

↑ **Parent:** [Active transport](#active-transport)

A membrane pump coupling ATP hydrolysis to sodium extrusion and potassium uptake. Its gradients support electrical excitability and secondary transport; membrane location matters for epithelial directionality.

### Exocytosis

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane transport](#membrane-transport)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocytosis)

[Exocytosis](#exocytosis) releases the contents of a membrane-bounded compartment to the extracellular space by fusion with the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). [Synaptic vesicle](neuroscience.md#synaptic-vesicle) fusion is an example. Its contents are released, while its membrane becomes connected to the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md); see [synaptic vesicle](neuroscience.md#synaptic-vesicle) for recovery and reuse.

### Endocytosis

↑ **Parent:** [Membrane transport](#membrane-transport)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis)

[Endocytosis](#endocytosis) takes material into a [biological cell](biological-cell.md) by inward budding and separation of part of its [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md). At a [chemical synapse](neuroscience.md#chemical-synapse), it helps recover membrane and [proteins](protein.md) after [synaptic vesicle](neuroscience.md#synaptic-vesicle) [exocytosis](#exocytosis). This recovery is distinct from uptake of individual [neurotransmitter](neuroscience.md#neurotransmitter) [molecules](molecule.md) through [membrane transport proteins](#membrane-transport-protein).

#### Synaptic vesicle recycling

↑ **Parent:** [Endocytosis](#endocytosis)

[synaptic vesicle recycling](#synaptic-vesicle-recycling) retrieves and reuses membrane and components after synaptic release. Uptake, uncoating, sorting and preparation for subsequent release are distinct measurable steps.

#### Clathrin-mediated endocytosis

↑ **Parent:** [Endocytosis](#endocytosis)

[clathrin-mediated endocytosis](#clathrin-mediated-endocytosis) internalizes selected surface material through assembly of adaptors and a [clathrin](protein.md#clathrin) coat, membrane deformation, scission and subsequent coat release. Counts of coated puncta alone do not distinguish initiation, successful internalization and stalled intermediates.

#### Phagocytosis

↑ **Parent:** [Endocytosis](#endocytosis)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phagocytosis)

[Phagocytosis](#phagocytosis) encloses a large extracellular particle by extending and closing the [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) around it. It is one possible conceptual route for internalizing a symbiotic partner, but a modern phagocytic pathway should not be assumed to have existed before eukaryotic organization evolved.

## Fluid mosaic model

↑ **Parent:** [Cell membrane](cell-membrane.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_mosaic_model)

The [fluid mosaic model](#fluid-mosaic-model) describes a [cell membrane](cell-membrane.md) as a fluid [lipid bilayer](mathematical-biology.md#lipid-bilayer) with embedded [membrane proteins](#membrane-protein). It connects a continuous barrier to a mosaic of functional components: [diffusion](thermodynamics.md#diffusion) within the membrane can coexist with selective passage across it. S. J. Singer and G. L. Nicolson presented the [fluid mosaic model](#fluid-mosaic-model) in Science in 1972; see [Singer and Nicolson, The fluid mosaic model of the structure of cell membranes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4333397/). The useful picture does not require every [protein](protein.md) to move freely: attachments and interactions can restrict movement, as discussed in [Cooper, The Cell: structure of the plasma membrane](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9898/).
