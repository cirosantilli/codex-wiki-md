# Characterization of linear surface-area isometries

↑ **Parent:** [Orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md)

For a real three-dimensional [linear map](linear-map.md) $A$, preservation of every [surface area](surface-area.md) is equivalent to being an [orthogonal matrix](orthogonal-matrix.md). For necessity, diagonalize the [symmetric matrix](symmetric-matrix.md) $A^TA$ in an [orthonormal basis](orthonormal-basis.md), with nonnegative [eigenvalues](eigenvalue.md) $\lambda_i$. The three unit [coordinate](coordinate-linear-algebra.md) [parallelograms](parallelogram.md) have image [areas](surface-area.md) $\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2}$, $\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_3}$, and $\sqrt{\lambda_2\lambda_3}$. Setting all three equal to $1$ forces every $\lambda_i=1$. Sufficiency follows from preservation of [dot products](dot-product.md) and $\|\mathbf u\times\mathbf v\|^2=\|\mathbf u\|^2\|\mathbf v\|^2-(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)^2$. This specifically uses dimension three; in dimension two, preservation of planar [area](surface-area.md) alone only requires $|\det A|=1$.
