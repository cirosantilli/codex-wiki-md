# Convergence of a sequence in a topological space

↑ **Parent:** [Topological space](topological-space.md)

A [sequence](sequence.md) $(x_n)$ in a [topological space](topological-space.md) converges to $x$ if every [open](open-set.md) [neighbourhood](neighbourhood-mathematics.md) of $x$ contains all sufficiently late terms:

$$
\forall U\text{ open with }x\in U\quad\exists N\quad\forall n\geq N:\ x_n\in U.
$$

For a [metric space](metric-space.md), this is the usual [limit of a sequence](limit-of-a-sequence.md) condition expressed through [open balls](open-ball.md). For a [Hausdorff space](hausdorff-space.md), distinct proposed limits have [disjoint](disjoint-sets.md) [open](open-set.md) [neighbourhoods](neighbourhood-mathematics.md), so a [sequence](sequence.md) cannot eventually stay in both and the limit is unique. The [sequence convergence in the cofinite topology](sequence-convergence-in-the-cofinite-topology.md) demonstrates what can change when that separation property is absent.[Definition 1.5.5, Proposition 1.5.11 and Proposition 1.6.20: neighbourhood convergence, uniqueness and the metric formulation](point-set-topology-by-romyar-sharifi/chapter-1-topological-spaces.md)
