# Inertia

↑ **Parent:** [Classical mechanics](classical-mechanics-split.md)

[Inertia](inertia.md) is a body's persistence in its state of rest or constant [velocity](velocity.md) when the net [external force](external-force.md) vanishes in an [inertial frame](inertial-frame.md). It is not an extra [force](force-split.md) that pushes a moving body forward. [Newton's first law](newton-s-first-law.md) describes this persistence; for a [point mass](point-mass.md), [Newton's second law](newton-s-second-law.md) quantifies how a nonzero net [external force](external-force.md) changes its [momentum](momentum-split.md). At constant [mass](mass.md), $\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=m\mathbf a$, so a larger [mass](mass.md) gives a smaller [acceleration](acceleration.md) for the same net [force](force-split.md).

A moving cart does not need a forward net [force](force-split.md) to maintain its [velocity](velocity.md). Ordinary carts slow because [friction](friction.md) and [drag force](drag-physics.md) produce an opposing net [external force](external-force.md); reducing those interactions reveals [Newton's first law](newton-s-first-law.md) more clearly. The [air-track test of inertial motion](air-track-test-of-inertial-motion.md) separates this persistence from the initial push. In [overdamped particle dynamics](overdamped-particle-dynamics.md), the inertial [acceleration](acceleration.md) term is small compared with [drag force](drag-physics.md) over the [time](time-in-physics.md) scales considered; that approximation does not mean that [inertia](inertia.md) has disappeared.
