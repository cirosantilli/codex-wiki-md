# Logarithmic lubrication resistance of a sphere near a wall

↑ **Parent:** [Reynolds equation](reynolds-equation.md)

A sphere of radius $a$ at gap $a\epsilon$ translates at $U\mathbf e_x$ and rotates at $\omega\mathbf e_y$ above a stationary wall in a right-handed coordinate system. At leading order its gap is $h=a\epsilon+r^2/(2a)$. In its translating frame the lower and upper tangential velocities are $-U\mathbf e_x$ and $-a\omega\mathbf e_x$. The [Reynolds lubrication equation](reynolds-equation.md) gives

$$
p=\frac{6\mu}{5}(U+a\omega)\frac{x}{h^2}.
$$

Integrating the upper-boundary [traction](traction.md), including pressure on the sloping surface, and its moment about the sphere centre gives the sphere-on-fluid [force](force.md) and [torque](torque.md)

$$
\begin{pmatrix}F_x\\G_y/a\end{pmatrix}\sim\frac{4\pi\mu a}{5}\log(1/\epsilon)\begin{pmatrix}4&-1\\-1&4\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U\\a\omega\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The off-diagonal symmetry is required by the [Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow](lorentz-reciprocal-theorem-for-stokes-flow.md). A [torque-free](torque-free.md) sphere therefore has $a\omega=U/4$ and leading drag $3\pi\mu aU\log(1/\epsilon)$. Compare the rigid-wall terms of [Bertin et al., equations (4.4)–(4.5)](https://vincent-bertin.github.io/Papers/09_Bertin2022JFM.pdf); those forces act on the sphere and have the opposite sign.
