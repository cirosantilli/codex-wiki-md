<h1 id="heisenberg-s-microscope">Heisenberg's microscope</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg's_microscope)

[Heisenberg's microscope](heisenberg-s-microscope.md) is a [thought experiment](thought-experiment.md), not a report of a performed measurement. In [Werner Heisenberg](werner-heisenberg.md)'s Nobel lecture, The development of quantum mechanics, delivered on 11 December 1933, the imagined [electron](electron.md) is illuminated with very short-wavelength light and observed through a high-resolution [microscope](microscope.md). A scattered [photon](photon.md) entering the observer's eye would, through [Compton scattering](compton-scattering.md), eject the [electron](electron.md) from its original path. The passage challenges a classical [trajectory](trajectory.md) picture; its microscope is hypothetical. The lecture date is 1933 even though the award is the [Nobel Prize in Physics in 1932](nobel-prize-in-physics-in-1932.md).[Nobel Lectures, Physics 1922–1941; Werner Heisenberg, The development of quantum mechanics, heading and printed pp. 291–292; text inspected in the Studyres transcription](nobel-lectures-physics-1922-1941/the-development-of-quantum-mechanics-by-werner-heisenberg.md)

The following idealized geometry makes the tradeoff visible. Let the collected light have [wavelength](wavelength.md) $\lambda$ and let the [microscope objective](microscope-objective.md) accept outgoing directions within a half-angle $\alpha$ about the vertical axis. Take $x$ transverse to that axis and hold the incoming [photon](photon.md) [momentum](momentum-split.md) fixed. A [diffraction](diffraction.md)-limited estimate of transverse resolution has scale $\delta x\sim\lambda/\sin\alpha$. The unobserved outgoing direction admits transverse [photon](photon.md) [momentum](momentum-split.md) components between approximately $-(h/\lambda)\sin\alpha$ and $+(h/\lambda)\sin\alpha$, neglecting the angular variation of the scattered [wavelength](wavelength.md). By [conservation of momentum](momentum-conservation.md), uncertainty in that component leaves uncertainty in the transverse recoil of the [electron](electron.md):

$$
\delta p_x\sim\frac{h}{\lambda}\sin\alpha,
\qquad
\delta x\,\delta p_x\sim h.
$$

Here $h=2\pi\hbar$ is the [Planck constant](planck-constant.md); the two $\delta$ symbols denote heuristic resolution and recoil scales, not the [standard deviations](standard-deviation.md) in the [Heisenberg uncertainty relation](heisenberg-uncertainty-relation.md). Increasing the acceptance angle improves the estimated resolution while broadening the allowed recoil range. Numerical factors depend on the resolution criterion and angular distribution; this scaling argument cannot supply the sharp constant $\hbar/2$.

The [thought experiment](thought-experiment.md) makes the interaction needed to obtain an image memorable. The [preparation uncertainty](preparation-uncertainty.md) theorem has broader use: it applies to the prepared [quantum state](quantum-state.md) even when the two [observables](observable.md) are measured on separate copies. No claim that [Werner Heisenberg](werner-heisenberg.md) built this [gamma ray](gamma-ray.md) apparatus is needed to understand either argument.
