# Binomial inversion

↑ **Parent:** [Binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md)

These two finite-sum transformations are inverses over any [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md). Substitute one sum into the other and use $\binom nk\binom kj=\binom nj\binom{n-j}{k-j}$. The inner alternating sum is $(1-1)^{n-j}$ by the [binomial theorem](binomial-theorem.md), so only $j=n$ survives. In a [Mahler expansion](mahler-s-theorem.md), $b_n$ is the nth [finite difference](finite-difference-split.md) of the sampled values $a_k$.
