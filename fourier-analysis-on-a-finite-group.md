# Fourier analysis on a finite group

↑ **Parent:** [Non-abelian additive combinatorics](non-abelian-additive-combinatorics.md)

For a [finite group](finite-group.md), choose one [unitary irreducible representation](unitary-irreducible-representation.md) $\rho$ of degree $d_\rho$ from each equivalence class. One consistent normalized [Fourier transform on a finite group](fourier-transform-on-a-finite-group.md) convention is $\widehat f(\rho)=\mathbb E_xf(x)\rho(x)$. The [Schur orthogonality relations](schur-orthogonality-relations.md) give

$$
f(x)=\sum_\rho d_\rho\operatorname{tr}(\widehat f(\rho)\rho(x)^*),
\qquad
\mathbb E_x|f(x)|^2=\sum_\rho d_\rho\|\widehat f(\rho)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
$$

The [normalized convolution on a finite group](normalized-convolution-on-a-finite-group.md) satisfies $\widehat{f*g}(\rho)=\widehat f(\rho)\widehat g(\rho)$ in this convention. Using $\rho(x)^*$ in the transform instead reverses that product order for scalar functions with the same [normalized convolution on a finite group](normalized-convolution-on-a-finite-group.md) convention. The [Fourier transform on a finite group](fourier-transform-on-a-finite-group.md) has matrix-valued components even when the original function is scalar-valued.
