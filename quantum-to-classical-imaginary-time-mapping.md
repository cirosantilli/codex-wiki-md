# Quantum-to-classical imaginary-time mapping

↑ **Parent:** [Euclidean path integral](euclidean-path-integral.md)

A local $d$-dimensional [quantum field](quantum-field.md) at finite [temperature](temperature-split.md) can often be represented by an anisotropic classical-looking [action](action.md) in $d+1$ dimensions, with a finite extra imaginary-time extent. The zero-temperature limit makes that extent infinite. A real nonnegative statistical weight is an additional requirement: fermionic signs and imaginary terms can obstruct interpretation as an ordinary classical [probability](probability.md) [distribution](distribution-mathematical-analysis.md).
