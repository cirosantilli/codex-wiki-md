# Negative melting-point slope of water

↑ **Parent:** [Common-pressure melting-point slope](common-pressure-melting-point-slope.md)

For a local illustrative [ice](ice.md)–[water](water.md) [melting](melting.md) calculation, stipulate $T_m=273.15\,\mathrm K$, [latent heat of fusion](latent-heat-of-fusion.md) $L=334\,\mathrm{kJ\,kg^{-1}}$, [solid](solid.md) [mass density](density.md) $\rho_s=917\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, and [liquid](liquid.md) [mass density](density.md) $\rho_l=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. These are explicitly stipulated rounded inputs, not measurements made here. The [specific volume](specific-volume.md) change on [melting](melting.md) is

$$
\Delta v=\frac{1}{\rho_l}-\frac{1}{\rho_s}=-9.0513\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}}.
$$

The [common-pressure melting-point slope](common-pressure-melting-point-slope.md) therefore gives

$$
\frac{dT_m}{dp}=\frac{T_m\Delta v}{L}=-7.4022\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{K\,Pa^{-1}}\simeq-0.07402\,\mathrm{K\,MPa^{-1}}.
$$

A small $1\,\mathrm{MPa}$ increase in the common [pressure](pressure-split.md) thus lowers the [melting](melting.md) [temperature](temperature-split.md) by about $0.074\,\mathrm K$ in this local model. Positive [latent heat](latent-heat.md) does not force a positive [phase coexistence curve](phase-coexistence-curve.md) slope: [ice](ice.md) contracts on conversion to [liquid](liquid.md) [water](water.md). Increasing [pressure](pressure-split.md) favors the smaller [specific volume](specific-volume.md). This calculation compares equilibrium states of the same two [thermodynamic phases](thermodynamic-phase.md); extending its tangent to large [pressures](pressure-split.md) would require updated [mass densities](density.md), [latent heat of fusion](latent-heat-of-fusion.md), and the relevant [ice](ice.md) [phase diagram](phase-diagram.md).
