# Why microscopic differences fade near criticality

↑ **Parent:** [Renormalization group](renormalization-group.md)

Near a [renormalization-group fixed point](renormalization-group-fixed-point.md), expand a difference between two nearby models in [eigenvectors](eigenvector.md) of the [stability matrix](stability-matrix.md). An [irrelevant operator](irrelevant-operator.md) direction has $y_i<0$, so its dimensionless coefficient shrinks under increasing length factor $b$. A [relevant operator](relevant-operator.md) direction grows and must be tuned to study the same critical behavior. This is a mechanism for losing microscopic detail, not an assertion that every parameter disappears. Critical [temperature](temperature-split.md), individual amplitudes and regular contributions to [free energy](thermodynamic-free-energy.md) can remain material dependent.

For the solvable example, write $A=\epsilon/g_0-1$. Its exact solution gives $g-\epsilon=-\epsilon A b^{-\epsilon}+O(b^{-2\epsilon})$. At the thermal stopping scale $b_*=|t_0|^{-1/y_t}$ this is of order $|t_0|^{\epsilon/y_t}$. If the stopped [correlation length](correlation-length.md) is smooth in $g$, the leading correction to its power law has that order. Thus two microscopic couplings can yield the same limiting [critical exponent](critical-exponent.md), yet approach it at different rates.

This mechanism makes [universality](universality-of-critical-phenomena.md) useful: one tractable representative can predict [critical exponents](critical-exponent.md) and normalized scaling forms for many systems in its [universality class](universality-class.md). A [liquid-gas critical point](liquid-gas-critical-point.md) and a scalar magnetic transition can share a long-distance description even though their microscopic constituents differ. The scope must be checked: spatial dimension, [order parameter](order-parameter.md) symmetry, interaction range, and extra soft variables can change the [renormalization-group fixed point](renormalization-group-fixed-point.md).

In his lecture, [Kenneth Geddes Wilson](kenneth-geddes-wilson.md) connects limiting [renormalization-group fixed points](renormalization-group-fixed-point.md) with common critical behavior and states the limits of his elementary truncation.[Section II, pp. 110–112: truncation limits and common critical behavior](the-renormalization-group-and-critical-phenomena-by-kenneth-geddes-wilson.md)
