# Order of a finite group

↑ **Parent:** [Finite group](finite-group.md)

The [order of a finite group](order-of-a-finite-group.md) is its [cardinality](cardinality.md), written $|G|$. The [identity element](identity-element.md) is included: $|\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z|=6$, and the [trivial group](trivial-group.md) has order one, rather than zero.

Keep this count distinct from the [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md). In the additive [finite cyclic group](finite-cyclic-group.md) $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, the element $1$ has order six, $2$ has order three, $3$ has order two, and $0$ has order one. Each element's order is the least positive number of repetitions needed to return to the [identity element](identity-element.md) $0$. The [order of an element of a finite cyclic group](order-of-an-element-of-a-finite-cyclic-group.md) computes these periods directly.

The count also constrains [subgroups](subgroup.md): [Lagrange's theorem](lagrange-s-theorem.md) gives $|G|=[G:H]|H|$ for a [subgroup](subgroup.md) $H$ of a [finite group](finite-group.md) $G$, where the [index of a subgroup](index-of-a-subgroup.md) counts its [cosets](coset.md). For $H=\{0,2,4\}$ inside $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, the two [cosets](coset.md) are $\{0,2,4\}$ and $\{1,3,5\}$, so $6=2\cdot3$. Divisibility is a necessary constraint; it does not imply that every divisor occurs as the [order of a group element](order-of-a-group-element.md), as the [Klein four-group](klein-four-group.md) shows.
