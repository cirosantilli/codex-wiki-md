# Potential of a conservative vector field

↑ **Parent:** [Conservative vector field](conservative-vector-field.md)

A potential for a [conservative vector field](conservative-vector-field.md) $X$ is a [function](function-split.md) with [scalar](scalar.md) values $\Phi$ whose [gradient](gradient.md) is $X$. Potentials differ by a constant on each connected component. The [fundamental theorem for line integrals](fundamental-theorem-for-line-integrals.md) gives $\int_\gamma X\cdot d\mathbf x=\Phi(\gamma(1))-\Phi(\gamma(0))$. In mechanics a conservative force is conventionally written $-\nabla V$, so its potential energy is $V=-\Phi$. This spatial potential differs from the [scalar potential](scalar-potential.md) $V(\phi)$ that denotes the derivative-free energy density of a [scalar field](scalar-field.md).
