# Eisenstein criterion

↑ **Parent:** [Commutative algebra](commutative-algebra-split.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_criterion)

For a primitive polynomial

$$
F(X)=a_nX^n+\cdots+a_0\in\mathbb Z[X],
$$

if a prime $p$ does not divide $a_n$, divides every $a_j$ for $j<n$, and has $p^2\nmid a_0$, then $F$ is irreducible. If $F=GH$, reduction modulo $p$ forces both $\overline G$ and $\overline H$ to be positive-degree monomials. Their constant terms are consequently divisible by $p$, making $p^2\mid F(0)$, a contradiction. [Gauss lemma for polynomials](gauss-lemma-for-polynomials.md) transfers the result between $\mathbb Z[X]$ and $\mathbb Q[X]$.
