# Conserved transverse optical momentum

↑ **Parent:** [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md)

For a positive [refractive index](refractive-index.md) depending only on $x$, [Fermat principle](fermat-principle.md) applied to $\int\mu(x)\sqrt{1+y'^2}\,dx$ has $y$ as a [cyclic coordinate](cyclic-coordinate.md). The [Euler-Lagrange equation](euler-lagrange-equation.md) therefore makes the displayed quantity constant along a stationary ray. When $\mu^2>p^2$, its slope is $y'=p/\sqrt{\mu^2-p^2}$. If $\theta$ is the angle to the $x$ axis, this is conservation of $\mu\sin\theta$, the stratified-medium form of [Snell's law](snell-s-law.md). For fixed endpoint values, the integrand has second derivative $\mu(1+y'^2)^{-3/2}>0$ with respect to $y'$. Its strict [convexity](convex-function.md) and the constant momentum show, by the tangent-line inequality and integration, that any such ray is the unique minimum among competing graph paths with the same endpoints.
