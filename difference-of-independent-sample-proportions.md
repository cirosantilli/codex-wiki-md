# Difference of independent sample proportions

↑ **Parent:** [Sample proportion](sample-proportion.md)

For two [independent](independent-random-variables.md) samples of [Bernoulli random variables](bernoulli-distribution.md), the difference of their [sample proportions](sample-proportion.md) estimates the difference of population [probabilities](probability.md). Since the samples are [independent](independent-random-variables.md),

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat p_1-\widehat p_0)=\frac{p_1(1-p_1)}{n_1}+\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n_0}.
$$

Substitution of the [sample proportions](sample-proportion.md) gives a [standard error](standard-error.md) for a large-sample [Wald confidence interval](wald-confidence-interval.md). This interval is a descriptive comparison of the specified groups; a [causal effect](causal-effect.md) needs further assumptions. Sparse counts and [probabilities](probability.md) near zero or one can make the [normal approximation](normal-approximation.md) poor. Testing equality can instead use a null-pooled estimate of the common [probability](probability.md) for the test's [standard error](standard-error.md).
