# Three-level laser

↑ **Parent:** [Laser](laser-split.md)

A [three-level laser](three-level-laser.md) pumps emitters out of their [ground state](ground-state.md) into a higher pump state, which rapidly relaxes into the upper lasing state. The lower lasing state is the [ground state](ground-state.md) itself. [Photon absorption](photon-absorption.md) on the lasing transition therefore competes with [stimulated emission](stimulated-emission.md) until enough emitters have been removed from the ground state. This is the [ground state](ground-state.md) depletion problem implicit in [population inversion](population-inversion.md).

The model below treats nondegenerate lasing [energy levels](energy-level.md) with equal absorption and emission cross-sections. Its inversion condition is $N_2>N_1$. Real materials require the relevant sublevel populations, degeneracies and transition strengths; the simple half-population condition should not be imposed on every optical transition. Extra levels let the pump and the emitted [light](light.md) act on different transitions, avoiding the steady two-level saturation limit discussed in [population inversion](population-inversion.md).
