<h1 id="b/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

[Conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion](../../../../../conformal-invariance-of-planar-brownian-motion.md) means invariance after changing the clock, not preservation of the original speed. If $f:D\to D'$ is a conformal bijection and $Z$ is planar [Brownian motion](../../../../../brownian-motion-split.md) stopped on exiting $D$, put

$$
A_t=\int_0^{t\wedge T_D}|f'(Z_s)|^2\,ds.
$$

Then $f(Z_t)$ has the law of planar Brownian motion started at $f(Z_0)$ and run at time $A_t$, up to the corresponding lifetime. Equivalently, composing with the inverse clock produces ordinary Brownian motion in $D'$.

Indeed, the real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic, so [Itô's formula](../../../../../ito-s-lemma.md) gives zero drift. The [Cauchy-Riemann equations](../../../../../cauchy-riemann-equations.md) give equal coordinate brackets $A_t$ and zero cross bracket. The [Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion](../../../../../levy-characterization-of-multidimensional-brownian-motion.md) after time change identifies the image. In particular the [harmonic measure](../../../../../harmonic-measure.md) of boundary arcs is carried to their conformal images.
