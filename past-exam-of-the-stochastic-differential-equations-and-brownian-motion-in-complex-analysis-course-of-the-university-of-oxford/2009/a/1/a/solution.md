↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Let $V=\sum_{n\geq1}\|X_n\|_\infty$. It is a nonnegative extended [random variable](../../../../../random-variable-split.md), and $\mathbb EV<\infty$ implies $\mathbb P(V<\infty)=1$. Otherwise its expectation would be infinite. Intersect this event with the countably many probability-one events on which the individual sample paths are continuous.

For every path in that intersection, $\sum_n\|X_n(\cdot,\omega)\|_\infty<\infty$. The [Weierstrass M-test](../../../../../weierstrass-m-test.md) gives absolute and uniform convergence of $\sum_nX_n(t,\omega)$ on the entire stated time domain. A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous. Therefore **$Y$ has continuous paths almost surely**. The finite expectation is used to obtain pathwise summability before applying this deterministic theorem.
