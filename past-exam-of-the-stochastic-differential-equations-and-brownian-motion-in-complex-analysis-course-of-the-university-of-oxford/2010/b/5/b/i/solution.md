<h1 id="b/5/b/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

By [conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion](../../../../../../conformal-invariance-of-planar-brownian-motion.md), $w(Z_{T_D})$ has the uniform boundary law from part (a), because the image process starts at zero. A [disk automorphism](../../../../../../automorphism-of-the-unit-disk.md) extends to an orientation-preserving boundary diffeomorphism. Choose a continuous angular lift $\phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfying

$$
w(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\phi(\theta)},\qquad
\phi(\theta+2\pi)=\phi(\theta)+2\pi,\qquad\phi'(\theta)>0.
$$

The principal-angle version may have a branch jump, which is harmless for the density. The [change of variables for probability densities](../../../../../../change-of-variables-for-probability-densities.md) yields the exit-angle density $\phi'(\theta)/(2\pi)$. Differentiating the boundary relation gives

$$
i e^{i\theta}w'(e^{i\theta})=i\phi'(\theta)e^{i\phi(\theta)}.
$$

Taking absolute values proves $\phi'(\theta)=|w'(e^{i\theta})|$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P^{z_0}(\theta_{T_D}\in d\theta)=\frac{|w'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi}\,d\theta.}
$$

The angular lift explains rigorously the derivative appearing in the printed hint despite the principal-angle branch cut.
