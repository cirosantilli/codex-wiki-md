# Irreducible traceless matrix Lie algebra is semisimple

↑ **Parent:** [Semisimple Lie algebra](semisimple-lie-algebra-split.md)

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex [vector space](vector-space-split.md) and let $L$ be a [Lie subalgebra](lie-subalgebra.md) of its traceless [endomorphisms](endomorphism.md) acting irreducibly. Its [solvable radical](radical-of-a-lie-algebra.md) $R$ is simultaneously triangularizable by [Lie's theorem](lie-s-theorem.md). The resulting finite set of diagonal [characters](character-of-a-representation.md) is invariant under conjugation by $e^{tx}$ for $x\in L$, because this conjugation preserves the [ideal](ideal-of-a-lie-algebra.md) $R$ and gives an equivalent [representation](group-representation.md). Each [character](character-of-a-representation.md) varies continuously with $t$ in a finite set, hence is constant; differentiation gives $\lambda([x,r])=0$.

A nonzero common $R$-[eigenspace](eigenspace.md) exists by [Lie's theorem](lie-s-theorem.md). The displayed vanishing makes it invariant under all of $L$: $r(xv)=\lambda(r)xv+\lambda([r,x])v=\lambda(r)xv$. Irreducibility therefore makes it all of $V$. Thus $R$ acts by scalars, and the zero-[trace](matrix-trace.md) condition forces those scalars to vanish. The [matrix](matrix.md) [representation](group-representation.md) is faithful, so $R=0$. The traceless condition matters: adjoining scalar [matrices](matrix.md) to an irreducible semisimple algebra gives a reductive algebra with nonzero [centre](center-of-a-lie-algebra.md).
