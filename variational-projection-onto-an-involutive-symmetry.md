# Variational projection onto an involutive symmetry

↑ **Parent:** [Variational principle](variational-principle.md)

Let $H$ be a lower-bounded self-adjoint [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) and $I$ a commuting self-adjoint operator with $I^2=1$. For a normalized trial vector $\phi$, define $a=\langle H\rangle_\phi$, $b=\langle I\rangle_\phi$, $c=\langle HI\rangle_\phi$. The [Rayleigh quotient](rayleigh-quotient.md) on $\phi+tI\phi$ is $[(1+t^2)a+2tc]/[1+t^2+2tb]$, with derivative $2(c-ab)(1-t^2)/(1+t^2+2tb)^2$. Unless $c=ab$, its extrema are the normalized nonzero vectors $\phi\pm I\phi$, with energies $(a\pm c)/(1\pm b)$. Their difference is $2(c-ab)/(1-b^2)$, identifying the maximum and minimum.

The orthogonal projections $(1\pm I)/2$ split any trial energy into a weighted mean of the two sector energies, so one nonzero sector projection never raises the trial energy. If $|b|=1$, one projected vector is zero and its quotient is undefined; the remaining family has constant energy. Exchange symmetry makes these sectors bosonic and fermionic spatial wavefunctions. Stationarity within this two-dimensional trial space does not generally make a vector an exact eigenvector of $H$.
