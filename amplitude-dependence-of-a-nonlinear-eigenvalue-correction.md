# Amplitude dependence of a nonlinear eigenvalue correction

↑ **Parent:** [Leading nonlinear eigenvalue shift in a Sturm-Liouville problem](leading-nonlinear-eigenvalue-shift-in-a-sturm-liouville-problem.md)

Suppose a real simple leading [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) is $y_0=C\phi$ and a first-order correction satisfies $L_0v=-\nu w y_0-y_0^p$, where $p\geq2$ is an [integer](integer-split.md), $L_0$ is [self-adjoint](self-adjoint-operator.md) with [null space](kernel-of-a-linear-map-split.md) spanned by $\phi$, the [boundary conditions](boundary-condition.md) are homogeneous and independent of the parameter, and $w>0$. The [Fredholm solvability condition](fredholm-solvability-condition.md) gives

$$
\nu(C)=-C^{p-1}\frac{\int\phi^{p+1}\,dx}{\int w\phi^2\,dx}.
$$

Indeed, [orthogonality](orthogonal-vectors.md) of the forcing to $\phi$ gives $\nu C\int w\phi^2+C^p\int\phi^{p+1}=0$, and $C\ne0$ permits division. Thus the numerical [eigenvalue](eigenvalue.md) correction depends on the chosen leading amplitude; fixing an [eigenfunction](eigenfunction.md) normalization is part of defining the nonlinear branch. This calculation assumes a perturbative branch and does not by itself establish that branch's existence.
