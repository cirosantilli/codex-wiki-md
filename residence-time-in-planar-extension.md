# Residence time in planar extension

↑ **Parent:** [Keller four-roller polymer experiment](keller-four-roller-polymer-experiment.md)

An original local flow model has [velocity](velocity.md) $\mathbf v=(\dot\epsilon x,-\dot\epsilon y,0)$ for constant [extensional strain rate](extensional-strain-rate.md) $\dot\epsilon>0$. Its [divergence](divergence.md) is zero, and its trajectories satisfy the displayed equations. Material approaches along $y$ and leaves along $x$. If the idealized observation region ends at $|x|=L$, a trajectory starting at $0<|x_0|<L$ has residence [time](time-in-physics.md)

$$
t_{\rm exit}=\frac{1}{\dot\epsilon}\log\frac{L}{|x_0|}.
$$

For chosen $\dot\epsilon=1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $L=1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $|x_0|=0.01\,\mathrm{mm}$, this is $\log100\,\mathrm s\simeq4.61\,\mathrm s$. Starting ten times farther from the symmetry line halves this particular residence [time](time-in-physics.md). The mathematical trajectory $x_0=0$ never exits in this model; [diffusion](diffusion.md), imperfect flow and finite apparatus prevent treating that ideal trajectory as a guaranteed infinite exposure in an experiment.
