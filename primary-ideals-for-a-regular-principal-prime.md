# Primary ideals for a regular principal prime

↑ **Parent:** [Primary ideal](primary-ideal.md)

If a [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) $P=(r)$ is generated by a [non-zero-divisor](non-zero-divisor.md), its $P$-[primary ideals](primary-ideal.md) are exactly $(r^k)$ for $k\geq1$, in any [commutative ring](commutative-ring.md). To prove $(r^k)$ is [primary](primary-ideal.md), if $ab\in(r^k)$ and $a\notin(r)$, the [prime ideal](prime-ideal.md) condition gives $b=rb_1$; cancelling $r$ and inducting gives $b\in(r^k)$. Also $\sqrt{(r^k)}=(r)$. Conversely, for a $P$-[primary ideal](primary-ideal.md) $Q$, choose the least $k$ with $r^k\in Q$. If $x\in Q\setminus(r^k)$, factor $x=r^ju$ as far as possible with $0\leq j<k$. Then $u\notin(r)$, so the [primary ideal](primary-ideal.md) condition forces $r^j\in Q$, a contradiction. Thus $Q=(r^k)$. The powers are strictly decreasing: equality $(r^k)=(r^{k+1})$ would let cancellation give $1=rc$. This also proves uniqueness of $k$.
