# Galactic-centre stellar-orbit experiments

↑ **Parent:** [Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre](stellar-orbits-at-the-galactic-centre.md)

[Rainer Schödel](rainer-schodel.md), [Reinhard Genzel](reinhard-genzel.md) and collaborators combined NTT imaging with VLT adaptive-optics [infrared](infrared.md) observations. Silicon-monoxide [masers](maser-split.md) registered stellar images to the radio position of [Sagittarius A\*](sagittarius-a.md). Their 2002 observations included [S2](s2-star.md)'s closest approach; a fitted [Kepler orbit](kepler-orbit.md) constrained a concentrated central mass. Rejecting a particular extended cluster also required its density and survival assumptions.[PDF pp.2–5, image registration, fitted orbit and extended-mass tests](closest-star-seen-orbiting-the-supermassive-black-hole-at-the-centre-of-the-milky-way-by-rainer-schodel-and-others.md)

[Andrea Ghez](andrea-m-ghez.md) and collaborators combined Keck speckle imaging and laser-guide-star [adaptive optics](adaptive-optics.md) with [infrared](infrared.md) spectra. Image fitting supplied angular positions; spectral lines supplied [velocities](velocity.md). The 2008 analysis treated optical distortion, coordinate alignment and blended sources: a shifting photocentre can imitate motion. Joint angular and spectral fitting constrained both distance and mass, with assumptions about central radial motion affecting the result. Apparatus calibration and confusion tests are therefore part of the dynamical inference.[PDF §§2–4 and Appendices A–C: imaging, spectroscopy, source confusion and orbital fit](measuring-distance-and-properties-of-the-milky-way-s-central-supermassive-black-hole-with-stellar-orbits-by-andrea-m-ghez-and-others.md)
