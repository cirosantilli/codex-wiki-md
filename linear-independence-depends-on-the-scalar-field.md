# Linear independence depends on the scalar field

↑ **Parent:** [Linear dependence](linear-dependence.md)

Consider the [vectors](vector.md) $1,i$ in the [complex numbers](complex-number.md), where $i$ is the [imaginary unit](imaginary-unit.md). With [scalar](scalar.md) [field](field.md) $\mathbb R$, a [linear relation](linear-relation.md) $a\cdot1+b\cdot i=0$ has $a,b\in\mathbb R$. Taking its [real part](real-part.md) and [imaginary part](imaginary-part.md) gives $a=b=0$, so the pair is [linearly independent](linear-independence.md). Every [complex number](complex-number.md) $x+iy$ also has [coefficients](coefficient.md) $(x,y)$ in the [real numbers](real-number.md), so $1,i$ form a [basis](basis.md) and the [dimension of a vector space](dimension-vector-space.md) is two.

With [scalar](scalar.md) [field](field.md) $\mathbb C$, the same pair is [linearly dependent](linear-dependence.md), since

$$
i\cdot1+(-1)\cdot i=0
$$

uses the nonzero [coefficients](coefficient.md) $(i,-1)$ from the [complex numbers](complex-number.md). The single [vector](vector.md) $1$ is now a [basis](basis.md), because $z=z\cdot1$ for every [complex number](complex-number.md) $z$, giving [dimension of a vector space](dimension-vector-space.md) one. For example $3+i=3\cdot1+1\cdot i=(3+i)\cdot1+0\cdot i$: the second representation uses a [coefficient](coefficient.md) unavailable over the [real numbers](real-number.md). Specifying the [field](field.md) is part of the definition of [linear independence](linear-independence.md).
