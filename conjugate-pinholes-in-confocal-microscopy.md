# Conjugate pinholes in confocal microscopy

↑ **Parent:** [Confocal microscopy](confocal-microscopy.md)

Two planes are optically conjugate when an [optical lens](lens-optics.md) system images one onto the other. In [confocal microscopy](confocal-microscopy.md), an illumination pinhole is imaged onto the selected specimen point, and that specimen point is imaged onto the detection pinhole. Thus the two [apertures](aperture.md) select corresponding positions in different planes; they need not occupy the same physical plane. Modern [laser](laser-split.md) instruments can supply the illumination point with an [optical fiber](optical-fiber.md) or a suitable focused [laser](laser-split.md) mode instead of a separate metal illumination pinhole. [Amos, McConnell and Wilson, Confocal microscopy, manuscript pp. 5, 7–8: conjugate foci, Fig. 3, fiber-delivered illumination](confocal-microscopy-chapter-manuscript-by-brad-amos-and-others.md)

A fluorescent emitter at the selected [optical focus](focus-optics.md) forms a compact [point spread function](point-spread-function-split.md) at the detection [aperture](aperture.md). An emitter displaced axially produces a broadened spot there through [defocus](defocus-aberration.md); a smaller fraction of its collected [light](light.md) passes. The emitter still exists and still emits [photons](photon.md). Rejected [photons](photon.md) strike the opaque mask or other parts of the instrument instead of contributing to the accepted detector channel. Neither a finite pinhole nor [diffraction](diffraction.md) makes the accepted out-of-focus contribution exactly zero.
