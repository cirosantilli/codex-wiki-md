# Sudden approximation

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum mechanics](quantum-mechanics-split.md)

The [sudden approximation](sudden-approximation.md) treats a rapid, finite change of a [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) as leaving the [quantum state](quantum-state.md) approximately unchanged during the switch. The [energy](energy.md) measurement can nevertheless change immediately: the same [quantum state](quantum-state.md) has different [probability amplitudes](probability-amplitude.md) in the new [eigenbasis](eigenbasis.md). Compute their [inner products](inner-product.md) with the new [energy eigenstates](energy-eigenstate.md), then apply the [Born rule](born-rule.md). The [two-level sudden quench](two-level-sudden-quench.md) below makes this distinction quantitative without assuming the change is small.

An ideal finite step has exact [quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step](quantum-state-continuity-at-a-finite-hamiltonian-step.md); a finite-duration implementation has a small evolution error under the bounded-model conditions below. An [impulsive quantum kick](impulsive-quantum-kick.md) is different: its [Hamiltonian operator](hamiltonian-quantum-mechanics.md) grows as its duration shrinks, retaining a finite integrated [action](action.md) and a finite change of [quantum state](quantum-state.md). A [sudden approximation](sudden-approximation.md) therefore concerns short evolution during switching, not [conservation of energy](conservation-of-energy.md) during externally driven change. The subsequent [unitary time evolution](unitary-time-evolution.md) can be substantial.[Talkner, Lutz and Hänggi, p. 2, paragraph before equation (7): sudden switch gives the identity time-evolution operator](fluctuation-theorems-work-is-not-an-observable-by-peter-talkner-and-others.md)
