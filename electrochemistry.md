# Electrochemistry

↑ **Parent:** [Chemistry](chemistry-split.md)

[Electrochemistry](electrochemistry.md) studies how [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md), transport, and [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md) couple to [electric charge](electric-charge.md) and [electric potential](electric-potential.md). In a cell, [Electrons](electron.md) travel through the external circuit, while mobile [ions](ion.md) carry [electric current](electric-current.md) through the [electrolyte](electrolyte.md). At the [electrodes](electrode.md), [redox](redox.md) reactions connect these two forms of [electric charge](electric-charge.md) transport. This connection makes it possible both to obtain electrical [work](work.md) from a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md) and to use electrical [work](work.md) to drive a [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md).

An [anode](anode.md) is an [electrode](electrode.md) where [oxidation](oxidation.md) occurs; a [cathode](cathode.md) is an [electrode](electrode.md) where [reduction](reduction-chemistry.md) occurs. These definitions refer to the [chemical reactions](chemical-reaction.md), rather than universal positive or negative terminal signs. In a [galvanic cell](galvanic-cell.md) delivering electrical [work](work.md), the [anode](anode.md) is negative and the [cathode](cathode.md) positive. In an [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md) driven by an external source, the [anode](anode.md) is positive and the [cathode](cathode.md) negative. See [Pingarrón et al., Terminology of electrochemical methods of analysis (IUPAC Recommendations 2019), §§3.1 and 3.4](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2018-0109) and [Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §17.2: galvanic cells](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/17-2-galvanic-cells).

For a worked example, consider an [electrolytic cell](electrolytic-cell.md) containing molten [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md), with the [anode](anode.md) connected to the positive terminal and the [cathode](cathode.md) to the negative terminal of an external source. At the [cathode](cathode.md), [sodium](sodium.md) [cations](cation.md) gain [Electrons](electron.md); at the [anode](anode.md), [chlorine](chlorine.md) [anions](anion.md) lose [Electrons](electron.md):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{cathode, reduction:}\quad&2\mathrm{Na}^++2e^-\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Na},\\
\text{anode, oxidation:}\quad&2\mathrm{Cl}^-\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cl}_2+2e^-,\\
\text{overall:}\quad&2\mathrm{Na}^++2\mathrm{Cl}^-\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Na}+\mathrm{Cl}_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first line has net [electric charge](electric-charge.md) zero on each side; the second has $-2e$ on each side. Adding the lines cancels the transferred [Electrons](electron.md) and preserves both [chemical atom](atom-physics.md) counts and net [electric charge](electric-charge.md). The [sodium](sodium.md) product is neutral, and the [chlorine](chlorine.md) product is a diatomic [molecule](molecule.md). These equations specify molten [sodium chloride](sodium-chloride.md); aqueous [chemical solutions](solution-chemistry.md) introduce competing [water](water.md) reactions. See [17.7 Electrolysis](chemistry-2e-by-paul-flowers-and-others/17-7-electrolysis.md) (§17.7: electrolysis of molten sodium chloride).

[Electrochemistry](electrochemistry.md) underlies [battery](electric-battery.md) charging and the manufacture of chemicals by [electrolysis](electrolysis.md). Its particularly useful idea is that an [Electron](electron.md) inventory in a balanced [chemical equation](chemical-equation.md) also accounts for transported [electric charge](electric-charge.md). Transport alone is a different question from an [electrode](electrode.md) [chemical reaction](chemical-reaction.md): an [ion](ion.md) may carry [electric current](electric-current.md) through the [electrolyte](electrolyte.md) without itself being consumed at an [electrode](electrode.md).

Historically, Michael Faraday introduced the terms [electrode](electrode.md), [electrolyte](electrolyte.md), [anion](anion.md), [cation](cation.md), and [ion](ion.md) in the terminology section of his 1834 paper, [Experimental Researches in Electricity, Seventh Series by Michael Faraday](experimental-researches-in-electricity-seventh-series-by-michael-faraday.md) (§§662–667). His §667 explicitly treats the direction assigned to [electric current](electric-current.md) as a convention. His [ion](ion.md) language predates the modern [Electron](electron.md) and [atomic nucleus](atomic-nucleus.md) picture used in the charge-counting example above.

For an [electrolyte](electrolyte.md) near [chemical equilibrium](chemical-equilibrium.md), a [Poisson-Boltzmann equation](poisson-boltzmann-equation.md) can approximate the distribution of [ions](ion.md) in an [electric potential](electric-potential.md). Such an equilibrium model does not by itself describe a cell carrying sustained [electric current](electric-current.md).
