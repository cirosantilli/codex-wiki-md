# Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution

↑ **Parent:** [Quantum ideal-gas statistics](quantum-ideal-gas-statistics.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell–Boltzmann_distribution)

The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution describes the random [velocities](velocity.md) and [speeds](speed.md) of particles in a classical [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) at [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md). Individual particles keep changing [velocity](velocity.md) through [collisions](collision.md), while the equilibrium [probability distribution](probability-distribution.md) stays fixed. For one species of particle [mass](mass.md) $m$ at positive [temperature](temperature.md) $T$, the characteristic scale is

$$
s=\sqrt{\frac{k_BT}{m}},
$$

where $k_B$ is the [Boltzmann constant](boltzmann-constant.md). A signed [velocity](velocity.md) component follows a [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md); the nonnegative magnitude follows the [Maxwell speed distribution](maxwell-speed-distribution.md). Their peaks differ because a [speed](speed.md) measurement groups together all directions of motion.

Use the gas rest frame, three spatial dimensions, and [nonrelativistic particles](nonrelativistic-particle.md), with $k_BT\ll mc^2$ where $c$ is the [speed of light](speed-of-light.md). Assume a dilute gas whose interactions between encounters can be neglected in the [ideal gas](ideal-gas.md) approximation and whose translational motion is classical. A sufficient condition for negligible quantum exchange effects is $n\lambda^3\ll1$, where $n$ is the [number density](number-density.md) and $\lambda$ the [thermal de Broglie wavelength](thermal-de-broglie-wavelength.md). Diluteness alone does not guarantee [thermal equilibrium](thermal-equilibrium.md); the gas must have equilibrated. These are the assumptions behind the classical [Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution](maxwell-boltzmann-velocity-distribution.md). [Fitzpatrick, The Maxwell distribution](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/sm1/lectures/node72.html) [Fitzpatrick, Quantum statistics in the classical limit](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/sm1/lectures/node82.html)

The connection to [Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics](maxwell-boltzmann-statistics.md) is the [Boltzmann factor](boltzmann-factor.md) for translational [kinetic energy](kinetic-energy.md), $E_{\rm kin}=m|\mathbf v|^2/2$. Normalizing $e^{-E_{\rm kin}/(k_BT)}$ over [velocity](velocity.md) gives a probability density; an occupation factor $e^{(\mu-E)/(k_BT)}$ instead specifies a mean occupation, with [chemical potential](chemical-potential.md) $\mu$. Their normalizations serve different purposes. [Fitzpatrick, Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/sm1/lectures/node81.html)

This connects microscopic motion to measurements: a [gas thermometer](gas-thermometer.md) uses the [ideal gas law](ideal-gas-law.md), [collisions](collision.md) and the [mean free path](mean-free-path.md) govern transport, and measurements of [spectral lines](spectral-line.md) use [thermal Doppler broadening](thermal-doppler-broadening.md) to infer [temperature](temperature.md). Each probes a different aspect of the same thermal motion.

In 1860, [James Clerk Maxwell](james-clerk-maxwell.md) presented the component and [speed](speed.md) laws in Part I, Proposition IV of _Illustrations of the Dynamical Theory of Gases_, pp. 22–23. His argument combined [independence](independent-random-variables.md) of the three [velocity](velocity.md) components with [isotropy](isotropy.md), and he explicitly compared the component law with the [normal distribution](normal-distribution.md) of observational errors. Independence is an assumption of that argument; perpendicular coordinate axes alone do not establish it. [Maxwell (1860), original article scan with annotations](https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/teaching/2559_Therm_Stat_Mech/handouts/Maxwell_1860_annot.pdf#page=5)
