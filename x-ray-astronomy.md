# X-ray astronomy

↑ **Parent:** [Observational astronomy](observational-astronomy.md)  
ⓦ [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_astronomy)

[X-ray astronomy](x-ray-astronomy.md) infers the properties of celestial sources from their [X-rays](x-ray.md). It requires instruments above the strongly absorbing layers of [Earth](earth.md)'s [terrestrial atmosphere](terrestrial-atmosphere.md). [Riccardo Giacconi](riccardo-giacconi.md)'s 2002 Nobel lecture connects the early rocket detections to later [grazing-incidence X-ray optics](grazing-incidence-x-ray-optics.md), which collect [photons](photon.md) over a large entrance area onto a small detector.[The Dawn of X-Ray Astronomy, introduction and section 4, pp. 123–124](the-dawn-of-x-ray-astronomy-by-riccardo-giacconi.md)

The measurement is an [inverse problem](inverse-problem-split.md): a count rate mixes incident [photon](photon.md) arrival rate, detector response, and background. A [point-like astronomical source](point-like-astronomical-source.md) produces a direction-dependent excess as the instrument scans; [diffuse X-ray background](diffuse-x-ray-background.md) contributes over many directions. Neither a count excess nor a narrow feature by itself determines the emission mechanism. The useful progression is from detecting an excess, to locating it, to measuring its [spectrum](spectrum-functional-analysis.md) and [light curve](light-curve.md).
