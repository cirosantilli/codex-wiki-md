# Exponential-rate likelihood-ratio test

↑ **Parent:** [Neyman-Pearson lemma](neyman-pearson-lemma.md)

For independent [exponential distribution](exponential-distribution.md) observations with rate $\lambda$, the likelihood ratio for a larger simple rate is decreasing in their sum. The [Neyman-Pearson lemma](neyman-pearson-lemma.md) therefore selects a lower-tail sum threshold $c$. The sum has a [gamma distribution](gamma-distribution.md), giving the displayed power; its derivative is $c e^{-\lambda c}(\lambda c)^{n-1}/(n-1)!>0$. Calibrating at $\lambda_0$ gives size $\alpha$ for the composite null $\lambda\le\lambda_0$ and a uniformly most powerful test against larger rates.
