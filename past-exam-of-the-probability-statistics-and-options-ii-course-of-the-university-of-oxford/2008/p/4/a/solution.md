<h1 id="p/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A real square [QR factorization](../../../../../qr-decomposition.md) is $A=QR$ with $Q^\mathsf TQ=I$ and $R$ upper triangular. For an [invertible matrix](../../../../../invertible-matrix.md) it can be made unique by requiring positive diagonal entries of $R$.

If $A$ is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md), then $R=Q^\mathsf TA$ is [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) as well. An [orthogonal](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) [upper triangular matrix](../../../../../upper-triangular-matrix.md) is diagonal with entries $\pm1$: its first column has just its first entry, which must have modulus one, and [orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) then zeros the rest of the first row; repeat inductively on the lower block. Thus

$$
\boxed{R=\operatorname{diag}(\pm1,\ldots,\pm1),}
$$

and under the positive-diagonal convention $\boxed{R=I}$, $Q=A$.
