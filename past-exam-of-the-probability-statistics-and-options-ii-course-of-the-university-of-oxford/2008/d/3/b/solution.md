<h1 id="d/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Let $S_N=\sum_{k=0}^N a_k$. These are nonnegative measurable functions increasing to the extended-valued sum $S$. [Monotone convergence](../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\int S=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\int a_k<\infty.}
$$

A [nonnegative function](../../../../../nonnegative-function.md) with finite integral cannot be infinite on a set of positive measure. Hence $S(x)<\infty$ [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), which is exactly almost-everywhere convergence of the nonnegative series. One may redefine the sum arbitrarily on the exceptional [null set](../../../../../null-set.md) to obtain an ordinary [integrable](../../../../../integrability.md) representative.
