<h1 id="d/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

On the product of two sigma-finite measure spaces, [Tonelli's theorem](../../../../../tonelli-theorem.md) states that a nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md) has measurable iterated-integral functions and

$$
\int_{X\times Y}f\,d(\mu\times\nu)=\int_X\int_Yf\,d\nu\,d\mu=\int_Y\int_Xf\,d\mu\,d\nu,
$$

with values allowed to be infinite. Fubini's theorem states that if $f$ is measurable and $\int_{X\times Y}|f|<\infty$, then its sections are [integrable](../../../../../integrability.md) [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), both iterated integrals exist as finite integrals, and the same equality holds, also for real or complex signed functions. Tonelli applied to $|f|$ can first be used to verify the absolute-integrability hypothesis of Fubini.
