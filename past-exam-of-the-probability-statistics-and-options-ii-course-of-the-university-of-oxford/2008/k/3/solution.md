<h1 id="k/3/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [3](../3.md)

For [wall-induced motion of a planar potential dipole](../../../../wall-induced-motion-of-a-planar-potential-dipole.md), the [complex velocity](../../../../complex-velocity.md) convention is $F'(z)=v_x-iv_y$. A singularity transported by the surrounding flow therefore satisfies $\dot{\bar a}=F'_{\mathrm{reg}}(a)$, where its own term $m/(z-a)$ is removed before differentiating. The images at a given instant depend on that instantaneous position, but the spatial [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) treats their parameters as fixed.

For the circular wall, the [derivative](../../../../derivative.md) of the regular image is $\bar m/(1-\bar a z)^2$. For the quadrant, differentiate its three image terms. For the sloping wall, differentiate its single image term. This gives respectively

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\text{(a)}\quad\dot{\bar a}&=\frac{\bar m}{(1-|a|^2)^2},\\
\text{(b)}\quad\dot{\bar a}&=-\frac{\bar m}{(a-\bar a)^2}+\frac{\bar m}{(a+\bar a)^2}+\frac m{4a^2},\\
\text{(c)}\quad\dot{\bar a}&=\frac{i\bar m}{(a+i\bar a)^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$

These are equations for $d\bar z_1/dt$, as printed. Conjugating gives equations for $dz_1/dt$ if desired; no self-induced singular velocity is included.
