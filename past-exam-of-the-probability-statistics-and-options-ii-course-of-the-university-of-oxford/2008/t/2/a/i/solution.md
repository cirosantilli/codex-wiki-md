<h1 id="t/2/a/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

A [mixed strategy](../../../../../../mixed-strategy.md) for Player II is a probability vector $q\ge0$ with $\sum_jq_j=1$, specifying the random choice of a column. If Player I chooses row $i$, the expected payment is $(Aq)_i$. A mixture of rows cannot exceed the [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) of these row expectations, so the smallest payment Player II can guarantee is

$$
\boxed{v=\min_{q\ge0,\ \mathbf1^\mathsf Tq=1}\max_i(Aq)_i.}
$$

An optimal strategy is a vector attaining that [minimum](../../../../../../minimum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md), which exists by [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of the [probability simplex](../../../../../../probability-simplex.md) and [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md). The [minimax theorem](../../../../../../minimax-theorem.md) identifies this guaranteed loss with Player I's maximal guaranteed gain, the value of the finite [zero-sum game](../../../../../../zero-sum-game.md).
