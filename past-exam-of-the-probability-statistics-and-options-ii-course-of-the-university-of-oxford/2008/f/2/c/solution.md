<h1 id="f/2/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

If a [differentiable](../../../../../differentiable-function.md) function has a constrained local extremum at a point of a smooth surface, its [derivative](../../../../../derivative.md) vanishes in every tangent direction; equivalently its [gradient](../../../../../gradient.md) is normal to the surface. For a regular equation $F=0$ this means $\nabla V=\lambda\nabla F$ for some real multiplier.

First the box is centred at the origin: holding the other coordinates of a [vertex of a polyhedron](../../../../../vertex-of-a-polyhedron.md) fixed and comparing the two possible coordinates $u,v$ in the first direction gives $u^2=v^2$, so the distinct extremes are opposites; the other two directions give the same conclusion. Write its positive half-lengths as $a r,b s,c t$, with $r^2+s^2+t^2=1$. Its volume is $8abc\,rst$. A degenerate box has zero volume, so a positive [maximum](../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is interior in $r,s,t>0$. [Lagrange multipliers](../../../../../lagrange-multiplier.md) applied to $\log(rst)$ give $1/r=2\lambda r$, and the analogous equations imply $r=s=t=1/\sqrt3$. Alternatively the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality applied to $r^2,s^2,t^2$ proves global maximality. Thus

$$
\boxed{\text{side lengths }\frac{2a}{\sqrt3},\frac{2b}{\sqrt3},\frac{2c}{\sqrt3},\qquad V_{\max}=\frac{8abc}{3\sqrt3}.}
$$
