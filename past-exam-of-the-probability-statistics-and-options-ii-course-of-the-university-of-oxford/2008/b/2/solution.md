<h1 id="b/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

The [characteristic](../../../../characteristic-of-a-field.md) is the smallest positive integer $p$ with $p1_F=0$. Such an integer exists because the additive [group](../../../../group-split.md) is finite. If it factored as $p=ab$ with $1<a,b<p$, then $(a1_F)(b1_F)=0$ with both factors nonzero, contradicting the absence of zero divisors. Hence it is [prime](../../../../prime-number-split.md). The elements generated by $1_F$ form a subfield isomorphic to $\mathbb F_p$. The finite set $F$ is a [finite-dimensional vector space](../../../../finite-dimensional-vector-space.md) of some [dimension](../../../../dimension-vector-space.md) $a\ge1$ over that subfield, so $\boxed{|F|=p^a}$.

A nonzero [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) of [polynomial degree](../../../../degree-of-a-polynomial.md) $n$ over a [field](../../../../field.md) has at most $n$ distinct roots. This follows by [induction](../../../../mathematical-induction.md): a root $r$ gives a factor $X-r$, and every different root must be a root of the remaining factor by cancellation. Thus $X^n-1$ has at most $n$ roots. In the finite multiplicative [group](../../../../group-split.md), the elements whose order divides $n$ are exactly those satisfying $x^n=1$. The supplied cyclicity criterion therefore proves that **the multiplicative [group](../../../../group-split.md) is cyclic**.

For the [prime](../../../../prime-number-split.md) of the specified form write $m=2^r s$ with $s$ odd. If $s>1$, then $2^{2^r}+1$ is a proper factor of $(2^{2^r})^s+1$, impossible. Thus $m$ is a [power of two](../../../../power-of-two.md). Since $2^m\equiv-1\pmod p$, the order of two divides $2m$ but does not divide $m$. All divisors of the [power of two](../../../../power-of-two.md) $2m$ other than $2m$ itself divide $m$. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=2m.}
$$

It generates the multiplicative [group](../../../../group-split.md) precisely when $2m=p-1=2^m$. For positive integers $m$ this holds exactly at $m=1,2$: from $m=3$ onward $2^m>2m$, and the inequality persists on increasing $m$. These give $\boxed{p=3\text{ or }5}$, equivalently the stated range among Fermat [primes](../../../../prime-number-split.md).
