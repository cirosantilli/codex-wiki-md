<h1 id="r/2/i/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For a finite [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) choose a simple path of [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) length. Every neighbor of its final [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) must already lie on the path, since otherwise that [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) could be appended. The final [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) has [degree](../../../../../../degree-graph-theory.md) at least two, so besides its predecessor it has another neighbor earlier on the path. That [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) together with the intervening path segment makes a [cycle in a graph](../../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md).

Finiteness is needed for this assertion in general: the two-sided infinite path has [degree](../../../../../../degree-graph-theory.md) two at every [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and no [cycle in a graph](../../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md). The customary finite-[graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) convention is used here and in the subsequent cube questions.
