<h1 id="h/2/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Measure the axial coordinate $z$ from the [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). The slice radius is $z\tan\alpha$ and its [mass](../../../../../mass.md) is $dm=3mz^2H^{-3}\,dz$, since cross-sectional area is proportional to $z^2$. Its axial moment is $\tfrac12(z\tan\alpha)^2dm$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{C_O=\frac{3mH^2}{10}\tan^2\alpha.}
$$

For an axis through the [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) perpendicular to the [cone](../../../../../cone-geometry.md) axis, a thin disc contributes $[z^2+\tfrac14z^2\tan^2\alpha]dm$: the second term is its diametral moment. Integration gives the two equal transverse principal moments

$$
\boxed{A_O=B_O=\frac{3mH^2}{5}\left(1+\frac{\tan^2\alpha}{4}\right).}
$$

Rotational symmetry eliminates the mixed moments and makes these the principal axes. The [centre of mass](../../../../../center-of-mass.md) is at $3H/4$, so the parallel-axis theorem would give $A_G=3mH^2/80+3mH^2\tan^2\alpha/20$ and $C_G=C_O$.
