<h1 id="h/2/b/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

The coordinate $\psi$ is cyclic, so $p_\psi=C(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)$ is conserved. Initially the spin is zero, hence $\boxed{\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta=0}$ throughout. The coordinate $\phi$ is also cyclic. After imposing this zero spin, its conserved momentum becomes $(A+mh^2)\dot\phi\sin^2\theta$. Initially $\theta=\pi/2$ and $\dot\phi=\omega$, so $\boxed{\dot\phi\sin^2\theta=\omega}$.

The equation for $z$ is

$$
\frac d{dt}\left[m(\dot z-h\sin\theta\dot\theta)\right]=-mg.
$$

The quantity in parentheses is the vertical velocity of the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md). Initially that velocity and its height are both zero: the initial [rotation](../../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) is horizontal and the [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) starts at rest at the reference height. Twice integrating gives

$$
\boxed{z+h\cos\theta=-\frac12gt^2.}
$$

Independently, the frictionless vertical wire exerts only horizontal force. [Gravity](../../../../../../gravity.md) is therefore the only vertical external force on the body, and the [centre of mass](../../../../../../center-of-mass.md) undergoes vertical free fall. This gives the same equation without the angular equations.
