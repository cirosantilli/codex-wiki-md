↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

**The scheme is not uniform in general.** A particular successful path has [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) equal to the product of the reciprocals of its available-neighbour counts. Those counts depend on the path geometry.

For length four, the straight path $(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(4,0)$ has [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) $1/(4\cdot3\cdot3\cdot3)=1/108$. The path $(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1),(-1,1)$ has only two available neighbours at its last choice, because the previous point and the origin block two directions. Its [probability](../../../../../../probability.md) is $1/(4\cdot3\cdot3\cdot2)=1/72$. There is no trapping before four steps, so these are already the output probabilities. They differ, disproving uniformity on $S_4$.

At lengths where restarts occur, conditioning an attempt on success normalizes these path probabilities by a common success [probability](../../../../../../probability.md); it does not remove the unequal products.
