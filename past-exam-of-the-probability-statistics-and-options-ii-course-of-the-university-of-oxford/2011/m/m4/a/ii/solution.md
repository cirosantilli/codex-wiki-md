<h1 id="m/m4/a/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

A [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) is useful for proving convergence in distribution, including for laws without a finite [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md): the continuity theorem says that pointwise convergence of characteristic functions to a [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) continuous at zero implies convergence in distribution to its law. For example, the [Cauchy distribution](../../../../../../cauchy-distribution.md) has a [characteristic function](../../../../../../characteristic-function-split.md) although its [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) is not finite on any neighbourhood of zero.

A [moment-generating function](../../../../../../moment-generating-function.md) is especially useful for exponential tail bounds: for $t>0$ where it is finite, [Markov's inequality](../../../../../../markov-s-inequality.md) gives

$$
\boxed{\Pr(X\geq a)\leq e^{-ta}M_X(t).}
$$

Optimizing over $t$ yields a [Chernoff bound](../../../../../../chernoff-bound.md). The positive real exponential in this argument permits the inequality; a unit-modulus complex exponential does not give the corresponding tail bound.
