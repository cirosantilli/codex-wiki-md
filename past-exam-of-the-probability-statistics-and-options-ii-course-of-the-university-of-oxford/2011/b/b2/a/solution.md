↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) is a finite chain $1=G_0\lhd G_1\lhd\cdots\lhd G_r=G$ in which each inclusion is proper, each [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) is normal in the next, and every quotient $G_i/G_{i-1}$ is a nontrivial [simple group](../../../../../simple-group.md). These quotients are the [composition factors](../../../../../composition-factor.md).

For a finite group, use induction on its order. The trivial group has the empty series. Otherwise choose a maximal proper [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) $N$; such a [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) exists because there are finitely many subgroups and $1$ is proper. The correspondence theorem says normal subgroups of $G/N$ correspond to normal subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, so maximality makes $G/N$ simple. Inductively construct a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) of the smaller group $N$ and append $G$. This proves existence.

A nonabelian example with abelian factors is

$$
\boxed{1\lhd A_3\lhd S_3,\qquad\text{factors }C_3,C_2.}
$$

Both factors are simple because their orders are prime.
