<h1 id="b/b2/d/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [D](../d.md)

Given a prime factorization $n=p_1\cdots p_r$, repeatedly apply part (c) to the [cyclic group](../../../../../cyclic-group.md) of order $n$: it has a chain of subgroups of successive orders

$$
n,\quad n/p_1,\quad n/(p_1p_2),\quad\ldots,\quad1.
$$

Read in increasing order, this is a [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) with factors isomorphic to the cyclic groups of orders $p_1,\ldots,p_r$. Any second prime factorization of the same integer produces another [composition series](../../../../../composition-series.md) of the same group $C_n$.

The [Jordan–Hölder theorem](../../../../../jordan-holder-theorem.md) equates the two multisets of simple factors. Since $C_p\cong C_q$ for primes $p,q$ implies $p=q$ by equality of group orders, the two prime multisets agree. Hence

$$
\boxed{\text{The prime factorization is unique up to permutation}.}
$$

For $n=1$ both factor lists are empty, corresponding to the trivial group.
