<h1 id="d/d4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

The partial sums $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^na_k$ are nonnegative measurable functions and increase pointwise to the extended-valued sum $S=\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k$. The [monotone convergence theorem](../../../../../monotone-convergence-theorem.md) and finite linearity give

$$
\int S\,dx=\lim_n\sum_{k=1}^n\int a_k\,dx
=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\int a_k\,dx<\infty.
$$

A nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md) with finite integral is finite [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md); if it were infinite on a positive-measure set, its integral would be infinite. Define it arbitrarily on the null exceptional set if a real-valued representative is desired. Hence the series converges [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md) to an integrable function and

$$
\boxed{\int\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k(x)\,dx=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\int a_k(x)\,dx.}
$$
