<h1 id="c/c2/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Choose a nonzero quadratic nonresidue $c$ modulo $q$. By the [Chinese remainder theorem](../../../../../../chinese-remainder-theorem.md), choose a positive integer $k$ satisfying

$$
k\equiv1\pmod4,\qquad k\equiv1\pmod b,\qquad k\equiv c\pmod q.
$$

The moduli are pairwise [coprime](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md), and $k$ is [coprime](../../../../../../coprime-integers.md) to $qb$. Jacobi reciprocity, with $k\equiv1\pmod4$, gives

$$
\left(\frac bk\right)=\left(\frac kb\right)=1,\qquad
\left(\frac qk\right)=\left(\frac kq\right)=-1,
\qquad\left(\frac{-1}k\right)=1.
$$

Since $t$ is odd, multiplying these symbols yields

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\pm q^t b}k\right)=(-1)^t=-1.}
$$

When $b=1$ its congruence imposes no restriction and its Jacobi factor equals one.
