<h1 id="b/b2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Choose one regular [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) inscribed in the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md) and let $H$ be its stabilizer among [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) of the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md). The twelve [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) of that [tetrahedron](../../../../../tetrahedron.md) preserve the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md); geometrically these are its [identity](../../../../../identity-element.md), eight [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) by $\pm2\pi/3$ about tetrahedral [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) axes and three half-turns about opposite tetrahedral [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) axes. They act faithfully on its four [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and give the twelve even permutations. Thus $\boxed{H\cong A_4,\quad|H|=12}$ without using part (ii).

To check the core condition geometrically, the fifteen half-turns of the [dodecahedron](../../../../../dodecahedron.md), about opposite-[edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md)-midpoint axes, are conjugate under its [rotations](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md). A [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) contained in $H$ cannot contain even one of these half-turns, because normality would then force all fifteen into a [group](../../../../../group-split.md) of order twelve. On the other hand every nontrivial [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of $A_4$ contains a half-turn: if it contains a three-cycle, its conjugates and their inverses generate $A_4$; otherwise its nonidentity elements are among the three double transpositions, which are conjugate in $A_4$. Apply this observation to any [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) of the full [rotation](../../../../../rotation-mathematics.md) [group](../../../../../group-split.md) lying inside $H$. It must be trivial, proving exactly the required condition.
