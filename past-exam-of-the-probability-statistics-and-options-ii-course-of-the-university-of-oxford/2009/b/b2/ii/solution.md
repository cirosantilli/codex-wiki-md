<h1 id="b/b2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

Act by left multiplication on the five [cosets](../../../../../coset.md) of $H$. The kernel is the core $\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}$, a [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) contained in $H$, so the hypothesis makes it trivial. Thus $G$ embeds in $S_5$, and its image has order $60$ and index two. An index-two [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) is normal, so the quotient is of order two. Every three-cycle maps trivially to that quotient, since its order divides three. Three-cycles generate $A_5$, so the image contains $A_5$; their equal order gives $\boxed{G\cong A_5}$.

For [completeness](../../../../../completeness.md), prove simplicity rather than merely invoking it. The nonidentity even cycle types in $S_5$ are three-cycles, products of two disjoint transpositions and five-cycles. In $A_5$, a three-cycle has [centralizer](../../../../../centralizer.md) of order three, so its [conjugacy class](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md) has size $20$. A double transposition has [centralizer](../../../../../centralizer.md) of order four in $A_5$, giving class size $15$. A five-cycle has [centralizer](../../../../../centralizer.md) its cyclic [subgroup](../../../../../subgroup.md) of order five, giving class size $12$; the $24$ such elements therefore split into two classes of size $12$. These account for all $60$ elements with the [identity](../../../../../identity-element.md).

A [normal subgroup](../../../../../normal-subgroup.md) is a union of whole [conjugacy classes](../../../../../conjugacy-class.md), containing the [identity](../../../../../identity-element.md), and its order must divide $60$. No nonempty proper sum of the class sizes $15,20,12,12$, plus one, is a divisor of $60$ greater than one: the possible proper nontrivial sums are $13,16,21,25,28,33,36,40,45,48$. Only the full union has order $60$. Hence $\boxed{A_5\text{ is simple}}$, and so is $G$.
