↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

For product measure spaces, [Tonelli's theorem](../../../../../tonelli-theorem.md) states that for a nonnegative [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md) the product-space integral equals either iterated integral, allowing the value $+\infty$. Fubini's theorem states that if a [measurable function](../../../../../measurable-function.md) has finite integral of its absolute value, then its sections are [integrable](../../../../../integrability.md) [almost everywhere](../../../../../almost-everywhere.md), both iterated integrals exist and equal the product-space integral. The absolute-integrability hypothesis is what permits this conclusion for a signed function. These statements apply to the Lebesgue product spaces used here, which are sigma-finite.
