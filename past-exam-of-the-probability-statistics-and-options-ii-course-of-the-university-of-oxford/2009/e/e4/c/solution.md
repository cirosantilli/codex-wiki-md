<h1 id="e/e4/c/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [C](../c.md)

The difference map $(z,w)\mapsto z-w$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) and $X$ is its [inverse](../../../../../inverse-element.md) image of the [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, so $X$ is open in $\mathbb C^2$. For fixed $z$, the [equivalence class](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) is $\{z\}\times(\mathbb C\setminus\{z\})$, [homeomorphic](../../../../../homeomorphism.md) to a punctured plane. It is [path-connected](../../../../../path-connected-space.md): two points can be joined by a polygonal path avoiding the missing point. Thus all [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) are [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md).

The projection $\pi$ is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) and onto, since $(z,z+1)\in X$. If $U$ is open in $X$, it is open in $\mathbb C^2$ because $X$ is open. Product-coordinate projection is open: around any $(z,w)\in U$ there is a product of open disks contained in $U$, whose first disk is a neighborhood inside $\pi(U)$. Hence $\pi(U)$ is open.

Its fibers are precisely the [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md), so it induces a [bijection](../../../../../bijection.md) $\bar\pi:X/R\to\mathbb C$. [Continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) follows from the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) and $\pi=\bar\pi\circ q$. If $V$ is open in $X/R$, then $q^{-1}(V)$ is open in $X$ and $\bar\pi(V)=\pi(q^{-1}(V))$ is open. Thus the [inverse](../../../../../inverse-element.md) is [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) too, proving $\boxed{X/R\cong\mathbb C}$ as [topological spaces](../../../../../topological-space.md). The plane is [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md), so the [connected](../../../../../connected-space.md)-fiber equivalence in (b)(ii) gives $\boxed{X\text{ is connected}}$.
