<h1 id="m/m4/e/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For fixed $\theta$, [Taylor expansion](../../../../../../taylor-expansion.md) at zero gives $e^h-1-h=h^2/2+O(h^3)$. With $h=\theta/\sqrt{\lambda t}$, the logarithm of the moment generating function tends to $\theta^2/2$. Thus $M_{U_t}(\theta)\to e^{\theta^2/2}$ on a neighborhood of zero. The [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) theorem for moment generating functions identifies the limit as

$$
\boxed{U_t\Rightarrow N(0,1)\quad(t\to\infty).}
$$

This is the centered Poisson normal limit with its exact [variance](../../../../../../variance-split.md) scaling.
