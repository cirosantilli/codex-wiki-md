<h1 id="p/p3/d/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Set $u=\sum_{k=1}^n\langle x,e_k\rangle e_k$, with the convention that the [inner product](../../../../../../inner-product-split.md) is linear in its first argument. [Orthonormality](../../../../../../orthonormal-set.md) gives $\langle x-u,e_j\rangle=0$, hence $x-u\perp U_n$. For any $v\in U_n$ the [Pythagorean identity](../../../../../../pythagorean-theorem-in-an-inner-product-space.md) yields

$$
\|x-v\|^2=\|x-u\|^2+\|u-v\|^2\ge\|x-u\|^2.
$$

Thus this $u$ is the [best approximation](../../../../../../best-approximation-in-a-normed-space.md), and uniqueness gives $\boxed{P_nx=\sum_{k=1}^n\langle x,e_k\rangle e_k}$. No [completeness](../../../../../../completeness.md) assumption on $H$ is necessary for this finite-dimensional construction.
