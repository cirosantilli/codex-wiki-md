↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

Assume the intended nondegenerate [interval](../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) $a<b$ and real-valued [continuous functions](../../../../../continuous-function.md). The integral is bilinear and symmetric. Since $w>0$, it gives $\langle f,f\rangle\ge0$. If a [continuous](../../../../../continuous-function.md) $f$ is nonzero at some point, [continuity](../../../../../continuous-function.md) makes $f^2w$ strictly positive on a subinterval of positive length, so its integral is positive. Hence $\langle f,f\rangle=0$ forces $f=0$, proving positive definiteness. [Orthogonality](../../../../../orthogonal-vectors.md) means $\boxed{\int_a^bf(x)g(x)w(x)dx=0}$. If $a=b$, the displayed integral is identically zero and is not an [inner product](../../../../../inner-product-split.md) on the stated function space; that degenerate case must be excluded.
