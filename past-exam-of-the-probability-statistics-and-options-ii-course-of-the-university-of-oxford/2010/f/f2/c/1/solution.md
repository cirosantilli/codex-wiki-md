<h1 id="f/f2/c/1/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [1](../1.md)

Work on the positive orthant, where the real powers defining $g$ are smooth. Its closure under the constraint is the [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) weighted simplex $\overline M=\{x_i\ge0,\sum p_ix_i=1\}$, since $0\le x_i\le1/p_i$. The product extends continuously there, is zero on its boundary when $n>1$, and is one at $(1,\ldots,1)$. Thus a [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is attained in the positive interior.

At an interior maximizer the constraint [gradient](../../../../../../gradient.md) $(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ is nonzero. The multiplier equations give

$$
p_i\frac{g(x)}{x_i}=\lambda p_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_i=g(x)/\lambda\text{ for every }i.
$$

All coordinates are equal, and the weighted constraint forces each to equal one. Therefore the [maximum](../../../../../../maximum-of-a-subset-of-a-total-order.md) is one and $\boxed{g(x)\le1}$ on $M$. For $n=1$ the constraint itself gives $x_1=1$, so the same conclusion holds directly. Equality occurs exactly at the all-ones point.
