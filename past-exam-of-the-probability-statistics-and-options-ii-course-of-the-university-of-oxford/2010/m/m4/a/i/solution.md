↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

The [inverse](../../../../../../inverse-element.md) change of variables is $x=u$, $y=uv$, whose [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) is $u$. Since $X,Y$ are [independent](../../../../../../independent-random-variables.md), the original [joint density](../../../../../../joint-probability-density.md) is $f(x)g(y)$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f_{U,V}(u,v)=|u|f(u)g(uv)\quad(u,v\in\mathbb R).}
$$

The transformation is nonsingular away from $u=0$; this line has probability zero because $X$ has a [density](../../../../../../probability-density-function.md), so no extra mass is omitted.
