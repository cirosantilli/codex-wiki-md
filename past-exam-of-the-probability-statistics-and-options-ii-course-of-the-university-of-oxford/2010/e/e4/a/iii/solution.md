<h1 id="e/e4/a/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

In any [topological space](../../../../../../topological-space.md) a closed subset of a [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) set is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md): add its open complement to an open cover and take a finite subcover of the [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) set. However, [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) subsets need not be closed in a general space. In the indiscrete two-point space a singleton is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) but not closed. Conversely a closed subset need not be [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md): $\mathbb R$ itself is closed in $\mathbb R$ but the cover $\{(-n,n):n\ge1\}$ has no finite subcover.

In a [Hausdorff space](../../../../../../hausdorff-space.md) every [compact subset](../../../../../../compact-space.md) $K$ is closed. Given $x\notin K$, separate $x$ and each $k\in K$ by disjoint neighborhoods; [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) selects finitely many neighborhoods covering $K$, and the intersection of their companion neighborhoods of $x$ misses $K$. Thus the complement is open. Closedness still need not imply [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md), as the same real-line example shows. It does imply [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) if the ambient space is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md).

In a [metric space](../../../../../../metric-space.md) [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) implies [boundedness](../../../../../../boundedness.md): finitely many radius-one balls cover the set, so their centers have a finite maximal distance from any fixed center and the triangle inequality gives a global bound. The converse is false. An infinite set with discrete metric $d(x,y)=1$ for $x\ne y$ is [bounded](../../../../../../boundedness.md), but its singleton cover has no finite subcover. It is even closed and [complete](../../../../../../completeness.md), so closedness plus [boundedness](../../../../../../boundedness.md) alone does not suffice in general [metric spaces](../../../../../../metric-space.md).
