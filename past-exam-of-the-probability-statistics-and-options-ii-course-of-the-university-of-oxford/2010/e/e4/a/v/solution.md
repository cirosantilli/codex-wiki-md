<h1 id="e/e4/a/v/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [V](../v.md)

The image $f(K)$ is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md): pull any open cover back by the [continuous map](../../../../../../continuous-map.md), use a finite subcover of $K$, then map forward. For a general topological target, closedness is not guaranteed; a [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) image into the indiscrete two-point space can be a nonclosed singleton. No [connectedness](../../../../../../connected-space.md) assertion follows merely from [compactness](../../../../../../compact-space.md) of $K$.

If $Y$ is metric, $f(K)$ is [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md), therefore closed in $Y$, [bounded](../../../../../../boundedness.md) and [totally bounded](../../../../../../totally-bounded-space.md). It is also [complete](../../../../../../completeness.md) in its induced metric and [sequentially compact](../../../../../../sequentially-compact-space.md). For the latter properties, [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) [metric spaces](../../../../../../metric-space.md) have a convergent subsequence of every sequence; a [Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) with one convergent subsequence converges to that same limit. Total [boundedness](../../../../../../boundedness.md) follows by selecting finitely many balls of each prescribed radius. These are properties of the image itself, irrespective of whether $Y$ is [complete](../../../../../../completeness.md).
