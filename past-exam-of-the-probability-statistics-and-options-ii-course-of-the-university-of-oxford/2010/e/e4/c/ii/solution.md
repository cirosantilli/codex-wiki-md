<h1 id="e/e4/c/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

[Continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) and nonvanishing on the [compact](../../../../../../compact-space.md) [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) imply

$$
0<m:=\min_{[0,1]}|\alpha|\le\max_{[0,1]}|\alpha|=:M<\infty.
$$

Hence $m\|f-g\|_\infty\le d_\alpha(f,g)\le M\|f-g\|_\infty$. A $d_\alpha$-[Cauchy sequence](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md) is therefore uniformly [Cauchy](../../../../../../cauchy-sequence.md), so (i) gives a [continuous](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) [uniform limit](../../../../../../uniform-limit.md); the upper bound then gives convergence in $d_\alpha$. Thus $\boxed{(C([0,1]),d_\alpha)\text{ is complete}}$. The positive lower bound is what makes the argument work.
