A sufficient differentiation theorem is the following local version. Suppose $u(t,\cdot)$ is [integrable](../../../integrability.md), $u(\cdot,x)$ is continuously [differentiable](../../../differentiable-function.md) for almost every $x$, and for each [compact](../../../compact-space.md) parameter [interval](../../../interval-mathematics.md) there is an [integrable](../../../integrability.md) $g(x)$ bounding $|\partial_tu(t,x)|$ uniformly there. Then $I'(t)=\int_E\partial_tu(t,x)\,dx$. The [mean value theorem](../../../mean-value-theorem.md) bounds the difference quotients by $g$, and [dominated convergence](../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) proves the formula. Integrability at one parameter value together with the [derivative](../../../derivative.md) bound also suffices locally.
