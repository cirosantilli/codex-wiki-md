<h1 id="d/d4/1/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

The $k$th [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md) of the integrand is $(\cos x)^k\cos(t\cos x+k\pi/2)$, whose absolute value is at most one. The integration [interval](../../../../../../interval-mathematics.md) has finite measure. Repeated application of the differentiation theorem therefore gives

$$
\boxed{J_0^{(k)}(t)=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}(\cos x)^k\cos(t\cos x+k\pi/2)\,dx\quad(k\ge0).}
$$

[Dominated convergence](../../../../../../dominated-convergence-theorem.md) also gives [continuity](../../../../../../continuous-function.md) of every [derivative](../../../../../../derivative.md), proving infinite [differentiability](../../../../../../differentiability.md).
