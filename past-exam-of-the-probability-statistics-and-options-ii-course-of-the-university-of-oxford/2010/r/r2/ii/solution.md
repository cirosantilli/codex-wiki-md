↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

There are ten labels. The successive deleted [leaves of a graph](../../../../../leaf-of-a-graph.md) are $3,4,5,1,6,7,2,9$, paired with the respective entries $1,1,1,2,2,2,8,8$. The final two labels are $8,10$. Thus the [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) has [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) set

$$
\boxed{\{13,14,15,12,26,27,28,89,8\!\!\mathbin{-}\!10\}.}
$$

Here $ij$ denotes the unordered [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) $\{i,j\}$; the last [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is written with a separator to distinguish label ten. Equivalently its adjacency lists are $1:\{2,3,4,5\}$, $2:\{1,6,7,8\}$, $8:\{2,9,10\}$, with all other [vertices](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) [leaves of a graph](../../../../../leaf-of-a-graph.md). These lists make the constructed [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) unambiguous.
