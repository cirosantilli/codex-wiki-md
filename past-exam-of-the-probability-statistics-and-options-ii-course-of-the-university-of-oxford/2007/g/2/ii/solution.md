<h1 id="g/2/ii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Ii](../ii.md)

For $F(y,y')=y\sqrt{1+y'^2}$, the [Euler-Lagrange equation](../../../../../euler-lagrange-equation.md) is

$$
\sqrt{1+y'^2}-\frac d{dx}\frac{yy'}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}=0,
\qquad yy''=1+y'^2.
$$

Since $F$ has no explicit $x$ dependence, the [Beltrami identity](../../../../../beltrami-identity.md) gives

$$
F-y'F_{y'}=\frac{y}{\sqrt{1+y'^2}}=C>0.
$$

Solving this [first integral](../../../../../first-integral.md), or substituting into the second-order equation, yields

$$
\boxed{y(x)=C\cosh\!\left(\frac{x-K}{C}\right),\qquad C>0.}
$$

The constants satisfy $y_1=C\cosh((x_1-K)/C)$ and $y_2=C\cosh((x_2-K)/C)$. There need not be a solution for arbitrary endpoint data, and when there is one these equations can have more than one pair of constants. These are the possible stationary [catenoids](../../../../../catenoid.md), so the [axisymmetric second variation of a catenoid](../../../../../axisymmetric-second-variation-of-a-catenoid.md) still matters when deciding whether they minimize [surface area](../../../../../surface-area.md).
