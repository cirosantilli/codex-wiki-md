<h1 id="s/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For every [perfect matching](../../../../../perfect-matching.md) $M$, each [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) occurs exactly once, so the feasibility inequalities give

$$
\sum_{e\in M}c_e\ge\sum_{\{u,v\}\in M}(x_u+x_v)=\sum_{v\in V}x_v.
$$

If a [perfect matching](../../../../../perfect-matching.md) $M^*$ uses only tight [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), equality holds. It therefore attains a lower bound valid for every [perfect matching](../../../../../perfect-matching.md), proving **minimum cost**. In a [bipartite graph](../../../../../bipartite-graph-split.md) this is the [weak duality](../../../../../weak-duality.md) and [complementary slackness](../../../../../complementary-slackness.md) certificate for the [assignment problem](../../../../../assignment-problem.md). The [Hungarian algorithm](../../../../../hungarian-algorithm.md) maintains feasible row and column potentials and seeks a [perfect matching](../../../../../perfect-matching.md) among tight [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md).
