<h1 id="b/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

An element $\alpha$ is an [algebraic element](../../../../algebraic-element.md) over $F$ if some nonzero [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md) in $F[X]$ vanishes at $\alpha$. A [field extension](../../../../field-extension.md) is an [algebraic extension](../../../../algebraic-extension.md) if each of its elements is an [algebraic element](../../../../algebraic-element.md) over $F$. If $[K:F]=d<\infty$, the $d+1$ [vectors](../../../../vector.md) $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^d$ are [linearly dependent](../../../../linear-dependence.md), producing such a [polynomial](../../../../polynomial-split.md). Thus every [finite field extension](../../../../finite-field-extension.md) is an [algebraic extension](../../../../algebraic-extension.md).

The converse fails for the [field of algebraic numbers](../../../../field-of-algebraic-numbers.md) $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. It is an [algebraic extension](../../../../algebraic-extension.md) of $\mathbb Q$ by definition, but it contains primitive $p$th [roots of unity](../../../../root-of-unity.md) of degree $p-1$ for arbitrarily large primes $p$, by the permitted irreducibility of the [cyclotomic polynomial](../../../../cyclotomic-polynomial.md). The [tower law](../../../../tower-law.md) would make every such degree at most $[\overline{\mathbb Q}:\mathbb Q]$ if the latter were finite, a contradiction.
