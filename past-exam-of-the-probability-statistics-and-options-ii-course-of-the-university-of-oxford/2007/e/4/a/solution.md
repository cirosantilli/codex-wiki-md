<h1 id="e/4/a/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

For the canonical [surjection](../../../../../surjective-function.md) $p:X\to X/{\sim}$, define a subset $W$ of $X/{\sim}$ to be open exactly when $p^{-1}(W)$ is open in $X$. This is the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md). The empty set and the whole quotient have open [inverse images](../../../../../preimage.md). Moreover [inverse images](../../../../../preimage.md) commute with arbitrary unions and finite intersections, so the corresponding axioms of a [topology](../../../../../topology-split.md) follow directly from those on $X$.

The map $p$ need not be an [open map](../../../../../open-map.md). For example, on $X=\mathbb R$ identify every point of $[0,1]$ to one point and leave all other classes singletons. The set $U=(-1/2,1/2)$ is open, but

$$
p^{-1}(p(U))=(-1/2,1]
$$

is not open. The definition of the [quotient topology](../../../../../quotient-topology.md) therefore shows that $p(U)$ is not open. The obstruction is that saturating an [open set](../../../../../open-set.md) by [equivalence classes](../../../../../equivalence-class.md) need not preserve openness.
