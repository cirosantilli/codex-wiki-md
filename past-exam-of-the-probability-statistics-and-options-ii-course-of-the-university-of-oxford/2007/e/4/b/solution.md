<h1 id="e/4/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Treat the two squares as a single edge-pairing construction, including the identifications between different squares. Number the corners of the left square counterclockwise from its lower left corner by $A,B,C,D$, and similarly use $E,F,G,H$ on the right. The single arrows identify $D$ with $H$ and $C$ with $E$. The double arrows identify $G$ with $F$ and $H$ with $G$. The triple arrows identify $A$ with $E$ and $B$ with $F$. The quadruple arrows identify $A$ with $C$ and $D$ with $B$.

Thus there are precisely two [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) classes, $\{A,C,E\}$ and $\{B,D,F,G,H\}$. There are four [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) classes and two two-dimensional cells, so the resulting closed connected [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md) has [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md)

$$
\chi=2-4+2=0.
$$

It is a [nonorientable surface](../../../../../non-orientable-surface.md): on the left square the quadruple-arrow sides have the same orientation relative to a traversal of its boundary, giving a twisted band after gluing. Equivalently, no choice of orientations for the two faces makes the induced directions opposite on every paired [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md). The [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) links are circles, so these [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) identifications produce a closed [surface](../../../../../topological-surface-split.md), rather than a singular cell space. The [classification of compact surfaces](../../../../../classification-of-compact-surfaces.md) now gives

$$
\boxed{\text{Klein bottle}.}
$$

A [torus](../../../../../torus.md) also has [Euler characteristic](../../../../../euler-characteristic.md) zero, but is excluded by the failed orientation test.
