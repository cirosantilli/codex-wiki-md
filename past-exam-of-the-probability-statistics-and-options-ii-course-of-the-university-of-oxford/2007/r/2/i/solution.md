<h1 id="r/2/i/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [I](../i.md)

Every facial boundary [walk](../../../../../walk-in-a-graph.md) has length at least five. Bridge excursions can repeat an [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), but deleting those excursions leaves a boundary containing a [cycle in a graph](../../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md); the finite girth assumption ensures the [graph](../../../../../graph-split.md) is not a [tree](../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md). Thus the shortest-cycle condition still gives the lower bound five even when bridges occur. Summing boundary lengths counts each [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) twice, so $5f\le2m$. Using [Euler formula for a connected planar graph](../../../../../euler-formula-for-a-connected-planar-graph.md),

$$
2=n-m+f\le n-m+\frac{2m}{5}=n-\frac{3m}{5}.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{m\le\frac53(n-2).}
$$
