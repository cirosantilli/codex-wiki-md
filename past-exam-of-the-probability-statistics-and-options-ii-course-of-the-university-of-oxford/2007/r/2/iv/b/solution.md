<h1 id="r/2/iv/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

Two dual [faces of a plane graph](../../../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md) share an [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) exactly when their corresponding original [vertices](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) are adjacent. Their face-adjacency [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md) is therefore the original [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md), which is a nontrivial [bipartite graph](../../../../../../bipartite-graph-split.md). Colour the face for $r$ with one colour and alternate along the original [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md). This gives a proper [face colouring](../../../../../../face-colouring.md) with two colours, and at least two are needed because there is an [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) separating distinct faces. Hence the minimum is

$$
\boxed{2.}
$$
