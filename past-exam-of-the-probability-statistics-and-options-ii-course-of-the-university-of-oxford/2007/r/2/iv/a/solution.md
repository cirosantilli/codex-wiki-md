↑ **Parent:** [A](../a.md)

A plane [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) has one [face of a plane graph](../../../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md), so its [dual graph](../../../../../../dual-graph.md) has one [vertex](../../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md). Every original [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is a bridge incident to that same face on both sides, so every dual [edge](../../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) is a loop. Thus the dual drawing in the figure is a bouquet of eight loops, arranged with the displayed nesting inherited from the [tree](../../../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md). It has nine [faces of a plane graph](../../../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md), matching $f^*=n=9$. The nesting matters for which faces meet; a bouquet drawn as eight unrelated petals would have a different face-adjacency [graph](../../../../../../graph-split.md).
