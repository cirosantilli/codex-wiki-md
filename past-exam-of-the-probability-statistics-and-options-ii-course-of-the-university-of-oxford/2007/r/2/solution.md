<h1 id="r/2/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [2](../2.md)

Choose a [spanning tree](../../../../spanning-tree.md) of the connected plane [graph](../../../../graph-split.md). A [tree](../../../../tree-graph-theory-split.md) with $n$ [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) has $n-1$ [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) and one [face of a plane graph](../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md), so $n-(n-1)+1=2$. Add the remaining drawn [edges](../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) one at a time. Each joins two existing [vertices](../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) and closes a [cycle in a graph](../../../../cycle-in-a-graph.md); its interior lies in one current [face of a plane graph](../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md), splitting that [face of a plane graph](../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md) into two by the [Jordan curve theorem](../../../../jordan-curve-theorem.md). Thus $m$ and $f$ increase together while $n$ stays fixed. The quantity $n-m+f$ remains two, proving [Euler formula for a connected planar graph](../../../../euler-formula-for-a-connected-planar-graph.md).
