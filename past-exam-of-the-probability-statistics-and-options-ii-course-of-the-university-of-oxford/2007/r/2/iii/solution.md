<h1 id="r/2/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

For a fixed plane embedding, place one dual [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) in each [face of a plane graph](../../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md) and draw a dual [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) across each original [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md), joining its incident faces. These dual [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) can be drawn without mutual crossings, so this constructs a plane [dual graph](../../../../../dual-graph.md). Each original [vertex](../../../../../vertex-graph-theory.md) corresponds to a dual [face of a plane graph](../../../../../face-of-a-plane-graph.md), while each [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) gives exactly one dual [edge](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md). Hence

$$
\boxed{n^*=f,\qquad m^*=m,\qquad f^*=n.}
$$

Bridges give dual loops and parallel dual [edges](../../../../../edge-of-a-graph.md) are allowed. On the sphere the dual of the resulting embedding is the original embedding, so planarity is preserved in both directions for this dual pair. The geometric [dual graph](../../../../../dual-graph.md) is defined using an embedding; the converse is understood in that setting, rather than as a definition for an arbitrary nonplanar drawing.
