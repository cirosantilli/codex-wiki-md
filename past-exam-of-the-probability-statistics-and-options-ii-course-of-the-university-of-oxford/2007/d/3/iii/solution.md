<h1 id="d/3/iii/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [Iii](../iii.md)

Put $u=x-y$ and $v=x+y$. The square becomes $(-1,1)^2$, with $x=(u+v)/2$, $y=(v-u)/2$ and absolute [Jacobian determinant](../../../../../jacobian-determinant.md) $1/2$. On its support the denominator $v+2$ exceeds one and $|u|<1$, so the nonnegative integrand is bounded by one on a set of finite area; it is therefore [Lebesgue integrable](../../../../../lebesgue-integrable-function.md). The [change of variables formula](../../../../../change-of-variables-formula.md) and [Fubini's theorem](../../../../../fubini-s-theorem.md) give

$$
\int_{\mathbb R^2}f^{2n}
=\frac12\int_{-1}^1u^{2n}\,du\int_{-1}^1(v+2)^{-2n}\,dv
=\boxed{\frac{1-3^{1-2n}}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}}.
$$
