<h1 id="p/3/b/solution">Solution</h1>

↑ **Parent:** [B](../b.md)

For $d=p-q$, all $d(x_i)$ and $d'(x_i)$ vanish. Between each consecutive pair of nodes, [Rolle's theorem](../../../../../rolle-theorem.md) gives one additional zero of $d'$. Along with the $n+1$ nodal zeros, this supplies $2n+1$ distinct zeros on the closed interval. Since $d'$ is a [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) of degree at most $2n$, it must be the zero [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md). Thus $d$ is constant, and $d(x_0)=0$ makes it zero. Hence **the interpolating [polynomial](../../../../../polynomial-split.md) is unique**.

The direct zero count includes the two endpoints; its guaranteed interior count before the degree argument is $2n-1$. After $d'=0$ is proved, $d'$ vanishes at every point of the open interval, so the stronger interior-zero assertion in the source follows as well. Another immediate uniqueness proof is that $d$ has a double zero at every node, so is divisible by $\prod_i(x-x_i)^2$ of degree $2n+2$, although its own degree is at most $2n+1$.
