# Countable sets have zero probability under a density

↑ **Parent:** [Absolutely continuous probability distribution](absolutely-continuous-probability-distribution.md)

If a [random variable](random-variable-split.md) has a [probability density function](probability-density-function.md), each singleton has probability zero because its density has zero [integral](integral-split.md) there. For a [countable set](countable-set.md) $D=\{d_1,d_2,\ldots\}$, remove duplicates to make the events $\{X=d_i\}$ disjoint. [Countable additivity](countable-additivity.md) gives $\mathbb P(X\in D)=\sum_i\mathbb P(X=d_i)=0$. The proof does not extend to uncountable unions merely because each constituent has zero probability.
