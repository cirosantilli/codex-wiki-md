# Phase-conjugate mirror

↑ **Parent:** [Phase conjugation](phase-conjugation.md)

A [phase-conjugate mirror](phase-conjugate-mirror.md) is an optical device that implements [phase conjugation](phase-conjugation.md) at its return surface. It may generate the return using an independently supplied beam, as in [digital optical phase conjugation](digital-optical-phase-conjugation.md); its name specifies the field transformation, rather than a particular reflecting material. Conjugating the transverse [complex amplitude](complex-amplitude.md) and launching the returning longitudinal carrier produces the complete reversed [wavevector](wavevector.md). A spatially constant $\gamma$ leaves the shape of the [wavefront](wavefront.md) unchanged; a spatially varying $\gamma$ would add its own [wavefront error](wavefront-error.md).

An ordinary flat mirror obeys the [law of reflection](law-of-reflection.md). At a surface $z=L$, its reflected [plane wave](plane-wave.md) has [wavevector](wavevector.md) $(k_x,k_y,-k_z)$, whereas a [phase-conjugate mirror](phase-conjugate-mirror.md) returns $(-k_x,-k_y,-k_z)$. Ordinary [specular reflection](specular-reflection.md) preserves the tangential [wavevector](wavevector.md). At normal incidence a single [plane wave](plane-wave.md) can therefore look identical in the two cases, apart from an overall [wave phase](phase-waves.md) and strength; oblique incidence or a curved [wavefront](wavefront.md) reveals the distinction. In boundary-envelope notation an ideal ordinary mirror multiplies the incident [complex amplitude](complex-amplitude.md) by a constant reflection factor $r$, while an ideal [phase-conjugate mirror](phase-conjugate-mirror.md) returns its [complex conjugate](complex-conjugate.md) times $\gamma$.
