# Free strain and blocked magnetostrictive stress

↑ **Parent:** [Magnetostriction](magnetostriction.md)

An [eigenstrain](eigenstrain.md) is the deformation a material would prefer locally without applied [stress](stress.md). In a one-dimensional [linear elasticity](linear-elasticity.md) model, let $E$ be [Young's modulus](young-s-modulus.md), take tension as positive [stress](stress.md), and write $\sigma=E(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\rm mag})$. A free rod has $\sigma=0$ and follows $\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\rm mag}$. A perfectly blocked rod has $\varepsilon=0$ and $\sigma=-E\varepsilon^{\rm mag}$. Thus positive preferred elongation produces compressive [stress](stress.md) when blocked.

For stipulated $E=100\,\mathrm{GPa}$, cross-sectional area $A=1\,\mathrm{mm^2}$, and $\varepsilon^{\rm mag}=20\times10^{-6}$, blocking gives $\sigma=-2\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and axial [force](force-split.md) $F=\sigma A=-2\,\mathrm N$. This idealization ignores changes of [magnetization](magnetization.md) caused by the load itself. A real [magnetostrictive actuator](magnetostrictive-actuator.md) therefore needs both magnetic and mechanical constitutive information: a free-length measurement alone does not determine its loaded motion.
