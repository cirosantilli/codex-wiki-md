# Heat released by hydrogen spin conversion

↑ **Parent:** [Spin isomers of hydrogen](spin-isomers-of-hydrogen.md)

As $t\to0$, the conditional mean rotational [energies](energy-split.md) approach $U_{\rm p}/N=0$ and $U_{\rm o}/N=2k_{\rm B}\theta_r$, using the para $J=0$ level as the common zero. A frozen mixture with ortho fraction $x$ therefore retains $U_{\rm fr}/N\to2xk_{\rm B}\theta_r$. If a bath keeps the sample at low [temperature](temperature-split.md) while a [catalyst](catalyst.md) permits conversion to almost pure [parahydrogen](parahydrogen.md), the rotational [heat](heat-split.md) delivered to the bath tends to $Q_{\rm out}/N=2xk_{\rm B}\theta_r$, or $3k_{\rm B}\theta_r/2$ for [normal hydrogen](normal-hydrogen.md).

One may shift the ortho ladder to its own lowest level for computing its conditional [heat capacity](heat-capacity.md), because a constant [energy](energy-split.md) offset disappears on differentiation at fixed composition. That offset must be restored before comparing species or calculating conversion [heat](heat-split.md). Otherwise the calculation loses precisely the stored rotational [energy](energy-split.md) that conversion releases. The expression above accounts for the internal rotor contribution at fixed bath [temperature](temperature-split.md); it does not model liquid intermolecular [energies](energy-split.md), [latent heat](latent-heat.md), or an adiabatic temperature rise.

A [catalyst](catalyst.md) changes the conversion rate and allows heat removal during cooling; it does not change the free-gas equilibrium weights at a specified [temperature](temperature-split.md). This connects the microscopic exchange rule to refrigeration: delayed conversion supplies a continuing internal [heat](heat-split.md) source. [Rotational Raman spectroscopy](rotational-raman-spectroscopy.md) offers a complementary population probe because its $\Delta J=\pm2$ transitions stay within the even or odd ladder; line intensities require the appropriate species populations and transition response, not a bare $3:1$ intensity rule.
